Van Halen

Van Halen was an American rock band formed in Pasadena, California, in 1973 by brothers Eddie and Alex Van Halen with singer David Lee Roth and bassist Michael Anthony. Credited with restoring hard rock to the forefront of the music scene, the band is known for its energetic live shows and for Eddie Van Halen's virtuosic guitar technique, in particular his popularisation of two-handed tapping. The band sold more than 80 million records worldwide and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007.

History

The band's self-titled debut album (1978), featuring "Runnin' with the Devil" and the guitar instrumental "Eruption", reached number 19 on the Billboard 200 and became one of the best-selling debut albums in the United States. Successive albums — Van Halen II (1979), Women and Children First (1980), Fair Warning (1981) and Diver Down (1982) — consolidated their reputation, and 1984 produced their only Billboard Hot 100 number one single, "Jump".

David Lee Roth left in 1985 and was replaced by Sammy Hagar, with whom the band achieved four consecutive number-one albums: 5150 (1986), OU812 (1988), For Unlawful Carnal Knowledge (1991) and Balance (1995). After a brief period with Gary Cherone (1996–1999) and reunions with both Hagar and Roth, the band released its final studio album, A Different Kind of Truth, in 2012, with Wolfgang Van Halen on bass. Following Eddie Van Halen's death in October 2020, Wolfgang confirmed that the band had ended.
