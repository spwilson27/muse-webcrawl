Dysnomia (moon)

Dysnomia (minor-planet designation 136199 Eris I Dysnomia) is the only known moon of the dwarf planet Eris and is the second-largest known moon of a dwarf planet, after Pluto's moon Charon. It was discovered in September 2005 by Mike Brown and the laser guide star adaptive optics team at the W. M. Keck Observatory.

Characteristics

Dysnomia has an estimated diameter of about 700 km and orbits Eris at a distance of roughly 37,000 km with a period of about 15.8 days in a nearly circular orbit. Its orbit allowed astronomers to determine the mass of Eris precisely, which showed Eris to be slightly more massive than Pluto — a finding that contributed to the IAU's 2006 reclassification of both as dwarf planets.

Name

Dysnomia is named after the Greek goddess of lawlessness, daughter of Eris. Before receiving its official name it was nicknamed "Gabrielle" by its discoverers, after the companion of Xena — Eris itself having been nicknamed "Xena". Before its numbering, the moon's provisional designation was S/2005 (2003 UB313) 1.
