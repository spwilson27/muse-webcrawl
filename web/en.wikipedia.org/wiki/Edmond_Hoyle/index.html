Edmond Hoyle (1672 – 29 August 1769) was an English writer best known for publishing the rules and strategy of card games, above all whist. His name became so synonymous with authoritative rules for games that the phrase "according to Hoyle" entered the English language to mean "by the book" or "in accordance with the accepted rules".

Life and works

Little is known of Hoyle's early life; he may have been a barrister by training. By the 1740s he was giving lessons in whist to fashionable London society. In 1742 he published A Short Treatise on the Game of Whist, containing the laws of the game and mathematical calculations of odds — it was an immediate success and became his signature work, often pirated. He followed it with treatises on piquet (1744), quadrille (1745), brag, backgammon and chess (1761), and on the doctrine of chances.

Legacy

Hoyle's rules for whist, as adopted by the clubs of London, remained the standard code of the game for more than a century, until superseded by a new official code in 1864. Generations of later rulebooks have borne his name — Hoyle's Rules of Games remains in print in updated editions — and "Hoyle" has been used as a brand for card-game software. He died in London in 1769 at the age of 97 and was buried at St Marylebone churchyard. In 1979 he was among the first inductees into the Poker Hall of Fame, in recognition of his foundational role in codifying games of skill and chance.
