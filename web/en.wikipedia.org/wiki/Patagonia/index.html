Patagonia

[Image: Fitz Roy and the Patagonian steppe in Argentina]
Fitz Roy and the Patagonian steppe in Argentina

Patagonia is a geographical region that encompasses the southern end of South America, governed by Argentina and Chile. It includes the southern section of the Andes (with the Northern and Southern Patagonian Ice Fields, the largest ice fields in the Southern Hemisphere outside Antarctica), the arid steppes and deserts of the east, and the subantarctic coasts of Tierra del Fuego.

Named by Magellan's expedition in 1520 after the Patagones, the (exaggeratedly) giant natives described by chronicler Antonio Pigafetta, Patagonia was incorporated into Argentina and Chile only in the late 19th century, after campaigns that devastated Indigenous peoples such as the Tehuelche and Mapuche. It is famous for the Perito Moreno Glacier, Mount Fitz Roy, the Valdés Peninsula's whales and penguins, Welsh settlements in Chubut, and Ushuaia, 'the end of the world'.
