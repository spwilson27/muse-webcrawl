Goldsmith

A goldsmith is a metalworker who specialises in working with gold and other precious metals. Nowadays they mainly specialise in jewellery-making, but historically goldsmiths have also made silverware, platters, goblets, decorative and serviceable utensils, and ceremonial or religious objects.

Goldsmiths have worked gold since prehistoric times, exploiting its malleability and resistance to corrosion. In medieval Europe, goldsmiths organised into guilds, such as the Worshipful Company of Goldsmiths in London, and were entrusted with hallmarking precious metal to guarantee its purity — a practice that survives today. Because of their custody of precious materials, goldsmiths in England and elsewhere became early bankers: depositors left gold with them for safekeeping, receiving notes that evolved into paper money, a system known as goldsmith banking.

Skills and techniques

A goldsmith must be skilled in metal forming, soldering, sawing, filing, forging, casting, and polishing, as well as related arts such as engraving, enamelling, and stone-setting. Traditionally the skills were transmitted through apprenticeships lasting many years. Modern goldsmiths add computer-aided design, laser welding, and lost-wax casting techniques to the traditional repertoire.
