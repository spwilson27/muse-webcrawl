Puteaux (French pronunciation: [pyto]) is a commune in the western suburbs of Paris, France, in the department of Hauts-de-Seine. It is located on the left bank of the river Seine, about 8.7 kilometres (5.4 mi) from the centre of Paris, and is bordered by Neuilly-sur-Seine and Courbevoie to the north, Suresnes to the west and the Bois de Boulogne across the river to the south.

A large part of the La Défense business district — the largest purpose-built business district in Europe — lies within the boundaries of Puteaux (the remainder falling in Courbevoie and Nanterre). The commune therefore hosts the headquarters and offices of many major French and international companies, and its modern skyline of office towers contrasts with the older residential streets near the Seine.

History

The name Puteaux is traditionally derived from the Latin puteoli, "little wells," a reference to the wells dug in the locality's limestone soil. The village grew up along the road from Paris to Saint-Germain, and for centuries its economy rested on vineyards, market gardening, rose growing and stone quarrying. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries Puteaux industrialised, becoming known for automobile manufacturing, armaments, printing inks and perfumery; the island in the Seine opposite the town, the Île de Puteaux, developed as a sporting and leisure ground.

Transport

Puteaux is served by Puteaux station on the Transilien suburban rail network, by tramway line T2, and by Esplanade de La Défense and La Défense stations on Paris Métro Line 1 within or at the edge of the La Défense quarter. Several bridges, including the Pont de Neuilly and the Pont de Puteaux, connect the commune across the Seine.

Culture

In the early twentieth century the town gave its name to the Puteaux Group, a circle of artists associated with Cubism — among them Jacques Villon, František Kupka, Albert Gleizes and Jean Metzinger — who met at Villon's studio in the town and mounted the landmark Salon des Indépendants exhibition of 1911. The Section d'Or exhibition of 1912 grew out of this circle.

Notable people

The commune has been home to figures from the arts, sport and business; the actress and singer Fréhel was born in Paris to a family from the area, and the town has long-standing links with French cycling and rowing through its Seine-side sporting facilities.
