French Indochina

French Indochina was a grouping of French colonial territories in Southeast Asia from 1887 until 1954, comprising the present-day countries of Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia.

Formation

The federation united the colony of Cochinchina with the protectorates of Annam, Tonkin, Cambodia, and later Laos, administered from Hanoi. France exploited the region's rice, rubber, and minerals, built infrastructure, and faced growing nationalist resistance, notably from the Viet Minh under Ho Chi Minh.

End

After Japanese occupation during the Second World War, France's attempt to restore control led to the First Indochina War, ending with the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu and the 1954 Geneva Accords, which dissolved Indochina into independent states and partitioned Vietnam.
