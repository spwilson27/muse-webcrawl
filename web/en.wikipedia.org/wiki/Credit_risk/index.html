Credit risk

Credit risk is the chance that a borrower does not repay a loan or meet contractual obligations. It refers to the risk that a lender may not receive the owed principal and interest, resulting in interruption of cash flows and increased costs for collection.

Credit risk

Also called: Default risk
Managed by: Credit analysis, collateral, diversification

Types

Credit risk includes sovereign risk, when a government is unwilling or unable to pay; concentration risk, from large exposure to a single borrower or sector; and downgrade risk, when a credit rating agency lowers a borrower's rating.

Assessment

Lenders assess creditworthiness using the five Cs — character, capacity, capital, collateral and conditions — as well as credit scores, financial statements and credit ratings from agencies such as Moody's, S&P and Fitch. Expected loss is commonly modelled as probability of default × loss given default × exposure at default.

Mitigation

Techniques include collateral, loan covenants, diversification, credit insurance and derivatives such as a credit default swap. Basel accords require banks to hold capital against credit risk.
