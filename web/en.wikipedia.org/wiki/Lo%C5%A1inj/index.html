Lošinj (Italian: Lussino) is a Croatian island in the northern Adriatic Sea, in the Kvarner Gulf south of the city of Rijeka. It is known for its mild climate, pine forests, maritime heritage and health tourism, and together with the neighboring island of Cres — to which it is connected by a swing bridge at Osor — it forms part of Primorje-Gorski Kotar County.

Geography

The island stretches about 33 km from north to south. Its highest peak, Televrin on Mount Osorščica, rises to 588 m. The main settlements are Mali Lošinj, the largest town, and the older, picturesque Veli Lošinj. A channel at Privlaka, dug in Roman times, separates Lošinj from Cres for shipping.

History

Settled since prehistory, Lošinj was ruled in turn by Rome, Byzantium, Venice, Austria-Hungary, Italy and Yugoslavia before becoming part of Croatia. In the nineteenth century Mali Lošinj was one of the Adriatic's great shipbuilding and shipping centres, and its captains' villas and a famed maritime school date from this era. The island's clean air made it an Austro-Hungarian climatic health resort.

Tourism and nature

Today tourism dominates the economy. The surrounding waters are home to a resident population of bottlenose dolphins, protected by a marine institute based in Veli Lošinj, and the island markets itself as an "island of vitality" with aromatherapy gardens and walking trails.
