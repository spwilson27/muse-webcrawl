Sanitary and phytosanitary measures

Sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) measures are laws, regulations, and procedures applied by governments to protect human, animal, or plant life or health from risks arising from additives, contaminants, toxins, disease-causing organisms in food, and from pests and diseases carried by animals, plants, or their products. "Sanitary" measures relate to human and animal health; "phytosanitary" measures relate to plant health.

The WTO SPS Agreement

The World Trade Organization's Agreement on the Application of Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures, concluded in the Uruguay Round and in force since 1995, sets the rules governing SPS measures in international trade. It affirms members' right to protect health but requires that measures be applied only to the extent necessary, be based on scientific principles and risk assessment, and not arbitrarily discriminate between countries or constitute disguised restrictions on trade.

The agreement encourages harmonization with international standards set by three recognized bodies — the Codex Alimentarius Commission (food safety), the World Organisation for Animal Health (animal health), and the International Plant Protection Convention (plant health), known collectively as the "three sisters". Measures conforming to these standards are presumed consistent with the SPS Agreement.

Trade disputes

Because SPS measures can act as non-tariff barriers, they have featured in prominent WTO disputes, including cases over hormone-treated beef, genetically modified organisms, and disease-related import bans on poultry and livestock. The SPS Committee in Geneva oversees implementation and provides a forum for trade concerns.
