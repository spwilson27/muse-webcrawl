Redaction


A heavily redacted government document.

Redaction is a form of editing in which a document is prepared for publication by removing, obscuring, or censoring material. Redacted passages typically appear as solid black bars or blanked-out spaces, indicating that sensitive information has been withheld while the remainder of the document remains readable.

Purposes

Redaction is used to protect classified information, personal privacy, trade secrets, ongoing investigations, and legally privileged material. Government agencies releasing documents under freedom of information laws, such as the United States Freedom of Information Act, routinely redact names, addresses, and operational details before publication.

Methods and failures

Historically, redaction was done with black marker ink or by physically cutting text from photocopies. In the digital era, improper redaction has repeatedly caused embarrassment: applying a black rectangle as a transparent overlay in a PDF, rather than removing the underlying text, allows the hidden material to be recovered by copying and pasting. Several high-profile legal filings and government reports have leaked information in this way. Proper digital redaction permanently deletes the underlying text and metadata.

In textual scholarship

In biblical and literary studies, "redaction" also refers to the process by which earlier source texts were edited and combined into a later composite work, studied by redaction criticism. The hypothetical editors are called redactors.
