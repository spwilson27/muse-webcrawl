Giovanni "Gianni" Agnelli (12 March 1921 – 24 January 2003), nicknamed L'Avvocato ("The Lawyer"), was an Italian industrialist and the principal shareholder of Fiat. As the head of Fiat, he controlled 4.4% of Italy's GDP, 3.1% of its industrial workforce, and 16.5% of its industrial investment in research. He was the richest man in modern Italian history.

Early life

Agnelli was born in Turin, the son of Edoardo Agnelli and Virginia Bourbon del Monte, and the grandson of Giovanni Agnelli, founder of Fiat. Educated in Turin, he took a law degree — the source of his nickname — and served as a cavalry officer during the Second World War. After his father's early death in 1945, control of the company passed through regents until Gianni took over.

Fiat and public life

Agnelli became president of Fiat in 1966 and led its expansion into a diversified conglomerate spanning automobiles, trucks, tractors, aerospace, publishing (La Stampa) and finance, largely through the family holding company IFI. He was also closely associated with the football club Juventus, owned by the family since 1923. Appointed senator for life in 1991, he remained Italy's most influential businessman until his death in 2003. He was also a celebrated style icon, famous for wearing his wristwatch over his shirt cuff.
