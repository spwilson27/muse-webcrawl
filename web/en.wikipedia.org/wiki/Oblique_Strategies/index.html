Oblique Strategies (subtitled Over One Hundred Worthwhile Dilemmas) is a deck of cards created by the musician Brian Eno and the artist Peter Schmidt, first published in 1975. Each card bears a terse, often cryptic instruction or aphorism — such as "Honour thy error as a hidden intention", "Use an old idea", "Emphasise differences" or "Ask your body" — intended to break creative deadlock by imposing an unexpected constraint or change of perspective.

Origins

Eno and Schmidt had independently been keeping lists of working principles: Schmidt's were gathered in his 1970s notes (published as The Thoughts Behind the Thoughts), while Eno used similar prompts in the recording studio. Comparing their lists, they combined them into a single deck, produced in a limited edition in 1975. Eno famously drew on the cards while producing records for David Bowie, Talking Heads and others, using a drawn card as an instruction for how to proceed when a session stalled.

Editions and use

Several editions have appeared (1975, 1978, 1979, 1996 and an expanded 2001 edition), with wording revised and cards added or dropped; some cards in early decks were left blank. The deck has been emulated in software, websites and mobile apps, and its method — a form of lateral thinking related to the I Ching and to surrealist games — has been adopted by writers, designers and programmers as a general creativity tool.

Reception

Oblique Strategies is regarded as a landmark of creative practice: an early, portable "creativity machine", it reframed the artist's block as a problem soluble by arbitrary but binding rules. The cards are frequently cited by musicians and producers, and original first editions are collectable items.
