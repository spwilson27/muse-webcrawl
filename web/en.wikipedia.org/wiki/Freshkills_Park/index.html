Freshkills Park

The capped landfill mounds, now grassland, rise up to about 225 feet.

Freshkills Park is a public park being built atop the former Fresh Kills Landfill on the western shore of Staten Island in New York City. At about 2,200 acres (890 ha), it will be nearly three times the size of Central Park, making it the largest park developed in New York City in over a century. The site takes its name from Fresh Kill, the tidal creek (from the Dutch versche kill, "fresh stream") that runs through it into the Arthur Kill.

History

The landfill opened in 1948, intended as a temporary facility, and grew into what was for decades the largest landfill in the world, receiving much of New York City's refuse — at its peak some 29,000 tons per day. Its mounds, rising to roughly 225 feet (69 m), became among the highest points on the eastern seaboard south of Maine. Local opposition led to a 1996 agreement to close it; the last regular barge of trash arrived in March 2001. The site was briefly reopened after the September 11 attacks to receive and sift debris from the World Trade Center.

The park

Following a design competition won in 2003 by landscape architect James Corner Field Operations, the landfill is being capped with an impermeable cover, soil and plantings, with systems to collect landfill gas and leachate. The park is being opened in phases through the 2030s, with sections such as Schmul Park, the Owl Hollow fields and the North Park phase already accessible, offering meadows, wetlands, creeks for kayaking, trails and wildlife habitat on a landscape built entirely over the capped waste.
