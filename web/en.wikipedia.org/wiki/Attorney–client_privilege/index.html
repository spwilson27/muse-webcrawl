Attorney–client privilege

Attorney–client privilege is a legal privilege in common law jurisdictions that protects confidential communications between a client and their lawyer made for the purpose of obtaining or giving legal advice. Neither the client nor the lawyer can generally be compelled to disclose such communications in legal proceedings.

Scope

The privilege belongs to the client, who alone may waive it. It generally covers oral and written communications made in confidence, and survives the end of the representation and the client's death. It does not protect the underlying facts, pre-existing documents, or communications made in furtherance of a crime or fraud (the crime-fraud exception).

Rationale

The privilege is intended to encourage full and frank disclosure by clients to their lawyers, enabling effective legal advice and representation. Related doctrines include the work-product doctrine and, in civil law systems, professional secrecy obligations.
