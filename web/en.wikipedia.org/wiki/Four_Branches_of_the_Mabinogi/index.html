Four Branches of the Mabinogi

The Four Branches of the Mabinogi (Welsh: Pedair Cainc y Mabinogi) are the earliest prose stories in the literature of Britain. Originally written in Wales in Middle Welsh, they are preserved in the White Book of Rhydderch (c. 1350) and the Red Book of Hergest (c. 1382–1410). The stories draw on pre-Christian Celtic mythology, international folktale motifs, and early medieval Welsh tradition.

The four branches

  • Pwyll Pendefig Dyfed ("Pwyll, Prince of Dyfed") — Pwyll exchanges places with Arawn, king of the otherworld Annwn, for a year, and later marries Rhiannon; their son Pryderi is born and mysteriously lost and restored.
  • Branwen ferch Llŷr ("Branwen, Daughter of Llŷr") — Branwen's marriage to the king of Ireland leads to insult, war between Britain and Ireland, and the death of nearly all the warriors of both islands.
  • Manawydan fab Llŷr ("Manawydan, Son of Llŷr") — Manawydan and Pryderi return to a Dyfed emptied by an enchantment, which they break by outwitting the enchanter Llwyd ap Cil Coed.
  • Math fab Mathonwy ("Math, Son of Mathonwy") — the magician-king Math, his nephews Gwydion and Gilfaethwy, the creation of Blodeuwedd from flowers, and the tragic love of Lleu Llaw Gyffes and Blodeuwedd.

Significance

The Four Branches form the core of the collection popularly known as the Mabinogion, a title popularised by Lady Charlotte Guest's 19th-century English translation. They are foundational texts of Welsh literature and a major source for Celtic mythology.
