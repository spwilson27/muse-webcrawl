The Maracanã Stadium, officially Estádio Jornalista Mário Filho, is a football stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Opened in 1950 for the FIFA World Cup, it once held crowds approaching 200,000 and remains one of the most famous stadiums in the world.

History

In the 1950 World Cup final round decider, Uruguay famously defeated Brazil 2–1 before a huge crowd in the match remembered as the Maracanaço. The stadium was extensively rebuilt for the 2014 FIFA World Cup, hosting the final in which Germany beat Argentina, and staged the opening and closing ceremonies of the 2016 Summer Olympics.

Events

Beyond football, Maracanã has hosted concerts, papal masses and the 2007 Pan American Games. It is the home ground of Flamengo and Fluminense, whose Fla–Flu derby is one of Brazil's fiercest rivalries.

Records

The official record attendance was 194,603 for Brazil vs Paraguay in 1963; modern all-seater capacity is under 80,000 after renovations for safety and comfort.
