Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), also called benign enlargement of the prostate, is a non-cancerous increase in the size of the prostate gland. It is one of the most common conditions of ageing men: histological BPH is present in about half of men in their sixties and up to 90% of men over 85. Although the enlargement itself is benign, growth of the transition zone of the gland can compress the urethra and obstruct urine flow.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms are grouped as lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS): storage symptoms such as frequency, urgency and nocturia (waking at night to urinate), and voiding symptoms such as weak stream, hesitancy, intermittency, straining and a feeling of incomplete emptying. Complications include acute urinary retention, recurrent urinary tract infections, bladder stones and, in severe untreated cases, kidney damage. Severity is often quantified with the International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS).

Causes and diagnosis

BPH results from proliferation of both stromal and epithelial cells in the periurethral transition zone, driven by ageing and androgens — particularly dihydrotestosterone (DHT), produced from testosterone by the enzyme 5-alpha-reductase. Diagnosis rests on history, digital rectal examination, urinalysis, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing (also used to screen for prostate cancer, which BPH does not cause and does not progress to), uroflowmetry and ultrasound measurement of gland volume and post-void residual urine.

Treatment

Mild symptoms are managed with watchful waiting and lifestyle changes (reducing evening fluids, caffeine and alcohol). First-line medicines are alpha blockers such as tamsulosin, which relax prostatic smooth muscle, and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors such as finasteride and dutasteride, which shrink the gland over months; combination therapy is common. Procedural options range from the long-standard transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) and laser enucleation to newer minimally invasive treatments such as prostatic urethral lift, water vapour therapy and prostate artery embolisation.
