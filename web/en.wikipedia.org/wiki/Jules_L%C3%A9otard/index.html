Jules Léotard

Born1 March 1838, Toulouse, France
Died17 August 1870, Toulouse, France
OccupationAcrobat, trapeze artist
Known forInventing the flying trapeze; the leotard garment

Jules Léotard (1838–1870) was a French acrobat who developed the flying trapeze act and performed the first flying trapeze performance. The one-piece garment worn by gymnasts and dancers, the leotard, is named after him.

Career

The son of a gymnastics instructor in Toulouse, Léotard trained on apparatus his father rigged above the family swimming pool. He gave his first public trapeze performance at the Cirque Napoléon in Paris in 1859, leaping between trapezes suspended over a stack of mattresses.

Fame

Léotard performed across Europe, including in London, where his daring act made him a celebrity. He wore a close-fitting knitted costume of his own design to allow freedom of movement, and the garment took his name. He inspired the 1867 popular song "The Daring Young Man on the Flying Trapeze".

Death

His career was cut short when he died in 1870, aged 32, reportedly of an infectious disease, and he was buried in Toulouse.
