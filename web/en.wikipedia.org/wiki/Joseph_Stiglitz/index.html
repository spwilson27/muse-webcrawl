Joseph Eugene Stiglitz (born 9 February 1943) is an American economist, a founder of the economics of information, and a prominent critic of market fundamentalism and of the management of Globalization. He shared the 2001 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (with George Akerlof and Michael Spence) for analyses of markets with asymmetric information.

Career and ideas

Stiglitz's models showed how screening, signalling, adverse selection and moral hazard pervade credit, insurance and labour markets (including efficiency-wage theory with Shapiro), undermining the assumption that markets are generally efficient — a body of work central to modern Microeconomics. He chaired President Clinton's Council of Economic Advisers (1995–97) and was chief economist of the World Bank (1997–2000), resigning after clashes over IMF crisis policy in East Asia, recounted in Globalization and Its Discontents (2002). His later work, including The Price of Inequality (2012), attacks rising inequality and rent-seeking and argues for active government.
