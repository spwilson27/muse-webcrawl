The Apgar score is a method of quickly assessing the health of a newborn baby immediately after birth. It was devised in 1952 by the American anaesthesiologist Virginia Apgar and has since become standard practice worldwide. The score is determined by evaluating the newborn on five criteria — Appearance (skin colour), Pulse (heart rate), Grimace (reflex irritability), Activity (muscle tone), and Respiration — each scored 0, 1, or 2, for a maximum of 10. The backronym spells Apgar's own surname.

Interpretation

The test is performed at one and five minutes after birth, and may be repeated if the score remains low. Scores of 7 and above are generally regarded as normal; 4–6 is fairly low and 0–3 critically low, indicating that the baby needs immediate resuscitation. The score guides the urgency of care but is not by itself a predictor of long-term health, and it was not designed to forecast neurological outcome. Virginia Apgar presented the system in 1952 and published it in 1953; she later developed it further with collaborators, and the score remains a foundational measure in neonatology and obstetrics.
