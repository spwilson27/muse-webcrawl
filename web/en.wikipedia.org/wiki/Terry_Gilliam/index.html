Terry Gilliam

Born: November 22, 1940, Minneapolis, Minnesota, U.S.
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, animator, actor
Years active: 1968–present

Terrence Vance Gilliam (born November 22, 1940) is an American-born British filmmaker, comedian, collage animator, and actor. He gained fame as a member of the Monty Python comedy troupe, where he was the animator of the group's surreal cut-out animations and appeared in sketches. He later established a career as a film director whose work is known for its highly imaginative fantasy worlds, baroque visual style, and themes of imagination versus oppressive bureaucracy.

Film career

Gilliam co-directed Monty Python and the Holy Grail (1975) and went on to direct Jabberwocky (1977), Time Bandits (1981), Brazil (1985), The Adventures of Baron Munchausen (1988), The Fisher King (1991), 12 Monkeys (1995), Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1998), The Brothers Grimm (2005), Tideland (2005), The Imaginarium of Doctor Parnassus (2009), The Zero Theorem (2013), and The Man Who Killed Don Quixote (2018), the last a project he pursued for nearly three decades. His films have often struggled at the box office but developed strong cult followings, and Brazil and 12 Monkeys are frequently ranked among the greatest films of their kind.

Style

Gilliam's films are characterized by wide-angle lenses, tilted (Dutch) camera angles, grotesque and whimsical production design, and dystopian or fantastical settings in which dreamlike imagination collides with authoritarian reality.
