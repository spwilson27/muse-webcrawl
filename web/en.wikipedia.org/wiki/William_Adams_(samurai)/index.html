William Adams (24 September 1564 – 16 May 1620), known in Japan as Miura Anjin ("the pilot of Miura"), was an English navigator who became the first Englishman to reach Japan. He rose to become a trusted advisor to the shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu, was granted the rank of samurai and the status of hatamoto, and played a key role in opening Dutch and English trade with Japan.

Voyage to Japan

Adams served as pilot of the Dutch ship Liefde, part of a five-ship expedition that left Rotterdam in 1598 seeking trade with the East Indies via the Strait of Magellan. After a disastrous Pacific crossing, the storm-battered Liefde reached Kyushu in April 1600 with only a handful of surviving crew.

Service to Tokugawa Ieyasu

Adams was brought before Tokugawa Ieyasu, who questioned him closely about navigation, shipbuilding and world geography. Impressed, Ieyasu retained him at court, where Adams supervised the construction of Japan's first Western-style sailing ships and advised on trade and foreign affairs. He was granted an estate at Miura, a Japanese wife, and the two swords of a samurai — an unprecedented honour for a foreigner.

Trade and later life

Adams helped secure trading privileges for the Dutch East India Company in 1609 and for the English East India Company, which established a factory at Hirado in 1613. He undertook trading voyages to Southeast Asia in the 1610s but was never permitted to return to England. He died at Hirado in 1620.

In popular culture

Adams's life inspired James Clavell's 1975 novel Shōgun, in which he appears as John Blackthorne, and its television adaptations.
