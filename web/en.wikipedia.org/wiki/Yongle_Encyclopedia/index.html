The Yongle Encyclopedia, or Yongle Dadian (永樂大典, "Great Canon of the Yongle Era"), was a Chinese leishu encyclopedia commissioned in 1403 by the Yongle Emperor of the Ming dynasty and completed in 1408. Employing some 2,000–3,000 scholars at Nanjing, it gathered the contents of thousands of books — history, geography, philosophy, arts, sciences and literature — into 22,937 manuscript chapters bound in 11,095 volumes, comprising about 370 million Chinese characters. In total physical size it remained the world's largest general encyclopedia until surpassed by Wikipedia some 600 years later.

Compilation

The work was first drafted under the scholar Xie Jin in 1404, then comprehensively revised and expanded under the direction of Xie Jin and the monk-counsellor Yao Guangxiao. Unlike most leishu, which excerpted and summarised, the Dadian reproduced its source texts in full under headings arranged by rhyme, preserving many works that would otherwise have been lost entirely. Only one manuscript copy of the original was made; it was moved to Beijing when the Yongle Emperor transferred the capital there.

Loss and survival

The original Nanjing manuscript disappeared — its fate is unknown — and a hand-copy made under the Jiajing Emperor in 1562–1567 became the sole exemplar. Over the following centuries volumes were progressively lost to fire, theft and war; the sack of Beijing during the Boxer Rebellion of 1900 destroyed many of those remaining in the Hanlin Academy. Today only about 400 volumes — roughly 3.5 percent of the work — survive, scattered among libraries and museums in China and around the world, including the National Library of China and the British Library.

Legacy

Surviving volumes are precious because scholars have extracted hundreds of otherwise-lost books from them; Qing-dynasty editors recovered some 385 complete works from the encyclopedia when compiling the Siku Quanshu. Facsimile and digital editions have been published to reunite the scattered volumes.
