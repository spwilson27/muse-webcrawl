Gymnastics is a group of sports that requires strength, flexibility, balance, agility, coordination and endurance, performed on apparatus or on the floor. The most widely practised discipline is artistic gymnastics: men compete on floor exercise, pommel horse, still rings, vault, parallel bars and horizontal bar; women on vault, uneven bars, balance beam and floor exercise. Other disciplines include rhythmic gymnastics (women only at the Olympics, using ribbon, hoop, ball and clubs) and trampoline.

Gymnastics has been part of every modern Olympic Games since 1896. Its defining performers include Nadia Comăneci, whose "perfect 10" at Montreal 1976 changed the sport's scoring culture, and Simone Biles, the most decorated gymnast in history, whose difficulty-defying skills include several elements named after her; she returned from a two-year break to lead the United States to team gold at Paris 2024. Japan's Kōhei Uchimura is widely considered the greatest male gymnast. Modern scoring uses separate difficulty (D) and execution (E) panels, an open-ended system introduced in 2006.
