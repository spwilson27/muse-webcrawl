Paresis

Paresis is a medical condition in which voluntary movement is reduced — a partial loss of muscle function, or weakness, as distinct from paralysis (or plegia), the total loss of function in the affected part. The term derives from Greek paresis, "letting go" or "slackening".

Types

Paresis is described by its distribution: monoparesis (one limb), hemiparesis (one side of the body, commonly from stroke), paraparesis (both legs), and tetraparesis/quadriparesis (all four limbs). Causes include stroke, multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy, peripheral nerve injury, and spinal cord lesions. "General paresis" or "general paralysis of the insane" was the historical name for a neuropsychiatric manifestation of late-stage syphilis (neurosyphilis), once a common cause of asylum admissions and now rare where penicillin is available.

Assessment

Muscle power is graded clinically on the Medical Research Council scale from 0 (no contraction) to 5 (normal power). Paresis is scored in the intermediate grades; management depends on treating the underlying cause and on rehabilitation and physiotherapy.
