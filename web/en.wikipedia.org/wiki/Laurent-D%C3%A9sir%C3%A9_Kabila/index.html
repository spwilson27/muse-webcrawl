Laurent-Désiré Kabila

Born: 27 November 1939, Jadotville, Belgian Congo

Died: 16 January 2001 (aged 61), Kinshasa, DR Congo

Office: President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (1997–2001)

Laurent-Désiré Kabila (27 November 1939 – 16 January 2001) was a Congolese rebel leader and politician who served as the third President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from 1997 until his assassination in 2001. A veteran opponent of Mobutu Sese Seko, he led the Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo (AFDL), backed by Rwanda and Uganda, in the First Congo War, overthrowing Mobutu and renaming Zaire the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Early career

Born in Jadotville (now Likasi), Kabila studied political philosophy in France and attended the University of Dar es Salaam. A follower of Patrice Lumumba, he fought in the Simba rebellion of 1964–65, with support that included a small detachment led briefly by Che Guevara. He later ran the Hewa Bora maquis in South Kivu before going into exile in Tanzania, where he spent years in business and as a marginal opposition figure.

Presidency

Kabila entered Kinshasa in May 1997. His rule became increasingly authoritarian; in 1998 he ordered his Rwandan and Ugandan backers out of the country, triggering the Second Congo War in which those states backed rebels against him while Angola, Zimbabwe, and Namibia supported him. On 16 January 2001 he was shot by one of his bodyguards, Rachidi Kasereka, and died; his son Joseph Kabila succeeded him.
