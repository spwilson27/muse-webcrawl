Juan Ramón Jiménez Mantecón (23 December 1881 – 29 May 1958) was a Spanish poet who received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1956 "for his lyrical poetry, which in Spanish language constitutes an example of high spirit and artistical purity." A central figure of the Generation of '27's predecessors and of Spanish modernism (modernismo), he is best known internationally for the prose-poem cycle Platero y yo ("Platero and I"), about a poet and his little silver donkey.

Early life and early poetry

Born in Moguer, in the province of Huelva, Jiménez studied law and painting briefly at the University of Seville before devoting himself to poetry under the influence of Rubén Darío. His first books, Almas de violeta and Ninfeas (both 1900), announced a melancholic, musical style; the death of his father in 1900 precipitated a depression that required stays in sanatoriums in France and Madrid. In Madrid he moved in the circle of the Residencia de Estudiantes and became a mentor to younger poets of the Generation of '27, including Federico García Lorca.

Maturity and "pure poetry"

Jiménez progressively stripped his verse of ornament in pursuit of poesía pura ("pure poetry"). Platero y yo (1914) is a lyrical portrait of Andalusian village life. His marriage in New York in 1916 to Zenobia Camprubí — his lifelong collaborator and translator, with whom he translated Tagore into Spanish — inspired Diario de un poeta recién casado (1917), a landmark of Spanish free verse. In the 1920s and 1930s he edited influential literary magazines and refined his "obra," continually revising his complete poems.

Exile and Nobel Prize

At the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War in 1936, Jiménez — a supporter of the Republic — went into exile, living in Cuba, the United States, and finally Puerto Rico, where he taught at the University of Puerto Rico. He never returned to Spain. In 1956 he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature; three days after the announcement, Zenobia died of cancer, and Jiménez never recovered from the loss. He died in San Juan on 29 May 1958. Their remains were transferred to Moguer in 1959.

Legacy

Jiménez's conception of poetry as an absolute pursuit of beauty and truth shaped modern Spanish verse, and his mentorship formed a bridge between the Generation of 1898 and the Generation of '27. Platero y yo remains one of the most widely read works in Spanish, adapted for children and set to music by Mario Castelnuovo-Tedesco.
