Trail running

Trail running is a sport-activity that combines running and hiking on trails in natural environments, often in mountainous terrain, as opposed to running on paved roads or tracks. It is similar to both mountain running and fell running. Courses may cross hills, mountains, deserts, forests and narrow paths, with significant elevation gain and loss.

Equipment

Trail shoes differ from road shoes in having aggressive tread, toe protection and often a lower heel-to-toe drop. Runners in long events carry hydration vests, food, and mandatory safety kit that may include a waterproof jacket, map, compass, whistle and emergency blanket; races in exposed terrain enforce kit checks.

Races

The sport's flagship events include the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc (UTMB) in the Alps, the Western States Endurance Run in California, and the Hardrock Hundred Mile Endurance Run in Colorado. Distances range from short 10 km events to ultramarathons over 200 km. The International Trail Running Association (ITRA), founded in 2013, provides a points system used to rate runners and qualify them for major races.

Growth

Trail running grew rapidly in the 2000s and 2010s as an alternative to road racing, combining endurance sport with access to wild landscapes. Governing arrangements vary: in some countries it falls under athletics federations, while mountain running has its own world championships under World Athletics.
