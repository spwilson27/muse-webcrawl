Federal Security Service

The Federal Security Service of the Russian Federation (FSB; Russian: Федеральная служба безопасности, ФСБ) is the principal security agency of Russia and the main successor agency to the Soviet Union's KGB.

Federal Security Service

Formed: 3 April 1995
Preceding agency: FSK; KGB (Soviet)
Headquarters: Lubyanka Building, Moscow
Director: Alexander Bortnikov (since 2008)

History

After the dissolution of the KGB in 1991, its domestic functions passed through the Ministry of Security and the Federal Counterintelligence Service (FSK) before President Boris Yeltsin created the FSB in 1995. Under Vladimir Putin, himself FSB director in 1998-99, the service's powers and budget expanded greatly.

Roles and powers

The FSB is responsible for internal security, counter-intelligence, counter-terrorism, border security (via the Border Service), surveillance, and combating organized crime and drug smuggling. Its special forces include the Alpha and Vympel groups of the Special Purpose Centre.

Controversies

The FSB has been accused by critics and foreign governments of involvement in assassinations, including the 2006 poisoning of Alexander Litvinenko in London, suppression of dissent, and cyber operations. Russian authorities deny many allegations.
