Donato di Niccolò di Betto Bardi (c. 1386 – 13 December 1466), better known as Donatello, was an Italian sculptor of the Renaissance. Born in Florence, he studied classical sculpture and developed a complete knowledge of the field, exhibiting a versatile talent. Donatello worked with stone, bronze, wood, clay, stucco, and wax, and he developed his own style of bas-relief, the rilievo stiacciato ("flattened relief"), which created far greater depth than conventional low relief.

Donatello worked in Florence, Prato, Siena and Padua, primarily for patrons including the Medici family, the Florentine cathedral authorities (Opera del Duomo), and the Republic of Venice. He is widely considered among the founders of the Renaissance in sculpture along with his near-contemporary Filippo Brunelleschi.

Early life and career

Donatello was the son of Niccolò di Betto Bardi, a member of the Florentine Wool Combers Guild. He was educated in the workshop of Lorenzo Ghiberti, and first appears in records assisting Ghiberti on the north doors of the Florence Baptistery. His early marble figures for Florence Cathedral and Orsanmichele — including the Saint George (c. 1415–1417) — established his reputation for psychologically intense, naturalistic sculpture.

Major works

David

Donatello's bronze David (c. 1440s) is famous as the first unsupported standing nude figure cast since classical antiquity. Standing over Goliath's severed head, the youthful David wears only a hat and boots. The statue, commissioned by Cosimo de' Medici, is now in the Bargello Museum in Florence.

Gattamelata

During his decade in Padua (1443–1453), Donatello created the monumental bronze equestrian statue of the condottiere Erasmo da Narni, nicknamed Gattamelata, in the Piazza del Santo. It was the first full-scale bronze equestrian monument of the Renaissance, modelled on ancient Roman examples such as the statue of Marcus Aurelius.

Penitent Magdalene

The wooden Penitent Magdalene (c. 1453–1455), carved on his return to Florence, shows an emaciated, ageing Mary Magdalene, a striking departure from conventional idealised depictions, and is held in the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo.

Style and legacy

Donatello's work combined classical influence with an emotional realism new to the period: his figures express introspection, tension and humility as readily as triumph. His innovations — the stiacciato relief, the revived bronze nude, the Renaissance equestrian monument — profoundly influenced later sculptors, including Michelangelo.
