Indore State
Princely state of India, 1818–1948
Capital: Indore (Maheshwar earlier)
Ruled by the Holkar dynasty

Indore State, also known as the Holkar State, was a princely state of India during the British Raj, ruled by the Holkar dynasty of the Maratha Confederacy. Centred on the city of Indore in the Malwa region of present-day Madhya Pradesh, it was one of the most important Maratha states, entitled to a 19-gun salute.

History

The state was founded by Malhar Rao Holkar, a commander in the service of the Peshwa, who received the Indore territory in the 1730s. His daughter-in-law Ahilyabai Holkar, who ruled from Maheshwar in the later eighteenth century, became renowned for her administration, public works and temple building. The Holkars fought the British in the Third Anglo-Maratha War, and by the Treaty of Mandsaur in 1818 Indore became a princely state under British paramountcy.

Later rulers included Yashwantrao Holkar II, the last ruling Maharaja, who acceded the state to the Dominion of India in 1948; Indore then became part of Madhya Bharat and subsequently of Madhya Pradesh.
