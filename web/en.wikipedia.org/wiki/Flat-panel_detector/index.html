Flat-panel detector

Flat-panel detectors are a class of solid-state x-ray digital radiography devices similar in principle to the image sensors used in digital photography and video. They are used in both projective radiography and as an alternative to x-ray image intensifiers (IIs) in fluoroscopy equipment.

Principles

Flat-panel detectors convert X-rays into electrical charges. Two main technologies exist: indirect conversion, in which a scintillator such as caesium iodide converts X-rays to light that is then detected by a photodiode array, and direct conversion, in which a photoconductor such as amorphous selenium converts X-ray photons directly into charge.

Advantages

Compared with film and image intensifiers, flat-panel detectors offer higher spatial resolution, wider dynamic range, less geometric distortion, and immediate digital images suitable for picture archiving and communication systems (PACS).

Applications

They are used in general radiography, mammography, fluoroscopy, cone-beam computed tomography, and industrial non-destructive testing.
