Dorothea of Brandenburg

Dorothea of Brandenburg (1430/1431 – 10 November 1495) was Queen of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden as the wife of King Christian I of Denmark. She was the daughter of John, Margrave of Brandenburg-Kulmbach, known as John the Alchemist, and Barbara of Saxe-Wittenberg. She served twice as regent of Denmark and was a notable landowner and political figure in the Kalmar Union.

Marriages

Dorothea was first married in 1445 to Christopher III of Denmark (Christopher of Bavaria). Widowed in 1448, she remarried in 1449 to his successor, Christian I, in accordance with arrangements that allowed her to retain her valuable dower lands. With Christian she had several children, including John, who succeeded as king of Denmark, Norway and Sweden; Margaret, who married James III of Scotland; and Frederick I of Denmark.

Political role

Dorothea was renowned for her financial acumen and her defence of her dower estates in Denmark and Sweden. She acted as regent during her husband's absences and again during the minority of her son John. She famously travelled to Rome and obtained papal permission for the founding of the University of Copenhagen in 1475, and she pursued lengthy legal claims to recover her Swedish dower properties from the Swedish regent Sten Sture the Elder, pawning some of her lands to finance her claims.

Death

Dorothea died at Kalundborg in 1495 and was buried in Roskilde Cathedral beside Christian I, in a chapel she had endowed.
