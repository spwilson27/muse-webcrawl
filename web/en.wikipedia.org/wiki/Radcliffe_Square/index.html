Radcliffe Square is a square in central Oxford, England, widely regarded as the architectural and symbolic heart of the University of Oxford. At its centre stands the Radcliffe Camera, a circular library building completed in 1749 to a design inspired by James Gibbs, funded by a bequest from the physician John Radcliffe.

Surroundings

The square is ringed by some of Oxford's most celebrated buildings. To the north is the Old Schools Quadrangle of the Bodleian Library, to which the Radcliffe Camera is connected by an underground tunnel. To the east is All Souls College; to the south, the University Church of St Mary the Virgin, whose spire overlooks the square; and to the west, Brasenose College and the Divinity School. The cobbled square is closed to most traffic and is a favourite subject for views of "the dreaming spires" of Oxford.

History

The site was largely occupied by houses and Brasenose College property before being cleared in the 1730s for the new library building. The square took its present form as the surrounding college and university buildings were completed through the 18th and 19th centuries. Today the Radcliffe Camera functions as reading rooms of the Bodleian Library, and the square itself is a gathering point for students, tourists, and university processions.
