Atbash

Atbash (Hebrew: אתב"ש) is a monoalphabetic substitution cipher originally used to encrypt the Hebrew alphabet. It can be modified for use with any known writing system with a standard collating order. The name derives from the first, last, second, and second-to-last Hebrew letters (Aleph–Taw–Bet–Shin): the first letter is replaced by the last, the second by the second-to-last, and so on.

In the Bible

Several instances of Atbash occur in the Hebrew Bible. In the Book of Jeremiah, "Sheshach" is an Atbash cipher for "Babylon", and "Leb Kamai" stands for "Chaldeans". Such usage may have protected the writer or added an element of wordplay.

Modern usage

Applied to the Latin alphabet, Atbash maps A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X, and so forth. It is a specific case of an affine cipher and offers no real security against cryptanalysis, but appears in puzzles and puzzle hunts.
