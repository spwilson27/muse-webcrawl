Isomerase

In biochemistry, an isomerase is an enzyme that catalyses the conversion of a molecule from one isomer to another — that is, a structural rearrangement within a single molecule, without the net addition or removal of atoms. Isomerases form class 5 (EC 5) in the Enzyme Commission classification of enzymes.

Classification

Isomerases are subdivided according to the type of isomerisation they catalyse. These include racemases and epimerases (EC 5.1), which invert stereochemistry at a chiral centre; cis–trans isomerases (EC 5.2); intramolecular oxidoreductases (EC 5.3), such as those interconverting aldoses and ketoses; intramolecular transferases, or mutases (EC 5.4); and intramolecular lyases (EC 5.5). A further subclass covers enzymes catalysing conformational changes (EC 5.6), including protein disulfide isomerase and topoisomerases acting on nucleic acids.

Examples

Well-known isomerases include glucose-6-phosphate isomerase, which interconverts glucose 6-phosphate and fructose 6-phosphate in glycolysis; triose-phosphate isomerase, one of the most catalytically efficient enzymes known; ribulose-phosphate 3-epimerase in the pentose phosphate pathway; and peptidyl-prolyl isomerases, which assist protein folding. DNA topoisomerases, classified among the isomerases, relieve torsional stress during replication and transcription.
