Circle packing theorem

The circle packing theorem (also known as the Koebe–Andreev–Thurston theorem) describes the possible tangency relations between circles in the plane. It states that for every planar graph, there is a packing of circles in the plane whose tangency (intersection) graph is the given graph: each vertex corresponds to a circle, and two circles are tangent exactly when the corresponding vertices are joined by an edge.

Statement and history

The theorem was first proved by Paul Koebe in 1936 for packings on the sphere, and was rediscovered and generalised by E. M. Andreev and William Thurston. Thurston observed that the theorem implies a discrete analogue of the Riemann mapping theorem: circle packings can be used to approximate conformal maps between domains, by refining a hexagonal packing.

Uniqueness and applications

For a triangulation of the sphere, the packing is unique up to Möbius transformations, a rigidity result related to work of Oded Schramm and others. Circle packings have applications in graph drawing, mesh generation, computational conformal geometry and brain imaging, and form part of the theory of discrete analytic functions.
