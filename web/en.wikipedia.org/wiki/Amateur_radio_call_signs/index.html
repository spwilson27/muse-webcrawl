Amateur radio call signs are unique identifiers assigned by national regulators to licensed amateur radio operators and stations. A call sign typically consists of a prefix identifying the country (allocated internationally by the International Telecommunication Union), a numeral, and a suffix of one to four letters — for example, W1AW (United States), G4SJX (England), or JA1ABC (Japan). Operators must identify their station by its call sign at prescribed intervals, making call signs the fundamental addressing system of amateur radio.

Structure and allocation

The ITU allocates blocks of prefixes to each country; national authorities such as the FCC in the United States, Ofcom in the United Kingdom, and their counterparts elsewhere assign individual signs, often sequentially as licences are granted. Historically, the numeral indicated the district within a country where the station was located. Many administrations permit vanity call signs — personally chosen combinations, often commemorating initials or former signs — and issue special prefixes for contests, anniversaries, and expeditions (DXpeditions) to rare locations. Club stations, repeater stations, and space stations (such as those aboard the International Space Station) receive their own signs.

Uses

Call signs are exchanged in every contact and recorded in station logs; they appear on QSL cards confirming contacts and form the basis of award schemes such as DXCC, which credit operators for confirmed contacts with different "entities". Online databases (callbooks and sites such as QRZ.com) map call signs to licensees. Portable and mobile operation is indicated by appending suffixes such as /P, /M, or the prefix of a visited country under reciprocal licensing arrangements such as CEPT.
