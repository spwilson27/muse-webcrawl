Dinorwig power station

LocationLlanberis, Gwynedd, Wales
TypePumped-storage hydroelectric

Dinorwig power station (Welsh: Gorsaf bŵer Dinorwig), also known as "Electric Mountain", is a pumped-storage hydroelectric station near Llanberis in Snowdonia, north Wales. With an installed capacity of 1,728 megawatts from six reversible turbine-generators, it is one of the largest pumped-storage schemes in Europe. Its underground generating hall — excavated inside Elidir Fawr in one of the largest man-made caverns in the world — can take the station from standby to full output in about 12 seconds, making it vital for covering sudden surges in demand on the British grid, famously during television commercial breaks.

Operation

At times of low demand, the machines pump water from Llyn Peris up to the enlarged reservoir of Marchlyn Mawr, some 600 metres above. At peak demand the flow reverses and the water drives the turbines. The system stores about 9.1 GWh of energy with a round-trip efficiency of roughly 74–76%. Construction began in 1974 in and around the disused Dinorwic slate quarry, cost about £425 million, and required excavation of a kilometre-long access tunnel and an underground transformer hall; great care was taken to conceal the surface works within the national park. The station was officially opened by the Prince of Wales in 1984.

Visitor centre

The "Electric Mountain" visitor centre at Llanberis offered tours into the mountain to view the turbine hall from a gallery; tours were suspended during later refurbishment works. The station is operated by First Hydro Company (owned by ENGIE and Brookfield).
