Data room

A data room is a secure space used for storing and distributing documents, used to facilitate the due diligence process during a mergers and acquisitions transaction, loan syndication, or complex legal proceedings. Historically a physical room — often at the seller's premises or its law firm's offices — where bidders could inspect documents under supervision, the data room is today almost always a virtual data room (VDR): an online repository with controlled access.

Virtual data rooms

A VDR provider hosts documents on a secure server, granting named users permissioned access. Typical controls include granular folder- and document-level permissions, watermarking, restrictions on printing and downloading, two-factor authentication, and a complete audit trail recording who viewed each document and when. Documents are indexed in a structured folder tree — corporate records, financial statements, contracts, intellectual property, employment, tax, and litigation — and the index itself becomes part of the transaction record.

Uses

Beyond M&A, data rooms are used in fundraising, real-estate transactions, insolvency and restructuring proceedings, litigation disclosure, and secure sharing of confidential information between joint-venture partners and regulators. Providers in the market include Datasite, Intralinks, and Firmex.
