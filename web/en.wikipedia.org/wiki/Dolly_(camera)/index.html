Dolly (camera)

A camera dolly is a wheeled cart or similar device used in filmmaking to create smooth horizontal camera movements. The camera is mounted to the dolly and the camera operator and focus puller or assistant camera ride it to create a dolly shot.

A dolly may run on purpose-laid track or on pneumatic tyres over a smooth surface. Moving the camera physically toward or away from a subject ("dollying in" or "dollying out") differs from a zoom, because it changes perspective and parallax. A dolly combined with a simultaneous opposite zoom produces the dolly zoom effect, famously used in Vertigo (1958) and Jaws (1975).

Types

Studio and location dollies range from simple doorway dollies to large hydraulic-arm dollies such as those made by Chapman-Leonard and Fisher, which allow the camera to be raised and lowered during a move. The specialist crew member who pushes and steers the dolly is the dolly grip. Related devices include tracking vehicles, cranes and Steadicams.
