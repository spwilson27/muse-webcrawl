The Texas Eagle is a long-distance passenger train operated by Amtrak between Chicago, Illinois, and San Antonio, Texas, running daily over a route of about 1,306 miles (2,102 km). Three times a week, through cars are attached at San Antonio to the Sunset Limited, extending service to Los Angeles and making it one of the longest train routes in the United States at 2,728 miles.

Route

The train travels south from Chicago through Springfield and St. Louis, then across Arkansas (including Little Rock and Texarkana) and into Texas, serving Dallas, Fort Worth, Austin, and San Marcos before terminating at San Antonio. It largely follows the historic route of the Missouri Pacific Railroad's Texas Eagle, whose name Amtrak adopted in 1988; the Amtrak service began in 1981 as the Eagle.

Equipment and services

The train typically uses Superliner double-deck equipment, including coaches, a dining car, a Sightseer Lounge, and sleeping cars with roomettes and bedrooms. It is one of Amtrak's busier long-distance routes.
