Charles Saatchi (born 9 June 1943) is an Iraqi-British businessman and art collector, co-founder with his brother Maurice of the advertising agency Saatchi & Saatchi, and founder of the Saatchi Gallery in London.

Advertising

Born in Baghdad to a Jewish family that moved to London in 1947, Saatchi began his career as a copywriter. In 1970 he and Maurice founded Saatchi & Saatchi, which grew into one of the world's largest advertising agencies, famous for the 1979 "Labour Isn't Working" poster for the Conservative Party. The brothers later founded M&C Saatchi in 1995.

Art collecting

Saatchi began collecting contemporary art in the 1970s. His championship of the Young British Artists — including Damien Hirst and Tracey Emin — through exhibitions such as Sensation (1997) made him one of the most influential collectors of his era; his buying and selling could make or break artists' markets. His gallery moved in 2008 to the Duke of York's Headquarters in Chelsea.
