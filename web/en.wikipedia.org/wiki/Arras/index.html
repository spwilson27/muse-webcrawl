Arras (French pronunciation: [aʁɑs]; Picard: Aro) is the prefecture of the Pas-de-Calais department and the historic capital of the county of Artois in northern France, on the river Scarpe. Founded as Nemetacum by the Celtic Atrebates — from whom its name derives — it became famous in the Middle Ages for its tapestry workshops, whose products gave the English word "arras" for a wall hanging.

History

Arras was an important cloth and tapestry town in the medieval County of Flanders and later in the French province of Artois. It passed between France, Burgundy and the Spanish Netherlands before being definitively annexed by France under the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659. The town hall and its 75-metre belfry, begun in the 15th century, together with the two great squares — the Grand-Place and the Place des Héros — are lined with 155 baroque Flemish-style houses.

First World War

Arras stood close to the front line for much of the First World War. The Battle of Arras in April–May 1917 saw British and Commonwealth forces, including Canadians fresh from the capture of Vimy Ridge, advance against German positions; the battle's opening included the celebrated tunnel systems under the city, such as the Wellington Quarry, where thousands of troops sheltered before the attack. The city was heavily damaged and was rebuilt in its original style after the war.

Main sights

Landmarks include the belfry (inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage list as part of the Belfries of Belgium and France), Arras Cathedral, the Citadel built by Vauban, the Museum of Fine Arts in the former Abbey of Saint-Vaast, and the nearby Canadian National Vimy Memorial. Maximilien Robespierre, leader of the French Revolution, was born in Arras in 1758.
