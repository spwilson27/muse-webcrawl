Marennes is a former commune in the Charente-Maritime department in southwestern France. On 1 January 2019 it merged with Hiers-Brouage to form the new commune of Marennes-Hiers-Brouage.

Oysters

Marennes is world-famous for its oysters, farmed in the Seudre estuary's former salt marshes (claires) since the 19th century. The algae Haslea ostrearia gives Marennes-Oléron oysters their distinctive green tint and prized flavour; the oysters hold protected geographical status.

Sights

The late Gothic church of Saint-Pierre, with one of the tallest bell towers in the region, dominates the town. Nearby Brouage is a star-shaped citadel town founded in 1555. Marennes faces the Île d'Oléron across the Pertuis de Maumusson strait.

History

The town prospered on the salt trade and later on oyster farming after an 1868 shipwreck seeded Portuguese oysters in the estuary, later replaced by Pacific oysters.
