Isotopes of iodine

Iodine (I, atomic number 53) has 40 known isotopes, from 108I to 147I, of which only one, 127I, is stable and makes up essentially all natural iodine. All others are radioactive, most with very short half-lives.

Iodine-131 and health

Because the thyroid concentrates iodine, radioactive 131I released in accidents at Chernobyl (1986) and Fukushima (2011) posed a thyroid-cancer risk, especially to children. Taking stable potassium iodide tablets saturates the thyroid and blocks uptake of the radioisotope. Medically, the same property is exploited: 131I therapy destroys thyroid tissue in cancer and Graves' disease.

Iodine-129

The long-lived 129I is produced by cosmic rays and by nuclear fission; ratios of 129I to 127I are used in environmental science and in dating groundwater.
