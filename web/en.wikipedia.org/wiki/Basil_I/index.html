Basil I

Basil I, called the Macedonian (Greek: Βασίλειος ὁ Μακεδών; 811 – 29 August 886), was Byzantine emperor from 867 to 886. Born a peasant in the theme of Macedonia, he rose through ability and patronage to become co-emperor and then murdered his benefactor Michael III to seize the throne. He founded the Macedonian dynasty, under which the Byzantine Empire reached its medieval zenith.

Rise to power

Of humble, possibly Armenian, origin, Basil came to Constantinople as a young man and attracted attention for his strength — famously throwing the emperor's Bulgarian wrestling champion. Entering the service of the courtier Theophilitzes and then of Michael III, he became imperial chamberlain (parakoimomenos) and was crowned co-emperor in 866. On the night of 23–24 September 867 his men murdered Michael III, leaving Basil sole emperor.

Reign

Basil was an energetic administrator and lawgiver: he began the great legal revision completed under his son as the Basilika, reformed finances, and campaigned successfully in the east against the Arabs and the Paulicians, recovering territory in Cilicia and along the Euphrates. In Italy, Byzantine influence was partially restored through alliance with Louis II against the Arabs of Bari. His reign also saw the mission of Cyril and Methodios bear fruit in Slavic lands, though relations with Rome and the new Bulgarian church were contentious.

Death and succession

Basil died in 886, reportedly from fever after a hunting accident. He was succeeded by his son Leo VI "the Wise".
