Urbanisation in India

Urbanisation in India began after independence and is a major demographic trend in the country. India has one of the largest urban populations in the world, and the share of people living in towns and cities has risen steadily with each census, driven by natural population growth, rural-to-urban migration, and the reclassification of rural settlements as towns.

History

The Indus Valley Civilisation was one of the world's earliest urban cultures. Cities remained important centres through the Mughal and colonial periods, when ports such as Bombay, Calcutta, and Madras grew as administrative and trading hubs. After 1947, planned industrial cities and state capitals were built, and economic liberalisation from 1991 accelerated growth in metropolitan regions.

Trends and challenges

According to the 2011 census, about 31 per cent of India's population lived in urban areas, spread across thousands of towns and more than fifty cities with over a million inhabitants. Rapid urbanisation has strained housing, water supply, sanitation, and transport, and a large share of the urban population lives in slums. Government programmes such as the Smart Cities Mission and AMRUT aim to improve urban infrastructure and governance.
