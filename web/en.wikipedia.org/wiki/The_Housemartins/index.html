The Housemartins were an English indie pop group formed in Hull in 1983 by songwriter Paul "P.D." Heaton (vocals), Stan Cullimore (guitar), Ted Key (bass) and Chris Lang (drums). The band's music combined guitar pop with Heaton's sardonic, socially conscious lyrics and close harmony singing influenced by gospel and soul. They billed themselves ironically as "the fourth best band in Hull".

History

After building a following through John Peel sessions, the band signed to Go! Discs and released their debut album London 0 Hull 4 in 1986, which reached number 3 in the UK Albums Chart and included the top-ten single "Happy Hour". Bassist Norman Cook — later famous as Fatboy Slim — replaced Ted Key in 1986, and drummer Dave Hemingway replaced Hugh Whitaker in 1987.

In late 1986 their a cappella cover of the Isley Brothers' "Caravan of Love" reached number one in the UK, becoming one of the few a cappella singles ever to top the chart. A second album, The People Who Grinned Themselves to Death (1987), produced the singles "Five Get Over Excited" and "Build".

Breakup and legacy

The band split amicably in 1988. Heaton and Hemingway went on to form the Beautiful South, while Cook achieved worldwide success as Fatboy Slim. The compilation Now That's What I Call Quite Good appeared in 1988, and the retrospective Soup (2007) gathered the best of both the Housemartins and the Beautiful South.
