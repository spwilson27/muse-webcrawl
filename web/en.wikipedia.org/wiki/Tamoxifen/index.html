Tamoxifen

Tamoxifen, sold under the brand name Nolvadex among others, is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) used to prevent and treat breast cancer. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and has been in clinical use since the 1970s.

Medical uses

Tamoxifen is used to treat estrogen receptor-positive (ER+) breast cancer in women and men, to reduce the risk of invasive breast cancer after ductal carcinoma in situ, and to lower breast-cancer risk in high-risk women. Treatment typically lasts five to ten years.

Mechanism of action

Tamoxifen is a prodrug; its metabolites, principally 4-hydroxytamoxifen and endoxifen (formed via the enzyme CYP2D6), bind estrogen receptors and block estrogen signalling in breast tissue, while acting as partial agonists in bone and the uterus.

Side effects

Common effects include hot flushes and menstrual changes. Rare but serious risks include venous thromboembolism and an increased incidence of endometrial cancer. Tamoxifen was first synthesised at ICI Pharmaceuticals in the 1960s as a candidate contraceptive before its anti-cancer value was recognised.
