Aldwych is a crescent-shaped one-way street and surrounding district in the City of Westminster in central London. It forms the eastern end of the Strand, running from the Strand past the churches of St Mary le Strand and St Clement Danes towards Kingsway. The name derives from the Old English Ealdwic — "the old settlement" — recalling the Saxon trading town of Lundenwic that flourished here in the 7th to 9th centuries.

History

The modern street was created by the London County Council's Kingsway–Aldwych improvement scheme, one of the largest urban redevelopments of Edwardian London, formally opened in 1905. The sweeping crescent replaced a warren of slum streets and was lined with grand buildings including Australia House (opened 1918), India House, and Bush House — from 1941 to 2012 the home of the BBC World Service, and since redeveloped by King's College London.

Theatres and landmarks

Aldwych gives its name to the Aldwych Theatre (opened 1905), famous for the Aldwych farces of the 1920s and later the London home of the Royal Shakespeare Company, and faces the Novello Theatre across the crescent. The street's disused Aldwych Underground station, closed in 1994, survives as a popular film and television location. A pedestrianisation scheme completed in the 2020s transformed the western end of the Strand and Aldwych around St Mary le Strand into a largely traffic-free public space.
