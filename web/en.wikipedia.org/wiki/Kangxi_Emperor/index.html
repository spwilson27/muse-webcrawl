Kangxi Emperor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Court portrait of the Kangxi Emperor in informal dress, reading]

The Kangxi Emperor (1654–1722), personal name Xuanye, was the third emperor of China's Qing dynasty and its longest-reigning (1661–1722, sixty-one years). Ascending the throne at seven, he outmanoeuvred his regents, crushed the Revolt of the Three Feudatories (1673–1681), took Taiwan (1683), checked Russian expansion at the Amur (Treaty of Nerchinsk, 1689 — China's first treaty with a European power on equal terms), and broke the Zunghar Mongols in personal campaigns. A tireless, intellectually curious ruler who studied mathematics and astronomy with Jesuit tutors, he laid the foundations of the Qing golden age.

Consolidation of Qing rule

Kangxi arrested the over-mighty regent Oboi in 1669 and ruled in person thereafter. The Eight Banners' campaigns unified the empire's coasts and south; Taiwan's Zheng regime surrendered in 1683. In the north he campaigned in person against Galdan of Zungharia (victories at Ulan Butung 1690 and Jao Modo 1696) and secured Outer Mongolia's allegiance; his reign fixed Qing borders with Russia along the Amur and brought Tibet into the Qing orbit (1720).

Government and culture

Kangxi froze land-tax quotas ('never add taxes' edict of 1712), toured the south to court the scholar-gentry, and sponsored the great Kangxi Dictionary, the imperial atlas drawn with Jesuit surveyors, and the Complete Tang Poems. Yet his last years were darkened by a poisonous succession struggle among his many sons — two depositions of the heir Yinreng — a crisis his successor Yongzheng inherited and answered with the secret-heir system.
