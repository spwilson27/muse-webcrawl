Phosphoric acid

Phosphoric acid (orthophosphoric acid, phosphoric(V) acid) is a colourless, odourless phosphorus-containing inorganic acid with the chemical formula H3PO4. The pure compound is a solid, but it is commonly encountered as an 85% aqueous solution, a colourless, syrupy liquid.

FormulaH3PO4
Molar mass97.99 g/mol
AppearanceColourless solid or syrupy liquid
Acidity (pKa)2.15, 7.20, 12.35

Production

Phosphoric acid is produced industrially by two routes: the wet process, in which phosphate rock is treated with sulfuric acid, giving fertilizer-grade acid; and the thermal process, in which elemental phosphorus is burned and hydrated, giving a purer product.

Uses

Most phosphoric acid is converted to phosphate fertilizers. It is also used as a food additive (E338) to acidify foods and drinks such as colas, as a rust remover, and in dentistry and detergents. Its salts, the phosphates, play central roles in biochemistry, including DNA, RNA, and ATP.
