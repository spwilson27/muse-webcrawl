Maquis du Vercors

The Maquis du Vercors was a group of French Resistance fighters (maquisards) based in the Vercors Massif, a remote limestone plateau south-west of Grenoble, during the Second World War. It was destroyed by German forces in July 1944 in one of the largest anti-partisan operations in Western Europe.

Formation

The Vercors, with its steep cliffs and few access roads, seemed a natural fortress. From 1942 refugees, escaped prisoners and young men avoiding labour conscription in Germany gathered there. Plan Montagnard envisaged using the plateau as a base to support an Allied landing.

Battle of Vercors

After the Normandy landings, the Free Republic of Vercors was proclaimed on 3 July 1944. On 21 July some 8,000–10,000 German troops, including glider-borne forces landing at Vassieux-en-Vercors, attacked. The maquis, lightly armed and expecting Allied support that never came in force, was overwhelmed within days; around 840 French, including many civilians massacred at Vassieux and La Chapelle-en-Vercors, were killed.

Legacy

The tragedy became a lasting symbol of the French Resistance, commemorated by memorials and a museum at Vassieux-en-Vercors.
