Extreme sport

Extreme sports, also called action sports or adventure sports, are recreational activities perceived as involving a high degree of risk of injury or death. These activities often involve speed, height, a high level of physical exertion and highly specialized gear. Examples include BASE jumping, big wave surfing, BMX, skateboarding, snowboarding, motocross, free climbing, wingsuit flying, and street luge.

History

The term was popularized in the 1990s, particularly by the X Games, an annual competition launched by ESPN in 1995. The traditional definition of extreme sports has expanded; activities once considered mainstream, like skateboarding, BMX, and surfing, are now Olympic sports (added at the Tokyo 2020 Games).

Motivation and risk

Participants cite the adrenaline rush, a sense of flow, mastery, and escape from routine. However, the risks are substantial: studies document high rates of head and neck injuries in some action sports. The marketing of extreme sports appeals strongly to younger demographics, and energy-drink brands such as Red Bull are major sponsors of events and athletes.

Classification

Extreme sports are often divided into those taking place on land (skateboarding, mountain biking), on water (surfing, kitesurfing, whitewater kayaking), in the air (skydiving, paragliding), and on snow and ice (freestyle skiing, ice climbing).
