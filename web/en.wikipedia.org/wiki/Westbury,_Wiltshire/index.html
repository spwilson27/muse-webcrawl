Westbury is a town and civil parish in Wiltshire, England, lying about 4 miles (6.4 km) south of Trowbridge, at the north-west edge of Salisbury Plain. It is best known for the Westbury White Horse, a hill figure cut into the chalk of Bratton Down, about a mile east of the town.

History

Westbury was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Westberie. The town grew as a market town serving the surrounding agricultural land. In the 19th century, Westbury became a railway junction where lines from London, Bristol and the south coast met, and it developed industries including glove-making and ironworking.

The Westbury White Horse is the oldest of the white horses of Wiltshire, although the figure seen today was restored in 1778, replacing an earlier horse. The hill figure is a local landmark and a symbol of the town.

Geography and transport

Westbury railway station is a junction on the Wessex Main Line and the Reading to Taunton line. The town is served by roads to Trowbridge, Warminster and Frome, and lies near the A36.
