Anaerobic exercise

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anaerobic exercise is a type of exercise that breaks down glucose in the body without using oxygen; anaerobic means "without oxygen". In practical terms, anaerobic exercise is more intense but shorter in duration than aerobic exercise. It includes high-intensity interval training (HIIT), heavy weight lifting, and sprinting, all of which rely on energy systems that can supply adenosine triphosphate (ATP) rapidly but only for brief periods.

Metabolism

During short, intense activity the muscles draw on two oxygen-independent pathways. The phosphagen system uses stored ATP and phosphocreatine to fuel efforts lasting up to about ten seconds. For efforts lasting from roughly ten seconds to two minutes, anaerobic glycolysis predominates: glucose or glycogen is broken down to pyruvate and, when oxygen supply is insufficient, converted to lactate, yielding ATP far faster than oxidative phosphorylation but much less efficiently — two ATP molecules per glucose molecule rather than roughly thirty.

Examples

  • Weight training — lifting heavy loads for low repetitions.
  • Sprinting — maximal running, cycling, or swimming efforts over short distances.
  • High-intensity interval training (HIIT) — repeated bursts of near-maximal effort separated by rest or low-intensity recovery.
  • Powerlifting and Olympic weightlifting — single maximal lifts.
  • Plyometrics — explosive jumping and bounding movements.

Benefits and considerations

Anaerobic training increases muscular strength, power, and muscle mass, improves bone density, and raises the body's tolerance of lactate accumulation. According to exercise guidelines such as those of the American College of Sports Medicine, adults benefit from combining muscle-strengthening anaerobic activities on two or more days per week with regular aerobic exercise. Because of its intensity, anaerobic exercise carries a higher risk of strain injury and is usually introduced progressively, with adequate warm-up and recovery.

Comparison with aerobic exercise

Aerobic exercise uses oxygen to meet energy demands during sustained, moderate-intensity activity such as distance running or cycling, whereas anaerobic exercise is performed at intensities above the lactate threshold where energy demand outstrips oxygen delivery. Most sports and training programmes combine both modes.
