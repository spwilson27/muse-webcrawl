Observability (software)

In software engineering, observability is a measure of how well the internal states of a system can be inferred from knowledge of its external outputs. Borrowed from control theory, the term has come to describe the practice of instrumenting distributed systems so that engineers can understand, debug, and troubleshoot them in production — especially failure modes that were not anticipated in advance.

Telemetry signals

Observability is commonly built on three kinds of telemetry, sometimes called the "three pillars": metrics (aggregated numerical measurements over time), logs (timestamped event records), and traces (records of requests propagating through services, as in distributed tracing). Modern practice emphasises wide, structured, high-cardinality events that allow arbitrary questions to be asked of the data after collection. The OpenTelemetry project provides vendor-neutral standards and tooling for generating and collecting such telemetry.

Relation to monitoring

Observability is often contrasted with traditional monitoring: monitoring answers predefined questions via dashboards and alerts about known failure modes, whereas observability aims to let engineers explore system behaviour interactively to diagnose novel problems. In practice the two overlap heavily, and observability platforms typically include monitoring features.
