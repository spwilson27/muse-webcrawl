The Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park (Italian: Parco Nazionale del Gran Sasso e Monti della Laga) is one of the largest national parks in Italy. Established in 1991, it protects some 150,000 hectares across the regions of Abruzzo, Lazio and Marche, encompassing two contrasting mountain systems of the central Apennines: the limestone massif of the Gran Sasso d'Italia and the sandstone-and-marl Monti della Laga, noted for their waterfalls and beech forests.

Geography

The Gran Sasso chain culminates in Corno Grande (2,912 m), the highest summit in the Apennines, beside which lies the Calderone glacier — long the southernmost glacier in Europe. Below the peaks stretches Campo Imperatore, a vast alpine plateau at about 1,800 m sometimes called "Little Tibet", famous for its summer grazing, the historic hotel where Mussolini was imprisoned (see the Gran Sasso raid), and an important astronomical observatory. The Laga mountains to the north are softer, heavily forested and rich in watercourses. Historic towns and villages within or at the edge of the park include L'Aquila, Assergi, Santo Stefano di Sessanio, Castel del Monte, Amatrice, Accumoli and Arquata del Tronto, several of them severely damaged in the central-Italy earthquakes of 2009 and 2016.

Flora and fauna

The park is a biodiversity hotspot at the junction of Mediterranean and continental climates. Its emblematic species include the Apennine chamois — reintroduced from the 1990s and now numbering several hundred on the Gran Sasso — the Apennine wolf, the Marsican brown bear (more numerous further south but present in the Laga), wildcats, golden eagles, and a rich alpine flora with rare endemics such as the Apennine edelweiss. Ancient beech forests of the Laga form part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of primeval beech forests of Europe.

Human presence and tourism

Unlike wilderness parks, the area has been inhabited for millennia: transhumant sheep-rearing, terraced farming, hermitages and fortified hill towns are integral to its landscape. The park authority promotes hiking (including the high-level traverses of Corno Grande), ski touring at Campo Imperatore and Prati di Tivo, canyoning in the Laga gorges, and the food traditions of the area — lentils of Santo Stefano, saffron, pecorino cheeses and the amatriciana sauce of Amatrice.
