Hille–Yosida theorem

The Hille–Yosida theorem is a characterization of the generators of strongly continuous one-parameter semigroups of linear operators on Banach spaces. It is often stated in the form that a closed, densely defined operator A is the generator of a C0 semigroup if and only if certain resolvent conditions hold.

Statement

Let A be a linear operator defined on a subset of a Banach space X. The theorem relates the resolvent operator (λI − A)−1 to exponential bounds. A sufficient condition is that for all λ > ω and all n, the powers of the resolvent satisfy ‖(λI − A)−n‖ ≤ M/(λ − ω)n.

History and applications

The theorem is named after Einar Hille and Kōsaku Yosida, who obtained it independently around 1948. It is fundamental in the theory of semigroups used to solve abstract Cauchy problems, partial differential equations, and evolution equations.
