Louis Philippe I (6 October 1773 – 26 August 1850), nicknamed the Citizen King, was King of the French from 1830 to 1848, the last king of France and the penultimate monarch of France. As Duke of Orléans before becoming king, he spent 21 years in exile after he left France amid the French Revolution.

He was proclaimed king after the July Revolution of 1830 toppled Charles X, ruling a constitutional regime known as the July Monarchy that favoured the wealthy bourgeoisie.

Early life and exile

The eldest son of Louis Philippe II, Duke of Orléans ("Philippe Égalité"), he fought in the revolutionary armies at Valmy and Jemappes before defecting to Austria in 1793 with General Dumouriez. He lived in exile in Switzerland, Scandinavia, the United States, and England, returning to France after Napoleon's fall.

July Monarchy

As "King of the French"—a title implying rule by popular will rather than divine right—Louis Philippe cultivated a bourgeois image, carrying an umbrella and dispensing with court ceremony. His reign saw industrial expansion, railway building, the conquest of Algeria, and growing discontent over corruption, restricted suffrage, and the conservative ministry of François Guizot. The Revolution of 1848 forced his abdication; he fled to England, where he died in 1850.
