German Research Foundation

The German Research Foundation (German: Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft, DFG) is a German research funding organisation that promotes research at universities and publicly funded research institutions. With an annual budget of roughly €3.5–4 billion, drawn mainly from the federal government and the German states (Länder), it is the largest research funding body in Germany and one of the largest in Europe. The DFG is a self-governing association of the scientific community: its members are universities and other research institutions, and its funding decisions are taken by researchers themselves on the basis of peer review.

History

The DFG's predecessor, the Notgemeinschaft der Deutschen Wissenschaft ("Emergency Association of German Science"), was founded in 1920 under the leadership of the chemist Fritz Haber and the former Prussian minister Friedrich Schmidt-Ott to keep German research alive amid post-war economic crisis. It was renamed the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft in 1929. After the Nazi seizure of power the organisation was brought into line and its funds channelled toward regime priorities; it was dissolved at the end of the Second World War. The present DFG was founded in Bonn in 1951 by merging the post-war research council and the re-established emergency association, and it expanded to cover the whole federal republic, including the eastern states after reunification in 1990.

Activities

The DFG funds individual research projects and fellowships, coordinated programmes such as Collaborative Research Centres and Research Training Groups at universities, and major long-term projects in the humanities. Together with the German Council of Science and Humanities it has run the federal Excellence Strategy (successor to the Excellence Initiative) that designates clusters of excellence and universities of excellence. Review boards, elected by the research community every four years, organise the peer review of proposals. The DFG also advises parliaments and public authorities on scientific matters and maintains offices abroad, including in Washington, New York, New Delhi, and Tokyo.
