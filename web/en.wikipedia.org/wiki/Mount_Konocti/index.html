Mount Konocti is a prominent volcanic mountain on the southern shore of Clear Lake in Lake County, California. Rising to 1,312 metres (4,305 ft), it is part of the Clear Lake volcanic field and is one of the most recognisable landmarks of the North Coast Ranges.

The name Konocti comes from the Pomo language, spoken by the indigenous peoples of the Clear Lake basin, who inhabited the region for thousands of years. Pomo traditions hold the mountain in significance, and archaeological sites around the lake document some of the longest continuous settlement in California.

Geology

Mount Konocti is a dacite lava dome complex that grew episodically over roughly the last 350,000 years, most recently about 11,000 years ago. Its multiple peaks were formed by successive intrusions and flows of viscous lava. Although the Clear Lake volcanic field is considered potentially active by the United States Geological Survey — and supplies the nearby Geysers geothermal field — Konocti itself has not erupted in recorded history.

Recreation

For much of the twentieth century the mountain was privately owned and closed to the public. Lake County purchased the upper mountain in the 2000s, and the Mount Konocti County Park now provides hiking trails to the Wright Peak summit, with panoramic views over Clear Lake and surrounding counties.
