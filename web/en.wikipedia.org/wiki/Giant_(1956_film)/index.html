Giant is a 1956 American epic Western drama film directed by George Stevens, from a screenplay adapted by Fred Guiol and Ivan Moffat from Edna Ferber's 1952 novel. It stars Elizabeth Taylor, Rock Hudson and James Dean and tells the story of a Texas cattle ranching family and their changing fortunes across a quarter century of oil wealth and shifting social mores. It was Dean's third and final starring film role; he died in a car accident shortly before its completion.

Plot

Wealthy rancher Jordan "Bick" Benedict (Hudson) travels to Maryland to buy a stud horse and falls for the spirited Leslie Lynnton (Taylor), whom he brings home to the vast Benedict ranch, Reata, in Texas. Leslie struggles with the patriarchal ranch culture and the family's treatment of its Mexican-American workers. The surly ranch hand Jett Rink (Dean), who has long resented Bick, inherits a small strip of land from Bick's late sister; refusing to sell it back, he strikes oil on it and becomes spectacularly rich, building an empire of hotels and oil fields but remaining embittered and empty. Over the following decades the Benedict children disappoint their father's expectations, racial prejudice festers — culminating in Bick defending his Mexican daughter-in-law and grandchildren in a diner brawl — and the film portrays Texas's transformation from cattle to oil.

Production and reception

Shot largely in and around Marfa, Texas, the film became a landmark of the region; the ruins of the Reata mansion façade built for the production remain a local landmark. Giant was a major critical and commercial success, nominated for ten Academy Awards including Best Picture; Stevens won his second Oscar for Best Director, and Dean received a posthumous Best Actor nomination. The film is noted for its unusually candid treatment, for its time, of racism against Mexican-Americans. In 2005 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically and aesthetically significant.
