New Year's Day is the first day of the year in the Gregorian calendar, 1 January. It is a public holiday in most countries and is celebrated with fireworks, gatherings and resolutions, following New Year's Eve festivities the night before.

History

The Roman calendar under Julius Caesar began the year on 1 January, a month named for Janus, the two-faced god of beginnings. In medieval Europe the start of the year varied — Christmas, 25 March and Easter were all used — until the Gregorian calendar reform of 1582 restored 1 January, adopted gradually across countries.

Customs

Common customs include making New Year's resolutions, attending parties and fireworks displays, watching parades such as London's New Year's Day Parade and the Rose Parade in Pasadena, and sporting events. In many countries traditional foods are eaten for luck, such as lentils in Italy, black-eyed peas in the southern United States, and twelve grapes at midnight in Spain (technically on New Year's Eve).

In the United Kingdom, 'first-footing' and the singing of 'Auld Lang Syne' are traditional; in Japan, hatsumōde shrine visits and osechi dishes mark the holiday period.
