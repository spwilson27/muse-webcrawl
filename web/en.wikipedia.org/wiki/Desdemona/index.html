Desdemona

Desdemona is a character in William Shakespeare's tragedy Othello (c. 1601–1604). She is a Venetian gentlewoman who elopes with and marries Othello, a Moorish general in the service of Venice, defying her father Brabantio.

Over the course of the play, the ensign Iago manipulates Othello into believing that Desdemona has been unfaithful with his lieutenant, Cassio. Desdemona's lost handkerchief — Othello's first gift to her — becomes Iago's planted "proof" of the affair. Despite her innocence and her pleas, Othello smothers her in their bed. When the truth is revealed by Iago's wife Emilia, Othello kills himself.

Desdemona is often read as a figure of innocence, fidelity, and patience, though modern productions and criticism have also stressed her assertiveness in choosing her marriage and defending herself. The name derives from the Greek dysdaimon, "ill-fated". Shakespeare's source was a tale in Cinthio's Gli Hecatommithi, in which the character is the only one named.
