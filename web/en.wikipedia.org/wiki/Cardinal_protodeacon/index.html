Cardinal protodeacon

The cardinal protodeacon is the longest-serving cardinal deacon in the College of Cardinals of the Catholic Church — formally, the most senior cardinal of the order of deacons, determined by date of creation as cardinal.

Role

The protodeacon's best-known duty is to announce the election of a new pope from the central balcony (loggia) of St Peter's Basilica following a conclave, with the traditional Latin formula beginning Annuntio vobis gaudium magnum: habemus Papam! ("I announce to you a great joy: we have a pope!"). He then gives the new pope's baptismal name and chosen papal name before the pope appears to give the Urbi et Orbi blessing.

Other functions

Historically the protodeacon crowned the new pope with the papal tiara; since John Paul I declined coronation in 1978, he instead places the pallium on the pope's shoulders at the inauguration Mass. The protodeacon also has ceremonial precedence among the cardinal deacons and assists at certain papal liturgies.

Eligibility

If the protodeacon is himself elected pope, the announcement is made by the next cardinal deacon in seniority. A protodeacon who has been a cardinal deacon for ten years may opt for promotion to the order of cardinal priests while retaining seniority.
