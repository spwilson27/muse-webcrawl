Uzeyir Hajibeyov

Uzeyir Hajibeyov
Born: 18 September 1885, Aghjabadi, Russian Empire
Died: 23 November 1948, Baku, Azerbaijan SSR
Occupation: Composer, conductor, musicologist
Known for: Leyli and Majnun (1908); composer of the Azerbaijani national anthem

Uzeyir bey Abdulhuseyn oglu Hajibeyov (18 September 1885 – 23 November 1948) was an Azerbaijani composer, musicologist, conductor and public figure, regarded as the founder of Azerbaijani classical music and opera. He is celebrated for fusing Western classical forms with the modal traditions of Azerbaijani mugham.

Life

Hajibeyov was born in Aghjabadi in the Karabakh region and grew up in Shusha, a noted cultural centre. Educated in Shusha and at the Transcaucasian Teachers Seminary in Gori, he moved to Baku, where he worked as a teacher and journalist before devoting himself fully to music. In the 1920s he studied composition in Moscow and at the Azerbaijan State Conservatoire, which he later headed. He spent his final years in Baku, receiving honours including People's Artist of the USSR (1938) and the Stalin Prize.

Works

His opera Leyli and Majnun, premiered in Baku on 25 January 1908, is considered the first opera of the Muslim East, blending mugham improvisation with written choruses and arias. Other stage works include the operas Sheikh Sanan, Rustam and Zohrab, Shah Abbas and Khurshid Banu and Asli and Karam, the operetta Arshin Mal Alan (1913) — his most internationally performed work — and the culminating opera Koroghlu (1937). He composed the music of the national anthem of Azerbaijan and of the 1918–1920 Azerbaijan Democratic Republic.

Legacy

Hajibeyov founded Azerbaijan's first music school and conservatoire traditions, systematised the theory of Azerbaijani folk music in his 1945 treatise Fundamentals of Azerbaijani Folk Music, and trained a generation of composers including Gara Garayev and Fikret Amirov. His birthday, 18 September, is celebrated in Azerbaijan as National Music Day.
