Frame rate

Frame rate (expressed in frames per second, fps) is the frequency at which consecutive images (frames) are captured or displayed. The term applies to film and video cameras, computer graphics and motion capture systems. Human vision perceives roughly continuous motion above about 10–12 fps, though perception varies.

Historical standards

Silent films were often shot at 16–18 fps; sound film standardized on 24 fps. Television adopted rates tied to mains frequency: 25 fps for PAL and SECAM regions and approximately 29.97 fps for NTSC. Digital cinema and high frame rate productions have used 48, 60 and 120 fps.

Video games and displays

In video games, 30 fps and 60 fps are common targets, with high-refresh monitors supporting 120 Hz and above. Frame rate interacts with refresh rate, response time and techniques such as variable refresh rate to affect perceived smoothness and input latency.

Frame time

Frame time — the milliseconds needed to render one frame — is often a better measure of consistency than average fps, since uneven frame pacing causes stutter even at high average rates.
