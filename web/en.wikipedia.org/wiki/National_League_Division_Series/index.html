National League Division Series

The National League Division Series (NLDS) is one of the two opening-round playoff series of Major League Baseball's National League postseason, the counterpart of the American League Division Series. The winners of the two NLDS series advance to the National League Championship Series to determine the pennant winner that faces the American League champion in the World Series.

History

The Division Series format arose when each league split into two divisions in 1969, though a championship series alone decided the pennant until 1981, when a strike-shortened split season produced a one-off Division Series. The permanent Division Series began in 1995 (the 1994 postseason having been cancelled by a strike), after each league realigned into three divisions in 1994 and a wild-card team was added to the playoffs.

Format

The series is played in a best-of-five format, currently with a 2-2-1 home-field pattern. Under the format in place since the 2022 expansion of the postseason, the two division winners with the best records receive byes, the lowest-seeded division winner and three wild-card teams contest the Wild Card Series, and NLDS matchups pair the top seeds against the wild-card round winners.
