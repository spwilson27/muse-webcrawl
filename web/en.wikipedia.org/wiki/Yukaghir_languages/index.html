Yukaghir languages

RegionKolyma and Indigirka basins, Yakutia and Magadan Oblast, Russia
MembersNorthern (Tundra) Yukaghir; Southern (Forest/Kolyma) Yukaghir
SpeakersA few hundred combined; severely endangered
StatusSmall family or isolate; proposed links to Uralic remain unproven

The Yukaghir languages are a small family of two closely related languages — Northern (Tundra) Yukaghir and Southern (Forest) Yukaghir — spoken by the Yukaghir people in northeastern Siberia. Once spread over a far larger area, they are now critically endangered, with only a few hundred mostly elderly speakers.

Classification

Yukaghir is often treated as an isolate or "Paleo-Siberian" grouping of convenience. A proposed relationship with the Uralic languages has attracted scholarly attention but is not generally accepted; shared features may reflect ancient contact.

Features

The languages are agglutinative, with rich case systems, focus marking, and verb morphology sensitive to transitivity and switch-reference-like distinctions. Northern Yukaghir retains a tradition of pictographic writing on birch bark among some groups.

Documentation

Yukaghir was documented from the 19th century onward, notably by Waldemar Jochelson; modern documentation and community teaching projects aim to slow the language shift to Russian and Yakut.
