Udacity

Udacity, Inc. is an American for-profit educational technology company founded in 2011 by Sebastian Thrun, David Stavens, and Mike Sokolsky. It offers online courses and credential programs known as "Nanodegrees," primarily in technology fields such as artificial intelligence, data science, programming, autonomous systems, and cloud computing.

Origins

Udacity grew out of a 2011 Stanford experiment in which Thrun and Peter Norvig offered their artificial intelligence course free online; it attracted some 160,000 students worldwide. Thrun left his tenured position to launch Udacity in early 2012, with initial courses in computer science. The name is a portmanteau of "audacity" and "university for you."

Business model

Udacity became a pioneer of the massive open online course (MOOC) movement, but after low completion rates in early university partnerships it pivoted toward vocational, job-oriented training built with corporate partners such as Google, Amazon, and Mercedes-Benz. Its Nanodegree programs, launched in 2014, bundle project-based courses with human code review and mentoring. In later years the company shifted much of its focus to enterprise training contracts. In 2024 India's upGrad agreed to acquire Udacity.

Notable programs

Well-known offerings include the Self-Driving Car Engineer Nanodegree, launched in 2016 with partners from the autonomous-vehicle industry, the Deep Learning and AI Programming with Python programs, and the "Flying Car" nanodegree. Udacity's instructors have included industry practitioners as well as academics.
