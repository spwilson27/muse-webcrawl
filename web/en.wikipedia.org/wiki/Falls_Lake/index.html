Falls Lake is a 12,410-acre (50.2 km²) reservoir located in Durham, Wake, and Granville counties in the U.S. state of North Carolina. It was created by the construction of Falls Dam on the Neuse River by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, completed in 1981. The lake stretches about 28 miles up the Neuse from the dam near Falls, North Carolina, northwest of Raleigh.

Purpose

The Falls Lake project was authorised for flood control, water supply, recreation, and water-quality protection. The lake provides drinking water for Raleigh and surrounding communities in Wake County, and helps protect downstream areas, including the city of New Bern, from Neuse River flooding.

Recreation

The Falls Lake State Recreation Area manages over 5,000 acres of parkland in seven access areas around the southern shore, offering swimming beaches, boat ramps, campgrounds, picnic areas, and trails. A long section of the Mountains-to-Sea Trail runs along the lake's southern shore. Boating, fishing for largemouth bass, crappie and catfish, and hunting in adjacent game lands are popular activities.

Water quality

Falls Lake has faced persistent nutrient pollution from development in its rapidly growing watershed. The North Carolina Falls Lake Rules, adopted in 2010, established a staged nutrient-management strategy requiring reductions in nitrogen and phosphorus from wastewater, stormwater and agriculture in the upper Neuse basin.
