Marathon world record progression

This is a summary of the progression of the marathon world record as recognised by World Athletics. The marathon distance of 42.195 kilometres (26 miles 385 yards) was standardised at the 1908 London Olympics and adopted by the IAAF in 1921. World records in the marathon were not officially ratified until 1 January 2004; before that, the fastest times were referred to as "world bests".

Men

The men's record fell steadily through the twentieth century, from Johnny Hayes's 2:55:18 in 1908 to under 2:20 by the 1950s (Jim Peters), 2:09:36 by Derek Clayton in 1967, and 2:06:50 by Belayneh Dinsamo in 1988. Khalid Khannouchi first broke 2:06 in 2002; Paul Tergat ran 2:04:55 in Berlin in 2003, and Haile Gebrselassie lowered the mark to 2:03:59 in 2008. Eliud Kipchoge set 2:01:39 in Berlin in 2018 and 2:01:09 in Berlin in 2022, before Kelvin Kiptum ran 2:00:35 at the 2023 Chicago Marathon, the first ratified time under 2:01.

Women

Women's marathon records were long unrecognised; Violet Piercy's 3:40:22 in 1926 stood for decades in the lists. The mark fell rapidly from the 1970s: Grete Waitz ran 2:25:42 in 1979, Joan Benoit 2:22:43 in 1983, and Paula Radcliffe 2:15:25 (mixed race) in London in 2003, a record that stood for 16 years. Brigid Kosgei ran 2:14:04 in Chicago in 2019, Tigst Assefa 2:11:53 in Berlin in 2023, and Ruth Chepngetich became the first woman under 2:10 with 2:09:56 in Chicago in 2024. In women-only races, Peres Jepchirchir's 2:16:16 (London, 2024) heads the separate list.
