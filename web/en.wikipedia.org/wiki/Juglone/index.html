Juglone

IUPAC name5-Hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone
FormulaC10H6O3
Molar mass174.16 g/mol
AppearanceYellow-orange solid

Juglone, also called 5-hydroxy-1,4-naphthalenedione, is an organic compound with the formula C10H6O3. It occurs naturally in the leaves, roots, husks and bark of trees in the walnut family, especially black walnut (Juglans nigra).

Occurrence and effects

Juglone is an allelopathic compound: it leaches into the soil around walnut trees and inhibits the growth of many other plants, including tomatoes, potatoes and apples, a phenomenon sometimes called walnut toxicity. In the living plant it is stored largely as a non-toxic glycoside and is oxidized to juglone when tissues are damaged or exposed to air.

Properties and uses

Juglone is a naphthoquinone related to lawsone, the dye of henna. It forms yellow-orange crystals and acts as a natural dye and stain, darkening skin on contact. It has been investigated for antimicrobial and anticancer activity, and walnut husks containing the compound were traditionally used to stain wood and to poison fish.
