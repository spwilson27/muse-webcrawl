Geothermal areas of Yellowstone

The geothermal areas of Yellowstone comprise more than 10,000 hydrothermal features — geysers, hot springs, mud pots, fumaroles, and travertine terraces — within Yellowstone National Park, the largest concentration of such features in the world, including more than half of all the world's geysers. They are the surface expression of the Yellowstone Caldera, a supervolcano system formed by eruptions 2.1 million, 1.3 million, and 640,000 years ago, beneath which a magma chamber heats groundwater that rises through fissures in the rhyolitic lava flows.

Major basins

The Upper Geyser Basin contains Old Faithful, the most famous geyser in the world, erupting roughly every 90 minutes to heights of 30–55 metres, along with Grand, Riverside, and Castle geysers. The Midway Geyser Basin holds the Grand Prismatic Spring, the largest hot spring in the United States (about 113 metres across), whose vivid colours are produced by pigmented thermophilic bacteria. The Norris Geyser Basin is the park's hottest and most changeable thermal area and includes Steamboat Geyser, the world's tallest active geyser. Other major areas include the Lower Geyser Basin and Fountain Paint Pots, the travertine terraces of Mammoth Hot Springs, and the West Thumb Geyser Basin on the shore of Yellowstone Lake.

Microbiology and safety

Yellowstone's hot springs host extremophile micro-organisms; the bacterium Thermus aquaticus, isolated from Mushroom Spring in 1969, provided the Taq polymerase that made the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) practical. The ground in thermal areas is thin and fragile, and the near-boiling, sometimes acidic water has killed or injured visitors who left the boardwalks.
