Circle hook

A circle hook, with the point turned perpendicularly back toward the shank

A circle hook is a type of fish hook that is sharply curved back in a circular shape, with the point turned perpendicularly toward the shank. When a fish swallows the bait and moves away, the hook slides to the corner of the fish's jaw and rotates into place, rather than lodging in the throat or gut. Because of this behaviour, circle hooks greatly reduce deep hooking and are widely promoted — and in some fisheries mandated — for catch and release and for reducing bycatch mortality.

Use and technique

Circle hooks differ from conventional J-hooks in the technique required: the angler must not strike sharply to set the hook, but instead allow the line to tighten steadily, since the hook sets itself as it is drawn to the jaw corner. They are used in recreational angling for species such as billfish, tuna, and catfish, and in commercial longline fisheries, where studies have shown substantial reductions in the mortality of sea turtles and other bycatch compared to J-hooks, particularly when combined with large finfish baits rather than squid.

Regulation and conservation

Fisheries managers in several countries require circle hooks in certain fisheries — for example, in United States Atlantic pelagic longline fisheries and in some recreational billfish tournaments — because turtles and released fish hooked in the jaw survive at much higher rates than gut-hooked animals. Non-offset circle hooks are specified in some rules because offset points can increase deep hooking. Hooks of similar principle were used historically by Pacific and other Indigenous peoples, some made from bone and shell.
