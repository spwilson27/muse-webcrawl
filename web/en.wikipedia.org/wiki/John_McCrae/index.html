John McCrae (30 November 1872 – 28 January 1918) was a Canadian poet, physician, author, artist and soldier during the First World War, and a surgeon during the Second Battle of Ypres. He is best known for writing the famous war memorial poem "In Flanders Fields" in May 1915, whose image of red poppies growing among the graves of fallen soldiers led to the remembrance poppy becoming one of the world's most recognised memorial symbols.

Early life and medical career

McCrae was born in Guelph, Ontario, into a Scottish immigrant family. He studied medicine at the University of Toronto, won a gold medal, and worked at hospitals in Toronto, Baltimore and Montreal, becoming a pathologist and lecturer at McGill University. He served as an artillery officer in the Second Boer War (1899–1902) before returning to medical practice.

First World War and "In Flanders Fields"

When Britain declared war in 1914, McCrae volunteered and was appointed surgeon with the 1st Brigade, Canadian Field Artillery. At the Second Battle of Ypres in April–May 1915 his dressing station endured gas attacks and constant shelling; he treated the wounded for seventeen days. After presiding over the burial of his friend Lieutenant Alexis Helmer on 2 May 1915, McCrae is said to have composed "In Flanders Fields" the following day, sitting on the step of a field ambulance and noting the poppies blowing between the wooden crosses. The poem was published in the London magazine Punch on 8 December 1915 and became one of the most popular poems of the war.

Death and legacy

McCrae later commanded No. 3 Canadian General Hospital at Boulogne. Exhausted and ill, he died of pneumonia with meningitis on 28 January 1918 and was buried with full military honours at Wimereux Cemetery. His birthplace in Guelph is now the McCrae House museum, and his poem remains central to Remembrance Day ceremonies across the Commonwealth.
