Elmore Leonard

Born: October 11, 1925, New Orleans, Louisiana, U.S.
Died: August 20, 2013 (aged 87), Bloomfield Township, Michigan, U.S.
Occupation: Novelist, short story writer, screenwriter

Elmore John Leonard Jr. (October 11, 1925 – August 20, 2013) was an American novelist, short story writer, and screenwriter. His earliest novels, published in the 1950s, were Westerns, but he became known for crime fiction and suspense thrillers, many of which have been adapted into successful motion pictures.

Career

Leonard began writing Westerns while working in advertising, publishing The Bounty Hunters in 1953. His Westerns Hombre and Valdez Is Coming were adapted into films. In the 1970s he turned increasingly to crime fiction, with works such as Fifty-Two Pickup, Swag, and Stick. Renowned for terse, realistic dialogue, he laid out his famous advice in "Elmore Leonard's Ten Rules of Writing" (2001).

Adaptations

His novels Get Shorty, Rum Punch (filmed as Jackie Brown), Out of Sight, and Be Cool were made into major films, and his short story "Fire in the Hole" became the basis of the television series Justified.
