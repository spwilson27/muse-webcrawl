Germania Inferior ("Lower Germania") was a Roman province located on the west bank of the Lower Rhine, in present-day southern Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg and the German Rhineland. Its capital was Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium, modern Cologne.

History

The region was conquered by Julius Caesar during the Gallic Wars and initially administered as part of Gallia Belgica, with a military district along the Rhine. Around AD 83–90 the emperor Domitian formally organised the district into the province of Germania Inferior, paired with Germania Superior upstream. The province supplied the Lower German Limes, a chain of forts along the Rhine manned by four legions at its height.

People and cities

Indigenous peoples included the Batavi — famed suppliers of imperial bodyguards and cavalry — the Cugerni and the Ubii, whose town became Cologne. Other major centres were Noviomagus (Nijmegen), Traiectum (Utrecht), Vetera (Xanten) and Bonna (Bonn).

Decline

Frankish pressure mounted through the 3rd and 4th centuries; after the Rhine frontier collapsed in 406–407, Roman administration disintegrated and the region passed into Frankish hands, though cities such as Cologne retained Roman character into the early Middle Ages. The Lower German Limes is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
