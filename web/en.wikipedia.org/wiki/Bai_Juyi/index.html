Bai Juyi (772–846), courtesy name Letian, was a renowned Chinese poet and government official of the Tang dynasty. One of the most prolific and widely read poets of imperial China, he wrote in a deliberately plain style meant to be understood by ordinary readers, and many of his nearly 3,000 surviving poems address social injustice, the burdens of taxation, and the sufferings of common people.

Life

Bai was born in Taiyuan to a family of modest scholarly standing. He passed the jinshi examination in 800 and rose through official posts at the capital, Chang'an. His outspoken memorials and satirical "New Yuefu" ballads displeased the powerful; after the assassination of the chief minister Wu Yuanheng in 815, Bai was demoted and exiled to Jiangzhou (Jiujiang) for overstepping his authority in demanding an investigation. There he wrote the celebrated "Song of the Pipa Player" (Pipa Xing). Later rehabilitated, he served as governor of Hangzhou, where he built a causeway on West Lake that still bears his name, and as governor of Suzhou, before retiring to Luoyang. In retirement he styled himself the "Hermit of Xiangshan" and enjoyed the company of monks and poets.

Poetry

Bai's most famous works include:

  • "Song of Everlasting Regret" (Chang hen ge, 806) — a long narrative poem on the love of Emperor Xuanzong for Yang Guifei and the An Lushan Rebellion.
  • "Song of the Pipa Player" (816) — a ballad in which the poet, in exile, meets a retired courtesan-musician on the river at night.
  • The New Yuefu poems (809) — fifty satirical ballads with titles such as "The Charcoal Seller", exposing corruption and hardship.

Tradition holds that Bai read each poem aloud to an elderly illiterate woman and revised any line she could not understand. He carefully collected and copied his own complete works, depositing copies in temples — one reason so many survive. His poetry was immensely influential in Japan, where "Song of Everlasting Regret" is woven into The Tale of Genji. Arthur Waley's English translations made him one of the best-known Chinese poets in the West.
