FIDE titles

FIDE titles are awarded by the world chess federation FIDE to players, arbiters, trainers and organizers, and are held for life. The player titles are based on a combination of a minimum published Elo rating and norms — certified performances in qualifying tournaments.

Player titles

  • Grandmaster (GM) — rating 2500 and three GM norms (performance rating of 2600 over at least 27 games). First formally awarded in 1950.
  • International Master (IM) — rating 2400 and three IM norms.
  • FIDE Master (FM) — rating 2300; no norms required.
  • Candidate Master (CM) — rating 2200; no norms required.

Parallel women's titles — Woman Grandmaster (WGM), Woman International Master (WIM), Woman FIDE Master (WFM) and Woman Candidate Master (WCM) — carry lower rating thresholds; women may also earn the open titles, as players including Judit Polgár have done. Titles can also be awarded directly for specified results, such as winning the World Junior Championship.

Other titles

FIDE also certifies International Arbiters and FIDE Arbiters, FIDE Senior Trainers and other trainers, and International Organizers. Titles are ratified by the FIDE Council on application by the player's national federation.
