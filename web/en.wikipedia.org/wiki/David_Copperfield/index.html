David Copperfield is a novel by English author Charles Dickens, narrated by the eponymous David Copperfield, detailing his adventures in his journey from infancy to maturity. It was published as a serial in 1849 and 1850 and then as a book in 1850. It is sometimes called Dickens's "favourite child", and is widely considered his most autobiographical work.

Plot

David Copperfield is born in Blunderstone, Suffolk, six months after his father's death. His idyllic early childhood with his mother Clara and the housekeeper Peggotty is destroyed when his mother marries the stern Edward Murdstone, who beats the boy; David bites his stepfather's hand and is sent away to Salem House, a brutal boarding school run by Mr Creakle, where he befriends Tommy Traddles and the brilliant James Steerforth.

When his mother dies, Murdstone withdraws David from school and sends the ten-year-old to work in a London wine-bottling warehouse — an episode drawn directly from Dickens's own childhood. David runs away to Dover to find his great-aunt Betsey Trotwood, who adopts him, renames him "Trot", and sends him to school in Canterbury, where he lodges with the lawyer Mr Wickfield and his daughter Agnes, and meets the 'umbleness' of the clerk Uriah Heep.

David becomes a proctor's clerk, then a parliamentary reporter, and finally a successful novelist. He falls in love with and marries the childlike Dora Spenlow; after Dora's early death he travels abroad, realises his lifelong love for Agnes Wickfield, returns to expose Uriah Heep's fraud against her father, and marries Agnes. Around this central arc unfold the fortunes of the Micawbers — the eternally optimistic, debt-ridden Wilkins Micawber and his family — of Steerforth and the Peggotty family, of Little Em'ly's seduction and rescue, and of the faithful Ham and Mr Peggotty's emigration to Australia.

Characters

  • David Copperfield — the narrator and protagonist.
  • Betsey Trotwood — David's formidable great-aunt, who expected him to be a girl.
  • Clara Peggotty — the family's loyal servant.
  • Edward Murdstone — the cruel stepfather.
  • Wilkins Micawber — the improvident optimist, modelled on Dickens's father.
  • Uriah Heep — the scheming, "ever so 'umble" clerk.
  • James Steerforth — David's charismatic, selfish school friend.
  • Dora Spenlow — David's first wife, his "child-wife".
  • Agnes Wickfield — David's moral anchor and second wife.

Themes and significance

The novel is a classic Bildungsroman, tracing the discipline of the heart and the making of a writer. Its themes include the vulnerability of children, the cruelty of debt and of the factory system, the moral effects of class, and the contrast between "disciplined" and undisciplined feeling. Critics have long noted the density of Dickens's own experience in it: the blacking warehouse, his father's imprisonment for debt, and his early love Maria Beadnell, the model for Dora.

Adaptations

David Copperfield has been adapted repeatedly for stage, film and television, including the 1935 MGM film, the BBC serials of 1966, 1974 and 1999, and Armando Iannucci's film The Personal History of David Copperfield (2019).
