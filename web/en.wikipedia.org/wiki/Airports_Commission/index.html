The Airports Commission was an independent commission established by the Government of the United Kingdom in 2012 to examine how the UK could maintain its status as an international aviation hub and to recommend options for additional airport capacity in London and the South East. It was chaired by the economist and former financial regulator Sir Howard Davies and is often called the Davies Commission.

Background

London Heathrow, operating two runways close to capacity, had long been at the centre of a politically contentious debate about a third runway. The coalition government that took office in 2010 cancelled the previous government's approval of Heathrow expansion and ruled out new runways at Heathrow, Gatwick and Stansted for that Parliament, instead creating the commission to build a consensus based on evidence.

Work and reports

The commission's interim report, published in December 2013, concluded that the UK would need one net additional runway in the South East by 2030 and shortlisted three options: a third runway at Heathrow (north-west runway), an extension of an existing Heathrow runway, and a second runway at Gatwick. It rejected proposals for a new hub airport in the Thames Estuary (the so-called "Boris Island").

The final report, published on 1 July 2015, unanimously recommended the Heathrow north-west runway scheme, subject to conditions on noise, air quality and night flights, judging it the option delivering the greatest economic benefits. In October 2016 the government announced that it favoured a third runway at Heathrow, endorsing the commission's recommendation.

Membership

Besides Davies, members included former Manchester Airports Group chief executive Geoff Muirhead, transport economist Vivienne Cox, and others drawn from aviation, planning and environmental fields. The commission wound up after delivering its final report.
