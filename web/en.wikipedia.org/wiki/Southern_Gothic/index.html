Southern Gothic

Southern Gothic is a subgenre of Gothic fiction set in the American South, combining Gothic elements — the grotesque, the macabre, decay and madness — with the region's history of slavery, defeat in the Civil War, poverty and rigid social codes. It flourished in the 20th century and remains influential in literature, film and television.

Characteristics

Southern Gothic works typically feature crumbling plantation houses, eccentric or damaged characters, haunted pasts and a landscape of heat, swamps and Spanish moss. Rather than supernatural horror, the genre often locates its darkness in social realities: racism, violence, religious fanaticism and the burden of a lost, mythologised past. Humour and the grotesque frequently coexist, as in the "Southern grotesque" of Flannery O'Connor.

Major writers

Key figures include William Faulkner, whose Yoknapatawpha County novels made the South's haunted history a universal theme; Carson McCullers; Eudora Welty; Flannery O'Connor; Tennessee Williams in drama; Harper Lee, whose To Kill a Mockingbird carries strong Gothic elements; and later writers such as Cormac McCarthy in his early novels. The mode has extended into film and television, from The Night of the Hunter to contemporary series set in Louisiana bayous.
