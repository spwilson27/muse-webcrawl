Gun politics in the United States

Gun politics in the United States concerns the long-running public and legislative debate over the regulation of firearms, centred on the Second Amendment to the U.S. Constitution, which protects "the right of the people to keep and bear Arms". The United States has the highest rate of civilian firearm ownership in the world.

Legal framework

Federal law, including the National Firearms Act (1934), the Gun Control Act (1968) and the Brady Act (1993), regulates categories of weapons and requires background checks for licensed dealer sales. Regulation varies widely by state, from permissive concealed-carry regimes to strict licensing and assault-weapon bans in states such as California and New York.

Supreme Court jurisprudence

In District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) the Supreme Court held that the Second Amendment protects an individual right to possess a handgun in the home. McDonald v. Chicago (2010) applied that right against the states, and New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (2022) established a history-and-tradition test for gun regulations and recognised a right to carry in public.

Advocacy

Prominent gun-rights organisations include the National Rifle Association (NRA) and Gun Owners of America; gun-control advocacy groups include Everytown for Gun Safety and the Brady Campaign. The debate intensifies after mass shootings, and public opinion polling shows durable divisions along partisan lines.
