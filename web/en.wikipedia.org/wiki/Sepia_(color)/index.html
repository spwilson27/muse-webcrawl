Sepia is a reddish-brown colour, a pigment originally made from the ink sac of the common cuttlefish Sepia. The word sepia is the Latinised form of the Greek σηπία, sēpía, meaning cuttlefish.

History

Sepia ink was commonly used for writing in classical antiquity and remained in use as an artist's drawing medium. In the 19th century, sepia became strongly associated with photography: albumen and silver prints treated with toning solutions took on a warm brown tone that also improved their resistance to fading. As a result, "sepia" became the characteristic colour of antique photographs.

In culture

Because of its association with old photographs, sepia toning is used in digital image editing and cinema to evoke nostalgia, age, or the historical past. Many photo filters simulate the sepia effect by converting an image to shades of warm brown.
