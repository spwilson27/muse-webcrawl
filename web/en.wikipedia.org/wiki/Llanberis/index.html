Llanberis is a village and community in Gwynedd, north-west Wales, lying on the southern shore of Llyn Padarn at the foot of Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon), the highest mountain in Wales. Its name derives from St Peris, an early Welsh saint to whom the parish church is dedicated. Long famous for the great Dinorwig slate quarry, Llanberis is today one of the main gateways for walkers and climbers visiting Snowdonia (Eryri National Park).

History

The village grew with the slate industry. The Dinorwig Quarry, opened in the late 18th century on the slopes of Elidir Fawr across the lake, became one of the two largest slate quarries in the world, employing over 3,000 men at its late-19th-century peak; its closure in 1969 devastated the local economy. The quarry workshops at Gilfach Ddu now house the National Slate Museum, part of the National Museum of Wales, and the slate landscape of north-west Wales — including Dinorwig — was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021. The 13th-century Dolbadarn Castle, built by Llywelyn the Great to guard the Llanberis Pass, still overlooks the lake.

Tourism and attractions

Llanberis is the starting point of the Snowdon Mountain Railway, a rack railway opened in 1896 that climbs to the summit of Snowdon, and of the Llanberis Path, the longest and most popular walking route up the mountain. The Llanberis Lake Railway runs along the northern shore of Llyn Padarn on the old quarry line. In the Dinorwig quarry workings, the Dinorwig Power Station — a vast pumped-storage hydroelectric scheme hidden inside Elidir Fawr, once nicknamed "Electric Mountain" — offers tours. The village is also a centre for rock climbing, with the disused quarry's slate faces a renowned (and demanding) climbing venue.
