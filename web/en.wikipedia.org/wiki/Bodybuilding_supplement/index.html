Bodybuilding supplement

Bodybuilding supplements are dietary supplements commonly used by those involved in bodybuilding, weightlifting, mixed martial arts, and athletics for the purpose of facilitating an increase in lean body mass or improving performance. They include protein powders, branched-chain amino acids, creatine, glutamine, essential fatty acids, meal replacements, pre-workout products, and weight gainers. Supplements are sold over the counter and widely marketed in gyms and online, though the scientific support for many products is weak or absent.

Common bodybuilding supplements: protein powder and creatine

Protein

Protein supplements — most often whey, casein, egg, soy, or blended plant proteins — are the most widely used category, intended to help athletes reach recommended intakes (often around 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight per day for muscle growth). Whey protein is fast-digesting and rich in leucine; casein digests slowly. Meta-analyses find that protein supplementation modestly augments gains in muscle mass and strength during resistance training, with benefits plateauing at higher intakes.

Creatine

Creatine monohydrate is among the most studied sports supplements. It increases muscle phosphocreatine stores, supporting performance in brief, high-intensity exercise, and is consistently shown to enhance strength and lean mass gains when combined with resistance training. Loading phases (about 20 g/day for 5–7 days) or steady low doses (3–5 g/day) both saturate muscle stores. It is not a steroid, and common fears about kidney damage in healthy people are not supported by evidence, though it causes some intracellular water retention.

Other products

  • Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs): leucine, isoleucine, valine; marketed for muscle protein synthesis, though whole protein appears equally or more effective.
  • Beta-alanine: raises muscle carnosine; small benefits for exercise lasting 1–4 minutes; causes a harmless tingling (paresthesia).
  • Citrulline, caffeine, nitrates: common pre-workout ingredients aimed at blood flow and alertness.
  • HMB: a leucine metabolite with some evidence in novices and older adults.
  • Weight gainers: high-calorie protein-carbohydrate powders.

Regulation and safety

In the United States, supplements are regulated under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA), which does not require pre-market proof of efficacy or safety. Investigations have repeatedly found some products contaminated with or spiked by anabolic steroids, stimulants (such as DMAA), and other banned substances, causing positive doping tests and health incidents; third-party testing programs (e.g., NSF Certified for Sport, Informed-Choice) exist to mitigate this risk. Sports bodies caution that no supplement substitutes for adequate diet, training, and sleep.
