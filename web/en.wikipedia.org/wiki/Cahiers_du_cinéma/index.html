Cahiers du cinéma

Cahiers du Cinéma (French for "cinema notebooks") is a French film magazine co-founded in Paris in 1951 by Lo Duca, Jacques Doniol-Valcroze, and André Bazin, the latter the most renowned of the three. Its dramatic criticism helped turn cinema into a serious intellectual subject, and the journal became the cradle of the French New Wave (Nouvelle Vague): young critics François Truffaut, Jean-Luc Godard, Éric Rohmer, Jacques Rivette and Claude Chabrol wrote for it before becoming filmmakers.

Auteur theory

Under Bazin's editorship (until his death in 1958), the magazine championed directors such as Alfred Hitchcock, Howard Hawks, Jean Renoir, Roberto Rossellini and Nicholas Ray against the French "tradition of quality". Truffaut's 1954 polemic "A Certain Tendency of the French Cinema" set out the politique des auteurs — the view that the director is the film's author — which Truffaut and his colleagues then put into practice in the New Wave films of 1959 onward. The journal's annual top-ten lists and its "yellow cover" era remain landmarks of film culture.

Later history

In the late 1960s and 1970s, under Jean-Louis Comolli and Jean Narboni, Cahiers took a Marxist-Maoist turn, producing famous ideological readings (e.g., of Young Mr. Lincoln). From the late 1970s, under Serge Daney, it returned to a more pluralist criticism. In 2020 the magazine was bought by a group of media and film figures; much of the editorial staff resigned in protest, an event covered internationally, before the journal continued with a new team.
