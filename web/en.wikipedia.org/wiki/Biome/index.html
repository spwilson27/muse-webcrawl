Biome

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A biome is a large community of plants and animals that occupies a major habitat type, characterised by the dominant vegetation and shaped above all by climate — temperature and rainfall. Major terrestrial biomes include tropical rainforest, savanna, desert, temperate grassland, temperate and boreal forest (taiga), tundra and Mediterranean scrub; aquatic biomes include freshwater and marine divisions.

Distribution

Biomes form broad bands by latitude and altitude: steamy rainforests near the equator, deserts in the subtropics where air descends, grasslands and temperate forests in mid-latitudes, coniferous taiga and treeless tundra toward the poles. Mountain slopes compress the same sequence into a short climb. Similar climates on different continents produced convergent forms — spiny, water-storing plants evolved independently in American cacti and African euphorbias.

Change

Climate change is shifting biome boundaries poleward and uphill, altering fire regimes and growing seasons faster than many species can migrate; deserts may expand in some regions while thawing tundra releases stored carbon. Conservation planning increasingly uses biomes and ecoregions (the WWF recognises several hundred terrestrial ecoregions) as units.
