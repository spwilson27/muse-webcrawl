Byerley Turk

The Byerley Turk (c. 1680 – 1714; also spelled Byerly Turk) was one of the three foundation sires of the modern Thoroughbred racehorse breed, along with the Darley Arabian and the Godolphin Arabian. He is generally considered the earliest of the three to come to England, and his direct sire line, through his great-great-grandson Herod, was for a time the dominant male line in Thoroughbred breeding.

Origins

Traditional accounts state that the horse was captured from the Turks at the Battle of Buda in 1686, during the Great Turkish War, and served as the war horse of Captain Robert Byerley. He was reputedly ridden at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690, where Byerley was saved by the horse's speed when caught by enemy forces. His true breed is uncertain: he was described as an Arabian, though modern scholarship suggests he may have been a Turkoman horse.

Stud career

The Byerley Turk stood at stud first at Middridge Grange in County Durham and later at Goldsborough Hall in Yorkshire. Unlike the Darley Arabian and Godolphin Arabian, he sired relatively few recorded foals, but his influence persisted through his son Jigg and grandson Partner, leading to the great 18th-century stallion Herod (foaled 1758), whose sire line dominated British racing for decades before being eclipsed by the Darley Arabian's line through Eclipse.

Legacy

Genetic studies of modern Thoroughbreds show that the Byerley Turk's Y-chromosome haplotype still exists in some male lines, and his name is commemorated in cavalry and racing history. The three foundation sires remain the basis of the Thoroughbred stud book established with the General Stud Book in 1791.
