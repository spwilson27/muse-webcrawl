Fernand Paul Achille Braudel (24 August 1902 – 27 November 1985) was a French historian, the leading figure of the second generation of the Annales school of historiography. He revolutionised historical writing by insisting on the slow rhythms of geography, economies, and social structures — the longue durée — over the short-term history of events and individuals. His masterpiece, The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II (1949), remains one of the most influential works of twentieth-century history.

Life and career

Braudel taught school in Algeria and then at the University of São Paulo in Brazil before returning to Paris. Captured in 1940, he spent most of the Second World War in German prisoner-of-war camps, where — astonishingly — he drafted much of The Mediterranean from memory in school notebooks. After the war he succeeded Lucien Febvre as the dominant figure of the Annales; he held a chair at the Collège de France, directed the VIe section of the École pratique des hautes études, edited the journal Annales, and founded the Maison des Sciences de l'Homme.

The longue durée

In his programmatic essay "History and the Social Sciences: The Longue Durée" (1958), Braudel distinguished three temporal layers: the almost motionless time of geography and environment; the slow cycles of economies, states, and civilisations (conjonctures); and the rapid foam of events (événements). His three-volume Civilization and Capitalism, 15th–18th Century (1979) applied this scheme world-wide, distinguishing material life, market economies, and capitalism proper. Late in life he began an unfinished history of French identity, L'Identité de la France (1986).

Influence

Braudel's concepts shaped world-systems analysis (Immanuel Wallerstein built directly on him), global history, and historical geography, and his name adorns research institutes including the Fernand Braudel Center at Binghamton University. Critics have charged him with geographical determinism and with writing structures in which human agency nearly disappears — a debate that itself testifies to his influence.
