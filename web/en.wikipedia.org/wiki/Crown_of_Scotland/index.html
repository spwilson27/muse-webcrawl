The Crown of Scotland is the crown used for the coronation of the monarchs of Scotland. Remade in its present form in 1540 for King James V, it is the oldest surviving crown in the United Kingdom. Together with the Sceptre and the Sword of State (and later the Elizabeth Sword), it forms the Honours of Scotland, the Scottish regalia, kept on display in the Crown Room of Edinburgh Castle.

The crown was last placed on a monarch's head at the coronation of Charles II at Scone in 1651, but it has been carried in procession for later events: at the opening ceremonies of the modern Scottish Parliament, at the royal "presentation of the Honours" ceremonies (including for Elizabeth II in 1953 and Charles III in 2023), and in processions down the Royal Mile.

History

A crown features in depictions of Scottish kings from the reign of David I onward, and the regalia was repeatedly captured, pawned, and remade through the medieval wars. The crown was remodelled several times; in 1540 James V had the Edinburgh goldsmith John Mosman refashion it using Scottish gold, incorporating elements of the earlier crown. James V first wore the new crown at the coronation of his queen, Mary of Guise, in February 1540. It was used at the coronations of Mary, Queen of Scots (1543, as an infant), James VI (1567), Charles I (1633), and Charles II (1651).

After the Treaty of Union of 1707, and because the Honours were symbols of Scottish nationhood that might rally Jacobites, the regalia were locked in a chest in Edinburgh Castle and famously forgotten for over a century, until Sir Walter Scott obtained royal permission to open the chest in 1818 and the Honours were rediscovered intact — amid popular sensation — and put on public display.

Description

The crown comprises a gold circlet set with 22 gemstones (including garnets and amethysts) and decorated with alternating fleurs-de-lis and crosses; from it rise four gold half-arches, surmounted by a monde (orb) enamelled blue and sprinkled with gold stars, topped by a cross set with pearls. Around the circlet and arches are 68 Scottish freshwater pearls. The cap of the crown is of red velvet, originally purple, with an ermine lining. It weighs about 1.64 kg (3 lb 10 oz).

From 1707 to today

Although Scottish monarchs after 1603 were crowned in England with St Edward's Crown, the Honours remained Scotland's national treasures. George IV included them in his theatrical 1822 visit to Edinburgh. In 1953, controversy attended the presentation of the Honours to Elizabeth II at St Giles' Cathedral — some Scots objected she was not crowned with Scotland's crown — and the Crown of Scotland was laid upon the coffin of Elizabeth II during her lying-at-rest in St Giles' in September 2022. In July 2023 the crown was carried in procession for Charles III's Scottish thanksgiving service, alongside the new Elizabeth Sword, after the historic sword of state was judged too fragile.

Ceremonial role

The crown is borne by the Duke of Hamilton (hereditary bearer) in royal processions. It appears on the Royal Arms of Scotland, on Scottish government insignia, and — in stylized form — on badges of Scottish institutions, where heraldic practice renders it recognizably with its four half-arches and pearl-studded cross.
