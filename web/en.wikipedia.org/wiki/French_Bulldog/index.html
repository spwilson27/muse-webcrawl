French Bulldog

The French Bulldog (French: Bouledogue Français) is a French breed of companion dog — a cross in the 1800s between toy Bulldogs imported from England and local Parisian ratters. It is a small, muscular dog with a smooth coat, a compact build, and the characteristic "bat ears" that are a hallmark of the breed.

History

During the Industrial Revolution, lacemakers from Nottingham who emigrated to France brought their toy bulldogs with them; in Paris these were crossed with local dogs, producing the breed known as the Bouledogue Français. The breed became fashionable in Parisian society and was later developed further in the United States, where the bat-ear type was fixed as the standard.

Characteristics and health

French Bulldogs typically weigh under 13 kilograms (28 lb). As a brachycephalic breed, they are prone to breathing difficulties, overheating, and spinal and eye disorders; breeding for extreme flat faces has been criticised on welfare grounds, and some jurisdictions restrict it.
