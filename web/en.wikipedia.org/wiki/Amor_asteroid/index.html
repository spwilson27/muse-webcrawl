Amor asteroid

The Amor asteroids are a group of near-Earth asteroids named after the archetype object 1221 Amor. They are a subgroup of the near-Earth objects. The orbital perihelion of these objects is close to, but greater than, the orbital aphelion of Earth: they approach the orbit of Earth from beyond it but do not cross it.

Definition

An asteroid is classified as an Amor object when its perihelion distance q satisfies 1.017 AU < q < 1.3 AU, where 1.017 AU is Earth's aphelion distance. Amor asteroids therefore have orbits that lie entirely outside Earth's orbit at present, though gravitational perturbations can evolve some of them into Earth-crossing orbits over time.

Members

Thousands of Amor asteroids are known, and the group is continually growing as surveys discover new objects. The largest and best known members include 1036 Ganymed, the largest Amor asteroid at roughly 35 kilometres across, and 433 Eros, the first asteroid orbited and landed upon by a spacecraft (NASA's NEAR Shoemaker, in 2000–2001). The famous asteroid 99942 Apophis was originally classified as an Aten asteroid before its orbit was refined. Some Amor asteroids cross the orbit of Mars; 1221 Amor itself, discovered by Eugène Delporte in 1932, has a perihelion of about 1.08 AU.

Relation to other groups

Along with the Apollo and Aten groups — whose members do cross Earth's orbit — and the Atira group, whose orbits lie entirely inside Earth's, the Amors make up the standard classification of near-Earth asteroids. Because they come near Earth but do not currently cross its orbit, Amor asteroids are considered less of an immediate impact hazard than Apollos and Atens, though orbital evolution can move individual objects between groups.
