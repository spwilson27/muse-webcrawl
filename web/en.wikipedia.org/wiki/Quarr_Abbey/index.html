Quarr Abbey is a monastery on the Isle of Wight, near Ryde, home to a community of Benedictine monks of the Solesmes Congregation. The name derives from the stone quarries nearby that supplied stone for Winchester and Chichester cathedrals.

History

The original abbey was founded in 1132 by Baldwin de Redvers and colonised by Cistercian monks from Savigny; it prospered as landowner, port operator and coastal defender before being suppressed in the Dissolution of the Monasteries in 1536 and largely demolished for building stone. The picturesque ruins survive beside the modern monastery.

In 1907 the estate was bought by French Benedictines of Solesmes exiled by France's anti-religious laws — the community that had earlier occupied Appuldurcombe House. Between 1911 and 1914 their monk-architect Dom Paul Bellot built the present abbey church in a striking brick style blending Romanesque, Byzantine and Moorish elements, now Grade I listed. The community maintains a farm, tea shop, art gallery and guesthouse, and is known for its chant and its bookbindery.
