MHC class II

Structure of an MHC class II molecule with bound peptide

MHC class II molecules are a class of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) proteins found mainly on the surface of professional antigen-presenting cells, such as dendritic cells, macrophages and B cells. They present peptides derived from extracellular proteins to CD4-positive helper T cells, a key step in initiating adaptive immune responses.

Structure

An MHC class II molecule is a heterodimer of an alpha and a beta chain, each with two extracellular domains. The membrane-distal domains form an open-ended peptide-binding groove that accommodates peptides typically 13–25 amino acids long. In humans the principal class II isotypes are HLA-DP, HLA-DQ and HLA-DR; the genes are highly polymorphic, influencing immune responsiveness and susceptibility to autoimmune disease.

Antigen processing and presentation

Newly synthesized class II molecules are protected in the endoplasmic reticulum by the invariant chain (CD74), which directs them to endosomal compartments. There the invariant chain is trimmed to the CLIP fragment, which the chaperone HLA-DM exchanges for peptides generated by proteolysis of endocytosed proteins. The loaded complex then moves to the cell surface for inspection by CD4+ T cells.

Clinical significance

Particular HLA class II alleles are strongly associated with autoimmune conditions such as type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis and coeliac disease, and class II matching is important in organ and stem-cell transplantation. Defects in class II expression cause bare lymphocyte syndrome, a severe immunodeficiency.
