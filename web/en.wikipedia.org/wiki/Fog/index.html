Fog

DefinitionCloud of tiny water droplets suspended at or near the Earth's surface
VisibilityLess than 1 km (0.62 mi) by convention
Droplet sizeTypically under 0.1 mm diameter

Fog is a visible aerosol consisting of tiny water droplets or ice crystals suspended in the air at or near the Earth's surface. Fog can be considered a type of low-lying cloud and is heavily influenced by nearby bodies of water, topography and wind conditions. By international definition, fog reduces visibility to less than 1 kilometre (0.62 mi); the similar but less dense phenomenon is called mist.

Types

Radiation fog forms on clear, calm nights as the ground loses heat by radiation and cools the moist air above it to saturation. Advection fog forms when warm, moist air moves horizontally over a cold surface, as with the famous fog of San Francisco, where warm Pacific air passes over the cold California Current. Other types include steam fog (over warmer water), upslope fog, valley fog, freezing fog, and ice fog in very cold polar air.

Effects

Fog is a major hazard for road, air and sea transport, and has been a factor in many accidents; airports and highways use fog lights and specialised procedures to cope with it. Conversely, some ecosystems and agricultural regions depend on fog drip — water condensing from fog onto vegetation — and projects in arid coastal regions harvest fog with mesh nets for drinking water.
