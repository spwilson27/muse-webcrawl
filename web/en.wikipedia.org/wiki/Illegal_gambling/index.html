Illegal gambling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Illegal gambling is gambling that is conducted in violation of the laws of the jurisdiction in which it occurs. Forms vary widely by place and era, but historically prominent examples include unlicensed bookmaking on sports and horse racing, underground casinos and card rooms, illegal slot machines, and numbers games (policy rackets) in which bettors pick numbers tied to published figures such as racetrack totals or stock prices.

History

In the United States, illegal gambling was a mainstay of organised crime through the 20th century: the numbers game flourished in urban neighbourhoods such as Harlem, where figures like Stephanie St. Clair and later Bumpy Johnson ran policy banks, while the Mafia drew revenue from bookmaking, floating crap games and skimmed Las Vegas casinos before corporate legalisation. The federal Wire Act of 1961 targeted interstate bookmaking, and the 1970 RICO Act was used against gambling networks.

Modern forms

Today illegal gambling includes offshore and unlicensed online casinos and sportsbooks, underground poker rooms, illegal slot machines in shops and bars, and match-fixing-related betting syndicates in Asia and elsewhere, where unlicensed markets on football and cricket are extensive. Estimates of illegal wagering run to tens or hundreds of billions of dollars annually worldwide, though figures are inherently uncertain.

Enforcement and policy debate

Enforcement ranges from vice raids on gambling dens to financial measures against payment processors and website blocking. The expansion of legal sports betting after the US Supreme Court's 2018 decision striking down the federal ban (PASPA) was justified in part as a way to displace illegal bookmakers, though unlicensed offshore sites and illegal machines remain widespread.
