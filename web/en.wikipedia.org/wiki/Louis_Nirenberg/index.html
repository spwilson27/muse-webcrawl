Louis Nirenberg

Louis Nirenberg (28 February 1925 – 26 January 2020) was a Canadian-American mathematician, considered one of the most outstanding mathematicians of the 20th century. He made fundamental contributions to partial differential equations (PDEs), in the theory of complex analysis and related geometries, and in the study of fluid dynamics and nonlinear phenomena. In 2015, he was awarded the Abel Prize alongside John Nash for their work on partial differential equations.

Career

Born in Hamilton, Ontario, and raised in Montreal, Nirenberg studied at McGill University before earning his doctorate at NYU under James Stoker with a thesis on nonlinear elliptic PDEs. He spent his entire career at NYU's Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences, which he directed from 1970 to 1972, and became one of its most influential figures, supervising 45 doctoral students.

Work

Highlights of Nirenberg's work include the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequalities, foundational regularity theory for elliptic equations (with Agmon and Douglis), the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem on integrable complex structures (1957), the John–Nirenberg inequality and the space BMO, work on the Navier–Stokes equations, and studies of symmetry of solutions via the method of moving planes (with Gidas and Ni). His 1982 paper with Brezis and Capuzzo Dolcetta is among the most cited in mathematics.
