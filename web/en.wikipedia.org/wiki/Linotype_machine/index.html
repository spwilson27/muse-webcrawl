Linotype machine

The Linotype machine is a "line casting" machine used in printing which is manufactured and sold by the former Mergenthaler Linotype Company and related companies. Patented in 1884 by Ottmar Mergenthaler, it revolutionized typesetting, especially for newspapers and magazines.

Operation

A keyboard operator types a line of text; the machine assembles brass matrices (molds for individual characters) plus wedge-shaped space bands in a line. The assembled line is then cast as a single piece of type metal — a "slug" (from "line o' type") — which is used for printing and afterwards melted down. The matrices are automatically redistributed to their magazine channels for reuse.

Impact

Compared with hand composition, the Linotype multiplied a compositor's output several-fold, drastically cutting the cost of daily newspapers and books. It dominated commercial typesetting from the 1890s until phototypesetting and computerized typesetting displaced it in the 1970s and 1980s.
