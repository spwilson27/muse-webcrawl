The Liberal Party of Canada (French: Parti libéral du Canada) is a federal political party in Canada. The party espouses liberalism and generally sits at the centre to centre-left of the Canadian political spectrum, with its main federal rivals being the Conservative Party on its right and the New Democratic Party on its left. Founded in 1867, it is the oldest active federal political party in Canada and has governed for most of the country's history — often called "the natural governing party" — including long periods under Wilfrid Laurier, William Lyon Mackenzie King, Lester B. Pearson, and Pierre Elliott Trudeau.

History

The Liberals emerged from the Clear Grits of Canada West and the Parti rouge of Canada East, uniting in opposition to the Conservative coalition of John A. Macdonald. After governing briefly under Alexander Mackenzie (1873–1878), the party won power under Laurier in 1896 and held it for fifteen years. In the twentieth century, Mackenzie King's Liberals dominated Canadian politics between the wars, built much of the welfare state with Pearson in the 1960s (public health insurance, the Canada Pension Plan, the national flag, and official bilingualism), and under Pierre Trudeau patriated the Constitution in 1982. Jean Chrétien's governments (1993–2003) eliminated the federal deficit. After the sponsorship scandal the party fell to historic lows in 2011, then returned to majority government under Justin Trudeau in 2015, governing as a minority after 2019 and legalising cannabis, introducing a national carbon price, and expanding child benefits. Trudeau resigned as leader in early 2025 amid low polling and pressure from within his party; former central banker Mark Carney won the leadership and then led the Liberals to victory in the 2025 federal election, fought amid tariff threats and annexation rhetoric from the United States.

Principles and support

The party combines support for free markets with an active federal government, social liberalism on questions such as abortion rights, same-sex marriage (legalised under Paul Martin in 2005), and multiculturalism, and has historically drawn strength from Quebec, Atlantic Canada, and urban Ontario. Its associated colour is red, and its supporters are commonly nicknamed "Grits", after the nineteenth-century Clear Grit movement.

Leaders

Liberal prime ministers include Alexander Mackenzie, Wilfrid Laurier, William Lyon Mackenzie King, Louis St. Laurent, Lester B. Pearson, Pierre Elliott Trudeau, John Turner, Jean Chrétien, Paul Martin, Justin Trudeau, and Mark Carney. King remains Canada's longest-serving prime minister, with more than 21 years in office across three periods.
