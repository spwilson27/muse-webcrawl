Toe

Toes are the digits of the foot of a tetrapod.

Toes are the digits of the foot of a tetrapod. Animal species such as cats that walk on their toes are described as being digitigrade. Humans, and other animals that walk on the soles of their feet, are described as being plantigrade; animals with hooves are unguligrade.

Human anatomy

The human foot normally has five toes on each foot, named (from medial to lateral) the hallux (big toe), the second toe, the third toe, the fourth toe and the little toe. Each toe consists of small bones called phalanges: two in the big toe and three in each of the others, connected by hinge-like interphalangeal joints. The toes are supplied by digital nerves, arteries and veins, and are moved by muscles and tendons originating in the leg and foot.

Function

The toes play an important role in balance, walking and running. The forefoot, including the toes, bears a large share of body weight during walking, and the toes grip the ground and provide the final push-off in each stride. The big toe in particular is critical for propulsion and stability; its importance increased as human ancestors adopted upright bipedal walking.

Variation and conditions

Variations include Morton's toe, in which the second toe is longer than the big toe, and extra or fused toes (polydactyly and syndactyly). Common conditions affecting the toes include bunions, hammer toe, ingrown toenails, gout and fractures.
