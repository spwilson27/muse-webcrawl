St Mary's Church, Haddington

St Mary's Parish Church in Haddington, East Lothian, Scotland, is the largest parish church in Scotland and one of the great late-medieval churches of the country, often called the "Lamp of Lothian". Standing beside the River Tyne, the cruciform church dates mainly from the late fourteenth and fifteenth centuries.

History

The church was badly damaged during the siege of Haddington in 1548–49, during the Rough Wooing, and again in later conflicts; the choir and transepts remained roofless ruins for centuries while the nave served the parish. A major restoration between 1971 and 1974 rebuilt the eastern arm, returning the whole church to use. The Lauderdale Aisle houses the elaborate monument of the Maitland family, and the tower, long lacking its crown, remains a landmark of the town.
