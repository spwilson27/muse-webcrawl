Serpent Mound

Great Serpent Mound
Location: Adams County, Ohio, USA
Length: approx. 411 m (1,348 ft)
Designated: National Historic Landmark (1964)

The Great Serpent Mound is a Native American effigy mound in Adams County, Ohio, the largest serpent effigy earthwork in the world. The mound winds along a bluff above Ohio Bush Creek in a series of undulating curves, forming the shape of a serpent with a curled tail and an open mouth appearing to grasp an oval object, sometimes interpreted as an egg.

Description

Measuring about 411 metres (1,348 ft) in length and averaging about 1 metre (3 ft) in height, the effigy follows the contours of the bluff. Its head overlooks the creek below. The sinuous body includes seven major curves before ending in a tightly coiled spiral tail.

Age and builders

The mound's date and cultural affiliation have long been debated. It was long attributed to the Fort Ancient culture (c. 1000–1650 CE), but radiocarbon studies have suggested an earlier origin in the Adena culture, around 300 BCE, with possible later modification. The question remains unresolved among archaeologists.

Interpretation

Various astronomical alignments have been proposed: the head and curves may align with solstice and equinox sunrises and sunsets, and some researchers have suggested the serpent represents a constellation or a comet. The oval at the mouth has been read as an egg, the sun, or the eye of the serpent.

Preservation

Harvard archaeologist Frederic Ward Putnam led efforts to preserve the site in the 1880s, and it passed to the Ohio Historical Society (now Ohio History Connection). Serpent Mound State Memorial is open to the public and has been placed on UNESCO's tentative list for possible World Heritage designation.
