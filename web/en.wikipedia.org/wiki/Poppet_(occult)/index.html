Poppet (occult)

In folk magic and witchcraft, a poppet (also known as poppit, moppet, mommet, or pippety) is a doll made to represent a person, for the purpose of casting spells on that person or to aid them through sympathetic magic. The concept rests on the principle that "like affects like": whatever is done to the image will affect the person it represents. Poppets are not exclusive to any single tradition; similar image magic is documented in ancient Egyptian, Greek, and Roman practice, in early modern European witchcraft, and in modern folk and occult traditions worldwide.

Materials and construction

Poppets are traditionally made from whatever materials are at hand: clay, wax, carved wood, corn husks, potatoes or other root vegetables, twigs, or — most commonly in modern practice — cloth sewn and stuffed with herbs, cotton, or other filling. To strengthen the link between the doll and its subject, practitioners may incorporate "taglocks," personal items associated with the person such as hair, nail clippings, handwriting, or a photograph. Features may be embroidered or painted on, and the doll may be dressed in colours associated with the working's intent.

Uses

Although poppets are popularly associated with curses — the stereotypical image of a doll stuck with pins derives in large part from fiction and museum displays — in folk practice they are at least as often used for beneficial purposes: healing, protection, fertility, prosperity, love, and binding harmful behaviour. In some traditions a poppet is made to represent a disease, which is then symbolically removed or buried. Poppets are also used as vessels for spirits or familiars, as representations of deities on altars, and as focuses for meditation and devotion.

History and cultural references

Image magic appears in the Greek Magical Papyri and in defixiones (curse tablets) of the ancient world, some accompanied by bound figurines. In early modern Britain, dolls of clay or cloth were among the objects sometimes produced in evidence at witch trials. The word "poppet" itself is an old English term of endearment for a small child or doll (compare "puppet," from the same root). In the 20th and 21st centuries, poppets have become a standard element of modern witchcraft and Wiccan practice. They are frequently — and often inaccurately — conflated in popular culture with the "voodoo doll" of Louisiana Voodoo and Haitian Vodou, traditions in which doll imagery serves rather different ritual functions.
