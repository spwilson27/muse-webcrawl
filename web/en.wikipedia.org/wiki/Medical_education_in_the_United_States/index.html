Medical education in the United States

Medical education in the United States follows a graduate-entry model. Aspiring physicians first earn a bachelor's degree with pre-medical coursework, then complete four years of medical school leading to the MD (or the DO at osteopathic schools), followed by three to seven years of residency training in a specialty, and optionally fellowship training.

Medical school

The first two years traditionally cover basic sciences and the principles of clinical medicine; the final two years consist of clinical clerkships in teaching hospitals. Admission is highly competitive and usually requires the MCAT examination. Tuition is among the highest in the world, and the average graduate carries substantial educational debt.

Licensing and residency

Graduates must pass the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) steps and complete residency accredited by the ACGME to practise independently. Residency positions are allocated through the National Resident Matching Program ("the Match"). Medical schools are accredited by the LCME; the system was reshaped by the 1910 Flexner Report, which closed many proprietary schools and anchored training in universities and science.
