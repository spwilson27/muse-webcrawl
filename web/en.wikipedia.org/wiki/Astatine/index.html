Astatine, element 85, the rarest natural element on Earth

Astatine is a chemical element with the symbol At and atomic number 85. It is the rarest naturally occurring element in the Earth's crust, occurring only as a fleeting decay product in uranium and thorium ores; the total amount in the crust at any moment is estimated at well under a gram. It sits in group 17 of the periodic table, below iodine among the halogens, and all of its isotopes are radioactive.

Discovery

The element was predicted by Mendeleev as "eka-iodine". Several claimed discoveries in minerals proved spurious before Dale R. Corson, Kenneth Ross MacKenzie and Emilio Segrè synthesised it at the University of California, Berkeley, in 1940 by bombarding bismuth-209 with alpha particles. They named it from the Greek astatos, "unstable". Traces of the isotope astatine-218 were later found in nature.

Properties and uses

Because no weighable quantity has ever been assembled, astatine's bulk properties are inferred from its position in the periodic table: it is expected to be a dark solid, more metallic than iodine, melting around 300 °C. Its longest-lived isotope, astatine-210, has a half-life of 8.1 hours. The isotope astatine-211, an alpha emitter with a 7.2-hour half-life, is investigated for targeted radiotherapy of cancers, since its short-range alpha particles can destroy tumour cells while sparing surrounding tissue.
