The Diocese of Oxford is a Church of England diocese covering the counties of Berkshire, Buckinghamshire and Oxfordshire — roughly the Thames Valley — plus a small part of Hampshire. With more than 800 parish churches and some 1,200 clergy and licensed ministers, it is one of the largest dioceses in the Church of England by both population (around 2.5 million people) and number of parishes. Its cathedral is Christ Church Cathedral in Oxford, unique in England in serving simultaneously as a college chapel.

History

The see was created by Henry VIII in 1542 out of the Diocese of Lincoln, with its first seat at Osney Abbey; in 1546 the bishop's seat moved to St Frideswide's Priory church, which became Christ Church Cathedral when Henry refounded the priory as the cathedral and college of Christ Church. The archdeaconries of Berkshire (transferred from Salisbury) and Buckingham (from Lincoln) were added in the 19th century, giving the diocese its present three-county shape. Today the Bishop of Oxford is assisted by three area bishops — Buckingham, Dorchester and Reading — corresponding to the three counties, an arrangement introduced in the 20th century as the episcopal area scheme.

Notable features

The diocese covers the University of Oxford, many independent schools, fast-growing new towns such as Milton Keynes, and large rural areas; its bishops have included Samuel Wilberforce, John Robinson and Richard Harries. Its diocesan offices are at Church House Oxford in Kidlington.
