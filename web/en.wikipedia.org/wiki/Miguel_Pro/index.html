Miguel Pro

Miguel Agustín Pro Juárez (January 13, 1891 – November 23, 1927), known as Blessed Miguel Pro, was a Mexican Jesuit priest executed by firing squad during the persecution of the Catholic Church under President Plutarco Elías Calles. He is a martyr of the Cristero War and was beatified by Pope John Paul II in 1988.

Life

Pro entered the Jesuit novitiate in 1911, but the anti-clerical violence of the Mexican Revolution forced his community into exile; he studied in the United States, Spain, Nicaragua, and Belgium, where he was ordained in 1925. Despite chronic ill health and three stomach operations, he asked to return to Mexico in 1926 — just as the Calles Law criminalized public worship and drove the Church underground.

Underground ministry

In Mexico City, Pro carried out a clandestine ministry: celebrating Mass in private homes, distributing Communion at secret stations, and aiding the families of jailed Cristeros. He evaded police using disguises — famously slipping away on a bicycle, and once escaping a raid by posing as a detective arriving on the scene. He was known for his humor and practical jokes as much as his piety.

Arrest and execution

After a failed assassination attempt on former president Álvaro Obregón in November 1927, Pro and his brothers were arrested on fabricated charges of involvement. Without trial, Calles ordered Miguel's execution. On November 23, 1927, Pro walked to the execution yard, forgave his executioners, knelt to pray, and refused a blindfold; he died holding a rosary and a crucifix, arms extended in the form of a cross, crying out "¡Viva Cristo Rey!" ("Long live Christ the King!"). Calles had the execution photographed, intending intimidation — but the images, widely circulated, instead made Pro a national symbol of resistance.
