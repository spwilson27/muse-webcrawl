Coyote (person)

Coyote
ActivityPeople smuggling
RegionMexico–United States border and Central America
Related termsPollero, human smuggler

A coyote, in the context of migration, is a person who smuggles migrants across borders for a fee, most commonly across the Mexico–United States border. The term derives from the coyote, an animal known for its cunning and adaptability. Coyotes are also sometimes called polleros ("chicken herders") in Mexican slang.

Migrants typically pay coyotes thousands of dollars to guide them across deserts, rivers, and border checkpoints, or to arrange fraudulent documents and transport. Fees rose substantially as border enforcement intensified from the 1990s onward, and smuggling has become a major revenue source for organised criminal networks in some regions.

Risks

The journeys arranged by coyotes can be extremely dangerous. Migrants face exposure, dehydration, and violence, and have died in large numbers in the deserts of the US Southwest and in incidents such as the abandonment of tractor-trailers. Coyotes may also abandon, extort, or traffic the people they are hired to transport.

Terminology

The word "coyote" has been used for border smugglers since at least the early twentieth century. Enforcement agencies generally use the neutral term "human smuggler", distinguishing smuggling (a consensual, paid service that ends at the border) from human trafficking (which involves coercion and exploitation), although smuggling operations can evolve into trafficking.
