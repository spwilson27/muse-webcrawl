Enewetak Atoll

Enewetak Atoll (also spelled Eniwetok) is a large coral atoll of 40 islands in the Pacific Ocean, forming part of the Ralik Chain of the Marshall Islands. It comprises about 5.85 square kilometres of land surrounding a deep lagoon of roughly 80 kilometres in circumference.

Aerial view of Enewetak Atoll.

History

The atoll was administered by Spain, Germany and Japan before being captured by United States forces in the Battle of Eniwetok in February 1944 during the Second World War. After the war it became part of the Pacific Proving Grounds: between 1948 and 1958 the United States conducted 43 nuclear tests there, including the first hydrogen bomb test, Ivy Mike, in 1952, which vaporised the island of Elugelab.

Clean-up and resettlement

The resident population had been evacuated in 1947. A major radiological clean-up in the late 1970s buried contaminated soil in a crater on Runit Island, sealed under a concrete cap known as the Runit Dome (the "Cactus Dome"). Islanders began returning in 1980, although parts of the atoll remain off-limits and some foods are unsafe; the long-term integrity of the dome under sea-level rise is a continuing concern.
