Christus Rex


Placeholder image: a monumental Cristo Rei (Christ the King)

Christus Rex (Latin for "Christ the King") is a title of Jesus based on the idea of the Kingship of Christ, the doctrine that Christ exercises sovereignty over creation and will rule at the end of time. The phrase is also the name of several monuments, churches, religious organisations and publications dedicated to Christ under this title.

The feast

Pope Pius XI instituted the Feast of Christ the King in his 1925 encyclical Quas primas, partly in response to the rise of secularism and totalitarian ideologies. Originally celebrated on the last Sunday of October, the feast was moved in the Roman calendar reform of 1969 to the last Sunday of the liturgical year, the Sunday before Advent, where its eschatological emphasis is stressed.

Monuments

Many monumental statues of Cristo Rei or Cristo Rey have been erected, including:

  • Cristo Rei, Almada, Portugal (1959) — a 28-metre statue overlooking Lisbon across the Tagus, inspired by Christ the Redeemer in Rio de Janeiro.
  • Christ the King, Świebodzin, Poland (2010) — among the tallest statues of Jesus in the world, at 33 metres plus its mound.
  • Cristo Rey, Cali, Colombia (1953), and the Cerro del Cubilete monument in Guanajuato, Mexico, a centre of devotion associated with the Cristero War.
  • Christ the King, Lubango, Angola, and the Cristo Rei of Dili, East Timor.

Other uses

Christus Rex is the title of a 1951 novel by Sigrid Undset about medieval England, and the name of an Australian-based traditionalist Catholic educational apostolate (the Christus Rex Society, founded by Michael Davies). The initials of the Christus Rex pilgrimage in France — the Paris-to-Chartres pilgrimage, Pèlerinage Notre-Dame de Chrétienté — are also associated with devotion to Christ the King.
