Casting director

A casting director is a professional responsible for selecting performers for roles in film, television, theatre, advertising and other productions. Working within the pre-production process, the casting director collaborates with the director, producers and writers to understand the characters required, then identifies, auditions and recommends actors for each part. In larger productions, casting directors may lead a team that includes casting associates and assistants.

Role

The casting director reads the script and develops breakdowns describing each character's age, appearance and qualities. They contact talent agents, issue casting calls, organise auditions and callbacks, and present shortlists to the director and producers. For leading roles they may research established actors and negotiate availability; for smaller roles and background performers they draw on extensive files of performers. Casting directors must balance creative judgement with practical constraints of budget, schedule and contractual obligations.

History and recognition

Casting emerged as a distinct profession in the early 20th century as the studio system replaced ad hoc selection of actors by directors and producers. In Hollywood, figures such as Marion Dougherty revolutionised casting in the 1960s and 1970s by championing character actors and unknown performers. Professional bodies include the Casting Society of America (founded 1982) and the Casting Directors' Guild in the United Kingdom. Casting has been recognised by awards bodies: BAFTA introduced a casting award for film in 2020, and the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences announced an Academy Award for Achievement in Casting, to be presented for films released from 2025.

Theatre and other media

In theatre, casting directors serve producers and directors of plays and musicals, organising open calls and agent submissions. Voice casting directors perform an equivalent function for animation and video games, while commercial casting covers advertising work.
