The Soviet Airborne Forces (Russian: Воздушно-десантные войска СССР, Vozdushno-desantnye voyska SSSR, abbreviated VDV) were the airborne arm of the Soviet Armed Forces. Formed in the early 1930s, they were the world's first large-scale airborne force and, by the end of the Cold War, the largest airborne organisation in existence, with a strength commonly estimated at between 60,000 and 100,000 personnel organised into seven airborne divisions plus training and support formations.

History

The Soviet Union pioneered military parachuting in the 1930s under the patronage of Marshal Mikhail Tukhachevsky, whose doctrine of deep operations envisaged airborne troops seizing objectives deep behind enemy lines in coordination with advancing mechanised forces. In 1935, during the Kiev manoeuvres, foreign observers watched some 1,200 paratroopers dropped in a single lift, followed by thousands more brought in by air-landing.

During the Second World War the Red Army formed airborne corps, but chronic shortages of transport aircraft limited their employment. Parachute forces were used at Vyazma in 1942 and in the disastrous Dnieper airborne operation of September 1943, in which thousands of paratroopers were dropped scattered over a wide area with heavy losses. Many airborne formations instead fought as elite infantry, re-roled as Guards rifle divisions.

After the war, under commanders such as Vasily Margelov, who led the VDV from 1954 to 1978, the force was transformed into a highly mechanised strategic reserve reporting directly to the Soviet high command. It acquired its own air-droppable armoured vehicles, including the BMD family of airborne infantry fighting vehicles, and the blue beret and striped telnyashka shirt that became its emblem.

Operations

Soviet airborne troops participated in the suppression of the Hungarian Revolution in 1956, spearheaded the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia in 1968 with the seizure of Prague's Ruzyně airport, and were among the first Soviet forces deployed to Afghanistan in December 1979, seizing key points in Kabul during Operation Storm-333. The 103rd Guards Airborne Division and other VDV units served throughout the Soviet–Afghan War.

Dissolution and legacy

With the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, VDV divisions stationed across the union republics were divided among the successor states. The Russian Federation retained the bulk of the force as the Russian Airborne Forces, which preserved the Soviet VDV's traditions, insignia, and motto "No one but us" (Никто, кроме нас!).
