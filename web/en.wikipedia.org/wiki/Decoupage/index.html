Decoupage

Decoupage (from French decouper, "to cut out") is the art of decorating an object by gluing coloured paper cutouts onto it, combined with paint effects, gold leaf and varnish, so the result looks like painting or inlay.

History

Related techniques arose in East Asian lacquerwork and Siberian tomb art; in 17th-18th century Europe, decoupage - called arte povera in Italy and Japanning in England - let craftspeople imitate expensive Asian lacquer and inlaid furniture. Mary Delany's botanical paper mosaics are celebrated examples.

Technique

Cutouts are glued to furniture, boxes or glass, then sealed under many layers of varnish or lacquer, sanded smooth so the images appear embedded. Modern craft decoupage uses printed napkins, rice paper and Mod Podge-style glues.
