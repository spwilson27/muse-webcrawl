Foundation series

The Foundation series is a science fiction book series written by American author Isaac Asimov. First published as a series of short stories and novellas from 1942 to 1950, and subsequently in three collections in 1951, 1952 and 1953, the trilogy — Foundation (1951), Foundation and Empire (1952) and Second Foundation (1953) — won the one-time Hugo Award for "Best All-Time Series" in 1966. Asimov later added four more books: two sequels, Foundation's Edge (1982) and Foundation and Earth (1986), and two prequels, Prelude to Foundation (1988) and Forward the Foundation (1993).

Premise

The premise of the series is that in the waning days of a future Galactic Empire, the mathematician Hari Seldon develops psychohistory, a new and effective mathematical sociology based on statistical laws of mass action. Using it, he foresees the fall of the Empire, which encompasses the entire Milky Way, and a 30,000-year dark age — but he also devises a plan to shorten the interregnum to a single millennium by establishing the Foundation, a repository of human knowledge, at the edge of the galaxy. The series follows the Foundation through a sequence of crises (the "Seldon crises") as it grows amid competing neighbours, and the threat of the Mule, a mutant conqueror whom psychohistory could not predict.

Influence and adaptation

The series was a major influence on later science fiction and on writers and thinkers outside the genre. A television adaptation, Foundation, premiered on Apple TV+ in 2021, created by David S. Goyer and Josh Friedman and starring Jared Harris as Hari Seldon and Lee Pace as Brother Day.
