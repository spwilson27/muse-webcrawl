The Mask of Sorrow, a 15-metre concrete face above Magadan.

The Mask of Sorrow (Russian: Маска скорби, Maska skorbi) is a monument located on a hill above Magadan, Russia, commemorating the many prisoners who suffered and died in the Gulag prison camps of the Kolyma region under Joseph Stalin's Soviet Union. It consists of a large concrete sculpture of a face, with tears coming from the left eye in the form of small masks. The right eye is in the form of a barred window. The back of the statue has a weeping woman and a man with his head in his hands.

Description

The statue is 15 metres (49 ft) high and takes up 56 cubic metres. From the monument there is a view over the town and Nagayeva Bay in the Sea of Okhotsk, where prisoners were landed. Inside the central sculpture there is a replica of a typical cell in a Stalin-era prison camp.

The monument was designed by the renowned sculptor Ernst Neizvestny, whose parents were victims of the Great Purge of the 1930s, and built by Kamil Kazaev. It was unveiled on 12 June 1996 with the financial help of the Russian government and significant contributions from the city governments of Russian cities.

Historical context

Magadan was founded in 1929 and grew as the administrative centre of Dalstroy, the state trust that exploited the mineral wealth of the Kolyma region using forced labour. Millions of prisoners passed through the port of Magadan on their way to the camps; deaths from cold, hunger, disease, and execution were enormous. The Mask of Sorrow was conceived as a memorial to all victims of political repression in the Soviet Union.

Planned sister monuments

Neizvestny intended the Magadan mask to be one corner of a "Triangle of Sorrow," with companion monuments in Yekaterinburg and Vorkuta. The Yekaterinburg monument, known as the Masks of Sorrow, was eventually completed by another sculptor and unveiled in 2017.
