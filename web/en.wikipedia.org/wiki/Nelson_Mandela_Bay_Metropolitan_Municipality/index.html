Nelson Mandela Bay Metropolitan Municipality

The Nelson Mandela Bay Metropolitan Municipality is one of eight metropolitan municipalities in South Africa. Located on the shores of Algoa Bay in the Eastern Cape province, it comprises the city of Gqeberha (formerly Port Elizabeth), the neighbouring towns of Uitenhage and Despatch, and surrounding areas. It is named in honour of Nelson Mandela, the anti-apartheid leader and first democratically elected president of South Africa.

History

The municipality was established on 5 December 2000 through the amalgamation of the municipalities of Port Elizabeth, Uitenhage and Despatch. It was the first metropolitan municipality in South Africa to be named after an individual.

Geography and economy

The metro covers roughly 1,950 square kilometres and has a population of around 1.2 million. Its economy is anchored by the Port of Ngqura and the harbour at Gqeberha, a major automotive manufacturing sector (including Volkswagen and Ford plants), the Coega industrial development zone, and tourism along the Algoa Bay coastline.

Government

The municipal council is elected by mixed-member proportional representation. The metro has frequently been governed by coalitions and has experienced periods of political instability, with several changes of mayor since 2016.
