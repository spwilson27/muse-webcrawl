Tommie Smith

Tommie C. Smith (born June 6, 1944) is an American former track and field athlete and wide receiver in professional football. He won the 200 metres at the 1968 Summer Olympics in a world record 19.83 seconds and, with teammate John Carlos, made the iconic raised-fist protest on the medal podium.

1968 protest

At the Mexico City Games on 16 October 1968, Smith won gold and Carlos bronze. During the medal ceremony they stood shoeless in black socks, each wearing a black glove, and raised a fist during the U.S. anthem to protest racism and human-rights abuses — an image that became one of the most famous in sports history. The IOC expelled them from the Games; they faced ostracism and threats at home, but the gesture is now widely honoured, including by a statue at San José State University.

Career

Smith set 11 world records in sprinting during his career at San José State under coach Bud Winter. He later played briefly in the NFL for the Cincinnati Bengals, worked as a coach and college professor, and received honours including the Arthur Ashe Courage Award (with Carlos) in 2008.
