The DeLorean time machine is a fictional time-travel vehicle from the Back to the Future film franchise, built by scientist Dr. Emmett "Doc" Brown from a DeLorean DMC-12 sports car. It is one of the most recognizable vehicles in cinema history, distinguished by its stainless-steel body, gull-wing doors, and the glowing "flux capacitor" that, in Brown's words, "is what makes time travel possible."

Operation

Time travel is achieved when the car reaches 88 miles per hour (142 km/h) while the flux capacitor is supplied with 1.21 gigawatts of electrical power. In the first film the power comes from plutonium and, in the climax, from a lightning strike; in later films the car is fitted with a "Mr. Fusion" home energy reactor that converts household waste into power. The destination time is set on keypad-controlled displays, and the car leaves twin trails of fire as it vanishes.

Evolution across the films

In Back to the Future Part II (1989) the DeLorean is upgraded with hover conversion, allowing it to fly, and travels to 2015, 1955, and an alternate 1985. In Part III (1990), stranded in 1885 without fuel, it is fitted with train wheels and pushed to speed by a locomotive before being destroyed by an oncoming train on its return to 1985.

Design and cultural impact

The time machine was designed by Ron Cobb and Andrew Probert and built under the supervision of Kevin Pike. The production used several modified DeLoreans, and surviving cars are displayed in museums and at Universal Studios. The design was chosen partly as a joke: Brown remarks that if you were going to build a time machine into a car, you might as well "do it with some style," and the car's futuristic stainless-steel look led a 1955 character to mistake it for an alien spacecraft.
