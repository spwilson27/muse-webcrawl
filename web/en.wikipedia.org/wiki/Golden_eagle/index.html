Golden eagle

The golden eagle (Aquila chrysaetos) is a bird of prey living in the Northern Hemisphere. It is the most widely distributed species of eagle. Like all eagles, it belongs to the family Accipitridae. It is one of the best-known birds of prey in the world, with a dark brown plumage and golden-brown feathers on the nape.

Distribution and habitat

The golden eagle occupies mountainous and open country across North America, Europe, Asia, and North Africa. It prefers remote areas largely undisturbed by humans, nesting on cliff faces or in large trees. Several subspecies are recognised, differing slightly in size and plumage.

Hunting and diet

Golden eagles hunt from the air, using acute eyesight, great speed in a dive, and powerful talons. Their diet consists mainly of hares, rabbits, marmots, and ground squirrels, but they also take foxes, young deer, and other birds. Pairs may hunt cooperatively.

Relationship with humans

The golden eagle is a revered symbol in many cultures and the national bird of several countries, including Mexico, Kazakhstan, and Albania. In Central Asia, Kazakh and Kyrgyz hunters traditionally train golden eagles for falconry. The species is protected in most of its range; collisions with wind turbines and electrocution remain threats.
