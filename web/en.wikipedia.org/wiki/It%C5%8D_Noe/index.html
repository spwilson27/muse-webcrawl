Itō Noe, Japanese anarchist and feminist writer

Itō Noe (伊東 野枝, 21 January 1895 – 16 September 1923) was a Japanese anarchist, social critic, author and feminist. She edited the feminist journal Seitō (Bluestocking) and translated works by Emma Goldman, becoming a leading voice for women's emancipation in Taishō-era Japan.

Life

Born in Fukuoka Prefecture, Itō moved to Tokyo, where she joined the Seitōsha literary society and took over editorship of Seitō in 1915, steering it toward social and political themes. She wrote frankly about free love, abortion and prostitution, and escaped an arranged marriage to live with the anarchist Ōsugi Sakae.

Death

In the chaos following the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923, Itō and Ōsugi were arrested by military police and beaten to death, along with Ōsugi's six-year-old nephew—an episode known as the Amakasu Incident, for which officer Amakasu Masahiko was convicted. Itō was 28 years old.
