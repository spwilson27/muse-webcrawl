Aerozine 50

Aerozine 50 is a 50:50 mix by weight of hydrazine and unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine (UDMH). Originally developed in the late 1950s by the Aerojet General Corporation as a stable fuel for the Titan II missile engines, Aerozine 50 is a hydrazine derivative used primarily as a hypergolic rocket propellant fuel, typically with nitrogen tetroxide (NTO) as the oxidizer.

Properties

Compared with pure hydrazine, the mixture has a lower freezing point and a somewhat reduced risk of freezing in cold environments, while still performing well; it is also less prone than hydrazine to explosive decomposition in contact with certain materials. Against UDMH alone, Aerozine 50 offers higher density and slightly better performance in some engines. Like all hydrazine-family fuels it is highly toxic, corrosive and carcinogenic, requiring careful handling, but it is storable at ambient temperatures for long periods and ignites spontaneously (hypergolically) on contact with NTO, which makes it attractive for missiles and spacecraft that must sit fueled and ready.

Use

Aerozine 50 powered the Titan family's two stages (LR87 and LR91 engines) and served in the AJ10 engine of the Delta second stage and the Apollo Service Propulsion System. It later flew in the Space Shuttle's Orbital Maneuvering System engines' predecessors' lineage of storable engines, and remains in use on the European Ariane-derived EPS upper stage lineage (with UDMH variants) and in various satellite and deep-space propulsion systems where storability matters more than the higher specific impulse of cryogenic propellants.
