Widows (TV series)

Widows is a British primetime drama serial broadcast on ITV in 1983. Written by Lynda La Plante, it follows four women whose armed-robber husbands are killed during a robbery, and who decide to carry out the robbery themselves. The series was a critical and popular success and is credited with launching La Plante's career as a leading writer of British crime drama.

The four widows: Dolly, Linda, Shirley, and Bella.

Plot

Dolly Rawlins (Ann Mitchell) is widowed when her husband Harry is killed in an armed robbery that goes wrong. Discovering Harry's meticulously kept ledgers detailing the robberies he planned, Dolly recruits the other widows — Linda Pirelli, Shirley Miller, and later Bella O'Reilly — to carry out an outstanding robbery themselves, evading both the police and a rival gangster who wants the proceeds for himself.

Legacy

A second series, Widows 2, followed in 1985, and a follow-up series, She's Out, in 1995, in which Dolly is released from prison. The original serial was remade as an American television miniseries in 2002, and its premise inspired the 2018 feature film Widows, directed by Steve McQueen.
