Alcide Amedeo Francesco De Gasperi (3 April 1881 – 19 August 1954) was an Italian politician and statesman who founded the Christian Democracy party and served as Prime Minister of Italy in eight successive coalition governments from 1945 to 1953. He is regarded as one of the founding fathers of the European Union, alongside Robert Schuman and Konrad Adenauer.

Early life

De Gasperi was born in Pieve Tesino in Trentino, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. He studied philosophy at the University of Vienna and entered politics as a representative of Italian-speaking Tyroleans in the Austrian Reichsrat. After the First World War, Trentino was annexed by Italy, and De Gasperi co-founded the Italian People's Party.

Fascist era and resistance

Opposed to Benito Mussolini's fascist regime, De Gasperi was imprisoned in 1927 and spent 16 months in jail. After his release he worked in the Vatican Library, quietly rebuilding a democratic Catholic political movement that became Christian Democracy in 1942–43.

Prime Minister

Following the fall of fascism and the end of the Second World War, De Gasperi became foreign minister and then prime minister in December 1945. He oversaw Italy's transition from monarchy to republic after the 1946 referendum, the peace treaty of 1947, the adoption of the republican constitution, and Italy's integration into the Western alliance, including membership in NATO and the European Coal and Steel Community. His governments implemented the Marshall Plan reconstruction programme.

Later life and legacy

De Gasperi lost the premiership in 1953 after his coalition failed to win a parliamentary majority, and he died the following year. He is remembered as the architect of post-war Italian democracy and a pioneer of European integration. The Catholic Church has opened his cause for beatification.
