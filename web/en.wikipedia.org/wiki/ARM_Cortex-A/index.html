ARM Cortex-A

DesignerArm Ltd.
ArchitectureARMv7-A, ARMv8-A, ARMv9-A
TypeApplication processor cores
Introduced2005 (Cortex-A8)

The ARM Cortex-A is a family of processor cores implementing the ARM architecture's "Application" profile, designed by Arm Ltd. and licensed to semiconductor companies for use in systems on a chip (SoCs). Cortex-A cores run the full ARM instruction set with memory management units, making them suitable for running rich operating systems such as Linux, Android, and iOS in smartphones, tablets, servers, and embedded systems.

Overview

The family complements the Cortex-R (real-time) and Cortex-M (microcontroller) profiles. The first core, the Cortex-A8 (2005), powered early smartphones including the iPhone 3GS. Successive generations widened the performance range: the Cortex-A7 and A53 offered power-efficient 64-bit-capable designs, the Cortex-A72, A76, and A78 targeted premium mobile performance, and the Cortex-X series (from 2020) pushed maximum single-thread performance. Neoverse cores adapt the same architecture for data-center use.

Architecture

Cortex-A cores implement ARMv7-A, ARMv8-A (introducing the 64-bit AArch64 execution state), or ARMv9-A, with extensions including NEON SIMD/floating-point units, virtualization support, and TrustZone security. Arm's big.LITTLE and DynamIQ schemes combine high-performance and high-efficiency cores in one cluster so workloads can migrate between them to balance speed and battery life.

Licensing

Licensees integrate Cortex-A cores into their own SoCs; major users have included Apple (early A-series chips), Qualcomm, Samsung, and MediaTek, though larger customers increasingly design custom cores under architectural licenses instead.
