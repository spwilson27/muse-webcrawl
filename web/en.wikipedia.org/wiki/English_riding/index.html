English riding

English riding is a style of horse riding that developed in England and is now practised worldwide. It encompasses the disciplines seen at the Olympic Games — dressage, show jumping and eventing — as well as showing, hunt seat equitation, saddle seat and English pleasure classes.

Equipment

The English saddle is relatively flat and light, without the deep seat and horn of a Western saddle, allowing the rider close contact with the horse. Bridles typically use a snaffle or double bridle, and the rider holds the reins in both hands. Attire is formal and traditional, including a hunt coat, breeches, tall boots and a helmet or hunt cap.

Style and gaits

English riders post (rise) to the trot in most disciplines, use direct rein aids, and emphasise the horse's carriage and responsiveness. The terminology of gaits differs slightly from Western usage: the canter is a three-beat gait, and the faster four-beat gait is the gallop or "hand gallop".

Disciplines

Dressage tests the horse's obedience and gymnastic development through prescribed movements; show jumping tests scope and carefulness over coloured fences; and eventing combines dressage, cross-country jumping over fixed obstacles, and show jumping over three days or in shortened formats. Fox hunting and hunter classes, judged on the horse's manners and way of going, are the historical roots of much English equitation.
