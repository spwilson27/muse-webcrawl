Spanish art

Spanish art has made important contributions to Western art, from the prehistoric paintings of Altamira through the Golden Age masters to Picasso, Dalí and Miró in the 20th century.

Early art

The cave art of Altamira (c. 36,000–13,000 years ago) is among the world's finest prehistoric painting. Roman, Visigothic, and Islamic art — notably the Alhambra — shaped the peninsula, followed by distinctive Romanesque and Mudéjar styles blending Christian and Muslim traditions.

Golden Age

In the 17th century, under Habsburg patronage, El Greco (Greek-born), Diego Velázquez, Zurbarán, Murillo and Ribera produced some of Europe's greatest painting, characterised by naturalism and tenebrism. Francisco Goya, bridging Enlightenment and Romanticism, followed in the late 18th century.

Modern art

Spain produced leading modernists: Pablo Picasso (co-founder of Cubism, painter of Guernica), Salvador Dalí (Surrealism), Joan Miró, and Antoni Gaudí in architecture. The Museo Reina Sofía and Prado anchor its collections.
