The Canadian Premier League (CPL; French: Première ligue canadienne) is a professional soccer league in Canada and the country's primary national league at the top of the Canadian soccer league system. Sanctioned by the Canadian Soccer Association, it held its inaugural season in 2019. The league was created to provide a domestic professional pathway for Canadian players ahead of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, co-hosted by Canada, the United States, and Mexico.

History

Canada had lacked a fully professional domestic top division since the original Canadian Soccer League folded in 1992; its three biggest clubs (Toronto FC, CF Montréal, and Vancouver Whitecaps FC) play in the American Major League Soccer. The CPL was approved by the CSA in May 2017 and launched on 27 April 2019 at Tim Hortons Field in Hamilton. Forge FC won the first championship (the North Star Cup) in 2019 and went on to dominate the league's early years, winning four of the first five titles.

Format and identity

The league plays a spring-to-autumn season followed by playoffs. Rosters must meet Canadian-content requirements, including a quota of under-21 domestic players and minutes, reinforcing the league's development mission. Clubs also compete in the Canadian Championship against MLS and semi-professional sides; CPL champions qualify for the CONCACAF Champions Cup. Media and commercial rights were held from launch by Canadian Soccer Business, under a long-term arrangement that became the subject of later disputes with the CSA.

Clubs

As of the mid-2020s the eight clubs are Atlético Ottawa, Cavalry FC (Calgary), Forge FC (Hamilton), HFX Wanderers FC (Halifax), Pacific FC (Langford, BC), Valour FC (Winnipeg), Vancouver FC, and York United FC (Toronto). FC Edmonton, a founding member, folded after the 2022 season. Expansion remains a stated goal of the league.
