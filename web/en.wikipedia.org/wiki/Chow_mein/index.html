Chow mein

Chow mein (from Cantonese 炒麵, chāu-mìhn, "stir-fried noodles") is a Chinese dish of stir-fried noodles with vegetables and sometimes meat or tofu. It exists in many regional and diaspora variations worldwide.

Chow mein
Origin: China (Guangdong)
Type: Noodle dish
Main ingredients: Wheat noodles, soy sauce, vegetables

Varieties

Crispy chow mein uses fried, flat noodles served as a base with a thick sauce; soft chow mein uses steamed or boiled noodles stir-fried in a wok. In Hong Kong style, noodles are pan-fried crisp on both sides. American Chinese, Indian (Hakka), Caribbean, and Peruvian (tallarín saltado / chifa) versions adapt the dish to local tastes.

Etymology and history

The dish spread with Cantonese emigration in the 19th and 20th centuries and became one of the best-known Chinese dishes abroad.
