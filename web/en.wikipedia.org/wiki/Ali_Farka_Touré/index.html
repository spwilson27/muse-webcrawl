Ali Ibrahim "Farka" Touré (31 October 1939 – 6 March 2006) was a Malian singer and guitarist, one of the most internationally celebrated African musicians of the 20th century. His music, rooted in Songhai, Bambara and other Malian traditions, is often described as the missing link between traditional West African music and American blues — a music he insisted flowed the other way, calling the blues a descendant of Malian sounds.

Life

Born in the village of Kanau on the Niger River near Timbuktu, Touré lost his father, a soldier, in 1940 and was raised in Niafunké. Nicknamed "Farka" ("donkey") for his stubbornness, he took up the gurmi and njarka (one-string fiddle) before being captivated by the guitar he heard from the Guinean virtuoso Keïta Fodéba, and by records of John Lee Hooker, whose style he recognised as kin to his own tradition. From the 1960s he performed on Radio Mali and recorded for French labels, becoming a national figure while continuing to farm his land at Niafunké, of which he later became mayor.

International career

Albums for World Circuit — Ali Farka Touré (1988), The Source (1992) and The River (1990) — brought global acclaim, capped by Talking Timbuktu (1994), recorded with Ry Cooder, which won the Grammy Award for Best World Music Album. He famously disliked touring and insisted music served his community first. His final collaborations, In the Heart of the Moon (2005) with kora master Toumani Diabaté, won a second Grammy, and the posthumously released Ali and Toumani (2010) a third. Touré died of bone cancer in Bamako in 2006 and was buried at Niafunké. His son Vieux Farka Touré continues his musical legacy.
