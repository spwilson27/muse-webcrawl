Virabhadra

Virabhadra (Sanskrit: वीरभद्र, Vīrabhadra) is a fierce and fearsome form of the Hindu god Shiva, created by the wrath of Shiva to destroy the fire sacrifice (yajna) of Daksha after the death of Shiva's consort Sati.

Mythology

According to the Puranas, Sati immolated herself after her father Daksha insulted Shiva and excluded him from a great sacrifice. Enraged, Shiva plucked a lock of his matted hair and dashed it to the ground, from which arose Virabhadra — described as tall as the sky, dark as storm clouds, with three burning eyes, a thousand arms, and a garland of skulls, accompanied by the goddess Bhadrakali. Virabhadra stormed the sacrifice, defeated the gods in attendance, and beheaded Daksha; Daksha was later revived with the head of a goat when the gods propitiated Shiva.

Iconography and worship

Virabhadra is depicted with four or more arms bearing a bow, arrows, sword, and shield, often shown holding Daksha's severed head or standing over the sacrifice ground. He is a principal deity of the Veerashaiva (Lingayat) tradition and is worshipped in temples across Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu, notably at Lepakshi, where a celebrated Vijayanagara-era temple bears his name.

In yoga, the warrior poses Virabhadrasana I, II, and III are named for him, representing the warrior's arrival, aim, and strike.
