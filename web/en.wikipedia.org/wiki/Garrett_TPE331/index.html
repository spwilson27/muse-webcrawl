The Garrett TPE331 (now Honeywell TPE331) is a turboprop aircraft engine designed and manufactured in the United States, originally by Garrett AiResearch and produced by Honeywell Aerospace since 1999. It was one of the first small turboprops to enter production and remains one of the most widely used engines in its class, with more than 13,000 delivered.

Design and development

Development began in the late 1950s; the engine was originally designed for helicopters as the TSE331 before being adapted as a fixed-wing turboprop. The TPE331 is a single-shaft engine with a two-stage centrifugal compressor, an annular reverse-flow combustion chamber and a three-stage axial turbine, driving the propeller through an integral reduction gearbox. Power output ranges from about 575 shp in early variants to around 1,650 shp (or equivalent thrust ratings) in later models.

Applications

The engine powered many of the most successful small turboprops, including the Mitsubishi MU-2, Cessna 441 Conquest II, Swearingen Merlin and Metroliner series, Dornier 228, Short Tucano (TPE331-12B) and the General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper UAV in its TPE331-10 form. TPE331 conversions are also popular upgrades for the Beechcraft King Air. A turboshaft relative, the TPE331-based CTS800 — developed jointly with Rolls-Royce as the LHTEC T800 — powers helicopters such as the AW159 Wildcat.

Variants

Designations include the TPE331-1 through -14 families, differing in gearbox configuration, power rating and single- or dual-installation use; the -14 powers the Dornier 228 and the -10 the MQ-9. Military versions carry the designation T76.
