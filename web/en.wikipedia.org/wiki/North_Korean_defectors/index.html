North Korean defectors

Common defection routes from North Korea

North Korean defectors are people who have managed to flee North Korea, most often settling in South Korea, where they are entitled to citizenship under South Korean law, which regards the entire Korean Peninsula as its territory. In South Korea they are officially termed talbukja (탈북자, "people who fled the North") or North Korean refugees.

Escape routes

Because the Korean Demilitarized Zone is heavily fortified and mined, most defectors travel north across the Yalu or Tumen rivers into China, then make their way to a third country — commonly through Laos, Vietnam, Thailand or Mongolia — from which they can reach South Korea. China does not recognise North Koreans as refugees and may repatriate them, so many live in hiding there, often with the help of brokers and Christian missionary networks.

Numbers and history

Defections rose sharply during the North Korean famine of the 1990s. Annual arrivals in South Korea exceeded 2,900 in 2009 before declining as border controls tightened. By the early 2020s, more than 34,000 North Korean defectors had resettled in South Korea. Arrivals fell dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic period, when North Korea sealed its borders.

Resettlement

On arrival, defectors are questioned by South Korean authorities and then attend the Hanawon resettlement centre for about three months of education intended to help them adjust to life in the South. They receive housing support and settlement funds, but many face discrimination, language differences in dialect and vocabulary, and difficulties finding employment and adjusting to a market economy.
