Stefan Starzyński

Born19 August 1893, Warsaw
Diedc. December 1939 (aged 46)
OfficeMayor of Warsaw (1934–1939)

Stefan Starzyński (19 August 1893 – c. 21–23 December 1939) was a Polish statesman, economist, military officer and publicist, best known as the mayor of Warsaw from 1934 to 1939 and as the heroic civil leader of the city during its siege in September 1939.

Career

Starzyński served in the Polish Legions during the First World War and in the Polish Army during the Polish–Soviet War. He worked in state administration and banking and was appointed mayor (city president) of Warsaw in 1934. His administration undertook ambitious modernisation: developing transport, sanitation, schools and housing, and promoting the vision of a modern capital.

Siege of Warsaw and death

Refusing to leave the besieged city in September 1939, Starzyński organised civil defence, delivered daily radio broadcasts that became a symbol of Polish resistance, and served as civil commissioner at the Warsaw Defence Command. After the city's capitulation he was arrested by the Gestapo in October 1939 and murdered in December 1939, probably at Palmiry; the exact circumstances of his death remain unknown.
