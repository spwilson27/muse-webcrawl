New York State Barge Canal

The New York State Barge Canal is a system of state-owned canals in New York, built between 1905 and 1918 to replace and enlarge the original Erie Canal and its lateral canals. The system consists of the Erie Canal, the Oswego Canal, the Champlain Canal, and the Cayuga–Seneca Canal, with a total length of about 525 miles (845 km). It connected the Hudson River and Lake Champlain with the Great Lakes, allowing barges far larger than those of the old canal to move freight across the state.

Construction

New York voters approved a $101 million bond act in 1903 to build the Barge Canal. Rather than simply widening the old Erie Canal towpath ditch, engineers largely riverized the route, using canalized sections of the Mohawk, Seneca, Oswego, and other rivers, with dams and locks raising and lowering vessels. The canal opened to through traffic in 1918. Its locks, about 328 feet long and 45 feet wide, could handle barges carrying up to roughly 3,000 tons, but the system was soon outcompeted by railroads and, later, trucks and the St. Lawrence Seaway.

Later use

Commercial traffic declined through the twentieth century, and the canal is used mainly by recreational boaters today. In 1992 the operating agency was renamed the New York State Canal Corporation, and the waterway was rebranded as the New York State Canal System. The canal corridor is also recognized as part of the Erie Canalway National Heritage Corridor. See also New York State Canal System.
