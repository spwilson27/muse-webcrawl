Clive Campbell (born 16 April 1955), better known by his stage name DJ Kool Herc, is a Jamaican-American DJ widely credited as a founding father of hip hop music. His parties in the Bronx in the 1970s established the breakbeat DJing technique at the heart of the genre.

Early life

Campbell was born in Kingston, Jamaica, and emigrated with his family to the Bronx, New York, in 1967. Influenced by the Jamaican sound system culture of his childhood, he built a powerful PA system and began throwing parties; his height and athletic build earned him the nickname "Hercules", shortened to Herc.

The birth of hip hop

On 11 August 1973, Herc and his sister Cindy Campbell hosted a back-to-school party in the recreation room at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx — an event often cited as the birth of hip hop. Herc pioneered the "breakbeat" technique: using two copies of the same record and two turntables, he switched between them to extend the percussive "break" sections dancers loved, a method he called the "Merry-Go-Round". He also coined the terms "b-boys" and "b-girls" for the breakdancers and developed a style of chanting and talking over records that fed into rapping, with Coke La Rock among the first MCs to perform with him.

Later life and recognition

Herc's influence waned in the early 1980s as Grandmaster Flash, Afrika Bambaataa and others developed his innovations, and he never recorded commercially at his peak. Recognition grew in later decades: 1520 Sedgwick Avenue was officially recognised by New York State in 2007 as a birthplace of hip hop, and in 2023 DJ Kool Herc was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.
