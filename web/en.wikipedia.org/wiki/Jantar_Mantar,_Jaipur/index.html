Jantar Mantar, Jaipur

The Jantar Mantar is a collection of nineteen architectural astronomical instruments built by the Rajput king Sawai Jai Singh II, the founder of Jaipur, Rajasthan. Completed in 1734, it features the world's largest stone sundial and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, part of the "Jantar Mantar, Jaipur" inscription of 2010 and the wider Hill Forts / Jaipur City listings.

Jantar Mantar, Jaipur
Location: Jaipur, Rajasthan, India
Built: c. 1734
Builder: Sawai Jai Singh II
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 2010

Description

The observatory, one of five Jantar Mantars, lies near the City Palace and Hawa Mahal. Its instruments are built of local stone and marble, many restored after damage, and were used to observe astronomical positions with the naked eye in the Ptolemaic tradition.

Instruments

The Vrihat Samrat Yantra is a 27-metre-high equinoctial sundial whose shadow moves visibly, telling local time to about two seconds' accuracy. Others include the Jai Prakash Yantra (two hemispherical bowls), the Ram Yantra, the Chakra Yantra for declination, the Digamsha Yantra, and the twelve Rashivalaya instruments, one for each zodiac sign.

Purpose and legacy

Jai Singh used the instruments to compile the Zij-i Muhammad Shahi astronomical tables, refine the calendar and cast horoscopes. The site fell into disuse in the late 18th century, was restored under British supervision in 1901 and again by the Archaeological Survey of India, and today is a major tourist attraction in Jaipur.
