The ISO metric screw thread is the most commonly used general-purpose screw thread in the world. It is one of a family of standards defined by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), characterised by a symmetrical V-shaped profile with a 60° thread angle and dimensions specified in millimetres. Metric threaded fasteners are designated by the letter M followed by the nominal major diameter and, for fine threads, the pitch.

Basic profile

The fundamental profile (ISO 68-1) is an equilateral-triangle V-thread. The thread height H of the sharp V is (√3/2) × pitch ≈ 0.866 × pitch. The basic profile truncates the external thread root and the internal thread crest so that the working depth of engagement is 5H/8. Tolerances and allowances are specified in ISO 965, with tolerance classes such as 6g for external and 6H for internal threads being the common medium-fit default.

Designation and preferred sizes

A coarse-thread fastener is designated simply by diameter, e.g. M8 means a nominal diameter of 8 mm with the standard coarse pitch of 1.25 mm. Fine threads include the pitch explicitly, e.g. M8×1 has a 1 mm pitch. ISO 261 gives the general plan of diameters (1–64 mm and beyond) and pitches; ISO 262 selects preferred sizes for bolts, screws and nuts.

History and interchangeability

Metric threads descend from the late-19th-century Système International (SI) thread adopted in France in 1898 and German DIN standards; the ISO metric profile unified these after the Second World War. It is similar in form to, but not interchangeable with, the inch-based Unified Thread Standard (UTS) used in the United States, which shares the 60° angle but uses inch diameters, threads-per-inch pitch specification, and its own tolerance system.
