Grime music is the broader musical scene and industry built around the grime genre: the artists, labels, radio stations, video platforms and live circuit that grew from the East London pirate radio culture of the early 2000s into one of the defining British youth musics of the 21st century. While the genre is defined by its 140 BPM sound, the scene is defined by its institutions — pirate sets, clash culture, independent labels and online video channels.

Scene and infrastructure

In grime's first decade, pirate radio was its engine: stations such as Deja Vu, Rinse FM and Heat FM broadcast rooftop sets where crews of MCs competed over DJ-mixed instrumentals, and recordings circulated as tapes, CDs and files. Television exposure came through the urban music channel Channel AKA (founded as Channel U), which gave low-budget grime videos national airplay. From the late 2000s, YouTube channels — SB.TV (founded by Jamal Edwards), Link Up TV and GRM Daily — became the scene's main shop window, launching the careers of the revival generation.

Independent and DIY release strategies defined the business: Wiley's Eskibeat, Skepta and Jme's Boy Better Know, and Stormzy's #Merky label all operated outside the major-label system even while licensing or distributing through it. Live grime moved from raves and youth-club clashes to festival main stages, with Stormzy headlining Glastonbury in 2019 — a symbolic peak for a scene that had long been policed out of London venues, notably under the Metropolitan Police's Form 696 risk-assessment regime, widely criticised for discriminating against grime and garage events before its abolition in 2017.

Influence

Grime music reshaped British popular culture: it normalised London accents and slang in chart music, pioneered the online-first release model later adopted across UK rap, and provided the template — and much of the production and MC talent — for afroswing and UK drill. It also became a vehicle for political commentary, from youth-club closures to the "Grime4Corbyn" campaign of the 2017 general election.
