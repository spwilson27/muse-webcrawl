The North German Confederation (German: Norddeutscher Bund) was a confederation of 22 German states north of the river Main, established in August 1866 under the leadership of the Kingdom of Prussia and formalised by a constitution that came into force on 1 July 1867. It existed until the proclamation of the German Empire on 18 January 1871, into which it was effectively absorbed.

The Confederation was created after Prussia's victory over Austria in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, which dissolved the old German Confederation. It represented a decisive step toward German unification under Prussian dominance, excluding Austria and the southern German states of Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden and Hesse-Darmstadt.

History

Following the Peace of Prague (23 August 1866), Austria withdrew from German affairs, and Prussia annexed Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau, Frankfurt and Schleswig-Holstein. The remaining northern states entered the August Alliance with Prussia on 18 August 1866. Otto von Bismarck, the Prussian minister president, drafted the federal constitution, which was adopted by a constituent Reichstag elected by universal male suffrage.

The constitution made King Wilhelm I of Prussia the Confederation's hereditary president (Bundespräsidium) and commander-in-chief of its armed forces, while Bismarck became federal chancellor, responsible only to the president. The southern states remained outside but were bound to Prussia by secret defensive alliances and joined the renewed Zollverein customs union in 1868.

During the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), the southern states acceded to the Confederation, and on 18 January 1871 Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles. The Confederation's constitution, with amendments, became the constitution of the German Empire.

Political system

The legislature consisted of the Reichstag, elected by universal and equal manhood suffrage — remarkably democratic for its time — and the Bundesrat, a council of delegates from the state governments in which Prussia held 17 of 43 votes. The federal executive rested with the Prussian king as president and with the chancellor, who presided over the Bundesrat. The Confederation controlled foreign policy, the military, customs, trade, currency, posts and railways, while the member states retained much domestic administration.

Member states

The Confederation comprised the Kingdom of Prussia, the Kingdom of Saxony, the grand duchies of Mecklenburg-Schwerin, Mecklenburg-Strelitz, Oldenburg and Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach, several duchies and principalities, and the free Hanseatic cities of Hamburg, Bremen and Lübeck — 22 states in total.

Legacy

Historians regard the North German Confederation as the institutional blueprint of the German Empire: its constitution, universal-suffrage Reichstag, federal council and chancellor system all carried over into 1871, and its legal and economic unification (including the adoption of a common commercial code and the groundwork for a unified currency) laid the foundations of the modern German state.
