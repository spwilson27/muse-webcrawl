The National Iranian Oil Company (NIOC; Persian: شرکت ملی نفت ایران) is a government-owned corporation under the supervision of Iran's Ministry of Petroleum, responsible for the exploration, production, refining, and export of Iran's crude oil and natural gas. It is regularly ranked among the world's largest oil companies, and it manages some of the largest hydrocarbon reserves on earth, including the giant Ahvaz, Marun, and Gachsaran fields.

History

NIOC was created following the nationalisation of Iran's oil industry in March–April 1951, when the Majlis, under Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh and the National Front, voted to take over the assets of the British-owned Anglo-Iranian Oil Company. Britain organised a worldwide embargo of Iranian oil, and after the 1953 coup NIOC entered a consortium agreement with Western oil companies, though formal ownership of the resources remained Iranian. After the 1979 revolution, foreign participation ended and NIOC assumed full control of operations.

Operations

Through subsidiaries such as the National Iranian South Oil Company and the Iranian Offshore Oil Company, NIOC produces crude oil and gas onshore and in the Persian Gulf, and oversees Iran's share of the South Pars/North Dome gas field, the largest natural gas field in the world. International sanctions since the 2000s have constrained its exports and access to technology, shifting its customer base largely toward Asia.
