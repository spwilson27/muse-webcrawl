Skat (card game)

Skat is a three-player trick-taking card game devised around 1810 in Altenburg, Germany. It is Germany's national card game and is played with a 32-card pack. In 2016 it was inscribed on Germany's national inventory of intangible cultural heritage.

Players: 3 (4 with a dealer sitting out)
Cards: 32
Deck: French- or German-suited
Origin: Altenburg, Germany, c. 1810

Overview

After an auction, the high bidder (the soloist) plays alone against the other two, who form a temporary partnership. The soloist chooses the game type: a suit game, grand (only jacks trump) or null (aiming to lose every trick), and may pick up the two-card skat to exchange cards.

Scoring

In suit and grand games the soloist must take at least 61 of the 120 card points to win. Game values depend on the trump suit, matadors (the sequence of top trumps held or missing), and declared bonuses such as schneider and schwarz.

Organisation

The German Skat Association (Deutscher Skatverband) oversees official rules and championships; the International Skat Players ' Association also maintains rules used internationally.
