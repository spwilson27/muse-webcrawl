Angioma

An angioma is a benign growth made up of blood vessels or lymphatic vessels. Angiomas are among the common vascular anomalies seen on the skin and in internal organs. Many are present at birth or appear in early childhood, while others, such as cherry angiomas, become more frequent with age.

Types

Common forms include haemangiomas, which are composed mainly of blood vessels, and lymphangiomas, which arise from lymphatic tissue. Infantile haemangiomas often grow during the first months of life and then slowly involute. Cherry angiomas are small red papules that usually occur on the trunk of adults.

Diagnosis and treatment

Most cutaneous angiomas are diagnosed clinically. Dermoscopy, ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging or biopsy may be used when the appearance is uncertain or an internal lesion is suspected. Treatment is often unnecessary, but laser therapy, cryotherapy, sclerotherapy or surgery may be used for bleeding, ulceration, pain, functional problems or cosmetic concerns.
