Corrèze (French pronunciation: [kɔʁɛz]; Occitan: Corresa) is a department in west-central France, in the region of Nouvelle-Aquitaine. It is named after the Corrèze River, a tributary of the Vézère, and its prefecture is Tulle. It is one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, formed from part of the former province of Limousin. Its INSEE and postal code is 19.

Geography

Corrèze lies at the western edge of the Massif Central. Its landscape rises from the Brive basin in the southwest to the high plateaus of the Millevaches in the northeast, where the department's highest ground approaches nearly 1,000 metres. The Dordogne River crosses the southeast, and the Vézère and Corrèze rivers drain the west. The largest town is Brive-la-Gaillarde, which is bigger than the prefecture, Tulle; other towns include Ussel, Égletons, and Uzerche.

History

The area was settled early and was later part of the viscounty of Turenne. During the Second World War the maquis of Limousin were active in the department; in June 1944, after an uprising in Tulle, SS troops murdered 99 men of the town in reprisal (the Tulle massacre) and deported others, days before the similar atrocity at Oradour-sur-Glane in the neighbouring Haute-Vienne.

Politics

Corrèze is associated with two French presidents: Jacques Chirac, who represented the department in the National Assembly and was a long-serving local figure, and François Hollande, who served as deputy for Corrèze, mayor of Tulle, and president of the departmental council before becoming president in 2012.
