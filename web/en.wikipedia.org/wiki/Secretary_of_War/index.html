Secretary of War

Secretary of War was the title of the head of the United States Department of War, a cabinet-level position responsible for the United States Army and military affairs generally, from 1789 until 1947. The Secretary was appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate, and for most of the office's existence the Secretary of War also supervised naval affairs until a separate Navy Department was created in 1798, and later oversaw the Army Air Forces.

History

The Department of War was established by Congress on 7 August 1789, and Henry Knox, who had served as Secretary of War under the Articles of Confederation, became the first holder under the Constitution. The Secretary administered the small frontier army, Indian affairs (until the Interior Department took them over in 1849), military pensions, and fortifications.

The office expanded enormously during wartime: under Edwin M. Stanton during the Civil War, and under Newton D. Baker in the First World War and Henry L. Stimson in the Second World War, the Secretary oversaw armies numbering in the millions. In peacetime, Secretaries such as John C. Calhoun and William Howard Taft (later President and Chief Justice) shaped army organisation and administration.

Abolition and succession

The National Security Act of 1947 abolished the Department of War, dividing it into the Department of the Army and the Department of the Air Force, both placed under a new Secretary of Defense. The last Secretary of War, Kenneth C. Royall, became the first Secretary of the Army. The civilian head of the Army today retains the title Secretary of the Army.
