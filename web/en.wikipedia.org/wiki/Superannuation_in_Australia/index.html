Superannuation in Australia

Superannuation in Australia, colloquially known as "super," is the system of compulsory and voluntary retirement savings through which Australians accumulate funds for retirement income. Employers are required by law to contribute a percentage of an employee's earnings — the Superannuation Guarantee — into a superannuation fund of the employee's choice. With assets of around AU$4 trillion, the system is among the largest pools of managed funds in the world.

History

Pension schemes for public servants and some white-collar workers existed from the 19th century, but coverage was narrow. In 1983 the Prices and Incomes Accord between the Hawke government and the ACTU traded wage increases for employer superannuation contributions, spreading occupational super across the workforce. The Keating government legislated the Superannuation Guarantee in 1992, starting at 3–4% of earnings and rising by schedule to 12% from 1 July 2025.

Structure

Savings are held in funds regulated by the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) or, for self-managed super funds (SMSFs), the Australian Taxation Office. Contributions and fund earnings are taxed concessionally (generally 15%), and benefits withdrawn after age 60 are typically tax-free. Members may make additional concessional (pre-tax) and non-concessional contributions, invest through default MySuper products or investment choice, and generally cannot access savings until reaching preservation age or retiring, subject to limited hardship and compassionate-ground exceptions. The government Age Pension provides a safety-net supplement alongside superannuation.

Scale and debate

Industry funds such as AustralianSuper and large retail funds dominate membership. Policy debate continues over tax concessions (which skew toward high earners), fees and insurance within super, the gender gap in balances, and proposals such as the Division 296 tax on very large balances.
