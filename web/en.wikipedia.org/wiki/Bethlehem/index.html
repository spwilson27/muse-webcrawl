Bethlehem is a city in the West Bank, Palestine, located about 10 kilometres (6 miles) south of Jerusalem. It is the capital of the Bethlehem Governorate of Palestine. In the Hebrew Bible, Bethlehem is identified as the city of David and the birthplace of his ancestor Ruth's family; in the New Testament Gospels of Matthew and Luke, it is the birthplace of Jesus, commemorated by the Church of the Nativity.

History

Bethlehem's name derives from the Hebrew Beit Lehem, "House of Bread" (the Arabic Beit Laḥm means "House of Meat"). The town is first mentioned in the Amarna letters of the 14th century BC. According to tradition, the Church of the Nativity was built over the grotto of Jesus's birth by the emperor Constantine's mother Helena in the 4th century; it is among the oldest continuously used churches in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2012.

Bethlehem passed through Roman, Byzantine, Muslim, Crusader, Ottoman, and British Mandate rule. After the 1948 Arab–Israeli War it was annexed by Jordan; Israel occupied it in 1967; and in 1995 it came under Palestinian Authority administration. The Israeli West Bank barrier surrounds part of the city, and its economy is heavily dependent on tourism and pilgrimage, especially at Christmas.

Demographics and culture

Bethlehem once had a Christian majority; today Christians form a minority of the city's population, though the mayor is by convention a Christian. The city is home to Bethlehem University, founded in 1973, and is known for olive-wood carving, mother-of-pearl work, and embroidery.
