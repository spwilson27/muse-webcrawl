Lenny Kaye (born December 27, 1946) is an American guitarist, composer, record producer and music writer, best known as the lead guitarist of the Patti Smith Group and as the compiler of the influential 1972 garage-rock anthology Nuggets: Original Artyfacts from the First Psychedelic Era, 1965–1968.

Career

Kaye grew up in New York and New Jersey and worked as a record-store clerk and freelance rock journalist, writing for publications including Rolling Stone, Crawdaddy and the Village Voice. While at Elektra Records he assembled Nuggets, a double album of American garage and psychedelic singles whose liner notes popularised the term "punk rock" for the 1960s style and which profoundly influenced the later punk movement.

In 1971 he began accompanying poet Patti Smith at readings; the collaboration grew into the Patti Smith Group, co-writing songs such as "Rock n Roll Nigger" and playing on albums including Horses (1975), Radio Ethiopia, Easter and Wave. After the group disbanded in 1979 he produced records for artists including Suzanne Vega (whose "Luka" he co-produced), Soul Asylum and Kristen Hersh, and formed his own band. He rejoined Smith when she returned to recording in the 1990s and has played with her ever since. In 2021 he published the memoir/history Lightning Striking: Ten Transformative Moments in Rock and Roll.
