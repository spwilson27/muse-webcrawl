Mary Had a Little Lamb

"Mary Had a Little Lamb" is an American nursery rhyme first published in 1830, with words by Sarah Josepha Hale. It is one of the most familiar children's rhymes in English and was the first recorded spoken text on Thomas Edison's phonograph.

Lyrics

Mary had a little lamb,
Its fleece was white as snow,
And everywhere that Mary went
The lamb was sure to go.

It followed her to school one day,
Which was against the rule.
It made the children laugh and play
To see a lamb at school.

Origin

The poem is commonly said to have been inspired by a real incident in which a girl named Mary Sawyer, of Sterling, Massachusetts, brought her pet lamb to school one day in 1816 or 1817, to the delight of her classmates. Hale published the first four verses in her 1830 collection Poems for Our Children. The first recorded sound, in 1877, was Thomas Edison reciting the rhyme into his phonograph.

Later uses

The rhyme has appeared in countless children's books, songs, and adaptations, and its melody is frequently used in music education.
