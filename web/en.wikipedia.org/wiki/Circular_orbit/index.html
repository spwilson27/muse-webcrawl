Circular orbit

A circular orbit is an orbit with a fixed distance around the barycenter; that is, in the shape of a circle. The orbit of a body moving with constant speed along a circular path is considered a special case in general orbit theory, in which the eccentricity is equal to zero.

TypeOrbit
Eccentricity0
Semi-major axisEqual to orbital radius
Orbital speedConstant

Speed

In a circular orbit, the speed is constant and given by:

v = √(GM / r)

where G is the gravitational constant, M is the mass of the central body, and r is the radius of the orbit.

Orbital period

The orbital period of a circular orbit follows from Kepler's third law:

T = 2π √(r³ / GM)

Acceleration

The centripetal acceleration required to maintain a circular orbit is directed toward the central body and has magnitude a = v²/r = GM/r². Circular orbits are approximated by many artificial satellites, including geostationary satellites and satellites in low Earth orbit.
