European–African–Middle Eastern Campaign Medal

European–African–Middle Eastern Campaign Medal
Type:Military campaign medal
Awarded for:Service in the European-African-Middle Eastern Theater during World War II
Presented by:United States Department of Defense
Established:6 November 1942

The European–African–Middle Eastern Campaign Medal is a military award of the United States Armed Forces which was first created on 6 November 1942 by Executive Order 9265 issued by President Franklin D. Roosevelt. The medal was intended to recognise those military service members who had performed military duty in the European Theater, including North Africa and the Middle East, during World War II.

History

The medal was designed by Thomas Hudson Jones, with the ribbon work carried out following a design by Colonel Robert P. Patterson. It was originally issued as a service ribbon; the full medal was authorised in 1946 and issued from 1947 onwards.

Design

The bronze medal's obverse shows a landing ship putting troops ashore amid aircraft overhead, representing amphibious assault operations. The reverse depicts an eagle perched on a date palm with the words "European African Middle Eastern Campaign". The ribbon features stripes of brown (for the sands of the desert), green (for European fields and forests), and the colours of the flags of the principal Axis adversaries, Germany and Italy.

Campaign participation

Service members who participated in designated campaigns, such as the North African landings, Sicily, Normandy, the Rhineland, and the air offensive over Europe, were entitled to wear bronze campaign stars on the ribbon; an arrowhead device denoted participation in an amphibious assault landing.
