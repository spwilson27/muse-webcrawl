Struve Geodetic Arc

Length2,820 km (1,750 mi)
Surveyed1816–1855
Countries10 (Norway to Ukraine)
UNESCO World Heritage SiteInscribed 2005

The Struve Geodetic Arc is a chain of survey triangulations stretching from Hammerfest in Norway to the Black Sea, through ten countries and over 2,820 kilometres (1,750 mi), which yielded the first accurate measurement of a meridian arc.

The chain was established and used by the German-born Russian scientist Friedrich Georg Wilhelm von Struve between 1816 and 1855 to establish the exact size and shape of the Earth. At that time, the chain passed through only three countries: the Union of Sweden-Norway, the Russian Empire, and a point in the region of present-day Ukraine. The measurements confirmed Newton's prediction that the Earth is flattened at the poles.

Survey points

The arc consists of 258 main triangles with 265 main station points. Of these, 34 original station points — marked by drilled holes in rock, iron crosses, cairns, obelisks, and other monuments — are protected. The northernmost point is near Fuglenes in Hammerfest, Norway, and the southernmost point is near Nekrasivka in the Odesa region of Ukraine.

World Heritage status

In 2005, the Struve Geodetic Arc was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List as a transnational property shared by Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Belarus, Moldova, and Ukraine. It was the first technical-scientific property and one of the longest linear sites on the list.
