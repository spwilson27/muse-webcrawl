Oxygen-burning process

The oxygen-burning process is a set of nuclear fusion reactions that take place in massive stars that have used up the lighter elements in their cores. Oxygen burning occurs after the star has contracted enough to raise the core temperature and density to roughly 1.5×109 K and about 1010 kg/m3. It follows the neon-burning process and precedes the silicon-burning process in the sequence of stellar nucleosynthesis stages.

Reactions

When oxygen burning begins, two oxygen-16 nuclei fuse. The principal reaction channels include:

ReactionProducts
16O + 16O28Si + 4He
16O + 16O31P + 1H
16O + 16O31S + n
16O + 16O32S + γ
16O + 16O30Si + 2 1H

The main products of the process are silicon-28 and sulfur-32, with lesser amounts of phosphorus and other intermediate-mass elements. As oxygen is depleted, energy production from this stage declines and the core contracts further, heating towards silicon burning, the final fusion stage before core collapse.

Timescale

Oxygen burning proceeds much faster than earlier stages: in a star of about 25 solar masses the oxygen-burning phase lasts on the order of months to a few years, compared with thousands of years for carbon burning and hundreds of thousands of years for helium burning.
