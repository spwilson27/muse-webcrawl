Eclipse (April 1, 1764 – February 26, 1789) was an undefeated 18th-century British Thoroughbred racehorse who won all 18 of his races and went on to become one of the most influential sires in the history of the breed. The overwhelming majority of modern Thoroughbreds — often estimated at around 95% of male-line pedigrees and a very large share of all pedigrees — trace to Eclipse, and the phrase "Eclipse first, the rest nowhere", coined during his career, remains the classic expression of a completely outclassed field.

Racing career

Eclipse was foaled during, and named for, the solar eclipse of April 1, 1764, at the Cranbourne Lodge stud of Prince William, Duke of Cumberland. After the Duke's death he was sold to the sheep salesman William Wildman, and later half, then wholly, to the Irish adventurer Colonel Dennis O'Kelly. A big, powerful chestnut — about 16 hands, higher at the quarters than the withers, with a famously long stride and an awkward temper — Eclipse did not race until he was five, making his debut at Epsom in May 1769. He won with such ease that O'Kelly is said to have declared, before the second heat, "Eclipse first, the rest nowhere" — meaning no other horse would even be within the judges' distance — and won heavily on the prediction.

Racing mainly in four-mile heats and King's Plates at courses including Epsom, Ascot, Newmarket, York and Winchester, Eclipse was never seriously extended: he usually cantered home, and rivals increasingly declined to oppose him, so that several of his "wins" were walkovers. He retired undefeated in October 1770.

Stud career and legacy

At stud, first at Clay Hill near Epsom and later at O'Kelly's Cannons stud, Eclipse sired 344 winners of races, including three Epsom Derby winners (Young Eclipse, Serjeant and Saltram). His skeleton, preserved after an autopsy by the veterinarian Charles Vial de Sainbel — who used Eclipse's proportions as the model of the perfect racehorse — is now in the collection of the Royal Veterinary College; his hooves were famously made into inkstands. DNA and pedigree studies confirm his dominance of the modern breed: the Eclipse sire line, through descendants such as Pot-8-Os and later Phalaris and Nearco, produced the great majority of today's champion racehorses. The Eclipse Stakes at Sandown Park (founded 1886) and the Eclipse Awards in North American racing commemorate his name.
