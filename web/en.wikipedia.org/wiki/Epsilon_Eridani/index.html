Epsilon Eridani (Latinized from ε Eridani), formally named Ran, is a star in the southern constellation of Eridanus. At 10.5 light-years away, it is the third-closest star system visible to the naked eye. It is a young orange dwarf younger than one billion years old, surrounded by two belts of rocky debris and a dusty disc.

Planetary system

Radial velocity observations announced in 2000 suggested a Jupiter-like planet (Epsilon Eridani b, sometimes called Ægir) orbiting at about 3.4 AU with a period near 7 years; its existence and parameters remain debated due to stellar activity. Infrared observations reveal dust rings analogous to an extended Kuiper belt.

In culture and SETI

As a nearby Sun-like star, it was one of the first targets of Project Ozma's search for extraterrestrial radio signals in 1960. It features frequently in science fiction, including as the setting of Babylon 5 and Isaac Asimov's works.
