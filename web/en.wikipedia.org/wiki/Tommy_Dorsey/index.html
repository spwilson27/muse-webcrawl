Thomas Francis Dorsey Jr. (November 19, 1905 – November 26, 1956) was an American jazz trombonist, composer, conductor and bandleader of the big band era. He was known as the "Sentimental Gentleman of Swing" because of his smooth-toned trombone playing. His technical skill on the trombone gave him a reputation as an outstanding musician, and his band was among the most popular of the swing era.

Early life and career

Dorsey was born in Shenandoah, Pennsylvania, the second son of a coal miner and bandmaster who taught both Tommy and his elder brother Jimmy Dorsey to play. The brothers worked together in dance bands through the 1920s, including Paul Whiteman's orchestra, and co-led the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra until a famous falling-out on stage in 1935, after which Tommy walked out to form his own band.

The Tommy Dorsey Orchestra

Tommy Dorsey's orchestra achieved major hits including "I'm Getting Sentimental Over You" (his theme song), "Marie", "The One I Love (Belongs to Somebody Else)", and in 1940 the chart-topping "I'll Never Smile Again", sung by a young Frank Sinatra with the Pied Pipers. Sinatra's years with Dorsey (1940–1942) were formative to his career. The band also featured at various times trumpeter Bunny Berigan, drummer Buddy Rich, and vocalists Jack Leonard, Edythe Wright and Jo Stafford.

Known as a strict disciplinarian and shrewd businessman, Dorsey kept his band at or near the top of the polls and sales charts throughout the swing era and adapted, less successfully, to the post-war decline of big bands.

Death and legacy

Dorsey died on November 26, 1956, aged 51, at his home in Greenwich, Connecticut, choking in his sleep after a heavy meal combined with sleeping medication. Jimmy Dorsey took over the band, which was later led by both brothers' name as the Dorsey Brothers orchestra until Jimmy's death in 1957. Tommy Dorsey's trombone style — notable for its legato phrasing and pure tone — remains influential, and his recordings of the late 1930s and early 1940s are considered classics of the big band era.
