California roll

California roll
Type: Uramaki sushi
Place of origin: disputed (Los Angeles or Vancouver)
Main ingredients: rice, nori, crab (or imitation crab), avocado, cucumber

The California roll is a type of uramaki (inside-out) sushi roll, typically containing crab or imitation crab, avocado and cucumber, with the rice on the outside of the nori and often sprinkled with sesame seeds or tobiko.

Origins

The roll's creation is disputed: it is most often credited to chef Ichiro Mashita at the Tokyo Kaikan restaurant in Los Angeles in the 1960s or 1970s, while Vancouver chef Hidekazu Tojo has claimed to have invented the inside-out roll in 1974. Substituting avocado for fatty tuna and hiding the seaweed inside helped introduce American diners to sushi.

Popularity

The California roll became one of the most popular sushi items in the United States and then worldwide, pioneering fusion rolls that incorporate local ingredients. Variants add mayonnaise, tempura shrimp, or spicy sauces, and the basic avocado-and-crab combination remains a standard gateway roll for sushi beginners.
