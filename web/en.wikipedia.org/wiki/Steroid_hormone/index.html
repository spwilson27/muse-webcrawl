Steroid hormone

A steroid hormone is a steroid that acts as a hormone. Steroid hormones can be grouped into two classes: corticosteroids (typically made in the adrenal cortex, hence cortico-) and sex steroids (commonly made in the gonads or placenta). Within those two classes are five types according to the receptors to which they bind: glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids (both corticosteroids) and androgens, estrogens, and progestogens (sex steroids).

Synthesis

All steroid hormones are synthesized from cholesterol, chiefly in the adrenal cortex and gonads, via a series of enzymatic steps (steroidogenesis) beginning with the conversion of cholesterol to pregnenolone by the enzyme CYP11A1 in mitochondria. Vitamin D derivatives are a further, closely related class of secosteroid hormones.

Mechanism of action

Being lipophilic, steroid hormones circulate bound to plasma proteins such as albumin and sex hormone-binding globulin, diffuse across cell membranes, and bind intracellular nuclear receptors (which are ligand-activated transcription factors). The hormone–receptor complex alters gene expression, producing effects over hours to days — slower, but more prolonged, than peptide hormone signalling. Some rapid, non-genomic effects are mediated by membrane-associated receptors.

Examples and medical use

Natural examples include cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, estradiol and progesterone. Synthetic steroids such as prednisone, dexamethasone and anabolic steroids are widely used as medicines, and imbalances of steroid hormones underlie disorders such as Cushing's syndrome, Addison's disease and congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
