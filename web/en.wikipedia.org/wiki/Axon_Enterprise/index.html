Axon Enterprise, Inc. is an American technology company headquartered in Scottsdale, Arizona, that develops technology and weapons for law enforcement and the military. Founded in 1993 as TASER International by brothers Rick and Tom Smith, the company is best known for its Taser brand of conducted energy weapons ("stun guns") and for its Axon line of police body-worn cameras and the cloud-based digital evidence management platform Evidence.com. In April 2017 the company renamed itself Axon Enterprise to reflect its expansion from weapons into cameras and software.

History

Rick Smith founded the company after two friends were shot and killed in a road-rage incident in Scottsdale, seeking a non-lethal alternative to firearms; he licensed technology descended from Jack Cover's original 1970s TASER device. The company went public in 2001. Its Taser weapons were widely adopted by police departments in the United States and abroad, alongside continuing debate and litigation over their safety.

In 2009 Axon launched its first body-worn camera business (TASER Pro). Demand for body cameras surged after 2014 amid public scrutiny of police use of force, and Axon became the dominant supplier to large U.S. agencies, bundling cameras with Evidence.com subscriptions. Subsequent growth added in-car video, interview-room recording, the Axon Records and Axon Dispatch (via acquisitions including Fūsus in 2024) software lines, drone and robotic systems through partnerships, and the Taser 7 and Taser 10 weapons. Axon's recurring software revenue and bundling strategy made it one of the most highly valued public-safety technology companies; it joined the S&P 500 in 2023.

Criticism and controversies

Axon has faced scrutiny over the safety of conducted energy weapons, the ethics of facial-recognition in body cameras (its AI ethics board resigned en masse in 2022 over a proposed Taser-equipped drone for schools, which the company then paused), and the competitive effects of its bundled contracts, including a U.S. Federal Trade Commission challenge related to its 2018 acquisition of rival VieVu.
