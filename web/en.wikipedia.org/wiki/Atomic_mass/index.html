Atomic mass

The atomic mass (ma) is the mass of a single atom of a chemical element, most often expressed in daltons (unified atomic mass units, symbol Da or u), where one dalton is defined as 1/12 of the mass of a free carbon-12 atom at rest in its ground state.

Definitions

The atomic mass of an isotope is close to, but not exactly, its mass number, because binding energies and the masses of protons, neutrons and electrons make the actual mass slightly different. The relative atomic mass (atomic weight) of an element is the weighted average of the atomic masses of its naturally occurring isotopes, and is the value listed on periodic tables.

Measurement

Atomic masses are measured precisely using mass spectrometry. For example, the atomic mass of carbon-12 is exactly 12 Da by definition, hydrogen-1 is about 1.007825 Da, and iron-56 about 55.9349 Da.

Related concepts

Atomic mass should not be confused with molecular mass (the sum for a molecule) or with the molar mass, expressed in grams per mole, which is numerically similar to the relative atomic or molecular mass.
