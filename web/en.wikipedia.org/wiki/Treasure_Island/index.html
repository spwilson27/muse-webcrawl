Treasure Island

Author: Robert Louis Stevenson
Published: 1883
Genre: Adventure, young adult

Treasure Island is an adventure novel by the Scottish author Robert Louis Stevenson, telling a story of "buccaneers and buried gold." Serialized in the children's magazine Young Folks in 1881–82 and published in book form in 1883, it is one of the most frequently dramatized novels and a foundational work of pirate fiction.

Plot

Young Jim Hawkins, an innkeeper's son, comes into possession of a treasure map drawn by the late Captain Flint. Squire Trelawney outfits the schooner Hispaniola under Captain Smollett, with Dr. Livesey as ship's surgeon—but the crew, secretly recruited by the ship's cook Long John Silver, consists largely of Flint's old pirates. At the island, Jim overhears the mutiny plot, meets the marooned Ben Gunn, and the party fights the mutineers for the stockade and the treasure, which Ben Gunn has already found and hidden in his cave.

Influence

The novel fixed the popular image of pirates: treasure maps marked with an X, one-legged seamen with parrots on their shoulders, the "black spot" summons, and songs such as "Fifteen men on the dead man's chest." Stevenson began the book by drawing a map to entertain his stepson Lloyd Osbourne. Its ambiguities—especially the genial, treacherous Silver—have drawn lasting critical attention, and it has been adapted dozens of times for stage, film, radio, and television.
