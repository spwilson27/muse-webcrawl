Nazca Lines

LocationNazca Desert, Peru
Createdc. 500 BC – AD 500 (Nazca culture)
StatusUNESCO World Heritage
[Image: The hummingbird geoglyph of the Nazca Lines]
The hummingbird geoglyph of the Nazca Lines

The Nazca Lines are a series of enormous geoglyphs etched into the Nazca Desert of southern Peru between about 500 BC and AD 500 by the Nazca culture: straight lines, geometric shapes and figures of animals and plants visible in full only from the air.

Description

Made by removing dark surface stones to expose pale soil, the figures include a hummingbird, a monkey with a spiral tail, a spider and a condor, some over 200 m long. Their purpose remains debated — astronomical calendars, water rituals and sacred pathways are leading ideas. Threats include the Pan-American Highway, which cuts through one figure, off-road vehicles and a changing climate bringing rare rains. The lines are a UNESCO World Heritage site; a viewing tower on the highway shows two figures for those not flying over.
