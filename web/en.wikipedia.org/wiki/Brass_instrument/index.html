Brass instrument

Brass instrument
ClassificationAerophone; labrophone
ExamplesTrumpet, trombone, French horn, tuba, cornet, euphonium

A brass instrument is a musical instrument that produces sound by sympathetic vibration of air in a tubular resonator, excited by the vibration of the player's lips against a cup- or funnel-shaped mouthpiece. Brass instruments are also called labrophones, and the defining feature is the lip vibration rather than the material: instruments such as the alphorn (wood) and the conch (shell) belong to the family despite not being made of brass.

How sound is produced

The player's buzzing lips act as a vibrating valve. The pitch is selected from the harmonic series of the tube by lip tension and air speed, and modified by lengthening the tube with valves, slides, or crooks. Most modern brass instruments use three piston or rotary valves, each adding a different length of tubing.

Families

The orchestral brass section typically comprises trumpets, French horns, trombones, and the tuba. Related instruments include the cornet, flugelhorn, euphonium, and baritone horn, central to the British brass band tradition.

History

Natural trumpets and horns were used for signalling and ceremony from antiquity. The invention of valves in the early nineteenth century, associated with Heinrich Stölzel and Friedrich Blühmel, made the full chromatic scale available and transformed the instruments' musical role.
