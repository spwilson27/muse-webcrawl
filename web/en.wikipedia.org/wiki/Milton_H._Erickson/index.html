Milton H. Erickson

Milton Hyland Erickson (5 December 1901 – 25 March 1980) was an American psychiatrist and psychologist specializing in medical hypnosis and family therapy. He is widely regarded as the father of modern hypnotherapy, and his "utilization" approach — tailoring therapy to the individual patient's own resources and experiences — influenced brief therapy, strategic family therapy, and neuro-linguistic programming.

Early life

Erickson was born in Aurum, Nevada, and raised in Wisconsin. At age 17 he contracted polio and was paralyzed; during his recovery he developed an acute sensitivity to body language, tone of voice and other subtle cues by watching his siblings communicate. He later credited this experience with shaping his understanding of nonverbal communication and the unconscious mind. He studied medicine and psychology at the University of Wisconsin, earning his medical degree in 1928.

Career

Erickson held posts at several institutions, including the Wayne County General Hospital in Michigan, where he served as director of psychiatric research and training, and later worked in Phoenix, Arizona, where he spent much of his later career in private practice. In 1948 a second bout of post-polio difficulties forced him to limit his work, and he conducted much of his later teaching from his home, where students visited from around the world.

Approach

Erickson's hypnotic technique differed from classical authoritarian hypnosis: he used indirect suggestions, metaphor, anecdote, confusion techniques and permissive language, working within trance states that occur naturally in everyday life. His case studies, many published posthumously by students such as Jay Haley and Ernest Rossi, describe creative interventions designed to produce therapeutic change with minimal sessions. Erickson founded the American Society of Clinical Hypnosis and was its first president, and he was the founding editor of the American Journal of Clinical Hypnosis.

Legacy

Erickson's work underlies Ericksonian hypnotherapy and influenced the development of solution-focused brief therapy and strategic therapy. The Milton H. Erickson Foundation, established in 1979, continues to sponsor training and conferences in his name.
