Turkish cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Turkish kebabs, meze and baklava]

Turkish cuisine is the cooking of Turkey, heir to the Ottoman palace kitchen, one of the world's great imperial cuisines, blending Central Asian, Middle Eastern, Balkan and Mediterranean traditions. It is famed for kebabs, meze, pide and lahmacun, stuffed vegetables (dolma), yogurt-based dishes, baklava and Turkish delight, and for Turkish coffee — inscribed by UNESCO — served in small cups with fortune-telling from the grounds.

Writers often rank it with French and Chinese among the world's great cuisines. Regional cooking is strong: Gaziantep is celebrated for pistachio baklava and kebabs; the Black Sea for anchovies and corn bread; the Aegean for olive-oil vegetable dishes (zeytinyağlılar).

1. Ottoman heritage

Ottoman heritage

The Topkapı Palace kitchens employed hundreds of cooks in specialised guilds — soup makers, kebab masters, halva confectioners — and spread dishes such as kebabs, sherbets and filo pastries across three continents. Ashure, imam bayıldı ('the imam fainted') and hünkar beğendi ('the sultan approved') carry their legends in their names.
