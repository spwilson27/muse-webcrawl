Boy band

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A boy band is a vocal group consisting of young male singers, usually in their teenage years or in their twenties at the time of formation. Generally, boy bands do not play instruments, either in recording sessions or on stage, instead singing and dancing, typically performing highly choreographed routines. Many successful boy bands of the late 1990s and 2000s had a significant impact on the music industry, and the format became a global phenomenon through groups such as New Kids on the Block, Backstreet Boys, NSYNC, and One Direction.

History

Precursors include the barbershop quartets of the early 20th century and vocal harmony groups such as the Ink Spots and the Four Seasons. The modern template — a manager-assembled group of young male singers marketed to a teenage audience — emerged in the 1960s with acts surrounding the Monkees and the Jackson 5, and was refined in the 1970s and 1980s by groups such as the Osmonds, Bay City Rollers, Menudo, and New Edition. New Kids on the Block's success in the late 1980s triggered a boom in the 1990s: in the United States, the Backstreet Boys and NSYNC sold tens of millions of albums, while in the United Kingdom and Ireland, Take That, Boyzone, and Westlife dominated the charts.

After a decline in the mid-2000s, the format revived in the 2010s with One Direction and with the worldwide spread of South Korean idol groups such as BTS, whose elaborate choreography, fan engagement, and training system descend in part from the boy-band tradition.

Characteristics

Boy bands typically feature close vocal harmonies, romantic ballads and up-tempo dance-pop songs, synchronised choreography, and carefully managed individual "personas" within the group (for example, "the shy one" or "the bad boy"). Their marketing emphasises image, merchandise, and fan loyalty, and their core audience has historically been pre-teen and teenage girls.
