Good laboratory practice

Good laboratory practice (GLP) is a set of principles intended to assure the quality and integrity of non-clinical laboratory studies — particularly safety studies of chemicals, pharmaceuticals, pesticides, and food additives — whose results are submitted to regulatory authorities. Under GLP, studies must be planned, performed, monitored, recorded, archived, and reported according to defined standards, so that the work can be fully reconstructed and audited.

History

GLP regulations were drafted by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 1976 and finalised in 1978–1979, following investigations (notably of Industrial Bio-Test Laboratories) that uncovered widespread fraud and sloppy practice in toxicology studies supporting product approvals. The OECD Principles of Good Laboratory Practice, first adopted in 1981, spread the system internationally; under the OECD's Mutual Acceptance of Data system, GLP-compliant safety data generated in one member country must be accepted in others.

Requirements

GLP requires, among other things: a designated study director; an independent quality-assurance unit; written standard operating procedures; characterisation of test and reference items; calibrated equipment; complete retention of raw data and specimens; and a final report that permits full reconstruction of the study. It is distinct from — though named in parallel with — good clinical practice (for human trials) and good manufacturing practice (GMP).
