The Battle of Sedgemoor was fought in the early hours of 6 July 1685 at Westonzoyland in Somerset, England, and was the final battle of the Monmouth Rebellion — and often described as the last pitched battle fought on English soil. The rebel army of James Scott, 1st Duke of Monmouth, an illegitimate son of Charles II who claimed the throne, was defeated by the royal army of King James II. Monmouth was captured and executed, and the rebellion's survivors faced the notorious "Bloody Assizes".

Background

Monmouth landed at Lyme Regis on 11 June 1685 with a small force and gathered several thousand West Country supporters, mostly untrained artisans and farm labourers armed with scythes and muskets. After failing to take Bristol, he fell back into Somerset, encamping at Bridgwater while the royal army under the Earl of Feversham camped at Westonzoyland on the edge of Sedgemoor.

The battle

Monmouth attempted a risky night attack across the moor. His men were delayed and lost direction at the drainage ditches (rhynes) crossing the moor; a shot alerted the royal camp, and the rebel infantry found itself halted before the Bussex Rhine under fire from regular troops and artillery. Rebel cavalry fled, and after roughly an hour and a half the rebel foot broke and was cut down in the pursuit.

Aftermath

Monmouth fled the field but was captured two days later near Ringwood and beheaded on Tower Hill on 15 July. Judge Jeffreys' Bloody Assizes sentenced hundreds of rebels to death or transportation to the West Indies.
