Divine Nine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Divine Nine is the nickname of the National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC), the umbrella organisation for nine historically African American fraternities and sororities in the United States. The term is also used to refer collectively to the nine member organisations themselves. The NPHC was founded on 10 May 1930 at Howard University in Washington, D.C.

Member organisations

  • Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity (1906, Cornell University) – the first intercollegiate Greek-letter fraternity for African American men.
  • Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority (1908, Howard University) – the first Greek-letter sorority for African American women.
  • Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity (1911, Indiana University).
  • Omega Psi Phi Fraternity (1911, Howard University).
  • Delta Sigma Theta Sorority (1913, Howard University).
  • Phi Beta Sigma Fraternity (1914, Howard University).
  • Zeta Phi Beta Sorority (1920, Howard University).
  • Sigma Gamma Rho Sorority (1922, Butler University).
  • Iota Phi Theta Fraternity (1963, Morgan State University), which joined the NPHC in 1997.

Role

Unlike most collegiate Greek organisations, Divine Nine membership is generally a lifelong commitment extending well beyond college, with graduate and alumni chapters, and the organisations emphasise public service, civil rights advocacy, voter registration, and community uplift. Their traditions include distinctive colours and calls, probate shows, and step shows, in which members perform synchronised percussive routines. Prominent members have included Martin Luther King Jr. (Alpha Phi Alpha), and Kamala Harris (Alpha Kappa Alpha).
