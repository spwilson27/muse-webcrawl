Gaius Valerius Flaccus (died c. AD 90) was a Roman poet of the Silver Age of Latin literature who flourished under the Flavian emperors. He is known for his unfinished epic poem the Argonautica, a retelling in eight books of the voyage of Jason and the Argonauts in search of the Golden Fleece, modelled on the earlier Hellenistic epic of Apollonius of Rhodes.

Life

Almost nothing is known of Valerius's life. He was a member of the quindecimviri sacris faciundis, one of Rome's priestly colleges, and his poem's dedication to Vespasian — praising among other things the emperor's campaigns and his son Titus's sack of Jerusalem (AD 70) — dates the work's commencement to the 70s AD. The rhetorician Quintilian, writing around AD 95, mentions Valerius's recent death as a great loss to Roman letters ("multum in Valerio Flacco nuper amisimus"), which suggests he died around AD 90 with his epic incomplete.

The Argonautica

The surviving poem breaks off abruptly in the eighth book, mid-speech by Medea, at 5,597 lines, covering the Argonauts' voyage as far as Colchis and the beginning of Jason's trials. Valerius writes in the grand Virgilian manner but with the compressed, allusive style of Silver Latin; his treatment deepens the psychology of Medea and overlays the myth with Stoic ideas of fate and a divine plan reflected in Jupiter's prophecy of history. The poem was rediscovered in manuscript at St. Gall in 1416 by the humanist Poggio Bracciolini and has attracted renewed scholarly attention for its sophisticated engagement with Virgil, Ovid, Lucan and Seneca.
