Sault Ste. Marie is a city in the U.S. state of Michigan, on the northeastern end of Michigan's Upper Peninsula on the Canada–United States border, and the county seat of Chippewa County. It is separated by the St. Marys River from its twin city, Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario.

History

The area was a long-standing meeting and fishing place for Ojibwe (Chippewa) people, who called it Baawitigong, "place of the rapids". French missionaries arrived in the 17th century; Jacques Marquette founded a Jesuit mission here in 1668, making it one of the oldest European settlements in the modern United States. The fur trade, and later lumber and shipping, drove its growth.

Soo Locks

The city is best known for the Soo Locks, which allow ships to travel between Lake Superior and the lower Great Lakes despite the rapids of the St. Marys River. Opened in 1855, the locks are among the busiest in the world and are vital to shipping iron ore and grain. They are a major tourist attraction, crowned by the annual Engineers Day.

Education

Sault Ste. Marie is home to Lake Superior State University, founded in 1946. The Sault Tribe of Chippewa Indians is headquartered in the area.
