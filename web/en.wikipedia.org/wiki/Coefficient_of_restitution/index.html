Coefficient of restitution

The coefficient of restitution (COR, also denoted by e) is the ratio of the final to initial relative speed between two objects after they collide. It normally ranges from 0 to 1, where 1 would be a perfectly elastic collision (no kinetic energy lost) and 0 would be a perfectly inelastic collision, in which the objects stick together (although they need not remain motionless). The term was introduced into mechanics in the work of Isaac Newton, who stated a version of the concept as Newton's law of restitution.

e = (speed of separation) / (speed of approach)

Values for common objects

For a ball bouncing on a fixed surface, the coefficient of restitution can be measured from the ratio of successive bounce heights: e = √(h/H), where H is the drop height and h the rebound height. A golf ball may have a COR of roughly 0.78, a tennis ball about 0.75, and a cricket ball around 0.73; governing bodies in sport regulate COR to standardise play — for example, baseball and golf authorities limit the liveliness of balls and club faces. A ball of modelling clay dropped on a hard floor has a COR close to zero.

Energy loss

The fraction of kinetic energy retained in a collision depends on the square of the coefficient: in a collision with a massive fixed object, the fraction of energy lost is 1 − e². Thus even a COR of 0.9 entails a 19% energy loss.
