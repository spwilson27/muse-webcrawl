Ark of Taste

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ark of Taste catalogues endangered heritage foods worldwide.

The Ark of Taste (Italian: Arca del Gusto) is an international catalogue of endangered heritage foods maintained by the Slow Food movement. It is designed to preserve at-risk foods that are sustainably produced, unique in taste, and part of a distinct ecoregion. Unlike a seed bank or livestock preservation programme, the Ark is primarily concerned with preserving foods and their production methods within their living cultural contexts—by drawing public attention to the risk of extinction, encouraging people to buy and eat them, and in some cases promoting active conservation.

History

The project was launched in Italy in 1996 following the first Slow Food international congress, and the first products were named in 1997. It has since grown to include several thousand products—fruits, vegetables, animal breeds, cheeses, cured meats, breads, sweets, and more—from more than 150 countries. Entries are nominated by Slow Food's network and evaluated by national and international commissions against criteria of taste quality, historical and cultural identity, small-scale and sustainable production, and genuine risk of disappearance.

Related projects

Foods in the Ark may become the focus of Presidia (Presìdi), Slow Food projects that work directly with small-scale producers to safeguard a product, its producers, and its territory. The Ark is also connected to the movement's broader work on agrobiodiversity, including the "10,000 Gardens in Africa" project and the Terra Madre network.

Examples

Listed products include Britain's raw-milk farmhouse cheeses, the American Lambert cherry and Blenheim apricot, Polish oscypek cheese, Cape Malay pickled fish, and numerous heritage livestock breeds and regional crop varieties.
