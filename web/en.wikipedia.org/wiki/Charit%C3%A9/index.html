The Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin is one of Europe's largest university hospitals, affiliated with the Humboldt University and the Free University of Berlin. Founded in 1710 as a plague house outside the city walls, it is among Germany's most research-intensive medical institutions.

History

Established by order of King Frederick I of Prussia in anticipation of plague, the Charité became a teaching hospital in the 19th century, associated with figures such as Rudolf Virchow and Robert Koch. During Berlin's division it lay in East Berlin and served as the GDR's leading hospital.

Today

The modern Charité operates across four campuses in Berlin, treats hundreds of thousands of patients annually, and trains a large share of Berlin's physicians and dentists.
