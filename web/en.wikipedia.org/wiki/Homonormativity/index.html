Homonormativity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Homonormativity is the privileging of homosexuality that adheres to heteronormative structures and values — such as monogamous marriage, domesticity, consumerism and gender conformity — within LGBTQ culture and politics. The term critiques the idea that acceptance for gay and lesbian people has been made conditional on their resemblance to heterosexual norms, and that this assimilation marginalises queer people whose lives do not fit those norms.

Origin of the term

The concept was developed by the sociologist Lisa Duggan in the early 2000s, notably in her essay "The New Homonormativity: The Sexual Politics of Neoliberalism" (2002) and the book The Twilight of Equality? (2003). Duggan described homonormativity as "a politics that does not contest dominant heteronormative assumptions and institutions but upholds and sustains them", linking it to neoliberalism, privatisation and the demobilisation of radical gay politics into a narrow agenda of marriage and military inclusion.

Discussion

Scholars have applied the concept to marketing aimed at affluent gay consumers (the "pink pound" or "pink dollar"), to pride events dominated by corporate sponsorship, and to debates within LGBTQ movements over priorities. Critics of the concept argue that seeking ordinary civic goods such as marriage is not inherently conservative, and that the term can be used to disparage people's freely chosen lives. Related concepts include homonationalism, developed by Jasbir Puar, and "pinkwashing".
