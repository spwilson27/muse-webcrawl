Ferdinand I (10 March 1503 – 25 July 1564) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1556, King of Bohemia, Hungary, and Croatia from 1526, and Archduke of Austria from 1521 until his death in 1564. He was the younger brother of Emperor Charles V, who entrusted him with the Austrian hereditary lands and later arranged his succession. His reign saw the consolidation of the Austrian Habsburg monarchy and a military stalemate with the Ottoman Empire in Hungary.

Early life and rise

Born in Spain to Philip the Handsome and Joanna of Castile, Ferdinand was raised largely at the court of his grandfather Ferdinand II of Aragon. After Charles V inherited the Spanish and imperial crowns, treaties between the brothers (1521–1522) granted Ferdinand the Austrian duchies and made him Charles's deputy in the Empire; he was elected King of the Romans in 1531.

King of Hungary and Bohemia

Following the death of his brother-in-law Louis II at the Battle of Mohács in 1526, Ferdinand was elected king by the diets of Bohemia and a faction of Hungary's nobility, though János Szapolyai contested the Hungarian crown and much of central Hungary fell under Ottoman rule after 1541, leaving Hungary divided into three parts for 150 years.

Emperor

Ferdinand played a key role in negotiating the Peace of Augsburg (1555), which legalized Lutheranism within the Empire on the principle cuius regio, eius religio. After Charles V's abdication, Ferdinand became emperor (formally recognized by the electors in 1558). Although Catholic, he pursued a relatively conciliatory religious policy, pressing the papacy for reforms, and negotiated truces with the Ottomans (notably the Treaty of Adrianople of 1562). He died in Vienna in 1564, dividing his Austrian lands among his three sons; the imperial crown passed to his eldest son, Maximilian II.
