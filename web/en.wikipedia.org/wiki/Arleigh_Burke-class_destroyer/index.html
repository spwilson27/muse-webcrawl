Arleigh Burke-class destroyer

The Arleigh Burke class of guided-missile destroyers (DDG) is a United States Navy class of destroyer built around the Aegis Combat System and the SPY-1D multi-function phased array radar. The class is named for Admiral Arleigh Burke, a famed World War II destroyer commander and later Chief of Naval Operations. The lead ship, USS Arleigh Burke (DDG-51), was commissioned in 1991.

Design

The ships are multi-mission destroyers designed primarily for anti-air warfare with the Aegis system, but also capable of anti-submarine, anti-surface, land-attack (Tomahawk cruise missile), and ballistic missile defence missions. Unlike the preceding cruisers and destroyers, the class was built with all-steel construction and incorporated lessons from the Falklands War and the attack on USS Stark.

Flights

The class has been built in successive "flights" with incremental improvements: Flight I (DDG-51 to 71), Flight II (DDG-72 to 78), Flight IIA (DDG-79 onward), which added hangars for two helicopters, and Flight III (beginning with DDG-125), built around the more powerful SPY-6 air and missile defence radar. With more than 70 ships delivered or building, it is one of the longest production runs of any US Navy surface combatant and forms the backbone of the Navy's destroyer force.
