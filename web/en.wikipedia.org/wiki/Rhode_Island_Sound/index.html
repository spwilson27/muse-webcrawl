Rhode Island Sound

Rhode Island Sound is a body of water off the southern New England coast, lying south of Rhode Island and east of Long Island Sound. It connects the sheltered waters around Narragansett Bay and Buzzards Bay with the Atlantic Ocean and forms part of an important coastal navigation and fishing region.

Location
Offshore Rhode Island and southeastern Massachusetts
Boundaries
Block Island and Long Island to the southwest and west; Martha’s Vineyard and the Elizabeth Islands toward the east and northeast

Geography

The sound opens southward to the Atlantic. Block Island stands near its southwestern approaches, while passages around Point Judith, Sakonnet Point and the Elizabeth Islands connect it to neighboring bays. Its waters are generally deeper and more exposed than nearby estuaries, with tides and currents shaped by the complex island coastline left by glaciation.

Ecology and fisheries

Seasonal mixing and currents support plankton, shellfish, squid, scup, flounder, striped bass and migratory marine mammals. The sound has long been used by commercial and recreational fisheries based in ports such as Point Judith and New Bedford. Warming waters have altered the distribution and abundance of several species.

Navigation and energy

Shipping routes serving Narragansett Bay, Fall River, New Bedford and Long Island pass through or near the sound. The Block Island Wind Farm, the first offshore wind farm in the United States, began operating nearby in 2016. Larger offshore wind projects have been proposed or developed in federal waters south of Rhode Island and Massachusetts, making the sound central to debates over clean energy, fishing access and marine conservation.

Distinction

Rhode Island Sound is not the same as Long Island Sound, which lies farther west between Long Island and Connecticut. Nor is it Narragansett Bay, the drowned river valley extending north into Rhode Island.
