Cable news

MediumCable television
First channelCNN (1980)
ExamplesCNN, Fox News, MSNBC, BBC News, Al Jazeera

Cable news refers to television news channels distributed by cable (and later satellite and streaming) rather than broadcast over the air. Such channels typically broadcast news continuously, 24 hours a day, combining live reporting, interviews, analysis, and opinion programming.

The format began with the launch of CNN in the United States in 1980, the first channel devoted entirely to news.

History

CNN's coverage of events such as the 1991 Gulf War demonstrated the power of continuous live news. Competition intensified in 1996 with the launch of Fox News and MSNBC, and internationally with BBC World News, Sky News, and Al Jazeera (1996).

Format

Cable news channels use rolling coverage, breaking-news banners, split screens, and panel discussions. Prime-time line-ups often emphasise opinion and commentary, which generally draw higher ratings than straight news reporting.

Impact and criticism

Cable news has been credited with accelerating the news cycle and providing live coverage of crises, but also criticised for sensationalism, partisan slant, and contributing to political polarisation. Audiences have increasingly shifted to online and social-media news sources.
