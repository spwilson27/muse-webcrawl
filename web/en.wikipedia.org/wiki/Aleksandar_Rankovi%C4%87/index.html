Aleksandar Ranković (28 November 1909 – 19 August 1983) was a Yugoslav communist politician of Serbian origin, considered the third most powerful man in Yugoslavia after Josip Broz Tito and Edvard Kardelj. He headed the Yugoslav military intelligence and secret police organisations, OZNA and its successor UDBA, and served as Vice President of Yugoslavia from 1963 to 1966.

Career

A tailor's apprentice in his youth, Ranković joined the Communist Party of Yugoslavia in 1928 and was imprisoned under the royal dictatorship. During the Second World War he was a senior Partisan organiser and a close associate of Tito. After the war he became federal minister of the interior and built up the security apparatus.

Fall from power

Ranković was removed from all positions at the Brioni Plenum of July 1966 after it was revealed that the secret police had wiretapped Tito's own residence. His fall marked a turning point toward decentralisation and liberalisation in Yugoslavia, and he was expelled from the League of Communists in 1966, though never tried.

Legacy

Ranković remains a controversial figure: to many Albanians in Kosovo he symbolises repression, while in Serbia his funeral in 1983 drew huge crowds and was seen as an early stir of Serbian national sentiment.
