Generally accepted accounting principles (United States)

AbbreviationUS GAAP
Standard-setterFinancial Accounting Standards Board (FASB)
Recognised byU.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)
CodificationFASB Accounting Standards Codification (since 2009)

Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) in the United States are the accounting rules and standards used to prepare, present, and report financial statements for a wide variety of entities, including publicly traded and privately held companies, non-profit organisations, and governments. US GAAP is set by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB), a private standard-setting body recognised by the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Framework and principles

US GAAP rests on a conceptual framework addressing the objectives of financial reporting and the qualitative characteristics of useful information, together with basic principles such as accrual accounting, the going-concern assumption, the historical cost principle, revenue recognition, matching of expenses, consistency, materiality, and full disclosure. Since 2009, authoritative GAAP for nongovernmental entities has been organised in the FASB Accounting Standards Codification. Governmental entities follow standards issued by the Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB), and federal entities those of the FASAB.

Relationship to IFRS

US GAAP differs in various respects from International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), used in most other major economies; since 2007, foreign private issuers filing with the SEC have been permitted to use IFRS without reconciliation to US GAAP. Convergence projects have aligned some standards, such as revenue recognition, but significant differences remain, and US domestic issuers continue to be required to use US GAAP.
