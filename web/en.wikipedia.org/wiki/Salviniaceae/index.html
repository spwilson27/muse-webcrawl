Salviniaceae

KingdomPlantae
ClassPolypodiopsida
OrderSalviniales
FamilySalviniaceae
GeneraAzolla, Salvinia

Salviniaceae is a family of heterosporous ferns in the order Salviniales. The family comprises two genera, Azolla (mosquito ferns) and Salvinia (watermoss), totalling roughly sixteen species. All members are small, free-floating aquatic plants of still or slow-moving fresh water, found on most continents. In the Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I), Salviniaceae is placed alongside Marsileaceae in Salviniales; older classifications sometimes placed Azolla in its own family, Azollaceae.

The two genera of the family: Salvinia (left) and Azolla (right), shown as floating mats.

Description

Members of the family float on the water surface, with small overlapping leaves and trailing filamentous roots (in Salvinia the third leaf of each whorl is modified into a submerged, root-like organ). They are heterosporous, producing two kinds of sporangia — megasporangia bearing single large megaspores and microsporangia bearing many small microspores — enclosed in structures called sporocarps. This heterosporous life cycle is shared with the related family Marsileaceae and is unusual among ferns.

Ecology and importance

Azolla species harbour the nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium Anabaena azollae in cavities in their leaves, and have been used for centuries in Asia as a green manure in rice paddies; a massive Azolla bloom in the Arctic Ocean around 49 million years ago (the "Azolla event") has been proposed as a contributor to the shift from greenhouse to icehouse Earth. Salvinia includes the aggressive invader Salvinia molesta, one of the world's worst aquatic weeds.
