I Love Bees

I Love Bees was an alternate reality game (ARG) that served as both a real-world experience and a viral marketing campaign for the video game Halo 2. Developed by 42 Entertainment, the game launched in July 2004 and concluded in November 2004, shortly before Halo 2's release. Players who solved the game's puzzles were rewarded with audio files that formed an audio drama expanding the Halo universe.

Launch

The game was revealed through a hidden message in a Halo 2 theatrical trailer: the Xbox website address at the end briefly flickered to show "ilovebees.com". The site appeared to be an amateur beekeeping website that had been hacked and corrupted, displaying scrambled text and mysterious countdowns. The corruption was attributed to an artificial intelligence from the year 2552, later identified as Melissa, the AI of a shipwrecked United Nations Space Command vessel, the Apocalypso.

Gameplay

Following clues on the site, players discovered GPS coordinates and times corresponding to payphones across the United States. At the appointed times, the phones would ring, and players answering them spoke with actors or recordings, answering questions to "activate" axons. Progress gradually unlocked a series of audio files, later known as "the axon files," which together told a self-contained story set in the Halo universe in the weeks before the Covenant invasion of Earth depicted in Halo 2.

Reception and legacy

I Love Bees attracted hundreds of thousands of players and is regarded as one of the most successful alternate reality games ever produced. It won awards for innovation and helped popularize the ARG format as a marketing tool. Its audio drama introduced characters and plot threads that later appeared in Halo novels and other media.
