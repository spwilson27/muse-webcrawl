Cyst

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cyst
DefinitionClosed sac-like structure with a distinct wall, filled with fluid, semi-solid, or gaseous material
Common sitesSkin, ovaries, kidneys, liver, joints, breast
TypicallyBenign

A cyst is a closed sac-like pocket of tissue that contains liquid, air, or other substances. Cysts can form anywhere in the body, in organs or soft tissues, and range in size from microscopic to very large. Unlike an abscess, a cyst is not usually caused by infection, though cysts can become infected. Most cysts are benign, but some cystic structures occur in association with tumours and require evaluation.

Common types

  • Epidermoid and pilar cysts — keratin-filled skin cysts, often loosely called "sebaceous cysts".
  • Ovarian cysts — fluid-filled sacs on or in an ovary, frequently functional and self-resolving.
  • Ganglion cysts — jelly-filled lumps near joints or tendons, especially the wrist.
  • Baker's cyst — fluid accumulation behind the knee.
  • Renal and hepatic cysts — common incidental findings; numerous cysts occur in polycystic kidney and liver disease.
  • Hydatid cysts — caused by the larval stage of the tapeworm Echinococcus.
  • Dentigerous and other jaw cysts, Bartholin's cysts, breast cysts, and pineal cysts also occur.

Diagnosis and treatment

Many cysts are found incidentally on imaging such as ultrasound, CT, or MRI. Treatment depends on type, size, and symptoms: asymptomatic simple cysts often need no treatment, while others may be drained, excised, or monitored. A related term in microbiology denotes the dormant, resistant stage of certain microorganisms and parasites.
