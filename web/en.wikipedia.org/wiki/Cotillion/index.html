Cotillion

Cotillion
Origin18th-century France
TypeSocial dance; formal ball; etiquette programme
RelatedDebutante ball, quadrille

A cotillion (also cotillon) is a social dance that originated in 18th-century France, and by extension a formal ball or a programme of dance and etiquette instruction for young people. The word derives from the French cotillon, meaning "petticoat" — a reference, in folk etymology, to the underskirts revealed by the dance's lively steps.

The dance

The original cotillion was a variant of the contredanse performed by four couples in a square formation, related to the quadrille that later superseded it. Cotillions were elaborate: a lead couple devised figures and changes, often involving props, games and the exchange of gifts or favours, which the other couples imitated. The dance was fashionable in France and England and was introduced to the United States around the time of the Revolutionary era.

Cotillion in the United States

In the American South and elsewhere, "cotillion" came to denote a formal ball at which debutantes are presented to society, typically wearing white gowns and gloves and escorted by young men in formal dress. Separately, junior cotillion programmes — taught in many communities to school-age children — combine ballroom dance instruction (waltz, foxtrot, swing) with lessons in manners, introductions and social confidence. Historic cotillion clubs, some founded in the nineteenth century, remain fixtures of social life in cities such as Richmond and Baltimore.
