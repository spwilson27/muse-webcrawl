Spam filtering

Spam filtering is the set of techniques used to detect and divert unsolicited bulk email (spam) before it reaches a user's inbox. Filters operate at mail servers, in desktop clients, and in webmail services, combining content analysis, sender reputation and authentication checks.

Techniques

  • Content filtering — rules or statistical classifiers score messages on words, phrases and structural features. Naive Bayes filtering, popularised in the early 2000s, learns from user-marked spam and ham.
  • Blocklists and allowlists — DNS-based lists (DNSBLs) flag IP addresses and domains known to send spam.
  • Authentication — SPF, DKIM and DMARC verify that mail is authorised by the sending domain, defeating spoofing.
  • Greylisting and rate limiting — temporarily rejecting mail from unknown senders exploits spammers' reluctance to retry.
  • Machine learning — large providers train models on billions of messages to catch evolving campaigns.

Trade-offs

Filters balance false negatives (spam delivered) against false positives (legitimate mail junked), the latter usually considered more costly. Spammers respond with image spam, word obfuscation and botnet distribution, making filtering an ongoing arms race.
