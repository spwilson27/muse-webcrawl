Lincoln Imp
LocationLincoln Cathedral, England
MaterialStone carving
Date13th century (Angel Choir)

The Lincoln Imp is a grotesque stone carving in Lincoln Cathedral in England, and the symbol of the city of Lincoln. The small figure sits cross-legged high on a pillar in the Angel Choir, and according to legend is a creature turned to stone by angels.

The legend

A popular 14th-century legend tells that Satan sent two imps to cause trouble on Earth. After wreaking havoc elsewhere they came to Lincoln Cathedral, smashing tables and tripping the bishop. When they threatened the Angel Choir, an angel appeared and ordered them to stop; one imp hid among the roof timbers while the other, sitting on a pillar, threw rocks at the angel — and was turned to stone, where he remains while his companion escaped.

Symbolism

The Imp appears on souvenirs, the badge of Lincoln City F.C. (nicknamed "The Imps"), and local products throughout the city. The carving dates to the building of the Angel Choir in the later 13th century and is one of many medieval grotesques in the cathedral.
