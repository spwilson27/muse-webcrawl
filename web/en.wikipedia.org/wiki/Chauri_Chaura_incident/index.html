The Chauri Chaura incident took place on 4 February 1922 at Chauri Chaura in the Gorakhpur district of the United Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh) in British India. A large crowd of protesters participating in Mahatma Gandhi's Non-cooperation Movement clashed with police, who opened fire. In retaliation, the crowd attacked and set fire to the police station, killing the 22 or 23 policemen trapped inside.

Background

By early 1922 the Non-cooperation Movement against British rule had mobilised mass support across India. In the Gorakhpur area, volunteers picketed liquor shops and protested high food prices. In the days before the incident, police had beaten local volunteer leaders and fired on picketers, inflaming local feeling.

The incident

On 4 February a procession of some 2,000–2,500 protesters marched towards the Chauri Chaura police station. When the crowd pressed forward, the police fired into the air and then into the crowd, killing several people. Enraged, the mob drove the policemen back into the station building, set it ablaze with kerosene, and killed those who tried to escape. Three civilians and 22 or 23 policemen died.

Consequences

Gandhi, horrified that the movement had turned violent, undertook a five-day fast and, through the Congress Working Committee's Bardoli resolution of 12 February 1922, suspended the Non-cooperation Movement nationwide — a decision that dismayed many colleagues but which he regarded as essential to his principle of nonviolence (ahimsa).

The colonial government prosecuted 225 people; 172 were committed for trial. The sessions court sentenced 19 men to death and others to transportation for life. On appeal, defended by Madan Mohan Malaviya, many sentences were reduced: 19 executions were confirmed and carried out in 1923, while most of the remaining prisoners received lesser terms. The site is now marked by a memorial, and the railway station at Chauri Chaura is a recognised landmark of the freedom struggle.
