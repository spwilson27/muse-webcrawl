Camelford

Camelford (Cornish: Camalanford) is a town and civil parish in north Cornwall, England, United Kingdom, situated in the River Camel valley northwest of Bodmin Moor. The town had a population of around 2,900 in recent censuses.

History

The name derives from the River Camel combined with "ford". Camelford is traditionally associated with Camelot and with the legendary Battle of Camlann, at which King Arthur is said to have been mortally wounded; nearby Slaughterbridge is claimed as the site. In the medieval period Camelford was a borough and market town, and it returned Members of Parliament as a rotten borough until the Reform Act 1832.

Water pollution incident

In July 1988, Camelford became notorious for a major water pollution incident, when 20 tonnes of aluminium sulphate were accidentally delivered into the wrong tank at the Lowermoor water treatment works, contaminating the drinking water supply to around 20,000 people in the area. It remains one of the worst water contamination incidents in British history, and its long-term health effects were debated for decades.

Geography

The town lies on the A39 road between Wadebridge and Bude, on the edge of Bodmin Moor. The River Camel flows through the town, and the Camel Trail cycle path passes nearby along the route of a former railway.
