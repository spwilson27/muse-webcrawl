Race (biology)

In taxonomy, a race is an informal rank in the taxonomic hierarchy for which various definitions exist. Sometimes it is used to denote a level below that of subspecies, while at other times it is used as a synonym for subspecies. It has been used as a higher rank than strain, with several strains making up one race in some contexts.

Definitions

Races may be genetically distinct populations of the same species, or may be defined in other ways, for example geographically, or physiologically. Genetic isolation between races is not complete, but genetic differences may accumulate that are not (yet) sufficient to warrant species-level distinction. In botany, where phenotype is more often influenced by environmental factors, race is often used in the sense of a physiological race — a population distinguished by a particular physiological trait, such as resistance to a pathogen, rather than by visible morphology.

Taxonomic context

In zoology, the formal rank below species is the subspecies, and "race" is generally avoided as a formal term, although the trinomial nomenclature used for subspecies effectively identifies much the same kind of population division. In botany, the formalised ranks below species include subspecies, varietas (variety), and forma (form). The use of "race" in scientific literature has declined as more precise terminology and molecular genetic methods have been adopted.

Race and humans

The application of "race" to human populations has a long and controversial history. Modern genetics and anthropology generally hold that human variation does not divide into discrete biological races: genetic variation within traditional racial groups exceeds the variation between them, and variation is overwhelmingly clinal. Scientific organisations have issued statements emphasising that race is primarily a social construct when applied to humans, though ancestry-linked population structure remains relevant in medicine and forensics.
