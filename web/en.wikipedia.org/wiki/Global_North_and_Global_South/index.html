Global North and Global South

The Global North and Global South are terms used in development studies, international relations and postcolonial scholarship to group countries by levels of wealth, industrialization and political influence. The terms are not strictly geographical: most of the Global North lies in the northern hemisphere, but Australia and New Zealand are classed with the North, while much of the South lies north of the equator.

Usage

The "Global South" gained currency after the Cold War as a less hierarchical successor to "Third World" or "developing countries". It broadly encompasses Africa, Latin America, the Caribbean, and much of Asia and Oceania, including large emerging economies such as China, India and Brazil. The Brandt Line, drawn in the 1980 report of the Brandt Commission, proposed a similar division looping south around Australia and New Zealand.

Criticism

Critics note that the binary grouping obscures huge differences within both categories and between fast-growing economies. Nevertheless the terms remain standard in United Nations, academic and activist discourse.
