The Battle of Dandanaqan was fought on 23 May 1040 between the Seljuk Turks, led by the brothers Tughril and Chaghri, and the Ghaznavid army of Sultan Mas'ud I. The Seljuk victory ended Ghaznavid rule in Khurasan and laid the foundation of the Great Seljuk Empire.

Background

Oghuz Turkmen tribes under the Seljuk family had migrated into Khurasan, grazing their herds on the steppe margins and raiding settled districts. Mas'ud marched from Nishapur toward Merv with a large, heavily equipped army including war elephants, intending to crush them once and for all.

Battle

The Seljuks refused a set-piece engagement, instead harassing the Ghaznavid column, fouling wells and cutting supply lines as it crossed the waterless steppe. Exhausted and thirsty, Mas'ud's army reached Dandanaqan, where mutiny and desertion — including the defection of some of his own Turkic troops — had already weakened it. On 23 May the Seljuks attacked in force and routed the Ghaznavids; Mas'ud fled the field with a small escort.

Aftermath

The Seljuks occupied Merv and Nishapur, and Tughril was proclaimed sultan. Mas'ud retreated toward India and was deposed and killed the following year. The battle is regarded as one of the turning points of medieval Islamic history, opening Persia and eventually Anatolia to Turkic rule.
