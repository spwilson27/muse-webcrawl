Robert Wyatt

Robert Wyatt (born Robert Wyatt-Ellidge, 28 January 1945) is an English musician, drummer, and singer, a founding member of the influential Canterbury scene band Soft Machine and of Matching Mole. His solo career, pursued after an accident left him paraplegic, has made him one of the most distinctive voices in British music.

Early life and Soft Machine

Wyatt was born in Bristol and grew up in Kent, where his home became a gathering place for musicians. In the mid-1960s he played in the Wilde Flowers, from which both Soft Machine and Caravan emerged. As drummer and vocalist of Soft Machine, Wyatt helped define the band's fusion of psychedelia, jazz, and progressive rock on albums including The Soft Machine (1968), Volume Two (1969), and Third (1970). Creative tensions led him to leave the band in 1971.

Matching Mole and accident

Wyatt formed Matching Mole — a pun on the French translation of "Soft Machine", machine molle — which released two albums in 1972. On 1 June 1973, during a birthday party for Gong's Gilli Smyth, Wyatt fell from a fourth-floor window and broke his back, leaving him paralysed from the waist down. The accident ended his drumming career but redirected him toward singing, keyboards, and songwriting.

Solo career

His 1974 solo album Rock Bottom, produced by Nick Mason of Pink Floyd and written largely before and during his hospital recovery, is widely regarded as a masterpiece — a dreamlike, deeply personal record featuring contributions from Ivor Cutler, Hugh Hopper, and Richard Sinclair. That same year his cover of the Monkees' "I'm a Believer" reached the UK Top 30 and earned a famous Top of the Pops appearance in his wheelchair.

Subsequent albums include Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard (1975), the politically charged singles of the early 1980s such as "Shipbuilding" (written for him by Elvis Costello and Clive Langer during the Falklands War), Old Rottenhat (1985), Dondestan (1991), Shleep (1997), Cuckooland (2003), and Comicopera (2007). His high, plaintive tenor voice and melancholic, jazz-inflected compositions have influenced generations of musicians, and his work is closely associated with his wife, the artist and lyricist Alfreda Benge, who paints his album covers.

Wyatt announced his retirement from recording in 2014. A biography, Different Every Time by Marcus O'Dair, appeared the same year, accompanied by compilation albums of the same name.
