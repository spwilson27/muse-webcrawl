Case management (US healthcare system)

In the United States healthcare system, case management is a managed care practice in which a designated professional — often a registered nurse or social worker — coordinates the care of individual patients, particularly those with complex, chronic or catastrophic conditions.

Role

Case managers assess a patient's needs, develop a plan of care, arrange and monitor services such as hospital discharge, home health and rehabilitation, and communicate among the patient, providers and insurers. The goals are to improve quality and continuity of care, promote self-management, and control costs by avoiding unnecessary hospitalisations and duplicated services. Certification is offered by bodies such as the Commission for Case Manager Certification (the CCM credential).
