Supranational union

[Image: The European Union — the leading example of a supranational union]

Overview

A supranational union is a type of political union in which member states delegate authority over certain matters to organs that can make decisions binding on the members — going beyond intergovernmental cooperation, in which states retain a veto on everything. The European Union is the foremost and essentially only full example: its Commission proposes, its Parliament and Council legislate, its Court's rulings bind member states, and its law has direct effect and supremacy in its fields (as established in Van Gend en Loos (1963) and Costa v ENEL (1964)).

Degrees and debates

Supranationalism is a spectrum: the African Union, ECOWAS (with its court), the Eurasian Economic Union and Mercosur have supranational elements of varying strength, while ASEAN and the UN are essentially intergovernmental. The term is contested in EU politics — sovereigntist movements (culminating in Brexit) resist transfers of authority to Brussels, while federalists seek more. In international-relations theory, supranational governance (Haas's neofunctionalism, "spillover") is contrasted with liberal intergovernmentalism (Moravcsik), which sees delegation as states' calculated choice.
