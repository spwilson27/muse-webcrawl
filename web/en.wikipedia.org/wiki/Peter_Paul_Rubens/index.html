Peter Paul Rubens

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Descent from the Cross (1612–14), central panel of the triptych in Antwerp Cathedral: muscular bearers lowering the body of Christ]

Sir Peter Paul Rubens (28 June 1577 – 30 May 1640) was a Flemish artist and diplomat, the most influential exponent of the Flemish Baroque. His paintings — energetic, sensual and richly coloured — combine the compositional grandeur of Michelangelo, the colour of Titian and the naturalism of Caravaggio, and his enormous Antwerp workshop produced some two thousand works for courts across Europe.

Rubens's best-known paintings include the Descent from the Cross (1612–14), The Raising of the Cross, the cycle of 24 monumental canvases on the life of Marie de' Medici for the Luxembourg Palace in Paris, and The Garden of Love.

Italy and return to Antwerp

After apprenticeship in Antwerp, Rubens spent eight years in Italy (1600–1608) in the service of the Duke of Mantua, copying the Renaissance masters and absorbing the new Baroque painting of Rome. On his return he became court painter to the Archdukes Albert and Isabella in Brussels, built a grand house and studio in Antwerp, and organised a workshop in which specialist pupils — including Anthony van Dyck — executed parts of his designs.

Diplomat and late work

Fluent in seven languages, Rubens undertook diplomatic missions to Spain and England, and was knighted by both Philip IV and Charles I; his ceiling paintings for the Banqueting House in Whitehall (1632–34) are his great English monument. After the death of his first wife he married the sixteen-year-old Hélène Fourment in 1630, whose golden, voluptuous figure recurs throughout his joyful late paintings of love, landscape and myth.
