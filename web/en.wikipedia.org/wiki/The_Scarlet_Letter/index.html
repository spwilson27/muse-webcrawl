The Scarlet Letter

The Scarlet Letter: A Romance is a work of historical fiction by American author Nathaniel Hawthorne, published in 1850. Set in the Puritan Massachusetts Bay Colony during the years 1642 to 1649, the novel tells the story of Hester Prynne, who bears a child out of wedlock and is condemned to wear a scarlet letter "A" on her chest as punishment for adultery.

Plot

Hester Prynne refuses to name her lover — the young minister Arthur Dimmesdale — and raises her daughter Pearl while enduring public shame. Her long-lost husband, Roger Chillingworth, arrives in the colony and, discovering the truth, devotes himself to tormenting Dimmesdale. The novel explores themes of legality, sin, and guilt, and culminates in Dimmesdale's public confession on the scaffold.

Themes and legacy

The novel is considered a masterpiece of American literature and a foundational work of the American Renaissance. Its rich symbolism — the letter itself, Pearl, the scaffold, and the forest — has made it one of the most studied novels in American classrooms. The introduction, "The Custom-House", describes Hawthorne's time working at the Salem Custom House.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times for film and stage, including a 1995 film starring Demi Moore.
