Studio One is one of Jamaica's most famous record labels and recording studios, founded in 1963 by Clement "Sir Coxsone" Dodd at 13 Brentford Road in Kingston. It is often called the "Motown of Jamaica".

History

Dodd had run a leading sound system in the 1950s and recorded local musicians to keep his dances supplied. At Studio One he employed the Skatalites as house band and recorded virtually every major Jamaican artist of the ska, rocksteady and early reggae eras: Bob Marley and the Wailers, Peter Tosh, Dennis Brown, Alton Ellis, Ken Boothe, Marcia Griffiths, the Heptones, Burning Spear and Sugar Minott, among many others. House musicians including Jackie Mittoo, Leroy Sibbles and Ernest Ranglin created "riddims" that have been versioned thousands of times.

Legacy

Dodd emigrated to New York in the late 1970s/1980s; Studio One recordings were extensively reissued, notably by Heartbeat Records and Soul Jazz Records, and its rhythms underpin dancehall and hip-hop sampling. Dodd died in 2004. Compare American independent labels such as United Artists in film.
