Panavision is an American motion picture equipment company founded in 1953, specializing in cameras and lenses for the film industry. Based in Woodland Hills, California, the company is known for its policy of renting — rather than selling — its equipment to film productions, a model that has made its cameras a near-universal standard on major Hollywood productions for decades.

Panavision rose to prominence in the 1950s with its widescreen innovations and went on to develop the Panaflex line of 35 mm production cameras, the widely adopted Primo lens series, and later digital cinema cameras including the Genesis and the Millennium DXL2. Its name, and the credit "Filmed with Panavision cameras and lenses," appears on countless films.

History

Panavision was founded in 1953 by Robert Gottschalk and cinematographer Richard Moore. The company initially supplied anamorphic projection lenses to cinemas during the widescreen boom that followed the introduction of CinemaScope, exhibiting technology derived from improving projection optics. Its Ultra Panatar projection lenses allowed theaters to show widescreen films with better quality, and the company soon moved into camera equipment.

In the 1960s and 1970s Panavision became a leading supplier of camera packages for feature films. The Panaflex, introduced in the 1970s, was a quiet, self-blimped 35 mm camera with a reflex viewing system that became an industry workhorse. In 2007 the company received an Academy Award of Commendation, and its founder Robert Gottschalk was awarded an Academy Honorary Award recognizing his contributions.

Equipment

  • Panaflex — a family of 35 mm sync-sound cameras, including the Gold II, Platinum and Millennium XL models.
  • Primo lenses — a widely used series of spherical prime lenses introduced in the late 1980s.
  • Genesis — a digital cinematography camera developed with Sony in the 2000s, using a Super 35-size sensor and accepting Panavision lenses.
  • Millennium DXL2 — a digital camera developed with RED, built around an 8K sensor and Panavision's large-format lens mount.

Business model

Unlike most camera manufacturers, Panavision does not sell its cameras and lenses; instead, it rents complete camera packages through its worldwide network of offices and affiliates. This allows the company to maintain, service and continually update its equipment, and it has helped standardize production workflows across the film industry.
