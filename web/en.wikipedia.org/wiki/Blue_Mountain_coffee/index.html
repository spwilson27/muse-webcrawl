Blue Mountain coffee

Jamaica Blue Mountain Coffee
TypeCoffee (Arabica)
RegionBlue Mountains, Jamaica
Elevation910–1,700 m (3,000–5,600 ft)
RegulatorJamaica Agricultural Commodities Regulatory Authority (JACRA)

Jamaica Blue Mountain Coffee is a classification of coffee grown in the Blue Mountains of Jamaica, one of the most expensive and sought-after coffees in the world. The coffee is noted for its mild flavour and lack of bitterness. Over the last several decades, most of the genuine crop has been exported to Japan, long the largest market for Blue Mountain beans.

History

Coffee was introduced to Jamaica in 1728, when Governor Sir Nicholas Lawes brought plants from Martinique, where they had descended from stock given to French naval officer Gabriel de Clieu. The cool, misty, high-altitude conditions of the Blue Mountains—with rich soil and frequent drifting cloud cover that slows cherry maturation—proved ideal for arabica cultivation.

Regulation and certification

The name is protected: only coffee grown within the legally defined Blue Mountain area, at elevations between about 910 and 1,700 metres, and inspected by the Jamaican regulator (formerly the Coffee Industry Board, now JACRA) may be sold as Jamaica Blue Mountain. Coffee from lower elevations in the region is classified as Jamaica High Mountain, and from elsewhere as Jamaica Prime. To counter widespread counterfeiting, certified coffee is exported in distinctive wooden barrels and must carry official certification.
