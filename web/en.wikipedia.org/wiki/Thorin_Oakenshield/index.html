Thorin Oakenshield is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's 1937 novel The Hobbit. Thorin is the leader of the Company of Dwarves who seek to reclaim the Lonely Mountain (Erebor) and its treasure from the dragon Smaug. He is the son of Thráin II, grandson of Thrór, and heir of the line of Durin, the Kings under the Mountain driven into exile when Smaug seized Erebor in the year 2770 of the Third Age.

Background

Thorin was born in 2746 of the Third Age in Erebor. As a young dwarf he escaped the sack of the mountain, and fought in the War of the Dwarves and Orcs; at the Battle of Azanulbizar (2799) his shield was split, and he snatched up an oak branch to defend himself — hence his epithet "Oakenshield". After his father Thráin disappeared on a doomed attempt to return to Erebor, Thorin became leader of his exiled people, who worked as smiths and miners in the Blue Mountains (Ered Luin).

In The Hobbit

At the urging of the wizard Gandalf, Thorin assembles a company of twelve other dwarves — including his nephews Fíli and Kíli — and hires the hobbit Bilbo Baggins as their "burglar". The company survives trolls, goblins, wargs, giant spiders and imprisonment by the Elvenking before reaching Lake-town and entering the mountain by a secret door. After Smaug is killed by Bard the Bowman, Thorin, succumbing to "dragon-sickness" — a consuming greed for the treasure, and especially for the Arkenstone, the heart of the mountain — fortifies Erebor against the Men and Elves who claim a share, and nearly brings about war. He relents only on the eve of the Battle of Five Armies, in which he fights valiantly and is mortally wounded alongside Fíli and Kíli, who die defending him. Reconciled with Bilbo, he dies with the words: "If more of us valued food and cheer and song above hoarded gold, it would be a merrier world."

Analysis and adaptations

Scholars read Thorin as a flawed heroic figure of the Norse and Anglo-Saxon world — proud, oath-bound, and brought low by the gold-hoard curse that echoes poems such as Beowulf. His name is taken from the Old Norse poem Völuspá's list of dwarves (dvergatal), as are most of the company's names. In Peter Jackson's film trilogy of The Hobbit (2012–2014), Thorin is played by Richard Armitage as a younger, more central tragic hero.
