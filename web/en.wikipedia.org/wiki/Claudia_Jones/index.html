Claudia Jones

Claudia Jones
Born: 21 February 1915, Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago
Died: 24 December 1964, London, England
Occupation: Journalist, activist
Known for: Founder of the West Indian Gazette; co-founder of the Notting Hill Carnival

Claudia Jones (born Claudia Vera Cumberbatch; 21 February 1915 – 24 December 1964) was a Trinidad and Tobago-born journalist and activist. She became a communist political figure in the United States and was deported to the United Kingdom as a result of her political activities, where she is remembered as a founder of London's Notting Hill Carnival.

Early life

Jones was born in Belmont, Port of Spain, Trinidad, then part of the British West Indies. At the age of nine she emigrated with her family to the United States, settling in Harlem, New York City. Her mother died when Claudia was twelve, and the family's economic hardship during the Great Depression shaped her early political consciousness.

Activism in the United States

Jones joined the Young Communist League in 1936, drawn by the Communist Party's campaign in defence of the Scottsboro Boys. She rose through the ranks of the Communist Party USA, editing its newspaper the Daily Worker and writing on the rights of black women. Her 1949 essay "An End to the Neglect of the Problems of the Negro Woman!" is a landmark statement of what later theorists would call intersectionality, arguing that black women faced a "triple oppression" of race, class and gender.

Under the repressive climate of McCarthyism, Jones was arrested and convicted under the Smith Act for her communist beliefs. After imprisonment, she was deported from the United States in 1955.

Life in Britain

In London, Jones founded the West Indian Gazette in 1958, Britain's first commercially successful black newspaper. Following the 1958 Notting Hill race riots, she helped organise a Caribbean carnival in St Pancras Town Hall in January 1959 — an event widely regarded as the precursor of the Notting Hill Carnival, first held outdoors in 1964.

Death and legacy

Jones died of heart disease on Christmas Eve 1964 and is buried in Highgate Cemetery, London, in a plot to the left of the grave of Karl Marx. A blue plaque in London commemorates her life, and she is honoured in Trinidad and Tobago. In 2008 she appeared on a Royal Mail postage stamp in the "Women of Distinction" series.
