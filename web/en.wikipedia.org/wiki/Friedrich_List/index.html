Georg Friedrich List (6 August 1789 – 30 November 1846) was a German economist, journalist and politician. He is best known for his theory of "national economics" and the infant industry argument, set out in his major work The National System of Political Economy (1841), which influenced economic policy in Germany, the United States and later in East Asia.

Life

List was born in Reutlingen in Württemberg. He rose quickly in the Württemberg civil service and became a professor of administration at Tübingen, but his liberal reform agitation led to prosecution and imprisonment; he emigrated to the United States in 1825. There he farmed, edited a German-language newspaper and promoted railway construction, becoming involved in building the Little Schuylkill railroad in Pennsylvania. Returning to Germany in the 1830s, he campaigned for the Zollverein customs union, for a German railway network, and for protection of young German industries against British competition.

Ideas

Against the free-trade doctrines of Adam Smith and David Ricardo, List argued that a nation's wealth lay in its productive powers rather than in exchange values alone. He proposed a stage theory of economic development and held that less developed nations should temporarily protect their "infant industries" with tariffs until they could compete with established industrial powers; free trade was appropriate only between nations at a similar level of development. His vision of a united, industrialised Germany linked railways, customs union and national policy into a single programme.

Legacy

List's ideas influenced the economic nationalism of the German Empire, Henry Charles Carey's American School, and 20th-century development economics. Financial troubles and depression dogged his final years, and he took his own life at Kufstein in 1846.
