Canon Inc. (Japanese: キヤノン株式会社, Kyanon kabushiki gaisha) is a Japanese multinational corporation headquartered in Ōta, Tokyo, specialising in optical, imaging and industrial products, such as lenses, cameras, medical equipment, scanners, printers, and semiconductor manufacturing equipment.

History

Canon traces its origins to the Precision Optical Instruments Laboratory founded in 1933 by Goro Yoshida and others, which produced the Kwanon, Japan's first 35 mm focal-plane-shutter camera prototype, named after the Buddhist bodhisattva of mercy. The company adopted the name Canon in 1947. It diversified from cameras into office copying machines (challenging Xerox with the NP system), bubble-jet printers, and later digital cameras, where its EOS system and image sensors made it the world's largest camera maker by market share.

Business

Canon's major product groups are printing (laser and inkjet printers, multifunction devices), imaging (EOS cameras, EF/RF lenses, camcorders, broadcast lenses), medical (CT and MRI systems, following the acquisition of Toshiba Medical Systems in 2016) and industrial (steppers and nanoimprint lithography for semiconductors). Canon has consistently ranked among the top holders of US patents.
