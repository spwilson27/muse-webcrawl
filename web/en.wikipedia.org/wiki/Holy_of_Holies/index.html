The Holy of Holies (Hebrew: Qodesh haQodashim, "Holy of Holies") was the innermost sanctuary of the Tabernacle and later of the Temple in Jerusalem, regarded as the dwelling place of the presence of God (the Shekhinah) on earth. Separated from the Holy Place by a veil, it could be entered only by the High Priest, and only once a year on Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement.

A model of the Second Temple; the Holy of Holies stood at its western, innermost end.

In the Tabernacle and First Temple

According to Exodus, the Tabernacle's Holy of Holies was a cubic chamber containing the Ark of the Covenant, topped by the mercy seat with two golden cherubim, within which were kept the tablets of the Law (and, by tradition, Aaron's rod and a jar of manna in front of it). In Solomon's Temple (the First Temple), the chamber was a windowless cube of twenty cubits, its walls lined with cedar and gold, housing the Ark beneath the outstretched wings of two great olivewood cherubim. The Babylonians destroyed the Temple in 586 BC, and the Ark disappears from the record.

The Second Temple

The Second Temple, completed under Zerubbabel and vastly rebuilt by Herod the Great, had a Holy of Holies that was famously empty: the Ark was gone, and later sources mention a foundation stone (the Even haShetiya) in its place. Only the High Priest entered, on Yom Kippur, to offer incense and sprinkle the blood of sacrifices for the atonement of Israel, pronouncing the divine name. The Temple was destroyed by the Romans in 70 AD. The site is now occupied by the Dome of the Rock on the Temple Mount in Jerusalem.

Religious significance

In Judaism the Holy of Holies remains the holiest place on earth; out of reverence and uncertainty over its exact location, observant Jews traditionally avoid entering the Temple Mount. In the New Testament, the tearing of the Temple veil at Jesus's death (Matthew 27:51) is read symbolically, and the Epistle to the Hebrews presents Christ as the high priest entering the true Holy of Holies. The term has passed into general English usage for any innermost, most sacred or private place, and the Latter-day Saint temples also contain a Holy of Holies room.
