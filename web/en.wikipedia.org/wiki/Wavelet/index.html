Wavelet

FieldSignal processing, mathematics
Key figuresJean Morlet, Alex Grossmann, Yves Meyer, Ingrid Daubechies

A wavelet is a wave-like oscillation with an amplitude that begins at zero, increases or decreases, and then returns to zero one or more times. Wavelets are used as building blocks to represent signals at different scales and positions, much as sine waves are used in Fourier analysis — but unlike sine waves, wavelets are localised in both time and frequency.

Wavelet transforms

The continuous and discrete wavelet transforms decompose a signal into shifted and scaled versions of a mother wavelet. The discrete wavelet transform, especially through filter-bank algorithms developed by Ingrid Daubechies and Stéphane Mallat, gives fast, invertible representations suited to compression and denoising.

Applications

Wavelets underpin the JPEG 2000 image compression standard, the FBI's fingerprint compression system, seismic signal analysis, numerical solutions of partial differential equations, and feature detection in audio and biomedical signals such as ECGs.
