Brown & Williamson

Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corporation was an American tobacco company and a subsidiary of British American Tobacco. Founded in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, in the 1890s by George T. Brown and his brother-in-law Robert L. Williamson, it became one of the major cigarette manufacturers in the United States.

Brands and history

The company's brands included Kool, Lucky Strike (in the United States for a period), Pall Mall, Viceroy, Raleigh, and Barclay. It introduced menthol cigarettes under the Kool brand in the 1930s and was long headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky.

Tobacco litigation and whistleblower

In the 1990s Brown & Williamson was at the centre of tobacco industry litigation. Former research executive Jeffrey Wigand disclosed that the company had manipulated nicotine and suppressed health research; internal documents he revealed became known as the "Cigarette Papers". His story was dramatized in the 1999 film The Insider.

Merger

In 2004 Brown & Williamson's U.S. operations merged with R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company to form Reynolds American, ending the company's existence as a separate manufacturer.
