Hiri Motu

Hiri Motu
Region: former Territory of Papua, Papua New Guinea
Type: pidginised form of Motu
Status: one of PNG's official languages

Hiri Motu, also known historically as Police Motu, is a pidginised and simplified form of the Motu language of the Port Moresby area of Papua New Guinea. Together with Tok Pisin and English, it is one of the country's official languages, though its use has been declining in favour of Tok Pisin.

Origins

The name derives from the hiri, the great trading voyages on which the Motu people sailed west in multi-hulled canoes (lakatoi) to the Gulf of Papua, exchanging clay pots for sago and canoe logs. Communication with the peoples of the Gulf produced a simplified contact variety of Motu. Under the colonial administration of British New Guinea and the Territory of Papua, the language was adopted as the medium of the armed native constabulary — hence "Police Motu" — and spread through the Papuan provinces as a lingua franca of government, missions and trade.

Features

Hiri Motu draws the great majority of its vocabulary from Motu but radically simplifies its grammar: the complex Austronesian verb prefixes and inclusive/exclusive pronoun distinctions of Motu are largely lost, word order is regularised, and periphrastic constructions replace inflections. Two broad varieties are distinguished — the Police Motu of general use and a version spoken in the Gulf (Central) area. Although few children now acquire it as a first language, it remains widely understood in the southern provinces and is heard in church, broadcasting and public life.
