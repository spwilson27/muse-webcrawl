The expressways (高速自動車国道, kōsoku jidōsha kokudō) of Japan form the country's principal network of limited-access toll roads, connecting the major cities of Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu, and Hokkaido. The network forms part of the Asian Highway Network.

History

Japan's first expressway, the Meishin Expressway between Rittō and Amagasaki, opened in 1963, followed by the Tōmei Expressway linking Tokyo and Nagoya in 1969. Construction was long coordinated by the Japan Highway Public Corporation, which was privatised in 2005 and split into the regional NEXCO companies (East, Central, and West Japan Expressway Companies).

Characteristics

Most national expressways are toll roads, with fees typically charged by distance and collected largely through the ETC electronic toll collection system. Speed limits are generally 100 km/h on rural sections and lower in urban or mountainous areas, though some newer sections permit 120 km/h. Numbered routes carry "E" designations (for example, E1 for the Tōmei/Meishin corridor), while urban networks such as the Shuto Expressway in Tokyo and the Hanshin Expressway in Osaka have their own numbering. Service areas and parking areas provide fuel, food, and rest facilities at regular intervals.
