Victor Talking Machine Company

The Victor Talking Machine Company was an American recording company and phonograph manufacturer that operated independently from 1901 until 1929, when it was acquired by the Radio Corporation of America (RCA) and became RCA Victor. It was headquartered in Camden, New Jersey, and was for decades the largest and most prestigious firm of its kind in the United States, responsible for the Victrola line of phonographs and an enormous catalogue of records.

History

The company was founded by Eldridge R. Johnson, a machinist who had improved Emile Berliner's gramophone and manufactured machines for the Berliner Gramophone Company. After legal disputes, Johnson organized his own firm in 1901, naming it "Victor" — a word said to reflect his victories in patent litigation. In 1906 Victor introduced the Victrola, a phonograph with the horn concealed inside a wooden cabinet, which made the machine an acceptable piece of home furniture and became a huge commercial success.

His Master's Voice

Victor's famous trademark showed a dog named Nipper listening to a phonograph, under the slogan "His Master's Voice," adapted from a painting by Francis Barraud. It became one of the most recognized corporate logos in the world.

Recordings

Victor's Red Seal records featured celebrated classical artists such as Enrico Caruso, whose recordings were both prestigious and highly profitable. The company was also a pioneer in recording jazz, blues, and country music, and in 1925 it adopted Western Electric's electrical recording process. After its sale to RCA, the Victor name lived on for decades in the RCA Victor label.
