Great Mosque of Xi'an

The Great Mosque of Xi'an (Chinese: 西安大清真寺) is one of the oldest and most renowned mosques in China, located in the Muslim Quarter of Xi'an, Shaanxi. Founded in 742 during the Tang dynasty (with much of the present complex dating from the Ming dynasty), it serves the city's Hui Muslim community.

Architecture

Unlike most mosques in the Middle East, the Great Mosque is built almost entirely in traditional Chinese style: a walled garden complex of courtyards, pavilions and stelae aligned on an east–west axis facing Mecca, without domes or a conventional minaret — its minaret takes the form of a pagoda. The prayer hall combines Chinese timber architecture with Arabic calligraphy and carved inscriptions of the Qur'an. Covering about 12,000 m², it remains an active place of worship and a major historical attraction.
