Kullervo Manner

Kullervo Akilles Manner (12 October 1880 – 15 January 1939) was a Finnish and Soviet politician. He served as Speaker of the Parliament of Finland in 1917 and, during the Finnish Civil War of 1918, as head of the Finnish People's Delegation — the government of the Finnish Socialist Workers' Republic ("Red Finland"). After the Red defeat he fled to Soviet Russia, where he co-founded the Finnish Communist Party and worked in the Comintern, before perishing in the Great Purge.


Kullervo Manner (1880–1939).

Life and career

Manner was born in Kokemäki, Finland, and worked as a journalist, editing the Social Democratic newspaper Työmies. He was elected to the Parliament of Finland for the Social Democratic Party and served as its Speaker in 1917, chairing the session that later declared Finnish independence in December of that year.

Civil War and exile

In January 1918 the Social Democrats seized power in southern Finland; Manner became chairman of the People's Delegation, effectively head of government, and later also commander-in-chief of the Red Guards. When the Whites won the war in May 1918, Manner escaped to Russia. There he helped found the Finnish Communist Party (SKP) in Moscow in August 1918 and became its second chairman, and he represented Finnish communists in the Communist International.

Death

Arrested during Stalin's Great Terror, Manner was sentenced to ten years in a labour camp and died in the Ukhta-Pechora camp (Ukhtpechlag) in the Komi Republic in January 1939.
