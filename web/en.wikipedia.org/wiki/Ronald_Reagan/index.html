Ronald Wilson Reagan (February 6, 1911 – June 5, 2004) was an American politician and actor who served as the 40th president of the United States from 1981 to 1989. Before politics he was a Hollywood actor and president of the Screen Actors Guild, and served as Governor of California (1967–1975).

Presidency

Reagan implemented supply-side "Reaganomics" — tax cuts (1981, 1986), deregulation, and increased military spending. He survived an assassination attempt in 1981, ordered the invasion of Grenada (1983), and weathered the Iran–Contra affair. His Cold War stance hardened ("evil empire," Strategic Defense Initiative) then softened into diplomacy with Mikhail Gorbachev (INF Treaty, 1987) near the Cold War's end.

Legacy

Reagan reshaped American conservatism and the Republican Party, is credited by supporters with economic growth and Cold War victory, and criticised for deficits, inequality, and his administration's AIDS response. He died of Alzheimer's disease in 2004 after a state funeral. See Roberts Court.
