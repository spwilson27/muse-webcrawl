Freiwillige Selbstkontrolle der Filmwirtschaft

FSK rating logos appear on films released in Germany.

The Freiwillige Selbstkontrolle der Filmwirtschaft (FSK, "Voluntary Self-Regulation of the Film Industry") is the German motion picture rating system organisation. Founded in 1949 in Wiesbaden, it is run by the Spitzenorganisation der Filmwirtschaft (SPIO) and assigns age ratings to films, videos, and other audiovisual media distributed in Germany.

Ratings

The FSK issues five age classifications: FSK 0 (released without age restriction), FSK 6, FSK 12, FSK 16, and FSK 18 (no release to young people). The ratings are legally binding for public exhibition and sale under the Youth Protection Act (Jugendschutzgesetz). Children aged six and over may attend FSK 12 films when accompanied by a parent or guardian. Examiners drawn from the film industry, public authorities, churches, and youth protection bodies view and classify submitted works.

History

The FSK was modelled partly on American self-regulation and on Allied occupation-era film controls, and was intended to pre-empt state censorship, which is prohibited in principle by the German Basic Law. Its decisions have periodically drawn controversy over cuts demanded for lower ratings and over depictions of violence.
