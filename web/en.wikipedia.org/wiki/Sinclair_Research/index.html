Sinclair Research Ltd is a British consumer electronics company founded by Sir Clive Sinclair in Cambridge. It originated as Sinclair Radionics in 1961, selling hi-fi components, radios and calculators by mail order, and became famous in the early 1980s for a series of inexpensive home computers — the ZX80, ZX81 and above all the ZX Spectrum — which brought computing to millions of British households and helped seed the UK's video games industry.

History

Sinclair Radionics produced pioneering miniature products, including the Microvision pocket television and the Black Watch digital wristwatch, whose difficulties damaged the company financially; in 1979 Clive Sinclair established Science of Cambridge (later Sinclair Research) as a vehicle for his computer projects. The ZX80 (1980) was the first personal computer available in the UK for under £100, and the ZX81 (1981) sold over 1.5 million units. The colour ZX Spectrum (1982) became one of Britain's best-selling computers and a cultural icon.

Decline and sale

The more ambitious Sinclair QL (1984) suffered from a rushed launch and reliability problems, and the company's C5 electric vehicle (1985) was a commercial failure that badly hurt its finances and reputation. In April 1986 Sinclair Research sold its computer business and the Sinclair brand for computers to Amstrad for £5 million. A smaller Sinclair company continued with projects such as the Cambridge Z88 portable computer (1987) through successor ventures.

Legacy

Sinclair's low-cost machines trained a generation of British programmers and are credited with launching the UK's software industry. The Spectrum remains the subject of an active retro-computing community, and Sinclair Research continues as an R&D and investment company.
