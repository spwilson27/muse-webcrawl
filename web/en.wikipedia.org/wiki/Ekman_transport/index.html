Ekman transport

Ekman transport
FieldPhysical oceanography
Named afterVagn Walfrid Ekman

Ekman transport is the net movement of surface ocean water caused by wind stress balanced by the Coriolis force. Within the Ekman layer, currents spiral with depth (the Ekman spiral, described by Swedish oceanographer Vagn Walfrid Ekman in 1905 following Fridtjof Nansen's observations), and the depth-integrated transport is directed 90° to the wind — to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere.

Mechanism

Wind drags the surface layer, which Coriolis deflects; each deeper layer is deflected further and moves more slowly. The net transport per unit width is τ/(ρf), where τ is wind stress, ρ density and f the Coriolis parameter.

Effects

Ekman transport drives coastal upwelling (e.g. off California and Peru, supporting rich fisheries), downwelling, and Ekman pumping/suction that forces the great subtropical gyres and influences El Niño dynamics and nutrient supply.
