German Instrument of Surrender

Signed: 7 May 1945 (Reims); 8 May 1945 (Berlin)
Location: Reims, France; Berlin-Karlshorst, Germany
Signatories: Alfred Jodl; Wilhelm Keitel; Hans-Georg von Friedeburg; Allied representatives

The German Instrument of Surrender was the legal document that effected the unconditional surrender of Nazi Germany's armed forces to the Allies, ending World War II in Europe. It was signed twice: first on 7 May 1945 in Reims, France, by Generaloberst Alfred Jodl, and again, in a definitive ratified form, on 8 May 1945 in Berlin-Karlshorst by Field Marshal Wilhelm Keitel, Admiral Hans-Georg von Friedeburg, and Colonel-General Hans-Jürgen Stumpff.

Background

Following the death of Adolf Hitler on 30 April 1945, Grand Admiral Karl Dönitz became head of state and sought to negotiate partial surrenders in the West while continuing to fight the Soviet Union. The Allies insisted on unconditional surrender on all fronts. The Reims signing was witnessed by representatives of the United States, the Soviet Union, and France, with General Walter Bedell Smith signing for the Supreme Headquarters Allied Expeditionary Force.

The Berlin signing

The Soviet government regarded the Reims document as a preliminary protocol and demanded a formal ratification in Berlin. The second act was signed shortly before midnight on 8 May 1945 at the headquarters of the Soviet military administration in Karlshorst, with Marshal Georgy Zhukov presiding for the USSR and Air Chief Marshal Arthur Tedder for the Western Allies. Because of the time difference, the surrender is commemorated on 8 May in Western Europe and North America (Victory in Europe Day) and on 9 May in Russia and other former Soviet states.

Text and legal effect

The instrument provided that all forces under German control would cease active operations at 23:01 Central European Time on 8 May 1945, remain in place, and surrender their weapons. It formed the legal basis for the Allied occupation of Germany, later supplemented by the Berlin Declaration of 5 June 1945, in which the four Allied powers assumed supreme authority in Germany.
