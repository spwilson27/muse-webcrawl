Shakti Peethas

The Shakti Peethas (Sanskrit: शक्ति पीठ, "seats of Shakti") are a network of Hindu temples dedicated to the goddess in her various forms, located across the Indian subcontinent. Each is believed to mark a place where a part of the body of the goddess Sati fell to earth.

Legend

According to the myth, Sati, wife of the god Shiva, immolated herself at her father Daksha's sacrifice after he insulted Shiva. The grief-stricken Shiva carried her corpse across the universe and danced the destructive Tandava, until Vishnu dismembered the body with his Sudarshana Chakra to restore order. The places where the body parts (or ornaments) fell became the Shakti Peethas.

The Peethas

Different texts list different numbers — commonly 51, though lists of 4 (Adi Peethas), 18 (Maha Peethas), 64 and 108 also exist. Among the most famous are Kamakhya in Assam, Kalighat in Kolkata, Jwalamukhi in Himachal Pradesh, Vaishno Devi in Jammu, Ambaji in Gujarat, Hinglaj in Balochistan (Pakistan), and the temples associated with parts of Sati's body in present-day India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Tibet and Sri Lanka. The four Adi Shakti Peethas are generally identified as Bimala (Puri), Kamakhya (Guwahati), Tara Tarini (Odisha) and Dakshina Kalika (Kolkata).

Significance

Together the Peethas map the sacred geography of Shaktism, the goddess-centred tradition of Hinduism, and remain among the most visited pilgrimage sites in South Asia, especially during Navaratri.
