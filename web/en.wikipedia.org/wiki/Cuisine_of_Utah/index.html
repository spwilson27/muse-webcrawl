Cuisine of Utah

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Utah scones — deep-fried dough served with honey butter.

The cuisine of Utah reflects the state's pioneer heritage, its large population of members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and its position in the American West. Utah's food traditions mix Mormon pioneer cooking — emphasizing stored grains, potatoes, and home canning — with Mexican, Native American, and broader Western influences, and the state has developed several distinctive dishes and condiments with a loyal local following.

Signature items

  • Fry sauce — a mixture of ketchup and mayonnaise (often with pickle juice or spices) served with french fries, popularized by the Utah-based Arctic Circle restaurant chain in the mid-20th century and now ubiquitous in the state.
  • Utah scones — unlike British scones, these are large pieces of yeast dough deep-fried and served hot with honey butter; they are related to Navajo frybread and to the sopaipilla.
  • Funeral potatoes — a casserole of potatoes (often hash browns), cheese, sour cream, and cream soup topped with cornflakes or potato chips, traditionally served at after-funeral luncheons and church gatherings; the dish is so emblematic that it was featured on a pin for the 2002 Salt Lake City Winter Olympics.
  • Pastrami burgers — hamburgers topped with pastrami, cheese, and fry sauce, popularized by Utah Greek-American diner chains such as Crown Burgers.
  • Jell-O — Utah has one of the highest per-capita Jell-O consumption rates in the United States, especially lime flavour; Jell-O salads figure prominently in church and family cooking, and the state legislature once entertained recognizing Jell-O as the state snack.

Other traditions

Dutch oven cooking, inherited from pioneer companies that crossed the plains, remains a strong tradition, celebrated in competitions and in dishes such as Dutch oven potatoes and cobblers. Utah is also a significant producer of tart cherries, celebrated with festivals, and of raspberries in the Bear Lake region, famous for its raspberry shakes. Salt Lake City and Park City have developed notable modern restaurant scenes, and the state hosts a large number of soda shops serving flavoured "dirty sodas" — soft drinks mixed with cream and syrups — which emerged as a popular alternative to coffee and alcohol.
