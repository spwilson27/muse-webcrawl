Pibroch

Pibroch is performed on the Great Highland bagpipe.

Pibroch (Scottish Gaelic: pìobaireachd, pronounced roughly "pee-broch") is a form of music of the Great Highland bagpipe. It is also known as ceòl mòr ("great music") to distinguish it from the lighter dance music of the pipes, ceòl beag ("little music"). Pibroch consists of a theme, called the ùrlar or ground, followed by a series of increasingly complex variations ending in a return to the ground.

Structure

A typical pibroch opens with the ùrlar, a slow melodic theme stated plainly. Successive variations elaborate the theme with rhythmic and ornamental figures, commonly including the siubhal (variation), taorluath and the dramatic crùnluath, each with a "doubling" in which the figure is intensified. The piece typically concludes with a repeat of the ground.

History and repertoire

The repertoire was developed over centuries, much of it by hereditary piping families such as the MacCrimmons of Skye, pipers to the MacLeods of Dunvegan, and the MacArthurs and MacGregors. Compositions include laments (such as "Lament for the Children" by Patrick Mòr MacCrimmon), salutes and gatherings composed for clan occasions, and martial tunes. Because the music was traditionally taught by sung syllables — the mnemonic system called canntaireachd — few early manuscripts exist; the first collections in staff notation appeared in the early 19th century.

Today some two or three hundred pibrochs survive, and pibroch performance is a central part of solo piping competition at events such as the Argyllshire Gathering and the Northern Meeting.
