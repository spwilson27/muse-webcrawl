The Battle of Batih (Polish: Bitwa pod Batohem), fought on 2 June 1652 during the Khmelnytsky Uprising, was a catastrophic defeat of a Polish–Lithuanian army by Cossack forces under Bohdan Khmelnytsky allied with Crimean Tatars.

Background

Following the Treaty of Bila Tserkva, Hetman Marcin Kalinowski led a Crown army into Ukraine to block Khmelnytsky's march toward Moldavia. The Polish camp near Batih was poorly fortified and the army divided by disputes among its commanders.

The battle

Cossack and Tatar forces attacked on 1–2 June, storming the camp. The Polish defences collapsed; Kalinowski was killed, and much of the army was destroyed. After the battle, thousands of Polish prisoners were massacred.

Consequences

Batih was one of the worst defeats in Polish history, sometimes compared with Cecora. It destroyed the Crown's field army in Ukraine and helped push Khmelnytsky toward seeking Muscovite protection, formalised in the Pereyaslav Agreement of 1654.
