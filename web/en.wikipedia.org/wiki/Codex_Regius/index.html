Codex Regius

The Codex Regius (Latin for "Royal Book"; Icelandic: Konungsbók) is an Icelandic codex in which many Old Norse poems from the Poetic Edda are preserved. Written in the 1270s, it contains 29 poems, and is the main source for most of the poems that are included in editions of the Poetic Edda.

History

The manuscript came to light in 1643, when it came into the possession of Brynjólfur Sveinsson, Bishop of Skálholt, who sent it as a gift to King Frederick III of Denmark — hence the name "Codex Regius". It was kept in Copenhagen until 1971, when it was returned to Iceland amid great public celebration; it is now held by the Árni Magnússon Institute for Icelandic Studies in Reykjavík.

Contents

The poems are divided into mythological poems about the Norse gods — including Völuspá, Hávamál and Vafþrúðnismál — and heroic lays about figures such as Sigurd the dragon-slayer. The manuscript has a famous lacuna: a gathering of eight leaves is missing, and the lost poems can be partly reconstructed from other sources.
