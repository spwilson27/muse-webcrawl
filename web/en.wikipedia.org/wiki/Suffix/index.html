Suffix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Suffixes are affixes placed after the stem of a word.

In linguistics, a suffix is an affix which is placed after the stem of a word. Common examples are case endings, which indicate the grammatical case of nouns and adjectives, and verb endings, which form the conjugation of verbs. Suffixes can carry grammatical information (inflectional suffixes) or lexical information (derivational suffixes). An inflectional suffix is sometimes called a desinence or grammatical suffix.

A word-final segment that is somewhere between a free morpheme and a bound morpheme is known as a suffixoid or a semi-suffix (e.g., English -like or German -freundlich, "friendly").

Inflectional suffixes

Inflection does not change the grammatical category of a word. In English, inflectional suffixes include the plural -s (dog → dogs), the possessive -'s, the third-person singular present -s, the past tense -ed, the progressive -ing, the comparative -er, and the superlative -est.

Derivational suffixes

Derivational suffixes create new words, often of a different class: -ness forms nouns from adjectives (happy → happiness), -ly forms adverbs, -ful and -less form adjectives, and -ize forms verbs. Languages such as Turkish, Finnish, and Japanese are noted for stacking long chains of suffixes on a single stem (agglutination).

Other uses

Outside linguistics, "suffix" also denotes letter or numeral additions in other systems, such as generational suffixes in personal names (Jr., III) and suffixes in chemical nomenclature (e.g., -ide, -ite, -ate).
