Islamic architecture

The Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, one of the earliest surviving monuments of Islamic architecture

Islamic architecture comprises the architectural styles of buildings associated with Islam, developing from the 7th century CE onward across the Middle East, North Africa, Spain, Persia, Central Asia, the Indian subcontinent, and beyond. It encompasses religious structures such as mosques and madrasas as well as palaces, tombs, caravanserais, and fortifications.

Characteristics

Common elements include the minaret, the dome, the pointed and horseshoe arch, the iwan, the sahn (courtyard), muqarnas vaulting, and extensive use of geometric patterns, arabesque ornament, and calligraphy rather than figural imagery in religious contexts. Hypostyle halls, four-iwan plans, and pishtaq portals are recurring compositional devices.

History

Early monuments include the Dome of the Rock (691–692) and the Great Mosque of Damascus under the Umayyads. The Abbasids developed Samarra's stucco styles; the Fatimids, Seljuks, Mamluks, Ottomans, Safavids, and Mughals each produced distinctive regional schools, from the Taj Mahal in India to Sinan's mosques in Istanbul and the Alhambra in Granada.

Influences

Islamic architecture synthesized late antique Roman and Byzantine, Persian, and local traditions, and in turn influenced European architecture, notably through Norman Sicily and Mudéjar Spain.
