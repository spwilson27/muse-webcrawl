Senate of Virginia

The Senate of Virginia is the upper house of the Virginia General Assembly, the state legislature of the Commonwealth of Virginia. It is composed of 40 senators elected from single-member districts to four-year terms with no term limits.

Leadership and procedure

The Lieutenant Governor of Virginia serves as President of the Senate, presiding over daily sessions and casting tie-breaking votes—an office that has decided control of the chamber in closely divided sessions. The President pro tempore, Majority Leader and the powerful Finance and Appropriations and Rules committees direct the Senate's business when it convenes at the Virginia State Capitol in Richmond for sessions beginning in January each year (60 days in even years, 30 in odd years, typically extended to 46).

History

The Senate descends from the upper house of the colonial legislature established under the Virginia Constitution of 1776; before 1776, the Governor's Council served as the upper chamber. Senators were elected by the House of Delegates until the Constitution of 1851 established direct popular election. Equal-population redistricting following the one person, one vote decisions of the 1960s reshaped the chamber's representation.
