Fantasia 2000 is a 1999 American animated musical anthology film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Walt Disney Pictures. The 38th Disney animated feature film and the sequel to Fantasia (1940), it consists of eight animated segments set to pieces of classical music conducted by James Levine, performed by the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, and introduced by hosts including Steve Martin, Bette Midler, Itzhak Perlman, Quincy Jones, James Earl Jones, Penn & Teller, and Angela Lansbury. The only segment retained from the original film is The Sorcerer's Apprentice, featuring Mickey Mouse.

History

Walt Disney had envisioned Fantasia as a continually updated "repertoire" film, but its poor initial box office ended the plan. His nephew Roy E. Disney revived the idea in 1991 and executive-produced the sequel through the 1990s. Fantasia 2000 premiered with a concert tour in December 1999 — at Carnegie Hall in New York, then London, Paris, Tokyo, and Pasadena — and on January 1, 2000 became the first animated feature released in IMAX format, in a four-month exclusive engagement, before general release in June 2000. The production blended traditional hand-drawn animation with computer-generated imagery, notably in the abstract opening segment.

Reception

The film received generally positive reviews, with praise for the Rhapsody in Blue and Firebird segments and the IMAX presentation, though it earned a modest $90.9 million worldwide against its large production budget and, like its predecessor, was regarded as a prestige project rather than a commercial triumph. It has since been released with Fantasia in home-video collections and on Disney+, and is commonly discussed as both a tribute to and a modernisation of Walt Disney's original concept.
