Zazie dans le Métro

Raymond Queneau's comic novel follows the irrepressible Zazie through Paris.

Zazie dans le Métro is a 1959 novel by French author Raymond Queneau, a leading member of the Oulipo group. Written in a mix of standard French and phonetically spelled colloquial and slang language, it tells of the adventures of the sharp-tongued young Zazie, who arrives in Paris to stay with her uncle Gabriel while her mother spends time with a lover.

Plot

Zazie's one ambition in Paris is to ride the Métro, but a strike has shut it down. Over a chaotic weekend she dashes around the city with (and often escaping from) her uncle Gabriel, a gentle giant who works as a drag performer, meeting a gallery of characters including the widow Mouaque, the policeman Pedro-surplus — who may be Satan — and the parrot Laverdure, whose catchphrase "Tu causes, tu causes, c'est tout ce que tu sais faire" ("you chatter, you chatter, that's all you know how to do") punctuates the book. At the end, the Métro reopens just as Zazie must leave; asked by her mother what she has done, she replies simply: "J'ai vieilli" — "I've grown older."

Style and reception

The novel's opening word, "Doukipudonktan" — a phonetic squashing of "D'où qu'ils puent donc tant?" ("How come they stink so much?") — announces Queneau's playful assault on written French. The book was an immediate popular success, made Queneau famous with the general reading public, and has become a modern classic. Barbara Wright's English translation is widely praised for recreating its verbal inventiveness.

Film adaptation

Louis Malle directed a film adaptation, Zazie dans le Métro, released in 1960, with Catherine Demongeot as Zazie and Philippe Noiret as Gabriel.
