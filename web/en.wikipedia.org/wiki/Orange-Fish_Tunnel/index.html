Orange–Fish Tunnel

The Orange–Fish Tunnel, in the Eastern Cape of South Africa, diverts water from the Orange River at the Gariep Dam westward to the Great Fish River valley and onward toward the Sundays River valley, for irrigation, municipal supply and hydroelectric purposes.

Completed in 1975 as the centrepiece of the Orange–Fish River Project, the tunnel is about 82.8 kilometres long, making it one of the longest water-supply tunnels in the world and the longest in the southern hemisphere. It is concrete-lined with an internal diameter of about 5.35 metres and carries water entirely by gravity.

The scheme transformed agriculture in the arid receiving valleys, supporting irrigation of tens of thousands of hectares, and also supplies water to towns including areas of the Nelson Mandela Bay metropolitan area via associated works.
