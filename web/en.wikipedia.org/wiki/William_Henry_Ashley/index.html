William Henry Ashley

William Henry Ashley (c. 1778 – March 26, 1838) was an American fur trader, entrepreneur, and politician. With Andrew Henry he founded the Rocky Mountain Fur Company, whose "mountain men" — including Jedediah Smith and Jim Bridger — opened much of the American West to the fur trade. Ashley is credited with devising the Rocky Mountain rendezvous system of 1825, and later served in the U.S. House of Representatives from Missouri.

Fur trade career

Ashley moved to Missouri around 1808 and prospered in land, lead mining, gunpowder manufacture, and militia service, becoming a brigadier general and Missouri's first lieutenant governor in 1820. In 1822 he and Andrew Henry advertised in St. Louis newspapers for "100 enterprising young men" to ascend the Missouri River — the recruits of "Ashley's Hundred" became legendary mountain men.

After the disastrous Arikara attack of 1823 disrupted river transport, Ashley reorganised the trade around overland supply caravans and an annual summer rendezvous at which trappers exchanged furs for goods. The first rendezvous was held in 1825 on Henry's Fork of the Green River. His expeditions pushed deep into the Rockies and, in 1824–25, parties associated with Ashley reached the Great Salt Lake region. In 1826 he sold the firm to Smith, David Jackson, and William Sublette and retired from the field.

Political career

Ashley served three terms in Congress (1831–1837), sitting on the Committee on Indian Affairs and advocating for western interests. He ran unsuccessfully for governor of Missouri in 1836 and died in 1838.
