Tourism in Mexico

Tourism in Mexico is a major sector of the national economy: Mexico consistently ranks among the world's most-visited countries, receiving over forty million international tourists in peak years, most of them from the United States and Canada. Tourism is one of Mexico's largest sources of foreign exchange and employment.

Destinations

The best-known destinations are the purpose-built and traditional beach resorts: Cancún and the Riviera Maya in Quintana Roo, Puerto Vallarta and Los Cabos on the Pacific side, Acapulco, Mazatlán, and Cozumel. Cultural tourism centres on Mexico City, Oaxaca, Guanajuato, Puebla and Mérida, and on archaeological sites of the pre-Columbian civilisations — Teotihuacan, Chichen Itza, Palenque and Monte Albán among them. The federal Pueblos Mágicos ("Magical Towns") programme promotes smaller towns of cultural or natural distinction. Mexico has more UNESCO World Heritage Sites than any other country in the Americas.

Development and challenges

The state agency FONATUR developed resort cities such as Cancún and Huatulco from the 1970s. The industry has faced challenges from the 2009 financial crisis and swine-flu outbreak, the COVID-19 pandemic (from which Mexico recovered unusually quickly by keeping borders open), and periodic concerns over crime and sargassum seaweed on Caribbean beaches. Recent projects include the Tren Maya railway around the Yucatán Peninsula.
