Huorn

Huorns are fictional tree-like beings in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing principally in The Lord of the Rings. They are described as trees that have become partly awake and mobile under the influence of the Ents, or alternatively as Ents that have grown wild, silent, and "treeish". Huorns are dangerous and unpredictable, hostile to Orcs and to Men who threaten the forests, and they communicate in a creaking, half-vegetable manner comprehensible mainly to Ents.

Role in The Lord of the Rings

The Old Forest on the borders of the Shire is said to contain trees with Huorn-like qualities, watchful and resentful of intruders; Merry Brandybuck speculates that its trees may have been "awakened" in deep antiquity. The Huorns' most prominent appearance comes during the War of the Ring. After the Ents' attack on Isengard, a great host of Huorns under Ent supervision marched to Helm's Deep, where they formed a dark forest into which Saruman's fleeing Orc army blundered after the Battle of the Hornburg. None of the Orcs emerged.

Treebeard explains to Merry and Pippin that Huorns are neither fully trees nor fully Ents: some trees have become talkative and quick under Entish influence, while some Ents have grown sleepy and rooted. Huorns move swiftly when roused — passing "like a wind" — prefer darkness and mist, and their anger at axe-bearing peoples dates back to the destruction of the great forests of earlier ages.

Interpretation

Commentators have linked the Huorns to Tolkien's love of trees and his dismay at industrial deforestation, as well as to the moving wood of Birnam in Shakespeare's Macbeth, which Tolkien felt had been disappointingly realized and which inspired both the Huorns and the Ents' march on Isengard. In Peter Jackson's film adaptation of The Two Towers (extended edition), a Huorn in the Old Forest tradition nearly crushes Merry and Pippin before Tom Bombadil intervenes.
