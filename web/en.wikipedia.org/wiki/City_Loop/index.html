City Loop

The City Loop (originally called the Melbourne Underground Rail Loop) is a mostly underground and partly elevated railway in the central business district of Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. It forms a loop around the city centre connecting the five stations of Flinders Street, Southern Cross, Flagstaff, Melbourne Central and Parliament, and is used by most of Melbourne's suburban rail lines. Construction began in 1971 and the loop opened in stages between 1981 and 1985.

The City Loop serves five central Melbourne stations.

Configuration

The loop consists of four parallel single-direction tunnels (the Burnley, Caulfield, Clifton Hill and Northern loops), each serving particular groups of suburban lines, plus elevated sections connecting to Flinders Street and Southern Cross. Trains from most lines travel around the loop in one direction in the morning peak and the reverse direction in the afternoon, a pattern long criticised for confusing passengers. Parliament, Melbourne Central and Flagstaff are underground stations; Flagstaff does not open on weekends.

History

Proposals for an underground city railway date to the 1920s, and the 1969 Melbourne Transportation Plan recommended the loop. The Melbourne Underground Rail Loop Authority (MURLA) was established in 1970, and tunnelling began in 1971. The first section, from Princes Bridge to Parliament via Melbourne Central (then Museum), opened in 1981; the full loop was completed in 1985. The project cost roughly A$460 million.

Future

The Metro Tunnel project, opened in 2025, removed the Sunbury, Pakenham and Cranbourne lines from the loop, freeing capacity. Longer-term plans under the Suburban Rail Loop and loop reconfiguration proposals would allow through-running and simpler operations.
