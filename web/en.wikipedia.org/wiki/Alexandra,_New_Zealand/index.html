Alexandra is a town in the Central Otago district of New Zealand's South Island. It lies on the banks of the Clutha River (Mata-Au) at its confluence with the Manuherikia River, and serves as the service centre for one of the country's principal stone-fruit and wine-growing regions.

History

The settlement arose during the Central Otago gold rush of the 1860s, founded after gold was found nearby in 1862. Originally known as Lower Township, it was renamed Alexandra in honour of Alexandra of Denmark, the Princess of Wales. Gold dredging on the Clutha continued well into the 20th century, and the distinctive dredge tailings still mark the river flats.

Climate and economy

Alexandra has one of New Zealand's most continental climates: hot, dry summers, cold winters, and some of the country's lowest annual rainfall. The climate suits orchards producing apricots, cherries, peaches and nectarines, and vineyards producing notable pinot noir. The town is a trailhead for the Otago Central Rail Trail and the Roxburgh Gorge Trail, and hosts the annual Great Easter Bunny Hunt and a spring blossom festival.

Landmarks

  • The Alexandra Clock, a large clock on Knobbies Range overlooking the town (installed 1968)
  • The historic shingle-roofed courthouse and Central Stories museum
  • The confluence lookout over the Clutha and Manuherikia rivers
