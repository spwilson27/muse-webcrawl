St Fagans National Museum of History

St Fagans National Museum of History (Welsh: Sain Ffagan: Amgueddfa Werin Cymru), commonly referred to as St Fagans after the village where it is located, is an open-air museum in Cardiff chronicling the historical lifestyle, culture, and architecture of the Welsh people. The museum is part of the wider network of Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales.

History

The museum was founded in 1946 through the donation of St Fagans Castle and its grounds by the Earl of Plymouth. It was inspired by Skansen, the open-air museum in Stockholm, and opened to the public in 1948. The museum's first curator was the Welsh folklorist Iorwerth Peate.

Buildings

The museum comprises more than forty buildings moved from locations all over Wales and re-erected in the grounds of St Fagans Castle, an Elizabethan manor house. They include a medieval church, a workingmen's institute, a Victorian school, farmhouses, cottages, a tollhouse, a tannery, shops, and a row of ironworkers' cottages (Rhyd-y-car) showing interiors from different periods. Traditional crafts are demonstrated on site, and native Welsh livestock breeds are kept on the museum's farm.

A major redevelopment in the 2010s added the Gweithdy (workshop) gallery and a reconstruction of Llys Llywelyn, a medieval Welsh prince's court based on the remains of Llywelyn the Great's hall at Rhosyr. St Fagans is one of Wales's most popular tourist attractions.
