The Seattle SuperSonics (commonly known as the Sonics) were an American professional basketball team based in Seattle, Washington. They played in the National Basketball Association (NBA) from 1967 until 2008, when the franchise relocated to Oklahoma City and became the Thunder. The SuperSonics won the NBA championship in 1979 and reached the NBA Finals in 1978 and 1996.

History

Early years and championship (1967–1979)

The franchise was founded as an expansion team in 1967, its name inspired by Boeing's supersonic transport project. After lean early seasons, the team improved under coach Bill Russell in the mid-1970s and then under Lenny Wilkens. The 1977–78 Sonics reached the Finals, losing to the Washington Bullets in seven games; the following season, led by Gus Williams, Dennis Johnson and Jack Sikma, Seattle defeated Washington in a rematch to win the 1979 NBA championship — the first major professional sports title for the city.

The Kemp and Payton era (1989–1998)

After a rebuilding period, the Sonics rose again behind point guard Gary Payton and forward Shawn Kemp, coached by George Karl. The 1995–96 team won a franchise-record 64 games and advanced to the NBA Finals, losing to Michael Jordan's Chicago Bulls in six games. Payton became the face of the franchise and one of the league's premier defensive guards.

Final years and relocation (2001–2008)

Starbucks executive Howard Schultz bought the team in 2001. Later teams featured Ray Allen and Rashard Lewis, and in 2007 the Sonics drafted Kevin Durant, who won Rookie of the Year. In 2006 Schultz sold the franchise to an Oklahoma City-based ownership group led by Clay Bennett. Following disputes over a new arena in Seattle, the team relocated in 2008 after a settlement with the city, which retained the SuperSonics name, colours and history for a potential future team. The relocated club became the Oklahoma City Thunder.

Legacy

The SuperSonics remain a potent symbol in Seattle sports culture, and efforts to bring an NBA expansion team back to the city have continued since the relocation. Retired numbers include those of Gus Williams, Nate McMillan, Lenny Wilkens, Jack Sikma, Fred Brown, Gary Payton and Spencer Haywood.
