Youngest Toba eruption

The Youngest Toba eruption, also called the Youngest Toba Tuff or YTT, was a supervolcanic eruption of the Toba caldera on the island of Sumatra, Indonesia, about 74,000 years ago. It was one of the largest explosive eruptions known on Earth in the past several million years, ejecting roughly 2,800 km³ of material and producing the lake-filled caldera now known as Lake Toba. The eruption has been hypothesised to have caused a global volcanic winter and a genetic bottleneck in early human populations, though the severity of both effects is debated.

The eruption

The eruption emptied a huge shallow magma chamber beneath what is now northern Sumatra, collapsing the roof to form a caldera 100 km long and 30 km wide. Ignimbrite and ash covered much of Sumatra, and Toba ash is found in sediments across the Indian Ocean, India, the South China Sea and as far away as East Africa—more than 7,000 km distant. Sulphur dioxide injected into the stratosphere is estimated at thousands of megatonnes, dwarfing historic eruptions such as Pinatubo in 1991.

Climatic effects

Ice-core records show a sharp sulphate spike and a cold interval around the time of the eruption. Some researchers proposed that Toba triggered a "volcanic winter" lasting years and accelerated the onset of the last glacial period, dropping global temperatures by several degrees. Other modelling and palaeoclimate studies suggest the cooling, while severe, was shorter and weaker than first claimed.

Human bottleneck hypothesis

In 1993 journalist Ann Gibbons linked Toba to a proposed genetic bottleneck that reduced the human population to perhaps a few thousand breeding individuals, and the idea was developed by Stanley Ambrose and others. Archaeological evidence from India, where stone tools occur both below and above Toba ash, and more recent genetic analyses have challenged the catastrophic version of the hypothesis, suggesting human populations in Africa and Asia survived without a near-extinction event, though regional disruption remains plausible.
