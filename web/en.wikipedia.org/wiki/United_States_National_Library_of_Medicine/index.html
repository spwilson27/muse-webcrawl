The United States National Library of Medicine (NLM), located on the campus of the National Institutes of Health in Bethesda, Maryland, is the world's largest biomedical library. It maintains and makes available a vast print collection and produces electronic information resources used by scientists, health professionals, and the public worldwide, including the PubMed bibliographic database, which is searched billions of times a year.

History

The library traces its origins to 1836, when Surgeon General Joseph Lovell established a small collection of medical books and journals. Under John Shaw Billings, who directed it from 1865, it grew into a great national collection; Billings launched the Index-Catalogue and Index Medicus, the forerunners of modern biomedical indexing. In 1956 the National Library of Medicine Act transferred the library to the Public Health Service and gave it its present name; it moved to Bethesda in 1962 and joined the NIH in 1968.

Information services

NLM's National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), created in 1988, operates GenBank, PubMed, and PubMed Central, the free full-text archive of biomedical literature. Other major services include MEDLINE, MedlinePlus (consumer health information), ClinicalTrials.gov, the Unified Medical Language System, and the Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) vocabulary. The library also coordinates the Network of the National Library of Medicine, a nationwide system of health information outreach, and preserves a major collection in the history of medicine dating to the 11th century.
