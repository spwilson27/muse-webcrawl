Havaianas

Havaianas (Portuguese for "Hawaiians") is a Brazilian brand of flip-flops founded in 1962 by Alpargatas S.A. Inspired by the Japanese zōri sandal, the original design combined a rubber sole patterned after rice straw with rice-grain texture and a V-shaped strap. Havaianas became a global fashion phenomenon in the 1990s and 2000s and is one of Brazil's best-known export brands.

History

The first Havaianas were produced in São Paulo in 1962 by the Scottish-founded Alpargatas company. Originally a cheap workman's sandal sold in blue-and-white, the flip-flops became ubiquitous in Brazil. A manufacturing quirk in 1969 produced a batch of green-soled pairs that sold unexpectedly well, leading to a wider range of colours.

Fashion phenomenon

In the mid-1990s the company repositioned the brand with new styles, prints and advertising, and Havaianas moved from basic footwear to a fashion item endorsed by models and celebrities. Flagship stores opened in cities including São Paulo (the Oscar Freire street store), New York, Paris and London. By the 2010s the brand sold well over 200 million pairs a year in dozens of countries, and customisation shops allowed customers to assemble their own colour combinations.

Design

The classic model consists of a secret-formula rubber sole — marketed for durability and flexibility — with a distinctive rice-grain textured footbed and butterfly-shaped strap plugs. Counterfeiting has been a persistent issue, and the brand has fought numerous trademark and trade-dress disputes internationally.
