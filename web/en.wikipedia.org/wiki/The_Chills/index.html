The Chills

The Chills are a New Zealand rock band formed in Dunedin in 1980. The band is the project of singer-songwriter Martin Phillipps, who is the group's sole constant member. The Chills were one of the flagship bands of the Dunedin sound and the Flying Nun Records label.

History

Martin Phillipps formed the Chills in 1980 after the breakup of his earlier band The Same. Early singles such as "Rolling Moon" (1982) and "Pink Frost" (1984) became hallmarks of the Dunedin sound. The band's single "Heavenly Pop Hit", from the 1990 album Submarine Bells, was an international minor hit and won them attention in the United States and United Kingdom.

The band's history was marked by an extraordinarily unstable line-up; dozens of musicians passed through the group, and Phillipps disbanded it several times amid personal difficulties during the 1990s. Submarine Bells won Album of the Year at the New Zealand Music Awards.

Later work

After a long quiet period, Phillipps revived the Chills with a stable line-up, releasing Silver Bullets (2015), their first album in nineteen years, followed by Snow Bound (2018), the live album Sprung, and Brave Words (2023). The 2019 documentary The Chills: The Triumph & Tragedy of Martin Phillipps chronicled Phillipps's career and health struggles.
