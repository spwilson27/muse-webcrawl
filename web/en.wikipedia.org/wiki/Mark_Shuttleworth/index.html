Mark Richard Shuttleworth (born 18 September 1973) is a South African and British entrepreneur, founder of the security firm Thawte, of the company Canonical, and of the Ubuntu Linux distribution. In 2002 he became the first African in space, flying as a self-funded space participant to the International Space Station aboard a Russian Soyuz spacecraft.

Thawte

Shuttleworth founded Thawte Consulting in 1995 in Cape Town, building it into one of the world's largest issuers of digital certificates for e-commerce. He sold the company to VeriSign in December 1999 for a reported US$575 million.

Spaceflight

After a year of training in Russia, Shuttleworth launched aboard Soyuz TM-34 on 25 April 2002 and spent eight days aboard the International Space Station conducting scientific experiments, returning on Soyuz TM-33. He is often described as the second "space tourist" after Dennis Tito, though he prefers the term space participant.

Ubuntu and Canonical

In 2004 Shuttleworth founded Canonical Ltd. to support Ubuntu, a Debian-based Linux distribution aimed at ease of use, funded through his Shuttleworth Foundation and investment vehicle HBD Venture Capital. He coined the project's philosophy of "Linux for human beings" and for years acted as Ubuntu's self-described "Self-Appointed Benevolent Dictator for Life" (SABDFL). He served as Canonical's CEO until 2009, then again from 2017.

Other work

The Shuttleworth Foundation supports social innovation and open-source education projects in South Africa, including the Freedom Toaster. Shuttleworth has also been involved in litigation with the South African Reserve Bank over capital export levies.
