The Battle of the Scheldt was a series of military operations undertaken by the First Canadian Army in northern Belgium and the southwestern Netherlands between 2 October and 8 November 1944, during the Second World War. Its object was to clear the banks of the Scheldt estuary so that the great port of Antwerp — captured intact on 4 September but unusable while the Germans held the estuary — could be opened to Allied shipping.

Background

After the rapid advance across France, Allied logistics were stretched to breaking point; supplies still came over the Normandy beaches and through Cherbourg. Antwerp's docks were intact, but the German Fifteenth Army, which had escaped across the estuary, dug in on the South Beveland peninsula, the island of Walcheren and the Breskens pocket south of the river, under orders to hold the estuary at all costs.

The battle

The battle comprised four main operations. In Operation Switchback, the 3rd Canadian Division cleared the Breskens pocket, fighting across flooded polders. Operation Vitality cleared South Beveland. Operation Infatuate attacked Walcheren, whose sea dykes the RAF breached to flood the island; amphibious landings at Westkapelle and Flushing on 1 November broke the defence, and Middelburg fell on 6 November. Meanwhile the 2nd Canadian Division fought north of Antwerp and along the causeway to South Beveland (Operation Pheasant and the fighting at Woensdrecht). In all, the First Canadian Army suffered some 12,873 casualties, roughly half of them Canadian, and took over 41,000 prisoners.

Aftermath

Minesweepers cleared the estuary during November, and the first Allied convoy reached Antwerp on 28 November 1944. The opening of the port transformed Allied logistics and made the final offensives into Germany possible.
