Acoustic guitar

An acoustic guitar is a fretted string instrument whose sound is produced by the vibration of its strings amplified acoustically by a hollow body, without electrical amplification. The modern acoustic guitar typically has six strings, tuned E–A–D–G–B–E, and is central to folk, country, blues, flamenco and popular music worldwide.

Construction

The guitar's wooden body — a top (soundboard) usually of spruce or cedar, with backs and sides of mahogany, rosewood or maple — resonates through its sound hole and internal bracing. X-bracing, developed by C. F. Martin in the nineteenth century, allowed large steel-string instruments such as the dreadnought to withstand high string tension and produce greater volume. Classical guitars use gut or nylon strings and fan bracing, and are played with the fingers rather than a pick.

History

The guitar descends from earlier European plucked instruments such as the vihuela and the Baroque guitar. Antonio de Torres Jurado standardised the classical guitar's size and fan bracing in 1850s Spain, while Martin and later Gibson developed the American steel-string flat-top and archtop. In the twentieth century the acoustic guitar became the emblematic instrument of singer-songwriters and folk revivalists, and electro-acoustic models with built-in pickups extended its use to large stages.
