Krasnodar Krai

Krasnodar Krai (Russian: Краснода́рский край, romanized: Krasnodarskiy kray) is a federal subject of Russia (a krai), located in the North Caucasus region in Southern Russia and administratively a part of the Southern Federal District. Its administrative centre is the city of Krasnodar. With a population of roughly 5.8 million, it is the third most populous federal subject in Russia.


The Black Sea coast near Sochi.

Geography

Krasnodar Krai borders Rostov Oblast to the north, Stavropol Krai and Karachay-Cherkessia to the east, and Abkhazia (Georgia) to the south; it encloses the Republic of Adygea. Its western edge is washed by the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov, and it faces Crimea across the Kerch Strait. The Kuban River divides the krai into a northern plain (steppe) portion and a southern mountainous portion along the Greater Caucasus.

Economy

The krai is one of Russia's principal agricultural regions, producing grain, sunflower oil, sugar and wine. Its Black Sea ports, including Novorossiysk — Russia's largest seaport — and Tuapse, handle a large share of the country's maritime exports, including oil. Tourism is significant, centred on the resort city of Sochi, which hosted the 2014 Winter Olympics, and on Anapa and Gelendzhik.

History

The region corresponds roughly to the historical Kuban, settled by Kuban Cossacks from the late 18th century. Krasnodar Krai was established on 13 September 1937, when Azov-Black Sea Krai was divided into Krasnodar Krai and Rostov Oblast.
