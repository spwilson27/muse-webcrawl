Hawaiʻi Aloha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Hawaiʻi Aloha

"Hawaiʻi Aloha", also known as "Kuʻu One Hanau", is a revered Hawaiian song written by the Reverend Lorenzo Lyons (1807–1886), a missionary known in Hawaii as "Makua Laiana" (Father Lyons). Lyons, who served at Waimea on the island of Hawaii, wrote the lyrics in the Hawaiian language and set them to the tune of the hymn "I Love Thy Kingdom, Lord" by Charles Zeuner. The song expresses deep love and loyalty to the Hawaiian homeland.

Text and use

The first verse begins: E Hawaiʻi e kuʻu one hānau ē / Kuʻu home kulaīwi nei — "O Hawaii, my birthplace, my native home". "Hawaiʻi Aloha" is sung at the closing of countless gatherings in Hawaii, from graduation ceremonies and sporting events to political rallies, often with participants standing and holding hands. It is widely regarded as an unofficial anthem of Hawaii and a standard of Hawaiian music, recorded by many artists and taught to generations of schoolchildren.

Composer

Lorenzo Lyons arrived in Hawaii in 1832 with the fifth company of American missionaries and spent the rest of his life there, composing numerous Hawaiian hymns and translating texts into Hawaiian. He is buried at Imiola Church in Waimea, which he founded.
