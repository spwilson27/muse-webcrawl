Mother Courage and Her Children (German: Mutter Courage und ihre Kinder) is a play written in 1939 by the German dramatist Bertolt Brecht (1898–1956), with significant contributions from Margarete Steffin. Four theatrical productions were produced in Switzerland and Germany between 1941 and 1952, the last being the most famous: Brecht's own production with the Berliner Ensemble in 1949, starring his wife Helene Weigel in the title role. The play is regarded as one of the greatest anti-war plays and a masterpiece of Brecht's epic theatre.

Plot

The play follows Anna Fierling, nicknamed Mother Courage, a canteen woman who follows the armies of the Thirty Years' War with her wagon, selling goods to soldiers and determined to profit from the war while protecting her three children: the brave Eilif, the honest Swiss Cheese, and the mute Kattrin. One by one, the war she lives off destroys them — Eilif is executed for the same act of violence for which he was earlier honoured; Swiss Cheese is shot when, as paymaster, he will not reveal the hiding place of the regimental cash box; and Kattrin is killed while drumming on a rooftop to warn a town of a surprise attack. Courage, having learned nothing, harnesses herself to the wagon and follows the army on.

Themes and technique

Brecht intended Courage not as a heroine but as a warning: her "courage" is endurance in the service of business, and war, the play argues, is the continuation of commerce by other means, in which the little people always pay. The production uses the devices of epic theatre — songs that comment on the action, placards announcing scenes, direct address — to produce the alienation effect (Verfremdungseffekt) and prevent simple identification with the heroine, although audiences persistently sympathised with her, much to Brecht's frustration. The character derives from Grimmelshausen's picaresque novel of the war and its heroine Courasche.

Legacy

The play has been translated and staged worldwide; major English-language productions have starred actresses including Diana Rigg, Meryl Streep, and Fiona Shaw. The figure of Mother Courage has entered the culture as an emblem of those who feed on war — and of indomitable, misdirected maternal survival.
