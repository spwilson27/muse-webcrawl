Intercession of saints

The intercession of saints is a doctrine held by the Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, and some other Christian traditions, according to which the saints in heaven can be asked to pray to God on behalf of the living. It is distinct from prayer addressed to the saints as if they themselves grant favors; rather, the saints are understood to intercede as fellow members of the communion of saints.

Basis and practice

Supporters cite the image in the Book of Revelation of the elders and angels offering the prayers of the saints to God, and argue that asking a saint's prayers is like asking a living fellow Christian to pray for one. The Virgin Mary is the most prominent intercessor, with titles such as Mediatrix, and patron saints are commonly invoked for particular places, occupations, and needs.

Criticism

Most Protestant traditions reject the practice, maintaining that Christ is the sole mediator between God and humanity (1 Timothy 2:5) and that prayer should be directed to God alone. The debate was a significant issue in the Reformation.
