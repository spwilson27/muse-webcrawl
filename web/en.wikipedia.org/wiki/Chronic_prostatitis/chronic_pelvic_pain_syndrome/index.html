Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome

Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS) is a pelvic pain condition in men, characterised by chronic pain in the perineum, pelvis, suprapubic area, or genitalia, often accompanied by urinary symptoms, lasting at least three of the preceding six months, in the absence of a demonstrable bacterial infection.

Classification

Under the NIH classification of prostatitis, CP/CPPS is category III, subdivided into IIIa (inflammatory), with white blood cells in prostatic secretions or post-massage urine, and IIIb (non-inflammatory), without. It is the most common type of prostatitis, accounting for the great majority of cases.

Causes and treatment

The cause is poorly understood and is thought to involve interacting immune, neurological, endocrine, and psychological factors rather than ongoing infection. Management focuses on symptom relief, including alpha blockers, anti-inflammatory drugs, pelvic floor physiotherapy, and multidisciplinary approaches; antibiotics are generally not effective unless infection is present.
