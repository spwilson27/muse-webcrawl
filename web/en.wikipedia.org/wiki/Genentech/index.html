Genentech

Genentech, Inc. — Biotechnology company. Founded 7 April 1976 by Herbert Boyer and Robert A. Swanson. Headquarters: South San Francisco, California. Parent: Roche (since 2009). Considered the founder of the biotechnology industry.

Genentech, Inc. is an American biotechnology corporation headquartered in South San Francisco, California. It is considered the founder of the biotechnology industry and became a wholly owned subsidiary of Roche in 2009. Its name is a portmanteau of "genetic engineering technology".

History

Genentech was founded in 1976 by venture capitalist Robert A. Swanson and biochemist Herbert Boyer, a pioneer of recombinant DNA technology. In 1977 the company produced the first human protein (somatostatin) in a bacterium, and in 1978 its scientists cloned human insulin, licensed to Eli Lilly and marketed as Humulin (1982), the first recombinant DNA drug. Genentech's 1980 initial public offering was a landmark event in the history of biotechnology and Wall Street.

Products

The company's notable medicines include human growth hormone (Protropin), the cancer drugs rituximab (Rituxan, with Biogen), trastuzumab (Herceptin), bevacizumab (Avastin), and ranibizumab (Lucentis) for eye disease, as well as ocrelizumab (Ocrevus) for multiple sclerosis and alteplase (Activase), a tissue plasminogen activator.

Roche ownership

Roche held a majority stake in Genentech from 1990 and acquired the remaining shares in 2009 for about $46.8 billion. Genentech continues to operate as Roche's research and early development arm in the United States, retaining its South San Francisco campus and name.
