Macrina the Younger

Macrina the Younger (Greek: Μακρίνα; c. 327 – 19 July 379) was an early Christian consecrated virgin and a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Anglican traditions. She was the eldest daughter of Basil the Elder and Emmelia of Caesarea, and the sister of the Cappadocian Fathers Basil the Great and Gregory of Nyssa, both of whom regarded her as their teacher in the ascetic life.

Life

Macrina was born in Caesarea in Cappadocia (in modern Turkey). When her fiancé died, she refused further marriage proposals and devoted herself to religious life. After her father's death she persuaded her mother to convert the family estate at Annisa in Pontus into a monastic community for women — one of the first such communities — where mistresses and servants lived as equals. She played a central role in the education of her younger brothers, particularly Peter of Sebaste, and influenced Basil's embrace of monasticism, which he later codified in his Rules.

Sources and theology

Nearly all that is known of Macrina comes from her brother Gregory of Nyssa, who wrote her biography, the Life of Macrina, and made her the principal speaker of his dialogue On the Soul and the Resurrection, set on her deathbed in 379. In the dialogue Macrina discourses learnedly on death, the soul and the resurrection, earning her the later description "the Teacher" and, from some modern scholars, the title of a fourth Cappadocian. Her feast day is 19 July.
