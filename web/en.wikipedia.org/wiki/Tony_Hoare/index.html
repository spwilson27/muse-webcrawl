Sir Charles Antony Richard Hoare (born 11 January 1934), generally known as Tony Hoare, is a British computer scientist who has made foundational contributions to programming languages, algorithms and software verification. He developed the sorting algorithm quicksort in 1959–60, Hoare logic for verifying program correctness, and the formal language CSP (Communicating Sequential Processes) for describing concurrent systems.

Career

Hoare read Classics and philosophy at Merton College, Oxford, and did national service in the Royal Navy, learning Russian. Working at the computer manufacturer Elliott Brothers from 1960, he implemented an ALGOL 60 compiler and invented quicksort while working on machine translation of Russian sentences. In 1968 he became Professor of Computing at Queen's University Belfast, moving in 1977 to Oxford, where he led the Programming Research Group until his retirement in 1999, after which he worked at Microsoft Research in Cambridge.

Contributions and famous remarks

His 1969 paper "An Axiomatic Basis for Computer Programming" founded Hoare logic (Hoare triples {P} C {Q}). His 1978 CSP paper and 1985 book shaped concurrency theory and inspired languages and tools from occam to Go channels. Receiving the Turing Award in 1980, he has been widely quoted on design: "There are two ways of constructing a software design: one way is to make it so simple that there are obviously no deficiencies, and the other way is to make it so complicated that there are no obvious deficiencies." In 2009 he apologised for inventing the null reference in ALGOL W, calling it his "billion-dollar mistake". He was knighted in 2000 for services to education and computer science.
