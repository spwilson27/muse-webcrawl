Modernismo

Modernismo is a literary and artistic movement that arose in Spanish America and Spain in the late 19th century. In literature, it is generally considered to have begun with the publication of Azul... (1888) by the Nicaraguan poet Rubén Darío, who became the movement's leading figure. Modernismo combined influences from French Parnassianism and Symbolism with a deliberate renovation of Spanish poetic language, imagery and metre.

The term is also applied, in Catalan and Spanish contexts, to the architectural and decorative-arts movement known elsewhere as Art Nouveau, exemplified in Barcelona by Antoni Gaudí, Lluís Domènech i Montaner and Josep Puig i Cadafalch.

Literary modernismo

Modernista writers sought beauty, musicality and cosmopolitan refinement, drawing imagery from classical mythology, exotic landscapes and precious materials. Important figures include José Martí, Manuel Gutiérrez Nájera, Julián del Casal, José Asunción Silva, Leopoldo Lugones and Delmira Agustini. The movement spread to Spain, where it influenced poets such as Juan Ramón Jiménez and the young Antonio Machado.

Characteristics

Modernismo is characterised by metrical experimentation, rich synesthetic imagery, aristocratic and escapist themes, and a rejection of the bourgeois materialism of the period. Although later writers reacted against its ornamental excesses — a shift sometimes called posmodernismo — the movement permanently renewed Spanish-language poetry and is regarded as the first literary movement to originate in Latin America and influence Spain, reversing the traditional direction of cultural flow.
