Flaxseed oil

Flaxseed oil, also known as flax oil or linseed oil (in its edible form), is a colourless to yellowish oil obtained from the dried, ripened seeds of the flax plant (Linum usitatissimum). The oil is obtained by pressing, sometimes followed by solvent extraction.

Flaxseed oil is a drying oil, meaning it can polymerise into a solid form, which makes industrial linseed oil a key ingredient in oil paints, wood finishes, putty and traditional linoleum. Edible flaxseed oil is a source of α-linolenic acid (ALA), an omega-3 fatty acid; it contains roughly 50–60% ALA, among the highest levels of any plant oil. Because it oxidises readily, edible flaxseed oil is sold in opaque bottles, refrigerated, and is not suitable for high-temperature cooking.

Uses

As linseed oil, it has been used for centuries as a preservative for wood and masonry and as the binder in oil-based paints. Boiled linseed oil (treated with metallic driers) speeds curing for wood finishing. Nutritionally, flaxseed oil is sold as a dietary supplement; the whole and ground seeds also provide fibre and lignans. Historically, linseed oil and flax fibres underpinned the manufacture of linoleum flooring, invented in the 1860s.
