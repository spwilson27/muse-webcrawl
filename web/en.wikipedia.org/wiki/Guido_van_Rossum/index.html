Guido van Rossum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Guido van Rossum, portrait photograph at a conference]

Guido van Rossum (born 1956) is a Dutch programmer best known as the creator of the Python programming language. He was Python's 'Benevolent Dictator for Life' (BDFL) — its lead decision-maker — from its inception until stepping down in 2018.

Born in Haarlem, Netherlands, van Rossum worked at CWI (Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica) in Amsterdam, where he began Python in 1989 as a successor to the ABC language.

Career

He later worked in the United States (NIST, CNRI, BeOpen), then at Google (2005–2012), where he developed the Mondrian code-review tool and worked on App Engine; then at Dropbox (2013–2019), helping migrate millions of lines of Python. In 2020 he joined Microsoft as a Distinguished Engineer, working on CPython performance (the Faster CPython project).

The 2018 controversy over a language feature (PEP 572, the 'walrus operator') led him to resign as BDFL; Python is now governed by an elected steering council under PEP 13.

Recognition

Honours include the Free Software Foundation's Award for the Advancement of Free Software, and he was named a Fellow of the Computer History Museum in 2019.
