United States federal budget

Overview
Fiscal year: October 1 – September 30
Spending (FY2023): about $6.1 trillion
Revenue (FY2023): about $4.4 trillion

The United States federal budget comprises the spending and revenues of the United States federal government. It is the financial plan through which the government funds public services, social insurance programs, defense, and interest on the national debt.

Budget process

Under the Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act of 1974, the President submits a budget request to Congress each February. House and Senate budget committees draft a concurrent budget resolution setting overall spending levels, after which twelve appropriations bills fund discretionary programs. Mandatory spending — including Social Security, Medicare, and Medicaid — flows from permanent authorizing laws rather than annual appropriations.

Spending and revenue

The largest spending categories are Social Security, health programs (Medicare and Medicaid), national defense, and net interest. Revenue comes chiefly from individual income taxes (about half of receipts), payroll taxes funding social insurance, and corporate income taxes.

Deficits and debt

When outlays exceed receipts, the government runs a budget deficit financed by issuing Treasury securities. The federal government ran surpluses from 1998 to 2001; deficits returned thereafter and spiked during the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic, reaching about $2.8 trillion in fiscal 2021.
