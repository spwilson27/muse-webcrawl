Cairo Conference
Dates: 22–26 November 1943
Location: Cairo, Egypt
Participants: Roosevelt, Churchill, Chiang Kai-shek

The Cairo Conference (codenamed Sextant) of November 1943 was a meeting of the Allied leaders of World War II held in Cairo, Egypt. It was attended by President Franklin D. Roosevelt of the United States, Prime Minister Winston Churchill of the United Kingdom, and Generalissimo Chiang Kai-shek of the Republic of China.

Cairo Declaration

The conference produced the Cairo Declaration, published on 1 December 1943, which stated the Allies' determination to continue the war against Japan until its unconditional surrender. It declared that territories Japan had seized from China, including Manchuria, Formosa (Taiwan) and the Pescadores, would be restored to the Republic of China; that Japan would be expelled from all other territories taken by violence; and that Korea would become free and independent "in due course".

Context

The leaders also discussed military operations against Japan in Burma and China. Immediately afterwards Roosevelt and Churchill travelled to the Tehran Conference to meet Joseph Stalin, who did not attend Cairo because the Soviet Union was not then at war with Japan.

Legacy

The Cairo Declaration is frequently cited in later disputes over the status of Taiwan, and its terms were reaffirmed in the Potsdam Declaration of 1945 and incorporated into the settlement imposed on Japan at the end of the war.
