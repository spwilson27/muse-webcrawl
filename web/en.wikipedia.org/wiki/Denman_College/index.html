Denman College was a residential adult education college in Marcham, Oxfordshire, England, operated by the National Federation of Women's Institutes (NFWI). It was founded in 1948 as the WI's national college, offering short courses in cookery, crafts, and a wide range of other subjects to WI members and the general public.

Marcham Park, the Georgian house that housed Denman College.

History

The college was named after Lady Gertrude Denman, long-serving chairman of the NFWI, and was formally opened in September 1948 in Marcham Park, a Georgian house set in parkland. Over the following decades the college expanded with teaching kitchens, craft studios, and residential accommodation, welcoming tens of thousands of students on courses ranging from one day to a week.

Courses

Denman was especially known for its cookery school and craft courses — including textiles, sugarcraft, and traditional handicrafts — as well as courses in history, music, languages, and personal development. It served as a social and educational hub for the WI movement.

Closure

The NFWI announced the closure of Denman College in 2020, citing financial pressures worsened by the COVID-19 pandemic; the college closed in 2021 and the Marcham Park estate was subsequently sold, with WI learning activities continuing in other forms.
