Stars and bars (combinatorics)

Stars and bars is a graphical technique in combinatorics for counting the number of ways to distribute indistinguishable objects into distinguishable bins. Objects are drawn as stars, and bars separate the bins.

Theorems

The number of ways to put n indistinguishable balls into k labelled bins (allowing empty bins) is the binomial coefficient C(n + k − 1, k − 1). If each bin must contain at least one object, the count is C(n − 1, k − 1). Equivalently, these count the solutions in non-negative (or positive) integers to x1 + … + xk = n.

Applications

The method solves many elementary counting problems, from distributing identical candies among children to counting multisets, and appears in probability and the derivation of Bose–Einstein statistics.
