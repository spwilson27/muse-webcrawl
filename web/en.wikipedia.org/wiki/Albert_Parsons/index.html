Albert Richard Parsons (June 20, 1848 – November 11, 1887) was an American socialist and later anarchist activist, newspaper editor and orator. A former Confederate soldier who became a Radical Republican and civil rights advocate in Texas during Reconstruction, he moved to Chicago and became one of the most prominent figures of the American labour movement. He was convicted and executed in connection with the 1886 Haymarket affair, and is remembered as one of the Haymarket martyrs.

Early life

Parsons was born in Montgomery, Alabama, into a family with Revolutionary-era roots, and was orphaned young. Raised in Texas, he served as a teenager in Confederate forces during the Civil War, then afterwards embraced Radical Republican politics, editing a newspaper that championed the rights of freed slaves — a position that made him enemies in Waco and drove him north. In Chicago he worked as a printer and joined the socialist movement.

Activism in Chicago

Parsons became a leading voice of the International Working People's Association and editor of its English-language newspaper, The Alarm. He was a celebrated speaker for the eight-hour day movement and helped organise the great demonstrations of May 1886. His wife, Lucy Parsons, was herself a formidable organiser and writer.

Haymarket and execution

On 4 May 1886, a bomb was thrown at police dispersing a peaceful rally at Haymarket Square in Chicago, killing several officers. Parsons had spoken at the rally but had left with his family before the explosion. Though no evidence linked him to the bomb, he was arrested with seven co-defendants and convicted of conspiracy to murder in a trial widely condemned as unjust. He refused to seek clemency on terms that would admit guilt, and was hanged on 11 November 1887 with August Spies, Adolph Fischer and George Engel. In 1893, Governor John Peter Altgeld pardoned the surviving defendants and denounced the trial.

The Haymarket martyrs became central figures in the international labour movement, and the protests over their case helped establish 1 May as International Workers' Day.
