Raigad Fort is a hill fortress in the Raigad district of Maharashtra, India, built by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj as the capital of his newly founded Maratha kingdom. In June 1674 Shivaji was crowned Chhatrapati at Raigad in a formal coronation ceremony — one of the pivotal moments of Maratha and Indian history — and the fort remained the seat of Maratha administration until its fall.

History

The site was an older fortification known as Chandragiri. Shivaji seized the hill in 1656 and had the fort rebuilt and enlarged on a grand scale under the supervision of his engineers and builders. The coronation in 1674 was conducted according to Vedic and Shakta rites by the scholar Gaga Bhatt, with dignitaries, Brahmin councils, and foreign envoys in attendance. After Shivaji's death in 1680 the fort passed to his successors; it was sacked by the Mughals under Aurangzeb in 1689 after the capture of Sambhaji, and later fell to the British East India Company in 1818, after which many of its structures were deliberately demolished.

Today

Ruined but restored in part, Raigad is a place of pilgrimage and Maratha pride. A ropeway carries visitors to the summit, and the samadhi (memorial) of Shivaji within the fort complex is a major memorial site. The fort has been nominated, with other Maratha forts, for UNESCO World Heritage status.
