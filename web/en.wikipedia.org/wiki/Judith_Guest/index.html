Judith Guest (born 29 March 1936) is an American novelist and screenwriter, best known for her debut novel Ordinary People (1976), which Robert Redford adapted into his Oscar-winning directorial debut film of the same name in 1980.

Life and career

Guest was born in Detroit, Michigan, and studied at the University of Michigan, earning a degree in education. She worked as a schoolteacher and wrote on the side for years without publishing. Ordinary People — famously the first unsolicited manuscript accepted by Viking Press in decades — tells the story of the Jarretts, an affluent suburban family disintegrating after the elder son's death in a boating accident and the younger son Conrad's suicide attempt and therapy. The novel was praised for its precise depiction of grief and family silence, and the film version won the Academy Award for Best Picture, with Timothy Hutton winning Best Supporting Actor as Conrad.

Later works

Guest's subsequent novels include Second Heaven (1982), Killing Time in St. Cloud (1988, with Rebecca Hill), Errands (1997), The Tarnished Eye (2004) — a crime novel drawing on an unsolved Michigan murder case — and The Golden Hour. She also co-wrote the screenplay for the 1987 film Rachel River. She is the great-niece of the popular poet Edgar Guest.
