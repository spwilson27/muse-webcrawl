Uherské Hradiště (Czech pronunciation: [ˈuɦɛrskɛː ˈɦraɟɪʃcɛ]; German: Ungarisch Hradisch) is a town in the Zlín Region of the Czech Republic, in the historical region of Moravian Slovakia (Slovácko). It lies on the Morava River and has a population of about 25,000. Together with Staré Město and Kunovice it forms an agglomeration of roughly 38,000 inhabitants.

Masaryk Square in the historic centre of Uherské Hradiště.

History

The town was founded in 1257 by King Ottokar II of Bohemia as a fortified market town on an island in the Morava River, protecting the border with Hungary — hence the name, meaning "Hungarian fortified settlement". The Baroque Church of Saint Francis Xavier and the Jesuit complex dominate the main square. The town suffered floods several times, most seriously in 1997.

Culture

Uherské Hradiště is a centre of the Moravian Slovak folk region, known for its wine production, folk costumes, and cimbalom music. Since 1975 it has hosted the Summer Film School (Letní filmová škola), one of the largest film festivals in the Czech Republic. The Museum of Moravian Slovakia (Slovácké muzeum) presents the ethnography of the region.

Sport

The town's football club, 1. FC Slovácko, plays in the Czech First League and won the Czech Cup in 2022. Its stadium, Městský fotbalový stadion Miroslava Valenty, lies in the town.
