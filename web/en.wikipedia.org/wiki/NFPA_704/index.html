NFPA 704

NFPA 704: Standard System for the Identification of the Hazards of Materials for Emergency Response is a standard maintained by the US-based National Fire Protection Association (NFPA). Its familiar placard, colloquially called the fire diamond (or safety square), quickly communicates the health, flammability and reactivity hazards of a material to emergency personnel.

The diamond

The sign is a square set on a point, divided into four coloured quadrants:

  • Red (top) — flammability, rated 0 (will not burn) to 4 (burns readily at normal temperatures).
  • Blue (left) — health hazard, rated 0 (no hazard beyond ordinary combustibles) to 4 (short exposure may cause death).
  • Yellow (right) — reactivity/instability, rated 0 (stable) to 4 (may detonate).
  • White (bottom) — special hazards, using codes such as OX (oxidiser), W (reacts with water — depicted with a strikethrough W), or SA (simple asphyxiant gas).

Use

NFPA 704 markings appear on buildings, storage tanks, transport containers and laboratory facilities throughout the United States. The system is intended primarily for emergency responders during incidents such as fires or spills, and is distinct from the workplace labelling of the Globally Harmonized System (GHS) used by OSHA. The standard was first adopted in 1960 and is periodically revised.
