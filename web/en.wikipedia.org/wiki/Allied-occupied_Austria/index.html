Allied-occupied Austria

Allied-occupied Austria refers to the period from 27 April 1945 to 27 July 1955, during which Austria was divided into four occupation zones and jointly occupied by the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, the United States, and France after the end of World War II. Like Berlin, the capital Vienna was subdivided into four zones, with a shared central district.

Background

Austria had been annexed by Nazi Germany in the Anschluss of 1938. The 1943 Moscow Declaration of the Allies described Austria as "the first free country to fall a victim to Hitlerite aggression" and promised the restoration of an independent Austria, while also holding it responsible for participation in the war. Soviet troops took Vienna in April 1945, and a provisional Austrian government under Karl Renner proclaimed independence on 27 April 1945.

The occupation

Unlike Germany, Austria was permitted a single elected government, recognised by all four powers after the November 1945 elections. The Allied Commission nevertheless supervised Austrian affairs, and the country bore occupation costs and Soviet extraction of assets in its eastern zone. Austria's status was a recurring issue in early Cold War diplomacy.

State Treaty and neutrality

On 15 May 1955 the four occupying powers and Austria signed the Austrian State Treaty, restoring full sovereignty. The last occupation troops withdrew by 25 October 1955, and on 26 October Austria declared permanent neutrality, a condition informally understood as the price of Soviet agreement. Austria then joined the United Nations in December 1955.
