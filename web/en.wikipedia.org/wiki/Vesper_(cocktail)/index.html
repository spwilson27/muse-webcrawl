Vesper (cocktail)

A Vesper, garnished with lemon peel.

TypeCocktail
Base spiritGin
ServedStraight up, in a cocktail glass
GarnishLemon peel
Ingredients60 ml gin, 15 ml vodka, 7.5 ml Lillet Blanc

The Vesper is a cocktail that was originally made of gin, vodka, and Kina Lillet. The drink was invented and named by Ian Fleming in the 1953 James Bond novel Casino Royale. In the novel, Bond orders "three measures of Gordon's, one of vodka, half a measure of Kina Lillet. Shake it very well until it's ice-cold, then add a large thin slice of lemon peel." He names the drink after the double agent Vesper Lynd.

Variations

Because Kina Lillet was reformulated in 1986 (and later discontinued in its original form), modern versions substitute Lillet Blanc, sometimes with a dash of aromatic bitters to approximate the original quinine bitterness, or Cocchi Americano, which is considered a closer match. The International Bartenders Association lists the Vesper among its official cocktails.

In popular culture

The drink's fame was renewed by the 2006 film adaptation of Casino Royale, in which Daniel Craig's Bond recites the recipe. The order "shaken, not stirred" associated with Bond's martinis is frequently attached to the Vesper, although the drink's defining feature in the novel is its unusual combination of gin and vodka.
