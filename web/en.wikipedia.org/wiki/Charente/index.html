Charente

Charente (French pronunciation: [ʃaʁɑ̃t]; Occitan: Charanta) is a department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of southwestern France. It is named after the Charente River, which flows westward through the department from the Massif Central to the Atlantic near Rochefort. The prefecture is Angoulême, and the department is best known internationally for the town of Cognac, which gives its name to the famous brandy produced in the surrounding vineyards.

History

Charente was one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, formed largely from the former province of Angoumois together with parts of Saintonge and Poitou. The region had been contested between France and England during the Hundred Years' War and was the birthplace of King Francis I, born at Cognac in 1494, under whom Angoulême rose to prominence.

Geography and economy

The department is mostly low-lying and rural, with rolling limestone hills in the north and east. Agriculture dominates, especially viticulture: the vineyards around Cognac and Jarnac produce the grapes distilled into cognac, one of France's major exports. Angoulême is a centre of paper-making and, in recent decades, of comics and animated film; it hosts the annual Angoulême International Comics Festival, one of the largest in Europe.

Main towns

Besides Angoulême and Cognac, the principal communes include Soyaux, La Couronne, Ruelle-sur-Touvre, Saint-Yrieix-sur-Charente, Gond-Pontouvre and Barbezieux-Saint-Hilaire.
