John F. Canny (born 1953) is an Australian computer scientist and the Paul and Stacy Yan Director of the Foundations of Data Analysis program and a professor of computer science at the University of California, Berkeley. He is best known for inventing the Canny edge detector, one of the most widely used algorithms in computer vision.

Canny developed the edge detector in 1986 while at MIT, formulating edge detection as an optimisation problem with criteria of good detection, good localisation and minimal response. The resulting multi-stage algorithm — smoothing, gradient computation, non-maximum suppression, double thresholding and hysteresis — remains a standard technique.

Research

His later research covers human–computer interaction, computing for education and health, and machine learning. He developed the concept of "livelihood" computing and has worked on systems for community health workers and large-scale collaborative learning.
