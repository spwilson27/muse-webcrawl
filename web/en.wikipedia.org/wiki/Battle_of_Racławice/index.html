The Battle of Racławice was fought on 4 April 1794, during the Kościuszko Uprising, between Polish forces under Tadeusz Kościuszko and a Russian detachment commanded by General Alexander Tormasov. Though modest in scale, it became one of the most celebrated battles in Polish national memory, above all for the charge of the peasant scythemen.

Background

After the Second Partition of Poland, Kościuszko proclaimed a national uprising at Kraków on 24 March 1794. Marching north to join other Polish forces, his small army of regulars, volunteers and some 2,000 peasants armed with war scythes (kosynierzy) encountered Tormasov's Russian detachment near the village of Racławice.

The battle

Kościuszko positioned his troops on high ground and, after an artillery exchange, led a bayonet attack supported by the scythemen. The peasant Wojciech Bartos, later ennobled as Wojciech Głowacki, famously seized a Russian cannon, extinguishing its fuse with his cap. The Russian infantry, pressed by regulars and scythemen, broke and withdrew, leaving their guns behind. Polish losses were light compared with the Russians'.

Aftermath

Racławice was a tactical rather than strategic triumph — Kościuszko could not exploit it, and the uprising was crushed by the end of 1794 — but its symbolic impact was immense. It proclaimed the peasantry's stake in the national cause; Kościuszko rewarded the scythemen and afterwards wore a peasant's sukmana coat in their honour. The battle is commemorated by the enormous Racławice Panorama in Wrocław, painted for its centenary.
