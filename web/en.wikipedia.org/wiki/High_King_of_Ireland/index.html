High King of Ireland

The High King of Ireland (Irish: Ardrí na hÉireann) was a historical title of grandiosity in medieval and early modern Ireland, claimed by the kings of Tara as overlords of the island. The historicity and real power of early holders is debated; later medieval high kings exercised varying degrees of authority over provincial kings.

High King of Ireland
Seat: Hill of Tara
First holder (legendary): Sláine mac Dela
Last holder: Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair (1166–1198)
Tradition: Kingship of Tara

Origins

Irish annals and sagas project the title back to mythical Fir Bolg kings and to Conn of the Hundred Battles and Niall of the Nine Hostages. Scholars note the concept of island-wide kingship grew in the 7th–8th centuries, promoted by the Uí Néill dynasty and Armagh churchmen.

Uí Néill dominance and alternation

From the 7th century the northern and southern Uí Néill alternated the kingship of Tara. Máel Sechnaill mac Maele Ruanaid (846–862) is often described as the first historical king to gain submission across Ireland, followed by Flann Sinna.

Brian Boru and later kings

Brian Boru of Munster broke the Uí Néill monopoly, becoming high king (1002–1014) before his death at Clontarf. Thereafter Diarmait mac Maíl na mBó, Toirdelbach Ua Briain, Muirchertach Ua Briain, Domnall Ua Lochlainn, Toirdelbach Ua Conchobair and his son Ruaidrí — the last high king — were described as "kings with opposition" (rígh Éireann co frithres).

End

The Norman invasion of 1169–71 ended Ruaidrí's authority; the English crown later created the Kingdom of Ireland (1542) under Henry VIII.
