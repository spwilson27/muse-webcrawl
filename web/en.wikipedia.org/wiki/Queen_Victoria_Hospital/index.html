The Queen Victoria Hospital (QVH), in East Grinstead, West Sussex, England, is a specialist National Health Service hospital renowned for its work in plastic surgery, reconstructive surgery, burns care, and maxillofacial surgery. It is managed by the Queen Victoria Hospital NHS Foundation Trust.

Queen Victoria Hospital, East Grinstead, famous for pioneering reconstructive surgery.

History

The hospital was founded in 1863 as the East Grinstead Cottage Hospital and was renamed in honour of Queen Victoria. During the Second World War, the pioneering plastic surgeon Sir Archibald McIndoe established a centre for the treatment of burned airmen at the hospital. His patients famously formed the Guinea Pig Club in 1941, a social and support group of Allied aircrew who had undergone experimental reconstructive procedures. McIndoe's holistic approach combined innovative surgery with rehabilitation and psychological support.

Services

Today QVH is a regional centre of excellence for reconstructive surgery, including breast reconstruction, hand surgery, head and neck surgery, and treatment of skin cancer, as well as providing a burns unit, sleep disorder services, and community diagnostic services.
