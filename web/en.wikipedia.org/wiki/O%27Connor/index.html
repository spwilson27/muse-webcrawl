O'Connor

O'Connor is an Irish surname derived from the Gaelic Ó Conchobhair, meaning "descendant of Conchobar" (a personal name often interpreted as "lover of canines" or "desire of the hound"). The name was borne by several distinct septs in Ireland, most famously the O'Connors of Connacht, who supplied the last High Kings of Ireland, Turlough O'Connor and his son Rory O'Connor (Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair, reigned 1166–1198).

History

The O'Connors of Connacht traced their descent from Conchobar, son of Tadhg, in the 10th century, and rose to dominate the province from their base at Clonalis near Castlerea, County Roscommon. Other O'Connor septs held territory in Kerry (O'Connor Kerry), Offaly, and Clare. Following the Anglo-Norman invasion and the later confiscations, many family members emigrated, and the surname became common throughout the English-speaking world.

Notable people

Notable bearers include Sandra Day O'Connor (1930–2023), the first woman to serve on the Supreme Court of the United States; Flannery O'Connor (1925–1964), the American novelist and short-story writer; Sinéad O'Connor (1966–2023), the Irish singer; and Frank O'Connor (1903–1966), the Irish writer. The name also attaches to places, including the Canberra suburb of O'Connor in Australia.
