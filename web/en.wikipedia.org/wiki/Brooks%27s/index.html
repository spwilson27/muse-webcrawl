Brooks's
Type: possessive form / surname usage
Style guides: AP, Chicago, MLA differ

Brooks's is the possessive form of the surname Brooks as recommended by several style conventions in which singular nouns ending in s take an additional apostrophe and s to form the possessive — for example, "Brooks's theorem" or "the Brooks's house style". Other guides prescribe the apostrophe alone, giving Brooks'.

Usage

The Associated Press Stylebook recommends just an apostrophe for proper names ending in s (Brooks'), while The Chicago Manual of Style and the MLA generally recommend adding 's (Brooks's) for singular possessives, including most names. The pronunciation of Brooks's normally adds an extra syllable, which guides favouring the written form that matches speech take as justification for the additional s.

Examples

The possessive appears in proper names such as Brooks's, a historic London gentlemen's club founded in 1764 (whose name is conventionally written with the apostrophe-s), and in references to works such as Brooks's theorem in graph theory.
