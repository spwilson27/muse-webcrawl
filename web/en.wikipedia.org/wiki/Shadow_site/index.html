Shadow site

A shadow site is an archaeological site whose features are visible from the air only as faint "shadows" cast by low sunlight across slight earthworks, or, in a related sense, a site revealed indirectly through cropmarks and soil marks rather than by standing remains.

Aerial archaeology

In aerial archaeology, low sun in the early morning or evening can throw banks, ditches and other low-relief earthworks into relief, producing shadow-marks that reveal features invisible at midday. Surveyors time flights for these conditions to record sites that have been levelled by ploughing but retain slight topographic variation.

Related terms

Shadow sites are distinguished from cropmark sites, where buried features affect crop growth, and soil-mark sites, where differences in soil colour show in ploughed fields. Together these are the classic categories of indirect archaeological evidence identified from the air.
