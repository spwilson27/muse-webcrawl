The Isaiah Scroll, designated 1QIsaa and sometimes called the Great Isaiah Scroll, is one of the seven original Dead Sea Scrolls discovered in Cave 1 at Qumran in 1946–47. Written on parchment in the Herodian square script of Hebrew, it contains almost the entire Book of Isaiah — all 66 chapters — and is the oldest complete manuscript of a biblical book known, predating the medieval Masoretic codices by roughly a thousand years.

Discovery

Bedouin shepherds found the scroll, together with six others, in jars in what became known as Qumran Cave 1. The scrolls passed through dealers in Bethlehem to Metropolitan Mar Athanasius Yeshue Samuel of the Syrian Orthodox Church, who took them to the United States; they were authenticated by scholars including William F. Albright and photographed by John C. Trever. Israel acquired the scroll in 1954 through an intermediary after Samuel advertised it for sale in the Wall Street Journal.

Description

The scroll is made of 17 sheets of parchment sewn together, forming a roll 7.34 metres long and about 26 centimetres high, containing 54 columns of text. Apart from small areas of damage — notably in chapters 1–2 — the text is complete. A second, more fragmentary Isaiah scroll (1QIsab) was found in the same cave.

Textual significance

The Great Isaiah Scroll provides the most important evidence for the text of Isaiah before the standardisation of the Hebrew Bible. It is generally close to the Masoretic Text but contains numerous spelling differences, grammatical variants and some significant variant readings; it lacks the standardised plene/defective spelling of later manuscripts and shows scribal corrections made both by the original scribe and later hands. Its closeness to the Masoretic tradition, despite its antiquity, was a landmark demonstration of the care with which the biblical text had been transmitted.

Present location

The scroll is kept at the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum in Jerusalem, where a facsimile is displayed wrapped around the central pillar; the original is stored under controlled conditions and shown only occasionally. A high-resolution digital image is available through the museum's Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library.
