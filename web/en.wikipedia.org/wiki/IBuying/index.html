IBuying

iBuying (instant buying) is a real-estate business model in which companies use pricing algorithms to make quick cash offers on homes, purchase them directly from sellers, carry out light repairs and resell them, charging a service fee for the speed and certainty of the sale. The model emerged in the United States in the mid-2010s.

History and companies

Opendoor, founded in 2014, pioneered the model, followed by services such as Zillow Offers, RedfinNow, Offerpad and brokerage-run programmes. iBuyers grew during the late-2010s housing boom but remained a small share of total transactions. In November 2021 Zillow shut down Zillow Offers after its algorithms overpaid for homes during a volatile market, taking a writedown of over US$500 million and cutting about a quarter of its workforce; Redfin closed RedfinNow in 2022. Opendoor and Offerpad continued operating.

Economics and criticism

iBuyers profit from service fees, resale margins and ancillary services, but carry inventory risk: valuation errors or falling prices quickly produce losses. Critics argue fees can exceed traditional agent commissions and that algorithmic buying may affect local prices, while proponents emphasise convenience and transaction certainty for sellers.
