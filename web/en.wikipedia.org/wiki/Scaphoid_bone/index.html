Scaphoid bone

Scaphoid bone
Part ofCarpus (wrist)
ArticulationsRadius, lunate, trapezium, trapezoid, capitate
NamesakeGreek skaphe, "boat"

The scaphoid bone is one of the carpal bones of the wrist. It is situated between the hand and forearm on the thumb side of the wrist, where it bridges the proximal and distal rows of carpal bones. It takes its name from its boat-like shape (Greek skaphe, a boat).

Structure

The scaphoid articulates with five bones: the radius proximally, the lunate medially, and the trapezium, trapezoid, and capitate distally. Its blood supply enters mainly at the distal pole and flows retrograde, making the proximal pole vulnerable to avascular necrosis after fracture.

Clinical significance

The scaphoid is the most commonly fractured carpal bone, typically after a fall onto an outstretched hand. Pain and tenderness in the anatomical snuffbox are characteristic. Because plain radiographs may initially appear normal, suspected fractures are often immobilised and re-imaged, or assessed with MRI. Complications include nonunion and avascular necrosis of the proximal fragment, which can lead to wrist arthritis.
