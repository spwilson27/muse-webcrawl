Changi Beach massacre

Changi Beach, site of the 1942 massacre

The Changi Beach massacre took place on 20 February 1942, during the Japanese occupation of Singapore in the Second World War. Sixty-six Chinese civilian men were shot and killed by Japanese Hojo Kempei (auxiliary military police) firing squads at the water's edge on Changi Beach.

Background

Following the fall of Singapore on 15 February 1942, the Japanese military began the Sook Ching operation, a purge of Chinese Singaporeans suspected of anti-Japanese sympathies. Suspects were screened at various centres, and those deemed hostile were transported to remote beaches and executed.

The massacre

On 20 February, a group of detainees who had been held at the former Changi military area were taken to the beach at Changi. They were lined up along the shoreline and shot; their bodies were left to be washed out to sea. The Changi Beach killings formed part of a series of execution sites around the island, including Punggol Beach and Tanah Merah Beach.

Commemoration

A memorial plaque at Changi Beach Park commemorates the victims. The events of Sook Ching remain a painful chapter in Singapore's history and are taught as part of the nation's account of the occupation years.
