Wodonga

Wodonga is a city in north-eastern Victoria, Australia, situated on the southern bank of the Murray River, opposite the New South Wales city of Albury, with which it forms the twin-city urban area of Albury–Wodonga. It is the seat of the City of Wodonga local government area and had a population of roughly 38,000 at the 2021 census (the combined Albury–Wodonga population exceeding 95,000).

History

The area was inhabited by the Wiradjuri and related Aboriginal peoples before European settlement. A customs post was established at the Murray crossing in the 1850s, and Wodonga was proclaimed a town in the following decades, growing as a rail and road junction. The name is derived from an Aboriginal word said to refer to an edible nut or bulrush found in nearby lagoons. Wodonga was declared a rural city in 1973 and a city in later years as part of Victoria's regional growth-centre policy, which also brought the establishment of a campus of La Trobe University.

Geography and economy

Wodonga lies on the Hume Highway between Melbourne and Sydney, about 300 km north-east of Melbourne. Lake Hume lies upstream to the east. The city's economy is based on manufacturing, logistics, defence (the nearby Bandiana military area), education, and services for the surrounding agricultural region. Junction Place and the former railway station precinct have been redeveloped as a civic and commercial centre.
