Amadís de Gaula

Author: Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo (redaction)
Language: Spanish
Genre: Chivalric romance
Published: 1508 (earliest surviving edition)

Amadís de Gaula (Spanish for "Amadis of Gaul") is a landmark work of Iberian literature, a chivalric romance recounting the adventures of the knight Amadís, his love for the princess Oriana, and his wars against magicians, giants and rival kings. The earliest surviving edition was printed in Zaragoza in 1508, prepared by Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo, who reworked older material — versions of the story appear to have circulated since the fourteenth century — and added a fourth book and the sequel Las sergas de Esplandián.

Plot

Amadís, secret son of King Perión of Gaul and Elisena, is set adrift at birth and raised in Scotland as the "Child of the Sea". As a knight he serves and loves Oriana, heiress of Lisuarte, king of Great Britain. The romance follows his feats against the enchanter Arcaláus, his defence of Oriana's rights, a period of penitent exile on the Poor Rock under the name Beltenebros, and eventual marriage and triumph. Montalvo's sequel introduced Amadís's son Esplandián and named a mythical island "California", the origin of the American state's name.

Influence

Amadís was the most popular of the Spanish romances of chivalry, spawning sequels and translations across Europe and defining the genre. It is famously the favourite reading of Don Quixote: in Cervantes's novel, the scrutiny of Quixote's library spares Amadís from the bonfire as the best of its kind. Its idealised chivalry, courtly love and marvellous adventures shaped Renaissance prose fiction throughout Europe.
