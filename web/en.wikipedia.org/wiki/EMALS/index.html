EMALS

The Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System (EMALS) is a type of aircraft launching system developed by General Atomics for the United States Navy. The system launches carrier-based aircraft by means of a catapult employing a linear induction motor rather than the conventional steam piston. EMALS was first installed on the lead ship of the Gerald R. Ford-class aircraft carriers, USS Gerald R. Ford (CVN-78).

Advantages

Compared with steam catapults, EMALS weighs less, occupies less space, requires fewer personnel, and can be more precisely controlled: the launch profile can be tailored to each aircraft type, reducing stress on airframes and enabling the launch of both heavier aircraft and lighter vehicles such as unmanned aerial vehicles that steam catapults cannot launch smoothly. It also recharges more quickly and is independent of the ship's steam plant.

Development and testing

Land-based testing took place at Naval Air Engineering Station Lakehurst in New Jersey, where the first aircraft launch — an F/A-18E Super Hornet — occurred in December 2010. The first shipboard launches from Gerald R. Ford took place in July 2017. Development was marked by cost growth and reliability concerns, and the system was publicly criticized, though the Navy continued with it for the Ford class and it has been selected for other navies' carriers, including France's future PANG carrier.

Related systems

EMALS is paired on the Ford class with the Advanced Arresting Gear (AAG), also built by General Atomics. China's Type 003 aircraft carrier Fujian uses a related electromagnetic launch system of domestic design.
