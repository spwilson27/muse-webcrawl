Windsock

A windsock (also wind cone or wind sleeve) is a conical textile tube, usually orange or striped orange and white, mounted on a pole at airports, farms, and industrial sites to indicate the direction and approximate speed of the wind. The sock extends horizontally downwind, and the amount of droop or the angle it makes gives a rough indication of wind speed.

Use and standards

Under standards used by the FAA and ICAO, a standard windsock is designed to orient itself to the wind at speeds as low as 3 knots and to be fully extended at 15 knots (28 km/h) or more. Alternating orange and white stripes aid visibility; the first stripe's droop can be used as a rough gauge — each stripe corresponds to roughly 3 knots of wind speed. Windsocks are a standard fixture at small airfields, helipads, and chemical plants, where they help workers judge wind direction in the event of a hazardous gas leak.

History

Simple wind indicators date to antiquity; the Roman vexillum-like standards and medieval weather vanes served related purposes. The modern fabric cone was standardized with the growth of aviation in the twentieth century. In Japan, carp-shaped windsocks (koinobori) are flown for Children's Day, a decorative relative of the aeronautical device.
