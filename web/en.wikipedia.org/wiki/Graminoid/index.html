Graminoid

Graminoids in grassland habitat

In botany and ecology, graminoids are herbaceous plants with a grass-like morphology, i.e. elongated culms with long, blade-like leaves. They are contrasted with forbs—herbaceous flowering plants that are not graminoids—and with woody plants.

Families

The plants most often referred to as graminoids belong to the families Poaceae (true grasses), Cyperaceae (sedges), and Juncaceae (rushes). These three families are not closely related phylogenetically but belong to different clades in the order Poales; their similar growth forms represent convergent evolution in adaptation to open, often grazed or fire-prone habitats.

Distinguishing features

Grasses, sedges and rushes can be told apart by their stems and leaves: sedges typically have triangular, solid stems ("sedges have edges"), rushes usually have round, solid or pith-filled stems ("rushes are round"), and grasses have round, usually hollow stems with distinct nodes ("grasses have joints"). Graminoids dominate many terrestrial biomes, including grasslands, savannas, steppes and salt marshes, and provide forage for grazing animals as well as staple cereal crops for humans.
