Nuclear submarine

A nuclear submarine is a submarine powered by a nuclear reactor. The performance advantages of nuclear submarines over conventional (diesel-electric) submarines are considerable: nuclear propulsion, being completely independent of air, frees the submarine from the need to surface frequently, and the large amount of power generated allows nuclear submarines to operate at high speed for long durations, with endurance limited chiefly by crew needs such as food.

History

The first nuclear-powered submarine, USS Nautilus (SSN-571), put to sea in 1955 and in 1958 completed the first submerged transit of the North Pole. The Soviet Union followed with the November-class boats from 1958. In 1960 USS Triton completed the first submerged circumnavigation of the Earth. Ballistic missile submarines (SSBNs) — beginning with USS George Washington in 1959 — became a central, survivable leg of nuclear deterrent forces.

Types

  • SSN — nuclear-powered attack submarines for anti-submarine and anti-ship warfare, intelligence and land attack with cruise missiles.
  • SSBN — ballistic missile submarines carrying submarine-launched ballistic missiles with nuclear warheads.
  • SSGN — guided-missile submarines converted or built to carry large numbers of cruise missiles.

Operators

Six navies operate nuclear submarines: the United States, Russia, the United Kingdom, France, China and India. The United States Navy operates an all-nuclear submarine force. Under the AUKUS agreement, Australia is set to acquire nuclear-powered (but conventionally armed) submarines with American and British assistance. Reactors use enriched uranium fuel and are refuelled rarely — some modern cores are designed to last the life of the boat.

Accidents

Notable losses include the US boats Thresher (1963) and Scorpion (1968) and the Russian Kursk (2000); several Soviet/Russian reactors and boats lie on the seabed, a long-standing environmental concern.
