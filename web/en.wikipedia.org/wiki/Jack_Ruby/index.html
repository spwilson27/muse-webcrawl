Jack Leon Ruby (born Jacob Leon Rubenstein; April 25, 1911 – January 3, 1967) was an American nightclub owner who murdered Lee Harvey Oswald on November 24, 1963, two days after Oswald assassinated President John F. Kennedy. The shooting took place in the basement of the Dallas Police Headquarters as Oswald was being transferred to the county jail, and was broadcast live on national television.

Early life

Ruby was born in Chicago to Polish Jewish immigrant parents and grew up amid poverty and family instability, spending part of his childhood in foster homes and juvenile institutions. As a young man he ran errands for Al Capone's organization and worked in scrap and novelty businesses. He served in the U.S. Army Air Forces during the Second World War. After the war he moved to Dallas, where he managed nightclubs and strip clubs, including the Carousel Club, and cultivated relationships with Dallas police officers, many of whom frequented his establishments.

Murder of Oswald

On the morning of November 24, 1963, Ruby drove downtown, wired money to one of his dancers at a Western Union office near police headquarters, and then walked into the basement garage through an entrance ramp. As Oswald was led out at 11:21 a.m., Ruby stepped forward and fired a single shot from a .38-calibre revolver into Oswald's abdomen. Oswald died at Parkland Hospital shortly afterward. Ruby told police he acted to spare Jacqueline Kennedy the ordeal of returning to Dallas for a trial, a claim he repeated in various forms thereafter.

Trial and death

Ruby was convicted of murder with malice in March 1964 and sentenced to death. The Texas Court of Criminal Appeals overturned the conviction in 1966, ruling that he could not have received a fair trial in Dallas and that an oral confession should have been excluded. He died of a pulmonary embolism, complicated by lung cancer, at Parkland Hospital on January 3, 1967, before a new trial could be held. Theories about Ruby's possible connections to organized crime and his motives have remained a persistent feature of assassination literature; the Warren Commission concluded Ruby acted alone, while the House Select Committee on Assassinations later noted his extensive underworld contacts.
