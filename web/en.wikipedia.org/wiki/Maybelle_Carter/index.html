Maybelle Carter

"Mother" Maybelle Carter (born Maybelle Addington; May 10, 1909 – October 23, 1978) was an American country musician and "among the first" women to be recognized as an instrumentalist in country music. She is best known as a member of the historic Carter Family act in the 1920s and 1930s and also as a member of Mother Maybelle and the Carter Sisters, a group consisting of her and her daughters.

Biography

Maybelle Addington was born in Nickelsville, Virginia, on May 10, 1909. In 1926 she married Ezra Carter, the brother of A. P. Carter. In 1927 she joined the Carter Family, one of the most influential groups in recorded country music history, alongside A. P. and her sister-in-law Sara Carter. The group's first recordings were made at the famous Bristol Sessions in August 1927.

Maybelle created an innovative style of guitar playing, known as the "Carter scratch" or Carter Family picking, in which she played melody lines on the bass strings while strumming rhythm on the higher strings, effectively making the guitar a lead instrument. She also played the autoharp and banjo.

The Carter Sisters

After the original Carter Family act disbanded in the early 1940s, Maybelle formed a new act with her daughters Helen, June (later June Carter Cash), and Anita, billed as Mother Maybelle and the Carter Sisters. They performed on radio and in 1950 became members of the Grand Ole Opry. Maybelle also backed Johnny Cash, June's husband, on recordings and tours.

Death and legacy

Carter died on October 23, 1978, in Nashville, Tennessee. She was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame as part of the Carter Family in 1970. Her guitar technique has influenced generations of folk, country, and rock musicians, and she is often called the "Mother of Country Music".
