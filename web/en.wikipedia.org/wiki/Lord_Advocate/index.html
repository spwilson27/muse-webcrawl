The Lord Advocate, also known as His Majesty's Advocate, is the principal legal adviser of the Scottish Government and the chief public prosecutor for Scotland. The office dates to the late fifteenth century (the first recorded King's Advocate, John Ross, was appointed in 1483). The Lord Advocate heads the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service and is responsible, with the Solicitor General for Scotland, for all prosecutions in Scotland, acting independently of ministers in that function.

Functions

As the Scottish Government's senior law officer, the Lord Advocate advises ministers on Scots law, represents the government in court, and has statutory responsibility for the investigation of sudden deaths and fatal accidents. Under the Scotland Act 1998 the Lord Advocate is a member of the Scottish Government, although by convention does not normally attend Cabinet. A distinctive feature of the role is the requirement that acts of the Lord Advocate (or Scottish Parliament legislation) incompatible with the European Convention on Human Rights are ultra vires.

History

Before devolution in 1999 the Lord Advocate was a United Kingdom government minister, typically sitting in Parliament and effectively Scotland's chief legal and prosecuting authority under the Secretary of State's department. The Scotland Act transferred the office to the devolved administration, while certain reserved functions pass through the Advocate General for Scotland, the UK Government's Scots law adviser, created at the same time.

Recent officeholders

Recent Lords Advocate include Colin Boyd (2000–2006), Elish Angiolini (2006–2011), Frank Mulholland (2011–2016), James Wolffe (2016–2021) and Dorothy Bain (from 2021). The dual nature of the office — government legal adviser and independent prosecutor — has periodically prompted debate about reform, including proposals to separate the two functions.
