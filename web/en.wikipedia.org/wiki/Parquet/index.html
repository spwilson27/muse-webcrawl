Parquet

TypeWood flooring
Origin17th-century France
PatternsHerringbone, chevron, Versailles, basket weave
MaterialsOak, walnut, mahogany and other hardwoods

Parquet (French for "small park" or compartment) is a geometric mosaic of wood pieces used for decorative flooring. Parquet patterns are often entirely geometrical and angular — squares, triangles and lozenges — or feature curved and natural shapes.

History

Parquet de Versailles, large diagonal squares, was introduced in 1684 at the Palace of Versailles to replace marble floors that required constant washing. Parquet became a hallmark of grand French interiors and spread through European aristocratic homes, then to middle-class housing in the 19th and 20th centuries as production industrialised.

Patterns and types

Common patterns include herringbone, chevron, brick-like and basket weave. Traditional parquet consists of solid wood blocks glued to a subfloor and sanded and finished in place. Modern parquet may be supplied as tiles or engineered boards with a hardwood wear layer, and the term is also used for parquet courts in basketball — famously the Boston Garden's alternating-panel floor.

Care

Parquet floors are durable and can be re-sanded multiple times, but are sensitive to excess moisture. Finishes include lacquer, oil and wax.
