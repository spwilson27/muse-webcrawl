Fleet admiral (United States)

Fleet admiral (abbreviated FADM) is a five-star flag officer rank in the United States Navy whose rewards uniquely include active duty pay for life. The rank of fleet admiral was created by an Act of Congress for World War II and is the highest rank in the United States Navy.

Holders

Only four officers have held the rank: William D. Leahy, Ernest J. King, and Chester W. Nimitz, all promoted in December 1944, and William F. Halsey Jr., promoted in December 1945. Leahy, as Chief of Staff to the Commander in Chief, was given precedence over the others.

Insignia

The sleeve insignia consists of five stripes, one wide and four narrow, and the collar insignia of five silver stars in a circle. No appointments have been made since 1945, and the rank is effectively dormant, though it remains part of the Navy's rank structure.
