The Harz Narrow Gauge Railways (German: Harzer Schmalspurbahnen, HSB) is a railway company that operates a network of metre-gauge lines in the Harz mountains of central Germany, in the states of Saxony-Anhalt and Thuringia. With around 140 kilometres of track, it is one of the largest narrow-gauge railway networks in Germany.

Network

The network comprises three interconnected lines: the Harzquerbahn running across the Harz from Wernigerode to Nordhausen; the Brockenbahn, a branch climbing from Drei Annen Hohne to the summit of the Brocken, the highest peak in northern Germany at 1,141 metres; and the Selketalbahn through the Selke valley to Quedlinburg.

Operations

The railways are famous for their extensive use of steam locomotives in daily scheduled service, employing a fleet of historic tank engines, many built in the 1950s. The Brocken line was closed to passenger traffic during the division of Germany, when the summit lay in the restricted border zone; it reopened after reunification in 1991. The HSB is a major tourist attraction in the region while also providing local transport.
