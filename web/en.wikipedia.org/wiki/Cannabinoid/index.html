Cannabinoid

Cannabinoids are several structural classes of compounds found primarily in the cannabis plant or as synthetic compounds. The most notable cannabinoid is the phytocannabinoid tetrahydrocannabinol (THC, Δ9-THC), the primary psychoactive compound in cannabis. Cannabidiol (CBD) is a major constituent of temperate Cannabis plants and a minor constituent in tropical varieties. At least 113 distinct cannabinoids have been isolated from cannabis, exhibiting varied effects.

Classes

Phytocannabinoids are produced by the cannabis plant, usually as carboxylic acids that decarboxylate upon heating. Endocannabinoids, such as anandamide and 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG), are produced naturally by animals, including humans, and act as neurotransmitters in the endocannabinoid system. Synthetic cannabinoids — including pharmaceutical preparations like dronabinol and nabilone, as well as illicit "Spice"-type compounds — are manufactured in laboratories.

Pharmacology

Most cannabinoids act principally on the cannabinoid receptors CB1, dense in the brain and central nervous system, and CB2, expressed mainly in immune tissues. THC is a partial agonist at both receptors and produces euphoria, altered perception, appetite stimulation, and analgesia. CBD has low affinity for these receptors but modulates other targets and is non-intoxicating; it is approved as the drug Epidiolex for certain severe epilepsies. Minor cannabinoids under study include cannabinol (CBN), cannabigerol (CBG), and cannabichromene (CBC).

Medical use and safety

Cannabinoid medicines are used for chemotherapy-induced nausea, spasticity in multiple sclerosis (nabiximols), and some seizure disorders. Adverse effects of THC-rich products include impaired short-term memory and coordination, anxiety, and, with heavy use, dependence and increased risk of psychosis in susceptible individuals. Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists sold illicitly can be far more potent and dangerous than THC.
