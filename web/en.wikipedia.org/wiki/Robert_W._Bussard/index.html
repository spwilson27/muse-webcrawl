Robert William Bussard (August 11, 1928 – October 6, 2007) was an American physicist who worked on nuclear fusion power research. He earned his PhD from Princeton University, worked at Los Alamos National Laboratory and in the US nuclear rocket programme, and co-founded research companies pursuing controlled fusion.

Bussard ramjet

In 1960 Bussard proposed the interstellar ramjet that bears his name: a spacecraft that would scoop interstellar hydrogen with a vast magnetic funnel and fuse it for propulsion. The concept became a staple of both engineering literature and science fiction.

Polywell

From the 1980s until his death, with funding largely from the US Navy, Bussard developed the Polywell, an inertial-electrostatic-confinement fusion device descended from the Farnsworth fusor, arguing it could be scaled to practical net-power fusion, potentially with advanced fuels. He publicised the work in a widely viewed 2006 talk and the designs were continued after his death by EMC2 Fusion.
