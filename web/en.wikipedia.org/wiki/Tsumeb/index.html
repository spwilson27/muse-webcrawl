Tsumeb
Country: Namibia
Region: Oshikoto Region
Elevation: approx. 1,310 m

Tsumeb (Otjiherero: Okavisume) is a city in the Oshikoto Region of northern Namibia, with a population of roughly 20,000. It is known as the "gateway to the north" of Namibia and is famous for its copper mine, one of the most mineralogically rich deposits in the world.

Mining

The Tsumeb ore body, a rare copper-lead-zinc-silver deposit hosted in a dolomite pipe, was mined from 1905 until 1996. It produced an extraordinary diversity of mineral specimens; more than 300 distinct mineral species have been recorded from the mine, dozens of them first described there. The town grew up around the operations of the Otavi Minen und Eisenbahn Gesellschaft during the German colonial period.

Geography and transport

Tsumeb lies near the eastern edge of Etosha National Park, roughly 60 km from the park's Namutoni gate. The town is a railhead on the line north from Windhoek and sits on the main road toward Ondangwa and the Angolan border. Lake Otjikoto, a sinkhole lake, lies about 25 km northwest of the town.
