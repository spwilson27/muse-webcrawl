Second Council of the Lateran

DateApril 1139
LocationLateran Palace, Rome
Convoked byPope Innocent II
AttendanceNearly 1,000 clergy (per contemporary accounts)

The Second Council of the Lateran was the tenth ecumenical council recognized by the Roman Catholic Church. Convoked by Pope Innocent II in April 1139 at the Lateran Palace in Rome, it met in the aftermath of the papal schism of 1130, in which the antipope Anacletus II had rivaled Innocent's claim to the papacy until Anacletus's death in 1138.

Proceedings

The council annulled the ordinations and appointments made by Anacletus II and his successor Victor IV, deposed bishops who had supported the schism, and excommunicated Roger II of Sicily, who had backed the antipope. Nearly one thousand churchmen are said to have attended, making it one of the largest councils of the Middle Ages.

Canons

The council issued some 30 canons. Among the most notable:

  • Condemnation of simony (the purchase of church offices) and of clerical marriage and concubinage, declaring such unions invalid — a major step in enforcing priestly celibacy (canon 7).
  • Prohibition of jousts and tournaments, with denial of Christian burial to those killed in them (canon 14).
  • Prohibition, under anathema, of the use of bows and crossbows against Christians (canon 29), a decree later generations largely ignored.
  • Condemnation of the heresies of Henry of Lausanne and Arnold of Brescia, and of usury.
  • Regulations on bishops' dress, clerical discipline, and the protection of churches and clergy from violence.

Legacy

The council consolidated the Gregorian Reform program of a disciplined, celibate clergy under papal authority, and its canons were incorporated into later collections of canon law, including Gratian's Decretum.
