Saint-Émilion AOC

Saint-Émilion is an Appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) for red wine situated in the Libournais region of Bordeaux, around the medieval town of Saint-Émilion on the right bank of the Dordogne. Unlike the Médoc across the Gironde, Saint-Émilion wines are dominated by Merlot and Cabernet Franc rather than Cabernet Sauvignon, producing rounder, earlier-maturing wines.

Appellations and classification

The main appellation covers some 5,400 hectares. Satellite and neighbouring appellations include Saint-Émilion Grand Cru, Lussac-Saint-Émilion, Montagne-Saint-Émilion, Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion and Saint-Georges-Saint-Émilion, plus the adjacent Pomerol. Uniquely in Bordeaux, Saint-Émilion operates its own classification, revised roughly every ten years (1955, 1969, 1985, 1996, 2006, 2012, 2022), ranking châteaux as Premier Grand Cru Classé A and B and Grand Cru Classé. Château Cheval Blanc and Château Ausone long held the top rank; in 2021 Cheval Blanc, Ausone and Angélus withdrew from the classification amid disputes over its criteria. Château Pétrus, the most famous wine of neighbouring Pomerol, is often grouped with Saint-Émilion in reputation.

Terroir

The finest vineyards sit on the limestone plateau and côtes around the town, with gravel and clay-limestone soils in the west near Pomerol. The vineyard landscape of Saint-Émilion — the first wine region so honoured — was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999.
