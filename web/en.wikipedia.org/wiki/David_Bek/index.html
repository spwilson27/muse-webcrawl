David Bek

David Bek (Armenian: Դավիթ Բեկ; died 1728) was an Armenian military commander and nobleman who led the Armenian liberation struggle in Syunik (the region of Zangezur in present-day southern Armenia) against Safavid Persian and Ottoman forces in the 1720s. He is celebrated as one of the great heroes of Armenian history.

Rebellion in Syunik

Invited by the Armenian meliks (princes) of Syunik, David Bek arrived from Georgia in 1722 with a small force and organised local resistance against Persian tax collectors and raiding Turkic tribes. He fortified the stronghold of Halidzor, defeated much larger Ottoman armies — most famously at the Battle of Halidzor in 1727 — and established a semi-independent Armenian principality in the region, minting his own coinage and corresponding with Peter the Great of Russia in hope of support.

Death and legacy

David Bek died in 1728, reportedly of illness, and command passed to Mkhitar Sparapet. His exploits were romanticised in Raffi's 19th-century historical novel David Bek and in Armen Tigranyan's opera of the same name (1950); his monument stands in Kapan, and he remains a symbol of Armenian national resistance.
