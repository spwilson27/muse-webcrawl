Nando Parrado

Fernando "Nando" Parrado (born 9 December 1949) is a Uruguayan businessman, writer and motivational speaker, best known as one of the sixteen survivors of Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571, which crashed in the Andes in 1972. Together with Roberto Canessa, he made the ten-day trek out of the mountains that led to the group's rescue.

The Andes disaster

Parrado, a 22-year-old university student and rugby player for the Old Christians club, was travelling to a match in Chile when the flight crashed on a glacier on 13 October 1972. His mother died in the crash and his sister Susana days later; Parrado himself lay in a coma for three days with a fractured skull. The survivors endured 72 days in extreme cold and starvation, eventually sustaining themselves on the bodies of the dead.

After an avalanche and the failure of attempts to walk east, Parrado and Canessa climbed westward from 13 December, crossing a 4,600-metre peak and descending into Chile, where they met the muleteer Sergio Catalán. The remaining survivors were rescued by helicopter on 22–23 December 1972.

Later life

Parrado became a successful businessman in Uruguay, working in his family's hardware company and in television production. He recounted his experience in the bestselling memoir Miracle in the Andes (2006, with Vince Rause) and gives lectures on survival, leadership and teamwork. He was portrayed by Ethan Hawke in the 1993 film Alive and served as a technical adviser on the 2023 film Society of the Snow.
