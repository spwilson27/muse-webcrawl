Connotation

A connotation is a commonly understood cultural or emotional association that a word carries in addition to its explicit or literal meaning, which is its denotation. For example, the words "home", "house", and "residence" share a similar denotation but differ in connotation: "home" suggests warmth and belonging, while "residence" sounds formal.

Usage in semantics

Linguists and philosophers distinguish the primary, dictionary meaning of a term from the secondary meanings it evokes. Connotations may be positive, negative, or neutral and may vary between speakers, communities, and eras; words such as "frugal" and "stingy" can describe the same behaviour with opposite connotations. In logic, the term has a technical sense from J. S. Mill: a concrete term's connotation is the set of attributes it implies, contrasted with its denotation, the objects it names.

Related concepts

Connotation overlaps with ideas of register, tone, and semantic prosody. Rhetoric and advertising rely heavily on connotation, choosing words for their associations ("freedom", "natural") rather than their literal content. Because connotations are shared conventions, misunderstanding them is a common source of difficulty for language learners.
