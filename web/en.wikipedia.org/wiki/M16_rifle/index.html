M16 rifle

M16 rifle
Type: Assault rifle
Origin: United States
Designed: 1959 (from the ArmaLite AR-15)
Cartridge: 5.56×45mm NATO
In service: 1964–present

The M16 rifle is a family of American military rifles adapted from the ArmaLite AR-15, designed by Eugene Stoner. Chambered for the 5.56×45mm NATO cartridge, it became the standard service rifle of the United States armed forces during the Vietnam War era and remains in service in many countries.

History

The rifle grew out of the US Army's search for a lightweight weapon firing an intermediate cartridge. Colt bought the rights to the AR-15 in 1959; the Air Force adopted it as the M16 in 1962, and the Army followed. Early M16s in Vietnam suffered reliability problems linked to propellant changes, lack of cleaning kits, and a non-chromed chamber; the improved M16A1 (1967) added chrome lining, a forward assist, and proper maintenance support.

Variants

The M16A2 (1980s) introduced burst fire, a heavier barrel, and adjustable sights. The M16A3 offered full-automatic fire, and the M16A4 added a flat-top receiver with Picatinny rail. The shorter M4 carbine, derived from the M16, progressively supplanted it in US service. Total production of M16-family weapons exceeds 8 million, and licensed or unlicensed copies are manufactured worldwide.

Characteristics

The M16 uses direct gas impingement, a rotating bolt, and extensive aluminium and polymer construction, giving a loaded weight of about 4 kg. Standard magazines hold 20 or 30 rounds; effective range is about 550 m for point targets with the A2/A4.
