The Leclerc is a French third-generation main battle tank developed and manufactured by GIAT Industries (now KNDS France, formerly Nexter). Named after General Philippe Leclerc de Hauteclocque, who led the French 2nd Armoured Division in World War II, it entered service with the French Army in 1992 and also serves with the United Arab Emirates.

Design

The Leclerc was designed for high mobility and automated systems. Its autoloader — holding 22 ready rounds in the turret bustle — eliminates the human loader, reducing the crew to three and permitting a high rate of fire of up to about 12 rounds per minute. The 120 mm smoothbore gun fires NATO-standard ammunition. The tank's modular composite armour can be supplemented with additional packages, and its digital fire-control system allows it to engage multiple targets in rapid succession, including while on the move. The V8X-1500 "Hyperbar" diesel engine gives a top road speed of about 71 km/h.

Service

France ordered 406 tanks and the UAE 436 (including variants), for a total production of over 860 vehicles. French Leclercs have deployed on peacekeeping missions in Kosovo and Lebanon. UAE Leclercs saw combat in Yemen from 2015, the type's first major combat use, where they were generally reported to have performed well. The tank is noted for its high unit cost, reflecting its advanced electronics and low production numbers.

Future

France is upgrading part of its fleet to the Leclerc XLR standard under the SCORPION programme, with improved armour, communications, and battlefield-management systems. In the longer term the Leclerc is intended to be replaced by the Franco-German Main Ground Combat System (MGCS).
