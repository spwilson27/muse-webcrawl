Monomial

In mathematics, a monomial is, roughly speaking, a polynomial which has only one term. Two definitions of a monomial may be encountered:

  1. A monomial, also called a power product, is a product of powers of variables with nonnegative integer exponents, or, in other words, a product of variables, possibly with repetitions. For example, x2yz3 is a monomial. The constant 1 is a monomial, being equal to the empty product and to x0 for any variable x.
  2. A monomial is a monomial in the first sense multiplied by a nonzero constant, called the coefficient of the monomial. A monomial in the first sense is a special case of this second definition, with coefficient 1. For example, −7x5 and (3 − 4i)x y z are monomials.

Degree

The degree of a monomial is defined as the sum of all the exponents of the variables, including implicit exponents of 1 for variables that appear without an exponent. For example, the monomial x2yz3 has degree 2 + 1 + 3 = 6. The degree of a nonzero constant monomial is 0. In the second definition, the degree of a monomial is still the degree of its power-product part.

Number of monomials

The number of monomials of degree d in n variables is a combinatorial quantity given by a binomial coefficient; it counts the number of multisets of size d drawn from n variables. This number is the dimension of the vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree d in n variables.

Notation

Monomial notation is used in multi-index notation, where for a multi-index α = (α1, …, αn) one writes xα = x1α1 x2α2 … xnαn. This compact notation is fundamental in the theory of partial differential equations and Taylor series in several variables.

Monomial basis and monomial orderings

The set of all monomials in given variables forms a basis of the vector space of polynomials, called the monomial basis. In the theory of Gröbner bases, a monomial ordering (or term order) is a well-ordering on the set of monomials compatible with multiplication; examples include lexicographic, graded lexicographic, and graded reverse lexicographic orders.
