Red Nose Day

Red Nose Day is a fundraising campaign run by the British charity Comic Relief. It has been held in the United Kingdom roughly every two years since 1988, and since 2023 annually. The day is the highlight of Comic Relief's appeal, built around a televised telethon on BBC One featuring comedy sketches, celebrity challenges, and appeals films, with the public encouraged to wear plastic red noses and take part in sponsored fundraising events.

History

Comic Relief was launched in 1985 by comedy scriptwriter Richard Curtis and comedian Lenny Henry, in response to the Ethiopian famine, from a refugee camp in Sudan. The first Red Nose Day took place on 5 February 1988, with more than 150 celebrities and comedians taking part; it raised £15 million. The event became a fixture of British popular culture, alternating years with Sport Relief.

Telethon

The televised appeal traditionally runs through the evening on BBC One, hosted by comedians and presenters, and features specially produced comedy sketches — over the years including crossovers and reunion specials of popular sitcoms — alongside short films fronted by celebrities visiting projects funded in the UK and Africa. Money raised supports projects tackling poverty, homelessness, mental health, and domestic abuse, among other causes.

Red noses

The red nose itself has been redesigned for successive campaigns, with various characters and materials; recent editions have emphasised plastic-free construction. Noses are sold in supermarkets and charity shops in the run-up to the appeal.

International versions

A United States version, Red Nose Day USA, was launched in 2015, organised by Comic Relief US and broadcast on NBC, with funds distributed to organisations working to end child poverty.
