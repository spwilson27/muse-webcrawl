Malham Cove

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Malham Cove is a large curved limestone formation 0.6 miles (1 km) north of the village of Malham, North Yorkshire, England. It was formed by a waterfall carrying meltwater from glaciers at the end of the last Ice Age more than 12,000 years ago.

Geology

The cove is a 70-metre (230 ft) high, 300-metre (980 ft) wide curved crag of Carboniferous limestone. Above the cliff is a limestone pavement of clints and grikes, a classic example of karst scenery within the Yorkshire Dales National Park. The pavement's joints were enlarged by mildly acidic rainwater.

Malham Beck

Today, Malham Beck flows underground and emerges from the cave system at the base of the cove. In December 2015, during Storm Desmond, heavy rainfall briefly caused a waterfall to flow over the cove for the first time in living memory, reported as the first time since 1824.

In popular culture

The limestone pavement was used as a location for Harry and Hermione's camping scene in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1 (2010). The cove is a popular site for rock climbing and for walks from Malham village.
