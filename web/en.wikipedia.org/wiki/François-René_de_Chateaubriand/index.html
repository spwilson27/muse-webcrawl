François-René, vicomte de Chateaubriand (4 September 1768 – 4 July 1848) was a French writer, historian, politician and diplomat who founded French Romanticism. Author of the novella Atala (1801) and the influential work Génie du christianisme (1802), he was a major figure of French literature of the first half of the nineteenth century. Descended from an old aristocratic family in Brittany, he served as French ambassador to London and Berlin and as minister of foreign affairs under the Bourbon Restoration.

Life

Born in Saint-Malo and raised at the family estate of Combourg, Chateaubriand joined the army in 1786. In 1791 he travelled to North America, an experience that supplied the exotic settings of his early fiction. Returning to France during the Revolution, he emigrated to England in 1793 and lived in poverty in London, writing his Essai sur les révolutions (1797). His mother's and sister's imprisonment and deaths during the Revolution, and his own return to the Catholic faith, shaped the Génie du christianisme, published in 1802, from which the stories Atala and René were drawn; René's hero became the archetype of the Romantic mal du siècle.

Under Napoleon he held a diplomatic post but resigned after the execution of the Duke of Enghien in 1804. After the Bourbon Restoration he entered politics, serving as ambassador to Berlin (1821) and London (1822) and as minister of foreign affairs (1822–1824), in which capacity he conducted the French intervention in Spain in 1823. Opposed to the July Monarchy after 1830, he withdrew largely from public life to write.

Works and legacy

Chateaubriand's masterpiece is his autobiography, the Mémoires d'outre-tombe ("Memoirs from Beyond the Grave"), published posthumously in 1849–1850, considered one of the greatest works of French prose. His lyrical descriptions of nature, his melancholy introspection, and his rehabilitation of Christian sentiment profoundly influenced Victor Hugo, who as a schoolboy wrote that he would be "Chateaubriand or nothing." He is buried, as he requested, on the islet of Grand Bé off Saint-Malo, facing the sea.
