Side mount

In grappling and mixed martial arts, the side mount — also called side control, cross mount, or in judo yoko-shiho-gatame — is a dominant ground grappling position in which the top practitioner lies perpendicularly across the opponent's chest or torso, controlling the opponent with body weight, hips, and arms while the bottom player's back is against the mat. It is one of the most common pinning positions in Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo, wrestling, and MMA.

Mechanics

From side control the top player typically sprawls the hips low and heavy across the opponent's diaphragm, controls the far arm, and blocks the near hip to prevent the bottom player from recovering guard. Common grips include the crossface (an arm under the head applying shoulder pressure to the jaw) and an underhook on the far arm. Variants include the standard side control, kesa gatame (scarf hold), reverse kesa, north-south position, and knee-on-belly.

Attacks and transitions

Side mount is a launching point for submissions such as the kimura, americana (keylock), arm triangle choke, straight armbar, and paper-cutter choke, as well as for transitions to the full mount or back control. In mixed martial arts it is also an effective striking platform, allowing the top fighter to land elbows, punches, and knees while remaining relatively safe from strikes.

Escapes

The bottom player's standard defences are to frame against the neck and hip, bridge and shrimp (hip escape) to insert a knee and recover guard, or to roll to the knees (turtle). Preventing the crossface and keeping elbows tight are considered fundamental survival skills for beginners.

In judo

In judo, side four-quarters hold (yoko-shiho-gatame) is one of the seven standard osaekomi-waza (holding techniques); maintaining it for 20 seconds scores ippon.
