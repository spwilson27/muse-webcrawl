Temptation

Temptation is a desire to engage in short-term urges for enjoyment that threatens long-term goals. In the context of some religions, temptation is the inclination to sin. Temptation also describes the coaxing or inducing a person into committing an act — by manipulation, curiosity, desire or fear of loss, or other motives — which they may perceive as good, pleasurable or necessary, though it conflicts with their values, health, duties or long-term interests.

In religion

In the Hebrew Bible, the serpent tempts Eve to eat the forbidden fruit in the Garden of Eden. In the New Testament, the Temptation of Christ describes Satan offering Jesus food, miraculous protection and worldly kingdoms during his forty days of fasting in the desert; Jesus refuses each offer. The Lord's Prayer includes the petition "lead us not into temptation". In Christian theology, temptation is distinguished from sin: being tempted is not itself sinful, but yielding to it is. In Islam, Satan (Shaytan) and the lower self (nafs) tempt humans away from obedience to God, and resisting temptation is a form of spiritual struggle (jihad al-nafs). In Buddhism, craving (tanha) and the tempter figure Mara, who tried to distract the Buddha on the night of his enlightenment, play an analogous role.

In psychology

Psychologists study temptation as a problem of self-control and intertemporal choice: an immediately rewarding option competes with a more valuable delayed outcome. Research on delay of gratification — famously Walter Mischel's "marshmallow test" — examined children's ability to resist a small immediate treat for a larger later one. Strategies for resisting temptation include avoiding cues, precommitment (binding oneself in advance), implementation intentions ("if–then" plans), reframing the reward, and mindfulness-based techniques used in addiction treatment. Temptation is central to models of addiction, relapse, dieting failure and impulse buying, and economists model it using hyperbolic discounting, in which preferences reverse as rewards draw near.

In literature and culture

Temptation is a perennial literary theme: Odysseus bound to the mast to hear the Sirens without yielding; Faust bargaining with Mephistopheles; Oscar Wilde's oft-quoted line, "I can resist everything except temptation". The word is also used commercially and in popular culture — for example as the name of bands, songs, television formats and products — trading on the allure of forbidden pleasure.
