Glycogen storage disease

A glycogen storage disease (GSD, also called glycogenosis) is one of a group of inherited metabolic disorders caused by enzyme deficiencies that affect the synthesis or breakdown of glycogen, the storage form of glucose in animals. Depending on the enzyme affected, abnormal quantities or abnormal forms of glycogen accumulate in the liver, muscles, heart and other tissues.

Classification

The diseases are traditionally numbered by the order in which the enzyme defects were identified. Major types include:

Signs and symptoms

Liver forms typically present in infancy with an enlarged liver (hepatomegaly), fasting hypoglycaemia, growth retardation and a doll-like facial appearance. Muscle forms present with exercise intolerance, cramps and, in McArdle disease, a characteristic "second wind" phenomenon. Infantile Pompe disease causes severe cardiomyopathy and muscle weakness and was historically fatal in infancy.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on clinical findings, biochemical tests, enzyme assays and genetic testing. There is no cure for most types; management centres on dietary therapy — frequent carbohydrate feeds, uncooked cornstarch, and avoidance of fasting — to prevent hypoglycaemia. Enzyme replacement therapy with alglucosidase alfa has transformed the outlook for Pompe disease.
