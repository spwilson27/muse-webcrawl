The Federated States of Micronesia (FSM), often called simply Micronesia, is an island country in Oceania, consisting of more than 600 islands in the Caroline Islands archipelago of the western Pacific Ocean. It is composed of four states, from west to east: Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, and Kosrae. The capital is Palikir on the island of Pohnpei. The country shares maritime borders with Guam, the Marshall Islands, Palau, and Papua New Guinea.

History

The islands were settled by Austronesian peoples over 4,000 years ago. The stone city of Nan Madol, built on artificial islets off Pohnpei, was the ceremonial center of the Saudeleur dynasty. Spain colonized the Carolines in the 16th–19th centuries, selling them to Germany in 1899. Japan seized the islands in 1914 and administered them under a League of Nations mandate. After heavy fighting in the Second World War, the islands became part of the United States-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands in 1947.

The FSM adopted its own constitution in 1979 and entered into a Compact of Free Association with the United States in 1986, achieving independence while the U.S. retained responsibility for defense. It joined the United Nations in 1991. The compact's economic assistance provisions have been periodically renewed, most recently in 2023–2024.

Geography and states

The federation's islands stretch across roughly 2,700 km of ocean. Chuuk Lagoon is one of the world's largest lagoons and a famous wreck-diving site. Yap is known for its traditional stone money (Rai stones). The climate is tropical, with heavy rainfall and exposure to typhoons; climate change and sea-level rise are major concerns.

Economy

The economy depends heavily on U.S. financial assistance under the Compact, government employment, subsistence agriculture, fishing, and tuna licensing fees. Emigration to the United States, whose citizens FSM nationals may freely enter to live and work under the Compact, is significant.
