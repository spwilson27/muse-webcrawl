The VVER (Russian: ВВЭР, vodo-vodyanoy energeticheskiy reaktor, "water-water energy reactor") is a series of pressurized water reactors originally developed in the Soviet Union, and now designed in Russia by OKB Gidropress. VVER plants are among the most widely built reactor types in Eastern Europe and Asia.

History

The first VVER prototype, the VVER-210, entered service at Novovoronezh in 1964. Successive generations increased output: the VVER-440 became the standard Soviet export model, followed by the larger VVER-1000 with a full containment structure. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union, development continued with the VVER-1200 (AES-2006 design) and the VVER-TOI, incorporating passive safety systems and longer fuel cycles.

Design

Like Western pressurized water reactors, a VVER uses ordinary water as both coolant and neutron moderator, with hexagonal fuel assemblies arranged in a steel pressure vessel. Distinctive features include horizontal steam generators and, in the VVER-440, a relatively compact containment. Later models added double containment, core catchers and passive heat removal to meet post-Fukushima safety expectations.

Models

Major variants include the VVER-440 (models V-230 and V-213), VVER-1000 (V-320 and others), VVER-1200 (V-491/V-509), the VVER-1500 study and the VVER-TOI. Export plants have been built or are under construction in countries including China (Tianwan), India (Kudankulam), Iran (Bushehr), Turkey (Akkuyu), Egypt (El Dabaa), Hungary (Paks II), Belarus (Ostrovets) and Bangladesh (Rooppur).
