Camphor

Camphor (/ˈkæmfər/) is a waxy, flammable, transparent solid with a strong aroma. It is a terpenoid with the chemical formula C10H16O. It is found in the wood of the camphor laurel (Cinnamomum camphora), a large evergreen tree found in East Asia, and also in the kapur tree, a tall timber tree from South East Asia. It also occurs in some other related trees in the laurel family, notably Ocotea usambarensis. Natural camphor is the dextrorotatory isomer, while synthetic camphor produced from turpentine oil is racemic.

Production

Camphor has been produced as a forest product for centuries, condensed from the vapor given off by the roasting of wood chips cut from the camphor laurel and later by passing steam through the pulverized wood and condensing the vapors. Taiwan was historically the world's leading producer, and camphor production there was a major government monopoly during the Japanese colonial period. In modern industry, camphor is synthesized from alpha-pinene, which is abundant in the oils of coniferous trees and can be obtained as a byproduct of the chemical pulping of wood; solvents used in the process include acetic acid and nitrobenzene.

Uses

Camphor is readily absorbed through the skin, producing a feeling of cooling similar to that of menthol, and acts as a slight local anesthetic and antimicrobial substance. It is an ingredient in many topical analgesic products, cough suppressants, and vapor rubs. Historically it was used as a plasticizer for nitrocellulose in the production of celluloid and smokeless gunpowder, as an embalming fluid, and in religious ceremonies: it is widely used in Hindu pujas, where it is burned as aarti. It is also used as a moth repellent, a flavoring in sweets in India and Europe, and in the manufacture of fireworks.

Safety

In large quantities, camphor is poisonous if ingested and may cause seizures, confusion, irritability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity. Regulatory agencies have limited the concentration of camphor in medicinal products because of toxicity concerns, particularly for children.
