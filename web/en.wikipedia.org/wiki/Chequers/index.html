Chequers

Chequers, or Chequers Court, is a 16th-century country house near the village of Ellesborough in Buckinghamshire, England, at the foot of the Chiltern Hills. Since 1921 it has been the official country residence of the serving Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, the rural counterpart to 10 Downing Street.

History

The house was built around 1565 by William Hawtrey. Its name may derive from an early owner who was an Exchequer official, or from the chequer trees in its grounds. Lady Mary Grey, sister of Lady Jane Grey, was confined here in the 1560s. Oliver Cromwell's family later owned the estate through marriage.

Gift to the nation

In 1917 the house was bought by Arthur Lee, later Viscount Lee of Fareham, who gave Chequers to the nation in 1921, under the Chequers Estate Act 1917, as a retreat for the prime minister of the day. David Lloyd George was the first prime minister to use it. Chequers has since hosted many diplomatic meetings; the 2018 "Chequers plan" for Brexit negotiations took its name from a Cabinet meeting held there.
