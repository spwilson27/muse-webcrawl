The All-China Women's Federation (ACWF) is a mass organisation for women in the People's Republic of China, founded on 24 March 1949. It is one of the country's major "people's organisations" and operates under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party, with branches at every administrative level down to villages and urban neighbourhoods.

Role and activities

The federation states its mission as representing and safeguarding women's rights and promoting equality between women and men. It conducts campaigns on women's health, education, employment, and political participation, runs training and poverty-alleviation programmes, and publishes the magazine Women of China. Its leaders have traditionally included senior women officials, and its presidents have often served as vice-chairpersons of the National People's Congress Standing Committee.

Criticism and debate

Scholars and activists have debated the federation's effectiveness as an advocate for women independent of state priorities, noting tensions between its role as a transmission channel for party policy and its claims to represent women's interests from below.
