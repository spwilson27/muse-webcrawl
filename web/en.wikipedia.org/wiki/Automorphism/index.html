Automorphism

In mathematics, an automorphism is an isomorphism from a mathematical object to itself. It is, in some sense, a symmetry of the object, and a way of mapping the object to itself while preserving all of its structure. The set of all automorphisms of an object forms a group, called the automorphism group of the object.

Definition

Precisely, an automorphism of an object X in a category is a morphism from X to X that is an isomorphism, that is, one possessing a two-sided inverse. For algebraic structures such as groups, rings and vector spaces, an automorphism is a bijective homomorphism from the structure to itself. The collection of all automorphisms of an object, under composition of morphisms, is itself a group.

Examples

The only automorphism of the set of real numbers as an ordered field is the identity map, whereas the field of complex numbers has a nontrivial automorphism, complex conjugation, as part of its Galois group over the rationals. In group theory, conjugation by a fixed element defines an inner automorphism; automorphisms that are not inner are called outer automorphisms. In graph theory, an automorphism of a graph is a permutation of its vertices that preserves adjacency, expressing the graph's symmetries.

Automorphism group

The automorphism group of an object is an important invariant in many areas of algebra and geometry. For example, the automorphism group of a regular polygon is the dihedral group, and the classification of finite simple groups has deep connections with automorphism groups.
