Probabilistic number theory

Probabilistic number theory
Field: Analytic number theory, probability theory
Key figures: Hardy, Ramanujan, Erdős, Kac, Turán

Probabilistic number theory is a subfield of number theory that uses probability theory and statistical methods to study the distribution of arithmetic functions and properties of integers.

Origins

The field arose from work by Hardy and Ramanujan in 1917 on the number of prime factors of an integer, which showed that this function has a well-defined "normal order". Pál Turán gave probabilistic proofs in the 1930s, and the subject crystallised with the Erdős–Kac theorem of 1940.

The Erdős–Kac theorem

The Erdős–Kac theorem states that the number of distinct prime factors of integers, suitably normalised, is asymptotically normally distributed — an application of the central limit theorem to number theory. It is considered the founding result of the modern probabilistic method in the field.

Methods

Techniques include modelling arithmetic functions as sums of quasi-independent random variables, moments and characteristic functions, and sieve methods. Results concern typical rather than worst-case behaviour of integers.
