The Bund

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Bund's historic buildings along the Huangpu River, viewed from Pudong]

The Bund (Chinese: 外滩; pinyin: Wàitān) is a waterfront area and protected historical district in central Shanghai. It runs along the western bank of the Huangpu River, facing the skyscrapers of Lujiazui in Pudong across the water, and is one of the most famous tourist destinations in China.

History

After Shanghai was opened as a treaty port following the First Opium War, the Bund became the financial and commercial centre of foreign concessions in China. Along Zhongshan Road stand 52 buildings of various Western architectural styles — Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, and Art Deco — formerly housing banks, trading houses, consulates, and the famous Peace Hotel. The HSBC Building of 1923 and the Customs House with its clock tower are among the best known.

Modern era

After 1949 many buildings were taken over by government offices; from the 1990s financial institutions returned, and the Bund was extensively renovated, including for Expo 2010. At night the floodlit facades and the view across to the Oriental Pearl Tower make it one of Shanghai's signature sights.
