Richard Lynn Carpenter (born October 15, 1946) is an American musician, singer, songwriter, and record producer, who formed half of the sibling duo the Carpenters alongside his younger sister Karen. He has had numerous roles including record producer, arranger, pianist, keyboardist, lyricist, and composer, as well as joining with Karen on harmony vocals.

Early life and career

Carpenter was born in New Haven, Connecticut, and the family moved to Downey, California, in 1963. A prodigious pianist, he studied music at California State University, Long Beach, where he met collaborators including John Bettis. In 1969 Richard and Karen signed with A&M Records, and the Carpenters went on to sell more than 90 million records worldwide with hits such as "(They Long to Be) Close to You", "We've Only Just Begun", "Superstar", and "Yesterday Once More".

Richard's arrangements — lush orchestration combined with Karen's contralto voice — defined the duo's signature soft-pop sound, and he is widely credited as the musical architect of the group.

Later work

Following Karen's death in 1983 from complications of anorexia nervosa, Richard largely withdrew from performing but continued producing and overseeing reissues and compilations of the Carpenters' recordings. He has worked to preserve their legacy through archival releases and documentaries, and has supported the Karen Carpenter Performing Arts Center at his alma mater.
