Natural selection

Natural selection is the differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype. It is a key mechanism of evolution, the change in the heritable traits characteristic of a population over generations.

Principles

Charles Darwin popularised the term in his 1859 book On the Origin of Species, drawing an analogy with artificial selection. Natural selection acts on variation within a population: traits that improve survival and reproduction become more common over generations.

Types

Selection may be stabilising, directional, or disruptive, and includes special cases such as sexual selection and kin selection.
