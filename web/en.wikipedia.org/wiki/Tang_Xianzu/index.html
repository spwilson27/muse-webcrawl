Tang Xianzu

Tang Xianzu (traditional Chinese: 湯顯祖; simplified Chinese: 汤显祖; 24 September 1550 – 29 July 1616), courtesy name Yireng (義仍), was a celebrated Chinese playwright of the Ming dynasty, often called the "Shakespeare of China". A native of Linchuan in Jiangxi province, he passed the imperial examinations and served as a county magistrate in Suichang before retiring to write.

The Four Dreams

Tang's masterpieces are the four plays collectively known as the Four Dreams of Linchuan, named for the dreams that drive their plots: The Purple Hairpin, The Peony Pavilion (1598), The Handan Dream and The Nanke Dream. The Peony Pavilion, a kunqu opera in 55 scenes, tells of Du Liniang, who dreams of a lover, dies of longing, and is resurrected by love — a celebration of qing (feeling) over Confucian restraint that became one of the most beloved works in Chinese theatre.

Legacy

Tang died in 1616, the same year as Shakespeare and Cervantes, a coincidence marked by joint commemorations four centuries later. The Peony Pavilion remains a cornerstone of the kunqu repertoire, recognised by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
