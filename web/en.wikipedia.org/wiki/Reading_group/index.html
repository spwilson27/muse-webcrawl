Reading group

A reading group, also called a book club or book discussion group, is a group of people who meet regularly — usually in homes, libraries, bookshops or online — to discuss a book they have all read. Members typically take turns choosing the title, or agree a reading list in advance, and meetings combine literary discussion with a social occasion.

History

Collective reading has roots in religious study circles, eighteenth-century salons and the literary societies of the nineteenth century. Modern book groups expanded rapidly in the late twentieth century, helped by television and media clubs such as Oprah's Book Club (launched 1996), whose selections reliably became bestsellers, and by publishers issuing "reading group editions" with discussion questions printed at the back.

Formats

Groups range from informal gatherings of friends to library-run and academic seminars. Some focus on a single genre or author; others read widely across fiction and non-fiction. Online communities and social cataloguing sites have added large-scale, asynchronous forms of the reading group, with forums, video meetings and shared annotations.
