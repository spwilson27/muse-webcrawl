The 3rd Infantry Division is a combined arms division of the United States Army based at Fort Stewart, Georgia. It is a direct subordinate unit of the XVIII Airborne Corps and U.S. Army Forces Command. Activated in 1917 for the First World War, it is one of the most decorated divisions in the Army, having fought in both world wars, Korea, and Iraq; its soldiers have earned more than 60 Medals of Honor.

First World War

The division was organised at Camp Greene, North Carolina, in November 1917 and deployed to France in 1918. It earned its nickname "Rock of the Marne" at the Second Battle of the Marne in July 1918, when its 38th Infantry Regiment held the line along the river against the final German offensive while neighbouring units fell back — prompting its motto Nous Resterons Là.

Second World War

The 3rd was one of the few U.S. divisions to fight the Axis in North Africa, Sicily, Italy, France and Germany. It made amphibious landings in French Morocco, Sicily, at Salerno and Anzio, and in southern France, then fought up the Rhône valley and into Bavaria and Austria. It spent 531 days in combat — more than any other American division — and suffered over 100 percent casualties by turnover. Its most famous soldier, Audie Murphy, became the most decorated American combat soldier of the war.

Korea and later service

In the Korean War the division fought from November 1950, including the defence during the Chinese intervention and the Iron Triangle battles, before standing guard duty in Germany during the Cold War. In 2003 the division was the first conventional ground unit to enter Baghdad during the invasion of Iraq, making the famous "Thunder Runs" into the capital; it deployed to Iraq three more times and has since sent brigades to Afghanistan and Europe.
