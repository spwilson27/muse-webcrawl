A bed and breakfast (typically shortened to B&B) is a small lodging establishment that offers overnight accommodation and breakfast. Bed and breakfasts are often private family homes and typically have between four and eleven rooms, with six being the average. In addition, a B&B usually has the hosts living in the house.

Description

The term "bed and breakfast" is also used to describe the level of catering included — as opposed to room-only, half-board, or full-board arrangements. Guests are accommodated overnight in private bedrooms, which usually have en-suite bathrooms, and breakfast is served in the morning, typically in a dining room or the host's kitchen. Many B&Bs are converted historic homes, farmhouses, or cottages, and they often market themselves on personal service, local character, and a quieter atmosphere than hotels.

History

Private homes taking in paying guests have a long history: monasteries offered lodging to travellers, and coaching inns provided rooms and morning meals for centuries. The modern B&B sector grew substantially after the Second World War, particularly in Britain — where the "bed and breakfast" sign became a fixture of seaside towns — and in the United States, where the restoration of historic houses in the 1970s and 1980s fuelled an innkeeping boom. In countries such as Australia and New Zealand, B&Bs are a common form of rural and boutique accommodation.

Regulation

B&Bs are generally subject to local zoning, fire-safety, and food-handling regulations, though requirements vary widely. In some jurisdictions the number of rooms or guests is capped to keep an establishment in the B&B category rather than classifying it as a hotel or guest house.
