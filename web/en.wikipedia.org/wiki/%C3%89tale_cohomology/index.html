Étale cohomology

Étale cohomology is a cohomology theory in algebraic geometry, introduced by Alexander Grothendieck and his school (notably in the SGA seminars, and with Michael Artin), that adapts the ideas of sheaf cohomology to schemes using the étale topology. It supplies the ℓ-adic cohomology theories needed to work over fields of arbitrary characteristic.

Motivation

Ordinary topological methods fail for varieties over finite or algebraically non-closed fields because the Zariski topology is too coarse. By replacing open immersions with étale morphisms, Grothendieck obtained cohomology groups that behave like the singular cohomology of complex varieties.

Applications

Étale cohomology was the key tool in Pierre Deligne's proof (1974) of the Weil conjectures, the last and hardest of André Weil's conjectures on zeta functions of varieties over finite fields. It also underlies the modern theory of Galois representations and much of arithmetic geometry.
