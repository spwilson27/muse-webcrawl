Cover

FieldTopology, set theory
DefinitionA collection of sets whose union contains a given set
Key notionsOpen cover, subcover, refinement, finite subcover
Central useDefinition of compactness

In mathematics, a cover (or covering) of a set X is a collection of sets whose union includes X as a subset. If the sets in the collection are open sets of a topological space, the cover is called an open cover. A subcover is a subcollection that still covers X, and a cover that refines another — each of whose sets is contained in some set of the other — is called a refinement.

Compactness

Covers are central to the modern definition of compactness: a topological space is compact if every open cover of it has a finite subcover. This formulation, developed in the early twentieth century, generalizes the Heine–Borel theorem, which states that a subset of Euclidean space is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded. Extracting finite subcovers is the engine behind many theorems of analysis, including the extreme value theorem and the uniform continuity of continuous functions on compact domains.

Other uses

The related notion of a cover appears throughout mathematics: a covering space in algebraic topology is a space that maps onto another "evenly"; Lebesgue's covering dimension measures the complexity of a space by the overlap order of its refinements; and in set theory, covering lemmas relate inner models to the set-theoretic universe. The Lebesgue number lemma guarantees that for an open cover of a compact metric space there is a scale below which every set of that diameter fits inside a single member of the cover.
