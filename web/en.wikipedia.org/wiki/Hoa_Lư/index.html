Hoa Lư

Hoa Lư was the capital of Vietnam from 968 to 1010, during the Đinh and Early Lê dynasties. Located in present-day Ninh Bình Province, about 100 km south of Hanoi, the citadel was set in a valley ringed by limestone karst mountains that served as natural fortifications.

History

In 968, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh ended the period of fragmentation known as the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords and founded the Đinh dynasty, naming the country Đại Cồ Việt and making Hoa Lư its capital. After his assassination, the regent Lê Hoàn took the throne in 980, founding the Early Lê dynasty, and repelled invasions by Song-dynasty China and Champa from the stronghold.

In 1010, Lý Công Uẩn (Emperor Lý Thái Tổ) issued the Edict on the Transfer of the Capital and moved the court to Đại La, renamed Thăng Long — modern Hanoi — judging its position more auspicious and defensible. Hoa Lư thereafter declined as a political centre.

Remains

Little of the original citadel survives apart from foundations, but temples dedicated to Đinh Tiên Hoàng and Lê Đại Hành stand on the site, near the supposed royal tombs on Mã Yên mountain. The surrounding area forms part of the Tràng An scenic landscape complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for its karst peaks and waterways.
