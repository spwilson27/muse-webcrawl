A cactus (plural cacti, cactuses, or less commonly cactus) is a member of the plant family Cactaceae, which comprises some 130 genera and roughly 1,750 known species. Cacti are native almost entirely to the Americas, from Patagonia in the south to parts of western Canada in the north; the single exception, Rhipsalis baccifera, also occurs in tropical Africa and Sri Lanka, probably dispersed by migratory birds.

Description

Cacti are stem succulents: the stem has become the main water-storing and photosynthetic organ, and in most species leaves are reduced or absent. The family's defining feature is the areole, a small cushion-like structure from which spines, branches, and flowers arise. Spines — highly modified leaves — reduce water loss, shade the stem, and deter herbivores. Almost all cacti use crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM), opening their stomata at night to fix carbon dioxide and closing them during the heat of the day to conserve water.

Shapes vary enormously: tree-like columnar forms such as the saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea) and cardón, barrel cacti, the flat-padded prickly pears (Opuntia), and epiphytic jungle cacti with flattened, leaf-like stems. Flowers are typically large, colourful, and short-lived, often opening at night for pollination by bats and moths.

Classification

The family is commonly divided into four subfamilies: Cactoideae (the largest, including most columnar and barrel forms), Opuntioideae (prickly pears and chollas), Pereskioideae (leafy, little-modified cacti), and Maihuenioideae.

Uses

Cacti are widely grown as ornamental houseplants. Several are important food plants: prickly pear pads (nopales) and fruits (tunas) are staples in Mexican cuisine, and the dragon fruit comes from Hylocereus species. Peyote (Lophophora williamsii) contains mescaline and has been used ceremonially by Indigenous peoples for millennia. Cochineal insects raised on Opuntia yield a red dye.

Conservation

Many species are threatened by habitat loss and illegal collection for the horticultural trade; nearly the whole family is listed under CITES. Introduced cacti, especially prickly pears, have become serious invasive weeds in Australia and South Africa.
