Captain (nautical)

The captain of a ship, also called the master (particularly in merchant shipping) or shipmaster, is the licensed mariner who holds ultimate command and responsibility of a merchant vessel. The captain is responsible for the safe and efficient operation of the ship, including its seaworthiness, safety and security, cargo operations, navigation, crew management, and legal compliance. The captain represents the ship's owner and may be considered the equivalent of a chief executive officer in business terms.

Captains typically hold an unlimited master's licence (or certificate of competency), issued by their flag state after examinations and sea-time requirements. The term "captain" is also a naval rank, but on merchant vessels it is a professional title held by the master regardless of rank.

Duties and responsibilities

The master's responsibilities include:

  • Navigation and safety of the vessel — ensuring the ship is navigated safely, in compliance with the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) and the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) convention.
  • Crew welfare and discipline — the master has authority over everyone aboard and is responsible for their safety and well-being.
  • Cargo — overseeing loading, stowage, and discharge, and signing bills of lading.
  • Legal and regulatory compliance — including pollution prevention under MARPOL, customs and immigration formalities, and maintaining official logbooks.
  • Emergency response — the master directs all emergency operations, including firefighting, damage control, and abandonment of ship.

Legal authority

The master has overriding authority under the International Safety Management (ISM) Code to make decisions with respect to safety and pollution prevention, even against the wishes of the shipowner. Historically, masters enjoyed wide legal powers, including the ability to perform marriages and burials at sea and, in earlier eras, to impose corporal punishment. The master is also obliged, under international law, to render assistance to persons in distress at sea.

Licensing

Certification of masters is governed internationally by the STCW Convention (Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers). A Master Mariner certificate (often called a "master's ticket") is the highest grade of seafarer certification, allowing the holder to command a vessel of any size, anywhere in the world.

In popular usage

In many navies, "captain" is both a rank and the form of address for the commanding officer of a vessel, whatever their actual rank. Aboard United States Navy vessels, for instance, the commanding officer is addressed as "captain" as a courtesy title.
