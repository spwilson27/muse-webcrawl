Jiménez dynasty

The Jiménez dynasty (Spanish: Dinastía Jimena) was a medieval ruling family that governed the Kingdom of Pamplona (later Navarre) and, through cadet branches, the kingdoms of Aragon, Castile, León, and Galicia between the 10th and 13th centuries.

Origins and rise

The dynasty takes its name from Jimeno of Pamplona, and its first king was Sancho Garcés I (reigned 905–925), who established the line on the throne of Pamplona. Under Sancho III "the Great" (1004–1035) the dynasty reached its greatest extent, ruling nearly all of Christian Iberia; on his death his realms were divided among his sons, giving rise to separate Jiménez lines in Navarre, Castile, and Aragon (through his illegitimate son Ramiro I).

Later history

Ferdinand I of León and Castile united the two kingdoms in the 1030s, and his descendant Alfonso VII was crowned "Emperor of All Spain." The Castilian line ended with Urraca and the succession of her son Alfonso VII of the Burgundian (Ivrea) line. The senior Navarrese branch continued until the death of Sancho VII in 1234, when Navarre passed to the House of Blois.
