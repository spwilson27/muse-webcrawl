Anacardiaceae

Common name: Cashew family
Order: Sapindales
Genera: ~80
Species: ~870

The Anacardiaceae, commonly known as the cashew family or sumac family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Sapindales, comprising roughly 80 genera and 870 species of trees, shrubs and woody vines, mainly in tropical and subtropical regions. Members are characterised by resin canals producing milky or clear, often irritating sap, alternate leaves, and typically drupe fruits.

Notable members

The family includes several economically important fruit and nut trees: mango (Mangifera indica), cashew (Anacardium occidentale), pistachio (Pistacia vera) and marula. The genus Rhus gives the sumacs, and Toxicodendron includes poison ivy, poison oak and poison sumac, whose urushiol oils cause allergic contact dermatitis in many people. Lacquer trees, smoke trees and pepper trees are also members; the resin of some species yields varnishes and lacquers.

Characteristics

Flowers are small, usually five-parted and borne in panicles; the fruit is most often a drupe with resinous mesocarp. Many species contain phenolic compounds in their sap which blacken on exposure to air. The family is closely related to the Burseraceae and Kirkiaceae within the Sapindales, and fossil records extend back to the early Tertiary.
