Line of succession to the Danish throne

The Danish Act of Succession, adopted on 27 March 1953 and most recently amended in 2009, governs the line of succession to the throne of Denmark. Succession is restricted to the descendants of Christian X and his wife, Alexandrine of Mecklenburg-Schwerin, born within approved marriages. Since a 2009 referendum and constitutional amendment, succession follows absolute primogeniture for those born after the change took effect in practice from the generation of Crown Prince Christian's cohort: the eldest child of the sovereign succeeds regardless of gender.

History

For centuries Denmark used agnatic (male-only) succession, replaced in 1853 by a semi-Salic arrangement, and in 1953 the Act of Succession admitted female succession with male-preference primogeniture — the change that allowed Margrethe II to succeed her father Frederik IX in 1972 despite having younger sisters only. The 2009 amendment equalized the position of eldest children. A dynast who marries without the monarch's consent, or whose marriage is not approved, loses succession rights for themselves and their descendants; the monarch must also belong to the Evangelical Lutheran Church.

Current line

Following the accession of Frederik X on 14 January 2024, the first places in the line are:

  1. Crown Prince Christian, eldest child of Frederik X
  2. Princess Isabella
  3. Prince Vincent
  4. Princess Josephine
  5. Prince Joachim, younger brother of Frederik X
  6. Count Nikolai of Monpezat
  7. Count Felix of Monpezat
  8. Count Henrik of Monpezat
  9. Countess Athena of Monpezat
  10. Princess Benedikte, sister of Margrethe II
