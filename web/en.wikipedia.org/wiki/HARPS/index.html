The High Accuracy Radial velocity Planet Searcher (HARPS) is a high-precision echelle planet-finding spectrograph installed at the European Southern Observatory's (ESO) 3.6 m telescope at La Silla Observatory in Chile. It achieved first light in 2002 and was commissioned in 2003. HARPS measures the radial velocity of stars with a precision of about 1 metre per second, allowing it to detect the tiny wobble induced in a star by an orbiting exoplanet.

Instrument

HARPS is a fibre-fed, cross-dispersed echelle spectrograph covering the visible wavelength range from 378 to 691 nm. It is housed in a vacuum vessel and held at a constant temperature to eliminate instrumental drift, and its wavelength scale is calibrated with a thorium-argon lamp. A second fibre can simultaneously record a reference spectrum or the sky background. This design made HARPS for many years the most precise radial-velocity instrument in the world.

Discoveries

HARPS has discovered well over a hundred exoplanets, with a particular strength in low-mass planets — super-Earths and Neptune-mass worlds — that earlier instruments could not detect. Its notable results include the first rocky super-Earth found in a star's habitable zone around Gliese 581, planets of the Gliese 667 C system, and Proxima Centauri b's confirmation work through complementary observations. A northern counterpart, HARPS-N, operates on the Telescopio Nazionale Galileo at La Palma.
