Aconitum napellus

KingdomPlantae
OrderRanunculales
FamilyRanunculaceae
GenusAconitum
SpeciesA. napellus

Aconitum napellus, commonly called monk's-hood, aconite or wolfsbane, is a species of highly toxic flowering plant in the genus Aconitum within the family Ranunculaceae, native to and endemic to western and central Europe. Its distinctive flowers have a hooded upper sepal resembling a monk's cowl.

Description

It is an herbaceous perennial growing to about 1 metre tall, with hairless stems bearing deeply divided leaves and dense racemes of dark purple to bluish-purple flowers in mid-summer.

Toxicity

All parts of the plant contain the alkaloid aconitine, one of the most potent plant neurotoxins known; ingesting even small amounts can cause fatal cardiac arrhythmias. Historically the poison was used on arrowheads and in warfare, and the plant's danger is reflected in folklore associating it with werewolves and witchcraft.

Cultivation

Despite its toxicity, it is widely grown in gardens for its striking flowers, and is also used in traditional and homeopathic medicine in extremely dilute preparations.
