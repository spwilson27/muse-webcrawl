2011 protests

The 2011 protests were a series of protests and revolutions that took place across the world during 2011, most notably the Arab Spring uprisings in the Middle East and North Africa, the Occupy movement in the United States and elsewhere, and anti-austerity movements in Europe.

Background

Protests were driven by a combination of factors: dissatisfaction with authoritarian governments, corruption, unemployment, rising food prices, and the after-effects of the 2008 financial crisis. Social media platforms played a major role in organizing and publicizing demonstrations.

Major movements

  • Arab Spring — beginning with Tunisia in late 2010, protests spread to Egypt, Libya, Syria, Yemen, Bahrain and other countries, toppling several long-standing regimes.
  • Occupy movement — beginning with Occupy Wall Street in September 2011, encampments protesting economic inequality spread to hundreds of cities worldwide.
  • Anti-austerity protests — in Greece, Spain (the Indignados movement) and other European countries, demonstrators opposed government spending cuts.
  • Other protests — significant movements also occurred in Chile (student protests), Israel (social justice protests), India (anti-corruption movement) and Russia (protests over election fraud).

Impact

The protest wave of 2011 reshaped politics in several regions. Time magazine named "The Protester" its Person of the Year for 2011, recognizing the global influence of the demonstrations.
