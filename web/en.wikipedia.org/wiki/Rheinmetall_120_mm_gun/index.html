Rheinmetall 120 mm gun

The Rheinmetall 120 mm gun is a smoothbore tank gun designed and produced in large numbers by the German Rheinmetall-DeTec AG company, reporting to service in 1979 as the main armament of the Leopard 2 main battle tank. It exists in two main barrel lengths, the L/44 and the longer L/55, and has been adopted by many NATO and allied armies.

Development

Development began in the 1960s as Western armies assessed the growing capability of Soviet armour. Rheinmetall produced a new 120 mm smoothbore design firing fin-stabilised ammunition, including armour-piercing fin-stabilized discarding sabot (APFSDS) kinetic-energy penetrators and high-explosive anti-tank (HEAT) rounds. Its ammunition uses semi-combustible cartridge cases.

Variants and users

The L/44 equips the Leopard 2 (up to the A5 standard), the American M1A1 and M1A2 Abrams (built under licence as the M256), the Japanese Type 90, and the South Korean K1A1. The longer L/55, introduced with the Leopard 2A6, offers higher muzzle velocity and range. A further development, the L/55A1, supports newer ammunition such as the DM73.

Ammunition

Successive generations of APFSDS ammunition (DM13, DM23, DM33, DM53, DM63 and DM73 in German service; M829 series in US service) have steadily improved penetration, while programmable high-explosive rounds such as the DM11 add capability against soft targets and helicopters.
