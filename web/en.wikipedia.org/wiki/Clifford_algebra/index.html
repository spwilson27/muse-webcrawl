Clifford algebra

In mathematics, a Clifford algebra is an algebra generated by a vector space equipped with a quadratic form, and is the most general algebra in which multiplication of a vector by itself is governed by that quadratic form. Introduced by the English mathematician William Kingdon Clifford in 1878 — building on the work of Hermann Grassmann and William Rowan Hamilton's quaternions — Clifford algebras unify and generalize the real numbers, complex numbers, quaternions, and several other hypercomplex number systems.

Definition

Given a vector space V over a field F with a quadratic form Q, the Clifford algebra Cl(V, Q) is an associative algebra with identity, together with a linear map from V into it satisfying v2 = Q(v) for every vector v, and a universal property: it is the freest such algebra, in the sense that any other linear map from V into an algebra satisfying the same relation factors uniquely through it.

Concretely, for an orthogonal basis the defining relations say that distinct basis vectors anticommute, while each basis vector squares to its value under the quadratic form. Products of distinct basis vectors (blades) form a basis of the algebra, which has dimension 2n when V has dimension n. Clifford algebras carry a natural Z/2 grading into even and odd parts.

Examples

The Clifford algebra of the one-dimensional space with a negative-definite form is the complex numbers; with a suitable two-dimensional negative-definite form, the even subalgebra gives the quaternions. The algebras of three-dimensional space underlie the Pauli matrices of quantum mechanics, and higher-dimensional versions underlie the Dirac gamma matrices of relativistic quantum theory.

Applications

Clifford algebras are fundamental in differential geometry and mathematical physics: they give rise to spin groups and spinor representations, appear in the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, and provide the algebraic language of geometric algebra used in some formulations of classical mechanics, computer graphics, and robotics. When the quadratic form is degenerate, the construction recovers the exterior algebra of Grassmann.
