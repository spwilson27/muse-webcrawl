Jurassic Park (novel)

Book information
Author: Michael Crichton
Published: 1990
Publisher: Alfred A. Knopf
Genre: Science fiction / techno-thriller
Followed by: The Lost World

Jurassic Park is a 1990 science fiction novel by the American author Michael Crichton. A cautionary tale about genetic engineering and chaos theory, it depicts the collapse of an amusement park of cloned dinosaurs on Isla Nublar, an island off Costa Rica, built by the InGen corporation of billionaire John Hammond.

Plot

After a series of mysterious animal attacks, palaeontologist Alan Grant, palaeobotanist Ellie Sattler and mathematician Ian Malcolm are invited to evaluate the park's safety. When disgruntled programmer Dennis Nedry disables the security systems to steal embryos, the dinosaurs escape, and the visitors must survive as the park's control unravels — an outcome Malcolm argues was inevitable in so complex a system. Crichton frames the chapters with the iterations of a fractal, reflecting the novel's mathematical themes.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was a bestseller and was adapted by Steven Spielberg into the blockbuster 1993 film Jurassic Park. Crichton's 1995 sequel, The Lost World, extended the story, and the books underpin the continuing Jurassic Park media franchise.
