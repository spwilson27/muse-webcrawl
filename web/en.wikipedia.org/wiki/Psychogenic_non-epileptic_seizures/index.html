Psychogenic non-epileptic seizures

Also called: PNES; dissociative seizures; functional seizures
Classification: Functional neurological disorder / conversion disorder
Diagnosis: Video-EEG monitoring (gold standard)
Treatment: Psychotherapy (e.g. CBT)

Psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (PNES) are paroxysmal episodes that resemble epileptic seizures but are not caused by abnormal electrical activity in the brain. They are regarded as a manifestation of functional neurological disorder (conversion disorder), often associated with psychological distress, trauma, and dissociation. Patients are not feigning; the events are involuntary.

Diagnosis

Because PNES can closely mimic epilepsy, misdiagnosis is common and many patients take antiseizure medication for years. The gold-standard diagnosis is capturing a typical event on video-EEG showing no epileptiform activity. Features favouring PNES include long duration, fluctuating course, side-to-side head movements, pelvic thrusting, and eye closure.

Treatment

Management centres on clear, empathetic explanation of the diagnosis, withdrawal of unnecessary antiseizure drugs, and psychotherapy — cognitive behavioural therapy has the strongest trial evidence. Co-occurring anxiety, depression, and PTSD are treated in parallel.
