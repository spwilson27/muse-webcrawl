Maudgalyayana

Maudgalyayana (Pali: Moggallāna), also known as Mahamaudgalyayana, was one of the Buddha's closest disciples and, together with Śāriputra, one of his two chief male disciples. He is regarded as foremost among the Buddha's disciples in supernatural powers (iddhi/siddhi), while Śāriputra was foremost in wisdom.

Life

According to Buddhist texts, Maudgalyayana was born in the village of Kolita (hence his other name, Kolita) near Rājagṛha, on the same day as Śāriputra, with whom he was a lifelong friend. Both were students of the skeptic teacher Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta before converting to Buddhism after hearing the teachings — Śāriputra through the monk Aśvajit, and Maudgalyayana following his friend. Maudgalyayana attained arahantship seven days after ordaining, through mastery of meditation and the four dhyanas.

Supernatural powers and teaching

The texts credit Maudgalyayana with abilities to travel to other realms, read minds, and visit heavens and hells — most famously in accounts of his journeys to report on beings reborn according to their karma. He plays a prominent role in the story of his mother, who was reborn as a hungry ghost; his offerings on her behalf form the basis of the Ullambana (Ghost Festival) tradition in East Asian Buddhism. In Mahayana texts such as the Lotus Sutra, he is predicted to attain Buddhahood.

Death

Traditional accounts say Maudgalyayana was stoned to death by bandits (in some versions, agents of rival ascetics) at Kāḷasilā near Rājagṛha, shortly before the Buddha's own passing. He is venerated throughout the Buddhist world, and his relics, along with those of Śāriputra, are enshrined at Sanchi and elsewhere.
