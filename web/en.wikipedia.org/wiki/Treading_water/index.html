Treading water

Treading water is a technique that allows a swimmer to remain in a vertical position in the water with the head above the surface, without propelling themselves in any direction. It is a fundamental water-safety skill and is used in sports such as water polo and synchronized swimming.

Technique

The most efficient method is the eggbeater kick, in which each leg moves in a circular motion like a breaststroke kick, alternating so that lift is continuous. The arms typically scull — moving side to side in small figure-eight motions — to provide additional lift and stability. Other methods include combining a breaststroke kick with sculling, a flutter kick, or the less efficient vertical "dog paddle".

Uses

Treading water is taught in swimming lessons and lifeguard training as a survival skill, since a person can sustain it far longer than vigorous swimming. In water polo, the eggbeater kick lets players keep their hands free and rise out of the water to pass and shoot. Military and rescue swimmers also train in treading water while supporting weight.

The phrase is also used figuratively, in the sense of maintaining a situation without making progress.
