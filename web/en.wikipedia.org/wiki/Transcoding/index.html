Transcoding

Transcoding is the direct digital-to-digital conversion of one encoding to another, such as for video data files (for example, converting an MPEG-4 file to an H.264 stream), audio files, or character encoding. In most cases, transcoding is performed with lossy compression, causing generation loss; however, lossless transcoding between lossless formats is also possible.

Process

A transcoder decodes the source bitstream into an intermediate raw format and then re-encodes it into the target format, adjusting parameters such as resolution, bitrate, frame rate, or container. Transrating (changing bitrate) and transsizing (changing picture size) are simpler related operations that may avoid a full re-encode. Tools such as FFmpeg and HandBrake are widely used for transcoding.

Applications

Transcoding is central to adaptive streaming services, which prepare multiple versions of each video at different quality levels so playback can adjust to network conditions. It is also used to prepare media for devices with limited codec support, to convert legacy archives, and in peer-to-peer and mobile content delivery. In character encoding, the term describes converting text between encodings such as UTF-8 and Latin-1.

Drawbacks

Because each lossy encode discards information, repeated transcoding degrades quality cumulatively. Transcoding is also computationally expensive, which has driven the development of hardware acceleration in GPUs and dedicated media engines.
