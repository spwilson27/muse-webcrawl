Ennio Morricone

Ennio Morricone (10 November 1928 – 6 July 2020) was an Italian composer, orchestrator and conductor who composed over 400 scores for cinema and television, as well as more than 100 classical works. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest film composers of all time, best known for his scores to Sergio Leone's Spaghetti Westerns, including The Good, the Bad and the Ugly (1966).

Early life

Born in Rome, Morricone studied trumpet and composition at the Accademia Nazionale di Santa Cecilia. He began as a jazz trumpeter and arranger of popular songs for Italian radio and television before turning to film scoring in 1961.

Career

His collaboration with schoolfriend Sergio Leone created the sound of the Spaghetti Western: whistling, whip cracks, electric guitar and ocarina in A Fistful of Dollars (1964), For a Few Dollars More (1965) and Once Upon a Time in the West (1968). Later acclaimed scores include The Mission (1986), The Untouchables (1987), Cinema Paradiso (1988), Malèna (2000) and Quentin Tarantino's The Hateful Eight (2015), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Original Score at age 87, having received an Honorary Oscar in 2007.

Legacy

Morricone's main theme for The Good, the Bad and the Ugly is among the most recognisable in cinema. He influenced composers and rock and metal musicians alike, conducted concerts worldwide into his 90s, and is commemorated in Rome, where he lived his whole life.
