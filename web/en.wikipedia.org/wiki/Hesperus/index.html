Hesperus

In Greek mythology, Hesperus (Greek: Ἕσπερος, Hésperos, "evening") is the Evening Star, the planet Venus in the evening. He is the son of the dawn goddess Eos and either Astraeus or Cephalus, and the brother of Phosphorus (Eosphorus), the Morning Star. The Roman equivalent is Vesper, from the Latin vesper, meaning "evening".

Mythology

Hesperus was described as the father of Daedalion and of the Hesperides, and was sometimes identified with Atlas or placed among the stars after death. In one account, Hesperus climbed Mount Atlas to observe the stars and vanished in a storm, thereafter shining as the brightest star in the evening sky, watched by his father.

Hesperus is Phosphorus

The Greeks eventually recognised that the evening star and the morning star were the same body. The identity "Hesperus is Phosphorus" became a classic example in philosophy of language: Gottlob Frege used the pair (as Abendstern and Morgenstern) to distinguish sense (Sinn) from reference (Bedeutung), since the two names refer to the same planet but present it in different ways.

Legacy

The name survives in terms such as hesperian (western/evening), in the Hesperides and the golden apples of their garden, and in planetary nomenclature for Venus.
