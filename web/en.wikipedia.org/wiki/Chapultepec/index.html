Chapultepec, more commonly called the "Bosque de Chapultepec" (Chapultepec Forest), is one of the largest city parks in Mexico City. Divided into four sections, it contains some of Mexico's most visited sites, including the Chapultepec Zoo, the Museo Nacional de Antropología, and Chapultepec Castle. The name derives from the Nahuatl Chapoltepēc, "at the grasshopper hill".

History

The hill was a retreat for Aztec rulers; Moctezuma I built aqueducts carrying the hill's spring water to Tenochtitlan, and Nezahualcoyotl landscaped the grounds. During the colonial period the viceroys built Chapultepec Castle (1785 onward). The park was the site of the Battle of Chapultepec (1847) during the Mexican–American War, in which the young cadets known as the Niños Héroes died defending the castle, then a military academy.

Attractions

The first section contains the zoo, the Museum of Anthropology, the Rufino Tamayo Museum, the Museum of Modern Art, and the Los Pinos cultural complex (the former presidential residence, opened to the public in 2018). Lakes, fountains such as the Fuente de Tláloc, and the Altar a la Patria monument to the Niños Héroes are major landmarks.
