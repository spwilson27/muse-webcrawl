The British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA) is an independent British trade association and charity that supports, develops and promotes the arts of film, television and video games in the United Kingdom. In addition to its annual award ceremonies, BAFTA runs a year-round programme of learning events and initiatives, including masterclasses, scholarships and mentoring schemes.

History

BAFTA was founded in 1947 as the British Film Academy, when David Lean and other leading figures of the British film industry brought together existing guilds. The Academy's first president was David Lean. In 1958 it merged with the Guild of Television Producers and Directors to form the Society of Film and Television Arts, and in 1976 it became the British Academy of Film and Television Arts, opening its headquarters at 195 Piccadilly in London. The distinctive theatrical-mask trophy was designed by American sculptor Mitzi Cunliffe in 1955.

Awards

BAFTA presents several major award ceremonies each year. The British Academy Film Awards ("the BAFTAs"), held in London during the international awards season, honour the best national and international films of the previous year. The British Academy Television Awards and Television Craft Awards cover UK television; the British Academy Games Awards, established in 2004, recognise video games; and BAFTA Scotland and BAFTA Cymru run ceremonies for Scottish and Welsh productions. Separate ceremonies exist for children's media. Winners are chosen by the Academy's voting membership of industry professionals.

Organisation

BAFTA is a membership organisation with branches in Scotland and Wales and outposts in Los Angeles (BAFTA Los Angeles) and New York. Its work is funded by membership subscriptions, donations and revenue from its awards ceremonies and commercial activities at its Piccadilly headquarters.
