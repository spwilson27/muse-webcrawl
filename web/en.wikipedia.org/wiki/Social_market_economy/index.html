Social market economy

The social market economy (German: soziale Marktwirtschaft), also called Rhine capitalism, is a socioeconomic model combining a free-market capitalist economic system alongside social policies and enough regulation to establish fair competition within the market and a generally welfare state.

Social market economy
OriginWest Germany, post-1945
Key figuresLudwig Erhard, Alfred Müller-Armack, Walter Eucken
SchoolOrdoliberalism

Origins

The concept was coined by Alfred Müller-Armack and implemented by Economics Minister (later Chancellor) Ludwig Erhard after the 1948 currency reform. Rooted in Freiburg-school ordoliberalism, it sought a "third way" between laissez-faire capitalism and socialist central planning: the state sets and enforces the rules (Ordnungspolitik) – competition law, stable money, open markets – but does not direct production, while social insurance, co-determination and redistribution cushion market outcomes.

Influence

It underpinned West Germany's Wirtschaftswunder (economic miracle), became the economic constitution of reunified Germany, influenced the European Union's stated aim of a "highly competitive social market economy" (Lisbon Treaty), and contrasts with Anglo-American liberal market models.
