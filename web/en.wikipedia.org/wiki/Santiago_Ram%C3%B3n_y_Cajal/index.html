Santiago Ramón y Cajal

Santiago Ramón y Cajal
Born: 1 May 1852, Petilla de Aragón, Spain
Died: 17 October 1934, Madrid
Known for: Neuron doctrine; Nobel Prize 1906

Santiago Ramón y Cajal (1852–1934) was a Spanish neuroscientist and histologist whose pioneering studies of the microscopic structure of the brain made him a founder of modern neuroscience.

Work

Using and improving Camillo Golgi's silver-staining method, Cajal produced extraordinarily detailed drawings of nerve cells, showing that the nervous system is composed of discrete individual cells — neurons — rather than a continuous network. This "neuron doctrine" became the foundation of modern brain science. He also described the growth cone of developing axons and inferred that signals flow in one direction through neurons.

Honours

Cajal shared the 1906 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Golgi. He held chairs at Valencia, Barcelona and Madrid, founded a school of Spanish histology, and wrote extensively, including scientific works and memoirs. His drawings remain iconic in neuroscience.
