Combing

Combing
IndustryTextile manufacturing
PurposeFibre preparation for spinning

Combing is a method for preparing carded fibre for spinning, in which the fibres are passed through a series of straight, fine teeth or pins that parallelise the fibres and remove short fibres (noil), impurities, and neps. The result is a smooth, strong, lustrous yarn.

Wool combing

In wool processing, combing distinguishes worsted production from woollen production: combed wool top consists of long, aligned fibres and spins into smooth, durable worsted yarns used for fine suiting. Traditional hand combs were pairs of heated, multi-row combs; mechanised systems include the Noble and French (rectilinear) combs.

Cotton combing

Combed cotton, from which a proportion of short fibres has been removed, is softer and stronger than carded cotton and is used for high-quality shirting and underwear fabrics.
