Cannabis in the United States

The use, sale, and possession of cannabis in the United States are governed by a patchwork of state and federal law. Cannabis remains illegal under federal law as a Schedule I substance under the Controlled Substances Act of 1970, but most states have legalised it in some form: as of the mid-2020s roughly half the states permit recreational use by adults and a large majority allow medical use, creating a persistent conflict between state and federal policy.

History

Hemp was grown in colonial America for fibre, and cannabis preparations were common medicines in the 19th century. Restriction began at state level in the early 1900s and culminated federally in the Marihuana Tax Act of 1937. The Controlled Substances Act of 1970 classified cannabis in Schedule I — high abuse potential, no accepted medical use — and the "war on drugs" era brought mandatory minimum sentences and mass arrests. The Shafer Commission's 1972 recommendation of decriminalisation was rejected by President Nixon.

State legalisation

California's Proposition 215 (1996) made it the first state to legalise medical cannabis. Colorado and Washington became the first states to legalise recreational use, by ballot initiative in 2012, and retail sales began in 2014. Legalisation has since spread state by state — including New York, Illinois, Virginia and Ohio — while a minority of states retain full prohibition. State markets are licensed and taxed, though federal illegality complicates banking, taxation (Internal Revenue Code §280E) and interstate commerce.

Federal policy and reform efforts

Federal enforcement against state-legal activity has varied by administration, guided at times by memos such as the 2013 Cole Memo (rescinded 2018). Congress has passed appropriations riders limiting interference with state medical programmes. In 2024 the Department of Justice proposed moving cannabis to Schedule III following a Department of Health and Human Services recommendation, a rescheduling process that remained politically contested. Public opinion shifted decisively: Gallup has recorded majority support for legalisation since 2013, reaching roughly 70% in the 2020s.

Effects and debates

Research and policy debate continue over legalisation's impacts on public health, adolescent use, impaired driving, crime, tax revenue and racial disparities in enforcement. Hemp — cannabis with less than 0.3% THC — was legalised federally by the Agriculture Improvement Act of 2018, spurring a large market in CBD and, controversially, intoxicating hemp-derived cannabinoids.
