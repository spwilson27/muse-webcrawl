String trio

A string trio performing: violin, viola and cello

A string trio is a group of three string players or a piece of music written for three string instruments. Most commonly, the ensemble consists of a violin, a viola, and a cello — the same combination, minus one violin, as the more famous string quartet. String trios are a significant form of chamber music, though their repertoire is smaller than that of the quartet.

History

The earliest string trios emerged in the Baroque era, often in the form of trio sonatas, though those usually required a continuo part. The classical string trio for violin, viola and cello developed in the mid-18th century alongside the string quartet. Joseph Haydn wrote a number of early trios, and Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart composed the Divertimento in E-flat major, K. 563 (1788), widely considered one of the greatest works in the genre.

Ludwig van Beethoven wrote five string trios, including the three of Op. 9, which he regarded among his finest early works before turning to the quartet. Franz Schubert left two trios, the second of which (D. 929's companion piece D. 581 and the single-movement D. 471) remains popular. In the 20th century, composers such as Arnold Schoenberg — whose String Trio, Op. 45 (1946), is a landmark of twelve-tone chamber music — Alfred Schnittke, and Sofia Gubaidulina expanded the repertoire.

Other combinations

Less common combinations include three violins, or two violins and cello. The string trio should be distinguished from the piano trio, in which a piano joins two string instruments.

Notable works

  • Mozart: Divertimento in E-flat major, K. 563
  • Beethoven: String Trios, Op. 3, Op. 8, Op. 9 Nos. 1–3
  • Schubert: String Trios D. 471 and D. 581
  • Dohnányi: Serenade in C major, Op. 10
  • Schoenberg: String Trio, Op. 45
