Frank Olson

Frank Rudolph Emmanuel Olson (July 17, 1910 – November 28, 1953) was an American bacteriologist and Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) employee who worked on biological warfare research. His death in 1953, after being covertly dosed with LSD as part of the CIA's MKUltra programme, became one of the most controversial episodes of the Cold War.

Career

Olson earned a doctorate in bacteriology from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. During the Second World War he served in the U.S. Army Chemical Corps and afterwards worked at the Army's biological warfare centre at Camp Detrick (later Fort Detrick), Maryland, on aerosol and toxin research. He later became acting chief of the Special Operations Division and worked jointly with the CIA on interrogation and mind-control experiments.

LSD dosing

On November 19, 1953, at a retreat at Deep Creek Lake, Maryland, CIA officer Sidney Gottlieb served Olson and several colleagues drinks laced with LSD without their knowledge. Olson reacted badly, becoming agitated, depressed and paranoid in the following days. He told his superiors he wanted to resign.

Death

The CIA arranged for Olson to be taken to New York City for psychiatric treatment. In the early hours of November 28, 1953, he went through the window of his tenth-floor room at the Hotel Statler and died. The death was reported to his family as an accident or suicide.

Revelations and investigations

The LSD dosing was only revealed in 1975 by the Rockefeller Commission's investigation into CIA activities. President Gerald Ford apologised to the Olson family, and Congress passed a private bill granting them compensation. Questions about the circumstances of Olson's death have persisted; a 1994 exhumation and forensic examination by James Starrs found evidence of a blow to the head before the fall, though a 1997 review by the Manhattan district attorney's office did not result in charges. The case is frequently cited in discussions of MKUltra and CIA accountability.
