Psychohistory

Psychohistory is an interdisciplinary field that combines the methodologies of historiography with the insights of psychology — particularly psychoanalysis — to understand the emotional origins of the social and political behavior of individuals, groups, and nations, past and present. The term is also used for a fictional science in Isaac Asimov's Foundation series.

History of the field

Sigmund Freud's Leonardo da Vinci and a Memory of His Childhood (1910) is an early example of psychobiography. Erik Erikson's studies of Gandhi and Martin Luther in the 1950s and 1960s gave the approach academic prominence. In 1973 Lloyd deMause founded the Journal of Psychohistory and articulated a theory of psychogenic modes of childrearing evolving through history. Critics have questioned psychohistory's reliance on retrospective psychological interpretation and its testability, and it remains a minority approach within academic history.

In fiction

In Asimov's Foundation novels, psychohistory is a mathematical science developed by Hari Seldon that combines history, sociology, and statistics to predict the future behavior of very large populations, much as the kinetic theory of gases predicts aggregate behavior without tracking individual molecules. Seldon uses it to foresee the fall of the Galactic Empire and to shorten the ensuing dark age.
