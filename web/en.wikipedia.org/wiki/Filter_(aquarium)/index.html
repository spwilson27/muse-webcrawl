Filter (aquarium)

Aquarium filters are devices used to remove excess food, decaying organic matter, and dissolved waste products from water in aquaria, keeping the water clear and its chemistry safe for fish and invertebrates. Filtration is generally divided into three types: mechanical, biological, and chemical.

Types of filtration

Mechanical filtration strains particles from the water with foam, floss, or filter socks. Biological filtration provides surfaces on which nitrifying bacteria colonize; these bacteria carry out the nitrogen cycle, converting toxic ammonia from fish waste into nitrite and then into much less harmful nitrate. Chemical filtration uses media such as activated carbon to adsorb dissolved compounds.

Filter designs

Common designs include internal power filters, hang-on-back filters, canister filters that sit beneath the tank, undergravel filters, sponge filters driven by air, trickle (wet/dry) filters, and sumps — especially in marine and reef aquariums, where protein skimmers and refugiums supplement the system. Filter media require periodic cleaning or replacement, though biological media are rinsed gently to preserve the bacterial colonies.
