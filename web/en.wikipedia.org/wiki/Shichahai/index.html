Shichahai

Shichahai (什刹海) is a scenic lake district in northwestern central Beijing, China, consisting of three lakes — Qianhai (Front Lake), Houhai (Back Lake), and Xihai (West Lake) — together with the historic neighbourhoods of hutongs, courtyards, temples, and princely mansions that surround them. The name is sometimes said to derive from the temples ("ten temples") once found around the lakes.

History

The lakes were once part of the old course of the Gaoliang River and formed the northern terminus of the Grand Canal during the Yuan dynasty, when grain barges from the south unloaded at the bustling markets on their shores. In the Ming and Qing dynasties the area became a fashionable residential district, home to officials' mansions including the Prince Gong Mansion, and to temples such as Guanghua Temple. The Silver Ingot Bridge (Yinding Bridge) spanning the narrow channel between Qianhai and Houhai is a noted vantage point, traditionally said to frame a view of the Western Hills.

Present day

Today Shichahai is a protected historical and cultural area, popular for its bars and restaurants along Houhai, rickshaw tours of the hutongs, and winter ice skating on the frozen lakes. Landmarks in and around the district include the Drum and Bell Towers, the former residence of Song Qingling, and numerous preserved siheyuan courtyard houses.
