Schottische

Schottische
TypePartner folk / ballroom dance
Time signature2/4 or 4/4
OriginCentral Europe, early 19th century
RelatedPolka, écossaise

The schottische is a partnered dance performed to music in 2/4 or 4/4 time, combining a series of stepping movements with a hopping turn. Despite its name — German for "Scottish" — the dance originated in central Europe in the early nineteenth century and became a fashionable ballroom dance across Europe and the Americas in the 1840s and 1850s, before settling into folk-dance traditions.

Steps

In its common form the couple, in ballroom hold, takes three steps (or a step-close-step pattern) to the side, hops, repeats in the opposite direction, then performs four hopping turns. Regional varieties include the German and Bohemian forms, the Scandinavian schottis, the Finnish sottiisi, and the Texas schottische of American old-time and western dance. In Britain the dance was sometimes called the "German polka".

Music and legacy

Schottische tunes are slower and more deliberate than the polka to which the dance is related. The rhythm influenced popular music: the Brazilian xote derives its name and pattern from the schottische, and the dance survives in contra dance, Scandinavian folk dance, and country-western repertoires.
