Bilqis

Bilqis is the name given in Islamic tradition to the Queen of Sheba.

Bilqis (Arabic: بلقيس, Bilqīs) is the name given in Islamic tradition to the queen of the Yemeni kingdom of Saba (Sheba), known in the Hebrew Bible as the Queen of Sheba. Her story is told in the Quran in Surah An-Naml (chapter 27), though the Quran does not give her name; it was supplied by later commentators and historians.

In the Quran

In the Quranic account, a hoopoe bird reports to the prophet-king Solomon (Sulaiman) that it has found a prosperous land ruled by a queen whose people worship the sun. Solomon sends the queen a letter inviting her to submit to God. After consulting her counsellors, she sends Solomon lavish gifts, which he rejects, and then travels to his court herself. Solomon has her throne brought to his palace and disguised; when she recognises it, and after entering a palace whose floor of glass she mistakes for water, she repents of sun-worship and submits with Solomon to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.

Later tradition

Medieval Muslim commentators elaborated her genealogy — some making her the daughter of a human king and a jinn mother — and supplied the name Bilqis. Tales grew up around her wisdom, her riddles to Solomon, and the marvels of her journey. In Yemen she remains a national symbol, associated with the ancient Sabaean capital of Marib and its dam; Ethiopian tradition, by contrast, knows the queen as Makeda and makes her the ancestress of the Ethiopian emperors through her union with Solomon.
