World3

World3 is a system dynamics model of interactions between population, industrial growth, food production, and limits in the Earth's ecosystem. It was created by a team at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology led by Donella and Dennis Meadows and Jørgen Randers for the Club of Rome, and formed the basis of the influential 1972 book The Limits to Growth.

The model

World3 — the third version of Jay Forrester's World models, programmed in the DYNAMO simulation language — aggregates the world into interacting sectors: population, capital (industrial and services), agriculture, nonrenewable resources, and persistent pollution. Feedback loops and delays between sectors drive the model's behaviour: exponential growth pressing against finite resource stocks and pollution sinks tends to produce "overshoot and collapse" of population and industrial output sometime in the 21st century in the model's "standard run".

Scenarios

The book presented twelve scenarios. In business-as-usual runs, growth is checked by resource depletion or pollution; stabilisation scenarios — combining population stabilisation, reduced consumption, pollution control, and investment in resource efficiency — allowed a sustainable equilibrium. The authors stressed that the model did not predict exact outcomes but illustrated modes of behaviour of the global system.

Reception and updates

The Limits to Growth sold millions of copies and provoked intense debate. Critics charged that the model underestimated technology and market adaptation; supporters pointed to the robustness of the overshoot pattern. The team published updates in 1992 (Beyond the Limits) and 2004 (The Limits to Growth: The 30-Year Update). Later comparisons by researchers such as Graham Turner (2008, 2014) found that historical data from 1970 to 2000 tracked the standard run more closely than alternative scenarios.
