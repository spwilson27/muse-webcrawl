Edmund Gerald "Pat" Brown (April 21, 1905 – February 16, 1996) was an American lawyer and politician who served as the 32nd governor of California from 1959 to 1967. A Democrat, he had previously served as district attorney of San Francisco and as attorney general of California.

Governorship

Brown's two terms were marked by an ambitious program of public works and institution building. He championed the California State Water Project, expanded the state's freeway system, and presided over a major growth of the University of California and state university systems under the 1960 Master Plan for Higher Education. He signed the Rumford Fair Housing Act and oversaw the state's response to the Watts riots of 1965.

Brown defeated former vice president Richard Nixon in the 1962 gubernatorial election. In 1966 he lost his bid for a third term to Ronald Reagan. His son, Jerry Brown, later served as governor of California in 1975–1983 and again in 2011–2019, making them one of the most prominent political families in the state. See also Jerry Brown.
