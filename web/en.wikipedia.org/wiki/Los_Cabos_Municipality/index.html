Los Cabos Municipality

Los Cabos is a municipality located at the southern tip of Mexico's Baja California Peninsula, in the state of Baja California Sur. It encompasses the two main towns of San José del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas, connected by a resort corridor of some 32 kilometres along the coast where the Sea of Cortez (Gulf of California) meets the Pacific Ocean.

Geography

The municipality covers the southernmost portion of the peninsula. Its landscape is arid desert meeting rocky coastline, noted for the granite arch of El Arco at Land's End in Cabo San Lucas. The municipal seat is San José del Cabo, the older of the two towns, founded around a Jesuit mission established in 1730.

Economy and tourism

Once a quiet area of fishing villages and ranches, Los Cabos was transformed from the 1970s onwards by federal tourism development. It is now one of Mexico's premier resort destinations, known for sport fishing, golf courses, beaches and whale watching. Los Cabos International Airport serves the region. The municipality's population has grown rapidly with the tourism industry, reaching roughly 350,000 in the 2020 census. See also Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo.
