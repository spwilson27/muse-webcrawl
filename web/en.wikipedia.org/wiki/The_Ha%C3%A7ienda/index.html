The Haçienda

The Haçienda was a nightclub in Manchester, England, opened in 1982 by Factory Records and the band New Order. Officially known as FAC 51 The Haçienda, it became one of the most famous venues of the acid house era and the heart of the "Madchester" scene.

LocationWhitworth Street West, Manchester
OwnerFactory Records
Opened21 May 1982
Closed1997

History

The club was funded largely from New Order's record sales and managed by Rob Gretton, with Factory founder Tony Wilson overseeing the project. Designed by Ben Kelly, the interior featured industrial styling, hazard stripes and a former yacht showroom's open floor. In its early years the club lost money and hosted gigs by bands such as The Smiths and an early performance by Madonna.

Acid house era

From 1986, DJs including Mike Pickering and Graeme Park championed Chicago house music, and nights such as Nude made the Haçienda central to the acid house explosion of 1988's "Second Summer of Love". Resident acts and visitors helped launch the careers of groups associated with Madchester, including Happy Mondays and The Stone Roses. Gang-related violence and financial problems plagued the club in the 1990s; it closed in 1997 and the building was demolished in 2002, replaced by apartments retaining the Haçienda name.

Legacy

The Haçienda is widely credited with popularising house music in Britain and remains a symbol of Manchester's musical heritage, commemorated in exhibitions, books and the film 24 Hour Party People.
