Bielefeld is a city in the Ostwestfalen-Lippe region in the north-east of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. With a population of about 340,000, it is the most populous city in the region and the economic and cultural centre of East Westphalia. It lies on both sides of the Teutoburg Forest.

History

Bielefeld was founded in 1214 by Count Hermann IV of Ravensberg as a fortified trading town at a pass through the Teutoburg Forest. The Sparrenburg Castle was built in the 13th century to guard the pass. From the 16th century the town became a centre of the linen trade; industrialisation in the 19th century brought spinning and weaving mills, sewing machines, bicycles and food processing. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt in a modern style.

Economy and institutions

Major companies headquartered in Bielefeld include the food manufacturer Dr. Oetker, the engineering firm Schüco, the media group Bertelsmann (in nearby Gütersloh), and the v. Bodelschwingh Bethel institutions, one of Europe's largest diaconal organisations. Bielefeld University, founded in 1969, is a major employer and research centre. The local football club is DSC Arminia Bielefeld.

The Bielefeld conspiracy

Since 1994 Bielefeld has been the subject of a satirical internet conspiracy theory (the Bielefeldverschwörung) claiming that the city does not exist and that evidence of it is fabricated by "them." The joke originated on Usenet and became one of the best-known German internet memes; the city embraced it with a marketing campaign, offering a €1 million prize in 2019 to anyone who could prove the city's non-existence.
