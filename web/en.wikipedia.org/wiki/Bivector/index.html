In mathematics, a bivector is an object of grade 2 in geometric algebra and exterior algebra, formed from the wedge product (exterior product) of two vectors. A simple bivector a ∧ b can be interpreted geometrically as an oriented plane segment: a piece of a plane with a definite area, equal to the area of the parallelogram spanned by a and b, and with an orientation given by the rotational sense from a to b.

Properties

Unlike vectors, bivectors in dimensions higher than three cannot in general be associated with a single axis. In three dimensions, the space of bivectors is three-dimensional, and a bivector can be identified with a vector perpendicular to its plane (its Hodge dual), which is the basis of the cross product and of the pseudovector representation of angular quantities such as torque and angular momentum.

A general bivector is a sum of simple bivectors. In four dimensions, a bivector need not be simple: it can require two plane segments, related to the self-dual and anti-self-dual decomposition important in the study of the electromagnetic field tensor, which is itself a bivector-valued object in spacetime algebra.

Applications

Bivectors generate rotations: the exponential of a bivector (suitably scaled) is a rotor, which in three dimensions reproduces the quaternion formula for rotation. Angular velocity, angular momentum and the magnetic field are naturally described as bivectors in geometric algebra, avoiding the axial-vector convention of Gibbsian vector analysis.
