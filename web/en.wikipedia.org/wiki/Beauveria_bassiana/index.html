Beauveria bassiana

A grasshopper killed by B. bassiana, showing the white muscardine growth.

Beauveria bassiana is an entomopathogenic fungus that grows naturally in soils throughout the world and acts as a parasite on various arthropod species, causing white muscardine disease. It belongs to the group of fungi used as biological insecticides to control pests such as termites, thrips, whiteflies, aphids and various beetles.

History

The species is named after the Italian entomologist Agostino Bassi, who in 1835 demonstrated that the muscardine disease of silkworms was caused by a microorganism — a landmark in the development of the germ theory of disease. The fungus was originally described as Botrytis bassiana before being transferred to the genus Beauveria, named in honour of the French mycologist Jean Beauverie.

Mode of action

Spores landing on an insect's cuticle germinate and penetrate into the body cavity, where the fungus proliferates, producing toxins and eventually killing the host within days. After death, a white mould grows out of the cadaver and releases new spores. Because the fungus can infect beneficial insects as well as pests, its application requires care, and it can occasionally cause allergic reactions in humans, though infections are extremely rare.
