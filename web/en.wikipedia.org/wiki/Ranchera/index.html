Ranchera

Ranchera (pronounced [ranˈtʃeɾa]) is a genre of traditional Mexican music, characterised emotionally by themes of love, patriotism and nature, and musically by its performance with mariachi ensembles. The term derives from rancho ("ranch"), as the songs originated among rural workers and were sung on the ranches of Mexico. Ranchera rhythms draw from European forms such as the waltz, polka and bolero.

A mariachi band, the classic ensemble for ranchera songs

Characteristics

Ranchera songs are typically written in simple verse-and-refrain structures and performed at slow to brisk tempos in 2/4 or 3/4 time. The traditional instrumentation is the mariachi: violins, trumpets, classical guitars, vihuela and guitarrón, with the solo singer — often wearing the embroidered charro suit — delivering the song with great emotional intensity, including the famous grito (shout) of "¡Ay, ay, ay!" Lyrics celebrate rural life, unrequited or lost love, drinking, honour and the homeland.

History

The genre took shape in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and spread after the Mexican Revolution as a symbol of national identity. It reached its golden age between the 1930s and 1960s through Mexican cinema's cine de oro and the recordings of iconic singers such as Jorge Negrete, Pedro Infante, Javier Solís and José Alfredo Jiménez — the latter the genre's greatest songwriter, author of standards like "El Rey". Later stars include Vicente Fernández, known as the "King of Ranchera", Juan Gabriel, Rocío Dúrcal and Lola Beltrán ("Lola la Grande").

In 2011 UNESCO inscribed mariachi music, central to the ranchera tradition, on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
