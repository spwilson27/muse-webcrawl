Southern Han

Southern Han (南漢)
Existed: 917–971
Capital: Xingwang Fu (Guangzhou)
Founder: Liu Yan (Emperor Gaozu)
Last ruler: Liu Chang

Southern Han (Chinese: 南漢; pinyin: Nán Hàn), originally called Great Yue (大越), was one of the Ten Kingdoms that existed during the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period of Chinese history. It was founded by Liu Yan in 917 in the far south, ruling the area of modern Guangdong and Guangxi, with its capital at Xingwang Fu — present-day Guangzhou (Canton).

History

The regime grew out of the military governorship (jiedushi) of Qinghai held by Liu Yin, whose brother Liu Yan succeeded him in 911 and declared himself emperor in 917, first of Yue and from 918 of Han. Guangzhou's maritime trade with Southeast Asia, India, and the Arab world made Southern Han wealthy; its court was noted for luxury, including palaces decorated with pearls and the extensive use of eunuchs in administration.

The last emperor, Liu Chang (r. 958–971), required officials to be castrated before serving and governed largely through palace favourites. In 970 the expanding Song dynasty invaded; Liu Chang surrendered in 971, and Southern Han became the fourth of the southern kingdoms absorbed into the Song reunification of China.

Archaeology

The mausoleums of the Southern Han emperors at Guangzhou — the Kangling and Deling tombs of Liu Yan and his father — were excavated in 2003 and are preserved at the Museum of the Mausoleum of the Nanyue King site area as the Southern Han Mausoleums Museum.
