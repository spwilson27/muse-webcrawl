Ring-tailed lemur

The ring-tailed lemur (Lemur catta) is a medium- to larger-sized strepsirrhine primate and the most internationally recognized lemur species, owing to its long, black-and-white ringed tail. It is endemic to the island of Madagascar.

Description

Adults weigh 2 to 3.5 kg and measure 39–46 cm in body length, with a tail longer than the body at 56–62 cm. The fur is grey to rosy brown on the back, white on the face and underside, with dark triangular eye patches and a black nose.

Behaviour and ecology

Unlike most lemurs, ring-tailed lemurs spend much time on the ground in the dry forests and spiny scrub of southern Madagascar. They live in multi-male, multi-female troops of 6 to 30, dominated by females. They are highly social and vocal, sunbathe in a characteristic upright posture on cold mornings, and males engage in "stink fights", scent-marking their tails and waving them at rivals. Their diet is mainly fruit and leaves, especially tamarind.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List due to habitat loss, hunting, and the pet trade; wild populations declined sharply in the 21st century, though it breeds readily in zoos worldwide.
