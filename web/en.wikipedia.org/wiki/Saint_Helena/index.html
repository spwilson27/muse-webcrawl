Saint Helena is a volcanic island in the South Atlantic Ocean, one of the most remote inhabited places on Earth. Discovered uninhabited by the Portuguese in 1502, it became an East India Company refreshment station on the route to Asia — Charles Darwin visited in 1836, near the end of the Beagle voyage. After the Suez Canal shortened sea routes, the island declined; for most of its modern history it was reachable only by ship (the RMS St Helena, retired 2018), until Saint Helena Airport opened to scheduled flights in 2017, ending one of the world's great travel odysseys.

Napoleon's exile

The island's fame rests on Napoleon Bonaparte, who was exiled here after the Hundred Days and Elba, arriving in October 1815. Confined at Longwood House under Governor Hudson Lowe, with whom he feuded constantly, Napoleon dictated his memoirs and legend — the Mémorial de Sainte-Hélène became a bestseller that rebuilt his reputation as a liberal hero — and died on 5 May 1821, aged 51 (the official finding was stomach cancer; arsenic-poisoning theories persist but are not the consensus). His tomb in the Valley of the Willows was emptied in 1840 when France brought his remains back to Paris (the retour des cendres). Longwood and the tomb remain French sovereign property on British soil, tended by a French consul — the island's principal tourist draw, along with Jacob's Ladder (699 steps), endemic wildlife and whale sharks.
