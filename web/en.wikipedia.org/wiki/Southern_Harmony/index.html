The Southern Harmony and Musical Companion is a shape-note hymn and tune book compiled by William "Singin' Billy" Walker and first published in 1835. It is one of the most influential of the southern tune books and remains in use in singing communities, particularly in Benton, Kentucky, where an annual "Big Singing" has been held since 1884.

Contents and notation

The book uses the four-shape patent-note system, in which noteheads are printed in different shapes for the syllables fa, sol, la and mi, allowing singers to sight-read parts in the fasola tradition of Sacred Harp singing. Walker's compilation mixed New England psalmody, folk hymns from the southern oral tradition and his own compositions. Several tunes first printed in Southern Harmony — including arrangements associated with Walker — later entered the standard hymn repertoire; the tune "Wondrous Love" is among its best-known settings. Walker claimed the tune "Amazing Grace" (New Britain) in his book, which helped popularise the melody now universally linked to John Newton's hymn.

Later editions and influence

Walker revised the book through several editions up to 1867. The 1854 edition introduced the appendix style of later printings. Although the related Sacred Harp (1844) eventually eclipsed it in the singing-school tradition, Southern Harmony singings continue in Kentucky and elsewhere, and the book was reprinted in facsimile in the 20th century. A copy was famously carried by composer William Duckworth and others studying American vernacular music.
