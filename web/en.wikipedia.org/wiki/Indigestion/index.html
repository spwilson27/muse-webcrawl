Indigestion

Indigestion (dyspepsia)
Specialty: Gastroenterology
Symptoms: Upper abdominal pain or burning, bloating, early fullness, nausea, belching
Causes: Functional (no structural cause), GERD, peptic ulcers, gastritis, medications
Treatment: Lifestyle measures, antacids, H2 blockers, proton-pump inhibitors

Indigestion, also known as dyspepsia, is a condition characterised by discomfort or pain in the upper abdomen, often accompanied by bloating, early satiety (feeling full quickly), nausea, belching and heartburn. It is extremely common, affecting a substantial fraction of adults each year.

Causes

Most cases are functional dyspepsia, in which no structural abnormality is found. Organic causes include gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD), peptic ulcer disease, gastritis (including that caused by Helicobacter pylori infection), gallstones and side effects of medicines such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. "Alarm" features — unintentional weight loss, difficulty swallowing, persistent vomiting, anaemia or blood in the stool or vomit — warrant prompt investigation, as they can signal serious disease including stomach cancer.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation may include testing for H. pylori ("test and treat") and upper endoscopy in older patients or those with alarm features. Treatment addresses the cause where one is found and otherwise includes dietary and lifestyle measures (smaller meals, avoiding late meals and trigger foods), acid suppression with antacids, H2-receptor antagonists or proton-pump inhibitors, and eradication therapy for H. pylori.
