The Second Ring Road (Chinese: 二环路; pinyin: Èr Huán Lù) is an urban expressway ring road in Beijing, China. It encircles the old city, roughly following the line of the former Ming dynasty city walls and their moat, and forms the innermost of Beijing's concentric ring roads. The road is approximately 32.7 kilometres (20.3 mi) long and has no traffic lights along its main carriageway, making it a fully controlled-access expressway.

History

The route's eastern and western sections were built in the 1980s along the line of the demolished city walls, whose course had earlier been used by the Beijing Subway's Line 2. In September 1992 the ring was completed as China's first urban road to be entirely free of grade crossings and traffic signals. Bridges and interchanges were added at major junctions, including with the Chang'an Avenue corridor and the roads radiating toward the airport and the city's railway stations.

Route

The ring is conventionally divided into four sections named for the compass points: the East Second Ring Road, South Second Ring Road, West Second Ring Road and North Second Ring Road. Landmarks along or near the route include the Bell and Drum Towers to the north, the Yonghe (Lama) Temple, Beijing railway station to the southeast, and the Beijing West railway station corridor to the southwest. Because it runs at the edge of the historic core, the Second Ring Road marks an important boundary in Beijing's traffic restriction policies and license-plate rationing schemes.

Traffic

The Second Ring Road is notorious for severe congestion, being one of the most heavily used urban expressways in China despite speed limits of around 80 km/h. Traffic management measures, including ramp metering and restrictions on non-local vehicles, are regularly applied along it.
