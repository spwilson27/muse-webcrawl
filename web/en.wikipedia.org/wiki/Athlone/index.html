Athlone (Irish: Baile Átha Luain, meaning "town of Luan's ford") is a town on the River Shannon near the southern shore of Lough Ree in Ireland. It lies close to the geographical centre of Ireland and straddles the border between County Westmeath (east bank) and County Roscommon (west bank, in Connacht). Athlone is one of the largest towns in the Irish Midlands and an important regional centre for commerce, education and transport.

History

The ford across the Shannon at Athlone was of strategic importance from early times. A bridge was built in 1129, and a castle followed in 1210 under the Normans. The town was besieged during the Williamite War in Ireland: it withstood a first Jacobite-era assault in 1690, but fell to Williamite forces under Godert de Ginkel in June 1691 after one of the bloodiest sieges of the war, paving the way for the Battle of Aughrim.

Geography

Athlone is roughly equidistant from Dublin and Galway, lying on the M6 motorway and the Dublin–Galway/Westport railway lines. The Shannon, Ireland's longest river, flows through the town, and Lough Ree lies a short distance to the north. The town's position at the centre of the island has made it a natural crossing point and hub.

Landmarks

  • Athlone Castle – a 13th-century castle, now a visitor centre and museum.
  • Church of Saints Peter and Paul – a prominent church on the west bank, completed in 1937.
  • Sean's Bar – a pub claiming to date from around AD 900, often cited as Ireland's oldest.
  • Luan Gallery – a municipal art gallery opened in 2012.

Education and economy

The town is home to the Technological University of the Shannon: Midlands campus (formerly Athlone Institute of Technology). Major employers include medical-device and pharmaceutical firms, telecommunications, and retail serving a wide midlands catchment.
