Gian Lorenzo Bernini

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Ecstasy of Saint Teresa (1647–52) in the Cornaro Chapel: the saint swooning on a cloud as a smiling angel aims a golden arrow]

Gian Lorenzo (or Gianlorenzo) Bernini (7 December 1598 – 28 November 1680) was an Italian sculptor and architect, the dominant figure of the Roman Baroque. Working almost exclusively for popes in Rome, he created the Baroque style of sculpture — dynamic, theatrical and illusionistic — and designed some of the city's most famous spaces, including the colonnade of St Peter's Square and the Fountain of the Four Rivers in Piazza Navona.

His early virtuoso marble groups for Cardinal Scipione Borghese — Apollo and Daphne, David and The Rape of Proserpina — remain in the Galleria Borghese, and his Ecstasy of Saint Teresa (1647–52) is considered the masterpiece of Baroque sculpture.

Prodigy of the Borghese

The son of the Mannerist sculptor Pietro Bernini, Gian Lorenzo was carving accomplished marble by his teens. For Scipione Borghese he produced four groups (1618–25) that announced a new sculpture: flesh pressing into fingers in the Rape of Proserpina, Daphne's toes rooting and fingers leafing at the instant of transformation, a David wound like a spring in action — carved, Vasari's successors noted, with Bernini's own face grimacing in a mirror for the expression.

Architect of papal Rome

Under Urban VIII and his successors Bernini received the great papal commissions: the bronze Baldacchino over the high altar of St Peter's (1624–33), the Cathedra Petri, the Scala Regia, the oval piazza and Tuscan colonnades before St Peter's (1656–67) — 'the motherly arms of the Church' — and the palazzi Chigi-Odescalchi and Montecitorio. Summoned to Paris by Louis XIV in 1665, his designs for the Louvre were rejected, but his bust of the king set a pattern for royal portraiture. A famous rivalry with Francesco Borromini colours the Roman Baroque.
