Josef Müller-Brockmann

BornMay 9, 1914, Rapperswil, Switzerland
DiedAugust 30, 1996, Zurich, Switzerland
OccupationGraphic designer, typographer, author
Known forSwiss Style; grid systems; Tonhalle Zürich posters

Josef Müller-Brockmann (9 May 1914 – 30 August 1996) was a Swiss graphic designer, typographer and author, regarded as a leading figure of the Swiss Style (International Typographic Style). His concert posters for the Tonhalle Zürich and his books on grid systems became canonical works of modernist design.

Career

Müller-Brockmann studied architecture, design and art history at the University of Zurich and the Kunstgewerbeschule Zürich. After an apprenticeship in advertising, he opened his own studio in Zurich in 1936. From 1951 he designed the celebrated series of posters for the Tonhalle Zürich concerts, using abstract geometric forms and Helvetica/ Akzidenz-Grotesk type to visualise musical structure.

In 1957 he co-founded, with Richard Paul Lohse, Hans Neuburg and Carlo Vivarelli, the journal Neue Grafik ("New Graphic Design"), which spread Swiss design principles internationally. He designed the visual identity for Swissair and the signage and wayfinding system for the Swiss Federal Railways (SBB).

Publications and influence

His books include The Graphic Artist and His Design Problems (1961), A History of Visual Communication (1971) and Grid Systems in Graphic Design (1981), the last of which codified the use of mathematical grids in layout and remains a standard teaching text. Müller-Brockmann taught at the Kunstgewerbeschule Zürich and Osaka University of Arts, and in 1967 founded the design consultancy Müller-Brockmann & Co.
