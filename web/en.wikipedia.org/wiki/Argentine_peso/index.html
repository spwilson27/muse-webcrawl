The Argentine peso (code ARS) is the currency of Argentina. The current peso was introduced on 1 January 1992 under the Convertibility Law, replacing the austral at a rate of 10,000 australes to one peso, and was initially pegged at par to the United States dollar.

History

Argentina has renamed and redenominated its currency repeatedly: the peso moneda nacional, peso ley, peso argentino, austral, and the 1992 peso. The currency board peg ended with the economic crisis of 2001–2002, after which the peso floated and depreciated sharply. Persistent inflation has repeatedly eroded its value, prompting a series of ever-larger banknote denominations.

Coins and banknotes

The peso is subdivided into 100 centavos. Banknotes are issued by the Central Bank of Argentina in denominations that have expanded over time in response to inflation.
