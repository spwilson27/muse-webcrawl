Limburg Cathedral (German: Limburger Dom), dedicated to St George and St Nicholas, is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Limburg an der Lahn, Hesse. It stands on a rocky outcrop above the river Lahn and is one of the finest and most complete examples of late Romanesque architecture in Germany, built at the transition to the Gothic style.

Architecture

Construction began around 1180 over the site of an earlier collegiate church, and the building was consecrated in 1235. Its silhouette is famous for its seven towers — a number said to symbolise the seven sacraments. The west front has two towers, the crossing is crowned by a taller octagonal tower, and four smaller turrets rise over the transepts and choir.

The exterior was originally painted in brilliant colours; traces of the medieval colour scheme were used in a restoration of the 1970s, returning the cathedral to its striking polychrome appearance. Inside, the elevation of the nave shows the multi-level wall structure (arcade, gallery, triforium and clerestory) characteristic of the transition to Gothic.

History

The church first served a collegiate foundation established around 910 by Conrad Kurzbold. It became a cathedral in 1827 with the creation of the Diocese of Limburg. The cathedral preserves notable medieval wall paintings and the tomb slab of its founder.
