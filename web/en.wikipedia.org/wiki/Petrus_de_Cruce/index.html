Petrus de Cruce

Born13th century, Amiens, France
Diedafter 1298
OccupationComposer, music theorist
EraArs nova (early)

Petrus de Cruce (Pierre de la Croix; fl. c. 1270–1300) was a French composer and music theorist of the late 13th century. He is one of the most significant figures of the late ars antiqua period and a central innovator of the motet, whose works stand at the threshold of the ars nova.

Life

Petrus was born in Amiens and probably studied at the University of Paris, where he may have earned the title of magister. Documents suggest he held a canonry at Amiens Cathedral and later had connections with the royal chapel in Paris. A record from the late 1290s indicates he was still living around 1298.

Music

Petrus is best known for his motets, which display a striking innovation: his tripla (upper voices) move in much shorter note values than earlier practice allowed, sometimes subdividing the breve into as many as seven semibreves. This rhythmic freedom allowed the texted upper voices to declaim long, florid passages of text over a slowly moving tenor. His best-known works include the motets Aucun ont trouvé/Lonc tans/Annuntiantes and S'amours eust/Prothopareus, preserved in the Montpellier Codex and other sources.

Theoretical writings

Petrus is credited with the Tractatus de tonis, a treatise on the church tones. His notational practices, described by theorists such as Jacobus of Liège in the Speculum musicae, influenced the transition toward the greater rhythmic flexibility characteristic of the ars nova composers of the following century, including Philippe de Vitry.
