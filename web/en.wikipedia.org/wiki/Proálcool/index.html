Proálcool

Proálcool (Programa Nacional do Álcool, the National Alcohol Program) was a Brazilian government programme launched in 1975 to increase the production of ethanol from sugarcane as a substitute for imported petroleum. It was one of the largest biofuel programmes in the world and made Brazil the pioneer of large-scale fuel ethanol.

Origins

The programme was decreed by President Ernesto Geisel on 14 November 1975, in response to the 1973 oil crisis and a collapse in world sugar prices. The government subsidised distillery construction, guaranteed ethanol purchases through Petrobras, fixed ethanol prices below petrol, and required blending of anhydrous ethanol into gasoline.

Expansion and decline

A second phase after the 1979 oil shock promoted cars running on pure hydrated ethanol; by the mid-1980s the great majority of new cars sold in Brazil were ethanol-fuelled. Falling oil prices, reduced subsidies and supply shortages undermined the programme in the late 1980s and 1990s, and neat-ethanol cars nearly disappeared.

Legacy

The introduction of flex-fuel vehicles from 2003 revived the sector on a market basis. Brazil's mandatory ethanol blend, distillery infrastructure and sugarcane agronomy all descend from Proálcool, and the programme is widely studied as both a model of energy policy and a cautionary tale about subsidies.
