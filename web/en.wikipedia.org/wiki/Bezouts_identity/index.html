Bézout's identity

Bézout's identity (also called Bézout's lemma) is a theorem in elementary number theory: let a and b be integers with greatest common divisor d. Then there exist integers x and y such that

ax + by = d.

Moreover, the integers of the form az + bt are exactly the multiples of d. The integers x and y are called Bézout coefficients for (a, b); they are not unique, but can be computed by the extended Euclidean algorithm.

History

The result is named after the 18th-century French mathematician Étienne Bézout, who proved it for polynomials; the statement for integers had in fact been published earlier, by Claude Gaspard Bachet de Méziriac in the 17th century. Bézout's identity is fundamental: it underlies the proof that a prime dividing a product divides one of the factors, the existence of modular inverses, and the solution of linear Diophantine equations.

Generalisation

The identity extends to more than two integers (the gcd of a set of integers is a linear combination of them) and to any principal ideal domain, where it corresponds to the fact that ideals are generated by a single element.
