Burberry Group plc is a British luxury fashion house established in 1856 by Thomas Burberry in Basingstoke, Hampshire. It is known for its trench coats, its distinctive camel, black, red and white tartan-like check pattern, and for inventing gabardine, a tightly woven, water-resistant yet breathable fabric patented in 1888.

History

Thomas Burberry opened his draper's shop in 1856, focusing on outdoor clothing for sportsmen. His gabardine revolutionised rainwear, replacing the heavy rubberised coats of the day. During the First World War, Burberry supplied the "trench coat", an adaptation of its officer's raincoat with epaulettes, D-rings and a storm flap, which became the house's signature garment. The Burberry check, introduced as a lining in the 1920s, became one of the world's most widely copied — and counterfeited — patterns.

Modern era

The brand was revived as a global luxury label from the late 1990s under chief executive Rose Marie Bravo and creative director Christopher Bailey, who became chief executive in 2014. Successors in the creative role include Riccardo Tisci (2018) and Daniel Lee (2022). Burberry remains listed on the London Stock Exchange and is headquartered in London, with its trench coats still made in Castleford, Yorkshire.
