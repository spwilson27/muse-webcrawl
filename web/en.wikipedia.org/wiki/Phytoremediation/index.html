Phytoremediation

TypeBioremediation technology
UsesSoil and water decontamination
TargetsHeavy metals, radionuclides, organic pollutants

Phytoremediation technologies use living plants to clean up soil, air and water contaminated with hazardous contaminants. It is defined as "the use of green plants and the associated microorganisms, along with proper soil amendments and agronomic techniques to either contain, remove or render toxic environmental contaminants harmless". The term combines the Greek phyto (plant) and the Latin remedium (restoring balance).

Processes

  • Phytoextraction — plants absorb contaminants, particularly heavy metals, from soil through their roots and concentrate them in harvestable shoots.
  • Phytostabilization — plants reduce the mobility and bioavailability of pollutants in soil, limiting their migration to groundwater.
  • Phytodegradation — plant enzymes break down organic contaminants taken up from soil or water.
  • Rhizofiltration — roots filter contaminants from water, used for lead, cadmium and radionuclides.
  • Phytovolatilization — plants take up contaminants and release them into the atmosphere in volatile form, for example selenium and mercury.

Hyperaccumulators

Some plants, known as hyperaccumulators, can concentrate metals to levels far above those in the surrounding soil. Examples include Thlaspi caerulescens (now Noccaea caerulescens) for zinc and cadmium, Alyssum species for nickel, and the fern Pteris vittata for arsenic. Indian mustard (Brassica juncea) has been used to remove lead from contaminated sites. Phytoremediation was notably tested after the Chernobyl disaster, where sunflowers were floated on ponds to absorb caesium-137 and strontium-90 from water.

Advantages and limitations

Phytoremediation is solar-driven, relatively inexpensive and aesthetically pleasing compared with excavation and landfill, but it is slow, limited to the depth of plant roots, and the contaminated biomass itself must be harvested and disposed of safely. It is best suited to large sites with low-to-moderate contamination.
