2008–2011 automotive industry crisis

Period2008–2011
CauseGlobal financial crisis, high fuel prices, credit crunch
Key eventsGM and Chrysler bankruptcies; government bailouts

The 2008–2011 automotive industry crisis was a severe downturn in the global automobile industry triggered by the financial crisis of 2007–2008, spiking fuel prices, and a collapse in consumer credit and vehicle sales.

United States

US auto sales fell from about 17 million to under 10 million vehicles a year at the trough. General Motors and Chrysler filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2009 and were restructured with tens of billions of dollars in US government support under the Troubled Asset Relief Program. Ford avoided bankruptcy but mortgaged its assets for credit. Brands including Pontiac, Saturn, Hummer, and Mercury were discontinued.

Global impact

Governments in Europe and Asia launched scrappage schemes ("cash for clunkers") to stimulate demand. The crisis accelerated industry consolidation, plant closures, and a strategic shift toward fuel-efficient and electric vehicles.
