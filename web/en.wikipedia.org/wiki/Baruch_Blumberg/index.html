Baruch Blumberg

Baruch Samuel Blumberg (July 28, 1925 – April 5, 2011), known as Barry Blumberg, was an American physician, geneticist, and anthropologist. He shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1976 with Daniel Carleton Gajdusek for "discoveries concerning new mechanisms for the origin and dissemination of infectious diseases".

Discovery of hepatitis B

In the 1960s, while studying inherited variations in blood proteins among populations around the world, Blumberg identified an antigen in the blood of an Australian Aboriginal person — the "Australia antigen" — which he subsequently showed to be the surface antigen of the hepatitis B virus (HBsAg). The discovery made it possible to screen blood donations for hepatitis B, greatly reducing transfusion-transmitted infection, and led directly to the development of the first hepatitis B vaccine, one of the first vaccines shown to prevent a human cancer (liver cancer).

Later career

Blumberg spent much of his career at the Fox Chase Cancer Center in Philadelphia and was a professor at the University of Pennsylvania. From 1999 to 2002 he served as the first director of the NASA Astrobiology Institute. He was also Master of Balliol College, Oxford, from 1989 to 1994. He died in 2011 while attending a conference at NASA Ames Research Center in California.
