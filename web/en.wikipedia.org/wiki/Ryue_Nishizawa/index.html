Ryue Nishizawa (born 1966) is a Japanese architect based in Tokyo. Together with Kazuyo Sejima he co-founded the firm SANAA in 1995; the two shared the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2010. He also runs his own practice, the Office of Ryue Nishizawa, founded in 1997.

Career

Nishizawa studied architecture at Yokohama National University and joined Kazuyo Sejima & Associates in 1990. Five years later the two established SANAA (Sejima and Nishizawa and Associates), whose work — including the 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa, the Rolex Learning Center in Lausanne and the New Museum in New York — is known for luminous, lightweight structures with fluid, open plans.

Independent work

Through his own office, Nishizawa has designed a series of small, precise buildings in Japan. The Moriyama House (2005) in Tokyo divides a residence into ten separate small volumes scattered across the plot and linked by gardens. The Teshima Art Museum (2010), on the island of Teshima in the Seto Inland Sea, is a single free-form concrete shell, 40 by 60 metres, with two oval openings to the sky, housing Rei Naito's installation Matrix. Other works include the Towada Art Center (2008) and the Garden & House in Tokyo (2011).
