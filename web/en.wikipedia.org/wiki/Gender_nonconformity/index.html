Gender nonconformity

Gender nonconformity or gender variance is behavior or gender expression by an individual that does not match masculine or feminine gender norms. A gender-nonconforming person may be described as gender variant, gender atypical, or gender diverse. Gender expression includes clothing, hair style, mannerisms, speech patterns, and social roles.

Gender expression varies widely across cultures and historical periods

Terminology

"Gender nonconformity" is the broad term used in psychology, medicine, and gender studies. It is distinct from transgender identity: a person may be gender nonconforming without identifying as transgender, and many transgender people conform to the norms of their gender. In childhood research, the term "gender-variant" has been widely used, while many community advocates prefer "gender-expansive" or "gender-creative" to avoid pathologizing language.

Social aspects

Responses to gender nonconformity vary greatly by culture and era. Some societies recognize institutionalized roles outside the male/female binary, such as hijras in South Asia, two-spirit roles in some Indigenous North American cultures, and fa'afafine in Samoa. In other contexts, gender-nonconforming people — particularly children and men — may face stigma, bullying, or violence. Studies find that gender nonconformity in childhood is correlated with later homosexual orientation in adulthood, and gender-nonconforming children are at elevated risk of abuse and of developing post-traumatic stress symptoms.

Health and policy

Mental health professionals increasingly affirm that gender nonconformity is not a disorder in itself. Legal protections vary: some jurisdictions prohibit discrimination based on gender expression or identity in employment, housing, and education, while others restrict gender-nonconforming expression in schools or public life.
