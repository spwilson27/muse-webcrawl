Football at the Summer Olympics

Football at the Summer Olympics, officially known as the Olympic football tournament, has been included in every Summer Olympic Games as a men's competition sport, except 1896 (the inaugural Games) and 1932 (in an attempt to promote the new FIFA World Cup). Women's football was added to the official program at the 1996 Atlanta Games.

History

Football appeared unofficially at the 1900 and 1904 Games before official tournaments began in 1908, when Great Britain won gold in London. Uruguay's victories in 1924 and 1928 were treated as world championships of their day and helped prompt FIFA to launch the World Cup in 1930. For much of the mid-20th century the Olympic tournament was dominated by Eastern European teams whose players retained nominal amateur status.

Format

Since the 1992 Barcelona Games, the men's tournament has been restricted to under-23 teams (the age limit applying at the start of the qualifying campaign), with each squad permitted up to three over-age players since 1996. The women's tournament has no age restriction and is contested by full senior national teams, ranking alongside the FIFA Women's World Cup as a major title. The tournaments are organized by FIFA in conjunction with the International Olympic Committee.

Notable results

Hungary and Great Britain are among the most successful men's teams with three gold medals each, while Argentina, Brazil, the Soviet Union, and Uruguay have won twice. In the women's tournament the United States has been the dominant side, winning four gold medals, with Germany, Canada, and Norway also champions.
