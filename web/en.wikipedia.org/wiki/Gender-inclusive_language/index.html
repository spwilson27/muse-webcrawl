Gender-inclusive language

Also calledGender-neutral language
FieldSociolinguistics
ExamplesSingular "they"; gender-neutral job titles

Gender-inclusive language, also called gender-neutral language, is language that avoids bias toward a particular sex or social gender, and avoids word choices which may be interpreted as discriminatory or demeaning. Advocates argue that generic masculine forms, gendered job titles, and similar usages can reinforce stereotypes or exclude women and non-binary people.

Strategies in English

Common strategies include using the singular they as a gender-neutral pronoun, replacing gendered occupational terms such as "fireman" or "chairman" with neutral alternatives such as "firefighter" or "chair", using plurals, and writing around pronouns altogether. Many style guides for journalism, academia and government now recommend some form of gender-neutral usage.

Other languages

In languages with grammatical gender, reform proposals are more complex. German debates centre on forms such as the Gendersternchen ("gender star") and other typographic devices; in French, écriture inclusive uses devices such as the middle dot. Swedish officially adopted the gender-neutral pronoun hen in 2015. In Spanish and Portuguese, neologistic endings such as -e are proposed by some speakers. Such reforms remain contested in many countries, with some institutions and governments restricting or prohibiting them.

Debate

Supporters view inclusive language as a matter of equal representation; critics argue that mandated reforms are artificial, hard to read, or politically motivated. Public opinion and official policy vary widely by language and country.
