Judiciary of Spain

The judiciary of Spain consists of courts and tribunals, composed of judges and magistrates (jueces y magistrados), who have the power to administer justice in the name of the King of Spain. Judicial power is regulated principally by the Spanish Constitution of 1978 and by the Organic Law of the Judicial Power (Ley Orgánica del Poder Judicial), first enacted in 1985.

The governing body of the judiciary is the General Council of the Judiciary (Consejo General del Poder Judicial, CGPJ), whose twenty members are appointed for five-year terms. Judges in Spain are career civil servants recruited through competitive public examinations (oposiciones) followed by training at the Judicial School.

Structure

The court system is organised on a unitary basis for the whole country, although some civil-law competences are devolved to the autonomous communities. The principal courts are:

  • Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo) — the highest court in all orders except constitutional matters, seated in Madrid. It has five chambers: civil, criminal, administrative litigation, social (labour), and military.
  • Audiencia Nacional — a central court with jurisdiction over major criminal cases such as terrorism, organised crime and offences against the Crown, as well as certain administrative and labour matters.
  • High Courts of Justice (Tribunales Superiores de Justicia) — the highest courts of each autonomous community.
  • Provincial Courts (Audiencias Provinciales) — hear serious criminal cases and civil appeals.
  • Courts of first instance, examining magistrates' courts (juzgados de instrucción), criminal courts, administrative courts, labour courts, juvenile courts and courts for violence against women.
  • Justices of the peace (jueces de paz) in smaller municipalities.

Constitutional Court

The Constitutional Court (Tribunal Constitucional) is not part of the judiciary proper. It is the supreme interpreter of the Constitution, ruling on the constitutionality of laws, conflicts of competence between the State and the autonomous communities, and individual appeals for protection of fundamental rights (recurso de amparo). It is composed of twelve magistrates appointed for nine-year terms.

Public Prosecution

The Public Prosecutor's Office (Ministerio Fiscal) promotes the action of justice in defence of legality, citizens' rights and the public interest. It is headed by the State Prosecutor General (Fiscal General del Estado), appointed by the King on the proposal of the Government.
