Madrid–Barajas Airport

Adolfo Suárez Madrid–Barajas Airport (IATA: MAD, ICAO: LEMD) is the main international airport serving Madrid, the capital of Spain. Opened in 1928 and located about 12 kilometres northeast of the city centre, it is Spain's busiest airport and one of the largest in Europe, handling more than 60 million passengers a year in peak years before the COVID-19 pandemic.

Terminals

The airport has two main terminal areas. Terminals 1, 2, and 3 form the older complex, while Terminal 4, opened in 2006, was designed by Antonio Lamela and Richard Rogers with a distinctive undulating bamboo-lined roof; its satellite building, T4S, is connected by an underground automated train. T4 is the home base of Iberia and the hub of the Oneworld alliance in Spain.

History

The airport was renamed in 2014 in honour of Adolfo Suárez, Spain's first democratically elected prime minister following the Franco era. It is operated by Aena and served by metro and commuter rail links to central Madrid.
