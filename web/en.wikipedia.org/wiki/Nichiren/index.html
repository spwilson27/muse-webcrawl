Nichiren

Nichiren
Born: 16 February 1222, Awa Province, Japan
Died: 13 October 1282, Ikegami, Japan
School: Nichiren Buddhism, Mahayana
Known for: Lotus Sutra devotion, Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō

Nichiren (1222–1282) was a Japanese Buddhist priest and philosopher of the Kamakura period who founded Nichiren Buddhism, a branch of Mahayana Buddhism based on devotion to the Lotus Sutra.

Life

Born Zennichimaro, son of a fisherman in Awa Province, he studied at Seichō-ji and on Mount Hiei. He concluded the Lotus Sutra was the Buddha's highest teaching and in 1253 proclaimed the chant Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō — devotion to the Mystic Law of the Lotus Sutra. His treatise Risshō Ankoku Ron (1260) argued Japan's disasters stemmed from rejecting the Lotus Sutra, angering authorities.

Persecution and exile

Nichiren survived an attempted execution at Tatsunokuchi in 1271 and was exiled to Sado Island, where he wrote major works and inscribed the Gohonzon mandala. Pardoned in 1274, he retired to Mount Minobu, founding Kuon-ji, and died at Ikegami in 1282.

Legacy

His followers founded schools including Nichiren Shū and, in the 20th century, lay movements such as Soka Gakkai, Risshō Kōsei-kai and Reiyūkai, spreading Nichiren Buddhism worldwide.
