Voyager 2

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Voyager spacecraft, showing the 3.7 m high-gain antenna.

Voyager 2 is a space probe launched by NASA on 20 August 1977, sixteen days before its twin Voyager 1. Taking advantage of a rare alignment of the outer planets, it is the only spacecraft to have visited all four giant planets: Jupiter (1979), Saturn (1981), Uranus (1986) and Neptune (1989), returning the first close-up images of the two ice giants and discovering volcanic activity on Jupiter's moon Io and plumes on Neptune's moon Triton.

Voyager 2 crossed into interstellar space in November 2018 at about 119 astronomical units from the Sun, joining Voyager 1 in the space between the stars. Powered by three radioisotope generators whose output declines yearly, it still returns data from a handful of instruments. Like its twin it carries the Golden Record, a phonograph disc of sounds and images of Earth. Signals from Voyager 2 now take more than eighteen hours to reach Earth.
