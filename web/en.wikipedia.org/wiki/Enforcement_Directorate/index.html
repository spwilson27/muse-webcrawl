Enforcement Directorate

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) is a domestic law enforcement and economic intelligence agency of the Government of India, responsible for enforcing economic laws and fighting economic crime. It functions under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, and is headquartered in New Delhi.

Mandate

The Directorate's principal statutes are the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA), under which it investigates money laundering and can attach and confiscate proceeds of crime, and the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), which governs foreign exchange violations. It also enforces provisions of the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018, and took over work once handled under the former Foreign Exchange Regulation Act.

History

The organisation began on 1 May 1956 as an "Enforcement Unit" in the Department of Economic Affairs, handling foreign exchange control offences. It was renamed the Enforcement Directorate in 1957 and progressively acquired its modern investigative role, expanding substantially after the PMLA came into force in 2005.

Organisation and controversies

The ED is headed by a Director, typically an Indian Revenue Service officer, and works through zonal and sub-zonal offices across India. Its summons, searches and arrests in high-profile political and corporate cases have drawn both support and criticism, with opposition parties alleging selective use against them and the agency defending its actions as impartial law enforcement. Several of its powers have been litigated, and in 2022 the Supreme Court of India upheld the core provisions of the PMLA.
