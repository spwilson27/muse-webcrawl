Lake Taupō

Lake Taupō (Māori: Taupō-nui-a-Tia) is a large crater lake in New Zealand's North Island, in the caldera of the Taupō Volcano. With a surface area of 616 km² (238 sq mi), it is the largest lake by surface area in New Zealand and the largest freshwater lake in Oceania. The town of Taupō stands on its northeastern shore, and the lake's outlet at its northern end is the source of the Waikato River, New Zealand's longest river.

The lake fills a caldera created largely by the colossal Oruanui eruption about 25,500 years ago, one of the largest known eruptions of the last 70,000 years. The more recent Hatepe eruption, around AD 232, was among the most violent of the last 5,000 years and deepened and enlarged the basin. The Taupō Volcano remains active beneath the lake.

Ecology and recreation

The lake is renowned for its rainbow and brown trout fishery, stocked since the late 19th century, and is fed by clear, well-oxygenated tributaries such as the Tongariro River. The celebrated Ngātoroirangi Mine Bay Māori rock carvings, accessible only by water, were created in the 1970s by master carver Matahi Brightwell. The lake hosts the annual Lake Taupō Cycle Challenge and is a hub for boating, swimming and tourism.
