Underground City, Montreal

The Underground City (French: Montréal souterrain), officially branded RÉSO since 2004, is a network of interconnected office towers, hotels, shopping centres, universities, museums and transit stations beneath downtown Montreal, Quebec. It is among the largest underground complexes in the world.

Network

The network began with tunnels linking Place Ville Marie, opened in 1962 under urban planner Vincent Ponte's vision, to the Central Station and hotels. It now comprises roughly 32 kilometres of tunnels connecting some 2,000 shops and restaurants, several Metro stations, commuter rail terminals, the Bell Centre arena and thousands of apartments and offices. An estimated half a million people use it daily in winter.

Significance

The underground city lets pedestrians move around downtown sheltered from Montreal's severe winters and hot, humid summers. Despite the popular name, much of the network is at or above ground level within buildings; the name RÉSO derives from the French word réseau, meaning network, and echoes the Metro's 1960s-era branding.
