Jacques Tourneur

Born12 November 1904, Paris, France
Died19 December 1977, Bergerac, France
OccupationFilm director
Notable worksCat People, I Walked with a Zombie, Out of the Past, Night of the Demon

Jacques Tourneur (1904–1977) was a French-American film director, celebrated for atmospheric low-budget horror films and film noir, in which suggestion and shadow replaced explicit effects.

Early life

The son of director Maurice Tourneur, Jacques moved to the United States as a child, worked in his father's productions, returned to France as an editor and director in the 1930s, then joined MGM in Hollywood, making shorts and B-features.

RKO horror and noir

With producer Val Lewton at RKO, Tourneur directed Cat People (1942), I Walked with a Zombie and The Leopard Man (both 1943) — classics of implied horror built on lighting, sound and the famous "Lewton bus" shock technique. He then directed the quintessential noir Out of the Past (1947) with Robert Mitchum, and films across genres: Stars in My Crown, The Flame and the Arrow, Wichita and the British horror Night of the Demon (1957), adapted from M. R. James.

Later work and legacy

Later work was mostly television (including The Twilight Zone) and two AIP features with Vincent Price. Tourneur retired to France. Critics rank him among the masters of cinematic atmosphere; his restraint profoundly influenced horror filmmaking.
