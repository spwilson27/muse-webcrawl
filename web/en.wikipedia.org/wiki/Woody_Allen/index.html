Woody Allen

Born: Allan Stewart Königsberg, 30 November 1935, New York City, U.S.

Occupations: Film director, writer, actor, comedian, playwright

Years active: 1950–present

Notable works: Annie Hall, Manhattan, Hannah and Her Sisters, Crimes and Misdemeanors, Midnight in Paris

Woody Allen (born Allan Stewart Königsberg, November 30, 1935) is an American filmmaker, actor, and comedian whose career spans more than six decades. Known for his prolific output — frequently writing, directing, and starring in a film a year — and for his distinctive persona as a neurotic, intellectual New Yorker, he is regarded as one of the most significant filmmakers of the New Hollywood era.

Early life and career

Allen was born in the Bronx and raised in Brooklyn in a Jewish family. As a teenager he earned money selling one-line jokes to columnists and was soon writing for television, including for Sid Caesar's Your Show of Shows stable of writers and for Candid Camera. In the early 1960s he became a successful stand-up comedian, developing the anxious, confessional monologue style that became his trademark, and published comic pieces in The New Yorker.

Film career

Allen's first film credit was writing and appearing in What's New Pussycat? (1965). His early directorial efforts — Take the Money and Run (1969), Bananas (1971), Sleeper (1973), and Love and Death (1975) — were broad comedies. Annie Hall (1977), co-starring Diane Keaton, won four Academy Awards including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Original Screenplay, and marked a turn toward more serious, autobiographical work. Manhattan (1979), shot in black and white by Gordon Willis, paired comedy with a romantic portrait of New York City.

Through the 1980s and 1990s Allen alternated comedies with dramas influenced by Bergman and Chekhov: Stardust Memories (1980), Zelig (1983), The Purple Rose of Cairo (1985), Hannah and Her Sisters (1986), Crimes and Misdemeanors (1989), and Husbands and Wives (1992). After a period of mixed reception, Match Point (2005), filmed in London, revived his critical standing; Vicky Cristina Barcelona (2008) and Midnight in Paris (2011) — his highest-grossing film, which won him a fourth Oscar, for Best Original Screenplay — followed. He has received a record sixteen Academy Award nominations for screenwriting and has won four Oscars in total.

Personal life and controversy

Allen's relationships with Diane Keaton and Mia Farrow were closely tied to his films; Farrow starred in thirteen of them. Their relationship ended in 1992 amid his relationship with Farrow's adopted daughter Soon-Yi Previn, whom he married in 1997. In 1992 his adopted daughter Dylan Farrow alleged that he had sexually molested her; Allen has denied the allegation, and investigations at the time did not result in charges, though the allegation has remained the subject of public dispute and affected the release of his later films, particularly following the MeToo movement. Allen is also an amateur jazz clarinettist who for decades performed weekly with a New Orleans-style jazz band in Manhattan.
