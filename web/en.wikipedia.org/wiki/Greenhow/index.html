Greenhow is a surname of English origin. It may also refer to places and institutions named after people bearing the name.

People

Notable people with the surname include Rose O'Neal Greenhow (1813 or 1817 – 1864), a Washington socialite and spy for the Confederacy during the American Civil War, whose intelligence network supplied information before the First Battle of Bull Run and who later drowned off Wilmington, North Carolina, while running the Union blockade; Edward Headlam Greenhow (1814–1888), an English physician and public health researcher known for his investigations of occupational disease and public health reports; and Thomas Michael Greenhow (1792–1881), an English surgeon who founded the Newcastle Eye Hospital and was a prominent figure in medicine in Newcastle upon Tyne.

Places and institutions

Greenhow Hill is a hamlet and high point in Nidderdale, North Yorkshire, England, historically a lead-mining village and among the highest settlements in Yorkshire. The Greenhow family name is also preserved in medical history through Greenhow's reports to the Privy Council on the health of industrial workers in Victorian Britain.
