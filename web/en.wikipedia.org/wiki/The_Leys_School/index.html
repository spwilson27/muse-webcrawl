The Leys School is a co-educational independent day and boarding school in Cambridge, England. Founded in 1875 as a Methodist school for the sons of lay church members, it occupies a site on Trumpington Road near the centre of the city and educates around 570 pupils aged 11 to 18.

History

The school was established in 1875 following a campaign within the Wesleyan Methodist Church for a school in the east of England comparable to its foundations elsewhere; its name derives from the site's association with "the Leys." It opened with a small cohort of boys and grew steadily, admitting girls in the later twentieth century as it moved to full co-education. During both world wars the school community served extensively, and its memorial chapel commemorates former pupils who died.

Campus and life

The Leys combines Victorian red-brick buildings with modern facilities including a theatre, music school, sports hall and swimming pool. Pupils are divided into houses for pastoral care and competition. Sport, particularly rugby, hockey and cricket, and the performing arts are prominent, as is a Combined Cadet Force contingent.

Notable alumni

Old Leysians include writers, scientists, sportsmen and clergy; the school's alumni association maintains close links with the Methodist Church and the wider Cambridge community.
