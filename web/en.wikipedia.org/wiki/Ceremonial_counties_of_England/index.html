Ceremonial counties of England

The ceremonial counties of England.

The ceremonial counties of England are areas to which a lord-lieutenant, the monarch's personal representative, is appointed. There are 48 ceremonial counties, defined by the Lieutenancies Act 1997. They are used for ceremonial purposes and form a widely used geographic reference framework, differing in some places from the administrative counties and unitary authority areas used for local government.

History

The lieutenancy areas evolved from the historic counties. The Local Government Act 1888 aligned most lieutenancies with the new administrative counties; later reorganisations in 1965, 1974 and the 1990s separated ceremonial and administrative geographies, and the 1997 Act fixed the present set of 48 counties, closely following the 1974 pattern with some adjustments.

List

The ceremonial counties, from smallest to largest by population, range from the City of London (the smallest) to Kent, while by area North Yorkshire is the largest. The full list includes Bedfordshire, Berkshire, Bristol, Buckinghamshire, Cambridgeshire, Cheshire, City of London, Cornwall, County Durham, Cumbria, Derbyshire, Devon, Dorset, East Riding of Yorkshire, East Sussex, Essex, Gloucestershire, Greater London, Greater Manchester, Hampshire, Herefordshire, Hertfordshire, Isle of Wight, Kent, Lancashire, Leicestershire, Lincolnshire, Merseyside, Norfolk, North Yorkshire, Northamptonshire, Northumberland, Nottinghamshire, Oxfordshire, Rutland, Shropshire, Somerset, South Yorkshire, Staffordshire, Suffolk, Surrey, Tyne and Wear, Warwickshire, West Midlands, West Sussex, West Yorkshire, Wiltshire and Worcestershire.
