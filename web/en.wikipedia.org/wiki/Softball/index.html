Softball is a bat-and-ball sport derived from baseball, played with a larger ball on a smaller field. It was invented in Chicago in 1887 as an indoor version of baseball — legend holds that the first ball was a boxing glove and the first bat a broom handle — and spread across the United States as an inexpensive recreation for all ages. Two principal variants exist: fastpitch, in which the pitcher delivers underhand with a full windmill motion at high speed, and slowpitch, in which the ball is lobbed in a high arc and must land in the strike zone, emphasising hitting.

Differences from baseball

Softball bases are closer together, the pitching distance shorter, the ball larger and (despite the name) only marginally softer, and games in fastpitch typically last seven innings. Because the ball is pitched from a flat circle rather than a mound, and released close to the batter, elite fastpitch reaction times rival those in baseball. Base stealing and bunting are used in fastpitch; slowpitch, the dominant recreational form in North America, forbids stealing and is played with ten fielders.

Competition

Women's fastpitch is a prominent collegiate sport in the United States, culminating in the Women's College World Series. Internationally the sport is governed with baseball by the WBSC; the United States and Japan have been the dominant national teams, meeting in the last several Olympic finals, and Japan won gold at the Tokyo 2020 Games. Softball was dropped from the Olympics after 2008, returned for Tokyo, and is scheduled again for Los Angeles 2028.
