Zhangjiajie (simplified Chinese: 张家界; traditional Chinese: 張家界; pinyin: Zhāngjiājiè) is a prefecture-level city in the northwestern part of Hunan province, China. It is world-famous for the dramatic quartzite sandstone pillar landscapes of the Zhangjiajie National Forest Park within the Wulingyuan Scenic Area, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1992.

Geography and attractions

The Wulingyuan area contains more than 3,000 narrow sandstone pillars and peaks, many over 200 metres high, formed by long-term physical erosion. The park's Southern Sky Column was renamed "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain" after the floating mountains of the 2009 film Avatar, whose scenery it partly inspired. The Bailong Elevator, a 326-metre glass lift built into a cliff face, is among the tallest outdoor elevators in the world.

Nearby Tianmen Mountain is reached by one of the world's longest cable cars and by a road with 99 hairpin bends; its natural arch, Tianmen Cave, is reached by a staircase of 999 steps. In 2016 the Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon opened a 430-metre glass-bottomed bridge, then the longest and highest of its kind.

Administration

The city administers two districts (Yongding and Wulingyuan) and two counties (Cili and Sangzhi). Its population is roughly 1.5 million, with significant Tujia, Bai, and Miao minority populations.
