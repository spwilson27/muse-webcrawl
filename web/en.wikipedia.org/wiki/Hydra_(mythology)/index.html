Lernaean Hydra

Hydra
Grouping: Greek mythology monster
Offspring/parents: Typhon and Echidna
Abode: Lake Lerna, Argolis
Slain by: Heracles (second labour), with Iolaus

The Lernaean Hydra or Hydra of Lerna (Greek: Λερναῖα Ὕδρα), more often known simply as the Hydra, is a serpentine water monster in Greek and Roman mythology. Its lair was the lake of Lerna in the Argolid, reputedly an entrance to the Underworld. The Hydra was offspring of Typhon and Echidna and sibling of monsters including Cerberus and the Chimera.

The second labour of Heracles

Killing the Hydra was the second of Heracles' Twelve Labours. The creature possessed many heads — accounts vary — and for each head severed, one or two more grew in its place; one head was immortal, and its breath and blood were virulently poisonous. Heracles, aided by his nephew Iolaus, who cauterised each neck stump with a torch to prevent regrowth, buried the immortal head under a rock. He dipped his arrows in the Hydra's blood, a poison that later caused his own death.

Aftermath and legacy

Because Heracles received help, Eurystheus refused to count the labour. The Hydra and the crab Karkinos sent against Heracles were placed in the sky as the constellations Hydra and Cancer. "Hydra" has become a metaphor for a problem that multiplies when attacked.
