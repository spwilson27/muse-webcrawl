Aisle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Aisle
DefinitionA passageway or divided space within a building or vehicle
ContextsChurch architecture; theatres; shops; aircraft cabins
EtymologyFrom Old French ele / Latin ala, "wing"

[Image: cathedral side aisle]

An aisle is, in general, a space for walking with rows of non-walking space on both sides. Aisles with seating on both sides, certain types of land, transportation in loose/tight formation such as a plane, or every place with a long stretch where two people could be walking side by side, can all be considered aisle types in their own right.

In architecture

In church architecture, an aisle is more specifically the area separated from the nave by colonnades or arcades — a passageway to either side of the nave separated from it by a row of columns or piers. Occasionally aisles stop at the transepts, but often aisles can be continued around the apse. Aisles thus form the wings of a basilican building. In Gothic architecture the roofs of the aisles are typically lower than that of the nave, allowing light to enter through clerestory windows.

The term is also used for the divisions in theatres, cinemas, shops and supermarkets, and for the central passage in an aircraft cabin — hence "aisle seat" as opposed to window or middle seat.

Weddings and ceremonies

By extension, the central aisle of a church or venue is the route of the wedding procession, giving rise to the expression "walking down the aisle". Similar ceremonial aisles appear in graduation ceremonies and in legislative chambers, where "crossing the aisle" denotes switching political allegiance.
