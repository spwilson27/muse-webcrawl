Grimsby, also known as Great Grimsby, is a port town and the administrative centre of North East Lincolnshire, England, lying on the south bank of the Humber Estuary near the North Sea coast. According to tradition the town was founded by a Danish fisherman named Grim; its name derives from Old Norse, "Grim's village".

History

Grimsby was a medieval port of some importance, but its great era began in the nineteenth century when the arrival of the railway and the construction of extensive docks by the Manchester, Sheffield and Lincolnshire Railway transformed it into one of the largest fishing ports in the world. The landmark Dock Tower was completed in 1852. At its peak, hundreds of trawlers landed fish at Grimsby from grounds as distant as Iceland and the Barents Sea.

Fishing decline and modern economy

The Cod Wars with Iceland and the imposition of exclusive fishing zones devastated the distant-water fleet during the 1970s, and fishing employment collapsed. Grimsby nevertheless remains a major centre for fish processing and seafood trading, handling a large share of the United Kingdom's seafood imports, and the town styles itself "Europe's food town". In recent years the port has also become a hub for operations and maintenance serving offshore wind farms in the North Sea. The neighbouring resort of Cleethorpes adjoins the town to the south-east.

Culture and sport

The town's football club, Grimsby Town, nicknamed the Mariners, plays at Blundell Park in Cleethorpes. The Fishing Heritage Centre documents the history of the port and its trawlermen. Grimsby has featured in popular culture through the 2016 comedy film Grimsby and as the home town of several musicians and comedians.
