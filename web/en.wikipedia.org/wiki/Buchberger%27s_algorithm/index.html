Buchberger's algorithm

In the theory of multivariate polynomials, Buchberger's algorithm is a method for transforming a given basis of a polynomial ideal into a Gröbner basis with respect to some monomial order. It was invented by Austrian mathematician Bruno Buchberger and published in his 1965 PhD thesis. It can be viewed as a generalisation of the Euclidean algorithm for univariate greatest common divisors and of Gaussian elimination for linear systems.

Description

Starting from a finite generating set F of the ideal, the algorithm repeatedly forms S-polynomials of pairs of basis elements, reduces them by the current basis, and adds any nonzero remainder to the basis. Buchberger's criterion states that a basis is a Gröbner basis if and only if every S-polynomial reduces to zero; the algorithm terminates because the monomial ideals generated by leading terms form an ascending chain, which stabilises by Dickson's lemma / Hilbert's basis theorem. Practical implementations (in systems such as Singular, Macaulay2, Maple, Mathematica and SageMath) use pair-selection strategies and Buchberger's criteria to discard useless pairs; later algorithms such as Faugère's F4 and F5 improve efficiency substantially.

Applications

Gröbner bases computed by the algorithm solve problems in algebraic geometry (solving polynomial systems, implicitisation), commutative algebra, robotics, coding theory and automated theorem proving.
