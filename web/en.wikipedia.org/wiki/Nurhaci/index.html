Nurhaci (Manchu: ᠨᡠᡵᡥᠠᠴᡳ; Chinese: 努爾哈赤; 1559–1626) was a Jurchen chieftain who united the Jurchen (Manchu) tribes of Manchuria and founded the state that became the Qing dynasty, which conquered China in 1644. Posthumously honoured as Qing Taizu, he is regarded as the founder of the Manchu nation and of the Qing imperial house of Aisin Gioro.

Rise to power

Nurhaci was born into the Gioro clan of the Suksuhu River tribe. In 1583 his father and grandfather were killed during a Ming-supported attack by a rival chieftain; Nurhaci, then in his twenties, began his career — tradition says with only thirteen suits of armour — and spent the next three decades subduing or absorbing the Jurchen tribes of the Jianzhou and Haixi confederations, completing the unification of the Jurchens by 1619.

He organised Manchu society into the Eight Banners, military-administrative units (initially four banners, expanded to eight by 1615) that became the backbone of Qing power, and in 1599 commissioned a written Manchu script, adapting the Mongolian alphabet. He moved his capital to Hetu Ala, then Liaoyang, and in 1625 to Shenyang (Mukden).

War with the Ming

In 1616 Nurhaci proclaimed himself Khan of the Later Jin dynasty (a name evoking the earlier Jurchen Jin dynasty). In 1618 he issued the "Seven Grievances" against the Ming dynasty and seized Fushun. His victory over a much larger Ming expeditionary force at the Battle of Sarhū (1619) is considered one of the decisive battles of East Asian history; he went on to take Liaodong, transforming the balance of power in north-east Asia. In 1626 his army was checked at the Battle of Ningyuan by the Ming general Yuan Chonghuan, whose cannon reportedly wounded him; Nurhaci died a few months later and was succeeded by his eighth son, Hong Taiji, who renamed the Jurchen the Manchus and the dynasty Qing.

Legacy

Nurhaci's unification of the Jurchens, creation of the Banner system and construction of a centralised state made possible his successors' conquest of Ming China. His mausoleum, the Fuling (East Tomb) near Shenyang, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties.
