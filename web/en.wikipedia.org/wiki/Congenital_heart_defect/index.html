Specialty: Cardiology, pediatrics
Frequency: ~1% of live births

Congenital heart defect

A congenital heart defect (CHD), also known as a congenital heart anomaly or congenital heart disease, is a defect in the structure of the heart or great vessels that is present at birth. It is the most common type of birth defect. Signs and symptoms depend on the specific type of defect, and may range from none to life-threatening.

Causes

Causes are generally unknown, though genetic factors, infections during pregnancy such as congenital rubella syndrome, certain medications, and conditions such as diabetes in the mother increase risk. Some defects are associated with chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome.

Types

CHDs are classified as cyanotic or acyanotic. Examples include ventricular septal defect (VSD), atrial septal defect (ASD), tetralogy of Fallot, transposition of the great arteries, patent ductus arteriosus, and hypoplastic left heart syndrome.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the defect and ranges from watchful waiting, to medication, to catheter procedures or open-heart surgery. Many defects require no treatment at all, while severe defects may require staged surgical repair beginning in infancy.

Epidemiology

Congenital heart defects affect roughly 8–10 per 1,000 live births worldwide. Advances in diagnosis and surgical treatment have greatly improved survival, so that most affected children now reach adulthood.
