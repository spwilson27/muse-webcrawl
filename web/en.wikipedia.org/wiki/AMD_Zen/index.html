Zen (first generation)

Zen

Designer: AMD (design led by Jim Keller)

Launched: March 2017 (Ryzen)

Process: 14 nm (GlobalFoundries)

Products: Ryzen, Ryzen Threadripper, Epyc

Predecessor: Excavator (Bulldozer family)

Successors: Zen+, Zen 2, Zen 3, Zen 4, Zen 5

Zen is the codename for the first iteration of a family of x86-64 computer processor microarchitectures from AMD, first launched with the Ryzen series of CPUs in February–March 2017. Designed from scratch under the leadership of Jim Keller, Zen was a clean-sheet replacement for the disappointing Bulldozer family and aimed for a roughly 40 percent improvement in instructions per clock over its predecessor — a target AMD said it exceeded, claiming about 52 percent.

Design

Unlike Bulldozer, which paired integer clusters sharing a floating-point unit, each Zen core has its own dedicated floating-point and integer units, simultaneous multithreading (two threads per core, analogous to Intel's Hyper-Threading), a reworked cache hierarchy with fast L1 and L2 caches and a shared L3, and a neural-network-based branch predictor. Cores are grouped into four-core "CPU Complexes" (CCX). Chips are built from dies connected by AMD's Infinity Fabric interconnect, allowing the same silicon to scale from desktop Ryzen up to server Epyc processors with up to 32 cores and workstation Threadripper parts.

Impact

Zen restored AMD's competitiveness against Intel after a decade in the wilderness: Ryzen offered many more cores than comparably priced Intel chips, disrupted desktop, workstation, and server markets, and began a sequence of successor architectures (Zen+, Zen 2 with its chiplet design, Zen 3, Zen 4, and Zen 5) built on its foundations.
