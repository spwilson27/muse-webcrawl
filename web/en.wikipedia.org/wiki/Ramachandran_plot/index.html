Ramachandran plot

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Ramachandran plot (also known as a Rama plot or [φ,ψ] plot) is a way to visualise the energetically allowed conformations of a protein backbone. It graphs the dihedral angle φ (phi) against ψ (psi) for each amino-acid residue in a protein structure. It was developed in 1963 by G. N. Ramachandran and colleagues at the University of Madras.

Principle

Most combinations of φ and ψ are forbidden because atoms in the polypeptide chain would collide (steric clash). The permitted combinations cluster in characteristic regions: one corresponding to α-helices, another to β-sheets, and a smaller region for left-handed helices. Glycine, lacking a side chain, can occupy much more of the plot; proline is much more restricted.

Use in structure validation

Because real protein structures overwhelmingly place their residues in the favoured regions, the plot is a standard quality check: a well-determined structure typically has over 90% of residues in the most favoured regions, and residues falling in disallowed regions flag possible errors in X-ray crystallographic or cryo-EM models.
