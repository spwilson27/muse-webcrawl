Dong Haichuan

Dong Haichuan (13 October 1797 or 1813 – 25 October 1882) was a Chinese martial artist from Wen'an County, Hebei Province, regarded as the founder of the internal martial art known as baguazhang (Eight Trigrams Palm). His system, built on the practice of walking in circles while executing palm changes, became one of the three major Chinese internal martial arts, alongside tai chi and xingyiquan.

Life

Accounts of Dong's early life are partly legendary. He is said to have studied various local martial arts in his youth and, according to tradition, to have learned circle-walking methods associated with Daoist practice during travels in the south of China. By the mid-19th century he was working in Beijing, where he served in the household of Prince Su. His skill came to the attention of the prince, who appointed him to teach martial arts.

Teaching

Dong taught students who often already had training in other styles, and he tailored instruction to their backgrounds; as a result, his disciples developed branches of baguazhang with differing flavours, several of which survive as the art's principal lineages. Among his best-known students was Yin Fu, a close disciple who taught in the imperial court, and Cheng Tinghua, founder of the Cheng style of baguazhang. Dong's tomb in Beijing bears inscriptions listing dozens of his disciples.

Legacy

Dong Haichuan is commemorated as the patriarch of baguazhang; his grave site in Beijing has been restored and remains a place of pilgrimage for practitioners.
