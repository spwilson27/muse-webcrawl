Uruguay v Brazil (1950 FIFA World Cup)

Uruguay v Brazil was the decisive match of the final round of the 1950 FIFA World Cup, held on 16 July 1950 at the Maracanã Stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. In front of an estimated crowd of around 200,000 spectators — still among the largest attendances in football history — Uruguay defeated the hosts 2–1 to win their second World Cup title. The match is famously known in Brazil as the Maracanaço ("the Maracanã blow") and is regarded as one of the greatest upsets in the history of the sport.

Background

The 1950 World Cup was decided by a final round-robin group rather than a straight final. Going into the last match, Brazil led the group and needed only a draw to become champions, having beaten Sweden 7–1 and Spain 6–1. Uruguay, winners of the inaugural tournament in 1930, had drawn with Spain and narrowly beaten Sweden. Brazilian newspapers had already proclaimed Brazil world champions before the match, and a victory song had been prepared.

The match

After a goalless first half, Friaça put Brazil ahead two minutes after the restart. Uruguay, captained by Obdulio Varela, levelled through Juan Alberto Schiaffino in the 66th minute. With eleven minutes remaining, Alcides Ghiggia dribbled past Bigode and beat goalkeeper Barbosa at his near post to give Uruguay the lead. The stadium fell into a stunned silence, which Ghiggia later described by saying that only three people had ever silenced the Maracanã: Frank Sinatra, Pope John Paul II, and himself.

Aftermath

The defeat caused profound national mourning in Brazil; the team changed its kit colours from white to yellow and green as a result. Goalkeeper Barbosa was blamed for the winning goal for the rest of his life. Uruguay's triumph, achieved by a nation of barely two million people against the hosts, remains one of football's most celebrated achievements.
