Left-libertarianism

Left-libertarianism, also known as egalitarian libertarianism, left-wing libertarianism or social libertarianism, is a political philosophy and type of libertarianism that stresses both individual freedom and social justice. Unlike right-libertarianism, left-libertarianism holds that natural resources belong to everyone in some egalitarian manner and cannot be appropriated outright without compensation to the community.

Overview

Left-libertarians combine the traditional libertarian principle of self-ownership with an egalitarian view of ownership of land and natural resources. In this they draw on the Georgist tradition of Henry George and on earlier figures such as Thomas Paine. Philosophers associated with the academic school include Philippe Van Parijs, Hillel Steiner and Michael Otsuka.

Approaches

Some left-libertarians advocate a land value tax and the sharing of resource rents through mechanisms such as a basic income — the so-called "real freedom" defended by Van Parijs. Others, including many in the mutualist and free-market anarchist traditions associated with Kevin Carson, argue that genuinely free markets, freed from state-granted privilege, would themselves undermine concentrated wealth and hierarchy.
