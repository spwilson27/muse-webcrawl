[Image placeholder] The Param Vir Chakra, India's highest wartime gallantry award.

The Param Vir Chakra (PVC; lit. "Wheel of Supreme Bravery") is India's highest military decoration, awarded for displaying distinguished acts of valour during wartime. It is the Indian equivalent of the British Victoria Cross and the American Medal of Honor. Instituted on 26 January 1950, the day India became a republic, the award was made retroactive to 15 August 1947. As of the most recent awards (for the 1999 Kargil War), it has been conferred only 21 times, of which 14 were posthumous.

Design

The medal was designed by Savitri Khanolkar, a Swiss-born artist who had settled in India. It is a circular bronze disc 1⅜ inches in diameter: on the obverse, the national emblem is centred, surrounded by four replicas of Indra's vajra (thunderbolt); on the reverse are embossed the words "Param Vir Chakra" in Hindi and English, separated by lotus flowers. It hangs from a plain purple ribbon. A recipient receives a monthly allowance, and the award carries a grant and pension provisions extended by several state governments.

History of recipients

The first recipient was Major Somnath Sharma, killed at the Battle of Badgam in November 1947 during the first Indo-Pakistani war. Awards span the wars of 1947–48, 1962 (Sino-Indian), 1965, 1971 and 1999 (Kargil), as well as the 1987 Operation Pawan in Sri Lanka. Three recipients were living at the time of the award in the 21st century grants: Subedar Major (then Rifleman) Sanjay Kumar and Grenadier Yogendra Singh Yadav, both of Kargil, and Subedar Major Bana Singh (1987, Siachen). Flying Officer Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon remains the only Indian Air Force recipient. The youngest recipient was Yadav, aged 19.

Precedence and privileges

The Param Vir Chakra takes precedence over all other Indian awards. Recipients are entitled to be saluted, receive a ceremonial guard of honour, and are commemorated in the Param Yodha Sthal at the National War Memorial in New Delhi, where bronze busts of all 21 awardees stand.
