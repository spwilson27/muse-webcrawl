Languages of Iran

Iran is a multilingual country. Persian (Farsi), an Iranian language of the Indo-European family, is the sole official language and the lingua franca of education, government and media, spoken natively by roughly half to three-fifths of the population and as a second language by most others. Article 15 of the Iranian constitution permits the use of regional languages in the press and mass media and for teaching their literature in schools.

Major languages

  • Persian — including the western Persian standard of Iran and regional varieties.
  • Azerbaijani (Azeri) — a Turkic language, the largest minority language, spoken in the northwest by perhaps 15–20% of the population.
  • Kurdish — several dialects (Kurmanji, Sorani, Southern Kurdish) in the west and northwest.
  • Luri — a group of Southwestern Iranian varieties spoken in the Zagros region, closely related to Persian.
  • Balochi — in Sistan and Baluchestan in the southeast.
  • Arabic — Khuzestani Arabic in the southwest; Arabic is also taught as the language of religion.
  • Gilaki and Mazandarani (Tabari) — Caspian languages of the north.
  • Turkmen — in the northeast; Talysh, Tati, Armenian, Georgian, Assyrian Neo-Aramaic and Qashqai are also spoken.

History

The Iranian plateau has been linguistically diverse since antiquity: Old Persian of the Achaemenid inscriptions, Middle Persian (Pahlavi) of the Sasanian era, and New Persian, written in a modified Arabic script since the early Islamic centuries, form the direct line to the modern standard. Many regional Iranian languages are endangered as Persian dominates schooling and broadcasting.
