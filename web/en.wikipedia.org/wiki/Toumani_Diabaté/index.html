Toumani Diabaté

Toumani Diabaté (10 August 1965 – 19 July 2024) was a Malian kora player and one of the most celebrated masters of the instrument. A griot by birth, he came from a long line of kora players — he often described himself as the 71st generation of musicians in his family — and his father, Sidiki Diabaté, was famed as the "King of the Kora".

Career

Diabaté came to international attention with the album Kaira (1988), a virtuoso solo recording made in London. He went on to record the landmark duo albums Songhai (1988) and Songhai 2 with the Spanish flamenco group Ketama and bassist Danny Thompson, and collaborations with Taj Mahal (Kulanjan, 1999), Björk, Damon Albarn, and the blues guitarist Ali Farka Touré. His albums with Touré, In the Heart of the Moon (2005) and Ali and Toumani (2010), both won Grammy Awards for Best Traditional World Music Album. His Symmetric Orchestra fused traditional Mandé music with modern arrangements, and Boulevard de l'Indépendance (2006) topped world music charts.

Legacy

Diabaté popularised the 21-string kora worldwide while defending Mali's musical heritage; he also served as a UNAIDS goodwill ambassador. His son Sidiki Diabaté is a leading kora player of the next generation. He died in Bamako in July 2024 after a short illness.
