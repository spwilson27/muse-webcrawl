Fighter-bomber

A fighter-bomber is a fighter aircraft that has been modified, or used primarily, as a light bomber or attack aircraft. It differs from bomber and attack aircraft primarily in its origins: as a fighter, although limited in the bombing role, it retains much of the fighter's air-to-air capability. Many fighter-bombers are essentially fighters adapted to carry bombs and rockets, and can defend themselves once their ordnance is dropped.

History

Second World War

The type emerged in the Second World War. The German Luftwaffe used fighter-bombers (Jagdbomber, or "Jabos") such as the Focke-Wulf Fw 190 in hit-and-run raids over southern England in 1942–43. The RAF's Hawker Typhoon, disappointing as a high-altitude interceptor, excelled at low-level ground attack with rockets and bombs. USAAF P-47 Thunderbolts and US Navy F4U Corsairs performed extensive air support in Europe and the Pacific.

Post-war development

In the Korean and Vietnam wars, types such as the F-84 Thunderjet, F-105 Thunderchief and F-4 Phantom II carried the main bombing burden for the US Air Force. The dedicated but flexible fighter-bomber gradually gave way to the strike fighter and multirole designs — aircraft designed from the outset for both missions, such as the F/A-18 Hornet and the F-15E Strike Eagle, a strike derivative of an air-superiority fighter.
