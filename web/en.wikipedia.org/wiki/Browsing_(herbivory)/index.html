Browsing (herbivory)

Feeding typeHerbivory
Food sourceLeaves, shoots, twigs and fruit of woody plants
ExamplesDeer, giraffes, goats, elephants

Browsing is a type of herbivory in which a herbivore (or, more narrowly defined, a folivore) feeds on leaves, soft shoots, or fruits of high-growing, generally woody plants such as shrubs and trees. Browsing is contrasted with grazing, in which animals feed on grasses and other low vegetation.

Browsers and mixed feeders

Animals may be dedicated browsers (giraffes, white-tailed deer in winter, goats), dedicated grazers (cattle, horses), or mixed feeders that switch with season and forage availability (elk, impala). Browsers typically have narrow muzzles and mobile, prehensile lips and tongues for selecting individual leaves, and digestive systems adapted to the defensive compounds of woody plants.

Ecological effects

Browsing shapes plant communities by suppressing tree regeneration above a visible "browse line", and plants respond with thorns, tannins, and other defenses. Heavy browsing by overabundant deer can alter forest structure and harm ground-nesting birds, while managed browsing by goats is used to clear invasive shrubs.
