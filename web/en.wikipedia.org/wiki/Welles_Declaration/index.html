Welles Declaration

Welles Declaration
Issued23 July 1940
AuthorSumner Welles, U.S. Acting Secretary of State
SubjectNon-recognition of Soviet annexation of the Baltic states

The Welles Declaration was a diplomatic statement issued on 23 July 1940 by Sumner Welles, the United States acting Secretary of State, condemning the June 1940 occupation of the Baltic states — Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania — by the Soviet Union and refusing to recognise their annexation as Soviet republics.

Background

In June 1940, under the shadow of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, the Soviet Union issued ultimatums to the three Baltic states, occupied them, and staged elections that produced governments requesting incorporation into the USSR.

Contents

Invoking the principles of the Stimson Doctrine of non-recognition, the declaration stated that the United States would not acknowledge territorial changes effected by force. The Baltic legations in Washington remained accredited, and the policy of non-recognition was maintained by successive administrations for five decades.

Legacy

The declaration underpinned the legal continuity of the Baltic states throughout the Cold War. All three countries restored their independence in 1990–1991, and the declaration is commemorated in the Baltic states as a landmark of American support.
