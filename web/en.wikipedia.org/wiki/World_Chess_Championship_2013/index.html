The World Chess Championship 2013 was a match between defending champion Viswanathan Anand and challenger Magnus Carlsen, held in Chennai, India, from 9 to 22 November 2013 under the auspices of FIDE. Carlsen won the match 6½ to 3½ after ten games, becoming the 16th undisputed World Chess Champion. At 22 years old, he was the second-youngest player to win the title outright, after Garry Kasparov.

Qualification

Carlsen earned the right to challenge by winning the Candidates Tournament in London in March–April 2013, finishing ahead of Vladimir Kramnik on tiebreaks after both scored 8½ points. Anand had held the title since 2007 and had defended it against Kramnik, Veselin Topalov and Boris Gelfand.

The match

The match was scheduled for twelve classical games, with the first player to reach 6½ points declared the winner. The first four games were drawn. Carlsen won games five and six, both times grinding down Anand in long endgames after errors by the champion. After two more draws, Carlsen won game nine following a blunder by Anand in a sharp position, and the tenth game was drawn, sealing the match with two games to spare. Carlsen won three games, drew seven and lost none.

Aftermath

The result ended Anand's six-year reign and began Carlsen's tenure as world champion, which lasted until he declined to defend the title in 2023. Anand won the 2014 Candidates Tournament to earn a rematch, which Carlsen won again in Sochi.
