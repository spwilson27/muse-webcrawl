Chemical industry

[Image: A petrochemical complex — distillation columns and flare stacks]

Overview

The chemical industry comprises the companies and organisations that develop and produce industrial, specialty and consumer chemicals. Central to the modern world economy, it converts raw materials — oil, natural gas, air, water, metals and minerals — into tens of thousands of different products: basic petrochemicals and polymers, fertilisers, pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, paints, adhesives, cosmetics and cleaning products. Global revenues are on the order of several trillion US dollars a year.

Structure and issues

The industry divides into commodity (bulk) chemicals, speciality chemicals and life sciences (pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals, often classed separately). The largest producers are in China (now the biggest market and producer), the United States and Europe (Germany's BASF is the largest chemical company by sales in many years). Landmarks of its history include the Haber–Bosch process (1909–13), the petrochemical revolution after 1945, and disasters that reshaped regulation — Seveso (1976), Bhopal (1984) and the subsequent Responsible Care and REACH regimes. Green chemistry and decarbonisation (the industry is energy- and emissions-intensive) are its current frontiers.
