ErbB

The ErbB family of proteins comprises four receptor tyrosine kinases related in structure to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), the first of the family to be discovered. Their name derives from their homology to the product of the avian erythroblastosis virus oncogene v-erbB. Signalling through ErbB receptors regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, migration and survival, and their deregulation is implicated in cancer.

MembersErbB-1 (EGFR/HER1), ErbB-2 (HER2), ErbB-3 (HER3), ErbB-4 (HER4)
TypeReceptor tyrosine kinases
LigandsEGF family, neuregulins

Members

The four members are ErbB-1, known as the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR or HER1); ErbB-2 (HER2/neu), which has no known direct ligand and acts as a preferred dimerisation partner; ErbB-3 (HER3), whose kinase domain is impaired but which signals strongly through heterodimers; and ErbB-4 (HER4). Ligands include epidermal growth factor (EGF), transforming growth factor-α, amphiregulin and the neuregulins.

Signalling

Ligand binding induces receptor dimerisation and autophosphorylation of tyrosine residues in the intracellular domain, recruiting adaptor proteins that activate pathways including Ras–Raf–MEK–ERK (MAPK) and PI3K–Akt. Because the receptors function as homo- and heterodimers, the family generates combinatorial signalling diversity.

Clinical significance

Overexpression or mutation of ErbB receptors occurs in many cancers: EGFR mutations in non-small-cell lung cancer and HER2 amplification in breast and gastric cancers. They are targets of approved drugs including the monoclonal antibodies trastuzumab and cetuximab and small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as gefitinib, erlotinib and lapatinib.
