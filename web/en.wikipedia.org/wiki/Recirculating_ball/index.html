Recirculating ball

The recirculating ball is a mechanism by which a rotating shaft can be converted into linear motion, or vice versa, with very low friction. It consists of a threaded worm shaft, a ball nut, and a set of ball bearings that recirculate between the two. The balls roll in the grooves of the worm and nut, so sliding friction is replaced by rolling friction.

History

The recirculating-ball steering gearbox was widely used in automobiles from the mid-20th century until rack-and-pinion steering became dominant. General Motors' Saginaw division popularized the design in the United States.

Applications

The mechanism is used in steering systems of trucks and off-road vehicles, where its durability and tolerance of high loads are valued, and in precision machine tools and aircraft control linkages. It is closely related to the ball screw, which is used in linear actuators and CNC machinery.
