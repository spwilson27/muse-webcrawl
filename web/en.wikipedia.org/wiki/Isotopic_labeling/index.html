Isotopic labeling

Isotopic labeling (or isotopic labelling) is a technique used in chemistry and biochemistry to track the passage of an isotope through a reaction, metabolic pathway or cell. Atoms in a reactant are replaced by atoms of a different isotope of the same element — a "label" or "tracer" — and the products are analysed to determine the mechanism or fate of the labelled atoms.

Labelled atoms are tracked through a reaction pathway.

Methods

Both radioactive isotopes (such as carbon-14 and tritium, detected by their radiation) and stable isotopes (such as carbon-13, nitrogen-15 and deuterium, detected by mass spectrometry or NMR) are used. Classic applications include proving that oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from water, elucidating reaction mechanisms, and measuring protein turnover with techniques such as SILAC (stable isotope labeling by amino acids in cell culture).
