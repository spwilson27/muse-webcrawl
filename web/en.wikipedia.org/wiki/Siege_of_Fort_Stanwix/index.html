Siege of Fort Stanwix

The siege of Fort Stanwix (also known as Fort Schuyler) took place from 2 to 22 August 1777 during the American Revolutionary War. A British expedition under Brigadier Barry St. Leger, advancing from Canada down the Mohawk Valley as the western prong of the Saratoga campaign, besieged the Continental Army garrison at Fort Stanwix in present-day Rome, New York. The garrison, commanded by Colonel Peter Gansevoort, refused to surrender, and the siege collapsed when a relief column under Benedict Arnold approached and St. Leger's Native American allies deserted.

Background

Fort Stanwix guarded the Oneida Carrying Place, the portage between the Mohawk River and Wood Creek on the route from the Hudson to Lake Ontario. In 1777 it formed part of General John Burgoyne's three-pronged plan to split the rebellious colonies: while Burgoyne advanced down the Lake Champlain corridor, St. Leger's mixed force of British regulars, Loyalists, Canadians, and Haudenosaunee warriors was to descend the Mohawk Valley to Albany.

The siege

Gansevoort's garrison of roughly 750 men, largely from the 3rd New York Regiment, held a fort they had rebuilt and stocked. A relief attempt by militia under Nicholas Herkimer was ambushed at the Battle of Oriskany on 6 August — one of the bloodiest engagements of the war — but during the fighting a sortie from the fort under Marinus Willett raided and plundered St. Leger's camps, destroying supplies and capturing standards. St. Leger, whose artillery was too light to breach the walls, maintained the siege but could make no progress, and his Indigenous allies grew discontented. When Benedict Arnold marched up the valley with a relief force and spread an exaggerated report of its size through a stratagem involving the captive Hon Yost Schuyler, St. Leger's allies abandoned the siege in large numbers, and on 22 August St. Leger withdrew to Canada.

Significance

The failure of the western prong contributed to the isolation and eventual surrender of Burgoyne at Saratoga. The siege is also famous in American tradition for the garrison's flag: lacking regulation colours, the defenders are said to have improvised a flag of red, white, and blue from captured and contributed cloth — often claimed as the first Stars and Stripes flown in battle. Fort Stanwix is preserved today as a National Monument in Rome, New York.
