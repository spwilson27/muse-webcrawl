George W. Bush Presidential Center

Location 2943 SMU Boulevard, Dallas, Texas, United States
Opened May 1, 2013
Architect Robert A.M. Stern Architects (building); Michael Van Valkenburgh Associates (landscape)
Named for George W. Bush, 43rd President of the United States

The George W. Bush Presidential Center is a complex on the campus of Southern Methodist University (SMU) in University Park, Texas, that includes the presidential library and museum of George W. Bush, the 43rd president of the United States, and the George W. Bush Policy Institute. The center was dedicated on April 25, 2013, in a ceremony attended by President Barack Obama and all four living former presidents, and opened to the public on May 1, 2013.

Library and museum

The George W. Bush Presidential Library and Museum is the 13th presidential library administered by the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA). It holds more than 70 million pages of textual materials, some 43,000 artifacts, 200 million emails, and nearly 4 million photographs from the Bush administration, as well as extensive audiovisual holdings.

The 14,000-square-foot museum features permanent exhibits on the major events and decisions of the Bush presidency, including the September 11 attacks, the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, Hurricane Katrina, the financial crisis of 2008, and domestic initiatives such as the No Child Left Behind Act and PEPFAR. A prominent feature is a full-size replica of the Oval Office as it appeared during Bush's presidency, and a 15-acre native Texas prairie landscape surrounds the building, planted with wildflowers, native grasses, and trees.

Bush Institute

The George W. Bush Institute, founded in 2009, is a public policy think tank housed within the center. Its programs address economic growth, education reform, global health, human freedom and democracy, military service and veterans' welfare, and women's leadership initiatives, including the First Ladies Initiative. It publishes research and hosts forums such as the Bush Institute–SMU Economic Growth Initiative.

Architecture and funding

The 226,000-square-foot building, designed by Robert A.M. Stern Architects, is clad in Texas limestone and brick to harmonize with SMU's Georgian Revival campus architecture; it was built to LEED Platinum standards. The Bush Foundation raised more than $500 million for the center's construction and endowment.
