Atticus Finch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Atticus Finch is a fictional character in Harper Lee's 1960 Pulitzer Prize–winning novel To Kill a Mockingbird. A widowed lawyer in the fictional town of Maycomb, Alabama, during the Great Depression, he is the father of Jeremy "Jem" Finch and Jean Louise "Scout" Finch, the novel's narrator. He also appears, in a controversially altered portrayal, in Lee's Go Set a Watchman (2015).

Role in the novel

Atticus is appointed to defend Tom Robinson, a Black man falsely accused of raping a white woman, Mayella Ewell. Despite presenting compelling evidence of Robinson's innocence, the all-white jury convicts him. Atticus embodies integrity, empathy and moral courage; his advice to Scout — that one never really understands a person "until you climb into his skin and walk around in it" — is among the novel's most famous lines. The character was inspired by Lee's father, Amasa Coleman Lee, a lawyer who had defended Black clients.

Portrayal and legacy

Gregory Peck won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal in the 1962 film adaptation, and in 2003 the American Film Institute named Atticus Finch the greatest hero in American cinema. The character has become an enduring symbol of principled lawyering, though later scholarship has debated his paternalism, and the depiction of an older, segregationist Atticus in Go Set a Watchman reignited that debate.
