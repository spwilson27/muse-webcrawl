Gosford Park

Directed by: Robert Altman
Screenplay by: Julian Fellowes
Starring: Maggie Smith, Michael Gambon, Kristin Scott Thomas, Helen Mirren, Clive Owen, Emily Watson
Release date: 2001
Running time: 137 minutes
Country: United Kingdom, United States
Budget: $19.8 million
Box office: $87.8 million

Gosford Park is a 2001 satirical black comedy mystery film directed by Robert Altman and written by Julian Fellowes. The film, which is influenced by Jean Renoir's classic 1939 film The Rules of the Game, follows a party of wealthy Britons plus an American producer, and their servants, who gather for a shooting weekend at Gosford Park, an English country house. A murder occurs after a dinner party, and the film presents an investigation from the servants' and guests' perspectives.

The film features an ensemble cast, which includes Maggie Smith, Michael Gambon, Kristin Scott Thomas, Charles Dance, William Moseley, Tom Hollander, Laurence Fox, James Wilby, Ryan Phillippe, Stephen Fry, Kelly Macdonald, Clive Owen, Helen Mirren, Eileen Atkins, Emily Watson, Alan Bates, Derek Jacobi, and Richard E. Grant.

Plot

In November 1932, Sir William McCordle, a wealthy industrialist, and his wife Lady Sylvia host a shooting party at their estate. Among the guests are Sylvia's imperious aunt, the Countess of Trentham, an American film producer researching for a Charlie Chan movie, and the matinee idol Ivor Novello. Below stairs, the visiting servants take the names of their employers, and the house's staff navigate their own hierarchies and secrets.

After dinner on the second night, Sir William is found stabbed in his study — having already been poisoned. The bumbling Inspector Thompson investigates, while the valet Robert Parks and the ladies' maid Mary Maceachran piece together the truth: Sir William had a history of exploiting the women in his factories, sending their illegitimate children to orphanages, and his past eventually caught up with him.

Reception and legacy

Widely acclaimed, the film was nominated for seven Academy Awards, winning Best Original Screenplay for Fellowes. It won numerous other awards including BAFTAs and a Golden Globe for Altman as Best Director. The film's writer, Julian Fellowes, later created the television series Downton Abbey, which shares its upstairs-downstairs themes and several cast members.
