White Book of Rhydderch

The White Book of Rhydderch (Welsh: Llyfr Gwyn Rhydderch) is a famous medieval Welsh manuscript, the earliest collection of Welsh prose texts and a major source for the tales known as the Mabinogion. Written around the middle of the 14th century, it was compiled for Rhydderch ab Ieuan Llwyd of Cardiganshire, a patron of poets. It survives in two volumes, now Peniarth MS 4 and Peniarth MS 5 in the National Library of Wales.

Contents

The White Book preserves the Four Branches of the Mabinogi, native Welsh tales such as Culhwch and Olwen and The Dream of Macsen Wledig, two of the Three Welsh Romances, saints' lives, religious texts and a Welsh version of the Charlemagne cycle. Together with the later Red Book of Hergest, which overlaps substantially, it transmits most of surviving medieval Welsh prose.

History

Five scribes are distinguished in the manuscript; one is identified as Hywel Fychan, also a scribe of the Red Book. The book passed through the hands of Welsh antiquaries including Robert Vaughan of Hengwrt, whose collection reached Peniarth, and was acquired by the National Library of Wales in the early 20th century. Its name parallels other colour-named Welsh manuscripts — the Black Book of Carmarthen, the Red Book, and the White Book of Hergest (destroyed by fire).
