Land registration

Land registration is a system by which ownership and other rights in land are recorded and guaranteed by a public authority, typically the state. It generally operates in official countries to provide legal certainty in land ownership and to facilitate conveyancing. Systems differ between registration of deeds (documents) and registration of title (ownership itself).

Principles

Title registration systems are often summarised by three principles: the mirror principle (the register reflects all material facts about the title), the curtain principle (purchasers need not investigate behind the register), and the insurance principle (the state indemnifies errors in the register). The Torrens title system, developed in South Australia in the 1850s by Robert Torrens, is a widely used model.

Practice

In England and Wales, HM Land Registry maintains the register of title; registration became compulsory on sale nationwide by 1990. In the United States, most land records operate as deeds-recording systems at county level, supplemented by title insurance. Many jurisdictions have digitised their registers, allowing electronic conveyancing and online searches.

Cadastre

Land registration is closely linked with the cadastre, the official map and register of land parcels used for taxation and planning.
