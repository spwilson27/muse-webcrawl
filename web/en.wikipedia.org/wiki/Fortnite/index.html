Fortnite

Fortnite is a video game and online platform developed by Epic Games that was released in 2017. It is available in seven distinct game mode versions that otherwise share the same general gameplay and game engine: Fortnite Battle Royale, a battle royale game in which up to 100 players fight to be the last person standing; Fortnite: Save the World, a cooperative hybrid tower defense-shooter and survival game in which up to four players fight off zombie-like creatures and defend objects with traps and fortifications they can build; and Fortnite Creative, in which players are given complete freedom to create worlds and battle arenas. Since 2023 the platform has also incorporated Lego Fortnite, Rocket Racing and Fortnite Festival, and the zero-build mode Fortnite OG revivals.

Battle Royale and its sister modes are free-to-play and monetised through V-Bucks, an in-game currency used to buy cosmetic items and the seasonal battle pass. Fortnite became a worldwide cultural phenomenon, drawing more than 125 million players in its first year and generating billions of dollars in revenue; registered accounts had surpassed 500 million by the early 2020s, and live in-game events — concerts by artists such as Travis Scott and Ariana Grande, and story events — drew tens of millions of concurrent viewers.

Gameplay

In Battle Royale, players skydive from a flying "Battle Bus" onto an island, scavenge weapons and resources, and fight as an encroaching storm shrinks the safe area; a distinctive feature is building — harvesting wood, brick and metal to construct walls, ramps and forts for cover and mobility. A "Zero Build" mode introduced in 2022 removes building for players who prefer pure shooting. Seasons and chapters regularly remake the island, introduce storylines and add crossover content from films, comics, anime and real-world celebrities.

Development and release

Fortnite was announced in 2011, inspired by the combination of construction games like Minecraft and Terraria with shooters; Save the World entered paid early access in July 2017. Seeing the success of PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds, Epic rapidly developed Battle Royale, releasing it free in September 2017. Versions followed for all major platforms including mobile. Fortnite was removed from Apple's App Store and Google Play in August 2020 after Epic introduced direct payments, triggering the antitrust case Epic Games v. Apple; it later returned to mobile via regulatory changes in the EU and court rulings in the US.

Cultural impact

Fortnite's dances ("emotes") spread widely in popular culture and sports celebrations; streamers such as Ninja became mainstream celebrities through it, and its 2019 World Cup awarded $30 million in prizes. The game is frequently described as a proto-metaverse platform hosting concerts, film screenings and brand experiences.
