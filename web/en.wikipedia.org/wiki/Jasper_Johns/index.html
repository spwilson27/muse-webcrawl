Jasper Johns (born May 15, 1930) is an American painter, sculptor, draftsman, and printmaker. Considered a leading figure of Pop art and Neo-Dada, he is famous for his depictions of the American flag, targets, numbers, maps, and other instantly recognizable signs, painted in richly worked encaustic surfaces. His work is commonly regarded as a bridge between Abstract Expressionism and the movements that followed.

Early career and Flag

Born in Augusta, Georgia, and raised in South Carolina, Johns moved to New York in 1952 after serving in the army in Japan during the Korean War. In 1954, he said following a dream, he began painting the American flag: Flag (1954–55), executed in encaustic and collage on fabric mounted on plywood, presented a familiar image as a painted object, raising questions about the difference between a flag and a picture of one. His first solo exhibition at the Leo Castelli Gallery in 1958 was a sensation; the Museum of Modern Art purchased several works, and Three Flags (1958) became one of the most reproduced American paintings.

Themes and technique

Johns painted "things the mind already knows" — flags, targets, numerals, letters, maps of the United States — in media including encaustic (hot wax), oil, and later crosshatch abstractions, prints in lithography and etching, and cast-metal sculpture of everyday objects like ale cans (Painted Bronze, 1960). His long creative partnership with Robert Rauschenberg, and friendships with John Cage and Merce Cunningham, placed him at the centre of the post-war New York avant-garde.

Recognition

Johns represented the United States at the Venice Biennale in 1988, winning the Golden Lion, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2011. In 1980 the Whitney Museum paid $1 million for Three Flags, then a record for a living American artist. In 2021–22 the Whitney and the Philadelphia Museum of Art staged the joint retrospective Jasper Johns: Mind/Mirror. His works remain among the most valuable by any living artist.
