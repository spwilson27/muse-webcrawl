Huey Percy Newton (February 17, 1942 – August 22, 1989) was an American revolutionary and political activist. He co-founded the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense with Bobby Seale in Oakland, California, in October 1966, and became the party's leader and chief theoretician, holding the title Minister of Defense.

Early life and founding of the Panthers

Newton was born in Monroe, Louisiana, and raised in Oakland, where his family had migrated during the Second Great Migration. He taught himself to read after graduating high school functionally illiterate (an experience recounted in his autobiography Revolutionary Suicide, 1973) and studied law at Merritt College and San Francisco Law School. With Seale he drafted the Panthers' Ten-Point Program demanding full employment, decent housing, exemption from the draft and an end to police brutality, and organised armed citizen patrols that observed police in Black neighbourhoods.

Trial and aftermath

In 1967 Newton was charged with killing Oakland police officer John Frey in a shootout in which Newton was also shot. His 1968 conviction for voluntary manslaughter sparked the international "Free Huey" campaign; the conviction was reversed on appeal in 1970 and two retrials ended in hung juries before charges were dismissed. The FBI's COINTELPRO program targeted the Panthers as the organisation spread nationally, running survival programs such as Free Breakfast for Children.

Later years and death

After a period of exile in Cuba (1974–77) facing further charges (later dropped or ending in acquittal), Newton returned and in 1980 earned a PhD from UC Santa Cruz with a dissertation on the repression of the Panthers. The party dissolved in 1982 amid internal decline and Newton's struggles with drug addiction. On 22 August 1989 he was shot and killed in Oakland by Tyrone Robinson, a drug dealer; Robinson was convicted of the murder.
