Pina Bausch

Philippine "Pina" Bausch (27 July 1940 – 30 June 2009) was a German dancer and choreographer who was a significant contributor to a neo-expressionist dance tradition now known as Tanztheater ('dance theatre'). She is regarded as one of the most influential choreographers of the twentieth century.

Career

Born in Solingen, Bausch studied at the Folkwang School in Essen under Kurt Jooss, a founder of German expressionist dance, and later at the Juilliard School in New York. In 1973 she became artistic director of the ballet of the Wuppertal opera house, soon renamed Tanztheater Wuppertal Pina Bausch, the company she led until her death.

Style and works

Bausch's pieces — among them The Rite of Spring (1975), staged on a soil-covered stage; Café Müller (1978); Kontakthof (1978); and Nelken (1982), set in a field of carnations — blend movement, speech, and striking stage imagery to explore relationships, memory, and the rituals of everyday life. Her working method drew on questions posed to her dancers, whose personal experiences shaped the material. Her work influenced theatre and popular culture alike; Wim Wenders's 3D documentary Pina (2011) paid tribute to her art.
