The Bishop of Winchester is the diocesan bishop of the Church of England Diocese of Winchester in the Province of Canterbury. The see is among the oldest and, in the Middle Ages, the richest in England; the bishop ranks fifth in the hierarchy of the Church of England and, together with Canterbury, York, London and Durham, is one of the five bishops entitled to sit in the House of Lords regardless of length of service. The bishop is also Visitor of several Oxford and Cambridge colleges and Prelate of the Order of the Garter.

History

The bishopric was founded in 635 by Birinus, apostle of the West Saxons at Dorchester-on-Thames; the seat was transferred to Winchester, the Saxon capital, around 660. Medieval bishops such as St Swithun, Æthelwold, William of Wykeham and Cardinal Henry Beaufort were among the most powerful figures in the kingdom, serving as chancellors and founding Winchester College and New College, Oxford. The bishop's residence is Wolvesey Palace, and his former London residence, Winchester House in Southwark, lent its name to the "Winchester geese" of the Southwark stews, which the bishops licensed.

The diocese today covers most of Hampshire and parts of Dorset (the Channel Islands were transferred from Winchester to Salisbury in 2022). The bishop signs "N. Winton", from the Latin Wintoniensis.
