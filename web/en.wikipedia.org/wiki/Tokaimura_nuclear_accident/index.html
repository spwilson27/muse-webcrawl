Tokaimura nuclear accident

The Tokaimura nuclear accident was a criticality accident that occurred on 30 September 1999 at a uranium conversion facility operated by JCO in Tōkai-mura, Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. It was the most serious civilian nuclear radiation accident in Japan before Fukushima, rated Level 4 on the International Nuclear Event Scale.

The accident

Three workers were preparing a small batch of enriched uranium fuel (18.8% U-235) for the Jōyō experimental fast breeder reactor. Pressed for time and following an unofficial shortcut not in the licensed procedure, they dissolved uranium oxide powder in nitric acid in stainless-steel buckets and poured the solution directly into a precipitation tank — adding roughly 16 kg of uranium, far beyond the 2.4 kg criticality safety limit for the vessel. At about 10:35 a.m. the solution reached critical mass and a self-sustaining fission chain reaction began, accompanied by a blue flash of Cherenkov radiation.

Consequences

The two workers closest to the tank received lethal doses (estimated at 17 and 10 sieverts). Hisashi Ouchi died after 83 days of treatment and Masato Shinohara after 211 days; the third worker survived. The chain reaction continued intermittently for about 20 hours until workers drained a cooling jacket and added boric acid. Some 310,000 residents were advised to stay indoors, and hundreds were exposed to low levels of radiation.

Aftermath

Investigations found JCO had routinely violated its licence conditions. The company pleaded guilty to negligence, several employees received suspended prison sentences, JCO paid compensation and lost its operating licence, and Japan strengthened nuclear safety regulation.
