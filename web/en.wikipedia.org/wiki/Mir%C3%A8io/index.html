Mirèio

Mirèio (in classical Occitan orthography; Mirèlha in Mistralian orthography, French: Mireille) is a long poem in Occitan by Frédéric Mistral, published in 1859. Written in twelve cantos in the Provençal dialect, it is considered the masterpiece of the Félibrige movement and the greatest work of modern Occitan literature.

Plot

The poem tells of the thwarted love between Mirèio, the daughter of a rich farmer of the Crau plain, and Vincèn, a poor basket-maker. Her father refuses the match and promises her to wealthier suitors; desperate, Mirèio flees across the Camargue to the church of Les Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer to pray to the Three Marys, but she is struck down by the sun on the journey and dies in the church in a vision of the saints. Along the way the poem vividly describes Provençal landscapes, customs, work, and legends.

Reception

Mirèio brought Mistral European fame; Alphonse de Lamartine praised it extravagantly, and Charles Gounod adapted it as the opera Mireille (1864). Together with the Félibrige, the poem helped revive Occitan as a literary language, work for which Mistral received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904.
