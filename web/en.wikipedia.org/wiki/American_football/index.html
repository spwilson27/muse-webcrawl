American football, known in the United States and Canada simply as football, is a team sport played by two teams of eleven players on a rectangular field with goalposts at each end. The offense, the team with possession of the oval-shaped football, attempts to advance down the field by running with the ball or throwing it, while the defense aims to stop the offensive team's advance and to take control of the ball for themselves. The offense must advance at least ten yards in four downs or plays; if they succeed, they receive a new set of four downs. Points are scored primarily by advancing the ball into the opposing team's end zone for a touchdown, or by kicking the ball through the opponent's goalposts for a field goal.

The sport evolved from soccer and rugby in the late 19th century; Walter Camp, the "Father of American Football", introduced the line of scrimmage, downs and the snap in the 1880s. The first game commonly cited was played between Rutgers and Princeton on 6 November 1869.

Professional game

The National Football League (NFL), founded in 1920, is the pre-eminent league, with 32 teams in two conferences (AFC and NFC). Its championship game, the Super Bowl, is among the most-watched television broadcasts in the United States. College football, governed by the NCAA, is also enormously popular. Legendary figures include Tom Brady, winner of seven Super Bowls, Jim Brown, and Joe Montana.
