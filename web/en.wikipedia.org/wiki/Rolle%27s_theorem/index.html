Rolle's theorem

Rolle's theorem
FieldCalculus
Named afterMichel Rolle
Stated1691

In calculus, Rolle's theorem states that if a real-valued function f is continuous on a closed interval [a, b], differentiable on the open interval (a, b), and f(a) = f(b), then there exists at least one point c in (a, b) at which the derivative f′(c) = 0.

History

The result was known in special cases to Indian mathematician Bhāskara II in the 12th century and was stated by Michel Rolle in 1691 for polynomials. The general modern version followed in the 19th century.

Significance

Rolle's theorem is the key lemma in the proof of the mean value theorem, and it expresses the intuitive fact that a smooth curve returning to the same height must somewhere level off.
