Block copolymer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A block copolymer is a copolymer formed when two or more homopolymer chains are linked together in distinct blocks along a single macromolecule. Because the blocks are often chemically incompatible, block copolymers undergo microphase separation, self-assembling into periodic nanostructures such as spheres, cylinders, gyroids and lamellae.

Synthesis

Block copolymers are typically made by controlled ("living") polymerisation techniques, including anionic polymerisation, in which monomers are added sequentially, and controlled radical methods such as ATRP and RAFT polymerisation. The classic commercial example is the styrene-butadiene-styrene (SBS) triblock copolymer.

Applications

Block copolymers are used as thermoplastic elastomers (for example in shoe soles and adhesives), as compatibilisers for polymer blends, as templates for nanopatterning in lithography, and in drug delivery vehicles such as polymeric micelles.
