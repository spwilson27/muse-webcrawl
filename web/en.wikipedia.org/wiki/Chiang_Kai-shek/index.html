Chiang Kai-shek

Chiang Kai-shek (October 31, 1887 – April 5, 1975), also known as Chiang Chung-cheng and the Generalissimo, was a Chinese politician, revolutionary and military leader who led the Republic of China from 1928 until his death. A protégé of Sun Yat-sen, he commanded the National Revolutionary Army, unified much of China during the Northern Expedition, led China through the Second Sino-Japanese War, and — after losing the Chinese Civil War to Mao Zedong's Communists in 1949 — ruled the Republic of China on Taiwan as its president under martial law.

Rise to power

Trained at military academies in China and Japan, Chiang joined Sun Yat-sen's movement and in 1924 became commandant of the Whampoa Military Academy. Following Sun's death in 1925, Chiang led the Northern Expedition (1926–28), defeating the warlords and nominally unifying China under the Kuomintang government at Nanjing. In April 1927 he broke violently with the Communists in the Shanghai massacre, beginning a decades-long conflict.

War with Japan

During the Nanjing decade, Chiang's government modernised parts of China while fighting Communist insurgencies and resisting Japanese encroachment. After the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of 1937, China fought Japan in a brutal eight-year war; Chiang's forces bore much of the conventional fighting, retreating to Chongqing, and China emerged as one of the Allied "Big Four". The conflict cost millions of lives and devastated the Nationalist state.

Civil war and Taiwan

Full-scale civil war resumed in 1946. Corruption, hyperinflation and military collapse led to defeat; in December 1949 Chiang evacuated his government, troops, and much of China's gold and art treasures to Taiwan. There he ruled as president of the Republic of China until his death in 1975, presiding over martial law and the White Terror, but also over land reform and rapid economic growth. He was succeeded by his son, Chiang Ching-kuo.

Legacy

Chiang remains a controversial figure: credited with unifying China and resisting Japan, he is also criticised for authoritarianism, corruption in his government, and responsibility for episodes such as the 1938 Yellow River flood. In Taiwan his memory is contested, while the Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall in Taipei remains a major landmark.
