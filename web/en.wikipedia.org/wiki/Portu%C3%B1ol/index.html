Portuñol

The twin border cities of Rivera (Uruguay) and Santana do Livramento (Brazil), a heartland of Portuñol.

Portuñol (in Portuguese, portunhol) is a portmanteau of the words portugués/português and español/espanhol, referring to any unsystematic mixture of Portuguese and Spanish. It arises along the borders between Portuguese-speaking Brazil and Spanish-speaking countries, and in contact zones in South America and the Iberian Peninsula.

Usage

Portuñol is used for practical communication where speakers of the two closely related languages meet without full command of the other tongue. Because Spanish and Portuguese share much vocabulary and grammar, speakers can often improvise an intelligible mixture, drawing on shared words and adjusting pronunciation and endings.

Uruguayan Portuñol

A more stable, native variety is spoken in northern Uruguay along the Brazilian border, around the twin cities of Rivera and Santana do Livramento. There, generations of bilingual contact have produced a recognised local dialect — sometimes called portuñol riverense — that is the mother tongue of many residents and a subject of sociolinguistic study. Uruguayan writer Fabián Severo has published poetry in this variety.

Status

Spontaneous Portuñol is not a standardised language and has no official status; attitudes toward it vary from pragmatic acceptance in border commerce to criticism in education, where it may be viewed as interference between the two standard languages.
