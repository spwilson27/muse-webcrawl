The Skyflash, known as the Sky Flash in marketing material, was a medium-range semi-active radar homing air-to-air missile derived from the American AIM-7 Sparrow missile and carried by Royal Air Force McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantoms and Tornado F3s, Italian Aeronautica Militare and Royal Saudi Air Force Tornados and Swedish Air Force Saab 37 Viggens.

History

Skyflash was developed by BAe Dynamics as the result of a series of studies beginning around 1972 into replacing the original Sparrow on British Phantoms. The missile replaced the Sparrow's seeker with a Marconi inverse monopulse seeker which gave it greatly improved performance, allowing it to work in clutter and giving it much better resistance to electronic countermeasures. The rocket motor was also replaced; the Royal Ordnance "Bristol Aerojet" motor added two seconds of burn time and, with it, greater range.

The missile entered service with the RAF in 1978, initially equipping the Phantom FGR.2. It was later carried by the Tornado F3, for which the improved Super Skyflash variant with an active radar seeker was proposed but not adopted. Skyflash remained in RAF service until the Tornado F3 force transitioned to the AIM-120 AMRAAM.

Design

Skyflash fitted into the semi-recessed launchers of the Phantom and Tornado, which reduced drag compared with underwing carriage. Its fuzing system used a Thorn EMI active radar fuze. Flight control was through an autopilot that enabled the missile to fly a proportional-navigation course before turning on its seeker for the terminal phase.

Operators

Operators included the United Kingdom, Italy, Saudi Arabia and Sweden, where the missile was designated Rb 71.
