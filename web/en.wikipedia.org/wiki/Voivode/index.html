Voivode

Voivode (also spelled voivod, voyvode, or voivoda) is a title denoting a military leader or warlord in Central, Southeastern and Eastern Europe since the Early Middle Ages. It primarily referred to the medieval rulers of the Romanian principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia, as well as governors of provinces in medieval Hungary, Serbia, Poland, and other Slavic lands.

Etymology

The word derives from the Slavic roots voi ("war, army") and voditi ("to lead"), thus literally meaning "army leader" or "war leader". It is related to terms translated as "duke" (from Latin dux) in Western European contexts.

Historical usage

In the Romanian lands, the title voievod was borne by the princes of Wallachia and Moldavia, the most famous being Vlad III the Impaler, voivode of Wallachia. In Transylvania, the voivode was the highest-ranking royal official, appointed by the King of Hungary. In Serbia, the title vojvoda was given to senior military commanders and was revived as the highest rank in the Serbian army in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, voivodes governed the provinces (voivodeships), a usage preserved in Poland's modern administrative divisions.

Modern usage

Today, the head of a Polish voivodeship is called a wojewoda (voivode), representing the central government in the province. In Serbia, "Vojvodina" — the northern autonomous province — takes its name from the historical title.
