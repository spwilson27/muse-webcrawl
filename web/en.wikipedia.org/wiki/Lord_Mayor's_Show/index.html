Lord Mayor's Show

The Lord Mayor's Show is an annual public procession in the City of London, England, dating back to the 13th century. It marks the inauguration of the new Lord Mayor of London, who travels from the City to the Royal Courts of Justice in Westminster to swear loyalty to the Crown. It is one of the oldest and grandest civic pageants in the world.

History

A charter of King John in 1215 allowed the citizens of London to elect their own mayor, on condition that the new mayor present himself to the king or his justices for approval. The journey to Westminster became an annual procession, at times travelling by barge on the River Thames — the origin of the word "float" for parade exhibits. Famous pageants were devised by writers including John Lydgate, Anthony Munday and Thomas Dekker, and the diarist Samuel Pepys recorded elaborate 17th-century shows. The gilded State Coach used today dates from 1757.

The modern show

The modern procession, held on a Saturday in November, runs for about three miles and includes some 7,000 participants: livery companies, military bands and units, charities, community groups, giant inflatables, and the Gog and Magog giant figures, traditional guardians of the City. It is followed in the evening by fireworks over the Thames. The Lord Mayor rides in the State Coach, escorted by the Honourable Artillery Company and the Pikemen and Musketeers.

Flotilla

In some years a river flotilla precedes the land procession, led by the row barge Gloriana, the Queen's Row Barge built for the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II in 2012.
