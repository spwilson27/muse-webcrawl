Taebong

Taebong (Korean: 태봉; Hanja: 泰封), also known as Later Goguryeo (후고구려), was a state established on the Korean Peninsula by the rebel leader Gung Ye in 901, during the Later Three Kingdoms period, after the decline of Unified Silla.

History

Gung Ye, a former monk and disaffected member of the Silla royal house, rose through the rebel armies of the late ninth century and established his own kingdom, initially styling it Later Goguryeo to claim succession from the ancient kingdom of Goguryeo. In 904 he renamed the state Majin, and in 911 changed the name again to Taebong, moving his capital to Cheorwon (Taejo later moved it back to Songak, modern Kaesong).

Gung Ye proclaimed himself the Maitreya Buddha incarnate and ruled with increasing religious fanaticism and tyranny, executing officials and even members of his own family whom he judged, using claimed mind-reading powers, to harbour treasonous thoughts. In 918 his generals — Hong Yu, Bae Hyeon-gyeong, Shin Sung-gyeom and Bok Ji-gyeom — deposed him in a coup and placed the popular commander Wang Geon on the throne. Wang Geon renamed the state Goryeo, which went on to reunify the Later Three Kingdoms by 936. Gung Ye fled and was killed by soldiers or peasants shortly afterwards.
