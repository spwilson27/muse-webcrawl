La Cantuta massacre

The La Cantuta massacre was the abduction and murder of a university professor and nine students of the Enrique Guzmán y Valle National Education University (known as "La Cantuta") in Lima, Peru, on 18 July 1992. The killings were carried out by Grupo Colina, a death squad attached to the Peruvian Army and operating under the government of President Alberto Fujimori during Peru's internal conflict with the Shining Path insurgency.

The abduction

In the early morning of 18 July 1992—two days after the Shining Path's deadly Tarata bombing in Miraflores—soldiers entered the university residences, which were under military control, and seized professor Hugo Muñoz Sánchez and nine students: Bertila Lozano Torres, Dora Oyague Fierro, Robert Teodoro Espinoza, Marcelino Rosales Cárdenas, Felipe Flores Chipana, Luis Enrique Ortiz Perea, Armando Richard Amaro Cóndor, Heráclides Pablo Meza, and Juan Gabriel Mariños Figueroa. The ten were never seen alive again.

Discovery and investigations

In 1993, clandestine graves containing burned human remains were located at Cieneguilla and Huachipa following anonymous information passed to journalists and congressman Henry Pease; forensic work and a set of keys identified the remains as those of some of the victims. The case, together with the Barrios Altos massacre, became emblematic of Fujimori-era human rights abuses. A 1995 amnesty law shielded the perpetrators until Peru's post-Fujimori courts annulled it.

Trials

Members of Grupo Colina were eventually convicted, and in 2009 Alberto Fujimori himself was convicted by a Peruvian court of human rights crimes including the La Cantuta killings and sentenced to 25 years' imprisonment. The Inter-American Court of Human Rights ruled against Peru in the case in 2006, ordering reparations to the victims' families.
