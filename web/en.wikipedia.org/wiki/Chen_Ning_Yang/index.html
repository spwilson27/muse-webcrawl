Chen Ning Yang (Chinese: 杨振宁; 1 October 1922 – 18 October 2025), also known as C. N. Yang or Yang Zhenning, was a Chinese-American theoretical physicist who made major contributions to statistical mechanics, condensed matter theory, particle physics, and gauge theory.

Work

Educated at National Southwestern Associated University and the University of Chicago (PhD 1948, under Edward Teller), Yang joined the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. With Robert Mills in 1954 he formulated Yang–Mills theory — non-abelian gauge theory — which became a foundation of the Standard Model. In 1956, with Tsung-Dao Lee, he proposed that parity might not be conserved in weak interactions; the suggestion was confirmed experimentally by Chien-Shiung Wu, and Yang and Lee shared the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics. His other contributions include the Lee–Yang phase transition theory and the Yang–Baxter equation.

Later life

Yang spent most of his career at Stony Brook University, where an institute bears his name. In later life he returned to China, taking a position at Tsinghua University in Beijing, and renounced his US citizenship in 2015. He died in Beijing in 2025 at the age of 103.
