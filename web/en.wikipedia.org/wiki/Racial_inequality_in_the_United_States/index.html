Racial inequality in the United States

Placeholder image: median wealth by race in the US.

Racial inequality in the United States identifies the social inequality that occurs between racial or ethnic groups within the United States, notably in wealth, income, employment, housing, education, health care, and the criminal justice system. Simply defined, racial inequality is the unequal distribution of resources, opportunities, and rights based on race.

The legacy of slavery, segregation under Jim Crow laws, redlining, and other discriminatory policies has contributed to persistent disparities between White Americans and other groups, particularly Black, Hispanic, and Native Americans.

Wealth and income

The racial wealth gap is among the most pronounced measures: Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances data have repeatedly found median White household wealth to be several times that of median Black and Hispanic households — in 2022, roughly $285,000 for White families compared with about $45,000 for Black families and $61,000 for Hispanic families. Gaps in median income are smaller but persistent.

Housing

From the 1930s, the Home Owners' Loan Corporation and Federal Housing Administration institutionalised redlining, denying mortgage credit to neighbourhoods with Black residents. The practice depressed homeownership and wealth accumulation in those communities for generations. The Fair Housing Act of 1968 outlawed housing discrimination, but studies document continuing disparities in lending, appraisal values, and residential segregation.

Criminal justice

Black Americans are incarcerated in state prisons at roughly five times the rate of White Americans, reflecting disparities in policing, sentencing (including the historical crack/powder cocaine sentencing differential), and socioeconomic conditions. Mass incarceration since the 1970s has been a major focus of research and reform movements.

Education and health

Funding of public schools through local property taxes perpetuates unequal resources between districts. Racial disparities are also documented in health outcomes, including maternal mortality, life expectancy, and access to care; Native American communities experience particularly severe shortfalls, rooted in treaty obligations and chronic underfunding of the Indian Health Service.
