Tatar (татар теле, tatar tele) is a Turkic language spoken by the Volga Tatars, mainly in Tatarstan and Bashkortostan and across Russia and Central Asia. It is the second most widely spoken language of the Russian Federation and, together with Russian, an official language of the Republic of Tatarstan.

Classification and dialects

Tatar belongs to the Kipchak branch of the Turkic languages and is closely related to Bashkir, Kazakh and Karakalpak. Its main dialects are the Central (Kazan) dialect, which forms the basis of the literary language, the Western (Mishar) dialect, and the Eastern (Siberian) dialects spoken by the Siberian Tatars.

Writing systems

Tatar was written in a form of the Arabic script (İske imlâ, later Yaña imlâ) until 1928, then in a Latin alphabet (Jaꞑalif) until 1939, when a Cyrillic-based alphabet was imposed in the Soviet Union. Since 2000 there have been efforts, partly restricted by federal law, to revive a Latin-based alphabet (Zamanälif). Crimean Tatar, a distinct language, is often confused with Volga Tatar.

Grammar

Like other Turkic languages, Tatar is agglutinative, uses vowel harmony, has no grammatical gender, and normally follows subject–object–verb word order. It has six cases and a rich system of converbs and evidential forms.
