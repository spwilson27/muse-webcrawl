Districts of Austria

Map of the districts and statutory cities of Austria.

Austria is divided into 94 districts (Bezirke), which form the second level of the country's administrative division below the nine federal states (Bundesländer) and above the municipalities. Of these, 79 are rural districts administered by a district commission (Bezirkshauptmannschaft), and 15 are statutory cities (Statutarstädte), which combine the functions of a municipality and a district.

Function

Districts are administrative units of the state rather than self-governing bodies: they have no elected councils. The district commission, headed by a district governor appointed by the state government, carries out tasks such as issuing passports and driving licences, business licensing, and elements of public health and social services administration. Vienna, the capital, is both a state and a statutory city and is itself divided into 23 municipal districts with a different role.

History

The district system dates to the reforms of 1848–1868 in the Austrian Empire, when district commissions were established as local arms of the state administration. The number and boundaries of districts have changed periodically since, most recently with mergers in Styria in 2012–2013 and the creation of new arrangements in 2017, when several districts were reorganised.
