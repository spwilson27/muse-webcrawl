1983 Cricket World Cup Final

DateJune 25, 1983
VenueLord's, London
ResultIndia 183 defeated West Indies 140
Margin43 runs
Player of the matchMohinder Amarnath

The 1983 Cricket World Cup Final was played at Lord's in London on June 25, 1983, between India and the two-time defending champion West Indies. India, captained by Kapil Dev, won by 43 runs in one of the great upsets in cricket history, transforming the popularity and commercial future of the one-day game in India.

Match summary

India batted first and scored 183, with Kris Srikkanth top-scoring with 38 against a formidable West Indian pace attack. The total appeared modest. In reply, Viv Richards attacked before Kapil Dev's running catch dismissed him for 33, a turning point. Mohinder Amarnath took three wickets with his medium pace and Madan Lal also took three as West Indies were bowled out for 140.

Legacy

The victory broke West Indies' World Cup monopoly and is remembered as the moment Indian cricket became a mass national spectacle. It encouraged the growth of one-day internationals in South Asia and set the stage for India and Pakistan to host the 1987 tournament, the first outside England.
