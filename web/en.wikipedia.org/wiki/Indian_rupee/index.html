Indian rupee

The Indian rupee (symbol: ₹; code: INR) is the official currency of India. It is issued and regulated by the Reserve Bank of India under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The rupee is subdivided into 100 paise. Its name derives from the Sanskrit rūpya ("wrought silver") via the silver coin rupiya introduced by Sher Shah Suri in the 16th century.

History

India was among the earliest issuers of coins, with punch-marked coins circulating from around the 6th century BCE. Sher Shah Suri's rupiya (1540–1545) set the standard carried forward by the Mughals and the British East India Company, which in 1835 established a uniform silver rupee coinage across British India. The rupee was decimalised in 1957, replacing the old system of annas and pies. After independence, India maintained a par value system, then a basket peg; the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis led to devaluation and a market-determined exchange rate from 1993.

Symbol and banknotes

The rupee symbol ₹, designed by Udaya Kumar and adopted in 2010, combines the Devanagari letter र (ra) with a double horizontal line evoking the equals sign and the Indian tricolour. Current banknotes of the Mahatma Gandhi series are issued in denominations of ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200 and ₹500; the ₹2,000 note introduced after the November 2016 demonetisation was withdrawn from circulation in 2023. Coins circulate in denominations from 50 paise to ₹20.

International role

The rupee is closely linked with the currencies of Nepal (pegged at ₹1 = NPR 1.60) and Bhutan (at par). India has promoted rupee-denominated trade settlement with several partner countries as part of a gradual internationalisation of the currency, while full capital-account convertibility remains a longer-term goal debated by committees such as the Tarapore Committee.
