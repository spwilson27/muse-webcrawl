Moses ben Maimon (1135/1138–1204), commonly called Maimonides and in Jewish tradition the Rambam, was a Sephardic rabbi, philosopher and physician, the preeminent Jewish thinker of the Middle Ages. His fourteen-volume Mishneh Torah codified Jewish law, while the Guide for the Perplexed, written in Judeo-Arabic, reconciled Aristotelian philosophy with Jewish theology through allegorical reading and a rigorous negative theology — God can be described only by what He is not.

Life and influence

Fleeing Almohad persecution, his family wandered Spain and Morocco before settling in Egypt, where he became court physician and leader of the Jewish community. His Thirteen Principles of Faith became a standard creed. The Guide influenced Thomas Aquinas, the scholastics, and later Spinoza and modern Jewish thought; controversy over his rationalism ('the Maimonidean controversy') split medieval Jewish communities.
