Oromia (Oromo: Oromiyaa) is a regional state in Ethiopia and the homeland of the Oromo people. The capital city of Oromia is Addis Ababa (also called Finfinnee in Oromo). It is bordered by the Somali Region to the east; the Amhara Region, the Afar Region and the Benishangul-Gumuz Region to the north; South Sudan, Gambela Region, Sidama Region, South Ethiopia Regional State and Kenya to the west; as well as the Dire Dawa, Harari Region and the Ethiopian capital Addis Ababa, which it completely surrounds.

Oromia is the largest regional state in Ethiopia by both area and population. In the 2007 census its population was about 27 million, more than 85 percent of them ethnic Oromo; more recent estimates place the population near 40 million. The region accounts for a large share of Ethiopia's agricultural output, including coffee (which originated in the Kaffa area), teff, wheat and livestock, and contains important natural features such as the Bale Mountains National Park and the Awash valley.

History

The Oromo, a Cushitic-speaking people, expanded northwards into the Ethiopian highlands in the 16th century. They were progressively incorporated into the Ethiopian Empire in the late 19th century under Menelik II. Modern Oromo nationalism developed in the 1960s and 1970s; the Oromo Liberation Front (OLF) was founded in 1973. The present federal region was established in 1995 under Ethiopia's ethnic federal constitution. The region has seen sustained political protest, notably the Oromo protests of 2014–2018, which contributed to national political change, and ongoing conflict involving the Oromo Liberation Army.

Administration

Oromia is divided into zones, including East and West Shewa, Arsi, Bale, Borana, Guji, the Hararghes, Illubabor, Jimma, Nekemte and others, and further into districts (woredas). Its government is headed by a regional president; the Prosperity Party has governed since the restructuring of the former ruling coalition party, the Oromo Democratic Party.
