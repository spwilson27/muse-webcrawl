Stolen Valor Act of 2005

The Stolen Valor Act of 2005 was a United States federal law that broadened the provisions of earlier statutes criminalising the unauthorized wearing, manufacture, or sale of military decorations or medals. It also made it an offence to falsely claim, orally or in writing, to have been awarded any military decoration or medal, including the Purple Heart and the Medal of Honor. The act was signed into law by President George W. Bush on December 20, 2006.

Provisions

Violations were misdemeanours punishable by fines and up to six months' imprisonment, or up to one year where the false claim involved the Medal of Honor, a service cross, the Silver Star, or the Purple Heart. The law was named for the 1998 book Stolen Valor by B. G. Burkett and Glenna Whitley, which documented impostors claiming military service and honours.

United States v. Alvarez

In 2012, the Supreme Court of the United States struck down the act's false-claim provision as a violation of the First Amendment in United States v. Alvarez. Xavier Alvarez, a California water-district board member, had falsely claimed to be a retired Marine and Medal of Honor recipient. The Court held that false statements alone, without legally cognisable harm, are protected speech, and that the act was not narrowly tailored.

Stolen Valor Act of 2013

In response, Congress passed the Stolen Valor Act of 2013, which criminalises falsely claiming military honours only when done with intent to obtain money, property, or another tangible benefit. President Barack Obama signed it into law on June 3, 2013.
