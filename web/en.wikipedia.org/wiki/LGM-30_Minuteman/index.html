The LGM-30 Minuteman is an American land-based intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) in service with the United States Air Force Global Strike Command. First deployed in 1962 during the Cuban Missile Crisis, it was the first ICBM to use solid propellant at scale, the first to carry multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs, with the Minuteman III of 1970), and for decades formed the backbone of the ground leg of the US nuclear triad. As of the 2020s, approximately 400 Minuteman III missiles remain deployed in silos across Montana, North Dakota and Wyoming.

Development

Minuteman grew out of late-1950s work by Colonel Edward Hall on solid-fuel rockets, which promised missiles that could be stored in silos and launched within minutes ("in a minute", hence the name, honouring also the Revolutionary War militia). Unlike liquid-fuelled Atlas and Titan missiles that needed lengthy fuelling, Minuteman could sit on alert for years. The three-stage design used lightweight composite casings and, in a production innovation, multiple suppliers to build the largest solid rocket motors of their time cheaply and reliably.

Variants and modernization

Minuteman I (1962) was followed by the more accurate Minuteman II (1965) and the MIRV-carrying Minuteman III (1970), which could strike three targets with improved accuracy and penetration aids. Successive programmes replaced the guidance systems, rocket motors and warheads, extending Minuteman III's life well beyond its intended decade. Under New START limits the deployed force was downloaded to a single warhead per missile. The missile is scheduled to be replaced by the LGM-35A Sentinel (formerly Ground Based Strategic Deterrent), developed by Northrop Grumman, though the programme has faced major cost growth and schedule review.
