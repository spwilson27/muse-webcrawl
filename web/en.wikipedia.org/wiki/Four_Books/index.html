Four Books

The Four Books (Chinese: 四書; pinyin: Sìshū) are Chinese classic texts illustrating the core value and belief systems in Confucianism. They were selected by the neo-Confucian philosopher Zhu Xi in the Song dynasty as an introduction to Confucian thought, and from 1313 to 1905 they formed the basis of the imperial civil-service examinations in China. Together with the Five Classics they constitute the Confucian canon.

The texts

  • Great Learning (Dàxué) — a chapter of the Book of Rites, on self-cultivation and governance.
  • Doctrine of the Mean (Zhōngyōng) — also from the Book of Rites, on harmony, balance and sincerity.
  • Analects (Lúnyǔ) — sayings and ideas attributed to Confucius and his contemporaries, compiled by his followers.
  • Mencius (Mèngzǐ) — dialogues of the Confucian philosopher Mencius, on human nature and benevolent government.

Significance

Zhu Xi wrote extensive commentaries on the Four Books, which became orthodoxy for the civil examinations. Memorisation and commentary on these texts shaped Chinese education and officialdom for nearly six centuries, and they remain central to the study of Confucianism.
