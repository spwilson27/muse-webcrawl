Fort Myer is a United States Army post in Arlington County, Virginia, adjacent to Arlington National Cemetery and across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C. Since 2009 it has formed part of Joint Base Myer–Henderson Hall. It is best known as the home of the 3rd US Infantry Regiment, "The Old Guard", the Army's official ceremonial unit and escort to the President.

History

The post began in 1863 as Fort Whipple, one of the Civil War defences of Washington, built on the confiscated Custis-Lee estate. It was renamed in 1881 for Albert J. Myer, the Army's first chief signal officer, who had established a signal school there. Fort Myer witnessed milestones in military aviation: in 1908 Orville Wright made demonstration flights for the Army at the post, and the first fatality of powered aviation, Lieutenant Thomas Selfridge, died there in a crash that September. The post also hosted the Army's cavalry showcase and, later, became the ceremonial heart of the Army in the capital region; Generals George S. Patton and other notable officers were stationed there, and quarters on "Generals' Row" house senior officers.

Present day

Fort Myer provides housing and support for service members working in the National Capital Region, hosts The Old Guard and the US Army Band ("Pershing's Own"), and its parade ground, Summerall Field, is the scene of ceremonies including Twilight Tattoo performances.
