Adobe Flash

Adobe Flash (formerly Macromedia Flash and FutureSplash) was a multimedia software platform used for producing animation, rich web applications, desktop applications, mobile apps, mobile games, and embedded web browser video players. Flash displayed text, vector graphics, and raster graphics to provide animations, video games, and applications, and allowed streaming of audio and video and the capture of mouse, keyboard, microphone, and camera input.

History

Flash originated with FutureWave Software's SmartSketch drawing program, which was reworked into FutureSplash Animator in 1996. Macromedia acquired FutureWave that year and renamed the product Flash. Adobe Systems acquired Macromedia in 2005. Flash became the dominant platform for online video and browser games through the 2000s, powering sites such as YouTube and Newgrounds.

Decline and end of life

The rise of HTML5, persistent security vulnerabilities, and Apple's refusal to support Flash on iOS accelerated its decline. Adobe announced in 2017 that Flash Player would be retired; support ended on December 31, 2020, and Adobe blocked Flash content from running in Flash Player beginning January 12, 2021. Preservation projects such as Ruffle and BlueMaxima's Flashpoint archive Flash content for posterity.

Related technologies

Flash content was authored in the Adobe Animate (formerly Flash Professional) environment and played in the Flash Player runtime; files used the SWF format, and scripting was done in ActionScript, an ECMAScript dialect. Adobe AIR allowed Flash applications to run as standalone desktop and mobile apps.
