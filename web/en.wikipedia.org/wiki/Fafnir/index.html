Fafnir

In Norse mythology, Fafnir (Old Norse: Fáfnir) is a son of the dwarf king Hreidmar who was turned into a dragon by the curse of the treasure he stole. He is slain by the hero Sigurd, whose story is told in the Völsunga saga, the Poetic Edda poems Reginsmál and Fáfnismál, and the Prose Edda.

Sigurd slays Fafnir, depicted on the Hylestad stave church portal.

Mythology

After the gods paid a ransom of cursed gold for the killing of the otter Ótr, Fafnir murdered his father Hreidmar to seize the hoard, drove off his brother Regin, and, consumed by greed, transformed into a dragon to guard the treasure on Gnitaheith. Regin fostered Sigurd and incited him to kill Fafnir. Sigurd dug a pit along the dragon's path, stabbed upwards into its belly as it passed, and, warned by birds after tasting the dragon's blood, learned of Regin's plan to betray him.

Legacy

Fafnir appears as Fafner in Richard Wagner's operas Das Rheingold and Siegfried, and has become a prototype of the treasure-guarding dragon in later fantasy literature.
