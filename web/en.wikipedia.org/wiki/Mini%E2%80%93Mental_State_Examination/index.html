Mini–Mental State Examination

The Mini–Mental State Examination (MMSE) is a 30-point questionnaire widely used in clinical and research settings to measure cognitive impairment and screen for dementia. It was introduced by Marshal Folstein, Susan Folstein and Paul McHugh in 1975.

Content

The test samples orientation to time and place, registration and recall of words, attention and calculation, language, and visual construction (copying intersecting pentagons). Administration takes about five to ten minutes. Scores of 24 or above are commonly treated as normal, with lower ranges suggesting mild (19–23), moderate (10–18) or severe (≤9) impairment.

Limitations

The MMSE is influenced by age, education and language, and is relatively insensitive to mild cognitive impairment and frontal-lobe dysfunction; alternatives such as the Montreal Cognitive Assessment are often preferred. The test is copyrighted and licensed commercially.
