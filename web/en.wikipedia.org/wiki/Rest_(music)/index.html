Rest (music)

A rest is a musical notation sign that indicates the absence of a sound — an interval of silence lasting for the duration of the rest. Each rest symbol corresponds in length to a particular note value, and rests are as important to rhythm as the notes themselves.

RestEquivalent note
Whole (semibreve) restWhole note
Half (minim) restHalf note
Quarter (crotchet) restQuarter note
Eighth (quaver) restEighth note
Sixteenth (semiquaver) restSixteenth note

The whole rest — a small rectangle hanging from the fourth line of the staff — also serves as a whole-measure rest in any time signature; the half rest is the same shape sitting on the third line. A dot placed after a rest increases its duration by half, as with notes. A rest spanning multiple measures may be notated with a long bar and a number indicating the count of silent measures.

In ensemble music, rests allow individual parts to fall silent while the musical texture continues; in solo music they articulate phrasing and metre. An entire composition built on silence, John Cage's 4′33″ (1952), consists of three movements during which the performer does not play.
