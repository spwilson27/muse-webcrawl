Power sum symmetric polynomial

In mathematics, specifically in commutative algebra, the power sum symmetric polynomials are a type of basic building block for symmetric polynomials, in the sense that every symmetric polynomial can be expressed as a polynomial in the power sum symmetric polynomials with rational coefficients — though not necessarily with integer coefficients.

Definition

The power sum symmetric polynomial of degree k in n variables x1, …, xn, written pk, is the sum pk = x1k + x2k + … + xnk. Thus p1 is the sum of the variables, p2 the sum of their squares, and so on.

Relations

Newton's identities relate the power sums to the elementary symmetric polynomials: for example, p1 = e1, p2 = e1p1 − 2e2, and p3 = e1p2 − e2p1 + 3e3. Using these identities, the power sums p1, …, pn generate the ring of symmetric polynomials over the rationals, although over the integers the elementary symmetric polynomials are needed. Products of power sums, indexed by partitions, form another basis of the space of homogeneous symmetric polynomials of each degree.

Applications

Power sums appear in the theory of polynomial equations (through the traces of companion matrices), in representation theory via the Frobenius characteristic map, and in statistics, where sums of powers of data values determine the sample moments.
