Collectivization in the Soviet Union

The Soviet Union introduced collectivization of its agricultural sector between 1928 and 1940 during the ascendancy of Joseph Stalin. It began during and was part of the first five-year plan, aiming to merge individual peasant farms into collective farms (kolkhozes) and state farms (sovkhozes).

Implementation

Following the grain procurement crisis of 1927–28, Stalin launched forced collectivisation in late 1929. Wealthier peasants labelled "kulaks" were expropriated, deported or executed; by 1937 about 93% of peasant households had been collectivised.

Famine

Collectivisation contributed to the catastrophic Soviet famine of 1932–1933, which killed millions, including roughly 3.3–3.9 million in Ukraine (the Holodomor) and about 1.5 million in Kazakhstan.
