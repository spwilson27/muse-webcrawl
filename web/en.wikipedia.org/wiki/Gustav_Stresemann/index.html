Gustav Stresemann

Gustav Stresemann
Born: 10 May 1878, Berlin
Died: 3 October 1929, Berlin
Chancellor of Germany: Aug–Nov 1923
Foreign Minister: 1923–1929
Party: German People's Party (DVP)
Prize: Nobel Peace Prize 1926 (shared)

Gustav Ernst Stresemann (1878–1929) was a German statesman who served as chancellor in 1923 and as foreign minister of the Weimar Republic from 1923 until his death. His pursuit of reconciliation with France and fulfilment of reparations obligations helped restore Germany's international standing after the First World War.

Early life

Born in Berlin to a brewer and innkeeper, Stresemann studied political economy at Berlin and Leipzig, earning a doctorate. He worked for trade associations and entered the Reichstag in 1907 for the National Liberal Party.

World War I and founding of the DVP

During the war he supported annexationist aims, but after defeat he founded the German People's Party and accepted the republic as a framework for gradual revision of the Treaty of Versailles.

Chancellorship and foreign ministry

As chancellor during the Ruhr crisis and hyperinflation in 1923, he ended passive resistance, stabilised the currency with the Rentenmark, and then as foreign minister negotiated the Dawes Plan (1924), the Locarno Treaties (1925), Germany's entry into the League of Nations (1926), and the Kellogg–Briand Pact (1928). With French foreign minister Aristide Briand he shared the Nobel Peace Prize in 1926.

Death and legacy

Stresemann died of a stroke in 1929, weeks before the Wall Street Crash. His death removed a key moderate figure, and German politics polarised sharply thereafter.
