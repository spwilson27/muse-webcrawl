Georges Perec

Georges Perec (7 March 1936 – 3 March 1982) was a French novelist, filmmaker, documentalist, and essayist. He was a member of the Oulipo group and one of the most significant French writers of the post-war period, celebrated for works built on formal constraints.

Born7 March 1936, Paris, France
Died3 March 1982, Ivry-sur-Seine, France
OccupationNovelist, essayist, filmmaker
MovementOulipo

Life

Perec was born in Paris to Polish Jewish immigrant parents; his father was killed in the Second World War and his mother died in the Holocaust. He was raised by relatives and studied history and sociology before working as an archivist in a neurophysiology laboratory.

Works

His first novel, Things: A Story of the Sixties (1965), won the Prix Renaudot. A Void (1969) is a lipogram written entirely without the letter "e". His masterpiece, Life: A User's Manual (1978), describes a Paris apartment block through an intricate structure based on the knight's tour in chess, and won the Prix Médicis.
