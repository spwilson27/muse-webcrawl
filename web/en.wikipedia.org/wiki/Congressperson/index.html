Congressperson

[Image placeholder: United States Capitol]

A congressperson is a member of a congress, most commonly a member of the United States Congress — either a senator or, more usually in everyday usage, a member of the United States House of Representatives. The gender-neutral term competes with "congressman" and "congresswoman"; members of the House are also formally addressed as "Representative".

Usage

In the United States, "congressman" was long used generically for House members of either sex, but as more women entered the House, "congresswoman" and the neutral "congressperson" became common. Senators are almost never called congresspersons in precise usage, since the Senate is a distinct chamber, although "member of Congress" technically includes them. The term is also used for members of national legislatures called congresses in other countries, such as the Congress of the Republic of Peru or the Congress of Deputies of Spain, and for participants in a party congress or a scientific congress in some contexts.

Duties

Members of Congress in the US system introduce and vote on legislation, serve on committees, conduct oversight of the executive branch, approve budgets and, in the House, originate revenue bills and bring articles of impeachment. A representative serves a two-year term for a specific congressional district, while senators serve six-year terms representing an entire state.
