Scream queen

A scream queen (a wordplay on screen queen) is an actress who is prominent and influential in horror films, either through a notable appearance or recurring roles. The term is also used for actresses who have become strongly associated with the genre through repeated appearances as victims, heroines or "final girls".

History

The archetype has roots in early cinema: Fay Wray's screaming performance in King Kong (1933) is often cited as an early example. Janet Leigh's shower scene in Psycho (1960) became iconic, and her daughter Jamie Lee Curtis became perhaps the most famous scream queen through the Halloween franchise beginning in 1978. Other prominent examples include Neve Campbell in the Scream series, Sigourney Weaver in the Alien films, Dee Wallace, Linnea Quigley, Barbara Crampton and Danielle Harris.

Characteristics

While early scream queens largely played damsels in distress, the archetype evolved to include resourceful protagonists who confront and sometimes defeat the antagonist. Critics have debated the term: some actresses embrace it as a badge of honour within the genre, while others have found it reductive.
