Indirect tax

An indirect tax (such as sales tax, value-added tax (VAT), excise duty or tariffs) is a tax imposed on goods and services rather than directly on income or profits. It is collected by an intermediary — for example a retailer — from the person who bears the economic burden of the tax, usually the consumer, and remitted to the government.

Contrast with direct taxes

Unlike a direct tax, where the taxpayer and the person bearing the burden are the same, with an indirect tax the legal obligation to pay falls on the seller while the economic cost is typically passed on to buyers through higher prices. Economists analyse this distinction through the concept of tax incidence.

Types

  • Sales tax — levied once at the point of retail sale.
  • Value-added tax — levied at each stage of production and distribution, with credits for tax already paid.
  • Excise tax — levied on specific goods such as fuel, alcohol and tobacco.
  • Customs duties / tariffs — levied on imported goods.

Criticism

Indirect taxes are often described as regressive because lower-income households spend a larger share of their income on taxed consumption. Supporters counter that they are broad-based, harder to evade, and less distorting of work incentives than income taxes.
