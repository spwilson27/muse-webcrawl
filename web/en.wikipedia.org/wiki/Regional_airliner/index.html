Regional airliner

Regional airliner
Typical capacityFewer than 100 passengers
Typical rangeShort- to medium-haul routes
ExamplesBombardier CRJ, Embraer E-Jet, ATR 72

A regional airliner or a feederliner is a small airliner that is designed to fly up to 100 passengers on short-haul flights, usually feeding larger carriers' airline hubs from surrounding small communities. This class of airliners is typically flown by the regional airlines that are either contracted by or subsidiaries of the larger airlines.

History

Regional flying developed after airline deregulation in the United States in 1978, when major carriers adopted hub-and-spoke networks and contracted smaller operators to feed passengers from small cities. Early types included turboprops such as the de Havilland Canada Dash 8 and the ATR 42/72 family.

Regional jets

In the 1990s the 50-seat Bombardier CRJ and Embraer ERJ regional jets transformed the market, allowing jet service to small communities. Later designs such as the Embraer E-Jet family and the Airbus A220 stretched capacity towards and beyond 100 seats, blurring the boundary with small mainline narrow-body aircraft. Turboprops remain common where fuel efficiency on very short sectors matters most.
