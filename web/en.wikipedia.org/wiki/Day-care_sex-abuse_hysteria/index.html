Day-care sex-abuse hysteria

Day-care sex-abuse hysteria refers to a wave of prosecutions in the United States (and, to a lesser extent, other countries) during the 1980s and early 1990s in which childcare workers were accused of sexually abusing children in their care, often in the context of alleged satanic ritual abuse. The cases are now widely regarded as a moral panic: many convictions were overturned, and later investigation showed that the children's testimony had often been shaped by highly suggestive interviewing techniques.

Major cases

The most famous case was the McMartin preschool trial in California (1983–1990), at the time the longest and most expensive criminal trial in American history; it ended with no convictions. Other prominent cases included the Fells Acres (Amirault) case in Massachusetts, the Kern County cases in California, the Little Rascals case in North Carolina, the Kelly Michaels case in New Jersey, and the Wenatchee investigations in Washington State.

Interviewing methods and aftermath

Investigators and therapists frequently used repeated, leading interviews, anatomical dolls, peer pressure and rewards to elicit disclosures from young children. Subsequent research on child suggestibility showed that such methods can generate detailed but false accounts. Many defendants spent years in prison before appeals courts reversed their convictions; some, such as Gerald Amirault, were eventually released after long legal battles, while a few convictions were never fully vacated. The episode spurred reforms in forensic interviewing of children, including structured, non-leading protocols.
