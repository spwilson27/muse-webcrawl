Digital Video Broadcasting

Digital Video Broadcasting (DVB) is a set of international open standards for digital television maintained by the DVB Project, an international industry consortium, and published by a joint technical committee of ETSI, CENELEC and the European Broadcasting Union. The DVB system transmits digital audio, digital video, and other data in an MPEG transport stream, using various means of transmission.

Standards family

  • DVB-S and DVB-S2 — satellite broadcasting
  • DVB-C and DVB-C2 — cable television
  • DVB-T and DVB-T2 — terrestrial broadcasting
  • DVB-H — handheld/mobile reception (later superseded)
  • DVB-IPTV — delivery over IP networks

Background

The DVB Project was founded in 1993, growing out of the European Launching Group for digital television. The first DVB standard, DVB-S for satellite, was agreed in 1993–94 and adopted by ETSI in 1994; services began in 1995. The standards share common elements — MPEG-2 (and later H.264/HEVC) compression, the MPEG transport stream, common scrambling and conditional access interfaces, and DVB service information — while differing in modulation schemes suited to each medium: QPSK/8PSK for satellite, QAM for cable, and COFDM for terrestrial.

Adoption

DVB standards have been adopted for digital television across Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia and much of Latin America. DVB-T2, the second-generation terrestrial standard, offers substantially increased capacity and robustness and is used for HD and UHD services in many countries. The main competing families are the ATSC standards of North America, ISDB of Japan and parts of Latin America, and DTMB of China.
