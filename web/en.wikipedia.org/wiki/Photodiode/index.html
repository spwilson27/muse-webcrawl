Photodiode

A photodiode is a semiconductor diode sensitive to light. When photons are absorbed in its depletion region they generate electron–hole pairs, producing a photocurrent proportional to the incident light intensity.

Operation

Photodiodes are usually operated in reverse bias (photoconductive mode) for speed, or at zero bias (photovoltaic mode) for low noise. Key parameters include responsivity in amperes per watt, quantum efficiency, dark current, noise-equivalent power and response time. Avalanche photodiodes exploit impact ionisation to provide internal gain.

Materials

Silicon photodiodes cover the visible and near-infrared; germanium and indium gallium arsenide extend sensitivity further into the infrared, while wide-bandgap materials such as silicon carbide respond to ultraviolet light.

Applications

Photodiodes are used in optical communications receivers, light meters, barcode scanners, medical sensors such as pulse oximeters, remote-control receivers, smoke detectors and scientific instruments.
