Corcomroe Abbey

Corcomroe Abbey
Location: Bellharbour, County Clare, Ireland
Order: Cistercian
Founded: c. 1194–1195
Disestablished: 1554

Corcomroe Abbey (Irish: Mainistir Chorco Modhruadh) is a Cistercian monastery of the late 12th/early 13th century in the north of the Burren region of County Clare, Ireland, near the village of Bellharbour. The abbey is noted for its fine Romanesque and early Gothic stone carving, including floral capitals carved with plants native to the Burren.

History

The abbey was founded around 1194–1195, traditionally by Donal Mór Ua Briain, king of Thomond, or by his son Donough Cairbreac Ó Briain, and was colonised by monks from Inislounaght Abbey. Its Cistercian dedication was to St Mary; the name means "abbey of the fertile rock district" or similar. Several of the Ó Briain kings of Thomond were buried there; the east wall of the chancel contains an effigy traditionally identified as Conor na Siudane Ua Briain, king of Thomond, who died in 1267/8.

The abbey was dissolved in 1554 during the Tudor dissolution, and the buildings fell into ruin, though the church continued to be used as a burial place. Today the roofless church — nave, chancel with ribbed vault, and carved chapter house remains nearby — is a National Monument in state care.
