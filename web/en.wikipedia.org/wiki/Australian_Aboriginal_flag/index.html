Australian Aboriginal flag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Australian Aboriginal flag is an official flag of Australia that represents Aboriginal Australians. It is one of the official national flags of Australia, and holds special legal and political status. It is often flown together with the national flag and with the Torres Strait Islander flag, which is also an official flag of Australia representing the Torres Strait Islander peoples.

It was designed in 1971 by Harold Thomas, an Aboriginal artist and activist of the Lardil people of the Northern Territory. It was first flown on 12 July 1971 at a land rights rally in Victoria Square in Adelaide, and was adopted by the Aboriginal Tent Embassy in Canberra the following year.

Symbolism

The black of the top half represents Aboriginal people; the red of the lower half represents the earth and the spiritual relationship of Aboriginal people to the land, as well as the blood that has been shed; and the yellow circle in the centre represents the Sun, the giver of life and protector.

Status and copyright

On 14 July 1995, the Governor-General proclaimed the flag an official "Flag of Australia" under the Flags Act 1953. Thomas held copyright in the design, which for many years restricted commercial use and caused public debate about access. In January 2022 the Australian Government purchased the copyright for $20.05 million, making the flag freely available for public use, while Thomas retained his moral rights as its creator.
