Cyclotomic field

The primitive roots of unity generate cyclotomic fields.

In number theory, a cyclotomic field is a number field obtained from the rational numbers by adjoining a complex primitive root of unity. For a positive integer n, the n-th cyclotomic field is obtained by adjoining a primitive n-th root of unity, and its degree over the rationals is given by Euler's totient function φ(n).

Galois theory

Cyclotomic fields are Galois extensions of the rationals with abelian Galois groups, canonically isomorphic to the multiplicative group of integers modulo n. By the Kronecker–Weber theorem, every finite abelian extension of the rationals is contained in some cyclotomic field. The minimal polynomial of a primitive n-th root of unity is the n-th cyclotomic polynomial.

Role in number theory

Cyclotomic fields were central to nineteenth-century attempts on Fermat's Last Theorem: Kummer's work on factorisation in cyclotomic integers introduced ideal numbers and the notion of regular primes. Cyclotomic theory also underlies results on Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, class number formulas, and Iwasawa theory, which studies towers of cyclotomic fields.
