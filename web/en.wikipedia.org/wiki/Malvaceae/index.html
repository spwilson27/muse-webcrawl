Common nameMallow family

Malvaceae, the mallow family, is a large family of flowering plants in the order Malvales, containing an estimated 244 genera and more than 4,200 species. Under the APG IV classification the family is broadly defined, absorbing the formerly separate families Bombacaceae, Sterculiaceae and Tiliaceae as subfamilies.

Characteristics

Members commonly have alternate, often palmately lobed leaves with stellate (star-shaped) hairs, mucilage-containing tissues, and flowers with numerous stamens fused into a tube around the style — a hallmark of the core mallows. Many have showy, hibiscus-like flowers with an epicalyx of bracts below the calyx.

Economic importance

The family includes some of the world's most valuable plants: cotton (Gossypium), the most important natural fibre crop; cacao (Theobroma cacao), source of chocolate; okra (Abelmoschus esculentus); durian; baobabs (Adansonia); balsa wood; kenaf and jute relatives; and popular ornamentals such as hibiscus, hollyhock, and linden/lime trees. Marsh mallow (Althaea officinalis) gave the family its English name and originally flavoured the confection of the same name.

Subfamilies

APG IV recognises nine subfamilies, including Malvoideae (mallows and cotton), Bombacoideae (baobabs, kapok, durian relatives), Byttnerioideae (cacao), Grewioideae, and Tilioideae (lindens).
