Hans Renold (31 July 1852 – 2 May 1943) was a Swiss-born British engineer and industrialist, founder of the Renold chain company in Manchester. He is credited with inventing the bush roller chain in 1880, a design that remains the standard form of chain drive used in bicycles, motorcycles, conveyors and industrial machinery worldwide.

Career

Renold came to England in 1873 and worked for a Manchester machinery firm before, in 1879, purchasing a small textile-chain business in Salford with borrowed money. In 1880 he patented the bush roller chain: by fitting bushes between the inner plates and rollers over them, the chain engaged sprocket teeth with rolling rather than sliding contact, greatly reducing wear and noise. The invention rapidly displaced earlier block and pin chains in the booming bicycle industry and then in general engineering.

The firm and labour relations

The business, Hans Renold Ltd, moved to a purpose-built factory at Burnage in Manchester in 1906. Renold and his son Charles Garonne Renold were noted as enlightened employers, introducing a 48-hour week in 1896 and developing systems of works councils, costing and production management that influenced British management thought. The company later merged with Coventry Chain to form Renold and Coventry Chain Co., and in 1967 became Renold Ltd.
