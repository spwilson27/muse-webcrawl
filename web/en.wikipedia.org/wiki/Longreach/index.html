Longreach is a town in Central West Queensland, Australia, situated on the Thomson River about 700 kilometres west of Rockhampton. It is the administrative centre of the Longreach Region and a major service town for the surrounding sheep and cattle grazing country. The town takes its name from the "long reach" of the Thomson River remarked upon by explorer William Landsborough in 1862.

History

The town was gazetted in 1888 and the railway from Rockhampton arrived in 1892, cementing Longreach as a regional hub. It is famous as one of the founding centres of Qantas: the airline was registered in Winton in 1920 but established its first operational base and hangar in Longreach in 1921, and early Qantas aircraft were assembled there. Longreach was also a significant site in the 1891 Australian shearers' strike, a formative episode in the Australian labour movement.

Attractions

The town's best-known attractions are the Qantas Founders Museum, which preserves the original 1922 hangar and displays aircraft including a Boeing 747, and the Australian Stockman's Hall of Fame, opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1988, which celebrates the pioneers of the outback. River cruises on the Thomson River and the nearby town of Ilfracombe also draw visitors.
