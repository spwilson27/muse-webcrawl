Béchamel sauce

Béchamel sauce (French: sauce béchamel) is a sauce traditionally made from a white roux (butter and flour) and milk. It is one of the five mother sauces of French cuisine as classified by Auguste Escoffier, and serves as the base for many other sauces, including Mornay sauce (with cheese) and sauce crème. In Italian cuisine it is known as besciamella and is essential in lasagne and cannelloni.

History

The sauce is named after Louis de Béchameil, marquis de Nointel (1630–1703), a financier and steward of Louis XIV, although similar milk-and-roux sauces existed earlier in Italian cooking — a version appears in the Italian tradition associated with Catherine de' Medici's cooks, and François Pierre de La Varenne published an early recipe in Le Cuisinier François (1651).

Preparation

Equal parts butter and flour are cooked briefly without browning to form a white roux; milk is then whisked in gradually and the sauce is simmered until it thickens and loses its raw flour taste. It is typically seasoned with salt, white pepper, and nutmeg. Consistency varies by use — thin, medium, or thick — according to the proportion of roux to milk.

Uses

Beyond lasagne, béchamel is used in gratins, croque-monsieur, moussaka, pastitsio, and as a binder for croquettes and soufflés.
