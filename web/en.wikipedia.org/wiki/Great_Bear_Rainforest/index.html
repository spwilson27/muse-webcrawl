Great Bear Rainforest

LocationCentral and North Coast, British Columbia, Canada
AreaAbout 6.4 million hectares (64,000 km²)
Notable wildlifeKermode (spirit) bear, wolves, salmon

The Great Bear Rainforest is a temperate rainforest on the Pacific coast of British Columbia, Canada, comprising 6.4 million hectares. It is part of the larger Pacific temperate rainforest ecoregion, which is the largest coastal temperate rainforest in the world.

The Great Bear Rainforest was officially recognized by the Government of British Columbia in February 2016, when an agreement was reached between the province, First Nations, environmentalists, and the forest industry under which some 85% of the forest would be permanently off-limits to industrial logging, with the remainder subject to strict ecosystem-based management.

Ecology

The forest consists of old-growth Sitka spruce, western red cedar, and hemlock, and its intact estuaries and salmon streams support grizzly bears, black bears, and the white-coated Kermode bear, a subspecies of black bear known as the "spirit bear". Coastal wolves, which feed extensively on salmon, are another distinctive population.

Conservation history

The name "Great Bear Rainforest" was coined by environmental groups during the 1990s campaigns against clearcut logging on the BC coast (the "War in the Woods"). The 2016 Great Bear Rainforest (Forest Management) Act put the land-use agreements into law.
