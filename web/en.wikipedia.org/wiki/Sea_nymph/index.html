Sea nymph

Sea nymph is a general term for the female water spirits of mythology, most familiar from Greek tradition as the Nereids, the fifty daughters of the sea god Nereus and the Oceanid Doris, and the Oceanids, daughters of Oceanus. Dwelling in the depths of the sea, sea nymphs were generally regarded as friendly to sailors, helping them weather storms and guiding ships to safety.

In Greek myth

The most famous Nereids include Thetis, mother of Achilles; Amphitrite, wife of Poseidon; and Galatea, beloved by the Cyclops Polyphemus. Sea nymphs attend Poseidon's court, ride dolphins and hippocamps, and appear throughout epic, lyric poetry and vase painting. Related figures in other traditions include the Norse hafgufa lore, Slavic rusalki (more dangerous beings) and the sirens, whose songs lured sailors to destruction.

In art and culture

Sea nymphs were a favourite subject of Renaissance and 19th-century painting and sculpture, of fountain design, and of ballet and opera, and the term survives in zoology in the names of various marine animals.
