Emergency landing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An emergency landing is a landing made by an aircraft in response to an emergency involving an imminent or ongoing threat to the safety and operation of the aircraft, or involving a sudden need for a passenger or crew member on board to receive medical assistance on the ground. It usually involves a forced diversion to the nearest or most suitable airport or airbase, or an off-airport landing or ditching if an airfield is unreachable.

Types

Emergency landings are commonly classified as:

  • Forced landing — the aircraft is compelled to land because of technical failure or damage, with little or no power available.
  • Precautionary landing — a planned landing at a site about which information is limited, prompted by concerns such as deteriorating weather, low fuel or developing mechanical problems while the aircraft is still fully controllable.
  • Ditching — a forced landing on water.

Causes

Common causes include engine failure, fire or smoke, loss of cabin pressure, hydraulic or flight-control failures, fuel exhaustion or contamination, severe weather, and medical emergencies among passengers or crew. Pilots declare an emergency using the Mayday call or by squawking transponder code 7700, which gives the aircraft priority handling from air traffic control.

Procedure

Flight crews follow memory items and checklists to manage the failure, communicate with air traffic control, prepare the cabin, and brief passengers. In airliners, pilots will usually dump or burn off fuel if the aircraft is above its maximum landing weight, and emergency services are placed on standby at the arrival airport.

Notable examples

Famous successful emergency landings include US Airways Flight 1549, which ditched in the Hudson River in 2009 with no fatalities after a bird strike disabled both engines, and Air Canada Flight 143, the "Gimli Glider", which glided to a dead-stick landing in 1983 after running out of fuel.
