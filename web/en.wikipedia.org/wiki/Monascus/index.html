Monascus is a genus of moulds in the family Aspergillaceae. The best-known species, Monascus purpureus, is used to produce red yeast rice, a traditional East Asian foodstuff and colouring agent made by fermenting rice with the fungus. Species of Monascus produce a range of pigments — yellow, orange and red — as well as bioactive compounds including monacolins.

Species and products

The genus includes species such as M. purpureus, M. ruber, M. pilosus and M. kaoliang. During solid-state fermentation on rice or other grains, Monascus species synthesise azaphilone pigments — including monascorubrin, rubropunctatin, monascin and ankaflavin — which colour the substrate deep red. They also produce monacolin K, chemically identical to the cholesterol-lowering drug lovastatin, which underlies the use of red yeast rice in dietary supplements.

Safety

Some strains can produce citrinin, a mycotoxin potentially harmful to the kidneys, and regulators in several jurisdictions limit citrinin levels in Monascus-fermented products. Commercial red yeast rice supplements have also been the subject of regulatory debate because of their variable monacolin content and drug-like effects.

Uses

Beyond red yeast rice, Monascus ferments are used to colour and preserve foods such as red fermented bean curd, rice wine and certain processed meats in China, Japan (where it is known as kōji for beni-kōji) and Southeast Asia, with a documented history stretching back more than a thousand years.
