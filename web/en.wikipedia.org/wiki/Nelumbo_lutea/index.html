Nelumbo lutea is a species of flowering plant in the family Nelumbonaceae, commonly known as the American lotus or yellow lotus. It is one of only two species in the genus Nelumbo, the other being the sacred lotus (Nelumbo nucifera) of Asia. It is native to North America, from the Great Lakes and eastern United States south through the Caribbean to Mexico and northern South America.

Description

The American lotus is an aquatic perennial growing in shallow lakes, ponds, and slow rivers. Its round, umbrella-like leaves, up to 60 cm across, are held above the water on long stalks and repel water strongly thanks to a waxy, micro-textured surface (the "lotus effect"). The large, fragrant, pale-yellow flowers, 18–28 cm wide, bloom in summer and are followed by distinctive showerhead-like seed pods. The seeds and rhizomes (tubers) remain viable and edible for long periods; seeds of the genus have germinated after centuries.

Uses

Native American peoples used the plant extensively as food: the seeds, rhizomes, and young leaves were eaten. It is cultivated as an ornamental for water gardens. In parts of its range it is locally abundant but is listed as threatened or endangered in some northern states at the edge of its distribution.
