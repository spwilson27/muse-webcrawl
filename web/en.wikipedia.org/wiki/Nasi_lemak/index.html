Nasi lemak

Course: Main course
Region: Malaysia
Main ingredients: Rice, coconut milk, pandan, sambal, anchovies, peanuts, egg, cucumber

Nasi lemak is a dish of Malay origin consisting of fragrant rice cooked in coconut milk and pandan leaf. It is widely considered the national dish of Malaysia and is also popular in Singapore, Brunei, southern Thailand and parts of Indonesia.

Components

The rice is traditionally served with a spicy sambal, crispy fried anchovies (ikan bilis), roasted peanuts, sliced cucumber and a boiled or fried egg. Common additions include fried chicken, beef or cuttlefish sambal, and rendang. It is often wrapped in banana leaf, which imparts additional aroma.

History and culture

The dish originated in Malay coastal communities, where coconut palms and seafood were plentiful. Once a breakfast food for farmers and fishermen, nasi lemak is now eaten at any time of day, sold everywhere from roadside stalls to high-end restaurants, and celebrated as a symbol of Malaysian food culture.
