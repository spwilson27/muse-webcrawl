Wendell Berry (born 1934) is an American novelist, poet, essayist, environmental activist, cultural critic and farmer. His writing explores the relationship between people and place, the virtues of local communities and sustainable agriculture, and the damage done by industrial economics to rural life.

Life and work

Born in Henry County, Kentucky, Berry studied at the University of Kentucky and Stanford University, taught in New York and Kentucky, and settled on a farm at Lane's Landing near Port Royal, Kentucky, where he has farmed and written for decades. His fiction is centred on the fictional Kentucky community of Port William, chronicled in novels such as Jayber Crow and Hannah Coulter and in many short stories.

Essays and activism

His landmark essay collection The Unsettling of America (1977) criticised industrial agriculture and argued for agrarian values of stewardship, limits and affection for place. Berry has also been an outspoken opponent of mountaintop-removal coal mining and a participant in civil disobedience on environmental questions.
