Markarian galaxies are a class of galaxies that have nuclei with excessive amounts of ultraviolet emissions compared with other galaxies. They are named after the Armenian astrophysicist Benjamin Markarian, who first drew attention to this type of galaxy in 1963 and catalogued them in a series of lists published from the mid-1960s onward.

The Markarian galaxies were discovered during the First Byurakan Survey (FBS), conducted with the 1-metre Schmidt telescope of the Byurakan Astrophysical Observatory in Armenia using an objective prism. The survey, carried out mainly between 1965 and 1980, identified galaxies with an ultraviolet continuum excess — a signature of intense star formation, active galactic nuclei, or both.

Characteristics

Markarian galaxies include many types: Seyfert galaxies, starburst galaxies, LINERs, and BL Lacertae objects. Well-known examples include Markarian 421 and Markarian 501, both BL Lac objects whose relativistic jets point toward Earth and which are prominent sources of very-high-energy gamma rays, and Markarian 231, one of the most luminous infrared galaxies known and a quasar in a merging system.

The catalogue grew to about 1,500 entries, designated Mrk 1 through Mrk 1541 or so. Follow-up observations in the radio, infrared, X-ray and gamma-ray bands have made the Markarian sample an enduring resource for studies of active galactic nuclei and galaxy evolution.
