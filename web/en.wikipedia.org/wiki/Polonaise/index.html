Polonaise

The polonaise is a stately dance of Polish origin, danced in 3/4 time at a moderate, processional tempo, and also the music written for or in the character of that dance. Its characteristic rhythm — an eighth note, two sixteenths, then four eighth notes — appears from the first bar.

History

Derived from Polish folk dances such as the chodzony ("walking dance"), the polonaise (Polish: polonez) became the ceremonial opening dance at courts across Europe from the seventeenth century onward. It was danced at coronations, weddings and balls, with couples processing in pairs led by the highest-ranking guests.

Art music

Composers from the Baroque onward wrote polonaises as keyboard and orchestral pieces; examples appear in the works of J. S. Bach, Telemann, Mozart, Beethoven, Weber and Schubert. The form reached its height with Frédéric Chopin, whose sixteen polonaises — including the "Military" in A major, Op. 40 No. 1, and the "Heroic" in A-flat major, Op. 53 — transformed the courtly dance into virtuosic expressions of Polish national pride.
