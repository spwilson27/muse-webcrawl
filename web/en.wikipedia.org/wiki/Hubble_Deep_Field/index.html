Hubble Deep Field

The Hubble Deep Field (HDF) is an image of a small region in the constellation Ursa Major, constructed from a series of observations by the Hubble Space Telescope. It covers an area about 2.6 arcminutes on a side — roughly one 24-millionth of the whole sky — yet contains an estimated 3,000 galaxies.

Observations

The field was chosen because it was free of bright foreground stars and other obstructions, giving a clear "keyhole" view of the distant universe. Over ten consecutive days in December 1995, Hubble's Wide Field and Planetary Camera 2 took 342 exposures in four wavelength bands totalling about 100 hours of observing time. The project was led by Robert Williams, director of the Space Telescope Science Institute, who devoted much of his discretionary time allocation to it.

Scientific significance

The image revealed galaxies in many stages of evolution, some seen as they were when the universe was less than a billion years old, and transformed the study of galaxy formation. It inspired follow-ups including the Hubble Deep Field South (1998), the Hubble Ultra-Deep Field (2004), and the eXtreme Deep Field (2012).
