Clear aligners

Clear aligners are orthodontic devices that are transparent, plastic forms of dental braces used to adjust teeth. They consist of a series of custom-made, removable trays that gradually move teeth into a desired position, and are marketed as a more cosmetically discreet alternative to fixed braces.

History

The concept traces to the tooth positioner of H. D. Kesling in the 1940s. The modern clear aligner industry began with Invisalign, introduced by Align Technology in the late 1990s and approved by the FDA in 1998, which used computer-aided design and 3D printing (stereolithography) to fabricate a sequence of aligners from a digital treatment plan.

Treatment

Patients wear each set of aligners for roughly one to two weeks, for at least 20–22 hours per day, removing them for eating and cleaning. Attachments — small tooth-colored composite bumps bonded to teeth — are often used to achieve more complex movements. Clear aligners are effective for mild to moderate crowding and spacing, though fixed appliances may be preferred for severe malocclusions.
