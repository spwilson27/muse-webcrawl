Peter Simon Pallas

Peter Simon Pallas FRS (22 September 1741 – 8 September 1811) was a German zoologist, botanist, ethnographer, explorer, and geographer who worked in Russia. He led celebrated expeditions across the Russian Empire under Catherine the Great.

Career

Born in Berlin, Pallas studied medicine and natural history and was elected to learned societies while still young. In 1767 he accepted Catherine II's invitation to become a professor at the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences. From 1768 to 1774 he led an expedition through Siberia and Central Asia, and later travelled in the Crimea and Caucasus, describing hundreds of species and collecting geological, botanical, and ethnographic observations published in his Travels through the southern provinces of the Russian Empire and works such as Zoographia Rosso-Asiatica.

Legacy

Many species bear his name, including Pallas's cat, Pallas's warbler, and Pallas's gull. The first pallasite meteorite, the Krasnojarsk mass he reported, is sometimes called Pallas iron.
