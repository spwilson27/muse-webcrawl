Polish–Russian War of 1792
Date: May – July 1792
Location: Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
Result: Russian victory; Second Partition of Poland

The Polish–Russian War of 1792 (also known as the War in Defence of the Constitution) was fought between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Russian Empire, aided by the Targowica Confederation of Polish magnates opposed to the reforms of the Constitution of 3 May 1791.

Course of the war

Russian forces invaded in May 1792 on two fronts, in Lithuania and Ukraine. Polish forces under Józef Poniatowski and Tadeusz Kościuszko won a notable victory at the Battle of Zieleńce on 18 June 1792, but were steadily pushed back by superior numbers. King Stanisław August Poniatowski, judging resistance hopeless, joined the Targowica Confederation and ordered a ceasefire in July.

Aftermath

The war ended the reform era of the Four-Year Sejm. In 1793 Russia and Prussia carried out the Second Partition of Poland, stripping the Commonwealth of roughly half its remaining territory.
