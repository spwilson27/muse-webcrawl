Chemical vapor deposition

A chemical vapour deposition reactor

Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) is a vacuum deposition method used to produce high-quality solid materials, usually as thin films. In a typical CVD process the substrate is exposed to one or more volatile precursor gases, which react and/or decompose on the heated substrate surface to produce the desired deposit, with volatile by-products removed by the gas flow. CVD is one of the workhorse technologies of semiconductor manufacturing, optics and protective coatings.

Process

The sequence of events in CVD includes transport of precursors to the chamber, adsorption onto the substrate, surface reactions (often catalysed by the surface itself), nucleation and film growth, and desorption and removal of by-products. Growth may be limited by mass transport or by surface reaction kinetics, which determines how uniformly the film coats complex shapes; CVD films are prized for being highly conformal, coating steps and trenches evenly — a key advantage over line-of-sight physical vapour deposition (PVD).

Variants

  • APCVD / LPCVD — atmospheric-pressure and low-pressure CVD; LPCVD gives excellent uniformity for batch wafer processing.
  • PECVD — plasma-enhanced CVD, which uses a plasma to drive reactions at lower temperatures, used for silicon nitride and oxide films.
  • MOCVD — metal-organic CVD, used to grow compound semiconductors such as GaAs and GaN for LEDs and lasers.
  • ALD — atomic layer deposition, a sequential, self-limiting variant giving atomic-scale thickness control.

Applications

CVD deposits polysilicon, silicon dioxide and nitride dielectrics, tungsten and copper interconnect materials, and barrier layers in integrated circuits. Beyond microelectronics it produces hard coatings on cutting tools, optical coatings, carbon nanotubes, large-area graphene grown on copper foil, and synthetic diamond films. Historically, the Siemens process for purifying silicon and the Mond process for nickel refining are related vapour-transport methods that predate modern CVD.
