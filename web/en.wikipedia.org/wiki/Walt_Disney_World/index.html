Walt Disney World

The Walt Disney World Resort, commonly called Walt Disney World or Disney World, is an entertainment resort complex in Bay Lake and Lake Buena Vista, Florida, near Orlando. Opened on October 1, 1971, it is operated by Disney Experiences, a division of the Walt Disney Company, and is the most visited vacation resort in the world.

Parks and attractions

The property covers roughly 25,000 acres (about 39 square miles), of which only part has been developed. It contains four theme parks — Magic Kingdom (1971), Epcot (1982), Disney's Hollywood Studios (1989) and Disney's Animal Kingdom (1998) — plus two water parks (Typhoon Lagoon and Blizzard Beach), the Disney Springs shopping and dining district, golf courses, campgrounds and more than two dozen resort hotels. Transportation links include the Walt Disney World Monorail System, buses, boats and the Disney Skyliner gondola system.

History

Walt Disney secretly purchased the Florida land in the 1960s through shell companies, planning a larger, more controlled successor to California's Disneyland together with an Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow (EPCOT). After Walt's death in 1966, his brother Roy O. Disney oversaw construction and insisted the resort be named Walt Disney World in his honour. The Reedy Creek Improvement District, created by Florida in 1967, gave Disney unusual self-governing powers over the land; the district was reorganised as the Central Florida Tourism Oversight District in 2023.
