Dendrite

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. This article is about the component of a neuron. For other uses, see Dendrite (disambiguation).

A dendrite (from Greek δένδρον déndron, "tree") is a branched protoplasmic extension of a nerve cell (neuron) that propagates the electrochemical stimulation received from other neural cells to the cell body, or soma, of the neuron from which the dendrites project. Dendrites are the main receptive surface of most neurons: they collect signals at thousands of synapses and integrate them before the neuron decides whether to fire an action potential along its axon.

Structure

Dendrites typically taper and branch extensively near the soma, forming a "dendritic tree" whose geometry varies characteristically between neuron types — from the elaborate arbors of cerebellar Purkinje cells to the tufted dendrites of cortical pyramidal neurons. Many dendrites are covered with small protrusions called dendritic spines, which receive most excitatory synaptic input and can change shape and size with activity, a property thought to underlie learning and memory. Unlike axons, dendrites generally lack myelin and contain ribosomes and other organelles near their bases, allowing local protein synthesis.

Electrical properties

Dendrites are not passive cables. Their membranes contain voltage-gated ion channels that can amplify or dampen synaptic signals, support back-propagating action potentials, and even generate local spikes. Synaptic inputs are summed — spatially and temporally — as excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials travel toward the soma. The branching geometry of the tree means that the location of a synapse strongly affects its influence, giving individual neurons rich computational capabilities.

Development and plasticity

Dendritic trees grow during development through branching and elongation guided by molecular cues, and they continue to remodel in adulthood. Experience, learning, and disease can all alter dendritic structure: spine loss and dendritic shrinkage are features of several neurological and psychiatric disorders, while enriched environments promote dendritic growth.
