VRLA battery

A valve-regulated lead-acid (VRLA) battery, commonly known as a sealed lead-acid (SLA) battery, is a type of lead-acid battery characterised by a limited amount of electrolyte absorbed in a plate separator or formed into a gel, and a pressure relief valve that recombines the oxygen and hydrogen generated during operation, minimising water loss so the battery requires no topping up.

Types

The two main types are absorbent glass mat (AGM) batteries, in which the electrolyte is held in a fibreglass separator, and gel batteries, in which the electrolyte is immobilised in a silica gel. Both designs allow the battery to be mounted in any orientation and used in enclosed spaces.

Applications

VRLA batteries are used in uninterruptible power supplies, emergency lighting, alarm systems, mobility scooters, backup power for telecommunications, and small off-grid energy storage. They are heavier and have lower energy density than lithium-ion batteries but are cheaper and widely recycled.
