Ephebic oath

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ephebic oath was an oath sworn by young men of ancient Athens as they entered military service as ephebes, typically at about eighteen years of age, upon enrollment in their deme registers. The text is preserved in an inscription from the deme of Acharnae (found in the sanctuary of Ares and Athena Areia) and is quoted by the orator Lycurgus in his speech Against Leocrates.

Text

The ephebe swore, in part: "I shall not bring shame upon the sacred weapons, nor shall I abandon the man beside me, wherever I am stationed. I shall defend the sacred and the secular, and I shall not hand down the fatherland smaller, but greater and better, so far as is in my power..." The oath went on to promise obedience to the magistrates and the laws, and invoked as witnesses a list of gods including Ares, Athena, Zeus, Thallo, Auxo, and Hegemone, as well as "the boundaries of the fatherland, the wheat, the barley, the vines, the olives, and the figs".

Significance

The oath bound the new citizen-soldier to his city, its constitution, and his comrades in arms, and it has attracted modern interest as a model civic oath; it has occasionally been administered, in adapted form, at ceremonies in modern institutions.
