Comic opera

Comic opera is a sung stage work with a light or sentimental subject, typically with a happy ending, often including spoken dialogue. National forms include Italian opera buffa, French opéra comique, German Singspiel and the English ballad opera and Savoy opera.

Era18th–19th centuries especially
Noted creatorsMozart, Rossini, Gilbert & Sullivan

History

Emerging in early 18th-century Naples and Venice as comic intermezzi between acts of serious opera — Pergolesi's La serva padrona (1733) is the famous example — opera buffa developed full-length works by Mozart (The Marriage of Figaro, Così fan tutte), Rossini (The Barber of Seville) and Donizetti. In France, opéra comique used spoken dialogue whatever its tone; in Germany the Singspiel gave Mozart The Magic Flute.

Later forms

The Victorian Savoy operas of Gilbert and Sullivan — H.M.S. Pinafore, The Pirates of Penzance, The Mikado — satirised British institutions, while Offenbach's operettas did the same for Second Empire Paris, feeding into operetta and modern musical theatre.
