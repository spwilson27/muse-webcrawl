Deaccessioning

FieldMuseology, archival science
MeaningPermanent removal of an object from a collection

Deaccessioning is the process by which a museum, library or archive permanently removes an object from its collection, typically to sell it, exchange it, transfer it to another institution, or because it has deteriorated beyond use. It is the opposite of accessioning, the formal addition of an object to a collection.

Criteria and process

Institutions usually deaccession works that fall outside their mission, duplicate other holdings, cannot be properly stored or conserved, or are discovered to be inauthentic or illegally acquired. Professional standards generally require approval by the governing board and documentation of the decision.

Use of proceeds and controversy

Ethical guidelines from bodies such as the Association of Art Museum Directors traditionally require that proceeds from sales be used only to acquire new works. Sales to fund operating costs or building projects have repeatedly provoked controversy, as with proposed sales by several American museums during the COVID-19 pandemic, when rules were temporarily relaxed.
