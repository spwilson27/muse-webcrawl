Central Bank of Argentina

The Central Bank of the Argentine Republic (Spanish: Banco Central de la República Argentina, BCRA) is the central bank of Argentina, established on May 28, 1935. It is charged with regulating the financial system and applying the exchange and monetary policy of the country, and it issues the Argentine peso.

History

The BCRA was created in 1935 following a monetary reform advised by a British mission led by Sir Otto Niemeyer, replacing the Currency Board (Caja de Conversión) that had operated since 1899. It took over note issue and reserves from the Banco de la Nación. Under its first president, Raúl Prebisch, the bank was a mixed public-private institution; it was nationalised in 1946 under Juan Perón, and thereafter monetary policy was frequently subordinated to government financing — a pattern critics blame for Argentina's chronic inflation.

Convertibility and after

The 1991 Convertibility Law pegged the peso one-to-one to the US dollar and restricted the bank's money creation, ending hyperinflation but eliminating its role as lender of last resort. After the 2001–02 crisis and devaluation, the BCRA rebuilt reserves. Its charter was amended in 2012 to broaden its mandate beyond price stability to include financial stability, employment and development. A long-running dispute over the removal of president Martín Redrado in 2010, and later debates over reserve transfers to the Treasury, repeatedly raised questions about the bank's independence.

Functions

The BCRA supervises banks and financial entities, manages international reserves, operates the payments system, sets reserve requirements and policy interest rates, and houses the Historic Museum of the Central Bank in its Buenos Aires headquarters.
