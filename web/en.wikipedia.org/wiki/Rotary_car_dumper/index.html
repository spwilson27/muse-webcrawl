Rotary car dumper

A rotary car dumper or wagon tippler is a mechanism used for unloading certain railroad cars, such as hopper cars, gondolas or lorries (tipplers, UK). It holds a car to a section of track and then rotates the track and car together to invert the car and pour out its contents. Used primarily for bulk solids such as coal, ore and grain, the rotary dumper unloads a car in well under a minute without uncoupling it from its train.

Operation

Cars intended for rotary dumping are fitted with rotating couplers, allowing a whole unit train to pass through the dumper one car at a time while remaining coupled. The dumper's cradle clamps the car, and a geared drive rotates the assembly through roughly 160 degrees, discharging the load into a pit below, from which conveyors carry the material to stockpiles, a power station or a ship loader. The car is then rotated back, unclamped and indexed forward.

Designs

Installations may handle a single car, or tandem dumpers two cars at once. Alternatives include bottom-discharge through hopper doors and C-frame or turnover dumpers for cars without rotary couplers. Dust suppression and thawing sheds (for frozen winter loads) are common auxiliaries in coal service.

Use

Rotary dumpers are standard equipment at coal-fired power stations, steelworks, mines and export terminals worldwide, where they make possible the rapid turnaround of unit trains of a hundred cars or more.
