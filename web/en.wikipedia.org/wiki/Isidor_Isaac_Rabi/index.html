Isidor Isaac Rabi

Born29 July 1898, Rymanów, Austria-Hungary
Died11 January 1988 (aged 89), New York City, US
Known forNuclear magnetic resonance; magnetic resonance method
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1944)

Isidor Isaac Rabi (29 July 1898 – 11 January 1988) was an American physicist who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1944 for his discovery of nuclear magnetic resonance, the technique that underlies magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and much of modern atomic and molecular spectroscopy.

Career

Born in Galicia and brought to New York as an infant, Rabi studied at Cornell and Columbia, then worked in Europe with Bohr, Pauli and Heisenberg. At Columbia University in the 1930s he developed the molecular-beam magnetic resonance method, applying an oscillating radiofrequency field to beams of atoms to measure the magnetic moments and spins of nuclei with unprecedented precision — work recognised by the Nobel Prize.

War work and public life

During the Second World War Rabi led work at the MIT Radiation Laboratory on radar and was a consultant to the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos, helping J. Robert Oppenheimer organise the laboratory. Afterwards he was central to the creation of Brookhaven National Laboratory and of CERN, served on the General Advisory Committee of the Atomic Energy Commission (which he chaired after Oppenheimer), and advised presidents on science. His Columbia department produced a string of Nobel laureates, and his resonance method opened the way to the atomic clock and the laser.
