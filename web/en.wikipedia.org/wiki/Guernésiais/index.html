Guernésiais
Native to: Guernsey, Channel Islands
Language family: Indo-European > Romance > Oïl > Norman
Status: Severely endangered

Guernésiais, also known as Guernsey French or Dgèrnésiais, is the variety of the Norman language spoken in Guernsey in the Channel Islands. It descends from the Old Norman brought by settlers whose language developed in relative isolation, and it is closely related to Jèrriais (spoken in Jersey) and to the Norman of mainland Normandy.

Status

Once the everyday language of most of the island's population, Guernésiais declined sharply during the 20th century under the influence of English, a process accelerated by the evacuation of Guernsey children to England during the German occupation (1940–1945). The 2001 census recorded only about 1,300 fluent speakers, around 2% of the population, most of them elderly. It is classified as severely endangered.

Revival efforts

Cultural organisations, festivals and school programmes promote the language, and it appears on some bilingual signage. Notable literature in Guernésiais includes the poetry of George Métivier, sometimes called Guernsey's national poet.
