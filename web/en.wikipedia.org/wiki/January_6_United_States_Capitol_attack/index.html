January 6 United States Capitol attack

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

January 6 Capitol attack
DateJanuary 6, 2021
LocationUnited States Capitol, Washington, D.C.
PerpetratorsSupporters of President Donald Trump, including members of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers
OutcomeCongress reconvened and certified the 2020 election results; 1,500+ people charged

On January 6, 2021, the United States Capitol Building in Washington, D.C., was attacked by a mob of supporters of then–President Donald Trump, two months after his defeat in the 2020 presidential election. The attack disrupted a joint session of Congress convened to count the Electoral College votes and formalize Joe Biden's victory.

That morning, Trump addressed a "Save America" rally at the Ellipse, repeating false claims of election fraud and urging supporters to march to the Capitol. Rioters breached police lines, scaled walls, and entered the building, occupying the Senate chamber and offices; lawmakers and Vice President Mike Pence were evacuated. The Capitol was cleared by evening, and Congress certified the election early the next morning.

Casualties and damage

Five people died during or shortly after the attack, including a rioter shot by police and a Capitol Police officer who died the following day. More than 140 police officers were injured. Damage and security costs ran into millions of dollars.

Aftermath

The House of Representatives impeached Trump for incitement of insurrection—his second impeachment—though the Senate acquitted him. The FBI investigation became the largest in U.S. history; more than 1,500 people were charged, and leaders of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers were convicted of seditious conspiracy. A House select committee investigated the attack in 2022. Upon returning to office in January 2025, Trump pardoned or commuted the sentences of nearly all those convicted in connection with the attack.
