Spanish language in the United States

Speakersover 40 million (home speakers)
RankSecond most spoken language in the U.S.
Major varietiesMexican, Puerto Rican, Cuban, Dominican, New Mexican

Spanish is the second most spoken language in the United States, after English. More than 40 million people speak Spanish at home, and the United States has one of the largest Spanish-speaking populations in the world — by some counts the second largest after Mexico when learners and second-language speakers are included. Spanish has been present in what is now the United States since the sixteenth century, in Florida, the Southwest, and Louisiana.

History

Large Spanish-speaking populations were incorporated into the United States by territorial expansion, including the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), which ended the Mexican–American War, and the acquisition of Puerto Rico in 1898. Twentieth-century immigration from Mexico, Cuba, Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, and Central and South America greatly expanded the Spanish-speaking population. Traditional varieties such as New Mexican Spanish preserve archaic features of colonial Spanish.

Use and status

Spanish is widely used in media, commerce, and public services, with major national networks such as Univision and Telemundo, hundreds of radio stations, and bilingual signage and ballots in many jurisdictions. Although several states have adopted English-only measures, Spanish-language education and bilingual programmes remain widespread. Research indicates a strong pattern of language shift to English by the third generation of immigrant families, while continued immigration replenishes the Spanish-speaking population. Mixed varieties such as "Spanglish" are common in bilingual communities such as the U.S.–Mexico border region, Miami, New York, Los Angeles, and Chicago.
