Organizational communication

Organizational communication is the study of how people communicate within organisational contexts, and of how organisations themselves communicate with external audiences. It is a subfield of communication studies drawing on management, sociology and psychology. Internally, communication flows downward (from managers to staff), upward (feedback and reporting) and horizontally (between peers and departments); externally it includes public relations, marketing communication and stakeholder engagement.

Perspectives

Scholars distinguish several approaches. The functionalist tradition treats communication as information transfer that can be measured and optimised. The interpretive tradition, influenced by Karl Weick's theory of organising, sees communication as the process by which shared meaning and organisational reality are created. Critical approaches examine power, identity and control in workplace discourse. The "communicative constitution of organizations" (CCO) perspective argues that organisations literally come into being through communication.

Topics

Research topics include organisational culture and climate, leadership communication, crisis communication, internal communication channels and media choice, socialisation of new members, and the effects of digital tools such as email, instant messaging and enterprise social networks on coordination and hierarchy.
