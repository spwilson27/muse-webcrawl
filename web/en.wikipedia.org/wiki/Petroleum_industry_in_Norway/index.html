Petroleum industry in Norway

The petroleum industry in Norway began with the discovery of the Ekofisk field in the North Sea in 1969 and transformed Norway from a modest maritime economy into one of the world's wealthiest countries. Norway is a major producer and exporter of oil and natural gas, the largest supplier of natural gas to Europe after the reduction of Russian pipeline gas in 2022. Petroleum revenues are channelled into the Government Pension Fund Global — the world's largest sovereign wealth fund, worth well over a trillion US dollars.

History

Norway asserted sovereignty over its continental shelf in 1963. Phillips Petroleum discovered Ekofisk in December 1969, and production began in 1971. The giant Statfjord (1974) and Troll gas field (1979) followed. Parliament laid down the "ten oil commandments" in 1971 asserting national control, and created Statoil (now Equinor) in 1972. Production peaked for oil around 2001 at some 3.4 million barrels per day and has since declined, partly offset by the giant Johan Sverdrup field, which came on stream in 2019.[1]

Policy and the fund

Norway taxes petroleum profits at a high marginal rate (78% including a special tax) and takes direct state interests in fields through Petoro. Since 1990, net state petroleum cash flow has been transferred to the Government Pension Fund Global, invested entirely abroad to avoid overheating the domestic economy; a fiscal rule limits annual spending to the fund's expected real return, estimated at 3%. The sector remains politically contested because of climate policy and continued licensing in mature and frontier (Barents Sea) areas.
