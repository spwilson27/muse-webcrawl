Gameplay

Gameplay is the specific way in which players interact with a game — in particular a video game. It is the pattern defined through the game's rules and mechanics, the connection between the player and the game, the challenges presented and the means of overcoming them, and the player's decisions and their consequences. The term is used in criticism and design to evaluate a game's interactive qualities separately from its graphics, sound and story.

Components

Gameplay emerges from a game's mechanics (the actions available to the player, such as jumping, shooting, trading or building), its systems (how mechanics interrelate — economies, physics, AI), and its game feel or "juice" (the moment-to-moment sensory feedback of control). Designers describe a core loop: the repeated cycle of action and reward that sustains engagement. Well-tuned gameplay balances challenge and skill, an idea formalised in Csikszentmihalyi's concept of flow.

Types

Gameplay may be linear or open-ended ("sandbox"), symmetric or asymmetric, cooperative or competitive, turn-based or real-time, and emergent — arising from system interactions rather than scripted events, as famously in immersive sims and simulation games. Genres are largely defined by characteristic gameplay: platformers by jumping, shooters by aiming, strategy games by resource and unit management.

In criticism

Reviews commonly score gameplay alongside presentation and longevity. Recordings of gameplay — trailers, "let's play" videos and livestreams — are a primary medium for marketing and for the spectator culture around esports. Legal systems generally treat gameplay itself as uncopyrightable, protecting only a game's expressive assets, a principle tested in cases over game clones.
