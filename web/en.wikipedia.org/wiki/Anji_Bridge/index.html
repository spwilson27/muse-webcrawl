The Anji Bridge (安濟橋, "Safe Crossing Bridge"), also known as the Zhaozhou Bridge, is China's oldest standing bridge and the world's oldest open-spandrel stone segmental arch bridge. It crosses the Xiao River in Zhao County, Hebei, about 40 km south-east of Shijiazhuang.

Design

Built between 595 and 605 AD during the Sui dynasty by the craftsman Li Chun, the bridge has a single main arch with a span of 37.37 metres and a total length of about 64.4 metres. Its shallow segmental arch — only about a quarter of a full circle — is pierced by two smaller arches in each spandrel. These open spandrels reduce the bridge's weight by roughly 15%, let floodwater pass through, and were not used in Europe until some 700 years later.

The arch is assembled from 28 thin limestone slabs joined with iron dovetails, giving it slight flexibility that has helped it survive more than ten major floods, several wars and earthquakes of magnitude 7 or more over fourteen centuries.

Recognition

The bridge remains in use and is a protected national heritage site. In 1991 the American Society of Civil Engineers designated it an International Historic Civil Engineering Landmark, and it has been proposed for the UNESCO World Heritage List.
