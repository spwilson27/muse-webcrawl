Welcome Stranger

The Welcome Stranger was the largest alluvial gold nugget ever found. It was discovered by Cornish miners John Deason and Richard Oates on 5 February 1869 at Moliagul in Victoria, Australia, about 14 kilometres northwest of Dunolly, during the Victorian gold rush.

Discovery

The nugget lay just below the surface near the roots of a tree on Bulldog Gully. Deason struck it with his pick while digging around the tree; the mass was so large that it broke the pick. The two men carried it secretly to Dunolly, where it was weighed at the London Chartered Bank. It measured about 61 by 31 centimetres and had a gross weight of roughly 109 kilograms, containing around 72 kilograms (about 2,300 troy ounces) of refined gold.

Legacy

The finders received just under £10,000 for the nugget. Because it was too large for available scales and was broken up on an anvil at Dunolly before being melted down in Melbourne and shipped to the Bank of England, no complete cast survives; a model based on contemporary drawings is displayed in Museums Victoria, and an obelisk marks the discovery site at Moliagul. The Welcome Stranger remains the largest recorded alluvial nugget, slightly exceeding the earlier Welcome Nugget found at Ballarat in 1858.
