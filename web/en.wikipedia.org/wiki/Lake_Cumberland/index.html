Lake Cumberland is a reservoir in Kentucky, United States, formed by the impoundment of the Cumberland River behind Wolf Creek Dam. It is one of the largest man-made lakes in the United States by volume and a major recreation destination in the southeastern part of the state.

History

Wolf Creek Dam, a combined concrete-gravity and earth-embankment structure, was authorised under the Flood Control Act of 1938 and completed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in 1952. Construction displaced communities and flooded farmland across several counties. The dam provides flood control, hydroelectric power, and water supply in addition to recreation.

Dam repairs

In 2007 the Corps lowered the lake by roughly 13 metres (43 ft) after discovering serious seepage through the dam's karst limestone foundation, which threatened catastrophic failure. A multi-year grouting and barrier-wall project followed; the lake was returned to normal levels in 2013–2014. The drawdown had major economic effects on marinas and houseboat tourism, for which the lake is famous — it bills itself as the "Houseboat Capital of the World", and many houseboats are manufactured locally.

Recreation

Lake Cumberland State Resort Park and dozens of marinas serve millions of visitors annually for boating, fishing (striped bass, crappie, walleye), and houseboating. The annual Lake Cumberland Poker Run is one of the largest powerboating events in the United States.
