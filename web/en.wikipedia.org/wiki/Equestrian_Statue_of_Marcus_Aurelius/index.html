Equestrian Statue of Marcus Aurelius

Equestrian Statue of Marcus Aurelius
Artist: Unknown
Year: c. 175 AD
Medium: Gilded bronze
Dimensions: 4.24 m (13.9 ft) high
Location: Capitoline Museums, Rome

The Equestrian Statue of Marcus Aurelius is an ancient Roman equestrian statue on the Capitoline Hill, Rome, Italy. It is made of bronze and stands about 4.24 m (13.9 ft) tall. The statue was erected c. 175 AD. Its original location is debated: the Roman Forum and the Palazzo Lateranense (collection of the Lateran Palace) have been proposed.

Although there were many equestrian imperial statues, they rarely survived because it was the common practice to melt down bronze statues for reuse as coinage or new statues in the Late Empire. Statues were also destroyed by Christian iconoclasts during the early medieval period; the emperor is depicted without weapons or armour, as he preferred to be portrayed as a bringer of peace rather than a military hero.

Description

The statue depicts Marcus Aurelius on horseback. He is bearded, in accordance with the fashion of the time, and wears a tunic and military boots but no armour. His right arm is raised in a gesture of clemency or address. Beneath the horse's raised hoof there was once, according to medieval accounts, the figure of a bound barbarian chieftain. The horse is depicted in motion, with one front leg raised; in Renaissance copies, such as the copy made for the piazza, the supported weight distribution became a celebrated engineering challenge.

History

The statue survived the Middle Ages in part because it was popularly believed to depict Constantine the Great, the first Christian emperor, and was therefore not melted down as a pagan idol. It stood for centuries at the Lateran. In 1538 Pope Paul III had it transferred to the Piazza del Campidoglio on the Capitoline Hill, where Michelangelo designed the pedestal and the oval paving pattern around it.

After centuries outdoors, the original was removed for conservation in 1981 and is now kept in the Palazzo dei Conservatori of the Capitoline Museums, in a purpose-built exedra. A replica, cast after the restoration and completed in 1997, stands in its place in the piazza.

Legacy

The statue is the prototype of the Renaissance and later equestrian monument, influencing works such as Donatello's Gattamelata and Verrocchio's monument to Bartolomeo Colleoni. It appears on the Italian 50-cent euro coin.
