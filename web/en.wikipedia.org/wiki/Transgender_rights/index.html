Transgender rights

Transgender rights concern the legal and social status of transgender people: the ability to change legal gender markers, protection from discrimination in employment, housing and services, access to gender-affirming healthcare, and recognition of identity in areas from identification documents to sport and military service. The movement developed from earlier gay rights activism and the mid-20th-century work of advocates such as Virginia Prince and organisations founded after the Stonewall riots of 1969.

Legal recognition

Countries differ widely. Argentina's 2012 Gender Identity Law allows adults to change legal gender by self-declaration without medical requirements; similar self-identification systems exist in several European and Latin American countries. Others require diagnosis, sterilisation (historically) or court approval, and some jurisdictions have recently tightened requirements or do not recognise changes at all. India's Supreme Court recognised a "third gender" in 2014.

Contested areas

Debates continue over participation in sex-segregated sport, access to single-sex spaces and prisons, medical treatment for minors, and how discrimination law should treat sex and gender identity. Public opinion and law vary sharply by country and continue to change as legislatures and courts consider these questions.
