Big Boy is a restaurant chain that began in 1936 as Bob's Pantry in Glendale, California, founded by Bob Wian. The chain is known for its mascot, a chubby boy in red-and-white checkered overalls holding a double-decker hamburger, and for the Big Boy sandwich of the same style, an early example of the double-deck burger later popularised nationally.

History

Wian created the double-deck hamburger in 1937 (legend says at the request of a customer wanting "something different") and named both sandwich and restaurant after "Big Boy," the nickname of a six-year-old customer, Richard Woodruff, whose likeness inspired the mascot design drawn on a napkin. The chain grew through franchising, notably by Marriott Corporation, which bought Big Boy in 1967, and through regional franchisees that operated under names such as Frisch's, Elias Brothers, Shoney's (originally), Bob's, and Eat'n Park.

Mascot and menu

The Big Boy statue became a fixture of American roadside culture. Menus centre on the Big Boy burger, served with a special sauce on a three-part bun, alongside Slim Jim sandwiches, hot fudge cakes and (at Frisch's locations) pumpkin pie and tartar-sauce-topped burgers. In Japan, a separate Big Boy chain developed independently under different ownership.

Decline and present

The chain shrank from hundreds of locations in its peak decades as regional franchisees diverged or dropped the brand; Frisch's, the largest remaining franchisee, ended its branding agreement in the 2020s. The parent company, headquartered in Michigan, continues to operate and franchise Big Boy restaurants, concentrated in Michigan, Ohio and California (as Bob's Big Boy, including the landmark 1949 Burbank restaurant).
