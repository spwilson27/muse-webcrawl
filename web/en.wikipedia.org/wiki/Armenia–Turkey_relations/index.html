Armenia–Turkey relations

Mount Ararat, in Turkey, is a national symbol of Armenia; the land border has been closed since 1993.

Armenia–Turkey relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Armenia and the Republic of Turkey. Turkey recognised Armenia's independence from the Soviet Union in 1991, but the two countries have never established formal diplomatic relations, and their shared border has been closed since 1993.

Historical background

Relations are overshadowed by the Armenian genocide of 1915–17 in the Ottoman Empire, which Armenia and most historians characterise as genocide, while Turkey disputes that characterisation. The border between the two states was fixed by the Treaty of Kars (1921). Turkey also objects to what it sees as Armenian territorial claims implied by references to "Western Armenia".

Border closure and the Karabakh conflict

In 1993, during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, Turkey closed its border with Armenia in solidarity with Azerbaijan. The blockade forced Armenia to route most trade through Georgia and Iran. In 2008–09, "football diplomacy" between presidents Serzh Sargsyan and Abdullah Gül led to the Zurich Protocols, signed in October 2009, which would have established diplomatic relations and opened the border; neither parliament ratified them, and Armenia formally annulled the protocols in 2018.

Normalisation efforts

In December 2021 both countries appointed special envoys for normalisation talks, which began in Moscow and Vienna in 2022. In February 2023, after the Turkey–Syria earthquakes, Armenia sent humanitarian aid and the Alican border crossing opened briefly for aid convoys for the first time in decades, raising hopes for a fuller reopening, including to third-country nationals.
