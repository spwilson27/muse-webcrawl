Ardashir I (Middle Persian: Ardašīr), also known as Ardashir the Unifier, was the founder of the Sasanian Empire, ruling from 224 to 242 AD. A native of Persis (Fars) in southwestern Iran, he overthrew the last Parthian king, Artabanus IV, and replaced the four-century-old Arsacid order with a centralized Iranian empire that endured until the seventh century.

Rise to power

Ardashir was the son of Papak, a local prince of Persis who seized the throne of Istakhr; Ardashir succeeded his brother Shapur as king of Persis around 208–212. From his base at Darabgerd he methodically conquered neighbouring principalities and, by 224, controlled southern Iran. On 28 April 224 he defeated and killed Artabanus IV at the Battle of Hormozdgan on the plain near Shushtar, ending Parthian rule. He was crowned "King of Kings of Iran" at Ctesiphon in 226.

Reign

Ardashir founded or refounded cities, including Ardashir-Khwarrah (modern Firuzabad) with its circular plan, and Zoroastrian fire temples became instruments of state centralization. He promoted Zoroastrianism as the state religion under the priest Kartir's predecessors, notably Tansar, and presented his house, descended from the legendary Sasan, as restorers of Iranian unity and of the glory of the Achaemenids. His wars against Rome, begun in the 230s over Armenia and Mesopotamia, were inconclusive; he captured Carrhae and Nisibis briefly but could not hold them. He also campaigned in the east, receiving the submission of rulers in Sistan, Khorasan, and Margiana.

Death and legacy

In about 240 Ardashir associated his son Shapur I with him as co-ruler and died in February 242. His investiture reliefs at Naqsh-e Rostam and Firuzabad, showing him receiving the diadem from the god Ahura Mazda, set the iconographic pattern of Sasanian kingship. Later Iranian tradition remembered Ardashir as the model just king, and sayings on government were attributed to him in Middle Persian literature.
