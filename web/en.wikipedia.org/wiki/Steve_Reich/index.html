Stephen Michael Reich (born October 3, 1936) is an American composer known for his contribution to the development of minimal music in the mid to late 1960s. His work is marked by its use of repetitive figures, slow harmonic rhythm and canons, and he is cited by critics and musicians as one of the most influential composers of the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

Early tape works and phasing

Reich developed the technique of phasing in his tape pieces It's Gonna Rain (1965) and Come Out (1966), in which two identical loops gradually move out of synchronisation. He transferred the process to live performance in Piano Phase (1967) and Clapping Music (1972), the latter requiring no instruments beyond two pairs of hands.

Major works

Music for 18 Musicians (1974–76) brought Reich international attention with its pulsing, shimmering textures. Different Trains (1988), for string quartet and tape, juxtaposed Reich's childhood train journeys in America with the deportation trains of the Holocaust, and won a Grammy Award. Later works include the multimedia opera The Cave (1993), City Life (1995) and Double Sextet (2007), which won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 2009.

Influence

Reich's music has influenced composers from Brian Eno to Philip Glass's contemporaries in the minimalist school, as well as rock and electronic musicians; his Electric Counterpoint has been widely sampled, notably by The Orb.
