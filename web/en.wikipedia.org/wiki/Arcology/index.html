Arcology

Arcology, a portmanteau of "architecture" and "ecology", is a field of creating architectural design principles for very densely populated and ecologically low-impact human habitats. The term was coined in 1969 by architect Paolo Soleri, who proposed enormous, self-contained megastructures housing entire cities as an alternative to urban sprawl and wasteful resource consumption.

Principles

An arcology combines living, working, agricultural, and recreational spaces in a single three-dimensional structure, minimizing the need for private automobiles, reducing land use, and recycling water and waste internally. By concentrating population, Soleri argued, an arcology could preserve surrounding wilderness and cut per-capita energy and material consumption dramatically.

Arcosanti and other projects

Soleri's experimental town Arcosanti, in the Arizona desert about 70 miles north of Phoenix, was begun in 1970 as a working prototype. Built largely by volunteer workshops using Soleri's silt-cast "earth casting" techniques, it remains only a small fraction of its planned size — intended ultimately for 5,000 residents — yet continues as an educational and architectural landmark. Other arcology proposals include the Japanese Shimizu Mega-City Pyramid, the X-Seed 4000 tower concept, and numerous designs for space habitats. The concept has strongly influenced science fiction, from the megacities of Judge Dredd and Blade Runner to the planet-city of Coruscant in Star Wars, and appeared in simulation games such as SimCity 2000.

Criticism

Critics note that arcologies would require unprecedented capital investment, centralized control of residents' environments, and untested social engineering, and that no full-scale arcology has ever been completed. Advocates respond that components — mixed-use megatowers, district energy systems, and dense transit-oriented development — are already being realized piecemeal.
