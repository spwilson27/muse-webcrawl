Ruth Gruber

Ruth Gruber (30 September 1911 – 7 November 2016) was an American journalist, photographer, author, and humanitarian. Born in Brooklyn to Russian Jewish immigrants, she earned a PhD from the University of Cologne in 1932 — at twenty among the youngest people then to do so — with a dissertation on Virginia Woolf.

Soviet Arctic and Alaska

Working for the New York Herald Tribune, Gruber travelled through the Soviet Arctic in the 1930s, among the first Western journalists to report from Siberia's far north, and published I Went to the Soviet Arctic (1939). During the Second World War she served as a special assistant to Secretary of the Interior Harold Ickes, studying Alaska's potential for post-war development and soldiers' settlement.

The Haven refugees and Exodus

In 1944 President Franklin D. Roosevelt authorised a group of 982 refugees, mostly Jews fleeing Nazi Europe, to be brought to the United States outside the immigration quotas. Gruber flew to Italy and escorted the group aboard the transport ship Henry Gibbins; they were interned as "guests" at the Fort Ontario Emergency Refugee Shelter in Oswego, New York, until granted residency in 1946 — the only such refugee group admitted to the U.S. during the war. Gruber chronicled the episode in Haven (1983), adapted as a television miniseries in 2001. In 1947 she covered the Exodus 1947 affair for the Herald Tribune, and her photograph of the refugee ship flying the Zionist flag above a swastika became iconic; she testified before the UN Special Committee on Palestine.

Later life

Gruber wrote some nineteen books, covered the Eichmann trial, and campaigned in later life for Ethiopian Jews and other displaced peoples. She died in Manhattan in 2016, aged 105.
