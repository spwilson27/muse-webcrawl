Cooper River (South Carolina)

LocationBerkeley and Charleston counties, South Carolina, United States
MouthCharleston Harbor
SourceConfluence of the West Branch and East Branch near Lake Moultrie
Named forAnthony Ashley Cooper, 1st Earl of Shaftesbury

The Cooper River is a mainly tidal river in the U.S. state of South Carolina. It is formed by the confluence of its East and West branches below Lake Moultrie in Berkeley County and flows generally south-eastward to join the Ashley River in forming Charleston Harbor. The river is named for Anthony Ashley Cooper, 1st Earl of Shaftesbury and one of the Lords Proprietors of the Carolina colony.

Course and history

The historic Cooper River was a relatively short tidal stream, but in the 1940s the Santee Cooper hydroelectric project diverted the Santee River into the Cooper via Lake Moultrie and the Pinopolis Dam, greatly increasing its flow. The diversion caused heavy siltation in Charleston Harbor, and in the 1980s much of the flow was rediverted back to the Santee River, restoring the Cooper closer to its earlier regime.

Crossings

The principal crossings near Charleston are the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge (opened 2005), a cable-stayed bridge carrying U.S. Route 17, and the older Silas N. Pearman and Grace Memorial bridges it replaced. The Ravenel Bridge is a well-known landmark and the site of the annual Cooper River Bridge Run.

Economy and environment

The river's banks host port terminals, former naval facilities, paper and chemical industries, and historic rice plantations such as those along its East Branch. The lower river supports recreational boating, fishing, and rowing, and its marshes form part of the Charleston Harbor estuary system.
