Doctrine of the Mean

The Doctrine of the Mean (Chinese: 中庸; pinyin: Zhōngyōng) is one of the Four Books of Confucianism. Traditionally attributed to Zisi, the grandson of Confucius, it was made part of the core Confucian canon by the Song-dynasty scholar Zhu Xi.

Content

The text expounds the virtues of moderation, balance, and sincerity (cheng). The "Mean" is not mere compromise but the perfectly balanced state in which emotions arise in due measure and the individual aligns with the Way of Heaven. Central themes include self-cultivation, the harmony between humanity and the cosmos, and the idea that the exemplary person (junzi) maintains equilibrium and constancy in all circumstances.

The concept has often been compared with Aristotle's doctrine of the golden mean in the Nicomachean Ethics.
