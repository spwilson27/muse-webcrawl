Herodian dynasty

Founded37 BCE — Herod the Great as King of Judea
FounderHerod the Great
OriginIdumaean / Edomite, client kings of Rome

The Herodian dynasty was a ruling dynasty of Idumaean origin that governed Judea and neighbouring territories as client kings of the Roman Republic and Empire, beginning with Herod the Great in 37 BCE.

History

Herod's father Antipater rose under the Hasmoneans and Julius Caesar. Herod, appointed king by the Roman Senate, ruled until 4 BCE, rebuilding the Second Temple in Jerusalem and founding Caesarea Maritima, Masada and Herodium, while brutally suppressing rivals, including within his family.

Division

On his death the kingdom was split among his sons: Archelaus (Judea, deposed 6 CE, becoming a Roman province), Herod Antipas (Galilee, tetrarch during the ministry of Jesus), and Philip (northeast territories). Herod Agrippa I briefly reunited the kingdom (41–44 CE); his son Agrippa II was the last Herodian ruler, siding with Rome in the First Jewish–Roman War.
