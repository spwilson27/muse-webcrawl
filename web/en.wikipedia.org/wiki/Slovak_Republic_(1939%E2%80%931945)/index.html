The Slovak Republic (1939–1945), also known as the First Slovak Republic or Slovak State, was a partially-recognised client state of Nazi Germany that existed from 14 March 1939 to April 1945, covering most of the territory of present-day Slovakia except for its southern districts ceded to Hungary.

History

Following the Munich Agreement and under German pressure, Slovakia declared independence from Czechoslovakia on 14 March 1939, one day before the German occupation of Bohemia and Moravia. The state was led by Jozef Tiso, a Catholic priest and leader of Hlinka's Slovak People's Party, which ruled as an authoritarian one-party regime.

The Holocaust in Slovakia

The Slovak government cooperated in the deportation of Slovak Jews: in 1942 it deported about 58,000 Jews to Nazi extermination camps, paying Germany a fee per deportee. Deportations resumed during the German occupation that followed the Slovak National Uprising of 1944.

End of the state

The Slovak National Uprising of August 1944 was suppressed by German forces. Soviet and Czechoslovak troops liberated the territory in the spring of 1945, and the republic ceased to exist; Tiso was executed for treason in 1947.
