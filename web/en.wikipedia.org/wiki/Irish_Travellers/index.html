Irish Travellers

Self-nameMincéirí, Pavees
RegionsIreland, United Kingdom, United States
LanguagesEnglish, Irish, Shelta

Irish Travellers (Irish: Lucht Siúil, "the walking people"), also known as Pavees or Mincéirí, are a traditionally peripatetic indigenous ethnic group native to Ireland. Genetic studies indicate that Travellers diverged from the settled Irish population several centuries ago, and they were formally recognised as an ethnic group by the Irish state in 2017.

Culture and language

Traveller culture places strong emphasis on extended family, and traditionally involved travelling in family groups and working in trades such as tinsmithing, horse dealing and seasonal farm labour. Many speak Shelta (also called Gammon or Cant), a language based largely on Irish with English influences, used as a marker of group identity. Horse culture remains prominent, with fairs such as Ballinasloe drawing large gatherings.

Demographics and issues

The 2022 census recorded about 33,000 Travellers in the Republic of Ireland, with further communities in Northern Ireland, Britain and the United States. Travellers experience markedly poorer health outcomes, lower life expectancy and lower educational attainment than the settled population, and face widespread discrimination. Accommodation — including halting sites and roadside camps — has been a long-standing policy issue.
