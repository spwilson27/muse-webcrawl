Polish Legions in World War I

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Polish Legions (Polish: Legiony Polskie) were a Polish military force formed in August 1914 in Galicia, the Austrian-ruled part of partitioned Poland, at the outbreak of the First World War. Created with Austro-Hungarian consent under the leadership of Józef Piłsudski, the Legions were intended as the nucleus of a future Polish army that would fight Russia — the partitioning power Piłsudski judged the most dangerous enemy — and advance the cause of an independent Poland.

Formation and campaigns

The Legions were organized into three brigades. The First Brigade, commanded by Piłsudski, marched from Kraków into Russian Poland in August 1914. The Second Brigade fought in the Carpathians and in Bukovina and Bessarabia, enduring the hard winter campaigns of 1914–15. The Third Brigade was raised in 1915. Legion units fought at battles including Łowczówek (1914), Konary and Jastków (1915), and the costly Battle of Kostiuchnówka (July 1916), the largest Polish engagement of the war on the Eastern Front, where the Legions held off superior Russian forces during the Brusilov offensive. At their peak the Legions numbered roughly 16,500 men, and about 25,000 served in their ranks over the course of the war.

Oath crisis and dissolution

After the Central Powers proclaimed a nominal Polish kingdom in November 1916, the Legions were to be transferred to German command as the Polish Armed Forces (Polnische Wehrmacht). In July 1917 most legionnaires of the First and Third Brigades refused to swear an oath of loyalty to the German Kaiser — the "oath crisis" — and were interned (at Beniaminów and Szczypiorno), while Piłsudski was imprisoned in Magdeburg fortress. The Second Brigade's soldiers under Józef Haller, protesting the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk in 1918, broke through the front at Rarańcza and later formed the core of Haller's "Blue Army" in France.

Legacy

Veterans of the Legions supplied a large part of the officer corps of the Polish Army that secured and defended Poland's independence in 1918–1921. The Legions' traditions, songs (including "My, Pierwsza Brygada" — "We, the First Brigade"), and the prestige of Piłsudski made them a founding legend of the Second Polish Republic.
