Isokelekel

Isokelekel (Pohnpeian: "the splendid noble"; also Isohkelekel) was a semi-mythical hero who conquered Pohnpei in the Caroline Islands (now in the Federated States of Micronesia), overthrowing the Saudeleur dynasty that ruled from the artificial island city of Nan Madol.

The legend

According to Pohnpeian oral tradition preserved in accounts such as Luelen Bernart's The Book of Luelen, Isokelekel was the son of a Pohnpeian woman and the thunder god Nansos. Hearing of the Saudeleur's oppression, he sailed from Kosrae (in some versions, from the mythical land of Katau) with 333 warriors. Demigods in the form of warriors accompanied him, and omens foretold his victory.

After ritual preliminaries, a friendship-feint with the Saudeleur lord, and an invasion staged as a ceremony, war broke out. Isokelekel's forces prevailed when one of his warriors, Nahlehlah, sacrificed himself, and when the tide was arranged in his favor. The last Saudeleur, Isokelekel's opponent, fled and, according to legend, transformed into a fish and vanished into a stream.

Reorganization of Pohnpei

Rather than ruling from Nan Madol, Isokelekel founded a new political order: he established the chiefly system of the nahnmwarki, paramount chiefs presiding over autonomous chiefdoms (wehi), with dual lines of chiefs (nahnmwarki and nahnken). This decentralized "titulary" system, headed by the Nahnmwarki of Madolenihmw, endured into the modern era and structures Pohnpeian traditional politics to this day.

The conquest is usually dated to the 17th century (often around 1628). Isokelekel's son became the first Nahnken of Sokehs, and Isokelekel himself was treated as a founding figure of quasi-divine status.
