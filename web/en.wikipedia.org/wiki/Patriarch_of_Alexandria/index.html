Patriarch of Alexandria

Saint Mark's Cathedral, Alexandria, seat associated with the Alexandrian patriarchate.

The Patriarch of Alexandria is the title held by the archbishop of Alexandria in Egypt, historically one of the five great sees of early Christianity known as the Pentarchy. Christian tradition holds that the Church of Alexandria was founded by Saint Mark the Evangelist around AD 42, making its bishops custodians of one of the most ancient Christian communities.

History

Alexandria's patriarchs — including Athanasius, Cyril, and Dioscorus — were central figures in the Christological controversies of the 4th and 5th centuries. The Council of Chalcedon (451) split the Egyptian church: the majority, who rejected the council, formed what became the Coptic Orthodox Church under its own Pope of Alexandria, while a Chalcedonian (Greek) patriarchate continued in communion with Constantinople. Today the title is borne by three different hierarchs: the Coptic Orthodox Pope of Alexandria, the Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Alexandria and all Africa, and the Coptic Catholic Patriarch of Alexandria; a Latin titular patriarchate existed until 1964.

The three patriarchs

  • Coptic Orthodox Pope of Alexandria — head of the Coptic Orthodox Church, by far the largest Christian body in Egypt; styled "Pope and Patriarch of Alexandria and of the See of Saint Mark".
  • Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Alexandria and all Africa — head of the Eastern Orthodox patriarchate, second in honour among Orthodox patriarchs after the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople.
  • Coptic Catholic Patriarch of Alexandria — head of the Coptic Catholic Church, in communion with Rome.
