Zenaida doves

Zenaida is a genus of doves in the family Columbidae, native to the Americas. The genus contains seven species, among them the mourning dove (Zenaida macroura), one of the most abundant and widespread birds in North America, and the eared dove (Z. auriculata) of South America. The genus was named by Charles Lucien Bonaparte in 1838 for his wife, Zénaïde Bonaparte. The extinct Socorro dove (Z. graysoni) survives only in captivity.

Description and biology

Zenaida doves are medium-sized, generally drab brown-grey doves with short rounded tails (long and pointed in the mourning dove), dark wing spots and soft, cooing calls. They feed mainly on seeds on the ground and, like other pigeons and doves, feed their nestlings on crop milk. They build flimsy stick nests, usually laying two white eggs. The eared dove is an agricultural pest in parts of South America and forms immense flocks; the eponymous Zenaida dove is the national bird of Anguilla.
