British Academy Film Awards

The British Academy Film Awards, more commonly known as the BAFTA Awards, are presented annually by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA) to honour the best British and international contributions to film. The ceremonies are held annually and are the British equivalent of the American Academy Awards (Oscars).

The BAFTA trophy takes the form of a theatrical mask.

History

The British Film Academy was founded in 1947 by a group of directors including David Lean, Alexander Korda and Carol Reed. It merged with the Guild of Television Producers and Directors in 1958 to form what became BAFTA. The first film awards were presented in 1949, covering films released in 1947 and 1948. The ceremony moved to its long-term home at the Royal Albert Hall in London in 2017, having previously been held at the Royal Opera House.

Categories

Major categories include Best Film, Best British Film, Best Director, Best Actor in a Leading Role, Best Actress in a Leading Role, Best Original Screenplay, Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Cinematography and Best Film Music. The BAFTA Fellowship is the Academy's highest honour, awarded for outstanding lifetime achievement in film or television.

The mask

The distinctive trophy is a theatrical mask designed in 1955 by American sculptor Mitzi Cunliffe, commissioned by the Guild of Television Producers. Each mask is cast in phosphor bronze and finished by hand in London.
