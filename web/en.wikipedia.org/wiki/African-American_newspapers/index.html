African-American newspapers

African-American newspapers are news publications in the United States that have targeted Black American audiences for nearly two centuries. The first, Freedom's Journal, was founded in New York in 1827 by Samuel Cornish and John Brown Russwurm, declaring "We wish to plead our own cause. Too long have others spoken for us."

History

The Black press campaigned against slavery, and after emancipation against lynching and Jim Crow segregation. Influential papers included Frederick Douglass's North Star, the Chicago Defender (founded 1905), which encouraged the Great Migration of Black Southerners to Northern cities, the Pittsburgh Courier, which promoted the "Double V" campaign during the Second World War, and the Amsterdam News in Harlem.

Role and decline

Black newspapers provided news, role models, and advertising absent from mainstream media, and were central to civil-rights organising. Most were weeklies with modest circulations; many closed in the late twentieth century as mainstream outlets integrated and advertising shifted, though dozens of titles and digital successors continue.
