Private security

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Private security is a protective service provided by private companies and individuals rather than by public law-enforcement agencies. The private security industry includes uniformed security guards, patrol and alarm-response services, armoured car cash transport, bodyguards and close-protection officers, private investigators, loss-prevention staff, and — in some contexts — private military and security companies providing armed services to governments and corporations.

Scale

In many countries, private security personnel substantially outnumber public police officers. In the United States, security guards number over one million, compared with roughly 700,000–800,000 sworn police officers. Large multinational firms such as Allied Universal and Securitas employ hundreds of thousands of guards worldwide. Growth has been driven by demand from retail, logistics, critical infrastructure, mass private property such as shopping malls and gated communities, and the outsourcing of ancillary policing tasks.

Regulation

Regulation varies widely by jurisdiction and typically involves licensing of companies and individual guards, background checks, and training requirements. Private security officers generally have no more legal authority than ordinary citizens — their powers derive mainly from property rights (such as the ability to exclude trespassers) and citizen's arrest laws — though some jurisdictions grant limited additional authority to certain categories, such as railroad police or campus security officers.
