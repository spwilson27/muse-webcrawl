Saint Albertus Magnus (c. 1200 – 15 November 1280), also known as Albert the Great and Albert of Cologne, was a German Dominican friar, philosopher, scientist and bishop. One of the most universal scholars of the Middle Ages, he was famous during his lifetime as the doctor universalis — the teacher who knew everything knowable in his age — and was the mentor of Thomas Aquinas.

Life

Born at Lauingen in Bavaria, Albert studied at the University of Padua, where he joined the Dominican Order in 1223. He taught at Cologne, Paris and other studia, holding a chair at the University of Paris from 1245, where Aquinas became his student. In 1260 he was appointed Bishop of Regensburg, resigning after three years to return to teaching and writing in Cologne, where he died in 1280.

Work

Albert wrote an enormous body of work covering logic, metaphysics, ethics, politics, and every branch of medieval natural science — minerals, plants, animals, astronomy, chemistry and physiology. He produced paraphrases and commentaries on nearly the whole corpus of Aristotle, making Aristotelian thought, enriched by Arabic and Jewish commentators such as Avicenna and Maimonides, fully available to the Latin West. Unlike many contemporaries, he insisted on observation and experiment in natural inquiry — "the aim of natural science is not simply to accept the statements of others, but to investigate the causes at work in nature."

Albert argued for the compatibility of faith and reason, holding that truths known by philosophy and theology cannot ultimately contradict one another. His defence of this harmony laid the groundwork for the scholastic synthesis completed by Aquinas.

Veneration

Albert was canonized and named a Doctor of the Church by Pope Pius XI in 1931, and in 1941 was declared patron saint of natural scientists. His feast day is 15 November.
