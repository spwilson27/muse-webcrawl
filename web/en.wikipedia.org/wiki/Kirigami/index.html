Kirigami

Kirigami
OriginJapan
MediumPaper, scissors or cutting knife
RelatedOrigami, paper cutting

Kirigami (Japanese: 切り紙, from kiru, "to cut", and kami, "paper") is a variation of origami in which the paper is cut as well as folded, resulting in a three-dimensional design that stands away from the page. Unlike traditional origami, kirigami permits cutting, and designs are often symmetrical, made by folding the sheet before cutting so that the unfolded result repeats the pattern.

History

Paper cutting traditions exist in many cultures, including Chinese jianzhi and Japanese monkiri. The term kirigami itself was popularised in the United States by Florence Temko, whose 1962 book Kirigami, the Creative Art of Papercutting introduced the craft to a wide audience. Kirigami is now a standard technique in Japanese school art education.

Techniques and applications

Common kirigami forms include greeting-card pop-ups, paper architecture (the elaborately folded building models developed by Masahiro Chatani), and snowflake-style ornaments. In recent decades kirigami principles — strategic cuts that let a flat sheet deploy into a 3D structure — have been adopted in engineering and materials science for stretchable electronics, solar panel arrays, metamaterials and medical devices.
