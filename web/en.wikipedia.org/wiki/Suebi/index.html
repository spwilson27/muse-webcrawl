The Suebi (or Suevi) were a large group of Germanic peoples first mentioned by Julius Caesar in connection with the invasion of Gaul by Ariovistus in 58 BC. Tacitus, in his Germania, used the name for a broad collection of peoples occupying much of eastern Germania, from the Baltic to the Danube, including the Semnones, Marcomanni, Quadi, Hermunduri, and Langobards. Their characteristic hairstyle, the Suebian knot, is known from Roman descriptions and from bog bodies such as the Osterby head.

History

Under Ariovistus, Suebi crossed the Rhine into Gaul before being defeated by Caesar. Later groups of Suebi played major roles in the fall of the Western Roman Empire: in 406 AD a coalition including Suebi crossed the frozen Rhine, traversed Gaul, and entered Hispania, where the Suebi founded a kingdom in Gallaecia (modern Galicia and northern Portugal) with its capital at Braga. That Suebic kingdom, converted from Arianism to Catholicism in the sixth century, endured until 585, when it was annexed by the Visigoths under Leovigild. Other Suebian descendants, the Alamanni of southwestern Germany, preserved the name in the region of Swabia.
