Old Uyghur alphabet

The Old Uyghur alphabet was used for writing the Old Uyghur language from about the 8th to the 17th century. It was derived from the Sogdian alphabet, itself a descendant of the Syriac script, and was originally written horizontally but later rotated to be written vertically from top to bottom, in columns read left to right — an orientation influenced by Chinese writing.

History

The Uyghurs adopted the script after the fall of the Uyghur Khaganate in 840, as they migrated into the Tarim Basin and established the kingdom of Qocho. It was used for Buddhist, Manichaean, and administrative texts; large caches survive among the Dunhuang and Turfan manuscripts. During the Mongol Empire the script was adapted to write Mongolian, becoming the Classical Mongolian script, and in the 16th century it was further adapted for Manchu. The Old Uyghur script itself fell out of use as the Uyghurs adopted Arabic-based writing following conversion to Islam.

Characteristics

Like its Sogdian parent, the alphabet was an abjad in which vowels were often unwritten or ambiguous, with letters taking different shapes depending on their position in a word. The script is encoded in Unicode as the Old Uyghur block.
