Bernardino de Sahagún (c. 1499 – 5 February 1590) was a Spanish Franciscan friar, missionary priest and pioneering ethnographer who participated in the Catholic evangelisation of colonial New Spain (present-day Mexico). He is best known as the author of the Historia general de las cosas de la Nueva España ("General History of the Things of New Spain"), a monumental bilingual encyclopedia of Aztec (Mexica) culture known today as the Florentine Codex.

Life

Born in Sahagún, in the Kingdom of León, Sahagún studied at the University of Salamanca and joined the Franciscan order. He sailed to New Spain in 1529, two years after the fall of Tenochtitlan, and spent the rest of his life there teaching at the Colegio de Santa Cruz de Tlatelolco, where he instructed the sons of Nahua nobility in Latin, Spanish and Christian doctrine.

The Florentine Codex

Working with Nahua elders and his trilingual students, Sahagún compiled information on Aztec religion, society, economics, natural history and history. The resulting work, written in Nahuatl and Spanish with thousands of illustrations, is considered one of the most remarkable ethnographic records of an Indigenous culture ever produced. Spanish authorities later confiscated much of his work, fearing it preserved pagan beliefs, but a copy survived and is now held in the Laurentian Library in Florence, Italy.

Legacy

Sahagún is often called the "father of ethnography" or the first anthropologist of the Americas, and his codex remains an indispensable source for the study of pre-conquest Mesoamerican civilisation.
