Eric Ries (born 22 September 1978) is an American entrepreneur and author. He is best known for developing the lean startup methodology, a movement in high-technology entrepreneurship that advocates building a minimum viable product, testing it with customers, and iterating through "build–measure–learn" feedback loops. His book The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses (2011) became a New York Times bestseller and helped popularise the approach in Silicon Valley and beyond.

Career

After graduating from Yale University, Ries co-founded Catalyst Recruiting, an online forum for college students to network with potential employers, which failed. He then joined the social networking company IMVU as a senior software engineer and later chief technology officer, working with investor and mentor Steve Blank, whose "customer development" ideas strongly influenced the lean startup method. Ries began writing about his experiences on his blog, Startup Lessons Learned, in 2008.

The Startup Way

In 2017 Ries published The Startup Way, extending lean startup thinking to large established organisations and proposing "entrepreneurial management" as a general discipline. He also founded the Long-Term Stock Exchange (LTSE), a US stock exchange approved by the SEC in 2019 that is intended to reward long-term-focused companies and investors.
