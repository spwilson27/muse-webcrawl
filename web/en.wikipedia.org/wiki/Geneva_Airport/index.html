Geneva Airport (French: Aéroport de Genève, IATA: GVA, ICAO: LSGG), formerly and still unofficially known as Cointrin Airport, is an international airport serving Geneva, Switzerland. It is located 4 km (2.5 mi) northwest of the city centre, in the municipalities of Le Grand-Saconnex and Meyrin, and lies close to the French border; uniquely, it has a French sector allowing passengers to arrive from and depart to France without passing Swiss customs.

History

The airfield at Cointrin was established in 1919, and a first terminal was built in 1928. Geneva's role as seat of the League of Nations and later of the European headquarters of the United Nations drove its development as an international gateway. The present main terminal dates from 1968, designed for an era of rapid growth in jet travel, and has been repeatedly expanded; an east wing for long-haul flights (Aile Est) opened in 2021. The airport handled over 17 million passengers in 2019 before traffic fell during the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequently recovered.

Facilities and operations

The airport has a single concrete runway, 04/22, of 3,900 m (12,795 ft) — among the longest in Switzerland — allowing unrestricted long-haul operations. It is a major base for easyJet Switzerland, the largest carrier by passengers, and a hub for SWISS. Passengers include large numbers of diplomats, bankers, and skiers travelling to Alpine resorts in winter, which produces strong seasonal peaks. The airport railway station provides direct trains to Geneva's main station in about seven minutes.

The airport is owned by the Canton of Geneva and operated as an autonomous public institution. Its proximity to residential areas makes noise and night-flight restrictions a recurring subject of local debate.
