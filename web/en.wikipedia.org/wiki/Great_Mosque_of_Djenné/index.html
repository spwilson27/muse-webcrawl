Great Mosque of Djenné

The Great Mosque of Djenné is the largest mud-brick building in the world and the finest example of the Sudano-Sahelian architectural style, located in the town of Djenné in Mali on the floodplain of the Bani River. The first mosque on the site was built around the 13th century; the present structure dates from 1907, rebuilt under French administration after the earlier building fell into ruin.

Architecture and festival

The mosque's massive adobe walls are reinforced with wooden palm-rib beams (toron) that project from the façade and double as scaffolding for annual repairs. Its three large minaret-like towers are topped with ostrich eggs, symbols of fertility and purity. Each year the whole community takes part in the Crépissage de la Grande Mosquée, a festive replastering of the mosque with mud that protects the fabric during the rainy season. The mosque and the old town of Djenné are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
