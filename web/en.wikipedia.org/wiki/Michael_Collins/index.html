Michael Collins
Born: October 31, 1930, Rome, Italy
Died: April 28, 2021 (aged 90), Naples, Florida, U.S.
Occupation: Astronaut, test pilot, U.S. Air Force major general
Missions: Gemini 10, Apollo 11

Michael Collins (October 31, 1930 – April 28, 2021) was an American astronaut who flew the Apollo 11 command module Columbia around the Moon in 1969 while his crewmates, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin, made the first crewed landing on the lunar surface. An Air Force officer and test pilot, Collins was the third group NASA astronaut selection of 1963 and had previously flown on Gemini 10, during which he performed two spacewalks.

Early life and military career

Born in Rome, Italy, where his father, U.S. Army Major General James Lawton Collins, served as a military attaché, Collins graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1952 and chose an Air Force commission. He flew F-86 Sabres in France and Germany and became an experimental test pilot at Edwards Air Force Base, logging over 4,200 hours of flying time.

NASA career

On Gemini 10 (July 1966), with John Young, Collins set a world altitude record and made the first rendezvous with two separate target vehicles, completing two EVAs including retrieving an experiment from the Agena-8 target. Originally assigned to Apollo 8, he required surgery for a cervical disc problem; reassigned to Apollo 11, he served as command module pilot. On July 20, 1969, he remained alone in lunar orbit for 21.5 hours while Armstrong and Aldrin were on the surface — during each orbit's far-side pass he was entirely cut off from communication, leading to descriptions of him as "the loneliest man in history," a label he deflected with humour and wrote about in his memoir Carrying the Fire (1974), widely regarded as the finest astronaut autobiography.

Later life

Collins left NASA in 1970, served briefly as Assistant Secretary of State for Public Affairs, and then became the first director of the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum, overseeing its opening in 1976; he later served as Under Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution. He was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom for Apollo 11. Collins died of cancer in 2021.
