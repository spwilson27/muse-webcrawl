Advanced Mobile Phone System

AMPS
Type: Analogue cellular telephone system (1G)
Developed by: Bell Labs
Launched: 1983 (United States)
Bands: 800 MHz

Advanced Mobile Phone System (AMPS) was an analogue mobile phone system standard originally developed by Bell Labs and first introduced in the Americas in 1983. It was the first generation (1G) of cellular technology deployed widely in North America.

Technology

AMPS used frequency-division multiple access (FDMA), dividing the 800 MHz band into 30 kHz voice channels modulated with FM. Cells reused frequencies at a distance, greatly increasing capacity compared with earlier mobile telephone services. Because calls were unencrypted analogue transmissions, they could be intercepted with ordinary radio scanners.

Successors and shutdown

Digital derivatives such as D-AMPS (IS-54/IS-136) and later CDMA and GSM systems superseded AMPS. United States carriers were permitted to shut down their AMPS networks in 2008, ending the service after twenty-five years.
