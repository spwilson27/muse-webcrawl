Ontogeny

Ontogeny (also ontogenesis) is the origination and development of an organism, from fertilisation through embryonic development to maturity and senescence. It contrasts with phylogeny, the evolutionary history of a lineage. The study of ontogeny is a central part of developmental biology.

History of the concept

Ernst Haeckel famously proposed that "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" — that an embryo passes through stages resembling its evolutionary ancestors — a strong claim now rejected, though comparative embryology remains important in evolutionary developmental biology (evo-devo). Earlier thinkers debated preformationism versus epigenesis before modern genetics and cell biology resolved development as the unfolding of a genetic programme interacting with its environment.

Stages

Typical stages include fertilisation, cleavage, gastrulation, organogenesis, growth, metamorphosis in many animals, and ageing. In plants, ontogeny proceeds from seed germination through vegetative and reproductive phases, with meristems sustaining growth throughout life.
