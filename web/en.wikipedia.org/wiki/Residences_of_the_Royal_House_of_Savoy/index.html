Residences of the Royal House of Savoy

The Residences of the Royal House of Savoy are a group of palaces, castles and hunting lodges in and around Turin in Piedmont, Italy, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997. Built from the 16th century onwards by the dukes and later kings of the House of Savoy, the residences form a "crown of delights" (corona di delizie) around the capital, designed to display the dynasty's power and taste as it rose from ducal to royal — and eventually, in 1861, to ruling all Italy.

History

When Duke Emmanuel Philibert moved the Savoy capital to Turin in 1563, the court began rebuilding the city and ringing it with residences linked by straight avenues. In Turin itself they include the Royal Palace, Palazzo Madama, Palazzo Carignano and the Palace of Valentino. Outside the city stand the hunting palace of Stupinigi, designed by Filippo Juvarra; the vast Palace of Venaria, a Baroque hunting seat rivalling Versailles; the castles of Rivoli, Moncalieri, Racconigi and Agliè; and the hilltop Basilica of Superga, the dynasty's mausoleum.

Significance

UNESCO recognised the group as an outstanding example of Baroque and Neoclassical monumental architecture and town planning, in which a single dynasty coordinated architects such as Amedeo di Castellamonte, Guarino Guarini and Filippo Juvarra over two centuries. Many residences now house museums and are open to the public; Venaria reopened in 2007 after one of Europe's largest restoration projects.
