The Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics of the Russian Academy of Sciences is a research institution in Chernogolovka, near Moscow. It was founded in 1964 by Isaak Markovich Khalatnikov, who directed it until 1992, and was created to carry forward the theoretical physics school of Nobel laureate Lev Landau after the 1962 car accident that ended Landau's scientific career. The institute's first staff were largely Landau's students and collaborators.

Research

The institute became internationally known for work in condensed matter physics, quantum field theory, particle physics, nonlinear dynamics and chaos, plasma physics, astrophysics and gravitation, and mathematical physics. Members of the institute included Alexei Abrikosov, who shared the 2003 Nobel Prize in Physics for work on superconductivity begun in Landau's circle, and Alexander Polyakov, known for fundamental contributions to quantum field theory. The associated "Landau minimum" examination tradition and the multi-volume Course of Theoretical Physics by Landau and Evgeny Lifshitz shaped generations of physicists worldwide.

History

During the Soviet period the institute was unusually internationally connected, and its weekly seminars were a focal point of Moscow theoretical physics. After 1991 many members took positions abroad while retaining ties to the institute, which continues as part of the Russian Academy of Sciences, with departments in Chernogolovka and Moscow.
