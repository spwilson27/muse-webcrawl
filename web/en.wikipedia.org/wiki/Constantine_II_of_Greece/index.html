Constantine II of Greece

Constantine II (Greek: Κωνσταντίνος Βʹ; 2 June 1940 – 10 January 2023) was the last King of Greece, reigning from 6 March 1964 until the abolition of the monarchy on 1 June 1973. A member of the House of Glücksburg, he succeeded his father, Paul I, at the age of 23.

Reign

Constantine's brief reign was dominated by political instability, including his clash with Prime Minister Georgios Papandreou in 1965 (the Apostasia), and by the military coup of 21 April 1967 that brought the Greek junta to power. In December 1967 his counter-coup failed and he fled into exile in Rome. The junta abolished the monarchy in 1973, and a referendum in 1974, after the fall of the junta, confirmed the republic.

Later life

Constantine won a gold medal in sailing (Dragon class) at the 1960 Rome Olympics as crown prince. He lived for many years in London, remaining close to the Danish and Spanish royal families — his sister Sofía was Queen of Spain. He returned to live in Greece in 2013 and died in Athens in 2023, receiving a private funeral attended by European royalty.
