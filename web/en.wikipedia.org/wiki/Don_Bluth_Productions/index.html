Don Bluth Productions was an American animation studio founded in 1979 by animator Don Bluth and colleagues who had left Walt Disney Productions, disillusioned with the state of Disney animation. The studio aimed to restore classical hand-drawn animation techniques and became Disney's chief rival in animated features during the 1980s.

History

After the short film Banjo the Woodpile Cat, the studio's first feature was The Secret of NIMH (1982), critically praised though a modest box-office performer. Partnerships with Steven Spielberg produced the hits An American Tail (1986) and The Land Before Time (1988). Financial pressures moved production to Ireland as Sullivan Bluth Studios, with further features including All Dogs Go to Heaven (1989) and Rock-a-Doodle (1991).

After a string of commercial disappointments in the early 1990s, Bluth and Gary Goldman joined Fox Animation Studios in Phoenix, where they made the successful Anastasia (1997) and Titan A.E. (2000), whose failure led to the studio's closure. Bluth's company had earlier also created the animation for the laserdisc arcade games Dragon's Lair (1983) and Space Ace.
