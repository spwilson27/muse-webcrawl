The American mink (Neogale vison) is a semiaquatic mammal of the mustelid family native to North America. It has a long, slender body, short legs, partially webbed feet, and a thick, water-repellent coat of dark brown fur, often with a white patch on the chin and throat. Males measure roughly 55–70 cm in length and weigh up to about 1.6 kg; females are notably smaller.

Ecology

American mink inhabit wetlands, rivers, lakes, and coastal areas, where they hunt fish, crayfish, frogs, small mammals, birds, and eggs. They are largely solitary and nocturnal, and are strong swimmers. The species has been widely introduced — largely through escapes and releases from fur farms — in Europe, South America, and Asia, where it is an invasive species implicated in declines of native fauna such as the European mink and the water vole.

Fur farming

The species is the principal mink raised for fur, and captive breeding has produced many colour mutations. Fur farming of mink has been controversial on animal-welfare grounds and faced additional scrutiny after outbreaks of SARS-CoV-2 among farmed mink in 2020. The domesticated form is sometimes distinguished as Neogale vison domesticus.
