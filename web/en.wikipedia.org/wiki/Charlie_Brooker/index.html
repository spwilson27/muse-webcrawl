Charlton "Charlie" Brooker (born 3 March 1971) is an English writer, satirist, broadcaster and producer. He is best known as the creator of the dystopian science-fiction anthology series Black Mirror, and for his media-criticism programmes such as Screenwipe and the satirical review shows Wipe, as well as the zombie drama Dead Set and the comedy series Nathan Barley (with Chris Morris).

Charlie Brooker, creator of Black Mirror

Early career

Brooker was born in Reading and grew up in Oxfordshire. He began his career as a cartoonist and video-games journalist, writing for PC Zone magazine, and created the cult satirical website TVGoHome. From 2000 he wrote a column, "Screen Burn", for The Guardian, filled with acerbic commentary on television, and developed the sitcom Nathan Barley from the TVGoHome material.

Television

Brooker's Screenwipe (2006–2009) dissected television form and content, spawning Newswipe and the annual Wipe reviews of the year. In 2008 he wrote Dead Set, a horror serial set in the Big Brother house. In 2011, with producer Annabel Jones, he launched Black Mirror on Channel 4; the series later moved to Netflix, where episodes such as "San Junipero" and the interactive film Bandersnatch won Emmy Awards and worldwide acclaim for their dark explorations of technology and society. Brooker also co-created the comedy detective spoof A Touch of Cloth and voices work on the Philomena Cunk programmes (Cunk on Earth) as writer and executive producer.

Personal life

Brooker is married to the television presenter Konnie Huq; they have two sons. His writing is characterised by bleak humour, misanthropy and an anxious fascination with media technology.
