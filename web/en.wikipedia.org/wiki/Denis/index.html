Denis is a masculine given name, the French form of the name Dennis, itself derived from the Latin Dionysius, from the Greek god Dionysus. The name became widespread in France and the Christian world through Saint Denis, the 3rd-century martyr and first bishop of Paris, who is the patron saint of France. The feminine form is Denise.

Saint Denis of Paris, the martyr from whom the name spread across Europe.

Saint Denis

Denis (in Latin, Dionysius) was a 3rd-century Christian martyr and saint. According to tradition, he was beheaded on Montmartre ("the mount of the martyr") in Paris and afterwards walked several miles carrying his head, preaching a sermon — one of the most famous cephalophore saints. The Basilica of Saint-Denis, built over his tomb, became the necropolis of the kings of France.

Usage

The name Denis is common in France, Ireland, and across the Francophone world, and appears in many languages. Variants include Denys, Deniz (Turkish, unisex, meaning "sea"), and Dennis (English). Notable bearers include the philosopher Denis Diderot and the actor Denis Leary.
