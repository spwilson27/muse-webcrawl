Marital property

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marital property is property acquired by either or both spouses during a marriage, which is subject to division on divorce or death under the matrimonial property regime of the relevant jurisdiction. It is contrasted with separate property — assets owned before marriage, or received during marriage by gift or inheritance.

Regimes in the United States

Nine U.S. states (including California, Texas, Louisiana, Arizona and Wisconsin) follow community property law, under which marital property is presumptively owned equally. The remaining states follow an equitable distribution system derived from English common law, in which courts divide marital property fairly — but not necessarily equally — considering factors such as the length of the marriage, contributions, and each spouse's economic circumstances.

Characterisation

Disputes commonly concern commingling of separate and marital funds, transmutation by written agreement, appreciation of separate assets due to marital effort, and the treatment of pensions, professional licences and businesses. Prenuptial agreements may alter the default rules.
