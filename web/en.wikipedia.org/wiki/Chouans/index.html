Chouans

The Chouans were royalist insurgents in Brittany, Maine and Lower Normandy who rose against the French Revolutionary government in the Chouannerie (1794–1800). Their name is said to derive from the call of the tawny owl (chat-huant) used as a signal, or from the nickname of an early leader, Jean Cottereau.

The rising

Driven by resistance to conscription, religious persecution of refractory clergy and local grievances, the Chouans waged guerrilla war alongside the larger Vendée revolt. Leaders included Georges Cadoudal, who later plotted against Napoleon.

Suppression

Republican columns under generals such as Lazare Hoche pacified the region through a mixture of force and conciliation; resistance flickered on into the early 1800s. Honoré de Balzac's novel Les Chouans (1829) fictionalised the conflict.
