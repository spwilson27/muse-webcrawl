Handlebody

In the mathematical field of geometric topology, a handlebody is a decomposition of a manifold into basic pieces. Handlebodies play an important role in low-dimensional topology — in particular in the theory of 3-manifolds — as well as in higher dimensions.

Definition

A handlebody of genus g in dimension n is obtained from a ball by attaching g 1-handles. In three dimensions, a genus-g handlebody is a regular neighbourhood of a bouquet of g circles embedded in the 3-sphere; its boundary is a closed orientable surface of genus g. A genus-0 handlebody is a ball, a genus-1 handlebody is a solid torus, and a genus-2 handlebody is a solid double torus.

Heegaard splittings

Every closed orientable 3-manifold can be split into two handlebodies glued along their common boundary surface — a Heegaard splitting. Handle decompositions more generally underlie Morse theory and the analysis of manifolds in all dimensions.
