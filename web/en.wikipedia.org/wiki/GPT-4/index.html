Generative Pre-trained Transformer 4 (GPT-4) is a multimodal large language model developed by the American artificial intelligence company OpenAI. Released on March 14, 2023, it is the fourth model in OpenAI's GPT series and can accept both text and image inputs and produce text outputs.

Capabilities

OpenAI reported that GPT-4 exhibits human-level performance on various professional and academic benchmarks. It passed a simulated bar exam with a score around the top 10% of test takers, and performed strongly on standardized tests including the SAT, GRE, and AP exams. Compared with earlier models, GPT-4 was described as more reliable, more creative, and better able to handle nuanced instructions and longer contexts, with context windows of 8,192 and 32,768 tokens offered at launch.

Training and alignment

GPT-4 was pre-trained on a large corpus of text and code, then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). OpenAI did not disclose the model's architecture details, parameter count, or training data, citing competitive and safety considerations. A technical report accompanying the release focused on evaluations and safety mitigations rather than implementation specifics.

Access and derivatives

GPT-4 was made available through ChatGPT Plus and an API waiting list, and was integrated into Microsoft products including Bing Chat (later Microsoft Copilot). Later variants included GPT-4 Turbo (2023), with a 128,000-token context window, and GPT-4o (2024), a natively multimodal version with faster audio and vision capabilities.

Criticism and limitations

Like other large language models, GPT-4 can produce plausible but incorrect statements ("hallucinations"), reflect biases in its training data, and its performance can vary unpredictably across tasks. Its closed development also drew criticism from researchers advocating open disclosure.
