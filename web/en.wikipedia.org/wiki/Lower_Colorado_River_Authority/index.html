Lower Colorado River Authority

The Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) is a nonprofit public utility created by the Texas Legislature in 1934. It manages the lower Colorado River of Texas, providing electricity, water and community services across a large part of Central and Southeast Texas.

History

LCRA was established in November 1934, during the Great Depression, to control floods, store water and generate hydroelectric power along the Colorado River. It completed a chain of dams forming the Texas Highland Lakes, including Buchanan Dam and Mansfield Dam, the latter impounding Lake Travis near Austin.

Operations

LCRA generates and transmits wholesale electricity to dozens of cities and electric cooperatives, operates hydroelectric plants at its dams, and also uses natural gas and coal generation and purchased power. It manages water supplies from the Highland Lakes for more than a million people, industry and agriculture, operates parks along the lakes and river, and supports community and economic development in its service area.

Governance

LCRA is governed by a 15-member board of directors appointed by the Governor of Texas with the advice and consent of the Texas Senate. It receives no state appropriations and is funded through its utility operations.
