Litany of the Saints

The Litany of the Saints (Latin: Litaniae Sanctorum) is a solemn prayer in the Roman Catholic Church and some other Christian traditions, consisting of a series of invocations of God, the Virgin Mary, angels and saints, each answered by a congregational response such as ora pro nobis ("pray for us"). It is among the oldest extant litanies, with roots in the early centuries of the Church and a form largely fixed by the time of Pope Gregory the Great (590–604).

Structure

The litany begins with the Kyrie eleison and invocations of the Holy Trinity, then calls upon Mary, the archangels Michael, Gabriel and Raphael, the apostles, martyrs, bishops, doctors of the Church, priests, religious and lay saints, in hierarchical order. A section of petitions ("From all evil, deliver us, O Lord") is followed by the Agnus Dei and concluding prayers.

Use

The Litany of the Saints is sung during the Easter Vigil — especially at baptisms — at ordinations, at the consecration of virgins and altars, in rogation processions, and at papal funerals and conclaves. It is also prescribed for the rite of exorcism. Local calendars may add patrons of the diocese or religious order.
