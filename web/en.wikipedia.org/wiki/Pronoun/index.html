Pronoun

A pronoun is a word or group of words that substitutes for a noun or noun phrase, usually referring to a participant in the discourse (such as the speaker or the person spoken about) or to someone or something already mentioned. Pronouns are a closed class in most languages and are among the most frequently used words.

Categories

Traditional categories include personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they), which vary by person, number, and case; demonstrative pronouns (this, that); interrogative pronouns (who, what); relative pronouns (who, which, that); indefinite pronouns (someone, anything); possessive pronouns (mine, yours); reflexive pronouns (myself, themselves); and reciprocal pronouns (each other).

Antecedents and reference

A pronoun typically refers to an antecedent — an earlier expression with which it is coreferential — though first- and second-person pronouns refer directly to discourse participants, and some pronouns are expletive or "dummy", as in "it is raining". The study of how pronouns are interpreted is central to syntax and semantics, including binding theory, which governs the distribution of reflexive and ordinary pronouns.

Across languages

Languages differ greatly in their pronoun systems: some encode clusivity (inclusive vs. exclusive "we"), social status (such as the T–V distinction between familiar and polite second-person forms), or grammatical gender; others, like Japanese, use open-class nouns in pronoun-like functions. Many languages permit pronoun dropping when reference is recoverable from verb agreement.
