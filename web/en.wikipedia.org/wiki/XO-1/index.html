XO-1 is a yellow dwarf star located approximately 560 light-years away from Earth in the constellation of Corona Borealis. The star has a magnitude of 11 and is not visible to the unaided eye, but can be seen through a small telescope. It is notable for hosting a transiting exoplanet, XO-1b, discovered by the XO Project.

Planetary system

In 2006, the XO Project, an international team of professional and amateur astronomers, discovered a Jupiter-sized exoplanet, designated XO-1b, orbiting the star. The planet was detected using the transit method: the star's light dims periodically as the planet passes in front of it. XO-1b is a hot Jupiter with an orbital period of about 3.94 days. Follow-up observations using the radial velocity method confirmed the planet's existence and allowed its mass to be measured, at roughly 0.9 times the mass of Jupiter.

The XO Project used an inexpensive telescope made from commercial camera lenses, demonstrating that modest equipment could contribute to exoplanet discovery. Amateur astronomers played an important role in the project by screening candidate light curves.
