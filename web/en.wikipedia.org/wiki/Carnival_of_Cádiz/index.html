The Carnival of Cádiz (Spanish: Carnaval de Cádiz) is one of the best-known carnivals in Spain and one of the most important in the world. Held in the Andalusian port city of Cádiz for around eleven days in February, it is famous above all for its satirical musical groups, whose witty, topical songs lampoon politicians and current events. It was declared a Festival of International Tourist Interest in 1980.

History

Cádiz's carnival grew from the city's trading links with Venice and Genoa in the 16th and 17th centuries, absorbing Italian traditions of masks and masquerades. Attempts were made to suppress it in various periods, most notably under Franco, when carnival was banned across Spain; in Cádiz it survived in semi-disguised form as the "Fiestas Típicas Gaditanas" from 1948, and recovered its name after the return of democracy.

The groups

The heart of the festival is the competition held in the Gran Teatro Falla (the COAC contest), in which groups compete in categories including:

  • Chirigotas — comic groups of about a dozen performers singing humorous, satirical songs in costume, the most popular category.
  • Comparsas — more serious, musically demanding groups with poetic, often political lyrics.
  • Coros — large choirs performing on decorated floats (bateas) in the street.
  • Cuartetos — small groups performing parodic theatre pieces.

Outside the official contest, unofficial street groups (agrupaciones callejeras or "illegales") perform on corners throughout the old town.
