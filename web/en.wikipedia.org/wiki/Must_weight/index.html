Must weight

Must weight is a measure of the amount of sugar in grape juice (must) and hence indicates the amount of alcohol that could be produced if the juice were fully fermented. It is an important indicator of grape ripeness and quality, and in Germany it forms the basis of the Prädikat classification of wines.

Measurement scales

Several scales are used around the world. The Oechsle scale, used in Germany, measures the density of the must relative to water: a must of 1.080 specific gravity has 80 degrees Oechsle. The Brix scale, widely used internationally, expresses sugar content approximately as a percentage by weight. The Baumé scale is common in France and Australia, and the Klosterneuburger Mostwaage (KMW or Babo) is used in Austria and Italy.

Use in winemaking

Winemakers monitor must weight during ripening to decide when to harvest: higher sugar levels mean riper fruit and potentially higher alcohol, though acid balance and flavour development also matter. Each scale can be converted to potential alcohol and to the other scales with standard tables.

Legal significance

German wine law assigns minimum must weights to quality levels from Kabinett through Spätlese and Auslese to Beerenauslese, Trockenbeerenauslese and Eiswein, which require progressively higher degrees Oechsle. Austrian law similarly uses KMW thresholds.
