Muziris (Malayalam: മുസിരിസ്; Greek: Μουζιρίς) was an ancient harbour and commercial centre on the Malabar Coast of southwestern India, near the mouth of the Periyar River in present-day Kerala. For several centuries around the turn of the Common Era it was the principal Indian port in the maritime trade between India and the Roman Empire.

Sources and trade

The anonymous 1st-century CE merchant's handbook, the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, names Muziris the first port of the kingdom of the Chera ruler "Keprobotos" (Keralaputra), crowded with ships from Arabia and the Greek world. Pliny the Elder complained that India drained the empire's gold — some 50 million sesterces a year — for luxuries; the great export was black pepper, for which Muziris and neighbouring Nelcynda shipped cargoes bought up-country, along with malabathrum, ivory, silk trans-shipped from the east, pearls and precious stones. Imports included Roman wine, olive oil, glass, coral, copper and vast quantities of gold and silver coin — hoards of Augustan and Tiberian aurei and denarii have been found across the Tamil and Kerala country.

The celebrated Vienna Papyrus preserves a 2nd-century contract for a loan on a single cargo from Muziris to Alexandria aboard the ship Hermapollon, valued after customs at some seven million sesterces. Tamil Sangam poems celebrate "Muciri," where the fine ships of the Yavanas (westerners) brought gold and left laden with pepper. The Peutinger Table even marks a "Temple of Augustus" in the Muziris area, implying a resident Roman trading community.

Location and decline

Muziris was long placed at Kodungallur (ancient Mahodayapuram), but excavations since 2006 at Pattanam, about 10 km south, have revealed a wharf, a dugout canoe, warehouse structures and dense deposits of Roman amphorae, terra sigillata, Rouletted ware and beads, leading many scholars to identify Pattanam with Muziris or its port complex. Tradition holds that a great flood in 1341 silted up the Periyar's mouth, shifting regional trade to the rising harbour of Kochi; the port had in any case declined with the waning of Roman trade after late antiquity. Kerala's Muziris Heritage Project now conserves the region's sites and museums.
