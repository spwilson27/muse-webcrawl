Dravidian peoples

The Dravidian peoples are an ethnolinguistic grouping native to South Asia who speak the Dravidian languages. There are around 250 million Dravidian speakers in the world. They form the majority of the population of South India, and are natively found in India, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Bangladesh, the Maldives, Nepal and Sri Lanka, with significant diaspora communities worldwide.

Languages

The most widely spoken Dravidian languages are Telugu (about 96 million speakers), Tamil (about 85 million), Kannada (about 59 million) and Malayalam (about 38 million); other major languages include Tulu, Gondi, Kurukh and Brahui — the last spoken far to the northwest in Baluchistan, Pakistan. Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam are the official languages of the southern Indian states of Andhra Pradesh/Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Kerala respectively, and have long literary traditions; Tamil is a classical language of India with a literature extending back over two thousand years, including the Sangam corpus.

Origins and history

The origins of the Dravidian-speaking peoples have been debated since the nineteenth century, when Robert Caldwell's comparative grammar established the Dravidian family as distinct from Indo-Aryan. Proposed connections between Dravidian languages and the language of the Indus Valley Civilisation remain unproven, as does the suggested Elamo-Dravidian relationship with Elamite of ancient Iran. Genetic and archaeological research suggests Dravidian speakers descend in large part from a mixture of ancient Iranian-related agriculturalists and indigenous South Asian hunter-gatherers (AASI).

Historically, Dravidian-speaking peoples built major kingdoms and empires, including the Tamilakam Sangam polities, the Chola, Chera and Pandya dynasties, the Satavahanas (debated), the Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Pallavas and the Vijayanagara Empire. In modern politics, Dravidian identity underpinned the Dravidian movement in Tamil Nadu — the Self-Respect Movement of Periyar and the Dravidian parties (DMK, AIADMK) that have governed the state since 1967 — and the linguistic reorganisation of Indian states in 1956.

Culture

Dravidian cultures are predominantly Hindu, with significant Christian, Muslim and Jain minorities. Distinctive cultural features include temple architecture in the Dravidian style (with towering gopurams), classical arts such as Bharatanatyam and Carnatic music, and, historically, matrilineal traditions among groups such as the Nairs of Kerala and the Tulu people.
