Asif Ali Zardari

Asif Ali Zardari

Born: 26 July 1955, Karachi

Office: President of Pakistan (2008–2013; 2024– )

Party: Pakistan Peoples Party

Spouse: Benazir Bhutto (m. 1987; died 2007)

Asif Ali Zardari (born 1955) is a Pakistani politician who has served as the 14th president of Pakistan since 2024, having previously held the office from 2008 to 2013. The widower of former prime minister Benazir Bhutto, he is co-chairman of the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) alongside his son Bilawal Bhutto Zardari. He is the first president in Pakistan's history to be elected to a second term.

Early life and marriage

Zardari was born into a wealthy Sindhi landowning family; his father Hakim Ali Zardari was a politician and businessman. In 1987 he married Benazir Bhutto, daughter of Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, in an arranged marriage that brought him into national politics. During Bhutto's two governments (1988–1990 and 1993–1996) he served as a minister, including as minister for environment and investment, and acquired the nickname "Mr Ten Percent" amid persistent — and contested — corruption allegations.

Imprisonment and exile

Zardari spent roughly eleven years in prison between 1990 and 2004 on charges including corruption and involvement in the murder of his brother-in-law Murtaza Bhutto; he was never definitively convicted, and cases were later dropped or overturned. Released in 2004, he lived in exile in Dubai and New York until the Bhuttos' return to Pakistan in 2007.

First presidency

After Benazir Bhutto's assassination in December 2007, Zardari became co-chairman of the PPP, led it to victory in the 2008 elections, and was elected president following Pervez Musharraf's resignation. His presidency saw the Eighteenth Amendment (2010), which transferred many presidential powers back to parliament. He completed a full five-year term — the first Pakistani president to do so — before returning to party politics. In March 2024, backed by a coalition government, he was elected president for a second time.
