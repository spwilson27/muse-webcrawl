Jay

Jays are a paraphyletic grouping of passerine birds in the crow family, Corvidae. They are medium-sized, often colourful and noisy birds, several of them noted for bright blue plumage, though Old World jays such as the Eurasian jay are pinkish-brown with blue wing patches.

Species

Familiar species include the blue jay of eastern North America, Steller's jay and California scrub-jay in the west, the Canada jay, the Eurasian jay, and the Siberian jay. Jays are not a single natural clade but a convenient grouping of corvid genera including Cyanocitta, Aphelocoma, Garrulus and Perisoreus.

Behaviour

Jays are intelligent, omnivorous woodland birds. Many cache acorns and other seeds, playing an important role in dispersing oaks. They are bold around humans, vocal mimics, and known for mobbing predators.
