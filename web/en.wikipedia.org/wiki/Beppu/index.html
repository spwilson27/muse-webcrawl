Beppu

Steam from hundreds of hot springs rises over Beppu, on the coast of Beppu Bay.

Beppu (別府市, Beppu-shi) is a city in Ōita Prefecture on the island of Kyushu, Japan. It is Japan's most famous hot spring resort: the city produces more hot spring water than anywhere else in the country and is home to well over two thousand individual springs and eight major hot spring districts, the Beppu Hatto. Steam vents across the city make its skyline instantly recognisable.

Hot springs and the Hells

The eight districts of Kannawa, Kamegawa, Shibaseki, Kankaiji, Hamawaki, Horita, Myoban and Beppu each have distinct waters used for bathing, therapy and cooking; the geothermally heated "hell steaming" (jigoku mushi) of seafood and vegetables is a local culinary tradition. Sand baths, in which bathers are buried in naturally heated sand, are offered at Takegawara Onsen and the Beppu Kaihin Sunayu.

The most spectacular springs are preserved for viewing rather than bathing as the Jigoku or "Hells of Beppu". They include Umi Jigoku ("sea hell"), a pond of boiling cobalt-blue water; Chinoike Jigoku ("blood pond hell"), with hot red clay waters; Tatsumaki Jigoku, a geyser; and Oniishibozu Jigoku, named for its grey mud bubbles resembling shaven monks' heads.

History and city

Beppu's springs were recorded in provincial gazetteers as early as the 8th century, and the town developed as a sanatorium — wounded soldiers recovered here during the war of the 1590s, and the United States military used the resort after the Second World War. The modern city was established in 1924. Tourism remains the mainstay of Beppu's economy, drawing millions of visitors a year, while Ritsumeikan Asia Pacific University on the hills above the city gives it an unusually international population. The Beppu–Ōita Mainichi Marathon is held annually between the two cities.
