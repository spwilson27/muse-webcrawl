James the Just

James the Just, or James, brother of the Lord (died AD 62 or 69), was a Jewish Christian leader and an important figure of the early Church in Jerusalem. He is traditionally identified as a brother of Jesus and the author of the Epistle of James in the New Testament.

Life

According to the Gospels, James was one of the brothers of Jesus who did not believe in him during his ministry, but after the resurrection — Paul reports that the risen Christ appeared to James (1 Corinthians 15:7) — he became leader of the Jerusalem church. In the Acts of the Apostles and Paul's letters, James presides over the Council of Jerusalem (c. AD 49), which decided that Gentile converts need not observe the whole of the Mosaic law. Paul calls James, along with Peter and John, a "pillar" of the church.

Death

The Jewish historian Josephus records that the high priest Ananus ben Ananus had James stoned to death in AD 62, an act that provoked protest from other Jews. Christian tradition, reported by Hegesippus, adds that James was first thrown from the pinnacle of the Temple.

Reputation

James was renowned for his piety and strict observance of the Law, earning the epithet "the Just" (Greek: ho dikaios). He is venerated as a saint in most Christian traditions.
