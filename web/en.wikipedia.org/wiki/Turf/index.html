Turf

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Turf may refer to several things related to grass or ground:

Sod and lawns

Turf commonly means sod — grass and the layer of soil beneath it held together by its roots — or a piece of this material, used to create an instant lawn or playing surface. Turf is grown commercially on turf farms, harvested in rolls or slabs, and laid over prepared soil. In Britain and Ireland, "turf" can also refer to peat cut from a bog and dried for use as fuel.

Sport

Turf is the grass surface of sports pitches and racecourses. In horse racing, "the turf" refers to the sport itself, and races run on grass are distinguished from those on dirt or synthetic surfaces. Artificial turf is a synthetic surface made to resemble natural grass, first widely known under the AstroTurf brand introduced in the 1960s, and now used in many stadiums with modern infill systems.

Other uses

  • In slang, the territory controlled by a gang or organisation.
  • In business, one's home market or area of responsibility.
  • Turf cutting and peat harvesting in Ireland, historically governed by turbary rights.
  • Turf Moor, the stadium of Burnley Football Club in England.
