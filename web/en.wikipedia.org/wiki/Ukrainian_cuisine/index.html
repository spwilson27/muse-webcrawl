Ukrainian cuisine

Ukrainian cuisine is the collection of cooking traditions of the Ukrainian people, shaped by fertile black earth (chernozem), a continental climate, and centuries of peasant agriculture. Its emblematic dish is borscht, the beet soup inscribed by UNESCO in 2022 on its list of intangible cultural heritage in need of urgent safeguarding.

Staples and dishes

Wheat and rye breads are central — Ukraine was long called the breadbasket of Europe. Soups include borscht in many regional forms, kapusniak (sauerkraut soup) and solyanka. Varenyky are filled dumplings (potato, cheese, cabbage, cherries); holubtsi are cabbage rolls; deruny are potato pancakes. Pork — especially cured salo (fatback) — garlic, dill, and sour cream recur throughout, along with kovbasa sausage and banosh cornmeal in the Carpathians.

Festive food

Christmas Eve features twelve meatless dishes including kutia (sweet wheat pudding); Easter brings paska bread and decorated pysanky eggs alongside horseradish and butter.
