Greywater

Greywater (also spelled graywater) is gently used wastewater from household sources such as bathroom sinks, showers, baths and washing machines. It excludes toilet waste, which is classed as blackwater, and in many definitions also excludes kitchen sink water because of its grease and food content. Greywater makes up the largest share of domestic wastewater volume.

Reuse

Because it is only lightly contaminated, greywater can be reused with little or no treatment for purposes that do not require drinking-quality water, such as subsurface garden irrigation and, after treatment, toilet flushing and laundry. Reuse reduces demand on freshwater supplies and the load on sewer systems, and is widely practised in water-scarce regions and promoted by green building standards. Risks include bacterial growth if greywater is stored for more than about a day, and the effects of detergents, salts and cleaning chemicals on soil and plants; for these reasons many jurisdictions regulate greywater systems through plumbing codes, and irrigation with untreated greywater is usually restricted to ornamental plants and subsurface application.
