Elative case

TypeGrammatical (locative) case
Meaning"Out of, from inside"
Example languagesFinnish, Estonian, Hungarian
Finnish suffix-sta / -stä

The elative case (from Latin efferre, "to bring out") is a grammatical case expressing motion out of, or origin from inside, a place or thing — the meaning "out of" or "from within". It forms part of the locative case systems of the Finnic and other Uralic languages.

In Finnish

In Finnish the elative is formed with the suffix -sta/-stä, chosen by vowel harmony: talosta "out of the house", koulusta "from school". It is one of three "internal" local cases, contrasting with the inessive ("in") and illative ("into"). Beyond literal motion, Finnish uses the elative for the material something is made of, the topic spoken about, the source of information, and the cause of an emotion, among other extended senses.

In other languages

Estonian has a cognate elative in -st. Hungarian expresses similar meanings with the suffix -ból/-ből. The term "elative" is also used in Arabic grammar for the comparative/superlative form of adjectives, an unrelated usage.
