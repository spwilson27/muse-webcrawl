Bodh Gaya is a city and religious site in the Gaya district of Bihar, India, famous as the location where Gautama Buddha is said to have attained enlightenment (Bodhi) while meditating under the Bodhi Tree. It is one of the four holy sites of Buddhism, alongside Lumbini, Sarnath and Kushinagar, and attracts pilgrims from around the world.

Mahabodhi Temple

The dominant structure is the Mahabodhi Temple, a stone pyramidal temple about 55 metres (180 ft) high. A first shrine was erected by the emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE; the present temple dates mainly from the 5th–6th centuries CE, in the late Gupta period, and was extensively restored in the 19th century under the direction of Alexander Cunningham and others. Behind the temple stands the Bodhi Tree, a sacred fig said to descend from the original tree. In 2002 the temple complex became a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Pilgrimage and monasteries

Monasteries and temples built by Buddhist communities from Tibet, Thailand, Japan, Myanmar, Sri Lanka and other countries surround the Mahabodhi complex, each in its national architectural style. An 80-foot statue of the Buddha, unveiled in 1989, is among the town's landmarks. The pilgrimage season runs mainly from October to March.
