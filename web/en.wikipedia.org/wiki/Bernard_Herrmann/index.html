Bernard Herrmann

Bernard Herrmann (born Maximillian Herman; 29 June 1911 – 24 December 1975) was an American composer and conductor best known for his work in film scoring. Over a career spanning four decades he wrote the music for Citizen Kane (1941), many films of Alfred Hitchcock — including Vertigo (1958), North by Northwest (1959) and Psycho (1960) — and Taxi Driver (1976). He won the Academy Award for The Devil and Daniel Webster (1941) and is widely regarded as one of the greatest film composers of all time.

Career

Herrmann studied at New York University and Juilliard, championed the music of Charles Ives, and became a staff conductor and composer for CBS radio, where he worked with Orson Welles on the Mercury Theatre broadcasts. He followed Welles to Hollywood for Citizen Kane and The Magnificent Ambersons. From the 1950s his collaboration with Hitchcock produced scores built on short, repeating motifs and striking orchestration — famously the shrieking strings of the Psycho shower scene, scored for string orchestra alone. The partnership ended after a dispute over Torn Curtain (1966).

Herrmann also scored science-fiction classics such as The Day the Earth Stood Still (1951, notable for its use of theremins) and Ray Harryhausen fantasy films, composed for television's The Twilight Zone, and wrote concert works including the cantata Moby Dick and the opera Wuthering Heights. He died hours after completing the recording sessions for Martin Scorsese's Taxi Driver, which was dedicated to his memory and earned him a posthumous Oscar nomination.
