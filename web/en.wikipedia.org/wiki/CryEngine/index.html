CryEngine

CryEngine (stylized as CRYENGINE) is a game engine designed by the German game developer Crytek. It has been used in all of their titles, with initial versions supporting the Far Cry and Crysis series, and continues to be updated to support new consoles and hardware. It has also been used by many third-party developers under Crytek's licensing scheme.

CryEngine
Developer: Crytek
Initial release: May 2, 2002
Written in: C++, Lua, C#
Platform: Windows, Linux, PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch
License: Proprietary (royalty-based)

History

CryEngine began as a technology demo ("X-Isle") shown to Nvidia, and was then developed into the engine behind Far Cry (2004). CryEngine 2 powered Crysis (2007), famed for pushing PC hardware. CryEngine 3 (2009) brought the engine to consoles and introduced the "Sandbox" real-time editor workflow and "What You See Is What You Play" philosophy. In 2016, CryEngine V was released with a "pay what you want" model, later changing to a royalty model. Amazon's Lumberyard engine was originally derived from CryEngine code licensed from Crytek.

Features

The engine is known for large outdoor environments, advanced vegetation and water rendering, dynamic lighting and physically based rendering, the Sandbox level editor, and integrated tools for animation, AI and audio. Notable games built on CryEngine include Crysis, Far Cry (original), Kingdom Come: Deliverance, Star Citizen (heavily modified), Hunt: Showdown and Prey (2017).
