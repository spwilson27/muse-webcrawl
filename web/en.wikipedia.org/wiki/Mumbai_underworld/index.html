Mumbai underworld

The Mumbai underworld refers to the organized crime networks, historically rooted in the city's docks and smuggling trade, that have operated in Mumbai (formerly Bombay), India. Its syndicates have been involved in smuggling, extortion, narcotics, illegal finance (hawala), and contract killing, and its figures have become staples of Indian popular culture.

History

From the 1960s, smugglers such as Haji Mastan, Yusuf Patel, and Varadarajan Mudaliar ("Varda Bhai") built empires moving gold, silver, and electronics around import restrictions. In the 1970s and 1980s, protégés including Dawood Ibrahim — whose D-Company rose from the city's gang wars — eclipsed the older dons. Dawood's organization was implicated in the 1993 Bombay bombings, after which he fled India and was designated a global terrorist; the gang wars of the 1990s among D-Company, Chhota Rajan's outfit, and the Arun Gawli and Amar Naik gangs drew intense police "encounter" campaigns that broke the gangs' street power.

In popular culture

The underworld has inspired a rich filmography — from Deewaar (1975) through Ram Gopal Varma's Satya (1998) and Company (2002) — as well as books such as Dongri to Dubai and Sacred Games.
