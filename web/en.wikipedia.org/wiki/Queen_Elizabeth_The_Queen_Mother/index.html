Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother (born Elizabeth Angela Marguerite Bowes-Lyon; 4 August 1900 – 30 March 2002) was Queen of the United Kingdom and the Dominions of the British Commonwealth from 11 December 1936 to 6 February 1952 as the wife of King George VI. She was concurrently the last Empress of India (1936–1947). After her husband died, she was officially known as Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother to avoid confusion with her daughter Queen Elizabeth II.

Early life and marriage

The youngest daughter of the Earl of Strathmore and Kinghorne, she grew up at Glamis Castle in Scotland. In 1923, after twice refusing him, she married Prince Albert, Duke of York ("Bertie"), the second son of George V. Their two daughters, Elizabeth (born 1926) and Margaret (born 1930), were born into a life expected to remain outside the direct line of succession — until the abdication of Edward VIII in December 1936 thrust Albert onto the throne as George VI.

Second World War

During the Blitz she refused to leave London or send her daughters abroad, declaring that the children would not leave without her, she would not leave without the King, and "the King will never leave". She visited bombed districts of the East End extensively; when Buckingham Palace itself was bombed in 1940 she remarked that she could now "look the East End in the face". Her morale-boosting presence made her, in Adolf Hitler's reported phrase, "the most dangerous woman in Europe".

Widowhood and longevity

Widowed at 51, she remained one of the most active and popular members of the royal family for another fifty years, carrying out hundreds of engagements, celebrated for her wit, her love of horse racing (she owned successful steeplechasers), and her longevity. She died peacefully in her sleep at the Royal Lodge, Windsor, on 30 March 2002, aged 101 — at the time the longest-lived member of the British royal family in history. An estimated 200,000 people filed past her coffin as she lay in state in Westminster Hall, and she was interred beside George VI in St George's Chapel, Windsor.
