Islamic State – West Africa Province

Islamic State – West Africa Province (ISWAP)
Active: 2015–present
Ideology: Salafi jihadism
Leaders: Abu Musab al-Barnawi (former); various commanders
Area of operations: Northeast Nigeria, Lake Chad Basin, Niger, Chad, Cameroon
Origin: Splinter of Boko Haram

The Islamic State – West Africa Province (ISWAP) is a militant Islamist group active in northeastern Nigeria and the Lake Chad Basin. It emerged from Boko Haram after its leader Abubakar Shekau pledged allegiance to the Islamic State (IS) in March 2015, whereupon the group was renamed the Islamic State's West Africa Province.

Split from Shekau

In 2016, IS leadership appointed Abu Musab al-Barnawi, a son of Boko Haram's founder Mohammed Yusuf, as the province's governor, objecting to Shekau's indiscriminate killing of Muslim civilians. Shekau rejected the appointment, splitting the movement: his faction reverted to the name Jama'at Ahl al-Sunna li al-Da'wa wa al-Jihad (JAS, widely called Boko Haram), while the IS-recognized faction under al-Barnawi became ISWAP.

Activities

ISWAP primarily targeted military and government personnel rather than civilians, and built governance structures in areas around Lake Chad, collecting taxes and providing some services in an effort to win local support. It carried out large attacks on military bases in Borno and Yobe states, including the assaults on Metele (2018) and numerous others, and fought alongside and against rival factions.

After Shekau died in May 2021 during an ISWAP offensive on his Sambisa Forest stronghold, ISWAP attempted to absorb his fighters and expand its control. ISWAP itself has suffered internal leadership disputes and the deaths of successive commanders, including al-Barnawi in 2021, and heavy losses to Nigerian military operations supported by the Multinational Joint Task Force.
