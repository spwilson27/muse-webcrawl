Neon sign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A neon sign is an electric sign made from long, luminous gas-discharge tubes that are bent into shapes and letters. The tubes contain rarefied neon or other noble gases; when a high-voltage current passes through the gas, it glows. Pure neon produces a fiery red-orange light; other colours are obtained with argon, mercury vapour, phosphor coatings, and tinted glass.

History

Neon gas was discovered in 1898 by William Ramsay and Morris Travers. The French engineer Georges Muse demonstrated the first commercial neon lamp in Paris in 1910, and his company sold the first neon advertising signs soon after; the first in the United States appeared in 1923 at a Los Angeles car dealership. Neon spread rapidly through American roadside culture — motels, diners, theatres, and the Las Vegas Strip — and through the streetscapes of cities such as Hong Kong and Tokyo. Use declined after the 1960s with the rise of plastic and fluorescent signage, but neon has enjoyed revivals as a retro and artistic medium.

Construction

Signs are hand-made by glassblowers ("tube benders") who heat glass tubing over gas burners, bend it to a pattern, fuse in electrodes, evacuate the tube, fill it with gas, and seal it. A transformer supplies several thousand volts. Craft neon workshops declined in number in the late 20th century, and LED "neon flex" products now imitate the classic look with lower energy use and greater durability, though enthusiasts maintain that they lack the depth and warmth of true gas-discharge tubes.

Cultural significance

Neon signage is strongly associated with mid-20th-century urban Americana, film noir, and nightlife districts worldwide. Preservation groups such as the Museum of Neon Art (founded 1981 in Los Angeles) and Hong Kong heritage advocates work to document and save historic signs as they are removed.
