Orange S.A. (French pronunciation: [ɔʁɑ̃ʒ]), formerly France Télécom S.A., is a French multinational telecommunications corporation. It has about 266 million customers worldwide and employs around 127,000 people (in 2022). The company is the largest provider of telecommunications services in France and one of the largest mobile network operators in Europe and Africa. It is a component of the Euro Stoxx 50 stock market index. Orange has been the company's main brand for mobile, landline, internet and internet protocol television (IPTV) services since 2006. It originated in 1994 as a British mobile network operator — the Orange brand was created by Hutchison and launched in the United Kingdom — and was acquired by France Télécom in 2000. On 1 July 2013 the whole group adopted the Orange name.

History

The company's origins lie in the French state telegraph administration of the French Revolution era and the government telecommunications directorate (DGT) of the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications. France Télécom was created in 1988 as a public operator and partially privatised in 1997–98. In 2000 it acquired the UK mobile operator Orange plc from Vodafone for about €40 billion, and also bought a controlling stake in the internet portal Wanadoo. Stéphane Richard became chief executive in 2011; Christel Heydemann succeeded him as CEO in April 2022. The French state remains a significant shareholder, holding roughly 23% of the capital.

Operations

Orange operates consumer and business services in France, Spain, Poland, Belgium, Romania and other European markets, and has a major presence in Africa and the Middle East (including Egypt, Morocco, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Jordan), where Orange Money is a leading mobile financial service. Its enterprise arm, Orange Business, provides cloud, cybersecurity and network services to companies worldwide. Orange is also a major operator of submarine cables and wholesale carrier services.
