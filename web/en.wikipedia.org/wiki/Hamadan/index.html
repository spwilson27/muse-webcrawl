Hamadan (Persian: همدان, pronounced [hæmeˈdɒːn]) is a city in western Iran, the capital of Hamadan Province. It lies in a green mountainous area in the foothills of the Alvand mountain range, about 360 kilometres southwest of Tehran, at an elevation of 1,850 metres. Hamadan is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Iran and is identified with ancient Ecbatana, the capital of the Median Empire.

History

According to tradition, Ecbatana was founded as the Median capital in the late 8th century BCE. After Cyrus the Great conquered Media in 550 BCE, the city became a summer residence of the Achaemenid kings, valued for its cool climate. It later passed through Seleucid, Parthian and Sasanian hands, was captured by Arab armies in the 7th century CE, and was repeatedly fought over in the medieval period. Hamadan was the home of the great physician and philosopher Avicenna (Ibn Sina), who died there in 1037, and of the poet Baba Tahir.

Sights

Principal attractions include the Mausoleum of Avicenna, built in 1952 with a tower inspired by the Gonbad-e Qabus; the tomb of Baba Tahir; the Ganjnameh inscriptions of Darius I and Xerxes I carved in rock near the city; the Stone Lion (Shir-e Sangi), a Hellenistic-era statue; and the tombs traditionally associated with Esther and Mordechai, a pilgrimage site for Iranian Jews.

Climate and economy

Hamadan has a continental climate with cold, snowy winters and mild summers, making it a summer resort. The city is known for leather goods, ceramics from nearby Lalejin, carpets, and agriculture in the surrounding plain.
