Dewsbury Minster

Dewsbury Minster
Location: Dewsbury, West Yorkshire, England
Denomination: Church of England
Dedication: All Saints
Diocese: Leeds

Dewsbury Minster, the Minster Church of All Saints, is the parish church in Dewsbury, West Yorkshire, England. There has been a church on the site since the 8th century, and the present building is a Grade II* listed building, largely rebuilt in the 19th century.

History

Tradition associates the site with Paulinus, who is said to have preached in the area in around AD 627, and a cross base in the churchyard is sometimes linked with this early preaching. Dewsbury was an important mother church in the Saxon period, and fragments of Anglo-Saxon carved stones are preserved in the heritage centre within the minster.

The church was largely rebuilt between 1895 and 1911 in the Gothic Revival style. In 1949 the vicar, Canon Hugh Jackson, founded a community dedicated to work among young people, and the minster became known for its social outreach. The church was designated a minster in 1994.

Heritage centre

The minster houses a heritage centre displaying Anglo-Saxon sculpture, including a portion of a 9th-century cross shaft, and exhibits on the history of Christianity in the region.
