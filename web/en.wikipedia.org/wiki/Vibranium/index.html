Vibranium

Vibranium is a fictional metal appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. It is most commonly depicted as having been mined in the fictional African nation of Wakanda, and it is famous as the material composing Captain America's shield and Black Panther's suit. The metal first appeared in Daredevil #13 (February 1966), written by Stan Lee and drawn by John Romita Sr.

Properties

Vibranium is portrayed as an extraordinarily durable substance that absorbs, stores, and releases large amounts of kinetic energy. Wakandan vibranium can absorb sound waves and vibrations, making objects made from it effectively indestructible by conventional means; it is also depicted as mutating living things exposed to it and as powering Wakanda's advanced technology. A second variety, Antarctic vibranium or "anti-metal", emits vibrations that liquefy other metals.

In other media

Vibranium is a central element of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, introduced in Captain America: The First Avenger (2011), in which Howard Stark describes it as the strongest substance on Earth, and explored further in Black Panther (2018), which depicts Wakanda's vibranium meteor and the technology built upon it.

Cultural impact

The metal has entered popular discussions of fictional materials and is frequently compared with real rare-earth and meteoritic metals. In 2016, scientists named features of real materials research with reference to it, and the fictional element is often used as a benchmark in speculative materials science.
