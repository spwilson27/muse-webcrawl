Rasgulla

Rasgulla is a syrupy dessert popular in the eastern part of the Indian subcontinent. It is made from ball-shaped dumplings of chhena (an Indian cottage cheese) and semolina dough, cooked in a light sugar syrup. The dish is characterized by its spongy, melt-in-the-mouth texture.

CourseDessert
RegionIndian subcontinent (Odisha / West Bengal)
Main ingredientsChhena, sugar syrup
VariationsRasmalai, rajbhog, khiramohana

Preparation

Fresh chhena is kneaded with a small amount of semolina until smooth, then rolled into small balls. The balls are dropped into boiling light sugar syrup, where they expand to roughly double their size as they cook. The finished rasgullas are served soaked in the syrup, sometimes flavoured with rose water or cardamom.

Origin dispute

The origin of rasgulla has long been contested between the states of Odisha and West Bengal. Odisha tradition links a similar offering, khiramohana, to the Jagannath Temple at Puri, while Bengal credits confectioner Nobin Chandra Das of Kolkata with popularizing the modern spongy version in the 19th century. In 2017 West Bengal received a Geographical Indication tag for "Banglar Rasogolla", and in 2019 Odisha received a GI tag for "Odisha Rasagola", recognizing the two as distinct regional variants.

Variations

Related dishes include rasmalai (flattened chhena discs in sweetened milk), rajbhog (larger balls with a nut filling), and kamalabhog. Canned rasgulla, industrialized in the 20th century, made the sweet widely available across India and abroad.
