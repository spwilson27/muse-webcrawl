Olaf II of Norway

Reign1015–1028
Bornc. 995
Died29 July 1030, Stiklestad
Feast day29 July

Olaf II Haraldsson (c. 995 – 29 July 1030), later known as Saint Olaf, was King of Norway from 1015 to 1028. A former Viking raider, he is credited with consolidating royal power in Norway and advancing the Christianization of the country.

Reign and death

Olaf returned to Norway in 1015 and was acclaimed king, defeating rival earls and unifying much of the kingdom. His harsh enforcement of Christianity and taxation alienated powerful chieftains, who allied with Cnut the Great; Olaf was driven into exile in 1028. Attempting to reclaim the throne, he was killed at the Battle of Stiklestad in 1030.

Veneration

Within a year of his death, miracles were reported at his tomb in Nidaros (Trondheim), and he was canonized locally in 1031. Saint Olaf became the patron saint of Norway, and his feast day, Olsok, is still observed.
