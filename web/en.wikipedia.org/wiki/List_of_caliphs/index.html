List of caliphs

A caliph is the chief Muslim religious and political leader, regarded as the successor (khalīfa) of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. This article lists the caliphs of the successive caliphates from 632 until the abolition of the Ottoman Caliphate in 1924, together with rival and regional claimants.

Rashidun Caliphate (632–661)

The first four caliphs — Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali — are known to Sunni Muslims as the Rashidun, the "Rightly Guided" caliphs. Their rule saw the initial expansion of Islam beyond Arabia into Persia, Egypt, and the Levant.

Umayyad Caliphate (661–750)

Mu'awiya I founded the hereditary Umayyad dynasty, ruling from Damascus. Fourteen Umayyad caliphs followed, including Abd al-Malik, builder of the Dome of the Rock, and Umar II, noted for piety. A branch survived in al-Andalus as the Caliphate of Córdoba (929–1031).

Abbasid Caliphate (750–1258; Cairo 1261–1517)

The Abbasids ruled from Baghdad, reaching their height under Harun al-Rashid. After the Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258, a shadow line continued in Mamluk Cairo until the Ottoman conquest of Egypt in 1517.

Other caliphates

The Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171) ruled from Cairo and claimed descent from Fatima. The Ottoman sultans assumed the caliphal title, used prominently from the eighteenth century until the Turkish Republic abolished the office on 3 March 1924; the last caliph was Abdulmejid II.
