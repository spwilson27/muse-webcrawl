Encore

Placeholder image: an artist returning for an encore

An encore is an additional performance given by performers at the end of a concert or other performance in response to prolonged applause from the audience. The word comes from the French encore [ɑ̃kɔʁ], meaning "again" or "still", which audiences shout to request a repeat or extra piece.

Practice

In classical music, soloists and orchestras traditionally offer a short, often virtuosic or beloved piece after the printed programme has concluded; famous encore pieces include works by Kreisler, Paganini caprices, and short piano pieces. Historically, audiences in the 18th and 19th centuries might demand the immediate repetition of a movement or aria, and composers such as Mozart expected such repeats. In rock and popular music concerts, the encore has become a ritualised set-piece: the band leaves the stage, house lights stay down amid chanting, and the performers return for a final set of songs, often including their biggest hits.

Conventions

Because encores are now expected, they are usually planned in advance even though presented as spontaneous. Some performers, notably in opera and jazz, may genuinely improvise them; a few artists are known for refusing encores. The term is also used metaphorically, and in broadcasting an "encore presentation" denotes a repeat showing of a programme.
