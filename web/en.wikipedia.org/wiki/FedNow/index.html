FedNow

The FedNow Service is an instant payment system developed by the United States Federal Reserve for depository institutions in the United States. Launched on 20 July 2023, it allows individuals and businesses to send and receive payments within seconds, at any time of day, every day of the year, with funds immediately available to the recipient.

Background

Before FedNow, most US bank transfers relied on the ACH network, which settles in batches and can take one to three business days. The private sector launched the RTP network, operated by The Clearing House, in 2017. In 2019 the Federal Reserve announced it would build a competing public system to ensure nationwide reach, particularly for smaller banks and credit unions, and FedNow went live in July 2023 with an initial group of participating institutions that has since grown to well over a thousand.

Operation

FedNow settles payments individually in central bank money between accounts held at the Federal Reserve, operating 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including holidays. Transaction limits have been raised progressively from an initial default of $100,000. The service supports credit transfers and requests for payment, and passes funds through ISO 20022 messaging. Because payments are instant and irrevocable, participating banks employ fraud screening and allow customers to set limits.
