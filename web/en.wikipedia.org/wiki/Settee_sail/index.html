Settee sail

A settee sail on a Mediterranean coasting vessel; note the very short forward luff.

A settee sail is a fore-and-aft quadrilateral sail on a long yard at a low angle to the mast. It is a development of the lateen sail: whereas a lateen is triangular, the settee has a short luff (forward edge) at the bottom, giving the sail a four-sided shape with a short head along the lower forward part of the yard. The settee rig avoids the difficulty of the lateen yard pressing against the mast on one tack and does not require the yard to be swung around the mast when tacking.

Use

The settee was widely used by coasting and fishing vessels in the Mediterranean, including Maltese and Greek craft, and further afield in the Indian Ocean in association with lateen-rigged traditions. Settee-rigged vessels include some types of xebec-like coasters and the Maltese speronara and Gozo boat. The rig was well suited to working to windward in the variable winds of enclosed seas with small crews.
