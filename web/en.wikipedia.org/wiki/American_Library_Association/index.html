American Library Association

The American Library Association (ALA) is a nonprofit organization based in the United States that promotes libraries and library education internationally. Founded on October 6, 1876, during the Centennial Exposition in Philadelphia, it is the oldest and largest library association in the world, with tens of thousands of members.

History

The ALA was founded by Justin Winsor, Charles Ammi Cutter, Samuel S. Green, James L. Whitney, Melvil Dewey, Fred B. Perkins, and Thomas W. Bicknell, among others, with the aim of enabling librarians "to do their present work more easily and more cheaply". It received its charter from the Commonwealth of Massachusetts in 1879. The association's headquarters moved to Chicago in the early twentieth century.

Activities

The ALA accredits library and information science programs, advocates for library funding and intellectual freedom, and publishes standards and journals including Booklist and American Libraries. It presents prestigious literary awards such as the Newbery Medal, Caldecott Medal, and Printz Award for children's and young adult books. Through its Office for Intellectual Freedom it documents censorship attempts and sponsors Banned Books Week each autumn.

Organization

The association operates through divisions (such as the Public Library Association and the Association of College and Research Libraries), round tables, and state chapters, and holds annual and midwinter conferences drawing librarians from around the world.
