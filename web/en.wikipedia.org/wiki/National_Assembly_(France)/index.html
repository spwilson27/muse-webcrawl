National Assembly (France)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The National Assembly is the lower house of the French Parliament, sitting at the Palais Bourbon in Paris. Its 577 deputies are elected in single-member constituencies by the two-round system for five-year terms (subject to dissolution by the President). Together with the Senate it enacts legislation, but the Fifth Republic's "rationalised parliamentarism" favours the government and, between the two chambers, gives the Assembly the last word on most bills — and sole power to dismiss the government by censure.

Position in the Fifth Republic

The Assembly's history tracks presidential power: for most of the Fifth Republic the presidential majority controlled it (the "fait majoritaire"), making it an amplifier of the Élysée; the 2000 shortening of the presidential term to five years and the electoral calendar entrenched this synchrony. Cohabitation (1986–88, 1993–95, 1997–2002) inverted the relationship, and the hung Assembly produced by the 2024 snap election — no bloc near a majority — revived government instability, censure and budget-by-decree struggles not seen for decades, testing the system's rationalised machinery (Article 49.3 passage without a vote, itself a censure trigger) under genuine parliamentary pluralism.
