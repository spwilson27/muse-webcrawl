The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker is an action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo for the GameCube, released in Japan in 2002 and worldwide in 2003. Set on a vast ocean dotted with islands — the flooded remnant of Hyrule — it is notable for its cel-shaded cartoon graphics and expressive "Toon Link" character design.

Story and gameplay

Centuries after the gods drowned Hyrule to seal away Ganondorf, a young boy named Link sets sail from Outset Island when the monstrous Helmaroc King abducts his sister Aryll. Aboard the talking boat the King of Red Lions, Link traverses the Great Sea by conducting the winds with the Wind Waker baton, recovers the shards of the Triforce of Courage, awakens the pirate Tetra as Princess Zelda, and confronts Ganondorf beneath the waves in sunken Hyrule Castle.

Reception and legacy

The art style initially divided fans expecting a realistic Zelda, but the game was a critical triumph, praised for its charm, animation, music and seafaring world; it sold over four million copies. Its visual approach and characters became fixtures of the series, and an HD remaster for Wii U (2013) streamlined the late-game Triforce quest.
