Democracy vouchers
Type: Campaign finance reform
First adopted: Seattle, Washington
Related: Campaign finance, Public financing

Democracy vouchers are a method of public campaign financing in which eligible residents receive publicly funded vouchers they can assign to participating political candidates. The aim is to broaden political participation and reduce reliance on large private donors.

Seattle program

Seattle voters approved the Honest Elections Seattle initiative in 2015. Eligible residents receive vouchers — in recent cycles four $25 vouchers — to give to candidates for city offices who accept spending limits and qualifying requirements. The program is funded by a property tax levy.

Rationale and criticism

Supporters say vouchers amplify small donors and encourage candidates to seek broad community support. Critics question cost, administrative complexity, unequal use across neighborhoods, and whether vouchers change electoral outcomes.

Other proposals

Similar ideas have been proposed at state and federal levels in the United States, sometimes under names such as democracy dollars. See Seattle.
