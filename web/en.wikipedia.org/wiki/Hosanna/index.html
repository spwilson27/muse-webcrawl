Hosanna

"Hosanna" is cried during Palm Sunday processions, recalling the entry into Jerusalem.

Hosanna is a liturgical word used in both Judaism and Christianity. It derives from the Hebrew hoshia-na (הושיעה־נא), "save, we pray", an appeal found in Psalm 118:25. In Jewish worship it is associated with the Hallel prayers and the festival of Sukkot, from which the seventh day takes the name Hoshana Rabbah. In Christian usage it became an acclamation of praise, echoing the cries of the crowd at Jesus's triumphal entry into Jerusalem: "Hosanna to the Son of David!" (Matthew 21:9).

Usage

In Judaism, hosannas are sung during Sukkot while worshippers circle the synagogue holding the lulav and etrog, with willow branches beaten on Hoshana Rabbah. In Christianity the word appears in the Sanctus of the Mass ("Hosanna in the highest"), in Palm Sunday liturgies, and in hymns and Christmas traditions.

Meaning

Although originally a plea for salvation, through its use in the Gospels and liturgy "hosanna" acquired the character of a shout of joyful praise and acclamation, comparable to "hallelujah".
