Fairhair dynasty

The Fairhair dynasty (Norwegian: Hårfagreætten) was a family of Norwegian kings founded by Harald I Fairhair, who according to the sagas unified Norway into a single kingdom around 872 after the Battle of Hafrsfjord. The dynasty is traditionally regarded as the first royal house of a united Norway, and its members ruled, with interruptions, until the 14th century.

Origins

According to the Icelandic sagas, Harald Fairhair was a son of Halfdan the Black of the Yngling dynasty. After his victory at Hafrsfjord, Harald became the first king to rule over most of coastal Norway. The historicity of the details in the saga accounts is debated by modern historians, who note that the unification was likely a gradual process rather than a single event.

Rulers

Harald was succeeded by his sons Eric Bloodaxe and Haakon the Good. Later notable members include Olaf Tryggvason, Olaf Haraldsson (Saint Olaf), Magnus the Good, Harald Hardrada, and the kings who ruled through the civil war era of the 12th and early 13th centuries, ending with Haakon IV. The male line of the dynasty is generally considered to have died out with Haakon V in 1319, after which Norway passed through marriage into unions with Sweden and Denmark.

Legacy

The Fairhair dynasty occupies a central place in Norwegian national identity. Harald's unification of Norway is commemorated at Haraldshaugen near Haugesund, and the dynasty's kings feature prominently in Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla.
