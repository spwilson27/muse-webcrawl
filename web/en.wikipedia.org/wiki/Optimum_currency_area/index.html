Optimum currency area

An optimum currency area (OCA) or optimal currency region is a geographic region in which it would maximise economic efficiency to have the entire region share a single currency. The theory, pioneered by economist Robert Mundell in a 1961 paper, weighs the efficiency gains of a common currency — eliminated transaction costs and exchange-rate uncertainty — against the loss of each country's independent monetary policy and exchange-rate adjustment.

Criteria

Subsequent work by Ronald McKinnon, Peter Kenen and others identified conditions under which a currency union works well:

  • Labour mobility across the region, so workers can move from depressed areas to booming ones;
  • Capital mobility and price and wage flexibility;
  • Risk sharing, such as fiscal transfer systems that cushion asymmetric shocks;
  • Similar business cycles and economic openness and diversification.

Application

The theory framed debates over European monetary union: the eurozone satisfies some criteria (goods and capital mobility) but has limited labour mobility and no large central fiscal transfer mechanism, weaknesses exposed by the euro area crisis after 2009. The United States is often cited as a more complete OCA because of its fiscal union and integrated labour market.
