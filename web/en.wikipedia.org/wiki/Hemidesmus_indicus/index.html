Hemidesmus indicus


Hemidesmus indicus
Family: Apocynaceae
Common names: Indian sarsaparilla, anantamul, nannari

Hemidesmus indicus, commonly known as Indian sarsaparilla (Hindi: अनंतमूल, anantamul), is a species of climbing plant in the family Apocynaceae found in South Asia. It is a slender, twining vine with opposite, narrow leaves and small greenish-purple flowers, occurring in open scrub and forest across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka.

Uses

The aromatic roots have a long history of use in Ayurvedic medicine, where the drug is known as sariva or anantamul and is prescribed for a wide range of conditions including skin diseases, urinary disorders, fever and as a general tonic and blood purifier. In southern India a cooling drink, nannari sherbet, is made from the roots, and root extracts are used to flavour beverages, in a similar role to true sarsaparilla (Smilax species). The plant was historically exported to Europe as "Indian sarsaparilla" and used as an adulterant or substitute for American sarsaparilla.

Chemistry

The roots contain coumarins, flavonoids, triterpenes and pregnane glycosides; the characteristic sweet odour is largely due to 2-hydroxy-4-methoxybenzaldehyde. Pharmacological studies have reported antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial activity, though clinical evidence for traditional uses remains limited.
