The Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway (Chinese: 京沪高速铁路; pinyin: Jīnghù Gāosù Tiělù) is a high-speed railway that connects two major economic zones in the People's Republic of China: the Bohai Economic Rim and the Yangtze River Delta. Construction began on 18 April 2008, and the line entered commercial service on 30 June 2011. At 1,318 km (819 mi) long, it is the world's longest high-speed line ever constructed in a single phase.

The line is one of the busiest high-speed railways in the world, carrying well over 100 million passengers per year. Trains on the line have operated at a top commercial speed of 350 km/h (217 mph), reducing the journey between Beijing and Shanghai to about four and a half hours.

History

The conventional Beijing–Shanghai railway had long been saturated, carrying a disproportionate share of China's rail traffic on a small fraction of the network's total track length. After more than a decade of debate over whether to build a conventional high-speed line or a maglev, the State Council approved a wheel-rail high-speed line in 2006. Construction officially began in April 2008, and a test run in December 2010 set a world record speed of 486.1 km/h for an unmodified commercial train.

Route

The line runs from Beijing South railway station to Shanghai Hongqiao railway station, passing through Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Anhui and Jiangsu provinces. It has 24 stations in total. Much of the line is elevated on viaducts, including the Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge, the longest bridge in the world.

Rolling stock

Services are operated by CRH380A and CRH380B trainsets, as well as newer Fuxing CR400AF and CR400BF trains, in 8-car and 16-car formations. Travel times for the fastest trains are around 4 hours 18 minutes, with stops only at major intermediate stations such as Nanjing South.
