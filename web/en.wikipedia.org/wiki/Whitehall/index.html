Whitehall is a road and area in the City of Westminster, Central London, forming the first part of the A3212 from Trafalgar Square to Parliament Square. It is the centre of the Government of the United Kingdom: most government departments have headquarters in or near the street, and "Whitehall" is used as a metonym for the British civil service and central government administration.

History

The name comes from the Palace of Whitehall, the main residence of English monarchs from 1530 until it was largely destroyed by fire in 1698; only Inigo Jones's Banqueting House survives, outside which Charles I was executed in 1649. Downing Street, home of the Prime Minister (No. 10) and the Chancellor of the Exchequer (No. 11), branches off Whitehall. The Cenotaph, the national war memorial designed by Edwin Lutyens, stands in the middle of the road and is the focus of the annual Remembrance Sunday service.

Government buildings

Buildings on or near Whitehall house the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, HM Treasury, the Ministry of Defence, the Cabinet Office, the Department of Health and Social Care, and Horse Guards.
