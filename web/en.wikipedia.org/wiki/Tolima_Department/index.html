Tolima is one of the 32 departments of Colombia, located in the central-western Andean region of the country. Its capital is Ibagué, known as the "Musical City of Colombia."

Geography

The department spans the Central and Eastern Cordilleras, divided by the valley of the Magdalena River. It hosts the Los Nevados National Natural Park, shared with neighbouring departments, and the snow-capped volcanoes Nevado del Tolima, Nevado del Ruiz (near its border), and the active Nevado del Huila.

History

The region was home to the Pijao people, famed for resisting Spanish conquest. Tolima was created as a sovereign state in 1861 and became a department under the 1886 constitution. In 1985 the eruption of Nevado del Ruiz devastated the town of Armero in the department's northeast, killing more than 20,000 people in one of history's deadliest volcanic disasters.

Economy

Agriculture dominates: rice, coffee, cotton, and sorghum are main products, and Tolima brands itself Colombia's rice capital.
