Stack (geology)

A stack or sea stack is a geological landform consisting of a steep, often vertical column or columns of rock in the sea near a coast, formed by wave-induced erosion. Stacks are a classic product of coastal geomorphology and are important refuges and nesting sites for seabirds.

A sea stack, detached from the headland by erosion

Formation

Stacks develop on headlands: waves exploit cracks at the base of a cliff to form a cave; the cave is deepened into a natural arch; when the arch's roof collapses, the seaward pillar is left isolated as a stack. Continued erosion at its base eventually topples the stack, leaving a low stump exposed at low tide.

Famous examples

Celebrated sea stacks include the Old Man of Hoy (Orkney), the Needles off the Isle of Wight, the Twelve Apostles in Victoria, Australia, and the Old Harry Rocks in Dorset. In St Kilda, Scotland, Stac an Armin (196 m) is the tallest sea stack in the British Isles.
