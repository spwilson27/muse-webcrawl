Borwein's algorithm

Borwein's algorithm
Devised byJonathan and Peter Borwein
PurposeComputing digits of π
ConvergenceQuartic (and higher-order variants)

Borwein's algorithm refers to a family of iterative algorithms devised by Jonathan Borwein and Peter Borwein in the mid-1980s for computing π and other constants to very high precision. Their famous quartic algorithm converges extraordinarily fast: the number of correct digits roughly quadruples with each iteration.

The quartic algorithm

The algorithm is based on Ramanujan's and the Chudnovsky-type modular equations for elliptic integrals. Starting from initial values involving √2, each iteration updates two sequences using arithmetic and geometric means generalised with square roots; the reciprocal of one sequence converges to π. After only a handful of iterations, hundreds of digits are correct.

Related algorithms

The Borweins also gave quadratically and quintically convergent iterations, and a nonic (order-nine) algorithm. Together with the Gauss–Legendre algorithm and the work of Salamin and Brent, these methods underpinned record computations of π in the late twentieth century. A related formula, the Bailey–Borwein–Plouffe formula (1995), allows computing isolated hexadecimal digits of π without computing the preceding digits.

Significance

The Borwein algorithms demonstrated the power of modular equations in numerical computation and were used by Yasumasa Kanada and others in record-setting calculations of π. Jonathan Borwein's work helped establish the field of experimental mathematics.
