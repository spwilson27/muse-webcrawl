Droeshout portrait

The Droeshout portrait is an engraving by Martin Droeshout the Younger (1601–1650) depicting William Shakespeare. It appears on the title page of the First Folio of Shakespeare's plays, published in 1623, seven years after Shakespeare's death, and is one of only two portraits of Shakespeare considered to have a good claim to authenticity, the other being the Chandos portrait.

Description

The engraving shows Shakespeare head-and-shoulders, facing slightly left, with a receding hairline, moustache and pointed beard, wearing a wide white collar over a doublet. Ben Jonson's verses opposite the portrait in the Folio praise its likeness, urging the reader to "looke / Not on his Picture, but his Booke".

States and assessment

Four states of the engraving exist, with progressively heavier shading. Art historians have debated its quality and accuracy, but it remains the most widely reproduced image of Shakespeare.
