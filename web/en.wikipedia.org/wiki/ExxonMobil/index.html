Exxon Mobil Corporation, doing business as ExxonMobil, is an American multinational oil and gas corporation headquartered in Spring, Texas. Formed in 1999 by the merger of Exxon and Mobil — both descendants of John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil — it is among the largest publicly traded energy companies in the world.

History

Exxon (formerly Standard Oil of New Jersey, or Jersey Standard) and Mobil (Standard Oil of New York) were two of the "Seven Sisters" that dominated mid-20th-century oil. Their 1999 merger, then the largest in history, reunited two pieces of the Standard Oil empire broken up in 1911.

The company is indelibly associated with the 1989 Exxon Valdez oil spill in Alaska's Prince William Sound, one of the worst environmental disasters of its era. Major recent moves include the giant Guyana offshore discoveries (from 2015) and the roughly $60 billion acquisition of Permian shale producer Pioneer Natural Resources, completed in 2024.

Operations

ExxonMobil explores for and produces oil and gas on six continents, operates major refineries and chemical plants, and markets fuels under the Exxon, Mobil and Esso brands. It has faced sustained criticism and litigation over its historical knowledge of, and public statements about, climate change, and over plastic pollution; the company maintains it is investing in lower-carbon technologies including carbon capture and lithium.
