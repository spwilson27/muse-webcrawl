The Academy of the Hebrew Language (Hebrew: הָאָקָדֶמְיָה לַלָּשׁוֹן הָעִבְרִית, HaAkademya laLashon ha'Ivrit) is the supreme statutory institution for the Hebrew language in Israel, established by the Knesset in 1953. It succeeded the Hebrew Language Committee (Va'ad ha-Lashon), founded by Eliezer Ben-Yehuda, the reviver of modern Hebrew.

Functions

The Academy sets standards for Hebrew grammar, orthography, punctuation and transliteration, and coins new words to fill gaps in the vocabulary of modern life, drawing where possible on ancient Hebrew roots. Its decisions are published in the official gazette and are binding on government and educational institutions. It also produces the Historical Dictionary of the Hebrew Language, a comprehensive scholarly project documenting the language across all periods.

History

The Hebrew Language Committee was first formed in 1890 and re-established in 1904, playing a central part in the revival of Hebrew as a spoken language. Following the establishment of the State of Israel, the Supreme Institute for the Hebrew Language Law (1953) transformed the committee into the Academy, modelled partly on the Académie Française.
