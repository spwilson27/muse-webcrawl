RJR Nabisco

RJR Nabisco, Inc. was an American conglomerate formed in 1985 by the merger of Nabisco Brands and R.J. Reynolds Tobacco Company. Headquartered originally in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, and later in New York, the company combined leading tobacco brands such as Camel and Winston with food brands including Oreo, Ritz, and Planters.

The leveraged buyout

RJR Nabisco is famous chiefly for its 1988 leveraged buyout, then the largest in history at approximately $25 billion. After CEO F. Ross Johnson proposed a management buyout at $75 per share, a bidding war ensued between Johnson's group (backed by Shearson Lehman Hutton) and Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co. (KKR). KKR ultimately prevailed with a bid of $109 per share. The contest was immortalized in the book Barbarians at the Gate (1989) by Bryan Burrough and John Helyar, and a subsequent television film. The deal became a symbol of 1980s corporate excess and the junk bond era.

Aftermath and breakup

Saddled with debt, RJR Nabisco sold assets and was burdened by tobacco litigation through the 1990s. In 1999, the company spun off Nabisco-related food businesses and sold its international tobacco operations to Japan Tobacco; the remaining US tobacco business became R.J. Reynolds Tobacco Holdings, while Nabisco was acquired by Philip Morris's Kraft Foods in 2000. The RJR Nabisco name ceased to be used.
