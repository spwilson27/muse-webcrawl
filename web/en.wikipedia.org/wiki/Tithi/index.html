Tithi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Hindu and other Indian calendars, a tithi is a lunar day, defined as the time required for the angular separation between the Sun and the Moon to increase by 12 degrees. Because the Moon's motion varies, a tithi does not correspond to a fixed period of time; it ranges from about 19 to 26 hours, and does not necessarily begin and end at sunrise.

Structure of the lunar month

A lunar month contains 30 tithis, divided into two pakshas: the waxing shukla paksha (15 tithis from new moon to full moon) and the waning krishna paksha (15 tithis from full moon to new moon). The 15 tithis are named: Pratipada, Dvitiya, Tritiya, Chaturthi, Panchami, Shashthi, Saptami, Ashtami, Navami, Dashami, Ekadashi, Dvadashi, Trayodashi, Chaturdashi and Purnima (full moon) in the waxing half, with Amavasya (new moon) completing the waning half.

Religious significance

Tithis determine the timing of festivals and observances: Ekadashi is a traditional fasting day, Purnima is associated with the birthdays of several deities, and Amavasya is a day for ancestral rites. Because a tithi may begin mid-day or span two calendar days, Hindu calendars specify the tithi current at sunrise, and an occasional tithi is omitted or repeated to keep the lunar month in step with the solar month.
