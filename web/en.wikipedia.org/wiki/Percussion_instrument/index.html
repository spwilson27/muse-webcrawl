Percussion instrument


Classification: Percussion (Hornbostel–Sachs)
Examples: Drums, cymbals, xylophone, timpani, maracas

A percussion instrument is a musical instrument that produces sound by being struck, shaken, scraped, or otherwise agitated. Percussion instruments are among the oldest musical devices known—drums and rattles appear in nearly every culture—and in the modern orchestra they form one of the principal sections, often called "the kitchen" for their variety.

Classification

In the Hornbostel–Sachs system percussion is divided chiefly into idiophones, which vibrate as a whole (cymbals, xylophone, bells), and membranophones, which sound through a stretched membrane (most drums). Musicians also distinguish pitched (tuned) percussion—timpani, marimba, vibraphone, tubular bells—from unpitched instruments such as snare drum, bass drum, hi-hat, and shakers.

Role in ensembles

In the orchestra, percussion provides rhythm, accent, and colour; a single percussionist may play many instruments in one work. The drum kit is the rhythmic foundation of jazz, rock, and popular music, while traditions worldwide feature distinctive instruments such as the West African djembe, the Indian tabla, Japanese taiko, and Latin American congas and cajón. Modern percussion repertoire ranges from marching bands to solo works by composers such as Iannis Xenakis.
