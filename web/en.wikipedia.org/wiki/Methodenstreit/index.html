Methodenstreit

Carl Menger and Gustav von Schmoller, the principals of the Methodenstreit

The Methodenstreit (German for "dispute over methods") was an economic controversy in the 1880s and 1890s between the Austrian School of economics, led by Carl Menger, and the German Historical School, led by Gustav von Schmoller, over the proper method of economic inquiry. It is one of the most famous methodological disputes in the history of economics.

Background

The German Historical School, dominant in German universities, held that economics should proceed inductively from detailed historical and statistical study, that economic laws were relative to time and place, and that deductive theorizing from abstract postulates was of limited value. Menger's Principles of Economics (1871) had founded the Austrian approach on deductive reasoning about individual action (methodological individualism), and his 1883 work Investigations into the Method of the Social Sciences with Special Reference to Economics attacked the historical school's rejection of exact theory.

The dispute

Schmoller reviewed Menger's book dismissively, and Menger replied with the polemical pamphlet The Errors of Historicism in German Economics (1884), addressed as open letters to Schmoller, who declined to respond in kind. The exchange became heated and personal. Substantively, the two sides differed on the universality of economic law, the relative priority of theory versus history, and the role of abstract deduction. Although the debate ended without a formal resolution, it helped crystallize the identity of the Austrian School and influenced later methodological discussions, including the distinction between theoretical and historical sciences drawn by Menger.

Legacy

The Methodenstreit anticipated enduring debates between theoretical modeling and empirical-historical approaches in economics and the social sciences generally. The term is also used retrospectively for related methodological controversies in other disciplines.
