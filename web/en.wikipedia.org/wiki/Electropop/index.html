Electropop

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Electropop is a popular music genre combining elements of electronic music and pop music. It is characterised by a heavy use of synthesisers, drum machines and electronic production within verse–chorus pop song structures, often with danceable rhythms and processed vocals.

History

The genre descends from the synth-pop of the late 1970s and 1980s, pioneered by acts such as Kraftwerk, Gary Numan, the Human League and Depeche Mode. After a period in which guitar rock and dance music dominated separately, electropop re-emerged in the 2000s through artists such as Goldfrapp, Ladytron, Robyn and Lady Gaga, whose 2008–2009 success brought synth-driven pop back to the centre of the mainstream charts.

During the late 2000s and 2010s, electropop production techniques — sidechained synths, four-on-the-floor beats, and maximalist electronic hooks — became a default sound of international pop, influencing artists across pop, hip hop and R&B, before the label gradually fell out of fashion as electronic production became ubiquitous.

Characteristics

Electropop typically features prominent synthesiser riffs and basslines, electronic drums, catchy melodic hooks and a polished, often futuristic aesthetic. It is closely related to, and frequently overlaps with, synth-pop, dance-pop and indietronica.
