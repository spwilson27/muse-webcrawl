Nuclear power in Pakistan

Nuclear power in Pakistan is provided by six commercial nuclear power plants. Pakistan is the first Muslim-majority country in the world to construct and operate civil nuclear power plants. The Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission (PAEC) is responsible for operating these plants, under the regulation of the Pakistan Nuclear Regulatory Authority.

History

Pakistan's first nuclear power plant, the Karachi Nuclear Power Plant (KANUPP), was a Canadian-supplied CANDU reactor that began operation in 1972 near Karachi. It operated until its shutdown in 2021. The Chashma Nuclear Power Plant in Punjab province hosts four Chinese-supplied pressurised water reactors (CNP-300 units), which entered service between 2000 and 2017. Two larger Hualong One (ACP-1000) units, K-2 and K-3, were constructed near Karachi with Chinese assistance and entered commercial operation in 2021 and 2022 respectively.

Capacity

Together the plants give Pakistan a nuclear generating capacity of roughly 3,500 megawatts, supplying a modest but growing share of the country's electricity. Pakistan is not a signatory to the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons, and its civil nuclear programme operates under facility-specific IAEA safeguards; China is its principal international partner in reactor construction.
