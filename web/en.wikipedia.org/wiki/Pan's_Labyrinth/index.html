Pan's Labyrinth (Spanish: El laberinto del fauno, lit. "The Labyrinth of the Faun") is a 2006 Spanish-Mexican dark fantasy film written, directed and co-produced by Guillermo del Toro. The film is set in Spain in the summer of 1944, five years after the Spanish Civil War, during the early Francoist period. It intertwines the harsh real world of Ofelia, a young girl who travels with her pregnant mother to live with her new stepfather, the sadistic Falangist Captain Vidal, with a mysterious fantasy world centred on an overgrown, abandoned labyrinth and a creature known as the Faun.

The Faun tells Ofelia that she is the reincarnation of Princess Moanna of the underworld, and that to reclaim her place she must complete three tasks before the full moon. Meanwhile, in the real world, Ofelia's mother grows increasingly ill, and guerrillas fighting the regime operate in the surrounding mountains, aided by the housekeeper Mercedes and the doctor Ferreiro.

Plot

Ofelia encounters several strange and magical creatures as she undertakes the Faun's tasks: a giant toad that has made its home in a dying fig tree, the terrifying Pale Man, a child-eating monster whose eyes sit in the palms of his hands, and the mandrake root she keeps beneath her mother's bed. As Vidal's cruelty escalates and the violence of the post-war repression closes in, Ofelia must choose between obedience and her own conscience. In the film's climax, she refuses to sacrifice her baby brother's blood, and is shot by Vidal — but her selflessness fulfils the final test, and she is welcomed back into the underworld kingdom as Princess Moanna.

Reception

Pan's Labyrinth premiered at the Cannes Film Festival in May 2006, where it received a standing ovation. It won three Academy Awards — for Best Art Direction, Best Cinematography and Best Makeup — and three BAFTA Awards, and is widely regarded as one of the finest fantasy films ever made and a landmark of Spanish-language cinema. Del Toro has described it as a spiritual successor to his earlier film The Devil's Backbone (2001).
