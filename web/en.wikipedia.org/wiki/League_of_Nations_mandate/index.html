League of Nations mandate

A League of Nations mandate represented a legal status under Article 22 of the Covenant of the League of Nations for certain territories transferred from the control of one country to another following World War I, or the legal instruments that created that status. The mandate system was established under the Treaty of Versailles and applied to the former colonies of the German Empire and the former Arab provinces of the Ottoman Empire.

Principle

Rather than annexing the defeated powers' territories outright, the victorious Allies declared them a "sacred trust of civilisation" to be administered by "advanced nations" on behalf of the League until the inhabitants were judged able to stand alone. The mandatory power reported annually to the Permanent Mandates Commission, and inhabitants could petition the League — a limited but unprecedented degree of international accountability for colonial rule.

Classes of mandate

Mandates were divided into three classes according to their presumed stage of development:

  • Class A mandates — former Ottoman territories in the Middle East, such as Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, and Transjordan — were provisionally recognised as independent nations subject to administrative advice. Iraq became independent in 1932; the others reached independence during or after World War II.
  • Class B mandates — former German colonies in central Africa, including Tanganyika, Ruanda-Urundi, and Togoland — were to be administered directly by the mandatory under guarantees of freedom of conscience, trade, and suppression of the slave trade.
  • Class C mandates — South West Africa and the Pacific islands including Nauru, Western Samoa, and the South Seas islands — could be administered as integral portions of the mandatory's territory.

Successor system

After World War II the surviving mandates (other than South West Africa, whose status remained disputed until Namibian independence in 1990) were converted into United Nations trust territories under the trusteeship system.
