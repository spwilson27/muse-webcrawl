Urysohn's lemma

In topology, Urysohn's lemma is a fundamental result stating that a topological space is normal if and only if any two disjoint closed subsets can be separated by a continuous function. Specifically, if A and B are disjoint closed subsets of a normal space X, then there exists a continuous function f from X to the unit interval [0, 1] such that f is 0 on A and 1 on B. Such a function is called a Urysohn function for A and B.

History and importance

The lemma was published by Pavel Urysohn in 1925. It is a cornerstone of general topology: it shows that normal spaces have "enough" continuous real-valued functions, and its proof is a key step in establishing the Urysohn metrization theorem and the Tietze extension theorem.

Proof idea

The standard proof constructs a family of open sets indexed by the dyadic rationals in [0, 1], nested in a way that mirrors the ordering of the rationals, using normality to separate successive sets. The desired function is then defined as the infimum of the indices of sets containing a given point; continuity follows from the nesting construction.
