The Stanley Cup is the championship trophy awarded annually to the National Hockey League (NHL) playoff champion. It is the oldest existing trophy to be awarded to a professional sports franchise in North America, and the International Ice Hockey Federation lists it among "the most important championships available to the sport".

The Cup was donated in 1893 by Lord Stanley of Preston, then Governor General of Canada, as a challenge cup for Canada's best amateur ice hockey club; professionals were admitted from 1906 and the NHL gained sole control in 1926. Uniquely among major North American trophies, the names of the winning players, coaches and staff are engraved on its bands, which are retired to the Hockey Hall of Fame when full. It was not awarded in 1919 (Spanish flu) or 2005 (lockout). By tradition, each member of the winning team spends a day with the Cup over the summer, and players famously refuse to touch it until they have won it.
