Texting while driving

Texting while driving, also called texting and driving, is the act of composing, sending, or reading text messages on a mobile phone while operating a motor vehicle. It is considered dangerous by the public, many governments, and road-safety researchers, and is illegal or restricted in many jurisdictions.

Danger

Texting while driving combines all three forms of driver distraction: visual (eyes off the road), manual (hands off the wheel), and cognitive (mind off driving). Studies by the Virginia Tech Transportation Institute (VTTI) found that text messaging increased the risk of a crash or near-crash event by 23.2 times for drivers of heavy vehicles and trucks compared with undistracted driving. Sending or reading a text takes a driver's eyes off the road for about 4.6 seconds on average — at 55 mph (89 km/h), long enough to travel the length of a football field.

The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) attributes thousands of deaths each year to distracted driving, with texting considered the most alarming distraction because of the attention it demands. Research consistently shows impairment comparable to, or worse than, driving with a blood alcohol concentration at the legal limit.

Prevalence

Surveys in the United States and elsewhere have repeatedly found that a substantial share of drivers — particularly teenagers and young adults — admit to texting while driving despite knowing the risks. The AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety has reported that young drivers are the age group most likely to be involved in distraction-related fatal crashes.

Laws and enforcement

As of the mid-2020s, nearly all US states ban text messaging for all drivers, and a majority also prohibit all hand-held mobile phone use while driving. The United Kingdom banned hand-held phone use while driving in 2003 and strengthened penalties in 2017 and 2022; the offense carries penalty points and fines, and newly qualified drivers can lose their licence. Many other countries, including Canada, Australia, and most of Europe, impose similar prohibitions. Enforcement challenges include the difficulty of proving a driver was texting rather than using a permitted function such as navigation.

Countermeasures

Countermeasures include public-awareness campaigns (such as the US "It Can Wait" and "U Drive. U Text. U Pay." campaigns), graduated driver licensing restrictions for novices, in-vehicle technologies and smartphone "driving modes" that silence notifications, and employer policies prohibiting phone use by professional drivers.
