Rocker (subculture)

Rockers, also known in Britain as leather boys or ton-up boys, were a youth subculture centred on motorcycling that emerged in the United Kingdom in the 1950s and peaked in the 1960s.

Inspired by American rock and roll music and films such as The Wild One (1953), rockers wore black leather jackets, jeans, and motorcycle boots, styled their hair in quiffs, and rode British motorcycles such as Triumphs and Nortons, often modified as café racers. They gathered at transport cafés such as the Ace Cafe on London's North Circular Road and raced—'ton-up' meaning exceeding 100 mph.

Mods and rockers

Rockers are famous for their rivalry with the scooter-riding, fashion-conscious mods, which erupted in seaside clashes at Brighton, Margate and Clacton in 1964, widely reported in the press and later analysed as a moral panic. The subculture influenced later punk and revival scenes.
