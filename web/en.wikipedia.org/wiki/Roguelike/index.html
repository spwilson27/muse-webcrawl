Roguelike (or rogue-like) is a subgenre of role-playing computer games traditionally characterized by a dungeon crawl through procedurally generated levels, turn-based gameplay, grid-based movement, and permanent death of the player character. Most roguelikes are based on a high fantasy narrative, reflecting the influence of tabletop role playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons.

The genre takes its name from Rogue (1980), written by Michael Toy and Glenn Wichman. Though the earlier games Beneath Apple Manor and Sword of Fargoal predate it, Rogue — widely distributed on university Unix systems — defined the template followed by Hack (1982) and its descendant NetHack (1987), Moria (1983) and Angband (1990), ADOM (1994), and Linley's Dungeon Crawl (1997).

Characteristics

The "Berlin Interpretation", adopted at the International Roguelike Development Conference 2008, lists high-value factors including random environment generation, permadeath, turn-based combat, grid-based movement, non-modal interaction, complexity, resource management, hack-and-slash combat, and exploration and discovery. Classic roguelikes render the dungeon in ASCII characters; later titles adopted tile graphics while retaining the mechanics.

Roguelites and modern revival

From the late 2000s, indie developers fused roguelike elements with other genres, producing "roguelites" — games with procedural levels and permadeath but with meta-progression that carries over between runs. Notable successes include The Binding of Isaac (2011), FTL: Faster Than Light (2012), Spelunky, Dead Cells (2018), Hades (2020, winner of the Hugo Award for Best Video Game), and Slay the Spire (2019), which popularised the deck-building roguelike. The blurring of definitions has kept the term itself a matter of lively debate in the community.
