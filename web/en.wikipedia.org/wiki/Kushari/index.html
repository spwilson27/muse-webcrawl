Kushari

Kushari (Egyptian Arabic: كشري), also spelled koshari or koshary, is Egypt's national dish and a widely popular street food. It is a mixture of rice, macaroni and lentils topped with a spiced tomato sauce, chickpeas and crispy fried onions, usually seasoned with garlic vinegar and hot sauce.

History

Kushari is thought to have developed in the mid-19th century, drawing on foodways that arrived with British troops from India — the name may derive from khichdi, an Indian dish of rice and lentils — combined with Italian pasta, a legacy of Egypt's large Italian community. Inexpensive and filling, it became a staple of working-class Cairo, sold from carts and small dedicated restaurants.

Preparation

The components — brown lentils, rice, small macaroni, and sometimes spaghetti — are cooked separately and layered. A tomato sauce flavoured with cumin and vinegar (daqqah) is spooned over, followed by chickpeas and a generous topping of golden fried onions. The dish is naturally vegan and is eaten at all hours; famous Cairo establishments include Abou Tarek.
