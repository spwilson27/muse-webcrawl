John Napier of Merchiston (1 February 1550 – 4 April 1617), nicknamed Marvellous Merchiston, was a Scottish landowner known as a mathematician, physicist and astronomer. He was the 8th Laird of Merchiston. He is best known as the discoverer of logarithms.

Logarithms

Napier published his invention in Mirifici Logarithmorum Canonis Descriptio (1614), the result of some twenty years of work. His logarithms, defined kinematically through two points moving along lines at related speeds, transformed calculation: multiplication and division could be reduced to addition and subtraction using tables. Henry Briggs, professor of geometry at Gresham College, visited Napier in 1615 and 1616, and the two agreed on the modified base-10 ("common" or Briggsian) logarithms published after Napier's death. Kepler's astronomical calculations were greatly accelerated by Napier's tables.

Napier's bones and other work

In Rabdologiae (1617) Napier described "Napier's bones", a set of inscribed rods for mechanical multiplication and division, as well as other calculating devices including an early form of lattice multiplication. He also popularised the use of the decimal point in printed arithmetic.

Theology and other interests

A committed Protestant, Napier devoted much energy to theology; his A Plaine Discovery of the Whole Revelation of Saint John (1593) identified the Pope as the Antichrist and attempted to calculate the date of the apocalypse. He also devised (in secret) military inventions including burning mirrors and a form of artillery, and made agricultural improvements on his estates. The unit of information the "neper" and Edinburgh Napier University commemorate his name, as does the lunar crater Neper.
