The Dabie Mountains (Chinese: 大别山; pinyin: Dàbié Shān) are a major mountain range located at the border between Hubei, Henan and Anhui provinces in central China. Running roughly northwest to southeast, the range forms the watershed between the Huai River to the north and the Yangtze River to the south.

Geography

The range extends for several hundred kilometres. Its highest peak is usually given as Mount Huo at about 1,774 metres. The mountains contain extensive forests and are an important biodiversity area in central China, with nature reserves such as the one at Tiantangzhai.

History

Because of their rugged terrain, the Dabie Mountains were a base area for communist guerrilla forces during the Chinese Civil War. The region was associated with the Eyuwan Soviet, one of the early communist base areas, and with the later Red Army's Fourth Front Army. In 1947, Liu Bocheng and Deng Xiaoping led a famous advance into the Dabie Mountains during the war against the Kuomintang.

Economy

Tea, chestnuts and forestry are important local products; the area's relative remoteness has made it a focus of poverty-alleviation and eco-tourism programs.
