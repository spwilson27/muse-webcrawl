Norman McLaren

Norman McLaren, CC CQ (11 April 1914 – 27 January 1987) was a Scottish-Canadian animator, director and producer known for his work for the National Film Board of Canada (NFB). He was a pioneer in a number of areas of animation and filmmaking, including hand-drawn animation, drawn-on-film animation, visual music, abstract film, pixilation and graphical sound.

Career

McLaren studied at the Glasgow School of Art, where he began experimenting by painting and scratching directly onto film stock. After making films for the British GPO Film Unit under John Grierson, he followed Grierson to Canada in 1941 to join the new National Film Board, where he worked for the rest of his life. His anti-war pixilation film Neighbours (1952), in which two men fight murderously over a flower, won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Short Subject. Blinkity Blank (1955) won the short-film Palme d'Or at Cannes. Later works such as Pas de deux (1968), using optical printing to multiply images of ballet dancers, are considered masterpieces.

Techniques and legacy

McLaren hand-drew soundtracks onto the optical track to create "animated sound", and developed chain-of-mix and other NFB innovations. His dictum — "Animation is not the art of drawings that move but the art of movements that are drawn" — is widely quoted. In 2006 the NFB named its Montreal headquarters' animation studios in his honour, and his films are inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register (Neighbours).
