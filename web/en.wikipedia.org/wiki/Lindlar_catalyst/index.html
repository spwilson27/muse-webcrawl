Lindlar catalyst

Lindlar catalyst
CompositionPalladium on calcium carbonate, poisoned with lead acetate (and often quinoline)
Named afterHerbert Lindlar
UseSemi-hydrogenation of alkynes to cis-alkenes

A Lindlar catalyst is a heterogeneous catalyst consisting of palladium deposited on a calcium carbonate support, which has been "poisoned" with lead acetate (and frequently further moderated with quinoline). It was developed by the Swiss chemist Herbert Lindlar at Hoffmann-La Roche and described in 1952.

The lead deactivates some of the palladium sites, so the catalyst hydrogenates alkynes but stops at the alkene stage instead of continuing to the alkane. Because the hydrogen adds to the same face of the triple bond (syn addition), the product is the cis (Z) alkene. The reaction is a classic method for preparing cis-alkenes, which are otherwise difficult to obtain selectively.

Preparation

The catalyst is prepared by depositing palladium chloride, reduced in situ, onto a slurry of calcium carbonate, followed by treatment with lead acetate. Commercial material typically contains about 5% palladium by weight. Quinoline is often added to the reaction mixture to further suppress over-reduction and improve cis selectivity.

Mechanism and selectivity

Hydrogen dissociates on the palladium surface; the alkyne, more strongly adsorbed than the alkene, is reduced preferentially. Poisoning lowers the activity of the surface so that the resulting alkene desorbs before it can be further reduced, and syn delivery of hydrogen gives the Z configuration. Prolonged reaction times or insufficient poisoning can still lead to over-reduction and cis–trans isomerisation, so uptake of one equivalent of hydrogen is monitored.

Applications

Lindlar hydrogenation has been used in syntheses of natural products and vitamins — notably in industrial routes to vitamin A and vitamin E intermediates and in the synthesis of insect pheromones, where the exact cis geometry of an alkene is essential for biological activity. A famous example is the semi-hydrogenation step in the synthesis of the leaf alcohol (cis-3-hexenol).
