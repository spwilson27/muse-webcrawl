William Cranch Bond

William Cranch Bond (September 9, 1789 – January 29, 1859) was an American astronomer and the first director of Harvard College Observatory. Largely self-taught, he began as a clockmaker in Dorchester, Massachusetts.

Career

Bond's private observatory and timekeeping work led Harvard to appoint him astronomical observer in 1839; the Great Refractor (15-inch) was installed in 1847, then among the largest telescopes in the world. With his son George Phillips Bond he discovered Saturn's moon Hyperion (1848, independently found by William Lassell) and the dusky Crêpe Ring (1850).

Photography and time service

The Bonds pioneered astronomical photography, taking an early daguerreotype of the Moon exhibited in 1851, and developed chronographic methods for determining longitude, providing a time service used by railroads and shipping.
