S-Bahn

S-Bahn is a type of suburban and commuter rail service found in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and other European countries. The name is an abbreviation of Stadtschnellbahn ("city rapid railway") or Stadtbahn. S-Bahn networks typically combine city-centre tunnels or main-line routes with suburban branches, offering frequent services with metro-like characteristics in the core.

History

The Berlin S-Bahn, electrified in the 1920s and given the name in 1930, and the Hamburg S-Bahn pioneered the concept. From the 1960s, West German cities such as Munich, Frankfurt and Stuttgart built new networks with downtown tunnels (Verbindungsbahn/Stammstrecke) linking suburban lines across the city.

Characteristics

S-Bahn services use heavy-rail trains, usually stop at all stations, run on clockface timetables, and are integrated into regional transport associations (Verkehrsverbünde) with unified ticketing alongside U-Bahn, trams and buses. The green "S" logo is a familiar symbol across the German-speaking world.

Networks

Major networks include Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Rhine-Ruhr, Stuttgart, Vienna and Zurich, the latter often cited as a model of integrated suburban rail.
