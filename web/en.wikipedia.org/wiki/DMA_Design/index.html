DMA Design was a British video game developer based in Scotland, best known for creating the Lemmings and Grand Theft Auto franchises. Founded by David Jones in Dundee in the mid-1980s, the company was acquired by Take-Two Interactive in 1999 and in 2002 was renamed Rockstar North, the lead studio behind the Grand Theft Auto series.

History

David Jones began writing games as a teenager and formed the company (first as Acme Software, then DMA Design — "DMA" being taken from the Amiga programming manuals' abbreviation for direct memory access) with friends including Russell Kay, Steve Hammond and Mike Dailly. After early titles such as Menace (1988) and Blood Money (1989) for Psygnosis, the studio achieved major success with Lemmings (1991), a puzzle game in which players guide hordes of lemmings to safety; it sold millions of copies and spawned numerous sequels and ports.

Grand Theft Auto and transition

In 1997 DMA released Grand Theft Auto, a top-down open-world action game whose controversial freedom of action made it a commercial success and a media controversy. The studio also developed Body Harvest for the Nintendo 64 and the sequel Grand Theft Auto 2 (1999). Following the Take-Two acquisition, DMA developed Grand Theft Auto III (2001), which transformed the series into a fully 3D open world and became one of the most influential games of its generation; shortly afterward the studio was rebranded as Rockstar North.
