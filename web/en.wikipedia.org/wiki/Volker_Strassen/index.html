Volker Strassen (born 29 April 1936) is a German mathematician and computer scientist, emeritus professor at the University of Konstanz, known for fundamental work on the complexity of algorithms. He was among the founders of the analysis of algorithms as a mathematical discipline.

Contributions

In his 1969 paper "Gaussian Elimination is not Optimal", Strassen published the Strassen algorithm, the first method for multiplying matrices faster than the conventional cubic-time algorithm — it multiplies two 2×2 block matrices with seven multiplications instead of eight, giving an exponent of about 2.807. The result launched the search for fast matrix multiplication, one of the central open problems of theoretical computer science, and his "laser method" later underpinned many record exponents. With Robert Solovay he introduced the Solovay–Strassen primality test (1977), one of the first probabilistic algorithms to gain wide acceptance, which helped establish randomised algorithms as a respectable paradigm. Strassen also contributed the concept of asymptotic spectrum in the study of tensor rank and bilinear complexity, and in probability theory he proved the law of the iterated logarithm and Strassen's theorem on the coupling of probability distributions.

Career and honours

Strassen studied at several German universities, received his doctorate from Göttingen in 1962 under Konrad Jacobs, worked at Berkeley and Erlangen, and from 1988 at Konstanz, later also directing research at the Max Planck Institute for Mathematics in Bonn. He received the Cantor Medal of the German Mathematical Society in 1999 and the Knuth Prize in 2008 for his contributions to efficient algorithms.
