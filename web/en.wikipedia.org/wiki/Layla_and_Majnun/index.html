Layla and Majnun

Layla and Majnun (Arabic: مجنون ليلى, "Layla's Mad Lover"; Persian: لیلی و مجنون) is an old story of Arab origin about the 7th-century Arabian poet Qays ibn al-Mulawwah and his lover Layla bint Mahdi (later Layla al-Aamiriya). The tale of their doomed love became one of the most popular romances in the Islamic world, retold in Arabic, Persian, Turkish, Azerbaijani, and Urdu literature.

Story

Qays falls in love with Layla at school. When he proclaims his love in verse, her family forbids the match, and Layla is married to another man. Qays, deranged by grief, wanders the desert composing poetry, earning the name Majnun ("madman" or "possessed"). In most versions both lovers die of sorrow and are buried side by side.

Nizami's version

The most famous literary treatment is the narrative poem by the Persian poet Nizami Ganjavi (1188), part of his Khamsa, which gave the story its canonical form and influenced countless later works. Fuzuli's 16th-century Azerbaijani version and the Azerbaijani composer Uzeyir Hajibeyov's 1908 opera Leyli and Majnun are also celebrated.

Legacy

The story is often read in Sufi tradition as an allegory of the soul's longing for divine love. It inspired works across Asia and beyond, and the phrase "Layla and Majnun" remains a byword for inseparable, tragic lovers in many cultures.
