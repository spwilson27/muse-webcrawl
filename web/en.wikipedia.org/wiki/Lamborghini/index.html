Automobili Lamborghini S.p.A. is an Italian manufacturer of luxury sports cars and SUVs based in Sant'Agata Bolognese, Italy. The company is owned by the Volkswagen Group through its subsidiary Audi. Lamborghini is known for its distinctive, aggressively styled mid-engine supercars, many of which are named after famous Spanish fighting bulls.

History

Lamborghini was founded in 1963 by Ferruccio Lamborghini, a successful manufacturer of tractors and agricultural machinery. According to company legend, Ferruccio decided to build sports cars after a dispute with Enzo Ferrari over the quality of a Ferrari he owned. The company's first production car, the 350 GT, debuted at the Turin Motor Show in 1963 and entered production in 1964.

The breakthrough model was the Miura of 1966, which established the mid-engine, two-seat layout as the standard for high-performance sports cars. The Countach, launched in 1974, became an icon of 1970s and 1980s automotive design with its sharp wedge shape and scissor doors. Ferruccio Lamborghini sold the company in the early 1970s amid financial difficulties, and Lamborghini passed through several owners, including the Chrysler Corporation, before being acquired by Audi in 1998.

Under Audi ownership, Lamborghini launched the Murciélago (2001) and the smaller Gallardo (2003), the latter becoming the best-selling Lamborghini of its era. These were followed by the Aventador (2011), the Huracán (2014), and the Urus SUV (2018), which dramatically expanded the company's sales volumes. Limited-production models such as the Reventón, Sesto Elemento, Veneno, and Sián have showcased hybrid technology and extreme design.

Models

Current production models include the Huracán (and its successor, the Temerario), the Revuelto hybrid V12 flagship, and the Urus performance SUV. Lamborghini engines have traditionally been naturally aspirated V10 and V12 units, and the company has been noted for resisting turbocharging and hybridization longer than many rivals, though recent models have adopted hybrid powertrains.

Motorsport

Although Ferruccio Lamborghini famously avoided factory racing, Lamborghini later developed a motorsport presence through its Squadra Corse division, running one-make Super Trofeo series and GT3 cars based on the Huracán. The company supplied engines to Formula One teams between 1989 and 1993 without notable success.
