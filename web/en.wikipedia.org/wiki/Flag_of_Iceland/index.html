The flag of Iceland (Icelandic: íslenski fáninn) is a blue field bearing a Nordic cross in white, with a red cross inside the white cross. It was officially described in the Law of the National Flag of Icelanders and the State Arms, enacted when Iceland became a republic in 1944, although the design had been in use since 1915.

History

A blue flag with a white cross was proposed in the early 20th century as Iceland sought symbols of independence from Denmark. The red cross was added at the suggestion of the Danish king's heraldic advisers, and the flag was approved by royal decree in 1915. When Iceland became a republic in 1944, the flag was retained with an altered, deeper blue shade.

Symbolism

The colours are commonly said to represent elements of Iceland's landscape: blue for the mountains and the sea, white for ice and snow, and red for the fire of the island's volcanoes. The design follows the Nordic cross tradition shared with the other Scandinavian flags.
