Spin-off (media)

In media, a spin-off (or spinoff) is a work — such as a radio program, television series, film, video game or novel — that is derived from an existing work, typically focusing on a character, setting or story element from the original in greater detail. Unlike a sequel, which continues a story forward, a spin-off usually explores a different strand of the same fictional universe, sometimes running concurrently with the parent work.

Types

A common form takes a supporting character from a successful series and gives them their own series, as when Frasier spun off from Cheers, Better Call Saul from Breaking Bad, and Angel from Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Other spin-offs shift the setting while keeping the format, as in the multiple Law & Order and CSI series, or transfer a concept to a new cast and place, as in Star Trek: The Next Generation. In publishing, shared-world anthologies and character-focused novels perform the same function, and in video games spin-offs may change genre while keeping the characters, such as the Pokémon puzzle and photography games.

History

Spin-offs were common in network radio and early television, where a character might be introduced in a "backdoor pilot" episode of an established show. The Andy Griffith Show itself originated as an episode of The Danny Thomas Show and in turn generated Gomer Pyle, U.S.M.C. and Mayberry R.F.D., illustrating the chains that spin-offs can form. Modern franchise management, especially in film universes such as Marvel and Star Wars, plans spin-offs deliberately as part of a shared universe strategy.
