NHIndustries NH90

Manufacturer: NHIndustries

First flight: 18 December 1995

Introduction: 2007

Role: Medium utility / naval helicopter

The NHIndustries NH90 is a European medium-sized, twin-engine, multi-role military helicopter developed and manufactured by NHIndustries, a consortium of Airbus Helicopters, Leonardo (formerly AgustaWestland) and Fokker Aerostructures. It was developed for NATO navies and armies from the 1980s onward and first flew in December 1995.

Variants

Two main variants are produced: the NFH (NATO Frigate Helicopter) for naval anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare, search and rescue, and the TTH (Tactical Transport Helicopter) for army troop transport, carrying up to 20 soldiers or equivalent cargo. The NH90 features a largely composite airframe, fly-by-wire flight controls — the first production helicopter so equipped — and a low radar cross-section design.

Service history

The type entered service in 2007 and has been operated by countries including France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain, Belgium, Finland, Sweden, Norway, Greece, Australia, New Zealand, Oman and Qatar. The programme has been troubled by delays, cost growth, corrosion and availability problems; Norway terminated its contract in 2022 and demanded refunds, while Australia retired its fleet (designated MRH-90 Taipan) early in 2023 following a fatal accident, replacing it with the American UH-60M Black Hawk. Other operators, including Germany and France, continue to upgrade and operate the type, and a major mid-life upgrade ("Block 1") is in development.

Specifications (general)

The NH90 has a maximum take-off weight of about 10,600 kg, a top speed around 300 km/h, and a range of roughly 800–1,000 km depending on variant and role. It is powered by two Rolls-Royce Turbomeca RTM322 or General Electric T700 engines.
