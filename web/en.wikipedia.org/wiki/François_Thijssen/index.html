François Thijssen

François Thijssen (also spelled Thijsen or Thyssen) was a Dutch sea captain in the service of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). He is remembered for his 1627 voyage aboard the ship 't Gulden Zeepaert (The Golden Seahorse), during which he charted a long stretch of the southern coast of Australia.

Voyage of 1627

Thijssen sailed from the Netherlands for Batavia in 1626. Blown off course on the Indian Ocean crossing in January 1627, the Gulden Zeepaert made landfall near Cape Leeuwin and then sailed east along the Great Australian Bight for some 1,500 kilometres, charting the coast as far as the islands off present-day Ceduna in South Australia. Among those aboard was Pieter Nuyts, a senior VOC official, after whom the Nuyts Archipelago is named; the region was long known on Dutch charts as Nuyts Land.

Legacy

Thijssen's charts recorded the most extensive early European survey of Australia's southern coastline. The islands of the Investigator Group and the St Francis and St Peter island groups in the Bight preserve the geography of his voyage, and the modern South Australian town of Thijssen's namesake features in regional histories of European exploration of Australia.
