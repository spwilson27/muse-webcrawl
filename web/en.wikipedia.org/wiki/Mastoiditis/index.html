SpecialtyOtolaryngology
SymptomsPain, swelling and redness behind the ear; fever; ear protrusion; discharge

Mastoiditis is an infection of the mastoid process, the honeycombed bony prominence of the skull behind the ear. The mastoid air cells communicate with the middle ear, so infection can spread from acute otitis media (middle-ear infection) into the mastoid bone. It is most common in young children and, before antibiotics, was a leading cause of childhood death; it remains a serious complication requiring urgent treatment.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features include fever, pain and tenderness over the mastoid, redness and swelling behind the ear, and displacement of the pinna downwards and outwards, making the ear appear to stick out. There may be discharge from the ear canal, hearing loss, and a bulging or perforated eardrum. Common causative organisms include Streptococcus pneumoniae, Streptococcus pyogenes, Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. CT scanning shows opacification and, in advanced cases, destruction (coalescence) of the mastoid air cells.

Complications

Untreated, the infection can erode through bone to form a subperiosteal abscess behind the ear or in the neck (Bezold's abscess), or spread inward to cause meningitis, brain abscess, facial nerve palsy or thrombosis of the sigmoid sinus — the route by which it was so often fatal in the pre-antibiotic era.

Treatment

Treatment is hospital admission with high-dose intravenous antibiotics, often combined with myringotomy (drainage through the eardrum, sometimes with a ventilation tube) to drain the middle ear. If the infection fails to settle, or complications are present, cortical mastoidectomy — surgical removal of the infected mastoid air cells — is performed. Widespread antibiotic treatment of ear infections has made mastoiditis uncommon, though it still occurs in a small percentage of children with otitis media.
