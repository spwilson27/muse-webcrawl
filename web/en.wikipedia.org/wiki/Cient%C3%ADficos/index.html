Científicos

The Científicos ("Scientists") were a circle of technocratic advisers to Mexican president Porfirio Díaz during the Porfiriato (1876–1911). Influenced by French positivism — especially the thought of Auguste Comte, filtered through Gabino Barreda — they promoted a doctrine of "order and progress" and scientific, authoritarian governance over liberal democracy.

Influence

Led informally by Finance Minister José Yves Limantour, the group championed fiscal stability, foreign investment, railways, and modernization of mining and industry. Their motto was sometimes summarized as "much administration, little politics." The Científicos dominated economic policy from the 1890s and amassed influence and, critics charged, personal wealth and monopolies through concessions.

Downfall

Rivalry with the military faction of Bernardo Reyes, resentment from excluded elites, and the group's association with an ageing Díaz made them a target of revolutionary rhetoric. The Mexican Revolution of 1910 swept away the Porfirian order, and the Científicos fled into exile or obscurity.
