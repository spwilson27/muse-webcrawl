Ismail ibn Ahmad, known as Ismail Samani, was the Samanid amir of Transoxiana and Khorasan from 892 until his death in 907. He made Bukhara the capital of a virtually independent Persian state and is celebrated as a founder of the Samanid Empire, under which New Persian literature and culture flourished.

Rise to power

Ismail was the son of Ahmad ibn Asad, who had been given the governorship of Ferghana by the Tahirids. After Ahmad's death, Ismail and his brother Nasr divided the family territories; in 892 Ismail seized Bukhara, deposing Nasr, and received the caliphal appointment to Transoxiana. He defeated the Saffarid ruler Amr ibn al-Layth at the Battle of Balkh in 900, securing Khurasan, and in 904 the Abbasid caliph recognised him as amir of all the lands east of Baghdad.

Reign

Ismail's reign brought stability and prosperity: trade along the Silk Road revived, coinage was regularised, and Bukhara, Samarkand, Merv and Nishapur became centres of learning and commerce. Persian re-entered official use alongside Arabic; the bureaucracy and court culture he patronised laid foundations for the literary renaissance of the tenth century. He died in November 907, shortly after an expedition against the Syr Darya frontier, and was buried in the domed mausoleum in Bukhara that bears his family's name.

Legacy

The Samanid Mausoleum (Somonî Mausoleum) in Bukhara is considered a masterpiece of early Islamic architecture. Ismail is a national hero in modern Tajikistan: the country's highest peak was renamed Ismail Somoni Peak (7,495 m), and the currency is the somoni, derived from his name.
