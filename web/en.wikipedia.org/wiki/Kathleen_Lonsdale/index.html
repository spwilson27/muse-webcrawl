Kathleen Lonsdale

Dame Kathleen Lonsdale DBE FRS (née Yardley; 28 January 1903 – 1 April 1971) was an Irish-British crystallographer who proved that the benzene ring is flat and hexagonal by X-ray diffraction methods. She was one of the first two women elected Fellows of the Royal Society, and the first woman professor at University College London.

Early life and education

Kathleen Yardley was born in Newbridge, Ireland, and moved to Essex as a child. At Woodford County High School she had to transfer to a boys' school to study mathematics and physics. She read physics at Bedford College, London, graduating in 1922 with one of the highest marks recorded. William Henry Bragg invited her to join his crystallography research team, and she took her PhD at University College London in 1924.

Scientific work

Working with Bragg and later at the Royal Institution, Lonsdale determined the structure of hexamethylbenzene (1928), showing that the benzene ring is a planar regular hexagon — a result of fundamental importance for organic chemistry — and in 1931 solved the structure of hexachlorobenzene, the first complex organic compound analysed by X-ray methods. She pioneered the use of Fourier methods and compiled standard X-ray tables for space-group analysis, and studied thermal diffuse scattering, diamond and bladder stones, among many other topics.

After working at Leeds, she returned to UCL in 1934, became Reader in 1936 and, in 1949, Professor of Chemistry and head of the Department of Crystallography — the first woman to hold a chair at UCL. With Marjory Stephenson, she was elected to the Royal Society in 1945, among its first female fellows.

Pacifism and later life

Lonsdale was a devout Quaker and committed pacifist. She was imprisoned for a month in Holloway Prison in 1943 for refusing to register for civil defence duties or pay the fine, an experience that made her an advocate of prison reform. She served as president of the British Association in 1967–68 and campaigned for nuclear disarmament and science's social responsibility. She was created a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1956.
