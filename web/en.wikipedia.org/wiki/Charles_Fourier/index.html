François Marie Charles Fourier (7 April 1772 – 10 October 1837) was a French philosopher and an influential early socialist thinker, later associated with utopian socialism. He proposed a radical reorganisation of society based on small, self-sufficient communities.

Ideas

Fourier envisioned communities called phalanxes (phalanstères) of about 1,600 people living in large communal buildings, organising work according to people's natural passions so that labour would become attractive. He was a sharp critic of commerce, poverty and the institution of marriage, and an early advocate of women's emancipation — he is often credited with coining the word "feminism".

Influence

Fourierist communities were founded in France and the United States, including Brook Farm's Fourierist phase and the North American Phalanx. His ideas influenced later socialist, cooperative and feminist thought.
