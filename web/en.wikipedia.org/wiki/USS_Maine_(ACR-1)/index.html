USS Maine (ACR-1)

USS Maine was a United States Navy ship that sank in Havana Harbor on 15 February 1898, contributing to the outbreak of the Spanish–American War. Commissioned in 1895, she was originally classified as an armored cruiser (ACR-1) but was often described as a second-class battleship.

Design and construction

Maine was laid down at the New York Navy Yard in 1888 and launched in 1889. She displaced about 6,682 tons and was armed with four 10-inch guns in two twin turrets mounted en echelon, plus 6-inch and smaller guns, and was protected by up to 12 inches of nickel-steel armor.

Loss

Sent to Havana in January 1898 to protect American interests during Cuba's war of independence from Spain, Maine exploded and sank on the night of 15 February 1898, killing about 260 of her crew. An official U.S. inquiry concluded that a naval mine had destroyed the ship, though later investigations suggested an internal explosion, possibly from a coal bunker fire igniting a magazine. The slogan "Remember the Maine! To hell with Spain!" rallied American public opinion, and the United States declared war on Spain in April 1898.

The wreck was raised in 1912, examined, and towed out to sea and scuttled in deep water. Her main mast stands at Arlington National Cemetery.
