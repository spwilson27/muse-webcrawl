Pâté

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pâté (UK: /ˈpæteɪ/, US: /pɑːˈteɪ/; French: [pɑte]) is a forcemeat, a paste made of ground meat and fat, commonly used as a spread in French and Belgian cuisines. Pâtés can be made from pork, poultry, fish or beef, mixed with fat and seasonings, and may be cooked in a crust (pâté en croûte) or in a terrine mould (pâté en terrine).

Varieties

Common types include pâté de foie gras, made from the fattened liver of goose or duck; pâté de campagne, a coarse country-style preparation; and liver pâtés found across northern and eastern Europe, such as Danish leverpostej and German Leberwurst-style spreads. Vietnamese bánh mì commonly includes a pork-liver pâté inherited from French colonial cuisine.

Serving

Pâté is typically served cold or at room temperature with bread, toast or crackers, often accompanied by cornichons or preserves. Pâté en croûte is a celebrated dish of French charcuterie with a world championship held in Lyon.
