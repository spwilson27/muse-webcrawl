Communist insurgency in Malaysia (1968–1989)

Malaysian security forces fought a low-intensity guerrilla war along the Thai border for two decades.

Date17 June 1968 – 2 December 1989
LocationMalay Peninsula and Sarawak, Malaysia
BelligerentsMalaysia (and allies) vs. Malayan Communist Party and allied groups
ResultPeace Agreement of Hat Yai (1989); dissolution of the MCP's armed units

The communist insurgency in Malaysia, also known as the Second Malayan Emergency, was an armed conflict between the Malayan Communist Party (MCP) and the security forces of Malaysia from 1968 to 1989. It followed the earlier Malayan Emergency (1948–1960), after which surviving MCP guerrillas had withdrawn to sanctuaries along the Malaysian–Thai border. From these bases, under secretary-general Chin Peng, the party rebuilt its forces and renewed its armed struggle.

Course of the conflict

The insurgency formally resumed with an ambush at Kroh–Betong in June 1968 that killed 17 members of the security forces. The MCP's armed wing, reorganised as the Malayan National Liberation Army, conducted assassinations — including of the Inspector-General of Police in 1974 and the Perak chief police officer in 1975 — ambushes, and sabotage, while building underground support networks, particularly among segments of the ethnic Chinese community. Splinter factions, the CPM–Marxist-Leninist and CPM–Revolutionary Faction, broke away in the early 1970s. In Sarawak, the North Kalimantan Communist Party waged a parallel insurgency that wound down by 1990.

Malaysia responded with large-scale operations such as Ops Selamat Sawadee, joint operations with Thailand, road-building and development programmes in the border region ("KESBAN" security-and-development doctrine), and resettlement and intelligence efforts. The conflict remained a low-intensity war concentrated in the northern peninsula; total casualties were far lower than in the first Emergency, with several hundred killed on each side over 21 years.

Peace settlement

With the Cold War ending and China having ceased support for Southeast Asian communist insurgencies, tripartite negotiations between Malaysia, Thailand, and the MCP produced the Peace Agreement of Hat Yai, signed on 2 December 1989. The MCP agreed to disband its armed units and destroy its weapons; in return, former guerrillas were permitted to return to Malaysia or settle in "peace villages" in southern Thailand with Thai citizenship. Chin Peng's own applications to return to Malaysia were ultimately rejected by the Malaysian courts, and he died in exile in Bangkok in 2013.
