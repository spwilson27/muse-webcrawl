Spice Girls
Origin: London, England
Years active: 1994–2000; reunions 2007–2008, 2012, 2018–present
Members: Mel B, Mel C, Emma Bunton, Geri Halliwell, Victoria Beckham

The Spice Girls are an English girl group formed in 1994, consisting of Mel B ("Scary Spice"), Mel C ("Sporty Spice"), Emma Bunton ("Baby Spice"), Geri Halliwell ("Ginger Spice") and Victoria Beckham ("Posh Spice"). They are widely regarded as the most successful British pop act since the Beatles and the best-selling female group of all time, having sold over 100 million records worldwide.

Career

Their debut single "Wannabe" (1996) topped charts in 37 countries, and the debut album Spice became the best-selling album by a female group in history. Follow-up singles including "Say You'll Be There", "2 Become 1" and "Spice Up Your Life", from the second album Spiceworld, cemented their dominance; the group also starred in the 1997 film Spice World. Halliwell left the group in 1998, and after the third album Forever (2000) the remaining members went on indefinite hiatus.

Cultural impact

The group popularised the slogan "girl power" and became icons of 1990s British popular culture, associated with Cool Britannia and an unprecedented merchandising phenomenon. They reunited for a world tour in 2007–08, performed at the closing ceremony of the 2012 London Olympics, and toured UK stadiums in 2019 without Beckham.
