Diables Bleus

Chasseurs alpins in the dark blue uniform that gave them their nickname

Diables Bleus (French: "Blue Devils") is the nickname of the chasseurs alpins, the elite mountain infantry of the French Army. The name derives from the dark blue uniform worn by the chasseurs, in contrast to the red trousers of the line infantry, and was popularised during the First World War — attributed variously to French admiration and to German respect for their tenacity in the Vosges mountains.

History

The chasseurs alpins were created in 1888 by converting battalions of chasseurs à pied for service in the Alps, in response to Italy's Alpini. In the First World War they fought with distinction in the Vosges, at the Hartmannswillerkopf and elsewhere, suffering heavy losses. In the Second World War they fought in Norway at Narvik in 1940 and later in the French Resistance and the liberation campaigns.

Traditions

The chasseurs alpins retain distinctive traditions, including the wide dark-blue beret known as the tarte, the blue dress uniform, and the hunting horn (cor de chasse) as their emblem. Today their role is carried on by the 27th Mountain Infantry Brigade, based in the French Alps.

The name "Diables Bleus" has also been adopted by other organisations, including French sporting clubs.
