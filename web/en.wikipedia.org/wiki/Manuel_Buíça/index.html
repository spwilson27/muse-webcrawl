Manuel dos Reis da Silva Buíça (30 December 1876 – 1 February 1908) was a Portuguese schoolteacher and former army sergeant who assassinated King Carlos I of Portugal and his elder son, Luís Filipe, Prince Royal, in the Lisbon Regicide of 1 February 1908.

Life

Buíça was born in Bouçoães, in the municipality of Valpaços. He served in the cavalry, reaching the rank of sergeant, and was a distinguished marksman. After leaving the army he worked as a teacher. Politically active in republican and Carbonária circles, he became embittered by the authoritarian government of João Franco under King Carlos.

The regicide

On 1 February 1908, as the royal family returned to Lisbon and their open landau crossed the Terreiro do Paço, Buíça stepped forward with a Winchester carbine concealed under his coat. Shooting from a distance of a few metres, he fired five shots, killing King Carlos and fatally wounding Prince Luís Filipe, who died shortly afterwards. Buíça was killed on the spot by police and royal guards, together with his accomplice Alfredo Luís da Costa, who had attacked the carriage with a revolver.

The assassinations gravely weakened the Portuguese monarchy; two years later, the revolution of 5 October 1910 proclaimed the Portuguese First Republic.
