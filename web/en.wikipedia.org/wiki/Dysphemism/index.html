Dysphemism

A dysphemism is an expression with connotations that are offensive, disparaging or harsher than a neutral alternative for the same referent. It is the opposite of a euphemism: where a euphemism softens, a dysphemism deliberately degrades, mocks or provokes — for example calling a politician a "stooge" instead of a "representative", or referring to a disliked food as "slop".

Uses

Dysphemisms are used in invective, satire, propaganda and political rhetoric to frame opponents unfavourably; derogatory nicknames for groups, places and institutions are common examples. They also occur in-group as jocular abuse among friends, where the offensiveness signals intimacy rather than hostility. The continuum between neutral terms, euphemisms and dysphemisms for a single concept is sometimes called the "euphemism treadmill" when applied to terms that repeatedly acquire negative associations.

In linguistics

The term is used in semantics and pragmatics to analyse how word choice expresses attitude. Allan and Burridge's work on euphemism and dysphemism treats both as strategies for managing face and social taboo in communication.
