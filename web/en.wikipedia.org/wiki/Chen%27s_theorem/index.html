Chen's theorem

In number theory, Chen's theorem states that every sufficiently large even number can be written as the sum of two primes, or of a prime and a semiprime (the product of two primes). The theorem, first proved by Chinese mathematician Chen Jingrun in 1966, with further details of the proof in 1973, was a major step toward Goldbach's conjecture.

Statement

Chen proved that every sufficiently large even integer 2N can be represented in the form 2N = p + P2, where p is prime and P2 is either prime or the product of exactly two primes.

Proof and developments

Chen's proof used advanced sieve methods, including weighted sieves and the large sieve. Subsequent work by others refined the result, but the full Goldbach conjecture — that every even number greater than 2 is the sum of two primes — remains unproved.
