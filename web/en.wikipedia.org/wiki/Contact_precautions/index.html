Contact precautions

Contact precautions are a set of infection control measures used in healthcare settings to prevent the transmission of infectious agents that are spread by direct or indirect contact with a patient or the patient's environment. They are one category of transmission-based precautions, used in addition to standard precautions, and are applied for patients known or suspected to be infected or colonised with epidemiologically important organisms such as MRSA, vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE), Clostridioides difficile, and norovirus.

PurposePrevent contact transmission of pathogens
Key elementsGloves, gowns, single room or cohorting, dedicated equipment, environmental cleaning
GuidelineCDC/HICPAC isolation precautions guidelines

Measures

Under contact precautions, healthcare personnel wear gloves and a gown when entering the patient's room or interacting with the patient, and remove them before leaving. Patients are placed in a single room where possible, or cohorted with patients carrying the same organism. Patient-care equipment is dedicated to one patient or cleaned and disinfected between uses, and rooms receive enhanced environmental cleaning. Hand hygiene — with soap and water where C. difficile or norovirus is involved, since alcohol-based rubs are ineffective against spores and some viruses — is essential.

Rationale

Many healthcare-associated pathogens survive on surfaces and on the hands of personnel, making contact the most common mode of transmission in hospitals. Transmission-based precautions supplement standard precautions, which apply to all patients regardless of diagnosis.
