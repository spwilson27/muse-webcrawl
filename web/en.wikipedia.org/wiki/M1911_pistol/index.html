M1911 pistol

The M1911 (also known as the Colt 1911) is a single-action, recoil-operated, semi-automatic pistol chambered for the .45 ACP cartridge. Designed by John Browning, it was the standard-issue sidearm of the United States Armed Forces from 1911 to 1985.

M1911
Type: Semi-automatic pistol
Designer: John Browning
Cartridge: .45 ACP
Service: 1911–present
Manufacturer: Colt's Manufacturing Company and others

History

Adopted after extensive trials, the M1911 served in both World Wars, Korea and Vietnam. An improved version, the M1911A1, was standardised in 1924 with changes to the grip safety, trigger and frame.

Design

The pistol uses a short-recoil tilting-barrel system with a swinging link, a single-stack magazine holding seven rounds, and both grip and manual thumb safeties. Its design has been widely copied and remains popular in civilian shooting sports and custom gunsmithing.
