Hammer Museum

The Hammer Museum, officially the Armand Hammer Museum of Art and Cultural Center, is an art museum and cultural centre in the Westwood district of Los Angeles, California, affiliated with the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). It was founded in 1990 by Armand Hammer, the chairman of Occidental Petroleum, to house his personal art collection. Following Hammer's death shortly after its opening, the museum came under UCLA's management in 1994 and reoriented itself toward contemporary art.

Collections and programmes

The museum's holdings include the Armand Hammer Collection of European and American paintings (with works by artists such as Rembrandt, Gustave Moreau and John Singer Sargent), the Hammer Contemporary Collection, and the Grunwald Center for the Graphic Arts, which holds one of the finest collections of prints and drawings on the US West Coast. The Hammer is known for free admission (made permanent in 2014), hundreds of free public programmes each year, and its biennial Made in L.A., launched in 2012 to showcase Los Angeles artists.

Building

The museum occupies a building designed by Edward Larrabee Barnes adjacent to the Occidental Petroleum tower in Westwood Village. A renovation and expansion programme designed by Michael Maltzan Architecture, completed in stages into the 2020s, added a new entrance, galleries and an auditorium and renamed the site along Wilshire Boulevard.
