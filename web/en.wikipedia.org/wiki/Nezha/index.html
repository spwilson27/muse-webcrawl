Nezha (Chinese: 哪吒; pinyin: Nézhā) is a protection deity in Chinese mythology, Taoism, and folk religion, usually depicted as a rebellious child warrior. The figure derives ultimately from the Indian child god Nalakuvara via Buddhist transmission, and became one of the most beloved characters of the Ming-dynasty novels Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen Yanyi) and Journey to the West.

Mythology

According to Investiture of the Gods, Nezha was born to the military commander Li Jing and Lady Yin after a pregnancy lasting three years and six months, emerging as a ball of flesh that split open to reveal a boy already wearing the Universe Ring and Red Armillary Sash. Taught by the immortal Taiyi Zhenren, the unruly child killed Ao Bing, the third son of the Dragon King Ao Guang, while bathing in the sea, provoking a confrontation that drove Nezha to suicide — returning his flesh to his mother and bones to his father — to spare his family. Taiyi Zhenren then rebuilt his body from lotus roots and blossoms, giving him the Fire-tipped Spear and Wind Fire Wheels. After further conflict, father and son were reconciled through the intervention of the Buddha-given golden pagoda, which Li Jing thereafter carried as the Pagoda-bearing Heavenly King. In Journey to the West, Nezha serves Heaven as a demon-quelling marshal and fights Sun Wukong.

Worship and culture

Nezha is worshipped as a guardian of children and, in Taiwan, as a patron of drivers and of the techno-dance troupe tradition of Electric-Techno Neon Gods performances. He remains a staple of Chinese screen adaptations, from the 1979 Shanghai animation Nezha Conquers the Dragon King to the animated blockbuster Ne Zha (2019) and its sequel Ne Zha 2 (2025), among the highest-grossing animated films of all time.
