Global Ocean Observing System

The Global Ocean Observing System (GOOS) is a global system for sustained ocean observations comprising the oceanographic component of the Global Earth Observing System of Systems (GEOSS). It is administered by the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC) of UNESCO, and joins the Global Climate Observing System (GCOS) and the Global Terrestrial Observing System, among others.

GOOS coordinates in situ and satellite observations of the oceans — including sea surface temperature, salinity, currents, sea level, and biogeochemical variables — through observing networks such as the Argo array of profiling floats, drifting and moored buoys, tide gauges, and research vessels. The system comprises a global module concerned with the ocean's role in climate, regional alliances, and coastal observing systems serving operational oceanography and coastal management.

Purpose

GOOS exists to define, design, and implement an integrated system for detecting and predicting changes in the marine environment, supporting climate research, weather and ocean forecasting, maritime safety, and the sustainable use of ocean resources.
