Giorgione (c. 1477/78–1510), born Giorgio Barbarelli da Castelfranco, was an Italian painter of the Venetian school during the High Renaissance. Despite a career cut short by his death from plague at about thirty-two, he is regarded as one of the most influential and enigmatic artists of the Renaissance, credited with introducing a new mood of poetic reverie into painting.

Life

Giorgione was born in the small town of Castelfranco Veneto. He moved to Venice and, according to Giorgio Vasari, apprenticed under Giovanni Bellini. The nickname "Giorgione" ("big George") reportedly referred to his stature and gentle manner. He received commissions from Venetian patricians and from Queen Caterina Cornaro's court at Asolo. He died in Venice during the plague of 1510.

Works

Very few paintings can be securely attributed to Giorgione. The most famous is The Tempest (c. 1508), whose subject — a soldier and a nursing woman in a stormy landscape — has never been conclusively explained. His Sleeping Venus was left unfinished at his death and completed by his younger colleague Titian, who had worked in Giorgione's orbit. Other works include the Castelfranco Madonna, The Three Philosophers and the Pastoral Concert (attribution disputed between Giorgione and Titian).

Legacy

Giorgione's soft modelling, atmospheric colour and private, poetic subjects profoundly influenced Titian and the whole development of Venetian painting.
