Bromine
Symbol: Br
Atomic number: 35

Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

Bromine is the chemical element with the symbol Br and atomic number 35. It is one of only two elements liquid at room temperature (with mercury): a dense, red-brown, fuming liquid that evaporates into a choking vapour of the same colour.

Bromine compounds are used in flame retardants, photographic film (silver bromide), fumigants and drilling fluids. Bromine is extracted from seawater and from brines such as those of the Dead Sea.

[Image: A sealed ampoule of red-brown liquid bromine with vapour above it]

History

Antoine Balard discovered bromine in 1826 in the salt marshes of Montpellier, France; Carl Jacob Löwig had independently isolated it in 1825 but published later. The name comes from the Greek bromos, stench.

Safety

Liquid bromine causes severe chemical burns and its vapour damages the lungs, so it is handled in sealed ampoules in teaching laboratories.
