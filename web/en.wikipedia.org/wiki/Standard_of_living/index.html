Standard of living

The standard of living is the level of income, comforts and services available to an individual, community or society. It encompasses material factors such as employment, housing, nutrition, clothing and consumer goods, and may also include access to education, healthcare, infrastructure, life expectancy and leisure. It differs from quality of life, which adds subjective well-being and non-material factors such as freedom, environment and security.

Measurement

The most common measure is real income per person — GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power and inflation. Composite indices include the Human Development Index (combining income, life expectancy and education), the OECD Better Life Index, and measures of the distribution of income such as the Gini coefficient, since an average can conceal wide disparities. The "decent living standard" concept counts physical infrastructure available to households: electricity, water, sanitation, durable housing and communications.

History

Standards of living were broadly flat for most of human history before rising dramatically with the Industrial Revolution, first in Britain and then across Western Europe, North America and East Asia. Historians debate when real wages began sustained growth and how far early industrial workers' living standards initially fell — the "standard of living debate" about the British Industrial Revolution. Since the mid-20th century, global life expectancy and literacy have risen sharply, and the UN Sustainable Development Goals frame many targets in living-standard terms.
