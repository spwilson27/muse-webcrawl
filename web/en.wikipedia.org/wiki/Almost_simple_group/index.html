Almost simple group

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In group theory, a group G is called almost simple if it fits between a non-abelian simple group and its automorphism group: that is, there is a (finite or infinite) non-abelian simple group T such that T ≤ G ≤ Aut(T), where T is identified with its inner automorphism group. Equivalently, an almost simple group is a group whose only proper nontrivial characteristic structure is a single non-abelian simple "layer".

Examples

Every non-abelian simple group is almost simple, as is its full automorphism group. The symmetric group S5 is almost simple because it is the automorphism group of the alternating group A5. The projective general linear groups PGL(n, q) are almost simple extensions of the simple groups PSL(n, q); other standard examples include the full groups of symmetries of exceptional simple groups obtained by adding diagonal, field and graph automorphisms.

Importance

Almost simple groups are a basic unit in the study of finite permutation groups and in the classification programme: the O'Nan–Scott theorem organises primitive permutation groups in terms of their socles, of which the almost simple case is a central type. Their maximal subgroups have been extensively studied in connection with the classification of finite simple groups.
