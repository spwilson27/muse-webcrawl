Suspension polymerization

Suspension polymerization is a heterogeneous free-radical polymerization process in which one or more monomers are dispersed as droplets in a continuous phase, usually water, and polymerized with a monomer-soluble initiator. vigorous agitation keeps the droplets suspended, and a stabiliser (suspending agent) such as polyvinyl alcohol or inorganic powders prevents them from coalescing.

Process

Each droplet behaves like a tiny bulk polymerization reactor. As polymerization proceeds the droplets become sticky and finally harden into solid polymer beads, typically 0.05–2 mm in diameter, which are easily filtered, washed and dried. The water phase removes the heat of reaction efficiently, giving good temperature control.

Applications

The process is used commercially to make polyvinyl chloride (PVC), polystyrene and expandable polystyrene, poly(methyl methacrylate), and ion-exchange resins. Compared with emulsion polymerization, the product is obtained as beads rather than a latex, and residual surfactant levels are low.
