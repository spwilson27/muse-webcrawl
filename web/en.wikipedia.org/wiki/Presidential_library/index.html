Presidential library

A presidential library complex in the United States

A presidential library is a repository of papers, records, and other historical materials relating to a president of the United States, typically combined with a museum exhibiting artifacts from the president's life and administration. The system of presidential libraries administered by the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) comprises libraries for presidents from Herbert Hoover onward.

Origins

Before the mid-20th century, presidential papers were considered the personal property of the president, and many were lost, destroyed, or scattered. Franklin D. Roosevelt broke with tradition by donating his papers to the federal government and raising private funds to build a library on his estate at Hyde Park, New York, which opened in 1941. Successors followed the pattern: a private foundation raises money to build a library and museum, which is then turned over to NARA to operate.

Legislation

The Presidential Libraries Act of 1955 formalized the system, authorizing the government to accept and maintain presidential libraries. The Presidential Records Act of 1978 ended the tradition of private ownership by declaring that presidential records created after January 20, 1981, belong to the United States, with NARA taking custody at the end of an administration. Earlier presidential libraries operate under deeds of gift with varying restrictions on access.

The libraries

The NARA system includes the libraries of Herbert Hoover (West Branch, Iowa), Franklin D. Roosevelt (Hyde Park, New York), Harry S. Truman (Independence, Missouri), Dwight D. Eisenhower (Abilene, Kansas), John F. Kennedy (Boston, Massachusetts), Lyndon B. Johnson (Austin, Texas), Richard Nixon (Yorba Linda, California), Gerald Ford (Ann Arbor and Grand Rapids, Michigan), Jimmy Carter (Atlanta, Georgia), Ronald Reagan (Simi Valley, California), George H. W. Bush (College Station, Texas), Bill Clinton (Little Rock, Arkansas), and George W. Bush (Dallas, Texas). Centers for Barack Obama and Donald Trump have been planned or developed under modified arrangements with the National Archives.

Museums and research

Each library includes a museum open to the public, often featuring replicas of the Oval Office as it appeared during that president's tenure, along with archives that make millions of pages of documents, photographs, and recordings available to researchers.
