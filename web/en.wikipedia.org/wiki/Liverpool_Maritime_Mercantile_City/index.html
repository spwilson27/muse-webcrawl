Liverpool – Maritime Mercantile City was a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Liverpool, England, inscribed in 2004 in recognition of the city's role as one of the world's great trading ports in the 18th and 19th centuries and its pioneering dock system and monumental commercial architecture. After a decade on the List of World Heritage in Danger, the property was deleted from the World Heritage List in July 2021 because of waterfront development — only the third delisting in the convention's history, after the Arabian Oryx Sanctuary (2007) and the Dresden Elbe Valley (2009).

The site

The inscribed area comprised six zones of the historic centre and docklands, including the Pier Head with its "Three Graces" — the Royal Liver Building, the Cunard Building and the Port of Liverpool Building — the Albert Dock (1846, with its revolutionary cast-iron, brick and stone fireproof warehouses), the Stanley Dock, the commercial district around Castle Street and Old Hall Street, and St George's Hall and the cultural quarter. UNESCO praised Liverpool as the supreme example of a commercial port city at the time of Britain's greatest global influence, noting its associations with the transatlantic slave trade, emigration and mass passenger shipping.

Danger listing and delisting

UNESCO placed the site in danger in 2012 over the proposed Liverpool Waters regeneration scheme — up to £5.5 billion of towers planned for the northern docks — and repeatedly warned that allocations and permissions were eroding the skyline and dock fabric that justified inscription. Approval of Everton FC's new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock in 2021 was the final straw: the World Heritage Committee, meeting online in an extended session chaired from Fuzhou in July 2021, voted narrowly to delete the property, citing "irreversible loss of attributes conveying the outstanding universal value". The decision was condemned by the city council and UK government as unjust while being welcomed by heritage campaigners who had opposed the developments.
