The secretary of state for justice is a secretary of state in the Government of the United Kingdom, with responsibility for the Ministry of Justice. The incumbent is a member of the Cabinet of the United Kingdom. Since its creation, the office has been held in conjunction with the ancient office of Lord Chancellor.

History

The post was created on 9 May 2007, when the Department for Constitutional Affairs was renamed the Ministry of Justice and took over responsibility for prisons, probation and sentencing policy from the Home Office. Jack Straw became the first secretary of state for justice while also serving as Lord Chancellor — the first Lord Chancellor to sit in the House of Commons.

Responsibilities

The justice secretary oversees the courts and tribunals of England and Wales, the prison and probation services, legal aid, and policy on sentencing and criminal law reform. As Lord Chancellor, the holder also has a constitutional duty to uphold the rule of law and the independence of the judiciary, and is responsible for the appointment process for judges through the Judicial Appointments Commission.
