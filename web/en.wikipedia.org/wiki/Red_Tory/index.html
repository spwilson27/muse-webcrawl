Red Tory

A Red Tory is an adherent of a centre-right political philosophy derived from the Tory tradition, predominantly in Canada but also found in Britain. Red Tories support social programs and a degree of economic interventionism while valuing tradition, community, and social order; they are generally opposed to the free-market orthodoxy of neoliberal "Blue Tories."

Origins in Canada

The term arose in Canadian politics to describe the progressive wing of the Progressive Conservative Party. The archetypal Red Tory was Ontario premier Bill Davis, whose governments invested heavily in education and public transit. The philosophy is associated with the communitarian thought of philosopher George Grant, whose Lament for a Nation (1965) mourned the erosion of a conservative, collectivist Canadian identity. Red Tories typically supported the welfare state, public broadcasting, and an independent economic nationalism.

In Britain

In the United Kingdom, "Red Toryism" was promoted by writer Phillip Blond as a communitarian alternative to both market liberalism and state socialism, emphasizing localism, cooperatives, and the revival of civil society.
