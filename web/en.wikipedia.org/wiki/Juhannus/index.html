Juhannus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Juhannus bonfire at midsummer]

Juhannus is the Finnish celebration of Midsummer, held on the Saturday between 20 and 26 June, with festivities beginning on Midsummer Eve. It is one of the most important holidays in Finland, comparable in significance to Christmas, and takes its name from John the Baptist (Johannes in Finnish), whose feast day falls on 24 June.

Traditions

Many Finns travel to summer cottages (mökki) in the countryside or by lakes. Central traditions include lighting large bonfires (kokko), raising a midsummer pole, sauna bathing, swimming, and gathering with family and friends. In northern Finland the midnight sun means the night never fully darkens.

Origins

The festival has pre-Christian roots as a celebration of the summer solstice and fertility, later Christianized as the feast of St. John. Old folk customs included spells and rituals, such as a maiden picking seven flowers to place under her pillow to dream of her future spouse.
