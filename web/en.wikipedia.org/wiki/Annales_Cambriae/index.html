Annales Cambriae

[Image: A manuscript page of medieval Welsh annals]

The Annales Cambriae ("Annals of Wales") are a set of medieval Latin chronicles of Welsh history, with entries covering the years from about 447 to 954 in the earliest version (later recensions extend to the 13th century). They were probably compiled at St Davids in Dyfed in the mid-10th century, drawing on older Irish and northern British material and a lost chronicle.

Contents and importance

The annals are terse — often a single line per year — but they preserve early entries that matter far beyond Wales. Two entries are famous in Arthurian studies: one for 516 recording the Battle of Badon, "in which Arthur carried the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ on his shoulders for three days and three nights," and one for 537 recording the Battle of Camlann, "in which Arthur and Medraut fell." Other entries name figures such as Maelgwn Gwynedd and record battles, plagues and the deaths of kings and bishops.

Versions

Three principal recensions survive, conventionally labelled A (Harley MS 3859, which also contains the Historia Brittonum), B and C, plus Welsh-language adaptations that fed into the Brut y Tywysogion.
