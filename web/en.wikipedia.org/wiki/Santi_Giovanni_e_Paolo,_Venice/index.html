Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Venice

The Basilica di San Zanipolo, formally the Basilica of Saints John and Paul (Italian: Santi Giovanni e Paolo), is one of the largest churches in Venice, Italy. Built by the Dominican Order in the Italian Gothic style, it served as the burial place of the doges of Venice after the 15th century and is sometimes called the Pantheon of Venice.

History

Legend holds that Doge Jacopo Tiepolo donated the site to the Dominicans after dreaming of white doves at the spot. The present church was constructed during the 13th and 14th centuries and consecrated in 1430. The great west portal was completed in 1458.

Interior and monuments

The vast interior contains the funerary monuments of some 25 doges, including Pietro Mocenigo, Andrea Vendramin, and Giovanni Mocenigo. Artworks include Giovanni Bellini's polyptych of Saint Vincent Ferrer, Lorenzo Lotto's Saint Antoninus Giving Alms, and Paolo Veronese's ceiling paintings in the Chapel of the Rosary. The equestrian monument to Bartolomeo Colleoni by Verrocchio stands in the campo outside the church.
