Sanquhar Declaration

The Sanquhar Declaration was a declaration made by followers of Richard Cameron in the town of Sanquhar in southwest Scotland on 22 June 1680. In it, the radical Presbyterian Covenanters disowned allegiance to Charles II and declared war against him as a tyrant and usurper.

Background

After the Restoration of Charles II in 1660, the Scottish government imposed episcopacy on the Church of Scotland, provoking resistance among Presbyterian Covenanters, whose conventicles were suppressed by force. Armed clashes culminated in the Covenanter victory at Drumclog and defeat at Bothwell Bridge in 1679. The most uncompromising Covenanters, the Cameronians, refused any indulgence or compromise with the crown.

The declaration

On 22 June 1680, Michael Cameron, brother of the preacher Richard Cameron, rode into Sanquhar with about twenty armed horsemen, sang a psalm, and read the declaration at the market cross. It renounced Charles Stuart as having "forfeited the crown" by perjury and tyranny, and declared war against him and his supporters. A month later Richard Cameron was killed in a skirmish at Airds Moss. The declaration became a defining statement of the Cameronian movement and its text was renewed on later occasions by the United Societies.
