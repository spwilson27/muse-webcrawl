Homily

A homily (Greek: ὁμιλία, homilía, meaning "discourse" or "conversation") is a commentary that follows a reading of scripture and is given by a member of the clergy during a religious service. In Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, and Eastern Orthodox churches, a homily is usually given during the Mass (or Divine Liturgy) at the end of the Liturgy of the Word. In many traditions it is distinguished from a sermon, which is generally a freestanding discourse on a chosen topic, whereas the homily is expected to explain and apply the particular scripture readings of the day.

Meaning and usage

The word originally referred to any popular discourse on a religious subject. The Catholic Encyclopedia suggests that the distinction between sermon and homily is that a sermon is delivered on a selected theme with an argumentative or instructional structure, while in a homily the preacher comments on the text just read, much as a teacher would explain a lesson. Because of this, homilies tend to be shorter and more directly tied to the day's readings.

History

The practice descends from the synagogue tradition of expounding the Law and the Prophets after their public reading, a custom reflected in the New Testament account of Jesus reading from Isaiah at Nazareth. Early Church Fathers such as Origen, John Chrysostom, and Augustine of Hippo left large bodies of homilies, many of which survive as key sources for early Christian theology and biblical interpretation. Chrysostom's name, meaning "golden-mouthed," reflects his reputation as a preacher.

Rites and rules

In the Roman Rite, the Second Vatican Council's reforms restored the homily as an integral part of the liturgy itself. Under current Catholic canon law, a homily is to be given on Sundays and holy days of obligation at Masses attended by the people, and it is reserved to a priest or deacon. In Eastern Orthodox practice, the homily typically follows the Gospel reading or comes near the end of the Divine Liturgy. Collections of model homilies, called homiliaries, were widely used by clergy throughout the Middle Ages.

In everyday modern English, "homily" can also be used more loosely, sometimes pejoratively, to mean a tedious moralizing lecture or a short saying about proper conduct.
