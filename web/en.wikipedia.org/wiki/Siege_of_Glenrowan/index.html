Siege of Glenrowan

Date: 27–28 June 1880
Location: Glenrowan, Victoria, Australia
Result: Kelly Gang destroyed; Ned Kelly captured

The siege of Glenrowan was the final confrontation between the Kelly Gang of bushrangers and the Victoria Police, fought on 27–28 June 1880 at Glenrowan, Victoria, Australia. It ended with the deaths of gang members Joe Byrne, Dan Kelly, and Steve Hart, and the capture of Ned Kelly.

Background

The gang planned to derail a police train by tearing up the rails near Glenrowan, then ambush the survivors. They occupied Ann Jones's Glenrowan Inn and held some sixty hostages. Schoolteacher Thomas Curnow, released from the inn, flagged down the train with a red scarf and a candle, averting the ambush.

The siege

Police surrounded the inn before dawn on 28 June. Ned Kelly, wearing his famous suit of homemade plate armour, emerged from the bush behind police lines and attacked, but was brought down by shots to his unarmoured legs. Joe Byrne was killed inside the inn. Police set the building alight in the afternoon; Dan Kelly and Steve Hart died inside, apparently by their own hands.

Aftermath

Ned Kelly was tried in Melbourne and hanged on 11 November 1880. The siege became one of the most famous episodes in Australian folklore.
