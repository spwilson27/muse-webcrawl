The Jogye Order, officially the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism (Korean: 대한불교조계종), is the largest Buddhist order in South Korea, representing the mainstream of Korean Seon (Zen) Buddhism. It administers roughly a quarter of Korea's temples—some 3,100, including major temples such as Tongdosa, Bulguksa and Haeinsa—and its headquarters are at Jogyesa in central Seoul.

History

The order takes its name from Mount Caoxi (Jogye in Korean), associated with Huineng, the Sixth Patriarch of Chan Buddhism. Korean Seon was consolidated in the Goryeo dynasty by the monk Jinul (1158–1210), founder of Songgwangsa, and later unified under Taego Bou in the 14th century. During the Joseon dynasty Buddhism was suppressed in favour of Confucianism, and monks retreated to mountain temples.

Under Japanese colonial rule, temple Buddhism was reorganised; after liberation, a dispute between celibate monks and married monks (a legacy of colonial policy) led in the 1950s–60s to the "purification movement". In 1962 the celibate Jogye Order was established as a unified integrated order. A smaller married order, the Taego Order, separated in 1969–70.

Practice and organisation

The Jogye Order emphasises Seon meditation (ganhwa Seon, the investigation of the hwadu), combined with doctrinal study and Pure Land practices. Its monastics are celibate, shave their heads and wear grey robes. The order is led by a Supreme Patriarch (Jongjeong) and an executive president, and runs retreat seasons (angeo) at its temples each summer and winter.
