Unmanned aerial vehicle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A white quadcopter drone flying against a blue sky with a camera gimbal]
A consumer quadcopter drone.

An unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV, or drone) is an aircraft with no human pilot aboard, flown remotely or autonomously. Military UAVs such as the MQ-9 Reaper conduct surveillance and strikes; small multirotor drones serve photography, agriculture, inspection and delivery trials, and have transformed warfare in the 2020s, notably in Ukraine, where cheap first-person-view drones hunt armour and artillery at scale.

History

Aerial torpedoes and target drones date to the First World War era; radio-controlled \'Queen Bee\' targets gave English the word \'drone\'. Vietnam-era reconnaissance drones (Firebee) and Israeli decoy/scout drones (1982 Lebanon war) showed the concept, and armed Predators (from 2001) made UAVs central to US operations.

Civil drones

Quadcopters from DJI (founded 2006) dominate the consumer market; regulators responded with registration, remote ID and altitude/geofencing rules after airport scares (Gatwick 2018). Commercial uses include surveying, film-making, crop spraying and medical delivery (Zipline in Rwanda and Ghana).

Warfare in the 2020s

The Russo-Ukrainian war industrialised small drones: FPV strike drones, long-range one-way attack drones (Shahed types) and drone boats reshaped tactics, air defence economics and naval operations, pushing counter-drone systems and electronic warfare to the fore.
