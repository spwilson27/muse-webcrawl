Progesterone (P4) is an endogenous steroid hormone and the principal human progestogen. It is essential to the menstrual cycle and to the establishment and maintenance of pregnancy, and also serves as a biochemical intermediate in the production of other steroid hormones, including corticosteroids and sex hormones.

Biological function

After ovulation, the corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which converts the oestrogen-primed endometrium into a secretory lining receptive to implantation, reduces uterine contractions, and thickens cervical mucus. If pregnancy occurs, production shifts to the placenta around the end of the first trimester; if not, progesterone falls and menstruation follows. Progesterone also prepares the breasts for lactation and modestly raises basal body temperature — a fact used in fertility-awareness methods.

Chemistry and synthesis

Progesterone is synthesised from cholesterol via pregnenolone. It was isolated and named in the 1930s (the name combines progestational steroidal ketone). Industrial production historically derived from diosgenin in yams via the Marker degradation.

Medical use

As a medication, progesterone is used in hormone replacement therapy, for supporting early pregnancy and preventing preterm birth in selected patients, in fertility treatment, and (usually as synthetic progestins rather than progesterone itself) in hormonal contraception. It is available orally — where first-pass metabolism is extensive — and by vaginal, intramuscular and other routes.
