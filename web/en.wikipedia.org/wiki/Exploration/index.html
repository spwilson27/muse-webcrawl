Exploration

Exploration is the act of travelling through an unfamiliar area in order to learn about it or search for resources. It occurs in many contexts, from the geographic exploration of new lands to scientific research, spaceflight, and the search for mineral and energy deposits.

Geographic exploration

Human exploration has proceeded in waves: Polynesian voyaging across the Pacific, the Norse voyages to North America, the European Age of Discovery (roughly the 15th to 17th centuries) which produced the first global maps, and the scientific expeditions of the 18th and 19th centuries. The poles were reached in the early 20th century, and the deep ocean and outer space remain frontiers.

Space exploration

Beginning with Sputnik 1 in 1957, space exploration has included crewed lunar landings, robotic probes to every planet, and permanent human presence aboard the International Space Station.

Motivations and consequences

Exploration has been driven by trade, empire, science, prestige, and curiosity. Its consequences have been profound and double-edged: new knowledge and exchange, but also colonisation, displacement of indigenous peoples, and the spread of disease.
