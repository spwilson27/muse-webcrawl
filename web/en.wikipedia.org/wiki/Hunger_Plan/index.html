Hunger Plan

The Hunger Plan (German: Hungerplan) was a Nazi German strategy developed during the Second World War to seize food supplies from the occupied Soviet Union and redirect them to Germany and German forces, deliberately consigning tens of millions of Soviet civilians to starvation.

Background and implementation

Drafted in spring 1941 under State Secretary Herbert Backe ahead of Operation Barbarossa, the plan treated the food surpluses of Ukraine and southern Russia as essential to Germany's war economy and assumed the "superfluous" urban population of the deficit zones would starve or flee to Siberia. In practice, occupation authorities requisitioned grain, blockaded cities such as Leningrad and Kharkiv, and starved Soviet prisoners of war on an enormous scale.

Death toll

Historians estimate that the plan and related policies killed several million people: roughly 3.3 million Soviet POWs died in German custody, many by starvation, alongside perhaps a million deaths in besieged Leningrad and millions more civilians across the occupied territories. The Hunger Plan is regarded as a central element of the Nazi war of annihilation in the East and was examined at the Nuremberg trials.
