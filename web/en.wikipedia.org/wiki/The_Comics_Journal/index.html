The Comics Journal

The Comics Journal, often abbreviated TCJ, is an American magazine of news and criticism of the comics medium, published by Fantagraphics Books of Seattle. Founded in 1976 as the successor to The Nostalgia Journal, it became known under publisher Gary Groth and editors including Kim Thompson for rigorous, often combative journalism and long, in-depth interviews with cartoonists.

History and character

The magazine championed the view of comics as an art form, promoted alternative and independent comics, and campaigned against work-for-hire practices in the mainstream industry. Its interviews with creators such as Jack Kirby, Charles Schulz, Art Spiegelman and Robert Crumb are regarded as essential documents of comics history. Its critical tone also provoked controversies and lawsuits, and it was a central voice in debates over standards and taste in the medium.

Format changes

The print magazine appeared in various formats over decades, from newsprint tabloid to a large-format journal running to hundreds of pages. In 2011 the publication moved most of its activity online at tcj.com, with print editions continuing as annual or semi-annual anthology-style volumes under editors including Kristy Valenti, Dan Nadel and R. J. Casey.
