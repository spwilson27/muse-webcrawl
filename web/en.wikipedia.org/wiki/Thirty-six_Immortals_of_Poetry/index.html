Thirty-six Immortals of Poetry


Heian court poets of the Thirty-six Immortals

The Thirty-six Immortals of Poetry (Japanese: 三十六歌仙, Sanjūrokkasen) are a canonical group of Japanese poets of the Asuka, Nara, and Heian periods selected by the eleventh-century courtier and critic Fujiwara no Kintō (966–1041) as exemplars of Japanese poetic ability. The list became a fixture of Japanese literary culture, frequently depicted in paintings and woodblock prints.

The selection

Kintō's selection drew on the great imperial anthologies of waka, especially the Kokinshū and the Man'yōshū. The group comprises thirty-one men and five women. Among its most celebrated members are:

  • Kakinomoto no Hitomaro — the pre-eminent poet of the Man'yōshū
  • Yamabe no Akahito
  • Ōtomo no Yakamochi
  • Ariwara no Narihira
  • Ki no Tsurayuki — compiler of the Kokinshū
  • Ono no Komachi — famed for her passionate verse and legendary beauty
  • Henjō, Semimaru, Sosei, Fun'ya no Yasuhide, and Fujiwara no Okikaze

Legacy

The concept of the "poetry immortals" (kasen) was later extended: a group of six earlier masters was called the Six Immortals of Poetry (Rokkasen), and Murasaki Shikibu selected another set, the Thirty-six Female Immortals of Poetry. In the Edo period the Thirty-six Immortals became a popular subject for portrait albums and ukiyo-e, and their poems were used in the card game uta-garuta, based on the anthology Hyakunin Isshu.
