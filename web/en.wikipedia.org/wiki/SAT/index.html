The SAT is a standardized test widely used for college admissions in the United States. Since its debut in 1926, its name and scoring have changed several times. The test is administered by the College Board, a private, non-profit organization, and was developed to assess the readiness of high school students for college-level work.

The SAT measures literacy, numeracy, and writing skills that are needed for academic success in college. As of 2023–2024 the test is administered digitally and is divided into two sections: Reading and Writing, and Math. Each section is scored on a scale of 200 to 800 points, for a total possible score of 1600.

History

The SAT was adapted from an Army IQ test and first administered in 1926 to 8,040 candidates. It was the brainchild of Harvard president James Bryant Conant, who wanted a test that would identify talented students regardless of their background. The name originally stood for "Scholastic Aptitude Test," later "Scholastic Assessment Test," before becoming simply "SAT" in 1997.

Major revisions occurred in 1994, 2005 (when a writing section and a 2400-point scale were introduced), and 2016 (when the test returned to the 1600-point scale and the essay became optional). In 2022 the College Board announced the transition to a fully digital, adaptive test, which was completed for U.S. students in spring 2024.

Structure

The digital SAT is section-adaptive: performance on the first module of each section determines the difficulty of the second module. The Reading and Writing section contains short passages with one question each, covering craft and structure, information and ideas, standard English conventions, and expression of ideas. The Math section covers algebra, advanced math, problem-solving and data analysis, and geometry and trigonometry; a calculator is permitted throughout.

Scoring

Scores are reported on the 400–1600 scale, accompanied by section scores and percentiles comparing the test-taker with a nationally representative sample. The College Board also reports benchmark scores indicating college and career readiness.

Criticism

The SAT has been criticized for correlations between scores and family income, questions about cultural bias, and the growth of a commercial test-preparation industry. Partly in response, a growing test-optional movement led many colleges to suspend or drop standardized testing requirements, a trend that accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic, though several selective universities have since reinstated testing requirements.
