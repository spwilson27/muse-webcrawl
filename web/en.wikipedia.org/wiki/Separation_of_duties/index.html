Separation of duties

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Separation of duties (SoD), also called segregation of duties, is the concept of having more than one person required to complete a sensitive task. In business, the separation by requiring more than one person to complete a task is an internal control intended to prevent fraud and error. The concept is also applied in information security, where it is considered a real-world analogue of the two-person rule.

In business and accounting

In accounting, the classic arrangement separates custody of assets, recording of transactions, authorisation of transactions, and periodic reconciliation into different hands. For example, the person who receives cash should not be the person who records it or deposits it, and the person who writes cheques should not reconcile the bank statement. This makes fraud require collusion between two or more people, greatly increasing the chance of detection.

In information security

In IT, SoD is a fundamental control in access governance: conflicting duties — such as creating user accounts and approving access, or developing code and deploying it to production — are kept apart. Regulations and standards including the Sarbanes–Oxley Act, PCI DSS, and various ISO frameworks require or recommend SoD controls for financially significant systems. In role-based access control, SoD constraints may be static (a user may not hold two conflicting roles) or dynamic (a user may hold both roles but may not exercise both in the same session or transaction).
