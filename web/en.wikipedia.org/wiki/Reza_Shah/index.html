Reza Shah Pahlavi (15 March 1878 – 26 July 1944) was the Shah of Iran from 1925 to 1941 and the founder of the Pahlavi dynasty. Rising from the ranks of the Persian Cossack Brigade, he led the 1921 Persian coup d'état, became minister of war and then prime minister (1923), and, after the Majlis deposed the Qajar dynasty, was crowned Shah in 1925 (coronation 1926).

Modernisation programme

Reza Shah pursued an ambitious programme of modernisation and secularisation: building the Trans-Iranian Railway, roads and state factories; founding the University of Tehran (1934); reforming the judiciary and education along Western lines; introducing surnames, conscription and a national army; and promoting Persian nationalism, including the 1935 request that foreign governments use the name "Iran". His social reforms included the 1936 Kashf-e hijab decree banning the veil, which expanded women's public participation but was enforced coercively and remains controversial.

Rule and centralisation

His government suppressed tribal autonomy, curtailed the power of the Shia clergy, censored the press, and imprisoned or exiled opponents. Relations with foreign powers were tense over the Anglo-Persian Oil Company concession, partially renegotiated in 1933, and over his efforts to counterbalance British and Soviet influence with German ties in the 1930s.

Abdication and death

In August–September 1941, Britain and the Soviet Union invaded Iran to secure oil supplies and the Persian Corridor supply route to the USSR. Reza Shah abdicated in favour of his son Mohammad Reza Pahlavi on 16 September 1941 and was exiled, first to Mauritius and then to Johannesburg, South Africa, where he died in 1944. His mausoleum at Rey was demolished after the 1979 revolution, and his body was later reported discovered during construction work in 2018.

Legacy

Reza Shah is credited with laying the institutional foundations of the modern Iranian state, while critics emphasise his authoritarianism and the heavy-handed imposition of reforms. His reign remains a touchstone in Iranian political debates.
