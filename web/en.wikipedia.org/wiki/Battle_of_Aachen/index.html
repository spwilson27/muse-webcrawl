Battle of Aachen

The Battle of Aachen was fought in October 1944 between American and German forces during the Second World War. Aachen was the first major German city to be attacked by Allied ground forces, and after weeks of fighting through its fortified suburbs and streets, it became the first German city captured by the Allies, surrendering on 21 October 1944.

Background

After the pursuit across France, the US First Army reached the German border in September 1944 and confronted the Siegfried Line (Westwall). Aachen, Charlemagne's ancient capital, sat in a salient between the line's two fortified belts and had great symbolic value: Hitler ordered it held at all costs and forbade surrender.

The battle

American forces of the VII and XIX Corps encircled the city, completing the ring by mid-October despite a German attempt to relieve it. The 1st Infantry Division then fought a brutal house-to-house battle against the garrison under Colonel Gerhard Wilck, using direct-fire artillery and tanks against strongpoints. Much of the civilian population had been evacuated or sheltered in bunkers. Wilck surrendered on 21 October, contrary to Hitler's orders. Each side suffered several thousand casualties, and the city centre was devastated.

Aftermath

Aachen's fall opened the way toward the Roer River and Cologne, though the wider offensive stalled in the Hürtgen Forest. The Americans installed a provisional German administration in the city, an early experiment in occupation government.
