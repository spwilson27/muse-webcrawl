İzmir (Turkish pronunciation: [ˈizmiɾ]; Greek: Σμύρνη, Smyrna) is a metropolitan province and the third most populous city in Turkey, after Istanbul and Ankara. It is on the Aegean coast of Anatolia, at the head of the sheltered Gulf of İzmir. The city has been one of the principal mercantile cities of the Mediterranean Sea for much of its history of more than three millennia.

History

One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the Mediterranean world, İzmir was founded as Smyrna by Greeks in the early first millennium BC and was later a major Roman and Byzantine city. The poet Homer is traditionally said to have been born here. Smyrna was one of the seven churches of Asia addressed in the Book of Revelation.

After Ottoman conquest in the 15th century, the city became a cosmopolitan trading port with large Greek, Armenian, Jewish, and Levantine communities. Following the First World War and the Greco-Turkish War, the Great Fire of Smyrna in September 1922 destroyed much of the old city; Turkish forces retook the city on 9 September 1922, a date still commemorated locally. The 1923 population exchange reshaped its demographics.

Geography and districts

The metropolitan area spreads around the gulf, with central districts including Konak, Alsancak, Karşıyaka, Bornova, and Buca. Kadifekale, the ancient acropolis on Mount Pagos, overlooks the city, and the Roman Agora of Smyrna survives as an open-air museum. The Kordon is a seafront promenade along the bay.

Economy and culture

İzmir's port is one of Turkey's largest export harbours. The city hosts the İzmir International Fair, first held in 1932. Landmarks include the 1901 Clock Tower in Konak Square, designed by the Levantine French architect Raymond Charles Père, and the historic Kemeraltı bazaar. The local cuisine is known for dishes such as boyoz, kumru, and İzmir köfte.
