Debt deflation

Debt deflation is a theory of economic recession developed by economist Irving Fisher in 1933 to explain the Great Depression.

Fisher argued that when an economy becomes over-indebted, attempts to liquidate debts trigger falling asset prices and a general price deflation. Because debts are fixed in nominal terms, deflation raises the real burden of remaining debt, causing bankruptcies, bank failures, falling output and further deflation — a self-reinforcing spiral.

Influence

The theory anticipated later work on financial instability, including Hyman Minsky's financial instability hypothesis, and was revived in analyses of the 2008 financial crisis.
