The Browning Arms Company is an American marketer of firearms and fishing gear, best known for designs by its founder, John Moses Browning, one of the most influential firearms designers of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The company is headquartered in Morgan, Utah, and is a subsidiary of the Belgian Herstal Group.

History

John Browning began designing firearms in Ogden, Utah, selling his first single-shot rifle patent to Winchester in 1883. He later designed for Colt, Remington and Fabrique Nationale (FN) in Belgium. After his death in 1926, his son Val Browning developed the company, completing designs such as the Browning Hi-Power pistol (with Dieudonné Saive) and the Superposed shotgun.

Products

Browning-branded firearms have included the Auto-5 semi-automatic shotgun (the first successful design of its kind), the BAR sporting rifle, the Citori over-and-under shotgun and the Buck Mark pistol. Many Browning firearms are manufactured by FN Herstal in Belgium and by Miroku in Japan.
