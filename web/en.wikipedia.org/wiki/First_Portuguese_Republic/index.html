First Portuguese Republic

The First Portuguese Republic (Portuguese: Primeira República Portuguesa) was the period of Portuguese history from the 5 October 1910 revolution, which overthrew the constitutional monarchy, until the military coup of 28 May 1926 that began the Ditadura Nacional and led to the Estado Novo of António de Oliveira Salazar. The republic was marked by radical anti-clerical legislation, chronic political instability — 45 governments in sixteen years — and Portugal's participation in the First World War.

The 1910 revolution

The regicide of King Carlos I and his heir in 1908 had fatally weakened the monarchy. On 5 October 1910, after fighting in Lisbon between republican insurgents, supported by naval units, and loyalist troops, the republic was proclaimed; King Manuel II fled into exile. A provisional government under Teófilo Braga organised elections to a Constituent Assembly, which adopted a constitution in 1911 and elected Manuel de Arriaga the first president.

Policies and instability

The republic enacted sweeping reforms in the spirit of the Portuguese Republican Party led by Afonso Costa: the separation of church and state, expulsion of religious orders, secular education, divorce and civil marriage, a new flag and anthem, and the adoption of a new currency, the escudo. Anti-clerical measures under Justice Minister Afonso Costa alienated the Catholic countryside. Parliamentary factionalism, military intrigue and street violence made governments ephemeral; President Sidónio Pais, elected after a 1917 coup, was assassinated in 1918, and the republic endured a brief monarchist restoration in the north (the Monarchy of the North) in 1919.

Portugal entered the First World War on the Allied side in 1916, sending an expeditionary corps to Flanders that suffered heavily at the Battle of the Lys in April 1918, and fighting German forces in Africa. War finance wrecked the public finances, fuelling inflation and social unrest.

Fall of the republic

The "Bloody Night" of October 1921, in which the prime minister and other politicians were murdered, symbolised the republic's collapse into disorder. On 28 May 1926 General Gomes da Costa launched a military rising at Braga that marched on Lisbon almost unopposed, ending the First Republic and opening the way to decades of authoritarian rule.
