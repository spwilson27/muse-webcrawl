Jules Alfred Huot de Goncourt (17 December 1830 – 20 June 1870) was a French author, best known for his collaboration with his elder brother, Edmond de Goncourt. Together the two brothers became leading figures of French naturalism and among the most influential chroniclers of nineteenth-century Parisian life.

Life and collaboration

Jules and Edmond were the sons of a minor official of the former Lorraine nobility. Jules, the younger by eight years, shared with Edmond an intense devotion to writing, collecting art, and documenting the society around them. After their mother's death in 1848 the two brothers were inseparable, living together in Paris, travelling together, and publishing under both their names.

The brothers first attracted attention with studies of eighteenth-century French society and art, such as L'Art du XVIIIe siècle (1859–1875), which helped revive interest in the French Rococo. Their novels, however, established their literary reputation. Sœur Philomène (1861) was followed by Renée Mauperin (1864) and by their masterpiece Germinie Lacerteux (1865), the unflinching story of a servant girl's double life, often cited as a founding text of French naturalism and a precursor of Émile Zola's work.

Their subsequent novels Manette Salomon (1867), set in the Paris art world, and Madame Gervaisais (1869) continued their clinical, richly textured exploration of contemporary manners.

The Journal

From 1851 the brothers kept a private diary, the Journal des Goncourt, a vivid and often acerbic record of literary Paris — its dinners, salons, gossip, and quarrels. Jules's death in 1870 from complications of syphilis devastated Edmond, who continued the Journal alone for another twenty-six years and published an expurgated edition during his lifetime.

Legacy

Edmond's will established the Académie Goncourt and the Prix Goncourt, France's most prestigious literary prize, first awarded in 1903. Jules's name is permanently joined with his brother's in the collective identity "the Goncourts," symbolised by the linked initials and shared authorship of their works.
