Overview

Moving Hearts is an Irish Celtic rock band formed in Dublin in 1981. The group brought together leading figures from the Irish folk revival — singer Christy Moore and bouzouki player Dónal Lunny, both formerly of Planxty — with rock musicians including guitarist Declan Sinnott, and fused them with the uilleann pipes and low whistle of Davy Spillane. Their sound combined traditional Irish melodies and instruments with electric guitars, saxophone and a powerful rhythm section, and their lyrics addressed contemporary politics in Ireland and beyond.

Career

Their debut album, Moving Hearts (1981), and Dark End of the Street (1982) were widely acclaimed. Singer Mick Hanly briefly replaced Christy Moore. By 1985 the band had turned almost entirely instrumental; the all-instrumental album The Storm (1985), featuring pieces such as "The Lark" and the title suite, is regarded as a landmark of Celtic music and a major influence on later acts from Riverdance's composers to contemporary folk-rock bands.

The band split in 1985 under financial pressure but has reunited for acclaimed concerts, including a celebrated series at Dublin's Vicar Street in 2007, documented on the live album and DVD Live in Dublin. Moving Hearts are credited with breaking down the barrier between Irish traditional music and rock and with helping to modernise the sound of Celtic music worldwide.
