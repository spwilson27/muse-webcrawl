Landes (French pronunciation: [lɑ̃d]; Gascon: Las Lanas) is a department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of southwestern France, on the Atlantic coast. It takes its name from the Landes forest, the largest maritime pine forest in western Europe, which covers much of its territory. Its prefecture is Mont-de-Marsan and its largest spa town is Dax.

History

Landes was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from parts of the provinces of Guyenne and Gascony. Before the 19th century, much of the department was a waterlogged, sparsely populated moorland (in French, landes) grazed by shepherds who famously moved on stilts. A law of 19 June 1857 required the drainage and afforestation of the moors, leading to the massive planting of maritime pines for timber and resin and transforming the department's economy and landscape.

Geography

Landes is the second-largest department of metropolitan France by area. It is bordered by Gironde to the north, Lot-et-Garonne and Gers to the east, Pyrénées-Atlantiques to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west, where a long, straight sandy coastline — the Côte d'Argent — hosts resorts such as Hossegor, Capbreton, Mimizan, and Biscarrosse, popular for surfing. Inland are freshwater lakes (étangs) behind the dunes, including the Étang de Cazaux et de Sanguinet and the Étang de Léon. The Adour river crosses the south of the department through Dax.

Economy and culture

Forestry and wood industries remain important, alongside maize farming, foie gras and duck production in the Chalosse region, tourism, and thermal spas at Dax and Eugénie-les-Bains. The department is known for its férias (festivals, such as the Fêtes de la Madeleine in Mont-de-Marsan), course landaise bull games, rugby union, and Gascon language heritage.
