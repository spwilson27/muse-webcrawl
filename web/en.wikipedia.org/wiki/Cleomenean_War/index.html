Cleomenean War

The Cleomenean War (229/228–222 BC) was fought between Sparta under King Cleomenes III and the Achaean League, later joined by Macedon under Antigonus III Doson. Cleomenes, having carried out revolutionary reforms at Sparta — cancelling debts, redistributing land, and restoring the traditional agoge-based citizen army — sought to make Sparta the dominant power in the Peloponnese at the expense of the Achaean League led by Aratus of Sicyon.

Course of the war

Cleomenes opened hostilities by seizing border towns belonging to Argos and the Achaeans. He defeated Achaean forces at Mount Lycaeum and Ladoceia (227 BC) and again, decisively, at Dyme (226 BC), and was invited into Corinth and Argos, where his social programme won popular support. Fearing revolution within its own cities, the Achaean League under Aratus appealed to Antigonus III of Macedon, surrendering Acrocorinth as the price of intervention.

With Macedonian troops in the field from 224 BC, Cleomenes was gradually driven back into Laconia. Antigonus retook Corinth and Argos, while Cleomenes, short of funds and men, freed helots who could pay for their freedom and sold citizenship to raise money. The war ended at the Battle of Sellasia (222 BC), north of Sparta, where the combined Macedonian and Achaean army crushed the Spartan phalanx. Of 6,000 Spartan hoplites, Plutarch reports that only 200 survived.

Aftermath

Cleomenes fled to Ptolemaic Egypt, where he later died. Antigonus entered Sparta — the first foreign army ever to do so — abolished Cleomenes's reforms, and restored the exiled opponents of the regime, though he treated the city leniently. Sparta's brief revival as a great power was over, and Macedonian influence in the Peloponnese was re-established.
