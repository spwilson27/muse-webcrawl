Olinde Rodrigues

Born: 6 October 1795, Bordeaux, France

Died: 17 December 1851 (aged 56), Paris, France

Known for: Rodrigues' formula; Euler–Rodrigues formula

Fields: Mathematics, banking, social reform

Benjamin Olinde Rodrigues (6 October 1795 – 17 December 1851), more commonly known as Olinde Rodrigues, was a French banker, mathematician and social reformer. He is remembered in mathematics for the Rodrigues rotation formula for vectors, the Rodrigues formula for Legendre polynomials, and the Euler–Rodrigues formula for rotation parameters.

Life

Rodrigues was born in Bordeaux into a well-to-do Sephardic Jewish family of Portuguese origin. His father was a banker and accountant. He studied at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand in Paris and took a doctorate in mathematics from the University of Paris in 1816, with a thesis on the attraction of spheroids that contained the result now called Rodrigues' formula.

Barred by his religion from the teaching career he wanted, Rodrigues entered the family banking business and became a successful stockbroker and financial adviser. He later directed the mortgage bank Caisse Hypothécaire.

Saint-Simonianism

Rodrigues became the closest disciple of the social theorist Henri de Saint-Simon, and after Saint-Simon's death in 1825 he led the Saint-Simonian movement, editing its journal Le Globe and writings such as the Doctrine de Saint-Simon. The movement advocated the abolition of inherited privilege, industrial planning, and the emancipation of women. Internal disputes led Rodrigues to break with the faction of Enfantin in 1832; he spent his final years in relative obscurity, though he advised railway promoter Paulin Talabot and campaigned for workers' associations after the Revolution of 1848.

Mathematics

Rodrigues' formula expresses the Legendre polynomials as repeated derivatives of a power function. His 1840 paper on the group of rotations anticipated the use of half-angle parameters equivalent to quaternions, three years before Hamilton discovered them; the Euler–Rodrigues parameters remain standard in attitude control of spacecraft.
