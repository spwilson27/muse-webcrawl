Battle of Edington

The Battle of Edington was fought in May 878 between the West Saxon army of Alfred the Great and the Great Heathen Army led by Guthrum. Alfred's victory was decisive: it ended the immediate Viking threat to Wessex — the last free Anglo-Saxon kingdom — and led to the baptism of Guthrum and the treaty that defined the Danelaw. The battle is generally identified with Ethandun in Wiltshire, near modern Edington.

Background

In January 878 Guthrum's Danes launched a midwinter surprise attack on Chippenham, driving Alfred into the Somerset marshes at Athelney. From his island refuge Alfred rebuilt his forces, and in the seventh week after Easter he summoned the fyrds of Somerset, Wiltshire and Hampshire to "Egbert's Stone", east of Selwood Forest. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle describes how the men of the shires rejoiced to see him.

The battle

Alfred marched on the Danish position at Ethandun. Asser's Life of Alfred describes the Saxons fighting "fiercely with a dense shield-wall" and, after a long struggle, winning the day. The Danes fled to their fortified camp (probably at Chippenham) and were starved into surrender after a fourteen-day siege.

Aftermath

Guthrum agreed to leave Wessex and accept baptism, with Alfred as his godfather; he took the baptismal name Æthelstan. The resulting Treaty of Alfred and Guthrum drew the boundary between Wessex and the Danelaw. Alfred used the ensuing peace to build the burh fortified towns and reorganise his army and navy — the foundations on which his successors would unite England.
