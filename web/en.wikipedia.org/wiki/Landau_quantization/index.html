Landau quantization

Landau quantization, introduced by Lev Landau in 1930, is the quantisation of the cyclotron orbits of charged particles in a uniform magnetic field. Instead of a continuum, the perpendicular kinetic energy of a free charged particle takes discrete values called Landau levels, equally spaced by the cyclotron energy ħωc.

Each Landau level is highly degenerate, with the number of states per unit area proportional to the magnetic field strength. Landau quantisation underlies the quantum Hall effects, the de Haas–van Alphen effect and Landau diamagnetism in metals.

The phenomenon is fundamental to the theory of electrons in solids in magnetic fields and to plasma and astrophysical plasmas.
