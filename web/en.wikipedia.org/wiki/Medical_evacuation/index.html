Medical evacuation


A helicopter conducting a medical evacuation

Medical evacuation, often shortened to medevac or medivac, is the timely and efficient movement and en route care provided by medical personnel to patients requiring urgent medical attention, typically from a battlefield or remote location to a medical facility. The term also covers the transfer of patients between hospitals. Casualty evacuation (casevac) is a related term for movement by non-medical personnel or without en route medical care.

Military medevac

Organised evacuation by air developed from balloon and early aircraft experiments in the First World War to the helicopter evacuations of the Korean and Vietnam Wars, when US Army "Dustoff" crews drastically reduced the time from wounding to surgery and improved survival. Modern military doctrine grades evacuation by priority (urgent, priority, routine) and uses dedicated platforms such as the UH-60 Black Hawk in the US and the concept of the "golden hour" to organise the chain from point of injury through forward surgery to definitive care.

Civilian medevac

Civilian air ambulances — helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft staffed by paramedics, nurses, and physicians — transfer critically ill or injured patients from accident scenes and rural hospitals to specialist centres. Longer international repatriation flights are arranged by assistance companies and insurers. Ground ambulances perform the majority of shorter-distance interfacility transfers.
