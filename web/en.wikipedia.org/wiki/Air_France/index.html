Air France, legally Société Air France, S.A., is the flag carrier of France, headquartered in Tremblay-en-France, north of Paris. It is a subsidiary of the Air France–KLM Group and a founding member of the SkyTeam airline alliance. The airline serves a global network of destinations in some 70 countries from its principal hub at Paris Charles de Gaulle Airport.

History

Air France was formed on 7 October 1933 from the merger of several French airlines, including Air Orient, Air Union, and the Compagnie Générale Aéropostale, inheriting pioneering mail routes to South America and Africa. After the Second World War the airline was nationalised and rebuilt a long-haul network, later becoming one of only two operators of the supersonic Concorde (1976–2003). In 2004 it merged with the Dutch carrier KLM to form Air France–KLM, while both airlines retained their brands and hubs.

Fleet and services

The airline operates a mixed fleet of Airbus and Boeing aircraft, including A320-family short-haul jets and A350 and Boeing 777/787 wide-bodies on long-haul routes. It offers La Première, Business, Premium Economy, and Economy cabins, and the Flying Blue frequent-flyer programme shared with KLM. Subsidiaries include the regional carrier Air France Hop and the leisure airline Transavia France.

Accidents

The deadliest accident in the airline's history was Air France Flight 447, an Airbus A330 that crashed into the Atlantic Ocean en route from Rio de Janeiro to Paris on 1 June 2009, killing all 228 people aboard. The only fatal Concorde crash, Flight 4590 in July 2000, also involved an Air France aircraft.
