John Mark is a figure mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles in the New Testament. He accompanied Paul and Barnabas on their missionary journeys and is traditionally often identified with Mark the Evangelist, the traditionally held author of the Gospel of Mark.

In Acts

John Mark first appears in Acts 12, where the Jerusalem church gathers at the house of his mother, Mary, after Peter's escape from prison. He travelled with Paul and Barnabas from Antioch on their first missionary journey, serving as their assistant, but left them at Perga in Pamphylia and returned to Jerusalem (Acts 13:13). Paul's refusal to take him on the second journey caused a sharp disagreement and the parting of Paul and Barnabas; Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus (Acts 15:36–41).

Later mentions and identification

Mark reappears in Paul's later letters — Colossians calls him a cousin of Barnabas, Philemon lists him among Paul's fellow workers, and 2 Timothy has Paul ask for Mark because "he is useful to me for ministry". First Peter refers to "my son Mark", and early church tradition, reported by Papias, described Mark as Peter's interpreter, leading many to identify John Mark with the evangelist, though some scholars treat them as distinct figures.
