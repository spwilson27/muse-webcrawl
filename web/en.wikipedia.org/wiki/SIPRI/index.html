The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI) is an independent international institute dedicated to research into conflict, armaments, arms control and disarmament. Established in 1966 on the initiative of Prime Minister Tage Erlander to commemorate 150 years of unbroken peace in Sweden, SIPRI is based in Solna, Stockholm, and is largely funded by the Swedish government while operating independently. It is consistently ranked among the world's most influential think tanks.

Work

SIPRI maintains widely cited open databases on world military expenditure, international arms transfers, arms production (the SIPRI Top 100 arms-producing companies), multilateral peace operations and nuclear forces. Its annual SIPRI Yearbook, first published in 1969, summarises developments in international security, armaments and disarmament and is a standard reference for journalists, policymakers and researchers. SIPRI's estimates of global military spending and of the world's nuclear arsenals (around 12,000 warheads in recent editions) are regularly headline news.

Organisation

The institute is directed by an international governing board appointed by the Swedish government. It has research staff from many countries and maintains offices and programmes connected to Beijing and Washington, D.C. Its research covers regions including Europe, Africa, Asia and the Middle East, and themes such as climate-related security risks and emerging military technology.
