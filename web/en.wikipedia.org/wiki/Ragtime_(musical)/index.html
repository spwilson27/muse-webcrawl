Ragtime (musical)

Ragtime is a musical with music by Stephen Flaherty, lyrics by Lynn Ahrens, and a book by Terrence McNally. It is based on the 1975 novel Ragtime by E. L. Doctorow, interweaving the stories of three groups in early-20th-century America: African Americans in Harlem, upper-class white suburbanites in New Rochelle, and Eastern European Jewish immigrants in New York City.

Productions

The musical premiered in Toronto in 1996 and opened on Broadway at the Ford Center for the Performing Arts on 18 January 1998, directed by Frank Galati with choreography by Graciela Daniele. The original cast included Brian Stokes Mitchell as Coalhouse Walker Jr., Audra McDonald as Sarah, and Marin Mazzie as Mother. It won four Tony Awards, including Best Book and Best Original Score, but ran at a financial loss and closed in January 2000 after 834 performances. A well-received Broadway revival directed by Marcia Milgrom Dodge opened in 2009.

Music

Flaherty's score pastiches period styles — ragtime, marches, cakewalks, and vaudeville — alongside sweeping anthems such as "Wheels of a Dream," "Back to Before," and "Make Them Hear You."

Reception

The show is noted for its large cast, epic scope, and themes of racism, immigration, and social change in the Progressive Era; it has become a staple of regional and amateur theatre.
