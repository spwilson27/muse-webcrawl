Visual novel

A visual novel (often abbreviated VN) is a form of digital interactive fiction, originating in Japan, that presents a narrative primarily through text accompanied by static or lightly animated character sprites, background art, music, and voice acting. Gameplay interaction is usually minimal — the player reads the story and periodically makes choices that branch the plot toward different routes and endings. Visual novels are typically produced for PC and consoles and encompass genres from romance and school drama to horror, mystery, and science fiction.

Structure and terminology

A typical visual novel alternates narration and dialogue in a text window overlaid on scene art, with character sprites whose expressions change as they speak. Branching choices may lead to character-specific "routes", good or bad endings, and sometimes require completing one route to unlock others. A subgenre with no (or almost no) choices is called a kinetic novel. Closely related are adventure games (ADV) with more investigation gameplay, dating sims with stat-raising mechanics, and adult-oriented eroge, which for many years formed a large share of the Japanese PC market.

History

The genre descends from early Japanese adventure games such as Chunsoft's sound novels (Otogirisō, 1992; Kamaitachi no Yoru, 1994) and Leaf's influential "Leaf Visual Novel Series" — which popularised the very term "visual novel" — including To Heart (1997). Key's Kanon (1999), Air (2000), and Clannad (2004) defined the emotional nakige ("crying game") style. Acclaimed works include Type-Moon's Tsukihime (2000) and Fate/stay night (2004), Nitroplus's dark titles, 5pb.'s Steins;Gate (2009), and the horror classic Higurashi When They Cry (2002–) by doujin circle 07th Expansion. Many successful visual novels are adapted into anime, manga, and light novels.

Outside Japan

Since the 2010s, localization and digital storefronts (notably Steam) have expanded the Western audience; Western-developed visual novels such as Doki Doki Literature Club! (2017) and tools like the free Ren'Py engine have made the form a staple of indie game development worldwide.
