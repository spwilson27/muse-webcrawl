Coloratura soprano

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Queen of the Night in The Magic Flute is the most famous coloratura role.

A coloratura soprano is a type of operatic soprano voice that specialises in music distinguished by agile runs, leaps, and trills. The term coloratura refers to the elaborate ornamentation of a melody, and coloratura sopranos are expected to execute intricate passages of fioritura with precision at high pitches, often reaching F6 and above.

Types

The lyric coloratura soprano has a warm, bright voice with a flexible upper extension — roles include Gilda in Rigoletto, Lucia in Lucia di Lammermoor, and Violetta in La traviata. The dramatic coloratura soprano combines agility with greater power and darker timbre, singing roles such as the Queen of the Night in Mozart's The Magic Flute, Norma, Abigaille in Nabucco, and Mozart's Donna Anna and Elettra.

Notable coloratura sopranos

Celebrated coloratura sopranos include Maria Callas, Joan Sutherland — dubbed "La Stupenda" after a triumphant Lucia — Beverly Sills, Edita Gruberová, Natalie Dessay, Diana Damrau, and Mado Robin, famed for notes above the staff. The repertoire written for the castrati and for 19th-century prima donnas remains the core of the coloratura tradition, revived in the mid-20th-century bel canto renaissance.
