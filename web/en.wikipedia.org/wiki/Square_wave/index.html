Square wave

An ideal square wave with equal high and low periods (50% duty cycle).

A square wave is a non-sinusoidal periodic waveform in which the amplitude alternates at a steady frequency between fixed minimum and maximum values, with the same duration at each level. In an ideal square wave the transitions between minimum and maximum are instantaneous. Real signals produced by electronic switches approximate this behaviour with finite rise and fall times.

Duty cycle

The proportion of the period during which the signal is at its high level is the duty cycle. A true square wave has a duty cycle of 50%; periodic pulse waveforms with other ratios are often called pulse waves or rectangular waves. In digital electronics, square waves are used as clock signals that synchronize the operations of circuits.

Fourier analysis

By Fourier analysis, a square wave can be decomposed into an infinite series of sine waves containing only the odd harmonics of the fundamental frequency: the 3rd, 5th, 7th and so on, with amplitudes falling off as 1/k. Truncating the series produces the overshoot near the transitions known as the Gibbs phenomenon. The rich harmonic content gives square-wave tones in music a hollow, clarinet-like quality, and square waves are a staple of subtractive synthesis and chiptune music.

Applications

Square waves appear in timing and clock signals, pulse-width modulation (PWM) for controlling power delivery, digital data transmission, function generators used in electronics testing, and as test signals in acoustics and audio engineering.
