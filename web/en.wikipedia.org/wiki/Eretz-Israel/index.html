Eretz-Israel

Eretz-Israel (Hebrew: אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל, Eretz Yisrael), meaning the Land of Israel, is a term in Jewish tradition referring to the geographic region promised in the Hebrew Bible to the Israelites. Its biblically described extent varies—sometimes "from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates", more often the territory between the Jordan River, the Mediterranean Sea, Lebanon and the Negev desert.

Religious significance

The Land holds central status in Judaism: many commandments (mitzvot) apply only within it, Jerusalem and the Temple are located there, and Jewish prayer is oriented toward it. The term appears throughout rabbinic literature and liturgy.

Modern usage

During the British Mandate, "Eretz-Israel" was the official Hebrew name for Mandatory Palestine (its initials, aleph-yod, appeared on stamps and coins). In modern political discourse, "Eretz Yisrael" is sometimes used by movements associated with Greater Israel claims, while the State of Israel's official name simply uses "Israel".
