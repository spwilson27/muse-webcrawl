Cycling monument

The Monuments are five classic one-day professional road cycling races generally considered to be the oldest, hardest and most prestigious single-day events in men's road cycling, alongside the Grand Tours and the World Championships. All five were founded before or just after the First World War and are run in Western Europe in spring or autumn.

The five Monuments

  • Milan–San Remo (Italy, March) — "La Classicissima"; the longest of the five at roughly 290 km, often ending in a bunch sprint on the Via Roma.
  • Tour of Flanders (Belgium, early April) — De Ronde; short, steep cobbled climbs (bergs) such as the Koppenberg, Oude Kwaremont and Paterberg.
  • Paris–Roubaix (France, mid-April) — the "Hell of the North" and "Queen of the Classics"; brutal cobbled farm tracks (pavé), finishing in the Roubaix velodrome; the winner receives a cobblestone trophy.
  • Liège–Bastogne–Liège (Belgium, late April) — "La Doyenne" (the Old Lady), the oldest, founded 1892; a hilly Ardennes course suited to climbers.
  • Giro di Lombardia (Italy, October) — the "Race of the Falling Leaves", closing the season around Lake Como, featuring the Madonna del Ghisallo climb.

Records

Only three riders have won all five Monuments in their careers: the Belgians Rik Van Looy, Eddy Merckx and Roger De Vlaeminck. Merckx holds the record with 19 Monument victories in total, including seven wins at Milan–San Remo. In women's cycling, the corresponding races have gained top-level status more recently, with editions of the Tour of Flanders (since 2004), Liège–Bastogne–Liège and Paris–Roubaix (since 2021) now established.
