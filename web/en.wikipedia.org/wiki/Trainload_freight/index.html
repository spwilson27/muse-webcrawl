Trainload freight

Trainload freight is a business sector of British Rail that operated between 1988 and 1994, responsible for moving bulk goods by rail in whole trains dedicated to a single commodity or customer, as opposed to wagonload freight, in which individual wagons from different customers were marshalled into mixed trains.

Formation

Trainload Freight was created as part of British Rail's sectorisation programme, which reorganised the railway into market-led business sectors. The sector took over the bulk trainload operations previously handled by the freight business, concentrating on high-volume flows such as coal, petroleum, metals, aggregates and chemicals, where a full train could be loaded at one origin and run directly to one destination.

Operations

The sector was organised into sub-sectors by commodity, including Trainload Coal, Trainload Petroleum, Trainload Metals and Trainload Construction, reflecting the industries it served. Its largest traffic was coal for the electricity supply industry, hauled from collieries and ports to power stations in "merry-go-round" trains that loaded and unloaded while moving slowly through the terminals. Locomotives allocated to the sector wore a distinctive two-tone grey livery with sub-sector decals.

Privatisation

In the run-up to the privatisation of British Rail, Trainload Freight was divided in 1994 into three regional freight companies — Loadhaul, Mainline Freight and Transrail Freight — which were subsequently sold, with all three passing to the North American-owned English, Welsh & Scottish Railway (EWS) in 1996.
