Public finance

[Image: a national treasury building with classical columns]

Public finance is the study of the revenue, expenditure and debt of governments — how the state raises money through Taxation, borrowing and fees, how it spends on public services, transfers and investment, and how those choices affect the economy. It is both a branch of Economics and the practical business of treasuries and finance ministries.

Questions and structure

Core questions include the efficiency and fairness of taxes (who really bears them — tax incidence), the justification for public provision of public goods and social insurance, redistribution and its disincentives, and the sustainability of public debt. In federal systems a further layer studies which level of government should tax and spend. The field's modern founder is often taken to be Richard Musgrave (The Theory of Public Finance, 1959), who organised the state's economic role into allocation, distribution and stabilisation branches — the last shared with Fiscal policy. Budgets, deficits and debt statistics published by finance ministries and the International Monetary Fund are its raw material.
