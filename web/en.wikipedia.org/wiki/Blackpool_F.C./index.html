Blackpool F.C.

Blackpool Football Club is a professional association football club based in the seaside town of Blackpool, Lancashire, England. Founded in 1887, the club is nicknamed the Seasiders and the Tangerines, after its home colours. It plays its home matches at Bloomfield Road, its ground since 1901.

History

Blackpool joined the Football League in 1896. The club's golden era came in the 1940s and 1950s under manager Joe Smith, when wingers Stanley Matthews and Stan Mortensen led the team to three FA Cup finals. After losing in 1948 and 1951, Blackpool won the 1953 final against Bolton Wanderers — the famous "Matthews Final" — coming back from 3–1 down to win 4–3, with Mortensen scoring a hat-trick.

The club spent much of the later twentieth century in the lower divisions. In 2010, under manager Ian Holloway, Blackpool won the Championship play-off final against Cardiff City and reached the Premier League for the first time, but were relegated after a single season despite widespread praise for their attacking play. A prolonged period of decline under the Oyston family's ownership, accompanied by fan boycotts, ended when the club went into receivership in 2019 and was bought by local businessman Simon Sadler.

Colours and rivals

Blackpool's tangerine shirts were adopted in the 1920s, reportedly inspired by a club official who had refereed a match involving the Netherlands. The club's main rivalry is the West Lancashire derby with Preston North End.

Honours

Blackpool's major honours include the FA Cup (1953), the Anglo-Italian Cup (1971) and several Football League Trophy wins, as well as play-off promotions in 2001, 2007, 2010 and 2021 — the club has a notable record of success in play-off finals.
