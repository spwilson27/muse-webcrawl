Pervez Musharraf (11 August 1943 – 5 February 2023) was a Pakistani military officer and politician who served as the tenth president of Pakistan from 2001 to 2008. He also served as the tenth chief of army staff of the Pakistan Army from 1998 to 2007.

Born in Delhi during British rule and raised in Karachi and Istanbul, Musharraf was commissioned into the Pakistan Army in 1964. He fought in the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965 and the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971, and later served in the elite Special Services Group (SSG). He rose through the ranks to become Chief of Army Staff in October 1998, appointed by Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif.

1999 coup and rule

In October 1999, after tensions with Sharif over the Kargil conflict — a military operation in Kashmir that Musharraf had helped plan — Sharif attempted to dismiss him while Musharraf was returning from a visit to Sri Lanka. The army responded by seizing power in a bloodless coup on 12 October 1999. Musharraf initially ruled as Chief Executive, then assumed the presidency in June 2001, and was confirmed in a controversial 2002 referendum.

Musharraf's government pursued economic liberalisation and privatisation, and Pakistan's economy grew during the early years of his rule. After the September 11 attacks, Musharraf allied Pakistan with the United States in the War on Terror, receiving substantial American military and economic aid. Domestically, he introduced "enlightened moderation," devolution of power to local governments, and media liberalisation.

Downfall and exile

In 2007, Musharraf suspended Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry, sparking the Lawyers' Movement, and imposed a state of emergency in November 2007 during which he suspended the constitution and dismissed senior judges. Facing impeachment proceedings by the new coalition government after the 2008 elections, he resigned the presidency on 18 August 2008 and went into self-imposed exile in London and Dubai.

He returned to Pakistan in 2013 to contest elections but was disqualified and placed under house arrest, facing charges including treason over the 2007 emergency and involvement in the assassinations of Benazir Bhutto and Akbar Bugti. In 2016 he was permitted to travel to Dubai for medical treatment. In December 2019, a special court sentenced him to death in absentia for high treason, though the verdict was later annulled by the Lahore High Court. Musharraf died in Dubai on 5 February 2023 from amyloidosis and was buried in Karachi.
