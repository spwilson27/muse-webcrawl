The Cornell Lab of Ornithology is a member-supported research unit of Cornell University in Ithaca, New York, dedicated to the study, appreciation and conservation of birds and other wildlife. It is housed in the Imogene Powers Johnson Center for Birds and Biodiversity within the 226-acre Sapsucker Woods Sanctuary.

History

The Lab was founded in 1915 by Arthur A. Allen, the first professor of ornithology in the United States. Allen and his students pioneered bird photography and sound recording. The Lab moved to its Sapsucker Woods site in 1957, and the current visitor centre opened in 2003.

Programs

The Lab runs some of the world's largest citizen-science projects. eBird, launched in 2002 with the National Audubon Society, collects hundreds of millions of bird observations used for distribution and population research. The Merlin Bird ID app identifies birds by description, photo and sound; its sound identification draws on the Macaulay Library, the world's largest archive of wildlife sounds and images. Other projects include Project FeederWatch, NestWatch, Celebrate Urban Birds, and BirdCast, which forecasts bird migration using weather radar. The Lab publishes the subscription reference Birds of the World and the magazine Living Bird, and operates the Bird Academy education platform.

Research

Lab scientists work in evolutionary biology, behavioural ecology, bioacoustics, conservation biology and data science, applying machine learning to large-scale biodiversity data. Its Center for Avian Population Studies and conservation science program inform land-management and policy decisions internationally.
