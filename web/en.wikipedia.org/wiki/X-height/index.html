x-height

In typography, the x-height is the distance between the baseline and the mean line of a typeface: the height of a typical lower-case letter such as x, a, c, e, m, n, o, r, s, u, v, w and z, excluding ascenders and descenders.

Significance

X-height is one of the major determinants of how large a typeface appears at a given point size and of its legibility at small sizes. Typefaces with a large x-height relative to their ascender height — such as many humanist sans-serifs designed for screens — tend to look bigger and read easily in body text, while typefaces with a small x-height have a more delicate, classical appearance but may need to be set larger.

Related measurements

Other vertical metrics include cap height (the height of capital letters), ascender height, descender depth, and the em size. In digital fonts, x-height is stored in font metadata (for example the sxHeight field of the OS/2 table) and is used by CSS through the ex unit and the font-size-adjust property, which lets browsers normalise perceived size when substituting fonts.
