Vitus Bering

Vitus Jonassen Bering (1681–1741) was a Danish cartographer and explorer in Russian service, after whom the Bering Strait, Bering Sea and Bering Island are named. He led two great expeditions to Russia's far east.

Expeditions

Commissioned by Peter the Great, Bering's First Kamchatka Expedition (1725–1730) sailed through the strait between Asia and America in 1728, proving the continents were separate, though fog prevented him sighting Alaska. The Great Northern Expedition (1733–1743), one of the largest exploration projects in history, mapped Siberia's Arctic and Pacific coasts; in 1741 Bering reached Alaska. On the return voyage his ship St. Peter was wrecked on Bering Island, where he died in December 1741.

Legacy

Bering's reports opened Alaska to Russian fur traders. Naturalist Georg Wilhelm Steller, who sailed with him, described species including Steller's sea cow, hunted to extinction within decades.
