Snow Crash is a science fiction novel by American writer Neal Stephenson, published in 1992. Like many of Stephenson's novels, its themes include history, linguistics, anthropology, archaeology, religion, computer science, politics, cryptography, memetics, and philosophy.

The book presents Stephenson's Metaverse, a successor to the Internet — a virtual reality-based shared space inhabited by user-controlled avatars, as well as system daemons. The term "metaverse" has since entered common usage to describe persistent virtual worlds.

Plot summary

In a near-future Los Angeles, the United States government has ceded most of its power to private organizations and entrepreneurs. Franchising, individual sovereignty, and private vehicles reign supreme; highway corporations compete to attract drivers to their roads, and all mail delivery is by hired courier. The remnants of government maintain authority only in isolated compounds where they jealously guard their power.

The protagonist, Hiro Protagonist, is a hacker and a pizza delivery driver for the Mafia. He meets Y.T. (short for "Yours Truly"), a young skateboard courier, during a failed delivery, and they team up to investigate the powerful computer virus/information drug/religion known as "Snow Crash".

The Metaverse

The Metaverse is depicted as an urban environment built along a 100-meter-wide road, the Street, that spans the entire 65,536 km circumference of a featureless, black, perfectly spherical planet. Authorized users access the Metaverse through public or personal terminals that project high-resolution, two-dimensional displays onto goggles worn by users.

Themes

The novel explores the concept of neurolinguistics and ancient Sumerian mythology, positing that the Sumerian language was the "firmware programming language" for the human brainstem, with the goddess Asherah as a virus and the god Enki as a counter-virus developed by the Sumerian priesthood. Stephenson links these ideas to modern computer viruses and the Tower of Babel narrative.

Reception and legacy

Snow Crash established Stephenson's reputation as a science fiction writer. The book appeared on Time magazine's list of 100 all-time English-language novels written since 1923. It is credited with popularizing the term "avatar" in its virtual-reality sense and has influenced the design of virtual worlds such as Second Life.
