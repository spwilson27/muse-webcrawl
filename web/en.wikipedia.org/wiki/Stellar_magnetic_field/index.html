Stellar magnetic field

A stellar magnetic field is a magnetic field generated by the motion of conductive plasma inside a star. This motion is created through convection, which is a form of energy transport involving the physical movement of material. A localized magnetic field exerts a force on the plasma, effectively increasing the pressure without a comparable gain in density.

The Sun

The Sun's magnetic field is the best studied. It drives the 11-year sunspot cycle, solar flares and coronal mass ejections, and reverses polarity with each cycle. Sunspots are regions where magnetic flux tubes break through the photosphere, temporarily cooling the surface.

Other stars

Fields are measured through the Zeeman effect on spectral lines and through spectropolarimetry. Young, rapidly rotating stars and fully convective red dwarfs can have fields thousands of times stronger than the Sun's, affecting stellar activity, starspots and the habitability of orbiting planets. Some massive stars host strong fossil fields apparently inherited from their parent molecular clouds. The study of stellar fields is part of the broader field of magnetohydrodynamics and dynamo theory.
