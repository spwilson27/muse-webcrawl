Kinich Ahau

Kinich Ahau (Kʼinich Ajaw) is the Yucatec Maya name for the sun god of Maya mythology and religion. His name is generally rendered "sun-eyed lord" or "sun-faced lord", from kʼin (sun/day), ich (eye/face), and ajaw (lord).

Attributes

In Maya art Kinich Ahau is typically shown as an elderly man with a Roman nose, a filed-tooth or square eye, and sometimes a beard or jaguar features; the kʼin sign is a common element of his portrait glyph. He is associated with the east, with music and poetry, and with the jaguar god of the underworld, who was regarded as the sun in its night-time passage. Temple masks of the god adorned pyramid facades at sites such as Kohunlich, where giant stucco masks of Kinich Ahau flank a central stairway.

Names and rulership

The god's name was adopted by Maya kings as part of their regnal titles, most famously by the Palenque dynasty: Kʼinich Kan Bahlam II, Kʼinich Kʼan Joy Chitam II, and Kʼinich Ahkal Moʼ Nahb III all incorporated "Kʼinich" into their names, identifying the king with the sun god.
