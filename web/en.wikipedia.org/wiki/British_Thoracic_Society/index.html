The British Thoracic Society (BTS) is the United Kingdom's professional society for respiratory medicine. It represents health professionals involved in the care of people with respiratory disease — including chest physicians, nurses, physiotherapists and scientists — and works to improve standards of care, promote research and education, and influence policy on lung health.

History

The society was formed in 1982 by the merger of the British Thoracic Association and the Thoracic Society. Its roots go back to the early 20th-century organisations concerned with tuberculosis, which was then widespread in Britain; as TB declined, the field broadened to cover asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), lung cancer, pneumonia, sleep-disordered breathing and other respiratory conditions.

Activities

The BTS is best known for its clinical guidelines, which are among the most widely used in British medicine: the BTS/SIGN guideline on the management of asthma, and guidelines on COPD, community-acquired pneumonia, pleural disease, oxygen therapy and non-invasive ventilation. It runs national clinical audits through its audit programme, holds a large Winter Meeting each year, and publishes the journal Thorax with BMJ. During the COVID-19 pandemic the society produced widely used guidance on respiratory care and the management of acute breathlessness.
