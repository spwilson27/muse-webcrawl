The Yamanote Line (Japanese: 山手線) is a loop service operated by East Japan Railway Company (JR East) in Tokyo, Japan. It is one of Tokyo's busiest and most important lines, connecting most of the city's major stations and urban centres, including Shinjuku (the world's busiest station), Shibuya, Ikebukuro, Ueno, Tokyo Station, and Shinagawa. A complete loop takes roughly an hour.

Service

Trains run in both directions ("outer" clockwise and "inner" counter-clockwise) at intervals as short as about two minutes at peak times, with no separate stopping patterns — all trains stop at all 30 stations. The line is painted in a distinctive yellow-green ("uguisu" or Japanese bush-warbler green), a colour used on train bodies and station signage. Ridership numbers in the millions of passengers per day, and the line is one of the few JR lines in central Tokyo to run entirely on its own right-of-way, sharing corridors with the Saikyō, Shōnan–Shinjuku, Keihin–Tōhoku and other services.

History

The line's origins lie in the Nippon Railway's Shinagawa–Akabane line of 1885; the loop was completed in 1925 with the opening of the elevated section between Tokyo and Ueno. The newest station, Takanawa Gateway, opened in March 2020 ahead of the Tokyo Olympics, bringing the total to 30 stations. The name "Yamanote" refers to the "high city" — the hilly, historically affluent districts on the western side of the city.
