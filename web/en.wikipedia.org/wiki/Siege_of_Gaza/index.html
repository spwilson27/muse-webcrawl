Siege of Gaza

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Gaza (September – November 332 BC) was an operation of Alexander the Great during his campaign against the Persian Empire. The fortified city of Gaza, on the route from Phoenicia into Egypt, was held for Persia by the eunuch Batis (Bates) with a garrison of Arab mercenaries, and refused Alexander's demand for surrender.

The siege

Gaza stood on a high mound, strongly walled, some distance from the sea. Alexander's engineers raised a great assault mound around the southern wall, employing siege machinery brought from the siege of Tyre. The defenders made sorties that burned the Macedonian works, and Alexander himself was wounded in the shoulder by a missile. After repeated assaults and mining of the walls, the Macedonians stormed the city on the fourth major attack; per the ancient sources, the male defenders were killed and the women and children enslaved.

Aftermath

Batis, captured alive, reportedly refused to speak to Alexander and, in a story modelled on Achilles and Hector, was dragged behind a chariot around the walls — an anecdote modern historians treat with caution. With Gaza taken, the road to Egypt lay open, and Alexander entered Egypt unopposed later in 332 BC.
