Degree (graph theory)

In graph theory, the degree (or valency) of a vertex of a graph is the number of edges incident to the vertex, with loops counting twice. The degree of a vertex v is commonly denoted deg(v). The maximum degree of a graph is written Δ(G) and the minimum degree δ(G).

Directed graphs

In a directed graph, one distinguishes the in-degree, the number of edges directed into the vertex, and the out-degree, the number directed out of it; the total degree is their sum.

Basic results

The handshaking lemma states that the sum of the degrees of all vertices equals twice the number of edges, because each edge contributes exactly two to the total degree count. A consequence is that every graph has an even number of vertices of odd degree. A vertex of degree 0 is isolated; a vertex of degree 1 is a leaf. In a complete graph on n vertices every vertex has degree n − 1, and a graph in which every vertex has the same degree k is called k-regular.

Degree sequences

The multiset of vertex degrees of a graph, written in non-increasing order, is its degree sequence. The Erdős–Gallai theorem characterises which sequences of nonnegative integers can be degree sequences of simple graphs, while the Havel–Hakimi algorithm constructs a graph from a realisable sequence.
