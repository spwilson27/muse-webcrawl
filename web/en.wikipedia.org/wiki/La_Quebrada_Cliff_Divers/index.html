The La Quebrada Cliff Divers (Spanish: Clavadistas de La Quebrada) are a group of professional high divers who perform daily shows for tourists in Acapulco, Mexico. They dive from cliffs 35 metres (115 ft) high into a narrow inlet of the Pacific below, timing their entry to coincide with the arrival of a wave that deepens the water.

The dive

The inlet at La Quebrada is only about 4–5 metres deep at low water in the surge channel; divers must calculate the moment an incoming wave raises the level enough to make the dive survivable, entering the water at speeds around 80 km/h. Before diving, performers traditionally climb the cliff face barefoot, stop at a small shrine to pray, and then leap — sometimes with burning torches in night shows. The evening performance, viewed from the terraces of the old El Mirador hotel or from a public viewpoint, is lit and ends with dives by several performers in sequence.

History

Local youths were diving at La Quebrada for sport and wagers by the early 20th century; organised professional shows date from 1934, when the divers formed an association. The spectacle became an emblem of Acapulco's golden age of tourism in the 1950s, featured in films and television — Elvis Presley's Fun in Acapulco (1963) included a fictionalised version of the dive — and the divers' guild remains a fixture of the city even as Acapulco's tourist trade has fluctuated.
