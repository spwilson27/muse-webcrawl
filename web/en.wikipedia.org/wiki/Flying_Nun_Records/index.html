Flying Nun Records is a New Zealand independent record label formed in Christchurch in 1981 by music store manager Roger Shepherd. The label became central to the development of the Dunedin sound, a lo-fi, guitar-based style of indie rock associated with bands from the southern city of Dunedin, and it remains one of the most influential independent labels in New Zealand music history.

History

Shepherd founded the label to record local post-punk bands that major labels ignored, beginning with releases by The Clean, whose single "Tally Ho!" (1981) reached the New Zealand top 20 despite minimal promotion. Early signings included The Chills, The Verlaines, Sneaky Feelings, The Stones and Look Blue Go Purple. Records were typically recorded cheaply, often on four-track equipment by Chris Knox of Tall Dwarfs, giving the catalogue its characteristic raw sound.

During the late 1980s and 1990s the label expanded internationally, licensing releases overseas and signing acts such as The Headless Chickens, Bailter Space, The Jean-Paul Sartre Experience and later The D4 and The Phoenix Foundation. Financial difficulties led to a sale to Festival Mushroom Records and subsequently Warner Music New Zealand. In 2009 Shepherd bought the label back, and Flying Nun resumed independent operation, reissuing its back catalogue and signing new artists. In 2021 the label marked its 40th anniversary.

Legacy

Flying Nun's roster influenced alternative rock worldwide; American bands including Pavement and Yo La Tengo cited Dunedin groups as inspirations. The label's catalogue is widely regarded as a cornerstone of New Zealand cultural identity.
