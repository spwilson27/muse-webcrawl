Arcangelo Corelli

Arcangelo Corelli
Born: 17 February 1653, Fusignano, Papal States
Died: 8 January 1713 (aged 59), Rome
Occupation: Violinist, composer
Era: Baroque

Arcangelo Corelli (17 February 1653 – 8 January 1713) was an Italian composer and violinist of the Baroque era. His music was key in the development of the modern genres of sonata and concerto, in establishing the preeminence of the violin, and in consolidating tonality as the organising principle of Western art music.

Life

Born in Fusignano, Corelli studied in Bologna before moving to Rome, where he rose to prominence as a virtuoso violinist and director of ensembles for patrons including Queen Christina of Sweden and Cardinal Pietro Ottoboni. Unlike many contemporaries he did not compose opera; his output consists entirely of instrumental chamber music published in six opus-numbered collections.

Works

Corelli's publications comprise sets of trio sonatas (Opp. 1–4), solo violin sonatas (Op. 5, including the famous variations on "La Folia"), and the Concerti grossi, Op. 6, published posthumously in 1714. The Op. 6 set, especially the "Christmas Concerto" (No. 8), helped define the concerto grosso form, contrasting a small concertino group with the full string orchestra.

Influence

Corelli's style — clear tonal harmony, singing melodies, and disciplined violin technique — shaped composers across Europe, including Handel and Vivaldi, and his sonatas remained models of the genre for a century.
