Manufacturing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A modern manufacturing assembly line

Manufacturing is the creation or production of goods with the help of equipment, labor, machines, tools, and chemical or biological processing or formulation. It is the essence of the secondary sector of the economy. The term may refer to a range of human activity, from handicraft to high-tech, but it is most commonly applied to industrial design, in which raw materials from the primary sector are transformed into finished goods on a large scale.

Manufacturing engineering, or the manufacturing process, are the steps through which raw materials are transformed into a final product. The manufacturing process begins with the product design, and materials specification. These materials are then modified through manufacturing processes to become the required part. Manufacturing can be performed manually or by using machines, and sometimes in an integrated combination of both.

History

Manufacturing in the modern industrial sense emerged during the Industrial Revolution, beginning in Britain in the late 18th century, when production shifted from artisan workshops to mechanised factories powered first by water and steam and later by electricity. The development of interchangeable parts, the assembly line — famously applied by Henry Ford to automobile production — and later automation and computer numerical control transformed productivity and the organisation of work.

In the 20th and 21st centuries, manufacturing became increasingly globalised, with supply chains spanning many countries. Lean manufacturing, pioneered by Toyota, emphasised waste reduction and continuous improvement, while recent decades have seen the rise of additive manufacturing (3D printing), robotics and so-called Industry 4.0 approaches integrating sensors and data analytics into production.

Types of manufacturing

Manufacturing processes are commonly grouped into categories including casting and moulding, machining, joining, shearing and forming, and additive processes. Production systems range from job-shop production of one-off items, through batch production, to continuous mass production of standardised goods.

The manufacturing sector is a major component of most national economies, employing large numbers of people and accounting for a significant share of gross domestic product in industrialised and industrialising countries alike.
