John Newton Brown

Born: June 29, 1803, New London, Connecticut, U.S.
Died: May 14, 1868 (aged 64), Providence, Rhode Island, U.S.
Occupation: Universalist minister, editor, author

John Newton Brown (June 29, 1803 – May 14, 1868) was an American Universalist minister, editor, and author, best remembered as the compiler of the Encyclopaedia of Religious Knowledge (1835), a major reference work on theology, church history, and biblical subjects that remained in print in revised editions for decades.

Life and career

Brown was born in New London, Connecticut. He entered the Universalist ministry as a young man and served congregations in several New England communities, gaining a reputation as an energetic preacher and debater in an era when Universalism—the doctrine that all souls would ultimately be reconciled to God—was expanding rapidly in the United States.

He edited Universalist periodicals and contributed extensively to denominational literature, writing on doctrine, biblical criticism, and religious history.

Encyclopaedia of Religious Knowledge

Brown's greatest work was the Encyclopaedia of Religious Knowledge; or, Dictionary of the Bible, Theology, Religious Biography, All Religions, Ecclesiastical History, and Missions, first published in 1835. The large single volume collected thousands of entries on biblical persons and places, doctrines, denominations, and missionaries. It was repeatedly reprinted and revised, and remained a standard desk reference for clergy and lay readers through much of the nineteenth century.

Later years

Brown continued his editorial and ministerial work until his death in Providence, Rhode Island, in 1868. His encyclopaedia is frequently cited by historians of American religion as evidence of the breadth of popular religious learning in the early republic.
