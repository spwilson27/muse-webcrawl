Growing season

Measured by: Frost-free days

Depends on: Climate, latitude, altitude

The growing season is the part of the year during which local weather conditions — principally temperature and rainfall — permit plants to grow. In temperate regions it is often defined as the period between the last killing frost in spring and the first killing frost in autumn.

Variation

Growing seasons are longest near the equator, where growth may continue all year, and shortest in polar and high mountain regions. In tropical areas the season may be governed more by the alternation of wet and dry seasons than by temperature. In parts of the Southern Hemisphere the growing season runs roughly from October to April.

Agriculture

The length of the growing season limits which crops can be grown in a region; farmers and breeders select varieties whose time to maturity fits the local season, and climate change has measurably lengthened frost-free seasons in many mid-latitude regions.
