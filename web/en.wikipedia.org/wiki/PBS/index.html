The Public Broadcasting Service (PBS) is an American public broadcaster and non-profit organization that provides television programming to a network of more than 350 member stations across the United States. It is the most prominent provider of educational and cultural television programming to American public television stations, distributing shows such as Sesame Street, PBS NewsHour, Masterpiece, and Antiques Roadshow.

History

PBS was established on November 3, 1969 by the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, taking over the functions of its predecessor, National Educational Television (NET), and began operations on October 5, 1970. Unlike commercial broadcast networks, PBS is owned collectively by its member stations and does not produce most of the programming it distributes; instead, shows are produced by member stations such as WGBH Boston, independent producers, and international partners.

Programming

PBS programming spans children's shows (on the PBS Kids block), news and public affairs, science series such as Nova, nature documentaries such as Nature, history series by filmmakers such as Ken Burns, drama (under the Masterpiece banner), and performing arts programmes.

Funding

PBS and its stations are funded by a combination of federal appropriations through the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, state and local support, viewer contributions ("viewers like you"), and corporate underwriting.
