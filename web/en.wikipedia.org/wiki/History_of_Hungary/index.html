History of Hungary

The history of Hungary begins with the Magyar conquest of the Carpathian Basin in 895–896 and the founding of the Christian Kingdom of Hungary by Saint Stephen I in 1000. The kingdom survived Mongol invasion, became a great power under Matthias Corvinus, was partitioned after the Battle of Mohács (1526), and spent centuries under Ottoman and Habsburg rule before the modern republic emerged in the 20th century.

The historic Kingdom of Hungary was much larger than the modern Hungarian state.

Medieval kingdom

The Magyars, a Finno-Ugric people from the steppe, settled the Carpathian Basin under Árpád. Stephen I was crowned king in 1000 or 1001 and Christianised the country. The Golden Bull of 1222 limited royal power. The Mongol invasion of 1241–42 devastated the kingdom; Béla IV rebuilt it with stone castles. Under the Angevin kings and then Matthias Corvinus (r. 1458–1490), Hungary became a major European power with a flourishing Renaissance court.

Ottoman era and Habsburg rule

At Mohács in 1526 King Louis II was killed and central Hungary fell under Ottoman rule for 150 years, while the west and north went to the Habsburgs and Transylvania became a semi-independent principality. After the reconquest of Buda (1686) the whole country came under Habsburg rule. The war of independence led by Francis II Rákóczi (1703–1711) failed, but Hungary retained a separate Diet and constitution within the monarchy.

Reform era and 1848

The reform era, led by István Széchenyi and Lajos Kossuth, modernised the economy and society. The Revolution of 1848 declared independence, but was crushed with Russian help in 1849. The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 created the Dual Monarchy, giving Hungary its own government under Franz Joseph.

20th century

After the First World War the Treaty of Trianon (1920) stripped Hungary of two-thirds of its territory and population. Under Regent Miklós Horthy, Hungary allied with Nazi Germany and lost again in 1945; some 437,000 Hungarian Jews were deported and murdered in 1944. A communist People's Republic was established in 1949. The Revolution of 1956, led by Imre Nagy, was crushed by Soviet tanks. János Kádár's "goulash communism" followed, until the democratic transition of 1989–90. Hungary joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004.
