Load factor (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, the load factor is the ratio of the lift of an aircraft to its weight, and represents a global measure of the stress ("load") to which the structure of the aircraft is subjected. It is expressed as a multiple of the acceleration of gravity, in units of g.

Definition

The load factor n is defined as n = L / W, where L is lift and W is weight. In straight and level flight, lift equals weight and the load factor is 1 g. In a level turn or during a pull-up from a dive, the wings must generate lift greater than the weight, so the load factor exceeds 1 g; in a banked turn at angle θ, the load factor is 1/cos θ, so a 60° bank produces 2 g.

Effects

High load factors increase the stall speed of the aircraft, since the wing must produce more lift at any given speed. Certification standards specify limit load factors that aircraft structures must withstand — typically +3.8 g for transport-category aeroplanes, higher for aerobatic aircraft, and negative limits for inverted manoeuvres. Pilots and passengers experience load factor as apparent weight, and sustained high g can cause grey-out, blackout, and G-LOC (g-induced loss of consciousness).
