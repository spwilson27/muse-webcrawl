Mount Vernon, Washington

Country: United States
State: Washington
County: Skagit (county seat)
Population (2020): approx. 35,000
Named for: Mount Vernon, Virginia

Mount Vernon is a city in and the county seat of Skagit County, Washington, United States. It lies on the Skagit River in the Skagit Valley floodplain, about 60 miles north of Seattle, and had a population of roughly 35,000 at the 2020 census. The city is best known for the Skagit Valley Tulip Festival held each April, which draws hundreds of thousands of visitors to the surrounding bulb fields.

History

The area was home to the Upper Skagit and other Coast Salish peoples before American settlers arrived. Jasper Gates and Joseph Dwelley settled at the site in 1870 at a place where logjams had blocked navigation on the Skagit River; after the jams were cleared in the late 1870s, a town grew up, platted in 1878 and named after George Washington's Virginia estate. Mount Vernon was incorporated in 1889 and became the county seat. Logging, dairy farming, and dike-building on the floodplain shaped its early economy; a major flood in 1909 and later floods led to an extensive dike system, and a downtown floodwall and riverwalk were completed in the 2010s.

Economy and culture

Agriculture remains central to the surrounding valley — potatoes, berries, dairy, and flower bulbs — while the city itself is a retail and services centre for the county, with Skagit Valley College located there. Interstate 5 and the Amtrak Cascades service at Mount Vernon station connect the city to Seattle and Vancouver, British Columbia. The historic Lincoln Theatre (1926) hosts concerts and films downtown.

Tulip Festival

The Skagit Valley Tulip Festival began in 1984 to showcase the valley's tulip, daffodil, and iris fields, among the largest bulb-growing areas in North America. Display gardens such as Roozengaarde and Tulip Town anchor the month-long April festival.
