Helix pomatia, known as the Roman snail, Burgundy snail or edible snail, is a species of large, air-breathing land snail native to Europe. Its shell reaches about 40–55 mm in diameter and is pale brown with darker bands.

Biology and use

The snail inhabits woodlands, meadows and gardens, feeds on plants, and hibernates over winter sealed behind a calcareous epiphragm; in summer drought it may aestivate. Like other stylommatophorans it is a simultaneous hermaphrodite famous for its courtship involving a calcareous "love dart". H. pomatia is the classic escargot species of French cuisine and is collected and farmed (heliciculture); because of overcollection it is protected in several countries and listed under the EU Habitats Directive.
