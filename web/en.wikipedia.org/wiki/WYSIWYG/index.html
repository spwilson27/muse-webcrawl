WYSIWYG

WYSIWYG (pronounced "wiz-ee-wig") is an acronym for "what you see is what you get". It describes software in which content displayed while editing appears essentially identical to the final output, such as a printed page or web page.

History

The phrase was popularized by comedian Flip Wilson's character Geraldine in the 1970s and adopted by computing. Early influential WYSIWYG systems included the Bravo word processor at Xerox PARC (1974), developed for the Alto computer, and Apple's MacWrite (1984). The phrase is often attributed in computing contexts to Charles Simonyi and the PARC environment.

Applications

WYSIWYG editors are used in word processors, desktop publishing, web design tools, presentation software and HTML editors such as Dreamweaver. Critics note that WYSIWYG hides underlying markup and can produce inconsistent formatting; alternatives include markup languages (LaTeX, HTML) sometimes described as WYSIWYM ("what you see is what you mean").
