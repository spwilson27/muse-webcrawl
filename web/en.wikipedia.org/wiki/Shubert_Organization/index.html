Shubert Organization

The Shubert Organization is a theatrical producing and theatre-owning organisation based in New York City, United States. It owns and operates 17 Broadway theatres, as well as off-Broadway's Little Shubert Theatre (now closed), and theatres in Boston and Philadelphia. It is the oldest professional theatrical management organisation in the United States and remains the largest owner of Broadway theatres.

History

The organisation was founded by the Shubert brothers — Sam, Lee, and Jacob J. Shubert — theatrical managers from Syracuse, New York, sons of Lithuanian Jewish immigrants. At the turn of the 20th century, American theatre was dominated by the Theatrical Syndicate, a booking monopoly. The Shuberts challenged the Syndicate by building and acquiring their own theatres and producing their own shows, recruiting stars such as Sarah Bernhardt who had been blacklisted by the Syndicate. By 1910 they had broken the monopoly and become the dominant force in American theatre.

After Sam's death in a 1905 train wreck, Lee and J.J. expanded the empire to dozens of theatres nationwide and hundreds of touring productions. In 1950 the United States government filed an antitrust suit against the Shuberts; a 1956 settlement required them to divest 12 theatres in six cities and give up their booking business.

In 1972–73 Lee's heirs transferred control to the not-for-profit Shubert Foundation, which uses theatre profits for philanthropic support of theatre and dance in the United States. The foundation is the largest institutional funder of theatre not-for-profit companies in the country.

Broadway theatres owned

Shubert theatres on Broadway include the Ambassador, Barrymore, Belasco, Booth, Broadhurst, Broadway, Cort (renamed the James Earl Jones Theatre in 2022), Golden, Imperial, Bernard B. Jacobs, Longacre, Lyceum, Majestic, Music Box, Gerald Schoenfeld, Shubert, and Winter Garden theatres.
