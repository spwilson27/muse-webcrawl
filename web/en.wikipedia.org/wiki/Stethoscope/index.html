Stethoscope

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The stethoscope is a medical device for auscultation — listening to the internal sounds of the body, such as those of the heart, lungs, and intestines. A typical acoustic stethoscope consists of a chest piece (with a diaphragm and/or bell) connected by flexible tubing to two earpieces. It is one of the most recognizable symbols of the medical profession.

History

The stethoscope was invented in 1816 by the French physician René Laennec at the Necker Hospital in Paris. Embarrassed by the impropriety of pressing his ear directly to a young woman's chest, and recalling that sound travels through solid wood, Laennec rolled a sheet of paper into a tube; the resulting monaural wooden cylinder became the first stethoscope, which he described in his 1819 treatise on mediate auscultation. Binaural designs with flexible tubes followed in the mid-19th century, notably those of George Cammann. In the 1940s Rappaport and Sprague refined the dual-head chest piece, and in the 1960s David Littmann introduced the lighter, tunable-diaphragm design that became the modern standard.

Types and use

The diaphragm side of the chest piece best transmits higher-frequency sounds such as normal heart sounds and breath sounds; the open bell, applied lightly, better picks up low-frequency sounds such as the S3 gallop and the rumble of mitral stenosis. Stethoscopes are used together with a sphygmomanometer to measure blood pressure by the Korotkoff sounds. Variants include paediatric and infant sizes, cardiology models with superior acoustics, electronic stethoscopes that amplify and can record or transmit sounds, Doppler stethoscopes, and the pinard horn used in midwifery.

Cultural significance

The stethoscope around the neck is a near-universal emblem of physicians and nurses, and white-coat ceremonies for medical students often include the presentation of a first stethoscope. Its role has been debated as point-of-care ultrasound has spread, but it remains a standard, inexpensive diagnostic instrument worldwide.
