Rice porridge

Rice porridge is a dish made by cooking rice in a large amount of water or milk until it breaks down into a thick, soft consistency. It is eaten across Asia and Europe, both as a savoury staple and as a sweet dessert, and is often given to children, the elderly and the sick because it is easy to digest.

Varieties

In China it is known as congee (zhou), flavoured with ingredients such as pork, century egg, ginger and spring onion. Japanese okayu is a plain, lightly salted version often eaten when unwell; Korean juk includes varieties with abalone, pumpkin or pine nuts. Vietnamese cháo, Filipino lugaw and arroz caldo, Thai khao tom, and Indonesian bubur are regional relatives.

In Europe

In Scandinavia, rice porridge cooked in milk (risengrød in Denmark, risengrynsgrøt in Norway, risgrynsgröt in Sweden) is a traditional Christmas dish, served with cinnamon, sugar and butter, and often with a whole almond hidden in the pot for luck. Rice pudding, baked or simmered with milk and sugar, is the related dessert in Britain and elsewhere.

Preparation

Typical ratios range from about 1:5 to 1:10 rice to liquid. Long simmering gelatinises the starch and breaks the grains down; leftover cooked rice can also be simmered to make a quicker version.
