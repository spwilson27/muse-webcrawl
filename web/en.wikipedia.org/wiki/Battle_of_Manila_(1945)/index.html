The Battle of Manila (Filipino: Labanan sa Maynila; 3 February – 3 March 1945) was a major battle of the Philippine campaign of 1944–45 during the Second World War. It was fought by American and Filipino forces against Japanese troops in Manila, the capital city of the Philippines. The month-long battle, which resulted in the death of at least 100,000 civilians and the complete devastation of the city, was the scene of the worst urban fighting by American forces in the Pacific theater.

U.S. troops in the ruins of Intramuros during the battle.

Background

General Douglas MacArthur's Sixth Army under Lieutenant General Walter Krueger landed at Lingayen Gulf on 9 January 1945 and advanced rapidly south toward Manila. MacArthur ordered a swift drive on the capital, led by the 1st Cavalry Division and the 37th Infantry Division, which entered northern Manila on 3 February and freed the civilian internees held at the University of Santo Tomas.

The battle

Although General Tomoyuki Yamashita had ordered the city's evacuation, Rear Admiral Sanji Iwabuchi of the Imperial Japanese Navy chose to defend Manila with some 17,000 naval and army personnel. His men entrenched themselves in the government buildings, the university, and the ancient walled city of Intramuros. American forces advanced methodically, street by street, supported by artillery fire that devastated whole districts. Japanese troops committed widespread atrocities against Filipino civilians, an episode known as the Manila massacre. The battle ended on 3 March 1945 with the destruction of Iwabuchi's command; Iwabuchi himself died by suicide.

Aftermath

Manila was left one of the most devastated capital cities of the Second World War, comparable to Warsaw. Historic Intramuros was largely destroyed. Roughly 1,000 American soldiers were killed, some 16,000 Japanese troops died, and at least 100,000 Filipino civilians perished in the fighting and massacres. The battle became a symbol of both liberation and tragedy in Philippine memory.
