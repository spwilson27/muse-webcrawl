Santa Cruz de Tenerife (Spanish: [ˈsanta ˈkruθ ðe teneˈɾife]), commonly abbreviated as Santa Cruz, is a city in Spain, the capital of the island of Tenerife and co-capital (with Las Palmas) of the autonomous community of the Canary Islands. Its port is one of the busiest in Spain, and the city is famous throughout the Spanish-speaking world for its Carnival, declared a Fiesta of International Tourist Interest.

History

The area was inhabited by the Guanche people, and a mummy cave complex at nearby San Andrés attests to pre-conquest settlement. On 3 May 1494 the Castilian conquistador Alonso Fernández de Lugo landed here and raised a holy cross (the santa cruz that gives the city its name) as he began the conquest of Tenerife, the last of the Canary Islands to fall (1496). For centuries it was a modest port and fishing village under the shadow of the inland capital, San Cristóbal de La Laguna.

Santa Cruz's defences repelled three famous attacks: by Robert Blake in 1657 (who destroyed a Spanish treasure fleet in the harbour), by John Jennings in 1706, and by Rear Admiral Horatio Nelson in 1797, who lost his right arm to a cannonball from the castle of San Juan in the Battle of Santa Cruz de Tenerife. The city became the capital of Tenerife and of the Canaries in the 19th century, and in 1927 the capital status of the archipelago was split between Santa Cruz and Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, an arrangement that continues today.

Landmarks and culture

The city's icon is the Auditorio de Tenerife "Adán Martín" (2003), Santiago Calatrava's sweeping white concert hall on the seafront, reminiscent of a breaking wave or a sail. Other landmarks include the twin Torres de Santa Cruz, the tallest residential towers in Spain when completed; the Plaza de España, rebuilt around an artificial lake beside the harbour; the church of La Concepción, with its distinctive tower; the García Sanabria park; and the Palmetum botanical garden, created on a former landfill. Just north lies the beach of Las Teresitas at San Andrés, covered in imported Saharan sand.

The Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, held in February, is considered the second most popular carnival in the world after Rio de Janeiro's, drawing hundreds of thousands of revellers with its gala election of the Carnival Queen, street parades (the Coso) and the comic "Burial of the Sardine".

Economy

The port handles cargo, ferries to the other islands and the mainland, cruise ships and one of the world's busiest bunkering trades; the Tenerife refinery, one of Spain's oldest, stands at the city's edge. Tourism and services dominate employment.
