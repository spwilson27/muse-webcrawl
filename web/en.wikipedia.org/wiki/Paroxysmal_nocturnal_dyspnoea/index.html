Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea

Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea (PND)
Specialty: Cardiology, pulmonology
Symptoms: Sudden night-time attacks of severe breathlessness waking the patient from sleep; cough; relief on sitting up
Cause: Left ventricular failure with pulmonary congestion
Related: Orthopnoea, cardiac asthma

Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND) is an attack of severe shortness of breath and coughing that generally occurs at night, one to several hours after falling asleep, and awakens the person from sleep. It is a symptom of left-sided heart failure and is related to orthopnoea, the breathlessness experienced on lying flat.

Mechanism

When a person with a failing left ventricle lies down, blood is redistributed from the legs and splanchnic circulation to the central circulation, and fluid that accumulated in the tissues during the day is reabsorbed. The weakened left ventricle cannot handle the increased venous return, so pressure rises in the pulmonary circulation and fluid transudes into the lung interstitium and alveoli, stiffening the lungs and stimulating receptors that trigger breathlessness. The attack classically builds over minutes; the sufferer sits upright, often at an open window gasping for air, and symptoms resolve over roughly 10–30 minutes after sitting or standing, as fluid redistributes away from the lungs.

Clinical significance

PND is considered a specific (though not perfectly sensitive) marker of heart failure and helps distinguish cardiac breathlessness from nocturnal asthma and other causes of night-time dyspnoea — although severe pulmonary congestion can itself provoke wheeze ("cardiac asthma"). Assessment includes history, examination for raised jugular venous pressure, a third heart sound and oedema, natriuretic peptide levels, chest X-ray, and echocardiography. Treatment is that of the underlying heart failure: diuretics to reduce preload, treatment of hypertension, ischaemia and arrhythmias, and disease-modifying drugs such as ACE inhibitors and beta-blockers.
