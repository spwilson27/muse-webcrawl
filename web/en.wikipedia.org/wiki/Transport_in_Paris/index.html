Transport in Paris

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transport in Paris is provided by an extensive, integrated network of metro, suburban rail, trams, buses, and national and international rail and air services, serving the city of Paris and the Île-de-France region of about twelve million inhabitants. The network is coordinated by Île-de-France Mobilités, with the RATP and SNCF as the principal operators. Payment is unified through the Navigo pass and contactless ticketing.

Métro and RER

The Paris Métro, opened in 1900, has sixteen lines and more than 300 stations, with trains running densely through the city proper; lines 1 and 14 are fully automated. The RER (Réseau Express Régional), five lines of express suburban trains running in tunnels beneath central Paris, connects the city to suburbs as far as 50 km away and carries over a billion passengers a year combined with the Métro. The Transilien network adds further suburban rail services from the mainline termini. The Grand Paris Express project is adding some 200 km of automated metro lines (15–18) and dozens of stations around the region, largely opening in stages from the mid-2020s.

Trams and buses

After the near-total dismantling of the first-generation tramways by the 1950s, modern tram lines (T1–T14) have been built since 1992, mostly in the suburbs, together with tram-train services. RATP and suburban operators run an extensive bus network; since 2016 policy under the city's climate plan has steadily pedestrianised quaysides, expanded bus lanes and cycle tracks, and restricted the most polluting vehicles through a low-emission zone.

Cycling and walking

The Vélib' bike-sharing scheme, launched in 2007, and hundreds of kilometres of newer cycle lanes have sharply increased cycling's mode share. Walking accounts for the largest share of trips within the city itself.

Rail and air gateways

Paris is the hub of the French TGV network: its great termini — Gare du Nord (the busiest station in Europe), Gare de Lyon, Gare de l'Est, Gare Saint-Lazare, Gare Montparnasse, Gare d'Austerlitz, and Gare de Bercy — connect to all of France and, via Eurostar and other services, to London, Brussels, Amsterdam, and Germany. Charles de Gaulle and Orly airports form one of Europe's largest airport systems, handling over 100 million passengers a year in normal times.

Roads

The Boulevard Périphérique ring road encircles the city; radial motorways connect to the national autoroute network. Car use within Paris has fallen steadily for decades as space is reallocated to transit, cycling, and pedestrians.
