Ochlocracy

Ochlocracy is government by the mob — the corrupted form of democracy in classical political theory.

Ochlocracy (from Greek okhlokratia, "rule of the crowd") is a form of government in which political power is exercised by an unruly mob — the mass of the population acting on impulse, intimidation, and passion rather than through law or deliberation. It is commonly known as mob rule.

Classical theory

The term was used by the Greek historian Polybius in his account of the cycle of constitutions (anacyclosis). In Polybius's scheme, each good form of government degenerates into a corrupt counterpart: kingship into tyranny, aristocracy into oligarchy, and democracy into ochlocracy, in which the people, habituated to living at others' expense and led by demagogues, resort to violence and lawlessness until a single ruler restores order. Aristotle similarly regarded ochlocracy as an extreme or deviant form of democracy in which the multitude rules in its own interest by decree rather than by law.

Distinguishing features

Ochlocracy is marked by decisions driven by the passions of the crowd, intimidation of officials and minorities, disregard for legal procedure, and susceptibility to demagogues who flatter and incite the mob. Later thinkers, including thinkers of the Enlightenment and the American founding generation, invoked the danger of mob rule in arguing for constitutional checks, representative institutions, and the rule of law in democratic systems.

Related concepts

Ochlocracy is related to but distinct from demagoguery, in which a leader manipulates the populace, and from majoritarian tyranny, often called the "tyranny of the majority", in which a majority legally but oppressively rules over a minority.
