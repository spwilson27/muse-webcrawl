Overcast

A completely overcast sky

Overcast or overcast weather, as defined by the World Meteorological Organization, is the meteorological condition of clouds obscuring at least 95% of the sky. However, the total cloud cover must not be entirely due to obscuring phenomena near the surface, such as fog, for the sky to be classified as overcast.

In technical weather observation, cloud amount is reported in oktas (eighths of the sky covered). An overcast sky is reported as 8 oktas. Overcast conditions are most often produced by extensive stratiform cloud layers such as stratus, altostratus and nimbostratus, frequently associated with warm fronts and mid-latitude cyclones.

Effects

An overcast sky diffuses sunlight, reducing direct solar radiation, shadows and glare, moderating daytime temperatures upward in winter and downward in summer, and producing even, soft illumination valued in photography. Prolonged overcast periods are common in oceanic climates at high latitudes in winter and in monsoon seasons.
