Simchat Torah

Simchat Torah (Hebrew: שִׂמְחַת תּוֹרָה, "Rejoicing with/of the Torah") is a Jewish holiday that celebrates and marks the conclusion of the annual cycle of public Torah readings and the beginning of a new cycle. It is celebrated on the 22nd day of Tishrei in Israel (where it coincides with Shemini Atzeret) and on the 23rd of Tishrei in the Diaspora.

Customs

The central feature of the holiday is the hakafot (circuits): all the Torah scrolls are removed from the ark and carried around the synagogue in joyous processions, usually accompanied by singing and dancing, seven times in the evening and again in the morning. Children often participate waving flags and receive treats. During the morning service the final portion of Deuteronomy (Vezot Haberakhah) is read, and immediately afterwards the first portion of Genesis (Bereshit), symbolising that the study of Torah never ends.

Honours

Two honourees are specially called up: the Chatan Torah ("Bridegroom of the Torah"), who receives the final aliyah of Deuteronomy, and the Chatan Bereshit ("Bridegroom of Genesis"), who receives the first aliyah of the new cycle; corresponding honours for women (Kallat haTorah and Kallat Bereshit) are observed in many communities.
