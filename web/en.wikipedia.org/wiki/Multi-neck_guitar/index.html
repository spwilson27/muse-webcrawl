Multi-neck guitar

A multi-neck guitar is a guitar that has two or more necks attached to a single body, allowing a player to switch quickly between different tunings, string counts, or sounds — for example a six-string and a twelve-string neck, or a fretted and fretless neck — without changing instruments.

History

Double-neck instruments date back centuries in the lute and harp-guitar families. In the 20th century, Gibson introduced the EDS-1275 double-neck electric guitar in the late 1950s, combining six- and twelve-string necks. The design became famous through players such as Jimmy Page of Led Zeppelin, who used it live for songs requiring both sounds.

Variants

Common combinations include six-string plus twelve-string, guitar plus bass, and fretted plus fretless. More exotic instruments have three or more necks; luthiers and manufacturers have built triple-necks and even five-neck showpieces. Multi-neck instruments are heavy and are often used partly for stage spectacle.

Notable players

Besides Page, well-known users include Don Felder of the Eagles ("Hotel California"), John McLaughlin, Steve Vai, and Rick Nielsen of Cheap Trick, known for multi-neck custom guitars.
