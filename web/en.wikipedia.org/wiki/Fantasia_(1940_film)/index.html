Fantasia (1940 film)

Fantasia is a 1940 American animated musical anthology film produced by Walt Disney Productions, with story direction by Joe Grant and Dick Huemer and production supervision by Walt Disney and Ben Sharpsteen. It consists of eight animated segments set to pieces of classical music conducted by Leopold Stokowski, seven of which are performed by the Philadelphia Orchestra.

The film's music critic and composer Deems Taylor acts as the film's master of ceremonies, introducing each segment in live-action. Disney settled on the film's concept in 1938 as work neared completion on The Sorcerer's Apprentice, originally an elaborate Silly Symphony cartoon designed as a comeback role for Mickey Mouse, who had declined in popularity.

Segments

The eight segments are: Toccata and Fugue in D Minor by J. S. Bach; Nutcracker Suite by Tchaikovsky; The Sorcerer's Apprentice by Paul Dukas, featuring Mickey Mouse as an aspiring magician; Rite of Spring by Igor Stravinsky, depicting the history of Earth and the reign and extinction of the dinosaurs; The Pastoral Symphony by Beethoven; Dance of the Hours by Amilcare Ponchielli, a ballet of ostriches, hippos, elephants and alligators; and Night on Bald Mountain by Modest Mussorgsky paired with Ave Maria by Franz Schubert.

Production and Fantasound

Fantasia was notable for its pioneering stereophonic sound system, Fantasound, developed for the film's roadshow engagements. At a cost of over $2 million — more than twice that of Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs — the film was a box-office disappointment on its initial release, partly because World War II had cut off the European market.

Legacy

Reissues gradually turned the film into a critical and commercial success. It is ranked among the greatest animated films ever made; in 1998 the American Film Institute placed it 58th on its list of the 100 greatest American films, and in 1990 it was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry. A sequel, Fantasia 2000, was released in 1999.
