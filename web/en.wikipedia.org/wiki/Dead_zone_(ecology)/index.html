Dead zone (ecology)

Dead zone
CauseEutrophication, hypoxia
ExamplesGulf of Mexico, Baltic Sea, Chesapeake Bay

Dead zones are hypoxic low-oxygen areas in oceans, lakes and rivers too oxygen-poor to support most marine life, caused mainly by eutrophication: excess nitrogen and phosphorus from fertiliser runoff and sewage fuel algal growth.

Formation

Dead algae sink and are decomposed by bacteria consuming oxygen; stratification prevents re-oxygenation, creating hypoxia. Mobile animals flee, sessile organisms suffocate.

Effects

Hundreds are documented worldwide; the Mississippi-fed Gulf of Mexico zone spans thousands of square kilometres in summer. Reducing nutrient runoff can reverse them, as in parts of the North Sea and Black Sea.
