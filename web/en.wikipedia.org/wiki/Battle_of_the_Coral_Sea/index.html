The Battle of the Coral Sea, fought from 4 to 8 May 1942, was a major naval battle of the Pacific War and the first naval engagement in history fought entirely by carrier aircraft, in which the opposing ships never sighted or fired directly on one another. It halted a Japanese seaborne offensive against Port Moresby in New Guinea.

Background

Japan's Operation Mo aimed to capture Port Moresby and Tulagi, extending Japan's defensive perimeter and threatening the sea lanes between the United States and Australia. Alerted by signals intelligence from codebreakers, Admiral Chester Nimitz sent Task Force 17 under Rear Admiral Frank Jack Fletcher, built around the carriers Yorktown and Lexington, to intercept, joined by an Australian-American cruiser force under Rear Admiral John Crace.

The battle

On 4 May Yorktown's aircraft attacked the Japanese landing at Tulagi. On 7 May both sides launched strikes based on faulty sightings: the Japanese sank the oiler Neosho and the destroyer Sims, while American aircraft found and sank the light carrier Shōhō — prompting the famous signal "Scratch one flattop". On 8 May the main carrier forces struck each other simultaneously. Lexington was fatally damaged by torpedoes and bombs and later scuttled; Yorktown was damaged. Japanese aircraft sank nothing further but badly damaged Shōkaku, while Zuikaku's air group was depleted.

Significance

Though Japan inflicted heavier tonnage losses, the Port Moresby invasion convoy turned back — the first Japanese offensive of the war to be checked. Critically, Shōkaku's damage and Zuikaku's air losses kept both carriers out of the Battle of Midway a month later, evening the odds in that decisive engagement.
