Grand staff

The grand staff (also called the great stave) is a pair of musical staves joined together and used to notate music for instruments with a wide pitch range, most notably the piano. It consists of a treble staff on top and a bass staff below, connected at the left by a brace and a line, and read simultaneously.

Structure

In piano music, the upper staff, in the treble (G) clef, is usually played by the right hand, and the lower staff, in the bass (F) clef, by the left hand, though the hands may cross or share material. Middle C (C4) is written on a ledger line between the two staves, one ledger line below the treble staff and one above the bass staff, giving the grand staff a continuous range of more than four octaves before further ledger lines are needed.

Usage

Besides the piano, the grand staff is used for other keyboard instruments (organ music often adds a third staff for the pedals), the harp, and some mallet percussion, and in reduced scores and hymnals, where vocal parts may be condensed onto two staves. In hymnals this is sometimes called short score or a piano-vocal reduction.
