Sète

Country: France

Region: Occitania

Department: Hérault

Population: approx. 45,000

Sète (French pronunciation: [sɛt]; Occitan: Seta), historically also spelled Cette, is a port city and commune in the Hérault department in the Occitania region of southern France. Situated on the Mediterranean coast between the Étang de Thau lagoon and the sea, at the eastern end of the Canal du Midi, it is sometimes called the "Venice of Languedoc" for its network of canals.

History

The town was founded in 1666 as the sea outlet of the Canal du Midi, whose construction under Paul Riquet linked the Atlantic to the Mediterranean. Sète grew into the most important French fishing port on the Mediterranean and a major commercial harbour trading wine and other Languedoc produce. The hill of Mont Saint-Clair rises above the town, offering views over the port, the lagoon famous for oyster farming, and the coast.

Culture

Sète is the birthplace of the poet Paul Valéry, who is buried in its marine cemetery and celebrated the town in his poem Le Cimetière marin, and of the singer-songwriter Georges Brassens. The town is known for water jousting tournaments held on its canals each summer, culminating in the Saint-Louis festival in August.
