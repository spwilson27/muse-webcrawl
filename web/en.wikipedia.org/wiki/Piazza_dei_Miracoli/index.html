Piazza dei Miracoli

The Piazza dei Miracoli ("Square of Miracles"), formally known as Piazza del Duomo ("Cathedral Square"), is a walled 8.87-hectare area in Pisa, Tuscany, Italy, recognised as a centre of European medieval art and one of the finest architectural complexes in the world. It is dominated by four great religious edifices in the Pisan Romanesque style: Pisa Cathedral, the Pisa Baptistery, the Leaning Tower (the cathedral's campanile), and the Camposanto Monumentale (monumental cemetery).

The cathedral, baptistery and leaning tower on the green of the square.

The monuments

  • Pisa Cathedral (begun 1063–1064), dedicated to the Assumption of the Virgin Mary, with bronze doors and a pulpit by Giovanni Pisano.
  • The Baptistery (begun 1152), the largest baptistery in Italy, notable for its acoustics and Nicola Pisano's pulpit of 1260.
  • The Leaning Tower (begun 1173), the freestanding bell tower whose unintended tilt — caused by soft ground — made it world-famous; stabilised by restoration works completed in 2001.
  • The Camposanto (begun 1278), a cloistered cemetery said to be built on soil brought from Golgotha, formerly decorated with extensive frescoes, many damaged in the Second World War and under long-term restoration.

Heritage status

The name "Piazza dei Miracoli" was popularised by the writer Gabriele d'Annunzio. The whole square was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987. The buildings were financed largely by the spoils of Pisa's maritime republic at the height of its power.
