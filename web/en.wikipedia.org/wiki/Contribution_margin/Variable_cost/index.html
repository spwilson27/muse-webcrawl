Variable cost

Variable costs are costs that change as the quantity of the good or service that a business produces changes. Variable costs are the sum of marginal costs over all units produced, and are usually the direct costs of production. They can also be considered normal costs. Fixed costs and variable costs make up the two components of total cost.

Examples

Direct materials, direct production labour, packaging, freight and sales commissions are typically classified as variable costs because they increase with each additional unit produced or sold. By contrast, rent, insurance and salaried management are generally fixed. Some costs are semi-variable: electricity, for example, may include a fixed connection charge plus a usage component that varies with production.

Behaviour

Total variable cost increases with output, while the variable cost per unit is often assumed constant within a relevant range in basic cost–volume–profit analysis. In reality, per-unit variable costs may fall with bulk purchasing discounts or rise when overtime and expedited shipping are needed near capacity. Variable costs are central to calculating the contribution margin (price minus variable cost per unit), which determines how much each sale contributes toward covering fixed costs and generating profit.
