Contribution margin

The contribution margin is a cost accounting concept: the selling price of a product minus all variable costs associated with it. It represents the portion of sales revenue that is available to cover fixed costs and, once those are covered, to contribute to profit.

Definitions

The unit contribution margin is the selling price per unit minus the variable cost per unit. The contribution margin ratio expresses the contribution margin as a percentage of sales revenue. Total contribution margin equals total revenue minus total variable costs.

Use in analysis

Contribution margin is central to break-even analysis: the break-even quantity equals total fixed costs divided by the unit contribution margin. Managers use it to decide which products to emphasize, whether to accept special orders (any price above variable cost adds contribution in the short run), and how changes in volume affect profit, as in cost–volume–profit analysis.

Distinction from gross margin

Unlike gross margin, which subtracts cost of goods sold (including some fixed manufacturing overhead under absorption costing), contribution margin subtracts all variable costs, including variable selling and administrative expenses, and no fixed costs.
