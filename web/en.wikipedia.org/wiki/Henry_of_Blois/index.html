Henry of Blois

Henry of Blois (c. 1096 – 8 August 1171), often identified as Henry of Winchester, was Abbot of Glastonbury and Bishop of Winchester during the civil war known as the Anarchy. He was the younger brother of King Stephen of England and a grandson of William the Conqueror through his mother, Adela of Normandy.

Early life

Henry was the fourth son of Stephen II, Count of Blois, and Adela, daughter of William the Conqueror. He was raised at the abbey of Cluny in Burgundy and took monastic vows, intending a career in the Church rather than the battlefield.

Career

In 1126 his brother Stephen arranged for him to become Abbot of Glastonbury, the richest abbey in England, and in 1129 he was consecrated Bishop of Winchester, holding both offices in plurality for the rest of his life. When Stephen seized the English throne in 1135, Henry became one of the most powerful men in the kingdom and served as papal legate from 1139 to 1143.

The Anarchy

During the civil war between Stephen and the Empress Matilda, Henry shifted allegiance more than once. In 1141 he defected to Matilda, then quarrelled with her and was besieged by Stephen's forces in Winchester, an episode that led to the Rout of Winchester and Matilda's flight. He later reconciled with his brother and helped broker the settlement that ended the war.

Later life and death

After Stephen's death in 1154, Henry's influence waned under Henry II, whose officials dismantled several of his castles. He withdrew increasingly to Cluny and to his estates, and died on 8 August 1171. He was a notable patron of the arts, remembered for commissioning illuminated manuscripts such as the Winchester Bible.
