Sneaker collecting

The sneaker collecting hobby, whose enthusiasts are often called sneakerheads, is the collecting, trading and admiring of sneakers as a hobby. Sneaker collecting developed together with sneaker culture, which grew from basketball and hip-hop culture in the United States in the 1980s, and has since become a global resale market estimated in the billions of dollars. Collectors seek limited editions, collaborations, original ("OG") colourways and vintage pairs, sometimes keeping them unworn ("deadstock") in their boxes as investments or display pieces.

History

Sneaker culture is commonly traced to 1985 and the release of the Air Jordan 1, marketed around basketball star Michael Jordan, which made a signature shoe a fashion object. Hip-hop acts such as Run-DMC (whose 1986 song "My Adidas" led to an endorsement deal) cemented sneakers as cultural symbols. Collecting spread through Japan, Europe and the skateboarding scene in the 1990s; boutiques and limited runs (e.g. Nike SB Dunks) created scarcity economies. From the 2010s, online drops, raffles, bots and resale platforms such as StockX and GOAT industrialised the market, and rare pairs have sold at auction for hundreds of thousands — even millions — of dollars.

Practices

Collectors distinguish general releases from limited "hype" releases, collaborations with designers and celebrities (Off-White, Travis Scott, Dior), and retro reissues. Shoes are graded by condition; authentication services verify legitimacy against a large counterfeit trade. Value is tracked like a stock market on resale platforms, giving rise to the term "sneaker investing" and to storage, insurance and consignment businesses. Conventions such as Sneaker Con and Got Sole host buy/sell/trade events.

Criticism

The culture has been criticised for artificial scarcity, bot-driven sellouts that exclude ordinary buyers, sweatshop labour behind coveted brands, waste from unworn shoes, and speculative bubbles. Violence over rare releases, prominent since the 1980s, continues to draw media attention. Brands have responded with raffle systems, anti-bot measures and wider releases of some models.
