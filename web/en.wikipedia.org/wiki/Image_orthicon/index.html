Image orthicon

The image orthicon was a television camera tube developed by RCA during the Second World War and introduced for broadcast use in the mid-1940s. Far more sensitive than the earlier iconoscope, it could produce usable pictures in low light and became the standard pickup tube of television studios for roughly two decades.

Operation

Light from the scene is focused onto a photocathode, which emits electrons in proportion to brightness. These are accelerated onto a thin target, where they build up a charge image; a scanning electron beam reads the target and produces the video signal.

Legacy

Image orthicon cameras were the workhorses of early television broadcasting, though the tubes were delicate, expensive and prone to image lag and sticking. They were gradually displaced by the smaller, cheaper vidicon and later solid-state sensors.
