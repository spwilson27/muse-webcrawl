Unas was an ancient Egyptian pharaoh, the ninth and last king of the Fifth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom, reigning for roughly 15 to 30 years in the mid-24th century BC. He succeeded Djedkare Isesi and was followed by Teti, founder of the Sixth Dynasty, who married Unas's daughter Iput and thereby legitimised his succession.

The pyramid of Unas at Saqqara, the smallest of the Old Kingdom royal pyramids, contains the earliest Pyramid Texts.

Reign and monuments

Little is known of political events in Unas's reign; inscriptions record trade and expeditions, including contact with Byblos and Nubia, and scenes of famine relief among desert peoples carved on his pyramid causeway. His pyramid complex at Saqqara, near the Step Pyramid of Djoser, is the smallest royal pyramid of the Old Kingdom, with a base of about 57.75 m and an original height of about 43 m. Its causeway, decorated with diverse relief scenes of transport, markets, and starving Bedouin, is among the finest known.

Pyramid Texts

Unas is best remembered because his pyramid's burial chambers were the first to be inscribed with the Pyramid Texts—a corpus of spells intended to protect the king and secure his afterlife among the gods. These texts, later copied into the pyramids of his successors, are the oldest large body of religious literature from ancient Egypt and a principal source for Old Kingdom religion. One famous spell, the "Cannibal Hymn", depicts the king devouring the gods to absorb their power.
