Spa is a city and municipality in the province of Liège, Belgium, situated in a valley of the Ardennes about 35 km southeast of the city of Liège. Famed since the 16th century for its healing mineral springs, it became so synonymous with curative waters that its name passed into English — and many other languages — as the generic word spa.

History

The ferruginous, naturally carbonated springs of Spa were visited from Roman times and rose to European fame in the 1500s; physicians such as William Turner praised the waters in print. By the 18th century Spa was the "café of Europe", a fashionable resort where royalty and aristocracy — including Peter the Great (1717) and much of Europe's nobility — took the waters, gambled at La Redoute (1763, among the world's oldest casinos) and promenaded. The Treaty-era Congress system and two world wars brought decline and occupation, but the town retained its baths (the Thermes de Spa, renewed in 2004) and casino.

Today

Spa is one of the eleven "Great Spa Towns of Europe" inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2021. Just outside town, the Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps hosts the Formula One Belgian Grand Prix. The town also lends its name to Spa water, bottled from local springs, and hosts music festivals including Les Francofolies.
