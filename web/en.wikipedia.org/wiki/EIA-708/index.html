EIA-708, more commonly known as CTA-708 (formerly CEA-708), is the standard for closed captioning for ATSC digital television (DTV) streams in the United States and Canada. It was developed by the Electronic Industries Alliance (EIA), whose consumer electronics functions are now part of the Consumer Technology Association.

Features

Compared with the earlier EIA-608 standard used in analogue television, EIA-708 greatly expands the caption author's control over presentation. It supports:

  • Up to 63 caption services in a single stream (six standard services and 57 additional);
  • Viewer-adjustable text size, font, color, opacity and edge style;
  • Multiple windows positioned anywhere on the screen, with text justification and scrolling;
  • A much larger character repertoire, including accented characters for many languages, via Unicode-like encoding.

Technical overview

Caption data are carried in the picture user data of the MPEG-2 video stream, at a maximum data rate of 9,600 bits per second for the DTV caption channel. Data are organized into service blocks; each service has its own caption channel with a defined language and aspect-ratio designation. Decoders present captions in windows defined by the caption provider, but viewers are offered override controls for text appearance, a feature intended to aid viewers with varying degrees of vision loss.

Mandates

The U.S. Federal Communications Commission requires DTV receivers with screens of 13 inches or larger to decode EIA-708 captions, and programming rules historically required EIA-608 data to be "translated" into the 708 format for digital broadcast. In practice, conversion quality from 608 to 708 varies, since 708's richer formatting cannot be fully derived from 608 data.
