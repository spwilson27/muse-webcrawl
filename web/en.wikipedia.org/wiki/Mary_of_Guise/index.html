Mary of Guise (French: Marie de Guise; 22 November 1515 – 11 June 1560), also called Mary of Lorraine, was Queen of Scotland from 1538 until 1542 as the second wife of King James V. She was the mother of Mary, Queen of Scots, and served as regent of Scotland from 1554 until her death in 1560.

Early life and marriages

Mary was the eldest daughter of Claude, Duke of Guise, head of one of France's most powerful families. In 1534 she married Louis II d'Orléans, Duke of Longueville, who died in 1537, leaving her a widow with two sons. In 1538 she married James V of Scotland by proxy and then in person at St Andrews, cementing the Auld Alliance between France and Scotland. Their two sons died in infancy; their daughter Mary was born in December 1542, days before James V's death.

Regency

After a period in which the Earl of Arran governed Scotland, Mary secured the regency for herself in 1554. She worked to preserve Scotland's Catholic faith and its French alliance, staffing government with French officials and provoking resentment among the Scottish nobility. Her increasingly harsh response to Protestant preaching helped trigger the Scottish Reformation crisis: the Protestant Lords of the Congregation rose in 1559, and Mary, besieged in Edinburgh, died of dropsy in June 1560. Within weeks the Treaty of Edinburgh withdrew French and English troops, and Scotland's parliament adopted a Protestant confession.
