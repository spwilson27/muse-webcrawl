Laxey

Laxey (Manx: Laksaa) is a village on the east coast of the Isle of Man. Its name derives from the Old Norse Laxa, meaning "salmon river". It had a population of around 1,700 in recent censuses. The village lies at the head of a wooded glen through which the Laxey River flows to the sea.

The Laxey Wheel

Laxey is famous for the Laxey Wheel, known as "Lady Isabella", built in 1854 to pump water from the Laxey lead and zinc mines. At 72 feet 6 inches (22.1 m) in diameter, it is the largest surviving working waterwheel in the world. The mines once employed over 600 men and were a major producer of lead, zinc, copper, and silver.

Transport

The village is served by the Manx Electric Railway and is the southern terminus of the Snaefell Mountain Railway, which climbs to the summit of Snaefell, the island's highest point. The A2 coast road from Douglas to Ramsey passes through Laxey.

Other features

Laxey Woollen Mills, founded in the 19th century, still produce Manx tartan. The harbour, built for the mining trade, is now used by fishing and leisure boats. Laxey Glen Gardens and the washing floors of the old mines are popular visitor attractions.
