Politburo of the Chinese Communist Party

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Politburo of the Chinese Communist Party, formally the Political Bureau of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China, is the highest political body of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) when the Central Committee is not in session. It consists of 24 members (as of the 20th Central Committee, elected in October 2022) and is headed by the party's General Secretary. In practice, the Politburo — and above all its seven-member Standing Committee — is the supreme decision-making body of the People's Republic of China, directing the state, the military and the party itself.

Selection and composition

Formally, the Politburo is elected by the Central Committee at its first plenary session following a National Congress, held every five years. In reality, membership is determined through consultations among the incumbent leadership and retired party elders. Members typically hold concurrent posts as heads of party departments, senior state offices, leaders of major provinces and municipalities, or senior People's Liberation Army officers. No woman has ever sat on the Standing Committee, and the 20th Politburo elected in 2022 had no female members for the first time in 25 years.

Functions

The Politburo meets roughly monthly to decide major policy questions — economic strategy, foreign relations, military affairs, appointments and ideological campaigns — and to ratify decisions prepared by the Standing Committee, which meets weekly. Decisions are formally by consensus. Between plenums of the Central Committee the Politburo exercises the committee's powers, while nominally remaining accountable to it.

History

A Political Bureau was first established in 1927, following the organisational model of the Soviet Communist Party. Under Mao Zedong the Politburo was often bypassed by informal bodies; during the Cultural Revolution its functioning was severely disrupted. In the reform era under Deng Xiaoping and his successors, Politburo membership was regularised with retirement-age norms (informally, "67 stay, 68 out" at congresses) — norms set aside at the 20th Congress in 2022, when Xi Jinping, then 69, took a third term as General Secretary and packed the Standing Committee with close associates.
