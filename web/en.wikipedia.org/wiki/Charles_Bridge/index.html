Charles Bridge (Czech: Karlův most) is a medieval stone arch bridge that crosses the Vltava river in Prague, Czech Republic. Its construction started in 1357 under the auspices of King Charles IV, and finished at the beginning of the 15th century. The bridge replaced the old Judith Bridge, built 1158–1172, which had been badly damaged by a flood in 1342.

As the only means of crossing the river Vltava until 1841, Charles Bridge was the most important connection between Prague Castle and the city's Old Town and adjacent areas. This land connection made Prague important as a trade route between Eastern and Western Europe. The bridge is 515.8 metres (1,692 ft) long and 9.5 metres (31 ft) wide, resting on 16 arches shielded by ice guards.

History

According to legend, construction began at 5:31 on 9 July 1357 — a palindrome of numbers (1357 9/7 5:31) chosen by royal astrologers to give the bridge strength. The bridge originally had no sculptural decoration except a crucifix; the famous avenue of statues was added between 1683 and 1714, mostly in the Baroque style. The best-known statue is that of St John of Nepomuk (1683), whose plaque is polished bright by visitors touching it for luck.

The bridge is protected by three towers: two on the Lesser Town side and one, the Old Town Bridge Tower, on the Old Town side, considered one of the finest Gothic towers in Europe. Since 1965 the bridge has been closed to vehicles and is reserved for pedestrians; it is now one of Prague's most visited monuments, crowded with artists, musicians, and souvenir sellers.

Statues

Thirty statues and statuaries line the bridge. Most originals, damaged by weather, have been replaced by replicas, with the originals preserved in the Lapidarium of the National Museum. The oldest surviving statue is the Crucifix; the newest groups date from the early 20th century.
