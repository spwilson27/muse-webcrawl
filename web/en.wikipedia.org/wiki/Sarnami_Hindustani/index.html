Sarnami Hindustani

Sarnami Hindustani (also called Sarnami Hindi or simply Sarnami) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken in Suriname by the Indo-Surinamese community, descendants of indentured labourers brought from British India between 1873 and 1916. Its name derives from Surinam plus Hindustani.

Origins and features

Sarnami developed as a koine of the North Indian languages and dialects spoken by the indentured immigrants, principally Bhojpuri and Awadhi, with contributions from Magahi, Maithili, and standard Hindi, and later borrowings from Dutch, Sranan Tongo, and English. Unlike standard Hindi, it is written most often in the Latin alphabet in Suriname, though Devanagari and Urdu scripts are also used.

Status and usage

Sarnami is the third most spoken language in Suriname, after Dutch and Sranan Tongo, with roughly 150,000 speakers, and it is also spoken by the Surinamese diaspora in the Netherlands. It is the language of a distinct musical tradition, baithak gana, and of Hindustani cultural and religious life in Suriname, though its use among younger speakers is declining in favour of Dutch.
