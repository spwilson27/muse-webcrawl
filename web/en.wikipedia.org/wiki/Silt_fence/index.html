Silt fence

A silt fence downslope of a construction area, trapping sediment.

A silt fence is a temporary sediment-control barrier used on construction sites to protect nearby water quality by slowing stormwater runoff and trapping eroded soil. It consists of a length of permeable geotextile filter fabric stretched between and attached to supporting posts, with the bottom edge buried in a shallow trench. Silt fences are a standard best management practice (BMP) under erosion and sediment control regulations such as construction stormwater permits.

How it works

The fence does not act primarily as a filter. By ponding runoff behind the fabric, it reduces the water's velocity so that suspended sediment settles out before the water seeps through the geotextile. A properly installed fence can retain a large share of coarse and medium sediment, though fine clays and dissolved pollutants pass through, so silt fences are combined with other controls such as sediment basins and stabilised site entrances.

Installation and maintenance

Fences are placed along contour lines on the downslope perimeter of a disturbed area, never across streams or concentrated flows. The fabric is typically woven or nonwoven polypropylene, supported by wooden or steel posts spaced about 1.8–3 m apart, with the toe buried at least 15 cm deep. Accumulated sediment must be removed when it reaches roughly one-third of the fence height, and torn or undermined sections repaired — poor installation and maintenance are the main causes of failure, including blowouts during heavy rain.

Limitations and alternatives

Silt fences are unsuitable for large drainage areas or steep slopes. Alternatives include compost filter socks, fiber rolls (wattles), sediment traps and check dams. "Super silt fence", reinforced with chain-link mesh, is used where greater strength is required.
