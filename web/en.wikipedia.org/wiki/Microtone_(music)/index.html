Microtone (music)

A microtone is a musical interval smaller than a semitone. In Western music the semitone — one twelfth of an octave in twelve-tone equal temperament — is conventionally the smallest standard interval, but many musical traditions and experimental composers use finer divisions of the octave.

Usage

Microtones occur naturally in many non-Western systems, including Arabic maqam, Turkish makam, and Indian classical music, whose śruti intervals divide the octave more finely than Western practice. In the West, composers such as Charles Ives, Harry Partch, Alois Hába, and Julián Carrillo wrote explicitly microtonal music, using quarter tones (half a semitone) and, in Partch's case, a 43-tone scale built on just intonation.

Notation and instruments

Microtonal accidentals, including half-sharps and half-flats, extend staff notation. Instruments with continuous pitch, such as the violin family, trombone, and voice, produce microtones readily; specially fretted guitars, retuned keyboards, and electronic instruments are used for fixed-pitch microtonal music.
