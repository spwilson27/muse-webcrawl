The Denver Broncos are a professional American football team based in Denver, Colorado. The Broncos compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) West division. The team plays its home games at Empower Field at Mile High.

Founded in 1960 as a charter member of the American Football League (AFL), the Broncos joined the NFL after the AFL–NFL merger in 1970. Under owner Pat Bowlen (1984–2019) the Broncos became one of the league's most successful franchises. In 2022 the team was sold to the Walton–Penner ownership group for a then-record US$4.65 billion.

History

The Broncos first reached the Super Bowl in the 1977 season, losing Super Bowl XII to Dallas. Quarterback John Elway, drafted in 1983, led Denver to five Super Bowls; after three losses he won back-to-back championships in Super Bowls XXXII (over Green Bay, with Terrell Davis as MVP) and XXXIII (over Atlanta, Elway MVP) before retiring.

In 2015, with veteran quarterback Peyton Manning and a dominant defence led by Von Miller, the Broncos defeated the Carolina Panthers in Super Bowl 50. Miller was named the game's MVP.

Orange Crush and traditions

The feared 1977 defence was nicknamed the "Orange Crush". Denver's home-field advantage at altitude (5,280 feet) and a long sellout streak dating to 1970 are famous. Retired numbers include those of John Elway (7), Floyd Little (44) and the No. 18 worn by Frank Tripucka and later Peyton Manning.

Rivalries

The Broncos share longstanding AFC West rivalries with the Kansas City Chiefs, Las Vegas Raiders and Los Angeles Chargers.
