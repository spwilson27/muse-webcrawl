Refractometer

A refractometer is a laboratory or field device for the measurement of an index of refraction (refractometry). The index of refraction is calculated from the observed refraction angle using Snell's law. For liquid samples, the refractive index is strongly influenced by concentration, so refractometers are widely used to determine the content of dissolved solids in a solution.

Types

  • Traditional handheld refractometers use the critical angle of total internal reflection; the user reads a scale (often in degrees Brix) through an eyepiece where a shadow line crosses it.
  • Abbe refractometers are benchtop instruments for accurate measurement of liquids and solids, using a thin film of sample between prisms.
  • Digital refractometers measure the critical angle with an LED and photodetector and display the result automatically, often with automatic temperature compensation.
  • Inline/process refractometers monitor concentration continuously in industrial pipelines.

Applications

Refractometers measure sugar content (Brix) of fruit, wort in brewing and must in winemaking; salinity in aquaculture and seawater; urine specific gravity and serum protein in clinical work; coolant and battery-acid concentration in automotive service; and purity/concentration in food, chemical and pharmaceutical production. Gemologists use specialised refractometers to identify gemstones by refractive index.
