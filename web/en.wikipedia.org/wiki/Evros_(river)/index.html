Evros (river)

Native names: Έβρος (Greek), Марица / Maritsa (Bulgarian), Meriç (Turkish)

Length: approx. 480 km

Source: Rila Mountains, Bulgaria

Mouth: Aegean Sea, near Alexandroupoli

The Evros, known as the Maritsa in Bulgarian and Meriç in Turkish, is one of the longest rivers of the Balkans, about 480 km long. It rises in the Rila Mountains of western Bulgaria, flows eastward across the Thracian plain past Plovdiv, then turns south at Edirne, where it is joined by the Tundzha and the Arda. For the last part of its course it forms the border between Greece (on the west) and Turkey (on the east), before emptying into the Aegean Sea near Alexandroupoli through a broad delta of lagoons and marshes.

Geography and nature

The river drains southern Bulgaria and European Turkey and Greek Thrace. Its lower valley is intensively farmed, and dams in Bulgaria regulate its flow; flooding has been a recurring problem in the Edirne region. The Evros Delta is an internationally important wetland, protected under the Ramsar Convention, with hundreds of recorded bird species including pygmy cormorants and Dalmatian pelicans at nearby Lake Vistonida complexes.

Border and history

The river has marked frontiers since the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) fixed the Greco-Turkish land border along it — a noted exception being the Karaağaç salient near Edirne. Since the 2010s the Evros has been a major crossing point for migrants and refugees entering the European Union, prompting Greece to fence sections of the bank and prompting international attention during the 2020 border standoff between Greece and Turkey.
