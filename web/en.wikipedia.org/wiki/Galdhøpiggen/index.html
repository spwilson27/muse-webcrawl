Galdhøpiggen (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈɡɑ̀ɫhøːˌpɪɡn̩]) is the highest mountain in Norway, Scandinavia, and Northern Europe. The 2,469-metre (8,100 ft) tall mountain is located in Lom Municipality in Innlandet county, within the Jotunheimen mountains. The mountain sits about 25 kilometres (16 mi) southwest of the village of Fossbergom and about 45 kilometres (28 mi) northeast of the village of Øvre Årdal.

The name derives from Old Norse words referring to the road (gald) and the peak (piggen). For a long time it was not recognised as Norway's highest summit; the Snøhetta mountain, visible from the old Oslo–Trondheim road, was assumed higher until the geologist Baltazar Mathias Keilhau established in the 1840s that Galdhøpiggen was the taller.

Climbing

The mountain was first climbed in 1850 by the schoolteacher Steinar Sulheim together with S. Flaatten and L. Arnesen. Today the most common routes are from Spiterstulen in Visdalen, crossing the Styggebreen glacier, and from Juvasshytta, where a guided glacier crossing leads to the summit, which carries a small cabin. Galdhøpiggen forms part of Jotunheimen National Park.
