Ohad Naharin (born 22 June 1952) is an Israeli choreographer and dancer, regarded as one of the leading figures in contemporary dance. He served as artistic director of the Batsheva Dance Company of Tel Aviv from 1990 to 2018 and developed Gaga, a movement language and training method now taught worldwide.

Career

Naharin began dancing with Batsheva in 1974 at the invitation of Martha Graham, who was then working with the company. After a decade in New York performing with Graham's company and others, and early choreographic work, he returned to Israel in 1990 to direct Batsheva, transforming it into one of the world's most acclaimed contemporary dance companies. He stepped down as artistic director in 2018 while remaining the company's resident choreographer.

Works and Gaga

Signature works include Kyr (1990), Deca Dance, a collage of excerpts from his repertoire, and Naharin's Virus. His piece Echad Mi Yodea, featuring dancers shedding their clothes to a Passover song, became famous and controversial. Gaga, developed partly as he recovered from a back injury, is a movement research method that trains dancers and non-dancers ("Gaga/people") to listen to bodily sensations, enhancing flexibility, stamina, and improvisational freedom. The 2015 documentary Mr. Gaga profiled his life and work.
