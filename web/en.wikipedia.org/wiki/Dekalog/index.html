Dekalog

Dekalog (Polish: Dekalog, also known as Dekalog: The Ten Commandments) is a 1989 Polish television drama series directed by Krzysztof Kieślowski and co-written with Krzysztof Piesiewicz. The series consists of ten one-hour films, each loosely inspired by one of the Ten Commandments, and is widely regarded as one of the greatest achievements of Polish cinema.

Overview

All ten episodes are set in and around a single dreary housing estate in Warsaw, and characters recur in glimpses across episodes. A silent, watchful figure (played by Artur Barciś) appears in each film, sometimes as a passer-by, sometimes in a small role, observing the characters at moments of moral crisis. The music is by Zbigniew Preisner.

Episodes

The films explore themes of faith, love, guilt, killing, adultery, covetousness and idolatry in modern, secular settings rather than through direct biblical illustration. Two episodes were expanded into feature films: A Short Film About Killing (Dekalog Five) and A Short Film About Love (Dekalog Six), both released in 1988.

Reception

The series won numerous international awards and is frequently listed among the finest works ever made for television. Stanley Kubrick wrote the foreword to the published screenplays, calling Kieślowski and Piesiewicz's scripts masterpieces.
