Bradenton is a city in and the county seat of Manatee County, Florida, United States. As of the 2020 census, the city's population was about 55,000. Bradenton is a principal city of the North Port–Sarasota–Bradenton metropolitan statistical area.

History

The area was explored by Hernando de Soto in 1539. The city is named after Dr. Joseph Braden, whose nearby fortified house was a refuge for early settlers during Seminole attacks. A town called Braidentown was platted in 1878 and incorporated in 1903; the name was later shortened to Bradenton.

Geography

Bradenton sits on Florida's Gulf Coast at the southern bank of the Manatee River, across from Palmetto. It is known for its beaches on nearby Anna Maria Island and for Tropicana Field-area industries, including the Tropicana juice company founded in the city.

Culture

The Village of the Arts is a working artists' community in renovated cottages. The city hosts spring training for the Pittsburgh Pirates baseball team at LECOM Park.
