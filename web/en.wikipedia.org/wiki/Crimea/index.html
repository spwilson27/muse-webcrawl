Crimea is a peninsula in Eastern Europe, on the northern coast of the Black Sea, almost entirely surrounded by the Black Sea and the smaller Sea of Azov. It has a population of about 2.4 million. The peninsula is connected to the mainland to the north by the narrow Isthmus of Perekop. Its largest city is Sevastopol, home to a major naval base; its capital is Simferopol.

History

Crimea's long history includes Greek colonies, Goths, the Khazars, Kievan Rus', the Mongols and the Genoese. From 1441 the Crimean Khanate, an Ottoman vassal state ruled by the Giray dynasty, dominated the peninsula. Russia annexed Crimea in 1783 under Catherine the Great. The peninsula gave its name to the Crimean War (1853–1856), in which Russia fought Britain, France, Sardinia and the Ottoman Empire.

Within the Soviet Union, Crimea was part of the Russian SFSR until 1954, when it was transferred to the Ukrainian SSR. In 1944 the entire Crimean Tatar population had been deported to Central Asia by Stalin's regime; they were permitted to return only from the late 1980s. After Ukraine's independence in 1991, Crimea remained an autonomous republic within Ukraine, while Russia's Black Sea Fleet stayed based at Sevastopol under lease agreements.

2014 annexation and aftermath

In February–March 2014, during the upheaval following Ukraine's Revolution of Dignity, Russian forces seized the peninsula, and a disputed referendum was followed by Russia's annexation of Crimea. The move was condemned by the UN General Assembly, which affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity; most countries continue to recognise Crimea as Ukrainian territory, and Western sanctions followed. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Crimea has been a key military staging area and itself a target of Ukrainian strikes, including on the Kerch Bridge linking it to Russia.

Geography

The peninsula's south coast, backed by the Crimean Mountains, has a mild, Mediterranean-like climate and was a famed resort region — Yalta hosted the 1945 Yalta Conference. The interior is steppe, and the east ends at the Kerch Peninsula.
