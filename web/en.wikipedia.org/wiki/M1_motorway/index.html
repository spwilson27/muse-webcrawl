The M1 motorway connects London to Leeds in England. It was the first inter-urban motorway to be completed in the United Kingdom, with its initial section opening in 1959. The motorway runs roughly parallel to the historic A5 and A6 routes and to the Midland Main Line railway.

History

The first stretch, between Watford and Rugby, was opened on 2 November 1959 by the Minister of Transport, Ernest Marples. The opening pre-dated the first section of the Preston Bypass on the M6, which had opened in 1958 as the country's first motorway, but the M1 was the first full-length inter-city motorway.

Originally, much of the M1 had no speed limit, no central reservation barrier, and no lighting. A 70 mph national speed limit was introduced in 1965. The motorway was progressively extended northwards to Leeds, reaching its present terminus in 1999 with the opening of the Leeds South Eastern Motorway link to the A1(M).

Route

From its southern terminus at Staples Corner in north London, the M1 passes through Hertfordshire, Bedfordshire, Buckinghamshire, Northamptonshire, Leicestershire, Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire, and South and West Yorkshire. Major junctions include the M25 near London, the M6 near Rugby, and the M62 near Leeds.

Several sections have been converted to smart motorway operation, with variable speed limits and, in places, all-lane running.
