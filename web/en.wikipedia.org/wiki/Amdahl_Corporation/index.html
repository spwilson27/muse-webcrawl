HeadquartersSunnyvale, California, U.S.

Amdahl Corporation was an American information technology company which specialised in IBM mainframe-compatible computer products, some of which were regarded as supercomputers competing with IBM's large systems. Founded in 1970 by Gene Amdahl, a former IBM chief architect, the company shipped its first machine, the Amdahl 470V/6, in 1975.

History

The 470V/6 was the first of the "plug-compatible mainframes": machines that ran IBM System/370 software but offered better price-performance, thanks in part to air-cooled LSI technology at a time when IBM's largest systems required water cooling. Amdahl's success forced IBM to compete more aggressively and established the plug-compatible industry. Fujitsu of Japan, an early investor, gradually increased its stake and acquired the entire company in 1997 for about $878 million.

Later years

As the mainframe market contracted in the 1990s, Amdahl diversified into servers, storage, and software services. Under Fujitsu ownership, the Amdahl name continued on enterprise systems and services for some years before being retired in favour of the Fujitsu brand.

Legacy

The company is also remembered through its founder's "Amdahl's law", a formula used in parallel computing to predict the theoretical speedup achievable when only part of a task can be parallelised.
