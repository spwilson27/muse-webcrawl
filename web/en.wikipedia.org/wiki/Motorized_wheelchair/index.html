Motorized wheelchair

A motorized wheelchair, also called a powerchair, power wheelchair, or electric wheelchair, is a wheelchair propelled by an electric motor rather than manual power. It is intended for people who cannot propel a manual wheelchair or who cannot walk functional distances because of disability or illness.

Design

Powerchairs typically consist of a base containing batteries, one or two motors, and drive wheels, with a seating system mounted on top. Drive configurations include front-wheel, mid-wheel, and rear-wheel drive, each with different maneuverability and handling characteristics. Most are controlled with a joystick, though alternative controls — chin, head, sip-and-puff, or switch arrays — are used by people with limited hand function. Power seating functions such as tilt, recline, elevating leg rests, and seat elevation may be fitted for pressure relief and positioning.

History

The modern powerchair traces to Canadian inventor George Klein and colleagues, who developed an electric wheelchair for injured veterans after the Second World War. Commercial production expanded in the 1950s, including by the British company that produced the first mass-market models.

Use and access

Powerchairs range from simple transportable models to complex rehabilitation chairs prescribed after clinical assessment. Legal classification and road use vary by jurisdiction; some countries limit pavement speed or require registration for faster outdoor models.
