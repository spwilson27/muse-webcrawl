The Union of Krewo (Polish: Unia w Krewie; Lithuanian: Krėvos unija) was a document issued by Jogaila, Grand Duke of Lithuania, on 14 August 1385 at Krewo Castle. In the document, Jogaila agreed to a set of pre-marital promises in exchange for the hand of the young Queen Jadwiga of Poland and the Polish crown. The agreement laid the foundation for the Polish–Lithuanian union that would last for more than four centuries.

Background

By the 1380s, both Poland and Lithuania faced pressure from the Teutonic Order, which used Lithuania's paganism to justify crusades against it. Poland, after the death of Louis I of Hungary in 1382, was ruled by his daughter Jadwiga, crowned in 1384. Polish nobles sought a husband for the queen who could strengthen the kingdom; Jogaila, Grand Duke of Lithuania, emerged as the preferred candidate, not least because a union would bring Lithuania's vast territories into alliance against the Teutonic Knights.

Terms

In the act, Jogaila promised, among other things, to:

  • accept Roman Catholic baptism together with his brothers, Lithuanian nobles, and subjects;
  • marry Jadwiga and be crowned King of Poland;
  • pay compensation to William of Austria, Jadwiga's former betrothed;
  • release Polish captives and recover lost Polish and Lithuanian territories;
  • "apply" (applicare) his Lithuanian and Ruthenian lands to the Crown of the Kingdom of Poland in perpetuity.

The meaning of the Latin word applicare has been debated by historians, who disagree over whether it implied incorporation of Lithuania into Poland or merely a personal union of the two states under one ruler.

Aftermath

Jogaila was baptized on 15 February 1386, taking the name Władysław, married Jadwiga on 18 February, and was crowned King of Poland on 4 March 1386. The baptism of Lithuania, the last pagan state in Europe, followed in 1387. The union was renewed and reshaped by later agreements, including the Union of Vilnius and Radom (1401), the Union of Horodło (1413), and ultimately the Union of Lublin (1569), which created the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.
