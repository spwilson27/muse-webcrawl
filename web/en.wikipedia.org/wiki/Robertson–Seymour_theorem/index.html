Robertson–Seymour theorem

The Robertson–Seymour theorem (also called the graph minor theorem) states that undirected graphs, partially ordered by the graph minor relation, form a well-quasi-ordering: there is no infinite sequence of graphs in which no graph is a minor of a later one. Equivalently, every set of graphs closed under taking minors can be characterised by a finite list of forbidden minors.

Background

The theorem was proved by Neil Robertson and Paul Seymour over a series of more than twenty papers on graph minors published between 1983 and 2004 — one of the largest collaborative proofs in mathematics. It generalises earlier results such as Wagner's theorem (planar graphs are those with no K5 or K3,3 minor) and Kuratowski's theorem, and confirms the conjecture of Kurt Wagner that every minor-closed graph property has finitely many forbidden minors.

Consequences

Because membership in any minor-closed class can be tested by checking for finitely many forbidden minors, and because minor testing is polynomial for a fixed graph, the theorem implies polynomial-time algorithms for many graph problems — though the proofs are non-constructive and do not themselves provide the forbidden sets. Robertson and Seymour received the Fulkerson Prize for their work.
