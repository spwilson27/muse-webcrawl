POW/MIA flag

The POW/MIA flag is a black flag depicting the silhouette of a prisoner of war beneath a guard tower, with the words "POW ★ MIA" above and the motto "You Are Not Forgotten" below. Created in 1971 during the Vietnam War, it became the emblem of concern for United States military personnel taken as prisoners of war or listed as missing in action.

History

The flag was created in 1971 by the National League of Families of American Prisoners and Missing in Southeast Asia, which commissioned Annin Flagmakers; artist Newton Heisley, a former pilot, designed the silhouette based in part on his son. The flag was never copyrighted and spread widely through veterans' organizations. See National League of Families for the organization behind it.

Display

The POW/MIA flag is the only flag besides the United States flag to have flown over the White House, and by statute it flies over federal buildings on designated days. National POW/MIA Recognition Day is observed on the third Friday of September. Since 2019, the National POW/MIA Flag Act requires the flag to be displayed whenever the U.S. flag is flown at specified federal sites.
