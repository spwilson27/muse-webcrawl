Operation Homecoming was the repatriation of American prisoners of war held by North Vietnam and the Viet Cong following the Paris Peace Accords ending U.S. involvement in the Vietnam War. Between 12 February and 4 April 1973, 591 American POWs were released and flown from Hanoi's Gia Lâm Airport to Clark Air Base in the Philippines aboard U.S. Air Force C-141 Starlifter transports, with further flights carrying prisoners released in South Vietnam.

The first group released included Navy pilot John McCain and the longest-held POW, Everett Alvarez Jr. At Clark, the returnees received medical examinations, new uniforms, and their first phone calls home before flying on to the United States, where they were greeted by crowds and, later, by President Richard Nixon at the largest dinner ever held at the White House, in May 1973.

Aftermath

Homecoming did not end the POW/MIA issue: more than 2,500 Americans remained unaccounted for in Southeast Asia, and the question of whether prisoners had been left behind became a prominent political and cultural theme, championed by groups such as the National League of Families and symbolized by the POW/MIA flag.
