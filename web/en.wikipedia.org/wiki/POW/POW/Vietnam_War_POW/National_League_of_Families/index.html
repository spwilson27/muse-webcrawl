The National League of POW/MIA Families, often called the National League of Families, is an American nonprofit organization founded by the wives and families of servicemen who were prisoners of war or missing in action during the Vietnam War. It was formally incorporated in 1970, growing out of informal networks of military wives, including Sybil Stockdale, who had begun organizing and pressing the government for information in the mid-1960s.

The League campaigned to publicize the plight of American POWs in North Vietnam, pressured the U.S. government to demand humane treatment and a full accounting, and later supported efforts to resolve the fates of missing personnel in Southeast Asia. It is best known for promoting the black-and-white POW/MIA flag — "You Are Not Forgotten" — which became the enduring national symbol of the issue and is flown at federal buildings across the United States.

Activities

The League continues to advocate for the fullest possible accounting of missing Americans, sends delegations and supports archival and field work related to remains recovery, and publishes updates on accounting results. It works alongside the Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency (DPAA) community of family organizations.
