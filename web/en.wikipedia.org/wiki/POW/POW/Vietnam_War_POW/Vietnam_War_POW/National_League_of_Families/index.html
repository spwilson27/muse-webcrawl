The National League of POW/MIA Families is an American nonprofit organization formed in 1970 by the families of United States servicemen listed as prisoners of war or missing in action during the Vietnam War. Spouses such as Sybil Stockdale and Carol North, frustrated by official secrecy, organized publicly to demand information about their husbands and to insist that the treatment of American prisoners become a national priority.

After the 1973 release of the POWs in Operation Homecoming, the League refocused on the fullest possible accounting for the missing, pressing successive administrations on negotiations, archival research, and remains recovery in Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia. The League popularized the POW/MIA flag, created in 1972, which by law flies over federal sites and remains the preeminent emblem of the accounting mission.
