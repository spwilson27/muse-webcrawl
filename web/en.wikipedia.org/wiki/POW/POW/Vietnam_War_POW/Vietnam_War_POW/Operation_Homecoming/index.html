Operation Homecoming

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dates12 February – 4 April 1973
POWs returned591
AircraftC-141 Starlifter, C-9 Nightingale

Operation Homecoming was the series of diplomatic negotiations and airlifts that returned 591 American prisoners of war from captivity in North and South Vietnam in the spring of 1973, as required by the Paris Peace Accords that ended direct American participation in the Vietnam War.

The first release took place on 12 February 1973 at Hanoi's Gia Lâm Airport, where freed prisoners — many of them aviators held for years at Hỏa Lò and other camps — boarded C-141 Starlifters for Clark Air Base in the Philippines. Subsequent releases continued through 4 April. At Clark the men were debriefed, examined, and telephoned their families; many then flew home to emotional airport reunions. Among the returnees were Everett Alvarez Jr., the first POW taken in 1964, and John McCain, who had been held for five and a half years.

Legacy

Homecoming brought the POW story to national attention, but the unresolved status of the missing — and allegations that some Americans had not been returned — sustained the POW/MIA movement for decades, led in part by the National League of Families.
