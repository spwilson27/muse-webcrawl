POW/MIA flag

The POW/MIA flag is an American flag designed as a symbol of concern for United States military personnel taken as prisoners of war (POWs) or listed as missing in action (MIA). It is black, bearing in white the silhouette of a prisoner before a guard tower and barbed wire, with the words "POW ★ MIA" and the motto "You Are Not Forgotten".

History

The flag was created in 1971 by Newt Heisley for the National League of Families of American Prisoners and Missing in Southeast Asia, during the Vietnam War. Graphic artist Heisley, himself a pilot in the Second World War, drew on his son's gaunt appearance after a tour in Vietnam for the prisoner silhouette. Annin Flagmakers produced the design, and it was never copyrighted, spreading widely.

Status

In 1988 the POW/MIA flag became the first flag other than the flag of the United States to fly over the White House, and in 1989 it was installed in the Capitol rotunda. Federal law and the National POW/MIA Flag Act of 2019 require it to be displayed at specified federal locations, including the Capitol, the White House, national cemeteries, and major military installations, and it is traditionally flown on National POW/MIA Recognition Day, the third Friday of September.
