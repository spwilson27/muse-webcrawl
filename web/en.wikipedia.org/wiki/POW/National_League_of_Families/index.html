National League of Families

The National League of POW/MIA Families, commonly called the National League of Families, is an American non-profit organisation founded in 1970 by the wives and families of servicemen who were prisoners of war or missing in action in Southeast Asia during the Vietnam War.

Mission and activities

The League campaigns for the fullest possible accounting of missing American personnel: the recovery and identification of remains, resolution of live-sighting reports, and access to records. It pioneered family-led advocacy on the POW/MIA issue, pressing successive US administrations to treat accounting as a priority in relations with Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia.

The POW/MIA flag

The League is best known for the black POW/MIA flag, designed in 1971 by Newt Heisley: a white silhouette of a prisoner's head before a guard tower and barbed wire, above the motto "You Are Not Forgotten". The flag is flown over the White House, the US Capitol, and, by law, at federal sites on designated days, and it remains displayed at post offices and veterans' facilities across the United States.
