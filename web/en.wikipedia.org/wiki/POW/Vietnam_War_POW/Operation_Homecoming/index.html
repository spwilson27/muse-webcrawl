Operation Homecoming

Operation Homecoming was the repatriation of American prisoners of war held in North Vietnam following the Paris Peace Accords of 27 January 1973. Between 12 February and 1 April 1973, 591 American POWs were released in phases at Hanoi's Gia Lam Airport and flown by U.S. Air Force C-141 Starlifters to Clark Air Base in the Philippines, and then onward to the United States.

Date12 February – 1 April 1973
LocationHanoi, North Vietnam; Clark AB, Philippines
Outcome591 American POWs repatriated

Background

The Paris Peace Accords required the release of all American prisoners within 60 days of the ceasefire. North Vietnam published a list of 555 prisoners in North Vietnam plus others held in the South and Laos, though the numbers immediately raised questions that fed into the broader POW/MIA issue.

The releases

The first group of 40 prisoners arrived at Clark Air Base on 12 February 1973 to huge media attention. Navy Captain Jeremiah Denton, a released prisoner, famously blinked the word "TORTURE" in Morse code during an earlier televised interview; among those released was future senator John McCain, shot down in 1967. Emotional reunions followed at bases across the United States, and President Nixon hosted the former POWs at the largest dinner in White House history in May 1973.

Legacy

Homecoming marked the formal end of the American combat role in the Vietnam War, but the absence of some men known to have been captured alive kept alive the advocacy of groups such as the National League of Families.
