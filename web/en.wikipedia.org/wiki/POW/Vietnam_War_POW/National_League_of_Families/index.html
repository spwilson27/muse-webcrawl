National League of Families

The National League of Families of American Prisoners and Missing in Southeast Asia, commonly called the National League of Families, is an American nonprofit organization founded by the wives and families of servicemen who were prisoners of war or missing in action during the Vietnam War. Incorporated in 1970, it became the principal voice demanding a full accounting of missing personnel.

Origins

The League grew out of informal networks of military wives, including Sybil Stockdale, wife of naval aviator James Stockdale, who had been pressing the government to publicize the mistreatment of American prisoners. In 1969 the families' campaign gained national attention, and the League was formally chartered on 28 May 1970 in Washington, D.C.

Activities

The League commissioned and distributed the POW/MIA flag, published newsletters, lobbied Congress, and sent delegations to Southeast Asia. After the 1973 repatriation in Operation Homecoming, it continued to press for the recovery of remains and resolution of discrepancy cases in the POW/MIA issue. The organization remains active in supporting U.S. government accounting missions worldwide.
