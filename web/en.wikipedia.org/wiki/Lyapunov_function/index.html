Lyapunov function

In the theory of ordinary differential equations (ODEs), Lyapunov functions (also called the Lyapunov's second method for stability) are scalar functions that may be used to prove the stability of an equilibrium of an ODE. Named after the Russian mathematician Aleksandr Mikhailovich Lyapunov, Lyapunov functions (also called Lyapunov candidate functions) are important to stability theory of dynamical systems and control theory.

Definition

For an autonomous system ẋ = f(x) with equilibrium at the origin, a continuously differentiable function V(x) is a Lyapunov function if it is positive definite (V(0) = 0 and V(x) > 0 for x ≠ 0) and its derivative along trajectories is non-positive (V̇(x) ≤ 0). If V̇ is strictly negative away from the origin, the equilibrium is asymptotically stable.

Intuition

A Lyapunov function can be pictured as a generalised "energy" of the system: if the energy is positive everywhere except at the equilibrium and never increases along trajectories, the state must remain near — or converge to — the equilibrium, like a ball settling at the bottom of a bowl.
