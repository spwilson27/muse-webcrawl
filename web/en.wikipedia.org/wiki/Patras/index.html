Patras (Greek: Πάτρα) is Greece's third-largest city and the regional capital of Western Greece, located in the northern Peloponnese on the Gulf of Patras, about 215 kilometres west of Athens. A major port facing Italy across the Ionian Sea, it is a commercial and communications hub, a university city, and home to the largest carnival in Greece.

History

Tradition attributes the city's founding to Patreus, who united Achaean settlements in the 2nd millennium BC. Patras was a member of the Achaean League. After Rome's conquest of Greece, Augustus refounded Patras as a Roman colony, Colonia Aroe Augusta Patrensis, and it prospered as a port; the apostle Andrew is believed to have been martyred here, and the city remains a place of pilgrimage, with his relics kept in the great Church of Saint Andrew, one of the largest churches in the Balkans.

In the Middle Ages the city passed between Byzantine, Frankish, Venetian and Ottoman hands. Patras played a symbolic role in the Greek War of Independence: tradition holds that the revolutionary banner was raised here in 1821. The 19th century brought prosperity through the currant trade, whose grand neoclassical warehouses still line the harbour. The city was Cultural Capital of Greece's region when it served as European Capital of Culture in 2006.

Geography and transport

Patras climbs from its harbour up the slopes of Mount Panachaiko. The old town, around the medieval castle, is divided into an upper and lower city joined by long staircases. Ferries link Patras with Ancona, Bari, Brindisi and Venice in Italy and with the Ionian islands. The Rio–Antirrio Bridge, opened in 2004 and among the world's longest cable-stayed bridges, connects the Peloponnese to mainland Greece just north-east of the city.

Culture and education

The Patras Carnival, running from January to Clean Monday, is famous for its satirical floats, treasure hunt and the huge parade of masked groups. The University of Patras, founded in 1964 at nearby Rio, together with the Technological Educational Institute, gives the city a large student population. Landmarks include the Roman Odeon, the Apollon Theatre (designed in the 1870s in Italianate style), the Achaia Clauss winery, and the lighthouse that serves as the city's emblem.
