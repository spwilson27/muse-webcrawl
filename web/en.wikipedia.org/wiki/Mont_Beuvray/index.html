Mont Beuvray

Mont Beuvray is a mountain in the Morvan massif of Burgundy, France, on the border of the Saône-et-Loire and Nièvre departments. Rising to 821 metres, it was the site of Bibracte, the capital oppidum of the Aedui, a powerful Gallic people allied with Rome.

Bibracte

Excavations begun in the 19th century and resumed from the 1980s under an international research program have revealed extensive fortifications (the murus gallicus), workshops, houses and public buildings of the late Iron Age and early Roman period. It was near Bibracte that Vercingetorix was proclaimed leader of the Gallic coalition in 52 BC, and Julius Caesar wrote parts of his Gallic War commentaries there. After the Roman conquest the population moved to the new city of Augustodunum (Autun), and the site was abandoned, though a Franciscan convent and chapel of Saint Martin later stood on the summit. A museum at the foot of the mountain presents the archaeology of the site.
