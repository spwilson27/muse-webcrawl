Nonparametric regression

Nonparametric regression is a category of regression analysis in which the predictor does not take a predetermined form but is constructed according to information derived from the data. Unlike linear regression, which assumes the conditional mean of the response is a fixed linear function of the predictors, nonparametric methods let the data determine the shape of the regression function, at the cost of requiring larger sample sizes.

Methods

Common techniques include kernel regression, in which the response at a point is a locally weighted average of nearby observations (the Nadaraya–Watson estimator and the local-polynomial generalisation by Fan and Gijbels); smoothing splines, which minimize a penalized sum of squares trading fit against roughness; regression trees and their ensembles; and Gaussian process regression (kriging), which places a prior over functions. The smoothing parameter or bandwidth — the crucial choice in all of these — is usually selected by cross-validation.

Trade-offs

Flexibility protects against model misspecification bias, but variance grows quickly with the number of predictors: the "curse of dimensionality" means data become sparse in high dimensions, and nonparametric estimates converge more slowly than parametric ones. Semiparametric models, such as generalized additive models, compromise by combining parametric and smooth nonparametric components.
