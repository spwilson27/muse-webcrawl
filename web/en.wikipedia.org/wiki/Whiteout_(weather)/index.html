Whiteout (weather)

Whiteout is a weather condition in which the contours and landmarks in a snow-covered zone become almost indistinguishable. It is a serious hazard for travellers in polar and mountainous regions.

Definition

A whiteout may be due to two different phenomena. In a blizzard whiteout, heavy falling or blowing snow reduces visibility to near zero, as in a severe snowstorm. In a diffuse-light whiteout, an unbroken snow cover and a uniformly overcast sky blend so that the horizon disappears, shadows vanish, and the observer is surrounded by a featureless white void; light levels remain high but depth perception and orientation are lost. This second type is most common in polar regions under a complete cloud layer over fresh snow.

Effects

Because the horizon cannot be seen and no shadows are cast, travellers may become disoriented, lose their sense of up and down, and be unable to judge distance or slope. Whiteouts have contributed to aircraft accidents — pilots flying visually may strike terrain — and to fatal exposure when people on foot lose the route between shelters, sometimes only metres apart. Safety practice in Antarctica and on glaciers requires roped travel, GPS navigation, and shelter-in-place procedures during whiteout conditions.
