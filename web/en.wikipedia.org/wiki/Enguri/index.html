Enguri

The Enguri (Georgian: ენგური, Abkhaz: Егры, Russian: Ингури) is a river in western Georgia. It is about 213 kilometres (132 mi) long and rises in the Greater Caucasus near the high mountain village of Ushguli in the region of Svaneti, close to the border with Russia. The river flows through deep mountain gorges southwestward and empties into the Black Sea near the village of Anaklia.

Course and basin

The Enguri drains a basin of roughly 4,060 square kilometres. Its upper course passes through the historic region of Svaneti, known for its medieval defensive towers, and the river is fed by glaciers and snowmelt from some of the highest peaks in the Caucasus, including Mount Shkhara, Georgia's highest mountain. The lower course forms part of the de facto boundary between Abkhazia and the rest of Georgia.

Enguri Dam

The river is best known for the Enguri Dam, a huge hydroelectric arch dam completed in 1978. At 271.5 metres (891 ft) high, it is one of the tallest concrete arch dams in the world. The associated power station, with an installed capacity of about 1,300 MW, supplies a large share of Georgia's electricity. The dam's reservoir, known as the Jvari reservoir, lies upstream in the gorge. Since the war in Abkhazia in the early 1990s, the dam stands on territory controlled by the Georgian government while the power station's generators are on the Abkhaz side, and the two sides cooperate in operating the facility.
