Escape pod

An escape pod, also called a lifeboat or escape capsule, is a small craft or capsule used to escape a larger vessel in an emergency, most commonly in science fiction depictions of spacecraft and submarines. Escape pods are typically designed to sustain their occupants for a limited period and to carry them to safety or to a survivable landing.

In fiction

Escape pods are a staple of science fiction film and television. In Star Wars (1977), the droids R2-D2 and C-3PO flee the captured Rebel blockade runner Tantive IV in an escape pod, carrying the Death Star plans to Tatooine. The Star Trek franchise features escape pods aboard Federation starships, and the film Alien (1979) ends with Ellen Ripley escaping the doomed Nostromo in the shuttle Narcissus — frequently described as a lifeboat or escape craft.

The concept reflects real maritime lifeboat practice translated into a spacefaring setting. Pods in fiction often have limited propulsion, basic life support, and a distress beacon, and are jettisoned by explosive bolts or a launch mechanism.

Real-world analogues

Actual spacecraft have employed related concepts. The Soviet Vostok capsule ejected its cosmonaut for a parachute landing, and several proposed crew vehicles have included launch escape systems — rockets or separable cabins designed to pull a crew away from a failing launcher. Submarines have carried rescue devices such as the McCann rescue chamber and, in fiction-influencing practice, escape trunks and free-ascent escape equipment. The International Space Station maintains docked Soyuz and Crew Dragon vehicles as de facto lifeboats for emergency evacuation.
