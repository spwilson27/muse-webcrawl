Max q

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Max q is the point of maximum aerodynamic stress during a rocket's ascent.

Max q is the point of maximum dynamic pressure during the launch of a rocket or other aerospace vehicle, and the point at which its airframe experiences maximum mechanical stress. Dynamic pressure, denoted q, is given by

q = ½ ρ v²

where ρ is the local air density and v is the vehicle's velocity relative to the air.

During launch

As a launch vehicle climbs, its speed increases while the density of the atmosphere decreases. Early in flight the speed term dominates and dynamic pressure rises; later, the falling density dominates and pressure decreases. Max q occurs at the maximum of this curve — for the Space Shuttle at roughly one minute after liftoff, at an altitude of about 11 km. Approaching max q, launch vehicles often throttle down their engines to reduce acceleration and keep aerodynamic loads within structural limits; NASA launch commentary traditionally announces "the vehicle is going through the period of maximum dynamic pressure" and later "go at throttle up".

Importance

Max q is a critical design constraint: the vehicle's structure must withstand the loads at this point, and it is also the most demanding phase for controllability in wind shear. The term is used by analogy in other fields, including finance and project management, for the point of maximum stress in a process.
