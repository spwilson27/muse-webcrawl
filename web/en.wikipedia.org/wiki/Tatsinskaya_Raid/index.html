Tatsinskaya Raid

Date16–28 December 1942
LocationTatsinskaya, Rostov Oblast, USSR
Part ofOperation Little Saturn, Battle of Stalingrad
ResultSoviet operational success; 24th Tank Corps largely destroyed
Soviet commanderVasily Badanov (24th Tank Corps)

The Tatsinskaya Raid was a deep armoured raid carried out by the Soviet 24th Tank Corps under Major General Vasily Badanov during Operation Little Saturn, in the later stages of the Battle of Stalingrad in December 1942. Penetrating nearly 240 kilometres behind German lines, the corps seized the airfield at Tatsinskaya on 24 December, destroying dozens of Luftwaffe transport aircraft used to supply the encircled German 6th Army.

Background

Following the encirclement of the 6th Army at Stalingrad in Operation Uranus, the Soviet command launched Operation Little Saturn on 16 December 1942 against the Italian 8th Army on the middle Don. The 24th Tank Corps was ordered to exploit the breakthrough and strike Tatsinskaya, from whose airfield Junkers Ju 52 transports flew supplies into the Stalingrad pocket.

The raid

Advancing rapidly across the frozen steppe, the corps reached Tatsinskaya on the morning of 24 December. Soviet tanks overran the airfield, crushing aircraft under their tracks; estimates of destroyed aircraft range from about 50 to over 70, including many Ju 52s and Heinkel He 111s. The loss forced the Luftwaffe to shift its airlift to Salsk and Zverevo, further from the pocket, sharply reducing the tonnage delivered.

Aftermath

German forces under XXXXVIII Panzer Corps counterattacked, and the 24th Tank Corps, short of fuel and ammunition, was nearly encircled. Fighting its way out toward Ilyichevka by 28 December, the corps lost most of its tanks, but the raid's strategic effect had been achieved. The corps was redesignated the 2nd Guards Tank Corps in recognition of the operation, and Badanov became the first recipient of the Order of Suvorov, 2nd class.
