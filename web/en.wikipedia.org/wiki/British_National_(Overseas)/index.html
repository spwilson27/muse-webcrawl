British National (Overseas)

Legal basisHong Kong Act 1985 and Hong Kong (British Nationality) Order 1986
EligibilityBritish Dependent Territories citizens by connection with Hong Kong who registered before the 1997 handover
Right of abode in UKNot automatic (but see BN(O) visa route, 2021)

British National (Overseas), abbreviated BN(O), is a class of British nationality created for people connected with Hong Kong at the time of its transfer from British to Chinese sovereignty in 1997. It was available to Hong Kong British Dependent Territories citizens who registered for the status before the handover; roughly 3.4 million people hold or held the status, and holders are issued distinctive British National (Overseas) passports.

Nature of the status

BN(O) status is a form of British nationality that, by itself, historically conferred no right of abode anywhere, including in Hong Kong or the United Kingdom. Holders were entitled to British consular protection outside China and Hong Kong, could hold a British passport, and could visit the UK without a visa, but were subject to immigration control if they wished to live or work there. The status cannot be passed to children and could only be acquired by registration during the period 1987–1997.

Under Chinese nationality law, ethnic Chinese BN(O) holders in Hong Kong are regarded as Chinese nationals, and China does not recognise the BN(O) passport for use in Hong Kong or mainland China — a position restated in 2021.

BN(O) visa route

Following the imposition of the Hong Kong national security law in 2020, the UK government announced that BN(O) status holders and their dependants would be offered a route to live, work and study in the UK. The Hong Kong BN(O) visa opened on 31 January 2021, allowing holders to settle for five years and then apply for settlement and, after a further year, British citizenship. Well over 100,000 applications were made in the scheme's first two years. China responded by ceasing to recognise BN(O) passports as valid travel documents.
