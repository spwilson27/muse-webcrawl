Music of Azerbaijan

The music of Azerbaijan includes the classical art form of mugham, the bardic tradition of the ashiqs, diverse folk music, and modern genres including jazz and pop. Azerbaijani musical culture draws on Turkic, Persian, and Caucasian influences.

Mugham

Mugham is a highly developed modal art music combining sung poetry with instrumental improvisation, typically performed by a trio of singer (khananda) accompanied by tar (lute) and kamancheh (spike fiddle), or an ensemble including balaban, nagara, and gaval. In 2008 Azerbaijani mugham was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Renowned performers include singer Alim Qasimov and the late Seyid Shushinski.

Ashiq art

Ashiqs are travelling bards who sing and tell tales while playing the saz, a long-necked lute. Their repertoire includes epic stories such as the Kitabi Dede Korkut tales and the romance of Koroghlu. The art of Azerbaijani ashiqs was added to the UNESCO list in 2009.

Folk and traditional music

Folk music features dances (yalli group dances), work songs, and lyrical songs. Traditional instruments include the tar, kamancheh, balaban (a double-reed wind instrument), zurna, nagara drum, and tutek flute. The playing of the balaban and the craftsmanship of the tar are also UNESCO-listed.

Classical and composed music

Uzeyir Hajibeyov (1885–1948), founder of Azerbaijani composed classical music, wrote Leyli and Majnun (1908), considered the first opera of the Muslim East, blending mugham with Western opera. Later composers include Gara Garayev, Fikret Amirov (creator of the "mugham symphony"), and Arif Malikov.

Jazz and popular music

Baku developed a distinctive jazz scene; Vagif Mustafazadeh pioneered "jazz-mugham" in the 1960s–70s. Azerbaijan debuted at the Eurovision Song Contest in 2008 and won in 2011 with Ell & Nikki's "Running Scared", hosting the contest in Baku in 2012. Meykhana, a rhythmic spoken folk tradition, remains popular.
