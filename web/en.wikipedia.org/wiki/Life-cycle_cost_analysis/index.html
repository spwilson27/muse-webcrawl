Life-cycle cost analysis

Life-cycle cost analysis (LCCA) is a method for assessing the total cost of owning an asset over its entire useful life. It takes into account initial investment costs, operating and maintenance costs, energy costs, replacement and disposal costs, and the residual or salvage value, usually discounting future costs to present value so that alternatives with different cost profiles can be compared fairly.

Applications

LCCA is widely used in building design and public procurement — for example in comparing construction options for highways, bridges and facilities under standards such as those of NIST and ASTM — and in defence acquisition, where support costs dominate. By revealing that the lowest purchase price is often not the cheapest option overall, it encourages investment in durability and energy efficiency.

Method

An analysis selects a study period and discount rate, estimates each cost element for each alternative, discounts them to a common base year, and sums them to a total life-cycle cost. Sensitivity analysis is often applied to uncertain inputs such as energy prices and service lives.
