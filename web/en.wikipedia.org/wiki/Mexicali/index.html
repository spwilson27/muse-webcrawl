Mexicali

Country: Mexico

State: Baja California

Municipality: Mexicali

Founded: 14 March 1903

Population (2020): approx. 854,000 (city); over 1,000,000 (municipality)

Elevation: approx. 8 m (26 ft)

Mexicali is the capital city of the Mexican state of Baja California and seat of the Municipality of Mexicali. The city, which was founded on 14 March 1903, is located on the Mexico–United States border, adjacent to its sister city of Calexico, California, with which it forms a dual-state, international metropolitan area (the Calexico–Mexicali area).

The name Mexicali is a portmanteau of "Mexico" and "California", just as Calexico combines "California" and "Mexico". Mexicali is a major industrial, agricultural, and commercial center in northwestern Mexico, and it is also notable for its large Chinese-Mexican community — the city has one of the most prominent Chinatowns in Mexico, known as La Chinesca — and for its extreme summer heat.

History

The region was historically inhabited by indigenous peoples including the Cucapá. Settlement of the modern city began with irrigation works in the Mexicali Valley in the early 20th century, developed with the construction of canals diverting Colorado River water to grow cotton. Chinese, Japanese, and East Indian laborers were recruited to work the fields, giving Mexicali a distinctive multicultural character. Mexicali became the state capital when Baja California was admitted as a state in 1952 (the capital had previously been Ensenada when it was a territory).

Geography and climate

Mexicali lies in the Mexicali Valley, an extension of the Imperial Valley, near the Cerro Prieto geothermal field and the landmark Cerro El Centinela. The city has an arid desert climate (Köppen BWh) with extremely hot summers — temperatures regularly exceed 45 °C (113 °F), and Mexicali has recorded some of the highest temperatures in Mexico — mild winters, and very little rainfall. The 2010 Baja California earthquake (magnitude 7.2), centered near the city, caused significant damage in the valley.

Economy

Mexicali's economy is anchored by agriculture in the surrounding valley (cotton, wheat, alfalfa, vegetables), a large maquiladora manufacturing sector (aerospace, electronics, medical devices, automotive components), and cross-border trade and commerce with the Imperial Valley of California. Companies such as Honeywell, Gulfstream, and Kenworth have major operations in the city. The Cerro Prieto geothermal plant is one of the largest geothermal power stations in the world.

Culture

The city is famous for its Chinese-Mexican cuisine — Mexicali-style Chinese food is considered distinctive in Mexico — as well as for its craft beer scene, which grew rapidly in the 2010s. The local baseball team, the Águilas de Mexicali, plays in the Mexican Pacific League. Cultural institutions include the Autonomous University of Baja California (UABC), founded in 1957, whose main campus is in the city.
