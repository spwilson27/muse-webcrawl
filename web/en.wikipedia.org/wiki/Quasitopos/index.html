Quasitopos

In category theory, a quasitopos is a generalization of a topos that is locally cartesian closed and has finite limits and colimits together with a classifier for strong subobjects (rather than all subobjects). Every elementary topos is a quasitopos, but the converse fails.

Definition

A category is a quasitopos if it has all finite limits and colimits, is locally cartesian closed, and admits a strong-subobject classifier: an object Ω with a monomorphism true: 1 → Ω such that strong monos (equivalently, monos equalized by their own pullback structure) are classified by characteristic maps into Ω. Strong monos include regular monos, so quasitoposes still support a rich internal logic, though subobjects of a general object need not all be classified.

Examples

The category of diffeological spaces and the category of Frölicher spaces are quasitoposes that are not toposes, providing convenient categories for differential geometry. Categories of "spaces" equipped with additional structure frequently form quasitoposes, motivating their study as settings for synthetic mathematics and homotopy theory.
