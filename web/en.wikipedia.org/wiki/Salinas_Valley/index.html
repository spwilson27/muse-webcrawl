The Salinas Valley is a long agricultural valley in Monterey County on California's Central Coast, running southeast from Monterey Bay between the Santa Lucia Range and the Gabilan Range, following the Salinas River toward Paso Robles. Its cool marine climate, long growing season and rich alluvial soils make it one of the most productive vegetable-growing regions on earth.

Agriculture

Called the "Salad Bowl of the World", the valley produces the great majority of the United States' lettuce, along with broccoli, strawberries, spinach, celery, cauliflower and wine grapes (notably in the Santa Lucia Highlands AVA). Icing and vacuum cooling, pioneered here in the early 20th century, let "iceberg" lettuce be shipped across the country; companies such as Fresh Express and Taylor Farms grew out of the valley. Farming depends on irrigation from groundwater, and overdraft, nitrate contamination and seawater intrusion near the coast are persistent concerns.

Steinbeck country

The valley is inseparably associated with the writer John Steinbeck, born in the city of Salinas in 1902. It forms the setting of East of Eden — whose opening describes the valley in detail — Of Mice and Men, The Grapes of Wrath (in part), Tortilla Flat and Cannery Row. The National Steinbeck Center stands in Salinas, and the valley's history of farm labour organising, including César Chávez's early campaigns, is a major theme of its literature and politics.

Communities

Besides Salinas, the county seat, valley towns include Gonzales, Soledad, Greenfield and King City. Mission San Carlos Borromeo and Mission Nuestra Señora de la Soledad date to the Spanish era, and Fort Hunter Liggett occupies the upper valley.
