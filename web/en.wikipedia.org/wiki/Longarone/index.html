Longarone is a town and comune in the province of Belluno, in the Veneto region of north-east Italy, situated in the valley of the Piave river below the Vajont gorge. It is known internationally as the community worst affected by the Vajont disaster of 9 October 1963.

Vajont disaster

On the night of 9 October 1963, a massive landslide of about 260 million cubic metres of rock slid from Monte Toc into the reservoir behind the Vajont Dam. The displaced water overtopped the dam — which itself survived almost intact — in a wave some 250 metres high above the crest, and descended on Longarone and the villages of Pirago, Rivalta, Villanova and Faè, obliterating them within minutes. Around 1,900 people died in total, the great majority in Longarone, which lost roughly a third of its population.

Rebuilding

The town was rebuilt on the same site in the years that followed, funded in part from compensation and national reconstruction programmes, and the area developed an eyewear and manufacturing industry. A memorial church and museum commemorate the disaster. In 2014 the neighbouring municipality of Castellavazzo was merged into Longarone.
