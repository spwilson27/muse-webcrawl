The Mayor of Greater Manchester is a directly elected metro mayor who chairs the Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA) and exercises devolved powers over transport, policing, fire and rescue, housing, planning, skills and economic development across the ten boroughs of Greater Manchester in North West England.

History

The office was created under a devolution deal agreed between the government and Greater Manchester's local authorities in November 2014, legislated for by the Cities and Local Government Devolution Act 2016. An interim mayor, Tony Lloyd, served from 2015. At the first election on 4 May 2017, the Labour candidate Andy Burnham, a former health secretary, won with about 63% of first-preference votes. Burnham was re-elected in 2021 with an increased majority and again in May 2024.

Powers

The mayor acts as the police and crime commissioner for Greater Manchester Police, oversees the Greater Manchester Fire and Rescue Service, controls a consolidated transport budget and franchised bus network (the Bee Network, rolled out from September 2023 — the first franchised bus system in England outside London in nearly forty years), and manages an investment fund and spatial development strategy. Further devolution deals in 2023 granted Greater Manchester and the West Midlands "trailblazer" single-settlement funding and deeper powers over skills, housing and net zero.

Elections

Elections were originally held under the supplementary vote system; from 2024 they use first-past-the-post following the Elections Act 2022. The mayor is elected for a four-year term alongside the leaders of the ten borough councils, who form the GMCA cabinet.
