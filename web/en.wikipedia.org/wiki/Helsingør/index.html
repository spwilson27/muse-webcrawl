Helsingør (Danish pronunciation: [helse̝ŋˈøɐ̯ˀ]; Swedish: Helsingör), classically known in English as Elsinore, is a city in eastern Denmark, at the narrowest point of the Øresund strait opposite Helsingborg, Sweden.

History

The city grew prosperous from the Sound Dues, levied from the 15th century on ships passing through the strait, collected under the authority of Kronborg Castle, built in its Renaissance form in the late 16th century. Kronborg is famous as Elsinore, the setting of Shakespeare's Hamlet.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Kronborg Castle (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), St Olaf's Church, the Carmelite Priory and St Mary's Church, and the modern Kulturværftet cultural centre and M/S Maritime Museum of Denmark, designed by Bjarke Ingels Group in the old dry dock.

Transport

Ferries connect Helsingør with Helsingborg in about 20 minutes, and railways link the city to Copenhagen.
