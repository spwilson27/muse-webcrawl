Thiol


General structure of a thiol, R–SH

In organic chemistry, a thiol (from Greek theion, "sulfur") is an organosulfur compound containing a carbon-bonded sulfhydryl group (C–SH or R–SH, where R represents an alkyl or other organic substituent). The –SH functional group itself is called a thiol group or a sulfhydryl group. Thiols are the sulfur analogues of alcohols, with sulfur in place of oxygen.

Many thiols have strong odors resembling that of garlic or rotten eggs. Thiols are used as odorants to help detect natural gas leaks (which in pure form is odorless), and the "smell of skunk" is caused by thiol compounds. The simplest thiol is methanethiol (CH3SH), a gas; others, such as ethanethiol, are volatile liquids.

Structure and bonding

The C–S–H bond angle is approximately 90°, much closer to the angle in hydrogen sulfide than the corresponding bond in alcohols, reflecting the minimal hybridization of sulfur. The C–S bond is longer (around 180 pm) and weaker than a C–O bond. Thiols are more acidic than alcohols, with pKa values typically around 10–11.

Nomenclature

Alkanethiols are named by adding the suffix -thiol to the alkane name, or by naming the alkyl group followed by mercaptan, an older term derived from Latin mercurium captans ("capturing mercury"), reflecting thiols' strong affinity for mercury ions.

Reactions

  • Oxidation — thiols are readily oxidized to disulfides (R–S–S–R), a reaction central to protein structure in the amino acid cysteine.
  • Alkylation — thiolates (R–S−) are powerful nucleophiles and react with alkyl halides to give thioethers.
  • Metal binding — thiols bind strongly to heavy metals, which is exploited in chelation therapy.

Biological importance

The amino acid cysteine contains a thiol group, and pairs of cysteines form disulfide bridges that stabilize protein tertiary structure. Glutathione, a thiol-containing tripeptide, is a major cellular antioxidant. Coenzyme A carries acyl groups as thioesters. The rubber-like odor compounds in skunk spray include (E)-2-butene-1-thiol and 3-methyl-1-butanethiol.
