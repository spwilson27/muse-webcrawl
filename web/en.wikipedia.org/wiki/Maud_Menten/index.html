Maud Menten

Maud Leonora Menten (20 March 1879 – 17 July 1960) was a Canadian physician and biochemist whose work with Leonor Michaelis on enzyme kinetics produced the Michaelis–Menten equation, one of the foundational results of biochemistry. She was among the first women in Canada to earn a medical degree and one of the first female medical researchers in her country.

Early life and education

Menten was born in Port Lambton, Ontario, and studied at the University of Toronto, earning her bachelor's degree in 1904 and her medical degree in 1911. Because women faced barriers to research positions in Canada, she pursued her career abroad: she worked in Berlin with Leonor Michaelis in 1912–13, earned a Ph.D. under Simon Flexner-related work at the University of Chicago in 1916, and later joined the University of Pittsburgh, where she remained for most of her career, becoming a full professor in 1948.

Michaelis–Menten kinetics

In their 1913 paper, published in Biochemische Zeitschrift, Michaelis and Menten studied the rate at which the enzyme invertase hydrolyses sucrose. Building on earlier work by Victor Henri, they derived the equation relating reaction velocity to substrate concentration, introducing what is now called the Michaelis constant, Km. The model — later re-derived by Briggs and Haldane under a steady-state assumption in 1925 — remains the standard description of enzyme-catalysed reactions.

Other contributions and life

Menten also worked on bacterial toxins, blood sugar regulation, haemoglobin, and histochemistry; she developed the azo-dye coupling reaction for alkaline phosphatase still used in staining. An accomplished linguist, musician, and painter, she learned to fly and drove one of the first automobiles in Pittsburgh. She was inducted into the Canadian Medical Hall of Fame in 1998.
