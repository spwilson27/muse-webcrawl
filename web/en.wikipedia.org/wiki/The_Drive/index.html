The Drive refers to an offensive series in the fourth quarter of the 1986 AFC Championship Game played on January 11, 1987, at Cleveland Municipal Stadium between the Denver Broncos and the Cleveland Browns. Broncos quarterback John Elway, in a span of 5 minutes and 2 seconds, led his team 98 yards in 15 plays to tie the game with 37 seconds remaining in regulation, culminating in a 20-yard field goal by Rich Karlis in overtime that sent Denver to Super Bowl XXI.

The sequence

Trailing 20–13 after a 48-yard touchdown pass from Bernie Kosar to Brian Brennan, Denver took possession at its own 2-yard line with 5:32 to play, the ball pinned deep after a punt and a mishandled return. Elway, in bitter cold and wind, methodically moved the Broncos downfield with completions to Sammy Winder, Steve Watson, Mark Jackson, and Steve Sewell, converting two third downs. With 39 seconds left, Elway found Jackson in the end zone on third-and-18 for a 5-yard touchdown pass, and Karlis's extra point tied the game at 20–20.

Overtime and aftermath

Cleveland received the overtime kickoff but was forced to punt. Elway drove Denver 60 yards in 9 plays, and Karlis kicked a 33-yard field goal — which appeared to many viewers to drift outside the upright, but was ruled good — 5:48 into overtime for a 23–20 victory.

The Drive is considered one of the most clutch performances in NFL history and the signature moment of Elway's Hall of Fame career. For Cleveland, the loss began a sequence of heartbreaks, followed the next season by "The Fumble" in the 1987 AFC Championship Game, also against Denver.
