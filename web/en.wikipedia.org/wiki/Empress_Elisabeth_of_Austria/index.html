Elisabeth (born Duchess Elisabeth Amalie Eugenie in Bavaria; 24 December 1837 – 10 September 1898), nicknamed Sisi, was Empress of Austria and Queen of Hungary by marriage to Emperor Franz Joseph I. Celebrated for her beauty and her famously long hair and slender figure, she chafed against the rigid etiquette of the Habsburg court and spent much of her later life travelling.

Life

Raised in the informal surroundings of Possenhofen in Bavaria, Elisabeth was chosen by Franz Joseph in 1853 in preference to her elder sister Helene, for whom the match had been intended; they married in Vienna in April 1854. Her relationship with her domineering mother-in-law, Archduchess Sophie, was fraught. Elisabeth withdrew increasingly from court after the death of her only son, Crown Prince Rudolf, at Mayerling in 1889, and devoted herself to travel, riding, poetry and the cult of her own beauty — she maintained an elaborate regime of diet, exercise and hair care.

Assassination and legacy

On 10 September 1898, while walking incognito in Geneva to board a steamer, Elisabeth was stabbed in the heart with a sharpened file by the Italian anarchist Luigi Lucheni. She died within the hour. Her life became the subject of the beloved Austrian Sissi film trilogy of the 1950s starring Romy Schneider, and of musicals, novels and the Netflix series The Empress.
