Channel 4 is a British free-to-air public broadcast television channel owned and operated by Channel Four Television Corporation. It was established to provide a fourth television service in the United Kingdom, in addition to the two BBC services and the single commercial ITV network. The channel began broadcasting on 2 November 1982.

History

Channel 4 was created under the Broadcasting Act 1980, following decades of discussion about a fourth channel. Unlike other broadcasters, Channel 4 does not produce its own programmes in-house; it commissions or buys all of its programming from independent production companies, a "publisher-broadcaster" model that helped transform the British television production industry. Its remit requires it to be innovative, experimental, and distinctive, and to serve tastes and interests not catered for elsewhere.

Its first programme was the gameshow Countdown. Over the following decades the channel became known for programmes such as Brookside, Big Brother, Peep Show, and its award-winning news programme Channel 4 News. A Welsh-language counterpart, S4C, launched the day before Channel 4.

Funding and ownership

Channel 4 is publicly owned but commercially funded, principally through advertising. It makes no profit for shareholders; surpluses are reinvested in programming. Related channels include E4, More4, Film4, 4seven, and the streaming service Channel 4 (formerly All 4). Proposals to privatise the channel were debated by the UK government in 2022 but abandoned in 2023.
