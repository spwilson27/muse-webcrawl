Mardi Gras

Mardi Gras
Also calledFat Tuesday; Shrove Tuesday
ObservedDay before Ash Wednesday; culmination of the Carnival season
Famous celebrationsNew Orleans and Mobile (United States); also Rio de Janeiro, Venice, Nice, Binche (as Carnival)

Mardi Gras (French for "Fat Tuesday") refers to the festive events of the Carnival season, culminating on the day before Ash Wednesday, the first day of Lent in the Western Christian calendar. Traditionally a last occasion for feasting and revelry before the fasting and penance of Lent, Mardi Gras is most famously celebrated in New Orleans, Louisiana, with weeks of parades organised by social clubs called krewes, masked balls, and the throwing of beads and trinkets to crowds. The first Mardi Gras in what is now the United States was celebrated by French colonists in 1699, and the oldest organised celebrations in the country are those of Mobile, Alabama, dating to the early 18th century.

New Orleans

New Orleans's Carnival season begins on Twelfth Night (6 January) and builds to five days of major parades ending on Fat Tuesday. Organising krewes include Rex (founded 1872, which established the official colours purple, green and gold — for justice, faith and power — and the anthem "If Ever I Cease to Love"), the Zulu Social Aid & Pleasure Club (founded 1909, famous for its hand-painted coconut throws), Endymion, Bacchus and the satirical Krewe du Vieux. Riders on floats throw strings of beads, doubloon coins, cups and plush toys to spectators shouting "Throw me something, Mister!" Other traditions include king cake — eaten throughout the season, with a tiny baby figure hidden inside — flambeaux carriers, masked balls, and the Mardi Gras Indians, African-American masking groups whose elaborate hand-sewn suits honour Native American allies of escaped slaves. Because the date of Easter varies, Mardi Gras can fall on any Tuesday between 3 February and 9 March.

Elsewhere

Related pre-Lenten carnivals include the Carnival of Rio de Janeiro, the Carnival of Venice with its masks, the Nice Carnival, Belgium's Carnival of Binche (UNESCO-listed), Germany's Karneval and Fastnacht, and Britain's Shrove Tuesday pancake customs. In the United States, Gulf Coast cities — Mobile, Biloxi, Pensacola, Galveston and others — hold their own Mardi Gras celebrations, and the Cajun country of Louisiana celebrates Courir de Mardi Gras, a rural run in traditional costume.
