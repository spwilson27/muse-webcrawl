Piet Pieterszoon Hein

Piet Pieterszoon Hein (25 November 1577 – 18 June 1629) was a Dutch admiral and privateer for the Dutch Republic during the Eighty Years' War. Hein was the first and last to capture a large part of a Spanish treasure fleet, a feat that made him a national hero in the Netherlands.

Early life and career

Hein was born in Delfshaven, the son of a sea captain. He went to sea at a young age and was twice captured by the Spanish and held as a galley slave, experience that gave him first-hand knowledge of Spanish shipping. As a commander for the Dutch East India Company and later the Dutch West India Company, he raided Spanish and Portuguese commerce in the Atlantic and the Caribbean.

Capture of the treasure fleet

In 1628, as lieutenant-admiral commanding a West India Company fleet, Hein intercepted the Spanish silver fleet in Matanzas Bay, Cuba, capturing ships and cargo worth about 11.5 million guilders — an enormous sum that financed the Dutch army for months and allowed the company to pay a remarkable dividend. It was the only time in history that an entire Spanish treasure fleet was captured.

Death and legacy

Appointed lieutenant-admiral of Holland, Hein was killed in 1629 during an action against Dunkirk privateers in the English Channel. A popular Dutch song celebrates his capture of the silver fleet, and several ships of the Royal Netherlands Navy have borne his name.
