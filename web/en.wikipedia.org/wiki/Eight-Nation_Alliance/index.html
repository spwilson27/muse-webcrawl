Eight-Nation Alliance

The Eight-Nation Alliance was a multinational military coalition formed in 1900 in response to the Boxer Rebellion in Qing-dynasty China. Its member nations were Japan, Russia, Britain, France, the United States, Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. The alliance intervened to relieve the siege of the foreign legations in Beijing and to suppress the Boxer movement, an anti-foreign and anti-Christian uprising supported in part by the Qing court of the Empress Dowager Cixi.

Background

By the late 19th century, foreign powers had carved spheres of influence out of China through unequal treaties. Resentment among the Chinese population, combined with drought and economic disruption, fuelled the rise of the Yihetuan — the "Boxers" — who attacked foreign missionaries, Chinese Christians and symbols of foreign influence. In June 1900 the Boxers entered Beijing and besieged the Legation Quarter, while the Qing court declared war on the foreign powers.

The intervention

A first relief expedition under British Vice-Admiral Edward Seymour was turned back. A second, larger force — some 45,000 to 50,000 troops, with the largest contingents from Japan and Russia — captured the Dagu Forts, took Tianjin in July, and fought its way to Beijing, lifting the siege on 14 August 1900. The imperial court fled to Xi'an. Alliance troops occupied the capital, and looting of the city was widespread.

Boxer Protocol

The conflict formally ended with the Boxer Protocol, signed on 7 September 1901. China agreed to pay a huge indemnity of 450 million taels of silver over 39 years, to execute or punish officials who had supported the Boxers, to raze the Dagu Forts, and to allow foreign troops to be stationed along the route from Beijing to the sea. The Protocol is remembered in China as one of the most humiliating of the unequal treaties.

Legacy

The intervention deepened foreign domination of China and contributed to the weakening of the Qing dynasty, which fell a decade later in the Xinhai Revolution of 1911. The Eight-Nation Alliance remains a powerful symbol in Chinese historical memory of the "century of humiliation".
