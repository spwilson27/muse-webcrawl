Lieutenant general is a senior military rank in many armies and air forces, ranking above major general and below general. In the United States it is the three-star rank (pay grade O-9). The title literally means the deputy of the "captain general" of an army.

Role

Lieutenant generals typically command corps-sized formations of 20,000 to 50,000 troops, or hold senior staff appointments such as deputy chief of staff, inspector general, or commander of a numbered army or air force. In the US system the rank is temporary and tied to a specific position, requiring Senate confirmation.

History

The rank dates to the 17th century, when the lieutenant general was second in command of an army under the captain general. George Washington was promoted to lieutenant general in 1798, the highest US rank of his era; Ulysses S. Grant received the rank in 1864 as commander of the Union armies. In Britain, lieutenant general remains a substantive rank in the Army and Royal Marines.

Equivalent ranks

The naval equivalent is vice admiral; in many air forces the equivalent is air marshal. In some countries the rank is styled "general of corps" or "army corps general".
