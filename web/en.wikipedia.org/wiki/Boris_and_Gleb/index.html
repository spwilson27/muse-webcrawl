Boris and Gleb

Died1015, Kievan Rus'
Venerated inEastern Orthodox Church, Roman Catholic Church
Feast day24 July (martyrdom); 3 May (translation of relics)
AttributesPrinces holding crosses and swords

Boris and Gleb (Old East Slavic: Борисъ и Глѣбъ) were the first saints canonized in Kievan Rus'. They were sons of Vladimir the Great and were murdered in 1015 during the succession struggle that followed their father's death. They are venerated as "passion-bearers" — martyrs who accepted death without resistance in imitation of Christ.

The murders

According to the Primary Chronicle and the hagiographic Skazanie (Tale of Boris and Gleb), after Vladimir's death in 1015 his eldest son Sviatopolk seized Kiev. Boris, returning from a campaign, refused to raise his hand against his elder brother and dismissed his retinue; Sviatopolk's assassins stabbed him at his camp on the Alta River. Gleb, summoned by a false message, was killed on the Dnieper near Smolensk. Their youngest brother Yaroslav eventually defeated Sviatopolk and promoted the cult of the murdered princes.

Veneration

Boris and Gleb became the first native saints of the Rus' church, with their relics enshrined at Vyshgorod. Their cult spread widely, and numerous churches and monasteries across Russia and Ukraine bear their names, including the famous Borisoglebsk monastery. Their feast is celebrated on 24 July.

Historiography

Scholars debate the details of the chronicle account, including the degree of Sviatopolk's responsibility, and the tale is regarded as a foundational text of Rus' Christian literature, shaping the ideal of non-resisting princely virtue.
