Combined Joint Task Force

A Combined Joint Task Force (CJTF) is a task force in NATO and allied military terminology that is both combined — composed of forces from two or more nations — and joint — composed of elements from two or more military services (army, navy, air force, and sometimes marines or space forces). CJTFs are ad hoc headquarters formed for a specific operation or mission, ranging from combat operations to peacekeeping and humanitarian relief.

Concept

The CJTF concept allows a coalition headquarters to be assembled rapidly from a standing command structure, drawing staff and units from contributing nations. NATO formalised the concept in the 1990s, following the end of the Cold War, so that operations could be conducted "out of area" and so that non-NATO partners — for example members of the Western European Union — could participate in, or even lead, missions using NATO assets. A CJTF headquarters is designed to be deployable and scalable, with a commander, a multinational staff, and component commands for land, maritime, air, and special operations forces.

Examples

Notable task forces designated as CJTFs include Combined Joint Task Force – Operation Inherent Resolve (CJTF-OIR), the US-led coalition headquarters directing operations against the Islamic State in Iraq and Syria from 2014; Combined Joint Task Force – Horn of Africa (CJTF-HOA), based at Camp Lemonnier in Djibouti; and CJTF-76 and successor headquarters in Afghanistan during Operation Enduring Freedom. The numbering and naming of CJTFs follows the conventions of the lead nation's command system.
