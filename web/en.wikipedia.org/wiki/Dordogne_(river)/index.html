Dordogne (river)

The Dordogne (Occitan: Dordonha) is a river in south-central and southwestern France. About 483 kilometres (300 mi) long, it rises in the Massif Central on the slopes of the Puy de Sancy and flows generally westward to join the Garonne at the Bec d'Ambès, north of Bordeaux, where the two rivers form the Gironde estuary.

Course

The Dordogne flows through the departments of Puy-de-Dôme, Corrèze, Lot, Dordogne, Lot-et-Garonne and Gironde, passing towns including Argentat, Souillac, Domme, Bergerac, Libourne and Saint-Émilion nearby. Its principal tributaries include the Vézère, the Isle, the Cère and the Dronne. The middle valley, in the Périgord, is famous for its limestone cliffs, castles (such as Beynac and Castelnaud), and prehistoric sites; the basin was designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in 2012.

History and use

The river was historically an important trade route, with flat-bottomed barges called gabarres carrying wine, timber and chestnuts downstream to Bordeaux. Several large hydroelectric dams, including Bort-les-Orgues and Marèges, were built on the upper river in the 20th century. The Dordogne is noted for salmon fishing and is a protected habitat of European importance.
