Christian Felix Klein (25 April 1849 – 22 June 1925) was a German mathematician known for his work in group theory, complex analysis, non-Euclidean geometry, and the associations between geometry and group theory. His 1872 Erlangen program, classifying geometries by their underlying symmetry groups, was a profoundly influential synthesis in mathematics.

Life

Klein was born in Düsseldorf and studied mathematics and physics at the University of Bonn, receiving his doctorate in 1868 under Julius Plücker. He held professorships at Erlangen (from 1872), the Technische Hochschule in Munich (1875), Leipzig (1880), and finally Göttingen (1886), where he remained until his retirement. At Göttingen he built the leading center of mathematics in the world, helped bring David Hilbert there, founded the journal Mathematische Annalen, and championed reforms in mathematics education.

Work

In the Erlangen program, delivered as his inaugural address at Erlangen in 1872, Klein proposed that each geometry be defined by its group of transformations and the properties invariant under them, unifying Euclidean, hyperbolic, elliptic, and projective geometries. He gave the first models of non-Euclidean geometry connected with projective geometry, studied the Klein bottle (a closed non-orientable surface), the Klein four-group, automorphic functions and Kleinian groups, and the icosahedron's connections to quintic equations and Galois theory. His Elementary Mathematics from a Higher Standpoint influenced school mathematics teaching.
