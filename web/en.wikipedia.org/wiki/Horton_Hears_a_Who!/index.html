Author: Dr. Seuss

Horton Hears a Who! is a children's book written and illustrated by Theodor Seuss Geisel under the pen name Dr. Seuss. It was published by Random House on August 28, 1954, and is the second book featuring Horton the Elephant, after Horton Hatches the Egg (1940).

Plot

Bathing in a pool in the Jungle of Nool, Horton hears a tiny voice coming from a speck of dust. The speck turns out to be the planet of the Whos, a tiny people whose city, Whoville, is in danger. Horton places the speck on a clover and vows to protect it, declaring, "a person's a person, no matter how small." Mocked and persecuted by the other jungle animals — the Sour Kangaroo and the Wickersham Brothers — Horton is eventually caged and the clover lost in a vast clover field. He finds it only with the help of the Whos shouting together, joined at the last moment by the smallest Who of all, JoJo, whose added "Yopp!" makes the Whos finally audible and proves their existence.

Themes and legacy

Geisel dedicated the book to a Japanese friend, and scholars have read it partly as an allegory of the post-war occupation of Japan and a repudiation of Geisel's wartime views. Its refrain "a person's a person, no matter how small" has been widely quoted — and controversially adopted by pro-life campaigners, against the wishes of Geisel and later his widow. The book was adapted into a 1970 animated television special directed by Chuck Jones and into a 2008 computer-animated feature film produced by Blue Sky Studios, starring Jim Carrey as Horton and Steve Carell as the Mayor of Whoville.
