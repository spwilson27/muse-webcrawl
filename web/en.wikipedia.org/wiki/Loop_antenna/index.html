Loop antenna

A small magnetic loop antenna with tuning capacitor

A loop antenna is a radio antenna consisting of a loop or coil of wire, tubing, or other electrical conductor, usually fed by a balanced source or feeding a balanced load. Loop antennas fall into two broad classes. Large loops, with a circumference approaching a wavelength, behave like folded dipole antennas. Small loops, with a circumference much less than a wavelength, respond primarily to the magnetic component of the radio wave and are often called magnetic loop antennas.

Small loops

Small loops have low radiation resistance and high losses, making them inefficient for transmitting at low power but excellent for reception: they are relatively insensitive to local electric-field noise, and they have a sharply directional response with two deep nulls perpendicular to the plane of the loop. These properties made them standard for radio direction finding, and ferrite loop ("loopstick") antennas are built into most AM broadcast receivers. A variable capacitor brings the loop to resonance, greatly increasing efficiency; transmitting magnetic loops made of copper tube are popular with amateur radio operators who have limited space.

Large loops

Full-wave loops, often square ("quad" elements) or circular, are used as resonant antennas in their own right and as the driven elements and reflectors of cubical quad arrays, offering slightly more gain than a dipole and good performance at modest heights.
