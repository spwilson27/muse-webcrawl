Districts of Germany

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Map of German districts (Landkreise and urban districts)

In Germany, a district (German: Landkreis, pronounced [lantkrais]) is an administrative unit between the Länder (federated states) and the local municipalities (Stadte and Gemeinden). In addition to these rural districts, there are urban districts (kreisfreie Stadte, literally "district-free cities"), which are cities large enough to perform district functions for themselves. Together, districts are responsible for a range of local administrative functions, though their exact responsibilities differ from state to state.

Responsibilities

Districts typically administer vehicle registration, waste disposal, hospitals, social welfare, building supervision, and secondary roads, and coordinate public transport and regional planning. They are governed by an elected district council (Kreistag) and a district administrator (Landrat) who serves as the chief executive.

History and reforms

The district system descends from the Kreise introduced in Prussia in the early 19th century. Since German reunification in 1990, and continuing in several states, territorial reforms have merged smaller districts into larger ones, reducing the total number to around 294 rural districts and about 107 urban districts.
