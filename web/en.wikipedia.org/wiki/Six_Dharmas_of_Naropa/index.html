The Six Dharmas of Naropa (Tibetan: ནཱ་རོ་ཆོས་དྲུག, Wylie: na ro chos drug), also known as the Six Yogas of Naropa, are a set of advanced Tibetan Buddhist tantric practices compiled by the Indian mahasiddha Naropa (1016–1100), who received them from his teacher Tilopa. They form a core completion-stage practice of the Kagyu school and are closely related to the Six Yogas of Niguma.

The six practices

  1. Tummo (gtum mo) — the yoga of inner heat, generating blissful warmth in the body.
  2. Illusory body (sgyu lus) — realising the illusory nature of phenomena and one's own body.
  3. Dream yoga (rmi lam) — maintaining awareness in the dream state.
  4. Clear light ('od gsal) — experiencing the luminosity of mind, especially in deep sleep and at death.
  5. Bardo (bar do) — practices for the intermediate state between death and rebirth.
  6. Phowa ('pho ba) — transference of consciousness at the moment of death.

Transmission

Naropa transmitted the teachings to Marpa the Translator, who brought them to Tibet; they passed through Milarepa and Gampopa into the Kagyu lineages. Traditionally the practices are taught only after preliminary practices (ngöndro) and under the guidance of a qualified teacher, since some involve powerful manipulations of the subtle body's winds and channels.
