Weatherbys is a British company founded in 1770 that provides administrative and commercial services to the horse racing industry, maintains the General Stud Book of Thoroughbred breeding, and operates a private bank. It has been run by the Weatherby family for seven generations and is based in Wellingborough, Northamptonshire.

History

James Weatherby, a solicitor and racing secretary, began keeping records of race results and pedigrees in the 1770s, publishing the first Racing Calendar in 1773. His nephew James Weatherby the younger published An Introduction to a General Stud Book in 1791, followed by the first volume of the General Stud Book in 1793 — the foundational pedigree record of the Thoroughbred breed, which Weatherbys still publishes. The firm became the official registrar of British racing under contract to the Jockey Club and later the British Horseracing Authority, handling entries, registrations, and racing colours.

Business

Weatherbys' racing division administers hundreds of thousands of transactions annually for owners, trainers, and breeders in Britain and Ireland. Weatherbys Bank, authorised as a private bank, offers banking and wealth services, notably to the racing community; Weatherbys Hamilton provides bloodstock insurance. The group also publishes stud books, statistical works, and pedigree research services used worldwide.
