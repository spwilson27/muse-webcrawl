Heiligenschein

Heiligenschein (German for "halo of the holy one," literally "holy shine") is an optical phenomenon in which a bright spot appears around the shadow of the viewer's head on a dewy surface. It is a form of opposition effect in which dew drops on grass act as small lenses: sunlight entering each droplet is focused and then retroreflected from the leaf surface behind the drop back toward the antisolar point, which is exactly where the viewer's shadow falls. The result is a soft, luminous halo surrounding the head's shadow, brightest on dew-laden grass at sunrise.

Explanation

Each spherical dew drop behaves as a simple lens, concentrating sunlight into a bright spot on the surface just behind it; light scattered from that spot returns preferentially along the incoming direction. Because the effect is centred on the antisolar point, only the region of ground around the observer's own shadow receives the returning light in visible concentration, and each viewer sees the halo around their own head alone. The mechanism is related to the brightening seen in the "hotspot" of aerial photographs and in observations of the lunar regolith at full moon, though there dry-surface retroreflection and shadow hiding are responsible.

History

The phenomenon attracted literary attention from the Italian sculptor Benvenuto Cellini, who in his autobiography described a halo around his head and interpreted it as a sign of divine favour; the German name derives from this association with sanctity, and the effect is sometimes called Cellini's halo. It is easily observed on any windless morning over a dew-covered lawn when the sun is low.
