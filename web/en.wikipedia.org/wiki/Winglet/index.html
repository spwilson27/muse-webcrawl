Winglet

A winglet is a near-vertical extension at the tip of an aircraft wing that reduces lift-induced drag by weakening and diffusing the wingtip vortices. By recovering some of the energy otherwise lost in the vortex, winglets improve fuel efficiency and range without a large increase in wingspan.

Principle and history

Endplates were fitted to wings as early as the 1910s, but the modern winglet was developed by NASA engineer Richard T. Whitcomb in the 1970s, building on his work on supercritical airfoils and area ruling. Flight tests on a KC-135 in 1979–80 demonstrated fuel savings of roughly 6–7%. The concept parallels the vertical tail feathers and slotted primary feathers of soaring birds.

Types and applications

Designs include the single upward blended winglet (as on the Boeing 737NG), split-tip and scimitar variants (737 MAX, A320neo sharklets), canted winglets (Boeing 747-400), and spiroid loops under study. Winglets also appear on wind-turbine blades, propellers, racing-car wings, and gliders. Retrofitting winglets to existing fleets became a major aftermarket business because even 3–5% fuel savings are economically significant.
