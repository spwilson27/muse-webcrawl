Jawless fish

Jawless fish, traditionally grouped as the Agnatha, are fish lacking jaws. The only living members are the hagfish (Myxini) and the lampreys (Petromyzontida), together known as the cyclostomes.

Characteristics

Jawless fish lack paired fins and jaws, have a cartilaginous skeleton, a persistent notochord, and round, sucker-like or rasping mouths. Lampreys are often parasitic blood-feeders; hagfish are deep-sea scavengers famous for producing copious slime.

Evolution

Agnathans were the dominant vertebrates of the Ordovician and Silurian periods, including the armoured ostracoderms. Jawed vertebrates (gnathostomes) evolved from jawless ancestors, and cladistic analyses place hagfish and lampreys as a monophyletic sister group (Cyclostomata) to all jawed vertebrates.
