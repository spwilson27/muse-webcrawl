Bell polynomials

Named afterEric Temple Bell
FieldCombinatorics

In combinatorics, the Bell polynomials, named in honour of Eric Temple Bell, are a family of polynomials used in the study of set partitions. The (incomplete or partial) exponential Bell polynomial counts the ways of partitioning a set with a specified block-size profile.

Definitions

The partial exponential Bell polynomial Bn,k(x1, …, xn−k+1) is a sum over all ways of writing a set of n labelled elements as a union of k non-empty blocks, weighted by the variables xj for blocks of size j. The complete exponential Bell polynomial Bn is the sum of the partial polynomials over all k, and Bn(1, 1, …, 1) is the n-th Bell number, the total number of partitions of an n-element set.

Applications

Bell polynomials appear in Faà di Bruno's formula for the derivatives of composite functions, in the moment–cumulant relations of probability theory, and in the exponential generating functions of combinatorial sequences. They generalise naturally to ordinary (non-exponential) Bell polynomials, which count partitions by block sizes in the ordinary generating function sense.
