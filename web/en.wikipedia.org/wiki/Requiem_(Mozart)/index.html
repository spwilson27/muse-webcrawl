The Requiem in D minor, K. 626, is a requiem mass by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756–1791). Mozart composed part of the Requiem in Vienna in late 1791, but it was unfinished at his death on 5 December of the same year. A completed version dated 1792, by Franz Xaver Süssmayr, was delivered to Count Franz von Walsegg, who had commissioned it — anonymously, through intermediaries — to be performed as if it were his own composition at a memorial service for his recently deceased wife.

History

The mysterious commission, delivered by an unidentified messenger, inspired romantic legends that Mozart, in failing health, believed he was writing a requiem for himself — a story dramatised in the play and film Amadeus. Mozart completed the Introitus and drafted much of the Kyrie, Sequence (through the Lacrimosa, which breaks off after eight bars), and Offertorium. His widow Constanze had the work completed — first approaching Joseph Eybler, then Süssmayr — so that she could collect the commission fee.

Structure

The Süssmayr completion divides the work into: I. Introitus (Requiem aeternam, Kyrie); II. Sequentia (Dies irae, Tuba mirum, Rex tremendae, Recordare, Confutatis, Lacrimosa); III. Offertorium (Domine Jesu, Hostias); IV. Sanctus; V. Benedictus; VI. Agnus Dei; VII. Communio (Lux aeterna). Alternative completions by modern scholars (including Robert Levin and Richard Maunder) have sought to revise Süssmayr's work.
