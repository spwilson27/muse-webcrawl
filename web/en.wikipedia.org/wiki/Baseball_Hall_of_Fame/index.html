National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum

The National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum is a history museum and hall of fame in Cooperstown, New York, operated by private interests. It serves as the central point of the history of baseball in the United States and displays baseball-related artefacts and exhibits, honouring those who have excelled in playing, managing, and serving the sport.

Established1936 (dedicated 1939)
LocationCooperstown, New York, U.S.
InducteesOver 340 (players, managers, executives, umpires)

History

The Hall was established in 1936, seating its legend partly on the now-discredited story that Abner Doubleday invented baseball in Cooperstown in 1839. The first class — Ty Cobb, Babe Ruth, Honus Wagner, Christy Mathewson, and Walter Johnson — was inducted at the museum's dedication in June 1939. The collection includes iconic artefacts such as Ruth's bat and the ball from famous games, plus a major research library.

Election

Players become eligible five years after retirement and are voted on by the Baseball Writers' Association of America, needing 75% of ballots for election; era committees consider older players, managers, umpires, and executives. Negro leagues stars were long excluded; Satchel Paige's 1971 induction began recognition later greatly expanded by a special 2006 election. Controversies include the exclusion of Pete Rose for gambling and of steroid-era stars such as Barry Bonds and Roger Clemens. See also Major League Baseball.
