List of English flags

This is a list of flags used in England at national, regional, county and civic levels, past and present. The national flag of England is the Cross of Saint George, a red cross on a white field, adopted in the later Middle Ages and also forming the core of the British Union Jack.

National and royal flags

FlagDescription
Flag of England (Cross of Saint George)A red (gules) cross on a white (argent) field. Associated with England since the 13th–14th centuries; flown widely at sporting events and from public buildings.
Royal Standard (as used in England)Quartered banner of arms: the three lions passant of England in the first and fourth quarters, the lion of Scotland in the second and the harp of Ireland in the third.
Royal Standard of England (historical)The banner of the royal arms of England — three gold lions passant guardant on a red field.

Ensigns and other official flags

FlagDescription
Red Ensign (civil ensign)Red field with the Union Jack in the canton; flown by British merchant ships, not England-specific but widely seen in English ports.
Flag of the City of LondonThe Cross of Saint George with the red sword of Saint Paul in the upper hoist canton.
Flag of the Church of EnglandThe Cross of Saint George with a different episcopal shield in the canton, varying by diocese.

Regional and county flags

Many English counties have registered flags, most adopted since 2000 through competitions run with the Flag Institute; they draw on historic arms, colours and symbols.

County / regionDescription
Cornwall (Saint Piran's Flag)A white cross on a black field; associated with Saint Piran and in use since the 19th century.
Devon (Saint Petroc's Cross)A white cross edged black on a green field; adopted in 2003.
NorthumberlandRed and yellow ("gold") squares based on the historic arms and Saint Oswald's banner.
YorkshireA white rose on a blue field, registered in 2008; the white rose is a far older county emblem.
LancashireA red rose on a gold field, registered in 2008.
EssexThree white seaxes (notched swords) and crowns on a red field, from the county arms.
KentThe white horse of Kent ("Invicta") rampant on a red field.
LincolnshireRed and blue quarters divided by a yellow cross over green, adopted in 2005.
NorfolkBlack and gold vertical bands with an ermine bend bearing royal arms details, registered in 2014.
East Anglia (Saint Edmund's flag)A blue cross edged white on red with three gold crowns, associated with Saint Edmund.

Historical flags

FlagDescription
Flag of Saint EdmundThree gold crowns on a blue field, linked with the Anglo-Saxon king and martyr Edmund.
Flag of Saint Edward the ConfessorA gold cross flory between five martlets (birds) on a blue field.
Tudor rose bannersStandards bearing the red-and-white Tudor rose, used in pageantry since the late 15th century.
Flags of the Commonwealth (1649–1660)Designs combining the crosses of Saint George and Saint Andrew, and the Irish harp.
