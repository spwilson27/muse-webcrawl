Pitch (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, pitch is the rotation of an aircraft about its lateral (side-to-side) axis, which runs from wingtip to wingtip through the centre of gravity. Pitching raises or lowers the nose of the aircraft. It is one of the three principal axes of rotation, together with roll about the longitudinal axis and yaw about the vertical axis.

Control

Pitch is controlled primarily by the elevator, a movable surface on the horizontal tail, which the pilot moves with the control column or stick. Pushing forward lowers the nose; pulling back raises it. Some aircraft use an all-flying tailplane (stabilator) instead. Pitch trim systems allow the pilot to relieve sustained control forces.

Aerodynamics

Changing pitch changes the angle of attack of the wings, which changes lift. Excessive pitch can lead to a stall. The natural longitudinal stability of most aircraft tends to restore a trimmed pitch attitude, governed by the position of the centre of gravity relative to the aerodynamic centre and the pitching moment generated by the tail.

Measurement

Pitch attitude is displayed on the attitude indicator (artificial horizon) and is measured in degrees nose-up or nose-down relative to the horizon.
