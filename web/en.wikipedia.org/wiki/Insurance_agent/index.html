Insurance agent

An insurance agent is a person who sells, solicits or negotiates insurance policies on behalf of an insurance company. Insurance agents are licensed by the jurisdiction in which they operate and are generally classified as either captive agents, who represent a single insurer, or independent agents, who may place business with several insurers. They are distinct from insurance brokers, who in many legal systems represent the policyholder rather than the insurer, though in everyday usage the terms are often conflated.

Roles and duties

Agents advise clients on their insurance needs, quote and compare coverage, complete applications, collect premiums and assist with renewals and claims. Because agents act on behalf of insurers, they typically have authority to bind coverage according to the terms of their agency agreement. Their compensation usually consists of commissions paid by the insurer — a percentage of the premium, higher in the first year for life insurance and smaller renewal commissions thereafter — and sometimes contingent commissions based on the volume and profitability of the business they place.

Licensing and regulation

In the United States, insurance agents are licensed by state insurance departments; applicants usually must complete pre-licensing education, pass an examination for each line of authority (such as life, health, or property and casualty), and meet continuing education requirements. In the United Kingdom, insurance intermediaries are regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority, and in India by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority. Agents owe duties of care to clients and insurers and can face discipline, fines or licence revocation for misrepresentation, mishandling of premiums, or selling unsuitable products.

Types

Common specialisations include life and health agents, property and casualty agents, and commercial lines agents serving businesses. The industry has changed with direct-to-consumer online sales, comparison websites and call-centre distribution, but agents remain important for complex products such as commercial insurance, annuities and estate-planning-related life insurance, where personal advice is valued.
