Isabella II (Spanish: Isabel II; 10 October 1830 – 9 April 1904) was Queen of Spain from 1833 until her deposition in 1868. The only queen regnant in modern Spanish history, she came to the throne as a child of two, and her reign was dominated by Carlist wars, military interventions in politics, and chronic instability that ended with the Glorious Revolution of 1868, which drove her into exile.

Accession and regency

Isabella succeeded her father Ferdinand VII in 1833. Ferdinand's decision to set aside the Salic law in favour of his daughter (the Pragmatic Sanction of 1830) was rejected by his brother Don Carlos, whose supporters — the Carlists — launched the First Carlist War (1833–1840). Isabella's mother, Maria Christina, governed as regent until 1840, followed by the progressive general Baldomero Espartero (1841–1843). In 1843, aged thirteen, Isabella was declared of age and began her personal reign.

Reign

Isabella's reign saw frequent changes of government, dominated by the moderate (moderado) and progressive (progresista) factions of liberalism and by powerful generals such as Narváez, Espartero and O'Donnell, who repeatedly intervened through pronunciamientos. Spain fought the Second Carlist War (1846–1849), the Hispano-Moroccan War (1859–1860) and intervened in Mexico in 1861. In 1846 the sixteen-year-old queen was married to her double first cousin Francis, Duke of Cádiz — an unhappy union surrounded by court intrigue and rumours about the paternity of her children, including the future Alfonso XII.

From the 1850s Isabella's unpopularity grew: she was perceived as arbitrary, favouring the moderados, surrounding herself with a religious camarilla (including the mystic Sor Patrocinio), and obstructing reform. The progressive revolution of 1854 brought Espartero back briefly, but by the 1860s opposition united monarchists of the rival line, democrats and republicans against her.

Deposition and exile

The Glorious Revolution of September 1868, led by Generals Prim and Serrano and Admiral Topete, forced Isabella to flee to France. In 1870 she abdicated in Paris in favour of her son Alfonso, who was restored as Alfonso XII in 1874 after the brief First Spanish Republic. Isabella lived in Paris at the Palacio Castilla until her death in 1904; she is buried at El Escorial.

Assessment

Historians debate Isabella's personal responsibility for the failures of her reign: she was poorly educated, manipulated by factions, and constrained by the political culture of the pronunciamiento, yet her conduct of the monarchy helped discredit the Bourbon dynasty and shaped the instability of Spanish politics into the twentieth century.
