Hairstyle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hairstyles are among the fastest-changing signals of fashion and identity.

A hairstyle, hairdo, haircut, or coiffure refers to the styling of hair, usually on the human head. Hairstyling is an aspect of personal grooming, fashion, and cosmetics, and carries strong social meaning: hair is widely read as a statement of identity, status, profession, religion, and belonging. Styles are achieved by cutting, arranging, and treating hair, and increasingly by colouring, extensions, and wigs.

History

Hairstyling is as old as recorded culture: ancient Egyptian elites shaved their heads and wore elaborate wigs; Roman styles tracked imperial fashion; in many African societies, intricate braiding encoded age, kinship, and wealth. The powdered coiffures of the eighteenth-century European court gave way to simpler styles after the French Revolution. The twentieth century saw rapid, media-driven change: the 1920s bob, wartime rolls, the 1950s pompadour and beehive, 1960s mops and Afros, punk spikes and mohawks in the 1970s, and the voluminous styles of the 1980s. Regulated hair remains common in militaries, schools, and some professions, making hair a recurring site of social conflict.

Elements and techniques

Stylists combine cutting (including layering, texturising, clipper work), setting techniques (rollers, braiding, twisting, blow-drying, flat-ironing), chemical treatments (permanent waves, relaxers, keratin treatments), colour (dyes, highlights, bleach), and additions (weaves, extensions, wigs, hairpieces). Tools range from scissors and razors to clippers, irons, and dryers; products include pomades, gels, mousses, sprays, and oils.

Cultural and religious dimensions

Many religions prescribe hair practices: the Sikh kesh (uncut hair under a turban), the Jewish peyot, Buddhist monastic shaving, the tonsure of Christian monks, and Islamic rules on grooming. Natural-hair movements have contested workplace and school rules that penalise Afro-textured styles — debates that produced legislation such as the CROWN Act in several US states, prohibiting discrimination based on hair texture and protective styles such as braids, twists, and locs.

Industry

Hairdressing is a major service industry worldwide, delivered in salons, barbershops, and at home; the global hair-care market (products and services) is valued in the tens of billions of dollars annually.
