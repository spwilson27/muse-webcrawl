The Baháʼí Faith is a monotheistic religion founded by Baháʼu'lláh in 19th-century Persia, teaching the essential worth of all religions and the unity of all humanity. Baháʼís believe God progressively reveals His will through Manifestations — including Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad, the Buddha and Baháʼu'lláh — and that humanity is reaching maturity, called to abolish extremes of wealth and poverty, establish world peace, universal education and equality of the sexes.

History

The faith grew from the Bábí movement: in 1844 the Báb declared himself a herald of 'Him whom God shall make manifest', identified by Baháʼís as Baháʼu'lláh, who announced his own mission in Baghdad in 1863. Persecuted in Persia and exiled by the Ottomans to Acre, Baháʼu'lláh wrote the Kitáb-i-Aqdas (Book of Laws) and letters to the world's rulers. His son ʻAbdu'l-Bahá led the faith's spread to the West; the administrative order — elected local and national Spiritual Assemblies and the Universal House of Justice in Haifa — has no clergy. Baháʼís in Iran remain subject to severe persecution.
