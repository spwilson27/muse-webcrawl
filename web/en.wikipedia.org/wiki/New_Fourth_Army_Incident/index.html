New Fourth Army Incident

The New Fourth Army Incident (皖南事变), also known as the Southern Anhui Incident, occurred in January 1941 during the Second Sino-Japanese War. It marked the effective end of the Second United Front between the Kuomintang and the Chinese Communist Party.

The incident

In late 1940, Chiang Kai-shek ordered the Communist-led New Fourth Army to vacate its positions south of the Yangtze River and move north. As a headquarters column of about 9,000 troops under Ye Ting and Xiang Ying marched through Maolin in southern Anhui, it was ambushed on 5–7 January 1941 by some 80,000 Nationalist troops. Most of the column was destroyed; Xiang Ying was killed, and Ye Ting was captured while negotiating. Chiang then announced the dissolution of the New Fourth Army.

Aftermath

The incident provoked outrage and international criticism. Zhou Enlai famously protested in a newspaper inscription lamenting the tragedy of "kin killing kin." The Communists ignored the dissolution order and rebuilt the New Fourth Army under Chen Yi and Liu Shaoqi, with headquarters in northern Jiangsu. Although open civil war was postponed while the war against Japan continued, mutual trust between the two parties never recovered.
