The Klarälven ("the clear river" in Swedish; Norwegian: Trysilelva in its upper course) is a river flowing through Norway and Sweden. Together with its continuation, the Göta älv, which drains Lake Vänern to the Kattegat, it forms the longest river system in Scandinavia, at about 720 km in total length.

Course

The river rises at Lake Rogen in Härjedalen, Sweden, flows into Norway where it is called the Trysilelva, and then re-enters Sweden in northern Värmland. There it becomes the Klarälven proper, a famously meandering river whose shifting sandbanks and oxbow lakes make it a classic example in geomorphology textbooks. It passes the towns of Ekshärad, Forshaga, and Karlstad before emptying into Lake Vänern through several delta channels near Karlstad.

Ecology and use

The river supports a well-known population of landlocked Atlantic salmon and brown trout migrating between Vänern and spawning grounds upstream. Historically it was a major timber-floating route for the Värmland sawmill and paper industries; timber floating ended in 1991. Today the river is used for hydroelectric power (with multiple stations along its course), recreation, rafting, and fishing tourism, while its meander belt is protected in several nature reserves for its bird and plant life.
