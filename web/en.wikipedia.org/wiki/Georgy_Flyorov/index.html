Georgy Flyorov

Georgy Nikolayevich Flyorov (also transliterated Flerov; 2 March 1913 – 19 November 1990) was a Soviet physicist known for his discovery, with Konstantin Petrzhak, of spontaneous fission, and for founding the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research laboratory in Dubna that now bears his name and became a leading center for the synthesis of superheavy elements.

Early work and spontaneous fission

Flyorov studied at the Leningrad Polytechnic Institute and worked under Igor Kurchatov at the Ioffe Physical-Technical Institute. In 1940, working with Petrzhak at the Radium Institute and using a sensitive ionization chamber in a deep underground station of the Moscow Metro to escape cosmic-ray background, he detected that uranium-238 nuclei occasionally split apart with no external trigger — the phenomenon of spontaneous fission, announced the same year.

The 1942 letter and the Soviet bomb project

Serving at the front after the German invasion, Flyorov noticed that Western journals had suddenly stopped publishing on nuclear fission and inferred a secret weapons program. In April 1942 he wrote directly to Joseph Stalin urging that the USSR begin work on an atomic bomb "without delay". The letter is credited with helping prompt the organization of the Soviet atomic project under Kurchatov, in which Flyorov participated.

Dubna and superheavy elements

In 1957 Flyorov founded the Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna, which he directed for three decades. There his group pioneered the synthesis of transfermium elements, reporting the creation of elements 102 through 107 and engaging in famous priority disputes with the Berkeley laboratory in California — part of the so-called Transfermium Wars. Element 114 was named flerovium (Fl) in his honour in 2012, and his laboratory continues as the Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions, a leader in superheavy-element research.
