Zulu (isiZulu) is a Southern Bantu language of the Nguni branch, spoken by the Zulu people chiefly in the KwaZulu-Natal province of South Africa. It is the most widely spoken home language in South Africa, the first language of roughly a quarter of the population, and is understood by over half of South Africans; it is one of the country's official languages. Zulu is closely related to Xhosa, Swazi (siSwati) and Ndebele, with which it shares considerable mutual intelligibility. The standard greeting is Sawubona ("I see you").

Phonology

Zulu is a tonal language with two phonemic tones (high and low), and it is famous for its click consonants, borrowed from neighbouring Khoisan languages. Three basic click types are written in the Latin orthography: dental clicks with c, alveolar clicks with q, and lateral clicks with x; each occurs in several variants (voiced, aspirated, nasalised, and so on). Zulu syllables are generally open (ending in a vowel), and the language is agglutinative.

Grammar

Like other Bantu languages, Zulu sorts nouns into classes (about fifteen in traditional analyses), each marked by a prefix — for example umu-/aba- for persons (umuntu "person", abantu "people"). Verbs and adjectives must agree with the noun class of their subject (and object) by means of concord prefixes. Word order is basically subject–verb–object. The first published grammar and dictionary of Zulu were produced in the mid-19th century, notably by the Norwegian missionary Hans Schreuder and by Bishop John William Colenso; the first Zulu-language newspaper, Ilanga lase Natal, was founded in 1903 by John Langalibalele Dube.

Literature and media

Zulu has a rich oral tradition of praise poetry (izibongo) and a growing written literature; writers such as Benedict Wallet Vilakazi, the first black South African to receive a doctorate, helped establish modern Zulu literature. The SABC radio station Ukhozi FM, broadcasting in Zulu, is among the largest radio stations in Africa by audience, and Zulu-language television dramas are widely popular.
