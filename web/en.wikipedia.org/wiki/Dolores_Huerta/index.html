Dolores Huerta

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dolores Clara Fernández Huerta (born April 10, 1930) is an American labor leader and civil rights activist. With César Chávez, she co-founded the National Farm Workers Association, which later became the United Farm Workers (UFW). She was the union's chief negotiator and a central organiser of the Delano grape strike and the nationwide grape boycott.

Early life

Huerta was born in Dawson, New Mexico, and raised in Stockton, California, where her mother ran a hotel that often sheltered farmworker families. She worked briefly as a schoolteacher before turning to community organising with the Community Service Organization in the 1950s.

Farm worker movement

In 1962 Huerta and Chávez founded the National Farm Workers Association. She lobbied for laws allowing farmworkers to vote in Spanish and obtain driver's licences, negotiated contracts with growers, and directed the UFW's consumer boycotts. She coined the movement's slogan "Sí, se puede" ("Yes, we can") in 1972. In 1975 she helped secure California's Agricultural Labor Relations Act, the first law recognising farmworkers' collective bargaining rights in the continental United States.

Later work and honours

Huerta founded the Dolores Huerta Foundation in 2002 to train community organisers. She received the Eleanor Roosevelt Award for Human Rights in 1998 and the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2012. The 2017 documentary Dolores chronicled her life.
