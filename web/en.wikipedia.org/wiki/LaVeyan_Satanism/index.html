LaVeyan Satanism

LaVeyan Satanism is the form of Satanism founded in 1966 by the American occultist and author Anton Szandor LaVey and organised through his Church of Satan. It is an atheistic religion: adherents do not believe in a literal Satan or other deities, instead treating Satan as a symbol of pride, liberty, individualism, and earthly values, and the practising Satanist as their own god. Its core texts are LaVey's The Satanic Bible (1969), The Satanic Rituals (1972), and his later essay collections.


Anton Szandor LaVey (1930–1997)

Beliefs and principles

The Satanic Bible sets out the Nine Satanic Statements, which endorse indulgence instead of abstinence, vital existence instead of spiritual pipe dreams, and kindness to those who deserve it instead of love wasted on ingrates, while rejecting the notion of turning the other cheek. LaVey's "Eleven Satanic Rules of the Earth" and the list of "Nine Satanic Sins" (including stupidity, pretentiousness, herd conformity, and lack of perspective) supplement the statements. The philosophy draws on Epicureanism, Ayn Rand's objectivism (LaVey acknowledged a debt to Atlas Shrugged), Nietzsche, and social Darwinist literature such as Ragnar Redbeard's Might Is Right, combined with ceremonial magic used as psychodrama rather than supernatural ritual.

History and organisation

LaVey founded the Church of Satan in San Francisco on Walpurgisnacht, 30 April 1966 (designated Year One, Anno Satanas). The church attracted celebrity and media attention in the late 1960s, then spun off splinter groups, notably the Temple of Set (1975). After LaVey's death in 1997 the priesthood passed to Peter H. Gilmore, who became High Priest in 2001. LaVeyan Satanism is distinguished from theistic Satanism and from the later activist Satanic Temple, which is a separate organisation with different emphases.
