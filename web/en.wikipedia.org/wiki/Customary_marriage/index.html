Customary marriage


A customary wedding celebration (placeholder image)

A customary marriage is a marriage entered into in accordance with the customs and traditions of a community, rather than (or in addition to) the civil registration procedures of the state. Such marriages are legally recognized in many jurisdictions, especially in Africa and parts of Asia, where customary law operates alongside statutory and religious law in a plural legal system.

Characteristics

Customary marriage is typically a union negotiated between families rather than merely between two individuals. Common features include the payment of bridewealth (such as lobola in southern Africa), a series of ceremonies and feasts, and community witnessing that gives the union social legitimacy. Many customary systems permit polygyny, and the marriage may acquire legal consequences — for property, inheritance and the status of children — without any civil ceremony.

Legal recognition

South Africa's Recognition of Customary Marriages Act 120 of 1998 gives customary marriages full legal status equal to civil marriages and provides for their registration. Similar recognition exists in Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria and Zimbabwe, often alongside rules reconciling custom with constitutional guarantees of equality. Recognition disputes frequently concern proof of the customary rituals, consent, and women's property rights on divorce or widowhood.
