Hardwood flooring

Hardwood flooring is a type of wood flooring made from timber that is harvested from hardwood trees such as oak, maple, walnut, cherry, and hickory. It is widely used in residential and commercial construction for its durability, appearance, and ability to be refinished.

Types

Hardwood flooring is generally divided into two categories:

  • Solid hardwood flooring — milled from a single piece of timber, typically 18–20 mm thick, and can be sanded and refinished many times.
  • Engineered wood flooring — composed of a top veneer of real hardwood bonded over layers of plywood or high-density fibreboard. It is more dimensionally stable and better suited to installation over concrete or below grade.

Installation

Solid hardwood is traditionally installed by nailing or stapling boards to a wood subfloor, with the boards running perpendicular to the floor joists. Engineered products may be glued down, nailed, or installed as a floating floor in which planks lock together without fasteners. Acclimation of the wood to the interior environment before installation is important to minimise expansion and contraction.

Finishes

Flooring may be finished on site or prefinished in the factory. Common finishes include polyurethane, oil-based finishes, and aluminium oxide coatings, the last of which is common on prefinished products and is exceptionally wear resistant. Site-finished floors allow for custom staining but require curing time before use.

Maintenance

Hardwood floors are cleaned by sweeping or vacuuming and occasional damp mopping. Because solid hardwood can be sanded, scratches and wear can be removed by refinishing, allowing a floor to last for many decades.
