Hydraulic machinery

Hydraulic machinery is machinery and tools that use liquid fluid power to do work. Heavy equipment is a common example; in this type of machine, hydraulic fluid is pumped to various hydraulic motors and hydraulic cylinders throughout the machine and becomes pressurised according to the resistance present. The fluid is controlled directly or automatically by control valves and distributed through hoses, tubes and pipes.

Force multiplication

The popularity of hydraulic machinery results from the very large amount of power that can be transferred through small tubes and flexible hoses, the high power density, and the wide range of actuators that can make use of this power. Hydraulic machinery operates on the principles of fluid mechanics summarised by Pascal's law: a small force applied to a small piston produces a pressure that acts on a larger piston to give a proportionally larger force.

Applications

Hydraulics power construction equipment (excavators, loaders, cranes), agricultural machinery, hydraulic presses, injection-moulding machines, aircraft flight controls and landing gear, ship steering and deck machinery, and vehicle braking and steering systems. See the hydraulic drive system article for the structure of a typical system.

History

Joseph Bramah patented the hydraulic press in 1795, and William Armstrong developed hydraulic cranes and accumulators in the nineteenth century, when networks of pressurised water mains supplied power in cities such as London. Oil hydraulics largely replaced water hydraulics in the twentieth century.
