Katherine Group

The Katherine Group is a group of five Middle English prose texts written in the early 13th century (c. 1190–1230), comprising saints' lives of Katherine of Alexandria, Margaret of Antioch and Juliana of Nicomedia, together with the treatises Hali Meiðhad ("Holy Virginity") and Sawles Warde ("The Guardian of the Soul").

Language and manuscripts

The texts are written in the distinctive "AB language", a highly standardised literary dialect of the West Midlands shared with the Ancrene Wisse — so called from their common scribal features in manuscripts Bodley 34 (A) and Royal 8 F.IV (B). The lives adapt Latin sources for an audience of devout women, often anchoresses.

Contents

Seinte Katerine, Seinte Margarete and Seinte Juliane retell the tortures and martyrdoms of three virgin saints. Hali Meiðhad is a letter praising virginity and denouncing earthly marriage, while Sawles Warde, adapted from a Latin work attributed to Hugh of St Victor, allegorises the soul as a household beset by the vices and defended by the virtues.
