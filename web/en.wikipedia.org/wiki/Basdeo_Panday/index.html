Basdeo Panday

Basdeo Panday (25 May 1933 – 1 January 2024) was a Trinidadian and Tobagonian lawyer, trade unionist and politician who served as the fifth Prime Minister of Trinidad and Tobago from 1995 to 2001. He was the first person of Indian descent and the first Hindu to hold the office.

Early life and union career

Born in Princes Town to a poor family of Indian indentured labourers' descendants, Panday worked in the sugar cane fields and as a primary school teacher before going to London, where he studied law at Lincoln's Inn and took degrees in law, economics and political science, while also acting in small film roles. On returning home he rose through the sugar workers' union, becoming a leading figure in the labour movement and president general of the All Trinidad Sugar and General Workers' Trade Union.

Politics

Panday entered Parliament in 1976 and co-founded the United National Congress (UNC) in 1989. Winning the 1995 general election in coalition, he became prime minister, steering the economy through energy-led growth. His government lost power in 2001 after a tied election and internal splits. He later faced corruption charges related to an airport project, of which he was ultimately cleared, and retired from politics in 2010.
