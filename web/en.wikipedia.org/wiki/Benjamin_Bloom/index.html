Benjamin Bloom

Benjamin Samuel Bloom (February 21, 1913 – September 13, 1999) was an American educational psychologist who made influential contributions to the classification of educational objectives and to the theory of mastery learning.

Bloom's taxonomy

In 1956 Bloom chaired the committee that published the Taxonomy of Educational Objectives, classifying learning goals in the cognitive domain from lower-order skills (knowledge, comprehension, application) to higher-order ones (analysis, synthesis, evaluation). Widely known as Bloom's taxonomy, the framework — later revised by his student Lorin Anderson and others — remains a standard tool in curriculum design and assessment.

Mastery learning and the 2 sigma problem

Bloom argued that nearly all students can master a subject given sufficient time and appropriate instruction, an approach developed as mastery learning. His 1984 paper on the "2 sigma problem" reported that one-to-one tutored students performed about two standard deviations above conventionally taught students, and challenged researchers to find group-teaching methods nearly as effective.

Career

Bloom spent most of his career at the University of Chicago, where he served as an examinations director and professor, and conducted influential studies of talent development in young people.
