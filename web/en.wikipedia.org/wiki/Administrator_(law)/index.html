In law, an administrator is a person appointed by a court to manage and settle an estate or an organisation in circumstances where no one has been validly appointed in advance. The term is used chiefly in two contexts: the administration of a deceased person's estate, and the administration of an insolvent company.

[Image: Letters of administration issued by a probate court]

Administration of estates

When a person dies intestate (without a valid will), or when the executors named in a will cannot or will not act, the probate court issues letters of administration granting an administrator authority over the estate. The administrator — historically called an administratrix if female — collects the deceased's assets, pays debts and taxes, and distributes the remainder to the heirs according to the rules of intestacy. Priority for appointment usually follows the closeness of kinship, beginning with the surviving spouse. The administrator differs from an executor, who is nominated by the deceased in the will itself and receives a grant of probate rather than letters of administration. Special forms include the administrator de bonis non (appointed to finish an administration left incomplete) and the administrator pendente lite (appointed during litigation over the estate).

Administration of companies

In the insolvency law of the United Kingdom, Australia and other jurisdictions, administration is a rescue procedure in which an insolvency practitioner, the administrator, takes control of a financially distressed company, displacing its directors, while a statutory moratorium shields the company from creditors' claims. The administrator must aim first to rescue the company as a going concern, or failing that to achieve a better result for creditors than liquidation, or to realise property for secured and preferential creditors.
