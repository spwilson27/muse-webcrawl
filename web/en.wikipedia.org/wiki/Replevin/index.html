Replevin

Replevin is a legal action by which a person may recover possession of goods that have been wrongfully taken or detained, pending a court's final determination of the rights of the parties. It originated in English common law as a remedy against wrongful distress, where a landlord had seized a tenant's goods for unpaid rent.

History

At common law, replevin allowed a person whose goods had been distrained to have them restored upon giving security (a replevin bond) to pursue the action and to return the goods if judgment went against them. Over time the action expanded to cover goods wrongfully taken or withheld in many circumstances, not only distress.

Modern practice

In many jurisdictions of the United States, replevin statutes permit a plaintiff to obtain prejudgment possession of specific personal property — for example, a vehicle or equipment subject to a security interest — upon posting bond and showing a probable right to possession. Because seizure before judgment raises due process concerns, statutes typically require notice and a hearing, a requirement shaped by US Supreme Court decisions such as Fuentes v. Shevin (1972). In other jurisdictions the equivalent action is known as "claim and delivery".

Distinction from other remedies

Unlike detinue or trover, which historically yielded damages, replevin seeks return of the specific goods themselves. It differs from ejectment, which concerns real property, and from self-help repossession, which is regulated separately and must generally be accomplished without breaching the peace.
