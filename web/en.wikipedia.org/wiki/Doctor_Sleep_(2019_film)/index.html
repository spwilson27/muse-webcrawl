Doctor Sleep is a 2019 American supernatural horror film written and directed by Mike Flanagan, based on Stephen King's 2013 novel of the same name, itself a sequel to King's 1977 novel The Shining. The film is also a direct sequel to Stanley Kubrick's 1980 film adaptation — a notable reconciliation, since King's novel sequels the book, while Flanagan's screenplay adapts King's novel while positioning the story as continuing Kubrick's film continuity, famously disliked by King. Ewan McGregor stars as the adult Danny Torrance, with Rebecca Ferguson as the villain Rose the Hat and Kyliegh Curran in her film debut as Abra Stone.

Plot

Decades after the events at the Overlook Hotel, Dan Torrance is a traumatised alcoholic drifting across America, numbing his psychic gift — the "shining" — with drink. He settles in Frazier, New Hampshire, joins Alcoholics Anonymous, and works as a hospice orderly, using his gift to comfort dying patients, who call him "Doctor Sleep." Meanwhile the True Knot, a cult of quasi-immortal beings led by Rose the Hat, sustain themselves by feeding on "steam," the psychic essence released by children with the shining whom they torture and kill. Teenager Abra Stone, whose shining is far stronger than Dan's, alerts him to the cult's murders; together they confront the True Knot, culminating in a return to the now-derelict Overlook Hotel, where Dan releases the hotel's starving ghosts on Rose and faces the echoes of his father.

Production

Flanagan, who had directed King's Gerald's Game (2017), recreated the Overlook's sets from Kubrick's blueprints on soundstages in Georgia and cast actors resembling the 1980 originals — including Carl Lumbly as Dick Hallorann and Alex Essoe as Wendy Torrance — rather than using digital de-ageing. King, who had long criticised Kubrick's film, approved Flanagan's approach to bridging the two versions. Warner Bros. released the film on 8 November 2019 in the United States (timed ahead of Halloween in some markets).

Reception

The film received generally positive reviews: critics praised McGregor's and Ferguson's performances, Flanagan's direction and the ambitious reconciliation of the King and Kubrick continuities, while some found the 152-minute runtime slow. It nevertheless disappointed at the box office, grossing about $72 million against expectations inflated by the success of It, leading Warner Bros. to shelve a planned prequel. A three-hour director's cut, released on home media in 2020, was widely considered an improvement and restored the film's reputation.
