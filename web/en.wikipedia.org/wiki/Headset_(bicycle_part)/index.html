Headset (bicycle part)

The headset is the set of bearings on a bicycle that provides a rotatable interface between the bicycle frame and the front fork steerer tube. It allows the rider to steer the front wheel while supporting loads between the fork and the frame's head tube.

Designs

Traditional threaded headsets use cups pressed into the head tube and locknuts on a threaded steerer; threadless headsets, introduced with mountain bikes in the 1990s, use a star nut or compression plug and are clamped by the stem. Integrated and internal headsets place cartridge bearings directly in or within the head tube.

Sizes and maintenance

Common steerer standards include 1 inch, 1⅛ inch, and tapered steerers combining 1⅛ inch at the top with 1½ inch at the bottom. Headsets require correct adjustment: too loose causes play and damage to the head tube, too tight makes steering stiff and wears bearings prematurely.
