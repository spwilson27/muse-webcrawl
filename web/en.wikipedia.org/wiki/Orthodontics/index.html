Orthodontics

Fixed appliances ("braces") are the most common orthodontic treatment.

Orthodontics is a specialty of dentistry that deals with the diagnosis, prevention, and correction of malpositioned teeth and jaws and misaligned bite patterns, known as malocclusion. It may also focus on modifying facial growth, in which case it is called dentofacial orthopedics. Orthodontic treatment uses appliances that apply gentle, sustained pressure to move teeth through the remodelling of alveolar bone, and it is undertaken for functional reasons (chewing, speech, oral hygiene) and for aesthetics.

History

Attempts to straighten teeth date to antiquity, but the specialty's modern founder is Edward Angle (1855–1930), who classified malocclusions (Angle's classes I, II, and III), founded the first school of orthodontics in 1900, and developed the edgewise appliance, the ancestor of contemporary brackets and archwires. In the 1970s direct bonding of brackets to enamel replaced cemented bands, and later decades brought nickel-titanium wires, self-ligating brackets, and clear aligner systems such as Invisalign (introduced in 1999).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines clinical examination with dental casts or digital scans, photographs, and radiographs including panoramic films and lateral cephalograms, from which cephalometric analyses trace skeletal and dental relationships. Treatment options include:

  • Fixed braces — brackets bonded to the teeth and connected by archwires, adjusted periodically.
  • Clear aligners — series of removable transparent trays changed every one to two weeks.
  • Removable and functional appliances — used especially in growing children to guide jaw development.
  • Headgear and temporary anchorage devices (mini-screws) — to provide anchorage for tooth movement.
  • Extractions or interproximal reduction — to relieve crowding, and orthognathic surgery for severe jaw discrepancies.

Typical comprehensive treatment lasts 18–30 months, after which retainers are worn to hold the teeth in their new positions while the supporting tissues stabilise.

Training

Orthodontists are dentists who complete two to three additional years of accredited specialty training. In many countries orthodontics was the first recognised dental specialty, and its practitioners' professional bodies include the American Association of Orthodontists (founded 1900).
