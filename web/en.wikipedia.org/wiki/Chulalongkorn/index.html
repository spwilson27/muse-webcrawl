Chulalongkorn (September 20, 1853 – October 23, 1910), also known as King Rama V, was the fifth monarch of the Chakri dynasty of Siam (now Thailand). His 42-year reign is remembered as the era in which Siam modernised its administration, economy and society while preserving its independence between British Burma and French Indochina — making Thailand the only Southeast Asian state never colonised.

Reforms

Educated in part by Anna Leonowens (later fictionalised in The King and I), Chulalongkorn travelled to British India and Europe as a young king. His reforms transformed Siam: the gradual abolition of slavery and corvée labour (completed in 1905), a centralised system of ministries and provinces (the monthon system), a modern civil service and judiciary, railways, telegraphs, a national currency reform and the beginnings of public education and sanitation.

Foreign relations

To keep independence Chulalongkorn ceded peripheral territories — Laos and Cambodia's claims to France, and Malay sultanates to Britain — while cultivating European royal courts on his tours of 1897 and 1907. The Franco-Siamese crisis of 1893, when French gunboats forced the Chao Phraya, marked the gravest threat of his reign.

Legacy

Chulalongkorn is revered in Thailand as a "Great Beloved King"; his death day, October 23, is a national holiday, and Bangkok's Chulalongkorn University bears his name. His equestrian statue stands before the Ananta Samakhom Throne Hall.
