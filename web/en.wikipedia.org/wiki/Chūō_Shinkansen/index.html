The Chūō Shinkansen (中央新幹線, Central Shinkansen) is a Japanese maglev railway line under construction between Tokyo and Nagoya, with plans to extend to Osaka. Built by Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central) using superconducting magnetic levitation (SCMaglev) technology, it is designed for a maximum operating speed of 500 km/h, which would cut the Tokyo–Nagoya journey to about 40 minutes and Tokyo–Osaka to about 67 minutes. The total cost of the full Tokyo–Osaka line has been estimated at around ¥9 trillion.

Route

The first phase runs from Shinagawa Station in Tokyo to Nagoya, a distance of about 286 km, of which roughly 86% is in tunnels, including deep tunnels beneath the Southern Japanese Alps. Intermediate stations are planned in Kanagawa, Yamanashi, Nagano, and Gifu prefectures. The Yamanashi Maglev Test Line, built from 1997 and extended to 42.8 km, forms part of the route; in 2015 a manned seven-car L0 Series train on the test line reached 603 km/h, a world speed record for rail vehicles.

History

Development of superconducting maglev began under Japanese National Railways in the 1960s–70s. After decades of testing, the Japanese government approved construction in 2011 and JR Central began work in 2014, largely self-funding the project. The opening of the Shinagawa–Nagoya section was originally targeted for 2027, but disputes with Shizuoka Prefecture over water flow from tunnelling in the Ōi River basin delayed construction there, and JR Central has acknowledged the 2027 opening is impossible. The Nagoya–Osaka section is planned for completion in the 2030s–2040s, accelerated by government loans.
