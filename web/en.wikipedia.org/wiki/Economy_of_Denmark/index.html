Economy of Denmark

The economy of Denmark is a developed mixed economy with a high standard of living, extensive government welfare measures and a strong dependence on foreign trade. Denmark is a member of the European Union but has retained its own currency, the krone (DKK), which is pegged to the euro through the ERM II mechanism. Nominal GDP is roughly US$400 billion, and GDP per capita is among the highest in the world.

Sectors

Services dominate output and employment, while agriculture, though small in share, is highly productive: Denmark is a major exporter of pork, dairy products and other foods. Industry is led by pharmaceuticals — Novo Nordisk, maker of insulin and GLP-1 medicines such as Ozempic, became one of Europe's most valuable companies — as well as shipping (Maersk, one of the world's largest container lines), wind turbines (Vestas and Ørsted), food processing machinery, design goods and Lego toys. North Sea oil and gas production, begun in the 1970s, made Denmark self-sufficient in energy for a period, but output has declined, and the country pursues an ambitious green transition based on wind power.

Labour market

Denmark is known for its "flexicurity" model, combining flexible hiring and firing rules with generous unemployment benefits and active labour-market policies delivered largely through collective agreements between trade unions and employers rather than statutory minimum wages. The tax-to-GDP ratio is among the highest in the OECD, funding universal health care, free education and extensive social transfers. Public debt is low and the country habitually runs current-account surpluses.

Trade

Exports of goods and services amount to well over half of GDP. Principal trading partners include Germany, Sweden, the United States, the United Kingdom and Norway. Leading exports are pharmaceuticals, machinery, meat and dairy products, and shipping services.
