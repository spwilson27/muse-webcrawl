Roberto Saviano

Roberto Saviano (born 22 September 1979) is an Italian writer, essayist and screenwriter. He is best known for Gomorrah (2006), a non-fiction exposé of the Camorra, the Neapolitan mafia, which sold millions of copies, was translated into dozens of languages, and was adapted into a 2008 film and a long-running television series. Because of explicit death threats from the Camorra's Casalesi clan, Saviano has lived under permanent police protection since 2006.

Gomorrah

Drawing on his upbringing in Casal di Principe and undercover work in ports and workshops, Saviano documented the Camorra's business empire — drugs, arms, counterfeit fashion, construction and toxic waste disposal in Campania. After a speech naming clan bosses at a public rally in Casal di Principe in 2006, threats escalated; intercepted communications later revealed a Casalesi plan to kill him, and he has since lived in constantly changing locations under Carabinieri escort.

Later work

ZeroZeroZero (2013) traces the global cocaine trade from Mexican cartels to Italian mafias; he co-created the Gomorrah TV series and the series ZeroZeroZero, and wrote the screenplay of Piranhas (2019), about Naples' teenage gangs. A prominent commentator on Italian politics and civil rights, he has been a defendant and plaintiff in defamation cases brought by politicians he criticised.
