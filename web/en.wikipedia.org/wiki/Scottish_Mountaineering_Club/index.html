The Scottish Mountaineering Club (SMC) is Scotland's senior mountaineering club, founded in Glasgow in 1889 by William Wilson Naismith and others. It played a foundational role in the development of climbing and hillwalking in Scotland, and its members pioneered many Scottish winter climbing routes.

Winter climbing in the Highlands, a pursuit the SMC helped establish.

History

The club's founders were inspired by the Alpine Club and by the growing interest in Scotland's hills during the "golden age" of Scottish mountaineering in the late Victorian era. Naismith is remembered for "Naismith's rule" for estimating walking times (1892). Members made early ascents and systematic explorations of the Cuillin, Ben Nevis and the Cairngorms.

Activities

The SMC publishes the Scottish Mountaineering Club Journal (since 1890) and, through its publishing arm, the definitive climbers' and hillwalkers' guidebooks to Scotland. A sister body, the Scottish Mountaineering Trust, founded in 1970, supports mountain access, conservation and literature. The club maintains several mountain huts (bothies and club huts) for members and guests.
