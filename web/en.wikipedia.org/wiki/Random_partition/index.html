A random partition is a probability distribution over the partitions of a set or of an integer, used in probability theory, combinatorics, and Bayesian statistics. Random partitions model situations in which a collection of items is divided into an unknown number of groups — for example, customers seated at tables, genes grouped into species, or documents clustered by topic — and the grouping itself is random.

Examples

A classic construction is the Chinese restaurant process, which generates a random partition of the positive integers: each successive customer sits at an occupied table with probability proportional to the number already seated there, or starts a new table with probability proportional to a concentration parameter. The partition induced by the Ewens sampling formula from population genetics has the same distribution. For integer partitions, random partitions under the uniform or Plancherel measures exhibit striking limit shapes studied by Logan, Shepp, Vershik, and Kerov: as the size of the partition grows, its (rescaled) Young diagram converges to a deterministic curve.

Applications

Random partitions underpin nonparametric Bayesian methods such as Dirichlet process mixture models, in which the number of clusters is inferred from data rather than fixed in advance. They also appear in combinatorics, representation theory, statistical mechanics, and the analysis of random permutations, whose cycle structure forms a random partition of the set of elements.
