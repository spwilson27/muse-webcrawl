Wolfgang Köhler

Wolfgang Köhler
Born: 21 January 1887, Reval (Tallinn)
Died: 11 June 1967, Enfield, New Hampshire
Known for: Gestalt psychology; insight learning

Wolfgang Köhler (1887–1967) was a German psychologist and phenomenologist who, with Max Wertheimer and Kurt Koffka, founded Gestalt psychology.

Work

While director of an anthropoid research station on Tenerife during the First World War, Köhler studied problem solving in chimpanzees, notably Sultan, and reported sudden "insight" learning in The Mentality of Apes (1917). In Berlin he directed the psychological institute and wrote Gestalt Psychology (1929). A critic of Nazi interference in science, he emigrated to the United States in 1935, teaching at Swarthmore College, and later developed the theory of psychophysical isomorphism and the "Köhler effect" in group motivation.
