History of Cyprus

The history of Cyprus spans more than ten thousand years, from Neolithic settlements such as Khirokitia to the modern divided island. Its strategic position in the eastern Mediterranean made it a crossroads of empires and cultures.

Ancient and medieval periods

Cyprus was famous in antiquity for its copper — the metal's name derives from the island — and was settled by Mycenaean Greeks from the 2nd millennium BC. It passed through Assyrian, Egyptian and Persian rule before Alexander the Great, then became a Ptolemaic and later a Roman province; tradition holds that the apostles Paul and Barnabas preached there in 45 AD. After the division of the Roman Empire it was Byzantine, was raided and partly occupied in the Arab–Byzantine wars, and was conquered by Richard I of England in 1191, who sold it to the Knights Templar and then to Guy de Lusignan. The Lusignan kingdom (1192–1489) was followed by Venetian rule.

Ottoman rule to the present

The Ottoman Empire conquered Cyprus in 1570–71, ruling for three centuries; a substantial Turkish Cypriot community dates from this period. Britain administered the island from 1878 and annexed it in 1914. Greek Cypriot demands for enosis (union with Greece) led to the EOKA campaign of the 1950s; independence came in 1960 under Archbishop Makarios III, with a constitution sharing power between Greek and Turkish Cypriots. Intercommunal violence followed in 1963–64. In 1974 a Greek-backed coup aiming at enosis prompted a Turkish military invasion; the island has since been divided between the internationally recognised Republic of Cyprus and the Turkish Cypriot north (which declared independence in 1983, recognised only by Turkey). The Republic of Cyprus joined the European Union in 2004.
