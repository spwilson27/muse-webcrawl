Charles F. Adams-class destroyer

The Charles F. Adams class is a ship class of 29 guided-missile destroyers (DDG) built between 1958 and 1967 for the United States Navy, the Royal Australian Navy and the West German Navy. Based on the Forrest Sherman-class hull with an enlarged superstructure for missile systems, the class was the U.S. Navy's first designed from the keel up around the Tartar surface-to-air missile.

Design

The class combined a steam-turbine plant of 70,000 shp for about 33 knots with a single Mk 11 or Mk 13 guided-missile launching system for Tartar (later Standard MR) missiles, an ASROC anti-submarine launcher, and two 5-inch/54 dual-purpose guns. Electronics included the SPS-40 air-search and SPS-52 3D radars and SPG-51 missile fire-control radars. Later in their careers many ships were modified to fire Harpoon anti-ship missiles.

Service

U.S. ships of the class served through the Vietnam War and the Cold War, with the last, USS Goldring, decommissioned in 1993. Three ships were built for Australia as the Perth class and three for West Germany as the Lütjens class (a modified "Type 103" design); the German ships served into the 2000s. Four ex-U.S. ships transferred to Greece in the 1990s formed the Kimon class. The name ship, USS Charles F. Adams (DDG-2), was long preserved as a museum ship project in Jacksonville, Florida, but was ultimately scrapped in 2020 after preservation efforts failed.
