Crosley Field

Former names: Redland Field (1912–1933)

Location: Cincinnati, Ohio, US

Opened: 1912

Closed: 1970

Demolished: 1972

Capacity: approx. 30,000 (final years)

Tenants: Cincinnati Reds (MLB)

Crosley Field was a Major League Baseball park in Cincinnati, Ohio, home of the Cincinnati Reds from 1912 through 1970. Located in the West End neighbourhood at the intersection of Findlay Street and Western Avenue, it was built on the site of earlier grounds and opened as Redland Field, taking its later name from team owner Powel Crosley Jr. in 1934.

Features

The park was famous for its sloping left-field terrace, a graded incline of about 15 degrees in front of the outfield fence that compensated for the street level beyond and confounded visiting outfielders. Its scoreboard, surmounted by a Longines clock, and the "Sun Deck" (later Moon Deck) in right field were other landmarks. Dimensions were intimate: the foul poles stood roughly 328 feet (left) and 366 feet (right) from home plate, with centre field around 387 feet.

History

Crosley Field hosted the 1919 World Series, in which the Reds defeated the Chicago "Black Sox", and the 1940 World Series, won by the Reds over Detroit. On 24 May 1935 it staged the first night game in major-league history, under lights installed by Crosley, with President Franklin D. Roosevelt symbolically switching on the lights from the White House. It hosted the All-Star Game in 1938 and 1953. The Reds played their last game there on 24 June 1970 before moving to Riverfront Stadium; the site was later redeveloped for industrial and office use, with a historical marker and a partial replica of the home-plate area at the Cincinnati Reds Hall of Fame.
