Popular Unity (Chile)

Supporters of the Popular Unity coalition in Santiago.

Popular Unity (Spanish: Unidad Popular, UP) was a left-wing political alliance in Chile that stood behind the successful candidacy of Salvador Allende for the 1970 presidential election. Formed in 1969, it brought together the Socialist Party, the Communist Party, the Radical Party, and several smaller leftist groups.

Programme

The coalition's programme, the "Chilean path to socialism" (vía chilena al socialismo), promised a democratic and constitutional transition to socialism. Key measures included the nationalisation of copper mining (achieved in 1971 with unanimous congressional support), accelerated agrarian reform, nationalisation of banks and large monopolies, expanded social welfare, and redistribution of income.

Government, 1970–1973

Allende won a plurality in the September 1970 election and was confirmed by Congress. His government initially raised wages, expanded employment and controlled prices, but by 1972–73 Chile faced soaring inflation, shortages, strikes — including a major truck owners' strike — and intense political polarisation, compounded by United States economic pressure and covert intervention.

Overthrow

On 11 September 1973 the armed forces under Augusto Pinochet overthrew the government in a coup d'état; Allende died in the presidential palace during the attack. The Popular Unity parties were banned under the military dictatorship, and the coalition ceased to exist, though its memory remained central to the Chilean left.
