Canis

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Carnivora
Family: Canidae
Genus: Canis Linnaeus, 1758

Canis is a genus of the Caninae that includes multiple extant species, such as wolves, dogs, coyotes and golden jackals. Species of this genus are distinguished by their moderate to large size, massive and well-developed skulls and dentition, long legs, and comparatively short ears and tails.

Species

Extant species commonly recognised within Canis include:

  • Canis lupus — gray wolf (including the domestic dog, C. l. familiaris, and the dingo)
  • Canis latrans — coyote
  • Canis aureus — golden jackal
  • Canis lupaster — African wolf
  • Canis simensis — Ethiopian wolf
  • Canis familiaris — domestic dog (when treated as a separate species)

The side-striped and black-backed jackals were formerly placed in Canis but are now usually assigned to the genus Lupulella.

Evolution and taxonomy

The genus name Canis means "dog" in Latin. It was coined by Carl Linnaeus in 1758. Members of the genus share 78 chromosomes, which allows many of them to interbreed and produce fertile hybrids. The genus evolved in North America during the Pliocene and spread to other continents across land bridges.
