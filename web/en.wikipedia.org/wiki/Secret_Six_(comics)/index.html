Secret Six (comics)

The Secret Six is the name of several comic book teams published by DC Comics. The original Silver Age team, created by writer E. Nelson Bridwell and artist Frank Springer, debuted in Secret Six #1 (1968) as a group of operatives directed by the unseen figure Mockingbird. The name is best known today for the modern incarnation: a team of villains and antiheroes working as mercenaries, created by writer Gail Simone, first in the miniseries Villains United (2005) and then in an acclaimed ongoing Secret Six series (2008–2011).

The original team

The 1968 series followed six strangers — including a former spy, a boxer, and a physicist — blackmailed or recruited into missions by the mysterious Mockingbird, whose identity was left unresolved when the series was cancelled after seven issues. The concept was revived in the 1980s in Action Comics Weekly, revealing a new generation of the team still overseen by the original Mockingbird, August Durant.

The modern team

Simone's version assembled characters who had refused to join Lex Luthor's Secret Society of Super Villains in Villains United: Catman (Thomas Blake), Deadshot (Floyd Lawton), Cheshire, Rag Doll (Peter Merkel Jr.), Scandal Savage (daughter of Vandal Savage), and a Parademon. Mockingbird was ultimately revealed to be Luthor himself, manipulating the team. The 2008 ongoing series, by Simone with artists including Nicola Scott and Jim Calafiore, built the Six into a dysfunctional family of mercenaries, adding Bane and Jeanette (the Silver Banshee) among rotating members. Storylines such as "Unhinged" and "The Depths" mixed black comedy with character drama, notably rehabilitating Catman from a joke character into a fan-favourite. The series ended in 2011 with issue #36 amid DC's New 52 relaunch; a further Simone-written volume ran in 2014–2016 with a largely new line-up.

Reception

The Simone series was a critical success frequently cited among the best DC titles of its period, praised for its morally grey ensemble writing and for sustaining a villain-led book without softening its characters. The team's popularity rests on its tone: a group of damaged professionals whose loyalty to one another is the only stable value in their world.
