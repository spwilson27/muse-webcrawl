John Heminges (baptised 25 November 1566 – 10 October 1630) was an English actor in the King's Men, the playing company for which William Shakespeare wrote. Together with Henry Condell, he was an editor of the First Folio, the posthumous collected edition of Shakespeare's plays published in 1623. Without Heminges and Condell's work, eighteen of Shakespeare's plays, including Macbeth, The Tempest and Twelfth Night, might have been lost.

Life and career

Heminges was born in Holt, Worcestershire, and at the age of twelve was apprenticed to a grocer in London; he later became a freeman of the Grocers' Company. By 1588 he was acting, and he is listed among the players in several companies before joining the Lord Chamberlain's Men, formed in 1594, of which he remained a member for the rest of his career.

Unlike many of his fellow actors, Heminges rarely took leading roles in surviving cast lists; he was instead the company's business manager and financial agent. Payments from the court for the company's performances were regularly made to him, and he handled the sharers' money, leased property and dealt with the owners of the Globe Theatre and the Blackfriars playhouse. Shakespeare left him money in his will to buy a mourning ring, as he did for Richard Burbage and Condell.

The First Folio

Seven years after Shakespeare's death, Heminges and Condell gathered the company's play manuscripts and supervised the publication of Mr. William Shakespeare's Comedies, Histories, & Tragedies (1623), known as the First Folio. Their prefatory letter "To the great Variety of Readers" states that they wished "to keep the memory of so worthy a Friend and Fellow alive as was our Shakespeare." The volume contained 36 plays, 18 printed for the first time.

Death and legacy

Heminges died in London in October 1630 and was buried at St Mary Aldermanbury. His will reflects a comfortable fortune accumulated over decades of careful management. He is remembered less as an actor than as one of the two men chiefly responsible for preserving Shakespeare's dramatic canon.
