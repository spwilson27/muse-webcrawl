Barbarians at the Gate

Barbarians at the Gate: The Fall of RJR Nabisco is a 1989 book by investigative journalists Bryan Burrough and John Helyar about the leveraged buyout (LBO) of RJR Nabisco, the American food and tobacco conglomerate. It is based on a series of articles written for The Wall Street Journal. The book recounts how chief executive F. Ross Johnson's proposed management buyout in October 1988 triggered a bidding war won by the private equity firm Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co. (KKR) at $109 per share — a total of about $25 billion, the largest LBO in history at the time.

Synopsis

The book traces RJR Nabisco's formation from R. J. Reynolds Tobacco and Nabisco, Ross Johnson's lavish corporate culture — including the company's fleet of jets (the "RJR Air Force") — and his plan, with Shearson Lehman Hutton, to take the company private at $75 a share. The announcement provoked rival bids from KKR under Henry Kravis. Over weeks of escalating auctions and boardroom manoeuvring, KKR prevailed at $109 per share; Johnson's side lost despite a nominally comparable offer, partly over the board's concerns about management's retention terms.

Reception and adaptation

The book became a bestseller and is widely regarded as a classic of business journalism, a vivid portrait of 1980s Wall Street excess. It was adapted into an Emmy Award-winning HBO television film of the same name in 1993, starring James Garner as F. Ross Johnson. The title has since entered business vocabulary as shorthand for hostile forces threatening a company's leadership.
