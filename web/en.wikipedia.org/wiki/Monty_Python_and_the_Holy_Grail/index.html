Monty Python and the Holy Grail

Monty Python and the Holy Grail is a 1975 British comedy film satirising the Arthurian legend, written and performed by the Monty Python comedy group: Graham Chapman, John Cleese, Terry Gilliam, Eric Idle, Terry Jones and Michael Palin. It was co-directed by Gilliam and Jones while Jones was doing most of the directing work.

The film, shot largely on location in Scotland on a modest budget, follows King Arthur (Chapman) and his knights of the Round Table as they embark on a surreal, low-budget quest for the Holy Grail, encountering absurd obstacles along the way: a self-governing collective of anarcho-syndicalist peasants, a Black Knight who refuses to admit defeat even when dismembered limb by limb, the Knights Who Say "Ni", a killer rabbit, and a French garrison that taunts them from the battlements.

Financed in part by members of the rock bands Led Zeppelin and Pink Floyd and by Jethro Tull frontman Ian Anderson, the film was a critical and commercial success and has since been ranked among the greatest comedy films of all time. Its dialogue ("It's just a flesh wound") and devices (coconut halves used to mimic hoofbeats, intermission crushing by the animator) have become touchstones of popular culture.

The film was adapted into the Tony Award-winning Broadway musical Spamalot in 2005. It is regularly voted one of the best comedies ever made in polls conducted by magazines and television channels.
