Pyridine

Pyridine
Formula: C5H5N
Molar mass: 79.10 g/mol
Melting point: −42 °C
Boiling point: 115 °C
pKa of conjugate acid: ≈5.2

Pyridine is a basic heterocyclic organic compound with the chemical formula C5H5N. It is structurally related to benzene, with one methine group (=CH−) replaced by a nitrogen atom. It is a highly flammable, colourless liquid with a distinctive unpleasant, fish-like smell, and is miscible with water and most organic solvents.

Occurrence and production

Pyridine occurs in coal tar and in trace amounts in many natural products; the pyridine ring is central to the vitamins niacin and pyridoxine and to alkaloids such as nicotine. Industrially it is synthesised chiefly by the Chichibabin pyridine synthesis — the condensation of formaldehyde, acetaldehyde, and ammonia over a catalyst — and worldwide production is on the order of tens of thousands of tonnes per year.

Uses

Pyridine is a widely used solvent and mild base in organic synthesis, a denaturant for alcohol, and a precursor to agrochemicals (including the herbicides paraquat and diquat), pharmaceuticals, and piperidine via hydrogenation. Its derivatives, the picolines and lutidines, are important industrial intermediates.
