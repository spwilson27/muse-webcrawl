M5 Stuart

The M5 Stuart is an American light tank of the Second World War, an improved version of the earlier M3 Stuart powered by twin Cadillac engines. It was supplied to British and Commonwealth forces under Lend-Lease and used throughout the war.

M5 Stuart
Type: Light tank
Origin: United States
Main armament: 37 mm Gun M6
Produced: 1942–1944
Crew: 4

Design and development

To free radial aircraft engines for other uses, Cadillac developed a powerplant of two Cadillac V8 automobile engines with Hydra-Matic transmissions. The resulting tank, standardised as the M5, also featured a redesigned, better-sloped hull.

Combat use

Like the M3, the M5 served primarily in reconnaissance and infantry-support roles, as its 37 mm gun was ineffective against later German armour. The chassis also served as the basis for the M8 howitzer motor carriage.
