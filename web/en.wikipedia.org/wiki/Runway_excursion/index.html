Runway excursion

A runway excursion is an incident in which an aircraft veers off or overruns the runway surface during takeoff or landing. Excursions are divided into overruns, in which the aircraft runs past the end of the runway, and veer-offs, in which it departs the side of the runway. They are among the most common categories of aviation accident worldwide.

Causes

Contributing factors include landing long or fast, contaminated runway surfaces (rain, snow, slush) that degrade braking, tailwinds, late or unstable approaches, rejected takeoffs initiated after V₁, mechanical failures such as loss of reverse thrust or brakes, and aquaplaning. Wet or contaminated runways and failure to execute a go-around from an unstable approach recur throughout accident investigations.

Consequences and mitigation

Many excursions end with no injuries, the aircraft stopping in the runway end safety area, but high-energy overruns into obstacles, water, or roads have caused major disasters; examples include Air France Flight 358 at Toronto (2005), TAM Flight 3054 at São Paulo (2007), and Pegasus Airlines Flight 2193 at Istanbul (2020). Mitigations include grooved runways, declared distances and runway condition reporting (the ICAO Global Reporting Format), runway safety areas, and engineered materials arresting systems (EMAS) — crushable beds that decelerate an overrunning aircraft. Industry and regulator programmes, including ICAO's Global Runway Safety Action Plan, target excursion risk specifically.
