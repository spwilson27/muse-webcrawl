Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (born 26 February 1954) is a Turkish politician who has served as the 12th president of Turkey since 2014. He previously served as prime minister from 2003 to 2014 and as mayor of Istanbul from 1994 to 1998. He is a co-founder of the Justice and Development Party (AK Party).

Early political career

Erdoğan rose through Islamist politics in the Welfare Party and was elected mayor of Istanbul in 1994, gaining a reputation for competent administration. In 1998 he was convicted of inciting religious hatred over a poem he recited, imprisoned for four months, and banned from politics, but he returned to found the AK Party in 2001, which won the 2002 general election.

Premiership

As prime minister (2003–2014), Erdoğan oversaw strong economic growth, EU accession negotiations, and early liberalizing reforms, but his government faced the 2013 Gezi Park protests and a major corruption scandal, and after 2013 relations with the Gülen movement collapsed.

Presidency

Elected president in 2014, Erdoğan survived a failed coup attempt in July 2016, after which sweeping purges followed. A 2017 referendum transformed Turkey into an executive-presidential system, effective after his re-election in 2018; he was re-elected again in 2023. His tenure has been marked by debates over press freedom, judicial independence, economic turmoil including high inflation, and an assertive foreign policy.
