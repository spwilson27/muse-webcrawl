The Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET) is a multidisciplinary professional engineering institution. The IET was formed on 31 March 2006 as a result of the merger of the Institution of Electrical Engineers (IEE), which dated from 1871, and the Institution of Incorporated Engineers (IIE). It is one of the world's largest engineering institutions, with a worldwide membership of over 150,000 in around 150 countries.

The IET is a registered charity in England and Wales and in Scotland. Its stated aim is to inspire, inform and influence the global engineering community to engineer a better world. The institution is a licensed body of the Engineering Council and can award the professional registrations of Chartered Engineer (CEng), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), Engineering Technician (EngTech) and ICT Technician (ICTTech).

History

The Society of Telegraph Engineers was founded in 1871, with the first meeting held on 17 May 1871. It was renamed the Society of Telegraph Engineers and Electricians in 1880 and became the Institution of Electrical Engineers in 1888. The IEE received a royal charter in 1921. In 2006 the IEE merged with the Institution of Incorporated Engineers to form the IET. In 2016 the IET was the second-largest membership organisation for engineers in the world.

Activities

The IET publishes journals and conference proceedings through its digital library, organises conferences and lectures, and runs the IET Faraday Challenge and other schools outreach programmes. It is a major producer of engineering standards and codes of practice in the United Kingdom, including the IET Wiring Regulations (BS 7671), which set the standard for electrical installation in the UK. The institution also presents the annual IET Innovation Awards and the A. F. Harvey Engineering Research Prize.

Headquarters

The IET's London home is Savoy Place on the Victoria Embankment, a building it has occupied since the early 20th century. Its other main site is Michael Faraday House in Stevenage, Hertfordshire.
