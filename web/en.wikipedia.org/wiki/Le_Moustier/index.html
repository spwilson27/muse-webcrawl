Le Moustier is an archaeological site consisting of two rock shelters in the commune of Peyzac-le-Moustier in the Dordogne department of France. It is the type site of the Mousterian stone-tool industry of the Middle Palaeolithic, the culture principally associated with the Neanderthals of Europe.

Excavations

The shelters were investigated from the 1860s, when the upper shelter yielded the flint assemblages that Gabriel de Mortillet used to define the Mousterian. Systematic excavations were later conducted by Denis Peyrony. In 1908 the Swiss antiquarian Otto Hauser excavated the near-complete skeleton of a Neanderthal adolescent, known as Le Moustier 1, in the lower shelter — a youth of about fifteen or sixteen years. In 1914 a Neanderthal infant, Le Moustier 2, was discovered; it was long thought lost before being rediscovered in museum stores and re-studied in the early 2000s.

Significance

The Mousterian industry defined at Le Moustier is characterised by prepared-core (Levallois) flaking and retouched side-scrapers and points, produced mainly between about 160,000 and 40,000 years ago. The Le Moustier 1 skull, reconstructed in the twentieth century, became one of the classic specimens used to define Neanderthal anatomy. The Le Moustier 1 skeleton was long held in Berlin; the skull was damaged and separated from the postcranial bones during the Second World War. The site forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Prehistoric Sites and Decorated Caves of the Vézère Valley.
