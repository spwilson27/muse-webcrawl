Yedioth Ahronoth

Yedioth Ahronoth (ידיעות אחרונות, "Latest News")
Type: Daily newspaper
Founded: 11 December 1939
Headquarters: Rishon LeZion (formerly Tel Aviv)
Language: Hebrew
Sister website: Ynet

Yedioth Ahronoth (lit. "Latest News") is an Israeli daily newspaper published in Tel Aviv (now printed in Rishon LeZion) in the Hebrew language. Founded in 1939, it was for decades Israel's best-selling and most widely read newspaper, and remains a central institution of Israeli media through its news website Ynet (launched 2000).

History

The paper was founded in 1939 by an investor group headed by Gershom Komarov; after a famous overnight split in 1948, its staff left to found the rival Maariv, and Yedioth Ahronoth passed to the control of the Mozes family, who have owned it since. Under long-time publisher Arnon "Noni" Mozes, the paper developed a mass-market tabloid style with large headlines and broad popular appeal, dominating Israeli circulation until the free daily Israel Hayom surpassed it in readership around 2010. Its Friday edition and weekend magazine remain market leaders, and Ynet is among Israel's most-visited news sites, with an English-language edition, Ynetnews.

Influence and controversy

Yedioth Ahronoth has exercised substantial political influence in Israel; critics across the spectrum have accused it of tilting coverage for or against particular politicians, while supporters credit it with major investigative reporting, including campaigns against corruption. The paper became a direct subject of national politics in the "Case 2000" investigation, concerning recorded conversations between Mozes and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu about possible favourable coverage in exchange for restrictions on Israel Hayom; Netanyahu was indicted in the affair (among other cases) in 2019. Veteran commentators associated with the paper include Nahum Barnea and Sima Kadmon.
