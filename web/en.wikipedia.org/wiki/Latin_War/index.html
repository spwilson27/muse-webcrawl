Latin War

Latium and its neighbours in the 4th century BC.
Date340–338 BC
LocationLatium and Campania, Italy
ResultRoman victory; dissolution of the Latin League

The Latin War (340–338 BC) was a conflict between the Roman Republic and its neighbours the Latins, allied with the Campanians, Volsci, Sidicini and Aurunci. The Latins demanded equality within the alliance — one consul and half the Senate to be chosen from among them — and Rome refused, leading to war.

Course of the war

Roman armies under the consuls Titus Manlius Torquatus and Publius Decius Mus won a hard-fought victory at the Battle of Veseris (340 BC) near Mount Vesuvius, where, according to legend, Decius Mus sacrificed himself (devotio) to the gods of the underworld to secure victory, while Manlius executed his own son for disobeying orders. Further Roman victories, including the naval Battle of Antium (338 BC), ended the war.

Settlement

Rome dissolved the Latin League in 338 BC and settled with each Latin town individually: some were annexed with full Roman citizenship, others received civitas sine suffragio or the status of allied states. The settlement became the template by which Rome incorporated Italy, granting graded political rights in exchange for military manpower.
