Solar radius

The solar radius is a unit of distance used to express the size of stars in astronomy, equal to the current radius of the Sun. Its value is approximately 695,700 kilometres (432,300 miles), or about 109 times the radius of the Earth, or roughly one tenth of an astronomical unit per ten radii — ten solar radii equal about 0.0465 AU.

Definition

In 2015 the International Astronomical Union adopted a nominal solar radius of exactly 6.957×108 metres, defined for use as a conversion constant. The Sun's actual radius is measured from the centre to the photosphere; because the Sun is a rotating fluid body it is slightly oblate, with the equatorial radius a few kilometres larger than the polar radius.

Measurements

Estimates of the solar radius have historically been made from transit observations, eclipses, and helioseismology. Space-based instruments such as the Michelson Doppler Imager aboard SOHO and the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager aboard the Solar Dynamics Observatory have refined the measurement and searched for small variations over the solar cycle; any genuine variation appears to be only a few kilometres at most.

Use

Stellar radii are conventionally expressed in solar units alongside solar mass and solar luminosity. For example, the red supergiant Betelgeuse has a radius of several hundred solar radii, while a typical white dwarf has a radius of about 0.01 solar radii.
