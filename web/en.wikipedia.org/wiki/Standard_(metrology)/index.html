Standard (metrology)

In metrology (the science of measurement), a standard (or etalon, from the French étalon, "standard") is an object, system, or experiment that defines or realises a particular unit of measurement or a value of a quantity, and against which other measuring instruments are calibrated. Standards are the physical embodiment of measurement units: they allow the accuracy of any measurement to be traced, through an unbroken chain of comparisons, to the definitions of the International System of Units (SI).

Hierarchy of standards

  • Primary standard — a standard designated or widely acknowledged as having the highest metrological qualities, whose value is accepted without reference to other standards of the same quantity. Historically these were artefacts, such as the International Prototype of the Kilogram; since the 2019 SI redefinition, primary standards realise units directly from defining constants of nature.
  • Secondary standard — a standard calibrated against a primary standard, used by national metrology institutes and accredited laboratories.
  • Reference standard — a standard, generally of the highest quality available at a location, from which local measurements are derived.
  • Working standard — a standard used routinely to calibrate or check measuring instruments in everyday laboratory or industrial practice.

Traceability

The essential property of a standard is traceability: its value must be linked to the SI definition through a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to the overall measurement uncertainty. National metrology institutes — such as NIST in the United States, the NPL in the United Kingdom and PTB in Germany — maintain national standards and coordinate through the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM) under the Metre Convention of 1875.

Examples

Classical examples include standard resistors and Josephson-effect voltage standards in electrical metrology, gauge blocks in dimensional metrology, standard platinum resistance thermometers in thermometry, and certified reference materials in chemistry. The 2019 redefinition of the SI, which fixed the numerical values of the Planck constant, elementary charge, Boltzmann constant and Avogadro constant, completed the transition from artefact standards to definitions based on constants of nature.
