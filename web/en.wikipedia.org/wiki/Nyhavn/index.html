Nyhavn

Nyhavn (Danish for "New Harbour") is a 17th-century waterfront, canal and entertainment district in Copenhagen, Denmark. Stretching from Kongens Nytorv to the harbour front just south of the Royal Playhouse, it is lined by brightly coloured 17th- and early 18th-century townhouses, bars, cafés, and restaurants, and is one of the city's most famous landmarks.

History

Nyhavn was constructed by King Christian V from 1670 to 1675, dug by Danish soldiers and Swedish prisoners from the Dano-Swedish War of 1658–60. It was originally a busy commercial port where ships from all over the world would dock, and the area was notorious for beer, sailors, and prostitution. The oldest house, at No. 9, dates from 1681.

Hans Christian Andersen

The fairytale writer Hans Christian Andersen lived in Nyhavn for many years, at numbers 20, 67, and 18, and wrote some of his first fairy tales while living there. Today the harbour is home to a heritage fleet of wooden ships, and a memorial anchor at its end commemorates Danish sailors who died in the Second World War.
