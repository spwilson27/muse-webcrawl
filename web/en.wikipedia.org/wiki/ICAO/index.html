The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations that coordinates international civil aviation. It was established by the Chicago Convention, signed in December 1944, and came into existence in April 1947. Its headquarters are in Montreal, Canada, and it has 193 member states.

Role

ICAO adopts Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs) covering air navigation, safety, security, environmental protection and the facilitation of air transport, published as Annexes to the Chicago Convention. It audits states' safety oversight, assigns the ICAO airport and airline codes, defines standards for machine-readable passports, and coordinates global air traffic management planning. ICAO standards are not self-executing law but are implemented by member states, which must file differences where they depart from them.

Governance

The Assembly of all member states meets at least every three years and elects a 36-state Council, which directs the organisation's work through the Secretariat and the Air Navigation Commission.
