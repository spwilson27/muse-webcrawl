Mitanni

Mitanni was a Hurrian-speaking kingdom in northern Mesopotamia and southeastern Anatolia, flourishing roughly from c. 1550 to c. 1260 BC. Its capital was Washukanni, whose location has not been securely identified. At its height it controlled territory between the Hittites to the west and Assyria to the east.

History

Mitanni kings such as Shaushtatar and Tushratta corresponded with the pharaohs of Egypt; the Amarna letters include Tushratta's correspondence, and a Mitanni princess married into the Egyptian royal family. The ruling elite bore Indo-Aryan names and invoked gods such as Mitra, Varuna, Indra, and the Nasatyas, and a horse-training text by Kikkuli preserves Indo-Aryan technical terms.

Decline

Weakened by succession disputes and Hittite and Assyrian pressure, the kingdom was reduced by Suppiluliuma I of Hatti and finally absorbed by Assyria under Shalmaneser I.
