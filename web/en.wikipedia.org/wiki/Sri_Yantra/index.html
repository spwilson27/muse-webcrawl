Sri Yantra

The Sri Yantra (Shri Chakra), the foremost yantra of the Shri Vidya tradition.

The Sri Yantra, also called the Shri Chakra, is a form of mystical diagram (yantra) used in the Shri Vidya school of Hinduism. It consists of nine interlocking triangles — four pointing upward, representing Shiva, and five pointing downward, representing Shakti — arranged around a central point (bindu). The overlapping triangles form 43 smaller triangles, and the figure is enclosed by two concentric circles of lotus petals and a square "earth citadel" frame with four doors.

Symbolism

The Sri Yantra is understood as a diagram of the cosmos and of the human body, and as the form of the goddess Tripura Sundari, the supreme deity of Shri Vidya. In meditation and worship (Sri Chakra puja), the practitioner journeys inward from the outer square through successive enclosures (chakras) to the bindu, mapping the stages of spiritual ascent; the geometry is also said to embody the structure of the Sanskrit syllables and the goddess's mantra.

Geometry

Constructing the diagram accurately is mathematically demanding: the triangles' vertices and intersections must align so that the figure closes without error, and traditional methods rely on careful geometric construction rather than measurement. Slight errors visibly distort the lattice, so canonical versions are transmitted through temple lineages. Famous examples include the three-dimensional Meru form of the yantra, a pyramidal version worshipped at temples such as those of the Kamakhya and Sringeri traditions.
