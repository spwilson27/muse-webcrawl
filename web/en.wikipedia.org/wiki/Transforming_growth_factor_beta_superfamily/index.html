Transforming growth factor beta superfamily

The transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) superfamily is a large group of structurally related cell regulatory proteins named after its first member, TGF-β1, discovered in 1983. It includes the TGF-βs, activins, inhibins, bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), growth differentiation factors (GDFs), anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), nodal and others — more than 30 members in humans.

Signalling

Members are secreted as dimers and bind combinations of type I and type II serine/threonine kinase receptors. The activated receptor complex phosphorylates receptor-regulated SMAD proteins (SMAD2/3 for TGF-β/activin branches, SMAD1/5/8 for BMP branches), which associate with SMAD4, enter the nucleus and regulate transcription. Inhibitory SMADs and extracellular antagonists such as noggin and follistatin modulate the pathway.

Biological roles

The superfamily controls embryonic patterning (including left–right asymmetry via nodal), bone and cartilage formation, wound healing, immune regulation and reproduction. Misregulation is implicated in cancer — TGF-β suppresses early tumours but promotes invasion in late stages — fibrosis, and hereditary disorders such as hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia and Marfan-related syndromes.
