Tuu languages

The Tuu languages, also called the Taa–ǃKwi (or Taa–ǃUi) family, are a language family spoken in the Kalahari region of Botswana, Namibia and South Africa. They are one of the three recognised families of the so-called Khoisan languages, the others being Khoe–Kwadi and Kxʼa. Tuu languages are famous for their large inventories of click consonants.

Branches

The family divides into two branches: Taa, whose surviving representative is the language of the same name (Taa, or !Xóõ) spoken in Botswana, and ǃKwi (ǃUi), a cluster of languages of South Africa, most of which are extinct or nearly so. The last fluent speaker of Nǁng (Nǀuu), once the most prominent ǃKwi language, died in the twenty-first century, though revival efforts continue. Taa has one of the largest phoneme inventories of any language, with well over one hundred consonants including many clicks.

Classification history

The family was proposed by linguists studying the non-Bantu click languages of southern Africa in the twentieth century; Anthony Traill's work on Taa and research by Tom Güldemann consolidated the grouping. All Tuu languages are severely endangered.
