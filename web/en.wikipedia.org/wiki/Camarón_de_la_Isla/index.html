Camarón de la Isla

José Monje Cruz (5 December 1950 – 2 July 1992), better known by his stage name Camarón de la Isla (Spanish: "Shrimp of the Island"), was a Spanish Romani flamenco singer. Considered one of the all-time greatest flamenco singers, he was noted for his collaborations with guitarist Paco de Lucía, with whom he recorded nine albums between 1969 and 1977, and for his role in creating the nuevo flamenco movement, which opened flamenco to influences from rock, jazz, and other genres.

Early life

Monje was born in San Fernando, Cádiz, in the neighbourhood known as La Isla ("the Island"), to a Romani family. His father was a blacksmith and his mother a basket weaver. His fair skin and hair earned him the childhood nickname "Camarón" (shrimp). He began singing in public as a child, performing in local ventas (roadside inns), and as a teenager moved to Madrid, where he sang in the tablao Torres Bermejas and came to the attention of the guitarist Paco de Lucía.

Career

Camarón's early albums with Paco de Lucía, beginning in 1969, revitalized traditional flamenco singing while remaining rooted in the classic palos. His 1979 album La leyenda del tiempo, produced with musicians from the Andalusian rock scene and setting texts by Federico García Lorca, was initially controversial among purists but is now regarded as a landmark that transformed flamenco. He later worked extensively with guitarists Tomatito and Vicente Amigo, and recorded albums including Soy gitano (1989) with the Royal Philharmonic Orchestra.

Death and legacy

Camarón died of lung cancer in Barcelona on 2 July 1992, aged 41. An estimated 100,000 people attended his funeral in San Fernando. He was posthumously awarded a Latin Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2000. A museum and interpretive centre dedicated to him opened in San Fernando, and his singular voice — marked by its emotional intensity and mastery of compás — remains the benchmark against which flamenco singers are judged.
