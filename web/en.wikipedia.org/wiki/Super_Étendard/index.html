The Dassault-Breguet Super Étendard (Étendard is French for "battle flag" or standard) is a French carrier-borne strike fighter flown by the French Navy and the Argentine Navy. Developed from the earlier Étendard IV, it entered service in 1978 and is best known internationally for its anti-shipping role with the Exocet missile during the Falklands War of 1982.

Design and development

The Super Étendard was a substantial redesign of the Étendard IVM, with a more powerful SNECMA Atar 8K-50 non-afterburning turbojet, a revised wing with improved high-lift devices, the Agave radar optimised for sea search and missile guidance, and a modern navigation and attack system. It was designed to deliver the AM39 Exocet anti-ship missile and, in French service, the ASMP nuclear missile and laser-guided bombs in later modernised forms (Super Étendard Modernisé, SEM).

Operational history

France operated the type from the carriers Clemenceau, Foch, and later Charles de Gaulle, seeing action over Lebanon, the former Yugoslavia, Afghanistan, Libya, and Iraq/Syria. The last French SEMs were retired in July 2016, replaced by the Rafale M. Argentina bought fourteen aircraft, of which five, with five Exocets, arrived before the 1982 Falklands War; flying from shore bases, Argentine Super Étendards sank the destroyer HMS Sheffield (4 May 1982) and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor (25 May 1982), making the type famous worldwide. Iraq also leased five French aircraft in 1983–84 during the Iran–Iraq War, using them against Iranian oil shipping in the "Tanker War". Argentina's remaining aircraft, supplemented by ex-French airframes acquired in 2019, have struggled with serviceability but remain nominally in naval service.

Specifications (approximate)

Single-seat, single-engine; length about 14.3 m; wingspan 9.6 m; maximum speed about Mach 1.3 (roughly 1,380 km/h at altitude); combat radius about 850 km with a typical strike load; armament includes two 30 mm DEFA cannon and up to 2,100 kg of stores on five hardpoints, including Exocet, Magic air-to-air missiles, bombs, and rockets.
