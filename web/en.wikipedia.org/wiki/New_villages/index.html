New villages (Chinese: 新村) are planned rural settlements, most famously those built in the New Territories of Hong Kong during the mid-twentieth century. Many were created to resettle farming families displaced by reservoir construction, military expansion, airport projects or urban development, while others were organised by rural committees to house indigenous villagers exercising their small-house rights.

History

Large-scale resettlement began in the 1950s, when families from flooded valleys such as those submerged by the Tai Lam Chung and Plover Cove reservoirs were moved into purpose-built villages with concrete houses, roads, schools and temples. Later villages, such as those in the Sai Kung area, combined traditional layouts with modern utilities.

Features

A typical new village contains rows of two- or three-storey village houses, an ancestral hall or temple, a village office, and communal open space used for festivals such as the Tin Hau birthday celebrations. Village affairs are handled by elected representatives under the rural committee system supervised by the Heung Yee Kuk.
