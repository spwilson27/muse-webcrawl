Pyruvate dehydrogenase


The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex

Pyruvate dehydrogenase is an enzyme (EC 1.2.4.1) that catalyses the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA. It is the first component enzyme (E1) of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC), a large multienzyme assembly that performs the link reaction between glycolysis and the citric acid cycle in mitochondria.

Structure and mechanism

Mammalian E1 is a heterotetramer of two alpha and two beta subunits and uses thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) as a cofactor. E1 decarboxylates pyruvate and reductively acetylates the lipoamide swinging arm of the second enzyme, dihydrolipoyl transacetylase (E2), which transfers the acetyl group to coenzyme A; the third enzyme, dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase (E3), reoxidises lipoamide with NAD+.

Regulation

The complex is regulated by reversible phosphorylation: pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases inactivate E1, while pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatases reactivate it. Insulin and calcium promote activation; ATP, NADH and acetyl-CoA promote inactivation.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the E1 alpha subunit gene (PDHA1, on the X chromosome) cause pyruvate dehydrogenase deficiency, a cause of congenital lactic acidosis and neurological impairment. Thiamine deficiency also impairs the complex, contributing to beriberi and Wernicke encephalopathy.
