"Worlds" is the largest debating tournament on earth.

The World Universities Debating Championship (WUDC), universally known in the debating community as Worlds, is the world's largest international debating tournament for university students. Held annually since 1981, usually over the New Year period and hosted by a different university each year, it crowns world champions in the British Parliamentary format, in which four two-person teams — two proposing and two opposing a motion announced only fifteen minutes beforehand — debate in each round.

History

The first championship was hosted by the University of Toronto in 1981 as an international invitational, growing out of North American and transatlantic debating exchanges. Through the 1980s and 1990s the tournament expanded dramatically as it travelled to universities including Glasgow, Princeton, Oxford, Ateneo de Manila and Stellenbosch, becoming a fixture involving several hundred teams. Recent hosts have included Chennai (2014), Thessaloniki (2016), Mexico City (2018), Bangkok (2020), Belgrade (2022), Madrid (2023), Ho Chi Minh City (2024) and Panama City (2025).

Format and categories

Teams compete in nine preliminary rounds, with motions set by an adjudication core of experienced former competitors; the top 48 teams "break" into knockout elimination rounds culminating in the Grand Final. Separate finals are held for teams debating in English as a Second Language (ESL) and English as a Foreign Language (EFL). The best individual speaker of the tournament is also recognised. There are no restrictions on the motions, which range across international politics, economics, ethics, law and culture.

Notable champions and influence

Historically the strongest universities have been Oxford, Cambridge, Harvard, Yale, Monash and the University of Sydney, each winning multiple titles. Alumni of the championship include heads of government, supreme court justices, journalists and business leaders in dozens of countries, and the tournament has driven the spread of the British Parliamentary format to schools and universities worldwide, spawning continental championships for Europe, Asia, Australasia, Africa and the Americas.
