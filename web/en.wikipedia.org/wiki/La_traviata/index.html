La traviata (Italian: [la traˈvjaːta], "The Fallen Woman") is an opera in three acts by Giuseppe Verdi, set to an Italian libretto by Francesco Maria Piave. It is based on La Dame aux camélias (1852), a play by Alexandre Dumas fils adapted from his own novel. The opera premiered at La Fenice in Venice on 6 March 1853 and, after initial difficulties, became one of the most frequently performed operas in the world.

Synopsis

The courtesan Violetta Valéry falls in love with the young Alfredo Germont and abandons her glittering Parisian life for the countryside. Alfredo's father, Giorgio Germont, persuades her to give Alfredo up to protect his family's reputation. Returning to her former life, Violetta is publicly humiliated by the jealous Alfredo; too late, the Germonts learn of her sacrifice. Dying of tuberculosis, Violetta is visited by a repentant Alfredo and his father before she dies in her lover's arms.

Composition and performance history

The 1853 premiere was hindered by casting and by audience discomfort with a contemporary subject, but Verdi revised the opera and a successful production followed at Venice's Teatro San Benedetto in 1854. Famous numbers include the drinking song "Libiamo ne' lieti calici" (the Brindisi), Violetta's "Sempre libera," and the duet "Parigi, o cara." Together with Rigoletto and Il trovatore, La traviata forms Verdi's popular middle-period trio.
