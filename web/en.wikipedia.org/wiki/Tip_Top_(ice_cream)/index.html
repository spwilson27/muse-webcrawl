Tip Top (ice cream)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tip Top is a New Zealand ice cream brand and former manufacturer founded in Auckland in 1936. It is one of the country's best-known food brands, famous for products such as the Trumpet ice cream cone, the Jelly Tip, Popsicle ice blocks and the Memphis Meltdown.

History

Tip Top began as an ice cream parlour business in Wellington and Auckland in the 1930s and expanded into a major manufacturer, opening a large factory at Mount Wellington, Auckland, in 1962. The company was owned for many years by Fonterra, the dairy cooperative, and in 2019 the Tip Top business was sold to Froneri, the global ice cream joint venture. Production ended at the Mount Wellington site, with manufacturing consolidated elsewhere.

Products and culture

The Trumpet, a wafer cone with a chocolate top launched in 1964, became an iconic New Zealand summer treat, and the Jelly Tip — vanilla ice cream coated in jelly and chocolate — is similarly regarded as a national classic. Tip Top's long-running advertising and its ubiquity in dairies (corner shops) made it a fixture of New Zealand popular culture, and its hokey pokey flavour remains strongly associated with the country.
