Grand-Pré (French for "great meadow") is a Canadian rural community in Kings County, Nova Scotia, at the eastern shore of the Minas Basin. It was the centre of Acadian settlement in the region from about 1680 until the beginning of the Expulsion of the Acadians in 1755.

History

Acadian farmers built extensive dyke systems (aboiteaux) to reclaim the tidal marshlands around Grand-Pré, creating rich farmland that made the community the breadbasket of Acadia. In the autumn of 1755, during the French and Indian War, British forces under Lt. Col. John Winslow gathered the Acadian men and boys in the church of Saint-Charles-des-Mines and read the deportation order; the population was expelled and the village burned. The deportation from Grand-Pré became the best known episode of the Expulsion, immortalised in Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's 1847 poem Evangeline.

Legacy and commemoration

After Confederation, the site became a place of pilgrimage for Acadian descendants. A memorial park was created in the 1910s–1920s, with a memorial church (built 1922) and a statue of Evangeline. Grand-Pré National Historic Site commemorates Acadian history and the deportation. In 2012 the Landscape of Grand Pré was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognising the dykeland landscape and its memorial significance.
