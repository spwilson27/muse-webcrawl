TraditionMahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism

In Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, the Five Tathagatas or Five Wisdom Buddhas (Sanskrit: pañcatathāgata), also called the Five Dhyani Buddhas, are five buddhas who represent the five aspects of the primordial Buddha's wisdom and the five families of tantric practice. They are central to the mandalas of the Yoga Tantras and are widely depicted in the art of Nepal, Tibet, China and Japan, where they are known as the Five Wisdom Kings' counterpart buddhas in Shingon Buddhism.

Associations

Each Tathagata is associated with a direction, colour, element, aggregate (skandha), poison to be transformed, and corresponding wisdom. Vairocana sits at the centre, embodying the wisdom of the ultimate reality of all things; Akshobhya transforms anger into mirror-like wisdom; Ratnasambhava transforms pride into the wisdom of equality; Amitabha transforms attachment into discriminating wisdom; and Amoghasiddhi transforms jealousy into all-accomplishing wisdom. Each also has a characteristic mudra (hand gesture) and emblem, such as Vairocana's teaching wheel gesture and Akshobhya's earth-touching gesture.

Origins and practice

The system developed in Indian tantric texts of roughly the 7th–8th centuries, including the Tattvasaṃgraha and the Guhyasamāja Tantra traditions. In practice, the five buddhas structure mandala meditation, consecration rituals and the layout of monuments such as Borobudur in Java, whose terraces are read by some scholars as a vast three-dimensional mandala of the five families.
