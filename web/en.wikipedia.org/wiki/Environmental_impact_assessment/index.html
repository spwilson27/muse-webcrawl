Environmental impact assessment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An environmental impact assessment (EIA) is the assessment of the environmental consequences of a plan, policy, program, or actual project prior to the decision to move forward with the proposed action. In this context, the term "environmental impact assessment" is usually used when applied to actual projects by individuals or companies, and the term "strategic environmental assessment" (SEA) applies to policies, plans and programmes.

History

EIA was formally introduced in the United States with the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) of 1969, which requires federal agencies to prepare an environmental impact statement for major actions significantly affecting the environment. The concept subsequently spread worldwide; the European Union adopted its EIA Directive in 1985, and most countries now have some form of EIA legislation.

Process

Typical stages include screening (whether an EIA is needed), scoping (which impacts to study), assessment and evaluation of impacts, preparation of an environmental impact statement, public consultation, decision-making, and post-decision monitoring. EIAs consider effects on air, water, soil, biodiversity, human health, and cultural heritage, and must examine alternatives and propose mitigation measures.
