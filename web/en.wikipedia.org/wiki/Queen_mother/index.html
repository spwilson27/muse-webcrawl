Queen mother

A queen mother is a queen dowager who is the mother of the reigning monarch. The term describes both her status as a former queen consort (or, occasionally, a queen regnant) and her relationship to the current sovereign.

Usage

In the United Kingdom, the best-known holder of the style was Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother (1900–2002), widow of George VI and mother of Elizabeth II, who was widely known simply as "the Queen Mother". Other European monarchies use equivalent titles, and in several African kingdoms — notably Eswatini, where the Ndlovukazi rules jointly with the king — the queen mother is a constitutional officeholder in her own right.

Position

A queen mother generally holds no formal governmental role in European monarchies but may undertake public duties and patronages, and she ranks high in the order of precedence. In Thailand, Queen Sirikit was styled Queen Mother after the accession of her son, King Vajiralongkorn.
