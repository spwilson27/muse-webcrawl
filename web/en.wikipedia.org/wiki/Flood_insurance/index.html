Flood insurance

Flood insurance is a type of property insurance that covers a dwelling or other insured property for losses sustained by water damage, specifically flooding, which is typically excluded from standard homeowners insurance policies.

United States programme

In the United States, most flood insurance is provided through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), created by Congress in 1968 and administered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA).

The programme makes federally backed insurance available in communities that adopt and enforce floodplain management ordinances.

Coverage and risk

Policies generally cover damage to the building and, optionally, its contents, subject to limits and deductibles, but usually exclude basements and property below the lowest elevated floor in some respects.

Premiums are based on flood risk, increasingly under risk-based pricing systems such as FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0.
