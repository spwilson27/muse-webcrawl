Terminal aerodrome forecast

A terminal aerodrome forecast (TAF) is a format for reporting weather forecast information, particularly as it relates to aviation. "Terminal aerodrome forecast" is the international standard designation for this type of forecast; in the United States the term terminal aerodrome forecast is officially used, though the format is universally known as TAF.

TAFs are issued at least four times a day, every six hours, for major civil airfields, and apply to a 24- or 30-hour period, sometimes covering a radius of about 8 km (5 statute miles) around the airport. They are prepared by national meteorological services — in the United States, by the National Weather Service — and are used by pilots and flight dispatchers in flight planning.

Format

A TAF uses a compact coded format similar to the METAR observation code. Elements typically include the station identifier, issue time, valid period, and forecast groups for surface wind, visibility, weather phenomena (rain, fog, thunderstorms, etc.), cloud amounts and heights, and sometimes temperature. Changes expected during the valid period are indicated with change groups:

  • FM ("from") — a rapid and significant change at a specified time
  • BECMG ("becoming") — a gradual change over a period
  • TEMPO ("temporarily") — fluctuations lasting less than an hour at a time and less than half the period overall
  • PROB30 / PROB40 — a 30% or 40% probability of the following conditions

Example

TAF KORD 121720Z 1218/1324 22012KT P6SM SCT045 BKN250 FM130300 24008KT P6SM FEW250 — a forecast for Chicago O'Hare issued at 17:20 UTC on the 12th, valid from 18:00 UTC on the 12th to 24:00 UTC on the 13th, beginning with southwesterly winds at 12 knots, visibility greater than 6 statute miles, scattered clouds at 4,500 feet and a broken ceiling at 25,000 feet, changing from 03:00 UTC to lighter winds and few clouds.
