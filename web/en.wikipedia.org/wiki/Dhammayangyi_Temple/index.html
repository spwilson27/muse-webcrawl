Dhammayangyi Temple is a Buddhist temple in Bagan, Myanmar, and the largest of all the temples in the Bagan archaeological zone. Built during the reign of King Narathu (1167–1170), it is famed both for its massive pyramidal bulk and for the exceptionally fine brickwork of its construction, as well as for the legends of cruelty surrounding its builder.

History

King Narathu, who came to the throne by assassinating his father Alaungsithu and his elder brother, is said to have built the temple to atone for his sins. Narathu was himself assassinated by invaders in 1170, before the temple was finished, and construction was abandoned; rubble-filled interior corridors of the unfinished temple reflect this. Legend claims Narathu executed a bricklayer — or in some versions had workers' hands cut off — if a pin could be inserted between the bricks, accounting for the mortarless precision of the masonry.

Architecture

The temple follows a Greek-cross-in-square plan similar to that of the earlier Ananda Temple, rising in receding terraces to a pyramidal profile. Much of the interior is filled with rubble, leaving only the outer corridors accessible. Two original Buddha statues remain in the western portico.
