Bartolomé de las Casas

Born11 November 1484, Seville, Spain
Died18 July 1566, Madrid, Spain
OccupationDominican friar, historian, social reformer
Known forDefence of the rights of Indigenous peoples of the Americas

Bartolomé de las Casas (11 November 1484 – 18 July 1566) was a Spanish clergyman, the first resident Bishop of Chiapas, and a prominent figure in the Spanish colonisation of the Americas. Initially a participant in the conquest as an encomendero, he underwent a profound conversion and spent the rest of his life campaigning for the rights of the Indigenous peoples against the abuses of the encomienda system.

Early life and conversion

Las Casas arrived in Hispaniola in 1502 and took part in expeditions against the Taíno. After witnessing massacres of Indigenous people, and following a sermon by Antonio de Montesinos in 1511 questioning the treatment of the natives, he renounced his encomienda in 1514 and joined the Dominican order.

Advocacy

He travelled repeatedly to Spain to lobby the Crown, contributing to the New Laws of 1542, which restricted the encomienda. In 1550–51 he defended the humanity and rights of the Indigenous peoples in the famous Valladolid debate against Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda.

Writings and legacy

His A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies (1552) documented Spanish atrocities and was translated across Europe, feeding the so-called Black Legend of Spanish cruelty. He is remembered in Latin America as the "Defender of the Indians".
