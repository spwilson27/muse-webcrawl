Polska (dance)


Dancing the polska in Sweden

The polska (Swedish plural polskor) is a family of Nordic folk dances and dance music in 3/4 time, originating in the 16th and 17th centuries and still danced in Sweden, Norway, Denmark (where it is called pols), and Swedish-speaking parts of Finland. The name means "Polish", reflecting the influence of Polish court dances that spread through Europe. The polska is traditionally danced by couples in a turning, spinning figure.

Music and forms

Polska tunes are typically played on fiddle, often with characteristic rhythmic accents that differ from the waltz: in the Swedish slängpolska regions, the emphasis varies, with the "short one-three" pattern typical of southern Sweden's sixteenth-note polskas. Regional variants include the hambo (a later, related dance), bondpolska, and Norwegian forms such as the springar and gangar. The polska tradition is central to Swedish folk music, maintained by spelmän (folk musicians) and gatherings called spelmansstämmor.
