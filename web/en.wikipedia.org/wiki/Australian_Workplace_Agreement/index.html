Australian Workplace Agreement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Signing an individual workplace agreement

An Australian Workplace Agreement (AWA) was a formalised individual agreement negotiated between an employer and an employee in Australia that governed the terms and conditions of employment. AWAs were introduced by the Workplace Relations Act 1996 and became a centrepiece of industrial relations debate, particularly after the Howard government's WorkChoices amendments of 2006. No new AWAs could be made after transitional legislation in 2008, and the Fair Work Act 2009 completed the return to collective enterprise agreements.

Operation

An AWA, once approved and lodged with the Employment Advocate (later the Workplace Authority), displaced the otherwise applicable industrial award for that employee entirely. Originally, AWAs had to satisfy a "no disadvantage test" against the relevant award; WorkChoices removed that test for agreements made between 2006 and 2007, replacing it with a limited "fairness test" in 2007 after public controversy. AWAs could run for up to five years, and at their peak more than a million Australian workers were covered by current or expired AWAs, concentrated in industries such as mining, retail, and hospitality.

Controversy

Unions and the Australian Labor Party argued that AWAs were used to cut penalty rates, overtime, and other conditions, and that individual workers lacked the bargaining power to refuse them; employer groups argued they enabled flexible, mutually beneficial arrangements and higher wages in sectors like mining. The issue was central to the 2007 federal election, which Labor won. The Workplace Relations Amendment (Transition to Forward with Fairness) Act 2008 prohibited new AWAs while allowing existing ones to continue until terminated or replaced, and permitted Individual Transitional Employment Agreements as a stopgap.

Legacy

AWAs remain a symbol of the contested politics of Australian labour law: the experiment with statutory individual contracting that substantially bypassed the century-old award system, and the subsequent restoration of collective bargaining under Fair Work legislation.
