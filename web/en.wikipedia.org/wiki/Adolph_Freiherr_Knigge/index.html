Adolph Freiherr Knigge (1752–1796)

Adolph Franz Friedrich Ludwig Freiherr Knigge (16 October 1752 – 6 May 1796) was a German writer and leading member of the Order of the Illuminati. He is best known for his treatise Über den Umgang mit Menschen ("On Human Relations", 1788), a foundational work on etiquette and social conduct whose author's name became a byword in German for books of manners — any modern German guide to etiquette is commonly called a "Knigge".

Life

Knigge was born in Bredenbeck in the Electorate of Hanover into a family of the lesser nobility. After the early death of his parents he was educated in Hanover and studied law at the University of Göttingen. He held a succession of court appointments, including posts in Kassel and Weimar, but his satirical writings and restless temperament repeatedly damaged his prospects, and he spent his later years in Bremen, where he served as a senior official (Oberhauptmann) and where he died in 1796.

The Illuminati

Knigge joined the Order of the Illuminati in 1780, taking the order name "Philo", after the Roman writer Quintus Tullius Cicero's dialogue figure. He became one of the order's most effective recruiters and organizers, but quarrelled with its founder, Adam Weishaupt, over doctrine and authority, and left the order in 1784.

Über den Umgang mit Menschen

Knigge's most influential book is less a manual of table manners than a practical philosophy of dealing with people of every rank and temperament. It advises readers on conduct toward monarchs, clergy, scholars, merchants, friends, and oneself, and is shot through with Enlightenment ideals of tolerance, self-knowledge, and prudence. Although modern "Knigge" etiquette guides retain little of the original's substance, the work remains in print in Germany and is regarded as a classic of German prose.

Other works

Knigge wrote novels, plays, and satires, including Die Reise nach Braunschweig (1792), a comic novel, and the autobiographical Roman meines Lebens (1781–83). He also translated works from French and English and wrote libretti and theatrical pieces.
