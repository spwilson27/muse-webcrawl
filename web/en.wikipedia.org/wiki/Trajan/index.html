Trajan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Trajan's Column in Rome, its spiral relief narrating the Dacian Wars]

Trajan (AD 53–117; Marcus Ulpius Traianus) was Roman emperor from 98 to 117, the second of the 'Five Good Emperors' and the soldier-emperor under whom the Roman Empire reached its greatest territorial extent. Born in Italica in Spain — the first emperor from a province — he was adopted by the elderly Nerva and succeeded peacefully in 98. His Dacian Wars (101–102, 105–106) annexed modern Romania and filled his treasury with its gold, funding the vast Forum and Column of Trajan in Rome; his Parthian campaign (113–117) took Armenia, Mesopotamia and briefly the Persian Gulf coast. The Senate awarded him the title Optimus Princeps — the Best Ruler — and later emperors were wished 'luckier than Augustus, better than Trajan'.

Conquests

Trajan's Column, its spiral frieze a primary source for the wars, shows methodical campaigning: roads, bridges (Apollodorus' great Danube bridge), sieges and the storming of Sarmizegetusa, ending in Decebalus' suicide and Dacia's annexation as a gold-rich province. In the east he annexed Nabataea (Arabia Petraea, 106), then in 113 invaded Parthia on the pretext of Armenia's throne, crowning a client king, deposing him, and marching to Ctesiphon and the Gulf — the empire's furthest reach, immediately troubled by revolts across the new provinces and a great Jewish diaspora uprising (115–117).

Death and assessment

Trajan fell ill (a stroke, perhaps) and died at Selinus in Cilicia in August 117 while returning to Italy; his ashes were placed in the Column's base. His successor Hadrian abandoned the new Mesopotamian provinces as untenable, fixing the eastern frontier Trajan had briefly surpassed. Dante set Trajan in Paradise (on a legend of his posthumous salvation through Gregory the Great's prayers) — a measure of his medieval fame as the just pagan emperor.
