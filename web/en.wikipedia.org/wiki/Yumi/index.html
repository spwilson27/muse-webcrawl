Yumi (弓) is the Japanese term for a bow. As used in English, yumi refers more specifically to traditional Japanese asymmetrical longbows, and includes the shorter hankyū (半弓, "half bow"). Yumi play an important role in the traditional Japanese martial art of kyūdō, although in modern times most yumi used in the sport are made of synthetic materials such as fibreglass and carbon fibre.

Shape and construction

The yumi is exceptionally tall, standing over two metres, and typically surpasses the height of the archer (kyūdōka). It is asymmetrical: the grip (nigiri) is positioned at approximately the bottom third of the bow, so the upper limb is much longer than the lower. This design is traditionally said to have allowed shooting from horseback, with the longer upper limb clearing the horse's neck, and from a kneeling position.

Traditional yumi (wakyū) are made of bamboo laminated around a wooden core, bound with rattan bindings and lacquered; the bowstring (tsuru) is traditionally made of hemp, though most modern strings are synthetic.

History

Asymmetrical bows appear in Japanese art from the Yayoi period onward. The yumi was the principal projectile weapon of the samurai on foot and on horseback for centuries, and its use survives in kyūdō, shrine archery rituals such as yabusame, and contests such as the ōmato archery held at Sanjūsangen-dō in Kyoto.
