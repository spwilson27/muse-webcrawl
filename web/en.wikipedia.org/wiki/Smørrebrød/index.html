Smørrebrød

Smørrebrød (Danish pronunciation: [ˈsmɶɐ̯ˌpʁœðˀ]; originally smør og brød, "butter and bread") is a traditional Danish open-faced sandwich that usually consists of a piece of buttered rye bread (rugbrød), a dense, dark brown bread, topped with commercial or homemade cold cuts, pieces of meat or fish, cheese or spreads, and garnishes.

Toppings

Classic toppings include pickled herring with onions; leverpostej (liver pâté) with bacon and mushrooms; roast beef with remoulade and crispy onions; smoked salmon; egg and shrimp; and dyrlægens natmad ("the veterinarian's midnight snack"), a layering of liver pâté, salted beef aspic and red onion. Elaborate restaurant versions can be highly decorative, and smørrebrød is traditionally eaten with a knife and fork, in an order beginning with herring and other fish, then meat, then cheese.

History and culture

The open sandwich has medieval roots in Scandinavia, but smørrebrød as a codified cuisine developed in the 19th century, when Copenhagen lunch restaurants published menus listing dozens of varieties. Figures such as Ida Davidsen became famous for menus of nearly 200 variants. It remains a staple of Danish lunches and festive occasions, typically accompanied by beer and snaps (akvavit).
