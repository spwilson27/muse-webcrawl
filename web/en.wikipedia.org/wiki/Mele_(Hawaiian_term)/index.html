Mele (Hawaiian term)

Mele
LanguageHawaiian (ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi)
Literal senseSong, chant; poetic text
Related termsOli (chant without dance), hula

Mele is a Hawaiian word meaning a song or chant, and by extension a poem or poetic composition. In traditional Hawaiian culture the term covers a continuum of vocal art from solemn ceremonial chant to composed song accompanying dance. The word is cognate with other Polynesian terms for chant and poetry.

As a linguistic term, mele distinguishes the textual-compositional side of Hawaiian performance: a mele is the words — usually composed by a haku mele (composer) — whereas oli refers to chant delivered without dance or instrumental accompaniment, and mele performed with dance become the text of hula. Mele texts are valued for their kaona, the veiled and layered meanings embedded by skilled composers.

Usage

The term appears in collocations such as mele pule (prayer chant), mele inoa (name song), mele koihonua (genealogical chant), and mele kanikau (lament). Nineteenth-century Hawaiian-language newspapers preserved thousands of mele, making the word central to the modern study of Hawaiian literature.
