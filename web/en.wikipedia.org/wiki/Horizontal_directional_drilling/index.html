Horizontal directional drilling

Horizontal directional drilling (HDD) is a steerable trenchless method of installing underground pipelines, conduits and cables in a shallow arc along a prescribed bore path by using a surface-launched drilling rig, with minimal impact on the surrounding area.

Process

The installation proceeds in three stages. First, a pilot hole is drilled along the designed path using a steerable drill head whose position is tracked by a walk-over locating system or a wireline guidance tool. Second, the pilot hole is enlarged by passing a reamer back through it, sometimes several times, until the bore is roughly one and a half times the diameter of the product pipe. Third, the prefabricated pipeline is pulled back through the enlarged hole.

Applications

HDD is widely used for river, road and railway crossings and for utility installations in congested urban areas where open-cut trenching would be disruptive or impossible. It is a key technology in the installation of fibre-optic cable, water and sewer lines, and oil and gas pipelines.

Drilling fluid

A bentonite-based drilling fluid lubricates the drill string, carries cuttings out of the bore, cools the electronics, and stabilises the borehole walls. Managing fluid returns and avoiding inadvertent returns ("frac-outs") into waterways is a central environmental concern on HDD projects.
