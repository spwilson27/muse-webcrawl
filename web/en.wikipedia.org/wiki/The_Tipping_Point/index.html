The Tipping Point: How Little Things Can Make a Big Difference is the debut book by Canadian journalist Malcolm Gladwell, first published by Little, Brown in 2000. It examines how social phenomena—fashion trends, crime waves, the spread of ideas and products—can spread like epidemics, suddenly reaching a critical threshold, or "tipping point", at which they cascade through a population.

The three rules of epidemics

Gladwell identifies three agents of change. The Law of the Few holds that epidemics depend on people with particular social gifts: Connectors, who know many people; Mavens, who accumulate and share knowledge; and Salesmen, who persuade others. The Stickiness Factor holds that a message must be memorable or practical enough to stick and prompt action. The Power of Context holds that behaviour is strongly shaped by environment; Gladwell famously discusses the "broken windows" theory and New York City's crime decline in the 1990s, and the rule of 150 (Dunbar's number) as a limit on stable social groups.

Case studies

Examples in the book include the revival of Hush Puppies shoes in the mid-1990s, the success of Sesame Street and Blue's Clues in children's education, teen smoking and suicide clusters in Micronesia, and the popularization of the phrase "tipping point" itself.

Reception

The book was a major bestseller and popularized its title phrase in business and politics. Academics have debated its claims, and Gladwell revisited and qualified some of them in Revenge of the Tipping Point (2024).
