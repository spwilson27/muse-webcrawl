The Northern Zhou (Chinese: 北周; pinyin: Běi Zhōu) was a Xianbei-led dynasty of China that lasted from 557 to 581 AD. One of the Northern dynasties of the Northern and Southern dynasties period, it succeeded the Western Wei and was eventually succeeded by the Sui dynasty, which reunified China. Its capital was Chang'an.

Founding

The dynasty was founded by Yuwen Jue, son of the paramount general Yuwen Tai, who had dominated Western Wei. In 557 Yuwen Tai's nephew Yuwen Hu forced the Western Wei emperor Gong to abdicate and installed Jue as Emperor Xiaomin of Northern Zhou. Yuwen Hu dominated the court as regent, deposing and killing Xiaomin within the year and ruling through his cousins, Emperors Ming and Wu.

Emperor Wu and the conquest of Qi

In 572, Emperor Wu (Yuwen Yong) ambushed and killed Yuwen Hu and assumed personal power. A rigorous ruler, he strengthened the army through the fubing militia system, promoted Confucian ritual institutions — his regime modelled its offices on the ancient Rites of Zhou — and in 574 launched a suppression of Buddhism (and Daoism), confiscating temple wealth and forcing clergy to return to lay life. In 577 Northern Zhou conquered its great rival, the Northern Qi, uniting northern China for the first time in decades.

Decline and fall

Emperor Wu died in 578 and was succeeded by his son Emperor Xuan, whose erratic rule and early death left the throne to a child, Emperor Jing. The regent Yang Jian, father of Xuan's empress, consolidated power and in 581 forced the boy emperor to abdicate, founding the Sui dynasty and ending Northern Zhou after 24 years.
