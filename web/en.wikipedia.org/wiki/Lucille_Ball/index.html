Lucille Désirée Ball (1911–1989) was an American actress, comedian, and producer whose sitcom I Love Lucy (1951–1957) became one of the most beloved and influential television programmes of all time. As co-founder and later sole head of Desilu Productions, she was the first woman to run a major television studio.

Career

Ball worked as a model and chorus girl before signing with RKO Pictures, where she appeared in dozens of films through the 1930s and 1940s, earning the nickname "Queen of the B's." Success on the radio comedy My Favorite Husband led CBS to adapt it for television; Ball insisted her real-life husband, Cuban bandleader Desi Arnaz, play her husband, and fought to film the show in Hollywood with multiple cameras before a live studio audience, using high-quality film — innovations that defined the sitcom format and made reruns and syndication possible.

Desilu Productions

Desilu produced The Andy Griffith Show, The Dick Van Dyke Show, and Mission: Impossible, and famously green-lit Star Trek. After her divorce from Arnaz, Ball bought out his share and ran the studio herself until selling it in 1967. She continued starring in The Lucy Show and Here's Lucy.

Legacy

Ball won four Emmy Awards and was among the first women inducted into both the Television Hall of Fame and honored with the Presidential Medal of Freedom (posthumously, 1989). Her hometown of Jamestown, New York hosts the Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum and the Lucille Ball Comedy Festival.
