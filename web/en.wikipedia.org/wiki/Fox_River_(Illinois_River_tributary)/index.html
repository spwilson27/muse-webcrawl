Fox River (Illinois River tributary)

The Fox River is a 202-mile-long (325 km) tributary of the Illinois River in the U.S. states of Wisconsin and Illinois. It rises near Menomonee Falls in Waukesha County, Wisconsin, flows southward through the Fox River Valley of northeastern Illinois — including the cities of Elgin, Aurora and St. Charles — and joins the Illinois River at Ottawa, Illinois.

Course

The river begins in southeastern Wisconsin and flows south through Waukesha, Waterford and Burlington before crossing into Illinois near Antioch. In Illinois it passes through the Chain O'Lakes and then along the western edge of the Chicago metropolitan area, through McHenry, Algonquin, Elgin, Geneva, St. Charles, Aurora, Oswego and Yorkville, before turning southwest to its confluence with the Illinois River at Ottawa.

History

The river is named for the Fox (Meskwaki) people who inhabited the region. French explorers travelled its valley in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. In the nineteenth century, dams built for water power made the Fox Valley one of Illinois's earliest industrial regions, and the river towns of Elgin, Aurora and St. Charles grew into manufacturing centres.

Dams and recreation

Fifteen dams impound the river between the Chain O'Lakes and Ottawa, creating a series of pools used for boating and fishing. The Fox River Trail and a chain of riverside parks follow much of the Illinois portion of the river.
