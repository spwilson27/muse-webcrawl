Arketamine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Arketamine is the (R)-enantiomer of ketamine.

Arketamine (INN; developmental code names PCN-101, HR071603), also known as (R)-ketamine or R(–)-ketamine, is the (R)-enantiomer of ketamine. Similarly to racemic ketamine and esketamine (the (S)-enantiomer), arketamine is a dissociative anesthetic and an NMDA receptor antagonist, and it has been investigated for potential antidepressant effects.

Pharmacology

Arketamine has lower affinity for the NMDA receptor than esketamine—roughly several-fold weaker—and correspondingly weaker anesthetic and psychotomimetic potency in preclinical studies. Research, notably by Kenji Hashimoto and colleagues in Japan, suggested that arketamine might produce longer-lasting antidepressant-like effects in rodent models than esketamine, with fewer side effects such as dissociation and abuse liability, possibly acting in part through its metabolite (R)-hydroxynorketamine or mechanisms distinct from NMDA antagonism.

Clinical development

Arketamine has been evaluated in early-phase clinical trials for treatment-resistant depression and other conditions. A formulation known as PCN-101 was developed by Perception Neuroscience; results of a phase 2 trial reported in 2023 did not show a clear separation from placebo on the primary endpoint, and development priorities shifted. The compound has never been marketed as a medicine, and interest in it continues as researchers seek rapid-acting antidepressants with improved safety profiles.
