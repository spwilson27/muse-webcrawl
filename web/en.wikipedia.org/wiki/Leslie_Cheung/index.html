Leslie Cheung

Leslie Cheung Kwok-wing (12 September 1956 – 1 April 2003) was a Hong Kong singer and actor, regarded as one of the founding superstars of Cantopop and one of the greatest Asian screen performers of his generation. During a 26-year career he released over 40 albums and starred in 56 films, celebrated for his androgynous stage persona and for daring, emotionally intense performances.

Career

Cheung rose to fame in the 1980s with hits such as "Monica" and "Wild Wind," rivalling Alan Tam in a chart rivalry that defined the golden age of Cantopop. He briefly retired from music in 1989–1990 amid an emigration wave, then returned in triumph. As an actor he delivered iconic performances in John Woo's A Better Tomorrow (1986), Stanley Kwan's Rouge (1988), Wong Kar-wai's Days of Being Wild (1990, winning the Hong Kong Film Award for Best Actor) and Happy Together (1997), and Chen Kaige's Farewell My Concubine (1993), the only Chinese-language film to win the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

Death and legacy

Cheung, who had long suffered from clinical depression, died by suicide on 1 April 2003, leaping from the Mandarin Oriental Hotel in Hong Kong. His death shocked Asia; thousands of fans gathered in mourning, and annual memorial events continue in Hong Kong on the anniversary. He remains a cultural icon in the Chinese-speaking world, honoured by CNN as one of the five most important Asian pop icons and remembered for his openness about his bisexuality and his long partnership with Daffy Tong.
