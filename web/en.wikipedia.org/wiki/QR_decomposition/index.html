QR decomposition

TypeMatrix decomposition
FactorsOrthogonal Q; upper triangular R
FieldLinear algebra

In linear algebra, a QR decomposition, also known as a QR factorisation, is a decomposition of a matrix A into a product A = QR of an orthogonal matrix Q and an upper triangular matrix R. QR decomposition is often used to solve the linear least squares problem and is the basis for the QR algorithm for computing eigenvalues.

Computation

Several methods compute the QR decomposition. The Gram–Schmidt process orthogonalises the columns of A directly, though the classical version is numerically unstable and is usually replaced by the modified Gram–Schmidt algorithm. Householder reflections annihilate subdiagonal entries column by column with orthogonal reflectors and are the standard method in numerical software for dense matrices. Givens rotations zero out individual entries and are attractive for sparse matrices and parallel implementation. For a square nonsingular matrix with positive diagonal entries in R, the decomposition is unique; more generally it exists for any real (or complex, with Q unitary) matrix.

Applications

To solve an overdetermined system Ax = b in the least-squares sense, one forms R x = QT b and solves by back-substitution, avoiding the numerically poorer normal equations. The decomposition also appears in the QR algorithm for eigenvalues, in solving linear systems, in orthogonalisation procedures, and in signal processing methods such as adaptive filtering.
