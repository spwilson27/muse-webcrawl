Knock Shrine (Irish: Cnoc Mhuire, "Hill of Mary") is a Roman Catholic pilgrimage site in the village of Knock, County Mayo, Ireland, where locals reported an apparition of the Virgin Mary, Saint Joseph, Saint John the Evangelist, and Jesus Christ as the Lamb of God on the gable wall of the parish church on 21 August 1879. It is an international Marian shrine and one of Ireland's most visited religious sites, drawing around a million pilgrims annually.

The apparition

On the evening of 21 August 1879, in heavy rain, fifteen people aged from five to seventy-five witnessed a silent tableau on the south gable of St John the Baptist's church: Mary, dressed in white with a crown, her hands raised in prayer; Joseph to her right; John, in bishop's vestments, to her left; and beside them an altar bearing a lamb and a cross, surrounded by angels. The witnesses watched for about two hours, reciting the rosary; remarkably, the ground around them and the gable remained dry despite the rain. Two ecclesiastical commissions of inquiry (1879 and 1936) found the testimony of the witnesses trustworthy.

Development of the shrine

Knock quickly became a place of pilgrimage, with reports of healings. The golden age of building came under Monsignor James Horan in the 1970s: the Basilica of Our Lady, Queen of Ireland — one of the largest churches in Ireland, seating about 10,000 — was completed in 1976, and Ireland West Airport (then Horan International Airport) opened nearby in 1985 at Horan's instigation. Pope John Paul II visited Knock in 1979, the centenary of the apparition, presenting a Golden Rose; Mother Teresa visited in 1993. Pope Francis visited in August 2018 during the World Meeting of Families. In 2021 Pope Francis formally recognised Knock as an International Shrine of Special Marian and Eucharistic Devotion.

Features

The shrine grounds include the Apparition Chapel, built around the original gable wall; the basilica; the Church of St John the Baptist; a museum; and a reconciliation chapel. The apparition is unique among major Marian shrines in that the figures did not speak or deliver a message, and in the prominence of the Lamb of God and altar, which gives the shrine a strongly Eucharistic character.
