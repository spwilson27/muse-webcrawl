Charismatic movement

The charismatic movement is an international trend within historic Christian churches — including Roman Catholic, Lutheran, Anglican, Methodist, Reformed and Orthodox traditions — characterised by the adoption of beliefs and practices similar to Pentecostalism, notably baptism in the Holy Spirit, speaking in tongues, prophecy and divine healing. It is conventionally dated from 1960, when Dennis Bennett, rector of St Mark's Episcopal Church in Van Nuys, California, publicly announced that he had received the baptism in the Holy Spirit.

History

Unlike classical Pentecostals, who formed separate denominations in the early twentieth century, charismatics sought renewal within their existing churches. The movement spread rapidly through mainline Protestant churches in the 1960s and reached the Roman Catholic Church from 1967, beginning with prayer groups among faculty and students at Duquesne University in Pittsburgh. The Catholic Charismatic Renewal received cautious encouragement from successive popes while remaining a lay-led movement of prayer groups, conferences and covenant communities.

Historians sometimes describe Pentecostalism as the "first wave" of the modern Spirit movement, the charismatic movement as the "second wave", and the neo-charismatic and Vineyard movements of the 1980s — emphasising signs and wonders, associated with figures such as John Wimber — as a "third wave".

Beliefs and practices

Charismatics emphasise a personal experience of the Holy Spirit, expressive worship with contemporary music and raised hands, prayer for healing, and the contemporary exercise of the spiritual gifts (charismata) listed in 1 Corinthians 12–14, including glossolalia, interpretation of tongues, prophecy and words of knowledge. Most charismatics retain the doctrines and structures of their parent traditions, including infant baptism and episcopal or presbyterian church government.

Scale

Together, Pentecostal and charismatic Christians number in the hundreds of millions worldwide and constitute one of the fastest-growing segments of Christianity, especially in Latin America, sub-Saharan Africa and Asia.
