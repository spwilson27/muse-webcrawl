Fabella

Lateral radiograph of the knee with a fabella visible posteriorly

The fabella is a small sesamoid bone located in the lateral head of the gastrocnemius muscle, behind the lateral femoral condyle of the knee. Its name comes from Latin faba, "bean", with the diminutive suffix; it is sometimes called the "bean bone".

Like the patella, the fabella is embedded within a tendon, and its posterior surface may be covered with cartilage that articulates with the femur. Its prevalence varies widely between populations and studies: it has historically been reported in roughly 10–30% of people, though some imaging studies report higher figures, and a 2019 analysis suggested its prevalence in knees worldwide had increased over the preceding century and a half, possibly in association with changes in nutrition and body size.

Clinical significance

The fabella is usually an incidental finding on knee radiographs, where it must be distinguished from a loose body or fracture fragment. In a minority of people it is associated with posterolateral knee pain known as fabella syndrome, in which the bone irritates surrounding structures during extension; it may also be implicated in nerve compression of the common peroneal nerve, or in irritation following total knee replacement. Treatment is usually conservative, with surgical excision (fabellectomy) reserved for persistent cases.

Comparative anatomy

Sesamoid bones in the gastrocnemius tendon are common in other mammals, where they form a regular part of the knee joint; in humans the fabella is regarded as a variable and partly vestigial structure.
