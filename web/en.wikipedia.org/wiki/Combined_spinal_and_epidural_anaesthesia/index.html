Combined spinal and epidural anaesthesia

SpecialtyAnaesthesiology
UsesSurgery below the umbilicus; labour analgesia

A combined spinal–epidural kit

Combined spinal and epidural anaesthesia (CSE) is a regional anaesthetic technique that combines the rapid, dense block of a spinal anaesthetic with the flexibility of an indwelling epidural catheter, which allows the block to be extended in duration and used for postoperative analgesia.

Technique

In the most common "needle-through-needle" technique, an epidural (Tuohy) needle is first placed in the epidural space using loss of resistance. A long, fine spinal needle is then passed through it to puncture the dura and inject local anaesthetic (often with an opioid) into the cerebrospinal fluid. The spinal needle is withdrawn and an epidural catheter is threaded through the Tuohy needle and left in place. Alternatives include separate needles at different interspaces, or a double-lumen ("sequential") approach.

Advantages and disadvantages

CSE offers the fast onset and reliability of a spinal with the ability to top up or prolong anaesthesia via the catheter, and permits lower intrathecal doses, which may reduce hypotension. Drawbacks include the inability to test the epidural catheter immediately, a small risk that the catheter migrates through the dural hole, post-dural-puncture headache, and the general risks of neuraxial blockade such as infection, bleeding, and nerve injury. A "walking epidural" variant using low-dose mixtures is popular in obstetrics because it relieves labour pain while preserving some mobility.
