Sexual Offences (Amendment) Act 2000

The Sexual Offences (Amendment) Act 2000 (c. 44) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that equalised the age of consent for homosexual and heterosexual sex in England, Wales and Scotland at 16 (the age in Northern Ireland, equalised separately, had been 17). It also created the offence of abuse of a position of trust. The bill was enacted using the Parliament Acts after the House of Lords rejected it repeatedly — only the third such use since 1949.

Background

Male homosexual acts were partially decriminalised in England and Wales by the Sexual Offences Act 1967 with an age of consent of 21, against 16 for heterosexual acts. In 1994 MPs voted to reduce it to 18; an amendment moved by Edwina Currie to equalise at 16 was defeated. The European Court of Human Rights found in the case of Chris Morris, and in the Sutherland case, that the differential age breached the right to respect for private life, and the Labour government committed to legislate. Ann Keen MP introduced the amendment in the 1997–98 session with government support.

Parliamentary struggle

The bill passed the Commons with large majorities on free votes but was defeated in the Lords in 1998, led by Baroness Young. Reintroduced in 1999 and again rejected by the Lords, the government invoked the Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949, and the bill was presented for royal assent in November 2000 without the Lords' agreement. The Speaker certified that the requirements of the Parliament Acts had been met. A judicial review challenge to the use of the procedure in connection with the Act's passage was unsuccessful.

Provisions

From 8 January 2001 the age of consent in England, Wales and Scotland was 16 regardless of the sexes of the participants; acts of buggery and gross indecency between consenting men aged 16 or over were decriminalised accordingly. To answer concerns about the protection of young people, the Act introduced the offence of abuse of position of trust, making it an offence for a person over 18 in a position of trust (such as a teacher or care worker) to engage in sexual activity with a person under 18 in their charge. The law was later consolidated in the Sexual Offences Act 2003.
