TRIPS Agreement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS) is an international legal agreement between all member nations of the World Trade Organization (WTO), in force from 1 January 1995 as Annex 1C of the Marrakech Agreement. It sets minimum standards for the protection and enforcement of copyright (incorporating the Berne Convention), trademarks, geographical indications, industrial designs, patents (a 20-year term; patentability in all fields of technology), trade secrets and plant varieties, and subjects them to WTO dispute settlement with trade sanctions — the innovation that made IP a trade discipline.

TRIPS was driven by IP-exporting industries of the US, Europe and Japan and contested from the start by developing countries. The access-to-medicines conflict — over patented antiretrovirals during the AIDS crisis — produced the Doha Declaration (2001) affirming flexibilities (compulsory licensing for public health) and eventually the Article 31bis amendment (in force 2017) allowing export under compulsory licence; a COVID-era ministerial decision on vaccine patents (2022) was narrower than the waiver proposed. TRIPS remains the floor of global IP law, supplemented by “TRIPS-plus” obligations in bilateral trade agreements.
