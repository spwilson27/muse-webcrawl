The Savannah River Site (SRS) is a nuclear reservation of the United States Department of Energy in South Carolina, on the Savannah River near Aiken. Established in 1950 during the early Cold War, it was built to produce plutonium and tritium for the United States nuclear weapons programme, operating five heavy-water production reactors and two chemical separation plants at its peak.

The 800 km² reservation is one of the largest sites in the U.S. nuclear complex.

History

In 1950 the Atomic Energy Commission announced that the DuPont company would build and operate a plant on a vast tract of South Carolina farmland; the towns of Ellenton and Dunbarton and several smaller communities were displaced, with about 6,000 residents relocated. The site's reactors (designated R, P, L, K and C) came online between 1953 and 1955, producing weapons materials for three decades; tritium production continues to the present in support of the nuclear stockpile. The last production reactors shut down in the late 1980s.

Present day

Since the end of the Cold War the site's principal missions have been environmental remediation of Cold War waste — including the vitrification of high-level waste at the Defense Waste Processing Facility — the management and disposition of surplus plutonium, and national laboratory research at the Savannah River National Laboratory. The site employs roughly 11,000 people.
