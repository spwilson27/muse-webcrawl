Sodium acetate

Sodium acetate, CH3COONa, also abbreviated NaOAc, is the sodium salt of acetic acid. This colourless deliquescent salt has a wide range of uses.

Applications

In the food industry it is used as a seasoning (giving salt-and-vinegar crisps their flavour) and as a preservative, with E number E262. In chemistry, mixtures of sodium acetate and acetic acid form a buffer solution for maintaining a relatively constant pH. In medicine it is used in intravenous fluids and in dialysis.

Thermal storage

Sodium acetate trihydrate melts at 58 °C and can be supercooled well below its melting point. Crystallisation is exothermic, and the salt is used in reusable hand warmers and heating pads: a metal disc in a supersaturated solution triggers crystallisation, releasing heat. The property of forming a supersaturated solution that crystallises into needle-like crystals has earned it the nickname "hot ice" in science demonstrations.

Production

It is produced industrially by reacting acetic acid with sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate, and in the laboratory by neutralising vinegar with baking soda.
