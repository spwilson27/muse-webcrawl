Robert LeRoy Parker (April 13, 1866 – November 7, 1908), better known as Butch Cassidy, was an American train and bank robber and the leader of a gang of outlaws known as the "Wild Bunch" in the American Old West. He participated in criminal activity for more than a decade at the end of the 19th century, but the pressures of being pursued by lawmen, notably the Pinkerton detective agency, forced him and his partner Harry Longabaugh — the "Sundance Kid" — to flee to South America.

Early life

Parker was born in Beaver, Utah Territory, the eldest of thirteen children of English Mormon immigrants Maximillian Parker and Ann Campbell Gillies. He grew up on his parents' ranch near Circleville, Utah. In his teens he worked at ranches and fell under the influence of a cattle rustler named Mike Cassidy, whose surname he later adopted. He left home and drifted through the ranch country of Utah, Colorado, and Wyoming, working as a butcher in Rock Springs, Wyoming — the job from which the nickname "Butch" derives.

Criminal career

Parker's first recorded major crime was the robbery of the San Miguel Valley Bank in Telluride, Colorado, on June 24, 1889, in which he and accomplices escaped with about $21,000. He was imprisoned in Wyoming in 1894 for horse theft and possibly cattle rustling; released in January 1896, he formed the gang that became known as the Wild Bunch, whose members included Harry Longabaugh (the Sundance Kid), Ben Kilpatrick, Harvey Logan (Kid Curry), and William Carver. The gang robbed banks and trains across the West, most notably the Union Pacific train near Wilcox, Wyoming, in June 1899, and relied on hideouts such as the Hole-in-the-Wall in Wyoming and Robbers Roost in Utah.

Flight to South America and death

In 1901 Parker and Longabaugh, accompanied by Longabaugh's companion Etta Place, sailed from New York to Buenos Aires, Argentina. There they purchased land near Cholila in Patagonia and lived openly as ranchers for several years before resuming robberies, including bank holdups in Argentina. By 1905 they were again fugitives. In November 1908 the pair were tracked to the mining town of San Vicente in southern Bolivia after robbing a mule train carrying a mining payroll. Cornered by Bolivian soldiers and police, they were killed in a gunfight on the night of November 6–7, 1908 — though the precise circumstances, and even the identification of the bodies, have been disputed ever since, giving rise to persistent legends that Parker survived and returned to the United States.

In popular culture

Cassidy's life was dramatised in the 1969 film Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid, starring Paul Newman as Cassidy and Robert Redford as the Sundance Kid, which won four Academy Awards and fixed the pair's image as charming, wisecracking outlaws in the popular imagination. The film's portrayal, while romanticised, drew on genuine episodes including the Wilcox robbery and the flight to Bolivia.
