Data integrity

Data integrity is the maintenance of, and the assurance of, the accuracy and consistency of data over its entire life cycle. It is a critical aspect of the design, implementation, and usage of any system that stores, processes, or retrieves data. Data that has integrity is said to be protected against unintended modification — whether from hardware faults, software errors, or malicious acts.

Types of integrity in databases

  • Entity integrity — every table has a primary key, and that key is unique and never null.
  • Referential integrity — foreign keys reference existing rows, so relationships between tables cannot be orphaned.
  • Domain integrity — values in a column fall within a defined set, format, or range, enforced by data types, check constraints, and rules.
  • User-defined integrity — business rules beyond the above categories, implemented in triggers, stored procedures, or application logic.

Physical and logical integrity

Physical integrity concerns protecting data against environmental and hardware failures — power loss, storage corruption, and natural disasters — using measures such as redundant storage, uninterruptible power supplies, and error-correcting memory. Logical integrity concerns keeping data correct in context: checksums and hash functions detect accidental changes, while database transactions and ACID properties prevent inconsistent states when operations fail partway through.

Regulatory context

In regulated industries such as pharmaceuticals, data integrity is a formal compliance requirement. Guidance from regulators such as the FDA summarises good practice in the ALCOA principles: records should be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, and accurate.
