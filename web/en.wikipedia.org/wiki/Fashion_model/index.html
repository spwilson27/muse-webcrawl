Fashion model

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fashion model is a person engaged to display, advertise, and promote clothing, accessories, and beauty products, or to serve as a visual aid for artists. Modelling is generally divided into two broad categories: fashion (editorial and runway) work and commercial work, which have different physical expectations and markets.

Types of modelling

Runway models present designers' collections at fashion shows and are typically required to meet strict height and size standards — conventionally at least 5 ft 9 in (175 cm) for women and 6 ft (183 cm) for men. Editorial models appear in fashion magazines such as Vogue and Harper's Bazaar. Commercial models advertise everyday products and catalogues and work across a wider range of ages, sizes, and looks; specialised fields include plus-size, petite, fitness, parts (hands, feet), and fit modelling.

History

The profession began in the nineteenth century with the house models ("mannequins") of couturier Charles Frederick Worth. Photography created the fashion photograph and, by the mid-twentieth century, celebrated models such as Lisa Fonssagrives, Dovima, and Twiggy. The 1980s–1990s produced the "supermodels" — Naomi Campbell, Cindy Crawford, Linda Evangelista, Christy Turlington, and Claudia Schiffer — who became global celebrities. In the twenty-first century, social media reshaped casting and fame, giving rise to influencer-models discovered and promoted online.

Industry and criticism

Models are usually represented by agencies in the fashion capitals of Paris, Milan, London, and New York, and work through castings and "go-sees". The industry has faced persistent criticism over extreme thinness requirements and their association with eating disorders, leading to reforms such as health-certificate laws in France and Israel and minimum-age and BMI rules adopted by some publishers and fashion weeks; diversity in size, age, and ethnicity has become a continuing debate.
