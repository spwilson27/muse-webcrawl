Szekszárd is a small city in southern Hungary and the county seat of Tolna County. Lying west of the Danube among the Szekszárd Hills, it is one of Hungary's oldest settlements and the centre of one of the country's most historic wine regions, renowned for its full-bodied red wines.

History

The site was inhabited in Roman times and was first mentioned in a document of 1015. King Béla I founded a Benedictine abbey here in 1061 and was buried in its church. The town suffered under Ottoman occupation in the 16th and 17th centuries and was later repopulated in part by German settlers, whose viticultural traditions shaped the local wine culture. The poet Mihály Babits, one of Hungary's major literary figures, was born in Szekszárd in 1883.

Wine region

The Szekszárd wine region, with roughly 2,000 hectares of vineyards on loess hills, is famous for Bikavér ("Bull's Blood"), a blended red wine based on Kékfrankos and Kadarka, and for some of Hungary's most respected varietal reds. Szekszárd and Eger are the two regions entitled to produce Bikavér.
