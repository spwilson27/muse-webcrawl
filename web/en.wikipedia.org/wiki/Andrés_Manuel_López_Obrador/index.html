Andrés Manuel López Obrador (born 13 November 1953), commonly known by his initials AMLO, is a Mexican politician who served as the 65th president of Mexico from 2018 to 2024. A dominant figure of the Mexican left, he campaigned on ending corruption, reducing poverty and what he called the "Fourth Transformation" of public life.

Early career

Born in the village of Tepetitán in Tabasco, López Obrador studied political science at the National Autonomous University of Mexico. He began in the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI), worked in indigenous affairs in Tabasco, and ran twice for governor of Tabasco, alleging fraud in the 1994 election.

Head of Government and presidential campaigns

As a leader of the Party of the Democratic Revolution (PRD), he served as Head of Government of Mexico City (2000–2005), where he gained popularity for social programmes and daily morning press conferences. He narrowly lost the 2006 presidential election to Felipe Calderón amid disputed counts, declaring himself "legitimate president" in protest, and lost again in 2012 to Enrique Peña Nieto.

Presidency

Founding the Morena party, he won the 2018 election by a landslide. His administration raised pensions and the minimum wage, pursued austerity for officialdom, created the National Guard, and launched large infrastructure projects such as the Tren Maya and the Dos Bocas refinery. Critics accused him of concentrating power and clashing with autonomous institutions; supporters credited him with reducing poverty. He was succeeded in 2024 by his protégée Claudia Sheinbaum.
