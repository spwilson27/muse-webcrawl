Mahapajapati Gotami

Mahapajapati Gotami

Mahapajapati Gotami (Pali: Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī) was the younger sister of Maya, the Buddha's mother, and became his aunt and foster mother after Maya's death seven days after his birth. She is revered as the founder of the order of Buddhist nuns (bhikkhunis): according to tradition, she persuaded the Buddha — with the intercession of his attendant Ananda — to permit the ordination of women.

Life

Mahapajapati was married to King Suddhodana of the Shakyas alongside her sister. After Maya died, she raised the infant Siddhartha as her own, entrusting her own children, Nanda and Sundari Nanda, partly to nurses. After Suddhodana's death she resolved to renounce the world.

Founding the nuns' order

The canonical account relates that Mahapajapati, accompanied by 500 Shakyan women, walked to Vesali and asked the Buddha three times for admission to the Sangha; refused, she persisted, and Ananda interceded. The Buddha then allowed the ordination, subject to eight special rules (garudhammas) subordinating nuns to monks. She attained arahantship and is honoured as foremost among the nuns; verses attributed to her appear in the Therigatha.

Legacy

Her story is central to modern debates and movements concerning the full ordination of women in Theravada and Tibetan Buddhism, where the bhikkhuni lineage had lapsed and its revival has been a major development in contemporary Buddhism.
