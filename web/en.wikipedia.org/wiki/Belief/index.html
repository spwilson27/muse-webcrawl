Belief

A belief is a mental attitude that some proposition is true, or that some state of affairs is the case. In epistemology, philosophers use the term "belief" to refer to attitudes about the world which can be either true or false; to believe something is to take it to be true. Beliefs are central to theories of knowledge, action and mind: along with desires, they explain why people act as they do, and in the classical analysis knowledge is defined as justified true belief — an analysis challenged by Gettier cases.

Belief in philosophy

Beliefs are usually understood as propositional attitudes: states of a subject directed at a proposition, such as believing that it will rain. Philosophical debates concern their ontology (whether they are dispositions to act, internal representations, or interpretive constructs), their degrees (belief admits of confidence levels, modelled in Bayesian epistemology as credences), and their relation to certainty, doubt and acceptance. Dispositionalists hold that to believe is to be disposed to act and speak as if the proposition were true; representationalists posit mental representations; interpretivists such as Daniel Dennett treat belief attribution as a predictive strategy rather than a description of inner mechanisms.

Formation and revision

Beliefs are formed through perception, testimony, memory, inference and socialisation, and revised in response to evidence — a process studied both normatively (Bayesian updating, coherence theories) and descriptively. Cognitive psychology documents systematic deviations: confirmation bias, belief perseverance, motivated reasoning and cultural cognition can make beliefs resistant to refutation. In psychopathology, fixed false beliefs are called delusions.

Belief in religion and everyday usage

In religious contexts, "belief" often denotes faith — trust in or commitment to a deity, doctrine or creed — rather than mere intellectual assent, and systems of religious doctrine are themselves called beliefs. Ordinary usage also distinguishes believing that something is true from believing in someone or something (trusting them or valuing them). Surveys of religious belief, political belief and conspiracy belief are staples of social science.
