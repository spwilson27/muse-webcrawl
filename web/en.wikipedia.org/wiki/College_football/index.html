College football

College football is gridiron football played by student-athletes at colleges and universities mainly in the United States and Canada. It was through college football's heavy usage that American football rules first gained popularity in the United States.

History

The first intercollegiate game was played on 6 November 1869 between Rutgers and Princeton under soccer-like rules. Walter Camp of Yale shaped the modern game in the 1880s, introducing the line of scrimmage and the system of downs. The forward pass was legalised in 1906, partly in response to a wave of deaths and serious injuries.

Structure

The top level, the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS), is organised into conferences such as the SEC, Big Ten, ACC, and Big 12. The season culminates in bowl games and the College Football Playoff, which expanded to 12 teams in the 2024 season. The Heisman Trophy is awarded annually to the most outstanding player.

Culture

College football is deeply embedded in university life, with marching bands, tailgating, intense rivalries such as Michigan–Ohio State and the Iron Bowl, and stadiums among the largest in the world — Michigan Stadium seats over 107,000.
