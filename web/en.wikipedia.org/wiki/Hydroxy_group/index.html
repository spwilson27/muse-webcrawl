Hydroxy group

A hydroxy or hydroxyl group is a functional group with the chemical formula –OH, composed of one oxygen atom covalently bonded to one hydrogen atom. It is the defining group of the alcohols and phenols, and occurs in sugars, many other biomolecules, and in the hydroxide ion OH− that gives bases their properties.

Properties

The hydroxy group is polar and can both donate and accept hydrogen bonds, which is why small hydroxy compounds such as methanol and ethanol are miscible with water and why sugars are highly soluble. In alcohols the group is not appreciably acidic (pKa ≈ 16–18), but in phenols and carboxylic acids it is more acidic, and it should not be confused with the free hydroxyl radical, •OH, a highly reactive species important in atmospheric chemistry and radiation damage. The presence of a hydroxy group is readily detected spectroscopically by a broad O–H stretching absorption in the infrared near 3200–3600 cm−1.

Reactions

Hydroxy groups are interconverted in many standard transformations: alcohols are oxidised to carbonyl compounds, deprotonated to alkoxides by strong bases, esterified with carboxylic acids, and converted to better leaving groups (e.g. tosylates or halides) for substitution reactions. Adding a hydroxy group to a molecule is called hydroxylation, a key step in metabolism carried out by enzymes such as cytochrome P450.
