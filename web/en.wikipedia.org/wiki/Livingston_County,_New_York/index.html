Livingston County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the Finger Lakes region south of Rochester. As of the 2020 census, the population was 61,834. Its county seat is Geneseo. The county is named for Robert R. Livingston, a member of the committee that drafted the Declaration of Independence.

History

Livingston County was formed in 1821 from parts of Genesee and Ontario counties. The Genesee River valley had long been home to the Seneca people, whose principal towns were destroyed during the Sullivan Expedition of 1779. After the American Revolution the area was opened to settlement, and the county became known for agriculture, including the Genesee Valley's nursery industry centred on Mount Morris.

Geography

The county covers about 640 square miles. The Genesee River runs northward through it, and Letchworth State Park — often called the "Grand Canyon of the East" — lies partly within the county along the river's gorge.

Communities

Towns include Avon, Caledonia, Conesus, Geneseo, Groveland, Leicester, Lima, Livonia, Mount Morris, North Dansville, Nunda, Ossian, Portage, Sparta, Springwater, West Sparta, and York. The State University of New York at Geneseo is a major employer and cultural presence.
