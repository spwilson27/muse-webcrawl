Watson Brake

LocationOuachita Parish, Louisiana, United States
PeriodMiddle Archaic, c. 3500–3000 BC
Features11 mounds connected by a ridge

Watson Brake is an archaeological site in present-day Ouachita Parish, Louisiana, from the Middle Archaic period. Dated to about 5,400 years ago (circa 3500 BC), Watson Brake is considered the oldest earthwork mound complex in North America — older than the Egyptian pyramids and Stonehenge.

The site consists of eleven mounds, up to about 7.6 metres (25 feet) high, connected by a ridge forming an oval roughly 260 metres (850 feet) across. It was built by a society of hunter-fisher-gatherers, challenging the earlier assumption that monumental architecture required agricultural societies with centralised leadership.

Discovery and research

The site was brought to professional attention in the 1980s by local resident Reca Jones, and excavations led by Joe Saunders in the 1990s produced radiocarbon dates that demonstrated its great antiquity. Part of the site is protected, though portions remain in private ownership.
