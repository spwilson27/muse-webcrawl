Labor relations

Labor relations is the field of study and practice concerned with the relationship between employers, employees, and the trade unions and government bodies that regulate that relationship. It encompasses collective bargaining, contract administration, grievance handling, union organizing, and the legal frameworks — such as the National Labor Relations Act in the United States — that govern representation and industrial action.

Scope

Practitioners in labor relations negotiate collective bargaining agreements covering wages, hours, benefits, and working conditions; administer those agreements through grievance and arbitration procedures; and manage responses to union organizing campaigns and strikes. The field overlaps with human resource management but is distinguished by its collective, rather than individual, focus and by the involvement of third parties such as labour boards, mediators, and arbitrators.

Approaches

Scholars analyse labor relations through frameworks such as John Dunlop's industrial relations systems model and the strategic choice framework of Kochan, Katz, and McKersie, which distinguish workplace, bargaining, and strategic levels of decision-making. Traditions vary widely by country, from the adversarial model of North America to the works-council and co-determination systems of Germany and the enterprise unions of Japan.
