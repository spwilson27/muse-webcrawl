Public library

A public library is a library that is accessible by the general public and is usually funded from public sources, such as taxes or government grants, and operated by civil servants or a library board. Unlike private or subscription libraries, public libraries lend books and other media free of charge to residents of their community and provide free access to information as a public good.

History

Libraries open to some form of public use existed in the ancient world, but the modern public library emerged in the nineteenth century with the growth of mass literacy and democratic ideals. In Britain, the Public Libraries Act 1850 allowed municipal boroughs to fund libraries from a penny rate; Manchester opened the first rate-supported free library in 1852. In the United States, the Boston Public Library (founded 1848) pioneered tax-supported service, and the steel magnate Andrew Carnegie funded the construction of more than 2,500 library buildings worldwide, on condition that communities provide sites and maintain the service from public funds.

Services

Core services include lending collections of books, periodicals and audio-visual and digital media (including e-books), reference and information services, children's and young adult programming, public computers and internet access, reading rooms and study space, and community events from storytimes to job-search help. Many systems lend non-traditional items such as tools, musical instruments and museum passes, and provide meeting rooms for local groups. Library collections are organised by systems such as the Dewey Decimal Classification or the Library of Congress Classification.

Governance and funding

Public libraries are typically governed by boards under municipal or county authority, sometimes as independent library districts with their own tax levies. Professional bodies such as the American Library Association and IFLA promote standards and defend principles of intellectual freedom, embodied in documents like the Library Bill of Rights. In the twenty-first century public libraries have faced funding pressures and debates over book challenges, while also expanding roles as digital access points and community hubs.
