Konrad Alexander Friedrich Duden (January 3, 1829 – August 1, 1911) was a German philologist and teacher who founded the well-known German language dictionary bearing his name, the Duden.

Life

Duden was born near Wesel in the Prussian Rhine Province and studied history, German philology, and classical philology at Bonn. He worked as a private tutor in Italy and then as a schoolteacher, becoming headmaster of the gymnasium in Hersfeld in 1876 and later in Wiesbaden. Concerned by the chaotic state of German spelling in schools, he published his Complete Orthographic Dictionary of the German Language (Vollständiges Orthographisches Wörterbuch der deutschen Sprache) in 1880, offering a single authoritative spelling list.

The Duden dictionary

Duden's dictionary, popularly known simply as "der Duden," became the prescriptive standard for German orthography. In 1902 the German federal council made the Duden's rules officially binding for government administration, and the dictionary consolidated the spelling reforms debated at the Orthographic Conference of 1901 in Berlin. The Duden has been published in many editions by Bibliographisches Institut in Mannheim and remains the best-known reference work for the German language.
