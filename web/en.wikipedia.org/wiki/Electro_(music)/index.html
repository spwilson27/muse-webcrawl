Electro (music)

The Roland TR-808 defines electro's drum sound

Stylistic originsHip-hop, funk, disco, electronic music, Kraftwerk
Cultural originsEarly 1980s, New York City and Los Angeles

Electro (or electro-funk) is a genre of electronic dance music and early hip-hop directly influenced by the Roland TR-808 drum machine, funk music, and the German group Kraftwerk. Records in the genre typically feature heavy electronic sounds, robot-like vocoder vocals, and drum-machine beats with little or no rapping, though early electro often included MCing. The style emerged in New York in the early 1980s and became a foundation of hip-hop and electronic dance music culture.

History

The breakthrough record was Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" (1982), produced by Arthur Baker and John Robie, which interpolated Kraftwerk's "Trans-Europe Express" and "Numbers" over an 808 beat. It was followed by classics such as "Clear" by Cybotron (Juan Atkins and Rik Davis), Jonzun Crew's "Pack Jam", Mantronix, and the West Coast scene of Egyptian Lover and the World Class Wreckin' Cru. Electro's themes — space, robots, technology, street life — were matched by the popping and boogaloo dancing of crews such as the Electric Boogaloos.

Decline and revival

By the late 1980s hip-hop moved toward sampling and harder lyrical styles, and electro mutated into Miami bass, freestyle and techno (Cybotron's Atkins went on to found Detroit techno). A revival came with 1990s–2000s electroclash and artists such as Anthony Rother and Drexciya's cult following, and the genre's beats and aesthetics remain influential in contemporary electronic and pop music.
