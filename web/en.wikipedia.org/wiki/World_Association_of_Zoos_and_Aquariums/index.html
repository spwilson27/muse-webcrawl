World Association of Zoos and Aquariums (WAZA)
Founded: 1935 (as IUCN-affiliated union of zoo directors)
Headquarters: Barcelona, Spain
Type: International NGO

The World Association of Zoos and Aquariums (WAZA) is the global alliance of regional associations, national federations, and individual zoos and aquariums dedicated to the care and conservation of animals and their habitats. Its members include leading zoos, aquariums, associations, affiliate organizations, and corporate partners in over 50 countries, collectively receiving hundreds of millions of visitors each year.

History and mission

WAZA traces its origins to the International Union of Directors of Zoological Gardens, founded in Basel, Switzerland, in 1935. The modern association adopted its present name in 2000. WAZA promotes cooperation among zoos and aquariums in animal care, breeding of endangered species, environmental education, and wildlife conservation, and it sets standards through codes of ethics and animal welfare guidelines. It works in partnership with the IUCN, CITES, and other conservation bodies, and coordinates global strategies such as the World Zoo and Aquarium Conservation Strategy.

Activities

WAZA supports conservation breeding programmes, publishes a journal and conservation case studies, and campaigns on issues including amphibian declines, sustainable palm oil, and marine litter. Membership requires adherence to WAZA's codes of ethics and welfare standards, and the association works with regional bodies such as EAZA, AZA, and ZAA.
