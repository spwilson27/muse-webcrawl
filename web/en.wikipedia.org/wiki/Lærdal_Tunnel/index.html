The Lærdal Tunnel (Norwegian: Lærdalstunnelen) is a 24.51-kilometre-long (15.23 mi) road tunnel connecting the municipalities of Lærdal and Aurland in Vestland county, Norway. Carrying European route E16, it is the world's longest road tunnel, surpassing the Gotthard Road Tunnel in Switzerland. Construction began in 1995 and the tunnel was opened by King Harald V on 27 November 2000; traffic began the following day.

Construction

The tunnel forms part of the main road between Oslo and Bergen and provides a ferry-free, winter-reliable alternative to the mountain passes. A total of about 2,500,000 cubic metres of rock was removed during construction, which cost approximately 1.082 billion Norwegian kroner. The tunnel is blasted through gneiss and is equipped with ventilation shafts, including a 18-kilometre connection to an air treatment plant, to keep air quality within acceptable limits along its length.

Design and safety

Because of the tunnel's exceptional length, special measures were taken to counter driver monotony and claustrophobia. The tunnel is divided into four sections by three large mountain caverns, lit with blue and yellow lighting intended to evoke a sunrise, which give drivers visual variety and a place to stop or turn. Rumble strips, emergency phones and extinguishers are placed at intervals, there are emergency lay-bys every 500 metres, and speed cameras enforce the 80 km/h limit. Mobile phone coverage and radio broadcasts are available throughout. The journey through the tunnel takes about 20 minutes.
