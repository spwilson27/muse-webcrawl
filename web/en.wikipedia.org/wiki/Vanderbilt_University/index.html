Vanderbilt University

Vanderbilt University (informally Vandy or VU) is a private research university in Nashville, Tennessee, United States. Founded in 1873, it was named in honour of shipping and railroad magnate Cornelius Vanderbilt, who provided its initial $1 million endowment in the hope that his gift would help to heal the sectional wounds inflicted by the Civil War.

History

The university was chartered by Methodist bishops of the South in 1873 and opened in 1875. It severed ties with the Methodist Church in 1914. In the 20th century it grew into a major research institution, desegregated in the 1950s–60s, and absorbed Peabody College in 1979.

Campus

The 330-acre campus near downtown Nashville is a national arboretum with more than 6,000 trees. Landmarks include Kirkland Hall, modeled on Italian architecture, and the residential Commons for first-year students.

Academics

Vanderbilt comprises ten schools and colleges, including Arts and Science, Blair School of Music, Engineering, Peabody College of education, Law, Medicine, Nursing, Divinity, Owen School of Management and a graduate school. It enrols roughly 13,000–14,000 students and is classified for very high research activity. Its medical centre is a leading academic hospital.

Athletics and student life

The Vanderbilt Commodores compete in NCAA Division I, primarily in the Southeastern Conference. The university is also noted for baseball championships, a cappella and service organisations.
