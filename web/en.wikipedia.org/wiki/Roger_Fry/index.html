Roger Fry

BornRoger Eliot Fry, 14 December 1866, London, England
Died9 September 1934 (aged 67), London
OccupationArt critic, painter, curator

Roger Eliot Fry (14 December 1866 – 9 September 1934) was an English painter and art critic and the central figure in bringing modern art — particularly Post-Impressionism — to British attention. His 1910 exhibition "Manet and the Post-Impressionists" at the Grafton Galleries shocked London and reshaped British taste; Fry's criticism, emphasizing formal qualities over narrative content, made him one of the most influential art critics of the early 20th century.

Early life and career

Fry was born in London to a prominent Quaker family and educated at Clifton College and King's College, Cambridge, where he became an Apostle. Trained as a painter, he first made his reputation as a scholar of Italian Old Masters, co-founding The Burlington Magazine in 1903. From 1905 to 1910 he was curator of paintings and then consultant at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, quarrelling with patron J. P. Morgan before being dismissed.

Post-Impressionist exhibitions

In 1910 Fry organized "Manet and the Post-Impressionists," showing Cézanne, Van Gogh, Gauguin, Matisse, and Picasso to a bewildered and often hostile London public; he coined the term "Post-Impressionism" for the occasion. A second exhibition in 1912 broadened the scope. Fry's championship of these artists, defended in essays such as "An Essay in Aesthetics" (1909) — which articulated the idea of "significant form," later developed by Clive Bell — helped seed British modernism, including the Vorticist and Bloomsbury circles.

Omega Workshops and later life

In 1913 Fry founded the Omega Workshops in Fitzroy Square, employing artists including Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant to produce Post-Impressionist furniture, textiles, and decorative objects; the venture closed in 1919. His later books include Vision and Design (1920), Transformations (1926), and monographs on Cézanne (1927) and Matisse (1930). In 1933 he became Slade Professor of Fine Art at Cambridge. Virginia Woolf, a close friend, published his biography in 1940. Fry died in 1934 after a fall at home.
