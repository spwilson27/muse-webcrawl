Regulation (European Union)

A regulation is a legal act of the European Union that becomes immediately enforceable as law in all member states simultaneously. Under Article 288 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, a regulation is binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all member states.

Difference from directives

Regulations differ from directives, which state objectives that member states must achieve but leave the form and methods of implementation to national authorities. A regulation, by contrast, requires no national transposing legislation and overrides conflicting national law.

Legal basis and adoption

Regulations may be adopted by the Council of the European Union and the European Parliament under the ordinary legislative procedure, or in some areas by the European Commission. Well-known regulations include the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the REACH regulation on chemicals.
