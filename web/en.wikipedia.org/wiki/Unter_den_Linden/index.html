Unter den Linden

Unter den Linden, looking west toward the Brandenburg Gate.

Unter den Linden ("under the linden trees") is a boulevard in the central Mitte district of Berlin, Germany. It runs from the City Palace and Museum Island in the east to Pariser Platz and the Brandenburg Gate in the west, and has been Berlin's most famous ceremonial avenue since the 18th century.

History

The street began in the 16th century as a bridle path from the palace to the royal hunting grounds of the Tiergarten. Under Frederick William, the Great Elector, it was planted with a thousand linden and walnut trees in 1647, giving it its name. Under Frederick the Great it was lined with grand buildings forming the Forum Fridericianum: the State Opera, St. Hedwig's Cathedral, the Royal Library, and the prince's palace.

In the 19th and early 20th centuries it was the grand boulevard of imperial and Weimar Berlin, lined with hotels, cafés, and ministries. Devastated in the Second World War, it lay in East Berlin during the Cold War and was rebuilt with embassies and the Humboldt University buildings around it. The equestrian statue of Frederick the Great, removed under the GDR, was restored to the median in 1980.

Sights

Landmarks along the boulevard include Bebelplatz (site of the 1933 Nazi book burning, commemorated by Micha Ullman's sunken library memorial), the Neue Wache memorial, the German Historical Museum in the former Zeughaus, and Humboldt University's main building. The western end at Pariser Platz is dominated by the Brandenburg Gate.
