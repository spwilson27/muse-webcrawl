Pulteney Bridge

Pulteney Bridge is a bridge over the River Avon in Bath, Somerset, England. Completed by 1774 to a design by Robert Adam in the Palladian style, it is one of only four bridges in the world lined with shops along both sides of its full length. It is a Grade I listed building.

History

The bridge was commissioned by William Pulteney, 1st Earl of Bath's heir William Johnstone — who took the name Pulteney — to connect Bath with the family's land in Bathwick across the river, which he planned to develop into a new suburb. Adam's design, with three arches supporting a shop-lined street, drew inspiration from Florence's Ponte Vecchio and Venice's Rialto Bridge. The bridge was modified over the following decades: floods in 1799 and 1800 led to rebuilding of the northern pier, and in the nineteenth century shop windows were altered. The south side was restored towards Adam's original design in the twentieth century.

The weir

Just downstream stands the horseshoe-shaped Pulteney Weir, built in the Middle Ages and rebuilt in its present form in the 1970s to prevent flooding. The bridge and weir form one of Bath's most photographed scenes and appear in the film adaptation of Les Misérables.
