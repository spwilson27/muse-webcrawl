Kailasa

Kailasa (Sanskrit: कैलास, Kailāsa) is a sacred mountain abode of the Hindu god Shiva, located in the Kailash Range of the Transhimalaya. In Hindu tradition Shiva resides there with his consort Parvati and their children, and the mountain is revered as the centre of the world and a stairway to heaven.

Religious significance

Kailasa is sacred in four religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Bon. Hindus regard it as Shiva's throne; Tibetan Buddhists identify it with Mount Meru and associate it with Milarepa; Jains revere it as Ashtapada, where the first Tirthankara Rishabhanatha attained liberation; Bon practitioners hold it as the axis of the world.

Pilgrimage

Pilgrims circumambulate the mountain in a 52-kilometre kora, believed to bring merit and spiritual cleansing. Climbing the peak itself is traditionally prohibited out of reverence, and its summit remains unclimbed. The sources of several great Asian rivers — the Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra and Karnali — lie near the mountain.
