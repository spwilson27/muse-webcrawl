Chariots of the Gods? Unsolved Mysteries of the Past (German: Erinnerungen an die Zukunft: Ungelöste Rätsel der Vergangenheit, "Memories of the Future: Unsolved Mysteries of the Past") is a 1968 book by the Swiss author Erich von Däniken. It advances the ancient astronauts hypothesis: that extraterrestrial beings visited Earth in prehistory, were taken for gods by early humans, and influenced ancient cultures and technologies. The book became an international bestseller and is one of the most influential works of pseudoarchaeology, credited with popularizing the ancient-astronaut idea for a mass audience.

Contents and claims

Von Däniken argues that structures and artefacts such as the Egyptian pyramids, Stonehenge, the moai of Easter Island, the Nazca Lines and the Piri Reis map could not have been produced without extraterrestrial help, and he reinterprets religious texts and myths — Ezekiel's vision as a spacecraft, the Ark of the Covenant as a radio device, and world flood and "sons of God" stories as records of alien contact. The book also discusses the sarcophagus lid of the Maya ruler Pakal, which von Däniken interpreted as depicting an astronaut in a rocket.

Reception

The book has sold tens of millions of copies worldwide and was adapted into a 1970 documentary film, nominated for an Academy Award. Archaeologists and historians overwhelmingly reject its claims as pseudoscience, pointing to factual errors, mistranslations, the dismissal of well-documented indigenous achievements, and cases where evidence was fabricated or drawn without attribution from earlier writers. Critics also note racial overtones in the assumption that non-European peoples could not have built their own monuments. Von Däniken wrote numerous sequels and remains a prominent figure in ancient-astronaut popular culture, later inspiring television series such as Ancient Aliens.
