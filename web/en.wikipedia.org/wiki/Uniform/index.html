Uniform

A uniform is a variety of costume worn by members of an organization while participating in that organization's activity. Modern uniforms are most often worn by armed forces and paramilitary organizations such as police, emergency services, security guards, and by participants in certain sports. They are also common in schools, workplaces, prisons, and hospitals.

Service personnel in uniform.

Purpose

Uniforms serve to identify the wearer as a member of a group, to signal authority or role, to promote cohesion and equality within an organization, and to provide practical or protective clothing suited to a task. Distinctive colours, insignia, and badges of rank allow members and the public to recognise roles at a glance.

Military uniforms

Military uniforms developed from the liveries and standardized clothing issued to soldiers from the seventeenth century onward. They range from full dress uniforms used on ceremonial occasions to service dress and camouflaged combat uniforms designed for concealment in the field. Regimental traditions are often preserved in details such as headgear, facings, and buttons.

School uniforms

School uniforms are required in many countries, including much of Asia, parts of Africa, the United Kingdom, and some schools in the United States. Supporters cite reduced socioeconomic distinction and improved discipline; critics cite cost and limits on self-expression.

Work and service uniforms

Airlines, hotels, restaurants, healthcare facilities, and sports teams commonly issue uniforms. In medicine, scrubs and white coats identify staff and support hygiene. In team sports, uniforms (or kits) distinguish opposing sides and display sponsors and player numbers.
