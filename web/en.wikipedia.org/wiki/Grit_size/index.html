Grit size

Grit size refers to the size of particles of abrasive material embedded in sandpaper, coated abrasives, and bonded abrasive products such as grinding wheels. The grit designation is nominally related to the number of openings per linear inch in a sieve through which the particles can pass: higher grit numbers indicate smaller, finer particles, while lower numbers indicate coarser grit and faster material removal with a rougher finish.

Standards

Several grading systems exist. The CAMI (Coated Abrasives Manufacturers Institute) system is used in the United States, while FEPA (Federation of European Producers of Abrasives) "P" grades are used in Europe and in international standards; the two scales diverge substantially at fine grits. Japanese Industrial Standards (JIS) provide another scale. Micron-graded products, used for very fine polishing, are specified directly by average particle size in micrometres.

Use

Woodworkers, metalworkers, and auto-body finishers typically progress through a sequence of increasing grit numbers, each step removing the scratch pattern of the previous, coarser abrasive. Skipping grits leaves scratches that later steps cannot remove efficiently.
