Golok

A golok is a cutting tool, similar to a machete, used across the Malay Archipelago region, including Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei and the Philippines.

Traditionally used for agriculture and clearing vegetation, goloks have blades that widen towards the tip, typically 15–45 cm long, made for heavy chopping. Regional variants include the parang of Malaysia and the Sundanese golok of West Java; the golok is also associated with the martial art pencak silat and with the Betawi people of Jakarta.

Military use

During the Second World War, golok-style blades were used in jungle warfare in Southeast Asia; British forces in Burma and Malaya carried similar blades.
