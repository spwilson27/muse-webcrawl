Leningrad Codex

The Leningrad Codex (Latin: Codex Leningradensis) is the oldest complete manuscript of the Hebrew Bible in Hebrew, using the Masoretic Text and Tiberian vocalization. It is dated to 1008 or 1009 CE and is preserved in the National Library of Russia in Saint Petersburg (formerly Leningrad), where it has been housed since the mid-19th century. The codex is the primary basis for modern critical editions of the Hebrew Bible, including the Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia (BHS) and its successor, the Biblia Hebraica Quinta.

Contents

The codex contains the entire Hebrew Bible — the Torah, the Prophets (Nevi'im), and the Writings (Ketuvim) — written in three columns per page, with full Masoretic notes (masora magna and masora parva) in the margins. Its colophon states that it was copied by the scribe Samuel ben Jacob in Cairo from a manuscript corrected by Aaron ben Moses ben Asher, the last and most famous member of the Ben Asher family of Masoretes of Tiberias. It is generally considered the most authoritative representative of the Ben Asher textual tradition, edging out the older but partially lost Aleppo Codex.

History

The manuscript was acquired in the 19th century by the Karaite collector Abraham Firkovich and became part of the Imperial Public Library collection in Saint Petersburg, which gave the codex its modern name. During the Soviet era the city was renamed Leningrad, and the name stuck in scholarship even after the city reverted to Saint Petersburg in 1991. In 1997 a full photographic facsimile edition was published (Eerdmans), edited by David Noel Freedman and others.

Significance

Because it is complete and its Masoretic apparatus is unusually careful, the Leningrad Codex serves as the base text (Grundtext) of the standard critical editions used by scholars and translators worldwide. It is also famous for its sixteen illuminated "carpet pages," decorative full pages of geometric patterns and micrographic text at the end of the codex, among the finest examples of early medieval Jewish book art.
