Sword and sorcery
Genre: Fantasy subgenre
Origin: Pulp magazines, 1930s
Key figure: Robert E. Howard (Conan)

Sword and sorcery (S&S) is a subgenre of fantasy featuring swashbuckling heroes, personal stakes, fast-paced action and supernatural menace, in contrast to the epic, world-saving quests of high fantasy.

History

Robert E. Howard created the archetype with Conan the Barbarian, Kull and Solomon Kane in Weird Tales in the 1930s. Fritz Leiber, who coined the term "sword and sorcery" in 1961, contributed Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser; others include Clark Ashton Smith, C. L. Moore (Jirel of Joiry), Michael Moorcock (Elric) and Karl Edward Wagner (Kane).

Characteristics

Stories are episodic, violent and romantic, set in pseudo-historical worlds of decadent cities, thieves, mercenaries and malign gods; magic is dangerous and corrupting. The genre influenced role-playing games, heavy metal and films such as Conan the Barbarian (1982).
