Alert Bay is a village on Cormorant Island, in the Regional District of Mount Waddington, British Columbia, Canada. Located on Broughton Strait near the northeastern coast of Vancouver Island, it is a centre of the Kwakwaka'wakw (Kwakiutl) First Nation, on whose traditional territory it stands.

History

The bay was named in 1860 after the Royal Navy survey vessel HMS Alert. The Kwakwaka'wakw community of 'Namgis has existed at the site for centuries, drawn by the rich salmon fishing of the Nimpkish River across the strait. In the 1870s a salmon cannery and sawmill were established, and Alert Bay grew into a significant fishing and trading settlement. An Anglican mission and, later, St. Michael's Indian Residential School (1929–1975) were located in the community; the school building was demolished in 2015.

Culture

Alert Bay is renowned for the U'mista Cultural Centre, founded in 1980 to house the potlatch collection — ceremonial regalia confiscated by Canadian authorities after the banned potlatch held by Dan Cranmer in 1921 and repatriated from national museums decades later. The village is also home to what is often described as the world's tallest totem pole, a 53-metre (173 ft) pole raised in 1973, carved in sections. The 'Namgis Big House and the community's active potlatch tradition make Alert Bay a focal point of Kwakwaka'wakw cultural life.

Access

The community is served by the Alert Bay Airport and by BC Ferries service from Port McNeill on Vancouver Island.
