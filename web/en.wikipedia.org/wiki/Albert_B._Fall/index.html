Albert Bacon Fall (26 November 1861 – 30 November 1944) was an American politician from New Mexico who served as Secretary of the Interior under President Warren G. Harding. He became the central figure of the Teapot Dome scandal and was the first United States Cabinet member in history to be convicted of a felony and imprisoned for crimes committed while in office.

Early career

Fall moved to the New Mexico Territory as a young man, worked in mining and ranching, studied law, and rose in territorial politics. When New Mexico became a state in 1912, he was chosen as one of its first two United States senators, serving until 1921.

Secretary of the Interior and Teapot Dome

As Secretary of the Interior, Fall persuaded the Navy Department to transfer control of naval petroleum reserves at Teapot Dome, Wyoming, and Elk Hills, California, to his department. He then leased them without competitive bidding to oilmen Harry F. Sinclair and Edward L. Doheny, from whom he received large payments — presented as loans and gifts — totalling hundreds of thousands of dollars. A Senate investigation exposed the transactions, and Fall resigned in 1923.

Conviction and later life

In 1929 Fall was convicted of accepting a bribe from Doheny, fined $100,000 and sentenced to a year in prison; ironically, Doheny himself was acquitted of giving the bribe. Fall served about nine months. His health ruined and his fortune lost, he died in El Paso, Texas, in 1944.
