Proto-Finnic language

RegionGulf of Finland area
Erac. 1 – 1000 AD (late stage)
FamilyUralic → Finnic
DescendantsFinnish, Estonian, Karelian, Veps, Livonian, and others

Proto-Finnic, also called Late Proto-Finnic and Early Finnic, is the reconstructed ancestor of the Finnic languages, which include Finnish, Estonian, Karelian, Veps, Ingrian, Votic and Livonian. It developed from Proto-Finno-Saamic and was spoken around the Gulf of Finland, breaking up into regional dialects during the first millennium AD and the early centuries of the second.

Development

Scholars distinguish early, middle and late stages of Proto-Finnic. Its formation involved contacts with Baltic, Germanic and Slavic speakers, from whom it borrowed hundreds of words — Baltic loans include words for family relations and farming, while early Germanic loans include *kuningas "king" (from Proto-Germanic *kuningaz). Crossings of the Gulf of Finland separated the northern (Finnish) and southern (Estonian) dialect groups.

Features

Late Proto-Finnic is reconstructed with consonant gradation, a rich system of about fifteen nominal cases in its descendants' making, vowel harmony, and long and short vowel and consonant quantity contrasts. Sound changes characteristic of Proto-Finnic include the loss of the Proto-Uralic *ŋ in most positions, the development of stems alternating between vowel and consonant grades, and the monophthongisation of certain diphthongs in some dialects.

Descendants

As the dialects diverged, they produced the modern Finnic languages; the most divergent, Livonian, was reshaped by heavy Latvian influence, while Finnish and Estonian remained partly mutually intelligible with Karelian and standard Estonian respectively.
