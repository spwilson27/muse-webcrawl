The Messeturm, or Trade Fair Tower, is a 63-storey, 257 m (843 ft) skyscraper in the Westend-Süd district of Frankfurt, Germany. When completed in 1990 it was the tallest building in Europe, a title it held until 1997. It is the second tallest building in Frankfurt after the Commerzbank Tower and remains one of Germany's tallest buildings.

Design

Designed by Helmut Jahn of Murphy/Jahn in a postmodern style, the tower consists of a square shaft of red sandstone cladding topped by a distinctive glass pyramid. Its name means "trade fair tower", after the neighbouring Frankfurt fairgrounds (Messe Frankfurt), to which it serves as an entrance landmark. It contains about 62,000 m² of office space and has only one public-accessible room, a restaurant near the top.

Tenants and surroundings

The tower stands beside the Festhalle and the Kap Europa congress centre, at the entrance to the Messe grounds. Major tenants have included major banks and financial services firms. On the plaza in front stands Jonathan Borofsky's sculpture Hammering Man.
