Norges Bank Investment Management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Norges Bank Investment Management (NBIM) is a division of Norges Bank, the central bank of Norway, responsible for managing the Government Pension Fund Global (GPFG), commonly known as the Norwegian sovereign wealth fund or "Oil Fund". The fund is among the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world.

Role and mandate

NBIM invests the fund's assets abroad in equities, fixed income, real estate, and renewable energy infrastructure, following a mandate set by the Norwegian Ministry of Finance. The fund holds stakes in roughly nine thousand companies worldwide, on average owning about 1.5 percent of all listed companies globally. Its long-term strategy targets a high allocation to equities (around 70 percent) as set by the benchmark index.

History

The Government Petroleum Fund was established in 1990, with the first capital transferred in 1996. NBIM was created in 1998 to manage the fund professionally within the central bank. The fund passed major milestones in the following decades, exceeding US$1 trillion in value in 2017. It was renamed the Government Pension Fund Global in 2006, though Norway does not tie it to specific pension liabilities.

Leadership and governance

NBIM is headed by a chief executive officer; Nicolai Tangen, formerly a hedge fund manager, took the role in September 2020 amid public debate over his personal holdings, which were placed in a blind trust. NBIM publishes all holdings annually and emphasizes responsible investment, excluding companies under ethical guidelines recommended by the Council on Ethics, including certain weapons producers and companies associated with severe environmental damage or human rights violations. It divested from fossil fuel producers in its portfolio to a limited degree while retaining integrated oil and gas companies.
