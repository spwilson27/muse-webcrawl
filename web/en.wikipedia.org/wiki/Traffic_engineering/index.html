Traffic engineering

Traffic engineering is a branch of civil engineering concerned with the safe and efficient movement of people and goods on roadways. It applies engineering techniques and traffic science to the planning, geometric design and operation of streets, highways, junctions and terminals, and their relationships with other modes of transport.

Scope

Traffic engineers design and time traffic signals, set speed limits, lay out intersections and roundabouts, plan one-way systems and lane markings, and analyse crash data to improve road safety. Their work draws on models of traffic flow — the relationship between flow, speed and density — and on standard references such as the US Highway Capacity Manual and Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices.

Traffic flow theory

A central concept is the fundamental diagram of traffic flow, which describes how the number of vehicles passing a point (flow) rises with density up to a road's capacity and then collapses into congestion. Traffic engineers use detectors, simulation software and origin–destination surveys to predict the effects of new developments and road schemes.

Modern developments

Intelligent transport systems, adaptive signal control, congestion charging and connected-vehicle data have expanded the field, while "complete streets" and Vision Zero policies have shifted emphasis from maximising vehicle throughput towards safety and the needs of pedestrians and cyclists.
