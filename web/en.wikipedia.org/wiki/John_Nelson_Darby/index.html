John Nelson Darby (18 November 1800 – 29 April 1882) was an English–Irish Bible teacher, interpreter and clergyman, one of the founders of the movement that became the Plymouth Brethren and the most influential early systematiser of dispensationalism, including the doctrine of a pretribulational rapture of the church. His teachings shaped much of later evangelical and fundamentalist Christianity, especially in North America through the Scofield Reference Bible.

Early life and ministry

Darby was born in Westminster to an Anglo-Irish landowning family and educated at Westminster School and Trinity College Dublin, where he graduated as a gold medallist in classics in 1819. He was called to the Irish bar in 1822 but soon abandoned law for the Church of Ireland, being ordained a priest in 1826 and serving as curate in County Wicklow. Disillusioned with the established church's demands on converts, he resigned his living and from 1827–28 began meeting in Dublin with like-minded believers — among them Edward Cronin, John Bellett and Francis Hutchinson — to "break bread" in simplicity, the seed of the Brethren movement.

Brethren leadership

Darby travelled and taught for the rest of his life across Britain, continental Europe, North America, Australia and New Zealand, founding assemblies and writing voluminously. A dispute at Plymouth and Bristol in the 1840s — the "Bethesda question" over receiving believers from assemblies judged doctrinally lax — split the movement in 1848 into Darby's "Exclusive" Brethren and the "Open" Brethren associated with George Müller and Benjamin Wills Newton.

Theology

Darby taught that history is divided into distinct "dispensations" in which God deals with humanity in different ways; that the church is a heavenly body distinct from Israel, removed from the earth at the rapture before a future tribulation; and that the present age is a parenthesis between the sixty-ninth and seventieth "weeks" of Daniel's prophecy. This framework, popularised in America by C. I. Scofield, became foundational for dispensational theology.

Bible translation

Darby produced his own translations of the Bible from the original languages into English (the "Darby Bible", New Testament 1867; complete Bible 1890, posthumous), French and German, notable for their literalness.
