Panning (confectionery)

Panning is a confectionery technique in which small centres — nuts, dried fruit, chocolate, candy pieces or chewing gum — are coated in successive thin layers of sugar, chocolate or other coatings by tumbling them in a large rotating drum called a panning machine. The process produces smooth, evenly coated sweets such as dragées, jelly beans, chocolate-coated nuts and raisins, and many sugar-coated candies.

Process

The centres are loaded into the pan, which rotates so that the pieces tumble and stay separated. Coating syrup or melted chocolate is added in small charges; each layer is allowed to dry or set before the next is applied. In hard panning, sugar syrup is built up into a crisp shell; in soft panning, syrup is combined with powdered sugar; and in chocolate panning, chocolate is layered and then sealed. Finally the pieces may be polished with edible waxes such as carnauba wax or shellac to give them a shine. Colours and flavours can be introduced in the finishing layers.

History and products

The technique is centuries old — sugar-coated almonds (dragées) were served at celebrations in early modern Europe and were once made by hand in swinging copper pans. Modern panned products include Jordan almonds, jelly beans, M&M's-style lentils, chocolate-covered coffee beans, and coated chewing gum pellets.
