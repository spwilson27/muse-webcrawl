Semple Stadium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Semple Stadium, Thurles, County Tipperary, Ireland. Capacity approx. 45,690. Home of Tipperary GAA.

Semple Stadium is the home of hurling and Gaelic football for County Tipperary in Ireland. Located in Thurles, County Tipperary, it is the second-largest Gaelic games stadium in Ireland, with a capacity of about 45,690, behind only Croke Park. The ground has been the home of Tipperary GAA for over a century and is revered as a spiritual home of hurling, since the Gaelic Athletic Association itself was founded in Thurles in 1884.

History

The site was purchased in 1910 and was long known simply as the Thurles Sportsfield. It was renamed in 1971 after Tom Semple, the legendary captain of Thurles Sarsfields and Tipperary teams of the early twentieth century. Major redevelopment in the 1980s, for the GAA centenary in 1984, added the stands now known as the Old Stand (Ardán Ó Chuinneáin) and New Stand (Ardán Ó Riáin), along with extensive terracing. Semple Stadium regularly hosts Munster Senior Hurling Championship finals and All-Ireland semi-finals, and in 1984 it staged the centenary All-Ireland hurling final between Cork and Offaly.

Features

The stadium has two covered stands and two open terraces, the Town End and the Killinan End. A modern scoreboard, floodlights, and the Dome — an indoor facility adjoining the ground — have been added in the twenty-first century. Because of Thurles' central location in Ireland's hurling heartland, the stadium is a frequent neutral venue for major championship fixtures.
