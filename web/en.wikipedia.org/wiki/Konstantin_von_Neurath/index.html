Konstantin von Neurath

Neurath as Reich Foreign Minister

Konstantin Hermann Karl Freiherr von Neurath (2 February 1873 – 14 August 1956) was a German diplomat who served as Foreign Minister of Germany (1932–1938) under the Weimar Republic and the early Nazi regime, and then as Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia (1939–1943). A conservative career diplomat of the old school, he lent respectability to Hitler's government while gradually being sidelined by more radical Nazis; he was convicted of war crimes at Nuremberg.

Diplomatic career

Born into Swabian nobility, Neurath entered the diplomatic service in 1901, served as ambassador in Copenhagen, Rome, and London, and was appointed Foreign Minister by Chancellor Franz von Papen in 1932. Hitler retained him in 1933 as a reassuring face for foreign governments; Neurath presided over Germany's withdrawal from the League of Nations and the remilitarisation of the Rhineland. Privately uneasy with Hitler's aggressive plans revealed at the Hossbach conference of 1937, he was replaced in February 1938 by Joachim von Ribbentrop amid the Blomberg–Fritsch affair.

Protector of Bohemia and Moravia

In March 1939, after the German occupation of the Czech lands, Neurath became the first Reich Protector. His administration dissolved the Czech parliament, closed Czech universities after student demonstrations in November 1939, and carried out arrests and executions, though he was regarded in Berlin as too lenient; real power increasingly passed to his deputy, Reinhard Heydrich, from 1941, and Neurath was formally removed in 1943.

Trial and death

At the Nuremberg Trial of the Major War Criminals, Neurath was convicted on all four counts — conspiracy, crimes against peace, war crimes, and crimes against humanity — and sentenced to fifteen years. He was released from Spandau Prison in 1954 on health grounds and died at his family estate in 1956.
