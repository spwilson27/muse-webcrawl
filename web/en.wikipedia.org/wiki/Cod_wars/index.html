Cod Wars

An Icelandic patrol vessel and a British trawler during the Cod Wars.

The Cod Wars (Icelandic: Þorskastríðin) were a series of confrontations between Iceland and the United Kingdom between 1958 and 1976, arising from Iceland's unilateral extensions of its exclusive fishing limits in the North Atlantic. Despite the name, they were not wars: there were no declared hostilities and only one fatality, but the disputes involved ramming, the cutting of trawl wires, naval escorts, and a serious diplomatic rift within NATO.

The three wars

The First Cod War (1958–1961) began when Iceland extended its limits from 4 to 12 nautical miles. Britain sent Royal Navy frigates to protect its trawlers, but in 1961 it accepted the 12-mile limit. The Second Cod War (1972–1973) followed Iceland's extension to 50 nautical miles; Icelandic coast guard cutters used newly developed trawl-wire cutters against British vessels, and an Icelandic engineer became the conflict's only fatal casualty in a collision-related accident. The Third Cod War (1975–1976) followed the extension to 200 nautical miles, coinciding with international moves toward 200-mile exclusive economic zones. There were dozens of ramming incidents and wire cuttings, and Iceland briefly broke off diplomatic relations with Britain — the first such rupture between NATO allies.

Outcome

In June 1976, under NATO mediation and with Iceland threatening to leave the alliance and close the strategically vital Keflavík air base, Britain conceded: British trawlers were withdrawn and the 200-mile limit accepted. The result vindicated Iceland's resource nationalism, devastated distant-water fishing ports such as Hull and Grimsby, and helped set the precedent for the worldwide adoption of 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zones under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.
