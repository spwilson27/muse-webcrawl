Máximo Gómez y Báez (18 November 1836 – 17 June 1905) was a Dominican general and military leader of the Cuban independence movement. He served as General-in-Chief of the Cuban Liberation Army during both the Ten Years' War (1868–1878) and the Cuban War of Independence (1895–1898), and is regarded as one of Cuba's greatest national heroes.

Military career

Trained as a soldier in the Dominican Republic, Gómez settled in Cuba and joined the independence uprising in 1868, rising quickly through the ranks with bold cavalry charges. After the Ten Years' War ended in compromise, he went into exile. Persuaded by José Martí to return, he signed the Montecristi Manifesto with Martí in 1895 and landed in Cuba to lead the new war.

Gómez waged a brilliant guerrilla campaign across the island alongside Antonio Maceo, carrying the invasion to western Cuba and devastating the sugar economy that sustained Spanish rule. After Spain's defeat in 1898, he refused political office and retired, famously declining most honours; he died in Havana in 1905 and was mourned nationally.
