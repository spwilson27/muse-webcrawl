Arminius

Arminius (18/17 BC – AD 21), known in German as Hermann, was a prince of the Cherusci tribe and a Roman officer who led a coalition of Germanic tribes to victory over three Roman legions at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9.

Life

Raised partly in Rome as a hostage, Arminius gained Roman citizenship and equestrian rank while serving as an auxiliary officer. Returning to Germania, he secretly organised resistance to the governor Publius Quinctilius Varus, then lured the Roman army into an ambush in the Teutoburg Forest, where legions XVII, XVIII and XIX were annihilated.

Legacy

The defeat halted Roman expansion east of the Rhine. Arminius resisted later campaigns by Germanicus but was murdered by members of his own tribe in AD 21. In the 19th century he was celebrated as a German national hero, commemorated by the Hermannsdenkmal monument (completed 1875).
