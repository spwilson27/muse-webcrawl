Cadw (Welsh pronunciation: [ˈkadu], "to keep" or "to protect") is the historic environment service of the Welsh Government and part of its Culture and Sport department. Cadw works to protect the environment of Wales, its historic buildings and structures; to promote public access to and appreciation of them; and to help people understand and care about their heritage.

Responsibilities

Established in 1984, Cadw inherited the stewardship of Wales's state-owned ancient monuments from predecessor UK government bodies. It cares for more than 130 historic sites, including many of Wales's most famous castles — Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech and Beaumaris among the Edwardian castles of Gwynedd, a UNESCO World Heritage Site — as well as abbeys such as Tintern, prehistoric monuments, and industrial sites including Blaenavon. Some sites are staffed with admission charges; many are free and unstaffed.

Beyond its own estate, Cadw advises on the scheduling of ancient monuments, the listing of historic buildings, registered historic parks and gardens, and historic landscapes in Wales. It maintained the national records of the built heritage, working alongside the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of Wales (RCAHMW), with which it was later planned to merge into a single body.
