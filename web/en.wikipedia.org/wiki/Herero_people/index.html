Herero people

The Herero (Otjiherero: Ovaherero) are a Bantu ethnic group inhabiting parts of Southern Africa, chiefly Namibia, with smaller populations in Botswana and Angola. Traditionally cattle-herding pastoralists, they speak Otjiherero and number roughly a quarter of a million people.

History

Herero groups migrated into present-day Namibia from the east around the 17th and 18th centuries, coming into conflict and trade relations with Nama and Damara communities over grazing land. In the 19th century, chiefs such as Maherero consolidated power at Okahandja and traded with Europeans for firearms.

German colonial rule and genocide

After Germany proclaimed the colony of German South West Africa in 1884, settler encroachment on Herero land provoked the Herero Wars of 1904. Following the Battle of Waterberg, General Lothar von Trotha issued an extermination order; tens of thousands of Herero (and Nama) died in the desert, in concentration camps, or of forced labour in what is widely recognised as the first genocide of the 20th century. In 2021 Germany formally recognised the genocide and pledged development aid to Namibia.

Culture

Cattle remain central to Herero identity, spirituality and the praise-poetry tradition. Herero women are known for the ohorokova, a Victorian-influenced full dress, worn with the otjikaiva headdress shaped like cattle horns. The sacred fire (okuruwo) is tended at the homestead of the chief, linking the living with their ancestors.
