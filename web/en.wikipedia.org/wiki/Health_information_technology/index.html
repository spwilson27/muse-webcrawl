Health information technology

Health information technology (HIT) is the area of information technology involving the design, development, creation, use and maintenance of information systems for the healthcare industry. Automated and interoperable systems are intended to improve medical care, lower costs, increase efficiency, reduce errors and improve patient satisfaction, while optimising reimbursement.

Components

  • Electronic health records (EHRs) and electronic medical records (EMRs) — longitudinal digital records of patients
  • Computerized provider order entry (CPOE) and clinical decision support
  • Health information exchange (HIE) — sharing records between organisations
  • Telehealth and remote monitoring
  • Patient portals and personal health records
  • Picture archiving and communication systems (PACS) for imaging; laboratory and pharmacy systems

Adoption and policy

Adoption accelerated under incentive programmes such as the U.S. HITECH Act (2009) and the Medicare/Medicaid "Meaningful Use" (later Promoting Interoperability) programme, which pushed EHR use among U.S. office-based physicians from under half in 2009 to over 85% within a decade. Standards for interoperability include HL7 and its successor FHIR; regulators have also moved against "information blocking" under the 21st Century Cures Act.

Benefits and challenges

Evidence links HIT to reduced prescribing errors and improved adherence to preventive care guidelines. Challenges include poor usability and alert fatigue, documentation burden associated with clinician burnout, interoperability gaps between vendors, cybersecurity risks including ransomware attacks on hospitals, and privacy concerns.
