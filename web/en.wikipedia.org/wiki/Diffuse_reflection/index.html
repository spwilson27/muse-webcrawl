Diffuse reflection

Diffuse reflection is the reflection of light or other waves or particles from a surface such that a ray incident on the surface is scattered at many angles rather than at just one angle as in the case of specular reflection. An ideal diffuse reflecting surface is said to exhibit Lambertian reflection, meaning that there is equal luminance when viewed from all directions lying in the half-space adjacent to the surface.

Mechanism

Diffuse reflection arises when light penetrates slightly into a material, undergoes multiple scattering events among its internal microstructure — pigments, fibres, crystals or grains — and a portion is absorbed and re-emitted back out through the surface in random directions. It is this subsurface scattering that gives most everyday objects their matte appearance and their visible colour: materials that absorb some wavelengths and scatter the rest appear coloured. By contrast, specular (mirror-like) reflection occurs at smooth surfaces where incident rays reflect coherently at a single angle.

Many real surfaces exhibit a mixture of diffuse and specular reflection. Polished materials with rough microscopic structure, transparent materials with scattering interiors, and surfaces coated in matte paint all combine the two behaviours in ways that are modelled in optics and computer graphics.

Lambert's cosine law

For an ideal Lambertian diffuser, the intensity of reflected light observed from a surface is proportional to the cosine of the angle between the viewing direction and the surface normal. This simple law, described by Johann Heinrich Lambert in the 18th century, underlies the basic diffuse shading term in rendering: surfaces facing a light source appear brightest, and brightness falls off as the surface turns away.

Applications

Diffuse reflection is fundamental to human vision — we see most non-luminous objects because of it — and to fields including photography (softboxes and reflectors), remote sensing (albedo measurements), materials science and 3D computer graphics, where diffuse reflectance maps (albedo textures) are a core component of physically based rendering.
