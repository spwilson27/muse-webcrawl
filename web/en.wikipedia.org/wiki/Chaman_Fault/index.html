The Chaman Fault is a major, active geological fault in western Pakistan and eastern Afghanistan. It is a left-lateral strike-slip fault system roughly 850 km long that accommodates part of the relative motion between the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate.

Geology

The fault runs from the triple junction near the Arabian Sea coast northward through Balochistan, passing near the town of Chaman on the Pakistan–Afghanistan border, and toward the Hindu Kush region. It forms part of the boundary zone that also includes the Herat Fault in Afghanistan.

Seismicity

The fault system has produced several significant historical earthquakes, including events near Quetta. The devastating 1935 Quetta earthquake occurred on a related fault in the same plate boundary system. Geodetic studies indicate continuing strain accumulation along portions of the fault.
