Shin Bet

Shin Bet (Israel Security Agency)
Founded: 1948
Jurisdiction: Israel and the occupied territories
Role: Internal security, counter-terrorism

The Shin Bet (Hebrew acronym for Sherut ha-Bitaḥon ha-Klali, General Security Service), officially the Israel Security Agency, is Israel's internal security service, responsible for counter-terrorism, counter-espionage, and the security of government officials and sensitive sites.

Organisation and history

Founded in 1948 out of the Haganah's intelligence arm, it is one of three main Israeli intelligence organisations alongside Mossad (foreign intelligence) and Aman (military intelligence). Its director reports directly to the Prime Minister. Notable episodes include the Bus 300 affair (1984), the failure to prevent the 1995 assassination of Yitzhak Rabin despite an informant inside the extremist milieu, and extensive operations in the West Bank and Gaza.
