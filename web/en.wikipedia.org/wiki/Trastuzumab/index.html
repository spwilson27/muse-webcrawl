Trastuzumab

Trade nameHerceptin, others
TypeMonoclonal antibody (humanized IgG1)
TargetHER2/neu (ERBB2) receptor
RoutesIntravenous, subcutaneous
Approved1998 (US FDA)

Trastuzumab, sold under the brand name Herceptin among others, is a monoclonal antibody used to treat HER2-positive breast cancer and stomach cancer. It is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds to the extracellular domain of the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu) protein, which is overexpressed in about 15–20% of breast cancers and associated with more aggressive disease.

Medical uses

Trastuzumab is used, usually with chemotherapy, in the treatment of metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer, as adjuvant therapy for early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer, and for HER2-positive metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer. Its introduction markedly improved survival for patients with HER2-positive disease. Related agents include the antibody–drug conjugates trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) and trastuzumab deruxtecan, and the related antibody pertuzumab.

Adverse effects

The most important adverse effect is cardiotoxicity, including left ventricular dysfunction and, in some cases, congestive heart failure, particularly when combined with anthracyclines; cardiac function is monitored during treatment. Infusion reactions and pulmonary toxicity also occur.

History

Trastuzumab was developed by Genentech scientists building on the discovery of the HER2 oncogene by researchers including Axel Ullrich and Dennis Slamon. It was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 1998.
