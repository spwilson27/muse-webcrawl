Deathly Hallows

The Deathly Hallows are three legendary magical objects in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series, introduced in the final novel, Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows (2007). According to "The Tale of the Three Brothers", retold in The Tales of Beedle the Bard, Death gave the Peverell brothers three gifts: the Elder Wand, the most powerful wand ever made; the Resurrection Stone, which can recall shades of the dead; and the Cloak of Invisibility, which renders its wearer truly invisible and never fades. Their combined symbol — a triangle enclosing a circle bisected by a vertical line — became one of the franchise's most recognisable icons.

Role in the plot

The Hallows are the obsession of the dark wizard Gellert Grindelwald and a lifelong interest of Albus Dumbledore, who sought them in his youth. Unlike Voldemort, who pursues Horcruxes, Harry Potter comes to possess or master all three Hallows in the course of the final book: he inherits the Cloak (descended from Ignotus Peverell), takes the Resurrection Stone hidden inside the first Golden Snitch he caught, and wins the allegiance of the Elder Wand from Draco Malfoy without ever holding it until the end. Harry's choice to accept death rather than flee distinguishes him, in Dumbledore's words, as the true "master of death".

Interpretation

The Tale of the Three Brothers is an inset fairy tale about humility before death: the two elder brothers, choosing power and the past, die young, while the youngest, who chooses the Cloak, greets Death "as an old friend" at the end of a long life.
