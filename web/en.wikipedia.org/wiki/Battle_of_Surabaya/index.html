The Battle of Surabaya was fought in October and November 1945 between Indonesian independence fighters and British forces in Surabaya, East Java, during the Indonesian National Revolution. It was the heaviest single battle of the revolution and became a national symbol of Indonesian resistance; its climax on 10 November is commemorated annually in Indonesia as Heroes' Day.

Background

Indonesia's independence was proclaimed on 17 August 1945, days after Japan's surrender. British forces of South East Asia Command landed in Surabaya in late October to disarm Japanese troops and evacuate internees, but clashed with Indonesian pemuda (youth) militias determined to resist any return of Dutch colonial rule. Brigadier A.W.S. Mallaby was killed on 30 October in disputed circumstances after a ceasefire had been arranged.

The battle

After an ultimatum demanding the surrender of weapons expired, British and Indian troops of the 5th Indian Division under Major General Robert Mansergh, supported by tanks, aircraft and naval gunfire, attacked on 10 November. Fierce street fighting raged for about three weeks before the city was secured, while most Indonesian forces withdrew to fight on as guerrillas. Radio orator Sutomo ("Bung Tomo") became famous for his broadcasts rallying the defenders.

Aftermath

The scale of resistance convinced Britain that Indonesian nationalism could not be suppressed and hastened British withdrawal and pressure on the Netherlands to negotiate. Surabaya earned the name "Kota Pahlawan" — City of Heroes.
