Flight of the Earls

The Flight of the Earls (Irish: Imeacht na nIarlaí) took place on 4 September 1607 (Old Style), when Hugh O'Neill, 2nd Earl of Tyrone, and Rory O'Donnell, 1st Earl of Tyrconnell, and about ninety of their followers, families, and retainers sailed from Rathmullan in Lough Swilly, County Donegal, for Spanish territory. Their departure marked the end of the old Gaelic order in Ireland.

The earls departed from Rathmullan, County Donegal

Background

O'Neill and O'Donnell had been the leading Irish commanders in the Nine Years' War (1593–1603) against the English crown. After their defeat, the Treaty of Mellifont (1603) allowed them to retain much of their land and titles under English law. In the following years, however, they faced encroachments on their authority, legal disputes, and the pressure of government officials and rival claimants, and feared arrest on charges of treason.

The departure

The party boarded a French ship at Rathmullan, intending to reach Spain. Diverted by weather, they landed in France and travelled overland through the Spanish Netherlands and Italy, eventually reaching Rome, where they were granted asylum and small pensions by Pope Paul V. O'Donnell died in Rome in 1608; O'Neill died there in 1616, still hoping in vain for a Spanish expedition to Ireland.

Consequences

The government in Dublin declared the earls traitors and confiscated their lands — some 500,000 acres in six Ulster counties. The confiscations cleared the way for the Plantation of Ulster, in which the land was granted to English and Scottish settlers, a colonisation whose legacy shapes Northern Ireland to this day. The "flight" is remembered in Irish history as the moment the Gaelic aristocracy of Ireland effectively ceased to exist.
