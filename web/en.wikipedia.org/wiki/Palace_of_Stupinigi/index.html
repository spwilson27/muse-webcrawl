Palace of Stupinigi

LocationStupinigi, Nichelino, near Turin, Italy
ArchitectFilippo Juvarra
Construction1729–1733 (main works)
StyleLate Baroque / Rococo
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (Residences of the Royal House of Savoy)

The Palace of Stupinigi (Italian: Palazzina di Caccia di Stupinigi) is one of the Residences of the Royal House of Savoy in Piedmont, Italy, located in Stupinigi, a suburb of the town of Nichelino, about 10 km southwest of Turin. Built as a royal hunting lodge in the early 18th century, it is a masterpiece of late Baroque architecture and since 1997 has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Savoy residences.

History

The site was originally occupied by a medieval castle belonging to the Savoy-Acaja line. Victor Amadeus II of Savoy commissioned the architect Filippo Juvarra to design a hunting pavilion (palazzina di caccia) after regaining the estate; construction ran from 1729 to 1733, with the palace officially inaugurated in 1733. Juvarra organised the plan as an elongated Saint Andrew's cross centred on an oval grand salon, with wings radiating into the surrounding hunting park.

Later Savoy rulers enlarged and embellished the palace. It served as a setting for court hunts, festivals and weddings, and in the Napoleonic era it hosted Napoleon Bonaparte in 1805 on his way to Milan. In the 19th century the palace housed a museum of natural history and furniture collections; the bronze statue of a stag by Francesco Ladatte, crowning the central dome, became its emblem.

Architecture

The core of the building is the great oval central hall, crowned by a dome and frescoed by Giuseppe and Domenico Valeriani with the Triumph of Diana, goddess of the hunt. Curved wings and pavilions were added by Benedetto Alfieri and others, and the surrounding avenues fan out into the former hunting grounds, now the Stupinigi Natural Park, which still supports deer and other wildlife.

Today

The palace today houses the Museum of Art and Furnishings (Museo di Arte e Ammobiliamento), displaying original Savoy furniture, paintings and decorative arts, and it is administered by the Ordine Mauriziano foundation. It remains one of the most visited of the Turin-area Savoy residences.
