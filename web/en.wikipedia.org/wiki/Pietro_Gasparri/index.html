Pietro Gasparri (1852–1934)

Pietro Gasparri (5 May 1852 – 18 November 1934) was an Italian Roman Catholic cardinal, diplomat and politician. He served as Cardinal Secretary of State of the Holy See from 1914 to 1930, under Popes Benedict XV and Pius XI, and was one of the most influential figures in Vatican diplomacy of the early twentieth century.

Early life

Gasparri was born in Capovallazza, a hamlet of Ussita in the province of Macerata, in the Papal States, the youngest of ten children of poor farmers. He studied at the Pontifical Roman Seminary and was ordained a priest on 31 March 1877. He earned doctorates in theology and in both civil and canon law, and taught canon law at the Urban College and later at the Catholic Institute of Paris.

Diplomatic career

In 1898 Pope Leo XIII appointed Gasparri titular Archbishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia and apostolic delegate to Peru, Bolivia and Ecuador. Returning to Rome in 1901, he became secretary of the Congregation for Extraordinary Ecclesiastical Affairs. In 1904 Pope Pius X charged him with directing the codification of canon law, a monumental project that produced the Code of Canon Law promulgated in 1917.

Cardinal Secretary of State

Gasparri was created a cardinal in 1907 and appointed Secretary of State by Benedict XV in October 1914, shortly after the outbreak of the First World War. He managed the Holy See's policy of impartial neutrality and drafted much of Benedict's 1917 peace note. Under Pius XI he negotiated a series of concordats with European states, culminating in the Lateran Treaty of 1929 with Italy, which created Vatican City State and settled the Roman Question. Gasparri signed the treaty alongside Benito Mussolini.

Later years and death

He resigned as Secretary of State in 1930 on grounds of age and health and was succeeded by Eugenio Pacelli, the future Pope Pius XII. Gasparri died in Rome on 18 November 1934.
