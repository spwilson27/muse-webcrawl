Plunging fire

Plunging versus grazing trajectories

Plunging fire is gunfire directed at a target from above, such that the projectiles fall on it at a steep angle, as distinct from grazing fire, in which the trajectory stays close to and roughly parallel with the ground.

Characteristics

Plunging fire is obtained at long ranges, where the descending branch of the trajectory is steep, or from elevated positions, or by high-angle weapons such as mortars and howitzers. Its beaten zone – the area on the ground struck by a cone of fire – is shorter but denser than that of grazing fire, and it can reach targets sheltered behind cover, in trenches, or on reverse slopes that are safe from flat-trajectory fire.

Employment

The concept was central to machine-gun doctrine in the First World War, when indirect machine-gun barrages were fired over friendly troops to plunge onto enemy positions at ranges of several thousand yards. Modern machine guns retain a limited indirect-fire capability using clinometers and firing tables, though mortars and artillery ordinarily fulfil the role today.
