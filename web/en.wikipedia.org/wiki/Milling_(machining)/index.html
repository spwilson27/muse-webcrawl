Milling (machining)

A machinist milling a workpiece on a vertical milling machine.

Milling is the process of machining using rotary cutters to remove material by advancing a cutter into a workpiece. It is one of the most commonly used processes in industry and machine shops today, capable of producing parts with complex geometries in metals, plastics, composites, and wood.

Process

In milling, the rotating cutting tool — typically a multi-point cutter such as an end mill, face mill, or drill — moves relative to a workpiece secured on a table or fixture. Because the cutter has many edges, material is removed in small chips, allowing precise control of dimensions and good surface finishes. Milling machines are classified as horizontal or vertical according to spindle orientation, and range from manual knee mills to multi-axis CNC machining centers. Key operations include face milling, peripheral (slab) milling, end milling, pocketing, drilling, and contouring.

Cutting parameters

Performance depends on cutting speed, feed rate, and depth of cut, chosen with reference to the tool and workpiece materials. Two modes are distinguished: climb milling (down milling), in which the cutter rotates with the direction of feed, and conventional milling (up milling), against it; climb milling generally gives better finishes but requires machines free of backlash.

History

Rotary filing and early milling machines appeared in the late 18th century; in the 19th century, American makers such as Joseph R. Brown developed the universal milling machine, and Frederick W. Taylor's work on cutting speeds helped put machining on a scientific footing.
