Nobuo Uematsu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Uematsu in concert

Nobuo Uematsu (植松 伸夫, born 21 March 1959) is a Japanese composer and keyboardist, best known for his work on the Final Fantasy series of role-playing video games developed by Square (now Square Enix). Largely self-taught, he is regarded as one of the most influential composers in video game music, credited with helping to raise the artistic ambitions of the medium.

Career

Uematsu joined Square in 1985 and composed for numerous early titles before scoring the first Final Fantasy in 1987. He went on to compose, alone or as principal composer, the scores for the first nine Final Fantasy games and much of the tenth, producing signature works including "One-Winged Angel" (Final Fantasy VII), "Liberi Fatali" (VIII), and "To Zanarkand" (X). Working within severe hardware limits on early consoles, he developed a melodic style drawing on classical music, progressive rock, and Celtic and Middle Eastern influences.

Later work

Uematsu left Square Enix in 2004, founding his own company, Smile Please, and studio Dog Ear Records, while continuing to contribute themes to later Final Fantasy titles including XIV and the VII Remake project. With director Hironobu Sakaguchi's Mistwalker studio he composed for Blue Dragon, Lost Odyssey, The Last Story, and Fantasian (2021), which he has described as likely his final full game score. He performs with his band Earthbound Papas.

Legacy

His music is performed worldwide in orchestral concert series such as Distant Worlds: Music from Final Fantasy and Dear Friends, and "Aerith's Theme" and other pieces have entered the repertoire of classical radio hall-of-fame polls. In 2024, Uematsu was reported to be scaling back new composition work for health reasons while continuing concert appearances.
