Pierre Perrin, poet and founder of the Paris Opéra.

Pierre Perrin (c. 1620 – 24 April 1675) was a French poet and librettist, regarded together with the composer Robert Cambert as a founder of French opera.

Career

Born in Lyon, Perrin came to Paris and attached himself to the household of Gaston d'Orléans, brother of Louis XIII, for whom he wrote court entertainments. His ambition was to naturalise Italian opera in France by writing lyric drama in French, arguing that the French language, properly handled, was as suitable for singing as Italian.

Pomone and the Opéra

With Cambert he created La Pastorale d'Issy (1659), often described as the first French opera, and in 1669 Louis XIV granted Perrin a royal patent to establish an Académie d'Opéra — the institution that became the Paris Opéra. Their pastoral Pomone opened the academy in 1671 and enjoyed a long and profitable run, the first true success of French-language opera.

Downfall

Perrin's triumph was brief. Poor management and dishonest associates left him bankrupt and imprisoned for debt; in 1672 the opera privilege was transferred to Jean-Baptiste Lully, who built the institution into the centre of French musical life. Perrin died in poverty in Paris in 1675.
