Chandragupta II, also known by his title Vikramaditya, was one of the most powerful emperors of the Gupta Empire in northern India, reigning from about 375/380 to 415 CE. His reign is often regarded as the high point of the Gupta "golden age" of classical Indian culture.

Reign

The son of Samudragupta, Chandragupta II extended Gupta power westward by defeating the Western Kshatrapas of Malwa, Gujarat, and Saurashtra, campaigns likely concluded by about 409 CE. He strengthened the empire through matrimonial alliances, marrying the Naga princess Kuberanaga and giving his daughter Prabhavatigupta in marriage to the Vakataka king Rudrasena II, which secured the Deccan frontier.

Culture

Tradition associates his court at Ujjain with the "nine gems" (navaratna), including the poet Kalidasa. The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Faxian visited India during his reign and described a prosperous, well-administered empire. Chandragupta II issued abundant gold coinage and took the title Vikramaditya ("sun of valour"); he is also credited in some traditions with the Mehrauli iron pillar inscription of a king named Chandra.
