Tier list

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A tier list is a ranking system that sorts items — commonly video game characters, but also foods, films, songs or almost anything — into ordered categories or "tiers". In the most widespread format, the tiers are labelled, from highest to lowest, S, A, B, C, D, and sometimes E and F below that. The "S" tier originates in Japanese video games, where it denoted a rank above A in grading systems; its exact derivation is uncertain, but it is often explained as standing for "super" or deriving from the Japanese word shū (秀, "excellent").

Use in gaming

Tier lists became prominent in fighting game communities, where players ranked playable characters by their competitive viability — how strong a character was in tournament play given the current "meta". Characters in the top tiers were considered dominant choices, while low-tier characters were seen as disadvantaged. Such lists are inherently subjective and frequently debated, changing as games are patched and strategies evolve.

Internet culture

In the late 2010s, tier lists spread beyond gaming into general internet culture, helped by websites such as TierMaker that let users build their own rankings by dragging images into rows. Streamers and video creators made "tier list" videos ranking everything from fast-food items to historical figures a popular genre, and the format became a common way of expressing and arguing about personal taste online.
