The Lockheed SR-71 "Blackbird" is a retired American long-range, high-altitude strategic reconnaissance aircraft developed by Lockheed's Skunk Works under Clarence "Kelly" Johnson. It was operated by the United States Air Force and NASA and remains the fastest air-breathing manned production aircraft ever flown.

Design

The SR-71 was designed to cruise at speeds above Mach 3 and altitudes above 80,000 feet, outrunning surface-to-air missiles rather than evading them by stealth alone, though it also incorporated early radar cross-section reduction. Its airframe was built largely of titanium alloys to withstand skin temperatures exceeding 300 °C from aerodynamic heating, and it was powered by two Pratt & Whitney J58 engines that functioned partly as ramjets at top speed. The aircraft leaked fuel on the ground because its fuel tanks were designed to seal only when the structure expanded at operating temperature.

Operational history

The Blackbird entered service in 1966 and flew reconnaissance missions over Libya, the Middle East, Eastern Europe and East Asia. No SR-71 was ever shot down; twelve were lost in accidents. It set numerous records, including a New York-to-London flight of 1 hour 54 minutes in 1974, and an absolute speed record of 2,193 mph in 1976 that still stands for manned air-breathing aircraft.

The type was retired by the USAF in 1989, briefly reactivated in the mid-1990s, and finally retired in 1998; NASA flew its last Blackbirds until 1999. Surviving aircraft are displayed in museums in the United States and at Duxford in the United Kingdom.
