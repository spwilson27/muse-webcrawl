Malolos Constitution

The Malolos Constitution, formally the Political Constitution of 1899, was the constitution of the First Philippine Republic. Drafted by the revolutionary congress meeting at Malolos, Bulacan, it was promulgated on January 21, 1899, and is regarded as the first republican constitution in Asia.

Background

After the declaration of Philippine independence from Spain on June 12, 1898, Emilio Aguinaldo convened a revolutionary congress at Barasoain Church in Malolos in September 1898. The congress, presided over by Pedro Paterno, debated the draft constitution prepared principally by Apolinario Mabini and Felipe Calderón, drawing on the constitutions of Spain, France, Belgium and several Latin American republics.

Provisions

The constitution established a democratic and republican state with sovereignty residing in the people. It provided for three branches of government: a unicameral legislature (the Assembly of Representatives), an executive headed by a president elected by the assembly for a four-year term, and an independent judiciary. The assembly was made more powerful than the president, reflecting the framers' distrust of executive authority.

The document contained a bill of rights guaranteeing civil and political liberties, and it declared the separation of church and state, a provision adopted only after heated debate and by a narrow vote. It also proclaimed the free and compulsory nature of primary education.

Legacy

The constitution was short-lived: the First Philippine Republic was overwhelmed by the Philippine–American War, which began only weeks after promulgation. Nevertheless, the Malolos Constitution remains a landmark in Philippine constitutional history and a symbol of the revolutionary generation's democratic aspirations.
