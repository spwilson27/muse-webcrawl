Lemmatisation

Lemmatisation (or lemmatization) in linguistics is the process of grouping together the inflected forms of a word so they can be analysed as a single item, identified by the word's lemma, or dictionary form. For example, the forms walk, walks, walked and walking share the lemma walk; irregular forms such as better and best are lemmatised to good. Lemmatisation is a standard preprocessing step in corpus linguistics, lexicography and natural language processing.

Methods

Early lemmatisers used hand-written rules and word lists; modern systems combine morphological dictionaries with statistical or neural models that use the word's part of speech and surrounding context to disambiguate — for instance, deciding whether saw is a form of see, a noun meaning a tool, or a proverb. Lemmatisation differs from stemming, which crudely truncates word endings heuristically without regard to the actual dictionary form: a stemmer might reduce both university and universe to univers, which is not a lemma of either.

Applications

Lemmatised corpora support dictionary-making, language teaching materials, information retrieval (so that a search for mice also matches documents containing mouse), stylometry and historical linguistics. The difficulty of lemmatisation varies greatly by language: it is relatively simple in English but much more complex in highly inflected or agglutinative languages such as Finnish, Turkish or Latin, and for historical texts with unstandardised spelling.

Relation to morphology

Lemmatisation depends on morphological analysis: identifying the correct lemma requires knowing the word's inflectional paradigm and, often, its syntactic role. In corpus annotation schemes such as the Universal Dependencies project, every token is annotated with its lemma alongside its part of speech and morphological features.
