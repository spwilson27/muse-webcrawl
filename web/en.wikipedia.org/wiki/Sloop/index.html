Sloop

A sloop is a sailboat with a single mast typically having only one headsail in front of the mast and one mainsail aft of (behind) the mast. This sail configuration — the Bermuda rig (or Marconi rig) with a single jib — is called a sloop rig, and is the most common rig on modern recreational sailboats.

The rig

A sloop's headsail may be a jib or a larger overlapping genoa, set on the forestay. The mainsail is usually triangular, its luff attached to the mast and its foot to a boom. Variants include fractional rigs, where the forestay meets the mast below the top, and cutter rigs, which carry two headsails — though a vessel with one mast and two headsails is formally a cutter rather than a sloop when the mast is stepped further aft. The word derives from the Dutch sloep.

History

The Bermuda sloop developed in the 17th and 18th centuries in Bermuda, prized by merchants and smugglers for its speed and weatherliness. In naval usage, "sloop" also denoted a class of warship — the sloop-of-war, smaller than a frigate — and in the Second World War the Royal Navy's Black Swan-class sloops were important convoy escorts. These naval uses are unrelated to the sailing rig, describing vessel size and role instead.

Advantages

The sloop rig is efficient upwind, simple to handle, and easy to automate with self-tacking jibs, which is why nearly all production cruising and racing yachts today are sloops.
