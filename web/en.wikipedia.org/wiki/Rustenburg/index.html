Rustenburg (Afrikaans for "Town of Rest") is a city in the North West province of South Africa, situated at the foot of the Magaliesberg mountain range. It is one of the oldest towns in the former Transvaal, founded in 1851 as a church and administrative centre for Voortrekker farmers, and was home to Paul Kruger, president of the South African Republic, whose farm Boekenhoutfontein lies nearby.

Mining

Rustenburg sits on the western limb of the Bushveld Igneous Complex and is the centre of the world's richest platinum-mining district; the Rustenburg area accounts for a large share of global platinum production, with major operations run by companies such as Anglo American Platinum, Impala Platinum, and Sibanye-Stillwater. The nearby Marikana area was the site of the 2012 miners' strike and the Marikana massacre, in which 34 striking miners were shot dead by police.

Sport

The city's Royal Bafokeng Stadium, owned by the Royal Bafokeng Nation, hosted matches of the 2010 FIFA World Cup and the 2013 Africa Cup of Nations. Nearby attractions include the Sun City resort and the Pilanesberg Game Reserve.
