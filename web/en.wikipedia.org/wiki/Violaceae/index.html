Violaceae is a family of flowering plants established in 1802, consisting of about 1,000 species in about 25 genera. It takes its name from the genus Viola, the violets and pansies. The family belongs to the order Malpighiales, one of the largest orders of flowering plants.

A violet, member of the family Violaceae

Description

Members of the family include herbs, shrubs, lianas and trees. Although Viola — the temperate genus of violets and pansies — is the best known, most genera are tropical shrubs and trees, such as Rinorea and Leonia. The flowers of Viola are strongly zygomorphic (bilaterally symmetrical), with five petals, the lowest forming a nectar spur. Many species also produce inconspicuous cleistogamous (self-pollinating, unopened) flowers. The fruit is typically a capsule that splits open explosively to scatter seeds, which in many species bear elaiosomes and are dispersed by ants.

Distribution and uses

The family is cosmopolitan, with Viola widespread in temperate regions and other genera concentrated in the tropics. Several species are grown as ornamentals, including pansies (Viola × wittrockiana) and sweet violets, and some have traditional medicinal uses. The family has historically been placed in its own order, Violales, though molecular phylogenetics now situates it firmly within Malpighiales.
