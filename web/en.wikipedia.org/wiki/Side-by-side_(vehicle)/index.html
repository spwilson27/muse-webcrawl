Side-by-side (vehicle)

A side-by-side, also called a UTV (utility terrain vehicle) or SxS, is a small off-road vehicle with side-by-side seating for two or more occupants, a steering wheel, pedals, and usually a roll cage. Unlike an all-terrain vehicle (ATV), which the rider straddles and steers with handlebars, the side-by-side is driven more like a small car or truck and is widely used for both recreation and utility work such as farming, ranching, and trail riding.

A recreational side-by-side (UTV) on an off-road trail.

Characteristics

Side-by-sides typically feature four-wheel drive, long-travel suspension, bench or bucket seats with seat belts, and a cargo bed or storage area at the rear. Engines are commonly in the 500–1000 cc range, with sport models exceeding 180 horsepower and using turbochargers. Continuously variable transmissions (CVT) are the norm. Manufacturers include Polaris (RZR and Ranger lines), Can-Am (Maverick and Defender), Yamaha, Kawasaki, Honda, and Arctic Cat.

Types

Utility models emphasize cargo capacity, towing, and work features for agriculture and land management, while sport and trail models emphasize speed, suspension travel, and handling. Four-seat and crew configurations extend passenger capacity. Some jurisdictions permit limited on-road use where equipped with lights, mirrors, and turn signals, though UTVs are generally not designed to federal motor vehicle safety standards for highway use.

Safety

The rollover protection structure (ROPS), seat belts, and side nets or doors are key safety features. Consumer safety authorities emphasize wearing helmets, using restraints, and keeping arms and legs inside the vehicle, since most fatal incidents involve rollovers and ejections.
