Tahara

Tahara, the ritual purification of the body before burial

Tahara (Hebrew: טהרה, "purity") can refer in Jewish tradition both to the general concept of ritual purity and, more specifically, to the ritual washing and purification of a dead body before burial. This article concerns the funerary rite.

The rite

The tahara is performed by the chevra kadisha (burial society) — men prepare men's bodies and women prepare women's — and is carried out with great care for the dignity of the deceased. The body is thoroughly washed, ritually purified with a continuous flow of water, dried, and dressed in plain white shrouds (tachrichim). Throughout, prayers and passages from the Song of Songs and other texts are recited, and the deceased is addressed with requests for forgiveness for any indignity. The body is then placed in the coffin, traditionally a plain wooden casket, together with broken pottery shards placed over the eyes and mouth in some customs, and earth from the Land of Israel.

Other uses of the term

The related concepts of tumah and tahara (impurity and purity) govern a broad system of biblical and rabbinic law concerning persons, vessels, food, and the Temple. The Red Heifer (parah adumah) ritual produced the ashes used in purification from corpse impurity, the most severe level of tumah.
