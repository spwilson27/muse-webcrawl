Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (commonly shortened to Alice in Wonderland) is an 1865 English children's novel by Lewis Carroll, the pen name of Charles Lutwidge Dodgson. A young girl named Alice falls through a rabbit hole into a fantasy world of anthropomorphic creatures and absurd adventures.

Plot

Bored by her sister's book, Alice follows a talking White Rabbit down a rabbit hole. In Wonderland she repeatedly changes size, swims in a pool of her own tears, attends a mad tea party with the March Hare, the Hatter and the Dormouse, meets the Cheshire Cat and the Duchess, plays croquet with the tyrannical Queen of Hearts using flamingos as mallets, hears the Gryphon and the Mock Turtle, and finally testifies at the trial of the Knave of Hearts. As the court dissolves into chaos, Alice wakes on the riverbank beside her sister.

Origins and legacy

Dodgson invented the story during a boat trip on the Thames on 4 July 1862 for Alice Liddell and her sisters, and later expanded his handwritten gift copy, Alice's Adventures Under Ground, for publication with illustrations by John Tenniel. The book is a landmark of literary nonsense, filled with wordplay, logic puzzles and parodies of Victorian schoolroom verse such as "You Are Old, Father William". Together with its sequel, Through the Looking-Glass (1871), it has never been out of print and has been adapted countless times for stage and screen.
