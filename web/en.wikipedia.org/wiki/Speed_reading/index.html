Speed reading

Speed reading is any of several techniques claiming to improve one's ability to read quickly. Methods include chunking (reading groups of words at once), minimising subvocalisation, skimming, scanning, and meta guiding with a finger or pointer. Typical adult reading speeds are around 200–300 words per minute, while speed-reading courses advertise 500 to several thousand words per minute.

History

The term gained currency with Evelyn Wood's Reading Dynamics courses of the late 1950s, promoted by students including US senators and President John F. Kennedy, who took a course and claimed very high reading speeds. Later approaches include PhotoReading and RSVP (rapid serial visual presentation) apps, which flash words one at a time.

Research

Scientific reviews of eye-movement research conclude that reading is constrained by visual acuity and language processing: the fovea can only take in a limited span of text per fixation, and comprehension falls sharply at extreme speeds. Skimming is useful when full comprehension is not required, but claims of reading thousands of words per minute with normal comprehension are not supported by evidence.
