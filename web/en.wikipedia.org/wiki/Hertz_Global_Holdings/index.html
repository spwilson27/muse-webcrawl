Hertz Global Holdings

Hertz Global Holdings, Inc. is an American car rental company based in Estero, Florida. It operates the Hertz, Dollar and Thrifty rental brands in approximately 160 countries in North America, Europe, Latin America, Africa, Asia, Australia, the Caribbean, the Middle East and New Zealand, with thousands of company-operated and franchised locations.

History

The company was founded by Walter L. Jacobs in Chicago in 1918 as Rent-A-Car, Inc., renting out a fleet of Model T Fords. In 1923 it was bought by John D. Hertz, president of the Yellow Cab Company, and renamed the Hertz Drive-Ur-Self System. Passing through owners including RCA and United Airlines, Hertz became part of the Ford Motor Company in 1994, was sold to private-equity investors in 2005 and went public again in 2006. It acquired Dollar Thrifty Automotive Group in 2012.

Bankruptcy and recovery

Devastated by the collapse of travel during the COVID-19 pandemic, Hertz filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in May 2020 — one of the highest-profile corporate failures of the crisis. It emerged from bankruptcy in mid-2021 after a bidding contest, with new investment led by Knighthead Capital and Certares. The company later drew attention for a large (subsequently scaled-back) commitment to electric vehicles, including orders for Tesla and Polestar cars, and for selling off much of its EV fleet as demand patterns shifted.

Operations

Hertz is one of the largest worldwide vehicle rental companies by sales, locations and fleet size, competing principally with Enterprise Holdings and Avis Budget Group.
