Monte Toc

Monte Toc is a mountain in the Carnic Prealps of north-eastern Italy, on the border between the provinces of Belluno and Pordenone, in the municipalities of Erto e Casso and Castellavazzo. It rises to about 1,921 metres above sea level and overlooks the valley of the Vajont stream.

The Vajont disaster

Monte Toc became infamous on the night of 9 October 1963, when a massive landslide of roughly 270 million cubic metres of rock detached from its northern slope and plunged into the reservoir behind the newly built Vajont Dam. The displacement of water generated an enormous wave — estimated at up to 250 metres high — which overtopped the dam and swept down the Piave valley, destroying the town of Longarone and several villages. Roughly 2,000 people were killed, making it one of the worst man-made disasters in European history.

Geology

The mountain's very name derives from the local Friulian dialect: toc means "rotten" or "crumbling," a reference to its unstable, fractured limestone slopes. Warning signs — creeping ground movement and small slides observed from 1960 onward — were documented but underestimated. The scar of the 1963 landslide, a vast bare amphitheatre on the mountainside, remains clearly visible today, and Monte Toc is a destination for memorial tourism and geological study.
