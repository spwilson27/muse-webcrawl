RecA

RecA is a 38 kilodalton protein essential for the repair and maintenance of DNA in bacteria. A RecA structural and functional homolog has been found in every species in which one has been seriously sought and serves as an archetype for this class of homologous DNA repair proteins, known generally as recombinases. The eukaryotic homologs are called RAD51.

Function

RecA has multiple activities, all related to DNA repair. When single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) accumulates at a damaged replication fork or double-strand break, RecA polymerises on it to form a nucleoprotein filament. This filament searches for homologous double-stranded DNA and catalyses strand exchange, the central step of homologous recombination. Recombination allows accurate repair of breaks and the restart of stalled replication forks.

SOS response

In its activated, DNA-bound state (RecA*), RecA also acts as a coprotease that triggers autocleavage of the LexA repressor, inducing the SOS response — a coordinated expression of more than 40 DNA repair genes in Escherichia coli. It likewise promotes cleavage of the UmuD protein to generate the error-prone polymerase V, which can replicate past DNA lesions at the cost of increased mutation.
