Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive

The Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive 2002/95/EC (RoHS 1), short for "Directive on the restriction of the use of certain hazardous substances in electrical and electronic equipment", was adopted in February 2003 by the European Union. The initiative was to prevent an overabundance of chemicals in electronics.

Restricted substances

RoHS restricts the use of six hazardous materials in the manufacture of various types of electronic and electrical equipment: lead (Pb), mercury (Hg), cadmium (Cd), hexavalent chromium (Cr6+), polybrominated biphenyls (PBB), and polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDE). Maximum concentration values are generally 0.1% by weight (1000 ppm), except cadmium at 0.01% (100 ppm).

RoHS 2 and RoHS 3

The recast directive 2011/65/EU (RoHS 2), which took effect in 2013, expanded the scope to all electrical and electronic equipment and added CE marking and conformity assessment requirements. Directive (EU) 2015/863 (RoHS 3) added four phthalates — DEHP, BBP, DBP, and DIBP — to the restricted list, bringing the total to ten substances.

Impact

Because the European Union is a large market, RoHS had a global impact: manufacturers worldwide redesigned products to remove lead solder and other restricted substances, and similar regulations were adopted in China, Japan, South Korea, and several US states.
