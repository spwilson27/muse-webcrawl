South East England

South East England is one of the nine official regions of England, consisting of the ceremonial counties of Berkshire, Buckinghamshire, East Sussex, Hampshire, the Isle of Wight, Kent, Oxfordshire, Surrey and West Sussex. It is the most populous region of the United Kingdom outside London, with a population of around 9.3 million, and the third largest region by area (19,096 km²). Major settlements include Brighton and Hove, Portsmouth, Southampton, Oxford, Reading, Milton Keynes and Canterbury.

Geography

The region surrounds London and is bounded by the English Channel to the south and the Thames Estuary to the east. Its landscape includes the chalk downlands of the North Downs and South Downs, the Weald, the Thames Valley and the coasts of Kent (the "Garden of England"), Sussex and Hampshire, with the Solent separating the Isle of Wight from the mainland.

Economy and transport

South East England is one of Europe's strongest regional economies, driven by services, technology (including the M4 corridor and "Silicon Valley" around Reading), the ports of Southampton and Dover, and Gatwick Airport. Major transport links include the M25, M1, M3, M4 and M40 motorways, Eurostar services through the Channel Tunnel from Ashford and Ebbsfleet, and ferry routes to France and the Isle of Wight.

Governance

Like other English regions, the South East has no directly elected regional government; it is represented in Parliament and administered at county and district level, with unitary authorities in several areas.
