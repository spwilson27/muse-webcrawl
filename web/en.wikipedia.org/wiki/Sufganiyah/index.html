Sufganiyah

A sufganiyah (Hebrew: סופגנייה; plural sufganiyot) is a round jelly doughnut eaten in Israel and around the world during the Jewish festival of Hanukkah. The doughnut is deep-fried in oil, filled with jelly or custard, and then topped with powdered sugar. Because Hanukkah celebrates the miracle of the oil that burned for eight days, foods fried in oil — including sufganiyot and latkes — are traditional for the holiday.

Type: Doughnut
Place of origin: Israel / Jewish diaspora
Main ingredients: Yeast dough, jelly or custard, powdered sugar

History

Fried dough balls soaked in syrup appear in early rabbinic literature, and jelly-filled doughnuts spread through Central and Eastern Europe as the German Berliner and the Polish pączki. Ashkenazi Jews brought jelly doughnuts to Israel in the early 20th century, where the Histadrut labour federation promoted them in the 1920s as a Hanukkah food that, unlike latkes, could not easily be made at home and so provided work for bakery employees. The Hebrew name derives from the Talmudic word sufganin, meaning spongy dough.

Variations

Traditional sufganiyot are filled with strawberry or raspberry jelly, but Israeli bakeries now produce elaborate versions filled with dulce de leche, chocolate, halva, espresso cream and many other flavours, often sold at premium prices in the weeks before Hanukkah. Miniature sufganiyot are also popular. Similar fried pastries are eaten by other Jewish communities, including the Sephardic bimuelos.
