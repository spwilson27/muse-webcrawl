Hubble sequence

Hubble sequence
Type: Galaxy morphological classification
Devised by: Edwin Hubble (1926)
Classes: E (elliptical), S (spiral), SB (barred spiral), Irr (irregular)
Diagram: Tuning-fork diagram

The Hubble sequence is a morphological classification scheme for galaxies invented by Edwin Hubble in 1926 and modified in 1936. It is often shown as a "tuning fork" diagram: elliptical galaxies (E0–E7, by apparent flattening) lie along the handle, while spiral (S) and barred spiral (SB) galaxies, graded Sa–Sc by bulge size and arm winding, form the two prongs. Lenticular (S0) galaxies sit at the junction; irregular galaxies fall outside the fork.

Properties along the sequence

Moving from elliptical to late-type spirals, galaxies generally become bluer, more gas-rich, and more actively star-forming. Hubble originally suggested an evolutionary sequence from "early" to "late" types, a terminology that persists though ellipticals are not now thought to evolve into spirals.

Extensions

Gérard de Vaucouleurs extended the scheme with finer stages, rings, and bars; the Yerkes and other systems classify by spectrum and concentration. Modern surveys such as Galaxy Zoo use Hubble types as a baseline.
