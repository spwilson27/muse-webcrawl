Gini coefficient

The Gini coefficient (or Gini index) measures statistical dispersion intended to represent income inequality or wealth inequality within a nation or group. It was developed by Italian statistician Corrado Gini in 1912.

Definition

It ranges from 0 (perfect equality, everyone has the same income) to 1 (or 100%, perfect inequality, one person has all income). It is defined as the ratio of the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve to the total area under the line of equality.

Interpretation

Scandinavian countries typically score around 0.25–0.28 for disposable income; the United States around 0.39–0.41; some highly unequal countries exceed 0.6. Limitations include sensitivity to middle vs extremes and different values for income vs wealth — wealth Gini is usually much higher. See Distribution of wealth.

Applications

Used by the World Bank, OECD and UN to compare inequality over time and between countries, and in fields from health to ecology.
