United Kingdom Special Forces (UKSF) is a directorate within the British Armed Forces that commands the United Kingdom's special forces units. It was created on the formation of the Directorate of Special Forces in 1987, bringing the Army's Special Air Service and the Royal Marines' Special Boat Service under a single command, and has since expanded.

Component units

The core of UKSF comprises the 22nd Special Air Service Regiment (22 SAS) and the Special Boat Service (SBS), together with the Special Reconnaissance Regiment (SRR, formed 2005), which specialises in covert surveillance. Supporting elements include the 1st Battalion, Parachute Regiment, as the core of the Special Forces Support Group (SFSG), 18 (UKSF) Signal Regiment, and Joint Special Forces Aviation Wing helicopters. Reserve elements include 21 and 23 SAS Regiments and the SBS Reserve.

Role and selection

UKSF units undertake roles including counter-terrorism, special reconnaissance, direct action and military assistance to friendly forces. Selection for the SAS and SBS is conducted jointly over about six months, beginning with the gruelling endurance phase in the Brecon Beacons, and is open to volunteers from across the armed forces. The UK government maintains a long-standing policy of not commenting on special forces operations.
