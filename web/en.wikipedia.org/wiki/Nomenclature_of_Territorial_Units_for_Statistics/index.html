Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics

The Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics (NUTS; French: Nomenclature des unités territoriales statistiques) is a geocode standard maintained by Eurostat for referencing the administrative divisions of countries for statistical purposes within the European Union. It was established in the early 1980s and given legal status in 2003.

Levels

Each country is divided into three hierarchical levels, broadly based on population:

  • NUTS 1 — major socio-economic regions of 3–7 million people (e.g. German Länder groups, Belgian regions)
  • NUTS 2 — basic regions of 800,000–3 million, the level used for EU regional (cohesion) policy and structural funds eligibility
  • NUTS 3 — small regions of 150,000–800,000

Below NUTS 3 are Local Administrative Units (LAU), mostly municipalities. NUTS codes begin with the two-letter country code followed by digits or letters for each level. Candidate countries and EFTA states are also assigned codes.

Use

NUTS 2 regions with GDP per capita below 75% of the EU average qualify as less-developed regions for cohesion funding, which makes the boundaries politically significant; classifications are revised at most every three years.
