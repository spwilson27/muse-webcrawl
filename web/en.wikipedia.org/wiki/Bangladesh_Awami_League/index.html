The Bangladesh Awami League (Bengali: বাংলাদেশ আওয়ামী লীগ) is one of the major political parties of Bangladesh. Founded in Dhaka in 1949 as the East Pakistan Awami Muslim League (later the Awami League), it played the central role in the Bengali nationalist movement, the 1970 election, and the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971 under the leadership of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, the country's founding president.

History

The party was formed by dissidents from the Muslim League, including Maulana Bhashani and Huseyn Shaheed Suhrawardy, with the young Sheikh Mujibur Rahman as a key organiser. It led movements for Bengali language rights, autonomy under the Six Points programme, and won a landslide in Pakistan's 1970 general election — a result the Pakistani military refused to honour, triggering the liberation war. After independence the Awami League formed Bangladesh's first government. Following Sheikh Mujib's assassination in 1975 the party was marginalised under military rule, returning to power in 1996 under his daughter Sheikh Hasina. It won elections again in 2008, 2014, 2018 and January 2024, governing continuously under Hasina until she was ousted and fled the country in August 2024 amid a mass student-led uprising, after which the party's position collapsed and its activities were later banned by the interim government pending trials.

Organisation and ideology

The party's constitution rests on the four principles of the original 1972 constitution: nationalism, socialism, democracy and secularism. Its electoral symbol is the boat (nouka). Affiliated organisations include the Chhatra League (students), Jubo League (youth) and Sramik League (labour).
