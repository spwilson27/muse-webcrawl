Michaelis–Menten kinetics

Field: Enzyme kinetics / biochemistry
Proposed: 1913 (Michaelis & Menten)
Key parameters: Vmax, Km

Michaelis–Menten kinetics is one of the best-known models of enzyme kinetics. It describes the rate of an enzymatic reaction as a function of substrate concentration, under the assumption that an enzyme reversibly binds substrate to form an enzyme–substrate complex that then releases product.

The equation

The initial reaction rate is v = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S]), where Vmax is the maximum rate at saturating substrate and Km, the Michaelis constant, is the substrate concentration at which the rate is half of Vmax. The model derives from the steady-state assumption developed further by Briggs and Haldane in 1925.

Significance

The model underlies most quantitative studies of enzyme activity and drug inhibition, although it does not apply to allosteric enzymes showing cooperative, sigmoidal kinetics.
