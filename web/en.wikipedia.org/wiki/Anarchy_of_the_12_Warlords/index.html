The Anarchy of the 12 Warlords (Vietnamese: Loạn thập nhị sứ quân) was a period of chaos and civil war in Vietnamese history spanning roughly 944 to 968, following the collapse of the Ngô dynasty. After the death of the ruler Ngô Quyền — who had won independence from China at the Battle of Bạch Đằng in 938 — rival regional lords seized territory across the Red River Delta, and by the mid-960s twelve warlords contended for power.

Background

Ngô Quyền died in 944, leaving the throne to his brother-in-law Dương Tam Kha, who usurped power. Ngô Quyền's son Ngô Xương Văn eventually reclaimed the throne and ruled jointly with his brother, but central authority steadily fragmented as local magnates raised their own armies. When Ngô Xương Văn was killed in battle in 965, the country dissolved into open warlordism.

The twelve warlords

Traditional lists name twelve lords, among them Ngô Xương Xí, Đỗ Cảnh Thạc, Phạm Bạch Hổ, Kiều Công Hãn, Nguyễn Khoan, Nguyễn Siêu, Nguyễn Thủ Tiệp, Kiều Thuận, Lã Đường, Dương Huy, Trần Lãm, and Ngô Nhật Khánh. Some, such as Phạm Bạch Hổ and Trần Lãm, later joined the rising power of Đinh Bộ Lĩnh rather than fighting him.

Reunification

Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, based at Hoa Lư, combined diplomacy — including a marriage alliance with the family of Trần Lãm — with military campaigns to defeat or absorb the warlords one by one between 966 and 968. In 968 he proclaimed himself emperor, founded the Đinh dynasty, named the country Đại Cồ Việt, and organised a centralised administration, ending the anarchy and establishing the framework of an independent Vietnamese state.
