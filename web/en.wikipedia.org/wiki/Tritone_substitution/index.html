Tritone substitution

The tritone substitution is a chord substitution technique common in jazz harmony in which a dominant seventh chord is replaced by another dominant seventh chord whose root lies a tritone (augmented fourth / diminished fifth) away. The most familiar use is replacing the V7 chord in a ii–V–I progression, producing a chromatic descending bass line.

How it works

Two dominant seventh chords a tritone apart share the same guide tones (third and seventh) with their roles reversed. For example, G7 (G–B–D–F) and D♭7 (D♭–F–A♭–C♭) share the pitches B/C♭ and F. Hence D♭7 can substitute for G7 and resolve to C major, moving to the tonic by semitone. The device is named for the interval between the two roots and was widely used by bebop musicians such as Dizzy Gillespie and Thelonious Monk; the standard "Tritone" bridge of rhythm changes tunes exploits related substitutions.
