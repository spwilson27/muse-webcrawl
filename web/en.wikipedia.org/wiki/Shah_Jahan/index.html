Shah Jahan

Shah Jahan I (born Shahab-ud-Din Muhammad Khurram; 5 January 1592 – 22 January 1666) was the fifth Mughal emperor, reigning from 1628 until 1658. His reign represented the height of Mughal architectural achievement and, under his son Aurangzeb's later expansion, the empire reached its greatest territorial extent. Shah Jahan is best known for commissioning the Taj Mahal in Agra, built in memory of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal.

Reign

The son of Jahangir, Prince Khurram distinguished himself in campaigns in Mewar and the Deccan before rebelling against his father in 1622; reconciled, he ascended the throne in 1628 after a succession struggle. His reign was marked by military campaigns in the Deccan, Central Asia and against the Portuguese at Hooghly, an opulent court, and — in 1630–1632 — a devastating famine in Gujarat and the Deccan. The Mughal Empire under Shah Jahan was among the richest states in the world.

Architecture

Shah Jahan's building programme defined the classical Mughal style in white marble and red sandstone: the Taj Mahal (1632–1653); the Red Fort and Jama Masjid in his new capital, Shahjahanabad (Old Delhi, founded 1639); the Moti Masjid in Agra Fort; and the Shalimar Gardens in Lahore. He also commissioned the fabled Peacock Throne, later carried off by Nader Shah of Persia in 1739.

Deposition and death

When Shah Jahan fell ill in 1657, his four sons fought a war of succession, won by Aurangzeb. Shah Jahan recovered but was deposed and imprisoned in Agra Fort, where — legend holds — he could view the Taj Mahal from his balcony. He died in 1666 and was buried beside Mumtaz Mahal in the Taj Mahal.
