United Nations Economic Commission for Europe

AbbreviationUNECE, ECE
Formation28 March 1947
HeadquartersGeneva, Switzerland
Member states56
Parent organizationUN Economic and Social Council

The United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE or ECE) is one of five regional commissions under the jurisdiction of the United Nations Economic and Social Council. It was established in 1947 to promote economic cooperation and integration among its member states.

Membership and work

UNECE has 56 member states, spanning Europe, North America (Canada and the United States), and Central Asia. Although its name refers to Europe, membership reflects the geopolitical settlement of its founding era. Its work covers transport, trade, statistics, environment, energy, housing and land management, and forestry. UNECE is well known for developing international norms and conventions, including the TIR Convention on road transport, regulations for vehicle safety harmonized through the World Forum for Harmonization of Vehicle Regulations (WP.29), and the UN/CEFACT standards for electronic business such as UN/EDIFACT.

History

Created in the aftermath of the Second World War, the commission initially focused on postwar reconstruction and on fostering trade between Eastern and Western Europe during the Cold War. After 1990 its work expanded to support transition economies in Central and Eastern Europe and Central Asia.
