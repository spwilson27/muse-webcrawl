Supply chain management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In commerce, supply chain management (SCM) deals with a system of procurement (purchasing raw materials/components), operations management (production), logistics and marketing channels, so that the raw materials can be converted into a finished product and delivered to the end customer. A more narrow definition, given by the APICS Dictionary, is "the design, planning, execution, control, and monitoring of supply chain activities with the objective of creating net value, building a competitive infrastructure, leveraging worldwide logistics, synchronising supply with demand and measuring performance globally".

Overview

A supply chain consists of all parties involved, directly or indirectly, in fulfilling a customer request: suppliers, manufacturers, transporters, warehouses, retailers, and customers themselves. SCM coordinates the flows of products, information, and finances between these parties. The term was popularised in the early 1980s, notably by consultant Keith Oliver, and the discipline grew with globalisation, outsourcing, and information technology such as ERP systems and electronic data interchange.

Key processes

  • Planning — demand forecasting, sourcing strategy, inventory planning
  • Sourcing — supplier selection, procurement, and contracting
  • Making — production scheduling, manufacturing, quality
  • Delivering — logistics, warehousing, transport, order fulfilment
  • Returning — reverse logistics for defective or end-of-life products

These correspond closely to the SCOR (Supply Chain Operations Reference) model maintained by the Association for Supply Chain Management. Well-known phenomena include the bullwhip effect, in which small changes in consumer demand amplify into large swings in orders further up the chain.

Trends

Contemporary SCM emphasises resilience (highlighted by pandemic-era disruptions), sustainability and ethical sourcing, digitalisation and real-time visibility, and risk management across multi-tier supplier networks.
