Tetraplegia

Tetraplegia, also known as quadriplegia, is paralysis caused by illness or injury that results in the partial or total loss of use of all four limbs and the torso. It is distinguished from paraplegia, in which the arms retain function while the legs and parts of the torso are affected. The loss may involve both motor and sensory function, and is typically caused by damage to the cervical spinal cord or the brain.

Causes

The most common cause is traumatic injury to the cervical spine, such as from motor vehicle collisions, falls, sports injuries, or violence. Non-traumatic causes include multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy, tumours, infections, and vascular events affecting the spinal cord or brainstem. The degree of impairment depends on the level of injury and whether the injury is complete or incomplete, graded using the ASIA Impairment Scale.

Complications and management

People with tetraplegia may experience spasticity, loss of bladder and bowel control, respiratory difficulties (particularly with high cervical injuries that affect the diaphragm), pressure ulcers, and autonomic dysreflexia, a potentially dangerous rise in blood pressure triggered by stimuli below the injury level. Management focuses on rehabilitation, assistive technology such as powered wheelchairs and environmental controls, prevention of secondary complications, and, in some cases, functional electrical stimulation.
