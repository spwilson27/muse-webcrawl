Polarimeter

A polarimeter is a scientific instrument used to measure the angle of rotation caused by passing polarised light through an optically active substance. Some chemical substances are optically active, and the polarised (unidirectional) light will rotate either to the left (counter-clockwise) or right (clockwise) when passed through these substances. The amount by which the light is rotated is known as the angle of rotation, and materials that rotate light to the right are called dextrorotatory, while those rotating it to the left are laevorotatory.

Construction

A polarimeter consists of a light source, a polariser that produces plane-polarised light, a sample tube containing the substance under investigation, a second rotatable polariser called the analyser, and a detector or eyepiece. By rotating the analyser until the transmitted light reaches a reference condition (often the half-shadow or extinction point), the observer determines the angle of rotation.

Applications

Polarimetry is widely used in the sugar industry, where the concentration of sugar solutions is measured with specialised polarimeters called saccharimeters. It is also used in chemistry to identify and characterise chiral compounds, in pharmaceuticals to check the purity of enantiomers, and in food science for quality control of oils, flavourings and other optically active products.
