The Toyota Prius is a hybrid electric vehicle produced by Toyota since 1997. Initially offered as a four-door sedan, it has been produced as a five-door liftback since 2003. The Prius is widely considered the first mass-produced hybrid car and became a symbol of environmentally conscious motoring; its name is Latin for "prior to" or "before".

History

The first-generation Prius (NHW10/NHW11) was launched in Japan in December 1997 under the slogan "Just in time for the 21st century", and reached export markets including North America and Europe in 2000. The second generation (XW20, 2003) adopted the distinctive five-door liftback body with a drag coefficient of 0.26 and a larger, more powerful Hybrid Synergy Drive system. The third generation (ZVW30, 2009) used a 1.8-litre engine and became the best-selling car line in California. A plug-in hybrid variant, the Prius Prime (Prius Plug-in Hybrid), followed, and the fifth generation (XW60) was unveiled in November 2022 with a sleeker design and a standard 2.0-litre hybrid system.

Technology

The Prius uses a series-parallel hybrid system combining a petrol engine running on the Atkinson cycle with one or two electric motor-generators and a planetary continuously variable transmission. A nickel-metal hydride battery (later lithium-ion in some markets) stores energy recovered through regenerative braking, allowing electric-only driving at low speeds and engine shut-off at stops.

Sales and reception

Cumulative global Prius sales passed one million in 2008 and three million in 2013. The car was popularised by celebrity owners and praised for fuel economy around 50 mpg (US) in combined driving, though sales declined in the late 2010s as buyers shifted to SUVs and fully electric vehicles.
