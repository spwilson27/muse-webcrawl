John Francis Bentley
Born: 30 January 1839, Doncaster, England
Died: 2 March 1902 (aged 63), London, England
Occupation: Architect
Known for: Westminster Cathedral

John Francis Bentley (30 January 1839 – 2 March 1902) was an English ecclesiastical architect whose most famous work is Westminster Cathedral in London, the mother church of the Roman Catholic Church in England and Wales.

Life and career

Bentley was born in Doncaster and trained in engineering and architecture in London, working initially on railway and engineering projects before turning to architecture. In 1862 he converted to Roman Catholicism, and his practice became centred on Catholic churches, schools, convents, and seminaries, many in the Gothic Revival style. He developed close working relationships with patrons including Cardinal Manning and refined skills in interior decoration, metalwork, and stained glass as well as building design.

Westminster Cathedral

In 1894 Bentley was commissioned by Cardinal Vaughan to design Westminster Cathedral. Determined to give London's Catholics a landmark distinct from the Gothic of the Anglican Westminster Abbey, and influenced by his travels in Italy, Bentley produced a design in an early Christian and Byzantine style, built of red brick with bands of white stone and a tall campanile. Construction began in 1895; the cathedral's vast interior was planned for extensive marble and mosaic decoration, much of it completed after his death. It is regarded as his masterpiece and one of the finest Victorian cathedrals in Britain.

Bentley died in 1902, the year before Westminster Cathedral opened for worship, reportedly shortly after being offered a Royal Gold Medal by the Royal Institute of British Architects.
