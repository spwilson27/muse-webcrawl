Inter-Korean summit

Inter-Korean summits are meetings between the leaders of North Korea and South Korea. Five such summit meetings have been held since 2000: in Pyongyang in 2000 and 2007, and three in 2018 — in April and May at Panmunjom and in September in Pyongyang.

The 2000 summit

The first summit, between South Korean president Kim Dae-jung and North Korean leader Kim Jong Il on 13–15 June 2000, was the centrepiece of Kim Dae-jung's Sunshine Policy. It produced the June 15th North–South Joint Declaration, in which the two sides agreed to work toward reconciliation and eventual reunification, expand economic cooperation, and arrange reunions of separated families. Kim Dae-jung received the Nobel Peace Prize later that year; it later emerged that South Korea had secretly paid North Korea some $500 million in connection with the summit, a scandal that damaged the policy's standing.

The 2007 summit

President Roh Moo-hyun walked across the Military Demarcation Line to meet Kim Jong Il in Pyongyang on 2–4 October 2007. The resulting declaration called for expanding economic projects such as the Kaesong Industrial Complex, establishing a peace regime, and holding further prime-ministerial and defence talks. Implementation stalled after a conservative government took office in Seoul in 2008.

The 2018 summits

After a decade of tension, Moon Jae-in and Kim Jong Un met on 27 April 2018 at Panmunjom — Kim becoming the first North Korean leader to cross into the South — and issued the Panmunjom Declaration, pledging a nuclear-free Korean Peninsula and steps toward a formal peace. A brief second meeting followed on 26 May, and a third, on 18–20 September 2018 in Pyongyang, produced agreements on military confidence-building and further economic cooperation. Momentum faded after the failed US–North Korea Hanoi summit of 2019.
