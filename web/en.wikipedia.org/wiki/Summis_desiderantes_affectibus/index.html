Summis desiderantes affectibus

The bull as printed at the front of the Malleus Maleficarum (1487)

Summis desiderantes affectibus ("Desiring with supreme ardor") is a papal bull issued by Pope Innocent VIII on 5 December 1484. Often called the "Witch Bull", it is best known as the preface to the Malleus Maleficarum, the notorious witch-hunting manual published in 1487. The bull confirmed the authority of the Dominican inquisitors Heinrich Kramer and Jacob Sprenger to prosecute witchcraft in Germany and denounced those who obstructed their work.

Background

Kramer had met resistance from local clergy and secular authorities in his inquisitorial campaigns in southern Germany; he was expelled from Innsbruck in 1485 by the bishop there after overzealous prosecutions. Seeking support, Kramer petitioned Rome, and Innocent VIII issued the bull, which set out an alarming catalogue of alleged witch activities: apostasy, incubi and succubi, killing infants in the womb, blasting crops and cattle, and preventing procreation.

Content

The bull appointed Kramer and Sprenger as inquisitors with full authority in the archdioceses of Mainz, Cologne, Trier, Salzburg and Bremen, and ordered bishops and officials to give them every assistance, threatening obstructionists with excommunication. It notably affirmed that witchcraft was a real and growing danger, though it broke little new theological ground — sorcery had long been punishable, and earlier popes had issued similar commissions.

Legacy

When Kramer published the Malleus Maleficarum in 1487, he printed the bull at its front, lending the treatise an appearance of papal endorsement it did not strictly have: the bull predates the book and does not mention it. Historians debate the bull's importance; it did not launch the great European witch hunts by itself, but it remains the most famous papal document on witchcraft and an emblem of the era's demonological fears.
