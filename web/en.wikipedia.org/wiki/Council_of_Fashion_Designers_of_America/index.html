The Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA) is a not-for-profit trade association of American fashion and accessory designers, founded in 1962 by the publicist Eleanor Lambert. Its more than 450 members are admitted by invitation, and its mission is to strengthen the influence and success of American designers in the global economy.

History and activities

Lambert created the CFDA to promote American fashion as a legitimate cultural and commercial force distinct from Paris. The council gained prominence with events such as the 1973 "Battle of Versailles" fashion show, which pitted American designers against their French counterparts. The CFDA organises the annual CFDA Fashion Awards — often called the "Oscars of fashion" — honouring excellence in womenswear, menswear, accessories and emerging talent, and it has owned and managed the official New York Fashion Week schedule.

Programmes

The CFDA runs the CFDA/Vogue Fashion Fund (established 2003) to mentor and finance emerging designers; scholarships and educational initiatives; sustainability and manufacturing programmes including support for New York's Garment District; and charitable campaigns such as Fashion Targets Breast Cancer. Past presidents and chairs include Stan Herman, Diane von Furstenberg (2006–2019) and Tom Ford. The organisation also advocates on issues from design-piracy legislation to diversity and health standards for models.
