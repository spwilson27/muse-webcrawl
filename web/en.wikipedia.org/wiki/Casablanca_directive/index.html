Casablanca directive

The Casablanca directive was a directive issued by the Anglo-American Combined Chiefs of Staff on 21 January 1943, during the Casablanca Conference of the Second World War. It defined the aims of the strategic bombing campaign conducted by RAF Bomber Command and the United States Army Air Forces against Nazi Germany.

Content

The directive stated the primary object of the bomber offensive: "the progressive destruction and dislocation of the German military, industrial and economic system, and the undermining of the morale of the German people to a point where their capacity for armed resistance is fatally weakened." It listed target systems in order of priority, including submarine construction yards, the German aircraft industry, transportation, oil plants, and other war industry targets.

Implementation

The directive provided the framework reconciling the British policy of night area bombing with the American policy of daylight precision bombing. It led to the Pointblank directive of June 1943, which prioritised the destruction of the German fighter force ahead of the planned invasion of France. The resulting Combined Bomber Offensive continued until the final months of the war in 1945.
