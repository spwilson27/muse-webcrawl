Cornaceae

Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Cornales
Family: Cornaceae
Type genus: Cornus

The Cornaceae are a cosmopolitan family of flowering plants in the order Cornales. The family contains roughly 85 species in two genera, Cornus (the dogwoods) and Alangium, under modern circumscriptions based on the APG system. The family has been divided into as many as 13–15 genera in some older classifications, and genera such as Nyssa are sometimes included in Cornaceae or placed in their own family, Nyssaceae.

Description

Members are mostly trees and shrubs, often deciduous, with simple opposite leaves (alternate in Alangium). Flowers are small, usually four-petalled, arranged in cymes or heads sometimes surrounded by showy bracts, as in the flowering dogwood. The fruit is a drupe. Species are distributed mainly in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with extensions into tropical Asia and South America.

Genera

In the broad sense (Cornaceae sensu lato) the family has included Aucuba, Corokia, Curtisia, Griselinia, Helwingia, Melanophylla and others, most of which modern systems assign to separate families. The narrow circumscription retains Cornus and Alangium.

Uses

Dogwoods are widely planted as ornamentals for their flowers, coloured stems and autumn foliage. The hard wood of some species has been used for tool handles and shuttles.
