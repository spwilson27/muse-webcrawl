The Mersey Railway was a passenger railway that connected Liverpool with Birkenhead and the Wirral Peninsula in England via the Mersey Railway Tunnel beneath the River Mersey. Opened on 1 February 1886, its tunnel was the world's first to pass under a major tidal estuary, and the railway was among the most heavily used underground lines of the Victorian era.

A train at the Mersey Railway Tunnel

History

The Mersey Railway was incorporated in 1866, but financial difficulties and the challenge of tunnelling beneath the river delayed construction. Work eventually began in 1881 under the contractor John Waddell, using designs by engineers James Brunlees and Douglas Fox. The tunnel was bored through sandstone bedrock and lined with brick; stationary steam pumps kept the workings dry.

When it opened in 1886, trains were hauled by powerful steam locomotives specially designed to work in the tunnel, including condensing engines. Ventilation was provided by large fans, but conditions underground remained smoky and unpleasant. Nonetheless the railway was an immediate success, carrying around ten million passengers in its first full year.

Electrification

To combat the poor air quality and competition from electric trams, the company electrified the line in 1903 using a 650 V DC fourth-rail system with multiple-unit trains built by George Westinghouse's British company. The Mersey Railway thereby became one of the first steam railways in Britain to convert to electric traction, and ridership recovered strongly.

Later history

The railway remained independent of the 1923 Grouping under the Railways Act 1921, running from Liverpool Central to Birkenhead Park, Rock Ferry, and later New Brighton and West Kirby via through services. It was nationalised in 1948 as part of British Railways. In the 1970s the line was integrated into the new Merseyrail network: the original Liverpool Central high-level station closed and trains were diverted into the underground Loop line, opened in 1977, which connected the Mersey Railway with the lines north of the city. The tunnel and much of the original route remain in daily use as part of Merseyrail's Wirral Line.
