Gravlax

Gravlax served with hovmästarsås (mustard-dill sauce).

Gravlax (Swedish: gravad lax, "buried salmon") is a Nordic dish consisting of salmon that is cured using a mixture of salt, sugar and dill. It is usually served as an appetizer, sliced thinly and accompanied by hovmästarsås ("maître d'hôtel sauce"), a sweet dill-and-mustard sauce, either on bread or with boiled potatoes.

History

During the Middle Ages, gravlax was made by fishermen who salted salmon and lightly fermented it by burying it in the sand above the high-tide line. The word derives from the Scandinavian grav, "to dig" or "grave", and lax (or laks), "salmon" — hence "buried salmon". The mild fermentation of the original method produced a stronger flavour than today's version.

Modern preparation

Modern gravlax is not fermented. Instead, fresh salmon fillets are coated with a dry cure of roughly equal parts salt and sugar, seasoned with dill and sometimes crushed juniper berries, black pepper, or aquavit, then wrapped, weighted and refrigerated for one to three days. The cure draws moisture from the fish, firming its texture and preserving it. Before serving, the cure is scraped off and the fish is sliced paper-thin on the bias. Variations use other oily fish, and additions such as beetroot — which stains the flesh deep pink — are common in contemporary recipes.
