Miroslav Klose
Born: 9 June 1978, Opole, Poland
Position: Striker
National team: Germany (71 goals in 137 caps)

Miroslav Klose (born 9 June 1978) is a German former professional footballer who played as a striker. He is the all-time top scorer in FIFA World Cup history, with 16 goals across four tournaments (2002, 2006, 2010, 2014), surpassing Ronaldo's record during Germany's 7–1 semi-final victory over Brazil in 2014, the tournament Germany went on to win.

Career

Born in Poland and raised in Germany, Klose rose from amateur football to Kaiserslautern, then Werder Bremen, where his heading ability and link-up play flourished. At Bayern Munich (2007–2011) he won two Bundesliga titles, before spending five seasons with Lazio in Italy, where he famously scored five goals in one Serie A match in 2013. Renowned for his aerial strength, unselfish play, and acrobatic somersault celebrations, he was also praised for sportsmanship — on two occasions he alerted referees to goals he had scored unfairly or penalties wrongly awarded in his favour.

Records and coaching

Klose is Germany's all-time leading scorer with 71 goals, and the only player to appear in four World Cup semi-finals. After retiring in 2016 he joined the German national team's coaching staff and later coached at Bayern Munich and as head coach in Austria.
