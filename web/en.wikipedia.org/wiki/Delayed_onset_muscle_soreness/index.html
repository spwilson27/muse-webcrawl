Delayed onset muscle soreness

Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is the pain and stiffness felt in muscles several hours to days after unaccustomed or strenuous exercise. The soreness typically begins within 24 hours, peaks between 24 and 72 hours, and subsides within a week.

Mechanism

DOMS is associated particularly with eccentric exercise — movements in which a muscle lengthens under load, such as running downhill. It is thought to result from microscopic damage to muscle fibers and the resulting inflammatory response, rather than from lactic acid buildup, an older theory now discarded.

Treatment

The soreness usually resolves on its own. Light exercise, massage, heat and anti-inflammatory medication may ease symptoms. A protective adaptation known as the repeated bout effect means the same exercise causes far less soreness when performed again.
