The 2015–16 Premier League was the 24th season of the Premier League. It is famous for Leicester City winning the title — widely considered the most unlikely triumph in English football history. Leicester, who had narrowly avoided relegation the previous season and began the campaign as 5,000–1 outsiders with bookmakers, were confirmed as champions on 2 May 2016 with two matches to spare.

Season summary

Defending champions Chelsea collapsed to a tenth-place finish, and manager José Mourinho was dismissed in December 2015 for the second time by the club. Jürgen Klopp took over at Liverpool, while Leicester, Arsenal, and Tottenham Hotspur contested the title through the spring.

Leicester's title run

Under Claudio Ranieri, Leicester combined a compact defence (Wes Morgan and Robert Huth), the midfield energy of N'Golo Kanté, and the goals of Jamie Vardy — who scored in a record eleven consecutive matches — and Riyad Mahrez, voted PFA Players' Player of the Year. Tottenham's 2–2 draw at Chelsea on 2 May handed Leicester the title, sparking celebrations around the world.

Managerial upheaval

The season saw an unusual number of high-profile changes: Brendan Rodgers, Dick Advocaat, Tim Sherwood, Steve McClaren, and Mourinho all left their posts, and both Manchester clubs announced new managers for the following season — Pep Guardiola at City and Mourinho at United.

European places and relegation

Arsenal finished second, Tottenham third, and Manchester City fourth. Aston Villa were relegated for the first time in the Premier League era, along with Norwich City and Newcastle United.
