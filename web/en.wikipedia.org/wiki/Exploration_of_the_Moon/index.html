Exploration of the Moon

The exploration of the Moon began with robotic spacecraft in the Space Race of the 1950s and 1960s and culminated in the crewed landings of NASA's Apollo program between 1969 and 1972. The Soviet Luna programme achieved the first human-made object to reach the Moon (Luna 2, 1959), the first photographs of the far side (Luna 3, 1959), and the first soft landing (Luna 9, 1966). The United States' Ranger, Surveyor, and Lunar Orbiter missions prepared the way for Apollo.

Apollo landings

On 20 July 1969, Apollo 11 astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed in the Sea of Tranquility while Michael Collins orbited above; Armstrong's first step was watched live by an estimated 600 million people. Five further landings followed through Apollo 17 in December 1972. In total, twelve astronauts walked on the Moon, conducting experiments, deploying instruments, and returning 382 kilograms of lunar rock and soil.

Later exploration

After a long lull, lunar exploration resumed with orbiters such as Clementine (1994), Lunar Prospector (1998), SMART-1, Kaguya, and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (2009). China's Chang'e programme achieved soft landings with Chang'e 3 (2013) and the first far-side landing with Chang'e 4 (2019), and returned samples with Chang'e 5 (2020) and from the far side with Chang'e 6 (2024). India's Chandrayaan-3 landed near the lunar south pole in 2023. NASA's Artemis programme aims to return astronauts to the Moon, targeting the south polar region where water ice exists in shadowed craters.
