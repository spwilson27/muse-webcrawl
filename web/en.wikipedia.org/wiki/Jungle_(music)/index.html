Jungle is a genre of dance music that emerged from the British rave scene and sound system culture in the 1990s. Emerging from breakbeat hardcore, the genre is characterised by rapid breakbeats (often cut up from records such as the "Amen break"), deep sub-bass lines, and influences from reggae, dub and dancehall, including MCs toasting over tracks. The style laid the foundations for drum and bass.

History

Jungle developed in London around 1992–1993 as producers combined the fast breakbeats of hardcore rave with the bass weight and ragga samples of Jamaican sound system culture. Pirate radio stations such as Kool FM and Rude FM spread the sound, and labels like Suburban Base, Moving Shadow, and Reinforced Records released defining tracks. Producers such as Shy FX, LTJ Bukem, A Guy Called Gerald, and Goldie became central figures; Goldie's 1995 album Timeless brought the related drum and bass sound to wide critical acclaim.

Media moral panic about the scene, and associations with the "junglist" subculture rooted in Black British communities, accompanied the genre's mid-1990s peak. By the late 1990s the smoother, more polished term "drum and bass" had largely replaced "jungle" commercially, though jungle itself survived and enjoyed revivals in the 2010s and 2020s, with artists such as Nia Archives and labels championing an old-school "new jungle" sound.

Characteristics

Jungle tracks typically feature chopped and time-stretched drum breaks at around 150–170 BPM, heavy sub-bass, samples from reggae and dancehall records, "rewind" culture (DJs pulling a popular track back to the start), and MCs providing rhythmic vocal accompaniment. Key breaks used include the Amen break, the Funky Drummer break, and the Think break.
