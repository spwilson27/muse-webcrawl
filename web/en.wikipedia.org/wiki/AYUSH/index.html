AYUSH

AYUSH is an acronym for the traditional and non-conventional systems of health care and healing recognised by the Government of India: Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy. The term is also used as shorthand for the Ministry of Ayush, the Indian government ministry responsible for developing education, research and propagation of these systems.

History

The Department of Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy (ISM&H) was created in 1995 under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and renamed the Department of AYUSH in November 2003. On 9 November 2014, the department was elevated to a full ministry, the Ministry of AYUSH, in order to give greater policy focus to traditional medicine. In 2021 its official stylisation was changed to "Ministry of Ayush".

Systems covered

  • Ayurveda — the classical medical system of India, based on texts such as the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita.
  • Yoga and Naturopathy — Yoga as a physical, mental and spiritual discipline; naturopathy emphasises drugless healing through diet, water, air and other natural elements.
  • Unani — a Greco-Arab system of medicine practised in South Asia, based on the humoral theory of Hippocrates and Galen as developed by Ibn Sina.
  • Siddha — a traditional system originating in Tamil-speaking regions of South India.
  • Homeopathy — the system founded by Samuel Hahnemann in the late 18th century, based on the principle of "like cures like" and highly diluted remedies.

Sowa-Rigpa (Tibetan medicine) was recognised under the AYUSH umbrella by an amendment in 2010.

Institutions and criticism

The ministry oversees research councils for each system (such as CCRAS for Ayurveda and CCRH for homeopathy), regulatory commissions for Indian medicine and homeopathy education, and the annual celebration of the International Day of Yoga on 21 June. AYUSH systems have been promoted by the government for primary health care, but their inclusion has also drawn criticism from the Indian Medical Association and evidence-based medicine advocates, particularly regarding homeopathy and the scientific validation of traditional remedies.
