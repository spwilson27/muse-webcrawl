Snøhetta (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈsnøːˌhɛtɑ]) is a Norwegian architectural and design practice known for its integrated approach to architecture, landscape architecture, interior architecture, product design and graphic design. The firm takes its name from Snøhetta, a mountain in the Dovrefjell range of Norway, traditionally held to be the home of the mythical figures of Norwegian folklore.

History

The practice began in 1987 as a collaborative workshop of architects and landscape architects in Oslo, and was formally founded in 1989 by Kjetil Trædal Thorsen and Craig Dykers. That year the young firm won the international competition to design the new Bibliotheca Alexandrina in Egypt, a landmark project completed in 2002 that brought Snøhetta international attention.

Major works

Snøhetta's best-known building is the Oslo Opera House (2008), whose sloping marble roof rises directly from the Oslofjord and invites the public to walk across it. The project won the European Union Prize for Contemporary Architecture (Mies van der Rohe Award) in 2009. Other prominent works include the National September 11 Memorial Museum Pavilion in New York, the expansion of the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art (2016), the Le Monde Group headquarters in Paris, the underwater restaurant Under in Norway (2019) — Europe's first underwater restaurant — and the Shanghai Grand Opera House.

Philosophy

The firm is known for its emphasis on the relationship between architecture and landscape, for democratic, publicly accessible buildings, and for interdisciplinary "transpositioning", in which staff exchange roles and disciplines during design. Snøhetta has also worked on social and environmental research projects, including the Svart hotel concept designed to produce more energy than it consumes.
