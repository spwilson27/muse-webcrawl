North Bass Island

North Bass Island is an island in Lake Erie, in the U.S. state of Ohio. It is one of the Bass Islands, a group of islands in the western basin of Lake Erie, and is administratively part of Put-in-Bay Township in Ottawa County. Of the three Bass Islands, North Bass is the northernmost and the least developed, lying close to the international border with Canada.

Geography

The island covers roughly 700 acres (about 280 hectares). Unlike its busier neighbors, South Bass Island (home to Put-in-Bay) and Middle Bass Island, North Bass has no large village and is characterized by vineyards, woods, and scattered cottages. Much of the island is devoted to grape growing, taking advantage of the moderating effect Lake Erie has on the local climate, which extends the growing season.

History

Like the other Lake Erie islands, North Bass was settled in the nineteenth century, and viticulture became its principal industry. The island's grapes were used by regional wineries, and wine production remains central to its identity. In the early twenty-first century the State of Ohio acquired a substantial share of the island, and North Bass Island State Park was established, preserving shoreline and interior land for public recreation, hiking, camping, and fishing while private vineyards continue to operate.

Transportation

The island is reached by private boat or by air via the North Bass Island Airport. There is no regular public ferry service comparable to that serving Put-in-Bay, which contributes to the island's quiet character. The year-round population is very small, swelling modestly in the summer months with seasonal residents and visitors.
