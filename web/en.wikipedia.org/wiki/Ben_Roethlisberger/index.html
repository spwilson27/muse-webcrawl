Benjamin Todd Roethlisberger Sr. (born 2 March 1982), nicknamed "Big Ben", is an American former professional football quarterback who played his entire 18-season career in the National Football League (NFL) with the Pittsburgh Steelers. Known for his size, strength and ability to extend plays outside the pocket, he led Pittsburgh to two Super Bowl championships.

Early life and college

Roethlisberger was born in Lima, Ohio, and raised in Findlay, where he was a three-sport star at Findlay High School; he did not play quarterback full-time until his senior year because the coach's son held the position. At Miami University in Ohio he set school records and led the RedHawks to a 13–1 season and a GMAC Bowl win in 2003.

Professional career

Drafted 11th overall in 2004, Roethlisberger became the starter in week 3 after injuries to Charlie Batch and Tommy Maddox and won his first 13 starts, earning Offensive Rookie of the Year. In his second season he became the youngest quarterback to win a Super Bowl (Super Bowl XL against Seattle). He won a second title in Super Bowl XLIII against Arizona, engineering the game-winning drive capped by Santonio Holmes's famous toe-tap touchdown catch. He also appeared in Super Bowl XLV, a loss to Green Bay.

Roethlisberger survived a serious motorcycle accident in 2006, riding without a helmet, and later faced sexual-assault allegations in 2009 and 2010; he was not criminally charged but was suspended for four games in 2010 under the NFL's personal conduct policy. Renowned for late comebacks and improvisational play, he retired after the 2021 season with more than 64,000 passing yards and 418 touchdown passes — among the top ten in NFL history in both categories at his retirement — and a 165–81–1 regular-season record as a starter.

Personal life

Roethlisberger is married with three children and lives in the Pittsburgh area. He has been involved in charitable work through the Ben Roethlisberger Foundation, supporting police and fire departments and service dogs.
