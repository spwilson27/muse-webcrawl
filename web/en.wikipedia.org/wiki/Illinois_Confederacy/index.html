Illinois Confederacy

The Illinois Confederacy, also referred to as the Illiniwek or Illini, was a group of twelve to thirteen Algonquian-speaking Native American tribes in the upper Mississippi River valley of North America at the time of European contact. The tribes included the Kaskaskia, Cahokia, Peoria, Tamaroa, Michigamea, Moingwena and others.

History

The Illiniwek occupied territory across present-day Illinois and parts of Missouri, Iowa and Arkansas. They lived in villages of several hundred to a few thousand people, farming maize, beans and squash and hunting bison seasonally. French explorers and missionaries, beginning with Jacques Marquette and Louis Jolliet in 1673, established missions and trade with the Illinois.

Decline

From the late seventeenth century the confederacy suffered devastating wars with the Iroquois Confederacy (during the Beaver Wars) and later with the Fox, Sauk and other peoples. Disease, warfare and displacement reduced the population from perhaps 10,000 or more in the late 1600s to a few hundred by the early nineteenth century. The remaining tribes removed to Kansas and then to Indian Territory (Oklahoma), where their descendants form the Peoria Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma.

Legacy

The state of Illinois takes its name from the French rendering of an Algonquian word for the Illinois River and its people.
