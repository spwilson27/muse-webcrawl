Daulatabad Fort, originally known as Devagiri, is a historic fortified citadel in the village of Daulatabad, about 16 kilometres northwest of Aurangabad in Maharashtra, India. It stands on a steep, conical basalt hill rising some 200 metres above the surrounding plain and is regarded as one of the most powerful and impregnable medieval fortresses in India.

History

The fort was founded in the 12th century by the Yadava king Bhillama V as Devagiri ("hills of the gods"), capital of the Yadava kingdom. It fell to Alauddin Khalji's forces in 1296 and was later annexed to the Delhi Sultanate. In 1327 Sultan Muhammad bin Tughluq renamed it Daulatabad ("city of fortune") and notoriously ordered the entire population of Delhi to move there, making it his new capital — an experiment reversed within a few years at enormous human cost. The fort later passed through Bahmani, Mughal, and Maratha hands; the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb made it a base for his Deccan campaigns.

Defences

Daulatabad's defences are celebrated for their ingenuity. The hill's sides were scraped smooth and steepened, and a rock-cut moat surrounds the citadel. Successive walls and gateways force attackers through confusing, divided passages; a dark, tortuous tunnel entrance — the andheri — could be flooded with smoke and hot oil, and false gates and a drawbridge add further traps. The only approach to the summit is a narrow bridge overlooked by defences. Within the complex stand the Chand Minar, a 63-metre tower erected in 1445 in imitation of Delhi's Qutb Minar, the Chini Mahal palace, and cannon-studded bastions.
