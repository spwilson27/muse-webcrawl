Natick (/ˈneɪtɪk/) is a town in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States, within the Greater Boston area. It is centred about 10 miles (16 km) west of Boston and lies along the route of the Boston Marathon, which passes through the town each Patriots' Day. Its population was 37,006 at the 2020 census.

History

Natick was founded in 1651 by the missionary John Eliot as a "Praying Town" for Christianised Massachusett Native Americans, on land beside the Charles River; the name is often interpreted as "place of hills". During King Philip's War (1675–76) the Natick Indians were interned on Deer Island, where many died. The town was formally incorporated in 1781 as English settlement came to dominate.

In the 19th century Natick became a shoe-manufacturing centre. The writer Harriet Beecher Stowe set part of Oldtown Folks in a fictionalised Natick, and Henry Wilson, a Natick cobbler who became Vice President under Ulysses S. Grant, was known as the "Natick cobbler".

Modern Natick

Natick is home to the U.S. Army Soldier Systems Center (Natick Soldier Systems Center), the military's principal research centre for soldier equipment, food and clothing. Retail centres include the Natick Mall, one of New England's largest shopping malls. The town brands itself the "Home of Champions", reflecting the success of its high school sports teams, and is the hometown of athlete Doug Flutie.
