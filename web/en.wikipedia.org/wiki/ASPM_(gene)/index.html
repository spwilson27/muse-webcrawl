ASPM (abnormal spindle-like microcephaly-associated) is a gene in humans that encodes the abnormal spindle-like microcephaly-associated protein. It is the human orthologue of the Drosophila melanogaster "abnormal spindle" (asp) gene. Defective forms of ASPM are associated with autosomal recessive primary microcephaly, specifically microcephaly primary hereditary type 5 (MCPH5), the most common form of the condition.

Function

The ASPM protein localises to centrosomes and mitotic spindle poles during cell division. It is thought to play a key role in regulating neurogenesis: by controlling the orientation of the mitotic spindle in neural progenitor cells, ASPM helps determine whether divisions are symmetric (producing two progenitor cells) or asymmetric. Loss of ASPM function shifts divisions toward premature neurogenesis, depleting the progenitor pool and producing a smaller brain.

Clinical significance

Mutations in ASPM are the most frequent cause of primary microcephaly, a condition in which the brain is markedly reduced in size while its overall architecture remains broadly normal. Affected individuals typically have intellectual disability but no other major neurological deficits.

Evolution

ASPM became widely known through studies of brain-size evolution in primates. The gene shows evidence of accelerated evolution and positive selection along the lineage leading to humans, and new alleles of ASPM (and the related gene microcephalin) spread within the last tens of thousands of years. Claims linking particular ASPM variants to normal variation in brain size or cognition have, however, been largely unsupported by later studies.
