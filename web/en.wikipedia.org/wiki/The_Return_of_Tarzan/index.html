The Return of Tarzan is a novel by American writer Edgar Rice Burroughs, the second in his series about the title character Tarzan. It was first published in the pulp magazine New Story Magazine in the issues for June through December 1913; the first book edition was published in 1915 by A. C. McClurg.

Plot summary

The novel picks up where Tarzan of the Apes left off. Having renounced his claim to Jane Porter for the sake of her happiness with William Clayton, Tarzan travels to Paris, where his friend Paul D'Arnot introduces him to society and he meets the Countess Olga de Coude and her villainous husband Count Raoul, entangled with the spy Nikolas Rokoff — Tarzan's nemesis. After adventures in espionage, a duel, and service with the French Foreign Legion in Algeria, Tarzan sails for Cape Town but is thrown overboard by Rokoff and washes ashore back in his native jungle. There he befriends the Waziri tribe, raids the lost city of Opar with its hoard of gold and its high priestess La, and finally rescues Jane, Clayton, and the shipwrecked castaways. The book ends with Tarzan and Jane married in the cabin where he was born.

Themes and sequels

The novel contrasts corrupt civilization with jungle honesty, a recurring Burroughs theme, and introduces Opar — inspired by the biblical Ophir — and La, who would return in later volumes. It was followed by The Beasts of Tarzan (1914). The story was adapted as the 1920 film serial The Revenge of Tarzan, and remains in print as the canonical second Tarzan book.
