Jōmon period

The Jōmon period (縄文時代, c. 14,000 – 1000 BCE) is the era of prehistoric Japan characterised by hunter-gatherer-fisher communities producing cord-marked (jōmon) pottery — among the earliest pottery in the world. Jōmon people lived in pit dwellings, made elaborate flame-style vessels and clay figurines called dogū, and developed settled village life sustained by fishing, gathering and hunting.

The period is usually divided into Incipient, Initial, Early, Middle, Late and Final phases. It ended with the arrival of wet-rice agriculture and continental migrants in the succeeding Yayoi period. In 2021, seventeen Jōmon sites in northern Japan were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
