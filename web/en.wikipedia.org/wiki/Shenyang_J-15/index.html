The Shenyang J-15 (Chinese: 歼-15), also known as the Flying Shark (飞鲨, Fēishā), is a Chinese all-weather, twin-engine, carrier-based fourth-generation multirole fighter aircraft developed by the Shenyang Aircraft Corporation. It is China's first domestically designed and produced carrier-borne fighter, operating from the aircraft carriers Liaoning, Shandong, and Fujian.

Development

The J-15 was developed from the J-11B, incorporating design elements from a T-10K-3 prototype of the Soviet Sukhoi Su-33 that China obtained from Ukraine in 2001. The aircraft first flew on 31 August 2009 and made its first arrested landing on Liaoning on 23 November 2012, flown by test pilot Dai Mingmeng. It features folding wings, a strengthened undercarriage, an arrester hook, and canards for carrier operations.

A J-15 taking off from the carrier Liaoning

Characteristics

The J-15 is powered by two WS-10 turbofan engines in later production batches and carries PL-8, PL-10, PL-12, and PL-15 air-to-air missiles as well as anti-ship and precision-guided weapons. It is one of the largest and heaviest carrier fighters in service. A catapult-capable variant, the J-15T, has been developed for the EMALS-equipped carrier Fujian.
