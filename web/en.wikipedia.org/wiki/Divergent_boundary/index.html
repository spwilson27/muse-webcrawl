Divergent boundary

In plate tectonics, a divergent boundary or divergent plate boundary (also known as a constructive boundary or extensional boundary) is a linear feature between two tectonic plates that are moving away from each other. Divergent boundaries within continents initially produce rifts, which eventually become rift valleys; most active divergent boundaries occur between oceanic plates and exist as mid-oceanic ridges.

Divergent boundary
Type: Plate boundary
Examples: Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift

Process

As plates separate, magma rises from the mantle to fill the gap, cooling to form new crust. Spreading rates vary from about 10 mm/yr to over 100 mm/yr. The process drives seafloor spreading, recorded in symmetric magnetic stripes either side of ridges.

Examples

  • Mid-Atlantic Ridge — separating the Americas from Europe and Africa; surfaces in Iceland.
  • East Pacific Rise
  • East African Rift — a continental rift splitting the African Plate into Somali and Nubian plates.
  • Red Sea and Gulf of Aden — young ocean basins.
  • Great Rift Valley and Baikal Rift Zone.

Effects

Divergent boundaries produce shallow earthquakes, volcanic activity, hydrothermal vents ("black smokers") supporting chemosynthetic ecosystems, and new ocean floor.
