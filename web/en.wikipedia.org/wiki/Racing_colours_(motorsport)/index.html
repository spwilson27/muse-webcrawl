Racing colours (motorsport)

Racing colours are the national paint schemes applied to racecars in international motorsport competitions. Before sponsorship liveries became dominant in the late 1960s, cars traditionally competed in the colour assigned to the entrant's country, a system formalised in the early decades of Grand Prix racing.

Origins

The scheme grew out of the Gordon Bennett Cup races of 1900–1905, in which teams competed as national entries. Colours were later codified by the AIACR (forerunner of the FIA). Famous assignments included British racing green for the United Kingdom, Rosso Corsa (racing red) for Italy, silver — later the Silver Arrows — for Germany, blue for France (Bleu de France), white with blue stripes for the United States, and white with a red disc for Japan.

Decline

From 1968, Formula One permitted commercial sponsorship, and cars increasingly carried sponsors' liveries. National colours survived longest in some categories and are still evoked by heritage liveries and national teams in junior formulae.

Notable traditions

British racing green became so iconic that manufacturers such as Jaguar and Aston Martin used it in road cars; Ferrari has raced in Rosso Corsa continuously, making red synonymous with the marque even in the sponsorship era.
