November 1918

November 1918 was a pivotal month at the end of the First World War, marked by the collapse of the Central Powers, the Armistice of 11 November 1918 that ended fighting on the Western Front, and revolutionary upheaval across Europe.

Key events

The month began with the Armistice of Villa Giusti (3 November) taking Austria-Hungary out of the war, as the Habsburg Empire dissolved into new states. In Germany, the Kiel mutiny of late October spread into the German Revolution: on 9 November Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed a republic in Berlin, Kaiser Wilhelm II abdicated and fled to the Netherlands, and on 11 November Matthias Erzberger signed the armistice in the Compiègne Wagon in France, effective at 11 a.m. — the "eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month."

Consequences

November 1918 also saw Poland regain independence (11 November under Józef Piłsudski), the proclamation of Czechoslovakia and the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes in preceding weeks, and the beginnings of the Weimar Republic with elections promised for January 1919. The month set the stage for the Paris Peace Conference and the Treaty of Versailles.
