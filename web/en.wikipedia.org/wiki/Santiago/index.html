Santiago

Santiago with the snow-capped Andes behind

Santiago, officially Santiago de Chile, is the capital and largest city of Chile, with about 6.8 million residents in its metropolitan area — roughly a third of the country's population. It lies in Chile's Central Valley between the Andes to the east and the Chilean Coast Range to the west.

Founded by Pedro de Valdivia in 1541, Santiago was the scene of the 1973 military coup, when the presidential palace La Moneda was bombed and President Salvador Allende died. Cerro San Cristóbal and Cerro Santa Lucía overlook the grid of the city, whose modern skyline in Providencia and Las Condes ('Sanhattan') includes Gran Torre Santiago, South America's tallest building at 300 metres. Winter smog trapped by the Andes is a chronic problem. The city hosted the 2023 Pan American Games.
