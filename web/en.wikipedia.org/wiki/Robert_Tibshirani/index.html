Robert Tibshirani

Robert Tibshirani
Born: 10 July 1956, Niagara Falls, Ontario, Canada
Field: Statistics, machine learning
Institutions: Stanford University (formerly University of Toronto)
Known for: The lasso method; generalised additive models; significance analysis of microarrays

Robert Tibshirani (born 10 July 1956) is a Canadian-American statistician, professor at Stanford University, widely known for proposing the lasso method for regression and variable selection, and for his influential books on statistical learning. He is a fellow of the Royal Society of Canada and has received honours including the COPSS Presidents' Award.

Career

Tibshirani studied statistics at the University of Waterloo and the University of Toronto, where he earned his doctorate in 1985 under the supervision of Bradley Efron at Stanford's intellectual orbit of modern computational statistics; he taught at the University of Toronto before joining Stanford's faculty, where he has held professorships in statistics and biomedical data science.

Contributions

In 1996 Tibshirani introduced the lasso (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator), a regression method that constrains the sum of the absolute values of coefficients, simultaneously shrinking them and setting some exactly to zero, thus performing variable selection — a foundational tool in modern high-dimensional statistics. With Trevor Hastie he developed generalised additive models, and he is co-author, with Hastie and Jerome Friedman, of The Elements of Statistical Learning (2001), one of the most widely used textbooks in machine learning, and of An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Gareth James and Daniela Witten. Other contributions include significance analysis of microarrays (SAM) for genomics and work on model selection and post-selection inference.
