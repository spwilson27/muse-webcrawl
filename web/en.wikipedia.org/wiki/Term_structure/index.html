Term structure

The term structure of interest rates is the relationship between interest rates (yields) and different terms to maturity for debt instruments of the same credit quality — graphically, the yield curve. It is a central object in fixed-income analysis: governments' yield curves summarise the market's pricing of money over time.

Shapes

A normal yield curve slopes upward (longer maturities pay more, compensating for risk and time). Curves may be flat, or inverted — short rates above long rates — a pattern historically associated with impending recessions. Steepening and flattening describe changes in slope.

Theories

Three classical explanations compete and combine: the expectations hypothesis (long rates average expected future short rates), liquidity preference (investors demand a premium for holding long bonds), and market segmentation (different maturities serve different clienteles). Modern practice fits smooth curves (e.g. Nelson–Siegel, Svensson) to bond prices and extracts spot and forward rates for pricing derivatives.
