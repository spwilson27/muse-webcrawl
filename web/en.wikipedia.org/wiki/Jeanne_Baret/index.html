Jeanne Baret

Jeanne Baret (27 July 1740 – 5 August 1807) was a French explorer and botanist, recognized as the first woman to complete a circumnavigation of the globe. She joined Louis Antoine de Bougainville's expedition of 1766–1769 disguised as a man, serving as valet and assistant to the naturalist Philibert Commerson.

Born27 July 1740, La Comelle, France
Died5 August 1807, Saint-Aulaye, France
Known forFirst woman to circumnavigate the globe

Expedition

As Commerson's assistant, Baret helped collect thousands of plant specimens in South America, the Pacific, and elsewhere; the flowering vine Bougainvillea was among the expedition's finds. Her sex was reportedly discovered in Tahiti in 1768, and she left the expedition at Mauritius.

Later life and recognition

Baret eventually returned to France, completing her circumnavigation, and was later granted a pension by the French state in recognition of her work. The plant genus Baretia and various species commemorations have honoured her belatedly.
