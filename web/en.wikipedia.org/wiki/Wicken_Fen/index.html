Wicken Fen

Wicken Fen, near the village of Wicken in Cambridgeshire, England, is one of Britain's oldest nature reserves and one of the few remaining fragments of the once vast Fenland wetland. Acquired by the National Trust from 1899 onwards — making it the Trust's first nature reserve — it preserves undrained fen habitat of sedge beds, reedbeds, and wet woodland (carr) that has largely disappeared elsewhere following centuries of drainage for agriculture.

Ecology and history

The reserve is a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Ramsar wetland, noted for extraordinary invertebrate diversity: thousands of species of insects, spiders, and other invertebrates have been recorded, along with plants such as marsh pea and fen violet, and birds including bitterns and marsh harriers. Charles Darwin collected beetles here in the 1820s, and the fen's long scientific history includes some of the earliest ecological experiments in Britain. Traditional management — cutting sedge for thatch and litter — maintains the open fen, and Konik ponies and Highland cattle graze parts of the reserve. The Wicken Fen Vision project aims to expand the wetland over surrounding farmland to create a landscape-scale habitat.
