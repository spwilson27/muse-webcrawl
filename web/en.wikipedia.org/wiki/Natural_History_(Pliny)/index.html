Natural History (Pliny)

Naturalis Historia

Author: Pliny the Elder

Published: AD 77–79

Language: Latin

Books: 37

Subject: Natural history, astronomy, geography, zoology, botany, mineralogy

The Natural History (Latin: Naturalis Historia) is a work by the Roman author Pliny the Elder, published in AD 77–79. It is one of the largest single works to survive from the Roman Empire and purports to cover all ancient knowledge, drawing on some 2,000 volumes by 100 authors.

Contents

Its 37 books cover cosmology and geography, humans, animals, plants and medicine, and metals and stones. Famous passages include accounts of art history — the main source for much of Greek and Roman art knowledge — descriptions of fantastical peoples at the edges of the world, and practical farming and mining lore. Pliny's nephew, Pliny the Younger, described his uncle's relentless note-taking; Pliny the Elder died in the eruption of Vesuvius in AD 79 while observing it.

Influence

The Natural History became the model for later encyclopedias, was heavily copied through the Middle Ages, and was among the first classical works printed (1469).
