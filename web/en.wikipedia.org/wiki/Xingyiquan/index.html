Xingyiquan

Xingyiquan (Chinese: 形意拳; pinyin: Xíngyìquán), also romanised as Hsing I Ch'üan, is a Chinese internal martial art. Its name means "Form-Intention Fist": the visible form (xing) is driven by the mind's intention (yi). Together with tai chi and baguazhang, it is one of the three classic internal (neijia) arts.

History

Legend attributes its creation to the Song-dynasty general Yue Fei, but the documented art traces to Li Luoneng (Li Nengran) in 19th-century Shanxi, who learned from Dai Longbang. Li's students, including Guo Yunshen — famed for his "crushing fist" (beng quan) — and later Sun Lutang, spread the art. Major branches include Shanxi, Hebei and Henan styles.

Principles and practice

Training centres on the standing posture santishi (three-body stance), the Five Element Fists (wuxing: splitting/metal, drilling/water, crushing/wood, pounding/fire, crossing/earth), and the Twelve Animal forms (dragon, tiger, monkey, horse, alligator, chicken, sparrowhawk, swallow, snake, tai bird, eagle and bear). Xingyi emphasises direct, explosive linear attacks, whole-body power and aggressive stepping forward.
