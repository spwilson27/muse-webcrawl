Journalism

[Image: Reporters' notebooks, recorders and press passes on a newsroom desk]

Journalism is the production and distribution of reports on events, facts, ideas and people, intended to inform society and support public life. Its core disciplines are verification, independence from the subjects covered, and accountability to the audience — codified in professional ethics codes and taught through beat reporting, interviewing and editing crafts.

History

From the Acta Diurna of Rome and 17th-century corantos, through the penny press of the 1830s, the telegraph and wire services, yellow journalism, 20th-century objectivity norms, broadcast and television news, to the internet and social-media eras, journalism's forms have tracked its technologies and business models.

Genres and debates

Journalism includes news, features, investigations, opinion and data journalism. Standing debates concern objectivity versus transparency, the collapse of local-news business models, press freedom and safety, and the effect of platforms and AI on the information ecosystem.
