The Plaza Hotel (often simply the Plaza) is a luxury hotel and condominium residence in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. It stands on the western side of Grand Army Plaza, from which it takes its name, at the corner of Fifth Avenue and Central Park South, adjacent to the southeastern corner of Central Park.

History

The present building, designed by Henry Janeway Hardenbergh in the French Renaissance château style, opened on October 1, 1907, replacing an earlier hotel of the same name on the site. Built at a cost of about $12.5 million, it was celebrated as one of the world's great hotels, with innovations and luxuries for its time. Over the twentieth century it hosted royalty, presidents, and celebrities, and became a fixture of New York social life through venues such as the Palm Court, the Oak Room, and the Persian Room.

The hotel passed through many owners, including Conrad Hilton, Donald Trump (who bought it in 1988 for about $390 million and called it his "Mona Lisa"), and Israel's El Ad Group, which purchased it in 2004. A major renovation closed the hotel in 2005; it reopened in 2008 as a mixed property combining a smaller hotel with luxury condominiums. The property was sold in 2018 to Katara Hospitality of Qatar for about $600 million, while Fairmont Hotels and Resorts continues to manage the hotel portion.

In popular culture

The Plaza is the setting of Kay Thompson's Eloise children's books (1955 onward), about a girl who lives at the hotel, and appears in films including Alfred Hitchcock's North by Northwest (1959), The Way We Were (1973), and Home Alone 2: Lost in New York (1992). F. Scott Fitzgerald set scenes of The Great Gatsby at the Plaza. Truman Capote's Black and White Ball was held in its Grand Ballroom in 1966, and the Beatles stayed there on their first American visit in 1964. The 1985 Plaza Accord, an agreement among major governments to depreciate the US dollar, was negotiated and named for the hotel.
