Motif-Index of Folk-Literature

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The six volumes of the revised Motif-Index (1955–1958).

The Motif-Index of Folk-Literature is a six-volume catalog of motifs — the traditional elements, incidents, and narrative devices found in folklore, mythology, ballads, and folktales — compiled by the American folklorist Stith Thompson. First published in 1932–1936 and issued in a greatly enlarged second edition in 1955–1958, it remains a fundamental reference work for the comparative study of folk narrative.

Classification system

Thompson defined a motif as "the smallest element in a tale having a power to persist in tradition." Motifs are arranged in a decimal classification divided into broad lettered categories:

Each entry gives the motif a number (for example, D810, "Magic object a gift"), a brief definition, and references to the tales and scholarly literature in which it occurs.

Relation to the Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index

The Motif-Index complements the Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index, which classifies whole tale types rather than their component motifs. Together, the two works — sometimes called the "Thompson system" — underpin the historic-geographic method of folklore research developed by the Finnish school of Kaarle Krohn and Antti Aarne. Thompson also wrote the standard survey The Folktale (1946).
