Unit blocks are a type of wooden toy block designed for children's construction play. They were developed in 1913 by the American educator Caroline Pratt, founder of the City and Country School in New York City.

The blocks are made from smooth hardwood, typically maple, and are based on a standard unit measuring 1⅜ by 2¾ by 5½ inches. All other pieces in the set are proportional multiples or fractions of this unit — halves, doubles, quadruples, cylinders, triangles, and curves — allowing children to build stable, mathematically harmonious structures.

Educational use

Unit blocks became a staple of early childhood education and kindergarten classrooms, valued for encouraging creativity, spatial reasoning, cooperation, and early mathematical thinking. Pratt's design influenced later generations of construction toys, and unit blocks remain widely used in preschools today.
