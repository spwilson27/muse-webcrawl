Charles-Camille Saint-Saëns (1835–1921) was a French composer, organist, conductor and pianist of the Romantic era. A child prodigy, he made his concert debut at age ten and became one of France's most celebrated musicians.

Works

His best-known works include The Carnival of the Animals (1886), the opera Samson and Delilah, the Danse macabre, the Third Symphony ("Organ Symphony"), and the tone poem Danse macabre. He wrote in nearly every genre, producing over 300 works.

Career

Saint-Saëns was organist of La Madeleine in Paris for twenty years and co-founded the Société Nationale de Musique in 1871 to promote French music. He was the first major composer to write music specifically for film, scoring L'Assassinat du duc de Guise in 1908.
