Rugby World Cup Sevens

The Rugby World Cup Sevens is the premier stand-alone international rugby sevens tournament, organised by World Rugby. First held in Edinburgh in 1993, it crowns world champions in the seven-a-side game for both men's and women's national teams; the men's winners receive the Melrose Cup, named for the Scottish town where sevens was invented in 1883.

History

England won the inaugural men's tournament in 1993. Fiji (1997, 2005, 2022) and New Zealand (2001, 2013, 2018) have been the dominant nations; Wales won a famous surprise title in Dubai in 2009. A women's tournament was added in 2009, won by Australia, with New Zealand taking the 2013, 2018 and 2022 titles. The 2022 edition in Cape Town saw Fiji win the men's and Australia the women's competition.

Format

Since 2018 the tournament has used a straight knockout format, in which every match eliminates a team, making it unusually unforgiving. Following the 2022 edition, World Rugby restructured its sevens calendar around the SVNS series, and the World Cup's future place in the calendar has been the subject of review.
