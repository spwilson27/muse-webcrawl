Multi-level marketing

Multi-level marketing (MLM), also called network marketing or direct selling, is a business model in which revenue comes both from selling products or services to consumers and from recruiting additional sellers, whose sales in turn generate commissions for those above them in a hierarchy or "downline".

Structure

Participants typically buy products from the company, sell them at retail, and recruit new participants. Compensation plans reward multiple levels of recruitment, so earnings depend heavily on building a large network. Well-known MLM companies have sold cosmetics, nutritional supplements, household goods, and essential oils.

Legality and criticism

Regulators distinguish lawful MLM from illegal pyramid schemes, in which returns are paid mainly from recruitment fees rather than genuine retail sales. Critics note that, by companies' own disclosures, the great majority of participants earn little or lose money, and the model has faced lawsuits and regulatory actions in several countries. Supporters argue it offers low-cost entry into self-employment.

Regulation

In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's 1979 ruling on Amway set standards requiring emphasis on retail sales to outside customers. Some jurisdictions restrict or ban MLM structures outright.
