Tamil script

The Tamil script (தமிழ் அரிச்சுவடி Tamiḻ ariccuvaṭi) is an abugida script used to write the Tamil language, spoken principally in Tamil Nadu, India, and in Sri Lanka, Singapore and Malaysia. It is also used for minority languages such as Badaga, Irula, Paniya and Saurashtra. Descended from the Tamil-Brahmi script via the Pallava and Vatteluttu traditions, the modern script was standardised in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Structure

The script has 12 vowels (uyireḻuttu, "soul letters"), 18 consonants (meyeḻuttu, "body letters"), and one special character, the āytam (ஃ). Consonants carry an inherent vowel /a/ that is modified by diacritic signs or suppressed by a dot (pulli) placed above the letter. Combining vowels and consonants yields 216 compound characters, for a total of 247 letters in the traditional inventory. Unlike most other Indic scripts, Tamil does not have separate letters for aspirated or voiced consonants; a single letter serves for both sounds, whose value is determined by position and context.

Numerals and usage

Tamil has its own set of numerals and fractions, though Arabic numerals are now standard in everyday use. The script is written from left to right. Ligatures, once common in printing, were largely eliminated in a government-led simplification reform in 1978, which regularised the shapes of several vowel signs.
