The Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews is one of the oldest golf clubs in the world. It is a private members' club based in St Andrews, Fife, Scotland, with its famous clubhouse situated adjacent to the first tee of the Old Course at St Andrews. The club is widely regarded as the worldwide "Home of Golf".

History

The club was founded on 14 May 1754 as the Society of St Andrews Golfers, when 22 "noblemen and gentlemen" signed the Articles and Laws in Playing at Golf and competed for the Silver Club. In 1834, King William IV became patron of the club and conferred the title "Royal and Ancient", and the club adopted its present name. The club purchased the Old Course's clubhouse site and built its iconic clubhouse in 1854.

Governance of golf

Historically, the R&A was the governing body for golf worldwide (except in the United States and Mexico, where the USGA governs). In 2004, the club's governing functions were transferred to a separate group of companies known as The R&A, which now organizes The Open Championship and jointly maintains the Rules of Golf with the USGA. The club itself continues as a private members' club with around 2,400 members worldwide.

Membership

The R&A admitted its first women members in 2014, ending a 260-year male-only policy following a vote in which 85% of members supported the change. Honorary members have included members of the royal family and leading figures in golf. The Prince of Wales (later Edward VIII) and subsequent royals have served as Captains of the club, an office traditionally marked by the new captain "driving in" from the first tee of the Old Course.

The Open Championship

The Open Championship was first played in 1860 and is organized by The R&A. It is the oldest golf tournament in the world and one of the four men's major championships, traditionally held on links courses in Scotland, England, and Northern Ireland.
