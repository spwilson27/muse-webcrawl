Kaya toast

Kaya toast is a dish consisting of two slices of toast with butter and kaya (coconut jam), commonly served alongside coffee and soft-boiled eggs. Popular in Singapore and Malaysia, it is regarded as a well-known breakfast item and is widely sold at kopitiams (traditional coffee shops) in the region.

Kaya toast
CourseBreakfast
Place of originSingapore / Malaysia
Main ingredientsToasted bread, kaya (coconut jam), butter

History

The dish is believed to have been created by Hainanese immigrants who worked as cooks on British ships and later settled in Singapore and Malaya. Unable to obtain Western fruit jams, they substituted a local spread made from coconut milk, eggs, and sugar, flavoured with pandan leaves.

Preparation

Bread is toasted, often over charcoal in traditional establishments, then spread generously with kaya and a thick slice of cold butter. The toast may be served with soft-boiled eggs seasoned with soy sauce and white pepper, and a cup of strong coffee.

Variations

Modern versions include crackers spread with kaya, steamed bread versions, and kaya served as a topping or filling in various pastries and desserts across Southeast Asia.
