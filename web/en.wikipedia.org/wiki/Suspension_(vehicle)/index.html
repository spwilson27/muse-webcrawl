Suspension (vehicle)

Suspension is the system of tires, tire air, springs, shock absorbers, and linkages that connects a vehicle to its wheels and allows relative motion between the two. Suspension systems serve a dual purpose: contributing to the vehicle's handling and braking for good active safety and driving pleasure, and keeping occupants comfortable and reasonably isolated from road noise, bumps, and vibrations.

Principles

The suspension must support the vehicle's weight (the sprung mass), absorb and damp road shocks through springs and dampers, and keep the tires in contact with the road. Key properties include camber, caster, toe, and wheel travel. Designers must trade ride comfort against handling precision: softer suspensions isolate occupants better, while stiffer ones reduce body roll and improve steering response.

Types

Dependent suspensions connect the wheels on an axle with a rigid beam, as in leaf-sprung live axles. Independent suspensions allow each wheel to move vertically on its own; common designs include the MacPherson strut, double wishbone, and multi-link layouts. Spring media include coil springs, leaf springs, torsion bars, and air springs. Modern systems may add anti-roll bars, adaptive dampers, or fully active suspension that controls each wheel electronically.
