The Zangger Committee, formally the NPT Exporters Committee, is an informal group of nuclear supplier states established in 1971 to reach a common understanding of the export-control obligations in Article III of the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT). It is named after its first chairman, Professor Claude Zangger of Switzerland.

Role

Article III.2 of the NPT requires that certain nuclear materials and equipment "especially designed or prepared" for nuclear use be exported to non-nuclear-weapon states only under International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) safeguards. The committee defined precisely which items this covers, producing the Trigger List — so called because exports of listed items "trigger" the requirement for safeguards. The list, published by the IAEA (as INFCIRC/209), includes reactors and their equipment, reprocessing and enrichment plants, and source and special fissionable materials.

Relationship to other regimes

The Zangger Committee predates and complements the Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG), founded in 1975, which has a broader membership and also controls dual-use items. The committee's understandings are not legally binding but are implemented through members' national export-control laws, and the committee continues to meet, usually in Vienna, to review and update its lists in light of technological change.
