Heaven's Gate (film)

Heaven's Gate is a 1980 American epic Western film written and directed by Michael Cimino, starring Kris Kristofferson, Christopher Walken, Isabelle Huppert, Jeff Bridges and John Hurt. Loosely based on the Johnson County War of 1889–93 in Wyoming, it follows a sheriff caught between wealthy cattle barons and the immigrant settlers they intend to eliminate. The film became notorious as one of the biggest box-office bombs in cinema history and is often credited with ending the director-driven "New Hollywood" era.

Production

Following the success of The Deer Hunter (1978), United Artists gave Cimino extraordinary creative freedom. Production in Montana and Idaho ran far over schedule and budget, growing from an initial $11.6 million estimate to roughly $44 million, amid stories of endless retakes, sets rebuilt to order, and an irrigation system installed to green a battlefield. Steven Bach's book Final Cut documented the troubled production.

Release and failure

The 219-minute premiere in November 1980 was savaged by critics — Vincent Canby called it "an unqualified disaster" — and the film was withdrawn after a week. Cimino re-edited it to 149 minutes for a wider 1981 release, but it grossed only about $3.5 million. The losses contributed to United Artists being sold by Transamerica to MGM, and studios tightened financial control over directors for decades afterwards.

Reassessment

Since the 2010s, restored versions — including a 216-minute director's cut supervised by Cimino and released by the Criterion Collection in 2012 — have been praised for Vilmos Zsigmond's cinematography and visual ambition, and the film is now frequently cited in critics' polls of underrated masterpieces.
