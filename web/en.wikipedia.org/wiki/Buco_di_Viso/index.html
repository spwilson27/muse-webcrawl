Buco di Viso

The Buco di Viso (French: Tunnel de la Traversette) is a pedestrian tunnel through the Cottian Alps between Italy and France, near Monte Viso. Dug in the late fifteenth century, it is regarded as one of the oldest Alpine tunnels and the first bored through the main Alpine watershed for transport.

History

The tunnel was excavated in 1479–1480 at the initiative of Ludovico II, Marquis of Saluzzo, with the agreement of King René of Provence. About 75 metres long and lying some 2,900 metres above sea level below the Col de la Traversette, it let salt traders and merchants pass between Piedmont and the Queyras without the dangerous climb over the col, which is blocked by snow much of the year.

Today

The tunnel still serves hikers on the Giro di Viso circuit around Monte Viso, though rockfall and snow can close it seasonally. It survives as a monument to late-medieval engineering and to the salt trade that linked Italy and France.
