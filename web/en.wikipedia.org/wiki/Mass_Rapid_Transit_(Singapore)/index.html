The Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) is a rapid transit system in Singapore and the principal mode of railway transport in the country. Construction began in 1983, and the first section, between Yio Chu Kang and Toa Payoh on what is now the North South Line, opened on 7 November 1987, making it Southeast Asia's second-oldest metro system after Manila's LRT.

Network

The network comprises six lines — the North South Line, East West Line, North East Line, Circle Line, Downtown Line, and Thomson–East Coast Line — serving well over 140 stations across the island, with further extensions and new lines (including the Jurong Region Line and Cross Island Line) under construction. Infrastructure is owned by the Land Transport Authority, while train operations are run by SMRT Trains and SBS Transit under licence.

Operations

Trains are driverless on the newer lines and run at frequencies of a few minutes during peak hours. Fares are distance-based and are paid using the EZ-Link or NETS FlashPay smart cards or contactless bank cards. The MRT, together with the complementary Light Rail Transit (LRT) systems, forms the backbone of Singapore's public transport network and carries several million passengers daily.

History

The decision to build the MRT followed a decade-long debate in the 1970s and early 1980s between proponents of an all-bus system and those favouring rail; the government concluded in 1982 that a rail-based system was necessary. The initial system cost around S$5 billion, then the largest public works project in Singapore's history.
