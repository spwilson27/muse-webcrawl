The Gold Standard is a standard and certification body for carbon offset projects in the voluntary carbon market and under compliance mechanisms. It was established in 2003 by the WWF, SouthSouthNorth, and Helio International, and is governed by the Gold Standard Foundation, a non-profit based in Geneva, Switzerland.

Origins and purpose

The standard was created to ensure that projects under the Kyoto Protocol's Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) genuinely reduced greenhouse-gas emissions and contributed to sustainable development in host countries — criteria that early CDM projects were accused of neglecting. Gold Standard certification therefore assesses not only emission reductions (additionality, monitoring, verification) but also local stakeholder consultation, safeguards, and sustainable-development benefits.

Gold Standard for the Global Goals

In 2017 the organisation launched the Gold Standard for the Global Goals, aligning certification with the UN Sustainable Development Goals and the Paris Agreement. Projects — including renewable energy, clean cookstoves, safe water supply, afforestation, and waste management — can certify quantified contributions to multiple SDGs in addition to issuing Verified Emission Reductions (VERs) traded as carbon credits.

Reception

The Gold Standard is generally regarded as one of the most rigorous voluntary carbon standards, and its credits have typically commanded premium prices; environmental groups have historically ranked it above less stringent programmes. Like all offset schemes, it has nonetheless faced scrutiny over baseline setting and the quality of some project types in periods of rapid market growth.
