Other namesNear-syncope, near-fainting
SpecialtyCardiology, neurology, emergency medicine

Presyncope, or near-syncope, is a state of lightheadedness, muscular weakness and feeling faint short of actual fainting (syncope). It reflects a transient reduction in blood flow to the brain that stops short of causing complete loss of consciousness. In clinical practice presyncope shares essentially the same spectrum of causes and the same evaluation as syncope, and the two are often investigated identically because near-fainting may precede a frank episode with injury.

Signs and symptoms

An episode typically begins with lightheadedness and dizziness, nausea, sweating, pallor, warmth, blurred or tunnel vision, ringing in the ears and a sense of unsteadiness. Unlike vertigo — a sensation of spinning — presyncopal dizziness is a feeling of impending faint. Episodes are usually brief, resolving when the person sits or lies down.

Causes

  • Reflex (vasovagal) presyncope — triggered by pain, fear, the sight of blood, or prolonged standing; an autonomic reflex causes vasodilation and/or bradycardia.
  • Orthostatic hypotension — a drop in blood pressure on standing, from dehydration, blood loss, medication effects, or autonomic failure (e.g. in diabetes or Parkinson's disease).
  • Cardiac causes — arrhythmias (bradycardia, tachycardia), aortic stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and other structural disease; these are the most concerning causes, particularly when presyncope occurs during exertion or while supine.
  • Other — hypoglycaemia, anaemia, hyperventilation and anxiety can mimic or contribute to episodes.

Evaluation

Assessment centres on a careful history (triggers, position, exertion, palpitations, chest pain, medications, family history of sudden death), orthostatic vital signs, and an electrocardiogram. Depending on the findings, further tests may include blood tests, echocardiography, ambulatory ECG monitoring or tilt-table testing. Red flags — exertional symptoms, chest pain, palpitations preceding the episode, or a family history of sudden cardiac death — warrant urgent cardiac evaluation.

Management

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause. Patients with vasovagal presyncope are advised about trigger avoidance, adequate hydration and salt intake, physical counter-pressure manoeuvres (leg crossing, handgrip, squatting), and lying down at the first symptoms. Medication review addresses offending drugs; cardiac causes may require pacemakers, anti-arrhythmic therapy or surgery. Because episodes can occur while driving or working at heights, safety counselling is an important part of care.
