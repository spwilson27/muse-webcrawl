Aérospatiale (French pronunciation: [a.e.ʁɔ.spa.sjal]) was a French state-owned aerospace and defence manufacturer active from 1970 to 1999. It built civil and military aircraft, helicopters, missiles, satellites and space launchers, and was a principal partner in the Concorde supersonic airliner and the Airbus consortium. In 1999 Aérospatiale merged with Matra Hautes Technologies to form Aérospatiale-Matra, which in 2000 combined with Germany's DASA and Spain's CASA to create EADS, the company that became the Airbus Group.

History

Aérospatiale was formed in 1970 by the merger of the state-owned Sud Aviation and Nord Aviation with the missile and space company SEREB. Sud Aviation brought the Caravelle airliner, the Alouette and Puma helicopter lines, and the French share of Concorde, developed jointly with the British Aircraft Corporation; the partnership produced the first commercial supersonic transport, which entered service in 1976. Aérospatiale was a founding partner of Airbus Industrie (1970) and of the Ariane rocket programme through its space division, which developed the Ariane family of launch vehicles for the European Space Agency.

Products

The company's helicopter division (from 1992 part of the Franco-German Eurocopter, now Airbus Helicopters) produced the Alouette III, Gazelle, Puma, Super Puma/Cougar, Dauphin and Écureuil (Squirrel) families. Fixed-wing products included the ATR turboprop airliners (with Italy's Aeritalia, from 1981), the Corvette business jet, the Epsilon trainer and the TB 30/TB 31 light aircraft of its SOCATA subsidiary. Its missiles division made the Exocet anti-ship missile, the Roland and Crotale air-defence systems (in part through Euromissile), and France's strategic ballistic missiles, including the submarine-launched M4 and M45. Aérospatiale also built satellites and was prime contractor for the Ariane launchers operated by Arianespace.

Merger and legacy

As part of European aerospace consolidation, the French government merged Aérospatiale with the Lagardère group's Matra defence business in 1999, and the combined company joined EADS in July 2000. Its activities survive within Airbus (civil aircraft, helicopters, space systems) and MBDA (missiles). The company name is also remembered in sport: the Aérospatiale works teams and the Toulouse aerospace industrial complex remain central to the French aviation industry.
