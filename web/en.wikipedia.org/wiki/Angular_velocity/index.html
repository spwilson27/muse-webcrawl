Symbolω

TypePseudovector

In physics, angular velocity is a pseudovector representation of how fast an object rotates or revolves about an axis, and in which plane. Its magnitude is the rotational speed — the rate of change of angular displacement — and its direction points along the rotation axis according to the right-hand rule.

Definition

For a particle moving in a circle, the angular velocity is ω = dθ/dt, where θ is the angular position. The tangential (linear) velocity is given by v = ω × r, so its magnitude is v = ωr, with r the radius. One full revolution per second corresponds to ω = 2π rad/s.

For a rigid body, every point shares the same angular velocity about the body's axis of rotation (in the simplest case of fixed-axis rotation). Angular velocity is the time derivative of angular displacement, and its own time derivative is angular acceleration, α = dω/dt.

Vector nature

Angular velocity is a pseudovector (axial vector): unlike ordinary polar vectors it does not reverse under mirror reflection, a property it shares with angular momentum, to which it is related by L = Iω for rotation about a principal axis with moment of inertia I.

Applications

Angular velocity is used everywhere rotating machinery, gyroscopes, planetary rotation, centrifuges and rotational spectroscopy are analysed, and is commonly measured in revolutions per minute (rpm) in engineering.
