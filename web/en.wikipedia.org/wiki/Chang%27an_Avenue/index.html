Chang'an Avenue

Chang'an Avenue (simplified Chinese: 长安街; traditional Chinese: 長安街; pinyin: Cháng'ān Jiē) is a major east–west thoroughfare in Beijing, China. It is often called the "Avenue of Eternal Peace" and is regarded, together with Tiananmen Square, as the symbolic heart of the Chinese capital. The avenue takes its name from Chang'an, the ancient capital of China during several dynasties, corresponding to modern Xi'an, whose name also means "eternal peace".

Chang'an Avenue runs east–west through central Beijing, passing directly in front of the Tiananmen gate of the Forbidden City. It separates Tiananmen Square to the south from the Zhongnanhai leadership compound and the Forbidden City to the north. Because of its location and ceremonial role, the avenue is used for major military parades and national celebrations, including the parades marking the anniversaries of the founding of the People's Republic of China.

Route

The avenue in its narrow sense runs from Dongdan in the east to Xidan in the west, but the name is commonly applied to the whole grand east–west axis of the city, extending from the Tongzhou District in the east to the Shijingshan District in the west, a total length of roughly 55 kilometres (34 mi), making it sometimes described as the "longest street in the world". Along its central section lie many of the most important buildings in China, including the Great Hall of the People, Zhongnanhai, the National Museum of China, the National Centre for the Performing Arts, Wangfujing and the Beijing railway station area.

History

The street originated in the Ming dynasty, when the segments east and west of Tiananmen were known separately as East Chang'an Avenue and West Chang'an Avenue. The two were joined after the gate area was cleared and remodelled following the founding of the People's Republic in 1949. The avenue was progressively widened in the 1950s, in the 1990s, and again before the 2008 Summer Olympics and the 2009 sixtieth anniversary parade. In its central section it is ten lanes wide.

During the Tiananmen Square protests of 1989 the avenue was the scene of the famous encounter between a lone protester, later known as "Tank Man", and a column of People's Liberation Army tanks on 5 June 1989, just east of the square near the Beijing Hotel.

Significance

Because buildings fronting the avenue face the route of state parades, heights and designs along Chang'an Avenue have long been subject to special planning controls. Number 1 East Chang'an Avenue style addresses along the street are among the most prestigious in Beijing, and many ministries, state media headquarters and major banks are located along it.
