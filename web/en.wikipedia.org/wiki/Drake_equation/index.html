Drake equation

Drake equation
Proposed: 1961, Frank Drake
Field: Astrobiology, SETI
Estimates: Number of communicative extraterrestrial civilisations in the Milky Way

The Drake equation is a probabilistic argument used to estimate the number of active, communicative extraterrestrial civilisations in the Milky Way galaxy. It was written by Frank Drake in 1961, not as a precise calculation but as an agenda for the first SETI meeting at Green Bank.

The equation

N = R* · fp · ne · fl · fi · fc · L, where R* is the star formation rate, fp the fraction with planets, ne habitable planets per system, fl the fraction developing life, fi intelligence, fc detectable communications, and L the civilisation's lifespan.

Criticism and current estimates

Estimates of N have ranged from millions to fewer than one, reflecting deep uncertainty in the biological and social factors. Exoplanet discoveries have firmed up the astronomical terms. The equation structures debate about the Fermi paradox.
