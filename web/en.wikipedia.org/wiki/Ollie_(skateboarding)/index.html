Ollie (skateboarding)

The ollie is a skateboarding trick where the rider and board leap into the air without the use of the rider's hands. It is the combination of stomping (also known as popping) the tail of the skateboard off the ground to get the board mostly vertical, jumping in front of the foot leading in the popping process, and sliding the front foot up the skateboard to level and guide the board to a higher point in the air.

The ollie is a fundamental skill in skateboarding. Ollies are necessary to leap onto, over, or off of any obstacle or terrain. Because the ollie is the basis for many other tricks—such as the kickflip and heelflip—mastering it is often the first step for a new skateboarder on their road to learning their craft.

Origin

The original flat ground ollie was developed in 1978 by Alan "Ollie" Gelfand in Hollywood, Florida. Gelfand made his first ollie in a half-pipe; he made the board pop off the ground by stomping the tail while jumping. He called it the "no hands ollie", and it was later simply called the ollie. Rodney Mullen adapted the ollie to flat ground in 1982, which allowed riders to perform tricks without ramps or other equipment.

Technique

To perform the ollie, the rider crouches slightly, then jumps while simultaneously popping the tail of the board downward against the ground with the back foot. As the board rises, the rider slides the front foot forward along the grip tape toward the nose, which levels the board in mid-air and lifts the tail. The rider then bends their knees, allowing the board to rise, before landing with both feet over the trucks and knees bent to absorb the impact.

Variations and records

Variations of the ollie include the switch ollie (performed riding switch), the fakie ollie, and the nollie (nose ollie), where the rider pops the nose of the board instead of the tail. The highest ollie on record has been measured at over 44 inches (about 112 cm) from flat ground.
