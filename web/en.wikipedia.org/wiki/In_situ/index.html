In situ

In situ (Latin for "in position" or "on site") is a phrase used across many disciplines to describe something that is studied, measured, preserved or occurring in its original place, as opposed to being removed to a laboratory or another location (ex situ) or modelled in a computer (in silico).

Uses

In conservation biology, in situ conservation protects species in their natural habitats, in contrast to ex situ conservation in zoos or seed banks. In archaeology, artefacts recorded in situ retain their context of deposition. In medicine, carcinoma in situ denotes abnormal cells that have not invaded surrounding tissue. In chemistry and materials science, in situ measurements and synthesis take place within the reaction environment itself; in Earth and space science, in situ data are gathered by instruments on the spot rather than by remote sensing.
