Rabbi Chaim Soloveitchik of Brisk

Chaim (Halevi) Soloveitchik (Yiddish: חיים סאָלאָווייטשיק, 1853 – July 30, 1918), also known as Reb Chaim Brisker, was a rabbi and Talmudic scholar credited with developing the Brisker method of Talmudic study, a conceptual and analytical approach to the Talmud that remains influential in yeshivas today. He was the rosh yeshiva of the Volozhin Yeshiva and later the rabbi of Brisk (Brest-Litovsk), and the founder of the Soloveitchik rabbinic dynasty's Brisker line.

Biography

Soloveitchik was born in Volozhin, in present-day Belarus, in 1853. His father, Rabbi Yosef Dov Soloveitchik (the Beis Halevi), was a leading Talmudic scholar. Chaim studied at the Volozhin Yeshiva and eventually became one of its principal lecturers, teaching alongside Rabbi Naftali Zvi Yehuda Berlin (the Netziv).

When the Volozhin Yeshiva was closed by the Russian authorities in 1892, Soloveitchik moved to Brisk (Brest), where his father had served as rabbi, and succeeded him in that position. In Brisk he became known both for his scholarship and for his extensive charitable activity, maintaining an open house for the poor and needy.

He died in Otwock, near Warsaw, in 1918, having fled Brisk during the First World War.

The Brisker method

Soloveitchik's major contribution was a new method of Talmudic analysis, often called the Brisker method (Brisker derech). The method emphasizes precise conceptual definitions and distinctions — frequently expressed as chakira, the division of a legal concept into two distinct components or understandings (often framed as tzvei dinim, "two laws"). A classic application concerns the writings of Maimonides, whose Mishneh Torah Soloveitchik analyzed with this approach.

His novellae were published posthumously as Chiddushei Rabbeinu Chaim Halevi, based on his writings and notes taken by students. The method was further developed by his son, Rabbi Yitzchak Zev Soloveitchik (the Brisker Rav), and became the dominant approach to Talmud study in many Lithuanian-style yeshivas.

Family

Soloveitchik's sons included Rabbi Moshe Soloveichik, who emigrated to the United States and became a rosh yeshiva at Yeshiva University, and Rabbi Yitzchak Zev Soloveitchik, who succeeded him in Brisk and later moved to Jerusalem. His descendants include Rabbi Joseph B. Soloveitchik of Boston.
