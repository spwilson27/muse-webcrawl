Djemal Pasha

Ahmed Djemal Pasha (Ottoman Turkish: احمد جمال پاشا, romanized: Ahmed Cemâl Paşa; 6 May 1872 – 21 July 1922) was an Ottoman military leader and one of the "Three Pashas" who ruled the Ottoman Empire during World War I, alongside Enver Pasha and Talaat Pasha.

Career

Born in Mytilene on the island of Lesbos, Djemal graduated from the Ottoman military academy and rose through the ranks of the Committee of Union and Progress. He served as Minister of Marine and as governor of Baghdad and Adana. During the war he commanded the Ottoman Fourth Army in Syria and Palestine, where his campaigns against the Suez Canal failed, and his harsh rule — including the execution of Arab nationalists in Beirut and Damascus in 1915–1916 — earned him the Arabic nickname al-Saffah ("the Blood Shedder"). He is also regarded as one of the architects of the Armenian genocide.

Death

After the Ottoman defeat in 1918 Djemal fled abroad, later advising the Afghan army. He was assassinated in Tbilisi, Georgia, on 21 July 1922 by Armenian operatives as part of Operation Nemesis.
