The Betwa River is a river in central and northern India, and a tributary of the Yamuna. It rises in the Vindhya Range (Raisen district) just north of Hoshangabad in Madhya Pradesh and flows north-east through Madhya Pradesh and Orchha into Uttar Pradesh.

Course

Nearly half of its course, which is not navigable, runs over the Malwa Plateau. The confluence of the Betwa and the Yamuna rivers is in Hamirpur district in Uttar Pradesh, in the vicinity of the town of Orchha. The Betwa is an important river of the Bundelkhand region.

Dams

The Rajghat Dam, a multipurpose project of the governments of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh, is built on the river near Lalitpur. The Matatila Dam, completed in 1958, is another major structure on the Betwa, used for irrigation and hydroelectric power generation. The Parichha Dam is also built on the Betwa, near Jhansi.

History

In Sanskrit, "Betwa" is Vetravati. According to legend, the river is named after a daughter of King Shashabindhu. The Betwa is mentioned in the epic Mahabharata along with the Charmanwati River. The historic town of Orchha, founded in the 16th century by the Bundela chief Rudra Pratap Singh, stands on its banks.
