Hyperphosphatemia

Hyperphosphatemia is an electrolyte disorder in which there is an elevated level of phosphate in the blood. In most people there are no symptoms; complications may include weak bones, muscle cramps, joint pain and deposits of calcium phosphate in soft tissues.

Causes

The most common cause is kidney failure, in which the kidneys cannot excrete phosphate normally. Other causes include hypoparathyroidism, diabetic ketoacidosis, tumour lysis syndrome, rhabdomyolysis (in which phosphate is released from damaged cells), and excessive intake of phosphate or vitamin D. Laboratory artefact from haemolysis can also falsely elevate measured levels.

Complications

Chronic hyperphosphatemia in kidney disease contributes to secondary hyperparathyroidism and chronic kidney disease–mineral and bone disorder, and is associated with vascular calcification and increased cardiovascular mortality. The resulting high calcium-phosphate product can cause calciphylaxis in severe cases.

Treatment

Management includes dietary phosphate restriction, phosphate binders taken with meals (such as sevelamer, lanthanum carbonate and calcium-based binders), and, in kidney failure, dialysis. Treatment of the underlying cause is central, and active vitamin D analogues and calcimimetics may be used to control associated parathyroid hormone excess.
