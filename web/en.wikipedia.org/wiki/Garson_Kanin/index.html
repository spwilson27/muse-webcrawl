Garson Kanin (November 24, 1912 – March 13, 1999) was an American writer and director of plays and films. He is best known as the author of the Broadway comedy Born Yesterday (1946) and, with his wife Ruth Gordon, as co-writer of the Katharine Hepburn and Spencer Tracy films Adam's Rib (1949) and Pat and Mike (1952).

Career

Kanin began in the theatre as an actor and assistant to George Abbott before moving to Hollywood, where he directed films including Bachelor Mother (1939) and My Favorite Wife (1940). During the Second World War he served in the Office of Strategic Services and co-directed the documentary The True Glory (1945).

Born Yesterday

His play Born Yesterday, about a corrupt scrap-metal tycoon and the ex-chorus girl he hires a tutor for in Washington, D.C., was a major Broadway success and was filmed in 1950 with Judy Holliday, who won an Academy Award.

Later work

Kanin wrote novels, short stories and memoirs, including Tracy and Hepburn: An Intimate Memoir (1971), and the play The Rat Race. He was married to actress and writer Ruth Gordon from 1942 until her death in 1985.
