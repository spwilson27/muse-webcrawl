Cool Hand Luke

Cool Hand Luke is a 1967 American prison drama film directed by Stuart Rosenberg, starring Paul Newman in the title role and George Kennedy, who won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor. The screenplay by Donn Pearce and Frank Pierson is based on Pearce's 1965 novel of the same name.

Plot

Luke Jackson, a decorated war veteran, is arrested for cutting the heads off parking meters while drunk and sentenced to two years on a Florida chain gang. His refusal to submit to the camp's hierarchy — including winning a brutal boxing match through sheer endurance and eating fifty hard-boiled eggs in an hour on a bet — earns him the admiration of his fellow prisoners, who nickname him "Cool Hand Luke." After repeated escape attempts and punishments, including being forced to dig and refill graves, Luke makes a final defiant stand and is shot, dying with a smile as he is driven away.

Legacy

The film is famous for the line spoken by the Captain (Strother Martin): "What we've got here is failure to communicate," which the American Film Institute ranked among the greatest movie quotations. In 2005 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." It is regarded as a classic anti-authoritarian film of the 1960s.
