Clark Atlanta University (CAU or Clark Atlanta) is a private, Methodist, historically black research university in Atlanta, Georgia, United States. It was formed in 1988 by the consolidation of Atlanta University (founded 1865) and Clark College (founded 1867). It is the largest member institution of the Atlanta University Center, the consortium of historically black colleges and universities in Atlanta.

History

Atlanta University was founded on September 19, 1865, by two former slaves, James Tate and Grandison Daniels, with support from the American Missionary Association and the Freedmen's Bureau. It was the nation's oldest graduate institution serving a predominantly African-American student body. Clark College, founded in 1867 by the Freedman's Aid Society of the Methodist Episcopal Church, was named for Bishop Davis Wasgatt Clark. In 1929 Atlanta University became exclusively a graduate institution in affiliation with Morehouse and Spelman colleges, forming the Atlanta University Center. W. E. B. Du Bois taught at Atlanta University and conducted his landmark Atlanta University Studies there.

The two institutions consolidated on July 1, 1988, forming Clark Atlanta University, unifying undergraduate and graduate education on Clark College's campus.

Academics

CAU offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees through schools of Arts and Sciences, Business Administration, Education, Social Work, and graduate studies. It is classified among "R2: Doctoral Universities — High research activity." The university is known for its Whitney M. Young Jr. School of Social Work and for the Center for Cancer Research and Therapeutic Development.

Campus and student life

The 126-acre campus lies southwest of downtown Atlanta, adjacent to the other Atlanta University Center schools. The Panthers compete in NCAA Division II athletics in the Southern Intercollegiate Athletic Conference. Notable alumni include civil rights leader Ralph Abernathy, political scientist Jewel Limar Prestage, and filmmaker and actor Kenya Barris (honorary associations aside, the university counts numerous educators, judges, and business leaders among its graduates).
