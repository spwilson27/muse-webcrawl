Zande language

Zande
Native to: Democratic Republic of the Congo, Central African Republic, South Sudan
Ethnicity: Azande
Native speakers: about 1.1 million
Family: Niger-Congo > Ubangian > Zande languages

Zande is an Ubangian language spoken by the Azande people of north-central Africa, principally in the northeast of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the southeast of the Central African Republic and western South Sudan. It is the most widely spoken of the Zande languages, with roughly a million native speakers, plus many second-language users for whom it serves as a regional lingua franca.

Classification

Zande belongs to the Zande subgroup of the Ubangian languages. Related languages include Barambo, Pambia and Nzakara. Its exact position within Niger-Congo remains debated, as Ubangian is sometimes treated as a primary branch.

Phonology and grammar

The language has a rich system of nasalised vowels and implosive consonants, and it is tonal, with tone distinguishing lexical and grammatical meanings. Word order is generally subject-verb-object. Zande is written in the Latin alphabet; a standard orthography was developed in the twentieth century and used in Bible translations and literacy materials.

Usage

Zande has been used in local administration, primary education and radio broadcasting in the Azande homeland. Publications include dictionaries and grammars prepared by missionaries and linguists, among them works associated with the early twentieth-century anthropological studies of the Azande by E. E. Evans-Pritchard.
