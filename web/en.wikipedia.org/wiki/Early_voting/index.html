Early voting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Early voting is a process by which voters in a public election can cast their ballots before the scheduled election day. It may take the form of in-person voting at designated early-voting centres over days or weeks, or be grouped with postal/absentee voting under labels such as "advance voting" (Canada) and "pre-poll voting" (Australia, where it has grown to a large share of the total vote).

Effects and debates

Early voting spreads administrative load, shortens election-day queues, and accommodates work and travel; research on turnout effects is mixed — convenience alone does not reliably raise overall turnout and can slightly reduce it by dissipating election-day mobilisation and media focus, unless combined with same-day registration. Critics note that early voters cast ballots before late campaign developments; administrators value the resilience it builds against weather, machine failures and disinformation on the day itself. The trend across democracies is towards more voting before election day, reshaping campaign strategy into a weeks-long turnout operation.
