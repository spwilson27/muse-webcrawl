Ultra-low-sulfur diesel

Ultra-low-sulfur diesel (ULSD) is diesel fuel with substantially lowered sulfur content. Since 2006, almost all of the petroleum-based diesel fuel available in North America and Europe has been of the ULSD type. In the United States, the Environmental Protection Agency required on-highway diesel to contain no more than 15 parts per million (ppm) sulfur, phased in from 2006; the European Union standard (EN 590) is 10 ppm.

Sulfur limit (US): 15 ppm
Sulfur limit (EU): 10 ppm
Predecessor: Low-sulfur diesel (500 ppm)
Purpose: Enable advanced exhaust aftertreatment

Why sulfur was reduced

Sulfur in diesel produces sulfur oxides and sulfate particulate matter when burned, contributing to acid rain and respiratory illness. More importantly, sulfur poisons advanced emission-control devices. Only with ULSD could manufacturers deploy diesel particulate filters (DPFs), NOx adsorbers and selective catalytic reduction (SCR) systems capable of meeting stringent Tier 2/ULEV and Euro 5/6 standards. The US EPA estimated the highway rule would prevent thousands of premature deaths annually.

Production and properties

ULSD is produced by severe hydrotreating (hydrodesulfurization) at the refinery. Removing sulfur also removes some naturally occurring lubricity compounds and slightly lowers energy content, so refiners add lubricity improvers and the fuel is blended to meet standards such as ASTM D975. ULSD has been progressively extended from highway vehicles to off-road, locomotive and marine applications in the US, and is compatible with biodiesel blends.
