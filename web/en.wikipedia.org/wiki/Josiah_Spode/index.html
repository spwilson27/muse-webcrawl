Josiah Spode


Josiah Spode
Born: 23 March 1733, Stoke-upon-Trent, Staffordshire, England
Died: 1797, Stoke-upon-Trent
Occupation: Potter
Known for: Founding Spode pottery; underglaze blue transfer printing; bone china standard

Josiah Spode (23 March 1733 – 1797) was an English potter and the founder of the Spode pottery works in Stoke-upon-Trent, Staffordshire.

Career

Orphaned young and raised in poverty, Spode was apprenticed in the Staffordshire potteries and worked for masters including Ralph Baddeley and Thomas Whieldon before setting up on his own in Stoke in the 1760s–1770s. He perfected underglaze blue transfer printing on earthenware, which allowed high-quality decorated tableware to be produced at scale, and his factory became one of the largest in the Potteries.

Legacy

His son Josiah Spode II developed the standard recipe for bone china — a mixture of china clay, china stone and bone ash — which became the benchmark for English porcelain. The Spode name, later part of the Portmeirion Group, remained associated with fine ceramics such as the Blue Italian pattern.
