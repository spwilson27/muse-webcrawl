The Ministry of Justice (MoJ) is a ministerial department of the Government of the United Kingdom, responsible for the judiciary, courts, prisons, and probation in England and Wales, as well as elements of constitutional and justice policy. It is headed by the Secretary of State for Justice, who also holds the historic office of Lord Chancellor.

History

The ministry was created on 9 May 2007, when the prison and probation functions of the Home Office were merged with the Department for Constitutional Affairs. The reorganization was intended to rebalance responsibilities after the Home Office's workload grew and followed reforms to the office of Lord Chancellor under the Constitutional Reform Act 2005.

Responsibilities

  • Courts and tribunals administration through HM Courts & Tribunals Service
  • Prisons and probation through HM Prison and Probation Service
  • Legal aid through the Legal Aid Agency
  • Sentencing and criminal justice policy
  • The judiciary's independence and judicial appointments framework
  • Human rights and constitutional reform policy
  • The Office of the Public Guardian and the Criminal Injuries Compensation Authority

Devolution

Justice is devolved in Scotland and Northern Ireland, which have their own justice departments and court systems; the MoJ's remit is principally England and Wales, though it retains some UK-wide responsibilities.
