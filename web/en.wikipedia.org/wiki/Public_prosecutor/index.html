Public prosecutor

A public prosecutor is an official of a state or public authority charged with conducting criminal proceedings on behalf of the public. The institution of public prosecution, now near-universal, historically replaced systems of private prosecution, in which victims or their families brought charges themselves. Titles and structures vary widely: district attorneys and state's attorneys in the United States, the Crown Prosecution Service in England and Wales, the ministère public and procureurs in France, and public prosecutors' offices (Staatsanwaltschaft) in Germany.

Functions

Prosecutors typically review police investigations, decide whether to charge suspects and with what offences, present the case at trial, and may conduct or direct further investigation. In many civil-law systems prosecutors are legally obliged to investigate both incriminating and exculpatory circumstances and to act impartially as "guardians of the law"; in adversarial systems they are party advocates but remain bound by duties of fairness and disclosure of exculpatory evidence.

Discretion

The extent of prosecutorial discretion differs by legal tradition. In systems based on the opportunity principle (such as England and Wales, and much of the United States), prosecutors may decline or discontinue cases that are not in the public interest; under the legality principle (as in Germany), they are in principle required to prosecute where sufficient evidence exists, subject to statutory exceptions for minor offences. Charging decisions, plea bargaining and sentencing recommendations give prosecutors substantial influence over outcomes, prompting continuing debate about accountability, consistency and mass incarceration.

Organisation and independence

Prosecution services may be organised as part of the executive (answerable to a minister of justice or attorney general), as an independent authority, or, in much of the United States, as locally elected offices. The tension between democratic accountability and independence from political direction in individual cases is a recurring theme, and many countries have created specialised prosecutors for corruption, organised crime and financial offences. At the international level, the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court conducts prosecutions of genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes.
