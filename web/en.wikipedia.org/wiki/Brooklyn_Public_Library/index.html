The Brooklyn Public Library is the public library system of the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. It is the sixteenth largest public library system in the United States and, with 61 branches, serves the borough's roughly 2.7 million residents. It is independent of the New York Public Library system, which serves Manhattan, the Bronx, and Staten Island.

History

The library was authorized by an act of the New York State legislature in 1892 and formally founded in 1896, consolidating earlier subscription and municipal libraries in Brooklyn. Its Central Library at Grand Army Plaza, an Art Deco building shaped like an open book, was begun in 1912 but not completed and opened until 1941 because of funding delays and the Great Depression.

Services

Besides lending books, media, and e-books, the system runs literacy programs, job-readiness services, the Brooklyn Collection local history archive, and the "Books Unbanned" initiative offering free e-book access to teenagers nationwide facing local book bans.
