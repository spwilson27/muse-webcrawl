North Yorkshire is a ceremonial county in Northern England. It is the largest county in England by land area, covering about 8,654 km². The county town is Northallerton; the largest settlement is the city of York. Other major towns include Harrogate, Scarborough, Middlesbrough (partially), Ripon, and Whitby.

Geography

Much of the county is rural and upland. Two of England's national parks lie largely within it: the Yorkshire Dales in the west, famous for limestone scenery and drystone walls, and the North York Moors in the east, a heather moorland plateau reaching the North Sea coast at Whitby and Scarborough. Between them runs the Vale of York. The county's highest point is Whernside (736 m), one of the Yorkshire Three Peaks.

History and administration

North Yorkshire was created in 1974 from the North Riding of Yorkshire together with parts of the East and West Ridings. Following local government reorganisation, a single unitary North Yorkshire Council took over on 1 April 2023, replacing the county council and seven district councils; the City of York remains a separate unitary authority within the ceremonial county. In 2024 a combined authority for York and North Yorkshire, with an elected mayor, began work.

Tourism

The county is one of England's most visited rural areas, drawing visitors to its national parks, heritage railways such as the North Yorkshire Moors Railway, ruined abbeys including Fountains and Rievaulx, and the city of York with its cathedral, York Minster.
