American Invitational Mathematics Examination

Administered by: Mathematical Association of America
First held: 1983
Format: 15 questions, 3 hours
Answers: Integers from 000 to 999

The American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME) is a selective mathematics competition in the United States, administered by the Mathematical Association of America as the second stage of the process that selects the American team for the International Mathematical Olympiad. Students qualify by scoring highly on the AMC 10 or AMC 12 competitions — roughly the top few percent of participants are invited.

Format

The AIME consists of 15 questions to be solved in three hours. Each answer is an integer between 000 and 999 inclusive, filled in on a grid; no calculators are permitted and there is no penalty for guessing, though the design of the answers makes random guessing almost never successful. Problems draw on pre-calculus mathematics — algebra, geometry, counting, probability and number theory — but at a level of ingenuity well beyond school examinations. Since 2020 the exam has been held in two versions, AIME I and AIME II.

Path to the Olympiad

A student's combined index of AMC and AIME scores determines qualification for the United States of America Mathematical Olympiad (USAMO) or the junior version (USAJMO), the proof-based rounds that follow. Strong AIME performance is also widely recognised in United States college admissions as evidence of exceptional mathematical ability.
