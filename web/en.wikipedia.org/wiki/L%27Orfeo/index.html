L'Orfeo

ComposerClaudio Monteverdi
LibrettistAlessandro Striggio
Premiere24 February 1607, Mantua

L'Orfeo, SV 318, is a late Renaissance/early Baroque opera by Claudio Monteverdi, with a libretto by Alessandro Striggio. It is the earliest opera that is still regularly performed and is based on the Greek legend of Orpheus and his descent to Hades to rescue Eurydice.

History

The opera was first performed during the Carnival of 1607 at the Ducal Palace in Mantua, commissioned by the Gonzaga court. Monteverdi published the score in 1609 — unusually detailed for its time, specifying a large orchestra of about 41 instruments whose timbres characterise scenes and characters.

Music

The work blends madrigal, monody, and instrumental ritornellos; its opening toccata and the lament "Tu se' morta" and Orfeo's aria "Possente spirto" are especially famous. Unlike earlier versions of the myth, the opera resolves with Apollo raising Orfeo to the heavens.
