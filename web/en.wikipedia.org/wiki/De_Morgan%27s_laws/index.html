De Morgan's laws

Named afterAugustus De Morgan
FieldLogic, set theory, Boolean algebra

De Morgan's laws are a pair of transformation rules in logic and Boolean algebra named after the nineteenth-century British mathematician Augustus De Morgan. They state that the negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the negations, and the negation of a disjunction is the conjunction of the negations: "not (A and B)" equals "(not A) or (not B)", and "not (A or B)" equals "(not A) and (not B)".

In set theory

Equivalent rules hold for unions and intersections of sets: the complement of a union is the intersection of the complements, and the complement of an intersection is the union of the complements.

History

Versions of the rules were known to medieval logicians, but De Morgan formalised them in the 1840s–1860s as part of his work on algebraic logic. They are fundamental in digital circuit design, set theory and computer programming, where they are used to simplify logical expressions.

Formal notation

In propositional logic the laws are written ¬(p ∧ q) ⟺ (¬p) ∨ (¬q) and ¬(p ∨ q) ⟺ (¬p) ∧ (¬q), where ¬ denotes negation, ∧ conjunction and ∨ disjunction.
