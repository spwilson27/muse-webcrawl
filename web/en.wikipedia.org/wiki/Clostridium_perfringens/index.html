Clostridium perfringens

KingdomBacteria
PhylumBacillota (Firmicutes)
GenusClostridium
Notable forFood poisoning, gas gangrene

Clostridium perfringens is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped, anaerobic, spore-forming bacterium found widely in soil and in the intestines of humans and animals. It is a common cause of food poisoning and of gas gangrene.

Food poisoning

Illness typically results from eating food — often meat and poultry dishes — in which spores survived cooking and germinated during slow cooling or reheating. The enterotoxin produced in the gut causes watery diarrhoea and abdominal cramps, usually resolving within about a day.

Gas gangrene

In contaminated deep wounds, C. perfringens can cause clostridial myonecrosis (gas gangrene); its alpha toxin, a phospholipase, destroys tissue, and gas produced by fermentation distends the tissues. The condition is a medical emergency requiring surgery and antibiotics.
