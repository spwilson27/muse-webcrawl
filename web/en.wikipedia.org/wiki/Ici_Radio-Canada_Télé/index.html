Ici Radio-Canada Télé (styled ICI Radio-Canada Télé, and often shortened to Télévision de Radio-Canada or SRC) is a Canadian French-language free-to-air television network owned by the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (known in French as the Société Radio-Canada). It is the French-language counterpart of CBC Television and serves francophone audiences across Canada, particularly in Quebec.

History

The network's flagship station, CBFT in Montreal, signed on September 6, 1952, as the first television station in Canada, broadcasting bilingually in its earliest months before English-language service moved to CBMT. The network grew through owned-and-operated stations and private affiliates, producing landmark Quebec television — including early téléromans (serial dramas) and programs such as La Famille Plouffe and Les Belles Histoires des pays d'en haut. Long-running institutions of the service include the newscast Le Téléjournal and the talk show Tout le monde en parle.

Branding

In 2013, as part of a corporate rebranding of CBC/Radio-Canada's French-language outlets under the "Ici" banner, the network was renamed Ici Radio-Canada Télé. The rebrand was controversial in Quebec, where some critics saw it as diminishing the historic Radio-Canada name; the corporation adjusted the rollout, but the "Ici" name was retained.

Programming and reach

The network broadcasts news, drama, comedy, sports (historically including La Soirée du hockey), and children's programming from production centres in Montreal and other cities, and is distributed nationwide by cable and satellite as the designated French-language public network — in some regions it is the only over-the-air French-language service.
