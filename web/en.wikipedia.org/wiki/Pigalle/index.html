Pigalle is an area in Paris, France, around the Place Pigalle, on the border between the 9th and 18th arrondissements. It is named after the sculptor Jean-Baptiste Pigalle (1714–1785). The district is famous for its nightlife, its historic cabarets, and its theatres and music venues.

History

In the late 19th century, Pigalle and neighbouring Montmartre became the centre of Paris's bohemian and artistic life, home to painters such as Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, who immortalised its cabarets. The Moulin Rouge, founded in 1889 at the foot of Montmartre on the Boulevard de Rochechouart, is the district's most famous landmark.

Nightlife and reputation

During the 20th century the area acquired a reputation as Paris's red-light district, with sex shops, cabarets and clubs concentrated along the Boulevard de Clichy and Boulevard de Rochechouart. In recent decades the southern part, sometimes nicknamed "SoPi" (South Pigalle), has gentrified, filling with fashionable cafés and boutiques, while the area around the main boulevards retains its nightlife character.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Moulin Rouge, the cabaret Le Divan du Monde, the La Cigale concert hall, and the Pigalle station of the Paris Métro. The district also hosts the Musée de l'érotisme and numerous guitar and music shops around the Rue Victor-Massé, associated with jazz guitarist Django Reinhardt and the composer Georges Ulmer, whose song "Pigalle" celebrated the quarter.
