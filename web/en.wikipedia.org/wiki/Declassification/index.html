Declassification

Declassification
DefinitionRemoval of classification restrictions from government information
US frameworkExecutive Order 13526; automatic declassification at 25 years
RelatedClassified information, freedom of information

Declassification is the process of removing the protections of classified information status, allowing previously secret material to be released to the public. It is the opposite of classification. Declassified documents may still be partially redacted, and declassification typically follows review under statutes, executive orders, or freedom-of-information legislation.

United States

In the United States, declassification is governed by a series of executive orders, most recently Executive Order 13526 (2009). It provides for automatic declassification of historically valuable records that are 25 years old, unless an agency obtains an exemption, and allows original classification authorities to declassify material earlier. Requests under the Freedom of Information Act and Mandatory Declassification Review allow outsiders to petition for release of specific records.

Notable declassifications

  • The Venona project decrypts of Soviet intelligence traffic, declassified beginning in 1995.
  • The existence of the National Reconnaissance Office, acknowledged in 1992, and of early CORONA spy-satellite photography, declassified in 1995.
  • British Ultra intelligence from Bletchley Park, revealed publicly in the 1970s.
  • Large releases of JFK assassination records under the 1992 JFK Records Review Board Act.

Challenges

Governments hold enormous backlogs of classified records; reviewers must page through millions of documents to catch material — nuclear weapons data, intelligence sources and methods — that must remain protected. "Born secret" categories such as US "Restricted Data" under the Atomic Energy Act are not subject to ordinary declassification at all.
