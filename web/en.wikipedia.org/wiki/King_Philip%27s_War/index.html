King Philip's War

Date1675–1676 (1678 in Maine)
LocationNew England, North America
Native leaderMetacomet ("King Philip"), Wampanoag sachem
ResultColonial victory; devastation of Native peoples of southern New England

King Philip's War (1675–1678), also called Metacom's War or the First Indian War, was an armed conflict between Native American inhabitants of New England and English colonists and their Native allies. It is named for Metacomet, the Wampanoag sachem known to the colonists as King Philip, and was proportionally one of the deadliest wars in American history.

Causes and course

Decades of colonial expansion, land pressure and legal humiliation of the Wampanoag — including the disputed death of the Christian Indian informer John Sassamon and Plymouth Colony's execution of three Wampanoag men — ignited war in June 1675. Fighting spread across Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island and Maine; towns such as Lancaster and Deerfield were destroyed, and the colonists retaliated with massacres of Native non-combatants, including at the Great Swamp Fight against the Narragansett.

Outcome

Metacomet was killed in August 1676, effectively ending the war in the south (fighting in Maine continued to 1678). Thousands of Native people were killed, enslaved or sold into exile, shattering Native power in southern New England, while the colonies suffered heavy casualties and debts that took decades to repay.
