Under Milk Wood

[Image: A Welsh fishing village at dawn — terraced houses above a harbour, gulls over the water, in the style of a radio-drama stage set.]

Under Milk Wood is a 1954 radio drama by the Welsh poet Dylan Thomas, later adapted for the stage, film and television. Subtitled "A Play for Voices", it follows a single spring day in the fictional Welsh fishing village of Llareggub, eavesdropping on the dreams, memories and secret lives of its inhabitants.

Synopsis

The play opens before dawn with two narrating Voices inviting the listener to share the sleeping town's dreams: blind Captain Cat dreams of his drowned shipmates and his lost love Rosie Probert; the Reverend Eli Jenkins composes his White Book of Llareggub; Mrs Ogmore-Pritchard dictates impossible standards of cleanliness to her two dead husbands. Through the day the Voices trace the village's routines — the postman Willy Nilly steaming open letters, Polly Garter singing as she scrubs, Mr Pugh plotting his wife's murder over breakfast — until night falls and the town dreams again.

History

Thomas worked on the piece, in various forms, for over a decade; it was first performed as a solo reading in New York in May 1953 and staged with a cast there days later. The definitive BBC radio production, with Richard Burton as First Voice, was broadcast in January 1954, two months after Thomas's death at the age of 39. It has been filmed twice and remains one of the most beloved works of Welsh literature.
