The Yongbyon complex, North Korea's principal nuclear research site.

The Yongbyon Nuclear Scientific Research Center (녕변원자력연구소) is North Korea's major nuclear facility, located in Nyŏngbyŏn County in North Pyongan Province, about 100 kilometres north of Pyongyang. Established with Soviet assistance in the 1960s, the complex has been the centre of the country's nuclear weapons programme, producing plutonium for its arsenal and serving as the focus of decades of international diplomacy over North Korea's nuclear activities.

Facilities

The site hosts an IRT-2000 research reactor supplied by the Soviet Union in 1965, fuel fabrication plants, and a gas-graphite moderated reactor rated at about 5 MWe, which began operating in 1986 and has been the principal source of weapons-usable plutonium. The associated Radiochemical Laboratory is a reprocessing plant used to extract plutonium from spent fuel. Construction of larger 50 MWe and 200 MWe reactors was halted in the 1990s. Since around 2010 North Korea has built an experimental light water reactor (ELWR) and a uranium enrichment plant at the site, the latter shown to visiting American scientist Siegfried Hecker in November 2010.

Diplomatic history

Under the 1994 Agreed Framework between the United States and North Korea, the graphite-moderated reactors were frozen in exchange for light water reactors and heavy fuel oil. After the agreement collapsed in 2002–2003, North Korea expelled IAEA inspectors and restarted the facilities. In 2007, under the Six-Party Talks, the complex was shut down, and in June 2008 its cooling tower was demolished before international media. Negotiations later broke down, and satellite imagery in subsequent years indicated renewed operations at both the reactor and the enrichment plant, including periods of reactor operation reported by the IAEA.
