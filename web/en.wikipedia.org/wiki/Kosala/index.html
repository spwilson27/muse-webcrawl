Kosala

Kosala was an ancient Indian kingdom in the region of present-day eastern Uttar Pradesh and southwestern Nepal, roughly corresponding to the historical region of Awadh. In the 6th century BCE it ranked among the sixteen great realms (Mahajanapadas) of northern India, and it is prominent in both the epic tradition and the earliest records of Buddhism and Jainism.

Geography and capitals

Kosala stretched from the Gomti River in the west to the Gandak in the east, and from the Himalayan foothills south to the Ganges. Its principal cities were Shravasti (the usual capital), Saketa or Ayodhya on the Sarayu River, and Kushavati. In the Ramayana tradition, Ayodhya is the capital of Kosala and the seat of King Dasharatha and his son Rama; the dynasty of Kosala was traced to the legendary King Ikshvaku of the Solar line.

History

Under King Prasenajit (Pasenadi), a contemporary of the Buddha, Kosala was one of the four great powers of the age, rivalling Magadha to the southeast. The Buddha spent many rainy-season retreats at the Jetavana monastery in Shravasti, and numerous discourses are set in Kosala. Relations with Magadha combined marriage alliances with wars; Prasenajit's sister Kosala Devi married Bimbisara of Magadha, and their son Ajatashatru later fought Kosala in turn.

Prasenajit was deposed by his son Vidudabha while away from the capital, and within a generation — by the late 5th or 4th century BCE — Kosala was annexed by an expanding Magadha, losing its separate identity as the Gangetic plain moved toward empire.
