Buryat (Buryat: Буряад хэлэн, Buryaad khelen) is a Mongolic language spoken by the Buryats, the largest Indigenous group of Siberia. It is spoken principally in the Republic of Buryatia and neighbouring regions of Russia around Lake Baikal, in northern Mongolia, and by a smaller community in Inner Mongolia, China.

Dialects

Buryat comprises numerous dialects, conventionally grouped into western (Ekhirit-Bulagat and Barguzin) and eastern (Khori and Aga) varieties, which differ in phonology, including the presence or absence of the phoneme /h/ corresponding to /s/ elsewhere. The literary standard in Russia is based on the Khori dialect. The Bargut dialect spoken in China is sometimes counted separately.

Writing systems

Buryat was historically written in the classical Mongolian vertical script. In the 1930s the Soviet Union introduced a Latin-based alphabet (itself preceded by the Buryat-specific Vagindra script of Agvan Dorzhiev), and in 1939 Cyrillic became official in Russia. In Mongolia, Buryat speakers write in Cyrillic in everyday life while the traditional script has been revived for cultural use. Language vitality is a concern, as Russian dominates education and media in Buryatia, though Buryat retains official status alongside Russian in the republic.
