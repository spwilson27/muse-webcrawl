Demurrage

Demurrage is a charge levied on the charterer or consignee for detaining a ship, container, or other transport equipment beyond the agreed free time (laytime) allowed for loading, unloading or collection. The term originated in maritime law and is now used across shipping, rail and container logistics.

Shipping

In charter parties, demurrage is liquidated damages paid by the charterer to the shipowner when a vessel exceeds the laytime specified for cargo operations; the daily rate is fixed in advance. The counterpart benefit for finishing early is despatch money, usually paid at half the demurrage rate. The maxim "once on demurrage, always on demurrage" means that laytime exceptions stop applying once the demurrage clock starts.

Container demurrage and detention

In container shipping, demurrage refers to charges for containers kept inside the terminal beyond free days, while detention applies to equipment held outside the port. During the supply-chain congestion of 2021–2022, soaring demurrage charges drew regulatory attention, including rules by the US Federal Maritime Commission requiring clearer invoicing.

Economic usage

The economist Silvio Gesell proposed "demurrage currency" — money that loses value over time, like demurrage on goods — to discourage hoarding; the idea influenced local stamp-scrip experiments such as the Wörgl experiment of 1932 and is discussed in some modern monetary and cryptocurrency designs.
