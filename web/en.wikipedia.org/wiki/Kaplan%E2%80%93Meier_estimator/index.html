Kaplan–Meier estimator

The Kaplan–Meier estimator, named after Edward L. Kaplan and Paul Meier (1958), is a non-parametric statistic used to estimate the survival function from lifetime data. It is widely used in medical research to measure the fraction of patients living for a certain amount of time after treatment.

Method

The estimator is a step function that decreases at each observed event time, multiplying the survival probability by the fraction of subjects at risk who survive that time. Censored observations — subjects lost to follow-up or still alive at the study's end — reduce the at-risk set without causing a step.

Applications

Kaplan–Meier curves are standard for analysing time-to-event outcomes such as death, relapse, or mechanical failure, and are commonly compared between groups using the log-rank test.
