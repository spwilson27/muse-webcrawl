Dae Heummu

Dae Heummu (Korean: 대흠무; Hanja: 大欽茂; died 793), posthumously known as King Mun of Balhae, was the third ruler of Balhae, a kingdom that controlled the northern Korean Peninsula and parts of Manchuria between the late 7th and early 10th centuries. He reigned from 737 until his death in 793, the longest reign in Balhae’s history, and is remembered for consolidating the state founded by his grandfather Dae Joyeong and for presiding over a period of territorial expansion and cultural flourishing.

Reign

Dae Heummu was the son of Dae Muye, the second king of Balhae (King Mu). On succeeding to the throne in 737, he continued his father’s programme of administrative reorganisation along Tang Chinese lines, completing a system of five capitals (the Supreme Capital Sanggyeong and four secondary capitals), fifteen prefectures, and sixty-two counties. He moved the seat of government several times, from Junggyeong to Sanggyeong (in the vicinity of modern Ning’an, Heilongjiang), which served as the principal capital for much of his reign.

Under Dae Heummu, Balhae extended its influence over the Amur River basin and maintained active diplomacy abroad. He dispatched numerous embassies to Tang China and to Japan, styling Balhae a prosperous, civilised kingdom — later Chinese sources praised it as the "Prosperous Country of the East". He used the era names Inan (仁安), Daheung (大興) and Boryeok (寶曆), extending the Daheung era for several decades.

Legacy and succession

Dae Heummu died in 793 and was given the temple name Munwang (King Mun). Because his designated heir had predeceased him, the throne passed briefly to his younger brother Dae Wonui and then to other descendants of Dae Joyeong, before eventually reaching his grandson Dae In-su (King Seon), under whom Balhae reached its greatest extent.
