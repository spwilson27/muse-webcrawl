Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service

The Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) is the public prosecution service for Scotland. It is responsible for the prosecution of crime in Scotland, the investigation of sudden or suspicious deaths, and the investigation of complaints against the police in criminal matters. All prosecutions on indictment in Scotland are brought in the name of the Lord Advocate.

Structure

The service is headed by the Lord Advocate, supported by the Solicitor General for Scotland. The Crown Office, based in Edinburgh, is the headquarters of the department, while procurators fiscal operate through local offices across Scotland, organised into sheriffdoms. Procurators fiscal receive reports from Police Scotland and other reporting agencies and decide whether to prosecute, offer a fiscal fine, or take no proceedings.

Functions

Procurators fiscal present cases in the sheriff courts and JP courts, while solemn cases in the High Court of Justiciary and the sheriff court are presented by advocates depute appointed by the Lord Advocate. The service also investigates deaths, supporting fatal accident inquiries, and handles confiscation proceedings under proceeds of crime legislation. See also Lord Advocate and Scots law.
