The London Borough of Tower Hamlets is an Inner London borough in East London, immediately east of the City of London and north of the River Thames. Created in 1965 from the metropolitan boroughs of Bethnal Green, Poplar, and Stepney, it takes its name from the historic Tower Hamlets, the area around the Tower of London that owed military service to the Tower. The borough encompasses much of the traditional East End as well as the redeveloped Docklands.

Districts and landmarks

Tower Hamlets includes Whitechapel, Bethnal Green, Bow, Stepney, Mile End, Spitalfields, and Brick Lane—famous for its curry houses and markets—and the Isle of Dogs, where the Canary Wharf financial district, anchored by One Canada Square, forms one of Europe's largest banking centres. The Tower of London and Tower Bridge stand on its western edge; other landmarks include the Museum of London Docklands, Victoria Park, Columbia Road flower market, and the East London Mosque. The borough has among the fastest-growing and densest populations in England, exceeding 320,000.

The borough's history is one of successive waves of immigration—Huguenot weavers, Irish labourers, Jewish refugees, and, most recently and numerously, Bangladeshis, who form the largest single ethnic group (around a third of residents, the highest proportion in the UK). Tower Hamlets juxtaposes the extreme wealth of Canary Wharf with some of England's highest rates of child poverty and overcrowded housing, and it is governed by an executive mayor and Tower Hamlets Council.
