Legcuffs

Legcuffs, also called leg irons or ankle shackles, are physical restraints used on the ankles of a person to allow walking but prevent running and kicking. They consist of two cuffs, larger versions of handcuffs, connected by a chain, and are used by police and corrections authorities when transporting prisoners and in other custodial settings.

Design and use

Modern legcuffs are made of steel with a ratcheting lock opened by a standard handcuff key. The connecting chain is long enough to permit a near-normal stride while preventing running. They are commonly combined with handcuffs and a waist chain during prisoner transport. Oversized cuffs are manufactured for use on the ankles, since standard handcuffs are too small for the purpose.

Safety and criticism

Improperly applied cuffs can cause nerve damage, swelling or abrasions, and restraint of people in medical or mental-health crises has drawn criticism. Policies in many agencies require double-locking the cuffs to prevent them tightening further during movement.
