Monoski

A monoski is a single wide ski on which the skier's two feet are mounted side by side, both facing forward, using standard ski bindings and ski boots; the skier uses ski poles as in normal skiing. Monoskiing differs from snowboarding, in which the feet are angled relative to the direction of travel and soft boots are normally used, and from skwal, a narrower board with feet placed one in front of the other.

Monoskiing became popular in the late 1960s and 1970s, particularly after designs by surfer Jack Marchand and later Bob McGovern, and enjoyed a following in the 1980s before being largely eclipsed by snowboarding. The technique is well suited to carving: with both legs working together, the monoski can be edged aggressively, and it floats well in powder snow. Because both legs are fixed to one board, the sport can be easier on skiers with certain knee problems, though falls differ from those on two skis. Small manufacturers and a devoted enthusiast community have kept the sport alive, and monoski designs have evolved with modern ski construction.
