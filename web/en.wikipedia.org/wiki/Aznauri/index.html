Aznauri

Aznauri (Georgian: აზნაური; pl. aznaurni) were a class of Georgian nobility, roughly comparable to the lower and middle ranks of feudal nobility elsewhere in Europe. The term derives from the Middle Persian āznāvar.

History

The aznauri formed the warrior-retainer stratum of medieval Georgia beneath the great princes (mtavari) and high nobles (didebuli). They held land in return for military service, staffing royal and princely courts and armies from the early medieval period through the Kingdom of Georgia's height in the 12th–13th centuries.

Later status

After the fragmentation of the Georgian kingdom and later under Persian, Ottoman and finally Russian rule, aznauri status persisted as a hereditary gentry rank. Following Georgia's annexation by the Russian Empire in the 19th century, many aznauri families were recognised as dvoryanstvo (Russian nobility), while others lost status amid disputes over documentation. The class was abolished after the Russian Revolution and Georgia's sovietisation in 1921.
