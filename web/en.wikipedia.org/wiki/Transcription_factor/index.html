Transcription factor

Transcription factor
Function: Regulates transcription of genes
Domains: DNA-binding, transactivation
Examples: p53, NF-κB, steroid receptors, homeobox proteins

In molecular biology, a transcription factor (TF) is a protein that controls the rate of transcription of genetic information from DNA to messenger RNA, by binding to a specific DNA sequence — usually an enhancer or promoter near the genes it regulates.

Mechanism

Transcription factors recruit or block RNA polymerase and chromatin-modifying enzymes, turning genes on or off in particular cells, times and conditions. They work combinatorially: a modest set — about 1,600 in humans — generates vast regulatory complexity. General transcription factors such as TFIID assemble the pre-initiation complex at most promoters.

Classes and significance

Families are classified by DNA-binding domains: helix-turn-helix, zinc finger, leucine zipper, helix-loop-helix and homeodomain proteins. Mutations cause developmental disorders and cancers — p53, the most famous, is mutated in roughly half of human tumours. Master regulators such as MyoD or the Yamanaka factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc) can reprogramme cell identity. See Gene expression and Enhancer.
