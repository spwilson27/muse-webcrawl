Manhunter is a 1986 American crime thriller film written and directed by Michael Mann. Based on the 1981 novel Red Dragon by Thomas Harris, it stars William Petersen as FBI profiler Will Graham, who comes out of retirement to hunt a serial killer known as the "Tooth Fairy" (Tom Noonan), enlisting the aid of imprisoned murderer Dr. Hannibal Lecktor, played by Brian Cox. The film was the first screen adaptation of Harris's Hannibal Lecter novels.

Plot

Former FBI profiler Will Graham, who retired after being nearly killed capturing Hannibal Lecktor, is persuaded by his old boss Jack Crawford to help investigate a killer who murders entire families during full moons. Graham reconstructs the killer's thinking from crime scenes and consults Lecktor in his cell, who covertly passes Graham's address to the killer. The murderer, Francis Dollarhyde — a film-processing technician who believes he is transforming into the "Great Red Dragon" of William Blake's painting — falls in love with a blind co-worker, Reba McClane, and struggles against his compulsion. Lecktor's taunting messages, a tabloid reporter is burned alive, and Graham ultimately deduces Dollarhyde's identity from home movies processed at his lab; in the climax Graham rescues his family and kills Dollarhyde at his home.

Production and style

Mann shot the film with cinematographer Dante Spinotti in a highly stylised visual scheme, using tinted colour — cool blues for Graham's world, disturbing greens and magentas for Dollarhyde's — to externalise the characters' psychology. The score draws on contemporary music by The Reds, Michel Rubini, Shriekback, Iron Butterfly ("In-A-Gadda-Da-Vida", used in the finale), and The Prime Movers. The title was changed from Red Dragon at the insistence of producer Dino De Laurentiis, partly because another film, Year of the Dragon, had recently flopped, and partly to ride the success of Mann's television series Miami Vice.

Reception and legacy

Manhunter underperformed at the box office on its initial release but has since been widely re-evaluated as one of the finest crime films of its era and the most psychologically acute of the Lecter adaptations, praised for Petersen's intense performance and Mann's formalist direction. The novel was filmed again as Red Dragon (2002), after the success of The Silence of the Lambs (1991), and the Graham–Dollarhyde story was later adapted in the television series Hannibal (2013–2015).
