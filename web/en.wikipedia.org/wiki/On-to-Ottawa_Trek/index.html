On-to-Ottawa Trek

The On-to-Ottawa Trek was a mass protest movement in Canada in 1935, during the Great Depression. More than a thousand unemployed men, many living in federal relief camps in British Columbia, rode freight trains eastward intending to bring their grievances directly to Prime Minister R. B. Bennett in Ottawa.

Background

Unemployed single men were housed in remote relief camps run by the Department of National Defence, where they worked for twenty cents a day. Conditions and low pay bred resentment, organized in part by the Workers' Unity League. In April 1935, camp workers in British Columbia went on strike and converged on Vancouver before deciding to take their protest to the capital.

The trek and the Regina Riot

Beginning 3 June 1935, strikers climbed aboard freight trains heading east, gathering support along the way. The federal government ordered the trek stopped at Regina, Saskatchewan. After negotiations between a strikers' delegation and the Prime Minister in Ottawa collapsed — Bennett denounced the leaders as radicals — the government moved to arrest the trek's leaders on 1 July 1935.

The attempted arrests in Regina's Market Square erupted into the Regina Riot: a city police detective was killed, dozens were injured, and many strikers were arrested. The trek was broken up and the men dispersed, most returning west. The episode badly damaged Bennett's government, which lost the 1935 federal election; the relief camp system was soon abolished.
