Pablo Escobar


Born: 1 December 1949, Rionegro, Colombia. Died: 2 December 1993 (aged 44), Medellín. Known for: leading the Medellín Cartel.

Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria (1949–1993) was a Colombian drug lord and founder of the Medellín Cartel. At the height of his power in the 1980s, his organization supplied an estimated majority of the cocaine smuggled into the United States, making him one of the wealthiest criminals in history — Forbes listed him among the world's billionaires for seven consecutive years.

Rise to power

Born in Rionegro and raised in Medellín, Escobar began with petty crime — stealing cars and gravestones — before entering the cocaine trade in the mid-1970s. He built a vertically integrated empire of laboratories, aircraft, and smuggling routes, protected by bribery and terror under the principle of plata o plomo ("silver or lead"). His wealth funded his enormous Hacienda Nápoles estate, complete with a private zoo, and philanthropic projects in Medellín's poor neighbourhoods that earned him a Robin Hood image among some residents.

War with the state

Briefly elected to Colombia's Congress in 1982, Escobar was exposed and expelled, then waged open war against the state to block extradition to the United States, ordering assassinations of officials and judges and bombings including the 1989 destruction of Avianca Flight 203 and the DAS building in Bogotá. In 1991 he surrendered under a no-extradition deal and was housed in his self-built prison, La Catedral, from which he escaped in 1992.

Death and legacy

After a 16-month manhunt by the Colombian Search Bloc, aided by U.S. agencies and shadowed by the vigilante group Los Pepes, Escobar was located in Medellín through radio triangulation and shot dead on a rooftop on 2 December 1993, the day after his 44th birthday. His life has been retold in books, films, and the Netflix series Narcos, and his legacy remains contested in Colombia.
