Rainwater harvesting

Roof runoff stored in a tank for later use.

Rainwater harvesting is the collection and storage of rainwater, rather than allowing it to run off, for reuse on site. Water is typically gathered from roofs or prepared ground catchments into tanks, cisterns, or recharge structures, and used for irrigation, livestock, flushing, washing, and — after treatment — drinking.

History

Rainwater harvesting is ancient: cisterns were used in the Negev and around the Mediterranean in antiquity, and traditional systems such as India's stepwells, johads, and tankas, Bermuda's roof catchments, and the check dams revived by community movements in Rajasthan remain in use. Runoff farming concentrated water on crops in arid regions.

Systems and benefits

A typical rooftop system comprises a catchment surface, gutters, a first-flush diverter to discard contaminated initial runoff, filtration, a storage tank, and a pump or gravity feed. Harvesting reduces demand on mains water and aquifers, lessens stormwater flooding and erosion, and provides a decentralised water supply — important in water-scarce regions and islands. Some jurisdictions, such as Bermuda and parts of India and Australia, mandate rainwater collection in new buildings.

Considerations

Water quality depends on catchment cleanliness, storage design, and treatment; stored water must be protected from mosquito breeding and contamination. See also cistern and water conservation.
