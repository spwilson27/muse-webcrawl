Omaha hold 'em, often simply called Omaha, is a community card poker game similar to Texas hold 'em. Each player is dealt four private cards ("hole cards") instead of two, and must make the best five-card hand using exactly two of their hole cards together with exactly three of the five community cards. This rule is the defining difference from Texas hold 'em and the source of most beginner mistakes in the game.

History

The game is generally traced to casino executive Robert Turner, who is credited with introducing a four-card version of hold 'em in the early 1980s; poker player and promoter Bill Boyd spread it at the Golden Nugget in Las Vegas under the name "Nugget Hold'em" before the name Omaha took hold. The game grew steadily and exploded in popularity in Europe and online, where pot-limit Omaha became the second most popular poker variant after no-limit Texas hold 'em.

Rules

Betting structure mirrors Texas hold 'em: two blinds, four betting rounds (pre-flop, flop, turn, and river), and five community cards dealt in the centre. Because players hold four cards, starting-hand combinations multiply dramatically, hands run closer in value, and big made hands and strong draws are far more common — the nuts (best possible hand) plays a much larger role in Omaha strategy than in hold 'em.

Variants

  • Pot-limit Omaha (PLO) — the most popular form, with bets limited to the size of the pot.
  • Omaha hi-lo (Omaha eight-or-better) — the pot is split between the best high hand and the best qualifying low hand.
  • Five-card Omaha ("Big O") — players receive five hole cards, still using exactly two.
  • Courchevel — five hole cards with the first flop card exposed before the first betting round.

Omaha events are a fixture of the World Series of Poker, and the $10,000 Pot-Limit Omaha Championship is regarded as one of the most prestigious bracelets outside the Main Event.
