Joe Haldeman

Born: Joe William Haldeman, 9 June 1943, Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, U.S.

Occupation: Novelist

Genre: Science fiction

Notable works: The Forever War

Joe William Haldeman (born June 9, 1943) is an American science fiction author. He is best known for his novel The Forever War (1974), which won the Hugo, Nebula and Locus Awards and drew on his experience as a combat engineer in the Vietnam War, where he was wounded and received the Purple Heart.

Career

Haldeman's fiction frequently explores war, time dilation and the alienation of returning soldiers. The relativistic time-dilation at the heart of The Forever War — in which soldiers return to an Earth centuries older than they left it — is widely read as a metaphor for veterans' estrangement. Sequels include Forever Peace (1997), which also won the Hugo and Nebula Awards, and Forever Free (1999). His other novels include the World of Tiers tie-ins early in his career, Mindbridge, The Hemingway Hoax, and the Marsbound trilogy.

He taught writing for many years at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and was inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame in 2012. In 2010 he was named a SFWA Grand Master.
