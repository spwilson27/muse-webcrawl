Lucca is a city in Tuscany, central Italy, on the Serchio river, famous for its intact Renaissance-era city walls encircling the historic centre. It is the capital of the province of Lucca and the birthplace of the composer Giacomo Puccini.

History

Founded by the Etruscans and made a Roman colony in 180 BC, Lucca preserves its Roman grid plan and a piazza built into the remains of its Roman amphitheatre. In the Middle Ages it was an important stop on the Via Francigena pilgrimage route and grew rich on the silk trade, remaining an independent republic for centuries under leaders such as Castruccio Castracani. Dante spent part of his exile years in the city and mentions it in the Divine Comedy.

Napoleon made Lucca a principality for his sister Elisa Bonaparte Baciocchi in 1805; after the Congress of Vienna it became a duchy, then passed to Tuscany and unified Italy.

Sights

The massive tree-topped Renaissance walls, built in the 16th and 17th centuries, now form a pedestrian and cycling promenade around the old town. Landmarks include the cathedral of San Martino with the revered Volto Santo image, the church of San Michele in Foro, the Guinigi Tower crowned with oak trees, and Puccini's birthplace museum. Lucca hosts the summer Lucca Summer Festival and the Lucca Comics & Games convention.
