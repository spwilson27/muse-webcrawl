Metal Gear

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Metal Gear (Japanese: メタルギア, Metaru Gia) is a Japanese stealth video game franchise created by Hideo Kojima and developed and published by Konami. The first game, Metal Gear, was released in 1987 for the MSX2 home computer. The franchise is widely credited with pioneering the stealth game genre, asking players to infiltrate enemy installations while avoiding visual contact with patrols rather than fighting them head-on.

The series is named for its recurring superweapon: "Metal Gear", a bipedal walking tank capable of launching nuclear weapons from anywhere in the world, which the protagonist is typically sent to destroy.

Main series

The narrative arc follows soldiers codenamed Snake — chiefly Solid Snake and his genetic father Big Boss (Naked Snake) — across the Cold War and its aftermath. Principal entries include Metal Gear (1987), Metal Gear 2: Solid Snake (1990), the breakthrough 3D title Metal Gear Solid (1998, PlayStation), Metal Gear Solid 2: Sons of Liberty (2001), Metal Gear Solid 3: Snake Eater (2004), Metal Gear Solid 4: Guns of the Patriots (2008), Metal Gear Solid: Peace Walker (2010), and the open-world Metal Gear Solid V: Ground Zeroes (2014) and The Phantom Pain (2015). Spin-offs include the card-based Metal Gear Acid games, the hack-and-slash Metal Gear Rising: Revengeance (2013, developed with PlatinumGames), and the poorly received Metal Gear Survive (2018), the first entry made after Kojima's 2015 departure from Konami.

Themes and reception

The games are noted for their intricate, cinematic storytelling and for mature themes: nuclear deterrence and proliferation, the ethics of genetic engineering and cloning (the "Les Enfants Terribles" project), information control and censorship in the digital age, memes and cultural inheritance, the military-industrial economy of perpetual proxy war, and the moral cost of loyalty to country and unit. Long cutscenes, codec radio conversations, and fourth-wall-breaking devices (such as the Psycho Mantis boss fight reading the player's memory card) became series signatures. The core games have been critically acclaimed and the series has sold more than 60 million copies worldwide. Remakes and collections, including Metal Gear Solid Delta: Snake Eater, have revived the series under Konami.
