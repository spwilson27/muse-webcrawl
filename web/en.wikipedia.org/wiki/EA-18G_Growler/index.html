Boeing EA-18G Growler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Role: Electronic-warfare aircraft

Manufacturer: Boeing

First flight: 15 August 2006

Introduced: 22 September 2009

Primary users: United States Navy; Royal Australian Air Force

Developed from: F/A-18F Super Hornet

The Boeing EA-18G Growler is an American carrier-based electronic warfare aircraft, a specialised version of the two-seat F/A-18F Super Hornet. It replaced the Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowlers in service with the United States Navy, which began operating the type in 2009.

Design and capabilities

The Growler retains the Super Hornet's airframe, flight performance and most avionics, but replaces the internal cannon with electronics bays and carries AN/ALQ-99 (and later Next Generation Jammer) pods to jam enemy radar and communications. Wingtip pods house the AN/ALQ-218 wideband receivers. The crew of two — a pilot and an electronic countermeasures officer — can conduct escort and standoff jamming, suppression of enemy air defences, and can also employ AGM-88 HARM anti-radiation missiles and AIM-120 AMRAAM air-to-air missiles.

Operational history

The Growler's first combat deployment came during Operation Odyssey Dawn over Libya in 2011, and the type has since supported operations in Iraq, Syria and at sea worldwide. The Royal Australian Air Force acquired eleven Growlers, one of which was lost to an engine fire in 2018 and subsequently replaced. Germany selected a Growler variant in 2020 before later reversing towards a Eurofighter-based electronic warfare solution. The U.S. Navy plans upgrades centred on the Next Generation Jammer to keep the aircraft effective into the 2040s.
