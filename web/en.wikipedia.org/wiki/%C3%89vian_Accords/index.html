Évian Accords

Signed18 March 1962
LocationÉvian-les-Bains, France
PartiesFrance; Provisional Government of the Algerian Republic (FLN)

The Évian Accords are a set of peace treaties signed on 18 March 1962 in Évian-les-Bains, France, by France and the Provisional Government of the Algerian Republic, the government-in-exile of the FLN (Front de Libération Nationale). The accords ended the Algerian War (1954–1962) and led to the independence of Algeria following a referendum.

Background

The Algerian War began in November 1954 as an armed revolt by the FLN against French colonial rule. Algeria had been treated not merely as a colony but as an integral part of France, home to around a million European settlers (the pieds-noirs). The war grew increasingly brutal, involving torture, mass displacement, and the fall of the French Fourth Republic in 1958, which brought Charles de Gaulle back to power.

Negotiations

De Gaulle gradually moved toward accepting Algerian self-determination, announcing in 1959 that Algerians had the right to choose their future. Exploratory contacts with the FLN gave way to formal negotiations at Évian in May 1961, which broke down, and a resumed round at Évian in March 1962 that produced the final agreements.

Terms

The accords provided for an immediate ceasefire, a referendum on independence in Algeria, and the release of political prisoners. France retained use of the Mers El Kébir naval base for fifteen years and access to Saharan nuclear test sites and hydrocarbon resources for a period. Guarantees were offered for the rights and property of European settlers, though they proved largely ineffective in practice.

Aftermath

A ceasefire took effect on 19 March 1962. In the referendum of 1 July 1962, Algerians voted overwhelmingly for independence, which France recognised on 3 July; Algeria celebrates independence on 5 July. The end of the war triggered the mass exodus of nearly the entire pied-noir population and violence against harkis — Algerians who had served with the French — thousands of whom were killed.
