Beinn Eighe

Elevation1,010 m (3,314 ft) — Ruadh-stac Mòr
LocationWester Ross, Highland, Scotland
ListingMunro

Beinn Eighe is a mountain massif in Wester Ross in the Northwest Highlands of Scotland, near the villages of Kinlochewe and Torridon. It is a complex ridge with several summits, the highest of which, Ruadh-stac Mòr, reaches 1,010 metres (3,314 ft) and is a Munro.

Geology and features

The mountain is built of Torridonian sandstone capped with white Cambrian quartzite, which gives its upper slopes a pale, scree-covered appearance. Its northern corries include Coire Mhic Fhearchair, whose Triple Buttress is a famous climbing venue and is often regarded as one of the finest corries in Scotland.

Conservation

In 1951 Beinn Eighe became Britain's first National Nature Reserve, established to protect the remnant Caledonian pinewood of Coire na Glas Leathaid and the mountain's arctic-alpine flora. The reserve, now managed by NatureScot, covers about 4,800 hectares and is part of a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. A crashed Avro Lancaster bomber from 1951 remains scattered in the corrie below the Triple Buttress, its crew commemorated locally.
