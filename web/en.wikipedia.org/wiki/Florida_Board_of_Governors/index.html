Florida Board of Governors

The Florida Board of Governors is the governing body of the State University System of Florida, the system of twelve public universities in the U.S. state of Florida. Created by a constitutional amendment approved by Florida voters in 2002 and seated in 2003, the Board establishes system-wide policy, approves new degree programs and university budgets, and oversees the universities' individual boards of trustees.

Composition and powers

The Board has seventeen members: fourteen appointed by the Governor of Florida and confirmed by the Florida Senate, plus the Commissioner of Education, the chair of the Advisory Council of Faculty Senates, and the president of the Florida Student Association. The Board's authority includes defining the mission of each university, approving new academic programs, setting policies on tuition jointly with the legislature, and conducting performance-based funding evaluations. A Chancellor, appointed by the Board, serves as the system's chief executive.

History

The Board replaced the Florida Board of Regents, which had governed the university system until its abolition in 2001 as part of Governor Jeb Bush's education reorganization; a 2002 constitutional amendment championed by former U.S. Senator Bob Graham restored a statewide governing board while retaining local boards of trustees created in the interim.
