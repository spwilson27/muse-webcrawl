Giulio Douhet (30 May 1869 – 15 February 1930) was an Italian general and air power theorist. He was a key proponent and the father of strategic bombing in aerial warfare. His 1921 treatise Il dominio dell'aria (The Command of the Air) is among the most influential works on air power ever written.

Career

Commissioned into the artillery, Douhet early recognised the military potential of aircraft and dirigibles. He commanded Italy's first aviation battalion and, during the First World War, was an outspoken critic of Italian military leadership; his unauthorised memorandum attacking the conduct of the war led to his court-martial and imprisonment in 1916. He was exonerated after the Caporetto disaster vindicated some of his criticisms, and briefly headed Italy's aviation directorate under Mussolini's government in 1922.

Theory

Douhet argued that wars would be decided by air forces striking directly at the enemy's cities, industry and morale. He asserted that "the bomber will always get through", that land and naval forces would become secondary, and that massive bombing with explosive, incendiary and chemical weapons would collapse civilian morale and force a quick end to war. His ideas influenced air theorists such as Hugh Trenchard and Billy Mitchell and shaped strategic bombing doctrines of the Second World War.
