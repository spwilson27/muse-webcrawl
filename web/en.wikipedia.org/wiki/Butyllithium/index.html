Butyllithium refers to several organolithium compounds with the formula C4H9Li. The most widely used is n-butyllithium, a colourless liquid supplied commercially as a solution in alkanes such as hexanes. sec-Butyllithium and tert-butyllithium are stronger bases and are respectively more and most reactive of the three.

Properties

Butyllithium compounds are strongly basic and nucleophilic, and are pyrophoric in air. In solution they exist as aggregates — typically hexamers in hydrocarbons — which moderates their reactivity. They react violently with water and protic solvents, liberating butane.

Uses

n-Butyllithium is widely used as a strong base for deprotonation and halogen–lithium exchange in organic synthesis, and industrially as an initiator for the anionic polymerisation of dienes used in synthetic rubbers. tert-Butyllithium is used where a very strong, hindered base is needed.

Safety

Because of their pyrophoricity, butyllithium solutions must be handled under inert atmosphere with specialised techniques, and laboratory accidents involving tert-butyllithium have led to stricter safety guidance.
