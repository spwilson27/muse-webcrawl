The goat or domestic goat (Capra hircus) is a domesticated species of goat-antelope kept as livestock for its milk, meat, hair, and skins. It was domesticated from the wild bezoar ibex (Capra aegagrus) of Southwest Asia and Eastern Europe around 10,000 years ago, making it one of the earliest animals domesticated by humans. There are over 300 distinct breeds and roughly one billion goats worldwide, concentrated in Asia and Africa.

Biology

Goats are ruminants with a four-chambered stomach, browsing rather than grazing by preference — they prefer leaves, shrubs and vines, standing on their hind legs to reach them. Both sexes usually bear horns and the males (bucks or billies) grow beards and produce a strong odour in the rut. Females are called does or nannies, young are kids. Goats are famously agile climbers with excellent balance, have horizontal, slit-shaped pupils giving a wide field of vision, and are highly curious and intelligent. Lifespan is typically 15–18 years. "Fainting" (myotonic) goats stiffen and topple when startled due to a genetic muscle condition.

Domestication and history

Archaeological evidence from sites such as Ganj Dareh in Iran suggests goats were herded and culled in managed flocks by about 8000 BC. From Southwest Asia they spread with Neolithic farmers across Europe, Africa and Asia. Goats appear throughout mythology and religion — the Norse god Thor's chariot was pulled by goats, the zodiac sign Capricorn is a sea-goat, and the scapegoat of Leviticus symbolically carried away the sins of Israel. Because they thrive on marginal land, goats became known as the "poor man's cow."

Uses

Goats supply milk (the basis of cheeses such as chèvre and feta-style brined cheeses), meat (the most widely consumed red meat worldwide), fibre — cashmere from cashmere goats and mohair from Angoras — and skins. They are also used for brush and weed control, as pack animals, and as companion animals; miniature breeds such as the Nigerian Dwarf and African Pygmy are popular pets. Goat milk is often promoted for people sensitive to cow milk, though it is not suitable for those with true milk allergy.

Environmental impact

Hardy browsing can control invasive plants, but unmanaged feral goats have severely damaged island and fragile ecosystems from the Galápagos to Australia, prompting large eradication programmes. Their ability to denude vegetation in fragile arid lands has also implicated overstocked goat herds in desertification debates, though pastoralists dispute simplistic blame.
