Mixology

Mixology is the art and study of preparing mixed drinks, particularly cocktails. A mixologist — sometimes styled a "cocktail chef" or "bar chef" — is a bartender with a deeper knowledge of the history, composition, and flavour combinations of drinks, who may create new cocktails, develop drink menus, and study techniques such as infusions, fat-washing, and clarification.

History

The craft draws on the tradition of nineteenth-century American bartending exemplified by Jerry Thomas, whose 1862 Bar-Tender's Guide was among the first drink books published in the United States. The term "mixologist" itself dates to the nineteenth century but was revived in the 2000s during the craft cocktail movement, when bars in cities such as New York and London renewed interest in classic recipes, quality ice, house-made syrups, and historical accuracy.

Mixologist versus bartender

The distinction between the two titles is debated within the hospitality industry. Some use "mixologist" for specialists who design drinks and menus, while "bartender" covers the full service role; others regard the terms as effectively interchangeable, and some working bartenders dislike the term as pretentious.
