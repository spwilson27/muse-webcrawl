Theodor Curtius

Julius Wilhelm Theodor Curtius (27 May 1857 – 8 February 1928) was a German chemist best known for discovering the Curtius rearrangement, in which an acyl azide is thermally decomposed to an isocyanate. He also carried out foundational work on hydrazine, hydrazoic acid and other nitrogen-rich compounds, synthesising the first peptides by controlled stepwise methods and preparing ethyl diazoacetate. His name is attached to several reactions and reagents in organic chemistry.

Life and career

Curtius was born in Duisburg, the son of a factory owner. He first studied natural science at Heidelberg and Leipzig, at first intending a career in music, and took his doctorate at Leipzig in 1882 under Hermann Kolbe with work on amino acids. After a period with Adolf von Baeyer in Munich and as assistant to Adolph Wilhelm Hermann Kolbe, he habilitated and in 1886 became professor at Erlangen briefly, then moved to the University of Bonn as associate professor, succeeding August Kekulé there as full professor in 1897. He subsequently held chairs at Kiel (from 1898) and finally at Heidelberg, where he succeeded Victor Meyer and remained until his death.

Scientific work

In 1890 Curtius reported the reaction that bears his name: treatment of a carboxylic acid derivative to form an acyl azide, which on heating loses nitrogen and rearranges to an isocyanate; hydrolysis then gives an amine with one fewer carbon atom — related to the Hofmann and Lossen rearrangements. In the same year he isolated hydrazoic acid (HN3), and he prepared and characterised hydrazine and a host of its derivatives, work of great delicacy given the explosive character of these compounds. Curtius also developed a method of peptide synthesis via acyl azides (1902), producing benzoylated peptide chains and anticipating modern stepwise peptide chemistry, and he pioneered the chemistry of diazo compounds, describing ethyl diazoacetate in 1883.

Curtius was an enthusiastic mountaineer, was elected to several academies, and edited the Journal für praktische Chemie. He died in Heidelberg in 1928.
