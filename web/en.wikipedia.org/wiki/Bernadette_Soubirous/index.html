Bernadette Soubirous

Saint Bernadette Soubirous
Born7 January 1844, Lourdes, France
Died16 April 1879, Nevers, France

Bernadette Soubirous (7 January 1844 – 16 April 1879), born Marie Bernarde Soubirous, was a miller's daughter from Lourdes in south-western France who reported eighteen apparitions of the Virgin Mary in 1858. The apparitions took place at the grotto of Massabielle, where the figure identified herself in the local Occitan dialect as the "Immaculate Conception". Lourdes subsequently became one of the world's most visited Catholic pilgrimage sites, famous for its shrine and spring waters.

Life

Bernadette was the eldest of nine children of a poor family living in a damp former prison cell in Lourdes. Between 11 February and 16 July 1858 she reported the apparitions; civil and church authorities investigated her claims with initial scepticism. In 1862 the Bishop of Tarbes declared the faithful justified in believing in the apparitions.

In 1866 Bernadette left Lourdes for the convent of the Sisters of Charity at Nevers, where she worked as an assistant infirmarian and sacristan. She suffered chronic illness, including asthma and tuberculosis, and died on 16 April 1879 aged 35. Her body, exhumed several times, was reported to be largely incorrupt and is displayed in the chapel of the convent at Nevers.

Canonisation

She was beatified in 1925 and canonised by Pope Pius XI on 8 December 1933. Her story was popularised by Franz Werfel's 1941 novel The Song of Bernadette and its 1943 film adaptation.
