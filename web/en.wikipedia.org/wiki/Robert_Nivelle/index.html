Robert Nivelle


Born: October 15, 1856, Tulle, France
Died: March 22, 1924, Paris, France
Service: French Army
Commands: Commander-in-chief, French armies on the Western Front (1916–1917)

Robert Georges Nivelle (October 15, 1856 – March 22, 1924) was a French artillery officer who served as commander-in-chief of the French armies on the Western Front from December 1916 to May 1917, during the First World War.

Career

Nivelle rose rapidly during the war with aggressive artillery tactics. At the Battle of Verdun in 1916, commanding the Second Army, he retook Fort Douaumont and Fort Vaux from the Germans, becoming a national hero. His success — and his fluency in English, inherited from his English mother — led the government to appoint him over the cautious General Joffre.

The Nivelle Offensive

Nivelle promised a decisive breakthrough within 48 hours using a massive creeping barrage. The April 1917 offensive on the Chemin des Dames instead gained little ground at a cost of well over 100,000 French casualties in days. The failure shattered the army's confidence and triggered widespread mutinies in the French forces. Nivelle was replaced by Philippe Pétain in May 1917 and reassigned to North Africa, effectively ending his career.
