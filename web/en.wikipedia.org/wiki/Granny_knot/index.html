Granny knot

[Image: Granny knot]

The granny knot is a binding knot, used to secure a rope or line around an object. It is considered inferior to the reef knot (square knot), which it superficially resembles, because it slips and comes undone more easily and can jam under load.

Structure

The granny knot is a composition of two overhand knots tied in the same direction — right over right, then right over right again — whereas a reef knot ties right over left, then left over right. This asymmetry makes the granny capsize into two half hitches under strain.

Uses

Because of its poor security, the granny knot is generally a mistake — most often tied accidentally when attempting a reef knot. The Ashley Book of Knots lists it among knots to avoid for load-bearing purposes, though it persists in everyday use such as tying shoelaces (where its balanced cousin is preferable).
