Fausto Sozzini

Fausto Paolo Sozzini (Latin: Faustus Socinus; 5 December 1539 – 4 March 1604) was an Italian Renaissance humanist and theologian whose anti-Trinitarian teaching became the foundation of Socinianism, a major influence on later Unitarianism and on rationalist approaches to religion.

Life

Sozzini was born in Siena into a family of jurists. His uncle, Lelio Sozzini, had questioned orthodox doctrines including the Trinity, and Fausto developed these ideas further. After years moving between Italy, Switzerland, Transylvania and elsewhere in Europe — often under suspicion of heresy — he settled in Poland in 1579. There he became the leading theologian of the Polish Brethren (the Minor Reformed Church), a non-Trinitarian church that rejected infant baptism and refused military service and political office. Expelled from Kraków after persecution, he spent his final years protected by sympathetic nobles and died at Lusławice in 1604.

Teaching

Sozzini held that Scripture must be interpreted by reason; that God is one person, Jesus being a man chosen and raised up by God; that salvation comes through obeying Christ's teaching rather than through a substitutionary atonement, which he rejected as irrational; and that the soul is not naturally immortal but receives eternal life as a gift. His followers systematised his thought in the Racovian Catechism (1605), published by the Polish Brethren's academy at Raków.

Legacy

After the Polish Brethren were expelled from Poland in 1658, Socinian ideas spread to the Netherlands and England, where they influenced theologians, philosophers such as John Locke, and the emerging Unitarian churches.
