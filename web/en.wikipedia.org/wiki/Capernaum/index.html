Capernaum

Capernaum (Hebrew: כְּפַר נַחוּם, Kfar Naḥum, "Nahum's village"; Greek: Καπερναούμ, Kapernaoum) was a fishing village on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee in Galilee, inhabited from the Hasmonean period through the early Islamic era. It is best known as the centre of the ministry of Jesus in the Synoptic Gospels, which describe it as Jesus's own town after he left Nazareth, and as the home of the apostles Peter, Andrew, James, and John, and the tax collector Matthew. The ruins, at the site known in Arabic as Tell Hum, are now a major Christian pilgrimage destination.

History

Archaeological evidence suggests the village was established in the 2nd century BCE and grew prosperous from fishing, agriculture, and its position near a border crossing and trade route on the Via Maris branch along the lake. A Roman milestone and remains of a small garrison area reflect its administrative role; the Gospels mention a centurion stationed there. The town continued into the Byzantine period, when a monumental synagogue and an octagonal church were built, and it declined after the early Muslim period, possibly following the earthquake of 749.

Archaeology

The site is divided between a Franciscan property (western part), excavated from 1905 by German scholars and later by Franciscan archaeologists Virgilio Corbo and Stanislao Loffreda, and a Greek Orthodox property to the east. Key discoveries include basalt house foundations of the Roman-period village; a first-century house complex venerated as the House of Peter, over which a fifth-century octagonal church was built (a modern memorial church now stands above it); and the impressive white limestone synagogue, dated to the 4th–5th centuries CE, built over an earlier basalt structure that many identify with the synagogue mentioned in the Gospels.

In the New Testament

Matthew's Gospel calls Capernaum Jesus's "own city" (Mt 9:1). Episodes set there include the calling of fishermen disciples, the healing of Peter's mother-in-law, the paralytic lowered through the roof, the healing of the centurion's servant, and the Temple tax coin found in a fish's mouth. Despite the miracles, Jesus is recorded as reproaching Capernaum, along with Chorazin and Bethsaida, for its lack of repentance (Mt 11:23–24).
