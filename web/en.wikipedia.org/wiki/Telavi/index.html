Telavi (Georgian: თელავი) is a city in eastern Georgia, the principal city of the Kakheti region and a centre of the Georgian wine industry. It lies on the slopes of the Tsiv-Gombori range overlooking the Alazani valley, about 90 km east of Tbilisi.

History

Telavi was first mentioned in the 2nd century CE by the Greek geographer Claudius Ptolemaeus as Teleda. It grew in importance as a trading post on routes from the Middle East to Europe. In the 17th–18th centuries it was the capital of the Kingdom of Kakheti; King Erekle II, born and deceased in Telavi, rebuilt the town, and his palace and fortress (Batonis Tsikhe) still stand.

Wine

The surrounding Alazani valley is famed for vineyards and for traditional Georgian winemaking in clay qvevri vessels, a method inscribed by UNESCO. Numerous wineries and cellars around Telavi offer tastings of varieties such as Saperavi and Rkatsiteli.

Landmarks

Sights include the Batonis Tsikhe fortress, the 900-year-old plane tree, and nearby Alaverdi Monastery (11th century), one of the tallest Georgian cathedrals of its era.
