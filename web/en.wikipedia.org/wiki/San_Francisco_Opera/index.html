San Francisco Opera is an American opera company founded in 1923 by the Italian conductor and impresario Gaetano Merola. It is one of the leading opera companies in the United States and performs at the War Memorial Opera House in San Francisco, California, which has been its home since the house opened in 1932.

History

Merola, who had conducted touring opera in San Francisco, organised the company's first season in 1923 at the Civic Auditorium and Stanford's stadium. In 1932 the War Memorial Opera House opened — the first municipally owned opera house in the United States, built as a memorial to San Francisco's First World War dead. It was also the site of a landmark event in 1945, when it hosted sessions of the founding conference of the United Nations.

After Merola's death in 1953, Kurt Herbert Adler became general director, building the company's international reputation over nearly three decades, introducing many European artists to America and expanding the repertoire. Subsequent general directors included Terence McEwen, Lotfi Mansouri (noted for innovative productions and for introducing projected supertitles, first developed in Canada), Pamela Rosenberg, David Gockley, and Matthew Shilvock, who took over in 2016. Music directors have included Donald Runnicles (1992–2009) and Nicola Luisotti (2009–2018), with Eun Sun Kim becoming music director in 2021.

Programmes and reputation

The company presents a summer and fall season of standard repertoire, rarities and new works, and has mounted many notable premieres and revivals. Its training programmes — the Merola Opera Program (founded 1957) and the Adler Fellowship — are among the most respected singer-development schemes in the world. The San Francisco Opera Orchestra and Chorus are integral ensembles of the company.
