Bong Joon-ho

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Bong Joon-ho on the set of Parasite, monitor headphones around his neck, reviewing a take in the Parks' living-room set]

Bong Joon-ho (born 14 September 1969) is a South Korean film director, producer and screenwriter, among the most acclaimed filmmakers of the twenty-first century. His films mix popular genres with social criticism and tonal whiplash: Memories of Murder (2003), the monster film The Host (2006), Mother (2009), the English-language Snowpiercer (2013) and Okja (2017), and Parasite (2019), which won the Palme d'Or and four Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Director.

Born in Daegu and raised in Seoul, Bong studied sociology at Yonsei and co-founded a film club, then trained at the Korean Academy of Film Arts. His debut Barking Dogs Never Bite (2000) flopped; Memories of Murder, about Korea's first serial-murder case under the dictatorship, made him.

Style

Bong storyboards every shot himself, plans productions like military campaigns, and lets scenes swerve from farce to dread within a take; class runs through all his work — the train of Snowpiercer ordered by wealth front to back, the Seoul river monster as state incompetence, Parasite's vertical city. His actors (Song Kang-ho above all, in four films) and his genre literacy made him the bridge between Korean popular cinema and the world festival circuit.

After Parasite

The Oscar triumph made Bong a global figure — chair of the Venice jury in 2021 — and his science-fiction film Mickey 17, with Robert Pattinson, followed in 2025 after delays. He has used his platform to plead for subtitled cinema's acceptance, predicting (as he put it at the Golden Globes, in the much-quoted formulation) a richer world once audiences pass the one-inch barrier of subtitles — paraphrased here.
