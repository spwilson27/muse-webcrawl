West Wing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The West Wing of the White House is the structure in Washington, D.C. that houses the offices of the President of the United States. It contains the Oval Office, the Cabinet Room, the Situation Room and the offices of the president's senior staff, including the White House Chief of Staff, the press secretary and the national security advisor.

History

The West Wing was built in 1902 during Theodore Roosevelt's administration, when the executive offices were moved out of the main White House residence to reduce crowding. It was enlarged under William Howard Taft, who moved the Oval Office to its current location at the center of the south side of the building, and was rebuilt after a fire in 1929. A second floor was added under Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Because the president's staff works in the West Wing, the term "West Wing" is used as a metonym for the Executive Office of the President. The building is also the setting for the television series The West Wing (1999–2006), created by Aaron Sorkin, which dramatized the fictional administration of President Josiah Bartlet.
