Conjugated polymer

Conjugated polymers are organic macromolecules whose backbones contain alternating single and double bonds, so that π-electrons are delocalised along the chain. This conjugation gives the materials semiconducting and optical properties unusual for plastics, including electrical conductivity when doped and light emission when excited.

History

The field began with polyacetylene: in 1977 Alan Heeger, Alan MacDiarmid, and Hideki Shirakawa showed that exposing polyacetylene film to iodine vapour increased its conductivity by many orders of magnitude. The discovery of "synthetic metals" earned the three the 2000 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Materials and properties

Important conjugated polymers include polypyrrole, polyaniline, polythiophene and its soluble derivative PEDOT, poly(p-phenylene vinylene) (PPV), and polyfluorenes. Their band gaps can be tuned chemically, and many are solution-processable, allowing printing of electronic devices on flexible substrates.

Applications

Conjugated polymers are used in organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs), organic solar cells, field-effect transistors, antistatic and corrosion-resistant coatings, supercapacitors, sensors, and bioelectronics such as neural interfaces.
