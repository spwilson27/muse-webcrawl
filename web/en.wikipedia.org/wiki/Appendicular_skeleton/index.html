The appendicular skeleton is the portion of the skeleton of vertebrates consisting of the bones that support the appendages. In humans, it comprises 126 of the 206 bones of the adult skeleton and is divided into the upper and lower appendages and their associated girdles. The appendicular skeleton joins to the axial skeleton — the skull, vertebral column, and thoracic cage — which forms the body's central axis.

Components

Pectoral girdle and upper limb

The pectoral (shoulder) girdle consists of the clavicle and scapula on each side, attaching the upper limb to the trunk; unlike the pelvic girdle, it is connected to the axial skeleton only at the sternoclavicular joint, allowing great mobility. Each upper limb contains 30 bones: the humerus in the arm; the radius and ulna in the forearm; eight carpal bones in the wrist; five metacarpals in the palm; and fourteen phalanges in the fingers.

Pelvic girdle and lower limb

The pelvic girdle is formed by the two hip bones (ossa coxae), each a fusion of the ilium, ischium, and pubis, and it articulates firmly with the sacrum of the axial skeleton to transmit body weight to the lower limbs. Each lower limb also contains 30 bones: the femur (the body's longest and strongest bone) in the thigh; the patella (kneecap); the tibia and fibula in the leg; seven tarsal bones in the ankle and heel, including the calcaneus; five metatarsals; and fourteen phalanges in the toes.

Function

The appendicular skeleton is concerned primarily with movement and manipulation of the environment. The upper limbs, freed from weight-bearing in bipedal humans, are specialised for grasping and fine motor control, while the lower limbs are adapted for support and locomotion. The girdles serve as movable bases that position the limbs and as attachment sites for the muscles that move them. Joints of the appendicular skeleton — such as the ball-and-socket shoulder and hip, the hinge elbow and knee — permit the wide range of motions required for locomotion and handling objects.

Development and variation

The appendicular skeleton develops from limb buds that appear in the fourth week of embryonic life; the bones form mainly by endochondral ossification from cartilage models. The total count of 126 bones applies to adults: at birth several elements are unfused, and counts differ in other vertebrates, where limbs may be modified into wings or flippers or lost entirely, as in snakes.
