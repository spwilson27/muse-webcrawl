County Waterford (Irish: Contae Phort Láirge) is a county in the province of Munster in south-eastern Ireland. It is named after its county town, Waterford, Ireland's oldest city, founded by the Vikings in 914 AD. The county is nicknamed "the Déise" after an ancient territory and people of the region, whose name survives in the baronies of Decies.

Geography

Waterford is bordered by Counties Wexford, Kilkenny, Tipperary and Cork. The River Suir forms much of its northern boundary; the River Blackwater flows through the west of the county past Lismore. The Comeragh Mountains rise in the centre, with Knockmealdown nearby, and the coast — the "Copper Coast" between Tramore and Dungarvan — is a UNESCO Global Geopark known for its mining heritage and cliffs. Dungarvan is the second town of the county; much of West Waterford, including Ring (An Rinn), is a Gaeltacht area.

History

Viking Waterford was one of the principal Norse towns of Ireland; Reginald's Tower, at the apex of the city's Viking Triangle, is the oldest civic building in Ireland. The county was a stronghold of the Anglo-Norman and later civic merchant tradition, and the motto of the city, Urbs Intacta Manet ("the city remains unconquered"), commemorates its resistance to sieges. Waterford Crystal, founded in 1783, made the county's name famous worldwide for glassmaking.

Sport

Waterford GAA's hurlers were All-Ireland champions in 1948 and 1959, and the county remains a major hurling power in blue and white. The blaa, a soft white bread roll unique to Waterford, holds EU protected geographical status.
