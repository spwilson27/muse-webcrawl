Mor Hananyo Monastery

Mor Hananyo Monastery (Syriac: ܕܝܪܐ ܕܡܪܝ ܚܢܢܝܐ, Dayro d-Mor Ḥananyo; Turkish: Deyrulzafaran Manastırı; Arabic: دير الزعفران) is an important Syriac Orthodox monastery located three kilometres south-east of Mardin, Turkey, in the Syriac cultural region known as Tur Abdin.

The monastery is also called the "Saffron Monastery" (Deyrulzafaran), a name usually explained either by the saffron colour of its stone or by saffron once grown around it. Mor Hananyo served as the seat of the Patriarch of Antioch of the Syriac Orthodox Church from 1293 until 1932.

History

The site was originally a temple dedicated to the Mesopotamian sun god Shamash, later used as a Roman citadel. The monastery itself was founded in 493 by Mor Shlemon, and named for Mor Hananyo, bishop of Mardin and Kfartuta, who renovated it in the 8th century. Over the centuries it became one of the most important centres of Syriac learning, producing numerous manuscripts and scholars.

In 1293, after a period in which the patriarchate moved between monasteries, Mor Hananyo became the official residence of the Syriac Orthodox Patriarch of Antioch. It remained so until 1932, when Patriarch Ignatius Elias III moved the patriarchal seat to Homs in Syria following political upheavals in Turkey. The monastery possesses a printing press installed in the 19th century, one of the first in the region, which printed books in Syriac, Arabic and Turkish.

Architecture

The complex includes the Church of Mor Hananyo, the House of Saints (Beth Qadishe), containing the tombs of patriarchs and metropolitans, and the Church of the Virgin Mary, considered the oldest part of the complex. Beneath the monastery lies an ancient underground chamber associated with the earlier sun temple. The buildings are constructed of golden-hued limestone characteristic of the Mardin region.

The monastery remains an active religious centre, headed by the metropolitan of Mardin, and is a major pilgrimage and tourist site in south-eastern Turkey.
