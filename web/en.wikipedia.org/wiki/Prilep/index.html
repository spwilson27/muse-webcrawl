Prilep (Macedonian: Прилеп) is a city in the southern part of North Macedonia, at the northern edge of the Pelagonia plain, the country's largest valley. It is one of Macedonia's oldest cities and is known as a centre of tobacco growing and processing, for its marble quarries, and for the medieval fortress of Markovi Kuli ("Marko's Towers") that overlooks it. The city was the seat of the legendary hero King Marko (Prince Marko), whose memory looms large in Balkan folklore.

History

The area was settled in antiquity — the ancient city of Styberra lay nearby — and Prilep grew important under the medieval Serbian and Bulgarian empires. In the late 14th century it was the capital of the lordship of King Vukašin and his son Marko, nominal Ottoman vassals; after Marko's death in 1395 Prilep passed fully under Ottoman rule, which lasted until the Balkan Wars of 1912–13. The city was a centre of the Macedonian revolutionary movement; the Ilinden Uprising of 1903 reached the Prilep region. On 11 October 1941 an attack on the Bulgarian police station in Prilep began the Macedonian uprising against Axis occupation, a date still celebrated as a national holiday; Prilep's partisans earned it the honorific "hero city".

Economy

Prilep is the heart of Macedonia's tobacco industry: the surrounding plain produces prized Oriental tobacco, and the city's Tobacco Institute, founded in 1924, is one of the oldest scientific institutions in the Balkans. White marble from nearby Sivec is exported worldwide and was used in prestigious buildings abroad; food processing, textiles and electronics are also significant employers.

Culture and sights

The ruins of Markovi Kuli and the 14th-century Treskavec Monastery, perched on a rocky peak, draw visitors. The old bazaar (Čaršija) preserves Ottoman-era crafts shops, mosques and a clock tower. Each summer Prilep hosts a popular beer festival, Pivo Fest, and the city is a base for exploring Pelagonia and Mount Pelister's national park nearby.
