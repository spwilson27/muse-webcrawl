Nyaya Sutras

The Nyāya Sūtras is an ancient Sanskrit text foundational to the Nyaya school of Hindu philosophy, traditionally attributed to Akṣapāda Gautama. Composed between roughly the 6th century BCE and the 2nd century CE in layers, the work systematises logic, epistemology, and the rules of rational debate, and it profoundly influenced Indian philosophy for more than two thousand years.

Contents

The text comprises five books (adhyāya), each divided into two daily lessons, for a total of about 528 aphorisms (sūtras). It opens by listing sixteen categories (padārtha) whose correct knowledge is said to lead to liberation, including the means of knowledge (pramāṇa), the objects of knowledge (prameya), doubt, purpose, example, tenet, the members of an inference, reflection, ascertainment, discussion, disputation, and the grounds for defeat in debate.

Epistemology

The Nyaya Sutras recognise four independent means of valid knowledge: perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), comparison (upamāna), and verbal testimony (śabda). Its five-membered inference — proposition, reason, example, application, and conclusion — became a model for Indian logic. Later commentators, beginning with Vātsyāyana's Nyāya Bhāṣya, expanded the text's analysis, and the tradition eventually merged with the Vaiśeṣika school and, in the medieval period, developed into Navya-Nyāya, the "new logic" of Gaṅgeśa and his successors.
