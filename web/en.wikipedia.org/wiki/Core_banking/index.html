Core banking

Core banking is a banking service provided by a group of networked bank branches where customers may access their bank account and perform basic transactions from any of the member branch offices. The term is also used for the centralised software systems — core banking systems — that process a bank's day-to-day transactions.

Overview

Type: Banking technology / service model

Key functions: Deposits, loans, payments, account management

History

Before computerisation, branch banking required each branch to keep its own ledgers, and customers could transact only at their home branch. From the 1970s onward, and accelerating with the CORE (Centralised Online Real-time Exchange) systems adopted widely in the 1990s and 2000s, banks centralised their records so that an account could be operated from any branch, ATM or electronic channel.

Core banking systems

A core banking system posts and processes deposits, loans and credits, maintains customer and account master data, and interfaces with channels such as ATMs, internet banking and payment networks. Major commercial platforms include Temenos Transact, Finacle, Flexcube and TCS BaNCS. Modernisation of ageing core systems is a major theme in banking technology, with banks moving toward modular, cloud-based and real-time platforms.
