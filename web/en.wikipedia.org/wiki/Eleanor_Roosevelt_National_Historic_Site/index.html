Eleanor Roosevelt National Historic Site

The Eleanor Roosevelt National Historic Site preserves Val-Kill, the home of Eleanor Roosevelt in Hyde Park, New York. It was the only residence Eleanor Roosevelt personally owned, and it became her principal home after the death of her husband, President Franklin D. Roosevelt, in 1945. The site is administered by the National Park Service.

History

The property, about two miles east of the Roosevelt family estate at Springwood, was originally used for picnics along the Fall Kill. In the 1920s Eleanor Roosevelt and her friends Nancy Cook and Marion Dickerman built Stone Cottage and later a factory building for Val-Kill Industries, an experiment in providing rural employment through furniture making. The large house, Val-Kill Cottage, was constructed in 1937 by converting the factory building, and Eleanor lived there until her death in 1962.

Preservation

The site was authorized in 1977 and established as a unit of the National Park System; it was formally dedicated in 1984 on the centenary of Eleanor Roosevelt's birth. It is one of few National Park Service sites devoted to a First Lady. Ranger-led tours interpret her work in human rights, including her role in drafting the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
