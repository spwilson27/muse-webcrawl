The Anaheim pepper is a mild to medium variety of the New Mexico chile pepper (Capsicum annuum 'New Mexico Group', cv. Anaheim). It is named for the city of Anaheim in California, where it became widely cultivated after farmer Emilio Ortega brought seeds from New Mexico in the early 1900s and founded the Ortega brand of canned chiles. The peppers are commonly used in Mexican and Southwestern American cuisine.

Description

Anaheim peppers are elongated, slightly curved pods usually harvested green, though they ripen to red. They have a thick flesh suitable for roasting and stuffing, and a mild heat, typically 500 to 2,500 Scoville heat units, though heat varies with growing conditions.

Culinary use

Roasted and peeled green Anaheims are a staple of New Mexican cuisine, used in chile verde, salsas, and as the classic pepper for chiles rellenos. Canned diced green chiles sold throughout the United States are usually Anaheim-type peppers. Red, ripe fruits may be dried and strung into ristras.

Related varieties

The Anaheim belongs to the group of New Mexico chiles developed at New Mexico State University from landrace peppers, including 'New Mexico No. 9' (released 1913 by Fabian Garcia) and later 'Hatch' chiles grown in the Hatch Valley. Similar mild long green chiles grown in California are sometimes marketed as "California" or "Magdalena" chiles.
