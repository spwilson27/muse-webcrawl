List of flood myths

Flood myths are narratives in which a great flood, usually sent by a deity or deities, destroys civilization, often in an act of divine retribution. Parallels are often drawn between the flood waters of these myths and the primeval waters of creation found in many cultures. This article lists flood myths from around the world.

The story of a divinely sent flood survived by a chosen few appears in cultures worldwide.

Near East

  • Sumerian flood story — the hero Ziusudra survives a flood sent by the gods, in one of the oldest recorded flood narratives.
  • Epic of Gilgamesh — Utnapishtim builds a vessel to survive the flood and releases birds to find dry land.
  • Atrahasis — the Akkadian epic in which the god Enlil sends a flood to reduce the human population.
  • Noah's Ark — in the Book of Genesis, Noah builds an ark to save his family and pairs of animals from a worldwide flood; the story is also found in the Quran.

Europe

  • Deucalion — in Greek mythology, Deucalion and Pyrrha survive a flood sent by Zeus and repopulate the earth.
  • Lyonesse and Cantre'r Gwaelod — legendary lands lost beneath the sea in Cornish and Welsh tradition.
  • Ystoria Taliesin and Welsh flood myths — the bursting of Llyn Llion.
  • Irish mythology — Cessair and her company arrive in Ireland before a flood; only Fintan mac Bóchra survives.
  • Bergelmir — in Norse mythology, the frost giant who survives the flood of Ymir's blood.

Asia

  • Gun-Yu flood myth — the Great Flood of China, controlled by Yu the Great through dredging and channeling.
  • Matsya — in Hindu tradition, the fish avatar of Vishnu warns Manu of a coming deluge.
  • Nüwa — repairs the heavens after a great flood in Chinese mythology.

Americas

  • Aztec mythology — the Fourth Sun is destroyed by a flood; survivors Tata and Nene are turned into dogs.
  • Maya mythology — the Popol Vuh describes a flood sent to destroy the wooden people.
  • Inca mythology — Viracocha destroys the giants with a great flood.
  • Native American traditions — many tribes, including the Ojibwe and Choctaw, preserve flood narratives involving earth-divers and survivors on rafts or mountains.

Oceania and Africa

  • Aboriginal Australian traditions — dreamtime stories of the sea rising to cover the land.
  • Yoruba mythology — Olokun sends a flood against humanity.
