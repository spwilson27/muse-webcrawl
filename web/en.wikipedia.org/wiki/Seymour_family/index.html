Seymour family

The Seymour family is an English aristocratic family that rose to prominence in the Tudor period. Descended from a Norman family (de St. Maur, later Seymour) settled in Monmouthshire and the West Country, the family came to national attention when Jane Seymour became the third queen consort of Henry VIII and mother of Edward VI.

Rise under the Tudors

Sir John Seymour of Wolf Hall (Wulfhall), Wiltshire, was the father of Jane Seymour (c. 1508–1537) and of her brothers Edward and Thomas. Edward Seymour became Duke of Somerset and Lord Protector of England during the minority of Edward VI, effectively ruling the kingdom from 1547 until his fall in 1549 and execution in 1552. Thomas Seymour, Baron Seymour of Sudeley, married the dowager queen Catherine Parr and was executed in 1549 for treasonous ambition.

The Somerset dukedom

The dukedom of Somerset was restored for a descendant, Edward Seymour, in 1660. Later generations of the family included the soldier and politician Algernon Seymour, 7th Duke; the Tory politician Edward Seymour, 8th Duke; and in the 19th century Edward St Maur, 11th Duke of Somerset, a mathematician and president of learned societies. Another branch produced the Marquesses of Hertford: Francis Seymour-Conway, 1st Marquess of Hertford, and his descendant Francis Seymour, 5th Marquess, whose family seat was Ragley Hall, Warwickshire; the notorious 3rd Marquess was a model for characters in Vanity Fair and Disraeli's novels.

Other notable members

Admiral Sir Michael Seymour served in the Napoleonic era; his nephew Sir Michael Seymour was a commander in the Baltic and China stations. Admiral Sir Edward Hobart Seymour commanded British naval forces during the Boxer Rebellion relief expedition of 1900. The family seat of the Dukes of Somerset is Maiden Bradley House in Wiltshire, with Berry Pomeroy Castle in Devon historically associated with the family.
