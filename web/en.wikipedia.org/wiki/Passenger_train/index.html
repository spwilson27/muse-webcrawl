Passenger train

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Passengers boarding a sleek intercity train at a modern station]
Boarding an intercity service.

A passenger train is a train used to transport people along a railway line, ranging from urban metros and commuter services through regional and intercity trains to overnight sleeper trains and luxury tourist trains. Passenger rail is the backbone of public transport in much of Europe and East Asia, while in North America it is concentrated in the Northeast Corridor and commuter networks. Train categories in Europe (ICE, TGV, Eurostar, regional, S-Bahn) reflect its central role.

Categories

By speed and distance: rapid transit and commuter rail (frequent stops, high capacity); regional trains; intercity (limited stops, 160–230 km/h); and high-speed trains (250+ km/h). Night trains have revived in Europe in the 2020s as a climate-conscious alternative to short flights (ÖBB Nightjet expansion).

Rolling stock

Electric and diesel multiple units dominate for their acceleration and flexibility; locomotive-hauled coaches persist on long-distance and sleeper routes. Amenities scale from airline-style seating to dining cars, observation cars and private sleeper compartments.

Economics

Few intercity passenger railways cover full costs from fares (exceptions include some Japanese and French high-speed lines); public service obligations subsidise regional services for social and environmental reasons. Ridership records were set in many countries in 2023–24 after COVID recovery.
