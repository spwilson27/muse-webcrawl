Beijing National Aquatics Centre

The "Water Cube" at night, Beijing.

The Beijing National Aquatics Centre, officially the National Aquatics Centre and nicknamed the Water Cube (and, after its conversion, the "Ice Cube"), is a swimming centre at the Olympic Green in Beijing, China. It was built for the aquatic competitions of the 2008 Summer Olympics and was designed by the Australian firm PTW Architects with Arup and Chinese partners.

Architecture

The building's distinctive form derives from the geometry of soap bubbles and foam, formalised using the Weaire–Phelan structure; its steel space frame is clad with more than 3,000 ETFE cushions that give the facade a bubbly, water-like appearance and reduce energy use by capturing solar heat. Ground was broken in December 2003 and the centre was completed in early 2008.

The Games and later use

At the 2008 Olympics the venue hosted swimming, diving and synchronised swimming; 25 world records were broken there, prompting debate about the contribution of the fast pool and new swimsuits. In 2010 part of the building opened as a water park. For the 2022 Winter Olympics it was converted to host curling, gaining the nickname "Ice Cube" — the first venue to serve both Summer and Winter Games in this way.
