Tsundoku

Tsundoku
Japanese: 積ん読
Meaning: Acquiring books and letting them pile up unread

Tsundoku (Japanese: 積ん読) is a Japanese term for acquiring reading materials and letting them pile up in one's home without reading them. The word combines tsunde-oku ("to pile things up ready for later") with dokusho ("reading books").

Usage

The term dates to the Meiji era and was originally used in a self-deprecating or humorous sense. It differs from bibliomania in that it does not necessarily imply an obsessive compulsion; the accumulated books are typically intended to be read eventually. In recent years the word has been adopted in English-language media to describe the common habit of book lovers whose purchases outpace their reading.

Related concepts

The writer Nassim Nicholas Taleb popularised the related idea of the "antilibrary" — a collection of unread books whose value lies in the knowledge they represent as not yet known. Commentators have debated whether tsundoku should be regarded as a vice or as a benign, even beneficial, expression of curiosity.
