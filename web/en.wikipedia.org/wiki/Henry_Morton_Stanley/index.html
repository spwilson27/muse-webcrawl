Sir Henry Morton Stanley (born John Rowlands; 28 January 1841 – 10 May 1904) was a Welsh-American journalist and explorer famous for his exploration of central Africa and his search for the Scottish missionary David Livingstone, whom he found in 1871 at Ujiji on Lake Tanganyika, greeting him (by his own account) with the words "Dr. Livingstone, I presume?" He later worked for King Leopold II of Belgium, laying the groundwork for the Congo Free State.

Early life

Born illegitimately in Denbigh, Wales, Rowlands was raised in a workhouse. At 18 he emigrated to New Orleans, where he took the name of a cotton merchant, Henry Hope Stanley. He fought, was captured, and changed sides in the American Civil War, then became a roving journalist for the New York Herald, covering campaigns against Native Americans and the British expedition to Abyssinia.

Search for Livingstone

Commissioned by publisher James Gordon Bennett Jr. to find Livingstone, who had vanished seeking the Nile's source, Stanley reached Ujiji on 10 November 1871 and found the ill explorer — the only other European for hundreds of miles. Their meeting became one of the most famous episodes of Victorian exploration, though skeptics have questioned the exact wording of the greeting and pages were torn from Stanley's diary.

Congo and Leopold II

In 1874–77 Stanley traversed Africa from east to west, circumnavigating Lakes Victoria and Tanganyika and descending the Congo River to the Atlantic — proving it a navigable highway into the interior. From 1879 to 1884 he returned as agent of Leopold II, building roads and stations and extracting treaties from local chiefs, work that underpinned the king's personal Congo Free State (1885–1908), notorious for atrocities in the rubber trade. Stanley's own expeditions were extremely violent by modern accounts, and his reputation has been reassessed accordingly. His last great expedition (1886–89), ostensibly to relieve Emin Pasha in Equatoria, crossed the Ituri rainforest at enormous cost of life. He later served as a Member of Parliament (1895–1900) and was knighted in 1899.
