Battle of Kollaa

The Battle of Kollaa was fought from 7 December 1939 to 13 March 1940 — the whole duration of the Winter War — around the Kollaa River (Kollaanjoki) in Ladoga Karelia, north of Lake Ladoga. A single Finnish division held a narrow front against repeated attacks by several Soviet divisions, and the line was still unbroken when the war ended. Kollaa became one of the legendary defensive stands of Finnish military history, giving rise to the phrase "Kollaa holds" (Kollaa kestää).

Background

The Soviet 8th Army advanced north of Lake Ladoga hoping to outflank the Mannerheim Line by cutting Finland in two. Finnish IV Corps fell back to the Tolvajärvi–Kollaa region and counter-attacked successfully at Tolvajärvi in December, but along the Kollaa River the outnumbered 12th Division settled into a static defence on the west bank, in bitter cold reaching −40 °C and with almost no reserves.

The battle

For over three months the Soviets attacked the narrow Kollaa front with massed artillery, tanks and wave infantry assaults, at times firing tens of thousands of shells a day onto positions known to the Finns by nicknames such as "Killer Hill" and the "Termite Mound". The Finnish line, held by infantry regiments JR 34 and JR 35/36 rotations and the legendary sharpshooter Simo Häyhä — who served at Kollaa and was credited with over 500 kills before being gravely wounded on 6 March 1940 — bent but never broke. When a staff officer asked whether Kollaa would hold, the reported answer, "Kollaa holds, unless we are ordered to run", became a national catchphrase. Fighting continued until the Moscow Peace came into force at noon on 13 March 1940; the front had not been breached.

Legacy

Because the territory was ceded to the USSR in the peace treaty, the Kollaa battlefield lies in present-day Russia. The battle, together with Tolvajärvi, is credited with saving Finland's northern flank and remains a byword for tenacious defence; it features prominently in Antti Tuuri's novel The Winter War and its film adaptation.
