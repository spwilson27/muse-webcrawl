Ol Pejeta Conservancy

The Ol Pejeta Conservancy is a 360 km² (90,000-acre) not-for-profit wildlife conservancy in Central Kenya's Laikipia County, on the equator west of Nanyuki, between the foothills of the Aberdares and Mount Kenya. It is the largest black rhinoceros sanctuary in East Africa and hosts the last two remaining northern white rhinoceroses in the world, Najin and Fatu, who live there under constant protection.

Wildlife

The conservancy is home to the "Big Five" (lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhinoceros) as well as Grevy's zebra, Jackson's hartebeest, cheetahs and African wild dogs. It operates as a working cattle ranch integrated with wildlife conservation. The Sweetwaters Chimpanzee Sanctuary, established in 1993 within the conservancy, provides lifetime refuge for orphaned and abused chimpanzees from West and Central Africa; it is the only place in Kenya where chimpanzees, which are not native to the country, can be seen.

Northern white rhinos

In 2009, four northern white rhinos were moved to Ol Pejeta from Dvůr Králové Zoo in the Czech Republic. After the deaths of the males Suni (2014) and Sudan (2018) — Sudan was the last known male of the subspecies — only the females Najin and Fatu remain. An international consortium, BioRescue, is attempting to save the subspecies using assisted reproduction, including in-vitro fertilisation with preserved semen and southern white rhino surrogate mothers.

Community

Ol Pejeta works with surrounding communities, funding schools, health care and infrastructure from tourism and enterprise revenue, and supports community livestock programmes.
