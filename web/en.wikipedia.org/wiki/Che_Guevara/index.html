Ernesto "Che" Guevara (14 June 1928 – 9 October 1967) was an Argentine Marxist revolutionary, physician, author, guerrilla leader, diplomat and military theorist. A major figure of the Cuban Revolution, his stylized visage has become a ubiquitous countercultural symbol of rebellion and a global insignia in popular culture.

Early life and travels

Guevara was born in Rosario, Argentina, the eldest of five children in a middle-class family of Spanish and Irish descent. He studied medicine at the University of Buenos Aires. Motorcycle journeys across South America in 1952 — later recounted in The Motorcycle Diaries — exposed him to the poverty and exploitation he came to attribute to capitalist imperialism. In Guatemala in 1954 he witnessed the CIA-backed overthrow of President Jacobo Árbenz, which hardened his commitment to armed struggle.

Cuban Revolution

In Mexico in 1955 Guevara joined Fidel Castro's 26th of July Movement and sailed aboard the Granma to Cuba in November 1956. Rising to comandante, he led a column in the decisive Battle of Santa Clara in December 1958. After the revolution's victory he held senior posts — president of the National Bank and Minister of Industries — oversaw land reform and literacy campaigns, and presided over revolutionary tribunals at La Cabaña fortress, where opponents of the regime were executed, a record that remains controversial.

Congo and Bolivia

Guevara left Cuba in 1965 to foment revolution abroad, first unsuccessfully in the Congo, then in Bolivia, where his small guerrilla force was hunted by Bolivian troops advised by the CIA. He was captured on 8 October 1967 and executed the next day in the village of La Higuera. His remains, located in 1997, were repatriated to a mausoleum in Santa Clara, Cuba.

Legacy

Guevara remains a polarizing figure: revered by many as a martyr for the poor and an icon of resistance, and condemned by critics as a ruthless ideologue responsible for repression. Alberto Korda's 1960 photograph Guerrillero Heroico is considered the most reproduced photographic image in history.
