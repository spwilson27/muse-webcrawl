A call option (or simply a call) is a financial contract that gives its buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy a specified quantity of an underlying asset (such as a stock) from the seller at a predetermined price (the strike price) on or before a specified expiration date, in exchange for a payment called the premium.

Payoff and use

At expiry a call is worth max(S − K, 0), where S is the price of the underlying and K is the strike price; the buyer's profit is this amount minus the premium paid, while losses are limited to the premium. The writer (seller) of the call has the opposite exposure and potentially unlimited loss. Calls are used for speculation on rising prices, for hedging, and as building blocks of strategies such as covered calls, spreads and straddles.

Valuation

Option values are commonly modelled with the Black–Scholes formula and its extensions, depending on the underlying price, strike, time to expiry, volatility, interest rates and dividends. The sensitivity of a call's price to these inputs is summarised by the "Greeks", notably delta, gamma, theta and vega.
