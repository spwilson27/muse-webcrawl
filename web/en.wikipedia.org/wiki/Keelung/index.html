Keelung

Keelung (Chinese: 基隆; pinyin: Jīlóng), officially known as Keelung City, is a major port city situated in the northeastern part of Taiwan. It borders New Taipei and forms part of the Taipei–Keelung metropolitan area along with Taipei. Nicknamed the "Rainy Port" for its frequent rain and maritime history, the city is home to Taiwan's second-largest seaport, the Port of Keelung.

History

The region was originally inhabited by the Ketagalan, a Taiwanese indigenous people. Spanish forces established a settlement and fort here in the 1620s before being expelled by the Dutch, and the port later grew under Qing rule and Japanese administration into northern Taiwan's principal harbour.

Geography and climate

Keelung is surrounded by hills on three sides and opens to the East China Sea. It has a humid subtropical climate and is one of the rainiest cities in Taiwan, receiving heavy rainfall especially during the winter monsoon.

Culture

The city is famous for its night market at Miaokou, its Ghost Festival celebrations, and its seafood, including crab, oyster omelettes, and tempura-style snacks.
