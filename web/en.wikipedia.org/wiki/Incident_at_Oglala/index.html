The Incident at Oglala was a shootout that occurred on June 26, 1975, on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in Oglala, South Dakota, United States. During an attempt by Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) agents to serve arrest warrants, a firefight broke out in which FBI Special Agents Jack R. Coler and Ronald A. Williams, and American Indian Movement (AIM) member Joe Stuntz Kills Right, were killed.

The incident became one of the most controversial episodes in the history of relations between the United States federal government and Native American activists. AIM member Leonard Peltier was convicted of the murders of the two agents and sentenced to two consecutive life sentences, a conviction that has been the subject of decades of legal appeals and international campaigns for clemency. Peltier's sentence was commuted by President Joe Biden in January 2025, allowing him to serve the remainder in home confinement.

Background

The Pine Ridge Reservation was the site of intense conflict in the 1970s. In 1973, AIM activists and local supporters occupied the village of Wounded Knee for 71 days in protest against the tribal government of chairman Dick Wilson and federal Indian policy. Wilson's administration was accused of corruption and of using a private militia, the Guardians of the Oglala Nation (GOONs), against opponents. The period between 1973 and 1976, sometimes called the "reign of terror" by residents, saw dozens of violent deaths on the reservation.

The shootout

On June 26, 1975, FBI agents Coler and Williams entered the reservation searching for a young man wanted in connection with an alleged assault and theft of a pair of cowboy boots. Following a red pickup truck onto the property of the Jumping Bull family, the agents came under fire. A gun battle ensued involving AIM members who were camped on the property, including Leonard Peltier, Dino Butler, and Robert Robideau. Both agents were wounded and then killed at close range. Joe Stuntz, an AIM member, was also shot and killed, reportedly by a law enforcement sniper; his death was never formally investigated.

Trials

Butler and Robideau were tried in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, in 1976 and acquitted on grounds of self-defense. Peltier, who had fled to Canada, was extradited and tried separately in Fargo, North Dakota, in 1977. He was convicted of two counts of first-degree murder and sentenced to two consecutive life terms. Critics have long pointed to inconsistencies in the prosecution's evidence, including ballistics disputes and recanted witness statements, while the FBI and the agents' families maintained that the verdict was just.

Legacy

The incident was the subject of the 1992 documentary film Incident at Oglala, directed by Michael Apted and narrated by Robert Redford, and of Peter Matthiessen's book In the Spirit of Crazy Horse (1983). Peltier became one of the most prominent federal prisoners in the United States, with supporters including Amnesty International and several Nobel Peace Prize laureates calling for his release. On January 20, 2025, President Biden commuted Peltier's sentence to home confinement; Peltier was released from prison in February 2025.
