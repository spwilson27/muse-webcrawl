Penny (United States coin)

The United States one-cent coin, commonly known as the penny, is a unit of currency equaling one one-hundredth of a United States dollar. It has been the lowest-value physical unit of US currency since the abolition of the half-cent in 1857. The obverse of the modern penny has featured the profile of President Abraham Lincoln since 1909, the centennial of his birth.

History

The first US cent, the large cent of 1793, was nearly the size of a modern half-dollar. The small cent arrived with the Flying Eagle cent in 1857, followed by the Indian Head cent (1859–1909) and the Lincoln cent, designed by Victor David Brenner, whose wheat-ear reverse (1909–1958) was followed by the Lincoln Memorial reverse (1959–2008) and the Union Shield reverse from 2010. The 1943 cent was struck in zinc-coated steel because of wartime copper shortages.

Composition and cost

Since 1982 the cent has consisted of a zinc core (97.5%) with a thin copper plating. For many years the cost of manufacturing and distributing the coin exceeded its face value, fueling recurring debate over eliminating the penny, as Canada did in 2012.
