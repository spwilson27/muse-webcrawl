The Shaybanids (also spelled Shaibanids) were a Turko-Mongol dynasty of Uzbek khans who ruled the Khanate of Bukhara and much of Central Asia from 1500 to 1599. Descended from Shayban, the fifth son of Jochi and grandson of Genghis Khan, they displaced the Timurids in Transoxiana and contested Khurasan with Safavid Persia for most of the sixteenth century.

Origins and conquest

The Shaybanid Uzbeks coalesced in the steppes of western Siberia and the Dasht-i Qipchaq under Abu'l-Khayr Khan (r. 1428–1468). His grandson, Muhammad Shaybani (1451–1510), regrouped the Uzbek tribes and between 1500 and 1506 conquered Samarkand, Bukhara, Tashkent and Herat from the Timurids, driving the young Babur out of Samarkand (he would later found the Mughal Empire in India). Shaybani was killed in 1510 at the Battle of Merv by Shah Ismail I of Safavid Persia.

Rule in Bukhara

After a brief Safavid–Babur occupation, the Shaybanids recovered under Ubaydullah Khan of Bukhara (r. 1533–1540), who campaigned repeatedly into Khurasan. The dynasty's greatest ruler was Abdullah Khan II (r. 1583–1598), who reunified the appanage khanates, conquered Balkh, Fergana and (temporarily) Herat, reformed the currency, and patronised monumental building in Bukhara — including trading domes, madrasas and caravanserais that still define the city's old centre. Shaybanid rule operated through an appanage system in which princely relatives governed Samarkand, Tashkent, Balkh and other cities under the senior khan, a source of both resilience and chronic fragmentation.

Religion and culture

The Shaybanids championed Sunni Islam of the Hanafi school and the Naqshbandi Sufi order against Shi'a Safavid Persia, and frontier warfare between the two powers was framed in confessional terms. Their courts supported Persian and Chagatai Turkish literature, historiography (such as the Tarikh-i Abu'l-Khayr Khani), and architecture in Bukhara and Samarkand.

Decline

After Abdullah Khan II's death in 1598 and the murder of his son, the dynasty collapsed within a year; in 1599 power passed to the Janids (Ashtarkhanids), a Chinggisid line from Astrakhan.
