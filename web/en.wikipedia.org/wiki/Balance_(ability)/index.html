Balance (ability)

Balance, in biomechanics and physiology, is the ability to maintain the line of gravity (vertical line from the centre of mass) of a body within its base of support with minimal postural sway. The sense of balance, sometimes called equilibrioception, is one of the physiological senses: it allows humans and animals to walk, run, and stand without falling. It results from the integration of visual input, proprioception (information from muscles and joints), and signals from the vestibular system of the inner ear.

Mechanism

The vestibular apparatus of the inner ear — the three semicircular canals and the otolith organs (utricle and saccule) — detects angular and linear acceleration of the head. This information is combined in the brainstem and cerebellum with visual cues about the horizon and proprioceptive signals about body position. Muscular adjustments, organised mainly through the ankle strategy for small disturbances and the hip strategy for larger ones, keep the centre of mass over the feet. The Romberg test, in which a patient stands with feet together and eyes closed, is used clinically to assess these systems.

Balance disorders

Impairment of balance can arise from inner-ear disease (such as Ménière's disease, labyrinthitis, or benign paroxysmal positional vertigo), neurological disorders including Parkinson's disease and stroke, peripheral neuropathy, medications, and ageing. Loss of balance is a major risk factor for falls, which are a leading cause of injury and death among older adults.

Training

Balance can be improved by exercises that challenge the postural system, such as standing on one leg, tai chi, yoga, and the use of wobble boards and balance beams. Gymnasts, dancers, surfers, and martial artists train balance to a high degree, and rehabilitation programmes after injury typically include progressive balance work.

In animals and machines

Many animals exhibit balance abilities exceeding those of humans; cats, for instance, use a righting reflex to land on their feet. In robotics and transport, dynamic balance is implemented in devices such as the Segway and self-balancing robots using gyroscopes, accelerometers, and feedback control.
