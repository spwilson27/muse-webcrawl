Sheldon Adelson

Born: 4 August 1933, Boston, Massachusetts
Died: 11 January 2021 (aged 87), Malibu, California
Occupation: Founder, chairman and CEO of Las Vegas Sands
Spouse: Miriam Adelson

Sheldon Gary Adelson (4 August 1933 – 11 January 2021) was an American businessman and political donor, the founder and longtime chairman and CEO of the casino company Las Vegas Sands. He built the Venetian and Palazzo resorts in Las Vegas and pioneered the integrated-resort model in Asia with the Venetian Macao and Marina Bay Sands in Singapore, becoming one of the world's wealthiest people.

Career

Born to a poor family in Boston, Adelson's first fortune came from COMDEX, the computer trade show he co-founded in 1979, which he sold in 1995 for about $862 million. He bought the Sands Hotel and Casino in Las Vegas in 1988, added the Sands Expo convention centre, and in 1999 opened the Venetian, betting that convention business could fill a luxury all-suite resort midweek. Through Sands China he developed Macao's Cotai Strip, anchored by the Venetian Macao (2007), then the world's largest casino. He sold his remaining COMDEX-era businesses, took Las Vegas Sands public in 2004, and briefly ranked among the top three richest Americans before the 2008 financial crisis cut deeply into his fortune, which he largely rebuilt from Asian operations.

Politics and philanthropy

Adelson was among the largest donors in American politics, giving well over $400 million across his later years, overwhelmingly to Republican candidates and causes, and he was a prominent supporter of Israel, funding Birthright Israel and, with his wife Miriam, the Adelson Medical Research Foundation and a medical school at Ariel University. He also owned the Las Vegas Review-Journal and the Israeli newspaper Israel Hayom. He died of complications related to treatment for non-Hodgkin lymphoma in January 2021; Miriam Adelson inherited control of the company and later bought a majority stake in the Dallas Mavericks NBA team.
