Video production

Video production is the process of producing video content for television, film, advertising, corporate communications, events, and online distribution. Analogous to filmmaking but centered on electronic capture rather than film stock, it spans three stages: pre-production (planning, scripting, and logistics), production (capturing picture and sound), and post-production (editing, sound mixing, graphics, and finishing).

Stages

Pre-production

Pre-production covers concept development, scriptwriting, storyboarding, casting, location scouting, budgeting, and scheduling. Decisions made at this stage determine the cost and feasibility of everything that follows.

Production

During production (principal photography), the crew records the planned material. Key roles include the director, camera operator or cinematographer, sound recordist, gaffer (lighting), and production assistants. Equipment ranges from smartphone cameras to cinema cameras, drones, and studio lighting rigs.

Post-production

Post-production assembles the footage through video editing, color correction and grading, audio mixing and mastering, visual effects, titles and motion graphics, and final encoding for delivery formats.

Types of video production

  • Corporate video — training, internal communications, and promotional material.
  • Television and broadcast — news packages, documentaries, and entertainment.
  • Advertising — commercials and branded content.
  • Event videography — weddings, conferences, and performances.
  • Online video — content for platforms such as YouTube, including live streaming and viral video.

History

Video production began with electronic television cameras in the mid-20th century; the introduction of portable videotape recorders in the 1960s–70s, camcorders in the 1980s, and non-linear digital editing in the 1990s progressively lowered costs. Digital cameras and desktop editing in the 2000s, followed by smartphones and online platforms, democratized production so thoroughly that individual creators now reach audiences once reserved for broadcasters.
