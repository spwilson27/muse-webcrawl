The Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, officially designated Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington, is the most populous metropolitan statistical area in the U.S. state of Texas and the fourth-largest in the United States, encompassing 11 to 13 counties in North Texas. Locals commonly call it DFW or simply the Metroplex.

Geography and composition

The region centres on the two anchor cities of Dallas and Fort Worth, about 30 miles apart, with Arlington between them, and includes major suburbs such as Plano, Irving, Frisco, Denton, McKinney and Richardson. It is served by Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, one of the world's busiest airports.

Economy

DFW is a major centre for banking, commerce, telecommunications, technology (the "Telecom Corridor" in Richardson), energy, healthcare and logistics. Many Fortune 500 companies are headquartered there, and the region has seen rapid population growth driven by domestic migration.

Culture and sports

The metroplex is home to the Dallas Cowboys (NFL), Dallas Mavericks (NBA), Texas Rangers (MLB, based in Arlington), Dallas Stars (NHL) and FC Dallas (MLS), and to institutions such as the Dallas Museum of Art and Fort Worth's Kimbell Art Museum.
