Menthol

Menthol
Chemical formulaC10H20O
AppearanceColourless, waxy crystalline solid
Melting point36–38 °C (levomenthol)
Natural sourceMint oils, esp. cornmint and peppermint

Menthol is an organic compound, a monoterpenoid alcohol obtained from mint oils or made synthetically. It is a waxy, crystalline substance, clear or white in colour, that is solid at room temperature and melts slightly above it.

Properties and mechanism

Menthol produces a cooling sensation when applied to skin or mucous membranes because it activates the TRPM8 receptor, the cold-sensitive channel also triggered by low temperatures. It also has weak local anaesthetic and counterirritant properties.

Production and uses

Natural menthol is obtained by freezing and crystallising the oil of cornmint (Mentha arvensis), largely produced in India; synthetic menthol is manufactured industrially from citronellal or thymol. Menthol is used in cough drops, decongestant rubs, lip balms, toothpaste, chewing gum, and as a flavouring in cigarettes, where its use is regulated or banned in several jurisdictions.
