Alfred Garrievich Schnittke (24 November 1934 – 3 August 1998) was a Soviet and Russian composer. He was one of the most significant Russian composers since Shostakovich and is known for his method of "polystylism" — the deliberate juxtaposition and quotation of musical styles from many eras within a single work.

Life

Schnittke was born in Engels, in the Volga German Autonomous Republic, to a German-Jewish father and a Volga German mother. He began music studies in Vienna, where his father worked after the war, and later studied at the Moscow Conservatory. Soviet cultural authorities repeatedly obstructed his career; his Symphony No. 1 (1972), a sprawling polystylistic collage, was banned from performance in Moscow. He supported himself by writing more than sixty film scores.

Music

Schnittke's breakthrough came with the Concerto Grosso No. 1 (1977) for two violins, which mingles Baroque forms with tango, quotations, and abrasive modernist passages. Major works include nine symphonies, concertos for cello, violin, viola and piano, the Faust Cantata (1983), the Requiem, the ballet Peer Gynt, and the operas Life with an Idiot and Historia von D. Johann Fausten. In the 1980s his music was championed abroad by performers such as Gidon Kremer, Mstislav Rostropovich, and Gennady Rozhdestvensky. A series of strokes from 1985 gradually ended his composing; his Symphony No. 9 was left unfinished at his death in Hamburg.

Legacy

Schnittke's polystylism came to be seen as a defining response to late-Soviet culture, and his music is widely performed and recorded. He was awarded the Imperial Prize of Japan in 1992.
