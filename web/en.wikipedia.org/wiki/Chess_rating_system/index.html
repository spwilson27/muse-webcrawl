Chess rating system

A chess rating system is a system used in chess to estimate the strength of a player, based on their performance versus other players. Rating systems are used by chess organisations such as FIDE, national federations, and online chess servers. The best-known system is the Elo rating system, devised by Hungarian-American physicist Arpad Elo and adopted by the United States Chess Federation in 1960 and by FIDE in 1970.

The Elo system

In the Elo system, each player has a numerical rating. The expected score of a player against an opponent is computed from the rating difference using a logistic (or, in Elo's original formulation, normal) curve: a player rated 200 points higher is expected to score about 76 per cent. After an event, ratings are adjusted by comparing actual scores with expected scores, scaled by a development coefficient known as the K-factor. Lower K-factors are used for established and highly rated players so their ratings change more slowly.

Other systems

Alternatives include the Glicko and Glicko-2 systems, which track a ratings deviation measuring uncertainty, and the English Chess Federation's grading system. Many online platforms use Glicko variants. FIDE publishes rating lists monthly for standard, rapid and blitz chess.

Highest ratings

Magnus Carlsen achieved the highest FIDE standard rating in history, 2882, in 2014 (and again in 2019). The first official FIDE list in 1971 was headed by Bobby Fischer at 2760.
