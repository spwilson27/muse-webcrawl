Customer development

Customer development is a framework for building companies and products by testing assumptions about customers before and during product development. It was formulated by entrepreneur Steve Blank, first in The Four Steps to the Epiphany (2005) and later in The Startup Owner's Manual (2012, with Bob Dorf). Customer development is a foundational component of the lean startup methodology.

The four steps

  1. Customer discovery — founders state their hypotheses about the customer's problem and the proposed solution, then "get out of the building" to test them with prospective customers.
  2. Customer validation — the team develops a repeatable sales process and confirms that customers will pay, achieving early evidence of a market.
  3. Customer creation — the company scales demand creation and expands into its market.
  4. Company building — the organisation transitions from a temporary, learning-focused startup structure into a formal company with departments.

Principles

Central ideas include that "there are no facts inside the building", that a startup is a temporary organisation searching for a repeatable and scalable business model, and that failed hypotheses should trigger a "pivot" rather than continued execution. Customer development runs in parallel with agile product development: the product team iterates on features while the customer team iterates on the business model.

Influence

Eric Ries incorporated customer development into The Lean Startup (2011), combining it with the build–measure–learn loop and the minimum viable product. The approach is now taught in entrepreneurship programmes and accelerators worldwide, though critics note it can be harder to apply in deep-technology or regulated markets.
