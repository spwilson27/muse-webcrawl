Hashtag activism

Hashtag activism is the practice of using hashtags on social media platforms to promote political or social causes, raise awareness of issues, and coordinate collective action. The term is sometimes used neutrally and sometimes pejoratively, by critics who regard such activity as a low-effort substitute for offline organising — a related criticism captured by the term "slacktivism".

Examples

Prominent examples include #MeToo, which spread globally from 2017 as survivors of sexual harassment and assault shared their experiences; #BlackLivesMatter, founded in 2013 and central to protests against police violence; #BringBackOurGirls, which drew international attention to the 2014 Chibok schoolgirls kidnapping in Nigeria; and hashtags used during the Arab Spring and other protest movements to share information and organise demonstrations.

Analysis

Researchers note that hashtag campaigns can set news agendas, build collective identity and apply pressure on institutions at very low cost, and several campaigns have led to resignations, policy reviews and criminal investigations. Critics respond that visibility does not always translate into durable change, that online support can substitute for costlier action, and that platform algorithms and harassment can distort or suppress campaigns.
