Marquee Moon is the debut album by American rock band Television. It was released on February 8, 1977, by Elektra Records. In the years leading up to the album, Television had become a prominent act on the New York music scene and generated critical attention as a leading act in the early punk rock movement. The album was recorded in September 1976 at A & R Recording in New York City, produced by the band's frontman Tom Verlaine with recording engineer Andy Johns.

For Marquee Moon, Verlaine and fellow guitarist Richard Lloyd abandoned the power chords of contemporary punk in favour of rock and jazz-inspired interplay, melodic lines, and counter-melodies. Verlaine's lyrics combined urban and pastoral imagery, drawing on French poetry and 19th-century literature. The album's title track, which runs nearly ten minutes, features an extended guitar solo built on a rising scale.

Reception and legacy

Although it sold poorly in the United States — reaching only number 92 on the Billboard 200 — Marquee Moon was a critical success on release and fared better in Britain, reaching number 28. Retrospectively it is regarded as one of the greatest albums of all time: it has appeared in the top ranks of lists by Rolling Stone, NME, and Pitchfork, and is widely credited with helping to define post-punk and influencing generations of guitar bands, including R.E.M., Joy Division, and The Strokes.
