Respite care

TypeSocial care / healthcare service
PurposeTemporary relief for primary caregivers
SettingsHome, day centres, residential facilities

Respite care supports family caregivers

Respite care is the temporary care of a dependent person — such as a child or adult with a disability, a chronic illness, or an older person with dementia — provided so that the primary caregiver can take a break. Breaks may last from a few hours to several weeks.

Forms

Respite may be provided in the family home by a visiting carer, in adult day centres, through short residential or nursing-home stays, or informally by friends and relatives. Some schemes offer emergency respite when a caregiver falls ill.

Benefits

Research associates respite with reduced caregiver stress and delayed institutionalisation of the care recipient, although availability and funding vary widely between countries and care systems.
