Stechhelm

The stechhelm (German: Stechhelm, "jousting helm") was a type of heavy helmet used in Germany for the Stechen, a form of jousting in which knights charged one another with sharp lances. In English heraldic usage it is often called a frog-mouth helm (also spelled frog-mouthed helm), after its resemblance to the mouth of a frog.

Description

The stechhelm was developed towards the end of the 14th century and remained in use into the 16th century. It was constructed of heavy steel plates and bolted or strapped directly to the wearer's cuirass (breastplate), so that the weight was borne by the torso rather than the head and neck. The front of the helm formed a pointed, projecting face with a narrow horizontal sight slit positioned just below the topmost point. In the couched position of the charge, with the rider leaning forward, the slit came level with the eyes; as the rider sat up at the moment of impact, the solid lower face of the helm was presented to the opponent's lance point, protecting the eyes and face. This gave the helm its characteristic appearance, like a frog's mouth, angled sharply upward.

Use in heraldry

In German and Scandinavian heraldic tradition, the frog-mouth helm became the conventional helmet shown above the shields of burghers and lower nobility, in contrast to the barred tournament helm reserved for the higher nobility. In British heraldry, following the German convention, the frog-mouth helm denotes an esquire or gentleman.
