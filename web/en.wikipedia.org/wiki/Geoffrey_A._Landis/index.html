Geoffrey Alan Landis (born May 28, 1955) is an American scientist and science fiction writer. He works for the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) at the Glenn Research Center in Cleveland, Ohio, on advanced propulsion and photovoltaic technology, and is the author of the novel Mars Crossing and many short stories.

Science career

Landis holds degrees from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and a Ph.D. in solid-state physics from Brown University. At NASA he has worked on solar cell technology, the Mars Exploration Rovers, and concepts for interstellar propulsion, including laser-pushed light sails and the Bussard ramjet. He was a member of the science team for the Mars Pathfinder and Mars Exploration Rover missions and has studied proposals for exploring the surface of Venus with high-temperature electronics and solar-powered aircraft.

Writing

Landis's short fiction is noted for its hard science fiction rigor. His story "A Walk in the Sun" (1991) won the Hugo Award for Best Short Story, "The Singular Habits of Wasps" was a Hugo finalist, and "Falling onto Mars" won the Hugo Award for Best Short Story in 2003. His novel Mars Crossing (2000) won the Locus Award for Best First Novel. His story "Ripples in the Dirac Sea" won the Nebula Award. He has also published poetry, winning the Rhysling Award.
