Chanson de geste

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The chanson de geste (Old French for "song of heroic deeds", from Latin gesta, "deeds, actions accomplished") is a medieval narrative genre of epic poetry that flourished in northern France from the late eleventh century to the fourteenth century. Composed in Old French and written in verse, the chansons de geste celebrate the legendary deeds of heroic knights — above all Charlemagne and his paladins — often in the setting of the wars against the Saracens in Spain and the Holy Land.

About one hundred chansons de geste survive. They range in length from roughly 1,000 to more than 20,000 lines. The poems were performed, probably sung or chanted to a simple melody, by jongleurs who travelled from court to court.

Form and style

Chansons de geste are written in laisses: stanzas of irregular length, each laisse bound together by a single assonance (in the earliest poems) or rhyme (in later ones). The standard line is the decasyllable, with a strong caesura after the fourth syllable. The style is formulaic, relying on repeated epithets, parallel constructions and recurring scenes of combat, counsel and feasting — features that point to the genre's roots in oral tradition, though the surviving texts were composed in writing, possibly by clerks working with traditional material.

Principal cycles

The chansons were traditionally grouped into cycles organised around a central hero or lineage:

  • The Matter of France (la geste du roi), centred on Charlemagne and his paladins, includes the most famous example, The Song of Roland (La Chanson de Roland, c. 1100), which recounts the ambush at Roncevaux in 778 and the death of Roland.
  • The cycle of Guillaume d'Orange, following William of Orange and his family in their wars against Saracens in the south of France.
  • The cycle of the revolting vassals (la geste des barons révoltés), including Girart de Roussillon and Raoul de Cambrai, which depict conflicts between kings and their barons.
  • The Crusade cycle, dealing with the First Crusade and the deeds of Godfrey of Bouillon.

Historical background

The poems transform events of the eighth and ninth centuries — the Carolingian wars in Spain, Italy and Saxony — into legendary heroic narrative. Historical fact is freely reshaped: in the Song of Roland, the Basque ambushers of the historical rearguard action of 778 become four hundred thousand Saracens, and Charlemagne becomes a patriarch of two hundred years. Scholars long debated whether the chansons preserve continuous oral traditions stretching back to the events themselves, or whether they were largely created in the eleventh century, stimulated by the Crusades, pilgrimage routes and the cult of Charlemagne.

Legacy

The chansons de geste profoundly influenced later European literature. Their material passed into Spanish, Italian, German and Norse traditions; Ariosto's Orlando Furioso draws on the French matter of Charlemagne, and the romances of chivalry across medieval Europe owe much to the epic cycles. The Song of Roland remains the oldest surviving major work of French literature and the national epic of France.
