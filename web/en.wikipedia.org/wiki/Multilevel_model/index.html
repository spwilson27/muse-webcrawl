Multilevel model

Multilevel models (also known as hierarchical linear models, mixed-effects models, or random-effects models) are statistical models for data organized at more than one level — for example, students nested within classrooms within schools, or repeated measurements nested within individuals.

Motivation

Observations within the same group tend to be correlated, violating the independence assumption of ordinary regression. Ignoring the nesting understates standard errors and risks the ecological and atomistic fallacies. Multilevel models explicitly partition variance into within-group and between-group components, quantified by the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC).

Structure

A two-level random-intercept model gives each group its own intercept, drawn from a distribution whose variance is estimated; in a random-slopes model, regression coefficients also vary by group. Predictors may enter at any level, and cross-level interactions test whether group characteristics moderate individual-level effects. Estimation proceeds by maximum likelihood (ML or restricted ML) or Bayesian methods; the "partial pooling" of group estimates toward the overall mean is a hallmark of the approach.

Applications

Multilevel models are ubiquitous in education research, epidemiology, sociology, longitudinal studies, and meta-analysis. Famous applications include school-effectiveness research and multilevel regression with poststratification (MRP) in survey analysis.
