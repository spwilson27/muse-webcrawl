Vladivideos scandal

The Vladivideos scandal was a political scandal in Peru in 2000 that brought down the government of President Alberto Fujimori. The "Vladivideos" were thousands of secret videotapes recorded by Vladimiro Montesinos, head of the National Intelligence Service (SIN), showing him bribing politicians, judges, military officers, businessmen and media owners in his office to secure support for the Fujimori regime. When one tape was broadcast in September 2000, the regime collapsed within weeks.

Background

Montesinos, Fujimori's closest adviser, ran the SIN as the nerve centre of the regime, systematically purchasing the loyalty of congressmen, television channel owners, newspaper proprietors and judges with cash payments drawn from state and illicit funds. He videotaped the meetings — apparently as insurance and blackmail material — amassing an archive estimated at thousands of tapes stored at SIN headquarters and his residences.

Exposure

On 14 September 2000, opposition congressmen Fernando Olivera and Luis Iberico broadcast a tape showing Montesinos handing $15,000 to congressman Alberto Kouri to defect to Fujimori's party after the disputed 2000 election. The broadcast detonated the regime's legitimacy. Days later Fujimori announced he would deactivate the SIN and call new elections in which he would not run. Montesinos fled to Panama and then Venezuela.

Aftermath

Fujimori left Peru in November 2000 for a summit in Brunei and travelled to Japan, from where he faxed his resignation; Congress instead removed him for "moral incapacity". An interim government under Valentín Paniagua oversaw the release and broadcast of further Vladivideos, which became powerful public evidence of systemic corruption. Montesinos was captured in Venezuela in 2001 and received multiple long prison sentences in Peru for corruption, arms trafficking and human rights crimes. Fujimori was later extradited from Chile and convicted in Peru on corruption and human rights charges. The tapes remain a landmark case study of how documentation of bribery — recorded by the briber himself — can destroy an authoritarian system.
