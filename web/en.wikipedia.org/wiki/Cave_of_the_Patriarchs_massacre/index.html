Cave of the Patriarchs massacre

The Cave of the Patriarchs massacre, also known as the Ibrahimi Mosque massacre or the Hebron massacre, was a mass shooting carried out by Baruch Goldstein, an American-Israeli physician and extremist of the far-right Kach movement, on 25 February 1994 at the Cave of the Patriarchs (Ibrahimi Mosque) in Hebron, West Bank.

Attack

During the overlapping religious holidays of Purim and Ramadan, Goldstein entered the Isaac Hall of the shrine — a site sacred to both Jews and Muslims as the burial place of the patriarchs Abraham, Isaac and Jacob — dressed in his Israeli army reserve uniform and opened fire on Muslim worshippers at dawn prayers with an assault rifle. He killed 29 people and wounded 125 before survivors overpowered and killed him.

Aftermath

The massacre was condemned internationally and by the Israeli government, which outlawed the Kach movement as a terrorist organisation. Rioting across the occupied territories in the following days left more Palestinian and Israeli dead. A commission of inquiry headed by Justice Meir Shamgar criticised the army's deployment at the site. Following the massacre the shrine was divided into separate Jewish and Muslim sections, and Hebron itself was later split into Israeli- and Palestinian-administered areas under the 1997 Hebron Protocol. The attack also disrupted the Oslo peace process and was cited by Hamas in justification of subsequent suicide bombings.
