Cocktail attire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cocktail attire sits between casual and formal dress codes.

Cocktail attire is a dress code in Western social culture for early-evening occasions, more formal than smart casual but less formal than black tie. It takes its name from the cocktail parties popular in the 1920s and 1930s, when a distinct category of semi-formal evening dress emerged between day wear and full evening dress.

For men

Traditional cocktail attire for men consists of a dark suit—navy, charcoal, or black—with a dress shirt, tie, pocket square, and leather dress shoes. In recent decades the code has relaxed: a blazer with contrasting trousers and an open-collar shirt is often accepted at less formal events.

For women

For women, the classic garment is the cocktail dress, a knee-length (or increasingly, midi or short) dress in an elegant fabric, worn with heels and evening accessories. The "little black dress", popularized by Coco Chanel in the 1920s and famously worn by Audrey Hepburn in Breakfast at Tiffany's, became the archetypal cocktail dress. Dressy separates, jumpsuits, and tailored trouser suits are now widely accepted equivalents.

Usage

The code is common on invitations to weddings, receptions, theatre evenings, holiday parties, and corporate events. Hosts use it to signal festivity without the expense and rigidity of formal wear. Exact expectations vary by region and occasion, and modern etiquette guides note that cocktail attire allows more colour, pattern, and individual expression than black tie.
