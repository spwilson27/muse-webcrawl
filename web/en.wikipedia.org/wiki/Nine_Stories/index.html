Nine Stories

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


First edition (1953)

Nine Stories is a collection of short stories by American writer J. D. Salinger, published in April 1953 by Little, Brown and Company. It includes two of Salinger's best-known stories, "A Perfect Day for Bananafish" and "For Esmé—with Love and Squalor". Several of the stories concern members of the fictional Glass family, who recur throughout Salinger's work.

Contents

  1. "A Perfect Day for Bananafish"
  2. "Uncle Wiggily in Connecticut"
  3. "Just Before the War with the Eskimos"
  4. "The Laughing Man"
  5. "Down at the Dinghy"
  6. "For Esmé—with Love and Squalor"
  7. "Pretty Mouth and Green My Eyes"
  8. "De Daumier-Smith's Blue Period"
  9. "Teddy"

Background

Most of the stories had previously appeared in magazines, chiefly The New Yorker, between 1948 and 1953. "A Perfect Day for Bananafish", published in January 1948, introduced Seymour Glass and established Salinger's reputation. The collection is prefaced by a Zen kōan: "We know the sound of two hands clapping. But what is the sound of one hand clapping?"

Themes

The stories repeatedly explore the loss of innocence, the spiritual damage of war, and the gulf between sensitive, spiritually questing characters (often children) and the phony, materialistic adult world — a preoccupation that also drives Salinger's novel The Catcher in the Rye (1951).

Reception

The collection was widely praised on publication and spent several months on the New York Times bestseller list. It remains a central text of postwar American short fiction.
