Baumé scale

The Baumé scale is a pair of hydrometer scales developed by the French pharmacist and chemist Antoine Baumé in 1768 for measuring the density of liquids. Readings are expressed in degrees Baumé (°Bé). Two scales are used: one for liquids heavier than water and one for liquids lighter than water.

Definitions

Baumé's original scale was calibrated with pure water at 0 degrees and a 15 percent salt solution at 15 degrees. In modern practice the conversions to specific gravity (SG) at 60 °F (15.6 °C) are:

  • For liquids heavier than water: SG = 145 / (145 − °Bé)
  • For liquids lighter than water: SG = 140 / (130 + °Bé)

Because several national variants of the constants exist (145 versus 144.3, for example), conversion tables differ slightly between countries.

Uses

Degrees Baumé remain in everyday use in several industries. Winemakers measure grape must density in Baumé to estimate potential alcohol — roughly, one degree Baumé corresponds to one percent alcohol by volume in the finished wine. The scale is also used in sugar refining and syrups (closely related to the Brix scale), in pickling brines, and in parts of the petroleum and chemical industries, although API gravity has largely replaced it for crude oil.
