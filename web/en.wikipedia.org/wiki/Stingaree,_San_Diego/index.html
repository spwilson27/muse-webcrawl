Stingaree, San Diego

The Stingaree was a historic red-light district in San Diego, California, located in the area now occupied by the Gaslamp Quarter and parts of downtown near the waterfront. The name is thought to derive from the stingray — "you could get stung there" — or from sailors being "stung" (cheated) in its saloons and gambling halls.

From the 1880s until a police crackdown in 1912, the district contained numerous saloons, gambling halls, brothels, opium dens and cheap lodging houses clustered principally along Fifth Avenue south of Market Street. It adjoined San Diego's old Chinatown. Wyatt Earp operated gambling halls in the district during his years in San Diego in the late 1880s.

In November 1912, the city health and police departments raided the district, evicting prostitutes and demolishing a number of buildings ahead of the Panama-California Exposition of 1915. In the 1980s the area was redeveloped as the Gaslamp Quarter historic district, and the Stingaree's name was later used by a nightclub on the site.
