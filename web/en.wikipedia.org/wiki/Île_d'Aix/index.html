Île d'Aix

Île d'Aix is a small island in the Atlantic Ocean off the west coast of France, in the Charente-Maritime department of the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It lies between the Île d'Oléron to the northwest and the mainland port of Fouras to the east, at the mouth of the Charente river estuary.

The island is roughly crescent-shaped, about 3 kilometres long and 600 metres wide. It has a single village, also called Île-d'Aix, and is notable as one of the few French Atlantic islands where motor vehicles are largely absent; visitors and residents travel on foot or by bicycle, and goods are moved in small carts.

History

Because of its position guarding the roadstead of Rochefort and the mouth of the Charente, the island was heavily fortified from the 17th century onward. Fortifications were developed under Vauban and later engineers, and the island formed part of the defensive system protecting the naval arsenal of Rochefort, together with Fort Enet and Fort Louvois on the nearby coast.

The island is famous in French history as the last place in France where Napoleon Bonaparte stayed before his exile. After his defeat at Waterloo in 1815, Napoleon came to the Île d'Aix hoping to escape to the United States; blockaded by the Royal Navy, he surrendered to Captain Maitland of HMS Bellerophon in July 1815 and was taken from the island into exile on Saint Helena. The house where he spent his final nights in France is now the Musée Napoléonien.

In April 1809 the island's roadstead was the scene of the Battle of the Basque Roads, in which a British fireship attack led by Lord Cochrane inflicted heavy losses on a French fleet anchored near the island.

Geography and tourism

The island is divided between the fortified village in the south and open land, pine woods and small beaches to the north. A coastal path circles the island, passing the Fort de la Rade and offering views toward Île de Ré, La Rochelle and the Fort Boyard, the famous sea fort built on a shoal between Aix and Oléron. The island can be reached by passenger ferry from Fouras throughout the year, with additional seasonal services from La Rochelle and other ports.
