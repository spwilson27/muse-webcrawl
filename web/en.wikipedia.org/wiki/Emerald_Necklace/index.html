Emerald Necklace

The Emerald Necklace is a system of linked parks in Boston and Brookline, Massachusetts, designed by landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted beginning in 1878. It consists of a chain of parks, parkways, and waterways stretching about seven miles (11 km) from Boston Common near downtown to Franklin Park in Roxbury and Dorchester.

Components

The necklace includes Boston Common, the Public Garden, the Commonwealth Avenue Mall, the Back Bay Fens, the Riverway, Olmsted Park along the Muddy River, Jamaica Pond, the Arnold Arboretum, and Franklin Park, connected by parkways such as the Fenway, Park Drive, and the Arborway. The system covers roughly 1,100 acres.

History and purpose

Olmsted conceived the chain not only as recreation space but as a work of sanitary engineering: the Back Bay Fens was designed to store and flush tidal water and purify the polluted Muddy River and Stony Brook. The name "Emerald Necklace" comes from the way the parks hang around the neck of the city. The Emerald Necklace Conservancy, founded in 1997, works with public partners to restore and maintain the parks.
