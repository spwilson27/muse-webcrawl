Triosephosphate isomerase

Triosephosphate isomerase
EC number5.3.1.1
IdentifiersTPI, TIM, TPI1
ReactionDHAP ⇌ glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate
PathwaysGlycolysis, gluconeogenesis, Calvin cycle

Triosephosphate isomerase (TPI or TIM) is an enzyme (EC 5.3.1.1) that catalyses the reversible interconversion of the triose phosphate isomers dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP) and D-glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (G3P). It plays a central role in glycolysis and is essential for efficient energy production in the cell.

Function

In the fourth step of glycolysis, the enzyme aldolase splits fructose 1,6-bisphosphate into two three-carbon sugars, DHAP and G3P. Only G3P can proceed directly through the remaining steps of glycolysis, so TPI converts DHAP into G3P, allowing both halves of the original glucose molecule to be processed. Although the equilibrium of the isolated reaction strongly favours DHAP (roughly 96:4 at equilibrium), the constant removal of G3P by subsequent glycolytic steps pulls the reaction forward in vivo.

TPI also functions in gluconeogenesis, the pentose phosphate pathway, fatty acid synthesis, and, in photosynthetic organisms, the Calvin cycle.

Reaction kinetics and mechanism

TPI is famous as a "catalytically perfect" enzyme: its rate of catalysis is limited only by the rate at which substrate can diffuse to the active site. It accelerates the interconversion by a factor of roughly a billion over the uncatalysed reaction. The mechanism proceeds through an enediol (or enediolate) intermediate. A glutamate residue (Glu165 in the human enzyme) acts as a general base that abstracts a proton from the substrate, while a histidine residue (His95) acts as an electrophilic catalyst, donating a proton to stabilise the intermediate. A flexible loop (loop 6) closes over the active site when substrate binds, sequestering the reactive intermediate from solvent and preventing the escape of the toxic by-product methylglyoxal.

Structure

TPI is a homodimer; each subunit contains about 250 amino acid residues folded into a (β/α)8 barrel — eight parallel β-strands surrounded by eight α-helices. This fold, first observed in TPI, is one of the most common enzyme folds in nature and is consequently known as the TIM barrel. The active site lies at the C-terminal end of the β-strands.

Deficiency

Triosephosphate isomerase deficiency is a rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the TPI1 gene on chromosome 12. It is characterised by chronic haemolytic anaemia, progressive neurological dysfunction, susceptibility to infection, and in many cases early death. There is no effective treatment, and the disease is the most severe of the glycolytic enzymopathies.
