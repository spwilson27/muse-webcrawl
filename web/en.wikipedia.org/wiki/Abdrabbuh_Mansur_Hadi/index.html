Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi (Arabic: عبد ربه منصور هادي; born 1 September 1945) is a Yemeni politician and former military officer who served as President of Yemen from 2012 until 2022. Previously vice president under Ali Abdullah Saleh from 1994, he became acting president during the 2011 Yemeni uprising and was confirmed in office by a referendum-style election in February 2012. His presidency was consumed by the Yemeni civil war that began in 2014–15.

Early life and career

Hadi was born in the village of Thukain in Abyan Governorate in the British Aden Protectorate. He graduated from a military academy in Aden in 1964 and trained in Britain and Egypt. He served in the army of South Yemen, defected north after the 1986 South Yemeni civil war, and after Yemeni unification in 1990 became minister of defence briefly and then vice president in 1994.

Presidency

After President Saleh was injured in a bombing in June 2011, Hadi governed as acting president, and under the Gulf Cooperation Council transition plan he was elected unopposed in February 2012 for a two-year transitional term. His National Dialogue Conference and federalism plans failed to hold the country together. In September 2014 the Houthi movement seized Sanaa; in January 2015 Hadi resigned under house arrest, escaped to Aden, retracted his resignation, and in March 2015 fled to Riyadh as a Saudi-led coalition intervened on his government's behalf.

Transfer of power

From exile, Hadi remained Yemen's internationally recognised president, based between Riyadh and Aden, while his authority on the ground steadily waned. On 7 April 2022 he announced the transfer of his powers to a new Presidential Leadership Council headed by Rashad al-Alimi, ending his presidency.
