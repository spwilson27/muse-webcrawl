Hindu cosmology

Hindu cosmology is the description of the universe and its states of matter, cycles within time, physical structure and effects on living entities according to Hindu texts. It presents the universe as cyclically created and destroyed over immense timescales, with time conceived as a wheel (kalachakra) of repeating ages.

Structure of the universe

In Puranic cosmology the world is centred on Mount Meru, surrounded by concentric island-continents (dvipas) separated by oceans, of which Jambudvipa, containing India (Bharatvarsha), is the innermost. Above and below the earth lie multiple worlds (lokas): fourteen in the standard scheme, from Satyaloka, the realm of Brahma, at the top, to the subterranean realms of Patala below.

Time cycles

Hindu time is divided into repeating cycles. The four ages (yugas) — Satya, Treta, Dvapara and Kali — make up a mahayuga of 4,320,000 years, in which righteousness progressively declines. A thousand mahayugas form a kalpa, one day of Brahma (4.32 billion years), followed by a night of equal length in which the universe dissolves. Brahma's own lifespan of 100 such years is followed by complete dissolution (mahapralaya) and a new creation. The present era is traditionally held to be the Kali Yuga, beginning in 3102 BCE.

Creation

The oldest texts, such as the Rigveda's Nasadiya Sukta ("Creation Hymn"), speculate poetically on the origin of the cosmos from non-being. Later traditions attribute creation to Brahma, born from a lotus emerging from Vishnu's navel, while philosophical schools such as Samkhya describe the evolution of the world from primordial matter (prakriti) disturbed by consciousness (purusha).
