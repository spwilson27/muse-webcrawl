Senate (Australia)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Senate is the upper house of the Parliament of Australia, established by the Constitution of 1901 as the states' house of the federation: each of the six original states elects twelve senators (since the 1984 expansion) regardless of population, and the two mainland territories elect two each, for a chamber of 76. Senators serve staggered six-year terms (territory senators serve House terms), half the state senators facing election at each ordinary cycle.

Elected since 1949 by proportional representation (the Single transferable vote), the Senate is among the world's most powerful upper houses: it may reject or amend any bill except that it cannot originate or amend money bills (it may still reject them — the blockage of supply that precipitated the 1975 dismissal crisis), it operates a strong committee and estimates system scrutinising the executive, and because governments rarely control it, it is the chamber where minor parties and independents (the Greens most durably) exercise the balance of power. Its equal state representation gives Tasmania (population ~0.5 million) the same Senate weight as New South Wales (~8 million) — the federation's deliberate, and periodically contested, malapportionment.
