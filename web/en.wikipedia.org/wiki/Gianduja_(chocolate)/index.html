Gianduja (chocolate)

Place of originTurin, Piedmont, Italy
CreatedEarly 19th century (Napoleonic era)
Main ingredientsChocolate, hazelnut paste, sugar

Gianduja (Italian: gianduia) is a sweet chocolate preparation containing about 30 percent hazelnut paste, invented in Turin, Italy, during the Napoleonic period. It takes its name from Gianduja, a stock character of Piedmontese puppet theatre and carnival. The classic small chocolate made from it is the gianduiotto, an ingot-shaped praline produced in Turin since the nineteenth century.

History

When Napoleon's Continental System restricted imports of cocoa and other goods into Italy, Turinese chocolatiers stretched their limited cocoa supply by mixing it with paste made from the hazelnuts abundant in the Langhe hills. The resulting smooth blend proved popular in its own right. In 1865 the firm Caffarel presented gianduiotti during Turin carnival, associating them with the Gianduja mask and fixing the name.

Production and influence

Gianduja is made by finely grinding roasted hazelnuts with sugar and cocoa mass (and often cocoa butter and milk), producing a soft, sliceable or spreadable paste. It is the direct ancestor of modern chocolate-hazelnut spreads: Nutella, produced by Ferrero in Piedmont, was originally marketed in solid form as "Pasta Gianduja" in the 1940s before becoming a spread. Gianduja-style preparations are now used worldwide in pralines, ice cream and patisserie.
