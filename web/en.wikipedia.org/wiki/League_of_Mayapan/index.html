The League of Mayapan was a confederation of Maya city-states in the northern Yucatán Peninsula, traditionally said to have been formed in the late tenth century among the cities of Mayapan, Chichén Itzá, and Uxmal, and to have kept the peace in the region for some two centuries. According to later Yucatecan chronicles and the Books of Chilam Balam, ruling families sent representatives to Mayapan, which became the dominant capital.

History

Colonial-era Maya and Spanish sources describe a joint government seated at Mayapan, walled and densely populated, where the lords of the member cities maintained households — in effect hostages guaranteeing the peace. Historians debate how literal this "league" was: archaeology shows Mayapan's supremacy in the Postclassic period (c. 1200–1450), while Uxmal's florescence was much earlier and Chichén Itzá's power had waned before Mayapan's rise, so the three-city league may be a retrospective construct.

Fall

Around the mid-fifteenth century, the Xiu family, which traced its roots to Uxmal, led a revolt against the ruling Cocom dynasty of Mayapan. The city was sacked and abandoned (variously dated 1441 or 1461), and northern Yucatán fragmented into some sixteen rival provinces (kuchkabal), which is how the Spaniards found it in the 1520s–40s.
