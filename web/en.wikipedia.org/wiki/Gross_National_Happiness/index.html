Gross National Happiness

Gross National Happiness (GNH) is a philosophy and development framework that guides the government of Bhutan, measuring collective happiness and well-being rather than relying solely on Gross Domestic Product. The term was coined in 1972 by the fourth king of Bhutan, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, who declared that "Gross National Happiness is more important than Gross National Product."

Framework

GNH rests on four pillars: sustainable and equitable socio-economic development, environmental conservation, preservation and promotion of culture, and good governance. The operational GNH Index, developed by the Centre for Bhutan Studies, surveys nine domains — psychological well-being, health, education, time use, cultural diversity and resilience, good governance, community vitality, ecological diversity and resilience, and living standards.

Policy use

Bhutan's 2008 constitution directs the state to promote Gross National Happiness. Government proposals pass through a GNH screening tool, and the index surveys (2010, 2015, 2022) classify citizens as "deeply happy," "extensively happy," "narrowly happy," or "not-yet-happy" to target policy. The 2022 survey found about 48% of Bhutanese in the two happy categories.

International influence and criticism

GNH helped spur global interest in well-being economics, contributing to the UN's World Happiness Report and the adoption of the International Day of Happiness. Critics point to tensions between the philosophy and Bhutan's modernization, youth unemployment, and the treatment of the Lhotshampa (ethnic Nepali) population expelled or displaced in the 1990s.
