Modern award

Modern awards set minimum pay and conditions for whole industries and occupations in Australia

A modern award is an industrial instrument in Australian labour law that sets minimum terms and conditions of employment — including minimum wages, hours of work, overtime and penalty rates, allowances and leave loadings — for employers and employees in a particular industry or occupation. Modern awards operate together with the National Employment Standards under the Fair Work Act 2009.

History

Modern awards were created by the Australian Industrial Relations Commission (now the Fair Work Commission) in a process of "award modernisation" completed around 2010, which consolidated thousands of state and federal awards into a much smaller set of roughly 120 industry and occupational awards. The system descends from Australia's long history of compulsory conciliation and arbitration, dating to the early 20th century.

Operation

Employees covered by an award cannot be paid less than its rates, though enterprise agreements may replace award conditions if employees are better off overall. The Fair Work Commission reviews modern awards and adjusts minimum wages in an annual wage review. Awards do not cover all workers — high-income employees and those outside award coverage rely on contracts and statutory minimums.
