Whole note

In music notation, a whole note (American) or semibreve (British) is a note represented by a hollow oval note head with no stem, and is the longest note value in general use in modern notation. It is held for the duration of a whole bar in 4/4 time — four beats — equivalent to two half notes, four quarter notes, or eight eighth notes.

History and usage

The semibreve descends from the mensural notation of medieval and Renaissance music, in which it was at times among the shorter values; as note values successively halved in importance, it became the reference unit from which other durations (minim, crotchet, quaver) are named as fractions. A whole-note rest, a small rectangle hanging from a staff line, likewise fills an entire measure in most meters and is used as a full-measure rest regardless of the actual time signature. In figured-bass and drum notation, and in some early-music contexts, whole notes retain specialised uses.
