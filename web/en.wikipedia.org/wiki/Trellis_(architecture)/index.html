Trellis (architecture)

A garden trellis supporting climbing plants.

A trellis (from Latin trichilla) is an architectural structure, usually made from an open framework or lattice (crossbars) of interwoven or intersecting pieces of wood, bamboo, metal or plastic. The trellis is most often made to support climbing plants in a garden. It can also be a decorative element of a larger structure such as a pergola, gazebo or pavilion.

Uses

Trellises are used as supports for vines, roses, peas and other climbing plants, allowing them to grow vertically and maximizing sunlight and air circulation. Flat trellises can be fixed to a wall or fence, while freestanding panels divide garden spaces and provide privacy screening. In viticulture, trellis systems — such as vertical shoot positioning — train grapevines to optimize fruit quality and ease of harvest. Trellis panels are also used in buildings as sun-shading devices and, increasingly, as the framework for green walls and facades.

History

Lattice work for training vines dates to antiquity; trellises appear in Egyptian tomb paintings and were common in Roman gardens. In French formal gardens, elaborate wooden treillage formed galleries, pavilions and trompe-l'oeil perspectives. The word also gives rise to the heraldic term "trellisé," and trellises inspired the name of the trellis drainage pattern in geology and the trellis graph in computer architecture.
