Chart of accounts

A chart of accounts (COA) is a complete list of every account used by an organisation in its general ledger. It provides the organising structure of a business's bookkeeping: each financial transaction is recorded in one or more of the accounts in the chart, and financial statements such as the balance sheet and income statement are drawn up by summarising the balances of groups of accounts. The chart is typically arranged by account type and identified by account numbers or codes.

Structure of a typical chart of accounts

Structure

Accounts in a chart of accounts are usually grouped in the order in which they appear in the financial statements: assets first, then liabilities, then equity (capital), then revenue (income) accounts, and finally expense accounts. A common numbering scheme assigns ranges of numbers to each category — for example 1000–1999 for assets, 2000–2999 for liabilities, 3000–3999 for equity, 4000–4999 for income and 5000 and above for expenses — leaving gaps so that new accounts can be inserted in the right place. Larger organisations use multi-segment codes that also encode departments, locations or projects.

Standard charts

Some countries prescribe standard charts of accounts by law. France's Plan Comptable Général and Germany's standard charts (such as SKR03 and SKR04) are well-known examples used for statutory reporting and tax purposes, while in countries such as the United States and the United Kingdom each business designs its own chart, although accounting software packages supply common templates. A well-designed chart of accounts balances detail with simplicity: it must capture everything needed for management reporting and compliance without becoming unwieldy.
