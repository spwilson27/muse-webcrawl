Claude Mydorge

Claude Mydorge (1585 – July 1647), also known as "Mydorge" or by his Latin name "Claudius Mydorgius", was a French mathematician who worked on geometry and optics. He was treasurer of Amiens and later of the généralité of Tours, which gave him the leisure to pursue science.

Mathematics

Mydorge's principal work, Prodromi catoptricorum et dioptricorum (1631, expanded 1639), treated conic sections and their applications to optics. He also edited and reissued the Conics of Apollonius of Perga, and examined the classic problem of doubling the cube.

Connections

He was a friend and correspondent of René Descartes and Étienne Pascal, and a member of the circle around Marin Mersenne; tradition credits the young Blaise Pascal's attendance at such meetings as formative for his mathematical development.
