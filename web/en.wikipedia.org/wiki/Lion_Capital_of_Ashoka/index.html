Lion Capital of Ashoka

Lion Capital of Ashoka
Material: Polished sandstone
Created: c. 250 BCE
Present location: Sarnath Museum, Uttar Pradesh, India

The Lion Capital of Ashoka is a sculpture of four Asiatic lions standing back to back, on an elaborate base that includes other animals. A graphic representation of it was adopted as the official Emblem of India in 1950. It was originally placed on the top of the Ashoka pillar at the important Buddhist site of Sarnath by the Emperor Ashoka, in about 250 BCE during his rule over the Maurya Empire.

Description

The capital is carved from a single block of polished sandstone and stands about 2.15 metres (7 feet) high. Four lions stand back to back atop a cylindrical abacus decorated in relief with an elephant, a galloping horse, a bull and a lion, separated by intervening 24-spoked wheels (chakras). The abacus rests on a bell-shaped lotus base. The lions symbolise power, courage and confidence, while the animals on the abacus are associated with the four phases of the Buddha's life and the four cardinal directions.

Discovery

The pillar and capital were excavated in the early 20th century at Sarnath, the site of the Buddha's first sermon. The capital, one of the finest examples of Mauryan polish and sculptural craftsmanship, is preserved in the Sarnath Museum, while the broken pillar shaft remains at the site.

Emblem of India

On 26 January 1950, the day India became a republic, the Lion Capital was adopted as the national emblem. In the official adaptation, only three lions are visible, the fourth being hidden from view; the abacus in profile shows the chakra at the centre with a bull and a horse, above the motto Satyameva Jayate ("Truth alone triumphs") in Devanagari script. The emblem also appears on Indian currency, including the two-rupee coin and banknotes.
