Canadian football

Canadian football (French: football canadien) is a sport played in Canada in which two teams of 12 players each compete for territorial control of a field of play 110 yards (101 m) long and 65 yards (59 m) wide, attempting to advance a pointed oval-shaped ball into the opposing team's end zone. It is closely related to American football, from which it descends, but retains distinctive rules developed in Canada, including the larger field, 12 players per side, three downs to advance ten yards, unlimited backfield motion, and the single point (or "rouge").

History

Canadian football evolved from rugby football brought to Canada by British immigrants and soldiers in the 1860s. The first documented game took place at the University of Toronto in 1861. The Canadian Rugby Union was founded in 1884, and the sport gradually diverged from rugby with the introduction of the line of scrimmage and down system. The Grey Cup, donated in 1909 by the Earl Grey, became the championship trophy, and the professional Canadian Football League (CFL) was formed in 1958.

Rules differences

  • The field is larger (110 × 65 yards) with 20-yard end zones.
  • Twelve players per side, rather than eleven.
  • Three downs instead of four to gain ten yards.
  • The defence must line up one yard off the ball; offensive backs may be in motion toward the line of scrimmage at the snap.
  • A single point (rouge) is awarded when the ball is kicked into the end zone and not returned out.
  • No fair catch; punt returners are protected by a five-yard halo.

Structure of the sport

The CFL is the professional league, with nine teams competing for the Grey Cup, one of the largest single-game sporting events in Canada. University football (U Sports) culminates in the Vanier Cup, junior football in the Canadian Junior Football League, and the sport is governed nationally by Football Canada.
