Académie française

Formation22 February 1635
FounderCardinal Richelieu
TypeLearned society; language regulator
HeadquartersInstitut de France, Paris
Membership40 "Immortels"

The Académie française (French Academy) is the pre-eminent French council for matters pertaining to the French language. Officially established in 1635 by Cardinal Richelieu, the chief minister to King Louis XIII, it is the oldest of the five academies of the Institut de France and acts as an official authority on the language, publishing its official dictionary.

Membership

The Academy consists of forty members known informally as les immortels ("the Immortals"), after the Academy's motto, À l'immortalité ("To immortality"), inscribed on its official seal. New members are elected by the existing members and hold their seat (fauteuil) for life; elections to a vacant seat are major events in French intellectual life. Immortals wear the court dress known as the habit vert — an elaborately embroidered green uniform with a ceremonial sword — at formal sessions under the dome of the Institut.

Functions

The Academy publishes the Dictionnaire de l'Académie française, first issued in 1694, and issues opinions on usage, spelling reforms and proposed French terms to replace foreign borrowings. It also awards numerous literary and francophonie prizes, including the Grand prix de littérature and the Grand prix de la francophonie, and administers charitable foundations.
