Poincaré half-plane model

The Poincaré half-plane model is a model of two-dimensional hyperbolic geometry in which points are the points of the upper half-plane {(x, y) : y > 0} and lines are either vertical rays or semicircles perpendicular to the boundary (the x-axis). Named for Henri Poincaré, the model is conformal: angles measured in the model equal the true hyperbolic angles.

Metric

The distance element is ds = √(dx² + dy²) / y, so distances grow as points approach the boundary line, which represents infinity. The distance between two points can be computed from a formula involving the inverse hyperbolic cosine of a cross-ratio-like expression. Geodesics are exactly the semicircles and vertical lines described above.

Isometries

The orientation-preserving isometries of the half-plane model are the Möbius transformations with real coefficients and determinant 1 — the group PSL(2, ℝ) acting by fractional linear transformations. This makes the model especially convenient for number theory and the study of modular forms, since the modular group PSL(2, ℤ) acts naturally on it.

Relation to other models

The Cayley transform maps the half-plane model to the Poincaré disk model; stereographic projection relates it to the hyperboloid model. The half-plane model is widely used in complex analysis because the upper half-plane is a standard domain for analytic functions.
