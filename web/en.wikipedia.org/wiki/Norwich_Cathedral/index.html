Norwich Cathedral

Norwich Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of the Holy and Undivided Trinity, is an Anglican cathedral in Norwich, Norfolk, England. Founded in 1096 by Herbert de Losinga, the first Bishop of Norwich, it is built largely of flint and mortar faced with pale Caen limestone and is regarded as one of the finest and most complete Norman cathedrals in England.

Architecture

The cathedral retains most of its original Norman plan: a long nave of fourteen bays, transepts, and an apsidal east end with an ambulatory and radiating chapels, unique in England. The stone spire, at 96 metres the second tallest in the country after Salisbury, was completed in 1480 after earlier spires were struck by lightning and blown down. The cloisters are the largest monastic cloisters in England, with over a thousand carved roof bosses; the nave vault bosses likewise form one of the great ensembles of medieval sculpture, depicting biblical stories in sequence. The cathedral precinct, the Close, contains the medieval gates of Erpingham and St Ethelbert and many historic houses.

History

Construction began in 1096, and the monks of the Benedictine priory moved in around 1100; the church was consecrated in 1101 though completed later. After the Reformation the monastery was dissolved and the cathedral refounded with a dean and chapter. The mystic Julian of Norwich lived as an anchoress at St Julian's Church in the city during the cathedral's later medieval period. Today the cathedral serves the Diocese of Norwich and hosts a visitor and education centre opened in 2010, which won architectural awards.
