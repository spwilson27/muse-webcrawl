Amado Nervo

Amado Nervo, born Juan Crisóstomo Ruiz de Nervo (27 August 1870 – 24 May 1919), was a Mexican poet, journalist, and diplomat, one of the most beloved figures of Spanish-American modernismo. His poetry, marked by themes of love, spirituality, melancholy, and mysticism, was immensely popular with the reading public of his time and remains widely read in the Spanish-speaking world.

Life

Nervo was born in Tepic, in the state of Nayarit, Mexico. He studied at a Catholic seminary in Zamora, Michoacán, with the intention of becoming a priest, but abandoned religious studies and moved into journalism in Mazatlán and then Mexico City, where he worked for newspapers such as El Universal and co-founded the modernist Revista Moderna. He travelled in Europe, living for a time in Paris, where he frequented modernist circles and met Rubén Darío.

In 1905 he entered the Mexican diplomatic service, serving in Madrid, where he lived for many years. The death of his beloved partner, Ana Cecilia Luisa Daillez, in 1912 — his "amada inmóvil" ("immobile beloved") — inspired his most famous posthumous collection, La amada inmóvil (published 1922). In 1918 he was appointed Mexico's minister plenipotentiary to Argentina and Uruguay and moved to Montevideo.

Death and works

Nervo died in Montevideo, Uruguay, on 24 May 1919. His body was returned to Mexico aboard the Uruguayan cruiser Uruguay with full honours, stopping in several countries where tributes were held. His major collections include Perlas negras (1898), Místicas (1898), Los jardines interiores (1905), En voz baja (1909), Serenidad (1914), Elevación (1917), and El estanque de los lotos (1919). His best-known poem, "En paz" ("At Peace"), written near the end of his life, begins "Muy cerca de mi ocaso, yo te bendigo, Vida" ("Very near my setting sun, I bless you, Life").
