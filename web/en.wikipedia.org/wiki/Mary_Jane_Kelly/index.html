Mary Jane Kelly

Mary Jane Kelly
Born: c. 1863, Limerick, Ireland
Died: November 9, 1888 (aged about 25), Spitalfields, London, England
Cause of death: Murder
Known for: Fifth and final canonical victim of Jack the Ripper

Mary Jane Kelly (c. 1863 – November 9, 1888) is generally considered the fifth and final canonical victim of the unidentified serial killer known as Jack the Ripper, who murdered at least five women in the Whitechapel district of London in 1888. Kelly's murder was the most mutilated of the series.

Life

Kelly was reportedly born in Limerick, Ireland, and moved to Wales and then London, where she worked in prostitution in the East End. She lived at 13 Miller's Court, off Dorset Street in Spitalfields, with a partner, Joseph Barnett. The only known photograph of her was taken by police after her death.

Death

On the morning of November 9, 1888, Kelly's landlord sent an assistant to collect rent; finding her door locked, he looked through the window and saw her body on the bed. Unlike the other canonical victims, who were killed outdoors, Kelly was murdered indoors, giving her killer time and privacy. Police surgeon Thomas Bond estimated the time of death at around 3:30 a.m.

Investigation and identity

No one was ever charged with Kelly's murder. Inquests recorded a verdict of wilful murder against persons unknown. Later writers have proposed various theories about her identity and the identity of her killer, but none has been substantiated.
