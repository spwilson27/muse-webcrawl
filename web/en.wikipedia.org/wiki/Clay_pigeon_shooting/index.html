Clay pigeon shooting

Clay pigeon shooting, also known as clay target shooting, is the art of shooting a firearm at special flying targets known as clay pigeons or clay targets. The targets are discs, traditionally made of pitch and chalk, launched from machines called traps. The sport developed in the late nineteenth century as a humane substitute for shooting live pigeons, which had been a popular gambling sport.

Disciplines

The principal disciplines include trap shooting, in which targets are launched away from the shooter from a single trap house; skeet shooting, in which targets cross between two houses at either side of a semicircular field; and sporting clays, in which targets are presented at varied angles and speeds over a course designed to simulate game shooting. Trap and skeet are Olympic events governed internationally by the ISSF, while sporting clays is governed by bodies such as FITASC.

Equipment

Shotguns — most commonly over-and-under double-barrelled guns in 12 gauge — are used with small shot cartridges. Shooters typically wear eye and ear protection. Targets are usually 110 mm in diameter in international "frangible" form, designed to shatter visibly when struck.

History

Live pigeon shooting was an Olympic event in 1900, but public disapproval and the invention of reliable clay targets and throwing machines led to its replacement. A standardised clay target was developed in the United States in the 1880s by George Ligowsky, and the sport spread rapidly through Britain, America, and the Commonwealth.
