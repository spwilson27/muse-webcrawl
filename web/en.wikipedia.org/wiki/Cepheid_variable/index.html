Overview

A Cepheid variable is a type of variable star that pulsates radially, varying in both diameter and temperature and producing changes in brightness with a well-defined stable period and amplitude. Classical Cepheids are young, massive supergiants; the separate Type II Cepheids (including W Virginis stars) are older, low-mass stars.

The cosmic distance ladder

In 1908–1912 Henrietta Swan Leavitt discovered that Cepheid luminosity correlates tightly with pulsation period (the Leavitt law), making Cepheids standard candles: measure the period, infer the true brightness, compare with apparent brightness, and the distance follows. Edwin Hubble's 1923–24 identification of Cepheids in the Andromeda "nebula" proved it a separate galaxy and founded extragalactic astronomy. Cepheids still anchor the cosmic distance ladder used to measure the Hubble constant — and its current tension.
