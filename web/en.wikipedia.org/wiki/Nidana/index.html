Nidana

Nidāna (Sanskrit: निदान, Pali: nidāna) is a Sanskrit and Pali word meaning "cause," "motivation," or "occasion." In Buddhism it is most often used in reference to the Twelve Nidānas (Sanskrit: dvādaśa-nidāna), the twelve links of dependent origination ( pratītyasamutpāda ) that describe how ignorance and craving perpetuate the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra) and suffering (dukkha).

The twelve nidānas

The twelve links, presented in sequence, are:

  1. Avidyā — ignorance
  2. Saṃskāra — volitional formations
  3. Vijñāna — consciousness
  4. Nāmarūpa — name and form (mind and body)
  5. Ṣaḍāyatana — the six sense bases
  6. Sparśa — contact
  7. Vedanā — feeling
  8. Tṛṣṇā — craving
  9. Upādāna — clinging
  10. Bhava — becoming
  11. Jāti — birth
  12. Jarāmaraṇa — aging and death

Each link arises dependent on the preceding one; by ending ignorance, the chain can be broken and liberation attained. In the Tibetan Buddhist bhavacakra ("wheel of life"), the twelve nidānas are depicted as images around the outer rim of the wheel. In the Pali Canon, discourses explaining or named for the nidānas are collected in the Nidana Samyutta of the Samyutta Nikaya.

Other uses

The term nidana is also used in other Indian religions, and in Ayurveda it refers to the cause or diagnosis of disease. As a literary term it can denote an introductory tale or the occasion for a narration.
