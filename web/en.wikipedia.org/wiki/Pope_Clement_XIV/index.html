Pope Clement XIV (Latin: Clemens XIV; Italian: Clemente XIV; 31 October 1705 – 22 September 1774), born Giovanni Vincenzo Antonio Ganganelli, was head of the Catholic Church from 19 May 1769 until his death in 1774. He is best remembered for suppressing the Society of Jesus (Jesuits) in 1773.

Early life

Ganganelli was born in Santarcangelo di Romagna in the Papal States and joined the Order of Friars Minor Conventual (Franciscans) as a young man. He became a respected theologian and teacher, and was created cardinal by Clement XIII in 1759.

Pontificate

Clement was elected after a long conclave overshadowed by the question of the Jesuits, whom the Bourbon courts of France, Spain and Naples had expelled from their territories and whose suppression they demanded. After years of delay and negotiation, on 21 July 1773 Clement issued the brief Dominus ac Redemptor, dissolving the Society of Jesus throughout the Church. The order survived only in Prussia and Russia, whose rulers refused to promulgate the brief.

Domestically, Clement improved the administration of the Papal States, encouraged agriculture and trade, and founded the Museo Pio-Clementino in the Vatican, which became one of the world's great collections of classical antiquities. His later years were marked by ill health and anxiety; unfounded rumours claimed he had been poisoned by the Jesuits.

Legacy

Clement XIV's suppression of the Jesuits, undertaken under intense diplomatic pressure, remained controversial; the Society was restored universally by Pius VII in 1814.
