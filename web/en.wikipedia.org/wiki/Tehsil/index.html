Tehsil

A tehsil (also spelled tahsil, tahasil, or taluka in some regions) is an administrative unit of local government in Pakistan and India. It consists of a town or city that serves as its headquarters, together with the surrounding villages and rural territory. Tehsils are subdivisions of a district and are in turn divided into smaller units such as union councils (in Pakistan) or village panchayats (in India).

Terminology

The word derives from the Arabic taḥṣīl, meaning "collection" (of revenue), reflecting the unit's historical role in land-revenue administration. Equivalent or near-equivalent terms used across South Asia include taluka (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, and Goa), mandal (Andhra Pradesh and Telangana), and circle (in parts of the north-east). In Bangladesh the corresponding unit is the upazila.

Administration

A tehsil is headed by a tehsildar (tahsildar), a revenue officer responsible for land records, collection of land revenue, and certain magisterial functions. The tehsildar is assisted by naib tehsildars and by field staff such as patwaris or village accountants, who maintain records of land ownership and crops. The tehsil office is typically the lowest level at which citizens obtain certified land records, domicile and income certificates, and similar documents.

Role

Beyond revenue work, tehsils serve as units for conducting elections, census operations, disaster relief, and the delivery of development programmes. Because they are closer to citizens than district headquarters, tehsil offices are a primary interface between rural populations and the state.
