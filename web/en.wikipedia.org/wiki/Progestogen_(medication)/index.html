Progestogen (medication)

A progestogen, also referred to as a progestagen, gestagen, or gestogen, is a type of medication which produces effects similar to those of the natural female sex hormone progesterone in the body. Progestogens are used most commonly in hormonal birth control and menopausal hormone therapy. They can also be used in the treatment of gynecological conditions, to support fertility and pregnancy, to lower sex hormone levels for various purposes, and for other indications.

Types

Progestogens include the natural hormone progesterone itself and a large family of synthetic compounds known as progestins, such as medroxyprogesterone acetate, norethisterone, levonorgestrel, and drospirenone. Synthetic progestogens differ in their potency and in their secondary activity at other hormone receptors, including androgen, estrogen, glucocorticoid, and mineralocorticoid receptors.

Medical uses

Progestogens are components of most combined oral contraceptives and of progestin-only methods such as implants, injections, and hormonal intrauterine devices. In menopausal hormone therapy they are given together with an estrogen to protect the endometrium from unopposed estrogen stimulation. They are also used for menstrual disorders, endometriosis, and prevention of preterm birth in some settings.
