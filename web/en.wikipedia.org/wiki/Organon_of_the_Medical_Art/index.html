Organon of the Medical Art

The Organon of the Medical Art (German: Organon der Heilkunst) is the foundational text of homeopathy, written by the German physician Samuel Hahnemann. First published in 1810, it sets out Hahnemann's system of medicine in a series of numbered aphorisms. Hahnemann revised the work throughout his life, producing six editions; the sixth, completed in 1842, was published only posthumously in 1921.

Contents

The Organon opens with the declaration that "the physician's high and only mission is to restore the sick to health, to cure, as it is termed". It presents the central doctrines of homeopathy: the law of similars (similia similibus curentur, "like cures like"), derived from Hahnemann's experiments with cinchona bark; the use of a single remedy at a time; administration of the minimum dose; and the preparation of remedies by serial dilution and succussion (vigorous shaking), which Hahnemann called potentisation.

Later editions introduced the theory of miasms — chronic disease dispositions, principally psora, sycosis and syphilis — and the concept of a "vital force" whose disturbance produces disease and whose restoration produces cure. The sixth edition added instructions on the LM (fifty-millesimal) potency scale.

Editions and translations

The first edition appeared in Dresden in 1810 as Organon der rationellen Heilkunde; from the second edition (1819) the title became Organon der Heilkunst. The fifth edition (1833) is the most widely reprinted German version. English translations include those of Robert Ellis Dudgeon (from the fifth edition) and, for the sixth edition, William Boericke's 1922 translation, revised by later editors.

Reception

Within homeopathy the Organon is treated as the authoritative exposition of the system, and Hahnemann's followers organised practice around its aphorisms. Mainstream medicine regards homeopathy's claims — particularly that extreme dilutions beyond Avogadro's limit retain therapeutic effect — as unsupported by scientific evidence, and the vital-force and miasm theories as obsolete; the text is nonetheless studied as an important document in the history of medicine and of alternative healing movements.
