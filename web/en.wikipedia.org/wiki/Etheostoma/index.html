Etheostoma is a genus of small freshwater fish in the family Percidae, native to North America. Its members are known as darters, a name they share with other genera in the subfamily Etheostomatinae. With more than 150 recognised species, Etheostoma is one of the most species-rich genera of freshwater fishes in North America, and new species continue to be described.

Description

Darters are small, bottom-dwelling fishes, typically 3–10 cm long, that lack a swim bladder and rest on the substrate of streams and rivers, moving in short, quick darts. Males of many species develop brilliant breeding coloration — vivid reds, oranges, blues and greens — during the spring spawning season, making them among the most colourful temperate freshwater fishes. Well-known species include the rainbow darter (Etheostoma caeruleum), the johnny darter (E. nigrum), the orangethroat darter (E. spectabile) and the endangered snail darter (E. perlongum group, formerly Percina tanasi relatives).

Distribution and habitat

The genus is concentrated in the river systems of eastern and central North America, especially the Mississippi River basin and the drainages of the southeastern United States, a global hotspot of freshwater fish diversity. Most species inhabit riffles and runs of clear streams, feeding on insect larvae and other invertebrates.

Conservation

Because many species have tiny ranges confined to single river systems, darters are vulnerable to damming, siltation and pollution, and a number of species are listed as threatened or endangered.
