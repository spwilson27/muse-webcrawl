Juan de Fuca Plate

The Juan de Fuca Plate is a small tectonic plate in the northeastern Pacific Ocean, lying offshore of the Pacific Northwest between the Pacific Plate to the west and the North American Plate to the east. It is a remnant of the ancient Farallon Plate and is named after the Strait of Juan de Fuca.

Geology

New crust is created at the Juan de Fuca Ridge on the plate's western boundary, while its eastern edge subducts beneath North America along the Cascadia subduction zone, fuelling the volcanoes of the Cascade Range. The plate is bounded to the north by the Nootka Fault and the Explorer Plate, and to the south by the Blanco Fracture Zone and the Gorda Plate.

Seismic hazard

The subducting slab has produced great megathrust earthquakes, most recently the magnitude ~9 Cascadia earthquake of January 26, 1700, which generated a tsunami recorded in Japan. Smaller crustal earthquakes within the slab, such as the 2001 Nisqually earthquake, also affect the region.
