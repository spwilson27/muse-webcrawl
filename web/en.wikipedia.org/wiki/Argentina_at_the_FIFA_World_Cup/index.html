This is a record of Argentina's results at the FIFA World Cup. Argentina is one of the most successful national football teams in the world, having won three World Cups: in 1978, 1986 and 2022. Argentina has been runners-up three times: in 1930, 1990 and 2014. The team was present in all but four of the World Cups, being behind only Brazil and Germany in number of appearances.

Overview

Argentina played in the first ever World Cup final in 1930, losing 4–2 to hosts Uruguay. The team waited 48 years for its first title, won on home soil in 1978 under coach César Luis Menotti, defeating the Netherlands 3–1 after extra time in the final. The 1986 tournament in Mexico belonged to Diego Maradona, whose performances — including the "Hand of God" goal and the "Goal of the Century" against England in the quarter-finals — carried Argentina to a second title, beating West Germany 3–2 in the final.

After losing the 1990 final to West Germany and the 2014 final to Germany, Argentina, captained by Lionel Messi, won its third World Cup in Qatar in 2022, defeating France on penalties after a 3–3 draw in one of the most acclaimed finals in tournament history.

Records

Lionel Messi holds the record for most World Cup appearances (26) and contested five tournaments between 2006 and 2022. Gabriel Batistuta is the only player to score hat-tricks at two different World Cups (1994 and 1998). Just Fontaine aside, Guillermo Stábile's eight goals in 1930 long stood among the highest single-tournament tallies.
