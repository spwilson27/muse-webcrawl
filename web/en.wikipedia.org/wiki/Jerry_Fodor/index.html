Jerry Alan Fodor (22 April 1935 – 29 November 2017) was an American philosopher of mind and cognitive scientist, widely regarded as one of the founders of the computational theory of mind. Over a career at MIT, the City University of New York and Rutgers, he argued that thinking takes place in an innate symbolic system — a "language of thought" — and that much of the mind is composed of specialised, encapsulated modules.

Career

Fodor studied at Columbia and Princeton, where he wrote his doctorate under Hilary Putnam. He taught at MIT from 1963, where with Noam Chomsky and others he helped shape the emerging cognitive revolution against behaviourism, before moving to CUNY and then to Rutgers in 1988, where he remained until retirement.

Main ideas

In The Language of Thought (1975), Fodor proposed that mental processes operate over structured mental representations with a combinatorial syntax and semantics, just as computers operate over symbols. In The Modularity of Mind (1983) he argued that perceptual input systems are domain-specific, fast, mandatory and informationally encapsulated — a thesis that influenced evolutionary psychology. He also defended a robust nativism about concepts, representational realism, and, with Zenon Pylyshyn, criticised connectionism for failing to explain the systematicity of thought.

Criticisms of Darwinism

With Massimo Piattelli-Palmarini, Fodor co-authored What Darwin Got Wrong (2010), arguing that natural selection cannot explain the distribution of traits in evolution. The book provoked strong rebuttals from biologists and philosophers, and Fodor himself disclaimed any sympathy for intelligent design.
