Tafsir al-Tabari

The Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān ("The Comprehensive Exposition concerning the Interpretation of the Verses of the Qur'an"), commonly known as the Tafsir al-Tabari, is a classical Sunni tafsir (exegesis of the Qur'an) written by the Persian scholar Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari (838–923 CE). It is among the earliest complete surviving commentaries on the Qur'an and remains one of the most influential works in the tafsir tradition.

Content and method

Al-Tabari's commentary proceeds verse by verse through the entire Qur'an. For each verse or passage he collects and quotes earlier interpretations attributed to the Companions of the Prophet, their successors, and early authorities, giving the chains of transmission (isnad) for each report. He also discusses grammar, lexicography, variant readings (qira'at), abrogation, and legal implications, and frequently states his own preferred interpretation after weighing the transmitted reports. The work is the outstanding example of tafsir bi'l-ma'thur — exegesis based on transmitted tradition — and preserves a vast amount of early material that would otherwise be lost.

Influence

Later commentators, from Ibn Kathir to al-Suyuti, drew heavily on al-Tabari. Ibn Kathir's famous commentary is in large part an abridgement and sifting of al-Tabari's material. The Tafsir also serves historians as a repository of early Islamic tradition, complementing al-Tabari's great chronicle, the History of the Prophets and Kings. The full Arabic text runs to many volumes in modern editions; substantial portions have been translated into English in the SUNY Press translation project of al-Tabari's works.
