Cahiers de doléances

Date1789
Commissioned byLouis XVI
PurposeGrievances and demands for the Estates-General
NumberTens of thousands drafted

The cahiers de doléances (French for "books of grievances"; singular cahier de doléances) were the lists of complaints and demands that the three Estates of France — clergy, nobility, and Third Estate — drew up in the spring of 1789, on the orders of King Louis XVI, to be presented to the Estates-General when it assembled at Versailles in May of that year.

Background

Facing financial collapse, Louis XVI summoned the Estates-General for the first time since 1614. Following custom, every community was invited to record its grievances. Parishes drafted their own cahiers, guilds and corporations drafted theirs, and these were consolidated at district level before being carried to Versailles by the elected deputies. Tens of thousands of these documents were produced, and a very large number survive in French archives.

Contents

The cahiers vary widely. Peasant parishes complained above all of the weight of taxation (the taille, the hated salt tax or gabelle), feudal dues owed to seigneurs, tithes, game laws that protected lords' hunting, and the corvée labour duty on roads. Bourgeois cahiers demanded equality of taxation, careers open to talent, freedom of the press, and regular meetings of the Estates-General. Many cahiers of all three orders converged on the need for a constitution, consent to taxation, and individual liberties, while still expressing loyalty to the king. Noble and clerical cahiers were often surprisingly liberal on fiscal questions, though protective of their own status and the Church's position.

Significance

The cahiers are among the richest sources for historians of the French Revolution, offering a near-national survey of grievances and expectations on the eve of 1789. Their drafting was itself a political education for millions of French people. The reforming hopes they expressed helped shape the early work of the National Assembly, including the August Decrees abolishing feudal privileges and the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen.
