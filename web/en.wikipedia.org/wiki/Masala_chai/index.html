Masala chai

Masala chai (Hindi: मसाला चाय, "spiced tea") is a flavoured tea beverage made by brewing black tea with a mixture of aromatic herbs and spices, milk, and sweetener. It originated in South Asia and is widely consumed in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and the diaspora, as well as in cafés worldwide.

Preparation

Traditional masala chai is made by simmering water, strong black tea (typically Assam or CTC tea), milk, sugar, and a spice blend — commonly green cardamom, ginger, cinnamon, cloves, and black pepper, sometimes with star anise, fennel seed, or nutmeg — and straining the result. Recipes vary by region and household; some use a pre-mixed masala powder. In India, chai wallahs serve it in small glasses or kulhads (clay cups) at roadside stalls.

History

Tea cultivation was promoted in India under British rule, but chai-drinking became widespread in the 20th century as milk and spices were added and the price of tea fell. Today masala chai is a staple of South Asian daily life and a popular item in coffeehouse menus worldwide, where it is often served as a "chai latte" in some markets.
