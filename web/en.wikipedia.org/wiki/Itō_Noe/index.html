Itō Noe

Itō Noe (21 January 1895 – 16 September 1923) was a Japanese anarchist, social critic, author, and feminist. She edited the pioneering feminist journal Seitō and was murdered by military police in the Amakasu Incident following the Great Kantō earthquake.

Born21 January 1895, Fukuoka, Japan
Died16 September 1923, Tokyo, Japan
OccupationWriter, editor, activist
PartnerŌsugi Sakae

Life and work

Itō fled an arranged marriage to Tokyo, where she joined the Bluestockings (Seitō-sha) and became editor of their journal in 1915, publishing translations of Emma Goldman and her own essays on women's liberation, abortion, and free love. She later lived with the anarchist Ōsugi Sakae.

Death

In the chaos after the 1923 earthquake, Itō, Ōsugi, and Ōsugi's six-year-old nephew were arrested and beaten to death by a squad led by Lieutenant Amakasu Masahiko. The crime became a notorious symbol of pre-war state repression.
