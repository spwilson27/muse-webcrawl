Indonesian occupation of East Timor

The Indonesian occupation of East Timor began with Indonesia's invasion on 7 December 1975 and lasted until 1999, when East Timor voted for independence in a UN-sponsored referendum and subsequently became the independent state of Timor-Leste in 2002. The occupation was marked by widespread violence and a death toll estimated at between 100,000 and 180,000 people from conflict, famine and disease.

Background and invasion

East Timor had been a Portuguese colony until 1975. As Portugal withdrew, the Fretilin party declared independence on 28 November 1975; nine days later Indonesian forces invaded under Operation Seroja, citing anti-communist grounds and with tacit support from Western governments. Indonesia formally annexed the territory as its "27th province", a move never recognised by the United Nations.

Resistance and violence

The Falintil guerrilla army resisted from the mountains while a clandestine network operated in towns and abroad. Notable atrocities include the 1991 Santa Cruz massacre in Dili, filmed and broadcast worldwide, which galvanised international opinion. The 1996 Nobel Peace Prize awarded to Bishop Carlos Belo and José Ramos-Horta further raised the cause's profile.

Referendum and independence

After the fall of Suharto, Indonesia agreed to a referendum on 30 August 1999, in which 78.5% rejected autonomy within Indonesia. Pro-integration militias, backed by elements of the Indonesian military, responded with a scorched-earth campaign that destroyed much of the country's infrastructure before an Australian-led INTERFET force restored order. A UN administration (UNTAET) then governed until independence on 20 May 2002.
