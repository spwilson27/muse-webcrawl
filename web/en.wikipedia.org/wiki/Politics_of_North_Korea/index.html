Politics of North Korea

The politics of North Korea (officially the Democratic People's Republic of Korea) take place within the framework of the official state philosophy of Juche, a concept created by Hwang Jang-yop and later attributed to Kim Il Sung. In practice, North Korea is a totalitarian dictatorship ruled by the Kim dynasty through the Workers' Party of Korea.

Structure

The Supreme People's Assembly is formally the highest organ of state power, but real authority rests with the leader and the party. The State Affairs Commission, chaired by the leader, is the supreme policy-oriented leadership body. The country operates under a system combining party, army and state institutions, formerly guided by the Songun (military-first) policy.

Ideology

Juche stresses political, economic and military self-reliance. Society is classified by the hereditary songbun status system. Elections are held but feature single approved candidates, and the leading role of the Kim family is enshrined in the Ten Principles for the Establishment of a Monolithic Ideological System.
