Peripheral

A peripheral device, or simply peripheral, is an auxiliary device used to put information into and get information out of a computer. The term usually refers to devices that are external to the computer case, such as a keyboard, mouse, printer, scanner, monitor, or external storage drive, though some peripherals are installed internally.

Types

Peripherals are commonly divided into input devices, which send data to the computer (keyboards, mice, scanners, microphones, webcams); output devices, which receive data from the computer (monitors, printers, speakers, headphones); and input/output devices, which do both (touchscreens, external hard drives, USB flash drives, network adapters).

Connection

Modern peripherals connect through standard interfaces such as USB, HDMI, DisplayPort, and wireless protocols including Bluetooth and Wi-Fi. Earlier computers used serial ports, parallel ports, PS/2 connectors, and proprietary buses. Hot swapping — connecting or disconnecting a device while the system runs — is supported by USB and Thunderbolt.
