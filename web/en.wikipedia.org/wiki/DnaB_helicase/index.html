DnaB helicase

DnaB helicase is an enzyme in bacteria which opens the replication fork during DNA replication. It is the primary replicative helicase in Escherichia coli and many other bacteria. DnaB forms a hexameric ring that encircles single-stranded DNA and translocates in the 5′ to 3′ direction, unwinding the parental duplex ahead of the replisome at the expense of ATP hydrolysis.

DnaB assembles as a hexamer around the lagging-strand template.

Mechanism

Initially loaded onto the origin of replication by the loader protein DnaC, DnaB unwinds the double helix and recruits the primase DnaG, forming the primosome. The helicase's activity greatly stimulates primase, and the interaction between DnaB and the DNA polymerase III holoenzyme coordinates leading- and lagging-strand synthesis.

Homologues

Although functionally analogous proteins exist in eukaryotes (the MCM2-7 complex) and archaea, DnaB belongs to a distinct superfamily of helicases. Related bacterial proteins include the phage T7 gene 4 protein and the T4 gp41 helicase.
