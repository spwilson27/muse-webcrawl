Substance of very high concern

A substance of very high concern (SVHC) is a chemical substance (or group of substances) proposed for authorisation under the European Union's REACH Regulation. Listing a substance as an SVHC by the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) is the first step in the authorisation or restriction process, intended eventually to control or phase out the most hazardous substances in the EU market.

Criteria

Under Article 57 of REACH, substances may be identified as SVHCs if they are: carcinogenic, mutagenic, or toxic for reproduction (CMR) in category 1A or 1B; persistent, bioaccumulative and toxic (PBT); very persistent and very bioaccumulative (vPvB); or of "equivalent level of concern" — a case-by-case category that has covered endocrine disruptors and respiratory sensitisers.

Candidate List and obligations

Identified SVHCs are added to the Candidate List, first published in October 2008 and updated roughly twice a year; it now contains well over 200 entries. Inclusion triggers duties: suppliers must inform customers and consumers about safe use, notify ECHA of SVHCs in articles above 0.1% weight by weight, and (since 2021) register such articles in the SCIP database under the Waste Framework Directive. Candidate List substances may subsequently be moved to the Authorisation List (Annex XIV), after which their use requires specific authorisation.
