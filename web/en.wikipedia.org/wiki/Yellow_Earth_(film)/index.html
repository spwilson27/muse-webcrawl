Yellow Earth (simplified Chinese: 黄土地; traditional Chinese: 黃土地; pinyin: Huáng tǔdì) is a 1984 Chinese drama film directed by Chen Kaige in his directorial debut, with cinematography by Zhang Yimou. Produced by the Guangxi Film Studio, it is widely regarded as the landmark first work of China's Fifth Generation of filmmakers and the film that announced the arrival of a new Chinese cinema to the world.

Plot

In 1939, during the Second Sino-Japanese War, a Communist soldier named Gu Qing is sent from Yan'an to a remote, impoverished village on the loess plateau of Shaanbei (northern Shaanxi) to collect folk songs for propaganda troupes. He lodges with a poor peasant widower and his children — the shy young daughter Cuiqiao, who sings haunting local songs, and her younger brother Hanhan. Cuiqiao, promised in an arranged marriage to an older man, is drawn to the world Gu Qing describes, where women in Yan'an choose their own husbands. Gu Qing leaves promising to return, but when he finally comes back it is too late: Cuiqiao has been married off and, fleeing, drowns attempting to cross the Yellow River. The film closes with Hanhan running toward the distant soldier through a crowd praying for rain.

Style and reception

Shot on location amid the yellow loess cliffs and the Yellow River, the film is noted for its sparse dialogue, long static takes, and compositions that dwarf the human figures within an immense, indifferent landscape — imagery interpreted as a meditation on the weight of feudal tradition and the limits of revolutionary salvation. Initially criticised by some officials at home, Yellow Earth won wide acclaim abroad, including the Silver Leopard at the Locarno International Film Festival in 1985, and is regularly listed among the greatest Chinese films ever made.
