Automotive Ethernet

Automotive Ethernet refers to the use of Ethernet-based networking technologies as the in-vehicle communication backbone in automobiles. Unlike conventional consumer Ethernet, automotive variants are adapted for the electrical, thermal and electromagnetic environment of a vehicle, typically running over a single unshielded twisted pair of wires to reduce weight and cost.

Standards

The technology began with Broadcom's BroadR-Reach physical layer, later standardized by the IEEE as 100BASE-T1 (802.3bw) and 1000BASE-T1 (802.3bp), and extended by multi-gigabit standards such as 10GBASE-T1 (802.3ch). Higher-layer protocols used with automotive Ethernet include SOME/IP for service-oriented communication, DoIP (Diagnostics over Internet Protocol), and time-sensitive networking (TSN) profiles that provide deterministic latency for driver-assistance and control functions.

Applications

Automotive Ethernet supports the high bandwidth demanded by cameras and sensors for advanced driver-assistance systems, infotainment, and zonal vehicle architectures, in which a central computer communicates with zone controllers over an Ethernet backbone rather than running dedicated wiring to every device. It complements and in some roles replaces older in-vehicle networks such as CAN, LIN, FlexRay and MOST.
