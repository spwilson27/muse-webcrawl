Elasticity (economics)

[Image: a rubber band being stretched, the everyday metaphor for elasticity]

In Economics, elasticity is a measure of how responsive one variable is to a change in another, expressed as a ratio of percentage changes. The most used is the price elasticity of demand — the percentage fall in quantity demanded for a 1% rise in Price — introduced into economics by Alfred Marshall.

Uses

Demand with elasticity greater than one is 'elastic' (luxuries, goods with substitutes); less than one, 'inelastic' (necessities, addictive goods, fuel in the short run). Elasticity determines who bears a tax (the less elastic side bears more), whether a price rise raises or lowers total revenue, and how monopolists mark up over cost. Companion measures include price elasticity of supply, income elasticity (distinguishing normal, inferior and luxury goods) and cross-price elasticity (substitutes positive, complements negative). Estimates from data guide competition cases, tax design and forecasting throughout applied Microeconomics.
