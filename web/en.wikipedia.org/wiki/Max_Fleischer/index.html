Max Fleischer (born Majer Fleischer; July 19, 1883 – September 11, 1972) was an American animator, inventor, film director and producer, and a pioneer in the development of the animated cartoon. He founded Fleischer Studios with his brother Dave and created the characters Betty Boop, Koko the Clown and Popeye the Sailor (in animated form), as well as bringing Superman to the screen.

Career

Born in Kraków, Fleischer emigrated with his family to New York City as a child. He worked as a commercial artist and cartoonist before patenting the rotoscope in 1915, a device that allowed animators to trace live-action film frame by frame to achieve realistic movement. With the Out of the Inkwell series (1918–1929), featuring Koko the Clown interacting with the live-action world, the Fleischer brothers established themselves as Walt Disney's chief rivals.

Fleischer Studios

In the sound era, Fleischer Studios produced the Talkartoons and introduced Betty Boop, whose flapper personality made her a star, and licensed E. C. Segar's Popeye, whose cartoons eventually surpassed Mickey Mouse in popularity. The studio made two feature films, Gulliver's Travels (1939) and Mr. Bug Goes to Town (1941), and the celebrated Superman theatrical shorts of 1941–43. Burdened by debt and conflict with distributor Paramount Pictures, the studio was absorbed by Paramount in 1942 and reorganised as Famous Studios; Fleischer later worked in television animation.
