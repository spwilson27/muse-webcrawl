Entity–relationship model

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The entity–relationship model (or ER model) describes interrelated things of interest in a specific domain of knowledge. A basic ER model is composed of entity types (which classify the things of interest) and specifies relationships that can exist between entities (instances of those entity types).

The model was developed by Peter Chen and published in his 1976 paper "The Entity-Relationship Model — Toward a Unified View of Data". It is widely used in database design, where an ER model is typically implemented as a relational database schema.

Components

  • Entities — objects or concepts about which data is stored, shown as rectangles in Chen notation.
  • Attributes — properties of entities, shown as ovals; a key attribute uniquely identifies an entity.
  • Relationships — associations between entities, shown as diamonds, with cardinality constraints such as one-to-one, one-to-many, and many-to-many.

Notation

Several notations are in use, including Chen's original notation, crow's foot notation, and the (min, max) notation. The enhanced entity–relationship model (EER) adds constructs such as specialization, generalization, and categories.
