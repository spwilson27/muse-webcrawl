Ethiopian civil conflict (2018–present)

The Ethiopian civil conflict is an ongoing series of interlinked conflicts in Ethiopia that began in the late 2010s and escalated sharply with the Tigray War of 2020–2022. The violence has involved the Ethiopian federal government, regional forces, and various armed groups, producing a severe humanitarian crisis.

Background

After Abiy Ahmed became prime minister in 2018, Ethiopia pursued rapid political reforms, but ethnic tensions and disputes between the federal government and regional administrations deepened. Relations with the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF), which had dominated the former ruling coalition, deteriorated into open confrontation in late 2020.

Tigray War

Fighting erupted in the Tigray Region in November 2020 between federal forces (supported by Eritrean troops and Amhara regional forces) and the TPLF. The war caused mass displacement, widespread reports of atrocities, and a famine-like humanitarian emergency. A cessation of hostilities agreement was signed in Pretoria in November 2022.

Other fronts

Parallel conflicts continued elsewhere, including insurgency by the Oromo Liberation Army in Oromia and, from 2023, conflict between federal forces and Fano militias in the Amhara Region, where a state of emergency was declared.
