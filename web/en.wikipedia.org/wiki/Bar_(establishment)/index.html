Bar (establishment)

A bar, also known as a saloon, tavern, pub or cocktail lounge in different traditions, is a retail business establishment that serves alcoholic beverages, such as beer, wine, liquor, cocktails and other drinks. Bars typically maintain a counter — the "bar" itself, from which the establishment takes its name — at which drinks are poured by a bartender, and provide seating at the counter, at tables, or both. Many bars also serve food, from snacks to full meals, and provide entertainment such as live music, darts, pool or television sports.

History

Drinking establishments are ancient: Roman tabernae and popinae served wine to travellers and townspeople. In early modern Europe, alehouses, taverns and inns served local populations; in the 19th century the long counter bar became standard in American saloons, and the golden age of the cocktail bar followed. During Prohibition in the United States (1920–1933), illegal bars known as speakeasies flourished, and the cocktail lounge and tiki bar emerged in the decades after repeal.

Types of bar

  • Pub — a public house, especially in Britain and Ireland, combining bar service with a community atmosphere.
  • Cocktail bar — emphasises mixed drinks and bartending craft.
  • Wine bar — focuses on wine by the glass.
  • Sports bar — built around televised sport.
  • Dive bar — an informal, inexpensive neighbourhood bar.
  • Gay bar — catering principally to an LGBT clientele.
  • Hotel bar, bar within a restaurant, and non-alcoholic bars serving alcohol-free drinks.

Regulation

Bars are generally licensed by local or national authorities, with rules on opening hours, the legal drinking age of patrons, responsible service, and noise. Brewpubs and microbreweries combine a bar with on-site brewing.
