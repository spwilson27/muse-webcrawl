Greater Toronto Area

The Greater Toronto Area, commonly referred to as the GTA, is the most populous metropolitan area in Canada. It consists of the City of Toronto and the surrounding regional municipalities of Durham, Halton, Peel, and York. In total, the region contains 25 urban, suburban, and rural municipalities. The Greater Toronto Area forms the core of the wider Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area and of the Golden Horseshoe.

Definition

Statistics Canada's census metropolitan area (CMA) for Toronto is somewhat smaller than the GTA as conventionally defined, while the provincial planning region known as the Greater Golden Horseshoe is considerably larger. The GTA itself is a provincial and commercial designation rather than an administrative unit, and the municipalities within it retain their own governments.

Municipalities

Besides Toronto, the GTA includes major suburban cities such as Mississauga and Brampton in Peel Region, Markham and Vaughan in York Region, Oakville and Burlington in Halton Region, and Pickering, Ajax, Whitby, and Oshawa in Durham Region. Rapid growth in the suburban regions since the 1970s has made the GTA one of the fastest-growing metropolitan areas in North America.

Economy

The GTA is Canada's principal economic centre, home to the Toronto Stock Exchange, the head offices of Canada's largest banks, and major manufacturing, technology, film, and media industries. Toronto Pearson International Airport, located in Mississauga, is the busiest airport in Canada.

Transportation

The region is served by the 400-series highways, including Highway 401, one of the busiest highways in North America, and by GO Transit's regional rail and bus network, operated by Metrolinx. Within Toronto, the Toronto Transit Commission operates the subway, streetcar, and bus system.
