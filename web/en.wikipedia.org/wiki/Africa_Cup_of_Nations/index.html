The Africa Cup of Nations (French: Coupe d'Afrique des Nations), often abbreviated AFCON, is the principal international association football competition in Africa. Sanctioned by the Confederation of African Football (CAF), it has been held since 1957 and brings together the continent's national teams.

History

The first tournament was held in Khartoum, Sudan, in 1957 with only three participating teams — Egypt, Sudan, and Ethiopia — after South Africa was disqualified for offering to field a whites-only team. Egypt won the first two editions. The tournament grew steadily as African nations gained independence in the 1960s, and it expanded to 16 teams in 1996 and 24 teams in 2019. Since 2013 it has been staged in odd-numbered years so as not to clash with the FIFA World Cup.

Format

Teams qualify through preliminary rounds, joining the host nation in the finals tournament. The 24 finalists are drawn into six groups of four; the top two in each group and the four best third-placed teams advance to a knockout stage culminating in the final.

Successful teams

Egypt is the most successful nation with seven titles, including three in a row between 2006 and 2010. Cameroon has won five titles and Ghana four. Other winners include Nigeria, Ivory Coast, Algeria, Senegal, DR Congo, Zambia, Tunisia, Sudan, Ethiopia, Morocco, and South Africa. Ivory Coast won the 2023 edition (played in 2024) as hosts.
