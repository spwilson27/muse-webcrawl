Assassination of Rajiv Gandhi
Date21 May 1991
LocationSriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu, India
TargetRajiv Gandhi, former Prime Minister of India
PerpetratorsLiberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE); bomber Thenmozhi Rajaratnam ("Dhanu")
DeathsAt least 14, plus the bomber

The assassination of Rajiv Gandhi, former prime minister of India, occurred on 21 May 1991 at Sriperumbudur, near Madras (now Chennai), in Tamil Nadu. Gandhi was campaigning for the Congress party in the general election when a suicide bomber detonated an explosive belt as she bent to garland him. The bombing was carried out by the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), the Sri Lankan Tamil separatist organisation.

The attack

Gandhi arrived at the election rally shortly after 10 p.m. As he walked towards the dais, greeting supporters, a young woman — Thenmozhi Rajaratnam, known as Dhanu — approached, bent as if to touch his feet in a traditional greeting, and detonated the RDX-laden belt around her waist. The explosion killed Gandhi, the bomber, and at least 14 others, including police officers and Congress workers. The scene was photographed by a local cameraman, Haribabu, who also died in the blast; his recovered film became key evidence.

Motive and investigation

The LTTE sought to prevent Gandhi's return to power, fearing he would again send the Indian Peace Keeping Force to Sri Lanka, which had fought the Tigers during its 1987–1990 deployment under the Indo-Lanka Accord signed by Gandhi. A Special Investigation Team traced the conspiracy to LTTE intelligence chief Pottu Amman and the one-eyed operative Sivarasan. A special court convicted 26 defendants in 1998 and sentenced them to death; on appeal the Supreme Court confirmed death sentences for four, three of which were later commuted to life imprisonment, and the remaining convicts were progressively released, the last in 2022.

Aftermath

The assassination swung sympathy towards Congress, which won the election and formed a government under P. V. Narasimha Rao. The LTTE was banned in India, and the killing badly damaged the Tigers' international standing. A memorial now stands at the site in Sriperumbudur.
