Cajuns

The Cajuns (French: les Cadjins) are an ethnic group mainly found in Louisiana, descended from Acadian exiles from Canada's Maritime provinces who were expelled by the British in Le Grand Dérangement (1755–1764) and settled in the bayous and prairies of southern Louisiana, the region now called Acadiana. They are known for their French language, Catholic faith, distinctive cuisine, and music.

History

After the expulsion from Acadia, many exiles eventually reached Spanish-ruled Louisiana, settling from the 1760s onward. They blended with French Creoles, Spanish, German, Native American, and African neighbours. In the 20th century, compulsory English schooling suppressed Cajun French, but a cultural revival since the 1960s — aided by CODOFIL and festival culture — has celebrated Cajun identity.

Culture

Cajun cuisine features gumbo, jambalaya, étouffée, and boudin, seasoned boldly and built on the "holy trinity" of onion, celery, and bell pepper. Cajun music, driven by accordion and fiddle, influenced and differs from the zydeco of Black Creoles; the fais do-do is the traditional dance party.
