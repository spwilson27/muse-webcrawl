Racetam

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Racetams are a class of drugs that share a pyrrolidone nucleus — a five-membered lactam ring. Many are considered nootropics, substances claimed to enhance cognition. The first and best known is piracetam, synthesised in 1964 by Corneliu Giurgea at the Belgian company UCB, who coined the term "nootropic" to describe its purported effects.

Members

The family includes piracetam, oxiracetam, aniracetam, pramiracetam, levetiracetam, brivaracetam, phenylpiracetam, coluracetam and others. Some members have found established medical uses that diverge from the original nootropic idea: levetiracetam (Keppra) and brivaracetam are widely prescribed antiepileptic drugs, while piracetam is used in some countries for cortical myoclonus and was historically marketed for cognitive disorders.

Mechanism and evidence

No single mechanism unites the class. Proposed actions include positive modulation of AMPA-type glutamate receptors, effects on cholinergic systems, and changes in membrane fluidity; levetiracetam acts mainly by binding the synaptic vesicle protein SV2A. Evidence that racetams improve cognition in healthy people is weak and contested, and they are not approved as cognitive enhancers in many jurisdictions; in the United States several are sold as unregulated supplements or research chemicals, and phenylpiracetam is prohibited in sport by WADA as a stimulant.

Safety

Racetams are generally reported to have low toxicity, with headache, insomnia and irritability the most common side effects, but long-term data for many members are sparse because most were never taken through full clinical development.
