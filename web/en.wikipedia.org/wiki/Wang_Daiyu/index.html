Wang Daiyu

Wang Daiyu (c. 1590s–1658) was a Chinese Muslim scholar of the Hui people.

Born: c. 1590s, Nanjing, Ming China
Died: 1658, Beijing, Qing China

Wang Daiyu (Chinese: 王岱輿; c. 1590s–1658) was a Chinese Muslim (Hui) scholar and philosopher of the late Ming and early Qing dynasties. He is regarded as the earliest and one of the most important figures of the Han Kitab tradition — Chinese-language Islamic literature that expressed Islamic thought using the vocabulary of Confucianism, Buddhism and Daoism.

Life

Wang was born in Nanjing into a family of Hui scholars; his ancestors were said to have come from Arabia. He studied Arabic, Persian and Chinese classical learning, and devoted his life to teaching and writing in Nanjing and later Beijing, where he died in 1658.

Works

His best-known work is the Zhengjiao Zhenquan ("True Commentary on the True Teaching", 1642), which explains Islamic doctrine — the oneness of God, prophecy, worship and ethics — in terms accessible to Chinese readers steeped in Confucian thought. He also wrote the Qingzhen Daxue ("The Great Learning of Islam") and the Xizhen Zhengda. Together with later scholars such as Ma Zhu and Liu Zhi, Wang laid the foundations of a distinctly Chinese Islamic theology.
