Operation Alberich

Operation Alberich (German: Unternehmen Alberich) was the codename of the German military operation of February–March 1917, in which German forces on the Western Front withdrew from the salient between Arras and Soissons to the new, shorter and more heavily fortified positions of the Hindenburg Line (Siegfriedstellung).

The operation was named after Alberich, the dwarf of the Nibelungenlied and of Wagner's Ring cycle. The withdrawal shortened the German front by about 40 kilometres, freeing some thirteen divisions for other duties and frustrating Allied plans for a spring offensive against the old positions.

Scorched earth

As they withdrew, German forces systematically devastated the evacuated territory: villages were demolished, wells poisoned, roads cratered, trees felled and booby traps laid. Around 125,000 French civilians were deported from the zone. The destruction shocked Allied opinion and was widely condemned as wanton, and it slowed the Allied advance into the abandoned area.

Aftermath

British and French troops cautiously occupied the devastated zone and the outposts of the Hindenburg Line. The new German positions proved formidable and formed the backdrop to the fighting of 1917, including the Battle of Arras and the Nivelle offensive.
