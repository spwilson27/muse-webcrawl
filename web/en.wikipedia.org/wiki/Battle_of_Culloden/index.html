Battle of Culloden

The Battle of Culloden (Scottish Gaelic: Blàr Chùil Lodair) was fought on 16 April 1746, on Drummossie Moor near Inverness in the Scottish Highlands. It was the final confrontation of the Jacobite rising of 1745 and the last pitched battle fought on British soil. The Jacobite army of Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") was decisively defeated by the British government army under Prince William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland.

Background

Charles Edward Stuart landed in Scotland in 1745 to claim the British throne for his father, James Francis Edward Stuart. After victories at Prestonpans and Falkirk, the Jacobites marched into England as far as Derby before retreating. Exhausted, hungry and short of money, they fell back to Inverness. An attempted night march on Cumberland's camp at Nairn on 15 April failed, leaving the army tired and starving on the morning of the battle.

The battle

Against the advice of Lord George Murray, Charles chose to fight on the flat, open moor of Drummossie, ground well suited to government artillery and cavalry and poorly suited to the Highland charge. After an artillery exchange, the Jacobite clans charged; boggy ground and grapeshot broke up the attack. On the right the charge reached the government line but was repulsed by musket fire and bayonets; government dragoons then completed the rout. The battle lasted about an hour.

Aftermath

Jacobite casualties were catastrophic. In the days after the battle, government troops hunted fugitives, earning Cumberland the nickname "Butcher". Charles fled Scotland in September 1746. The government responded with the Disarming Act and the Heritable Jurisdictions Act, and proscribed Highland dress, seeking to destroy the clan system. The battlefield is preserved today by the National Trust for Scotland.
