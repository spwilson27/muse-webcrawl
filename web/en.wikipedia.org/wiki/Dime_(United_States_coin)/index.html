Diameter17.91 mm (0.705 in)

The dime, in United States usage, is a ten-cent coin, one tenth of a United States dollar. The denomination was first authorized by the Coinage Act of 1792. The dime is the smallest in diameter and the thinnest of all U.S. coins currently minted for circulation.

The dime's design has changed many times since it was authorized in 1792. The current design, the Roosevelt dime, features former President Franklin D. Roosevelt on the obverse and was issued in 1946, soon after his death. Roosevelt was chosen because he had supported the March of Dimes, a charitable organization that collected dimes to fund polio research.

History

The first dimes, the Draped Bust dimes, were minted in 1796. Early designs included the Capped Bust, Seated Liberty, and Barber dimes. The Mercury dime (1916–1945), depicting Liberty wearing a winged cap, preceded the Roosevelt dime. Until 1964 dimes were struck in 90% silver; since 1965 they have been copper-nickel clad.

Current design

The obverse of the Roosevelt dime bears the words LIBERTY and IN GOD WE TRUST, and the reverse shows a torch symbolizing liberty, flanked by an olive branch (peace) and an oak branch (strength and independence). The reverse also bears the inscriptions UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, E PLURIBUS UNUM, and ONE DIME.
