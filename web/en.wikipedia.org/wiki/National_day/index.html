National day

National day
TypePublic holiday / commemoration
Observed byMost sovereign states
SignificanceFounding, independence, constitution, or other defining event of the nation

A national day is a day on which celebrations mark the founding, independence, or another defining event of a nation or state. Most countries have a single national day, usually a public holiday, on which the state was founded, achieved independence, adopted a constitution, or — in monarchies — which marks the sovereign's official birthday. A small number of countries have no formally designated national day, and a few celebrate more than one.

Typical commemorations

National days most often commemorate independence from a colonial power (for example, Independence Day in the United States on 4 July, and India's Independence Day on 15 August), the formation of a republic or federation (Australia Day, 26 January; Switzerland's National Day, 1 August), the storming of the Bastille in France (14 July), or the birthday of a monarch (the Netherlands' King's Day). Celebrations typically include flag-raising, military parades, fireworks, speeches by heads of state, and awards of national honours.

Historical development

Formal national days became widespread in the 19th and 20th centuries alongside the rise of the nation-state and mass politics. Dates are sometimes contested or changed after political transitions, and some countries separate a "national day" celebrating statehood from other patriotic holidays marking unification, revolution, or liberation.
