Planned community

A planned community, planned city, planned town, or planned settlement is any community that was carefully planned from its inception and is typically built on previously undeveloped land. This contrasts with settlements that evolve organically over time.

The term new town refers to planned communities of the new towns movement in particular; examples include the new towns of the United Kingdom, built after the Second World War under the New Towns Act 1946, such as Milton Keynes.

History

Planned settlements date back to antiquity, including the grid-planned cities of the Indus Valley Civilization and Roman colonial towns. Planned capitals include Washington, D.C., Canberra, Brasília, New Delhi and Islamabad.

Master-planned communities

In the United States, a master-planned community is a residential plan that includes a significant number of recreational and commercial amenities, such as Irvine, California and Columbia, Maryland. Such developments are usually built by a single developer according to a comprehensive master plan.
