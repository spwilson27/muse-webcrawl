Vishvamitra

Vishvamitra (Sanskrit: विश्वामित्र, "friend of the world") is one of the most venerated rishis (sages) of ancient India and one of the Saptarishis, the seven great sages. Unusually among the great sages, he was born a Kshatriya king — as Kaushika, king of Kanyakubja — and attained the status of Brahmarishi, the highest rank of sage, through millennia of severe asceticism (tapas). He is credited as the seer of much of the third mandala of the Rigveda, including the Gayatri Mantra, and is a major figure in the Ramayana.

Legend

The wish-granting cow

As King Kaushika, Vishvamitra visited the hermitage of the sage Vasishtha and was astonished by the feast produced by Vasishtha's divine cow, Nandini (Kamadhenu), who could grant any wish. When Vasishtha refused to sell her, the king tried to seize her by force, but his armies were destroyed by the sage's spiritual power. Humiliated, Kaushika renounced his throne and undertook austerities to surpass Vasishtha — beginning a legendary rivalry.

Austerities and temptations

The gods tested his resolve repeatedly. Indra sent the apsara Menaka to distract him; Vishvamitra lived with her for years before realising the loss of his accumulated merit and driving her away (their daughter was Shakuntala). Another apsara, Rambha, he cursed to become a rock when she tried to tempt him. Rising through the ranks of sagehood — Rajarishi, Rishi, Maharishi — he finally created, in anger at being refused heaven for King Trishanku, a parallel universe with its own stars, until the gods relented. At last, after further thousands of years of tapas, Brahma named him Brahmarishi, and Vasishtha himself acknowledged him as an equal, ending the feud.

In the Ramayana

In Valmiki's Ramayana, Vishvamitra comes to King Dasharatha's court and demands the young prince Rama to protect his sacrifices from the demons Taraka and Maricha. He teaches Rama divine weapons (the Bala and Atibala), leads him and Lakshmana to Mithila, and there Rama breaks Shiva's bow and wins the hand of Sita. Much of the Bala Kanda is narrated by or about Vishvamitra.

Legacy

Vishvamitra is the seer of Rigveda book 3 and the revealer of the Gayatri Mantra (Rigveda 3.62.10), recited daily by millions. He is counted as the gotra ancestor (pravara) of the Kaushika gotra, and his story is a central Hindu exemplar of the power of determined asceticism to transcend birth.
