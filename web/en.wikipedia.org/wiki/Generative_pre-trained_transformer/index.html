A generative pre-trained transformer (GPT) is a type of large language model based on the transformer architecture, pre-trained on large corpora of text to predict the next token in a sequence, and then adapted to downstream tasks. Developed and named by OpenAI, GPT models are autoregressive and decoder-only: given preceding text, they generate continuations token by token.

Training

GPT models are first pre-trained on massive, diverse text datasets with a self-supervised language-modelling objective. This gives broad general capabilities that can then be specialised by fine-tuning on labelled examples, instruction tuning, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Scaling the model size, dataset and compute produced predictable gains described by neural scaling laws, with larger GPT models exhibiting emergent few-shot learning abilities — performing tasks from a few examples in the prompt without weight updates.

History

GPT-1 (2018) had 117 million parameters and demonstrated the pre-train/fine-tune paradigm for natural language understanding tasks. GPT-2 (2019, 1.5 billion parameters) generated strikingly coherent text and was initially released in stages over misuse concerns. GPT-3 (2020, 175 billion parameters) popularised few-shot prompting. GPT-4 (2023) added multimodal input (text and images) and substantially improved reasoning and examination performance, and GPT models became the engine of the chatbot ChatGPT, launched in November 2022.

Impact

GPT-style models reshaped natural language processing, displacing task-specific architectures, and are widely used for chatbots, coding assistance, translation, summarisation and content generation. They also raise concerns about hallucination (fluent but false output), bias, misinformation and copyright, which remain active research and policy topics.
