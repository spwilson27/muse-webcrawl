Europeana is the European Union's digital cultural heritage platform, providing free online access to tens of millions of digitised items — books, paintings, photographs, films, sound recordings and archival records — contributed by thousands of museums, libraries, archives and galleries across Europe.

History

The project grew from a 2005 letter by French president Jacques Chirac and five other European leaders calling for a European digital library, partly in response to Google's book-scanning ambitions. A prototype was launched in November 2008; demand was so heavy that the site crashed on its first day. Europeana relaunched in a more robust form in 2009.

Model and content

Europeana does not host most content directly; it aggregates metadata from partner institutions and links to items held on their own systems. Metadata is published under the CC0 waiver to encourage reuse, and the platform provides APIs used by developers, researchers and education projects. Thematic collections cover topics such as the First World War, migration, archaeology, fashion and sport.
