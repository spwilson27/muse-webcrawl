Human spine

The human spine, or vertebral column, is the central supporting structure of the body, extending from the skull to the pelvis. It protects the spinal cord, supports the head and trunk, and provides attachment points for the ribs and muscles of the back.

Structure

Adults typically have 33 vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral (forming the sacrum) and 4 fused coccygeal (forming the coccyx). Between most vertebrae lie intervertebral discs of fibrocartilage that cushion the column. Viewed from the side the spine has four curves — cervical and lumbar lordoses and thoracic and sacral kyphoses — that increase its strength and flexibility.

Function and disorders

The vertebral canal houses and protects the spinal cord, with spinal nerves exiting through intervertebral foramina. Disorders include scoliosis, herniated discs, spinal stenosis and osteoporotic fractures; back pain is among the most common causes of disability worldwide.
