The Hominini (hominins) form a taxonomic tribe of the subfamily Homininae (hominines). Hominini includes the extant genera Homo (humans) and Pan (chimpanzees and bonobos) and their extinct ancestors, excluding gorillas, which are placed in the tribe Gorillini.

Usage varies: some researchers restrict hominin to the human lineage after the split from chimpanzees, placing Pan in a separate tribe Panini. In the broader sense used in this classification, both lineages are hominins.

Evolutionary history

The human and chimpanzee lineages diverged roughly 5–7 million years ago. Fossil genera commonly assigned to the human side include Sahelanthropus, Orrorin, Ardipithecus, Australopithecus, Paranthropus, and Homo. Key adaptations of the human lineage include habitual bipedalism, enlarged brains (especially in Homo), reduced dentition, and tool use.

Classification debate

DNA evidence shows humans and chimpanzees are more closely related to each other than either is to gorillas, leading to revised classifications that group them together. Terminology — hominid, hominine, hominin — has shifted accordingly and remains a common source of confusion.
