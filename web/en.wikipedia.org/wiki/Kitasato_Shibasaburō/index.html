Baron Kitasato Shibasaburō (北里 柴三郎, January 29, 1853 – June 13, 1931) was a Japanese physician and bacteriologist. He is remembered as the co-discoverer of the infectious agent of bubonic plague in Hong Kong during an outbreak in 1894, almost simultaneously with Alexandre Yersin, and for his pioneering work on serum therapy with Emil von Behring.

Early life and education

Kitasato was born in Oguni, in present-day Kumamoto Prefecture. He studied medicine at Kumamoto Medical School and the University of Tokyo, graduating in 1883. From 1885 to 1891 he worked in Berlin in the laboratory of Robert Koch, where he became the first person to grow the tetanus bacillus in pure culture (1889) and, with Behring, developed the concept of antitoxins and serum therapy for diphtheria and tetanus.

Plague discovery

In 1894, during a plague epidemic in Hong Kong, Kitasato identified the bacterium responsible for the disease. Although Yersin's account was ultimately credited with greater accuracy, the organism was for a time named Pasteurella pestis; it is now known as Yersinia pestis.

Later career

Kitasato founded the Institute for Infectious Diseases in Tokyo in 1892 and later the Kitasato Institute in 1914. He was nominated for the first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1901 for his work on diphtheria antitoxin with Behring, though only Behring received the award. He was the first dean of the Keio University School of Medicine and was raised to the Japanese peerage as a baron in 1924.
