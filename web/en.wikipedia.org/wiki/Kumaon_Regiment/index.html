The Kumaon Regiment is one of the most decorated infantry regiments of the Indian Army. The regiment traces its origins to the 18th century and has fought in numerous campaigns. It draws its recruits primarily from the Kumaon region of Uttarakhand and the Ahir community of Haryana and neighbouring regions. The regimental centre is at Ranikhet, Uttarakhand.

History

The Kumaonis — the people of the Kumaon hills — have a long tradition of military service, serving under local rulers, the Gurkhas, and later the British. Kumaoni soldiers fought in both World Wars as part of the Hyderabad Regiment and other units; after Indian independence the regiment was reconstituted as the Kumaon Regiment. Its battalions have taken part in the Indo-Pakistani wars of 1947–48, 1965 and 1971, the Sino-Indian War of 1962, the Kargil War of 1999, and numerous counter-insurgency and peacekeeping operations.

Gallantry

The regiment has produced two recipients of the Param Vir Chakra, India's highest military decoration: Major Somnath Sharma, the first recipient of the award, who was killed defending Srinagar airfield at the Battle of Badgam in 1947, and Major Shaitan Singh, who was killed leading C Company of 13 Kumaon in its celebrated stand at the Battle of Rezang La in 1962, a position held almost to the last man. The regiment's battalions have earned many other battle honours and gallantry awards in every war fought by independent India.

Composition

The regiment comprises regular infantry battalions, Rashtriya Rifles units, and Territorial Army battalions. A number of its battalions are affiliated with the Naga Regiment, which was raised in 1985 with a substantial Kumaoni complement.
