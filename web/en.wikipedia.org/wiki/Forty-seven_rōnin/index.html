Forty-seven rōnin

The forty-seven rōnin (四十七士, Shijūshichishi), also known as the Akō incident or Akō vendetta, is a historical event in Japan (1701–1703) in which a band of leaderless samurai avenged the death of their lord, Asano Naganori, and were then ordered to commit seppuku. It became a national legend exemplifying the samurai code of bushido and loyalty.

Forty-seven rōnin
Date: 1701–1703
Location: Edo, Akō, Japan
Leader: Ōishi Yoshio
Outcome: Revenge achieved; rōnin ordered to commit seppuku

Story

Provoked by the court official Kira Yoshinaka, Asano attacked him in Edo Castle and was sentenced to seppuku; his lands were confiscated and his retainers became rōnin. Led by Ōishi Yoshio, 47 of them waited nearly two years, feigning dissipation, then raided Kira’s Edo mansion on a snowy night (30 January 1703), killed him, and laid his head at Asano’s grave at Sengaku-ji. The shogunate sentenced them to seppuku, which they performed on 20 March 1703.

Legacy

The tale, dramatised as Chūshingura in puppet theatre and kabuki, is Japan’s most famous vendetta story, retold in films including the 2013 47 Ronin. The graves at Sengaku-ji in Tokyo remain a pilgrimage site.
