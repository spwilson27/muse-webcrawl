Düsseldorf School of Photography

The Düsseldorf School of Photography (German: Düsseldorfer Photoschule) refers to a group of photographers who studied at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf in the mid-1970s under the influential photographers Bernd and Hilla Becher.

Approach

The Bechers' students adopted their teachers' typological, deadpan approach: large-format colour photographs of industrial and urban subjects, taken frontally under neutral, even light and often arranged in series or grids. Prominent members include Andreas Gursky, Thomas Struth, Thomas Ruff, Candida Höfer, Axel Hütte, Petra Wunderlich, and Jörg Sasse.

Influence

The school's monumental prints and detached, objective style — sometimes described as a continuation of the New Objectivity of August Sander — profoundly influenced contemporary art photography from the 1980s onward. Works by its leading figures, such as Gursky's Rhein II (1999), achieved record prices at auction and helped establish photography's central place in the contemporary art market.
