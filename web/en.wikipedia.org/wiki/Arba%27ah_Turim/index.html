Arba'ah Turim

The Arba'ah Turim (Hebrew: ארבעה טורים, "Four Rows"), often called simply the Tur, is an important Halakhic code of Jewish law composed by Jacob ben Asher (c. 1270–1340) in the 14th century. Its four-part structure later became the template for the Shulchan Aruch.

Author: Jacob ben Asher
Language: Hebrew
Composed: 14th century
Subject: Halakha (Jewish law)

The four sections

The work is divided into four "rows", named after the four rows of jewels on the High Priest's breastplate: Orach Chaim (daily life, prayer, and festivals), Yoreh De'ah (ritual prohibitions and dietary laws), Even Ha'ezer (marriage and family law), and Choshen Mishpat (civil and financial law).

Influence

The Tur drew on a wide range of earlier authorities, Sephardic and Ashkenazic. Joseph Karo's commentary Beit Yosef and his later code, the Shulchan Aruch, followed the Tur's structure, making it one of the most influential works in Jewish legal literature.
