Jiva

Jiva
Sanskritजीव (jīva)
MeaningLiving being; individual soul
TraditionsHinduism, Jainism, Sikhism

Jiva (Sanskrit: जीव, jīva) is a living being, or any entity imbued with a life force, in Hinduism and Jainism. The word itself originates from the Sanskrit verb-root jīv, meaning "to live". It has the same Indo-European root as the Latin word vivus, meaning "alive".

In Hindu philosophy, the jiva is the individual self or soul, bound to the cycle of birth and rebirth (samsara) by karma until it attains liberation (moksha). Schools differ on its relation to the ultimate reality: Advaita Vedanta identifies the innermost self (atman) with Brahman, while Dvaita maintains an eternal distinction.

In Jainism

In Jainism, jiva is the immortal essence of a living organism — human, animal, plant or even elemental being — distinct from the non-living ajiva. Jivas are innumerable, each inherently capable of omniscience, obscured by karmic matter until liberation.
