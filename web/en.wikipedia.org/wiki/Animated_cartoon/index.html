Animated cartoon

An animated cartoon is a film, typically humorous and often short, made by photographing a sequence of drawings or computer-generated images so that they appear to move. Cartoons are a major form of animation intended primarily for entertainment.

History

Early animated films emerged in the first decade of the twentieth century with pioneers such as J. Stuart Blackton and Émile Cohl. Winsor McCay's Gertie the Dinosaur (1914) demonstrated character animation, while Felix the Cat became the first cartoon star in the 1920s. Walt Disney's Steamboat Willie (1928) introduced synchronised sound, and the 1930s–1950s are regarded as the golden age of American theatrical cartoons, with studios such as Warner Bros. (Looney Tunes) and MGM (Tom and Jerry) producing classic shorts.

Television and digital era

From the 1960s, limited-animation television series brought cartoons to a mass audience. Japanese anime developed as a parallel global industry. Computer animation and later web cartoons transformed production from the 1990s onward, while hand-drawn styles remained influential.
