Clipping (signal processing)

Clipping
EffectWaveform peaks cut off; harmonic distortion
TypesHard clipping, soft clipping
RelatedOverdrive, distortion, limiting

Clipping is a form of distortion that limits a signal once it exceeds a threshold. When an amplifier is pushed beyond its maximum capability it clips the waveform: the peaks of the signal are flattened (in hard clipping) or progressively rounded (in soft clipping). In audio, clipping introduces strong odd-order harmonics and is usually considered undesirable in reproduction, while in electric guitar amplification it is deliberately exploited as the basis of overdrive and distortion effects.

Causes and effects

Clipping occurs when the demanded output voltage or current exceeds what an amplifier's power supply can deliver, or when a digital signal exceeds the maximum value representable (0 dBFS in digital audio), in which case samples are truncated at the limit. Analog circuits driven into saturation produce smoother soft clipping; digital clipping is abrupt and harsher. Clipped waveforms contain increased high-frequency energy, which can damage loudspeaker tweeters, and intermodulation distortion that reduces clarity.

Prevention and use

Engineers prevent unwanted clipping with proper gain staging, headroom, and limiters; recording and mastering aim to keep peaks below full scale (though the "loudness war" pushed commercial releases toward heavy limiting and occasional intentional clipping). Restoration of clipped recordings is possible to a limited extent by interpolating the lost peaks. Deliberate waveform clipping is also used constructively in guitar effects, some synthesizer designs, and in RF and communications circuits.
