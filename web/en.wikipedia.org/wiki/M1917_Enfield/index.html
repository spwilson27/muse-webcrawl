The M1917 Enfield, officially the United States Rifle, cal .30, Model of 1917, is an American modification and production run of the British Pattern 1914 Enfield (P14) rifle developed and manufactured during the First World War. Chambered for the .30-06 Springfield cartridge, it was the primary battle rifle used by the American Expeditionary Forces in Europe in 1917 and 1918, outnumbering the M1903 Springfield in front-line service.

History

When the United States entered the First World War in 1917, it faced a shortage of M1903 Springfield rifles. The British P14 rifle, designed at the Royal Small Arms Factory at Enfield, was already being produced in the United States by Winchester, Remington and Eddystone under British contract in .303 British. The US Army adapted the design to the .30-06 cartridge, and production of the M1917 began in 1917. By the end of the war, roughly 2.2 million M1917 rifles had been produced — more than the total of M1903 Springfields manufactured up to that point.

Design

The rifle uses a Mauser-style bolt action with a cock-on-closing bolt, a six-round internal magazine, and aperture rear sights protected by steel "ears" on the receiver. It is notably heavy but strong and accurate. Sergeant Alvin York famously used an M1917 during his Medal of Honor action in October 1918.

Later service

Large numbers of M1917s were stored after the First World War and issued again in the Second World War for training, rear-echelon troops and Lend-Lease supply to allies including Britain's Home Guard, China and Free French forces. Remington later adapted the action for the commercial Model 30 sporting rifle.
