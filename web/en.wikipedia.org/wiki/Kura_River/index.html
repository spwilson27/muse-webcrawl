SourceNortheastern Turkey, near Kars

The Kura (Georgian: მტკვარი, Mtkvari; Azerbaijani: Kür) is an east-flowing river south of the Greater Caucasus mountains that drains the southern slopes of the Caucasus into the Caspian Sea. At 1,515 kilometres it is the longest river in the South Caucasus and the largest river in Georgia and Azerbaijan.

Course

The river rises in northeastern Turkey in the former province of Kars, flows north-east into Georgia, passing through the Samtskhe–Javakheti region and the Borjomi Gorge, then turns east through the Georgian capital Tbilisi. Entering Azerbaijan, it is joined by the Aras, its largest tributary, and crosses the Kura–Aras lowland before emptying into the Caspian Sea in a small delta south of Neftchala.

Use and environment

The Kura is heavily used for irrigation and hydroelectric power, notably at the Mingachevir reservoir in Azerbaijan, one of the largest artificial lakes in the Caucasus. Its waters support sturgeon fisheries near the mouth. Pollution from industry and agriculture in all three basin countries, together with reduced flows, has degraded water quality, prompting international cooperation programmes for the Kura–Aras basin.
