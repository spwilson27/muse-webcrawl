The New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM) is a weekly peer-reviewed medical journal published by the Massachusetts Medical Society. Founded in 1812, it is among the most prestigious and widely read medical journals in the world.

History

The journal traces its origins to the New England Journal of Medicine and Surgery, founded in Boston in 1812 by John Collins Warren and others. It went through several mergers and name changes before adopting its present title in 1928.

Over two centuries it has published landmark papers, including early reports on anaesthesia and major clinical trials that changed medical practice.

Content and influence

NEJM publishes original research, review articles, clinical cases (notably the Case Records of the Massachusetts General Hospital), editorials and correspondence. Its articles cover internal medicine and its subspecialties, surgery, paediatrics, and public health.

The journal consistently records one of the highest impact factors among general medical journals, and its findings are frequently reported in the general news media.

Access

Historically a subscription journal, NEJM makes research articles freely available six months after publication and provides free access in many low-income countries.
