McKinsey & Company (informally known as McKinsey or "The Firm") is an American multinational strategy and management consulting firm founded in 1926 by University of Chicago professor James O. McKinsey. It is the oldest and largest of the "Big Three" management consultancies (MBB), alongside Bain & Company and Boston Consulting Group.

History

McKinsey founded the firm in Chicago as an accounting and management consultancy applying accounting principles to business management. After his death in 1937, the firm was reorganized; under managing director Marvin Bower (1950–1967), McKinsey adopted the model of a professional partnership—recruiting young graduates from elite business schools, using an "up or out" promotion policy, and serving only top executives (the CEO-level "one firm" partnership).

The firm expanded internationally from the 1950s onward, opening offices in London, Amsterdam, and around the world, and diversified into practice areas including operations, technology, sustainability, and public sector work. The McKinsey Global Institute, its research arm, was founded in 1990.

Notable work and influence

McKinsey is credited with popularizing concepts such as the matrix organization for General Electric, the 7S Framework (with Tom Peters and Robert Waterman), and the "War for Talent" thesis (1997). The firm recruits heavily from elite universities and its alumni include CEOs of major corporations, government officials, and founders of other firms. Alumni include Sundar Pichai, Sheryl Sandberg, and James Gorman.

Criticism and controversies

The firm has faced criticism over its role advising Enron, its work related to the opioid crisis (McKinsey agreed in 2021 to pay nearly $600 million to settle claims over advice to Purdue Pharma), its contracts with authoritarian governments, and work for U.S. immigration enforcement. McKinsey has responded by tightening client-selection policies.
