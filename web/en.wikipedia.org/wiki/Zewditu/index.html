Zewditu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zewditu I

Reign: 27 September 1916 – 2 April 1930

Regent: Ras Tafari Makonnen (later Haile Selassie I)

Born: 29 April 1876, Werreyimeda, Wollo

Died: 2 April 1930, Addis Ababa

Father: Menelik II

Title: Negiste Negest ("Queen of Kings"), Empress of Ethiopia

Zewditu (Amharic: ዘውዲቱ, 1876–1930) was Empress of Ethiopia from 1916 until her death in 1930. The eldest daughter of Emperor Menelik II, she was the first female head of state of an internationally recognized African country in the modern era and the first and only reigning empress of Ethiopia in her own right since antiquity.

Reign

Zewditu came to the throne in September 1916 after the deposition of her nephew, Iyasu V, who was accused of converting to Islam. Because she was a woman in a deeply traditional court, the regency was given to Ras Tafari Makonnen, who was also named heir to the throne. Power was shared between the devout, conservative Empress — closely aligned with the Ethiopian Orthodox Church — and the modernizing regent, an arrangement that produced both reform (including Ethiopia's entry into the League of Nations in 1923 and the formal abolition of slavery in 1924) and growing political tension.

Death and succession

Her husband, Ras Gugsa Wole, raised a rebellion in 1930 and was defeated and killed by forces loyal to the regent. Zewditu died days later, on 2 April 1930, in circumstances that contemporaries debated. Tafari succeeded her and was crowned Emperor Haile Selassie I in November 1930.
