Proserpina

Proserpina (Latin: Prōserpina) is an ancient Roman goddess whose cult, myths and mysteries were combined with those of Demeter and Persephone in Greek mythology. She is the daughter of Ceres (Demeter) and Jupiter (Zeus), and the wife of Pluto (Hades), god of the underworld.

Mythology

The best-known myth of Proserpina is her abduction by Pluto while she was gathering flowers on the plains of Enna in Sicily. Her mother Ceres searched the earth for her, neglecting the crops, until Jupiter brokered an agreement: because Proserpina had eaten pomegranate seeds in the underworld, she would spend part of each year with Pluto below the earth and part above with her mother. The myth explains the cycle of the seasons: when Proserpina is in the underworld, Ceres grieves and the earth is barren (winter); her return brings spring.

Cult

Proserpina's cult was introduced to Rome from southern Italy around 205 BC, during the Second Punic War, along with the cult of Dis Pater. She was worshipped at the Ludi Tarentini and later the Ludi Saeculares, and her festival, the Cerealia, was shared with Ceres in April.

In art and literature

The rape of Proserpina has been a popular subject in Western art, depicted by Bernini in his marble sculpture The Rape of Proserpina (1621–22), and by painters including Rembrandt and Rubens. Dante Gabriel Rossetti painted Proserpine in 1874. The myth is retold in Ovid's Metamorphoses and Fasti, and in Claudian's epic De raptu Proserpinae.

Namesake

The asteroid 26 Proserpina, discovered in 1853, is named after the goddess.
