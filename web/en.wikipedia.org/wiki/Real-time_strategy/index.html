Real-time strategy

Real-time strategy (RTS) is a subgenre of strategy video games that does not progress incrementally in turns, but allows all players to play simultaneously in "real time". Players generally build structures, gather resources, train units, and command armies to conquer or destroy opponents, often viewing the battlefield from a top-down perspective.

Gameplay

Typical RTS gameplay involves resource collection (such as gold, wood, or minerals), base construction, technology advancement through a "tech tree," and producing and micromanaging military units. The fog of war limits players' knowledge of the map. Success depends on both long-term strategic planning ("macro") and rapid, precise unit control ("micro").

History

The genre was popularized by Westwood Studios' Dune II (1992), which established the template of harvesting resources, building a base, and commanding units with the mouse. Warcraft: Orcs & Humans (1994) and Command & Conquer (1995) followed, and the genre reached its commercial peak in the late 1990s with Age of Empires (1997) and Blizzard's StarCraft (1998), which became a major esport, particularly in South Korea. StarCraft II (2010) continued that competitive tradition, while the multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) genre later emerged from RTS modding communities.
