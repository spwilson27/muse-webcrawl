The New York Review of Books

The New York Review of Books (or NYREV or NYRB) is a semi-monthly magazine with articles on literature, culture, economics, science and current affairs. Published in New York City, it is inspired by the idea that the discussion of important books is an indispensable literary activity. Esquire called it "the premier literary-intellectual magazine in the English language." Its motto, adopted from an early advertisement, is "No issue is less than remarkable."

History

The Review was founded during the New York City newspaper strike of 1962–63. Robert B. Silvers and Barbara Epstein, together with publisher A. Whitney Ellsworth and Elizabeth Hardwick, conceived a publication that would review books seriously and at length, in reaction against what they saw as the superficial reviewing practices of the New York Times Book Review. Jason Epstein, Barbara Epstein's husband and editor at Random House, helped launch the enterprise. The first issue appeared on February 1, 1963.

Silvers and Epstein edited the magazine together until Epstein's death in 2006; Silvers continued as sole editor until his death in 2017. In 2019, Ian Buruma's brief editorship ended and Emily Greenhouse and Gabriel Winslow-Yost became editors. Longtime contributors have included W. H. Auden, Susan Sontag, Joseph Brodsky, Hannah Arendt, Joan Didion, Oliver Sacks, and many other leading writers and scholars.

Content and influence

Each issue combines long review essays — often using a book or group of books as the occasion for a broader essay — with poetry, excerpts, and articles on politics and science. Its caricatures by artist David Levine became a visual signature. The publication has been a central forum for intellectual debate in the United States, publishing landmark essays during the Vietnam War era, the Cold War, and the Iraq War. In 1979 the founders established the London Review of Books as a sister publication.

NYRB Classics and other imprints

The Review's publishing division, New York Review Books, launched the NYRB Classics imprint in 1999, reissuing out-of-print works of fiction and nonfiction. Other imprints include NYRB Poets, NYRB Kids, and the New York Review Comics line. See also literary magazine and The New York Times Book Review.
