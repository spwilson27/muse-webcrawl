E8 (mathematics)

In mathematics, E8 is the name given to several closely related objects: the largest exceptional simple Lie algebra, of rank 8 and dimension 248; the corresponding Lie groups; and the E8 root lattice, an even unimodular lattice in eight dimensions.

The Lie algebra

The E8 Lie algebra has dimension 248 and rank 8. Its root system consists of 240 roots in eight-dimensional space, and its Weyl group has order 696,729,600. Unusually, the adjoint representation of E8 is also its smallest non-trivial representation, so the Lie algebra can be viewed as acting on itself. The Killing form and the structure of E8 were first described by Wilhelm Killing in the 1880s.

Applications

E8 appears in string theory, where the heterotic string has gauge group E8 × E8, and in geometry and number theory through the E8 lattice and related modular forms. In 2007, the character table of a split real form of the E8 Lie group was computed by a large international collaboration (the Atlas of Lie Groups project), a computation notable for the size of its output.
