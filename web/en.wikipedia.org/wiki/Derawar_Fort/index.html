Derawar Fort

Derawar Fort is a large square fortress in the Cholistan Desert near Bahawalpur in Punjab, Pakistan. Its forty enormous bastions rise from the desert and are visible for miles around. The fort was first built in the 9th century CE by Rai Jajja Bhati, a Rajput ruler of the Bhati clan, and was rebuilt in its present form in 1732–1733 by the Abbasi Nawab Sadeq Muhammad Khan I.

Description

The fortress forms a square roughly 1.5 kilometres in circumference, with walls about 30 metres high and forty circular bastions, ten on each side, built of fired brick. Inside stand a palace, a mosque modelled on the Moti Masjid of the Red Fort, barracks, and underground passages. Nearby is the marble mausoleum of the Abbasi nawabs, the former rulers of Bahawalpur State, who seized the fort in the 18th century and held it until the state's accession to Pakistan.

Significance

Derawar guarded trade routes across the Cholistan (Rohi) desert and remains the best-preserved of the desert forts of the region. It is on Pakistan's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status and is the site of an annual desert jeep rally.
