Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (IATA: SEA), commonly called Sea–Tac Airport or simply Sea–Tac, is the primary international airport serving Seattle and its metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Washington. It is located in the city of SeaTac, roughly 14 miles (23 km) south of downtown Seattle and 18 miles (29 km) north of Tacoma. Operated by the Port of Seattle, it is the largest airport in the Pacific Northwest and a hub for both Alaska Airlines and Delta Air Lines, offering extensive domestic service and trans-Pacific and transatlantic routes.

History

The airport was built by the Port of Seattle in 1944 to serve the region after the military took over Seattle's Boeing Field during the Second World War. It opened for commercial service in 1947 and received its first scheduled international flights in 1949, earning the "International" designation. The terminal has been expanded repeatedly: the satellite transit system (opened 1973) was among the first airport people-mover systems in the United States; a third runway opened in 2008; and a new International Arrivals Facility, connected by an aerial walkway, opened in 2022. The airport handled over 50 million passengers in 2019, and again surpassed that level in the mid-2020s after the pandemic recovery.

Facilities

Sea–Tac has three parallel north–south runways and a single main terminal with four concourses (A, B, C, D) plus two satellite terminals (N and S) reached by an underground automated transit system. The Link light rail's SeaTac/Airport station connects the airport to downtown Seattle. The airport is recognised for its programme of live music performances and Pacific Northwest art installations.

Airlines and destinations

Alaska Airlines and its regional subsidiary Horizon Air account for the largest share of traffic, followed by Delta, which built Sea–Tac into a trans-Pacific hub from 2014. International destinations include Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei, London, Frankfurt, Amsterdam, Doha and Mexico City, among many others.
