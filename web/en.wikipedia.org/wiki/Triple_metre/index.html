Triple metre

Triple metre (American spelling: triple meter) is a musical metre characterised by a primary division of the beat into three pulses per measure, typically notated with time signatures such as 3/4, 3/8, or compound 9/8. It contrasts with duple metre (two beats per bar), quadruple metre (four), and irregular or mixed metres.

Types

In simple triple metre, such as 3/4, each of the three beats divides into two. In compound triple metre, such as 9/8, each beat is a dotted note dividing into three. Triple metre underlies dances such as the waltz (3/4), the minuet, the mazurka, the sarabande, and the passacaglia, and it is common in slow movements of symphonies and sonatas. Its swinging, circular feel — ONE-two-three — distinguishes it from the march-like regularity of duple time.

History

Triple proportions were prominent in medieval and Renaissance mensural notation, where "tempus perfectum" (triple division) was symbolically associated with the Trinity. Baroque dance suites made the sarabande and minuet fixtures of triple metre, and the Viennese waltz turned it into a popular craze in the 19th century. In poetry, "triple metre" analogously describes verse with three beats per line or trisyllabic feet such as the anapaest and dactyl.
