Emergency medical services

Emergency medical services (EMS), also known as ambulance services or paramedic services, are emergency services that provide urgent pre-hospital treatment and stabilisation for serious illness and injuries and transport to definitive care. They may also be known by other terms, including emergency ambulance service and mobile intensive care.

Organization

EMS systems vary worldwide. In the Anglo-American model, care is delivered primarily by paramedics and emergency medical technicians who bring patients to hospital emergency departments ("load and go" for critical cases, with increasing on-scene treatment). In the Franco-German model, emergency physicians work extensively at the scene ("stay and play"). Most systems operate under medical oversight protocols, with levels of care ranging from basic life support to advanced life support.

Personnel and vehicles

Response units include ambulances, rapid response cars, motorcycle responders, and helicopters and fixed-wing air ambulances. Dispatch is coordinated through emergency telephone numbers such as 911 in North America and 112 in Europe, with call-takers prioritizing incidents using triage algorithms.

History

Organized battlefield casualty evacuation dates to Napoleon's surgeon Dominique Jean Larrey, who introduced "flying ambulances". Modern EMS developed in the 1960s and 1970s, influenced by studies of trauma mortality and pioneered by services in Belfast, Pittsburgh, and Miami.
