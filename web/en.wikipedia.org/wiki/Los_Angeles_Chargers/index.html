The Los Angeles Chargers are a professional American football team based in the Los Angeles metropolitan area. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) West division. The Chargers play their home games at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California, which they share with the Los Angeles Rams.

History

The franchise was founded in 1960 as a charter member of the American Football League (AFL), playing its first season in Los Angeles before moving to San Diego in 1961. As the San Diego Chargers, the team won the AFL championship in 1963 under head coach Sid Gillman and became famous in the late 1970s and early 1980s for the prolific "Air Coryell" passing offence led by quarterback Dan Fouts under coach Don Coryell. The team reached its only Super Bowl in the 1994 season, losing Super Bowl XXIX to the San Francisco 49ers.

After 56 seasons in San Diego, owner Dean Spanos announced in January 2017 that the team would return to Los Angeles, following the failure of efforts to secure a new publicly financed stadium in San Diego. The Chargers played three seasons at the soccer stadium Dignity Health Sports Park in Carson before moving into SoFi Stadium in 2020. Notable players in the Los Angeles era include quarterback Philip Rivers, who spent his final three San Diego and three Los Angeles seasons with the team, and Justin Herbert, drafted sixth overall in 2020 and named NFL Offensive Rookie of the Year. Jim Harbaugh became head coach in 2024.

Rivalries and identity

The Chargers' principal rivals are their AFC West opponents: the Las Vegas Raiders, Kansas City Chiefs and Denver Broncos. The team's colours are powder blue, sunshine gold and white; its original powder blue jerseys, reintroduced as the primary uniform in a 2020 redesign, are widely regarded among the most distinctive in the league.
