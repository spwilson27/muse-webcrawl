Bale Mountains

The Bale Mountains are a mountain range in the Oromia Region of south-eastern Ethiopia, south of the Awash River. They include Tullu Demtu, at 4,377 metres the second-highest point in Ethiopia, and the extensive Sanetti Plateau, one of the largest areas of Afroalpine habitat on the African continent.

Geography and ecology

The mountains rise south of the Bale Zone lowlands and are drained by rivers including the Wabe Shebelle. The northern slopes carry juniper and Hagenia forests, while the southern Harenna forest is one of Ethiopia's last large tracts of natural cloud forest. Above about 3,800 metres, the Sanetti Plateau supports giant lobelias and a high density of rodents, which in turn sustains raptors and the Ethiopian wolf.

Bale Mountains National Park

The Bale Mountains National Park, established in 1970 and covering about 2,200 square kilometres, is a key stronghold of the endangered Ethiopian wolf and the mountain nyala. It is also noted for endemic birds and amphibians. The park was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.

Conservation

Threats to the region include grazing pressure, settlement encroachment, and habitat fragmentation. Conservation programmes work with local communities on sustainable grazing and forest use.
