Segmented mirror

Placeholder image: hexagonal mirror segments of the James Webb Space Telescope.

A segmented mirror is an array of smaller mirrors designed to act together as a single large reflective surface. Segmented designs allow telescopes to be built with primary mirrors far larger than could be cast, transported, or supported as a single piece. The individual segments, often hexagonal, must be aligned to a fraction of the wavelength of light by computer-controlled actuators and edge sensors.

Examples

The twin Keck telescopes in Hawaii pioneered the technique in the 1990s with 10-metre primaries made of 36 segments each. The Gran Telescopio Canarias uses 36 segments over 10.4 metres, and the James Webb Space Telescope's 6.5-metre primary consists of 18 gold-coated beryllium segments that unfold in space. Future extremely large telescopes, such as the Extremely Large Telescope (798 segments, 39.3 metres) and the Thirty Meter Telescope (492 segments), rely entirely on segmentation.
