The MG 42 (Maschinengewehr 42) is a German general-purpose machine gun designed in 1942 and used extensively by the Wehrmacht and Waffen-SS during the second half of the Second World War. Intended to supplement and eventually replace the earlier MG 34, it was manufactured largely from stamped and welded steel parts, making it cheaper and faster to produce.

Design

The MG 42 uses a recoil-operated, roller-locked action derived from work by Werner Gruner and the team at Großfuß AG in Saxony. Its most distinctive characteristic is its extremely high cyclic rate of about 1,200 rounds per minute, which produced a sound compared by Allied soldiers to tearing canvas and led to nicknames such as "Hitler's buzzsaw". The barrel could be changed in seconds through a side-opening gate, an essential feature given the heat generated by sustained fire.

Influence

The design had a lasting influence on post-war machine guns. In West Germany it was rechambered for 7.62×51mm NATO as the MG 1 and, with modifications, the MG 3, which remains in service with many armies. The roller-delayed system influenced other weapons, and the American M60 was among the designs that drew on MG 42 features such as its feed mechanism.

Use

Fitted with a bipod as a light machine gun or mounted on the Lafette tripod as a heavy machine gun, the MG 42 formed the backbone of German infantry firepower from 1942 to 1945. It has remained in service in derivative forms for more than eighty years after its introduction.
