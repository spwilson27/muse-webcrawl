Magnificent Seven (stocks)

Magnificent Seven
MembersAlphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla
Term coined2023
Origin1960 film The Magnificent Seven

The Magnificent Seven is a group of seven high-performing United States technology stocks: Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta Platforms, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla. The term was coined in 2023 by Bank of America analyst Michael Hartnett, borrowing the title of the 1960 Western film, as the group drove most of the gains of the S&P 500 amid enthusiasm for artificial intelligence.

Performance and concentration

In 2023 the seven stocks collectively returned roughly three-quarters in value, vastly outperforming the remaining 493 stocks of the S&P 500, and by 2024 they accounted for around a third of the index's total market capitalisation — a concentration that analysts compared to the dot-com bubble and the earlier "Nifty Fifty" era. Nvidia's rapid rise on demand for AI accelerators made it emblematic of the trend; in June 2024 it briefly became the world's most valuable company.

Predecessor terms

The grouping succeeded earlier labels for big-tech outperformance, including "FAANG" (Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Netflix and Google), which excluded Microsoft and Tesla but included Netflix.

Criticism and debate

Commentators debated whether the group's valuations signalled a bubble, whether passive index investors were becoming dangerously exposed to a handful of names, and whether membership should change as relative performance shifted — for example, Tesla's weakness in early 2024 led some to speak of a "Magnificent Six".
