TIGER, the Topologically Integrated Geographic Encoding and Referencing system, is the digital geographic database developed and maintained by the United States Census Bureau. It describes the location and attributes of the geographic features the Bureau uses to conduct the decennial census and its surveys: roads, railroads, rivers, lakes, legal and statistical boundaries, and the addresses associated with street segments.

History

TIGER was created in the 1980s, in time for the 1990 census, by computerising the Census Bureau's paper map files and the US Geological Survey's digital line graphs. It replaced manual map-based address geocoding and for the first time gave the Bureau a single nationwide topological database. Before the 2010 census the Bureau carried out the MAF/TIGER Accuracy Improvement Project, realigning the database using GPS-collected control points and satellite imagery and greatly improving its positional accuracy.

Data model and distribution

The database is organised around edges (line segments between intersection nodes) with left- and right-side attributes, allowing features such as address ranges and the boundaries of census blocks, tracts and counties to be derived topologically. Extracts are published free of charge as TIGER/Line files, originally in a proprietary record format and since 2008 as shapefiles and geodatabases, updated annually. Because the data are a work of the US federal government, they are in the public domain.

Uses

TIGER data underpin census mapping and the geocoding of census responses. Outside the Bureau they have been widely used as a free source of street and boundary data for GIS applications, and formed an important early source of road data for community mapping projects such as OpenStreetMap in the United States.
