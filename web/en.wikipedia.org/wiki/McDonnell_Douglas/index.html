McDonnell Douglas Corporation was a major American aerospace manufacturing corporation and defence contractor, formed by the merger of McDonnell Aircraft and the Douglas Aircraft Company in 1967. It produced a string of successful commercial and military aircraft, including the DC-10 airliner and the F-15 Eagle fighter, before merging with Boeing in 1997 in a deal valued at about $13 billion.

History

Douglas Aircraft, founded by Donald Douglas in 1921, had built the DC-3 and the DC-8 and DC-9 jetliners, while James Smith McDonnell's St. Louis company was a leading builder of naval and air force fighters such as the F-4 Phantom II. Financial strain on Douglas from the DC-9 and DC-10 programmes led to the 1967 merger.

Programmes

The merged company built the wide-body DC-10, the MD-80 family of twinjets, and the MD-11; military products included the F-15 Eagle, F/A-18 Hornet (with Northrop), the KC-10 Extender tanker, and the C-17 Globemaster III transport. Its missile and space business produced the Delta rocket family and parts of the Harpoon and Tomahawk programmes. The DC-10's early safety record was marred by cargo-door accidents in the 1970s.

Merger with Boeing

After losing major competitions in the 1990s and trimming its commercial line-up, McDonnell Douglas agreed in December 1996 to merge with Boeing; the merger closed on 1 August 1997. The McDonnell Douglas logo — a globe circled by an aircraft, derived from the Douglas emblem — was retained in adapted form by Boeing.
