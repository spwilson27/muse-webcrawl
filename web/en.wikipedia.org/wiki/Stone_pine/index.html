Stone pine

The stone pine (Pinus pinea), also called the Italian stone pine, umbrella pine and parasol pine, is a tree of the pine genus native to the Mediterranean region. It has been cultivated for at least 6,000 years for its edible pine nuts, and its distinctive broad, umbrella-shaped canopy makes it one of the most recognisable trees of Mediterranean landscapes, notably around Rome.

Stone pine
Scientific name: Pinus pinea L.
Family: Pinaceae
Height: 12–35 m
Native range: Mediterranean basin
Conservation status: Least Concern

Description

The stone pine is a coniferous evergreen tree that can exceed 25 metres in height. Young trees form a bushy globe; with age the trunk becomes stout and clear of branches for much of its length, topped by a broad, flattened, umbrella-like crown. The bark is thick, reddish-brown and deeply fissured into vertical plates. Leaves are needles borne in pairs, 10–20 cm long. The cones are broad and heavy, 8–15 cm long, taking an unusually long 36 months to mature. The seeds — the pine nuts — are large, 15–20 mm, with a fragile wing that is ineffective for wind dispersal; they are spread mainly by birds and by humans.

Distribution and history

The prehistoric range of the stone pine is obscured by millennia of cultivation, but it is generally considered native to the Iberian Peninsula and the northern Mediterranean coast. The ancient Romans planted it extensively; stone pines planted along Roman roads and in imperial gardens still define parts of the Italian landscape, and the tree is a symbol of Rome itself — famously pictured in Respighi's tone poem Pines of Rome.

Uses

Pine nuts harvested from the cones are an ingredient of Italian pesto and are eaten across the Mediterranean and Middle East. The tree is also widely planted as an ornamental shade tree in parks, avenues and coastal gardens in Mediterranean-climate regions worldwide, including California, South Africa and Australia. The resinous wood is of modest value, and empty cones are used as decorative fire-lighters and fuel.
