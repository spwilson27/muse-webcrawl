The Man Without Talent (Japanese: 無能の人, Munō no Hito) is a manga by Yoshiharu Tsuge, serialised in the mid-1980s and among his final works. A work of autobiographical watakushi manga ("I-manga"), it depicts a failed cartoonist who has given up drawing and drifts through a series of hopeless money-making schemes while his family's patience wears thin. The English translation, by Ryan Holmberg, was published by New York Review Comics in 2020 and was nominated for major comics awards, including the Eisner Award for Best U.S. Edition of International Material—Asia.

Synopsis

Sukezō Sukegawa, a former gekiga artist modeled on Tsuge himself, has renounced manga. He sells stones gathered from the riverbank—arranged as decorative "suiseki"—at a riverside stall that earns almost nothing, dreams of becoming an auctioneer of antiques despite knowing nothing about them, tries bird-selling, and considers repairing cameras. His wife, once supportive, despairs of his impracticality, and his young son watches the family's fortunes decline. The narrative moves between these episodes and reflections on art, failure, and the refusal of productive life, closing in Tsuge's characteristic mixture of bleakness and dry, self-deprecating humour.

Themes and style

The work is a late flowering of Tsuge's semi-autobiographical mode, following earlier classics such as "Nejishiki" ("Screw Style", 1968). Critics have read it as an unflinching portrait of depression and artistic block, and as a satire of the value placed on talent and productivity. Tsuge's drawings are quiet and economical, lingering on river landscapes, stone textures, and the worn interiors of post-bubble Japan.

Adaptation

The manga was adapted into a 1991 live-action film, Munō no Hito, directed by and starring Naoto Takenaka, which won the FIPRESCI prize at the Venice Film Festival.
