Apple Newton

The Newton is a series of personal digital assistants (PDAs) developed and marketed by Apple Computer, Inc. from 1993 to 1998. The original MessagePad ran the Newton OS and featured handwriting recognition, making it one of the first devices in the class for which Apple executive John Sculley coined the term "personal digital assistant".

History

Development of the Newton platform began in the late 1980s. The first MessagePad was launched in August 1993. Its handwriting recognition was initially criticised and parodied, although it improved markedly in Newton OS 2.0. Later models included the MessagePad 2000 and 2100, and the eMate 300 laptop for education. Despite a devoted user base, the line was discontinued in 1998 after Steve Jobs returned to Apple. The Newton's concepts and its ARM processor heritage influenced later mobile devices, and Newton handwriting recognition technology later appeared in Mac OS X as Inkwell.
