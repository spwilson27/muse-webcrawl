Bolzano

Country: Italy
Region: Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol
Province: South Tyrol
Elevation: 262 m
Population: approx. 107,000
Languages: German, Italian, Ladin

Bolzano (German: Bozen) is the capital city of South Tyrol, an autonomous province in northeastern Italy. Located in a valley basin where the Talvera, Isarco and Adige rivers meet, it is surrounded by the Dolomites and by vineyards and orchards. Bolzano is a bilingual city: roughly three quarters of its inhabitants speak Italian and about a quarter German, with a small Ladin-speaking minority.

History

The area has been settled since prehistoric times — it lies a short distance from where Ötzi the Iceman, a Copper Age mummy now housed in the city's South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology, was found in 1991. The town grew around a Roman way station and a medieval market founded under the counts of Tyrol, prospering as a trading post on the Brenner route between Italy and the German-speaking world. It passed to Austria and remained Habsburg until 1919, when South Tyrol was annexed to Italy after the First World War. Under Fascism the city was heavily Italianised and industrialised; after the Second World War, autonomy statutes gradually restored German-language rights.

Culture and sights

Landmarks include the Gothic cathedral with its latticed spire, the medieval arcades (Lauben) of the old town, Walther Square (named for the minnesinger Walther von der Vogelweide), Maretsch and Runkelstein castles, and the Messner Mountain Museum Firmian founded by mountaineer Reinhold Messner. The Free University of Bozen-Bolzano, founded in 1997, teaches in German, Italian and English.

Economy

Bolzano regularly ranks among Italian cities with the highest quality of life. Its economy rests on services, tourism, retail for the surrounding valleys, and manufacturing including steel and food processing. The surrounding slopes produce wines such as Lagrein and St. Magdalener.
