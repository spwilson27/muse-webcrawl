United Nations Economic Commission for Europe

The United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE) is one of five regional commissions of the United Nations. Established by the Economic and Social Council in 1947 to promote postwar reconstruction and economic cooperation, it is headquartered at the Palais des Nations in Geneva, Switzerland.

Membership and work

UNECE has 56 member states across Europe, North America, the Caucasus and Central Asia, together with Israel. Despite its name, its work extends beyond Europe because Canada, the United States and Central Asian states participate. It provides a forum for policy dialogue, statistics, technical cooperation and the development of norms and conventions.

Standards and conventions

The commission is known for vehicle regulations harmonized through WP.29, trade facilitation standards developed with UN/CEFACT, transport agreements such as the TIR Convention, and statistics used for international comparison. Its environmental treaties include the Aarhus Convention on access to information and public participation, the Espoo Convention on environmental impact assessment, and agreements on air pollution and water. UNECE also works on housing, forestry, energy, innovation and sustainable development.

Role

During the Cold War, UNECE was one of the few institutions in which Eastern and Western states regularly negotiated economic and technical issues. Since 1991 it has supported transition economies and regional integration and now aligns much of its work with the Sustainable Development Goals. Its legal instruments often have effects far beyond its membership because manufacturers and governments adopt its standards globally.
