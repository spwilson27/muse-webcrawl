Twenty20

Twenty20 (abbreviated T20) is a shortened format of cricket in which each team bats for a maximum of 20 overs, producing a match of roughly three hours. It was introduced by the England and Wales Cricket Board in 2003 for inter-county competition.

Format and popularity

The fast pace, big hitting and entertainment-focused presentation of Twenty20 transformed cricket's economics. Franchise leagues such as the Indian Premier League (founded 2008), Australia's Big Bash League and the Caribbean Premier League attract large television audiences and salaries. At international level, the ICC Men's T20 World Cup is held roughly every two years; Twenty20 Internationals (T20Is) are played by all ICC members.

Impact

The format encouraged innovations such as ramp shots and slower-ball variations, data-driven match strategy, and specialist T20 players. Critics argue it threatens the primacy of Test cricket; supporters credit it with expanding the game's global audience and revenues.
