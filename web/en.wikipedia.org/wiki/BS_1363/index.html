BS 1363

BS 1363 is a British Standard which specifies the most common type of single-phase AC power plug and socket used in the United Kingdom. Distinctive characteristics of the system are shutters on the line and neutral socket holes, and a fuse in the plug. The standard was first published in 1947, shortly after the Second World War, and was partly motivated by the need to save copper by wiring houses with ring circuits.

Design

The BS 1363 plug has three rectangular pins: line (live), neutral, and earth. The pins are partially insulated with plastic sleeves near the base, a safety feature introduced to prevent contact with live pins as the plug is withdrawn. The plug contains a cartridge fuse, rated at 3 A, 5 A, or 13 A, which protects the appliance flex rather than the fixed wiring. Sockets incorporate shutters over the line and neutral apertures; the longer earth pin opens the shutters on insertion.

Use

BS 1363 plugs and sockets are used throughout the United Kingdom and in many countries with historical ties to Britain, including Ireland, Malaysia, Singapore, and Hong Kong, where closely related standards apply. The system is commonly paired with ring final circuits rated at 30 A or 32 A.

Safety

The design is widely regarded as one of the safest domestic plug systems in the world, owing to its shuttered sockets, fused plugs, insulated pins, and polarised connections. Critics note that the plugs are bulky and that an exposed plug lying pins-up presents a hazard when stepped on.
