Lemniscate of Bernoulli

The lemniscate of Bernoulli is a plane curve shaped like a figure eight, or infinity symbol, defined as the locus of points the product of whose distances from two fixed points (foci) equals the square of half the distance between the foci. It was first described by Jacob Bernoulli in 1694.

TypeAlgebraic curve
Equation(x² + y²)² = 2a²(x² − y²)
DegreeQuartic
Named forJacob Bernoulli

Equations

In Cartesian coordinates the curve satisfies (x² + y²)² = 2a²(x² − y²), where the foci lie at (±a, 0). In polar coordinates it takes the elegant form r² = 2a² cos 2θ.

Arc length and elliptic integrals

Measuring the arc length of the lemniscate led Giovanni Fagnano and Leonhard Euler to the first investigations of elliptic integrals. The lemniscatic sine and cosine functions, inverses of these integrals, generalise the ordinary trigonometric functions, and the associated constant is the lemniscate constant.

Properties

The lemniscate is the inverse of a rectangular hyperbola with respect to a circle centred at its centre. Its area is 2a². The curve is a special case of the Cassini oval.
