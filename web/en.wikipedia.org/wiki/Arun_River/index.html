Arun River

CountriesChina (Tibet), Nepal
LengthAbout 700 km
SourceTibetan Plateau
MouthJoins the Sun Kosi and Tamur to form the Sapta Kosi

The Arun River is a trans-Himalayan river that rises in Tibet (where it is known as the Pum Qu or Peng Qu) and flows south through Nepal, cutting one of the deepest gorges in the world between the Everest and Makalu massifs. It is the largest trans-Himalayan river passing through Nepal.

Course

The Arun crosses the main Himalayan range and flows through the Sankhuwasabha and Bhojpur districts of eastern Nepal before joining the Sun Kosi and Tamur rivers at Tribeni, forming the Sapta Kosi, which eventually joins the Ganges in India.

Hydroelectricity

The river's steep gradient and high discharge give it enormous hydropower potential, though it remains comparatively undeveloped. Projects such as the Arun-3 hydropower plant, being developed with Indian investment, are intended to supply electricity to Nepal and India. The Arun valley is also a trekking route toward Makalu Base Camp.
