Erfurt

Country: Germany
State: Thuringia
Population: approx. 215,000
First mentioned: 742 AD

Erfurt (German pronunciation: [ˈeːɐ̯fʊʁt]) is the capital and largest city of the Central German state of Thuringia, in the wide valley of the River Gera. Founded in 742 by Saint Boniface, it became an important trading centre in the Middle Ages, famed for the dye plant woad, and was a member of the Hanseatic League.

History

Erfurt's university, founded in 1379 (refounded after closure in the 19th century), was one of Germany's oldest; Martin Luther studied there and entered the Augustinian monastery in 1505. The city was the site of the 1808 Congress of Erfurt between Napoleon and Tsar Alexander I. In 2002 a school shooting at the Gutenberg Gymnasium shocked Germany. The Old Synagogue, dating from around 1100, is among the oldest surviving synagogue buildings in Europe, and the medieval Jewish–Christian heritage of Erfurt was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.

Sights

The ensemble of Erfurt Cathedral and the church of St Severi crowns the Domberg hill above the cathedral square. The Krämerbrücke ("merchants' bridge"), built in 1325, is the longest bridge in Europe lined continuously with inhabited houses. Other landmarks include the Petersberg Citadel, the Augustinian monastery, and the egapark horticultural grounds.

Economy and transport

Erfurt is a logistics hub and a centre for microelectronics and media (the children's channel KiKa is based there). Its main station is a major ICE junction on the Munich–Berlin high-speed line.
