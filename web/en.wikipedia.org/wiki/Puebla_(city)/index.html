Puebla (city)

Full name: Heroica Puebla de Zaragoza
Founded: 16 April 1531
Population: about 1.7 million (city)
State: Puebla, Mexico

Puebla, officially the Heroica Puebla de Zaragoza and formerly known as Puebla de los Ángeles, is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Puebla and one of the five oldest colonial cities in Mexico. It lies in a valley southeast of Mexico City, at an elevation of about 2,100 metres, beneath the volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl.

History

The city was founded in 1531 as a settlement for Spanish colonists on the route between Mexico City and Veracruz. It became famous for the Battle of Puebla on 5 May 1862, when General Ignacio Zaragoza led Mexican forces to victory over the invading French army — the origin of the Cinco de Mayo celebration. French forces took the city after a siege in 1863 but it was retaken in 1867.

Culture and landmarks

Puebla's historic centre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987, is noted for buildings decorated with Talavera tiles, the Baroque Puebla Cathedral, and the Biblioteca Palafoxiana, the oldest public library in the Americas. The city is the birthplace of mole poblano, chiles en nogada, and the china poblana costume tradition. Major industries include the large Volkswagen automobile plant.
