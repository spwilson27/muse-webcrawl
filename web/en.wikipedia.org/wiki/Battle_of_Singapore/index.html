The Battle of Singapore, also known as the Fall of Singapore, was fought in the South-East Asian theatre of World War II when the Empire of Japan invaded the British stronghold of Singapore. The fighting in Singapore lasted from 8 to 15 February 1942 and culminated in the surrender of some 80,000 British, Indian, and Australian troops — the largest surrender of British-led forces in history.

Singapore, the main British military base and economic port in Southeast Asia, had been the keystone of British defense planning (the "Singapore strategy"). Its fall followed the rapid Japanese conquest of the Malay Peninsula in December 1941 – January 1942, during which the Royal Navy's Force Z, including HMS Prince of Wales and HMS Repulse, was sunk by Japanese aircraft.

Background

Japanese forces of the Twenty-fifth Army under Lieutenant General Tomoyuki Yamashita landed in Malaya on 8 December 1941 and advanced swiftly down the peninsula, using bicycles, light tanks, and close air support. Allied forces under Lieutenant General Arthur Percival fell back across the Johore Strait to Singapore island by the end of January 1942. The naval base's great 15-inch guns, built to repel a seaborne attack, faced the wrong way — though contrary to popular myth they could be, and were, turned landward, albeit with mostly armour-piercing shells ill-suited to use against infantry.

The assault

On the night of 8–9 February, Japanese troops crossed the strait in barges and landed on the island's northwest coast, overwhelming the Australian positions at the Battle of Sarimbun Beach. Subsequent fighting at Kranji and for the high ground of Bukit Timah — including the reservoirs on which the city's water supply depended — went against the defenders. On 13–14 February, at Pasir Panjang ("Opium Hill"), men of the Malay Regiment under Lieutenant Adnan Saidi fought a celebrated last stand and were massacred after being overrun. Japanese forces were in fact short of supplies and ammunition, but Yamashita maintained relentless pressure and bluffed about his strength.

Surrender and aftermath

With water supplies failing and the city packed with refugees, Percival surrendered unconditionally on 15 February 1942 — Chinese New Year. Prime Minister Winston Churchill called the fall of Singapore the "worst disaster and largest capitulation in British history". The captured troops were sent to Changi and many were later used as forced labour on projects such as the Burma Railway. Under the Japanese occupation (Singapore was renamed Syonan-to), the Sook Ching massacres killed thousands of ethnic Chinese civilians. Singapore remained occupied until the Japanese surrender in September 1945.
