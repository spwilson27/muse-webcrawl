Overview

Monreale is a town in the Metropolitan City of Palermo, Sicily, on the slope of Mount Caputo overlooking the fertile Conca d'Oro plain. It is world-famous for its cathedral, founded by the Norman king William II in 1174, one of the supreme monuments of Norman-Arab-Byzantine art.

The cathedral

Monreale Cathedral contains about 6,341 square metres of gold-ground mosaics depicting the Old and New Testaments — the largest Byzantine mosaic cycle in Italy — and a celebrated Romanesque cloister with carved twin columns, each pair unique. It forms part of the UNESCO serial site "Arab-Norman Palermo and the Cathedral Churches of Cefalù and Monreale" (2015). William II and his father William I are buried there.
