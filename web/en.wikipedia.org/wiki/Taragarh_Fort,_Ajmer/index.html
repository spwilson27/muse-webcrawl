Taragarh Fort, Ajmer

Taragarh Fort is a fortress crowning the Nagpahari hill above the city of Ajmer in Rajasthan, India. One of the oldest hill forts in the country, it commands wide views over the city and the surrounding Aravalli hills, and should not be confused with the later Taragarh Fort at Bundi.

History

Tradition attributes the fort's foundation to the Chauhan king Ajayaraja II, founder of Ajmer, in the 8th–12th century period of Chauhan rule; it was traditionally said to have been named after Taragarh, and a shrine within is associated with Mira Sayyid Hussain, a governor who died fighting in the 13th century. The fort guarded the approach to Ajmer, a strategically coveted city that later came under the Delhi Sultanate, the Mughals and finally the British, who used the fort as a sanatorium for troops.

Architecture

The fort's massive stone ramparts follow the crest of the hill, with large bastions and reservoirs cut into the rock to store water for a besieged garrison. Within the walls stand the tomb and dargah of Miran Sayyid Hussain and the remains of palaces and barracks from successive occupations.

Access

A steep paved road and footpaths climb from Ajmer to the fort, which remains a popular viewpoint over the city and Ana Sagar lake below.
