Air vice-marshal

Country: United Kingdom and many Commonwealth air forces
Rank group: Air officer
NATO code: OF-7 (two-star rank)
Next higher: Air marshal
Next lower: Air commodore

Air vice-marshal (abbreviated AVM) is a two-star air officer rank in the Royal Air Force and many Commonwealth and other air forces. It ranks above air commodore and immediately below air marshal, and is equivalent to a rear admiral in the Royal Navy and a major general in the British Army.

History

The rank was introduced with the RAF's distinctive air rank titles in August 1919, adapted loosely from Royal Navy ranks. Air vice-marshals typically command groups or hold senior staff appointments. The rank insignia consists of a narrow light-blue band above a broad light-blue band on a black background, worn on the sleeve or shoulder. In the RAF, the command flag of an air vice-marshal is rectangular with two stars. Notable holders include wartime group commanders such as Trafford Leigh-Mallory and Keith Park, who commanded No. 11 Group during the Battle of Britain.

Use in other air forces

The rank (or its translation) is used in the air forces of Australia, New Zealand, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Ghana, Nigeria, and several other countries that modelled their rank structures on the RAF. In some services the title differs — for example "major general" is used by the United States Air Force for the equivalent grade.
