The Bloc Québécois (BQ; French pronunciation: [blɔk kebekwa], "Quebec Bloc") is a federal political party in Canada devoted to Quebec nationalism and the promotion of Quebec sovereignty. It was founded in 1991 by Lucien Bouchard and other Members of Parliament who left the Progressive Conservative and Liberal parties after the failure of the Meech Lake Accord. The Bloc fields candidates only in Quebec, where it has been one of the province's strongest federal parties; it is informally linked, though not formally affiliated, with the provincial Parti Québécois.

History

In the 1993 federal election, its first, the Bloc won 54 seats and — despite running candidates in only one province — became Her Majesty's Loyal Opposition, with Bouchard as Leader of the Opposition. Bouchard left in 1996 to lead the Parti Québécois and become Premier of Quebec. Under Gilles Duceppe (1997–2011) the party remained the dominant federal force in Quebec through the 2000s. In the 2011 election, swept aside by the NDP "orange wave" in Quebec, the Bloc collapsed to four seats and Duceppe resigned.

Revival

After several short-lived leaders, Yves-François Blanchet became leader in 2019; the Bloc rebounded to 32 seats in both the 2019 and 2021 elections, re-establishing itself as the second-largest party in Quebec in the House of Commons and a pivotal bloc in minority parliaments. In the 2025 election, dominated by questions of Canadian sovereignty amid trade conflict with the United States, the Bloc was reduced to 22 seats but retained its role as the voice of Quebec's interests in Ottawa.

Policies

The Bloc advocates Quebec sovereignty, protection of the French language (supporting Quebec's Bill 101 and Bill 96), provincial jurisdiction in areas such as health care and culture, and broadly social-democratic positions on federal issues affecting Quebec. Because it cannot form a government, it presents itself as accountable only to Quebec voters and free of the compromises of pan-Canadian parties.

Leaders

Lucien Bouchard (1991–1996), Michel Gauthier (1996–1997), Gilles Duceppe (1997–2011), Daniel Paillé (2011–2013), Mario Beaulieu (2014–2015), Rhéal Fortin (interim), Martine Ouellet (2017–2018), and Yves-François Blanchet (2019–present).
