Steve Prefontaine

Born: January 25, 1951, Coos Bay, Oregon, U.S.

Died: May 30, 1975 (aged 24), Eugene, Oregon, U.S.

Sport: Track and field (long-distance running)

Steve Roland Prefontaine (January 25, 1951 – May 30, 1975) was an American long-distance runner who competed in the 1972 Summer Olympics. Dubbed "Pre", he was a pioneer of the 1970s running boom in the United States. During his career, Prefontaine won seven NCAA national titles at the University of Oregon and set American records in every long-distance event from 2,000 to 10,000 meters.

Prefontaine was known for his aggressive front-running style and charismatic personality, which helped popularize running as a spectator sport. He was coached at Oregon by Bill Bowerman, the co-founder of Nike.

Career

At Marshfield High School in Coos Bay, Oregon, Prefontaine set a national high-school record for two miles. At the University of Oregon he won three NCAA cross country championships and four consecutive NCAA three-mile titles. At the 1972 Munich Olympics he finished fourth in the 5,000 meters after leading much of the race.

Death and legacy

On May 30, 1975, after a post-race party in Eugene, Prefontaine died in a car accident at the age of 24. The spot of the crash, marked by a memorial known as "Pre's Rock", has become a pilgrimage site for runners. His life has been portrayed in the films Prefontaine (1997) and Without Limits (1998), and the annual Prefontaine Classic track meet at Hayward Field in Eugene is named in his honor.
