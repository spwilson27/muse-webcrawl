Cyanuric chloride

Cyanuric chloride
Formula: C3Cl3N3
Appearance: white powder
Also called: 2,4,6-trichloro-1,3,5-triazine

Cyanuric chloride is an organic compound with the formula (NCCl)3. This white solid is the chlorinated derivative of 1,3,5-triazine and the trimer of cyanogen chloride.

Production and uses

It is prepared by trimerization of cyanogen chloride over a carbon catalyst at elevated temperature. Cyanuric chloride is a versatile reagent: its three chlorine atoms react successively at different temperatures, allowing stepwise substitution. It is the precursor to the widely used triazine herbicides such as atrazine and simazine, and to reactive dyes that bond covalently to cellulose fibres.

Reactivity

The compound hydrolyses readily to cyanuric acid. In organic synthesis it is used as a dehydrating agent, for converting alcohols to alkyl chlorides, and for activating carboxylic acids in peptide coupling.
