Tetramethylsilane

Tetramethylsilane (abbreviated TMS) is the organosilicon compound with the formula Si(CH3)4. It is the simplest tetraorganosilane and a structural analogue of neopentane with silicon at the centre. TMS is a building block in organometallic chemistry and a byproduct of the production of methyl chlorosilanes.

Use in NMR spectroscopy

Tetramethylsilane is best known as the internal standard for calibrating chemical shift in 1H, 13C and 29Si NMR spectroscopy, where its resonance is defined as 0.00 ppm. All twelve of its protons are chemically equivalent, giving a sharp singlet that lies upfield of most organic compounds because silicon is less electronegative than carbon and shields the methyl protons. Its volatility (boiling near room temperature) makes it easy to remove from samples.

Synthesis and reactions

TMS arises in the direct process (Müller–Rochow process), in which methyl chloride reacts with silicon–copper alloy to give methylchlorosilanes; TMS is a minor byproduct. It is extremely inert, resembling a hydrocarbon, but reacts with butyllithium to give trimethylsilyl lithium after deprotonation chemistry, and its chloride analogue, trimethylsilyl chloride, is a widely used silylating agent.
