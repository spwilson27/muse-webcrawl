Pyongyang Metro

Pyongyang Metro
Location: Pyongyang, North Korea
Opened: 6 September 1973
Lines: 2 (Chollima, Hyoksin)
Stations: 16
Depth: about 110 m — among the deepest metros in the world

The Pyongyang Metro is a rapid transit system in Pyongyang, the capital of North Korea. It consists of two lines — the Chollima Line and the Hyoksin Line — with a combined 16 stations. Construction began in 1968 with technical assistance from China and the Soviet Union, and the first section opened in 1973.

Stations

The stations are among the deepest of any metro system, lying roughly 110 metres underground, a design that allows them to double as bomb shelters; long escalator rides connect the platforms to the surface. Stations are named for revolutionary themes rather than geography — names such as Glory, Torch, Reunification, and Paradise — and are decorated with mosaics, murals, and sculptures celebrating the country's leaders and revolution. Foreign visitors are typically shown only a limited portion of the network, which long fuelled speculation about its true extent, though full rides for tourists have been permitted at times.

Rolling stock

The original trains were built in China, while later services used carriages acquired from former Berlin U-Bahn stock (types D and GI); domestically produced trains, including the more modern "Taeyang" type, have since entered service. Trains run at intervals of several minutes during peak hours.
