In Greek mythology, Selene (Greek: Σελήνη, "Moon") is the goddess and personification of the Moon, one of the Titans' children: daughter of Hyperion and Theia, and sister of Helios, the sun, and Eos, the dawn. She is traditionally depicted driving a chariot of white horses or bulls across the night sky, wearing a crescent moon upon her head, and was worshipped at the new and full moons.

Mythology

Selene's most famous myth is her love for the mortal shepherd-prince Endymion of Elis (or Mount Latmus in Caria). Struck by his beauty as he slept, she asked Zeus to grant him eternal youth and eternal sleep, and descended nightly to lie beside him; by him she was said to have fifty daughters, the Menae, associated with the lunar months. The Homeric Hymn to Selene describes her bathing in the ocean before rising, her rays illuminating the earth. She was also sometimes linked with Pan, who was said to have seduced her wrapped in a white sheepskin.

Worship and identification

Selene's independent cult was modest, and from the classical period onward she was increasingly identified with Artemis, goddess of the hunt and the moon, just as her brother Helios merged with Apollo; in Roman religion she corresponds to Luna. Her name gives the element selenium and the scientific term selenology, the study of the Moon.
