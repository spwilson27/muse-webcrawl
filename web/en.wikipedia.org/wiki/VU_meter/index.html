VU meter

A VU meter is used to measure variations in audio signal level. It is an instrument commonly used in recording studios, broadcasting, and audio equipment to display signal levels in volume units (VU). The VU meter was standardized in the United States in 1942, through a joint effort of Bell Telephone Laboratories, CBS, and NBC.

Characteristics

The VU meter has a specific ballistics characteristic: its needle takes 300 milliseconds to reach 99% of its steady-state deflection when a 1,000 Hz sine wave at 0 VU is suddenly applied, and it overshoots by 1 to 1.5 percent. This relatively slow response means the meter indicates average program level rather than peak level.

The standard scale shows 0 VU at the top of the "normal" range, with red markings above 0 VU and black below. The 0 VU reference corresponds to a power level depending on the standard in use: +4 dBu in professional audio or 0 dBm into 600 ohms in older telephone and broadcast practice.

Use

VU meters were once ubiquitous on mixing consoles, tape recorders, and broadcast equipment. Although largely replaced by LED and digital peak meters, they remain valued for showing perceived loudness, since their averaging behavior correlates more closely with human hearing than instantaneous peak meters.
