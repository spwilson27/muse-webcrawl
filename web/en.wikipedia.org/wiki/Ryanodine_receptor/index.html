Ryanodine receptor

IsoformsRyR1 (skeletal muscle), RyR2 (cardiac muscle), RyR3 (widespread)
LocationSarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum membrane
FunctionRelease of Ca2+ from intracellular stores
Named forRyanodine, a plant alkaloid that binds the channel

Ryanodine receptors (RyRs) are a class of intracellular calcium channels found in the membranes of the sarcoplasmic and endoplasmic reticulum of animal cells. They mediate the release of calcium ions from intracellular stores, a key step in excitation–contraction coupling in muscle and in many other signalling processes. At over 2 megadaltons, they are among the largest known ion channels.

Isoforms and function

Mammals have three isoforms. RyR1 predominates in skeletal muscle, where it is mechanically coupled to the dihydropyridine receptor and opens when the sarcolemma depolarises. RyR2 predominates in heart muscle and opens by calcium-induced calcium release. RyR3 is expressed more widely, including in neurons and smooth muscle. The channel is activated by cytosolic Ca2+, ATP and caffeine, and inhibited by Mg2+ and the drug dantrolene.

Clinical significance

Mutations in RYR1 cause malignant hyperthermia susceptibility and central core disease; mutations in RYR2 cause catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia and arrhythmogenic right ventricular dysplasia, potentially fatal heart rhythm disorders. Ryanodine itself, extracted from the plant Ryania speciosa, binds the channel with high affinity and was historically used as an insecticide; synthetic diamide insecticides targeting insect RyRs remain in wide agricultural use.
