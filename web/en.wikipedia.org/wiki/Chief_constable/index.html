Chief constable

A chief constable is the head of a territorial police force in the United Kingdom and the senior operational police officer in most forces in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Chief constables hold the office of constable under the Crown and enjoy operational independence: no politician or police authority may direct them in individual law-enforcement decisions.

Role and powers

The chief constable has direction and control of the force, is responsible for its operational effectiveness and efficiency, and appoints and disciplines its officers. In England and Wales, since 2012 the elected police and crime commissioner holds the chief constable to account, sets the budget and police and crime plan, and may require the chief constable to resign or retire — but operational decisions remain the chief constable's alone, a convention rooted in case law such as R v Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis, ex parte Blackburn.

Exceptions

The two London forces use different titles: the Metropolitan Police is headed by a Commissioner (the highest police rank in the UK) and the City of London Police likewise by a Commissioner. Non-geographic forces such as the British Transport Police and Ministry of Defence Police are also headed by chief constables.

Rank structure

Below the chief constable serve deputy chief constables and assistant chief constables, then the chief superintendent, superintendent, chief inspector, inspector, sergeant and constable ranks.
