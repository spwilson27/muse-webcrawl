Điện Biên is a province in the Northwest region of Vietnam, bordering Laos to the west and south and the Vietnamese provinces of Sơn La and Lai Châu. Its capital, Điện Biên Phủ, occupies the broad Mường Thanh valley and is famous as the site of the decisive 1954 battle of the First Indochina War. The province was created on 1 January 2004 when Lai Châu Province was divided into a new, smaller Lai Châu and Điện Biên.

Geography

Điện Biên is mountainous, with most terrain above 500 metres, dissected by the upper reaches of the Đà (Black) and Mã rivers and by tributaries of the Mekong. The Mường Thanh valley, about 20 km long, is the province's agricultural heartland, producing the renowned Điện Biên rice. Pha Đin Pass, on the border with Sơn La, is one of Vietnam's celebrated mountain passes.

History

The region was historically inhabited and governed by Tai peoples, notably the Tai Dam (Black Tai) of Mường Thanh, and paid tribute variously to Vietnam, Laos, and Burma. France incorporated it into French Indochina in the late nineteenth century. In 1954 the Việt Minh under Võ Nguyên Giáp besieged the French garrison in the valley at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu (13 March – 7 May 1954), a victory that precipitated the Geneva Accords and the end of French Indochina. Battle sites — A1 Hill, the De Castries bunker, and the war cemetery — are preserved as national relics.

People and economy

The province is ethnically diverse: Thái people form the largest group, alongside Hmong, Khmu, Dao, and Kinh (ethnic Vietnamese) communities. The economy is mainly agricultural — rice, maize, coffee, tea and livestock — supplemented by border trade with Laos through the Tây Trang crossing and by historical tourism centred on Điện Biên Phủ city and its airport.
