Market liquidity

In business and economics, market liquidity is the degree to which an asset or security can be quickly bought or sold in the market without affecting its price. A market is liquid when there are many buyers and sellers, transactions can be executed rapidly, and bid–ask spreads are narrow.

Characteristics

Liquidity is commonly described through dimensions of tightness (low transaction costs and narrow spreads), depth (large volumes can trade near current prices), immediacy (speed of execution) and resilience (prices recover quickly after a disturbance).

Importance

Liquid markets lower the cost of capital and allow investors to adjust portfolios efficiently. Conversely, a liquidity crisis — when funding dries up and assets can only be sold at fire-sale prices — played a central role in the 2008 financial crisis. Central banks often act as lenders of last resort to preserve market liquidity.
