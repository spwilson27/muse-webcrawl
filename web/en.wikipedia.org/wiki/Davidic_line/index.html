Davidic line

The Davidic line or House of David (Hebrew: בית דוד, romanized: Beit David) is the lineage of the Israelite king David. In Judaism it is based on texts from the Hebrew Bible, as well as later Jewish traditions. According to the biblical narrative, the kings of Israel and Judah after David traced their descent to him, and the promise in 2 Samuel 7 holds that God would establish David's throne forever.

Biblical account

The Books of Samuel and Kings describe David's descendants ruling in Jerusalem until the Babylonian destruction of the First Temple in 586 BCE. Genealogies in the Books of Chronicles extend the line into the Persian period, naming figures such as Zerubbabel.

Later traditions

In Judaism, the Messiah is expected to be a member of the Davidic line (the "Son of David"). In Christianity, the Gospels of Matthew and Luke present genealogies tracing Jesus's descent from David. Claims of Davidic descent were also made by the Babylonian Exilarchs and various medieval Jewish families.
