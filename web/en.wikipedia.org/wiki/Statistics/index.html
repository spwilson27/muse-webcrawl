Statistics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Statistics is the discipline that concerns the collection, organisation, analysis, interpretation and presentation of data. It includes designing surveys and experiments, summarising data with descriptive statistics, and drawing conclusions about populations from samples using probability theory — with applications in science, industry, government and everyday decision-making.

Contents1. Foundations
2. History

Foundations

Probability underpins statistical inference: confidence intervals, hypothesis tests and regression models quantify uncertainty. Bayesian statistics treats probability as degree of belief and updates it with data; frequentist methods dominate in many sciences. Modern statistics overlaps heavily with machine learning and data science.

History

Statistics grew from state record-keeping ('statistics' originally meant facts about the state), the probability theory of Pascal and Fermat, and the work of Galton, Pearson and Fisher, who founded much of modern statistical practice in the early 20th century.
