Interactive fiction

A text adventure displaying a room description and awaiting a typed command.

Interactive fiction (IF) is software simulating environments in which players use text commands to control characters and influence the story. Some works also include graphics or menus, but the classic form — the text adventure — presents prose descriptions and parses typed input such as take lantern or go north.

History

The genre began with Colossal Cave Adventure (1976) by Will Crowther and Don Woods, and was commercialised by Infocom, whose titles such as Zork (1980) and The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (1984) were bestsellers in the early home-computer era. The commercial market collapsed in the late 1980s, but hobbyist communities continued developing authoring systems such as Inform and TADS. From the 2000s, Twine enabled hypertext-based interactive stories by non-programmers, and the annual Interactive Fiction Competition (IFComp) has run since 1995.

Mechanics

Parser-based IF interprets natural-language-like commands against a simulated world model of rooms, objects, and rules. Choice-based IF instead offers explicit links or menu options. Common themes include puzzles, exploration, and narrative branching.

Notable works

Notable titles include Zork, Trinity, Anchorhead, Photopia, Counterfeit Monkey, and commercial revivals such as 80 Days. Modern IF overlaps with the visual novel and narrative video game genres.
