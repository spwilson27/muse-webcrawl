Princes' Gates

The Princes' Gates, the ceremonial entrance to Exhibition Place in Toronto.
LocationExhibition Place, Toronto, Ontario, Canada
Opened1927
ArchitectChapman and Oxley
StyleBeaux-Arts

The Princes' Gates is a triumphal arch monumental gateway at Exhibition Place in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. It serves as the eastern entrance to the grounds of the Canadian National Exhibition (CNE), the annual fair held at the site each summer.

The gates were built to commemorate the 60th anniversary of Canadian Confederation and were officially opened on August 30, 1927, by Edward, Prince of Wales, and Prince George, Duke of Kent — the two princes after whom the structure is named.

Design

Designed by the architectural firm of Chapman and Oxley, the gates are built in the Beaux-Arts style. The structure consists of a central archway flanked by curved colonnades of Ionic columns extending to either side. The central arch is topped by a large sculptural figure known as the "Goddess of Winged Victory," and the pylons at each end are adorned with carvings representing the industries and activities of Canada.

History

The Princes' Gates replaced an earlier entrance to the exhibition grounds and quickly became one of Toronto's best-known landmarks. The central figure was sculpted by Charles Adamson. In 1987 the original winged figure, which had deteriorated, was replaced with a polymer-based replica, and the original was placed in storage.

The gates stand at the foot of Strachan Avenue, and the area south of them leads toward the Toronto waterfront along Lake Ontario. During the CNE and other events at Exhibition Place, the gates remain the principal pedestrian entrance to the grounds.
