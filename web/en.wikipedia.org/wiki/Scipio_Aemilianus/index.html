Scipio Aemilianus

Publius Cornelius Scipio Africanus Aemilianus (185–129 BC), known as Scipio Aemilianus or Scipio Africanus the Younger, was a Roman general and statesman, famed for conquering and destroying Carthage in the Third Punic War (146 BC) and for capturing Numantia in Spain (133 BC).

Scipio Aemilianus
Born: 185 BC
Died: 129 BC, Rome
Consul: 147 BC, 134 BC
Battles: Siege of Carthage, Siege of Numantia

Family

Born into the Aemilii Paulli, he was adopted by Publius Cornelius Scipio, son of Scipio Africanus, the victor over Hannibal. He married Sempronia, sister of the Gracchi.

Career

Elected consul for 147 BC though underage, he took command in Africa, besieged Carthage, and in spring 146 BC stormed and destroyed the city, ending the Punic Wars. As consul again in 134 BC he restored discipline in Spain and starved Numantia into surrender.

Circle and death

Scipio patronised a circle of writers and thinkers including Polybius, Panaetius and Terence — the "Scipionic Circle" of Cicero's dialogues. He opposed some Gracchan reforms and died suddenly in 129 BC, amid rumours of foul play that were never proven.
