Kazimierz

Kazimierz (Yiddish: קוזמיר, Kuzmir) is a historical district of Kraków, Poland. Founded in 1335 by King Casimir III the Great as a separate chartered town on an island south of the Old Town, it became the centre of Jewish life in Poland after Jews expelled from Kraków resettled there in 1495. Today it is one of Kraków's most visited quarters, known for its synagogues, Jewish cemeteries and café culture.

History

The original Christian Kazimierz centred on the Corpus Christi Church and the town hall on Plac Wolnica. The Jewish quarter (Oppidum Judaeorum), walled off at Szeroka Street, grew into one of Europe's great centres of Jewish scholarship: the Remah Synagogue and cemetery preserve the tomb of the 16th-century rabbi Moses Isserles, and the Old Synagogue (c. 1557) is the oldest surviving synagogue in Poland. By the 1930s some 64,000 Jews lived in Kraków, most in Kazimierz. In 1941 the Nazis sealed the community into the Podgórze ghetto across the river; most were murdered at Płaszów and Auschwitz.

Revival

Long neglected under communist rule, Kazimierz revived dramatically after 1989, aided by Steven Spielberg's filming of Schindler's List (1993) on location there. The annual Jewish Culture Festival, founded in 1988, is one of the largest of its kind, and the district now mixes memorial sites with restaurants, galleries and nightlife.
