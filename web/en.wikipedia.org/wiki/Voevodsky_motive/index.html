Voevodsky motive

In algebraic geometry, a Voevodsky motive (also called a pure or mixed motive in Voevodsky's sense) is an object of the triangulated category of motives constructed by Vladimir Voevodsky in the 1990s. The construction gave the first fully satisfactory category of mixed motives over a field, fulfilling a program envisioned by Alexander Grothendieck, and it became the foundation of modern motivic homotopy theory.

Construction

Voevodsky's construction begins with the category of smooth schemes over a perfect field k. One first forms complexes of presheaves with transfers — presheaves equipped with additional "correspondence" maps generalizing algebraic cycles — and then localizes with respect to the Nisnevich topology and A¹-homotopy invariance. The resulting derived category DM(k) contains the category of Chow motives and admits the six operations familiar from étale cohomology.

Properties and applications

Within DM(k), every variety has a motive M(X), and cohomology theories such as algebraic K-theory, motivic cohomology, and étale cohomology are represented by objects of the category. Voevodsky used the theory to prove the Milnor conjecture on quadratic forms and the Bloch–Kato conjecture on Galois cohomology, work for which he received the Fields Medal in 2002. The t-structure on the subcategory of geometric motives relates to mixed Hodge theory, while the associated stable homotopy category SH(k) founded by Voevodsky and Fabien Morel underlies motivic homotopy theory.
