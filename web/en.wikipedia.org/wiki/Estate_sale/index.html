Estate sale

An estate sale or estate liquidation is a sale or auction to dispose of a substantial portion of the contents of a home and the personal property of a person who has died, is downsizing, divorcing, relocating, or liquidating assets. Unlike a garage sale, an estate sale typically involves the complete contents of a household — furniture, antiques, collectibles, jewellery, vehicles, and everyday items — and is often conducted by a professional estate sale company.

Process

Professional liquidators are usually engaged by the executor of an estate or the homeowner's family. The company organises, prices, photographs, and advertises the property, then conducts the sale over one to three days, usually inside the home, with items tagged and rooms arranged for browsing. Companies typically charge a commission of 25–50 percent of gross proceeds. Unsold items may afterwards be donated, consigned, or removed by buyout or clean-out services.

Sales are commonly advertised through dedicated listing websites, mailing lists, and local classifieds, and attract antique dealers, resellers, and collectors, some of whom queue for numbered entry tickets on the first morning. Prices are commonly reduced on successive days — for example, 25 percent off on the second day and 50 percent off on the final day.

Legal aspects

Estate sales differ from auctions of a deceased person's estate ordered by a probate court, though the terms are sometimes conflated. Where property is subject to probate, the executor's authority to sell derives from the court; privately arranged liquidations of a living person's property require no court involvement. Some jurisdictions require estate sale companies to hold business licences or second-hand dealer permits.
