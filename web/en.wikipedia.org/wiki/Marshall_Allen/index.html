Marshall Belford Allen (born May 25, 1924) is an American free jazz and avant-garde jazz alto saxophonist. He is best known as a longtime member — and, since 1995, the leader — of the Sun Ra Arkestra, the ensemble founded by the composer and bandleader Sun Ra. Allen joined the Arkestra in the 1950s and has remained with it for more than six decades, continuing to tour and record into his second century.

Early life

Allen was born in Louisville, Kentucky. During World War II he served in the U.S. Army, stationed in Paris, where he studied alto saxophone at the Paris Conservatory and played in dance bands. After returning to the United States, he settled in Chicago, playing with R&B and jump-blues groups before being drawn into Sun Ra's orbit around 1955–1957.

With the Sun Ra Arkestra

Allen became a fixture of the Arkestra during its Chicago, New York and Philadelphia periods, contributing alto saxophone, flute, oboe and piccolo, and occasionally the morrow and other instruments. He appears on scores of Arkestra recordings, from Jazz in Silhouette (1959) through the band's most experimental 1960s work. Known for his ability to produce extreme registers, multiphonics and piercing upper harmonics on the alto, Allen became one of the defining instrumental voices of the group alongside tenor saxophonist John Gilmore.

After Sun Ra's death in 1993, leadership passed briefly to Gilmore (d. 1995), and then to Allen, who has directed the Arkestra ever since, preserving its repertoire, costumes and "space is the place" philosophy while developing new material. Under Allen the Arkestra has continued to perform internationally and release albums, including Living Sky (2022) and Lights on a Satellite (2024). Allen celebrated his 100th birthday in May 2024 while still actively leading the band, and in 2025 released recordings marking his centenary, making him one of the oldest working bandleaders in jazz history.

Outside work

Allen has recorded as a sideman and leader outside the Arkestra, collaborating with musicians across the free-jazz and improvised-music scenes, and contributed to recordings by younger artists influenced by Sun Ra's legacy.
