Corn Du

Elevation: 873 m (2,864 ft)
Location: Brecon Beacons, Powys, Wales
Parent peak: Pen y Fan

Corn Du is a mountain in the Brecon Beacons National Park in Wales, the second-highest peak in southern Wales after its neighbour Pen y Fan. Its summit is 873 metres (2,864 ft) above sea level. The name is Welsh and means "black horn."

Geography

Corn Du lies immediately southwest of Pen y Fan, to which it is connected by a broad ridge. Together the two summits form the dramatic northern escarpment of the Brecon Beacons, overlooking the Usk valley and the town of Brecon. The mountain is composed of Old Red Sandstone, and its flat-topped summit is a typical feature of the range.

The Tommy Jones obelisk, a memorial to a five-year-old boy who died on the mountain in 1900, stands on the slopes below the summit and is a landmark on the popular walking route from the Storey Arms on the A470.

Access

The most popular ascent follows the well-made path from Pont ar Daf car park on the A470, reaching the summit in under an hour for fit walkers, and most visitors continue along the ridge to Pen y Fan. The hills are used for military training by the British armed forces, including the Special Air Service selection courses.
