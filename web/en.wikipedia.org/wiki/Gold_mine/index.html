Gold mine

A gold mine is a mine that produces gold. Gold mines exploit deposits of gold-bearing ore, which occur either as lode (hard rock) deposits in veins within rock, or as placer deposits of gold particles accumulated in sediments. Gold mining has been practised since prehistoric times and today takes place on every continent except Antarctica.

Types of mining

Surface methods include open-pit mining of low-grade disseminated ores and placer mining in streams and gravels, historically by panning, sluicing and dredging. Underground mining follows rich quartz veins deep into the earth; South Africa's Mponeng mine, the world's deepest, extends nearly 4 kilometres below the surface. The largest producing mines in recent years include the Nevada Gold Mines complex in the United States, Muruntau in Uzbekistan, Grasberg in Indonesia, and Olimpiada in Russia.

Extraction

Extracted ore is crushed and the gold recovered by gravity separation, flotation, and chemical methods — most commonly cyanidation, in which a dilute cyanide solution dissolves the gold, which is then recovered by adsorption onto activated carbon (carbon-in-pulp) or zinc precipitation (Merrill–Crowe). Amalgamation with mercury, once widespread, is now discouraged because of its severe environmental and health effects, though it persists in artisanal mining.

Environmental and social issues

Gold mining can produce large volumes of waste rock and tailings, and incidents such as tailings dam failures and cyanide spills have caused serious environmental damage. Artisanal and small-scale gold mining employs millions of people worldwide but is associated with mercury pollution and hazardous working conditions.
