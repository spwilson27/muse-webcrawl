West Highland Line

The West Highland Line is a railway line in Scotland linking Glasgow with the western Highlands, running to Oban and to Fort William and Mallaig. It is widely regarded as one of the most scenic railway lines in the world.

Route

Trains leave Glasgow Queen Street and follow the River Clyde to Helensburgh before climbing through the hills of Argyll. At Crianlarich the line divides: one branch descends to Oban on the Firth of Lorn, while the main line continues across the wilderness of Rannoch Moor — where Corrour station, Britain's highest and one of its most remote, stands ten miles from the nearest public road — to Fort William beneath Ben Nevis. The extension from Fort William to the fishing port of Mallaig, opened in 1901, crosses the celebrated Glenfinnan Viaduct, a 21-arch concrete structure made famous worldwide by the Harry Potter films.

Services

ScotRail operates diesel services over the line, including the Caledonian Sleeper from London Euston to Fort William. In summer, the steam-hauled Jacobite train runs between Fort William and Mallaig. The line was built in the 1890s by the West Highland Railway and opened to Fort William in 1894.
