Holmfirth

Holmfirth is a small town in the Metropolitan Borough of Kirklees, West Yorkshire, England. It lies in the Holme Valley, on the edge of the Pennines, about 6 miles (10 km) south of Huddersfield. The town grew up around corn and textile mills powered by the River Holme and became a centre of the woollen cloth industry.

History

The name Holmfirth derives from Old English holegn (holly) and the Middle English firthe (wood). The town suffered severe floods, most famously in 1852, when the Bilberry Reservoir embankment burst, killing 81 people and destroying homes and mills. The Picturedrome, opened in 1913 as the Valley Theatre, is one of the oldest surviving purpose-built cinemas and now a well-known music venue.

Last of the Summer Wine

Holmfirth is best known nationally as the filming location of the BBC sitcom Last of the Summer Wine, which ran from 1973 to 2010 and was the world's longest-running sitcom. Locations such as Sid's Café and Nora Batty's steps remain visitor attractions, and the town's economy draws significant tourism from the association.

Events and culture

The town hosts an annual folk festival and a food and drink festival, and lies close to the Peak District National Park.
