Trevor William Hoffman (born October 13, 1967) is an American former professional baseball relief pitcher who played 18 years in Major League Baseball (MLB) from 1993 to 2010. A closer, he spent most of his career with the San Diego Padres, after beginning with the Florida Marlins, and finished with the Milwaukee Brewers.

Career

Hoffman was drafted by the Cincinnati Reds as a shortstop in 1989 and converted to pitching before being taken by the Marlins in the 1992 expansion draft. In June 1993 he was traded to San Diego in the deal that sent Gary Sheffield to Florida. In San Diego he became the rare closer defined by a devastating changeup — his signature pitch, thrown with the same arm speed as his fastball. When he entered home games, AC/DC's "Hells Bells" rang out over the stadium, in one of baseball's most famous entrance rituals, and fans dubbed the ninth inning "Trevor Time."

Records and honors

Hoffman was the first pitcher in MLB history to reach both the 500-save and 600-save milestones, and he retired with 601 saves, then the major league record, later surpassed by Mariano Rivera. He led the National League in saves twice, was selected to seven All-Star teams, and helped the Padres reach the 1998 World Series. The Padres retired his number 51 in 2011. In 2018 he was elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame in his third year on the ballot.
