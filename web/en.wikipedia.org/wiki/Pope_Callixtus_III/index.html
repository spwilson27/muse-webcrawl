Pope Callixtus III

Pope Callixtus III (31 December 1378 – 6 August 1458), born Alfonso de Borgia (Valencian: Alfons de Borja), was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 8 April 1455 to his death in August 1458. The first pope from the Borgia family and the first Valencian pope, he devoted his pontificate to organising a crusade to recover Constantinople from the Ottoman Turks, and he canonised Saint Vincent Ferrer.

Early life

Alfonso Borgia was born at Xàtiva in the Kingdom of Valencia, then part of the Crown of Aragon. He studied and taught law at the University of Lleida and entered the service of King Alfonso V of Aragon, becoming his vice-chancellor and a trusted diplomat. He helped end the Western Schism's remnant by persuading the antipope Clement VIII to submit in 1429, and was rewarded with the bishopric of Valencia. In 1444, Pope Eugene IV made him a cardinal. Known for legal learning, austere habits, and Spanish patriotism, he was elected pope at the age of 76 in the conclave of 1455, as a compromise candidate.

Pontificate

Callixtus made the recovery of Constantinople, conquered by Mehmed II in 1453, the overriding aim of his reign. He levied a tithe across Europe, built and dispatched a papal fleet to the Aegean, and sent legates — among them John of Capistrano and Cardinal Juan Carvajal — to raise troops. Papal support contributed to the successful defence of Belgrade in 1456 under John Hunyadi, and Callixtus ordered church bells rung at noon for the crusaders, the origin of the noon bell custom. After Belgrade he instituted the Feast of the Transfiguration (6 August) as a universal feast in thanksgiving for the victory.

His pontificate was also marked by nepotism: he made two nephews cardinals, including Rodrigo Borgia, the future Pope Alexander VI, whom he also appointed vice-chancellor, and advanced another nephew, Pedro Luis Borgia, in military office — a pattern that founded the Borgia family's Roman fortunes. In 1456 a papal commission under Callixtus reviewed the trial of Joan of Arc and annulled her condemnation. A later legend that he excommunicated Halley's Comet, which appeared in 1456, is unfounded; his bull of that year called for prayers against the Turks, mentioning the comet only as an ill omen in popular retelling.

Death and legacy

Callixtus III died in Rome on 6 August 1458 and was succeeded by Pius II. His crusading project largely died with him, but his promotion of the Borgias reshaped Roman politics for two generations.
