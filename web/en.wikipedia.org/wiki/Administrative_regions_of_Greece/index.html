Administrative regions of Greece

The thirteen regions (perifereies) of Greece.

The administrative regions of Greece are the country's thirteen second-level administrative entities, called peripheries (Greek: περιφέρειες, periféreies). Under the Kallikratis reform, in force since 1 January 2011, each region is a decentralised unit of local government headed by an elected regional governor and council, subdivided into regional units and municipalities.

Structure

Above the regions, the Greek state maintains seven decentralised administrations for central-government functions. Mount Athos has a special autonomous status. Attica, containing Athens and Piraeus, is by far the most populous region, with well over a third of the national population.
