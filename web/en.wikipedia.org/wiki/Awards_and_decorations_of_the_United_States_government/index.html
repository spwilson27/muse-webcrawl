Awards and decorations of the United States government

Awards and decorations of the United States government are civilian and military awards and decorations bestowed by federal authorities. The highest civilian awards are the Presidential Medal of Freedom, awarded by the President, and the Congressional Gold Medal, awarded by the United States Congress; both rank alongside the Presidential Citizens Medal and other presidential honours.

Civilian awards

The Presidential Medal of Freedom, established in 1963, recognises especially meritorious contributions to the security or national interests of the United States, world peace, or cultural or other significant endeavours. The Congressional Gold Medal has been awarded since the American Revolution, beginning with George Washington in 1776, and requires legislation. Federal departments and agencies also maintain their own decorations, such as the Department of State's Distinguished Honor Award and the intelligence community's National Intelligence Distinguished Service Medal.
