George Gershwin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: George Gershwin at the piano in a Manhattan apartment: sleeves rolled, cigar smoke, manuscript of Rhapsody in Blue open]

George Gershwin (born Jacob Gershwine; 26 September 1898 – 11 July 1937) was an American composer and pianist whose compositions spanned both popular and classical genres. Among his best-known works are the orchestral compositions Rhapsody in Blue (1924) and An American in Paris (1928), the songs 'Swanee' (1919) and 'Fascinating Rhythm' (1924), the jazz standard 'I Got Rhythm' (1930) and the opera Porgy and Bess (1935).

A Brooklyn song-plugger turned Tin Pan Alley songwriter with his brother Ira as lyricist, Gershwin premiered the Rhapsody with Paul Whiteman's band in 1924, fusing jazz and concert music in a single gesture. His songs — 'Summertime', 'Someone to Watch Over Me', 'They Can't Take That Away from Me' — form a core of the Great American Songbook. He died of a brain tumour in Hollywood at the age of 38, at the height of his fame.
