Munsee language

Munsee (Huluniixsuwaakan)
Native to: Canada (Ontario); formerly New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania
Family: Algonquian → Eastern Algonquian → Delawaran
Status: Critically endangered

Munsee (Munsee: Huluniixsuwaakan) is an Eastern Algonquian language historically spoken by the Munsee branch of the Lenape (Delaware) people in what is now the lower Hudson Valley, northeastern Pennsylvania, northern New Jersey, and Long Island. Together with Unami, it forms the Delaware languages.

History

Displacement and forced removals scattered Munsee speakers to Ontario, Wisconsin, Kansas, and Oklahoma; the language survived as a community first language longest at Moraviantown (Eelūnaapéewi Lahkéewiit) in Ontario, Canada, where it was documented extensively by linguists and fluent elders in the late twentieth century.

Status and revitalization

Munsee is critically endangered: fluent first-language speakers at Moraviantown have numbered only a handful in recent decades, and classes, dictionaries, and recordings now anchor revitalization work. Like other Algonquian languages it is polysynthetic, with animate/inanimate gender and a rich derivational morphology.
