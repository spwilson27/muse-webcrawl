Intel Core 2

Intel Core 2
Launched: July 2006
Discontinued: 2010s
Architecture: Intel Core microarchitecture, x86-64
Models: Duo, Quad, Extreme, Solo

Intel Core 2 is a processor family encompassing a range of consumer 64-bit x86-64 single-, dual-, and quad-core processors based on the Intel Core microarchitecture. Launched on 27 July 2006, it replaced the Pentium 4 and Pentium D lines and ended Intel's NetBurst era, delivering substantially higher performance per watt.

Models

The family included Core 2 Duo dual-core desktop and mobile chips (Conroe, Merom, later Penryn), Core 2 Quad quad-core models (Kentsfield, Yorkfield), the high-end Core 2 Extreme, and single-core Core 2 Solo for ultraportables. The 45 nm Penryn shrink added SSE4.1 and larger caches.

Impact

Core 2 processors re-established Intel's performance lead over AMD, powered Apple's Macintosh line after its transition to Intel, and were succeeded by the Core i3/i5/i7 families based on Nehalem from 2008.
