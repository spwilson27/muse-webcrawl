List of tropical cyclone records

This article collects records and extremes documented for tropical cyclones worldwide — hurricanes, typhoons and cyclones — since reliable records began.

Intensity

The most intense tropical cyclone ever recorded was Hurricane Patricia in the eastern Pacific (2015), with one-minute sustained winds of 215 mph (345 km/h) and a minimum central pressure of 872 mbar (25.75 inHg). The lowest pressure in the Atlantic basin was 882 mbar in Hurricane Wilma (2005). In the western North Pacific, Typhoon Tip (1979) recorded 870 mbar — the lowest pressure of any tropical cyclone worldwide — and was also the largest cyclone on record, with a diameter of about 2,220 km.

Longevity and distance

The longest-lived tropical cyclone was Hurricane/Typhoon John (1994), which lasted 31 days and travelled 13,180 km across the Pacific, crossing the International Date Line twice. In the Southern Hemisphere, Cyclone Freddy (2023) lasted more than five weeks and accumulated more cyclone energy than any storm previously recorded.

Rainfall

Cyclone Gamede (2007) produced world-record rainfall totals on Réunion Island, including 5,512 mm over nine days. Cyclone Hyacinthe (1980) holds the ten-day and total-storm rainfall records at the same location.

Deadliest

The deadliest tropical cyclone in history was the 1970 Bhola cyclone, which struck East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) and killed an estimated 300,000–500,000 people, mostly by storm surge. The deadliest Atlantic hurricane was the Great Hurricane of 1780, with at least 22,000 deaths.

Costliest

Hurricanes Katrina (2005) and Harvey (2017) share the record for the costliest tropical cyclone, each causing about US$125 billion in damage.
