Gene–culture coevolution

Gene–culture coevolution, also called dual inheritance theory, is a branch of theoretical population genetics and cultural evolution modelling how cultural practices can create selection pressures on genes, and how genetic dispositions in turn bias cultural transmission.

Examples

The classic case is lactase persistence: dairying cultures created a strong advantage for adults who could digest milk, selecting for the lactase-persistence allele. Other proposed examples include yam cultivation increasing malaria prevalence and thus selection for sickle-cell trait, and the effect of cooking on human anatomy. Key contributors include Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman, Boyd and Richerson, and Durham.
