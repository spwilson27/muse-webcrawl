The Three Musketeers (French: Les Trois Mousquetaires) is a French historical adventure novel by Alexandre Dumas, first published in 1844. It is the first of the author's three d'Artagnan Romances, followed by Twenty Years After (1845) and The Vicomte of Bragelonne (1847–1850). The novel recounts the adventures of the young Gascon d'Artagnan and his three musketeer friends Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, who live by the motto "all for one, and one for all".

Plot

In 1625, the young d'Artagnan travels to Paris to join the Musketeers of the Guard. He befriends the musketeers Athos, Porthos, and Aramis and becomes entangled in the intrigues of Cardinal Richelieu and his agent, the beautiful and deadly Milady de Winter. The friends race to England and back to recover Queen Anne's diamond studs—given to her lover, the Duke of Buckingham—before Richelieu can expose her. Later they take part in the Siege of La Rochelle. Milady engineers the assassination of Buckingham and murders d'Artagnan's beloved Constance Bonacieux; the musketeers capture her, and she is tried and executed. D'Artagnan is offered a commission in the musketeers, which he passes to each of his friends before accepting it himself.

Historical basis

Dumas drew on Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras's Mémoires de M. d'Artagnan (1700), based on the real Charles de Batz de Castelmore d'Artagnan. Richelieu, Louis XIII, Anne of Austria, and Buckingham were real figures, as was the Siege of La Rochelle.

Legacy

The novel is one of the most popular adventure stories ever written and has been adapted dozens of times for film, television, theatre, and animation.
