Lake Bracciano

Lake Bracciano (Italian: Lago di Bracciano) is a large volcanic lake in the Lazio region of Italy, about 30 kilometres northwest of Rome. With a surface area of roughly 57 square kilometres, it is one of the largest lakes in Italy and reaches a maximum depth of about 160 metres.

Geography

The lake occupies a caldera formed by intense volcanic activity between about 600,000 and 40,000 years ago. It has an almost circular shoreline and no islands. The towns of Bracciano, Anguillara Sabazia, and Trevignano Romano stand on its shores.

Uses

The lake serves as a drinking-water reservoir for Rome, and motorized navigation is restricted to protect water quality. It is a popular destination for sailing, windsurfing, swimming, and tourism. The Orsini-Odescalchi Castle in Bracciano, overlooking the lake, is a notable Renaissance landmark.

History

The area has been settled since Neolithic times; the submerged village site of La Marmotta, dating to around 5700 BC, was discovered beneath the lake's waters. In Roman times the emperor Trajan built an aqueduct to carry the lake's water to Rome.
