The Adventures of Pinocchio

The Adventures of Pinocchio (Italian: Le avventure di Pinocchio) is a children's fantasy novel by Italian author Carlo Collodi (the pen name of Carlo Lorenzini), published in book form in Florence in 1883 after serialisation in a children's magazine beginning in 1881. It tells the story of Pinocchio, a wooden marionette carved by the impoverished woodcarver Geppetto, who dreams of becoming a real boy but is prone to lying and mischief; his nose grows longer with each lie.

Plot

Geppetto carves a puppet from a talking piece of wood and names him Pinocchio. The puppet runs away, falls in with a series of tempters and tricksters — including the Fox and the Cat, who swindle and eventually hang him — and is repeatedly rescued by the Fairy with Turquoise Hair. Collodi originally ended the story with Pinocchio's hanging, but at his publisher's urging he continued: Pinocchio visits the Land of Toys, where lazy boys are turned into donkeys, and is swallowed by a giant shark (in the original, the Terrible Dogfish), inside which he finds Geppetto. Escaping, he works hard, cares for his ailing father, and behaves selflessly, until the Fairy rewards him by turning him into a real boy.

Themes and reception

The novel is harsher and more satirical than most later adaptations: it is set against the poverty of newly unified Italy and moralises about education, work, and obedience, while episodes verge on the grotesque. It became one of the most translated and best-selling books ever written, with Pinocchio an icon of Italian culture. Critics have read it as everything from a moral fable to an allegory of the working-class child's path to respectability.

Adaptations

The most famous adaptation is Walt Disney's animated film Pinocchio (1940), which softened the story, added Jiminy Cricket as a prominent conscience figure, and made the growing nose famous worldwide. Other notable versions include Luigi Comencini's 1972 television miniseries, Roberto Benigni's 2002 film, Matteo Garrone's 2019 film, and Guillermo del Toro's stop-motion Pinocchio (2022), which won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.
