Dribbling

Dribbling
SportsBasketball, association football, field hockey, ice hockey, bandy, water polo
PurposeMove the ball past opponents while retaining possession

In sports, dribbling is the technique of moving the ball along the playing surface under close control, past opponents, by repeatedly tapping, bouncing or pushing it while running. It is a fundamental skill in basketball, association football (soccer), hockey variants, bandy and water polo, and celebrated dribblers are among the most admired players in their sports.

Basketball

In basketball, dribbling is the legal way for a player to move with the ball: the ball must be bounced continuously with one hand, and a player who stops, holds the ball and resumes dribbling commits a double-dribble violation, as does carrying the ball by letting the hand go beneath it. Core moves include the crossover, between-the-legs and behind-the-back dribbles, spin moves and hesitation moves, all used to change direction or pace and beat a defender. Ball-handling is a defining skill of guards, epitomised by players such as Bob Cousy, "Pistol" Pete Maravich, Allen Iverson and Kyrie Irving.

Association football

In soccer, dribbling means running with the ball at one's feet, using quick touches with the inside, outside, laces or sole of the boot to keep it close while changing direction. Feints and named skills — stepovers, the Cruyff turn, the nutmeg, Garrincha's and Messi's close-control runs, Maradona's solo goals — make dribbling central to attacking play, and statistics now record successful dribbles and progressive carries as standard performance metrics.

Other sports

In field and ice hockey the ball or puck is pushed along with the stick ("stickhandling"), while in water polo players swim while dribbling the ball in front of them, shielded by their wake. In handball, dribbling by bouncing the ball is required to move beyond a limited number of steps.
