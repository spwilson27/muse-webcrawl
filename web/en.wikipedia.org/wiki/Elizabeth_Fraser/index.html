Elizabeth Davidson Fraser (born 29 August 1963) is a Scottish singer and musician, best known as the vocalist of the dream pop band Cocteau Twins. Her distinctive soprano voice and unconventional, often glossolalic singing style have made her one of the most acclaimed and influential vocalists in alternative music; she has been described as "the voice of God" by critics.

Cocteau Twins

Fraser formed Cocteau Twins in Grangemouth in 1979 with Robin Guthrie and Will Heggie. Across albums including Treasure (1984), Victorialand (1986) and Heaven or Las Vegas (1990), she developed a vocal approach that treated the voice as an instrument, using fragments of words, foreign phrases and invented syllables chosen for sound rather than literal meaning. The band's layered, reverb-heavy sound became a cornerstone of dream pop and the 4AD label aesthetic.

Collaborations

In 1983 Fraser sang the This Mortal Coil cover of Tim Buckley's "Song to the Siren", a landmark recording of the era. She later contributed vocals to Massive Attack's album Mezzanine (1998), including the single "Teardrop", one of the group's best-known songs, and has worked with artists including Peter Gabriel, The Future Sound of London and Jónsi. She performed the vocals for parts of Howard Shore's scores and sang in Elvish-influenced style for The Lord of the Rings film soundtracks.

Solo work and later years

After Cocteau Twins disbanded in 1997, Fraser recorded solo material, releasing the single "Underwater" (2000) and, much later, music with the project Sun's Signature (2022), formed with her partner Damon Reece. Her rare live appearances and recordings are highly anticipated events for fans of the band.
