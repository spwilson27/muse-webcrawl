United States Disciplinary Barracks

LocationFort Leavenworth, Kansas
Opened1875 (current facility 2002)
Managed byU.S. Army Corrections Command
TypeMaximum-security military prison

The United States Disciplinary Barracks (USDB), colloquially known as Leavenworth, is a military prison at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas. It is the Department of Defense's only maximum-security correctional facility and houses male service members convicted by courts-martial of serious offenses, including those sentenced to death under military law.

History

The facility was established in 1875 within the old Fort Leavenworth grounds, making it the oldest continuously operating military prison in the United States; prisoners themselves built much of the original "Castle" complex. In 2002 the USDB moved into a modern purpose-built facility on the fort. The nearby Midwest Joint Regional Correctional Facility, opened in 2010, houses lower-security inmates.

Inmates

The USDB holds enlisted prisoners with sentences of five years or more and officers of any sentence length. Notable inmates over the years have included Chelsea Manning, convicted of violations of the Espionage Act, and several soldiers convicted of war crimes. Military death row is maintained at the USDB.
