Mount Pleasant is a large suburban town in Charleston County, South Carolina, United States, lying on the east bank of Charleston Harbor at the mouths of the Wando and Cooper rivers. It is the fourth-largest municipality in the state; its 2020 census population was about 91,000, having grown rapidly since the 1970s. The town is connected to peninsular Charleston by the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge, a cable-stayed span opened in 2005 that is among the longest of its type in North America.

History

The area was first settled by English colonists in 1680 at "Old Town" on the Wando River. Through the 18th and 19th centuries Mount Pleasant was a summer resort village for Charleston planters escaping the heat and malaria of the Lowcountry rice plantations; the town was incorporated in 1837. The Old Village district preserves many antebellum houses. During the Civil War, the Confederate submarine H. L. Hunley was stationed at Breach Inlet nearby. Two modern landmarks anchor the town's identity: Patriots Point Naval & Maritime Museum, home to the aircraft carrier USS Yorktown, and Boone Hall Plantation.

Community

The town's waterfront neighbourhood of Shem Creek is known for restaurants and its shrimping fleet. Sweetgrass basket weaving by Gullah artisans — one of the oldest African art forms in America — is practised and sold along Highway 17, and Mount Pleasant has worked to preserve the craft's "basket makers' stands." Shem Creek and the harbour also feature in the town's identity as home of an annual Blessing of the Fleet. Growth management is a persistent local issue, as development has pushed the town's boundaries steadily northeast toward the Francis Marion National Forest.
