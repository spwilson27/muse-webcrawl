Virtual YouTuber

A virtual YouTuber (VTuber) is an online entertainer who uses a virtual avatar generated using computer graphics. Generally, VTubers are livestreamers and YouTubers represented by digital avatars — typically anime-inspired — whose movements and expressions are driven by a human performer using motion capture or face-tracking software.

History

Precursors include the virtual idol Kyoko Date (1996) and the English-language channel of Ami Yamato (from 2011), who used an animated avatar. The phenomenon took off in 2016 when Kizuna AI began posting videos, coining the term "virtual YouTuber"; her success triggered rapid growth in Japan. In 2017–2018 agencies industrialised the format: Anycolor's Nijisanji popularised Live2D livestreaming, and Cover Corporation's Hololive built large idol-style rosters. From 2020, English-language branches (Hololive English's "HoloMyth" generation, Nijisanji EN) and independent VTubers brought the format to global audiences, and VTubers became a significant category on Twitch as well as YouTube.

Technology and culture

Most VTubers use Live2D models that deform 2D illustrations in response to webcam face tracking; others use full 3D models with body tracking, via software such as VTube Studio or VRChat. Content includes gaming streams, "just chatting", singing (with original songs and concerts), and collaborative streams. Fandom practices — fan art, clipped highlights, and paid "Super Chat" donations — mirror those of streamers generally, with the added convention that the performer's real identity is usually kept private and the character treated as the persona.

Industry

Agencies scout talent through auditions, provide models, management and merchandising, and take a share of revenue. Leading performers attract millions of subscribers and rank among the most Super Chatted channels on YouTube. The format has also been adopted by companies and local governments in Japan for marketing and tourism promotion.
