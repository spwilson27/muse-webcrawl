Ischemic stroke

Specialty: Neurology

Cause: Blocked blood supply to the brain

Treatment: Thrombolysis, thrombectomy, antiplatelets

An ischemic stroke occurs when a blood vessel supplying blood to the brain is obstructed, causing brain tissue to be deprived of oxygen and nutrients. It accounts for roughly 85–87% of all strokes. The blockage is usually caused by a blood clot formed in the brain (thrombosis) or traveling from elsewhere in the body (embolism), often from the heart in atrial fibrillation.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the affected brain region and include sudden weakness or numbness of the face, arm, or leg (often on one side), confusion, trouble speaking or understanding speech, vision problems, dizziness, and severe headache. The FAST mnemonic (Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services) is used for rapid recognition.

Treatment

Treatment is time-critical: intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase or tenecteplase can be given within a narrow window after symptom onset, and mechanical thrombectomy can remove large-vessel clots in eligible patients. Long-term prevention includes antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy, statins, blood pressure control, and management of risk factors such as diabetes and smoking.
