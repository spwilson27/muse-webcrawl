General aviation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Rows of small Cessna and Piper light aircraft at a grass airfield]
Light aircraft at a small airfield.

General aviation (GA) is all civil aviation operations other than scheduled air transport and military flying: private and business flying, flight training, air taxi, agricultural aviation, air ambulance, aerial surveying and recreational flight. It is dominated by light piston aircraft (Cessna 172 — the most-produced aircraft in history, with over 45,000 built), turboprops and business jets, operating from thousands of small airfields. The United States has by far the largest GA community, with some 5,000 public-use airports.

Sectors

Business aviation (from Piper singles to Gulfstream jets) provides point-to-point travel outside airline networks; fractional ownership (NetJets) and charter broaden access. Utility GA includes crop spraying, pipeline patrol, parachuting, banner towing and medevac; flight schools train most of the world\'s airline pilots.

Regulation and safety

GA is regulated more lightly than airlines but has a markedly worse accident rate per hour, dominated by loss of control, weather encounters (VFR into IMC) and fuel exhaustion. Safety campaigns, glass cockpits, ADS-B and whole-aircraft parachutes aim to close the gap.

Community

Organisations such as AOPA and the EAA (whose Oshkosh AirVenture is the world\'s largest aviation gathering, ~600,000 visitors) sustain the sector. Electric trainers and eVTOL air taxis are emerging branches in the 2020s.
