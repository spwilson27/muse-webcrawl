Paul Andreu (10 July 1938 – 11 October 2018) was a French architect, civil engineer and writer. He became internationally known as the designer of major airports — above all Paris's Charles de Gaulle Airport — and later for landmark cultural buildings in China, including the National Centre for the Performing Arts in Beijing.

Early life and education

Andreu was born in Caudéran, near Bordeaux. He studied at the École Polytechnique (class of 1958) and the École Nationale des Ponts et Chaussées, qualifying as a civil engineer, and also trained as an architect at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. In 1967 he joined Aéroports de Paris, where he would spend most of his career shaping airport design worldwide.

Airports

At 29, Andreu was put in charge of the new airport north of Paris that became Charles de Gaulle Airport (opened 1974). Its Terminal 1 — a circular concrete drum with satellite boarding pods reached by moving walkways through the central void — was a radical, much-discussed essay in concrete and circulation. He went on to design the airport's Terminal 2 with its sweeping vaulted modules, and some forty airports abroad, including those of Jakarta, Dar es Salaam, Cairo, Abu Dhabi, Shanghai Pudong and Guangzhou. In May 2004 a section of the Terminal 2E vault at Charles de Gaulle collapsed, killing four people; a long inquiry examined design and construction failings, and Andreu publicly defended the project while acknowledging the tragedy.

Cultural buildings

In 1999 Andreu won the international competition for the National Centre for the Performing Arts in Beijing (opened 2007): a vast ellipsoid dome of titanium and glass — the "Giant Egg" — set in an artificial lake near Tiananmen Square, housing an opera house, concert hall and theatre. In France he completed the Grande Arche de la Fraternité at La Défense in collaboration with Johann Otto von Spreckelsen (opened 1989) and the terminal of the Channel Tunnel. Other Chinese works include the Guangzhou Gymnasium and the Shanghai Oriental Art Center.

Writing and later life

Alongside architecture Andreu wrote novels and memoirs, including La maison and works reflecting on building and memory. He was elected to the Académie des Beaux-Arts in 1996. He died in Paris in October 2018, shortly after returning from an exhibition of his work in China; tributes emphasised his combination of engineering rigour and poetic, sculptural form.
