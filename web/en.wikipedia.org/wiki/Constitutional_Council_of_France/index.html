Constitutional Council of France

The Constitutional Council (French: Conseil constitutionnel) is the highest constitutional authority in France. Created by the Constitution of the Fifth Republic of 4 October 1958, it reviews the constitutionality of legislation, supervises the regularity of elections and referendums, and rules on disputes concerning the division of competence between Parliament and the executive. Unlike the Conseil d'État, which is the supreme administrative court, the Constitutional Council deals only with constitutional questions.

History and role

Originally designed as a limited check ensuring that Parliament stayed within its enumerated legislative domain (Article 34), the Council's role expanded dramatically with its 1971 Freedom of Association decision, in which it incorporated the preamble's references to the Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789) and the preamble of the 1946 Constitution into the enforceable "block of constitutionality" (bloc de constitutionnalité). A 1974 reform allowed referral of laws by 60 deputies or 60 senators, making review a routine weapon of the parliamentary opposition.

Review procedures

The Council performs a priori review of organic laws and parliamentary rules (mandatory) and of ordinary laws referred before promulgation by the President, the Prime Minister, the presidents of the assemblies, or 60 deputies or senators. Since the constitutional revision of 23 July 2008 (effective 2010), it also performs a posteriori review through the question prioritaire de constitutionnalité (QPC), by which a litigant in an ordinary case may challenge a statute's conformity with constitutional rights; questions are filtered by the Cour de cassation or the Conseil d'État. Decisions of the Council are binding on all authorities and are not subject to appeal.

Elections

The Council proclaims the results of presidential elections, examines complaints about parliamentary elections, verifies the regularity of referendums, and may declare the President incapacitated under Article 7.
