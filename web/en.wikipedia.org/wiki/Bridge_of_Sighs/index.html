The Bridge of Sighs (Italian: Ponte dei Sospiri) is a bridge in Venice, northern Italy. The enclosed bridge is made of white Istrian stone, passes over the Rio di Palazzo, and connects the New Prison (Prigioni Nuove) to the interrogation rooms in the Doge's Palace. It was designed by Antonio Contino, whose uncle Antonio da Ponte designed the Rialto Bridge, and it was built in 1600.

Name

The view from the Bridge of Sighs was the last view of Venice that convicts saw before their imprisonment. The bridge's English name was popularised by Lord Byron in the 19th century and derives from the suggestion that prisoners would sigh at their final view of beautiful Venice through the window before being taken down to their cells.

In reality, the days of inquisitions and summary executions were over by the time the bridge was built, and the cells under the palace roof were occupied mostly by low-level offenders. Little could be seen from inside the bridge because of the stone grills covering the windows.

Design

The bridge is fully enclosed and roofed, with two separate corridors inside. One corridor was used for prisoners being taken to the prison, the other for their return, so that opposing parties in trials could not meet. Its exterior is decorated in an early Baroque style with small windows fitted with stone bars.

In culture

A local legend says that lovers will be granted eternal love and bliss if they kiss on a gondola at sunset under the Bridge of Sighs as the bells of St Mark's Campanile toll. The bridge has been widely imitated, including bridges of the same name at Oxford and Cambridge universities in England.
