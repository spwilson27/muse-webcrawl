Intestinal malrotation

Intestinal malrotation is a congenital anomaly of rotation of the midgut in the abdomen during fetal development. Normally the intestines make a series of rotations as they return to the abdominal cavity; in malrotation this process is incomplete or abnormal.

Complications

The abnormal positioning predisposes to midgut volvulus — twisting of the bowel around the superior mesenteric artery — which can cut off blood supply and is a surgical emergency, especially in infants. Bands of tissue known as Ladd's bands may obstruct the duodenum.

Presentation and diagnosis

Symptoms may include bilious vomiting in newborns, abdominal pain and distension. Diagnosis is typically made with an upper gastrointestinal contrast series or ultrasound.

Treatment

The standard surgical correction is the Ladd procedure, which untwists the bowel, divides the obstructing bands, widens the mesenteric base and usually removes the appendix.
