Vangelis
Born: Evángelos Odysséas Papathanassíou
29 March 1943, Agria, Greece
Died: 17 May 2022 (aged 79), Paris, France
Occupation: Composer, musician, producer
Genres: Electronic, progressive rock, ambient, film score

Vangelis (born Evángelos Odysséas Papathanassíou; 29 March 1943 – 17 May 2022) was a Greek musician, composer and producer known for his pioneering work in electronic music and for celebrated film scores including Chariots of Fire (1981), Blade Runner (1982), Antarctica (1983), The Bounty (1984), 1492: Conquest of Paradise (1992) and Alexander (2004). His score for Chariots of Fire won the Academy Award for Best Original Score, and its main theme topped the charts in the United States.

Vangelis, one of the most influential figures in electronic and film music.

Early life and career

Born in Agria, near Volos, Vangelis showed musical talent from a young age, experimenting with piano and traditional Greek instruments. In the 1960s he played in the pop group The Forminx before moving to Paris, where in 1968 he co-founded the progressive rock band Aphrodite's Child with Demis Roussos and Loukas Sideras. The band achieved European success with singles such as "Rain and Tears" and the ambitious double album 666 (1972).

Solo career

After Aphrodite's Child disbanded, Vangelis settled in London and established Nemo Studios. His solo albums of the 1970s — including Earth (1973), Heaven and Hell (1975), Albedo 0.39 (1976), Spiral (1977) and China (1979) — blended synthesizers, orchestral textures and world music influences. He also formed the duo Jon and Vangelis with Yes singer Jon Anderson, producing hits such as "I Hear You Now" and "I'll Find My Way Home".

Film scores

Vangelis's breakthrough in cinema came with Chariots of Fire (1981), whose synthesizer-driven main theme became iconic. The following year he scored Ridley Scott's Blade Runner; although the soundtrack album was delayed until 1994, the score is now regarded as a landmark of electronic film music. Other notable works include Missing (1982), 1492: Conquest of Paradise (1992) and Oliver Stone's Alexander (2004). Music from 1492 was widely used at sporting events across Europe.

Later work and death

In later decades Vangelis composed for stage, ballet and special events, including music for NASA missions and the 2004 Athens Olympics. His final studio album, Juno to Jupiter (2021), was inspired by NASA's Juno mission. Vangelis died in Paris on 17 May 2022, aged 79, of heart failure while being treated for COVID-19.

Legacy

A largely self-taught musician who famously did not read or write musical notation in the conventional way, Vangelis improvised and recorded much of his work in real time, layering synthesizers, percussion and acoustic instruments. He is regarded as one of the founding figures of modern electronic music and one of the most distinctive film composers of the late twentieth century.
