The Vienna General Hospital (German: Allgemeines Krankenhaus der Stadt Wien, abbreviated AKH) is the largest hospital and the principal teaching hospital of Vienna, Austria, affiliated with the Medical University of Vienna. Its current complex in the Alsergrund district, opened in 1994, is one of the largest hospital buildings in Europe.

History

The original Allgemeines Krankenhaus was founded in 1784 by Emperor Joseph II, converted from a veterans' home, and quickly became a center of the Vienna Medical School. In its courtyards worked and taught figures such as Ignaz Semmelweis, who in 1847 demonstrated that hand disinfection with chlorinated lime dramatically reduced deaths from puerperal fever in the hospital's maternity clinic; Karl Landsteiner, who discovered the blood groups there; and Theodor Billroth, a pioneer of abdominal surgery. The old complex also contains the Narrenturm ("Fools' Tower"), built in 1784 as one of Europe's first purpose-built asylums for the mentally ill and now a museum of pathological anatomy.

The present AKH, with its two distinctive towers and more than 2,000 beds, was built between 1974 and 1994 adjacent to the historic site, which now serves as the campus of the University of Vienna. The hospital employs thousands of staff and treats hundreds of thousands of patients annually, functioning as Austria's leading center for specialized medicine, transplants, and research.
