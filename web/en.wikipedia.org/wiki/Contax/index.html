Contax

Contax is a brand of cameras produced first by Zeiss Ikon of Germany from 1932, and later (from 1974) by the Japanese firm Yashica — subsequently Kyocera — under license, featuring Carl Zeiss lenses.

Zeiss Ikon era

The original Contax I (1932) was a precision 35 mm rangefinder designed by Heinz Küppenbender to rival the Leica. The Contax II (1936) and III added a combined rangefinder-viewfinder and exposure meter. After the war, production split between Jena (East) and Stuttgart (West); Zeiss Ikon also made the Contax SLR line (1949–1961) with early features such as a pentaprism.

Kyocera era

In 1974 Yashica launched the Contax RTS, a luxury electronic SLR system designed with Porsche styling input. Later milestones included the titanium-bodied G1/G2 autofocus rangefinders (1994), the 645 medium-format autofocus system (1999), and the N series. Contax digital cameras included the world's first full-frame DSLR, the Contax N Digital (2002).

End of production

Kyocera ceased Contax production in 2005 as the digital transition squeezed film-camera brands.
