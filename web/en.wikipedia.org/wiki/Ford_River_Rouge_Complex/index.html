Ford River Rouge Complex

LocationDearborn, Michigan, United States
Built1917–1928
ArchitectAlbert Kahn
DesignationNational Historic Landmark (1978)

The Ford River Rouge Complex (commonly known as the Rouge Complex, the Rouge, or simply The Rouge) is a Ford Motor Company factory complex located in Dearborn, Michigan, along the River Rouge. Construction began in 1917, and when it was completed in 1928, it was the largest integrated factory in the world, stretching more than a mile along the river.

Vertical integration

Designed by architect Albert Kahn, the Rouge embodied Henry Ford's vision of vertical integration: raw materials — iron ore, coal, limestone, rubber — arrived by ship, rail, and truck, and were transformed on site into finished automobiles. The complex included its own steel mill, glass plant, cement plant, power station, and byproduct facilities. At its peak in the 1930s it employed around 100,000 workers and produced a complete car, from ore to finished vehicle, in roughly 41 hours.

Later history

The Rouge was the site of the 1937 "Battle of the Overpass", in which union organisers were beaten by Ford security staff, an event that became a landmark in American labour history. The complex was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1978. In the early 2000s, the Dearborn Truck Plant was rebuilt within the complex with extensive green features, including one of the world's largest living roofs. Today the site produces Ford F-150 trucks, and public tours are conducted from The Henry Ford museum.
