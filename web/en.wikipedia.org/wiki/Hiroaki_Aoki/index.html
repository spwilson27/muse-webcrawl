Hiroaki Aoki

Hiroaki "Rocky" Aoki
Born: 9 October 1938, Tokyo, Japan
Died: 10 July 2008, New York City, US
Known for: founding Benihana

Hiroaki Aoki (Japanese: 青木 廣彰; 9 October 1938 – 10 July 2008), better known as Rocky Aoki, was a Japanese-American amateur wrestler and entrepreneur who founded the Benihana chain of Japanese teppanyaki restaurants, introducing hibachi-style dining theatre to the United States.

Early life and wrestling

Aoki grew up in Tokyo, where his parents ran a coffee shop. A successful wrestler in Japan, he emigrated to the United States in 1960, studied restaurant management in New York, competed on the American amateur wrestling circuit, and sold ice cream from a truck in Harlem, saving the money that became his first restaurant's stake.

Benihana

In 1964 he opened the first Benihana on West 56th Street in New York, named after the red safflower his parents had used as their shop's name. Chefs cooked at an iron griddle built into each table, performing knife flourishes and tricks — the flipping shrimp, the onion "volcano" — before the diners. The concept was a sensation, and the chain expanded across America and internationally, making Aoki one of the most recognisable Japanese businessmen in the US.

Later life

Aoki pursued adventure sports with the same energy: he raced offshore powerboats, surviving a near-fatal crash under the Golden Gate Bridge in 1979, and attempted trans-Pacific balloon crossings. In the 1990s he was convicted of insider trading, and control of Benihana passed through family litigation. He died of pneumonia in New York in 2008. His children include the DJ and producer Steve Aoki and the model and actress Devon Aoki.
