Unintended pregnancy

An unintended pregnancy is a pregnancy that is reported to have been either unwanted — that is, the pregnancy occurred when no children, or no more children, were desired — or mistimed, meaning it occurred earlier than desired. The concept of unintended pregnancy helps in understanding the fertility of populations and the unmet need for contraception. Most unintended pregnancies result from not using contraception, or from using contraception inconsistently or incorrectly.

Global statistics

Worldwide, the unintended pregnancy rate is approximately 45% of all pregnancies, though rates vary greatly by geographic region and socio-demographic group. An estimated 121 million unintended pregnancies occurred each year between 2015 and 2019, corresponding to a global rate of about 64 per 1,000 women aged 15–49. About 61% of unintended pregnancies ended in abortion during that period. Unintended pregnancy rates have declined globally since the early 1990s, though more slowly in low- and middle-income countries.

Effects

Unintended pregnancies are associated with delayed or absent prenatal care, higher rates of maternal depression, and poorer birth outcomes, particularly where pregnancies are unwanted. They carry significant social, psychological, and economic costs for individuals, families, and health systems. Pregnancies that end in unsafe abortion remain a leading cause of maternal mortality in regions where abortion is legally restricted.

Prevention

Preventing unintended pregnancy primarily involves education about sexual behaviour and improved access to effective contraceptive methods. Long-acting reversible contraceptives such as intrauterine devices and implants have the lowest typical-use failure rates. Emergency contraception can be used after unprotected intercourse. Public-health programmes that expand contraceptive access have been shown to reduce unintended pregnancy and abortion rates.
