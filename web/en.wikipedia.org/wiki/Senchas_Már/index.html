Senchas Már


Manuscript page of the Senchas Már (placeholder image)

The Senchas Már (Middle Irish for "great tradition" or "great compilation of knowledge") is a large collection of early Irish legal texts, compiled in the 7th or 8th century AD. It is the most extensive surviving body of Brehon law, the native legal tradition of early medieval Ireland, and comprises some forty-seven or forty-eight individual tracts dealing with subjects such as status, contract, kinship, suretyship, theft, distraint and legal procedure.

Contents

The compilation is traditionally divided into thirds. Its tracts include the Bretha Nemed material on privileged persons, texts on the law of neighbourhood and social relations, and procedural works such as Cóic Conara Fugill ("The Five Paths of Judgement"). Many of the tracts survive only in later manuscripts accompanied by extensive gloss and commentary added by medieval jurists.

Origins and authorship

Medieval tradition attributed parts of the compilation to legendary figures, including the poet Dubthach moccu Lugair, said to have recited the laws before Saint Patrick, but modern scholarship treats the Senchas Már as the work of a school of professional jurists (brithemain), possibly centred in the northern half of Ireland. Its prologue, partly pseudo-historical, describes how the laws were supposedly reconciled with Christianity.

Significance

Together with collections such as the Uraicecht Becc, the Senchas Már is the principal source for the social and legal organisation of early Ireland, preserving detailed information on rank, honour-price, clientship and the workings of kin groups. The texts were first systematically edited in the nineteenth century in the Ancient Laws of Ireland series, though modern scholars have criticised those translations, and much of the corpus has since been re-edited.
