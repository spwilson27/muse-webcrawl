Sumbawa (Indonesian: Pulau Sumbawa) is an Indonesian island in the Lesser Sunda Islands chain, lying between Lombok to the west and Flores to the east. It forms part of the province of West Nusa Tenggara. With an area of about 15,448 square kilometres, it is roughly three times the size of Lombok, though much more sparsely populated, with around 1.5 million inhabitants.

Geography

The island is markedly lobed, with the Sanggar Peninsula in the northeast separating Saleh Bay from Bima Bay. Sumbawa is volcanic in origin and forms part of the Sunda Arc. Its highest peak is Mount Tambora (2,722 m), whose eruption in April 1815 was the most powerful in recorded history: it ejected so much material into the stratosphere that 1816 became known worldwide as the "Year Without a Summer". Before the eruption, Tambora stood an estimated 4,300 m high.

People and administration

The island is divided culturally and linguistically into a western half inhabited by the Sumbawa (Samawa) people and an eastern half inhabited by the Bima (Mbojo) people, each speaking distinct languages. Administratively it comprises four regencies — West Sumbawa, Sumbawa, Dompu and Bima — plus the city of Bima. The economy is based on agriculture, including rice, maize, coffee and tamarind, and on mining: the huge Batu Hijau copper-gold mine operated in the southwest until its ore body was exhausted. Sumbawa's west coast surf breaks, such as those near Maluk, attract international surfers.
