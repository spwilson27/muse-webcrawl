The Brecker Brothers were an American jazz fusion duo of brothers Michael Brecker (1949–2007), tenor saxophonist, and Randy Brecker (born 1945), trumpeter and flugelhorn player. Among the most in-demand session musicians of the 1970s, they formed their own band in 1975, blending jazz improvisation with funk rhythms, rock energy and sophisticated horn arrangements, and helped define the sound of jazz fusion.

Career

Both brothers first worked together in the band Dreams (1969–1972) and in Horace Silver's and Billy Cobham's groups. Signing to Arista Records, the Brecker Brothers released their self-titled debut in 1975, featuring "Some Skunk Funk", which became a signature tune. Albums such as Back to Back (1976), Don't Stop the Music (1977), Heavy Metal Be-Bop (1978, recorded live in Japan), Detente (1980) and Straphangin' (1981) followed; their single "East River" reached the UK charts in 1979.

The brothers disbanded the group in 1982 to pursue solo careers, but reunited in the 1990s for the albums Return of the Brecker Brothers (1992) and Out of the Loop (1994), the latter winning two Grammy Awards. They continued touring until Michael Brecker's illness and death from leukemia in 2007.

Legacy

Michael Brecker is widely regarded as one of the most influential tenor saxophonists since John Coltrane, winning 15 Grammy Awards; Randy Brecker remains a leading trumpeter in jazz and studio work. The brothers' recorded output together and separately — reportedly thousands of sessions with artists from Frank Sinatra to Parliament-Funkadelic — made them central figures of the New York studio scene.
