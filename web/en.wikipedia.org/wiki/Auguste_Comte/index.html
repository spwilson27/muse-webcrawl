Isidore Marie Auguste François Xavier Comte (19 January 1798 – 5 September 1857) was a French philosopher, mathematician, and sociologist who founded the discipline of sociology and the doctrine of positivism. He is widely regarded as the first philosopher of science in the modern sense.

Life

Born in Montpellier, Comte studied at the École Polytechnique in Paris, from which he was expelled after participating in student unrest. From 1817 he worked as secretary to Henri de Saint-Simon, whose ideas strongly influenced him before their rupture in 1824. Comte developed his system in the Course of Positive Philosophy (1830–1842) and the System of Positive Polity (1851–1854). His intense, platonic love for Clotilde de Vaux, who died in 1846, shaped his later project of a Religion of Humanity, complete with priests, rituals, and a positivist calendar. He died in Paris in 1857.

Positivism and the law of three stages

Comte's best-known doctrine is the law of three stages: human thought, and each branch of knowledge, passes successively through a theological stage (explaining phenomena by gods and spirits), a metaphysical stage (explaining them by abstract forces and essences), and a positive stage, in which explanation gives way to the observation of laws—constant relations of succession and resemblance among phenomena. He arranged the sciences in a hierarchy of increasing complexity and decreasing generality: mathematics, astronomy, physics, chemistry, biology, and finally sociology, which he called at first "social physics."

Social thought

Comte coined the word sociology and divided it into social statics (the study of social order) and social dynamics (the study of social progress). He also coined the term altruism (from French altruisme) for selfless devotion to others, which he made the moral basis of his later system and its motto "Love as a principle, order as a basis, progress as a goal." Positivism influenced thinkers including John Stuart Mill and Émile Durkheim and had a notable political legacy, notably on the flag of Brazil, whose motto Ordem e Progresso derives from Comte.
