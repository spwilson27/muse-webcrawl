Gibson (cocktail)

The Gibson is a mixed drink made with gin and dry vermouth, garnished with a pickled cocktail onion. It is closely related to the martini, from which it differs chiefly in the garnish: where the martini typically takes an olive or a lemon twist, the Gibson is identified by its silverskin onion.

Recipe

Ingredients6 cl (2 oz) gin
1 cl (1/3 oz) dry vermouth
1–3 pickled cocktail onions
PreparationStir the gin and vermouth with ice, strain into a chilled cocktail glass, and garnish with the onion(s).
ServedStraight up, without ice

The drink appears on the International Bartenders Association's list of official cocktails as a variant within the martini family. Proportions vary with taste, from a wet 3:1 gin-to-vermouth ratio in older recipes to the very dry ratios favoured in the later twentieth century.

History and name

The Gibson's origin is disputed. One popular account credits San Francisco businessman Walter D. K. Gibson, who is said to have asked bartender Charles Connolly at the Bohemian Club in the 1890s for an improved martini, with the onion substituted for the olive. Another story attributes the drink to illustrator Charles Dana Gibson, creator of the "Gibson Girl"; a version of the tale holds that he challenged a bartender to improve the martini and the onion swap stuck, with the three onions sometimes jokingly said to represent the Gibson Girl's figure. Yet another theory links the name to a diplomat or to a preference for onions because they were thought to prevent colds.

In popular culture

The Gibson is famously the drink of Roger Thornhill in Alfred Hitchcock's North by Northwest (1959), and it enjoyed a surge of renewed attention after appearing in the Netflix chess drama The Queen's Gambit (2020), where it is associated with the character of Alma Wheatley and later Beth Harmon.
