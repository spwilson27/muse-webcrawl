Sheridan's Ride

Sheridan's Ride was a famous episode of the American Civil War, in which Union Major General Philip Sheridan rode from Winchester, Virginia, to the battlefield of Cedar Creek on October 19, 1864, rallied his retreating army, and led it to a decisive victory. The ride was immortalized in the popular poem "Sheridan's Ride" by Thomas Buchanan Read.

Background

Sheridan's Army of the Shenandoah had been campaigning in the Shenandoah Valley against Confederate forces under Jubal Early. On the morning of October 19, while Sheridan was returning from a trip to Washington and had stopped at Winchester, Early launched a surprise dawn attack on the Union camps at Cedar Creek, driving the Union army back in confusion.

The ride

Hearing the sounds of battle, Sheridan mounted his horse Rienzi and galloped roughly twenty miles to the battlefield, arriving in late morning. Along the way he rallied retreating soldiers and stragglers, turning them back toward the front. His arrival electrified the army; that afternoon the Union counterattacked and routed Early's forces, effectively ending Confederate resistance in the Valley.

The poem and legacy

Thomas Buchanan Read's poem, written shortly after the battle and published widely, made the ride a national legend and helped Sheridan's reputation. Sheridan later renamed his horse Winchester, and the animal was preserved after its death and displayed at the Smithsonian Institution.
