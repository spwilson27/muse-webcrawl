The Fairey Albacore was a British single-engine carrier-borne biplane torpedo bomber built by Fairey Aviation between 1939 and 1943 for the Royal Navy Fleet Air Arm. Designed as the successor to the Fairey Swordfish, it offered an enclosed, heated cabin and a more powerful engine, but was judged less nimble and ironically was retired before the Swordfish it was meant to replace.

Design and development

The Albacore was built to Air Ministry Specification S.41/36 for a three-seat torpedo/spotter/reconnaissance aircraft. Powered by a Bristol Taurus radial engine of about 1,065 hp (later 1,130 hp), it carried a crew of three in an enclosed cockpit. It could carry one 18-inch torpedo or up to 2,000 lb of bombs, and was among the first carrier aircraft fitted for dive-bombing duties.

Operational history

Albacores first saw action from land bases in 1940 and from carriers in 1941, including operations with HMS Formidable and HMS Victorious. They took part in the raid on Kirkenes and Petsamo, the Battle of Cape Matapan, the Malta convoys, and support of the landings in North Africa, Sicily, and Salerno. Nicknamed the "Applecore" by its crews, the type was progressively replaced by the Fairey Barracuda from 1943.
