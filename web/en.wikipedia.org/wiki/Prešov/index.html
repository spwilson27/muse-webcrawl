Prešov is a city in eastern Slovakia, the seat of the Prešov Region and the third-largest city in the country. It lies in the Košice Basin, in the valley of the river Torysa, and has a well-preserved historic centre with Renaissance and Baroque burgher houses.

History

First mentioned in 1247, Prešov grew wealthy on trade and on the nearby salt mines of Solivar. It was a free royal town and a centre of the Reformation in Upper Hungary; the Evangelical College, founded in 1667, became an important school. The city witnessed the bloody tribunal of 1687, in which Protestant townspeople were executed by Imperial authorities.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Gothic co-cathedral of St. Nicholas, the Neptune fountain, the Rákóczi Palace, and the salt-mining museum at Solivar. The city is home to the University of Prešov and is a centre of Slovak Rusyn and Ukrainian culture.
