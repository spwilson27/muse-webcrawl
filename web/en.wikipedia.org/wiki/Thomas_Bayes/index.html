The Reverend Thomas Bayes (c. 1701–1761) was an English statistician, philosopher and Presbyterian minister, best known for Bayes' theorem, which describes how to update the probability of a hypothesis as more evidence becomes available.

Theorem and influence

Bayes's key work, 'An Essay Towards Solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances', was published posthumously in 1763 by his friend Richard Price. Long a niche result, Bayesian methods exploded in importance with modern computing and now underpin spam filters, machine learning, medical testing and data analysis — a world away from the wager arguments of his near-contemporary Pascal.
