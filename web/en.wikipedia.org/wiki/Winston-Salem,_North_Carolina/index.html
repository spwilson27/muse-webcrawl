Winston-Salem is a city in Forsyth County, North Carolina, United States, formed in 1913 by the merger of the towns of Winston and Salem. With a population of about 250,000, it is the fifth-most populous city in the state and the center of the Piedmont Triad region alongside Greensboro and High Point.

History

Salem was founded in 1766 by Moravian settlers who had established the Wachovia tract in 1753; its restored Moravian settlement is now the Old Salem Museums & Gardens. Winston, founded in 1849 as the county seat, industrialized rapidly after the Civil War around tobacco (R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company, maker of Camel cigarettes) and textiles (Hanes). The merger of the two towns was approved in 1913.

Economy and culture

Historically dominated by tobacco and textiles, the economy has diversified into health care (Atrium Health Wake Forest Baptist, Novant Health), education (Wake Forest University, Winston-Salem State University, UNC School of the Arts), finance, and technology. The city brands itself the "City of Arts and Innovation," hosting the National Black Theatre Festival and the RiverRun International Film Festival.
