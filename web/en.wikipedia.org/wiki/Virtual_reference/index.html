Virtual reference

Virtual reference is a reference service initiated electronically, often in real time, where patrons employ computers or other Internet technology to communicate with reference staff, without being physically present. Communication channels used frequently in virtual reference include chat, videoconferencing, Voice over IP, co-browsing, e-mail, instant messaging and texting.

Virtual reference services grew rapidly in the early 2000s as libraries sought to extend their reach beyond the physical reference desk. Many libraries adopted commercial software or joined cooperative services that allowed librarians from multiple institutions to staff a shared desk around the clock.

History

Email-based reference services began appearing in academic libraries in the mid-1980s and 1990s. The introduction of web forms and, later, real-time chat software allowed patrons to receive immediate assistance. Large-scale cooperatives, such as the QuestionPoint service administered by OCLC, enabled libraries to pool staffing and offer 24/7 coverage.

Modes of service

Virtual reference is commonly provided through several channels:

  • Chat – real-time text-based interaction, sometimes with co-browsing.
  • Email – asynchronous answers, typically within one business day.
  • Instant messaging and SMS – quick questions answered via popular messaging platforms.
  • Videoconferencing – used for in-depth research consultations.

Guidelines and standards

The Reference and User Services Association (RUSA) of the American Library Association publishes guidelines for implementing and maintaining virtual reference services, covering staffing, training, privacy, and evaluation.
