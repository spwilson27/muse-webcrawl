Scientific nameAnguilla anguilla
FamilyAnguillidae

The European eel (Anguilla anguilla) is a species of eel, a snake-like, catadromous fish. It is normally around 60–80 cm (24–31 in) long and rarely reaches more than 1 m (3 ft 3 in). European eels are born in the Sargasso Sea in the Atlantic Ocean and migrate as larvae across the Atlantic to the coasts of Europe, a journey that can take years. They then spend most of their adult lives in freshwater rivers, streams, and estuaries before returning to the Sargasso Sea to spawn and die.

Life cycle

The life cycle of the European eel begins in the Sargasso Sea, where adults spawn and die. The leaf-shaped larvae, called leptocephali, drift with ocean currents towards Europe. As they approach the coast they metamorphose into transparent "glass eels", then pigmented elvers. Many ascend rivers and streams, becoming yellow eels that may live in freshwater for many years — sometimes decades — before maturing into silver eels and migrating back to the ocean to spawn.

Distribution and habitat

The species is found throughout Europe and parts of North Africa, from the Atlantic coasts of Scandinavia and Iceland to the Mediterranean and Black Seas. It inhabits rivers, lakes, estuaries, and coastal waters, and can travel short distances over wet land between water bodies.

Conservation

The European eel is classified as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Recruitment of glass eels has declined dramatically since the 1980s, owing to a combination of overfishing, barriers to migration such as dams and weirs, habitat loss, pollution, parasites such as Anguillicoloides crassus, and changes in oceanic conditions. The European Union adopted an eel recovery plan requiring member states to reduce anthropogenic mortality and allow at least 40% of silver eels to escape to the sea relative to pristine conditions.

Use by humans

Eels have long been an important food fish in Europe, eaten smoked, jellied, or fried, and glass eels (angulas) are a delicacy in Spain. Because eels cannot yet be bred through their full life cycle in captivity at commercial scale, eel farming depends on wild-caught glass eels, which intensifies pressure on the stock.
