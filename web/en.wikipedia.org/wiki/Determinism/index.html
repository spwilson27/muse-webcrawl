Determinism

Determinism
FieldMetaphysics
ThesisEvery event is necessitated by antecedent conditions and the laws of nature
Key debateCompatibility with free will
[Image: A row of falling dominoes, the standard image of causal determinism]

Determinism is the philosophical view that all events, including human choices, are completely determined by previously existing causes together with the laws of nature. Laplace imagined an intellect knowing all positions and forces could predict the entire future. Determinism's most debated consequence concerns Free will: compatibilists argue freedom can coexist with determination, incompatibilists deny it, and hard determinists accept the loss of free will.

Variants

Causal determinism is distinguished from theological predestination and from fatalism (which skips the causal chain). Quantum mechanics is often said to introduce indeterminism, though its interpretations differ, and randomness in any case does not by itself restore free will — a point philosophers underline.
