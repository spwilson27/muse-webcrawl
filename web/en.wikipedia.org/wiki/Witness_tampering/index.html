Witness tampering

Witness tampering
TypeObstruction of justice offence
U.S. federal law18 U.S.C. § 1512
MethodsIntimidation, threats, bribery, deception

Witness tampering is the act of attempting to improperly influence, alter, or prevent the testimony of a witness in a legal proceeding, or to hinder a person's communication with law enforcement. It is a form of obstruction of justice and is criminalised in most legal systems.

[Image: An empty witness stand in a wood-panelled courtroom]

Forms

Tampering can take many forms: threatening or physically harming a witness, offering bribes in exchange for false or withheld testimony, coaching a witness to lie, or misleading a person to keep them from testifying or reporting a crime. It also covers retaliation against a witness after they have testified. Persuading someone to commit perjury overlaps with subornation of perjury.

United States law

Under federal law, 18 U.S.C. § 1512 makes it a crime to knowingly use intimidation, threats, misleading conduct, or physical force to influence, delay, or prevent testimony in an official proceeding, or to hinder communication with a law enforcement officer or judge. Penalties increase sharply where tampering involves force or threats of violence. The Victim and Witness Protection Act of 1982 substantially strengthened these protections.

Notable cases and protections

Witness tampering charges have featured in organised-crime prosecutions and in high-profile political cases, including charges arising from the Mueller special counsel investigation. To counter tampering, governments operate witness protection programmes — most famously the U.S. Marshals' WITSEC programme — which relocate witnesses whose safety is at risk.
