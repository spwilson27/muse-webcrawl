Kinetic energy weapon

Kinetic energy weapon
TypeWeapon system
PrincipleDestruction by kinetic energy of impact

A kinetic energy weapon (also known as a kinetic weapon, kinetic energy penetrator, or kinetic kill vehicle) is a weapon system based solely on a projectile's kinetic energy to inflict damage, rather than on an explosive or other warhead carried by the projectile. The concept is applied across land, air, and space warfare.

Principle

The kinetic energy of a moving object is proportional to its mass and to the square of its velocity. A projectile travelling at very high velocity — several kilometres per second — can therefore deliver energy comparable to or exceeding that of chemical explosives of the same mass. Upon impact, this energy is converted into shock waves and heat that can penetrate armour or destroy a target outright.

Applications

  • Armour-piercing ammunition: Armour-piercing fin-stabilized discarding sabot (APFSDS) tank rounds use long, dense darts of tungsten or depleted uranium to penetrate tank armour.
  • Missile defence: Hit-to-kill interceptors such as the exoatmospheric kill vehicle of the Ground-Based Midcourse Defense system, the SM-3's LEAP kinetic warhead, and THAAD destroy ballistic missiles by direct collision at closing speeds of many kilometres per second.
  • Anti-satellite weapons: Kinetic-kill ASATs destroy satellites by collision, generating large debris clouds.
  • Orbital bombardment concepts: Proposed systems such as the "Rods from God" concept would drop dense rods from orbit to strike ground targets with meteor-like energy; none has been deployed.
