Aṉangu Pitjantjatjara Yankunytjatjara, known as the APY Lands, is a large, sparsely populated Aboriginal local government area in the far north-west of South Australia. It covers about 102,650 square kilometres — roughly ten percent of the state — and is home to the Pitjantjatjara and Yankunytjatjara peoples, collectively known as Aṉangu. Entry to the Lands is by permit only, under South Australian law.

History

The region remained largely beyond settler control into the twentieth century. The Presbyterian mission at Ernabella (now Pukatja), founded in 1937, was unusual in encouraging language and culture rather than suppressing them; it became the birthplace of the Ernabella Arts centre, the oldest continuously operating Indigenous art centre in Australia. Following years of campaigning, Premier Don Dunstan's government passed the Pitjantjatjara Land Rights Act 1981, which granted inalienable freehold title to the land to Aṉangu Pitjantjatjara, the incorporated body of traditional owners — one of the earliest and most generous land rights settlements in Australia. The Maralinga Tjarutja lands to the south were granted under separate legislation in 1984.

Governance

The APY Lands are administered under the APY Land Rights Act by an executive board elected from the communities. The area functions as a local government body under the name Aṉangu Pitjantjatjara Yankunytjatjara. Governance and service delivery — housing, health, power, and roads across enormous distances — have been recurrently challenging, and the region has been the subject of state inquiries and intervention programs, including efforts to address substance abuse and community safety.

Communities and economy

The main communities include Amata, Pukatja, Mimili, Indulkana, Fregon (Kaltjiti), Kalka, and Pipalyatjara, along with numerous smaller homelands. Art centres such as Ernabella Arts, Tjala Arts (Amata), and Iwantja Arts (Indulkana) are major employers and sources of income, and APY artists are prominently represented in national collections. The annual APY Art Centre Collective exhibitions and events like the Culata? — community cultural festivals support the art economy. Land management, including Indigenous Protected Areas and ranger programs, is another significant activity.

Geography

The Lands encompass the Musgrave Ranges — including Mount Woodroffe (Ngarutjaranya), at 1,435 m the highest peak in South Australia — and the Mann and Tomkinson Ranges to the west, set in arid sandplain and mulga country near the borders with the Northern Territory and Western Australia.
