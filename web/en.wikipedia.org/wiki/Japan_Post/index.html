Japan Post Co., Ltd. (日本郵便株式会社, Nippon Yūbin Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese company that operates Japan's postal service and its nationwide network of roughly 24,000 post offices. It is the operating company for mail delivery within the Japan Post Holdings group.

History

Japan's modern postal service dates to 1871, when Maejima Hisoka established a national system modelled on Britain's. For most of the 20th century the service was run by government ministries and, from 2003, by the public corporation Japan Post. Postal privatisation, championed by Prime Minister Junichiro Koizumi, split the corporation on October 1, 2007 into a holding company and four operating companies, including Japan Post Service for mail and the post-office network.

In 2012 the mail and counter-services companies were merged into the present Japan Post Co. It runs mail and parcel (Yu-Pack) delivery and the post office counters at which customers also access Japan Post Bank savings and Japan Post Insurance products on an agency basis.

Operations

Japan Post delivers billions of mail items annually, though volumes decline steadily with digital substitution, while parcel volumes have grown with e-commerce. Its post offices, present in virtually every municipality, also function as a community infrastructure, offering services such as local watch-over visits for elderly residents arranged with municipalities.
