Robin McLaurin Williams (21 July 1951 – 11 August 2014) was an American actor and comedian. Known for his improvisational genius and rapid-fire character voices in stand-up, he rose to fame as the alien Mork in the sitcom Mork & Mindy (1978–1982) and became one of the most beloved film actors of his generation, moving fluidly between manic comedy and acclaimed dramatic roles.

Career

Williams studied theatre at Juilliard (with Christopher Reeve among his classmates) and honed his act in San Francisco and Los Angeles comedy clubs. After Mork & Mindy, his film breakthrough came with Good Morning, Vietnam (1987), earning the first of four Academy Award nominations. He received further nominations for Dead Poets Society (1989) and The Fisher King (1991), and won the Oscar for Best Supporting Actor as therapist Sean Maguire in Good Will Hunting (1997).

Major films include Popeye (1980), The World According to Garp (1982), Moscow on the Hudson (1984), Hook (1991), Aladdin (1992, voicing the Genie), Mrs. Doubtfire (1993), Jumanji (1995), The Birdcage (1996), Patch Adams (1998), Bicentennial Man (1999), the darker One Hour Photo and Insomnia (both 2002), and the Night at the Museum series.

Personal life and death

Williams struggled at times with alcohol and substance abuse and spoke candidly about depression. He was an avid cyclist, video-game enthusiast (his daughter Zelda was named after the game character), and a tireless USO entertainer for American troops. He died by suicide at his home in Paradise Cay, California, in 2014; his widow and later medical reporting described an autopsy finding of diffuse Lewy body dementia, which had produced severe symptoms in his final year.

Legacy

Williams is widely ranked among the greatest comedians and improvisers of all time. San Francisco's Robin Williams Tunnel (formerly the Waldo Tunnel) and a meadow in Golden Gate Park bear his name.
