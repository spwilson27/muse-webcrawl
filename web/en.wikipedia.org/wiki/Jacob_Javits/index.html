Jacob Javits

Born: Jacob Koppel Javits, May 18, 1904, New York City, New York, U.S.

Died: March 7, 1986 (aged 81), West Palm Beach, Florida, U.S.

Office: United States Senator from New York (1957–1981)

Party: Republican

Jacob Koppel Javits (May 18, 1904 – March 7, 1986) was an American lawyer and politician who served as a United States senator from New York from 1957 to 1981. A member of the Republican Party, he was one of the most prominent liberal Republicans of his era, known for his support of civil rights, labor legislation, and internationalism.

Early life and career

Javits was born in New York City to Jewish immigrant parents. He worked his way through school selling goods door-to-door and attended night classes at New York University, eventually earning a law degree from NYU Law School. He practiced law in New York and became involved in Republican politics.

Political career

Javits was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1946 and served four terms. In 1954 he was elected Attorney General of New York. In 1956 he won election to the U.S. Senate, defeating New York City mayor Robert F. Wagner Jr. He was reelected in 1962, 1968, and 1974.

In the Senate, Javits championed civil rights legislation, the War Powers Resolution of 1973, and measures expanding federal support for education and the arts. He was a leading voice on foreign policy and a supporter of Israel. Diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), he lost the 1980 Republican primary to Alfonse D'Amato and also ran on the Liberal Party line in the general election.

Legacy

The Jacob K. Javits Convention Center in Manhattan, the largest convention center in New York City, is named in his honor, as is the Jacob K. Javits Federal Building. His papers are held at Stony Brook University.
