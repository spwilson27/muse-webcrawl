Systemic risk

Systemic risk
Field: Finance, economics
Opposite: Idiosyncratic (specific) risk
Regulators: FSB, central banks

In finance, systemic risk is the risk of collapse of an entire financial system or market, as opposed to risk associated with a single entity. It arises from interconnections, contagion, common exposures and pro-cyclical leverage.

Mechanisms

Bank runs, fire sales, counterparty chains and "too big to fail" institutions transmit shocks. The 2008 financial crisis — in which Lehman Brothers' failure froze credit markets — is the classic modern example. Systemic risk is distinct from systematic (market) risk in portfolio theory, which cannot be diversified away.

Regulation

Responses include macroprudential policy, higher capital and liquidity requirements (Basel III), stress tests, designation of systemically important financial institutions (G-SIBs/SIFIs), resolution regimes, and bodies such as the Financial Stability Board.
