Vigna is a genus of plants in the legume family, Fabaceae, comprising roughly one hundred species distributed mainly across the tropics of Africa, Asia and the Americas. The genus is named for the Italian botanist Domenico Vigna. Many species are important food crops, valued for their edible seeds and pods and for their ability to fix nitrogen in poor soils.

Cultivated species

Well-known crops in the genus include the cowpea (Vigna unguiculata), a staple of West African agriculture whose subspecies include the black-eyed pea and the long-podded yardlong bean; the mung bean (V. radiata); the urad bean or black gram (V. mungo); the adzuki bean (V. angularis); the moth bean (V. aconitifolia); and the rice bean (V. umbellata). Several of these were once classified in the genus Phaseolus before being reassigned.

Description

Vigna species are typically annual or perennial herbs with twining, trailing or erect stems, trifoliate leaves, and pea-like flowers in shades of yellow, purple or white. The fruit is a legume pod containing several seeds. Most species are adapted to warm climates and are notably drought-tolerant, making them vital subsistence crops in semi-arid regions.
