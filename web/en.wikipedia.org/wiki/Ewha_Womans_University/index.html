Ewha Womans University (Korean: 이화여자대학교) is a private women's university in Seoul, South Korea. Founded in 1886 by American Methodist missionary Mary F. Scranton, it is one of the most prestigious universities in South Korea and is commonly regarded as the largest female educational institution in the world. The name Ewha — "pear blossom" — was bestowed by Emperor Gojong, after the pear trees near the original campus site. The spelling "Womans" is a deliberate historical choice, reflecting the founders' view that every woman is worthy of respect in her own right.

History

Ewha began as a school for girls in Scranton's home with a single student, at a time when Confucian norms kept most Korean women out of formal education. It expanded into a high school, kindergarten and college, and in 1910 awarded Korea's first university-level degrees to women. Under Japanese rule the institution faced pressure to curtail its programs and use a Japanese name. After liberation in 1945 it was reorganised as a comprehensive university. Ewha graduates include many Korean "firsts" among women — in law, medicine, academia and politics — including Han Myeong-sook, the country's first female prime minister.

Campus

The campus in Seodaemun-gu, near the student district of Sinchon, blends historic stone buildings such as Pfeiffer Hall (1935) with the Ewha Campus Complex (ECC), a dramatic valley-like building designed by French architect Dominique Perrault and opened in 2008. The sunken complex houses lecture halls, a library, shops and a fitness centre beneath a landscaped green slope that has become a Seoul landmark and a popular destination for visitors.

Academics

Ewha comprises more than a dozen colleges and graduate schools, including medicine, law, engineering, art and design, and the Graduate School of International Studies, and operates Ewha Womans University Medical Center with hospitals in Mokdong and Seoul. Although founded for women, some graduate programs admit men. The university's motto is the Korean rendering of "Truth, Goodness, Beauty" (진·선·미).
