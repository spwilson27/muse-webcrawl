iTunes is a media player, media library, Internet radio broadcaster, mobile device management utility, and the client app for the iTunes Store, developed by Apple Inc. It is used to purchase, play, download, and organize digital multimedia on personal computers running macOS and Windows, and can also be used to rip songs from CDs as well as play content with the use of dynamic, smart playlists. Options for sound optimizations exist, as well as ways to wirelessly share the iTunes library.

Released on January 9, 2001, iTunes was originally based on SoundJam MP, a popular Macintosh MP3 player and CD ripper developed by Casady & Greene, which Apple acquired in 2000. Apple simplified the interface and added CD-burning capability. The iTunes Store opened on April 28, 2003, offering music downloads for 99 cents per song, and integration with the iPod helped make iTunes the dominant digital music platform of the 2000s.

History

Through successive versions, iTunes gained support for video, podcasts, audiobooks, and iOS app management. With the release of macOS Catalina in 2019, Apple split iTunes on the Mac into separate Music, Podcasts, and TV apps, with device management moved to Finder. The Windows version retained the iTunes name for several more years before Apple began migrating its functions to dedicated Apple Music, Apple TV, and Apple Devices apps.

Features

iTunes organizes media in a library database, supports playlists and smart playlists, metadata editing, and the Genius feature, which creates playlists from songs that "go great together." It historically synced content to iPod, iPhone, and iPad devices, and backed up iOS devices. Streaming via AirPlay, Home Sharing, and the integrated iTunes Store were central features throughout its life.

Criticism

iTunes was frequently criticized as bloated software, particularly on Windows, where users complained of slow performance and the accumulation of unrelated features. Apple's decision to split it into focused apps on macOS was widely seen as an acknowledgment of this criticism.
