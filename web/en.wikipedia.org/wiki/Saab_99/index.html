The Saab 99 was a mid-size car manufactured by Swedish automaker Saab from 1968 to 1984. It was the company's first genuinely new model line in nearly two decades, a larger and more modern companion to the Saab 96, and it established design and engineering themes — front-wheel drive, a wraparound windscreen, the ignition key between the front seats — that defined Saabs for decades.

Development

Designed under Sixten Sason, the 99 used a 1.7-litre (later 1.85 and 2.0-litre) slant-four engine developed with Triumph, mounted longitudinally and driving the front wheels. It was praised for its roadholding, safety engineering and winter capability. Body styles expanded from the original sedan to the Combi Coupé hatchbacks introduced in 1974.

99 Turbo

In 1978 Saab launched the 99 Turbo, one of the first production cars to use a small turbocharger tuned for everyday drivability rather than outright racing power. The Turbo became an icon of the brand, popularised turbocharging across the industry, and achieved rally success in the hands of Stig Blomqvist, who won the 1979 Swedish Rally in a 99 Turbo. The model's technologies carried directly into its successor, the Saab 900, launched in 1978; the 99 itself continued in simplified form, with its final years' production reworked as the Saab 90.
