Three Mile Island Nuclear Generating Station

Three Mile Island
Location: Susquehanna River, near Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, US
Reactors: 2 pressurised water reactors (Babcock & Wilcox)
Unit 1 operating: 1974–2019
Unit 2: severely damaged in 1979 accident

Three Mile Island Nuclear Generating Station (TMI) is a nuclear power plant located on an island in the Susquehanna River near Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, in the eastern United States. It had two pressurised water reactors. The plant is best known as the site of the most significant accident in US commercial nuclear power plant history, the partial meltdown of Unit 2 on 28 March 1979.

Units

Unit 1, which entered service in 1974, was unaffected by the accident and continued operating until September 2019, when it was shut down for economic reasons. In 2024, plans were announced to restart Unit 1 — renamed the Crane Clean Energy Center — under a long-term power purchase agreement to supply data centres, targeting a return to service later in the decade. Unit 2, which had begun operation only in December 1978, was permanently disabled by the 1979 accident; its defuelling and cleanup took until 1993, and the unit remains in monitored storage pending final decommissioning.

The 1979 accident

In the early hours of 28 March 1979, a feedwater failure led to a loss of coolant; a relief valve stuck open but an instrument panel falsely indicated it was closed. Operators, misreading the plant's state, reduced emergency cooling water, and the core became uncovered and partially melted. Some radioactive gas was released, though average doses to the surrounding population were very small and no deaths or injuries were attributed to the accident. Cleanup cost roughly $1 billion.

Impact

The accident devastated public confidence in nuclear power in the United States, prompted sweeping regulatory reforms (including the creation of the Institute of Nuclear Power Operations and much greater emphasis on operator training and control-room design), and contributed to a decades-long halt in new reactor orders. It remains rated level 5 on the International Nuclear Event Scale.
