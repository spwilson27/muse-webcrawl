Aries is one of the constellations of the zodiac, lying on the ecliptic between Pisces to the west and Taurus to the east in the Northern Hemisphere. Its name is Latin for "ram", and it represents the golden-fleeced ram Chrysomallos of Greek myth, whose fleece became the prize sought by Jason and the Argonauts. Aries is one of the 48 constellations listed by Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Stars

Aries has no very bright stars. Its brightest is Hamal (Alpha Arietis, magnitude 2.0), an orange giant about 66 light-years away; its name derives from the Arabic for "lamb". Sheratan (Beta Arietis) and Mesarthim (Gamma Arietis, a noted double star) mark the ram's horns. Historically the March equinox point — the "First Point of Aries" — lay in this constellation when the zodiac was codified; precession has since carried it into Pisces.

Deep-sky objects and history

The constellation contains few bright deep-sky objects; the spiral galaxy NGC 772 is its best-known galaxy. Babylonian astronomers knew the stars as the "Hired Man"; the ram identification became dominant in the Hellenistic period. In astrology, Aries is the first sign of the zodiac, associated with the period roughly 21 March – 19 April.
