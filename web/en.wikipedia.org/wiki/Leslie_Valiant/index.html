Leslie Valiant

Leslie Gabriel Valiant (born 28 March 1949) is a British-American computer scientist and computational theorist. He received the Turing Award in 2010 for his transformative contributions to the theory of computation, including the theory of probably approximately correct (PAC) learning.

Born28 March 1949, Budapest, Hungary
FieldsComputer science, machine learning theory
InstitutionsHarvard University
AwardsTuring Award (2010), Knuth Prize, Nevanlinna Prize

Contributions

Valiant introduced the framework of PAC learning in 1984, giving a rigorous mathematical foundation for the question of what machines can learn from examples. In algebraic complexity theory he defined the classes VP and VNP, analogues of P and NP, and showed the permanent is complete for VNP, linking algebraic circuit complexity to a famously hard polynomial.

Other work

He contributed to parallel computation (the bulk synchronous parallel model), holographic algorithms, and the theory of evolvability as a model of biological evolution. His books include Circuits of the Mind (1994) and Probably Approximately Correct (2013).
