Khayal

Khayal (also khyal; from Arabic/Persian for "imagination") is the predominant form of vocal music in the Hindustani classical tradition of North India. It is a free-flowing, improvisatory style built on a short composed piece (bandish) set to a raga and tala, around which the singer elaborates melodic improvisation.

Structure

A khayal performance typically begins with a slow, expansive bada khayal in a slow tempo, followed by a faster chhota khayal. Singers improvise using alap-like development, sargam syllables, and rapid taan passages, accompanied by tabla, harmonium or sarangi, and drone tanpuras.

History

Khayal evolved from dhrupad and qawwali influences between the 17th and 18th centuries, flourished in the Mughal and regional courts, and was codified in the 20th century by gharanas (schools) such as Gwalior, Agra, Kirana and Jaipur-Atrauli.
