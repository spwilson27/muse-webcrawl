Kaoru Ishikawa

Kaoru Ishikawa (石川 馨, 13 July 1915 – 16 April 1989) was a Japanese organisational theorist and professor at the University of Tokyo, renowned for his contributions to quality management. He is best known for the cause-and-effect diagram that bears his name — the Ishikawa or "fishbone" diagram — and for pioneering quality circles and company-wide quality control in post-war Japan.

Career

Ishikawa graduated in applied chemistry from the University of Tokyo in 1939 and worked in industry and the navy before returning to academia. He became involved with the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers (JUSE) and worked alongside visiting American experts W. Edwards Deming and Joseph Juran, adapting statistical quality control to Japanese industry.

Contributions

Ishikawa advocated "company-wide quality control" (CWQC), in which quality is the responsibility of every employee, not only inspectors. From 1962 he promoted quality circles — small groups of workers meeting voluntarily to solve workplace problems — which became a hallmark of Japanese manufacturing. His fishbone diagram (introduced in the 1960s) organises potential causes of a problem into categories such as manpower, methods, machines, materials, measurement and environment. He also popularised the "seven basic tools of quality" and emphasised internal customers: "the next process is your customer".

His book What Is Total Quality Control? The Japanese Way (1981) became a standard text. The Ishikawa Medal, awarded by JUSE, commemorates his work.
