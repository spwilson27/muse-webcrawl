Hugh Samuel Johnson (5 August 1882 – 15 April 1942) was an American Army officer, businessman and government administrator. A member of Franklin D. Roosevelt's Brain Trust, he helped draft the National Industrial Recovery Act of 1933 and became the first administrator of the National Recovery Administration (NRA), the centrepiece agency of the early New Deal.

Early life and military career

Johnson was born in Fort Scott, Kansas, raised in Oklahoma Territory, and graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1903. Commissioned in the cavalry, he served under and befriended George S. Patton, worked on water and park administration in California (including as an early administrator associated with Yosemite), and rose during the First World War as a principal drafter of the Selective Service (conscription) regulations and of wartime industrial-mobilization planning, reaching the rank of brigadier general in 1918 — then the youngest general officer in the Army.

Moline Plow and the New Deal

After retiring from the Army in 1919, Johnson became an executive and president of the Moline Plow Company, and later an adviser to financier Bernard Baruch. In 1932–33 he drafted much of the National Industrial Recovery Act. As NRA administrator from June 1933, he drove the rapid creation of industry "codes of fair competition", the Blue Eagle publicity campaign, and a nationwide parade and publicity blitz in the summer of 1933 that made him Time magazine's Man of the Year. His combative, erratic style, disputes with businesses and labour, and concern in Washington over his manner led Roosevelt to ease him out in the autumn of 1934; the Supreme Court struck down the NRA's code system in 1935.

Later life

Johnson became a syndicated newspaper columnist, broke with Roosevelt over the Court-packing plan and foreign policy, and supported isolationism before the attack on Pearl Harbor. He died in Washington, D.C., in April 1942.
