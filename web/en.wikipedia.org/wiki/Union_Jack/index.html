The Union Jack, officially the Union Flag, is the national flag of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. It combines the crosses of the patron saints of England, Scotland and Ireland: the red cross of St George, the white diagonal saltire of St Andrew on blue, and the red saltire of St Patrick.

History

The first Union Flag was created by royal proclamation in 1606, following the Union of the Crowns under James VI and I, superimposing the English cross of St George on the Scottish saltire of St Andrew. This design was adopted for the Commonwealth and then restored at the monarchy's return. The present flag dates from 1 January 1801, when the union with Ireland added St Patrick's red saltire, counterchanged with St Andrew's white saltire so that neither takes precedence over the other.

Usage and details

Strictly, "Union Jack" properly refers to the flag when flown as a jack aboard a naval vessel, though the term is used universally and Parliament declared in 1908 that the Union Flag should be regarded as the national flag. The flag has a correct way up: in the upper hoist canton, the white stripe of St Andrew should be above the red stripe of St Patrick — flying it upside down is traditionally a distress signal. Wales is not represented, having been legally part of the Kingdom of England when the original flag was designed.

The Union Flag appears in the canton of numerous other flags, including those of Australia and New Zealand and several British Overseas Territories.
