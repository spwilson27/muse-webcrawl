Wedderburn's little theorem

Wedderburn's little theorem
Field: Abstract algebra
Proved by: Joseph Wedderburn (1905)

Wedderburn's little theorem states that every finite division ring is a field; that is, a finite division ring is necessarily commutative. It was proved by Joseph Wedderburn in 1905, with the first complete proof later corrected and simplified by Leonard Dickson, and elegant proofs subsequently given by Ernst Witt and others.

Statement and proof ideas

Let D be a finite division ring with centre Z, where Z is a finite field with q elements and D has dimension n over Z. If n > 1, counting arguments using the class equation on the multiplicative group of D, together with properties of cyclotomic polynomials, yield a contradiction unless n = 1; hence multiplication is commutative and D is a finite field. The quintessential example that the theorem rules out would be a finite analogue of the quaternions.

Significance

The result shows that the axioms for a finite projective geometry or a finite division ring force commutativity, and it is a classic illustration of powerful counting methods in algebra. See also division ring.
