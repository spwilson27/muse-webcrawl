Royal Highness

TypeStyle / form of address
AbbreviationHRH
Used forPrinces and princesses of royal houses
Higher styleImperial Highness; Royal Majesty (historical)

Royal Highness is a style used to address or refer to some members of royal families, usually princes, princesses and dukes. It is abbreviated HRH. The style ranks below Imperial Highness and above Serene Highness, Grand Ducal Highness and Highness.

History

Styles of address developed in the European courts of the early modern period as rulers sought to grade the precedence of royal relatives. "Royal Highness" came into wide use in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; in Britain its use was formalised in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, with letters patent issued by George V in 1917 restricting the style to the children of the sovereign, the children of the sovereign's sons, and the eldest living son of the eldest son of the Prince of Wales — later extended to all children of the eldest son of the Prince of Wales by Elizabeth II in 2012.

Usage

In the United Kingdom, the style is borne by working members of the royal family; in 2020 the Duke and Duchess of Sussex agreed not to use their HRH styles while remaining holders of them. Many continental monarchies, including Spain, the Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark and Norway, use the style for members of their royal houses, though reforms in several countries have limited it to a smaller circle in recent decades.
