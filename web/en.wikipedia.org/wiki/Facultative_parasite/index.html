Facultative parasite

A facultative parasite is an organism that may resort to parasitism but does not absolutely rely on a host to complete its life cycle; it can survive and reproduce in a free-living state. This contrasts with an obligate parasite, which cannot complete its life cycle without a suitable host.

Examples

Well-known facultative parasites include the free-living amoebae Naegleria fowleri and Acanthamoeba species, which normally feed on bacteria in soil and water but can invade human tissues, causing rare but severe diseases such as primary amoebic meningoencephalitis and granulomatous amoebic encephalitis. Some fungi (e.g. Aspergillus) and certain nematode larvae likewise live freely but opportunistically infect hosts. In botany, facultative parasites such as some mistletoes can photosynthesise and survive, though poorly, without a host.

Significance

Because facultative parasites persist in the environment independently of hosts, they cannot be eliminated by treating hosts alone; control focuses on environmental exposure, water treatment and hygiene.
