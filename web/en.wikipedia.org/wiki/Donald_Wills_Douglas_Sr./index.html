Donald Wills Douglas Sr.

Donald Wills Douglas Sr. (April 6, 1892 – February 1, 1981) was an American aircraft industrialist and engineer who founded the Douglas Aircraft Company in 1921. Under his leadership the company produced many of the most important aircraft of the 20th century, including the DC-3 airliner and a long line of military and commercial aircraft.

Early life

Douglas was born in Brooklyn, New York. After attending the United States Naval Academy, he studied mechanical engineering at MIT, graduating in 1914. He worked in aviation during the First World War, including as chief engineer of the Glenn L. Martin Company, where he designed the Martin MB-1 bomber.

Douglas Aircraft Company

In 1920 Douglas moved to Los Angeles and founded what became the Douglas Aircraft Company. Its early successes included the World Cruiser aircraft that made the first aerial circumnavigation of the globe in 1924 for the U.S. Army Air Service. The company's DC series of airliners, beginning with the DC-1 and DC-2 and culminating in the DC-3 of 1935, revolutionised commercial air transport. During the Second World War, Douglas plants built nearly 30,000 aircraft, including the C-47 Skytrain and the SBD Dauntless dive bomber. Postwar products included the DC-6, DC-7, and the jet-powered DC-8.

Later years

Douglas remained president and then chairman of the company until its merger with McDonnell Aircraft in 1967, forming McDonnell Douglas. He died in Palm Springs, California, in 1981.
