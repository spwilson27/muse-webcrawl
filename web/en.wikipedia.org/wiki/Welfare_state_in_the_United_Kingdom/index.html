Welfare state in the United Kingdom

UK welfare state
Key reportBeveridge Report (1942)
Key legislationNational Insurance Act 1946; National Health Service Act 1946; National Assistance Act 1948
Main institutionNational Health Service (NHS)

The welfare state in the United Kingdom began to develop during the early 20th century and greatly expanded under the Labour government of 1945–1951. It is usually taken to mean the system of social security, publicly funded healthcare, public housing, education and related social services established largely on the principles set out in the 1942 Beveridge Report.

Origins

Early steps included the Old Age Pensions Act 1908 and the National Insurance Act 1911, introduced by the Liberal government of H. H. Asquith, which provided contributory insurance against sickness and unemployment for some workers. In 1942 the economist and social reformer William Beveridge published Social Insurance and Allied Services, identifying five "giants" to be defeated: Want, Disease, Ignorance, Squalor and Idleness.

The post-war settlement

The Labour government elected in 1945 under Clement Attlee implemented most of Beveridge's proposals: the National Insurance Act 1946 created a comprehensive contributory social security system; the National Health Service, founded in 1948 under Aneurin Bevan, provided healthcare free at the point of use funded from taxation; and the National Assistance Act 1948 replaced the old Poor Law. Large-scale council house building and the expansion of secondary education under the Education Act 1944 formed part of the same settlement.

Later developments

The broad post-war consensus on the welfare state lasted into the 1970s. From 1979 the Conservative governments of Margaret Thatcher sought to reduce state involvement, selling council housing and encouraging private provision, while spending on health and social security nevertheless continued to grow. Later reforms introduced market mechanisms into the NHS, expanded means-tested benefits and tax credits, and, from 2012, replaced many working-age benefits with Universal Credit. The State Pension age has been raised, and pension provision reformed with automatic enrolment into workplace pensions.
