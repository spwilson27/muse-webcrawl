Runge–Kutta methods

Field: Numerical analysis
Developed: c. 1900 (Carl Runge, Martin Kutta)
Use: Numerical solution of ordinary differential equations

Runge–Kutta methods are a family of implicit and explicit iterative methods for the numerical solution of ordinary differential equations. Developed around 1900 by Carl Runge and Martin Wilhelm Kutta, they improve on Euler's method by sampling the derivative at several points within each step.

The RK4 method

The classic fourth-order method (RK4) advances the solution using a weighted average of four slope estimates — at the start, twice at the midpoint, and at the end of the interval — achieving local error of order h^5 with modest computation.

Variants and use

Adaptive embedded pairs such as Runge–Kutta–Fehlberg and Dormand–Prince (used as the default in MATLAB's ode45) adjust step size automatically. Runge–Kutta methods remain the workhorse of scientific computing, engineering simulation, and physics engines.
