Saab Automobile AB was a Swedish manufacturer of automobiles, founded in 1945 when the aircraft maker Saab AB began a project to design a small car. Its products were known for idiosyncratic engineering rooted in aeronautical practice — front-wheel drive, early adoption of turbocharging, emphasis on safety — and for a loyal, unconventional customer base. The company's main plant was in Trollhättan.

History

The first production model, the Saab 92, appeared in 1949 with a streamlined, teardrop body shaped in a wind tunnel. Successive models — the 93, the long-lived 96 with its two-stroke and later V4 engines, and the 99 — built Saab's reputation in rallying and export markets. The Saab 900 of 1978 became the marque's signature car, and the 900 Turbo helped popularise turbocharging in production cars. The large 9000, developed jointly with Fiat, Lancia and Alfa Romeo, followed in 1984.

Losses led Saab AB to sell half the car business to General Motors in 1989, and GM took full ownership in 2000. Under GM, models such as the 9-3 and 9-5 increasingly shared platforms and components with Opel and other GM brands, a strategy that kept Saab alive but diluted its distinctiveness; the US-market 9-2X and 9-7X were built respectively from Subaru and GM SUV designs. Saab never returned to consistent profitability under GM, and during the 2008–09 financial crisis GM decided to sell or close it.

Bankruptcy and aftermath

In February 2010 GM sold Saab to the Dutch sports-car maker Spyker Cars, but financing problems and a production shutdown followed; Saab Automobile filed for bankruptcy on 19 December 2011. In 2012 the main assets were bought by National Electric Vehicle Sweden (NEVS), which briefly resumed limited production of the 9-3 in Trollhättan before losing the right to use the Saab name in 2016, when the defence company Saab AB declined to license the trademark further. The Saab marque has not been used on new cars since.
