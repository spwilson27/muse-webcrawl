The Triangulum Galaxy is a spiral galaxy 2.73 million light-years from Earth in the constellation Triangulum. It is catalogued as Messier 33 or NGC 598. With a diameter of about 61,000 light-years, it is the third-largest member of the Local Group of galaxies, after the Andromeda Galaxy and the Milky Way.

Observation history

The galaxy was probably first recorded by Giovanni Battista Hodierna before 1654 and was independently discovered by Charles Messier in 1764, who catalogued it as M33. Under exceptionally dark skies it can be glimpsed with the naked eye, making it one of the most distant objects visible without a telescope.

Structure

Triangulum is a pure-disc spiral galaxy without a prominent central bar or bulge. Its spiral arms contain numerous H II regions of active star formation, most notably NGC 604, one of the largest known star-forming regions in the Local Group. The galaxy is thought to be a satellite of the Andromeda Galaxy and may be connected to it by streams of neutral hydrogen and stars.
