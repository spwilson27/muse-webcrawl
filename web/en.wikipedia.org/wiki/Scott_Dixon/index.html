Scott Ronald Dixon (born 22 July 1980) is a New Zealand racing driver who competes in the IndyCar Series, driving the No. 9 Honda for Chip Ganassi Racing. He is a six-time IndyCar Series champion (2003, 2008, 2013, 2015, 2018 and 2020) and winner of the 2008 Indianapolis 500. Nicknamed "the Iceman" for his cool temperament and fuel-saving, methodical driving style, Dixon is among the most successful drivers in American open-wheel racing history.

Early career

Dixon was born in Brisbane, Australia, to New Zealand parents and raised in Auckland. He began racing karts at age seven and won numerous New Zealand titles. Unable to afford to continue, at age 13 he famously obtained a dispensation to race saloon cars, and in 1994 he was involved in a rollover crash in a Nissan Sentra at Pukekohe that drew national attention. He won the Australian Drivers' Championship (Formula Holden) in 1998, then moved to the United States, winning the Indy Lights championship in 2000 with PacWest.

IndyCar career

Dixon graduated to CART in 2001 and won at Nazareth in his third start. When PacWest folded in 2002 he joined Chip Ganassi Racing, and in the unified IndyCar Series won the championship in his first full season with the team in 2003, at age 23. His 2008 season included victory in the Indianapolis 500 from pole position and his second title. Further championships followed in 2013, 2015 (won on a tiebreak over Juan Pablo Montoya), 2018 and 2020.

Dixon's race wins place him second only to A. J. Foyt on the all-time list of American championship car racing victories, and he holds the record for most consecutive seasons with at least one win. He has also been a successful endurance racer, winning the 24 Hours of Daytona overall three times (2006, 2015, 2020). In the 2019 New Year Honours he was appointed a Companion of the New Zealand Order of Merit for services to motorsport.
