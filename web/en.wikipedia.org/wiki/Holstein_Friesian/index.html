Holstein Friesian

The Holstein Friesian is an international breed (or group of breeds) of dairy cattle. It originated in the Dutch provinces of North Holland and Friesland and in the adjoining region of Schleswig-Holstein in northern Germany. It is the dominant breed in industrial-scale dairy farming worldwide, known for its high milk yield, its distinctive black-and-white (occasionally red-and-white) pied markings, and its large size.

History

Dutch and German farmers bred cattle for maximal milk production on the abundant grasslands of the North Sea coast for centuries. In the 19th century, Friesian cattle were imported into North America, where the American "Holstein" population was developed through intensive selection; herd-book societies were founded in both the Netherlands and the United States in the 1870s–1880s. In the later 20th century the North American type — larger and higher-yielding — was widely re-imported to Europe and crossed into European Friesian populations, producing the modern global Holstein Friesian. Separate populations with somewhat different breeding goals are now sometimes distinguished, for example the American Holstein, the British Friesian, and the Dutch Friesian.

Characteristics and production

Holstein Friesians are large cattle: mature cows commonly weigh around 680 kg and stand about 145 cm at the shoulder. Their milk production is the highest of any dairy breed; well-managed cows routinely produce over 10,000 kg of milk per lactation, and record individuals have exceeded 30,000 kg in a year. The milk has a comparatively modest butterfat content of around 3.7% and protein of about 3.2%. Because of intense selection for yield, the breed is associated with relatively lower fertility and longevity than some less specialized breeds, a concern that has driven crossbreeding programs (such as with Jerseys and Scandinavian Reds) and the inclusion of health and fertility traits in modern breeding indices.
