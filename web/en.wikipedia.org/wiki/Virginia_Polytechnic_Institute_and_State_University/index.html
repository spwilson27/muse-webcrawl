Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, commonly known as Virginia Tech and traditionally VPI, is a public land-grant research university with its main campus in Blacksburg, Virginia. It also has educational facilities in six regions statewide, a research center in Punta Cana, Dominican Republic, and a study-abroad site in Riva San Vitale, Switzerland. Through its Corps of Cadets ROTC program, Virginia Tech is designated as one of six senior military colleges in the United States.

Founded in 1872, the university is the state's second-largest public university by enrollment. It offers about 280 undergraduate and graduate degree programs to more than 38,000 students and manages a research portfolio of more than $600 million. Virginia Tech is among the top U.S. universities in engineering and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

History

Virginia Tech was established under the Morrill Land-Grant Act of 1862. In 1872 the Virginia General Assembly purchased the facilities of Preston and Olin Institute, a Methodist school, and chartered the Virginia Agricultural and Mechanical College. The school became Virginia Agricultural and Mechanical College and Polytechnic Institute (VPI) in 1896. Women were admitted in 1921, and the institution gained university status in 1970 with its present name. Its distinctive architecture—Collegiate Gothic buildings faced in "Hokie Stone," a native limestone—defines the central campus, anchored by the Drillfield and Burruss Hall.

Academics

The university's colleges include the College of Engineering (the largest and most prominent), the College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, the Pamplin College of Business, the College of Science, the College of Architecture, Arts, and Design, the College of Liberal Arts and Human Sciences, the College of Natural Resources and Environment, and the Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine and the Virginia–Maryland College of Veterinary Medicine. Virginia Tech's cooperative extension and agricultural experiment stations serve its land-grant mission across the commonwealth.

Virginia Tech shooting

On April 16, 2007, a gunman killed 32 students and faculty members and injured 17 others in attacks at West Ambler Johnston Hall and Norris Hall before taking his own life. The massacre, then the deadliest school shooting in U.S. history, prompted nationwide reviews of campus security and mental-health policy and remains a defining tragedy in the university's history; April 16 is commemorated annually by the community, which adopted the phrase "We will prevail. We are Virginia Tech."

Athletics and traditions

The Virginia Tech Hokies compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The football program, long associated with coach Frank Beamer, plays at Lane Stadium and is known for its "Enter Sandman" entrance and the lunch pail defense tradition. The school colors—Chicago maroon and burnt orange—were adopted in 1896, and the HokieBird mascot evolved from the "Gobbler" nickname. The Corps of Cadets, dating to the school's founding, maintains military traditions and the Skipper cannon fired at football games.
