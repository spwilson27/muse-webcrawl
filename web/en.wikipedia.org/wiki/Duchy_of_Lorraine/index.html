Duchy of Lorraine

Duchy of Lorraine (Duché de Lorraine)
Status: Duchy of the Holy Roman Empire
Existed: 959–1766
Capital: Nancy

The Duchy of Lorraine was a duchy of the Holy Roman Empire in what is now north-eastern France. Descended from the division of Lotharingia, Upper Lorraine became a hereditary duchy in 1047 under the House of Alsace (later the House of Lorraine), which ruled it for more than seven centuries.

History

Lorraine sat on the contested frontier between France and the Empire and was repeatedly invaded and occupied by French kings, notably during the Thirty Years' War and by Louis XIV. Charles III and Leopold I nonetheless presided over periods of prosperity from the capital, Nancy.

In 1736 Duke Francis III Stephen ceded Lorraine as part of the settlement of the War of the Polish Succession: he received Tuscany in exchange and married Maria Theresa of Austria, founding the House of Habsburg-Lorraine. Lorraine was granted for life to Stanisław Leszczyński, the former king of Poland, and on his death in 1766 it was annexed by France.

Legacy

The Cross of Lorraine became a symbol of the region and later of Free France. The modern French region of Lorraine, now part of Grand Est, preserves the duchy's name and identity.
