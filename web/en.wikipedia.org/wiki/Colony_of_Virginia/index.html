Colony of Virginia

The Colony of Virginia was an English, later British, colony in North America from 1607 to 1776, when it became the independent Commonwealth of Virginia. Founded by the Virginia Company of London at Jamestown, it was England's first permanent colony in the Americas.

History

The first settlers endured the 'starving time' of 1609–10 and conflict and uneasy trade with the Powhatan Confederacy; the marriage of Pocahontas and John Rolfe, whose tobacco exports saved the colony economically, brought a brief peace. In 1619 the House of Burgesses, the first elected assembly in English America, met — and the first enslaved Africans arrived in Virginia, beginning the system of slavery that tobacco plantations expanded massively after Bacon's Rebellion (1676).

Society and revolution

A planter gentry came to dominate the colony, the largest and most populous of the Thirteen Colonies by the 18th century. Virginia produced four of the first five U.S. presidents — Washington, Adams's successor Jefferson, Madison and Monroe — and its 1776 Declaration of Rights influenced the U.S. Bill of Rights.
