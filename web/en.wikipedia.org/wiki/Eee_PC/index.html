The Asus Eee PC is a line of small, inexpensive laptop computers launched by ASUSTeK in October 2007. Designed as an affordable, portable machine for basic computing — the name "Eee" came from the slogan "Easy to learn, Easy to work, Easy to play" — the original Eee PC 701 had a 7-inch display, a solid-state drive of 2–8 GB, 512 MB of RAM, a slow Intel Celeron processor and a custom version of the Xandros Linux operating system, at prices from about US$200–400.

Impact

The Eee PC's success created the netbook category: within a year, Acer, HP, Dell, MSI and others had launched competitors, and Intel developed its Atom processor expressly for the class. Microsoft responded by extending the availability of Windows XP for netbooks. The line expanded to 8.9-, 10- and 12-inch models and, in the Eee Top and Eee Box, to desktops. Netbook sales collapsed after 2010 as tablets such as the iPad and cheap full-size laptops squeezed the category, and Asus discontinued the Eee PC line in 2013. The Eee PC is remembered as an influential step toward cheap, solid-state, cloud-oriented personal computing.
