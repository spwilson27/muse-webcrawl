Gloucestershire Regiment

The Gloucestershire Regiment, commonly referred to as the Glosters, was a line infantry regiment of the British Army from 1881 until 1994. It traced its origins to 1694, when Colonel Gibson's Regiment of Foot was raised. The regiment was formed under the Childers Reforms by the merger of the 28th (North Gloucestershire) and 61st (South Gloucestershire) Regiments of Foot.

The Glosters were unique in wearing a badge on both the front and the back of their headdress — the "back badge" commemorating the 28th Foot's stand at the Battle of Alexandria in 1801, when the regiment fought back-to-back against French attacks from both directions. The regiment served in the Boer War, both world wars, and most famously at the Battle of the Imjin River in the Korean War, where its 1st Battalion held Hill 235 — later named Gloster Hill — against overwhelming Chinese forces.

In 1994 the regiment amalgamated with the Duke of Edinburgh's Royal Regiment to form the Royal Gloucestershire, Berkshire and Wiltshire Regiment, whose traditions are now carried by The Rifles.
