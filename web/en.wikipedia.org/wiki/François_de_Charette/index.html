François de Charette

François Athanase de Charette de La Contrie (2 May 1763 – 29 March 1796) was a French Royalist soldier and politician. He served in the French Navy during the American Revolutionary War and was one of the leaders of the War in the Vendée against the French Revolutionary Government; his fame has made him a hero of the French counter-Revolution movement.

Early life and career

Born in Couffé, Charette served in the French Navy, fighting in the American Revolutionary War and attaining the rank of lieutenant de vaisseau. After the outbreak of the French Revolution, he emigrated briefly but returned to France in 1792 and participated in the defence of the Tuileries Palace on 10 August 1792.

War in the Vendée

When the peasants of the Vendée rose against the Republic in March 1793, they asked Charette to lead them. He accepted reluctantly and organized the Catholic and Royal Army of the Bas-Poitou. A skilled practitioner of guerrilla warfare, he fought a series of campaigns in the marais (marshes) around Machecoul and Noirmoutier. In June 1795 he renewed hostilities after receiving arms from Britain, but the failure of the Quiberon expedition deprived him of support. Captured near La Chabotterie on 23 March 1796, he was tried and shot by firing squad at Nantes on 29 March 1796.

Legacy

Charette remains one of the most celebrated figures of the Vendéen resistance; Napoleon is reported to have called him "the only great man" of the war in the Vendée.
