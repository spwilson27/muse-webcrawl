Dost Mohammad Khan Barakzai (Pashto/Dari: دوست محمد خان; 23 December 1793 – 9 June 1863) was the founder of the Barakzai dynasty and one of the prominent rulers of Afghanistan during the First Anglo-Afghan War. With the decline of the Durrani dynasty, he became Emir of Kabul in 1826, founding the Emirate of Afghanistan.

Early life and rise

Dost Mohammad was the son of Sardar Payendah Khan, chief of the Barakzai clan, who was executed by Zaman Shah Durrani in 1799. After years of civil war among the Barakzai brothers and the Durrani emperors, Dost Mohammad seized Kabul in 1826 and assumed the title of Amir al-Mu'minin (Commander of the Faithful) in 1835, choosing religious rather than imperial style.

First Anglo-Afghan War

British fears of Russian influence through Persia's siege of Herat led the Governor-General of India, Lord Auckland, to issue the Simla Manifesto (1838) and invade Afghanistan to restore the exiled Shah Shujah Durrani. Dost Mohammad fled to Bukhara in 1839 but returned and fought on; after defeat he surrendered in 1840 and was exiled to India. The occupation collapsed in the catastrophic British retreat from Kabul in January 1842, and Dost Mohammad was permitted to return, resuming the throne in 1843.

Later reign

During the second half of his reign Dost Mohammad consolidated the Afghan state: he annexed Balkh and Afghan Turkestan (1850s), Kandahar (1855), and finally Herat in May 1863, dying shortly afterward. He concluded treaties with British India in 1855 and 1857, remaining neutral during the Indian Rebellion of 1857 despite pressure to attack. He bequeathed to his successors the territorial core of modern Afghanistan.
