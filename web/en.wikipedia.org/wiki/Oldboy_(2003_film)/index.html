Oldboy (Korean: 올드보이) is a 2003 South Korean action thriller film directed and co-written by Park Chan-wook. A loose adaptation of the Japanese manga Old Boy, written by Garon Tsuchiya and illustrated by Nobuaki Minegishi, the film follows Oh Dae-Su, who is imprisoned in a cell resembling a hotel room for 15 years without knowing the identity of his captor or his captor's motives. When he is finally released, Dae-Su learns he must find his captor within five days, or something terrible will happen — and that he has become entangled in a web of conspiracy and violence.

The film premiered at the 2004 Cannes Film Festival, where it won the Grand Prix, the festival's second-highest honour; jury president Quentin Tarantino was a vocal champion of the film. Critically lauded, Oldboy is frequently cited as one of the greatest films of all time and one of the finest neo-noir films ever made. It is the second instalment of Park's Vengeance Trilogy, preceded by Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance (2002) and followed by Lady Vengeance (2005).

Plot

In 1988, businessman Oh Dae-su is arrested for drunkenness and misses his daughter's birthday. After a friend bails him out, Dae-su is abducted and wakes in a sealed hotel room, where he is held for 15 years, fed fried dumplings through a slot and gassed into sleep for haircuts. He learns from television that his wife has been murdered and he is the prime suspect. In 2003 he is suddenly released, given clothes, money, and a mobile phone, and taunted by his captor to discover the reason for his imprisonment within five days. befriending young sushi chef Mi-do, Dae-su tracks his prison to a private jail and confronts Lee Woo-jin, a wealthy man who gradually reveals their shared school history and the rumour Dae-su witnessed and repeated — with devastating consequences Woo-jin has engineered his revenge around.

Production

The film is noted for its stylised violence and its famous single-take corridor fight scene, in which Dae-su fights a corridor full of thugs with a claw hammer, filmed in one continuous shot. In another notorious scene, Choi Min-sik, a Buddhist and vegetarian, ate a live octopus; the scene required four takes and was widely discussed internationally.

Reception

Oldboy was praised for its direction, cinematography, editing, and performances. It was ranked among the best films of the 2000s in numerous critics' polls and placed 64th in the BBC's 2016 list of the 100 greatest films of the 21st century. It helped drive international interest in South Korean cinema.

Remake

An American remake, also titled Oldboy, was directed by Spike Lee and released in 2013, starring Josh Brolin and Elizabeth Olsen; it received mixed reviews and performed poorly at the box office.
