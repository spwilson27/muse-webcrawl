Computer virus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Stylised graphic of a magnifying glass over code with a warning symbol, representing virus detection]

A computer virus is a type of malware that, when executed, replicates itself by modifying other computer programs and inserting its own code into them. If this replication succeeds, the affected areas are then said to be 'infected' with a computer virus, a metaphor derived from biological viruses.

The concept was theorised by John von Neumann in 1949 (self-reproducing automata) and demonstrated playfully by Creeper on the ARPANET in 1971. The first viruses for personal computers appeared in the early 1980s — Elk Cloner (1982) spread between Apple II floppy disks — and Brain (1986), written by two brothers in Lahore, Pakistan, became the first widespread IBM PC virus. Viruses spread with their host files, later via documents with macros in the 1990s and email attachments, often carrying 'payloads' from pranks to data destruction.

As self-spreading worms and stealthier trojans took over the headlines, the pure virus became a smaller share of malware, but the word stuck as the generic term — hence 'antivirus' software, an industry founded in the late 1980s that grew into modern endpoint security.
