Ishvara

Ishvara

Tradition: Hindu philosophy

Meaning: Lord, supreme ruler, God

Related concepts: Brahman, Atman, Saguna Brahman

Ishvara (Sanskrit: ईश्वर, Īśvara) is a concept in Hinduism, varying in meaning across schools of Hindu philosophy. It generally refers to the supreme being, the lord or ruler of the universe, a personal God. In ancient and medieval Indian texts, Ishvara is variously identified with Shiva, Vishnu, or the supreme Self, depending on the tradition.

In the schools of philosophy

In Yoga philosophy, Ishvara is a special Self (purusha-vishesha) untouched by afflictions, toward whom devotion (Ishvara-pranidhana) is a means to samadhi. In Nyaya, Ishvara is inferred as the intelligent cause and organizer of the world. In Advaita Vedanta, Ishvara is Brahman associated with maya, the cosmic lord who governs the manifested universe, while ultimate reality is nirguna Brahman beyond attributes.

Devotional traditions

In Bhakti traditions, Ishvara is the personal deity worshipped by devotees — for Shaivas, Shiva; for Vaishnavas, Vishnu or Krishna. The term emphasizes God's immanence and sovereignty as the inner controller of all beings.
