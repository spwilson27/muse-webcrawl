River Test

SourceAshe, Hampshire
MouthSouthampton Water
Lengthabout 64 km (40 mi)
BasinHampshire, England

The River Test is a chalk stream in Hampshire, England. It rises near the village of Ashe, west of Basingstoke, and flows south through the towns of Whitchurch, Stockbridge and Romsey before joining Southampton Water near Totton. It is one of the most famous trout fly-fishing rivers in the world.

Course

The Test is fed by springs in the chalk of the Hampshire Downs, giving it exceptionally clear, constant-temperature water. Its tributaries include the Bourne Rivulet, the River Dever, the River Anton and the River Dun. Below Romsey the river divides into braided channels across its wide flood plain before entering the tidal estuary of Southampton Water.

Fly fishing

The Test is regarded as the birthplace of modern dry-fly fishing. In the late 19th century Frederic M. Halford and others developed the techniques of upstream dry-fly presentation on its beats around Stockbridge. The river remains famous for brown trout and for the mayfly hatches known locally as "duffer's fortnight".

Ecology

Much of the valley is protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest. The chalk-stream habitat supports water voles, Atlantic salmon, grayling and rare invertebrates, though abstraction and water quality remain concerns.
