Peter Gabriel

Born: Peter Brian Gabriel, 13 February 1950, Chobham, Surrey, England

Genres: Progressive rock, art rock, pop, worldbeat

Years active: 1967–present

Known for: Genesis co-founder; So; "Sledgehammer"; WOMAD

Peter Brian Gabriel (born 13 February 1950) is an English singer, songwriter and humanitarian. He rose to fame as the original lead singer of the progressive rock band Genesis, which he co-founded in 1967 while at Charterhouse School, and left in 1975 after the tour for The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway, having become famous for his theatrical stage costumes.

Solo career

Gabriel's first four solo albums are all titled Peter Gabriel; the fourth (1982), marketed as Security in the US, contained "Shock the Monkey". His fifth album, So (1986), was a major commercial success, containing "Sledgehammer" — whose innovative stop-motion video won nine MTV Video Music Awards — "Don't Give Up" (a duet with Kate Bush), "Big Time" and "In Your Eyes", the last famous from the film Say Anything.... Later albums include Us (1992), Up (2002), the orchestral covers project Scratch My Back (2010) and i/o (2023), his first album of new material in over two decades.

World music and activism

Gabriel founded the WOMAD festival in 1982 and the Real World Records label and studios in Wiltshire to promote world music. His soundtrack for The Last Temptation of Christ, released as Passion (1989), won a Grammy Award. A longstanding human-rights campaigner, he co-founded the organisation WITNESS and received Amnesty International's Ambassador of Conscience award in 2008. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice: as a member of Genesis (2010) and as a solo artist (2014).
