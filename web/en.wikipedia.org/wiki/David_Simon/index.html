David Judah Simon (born 9 February 1960) is an American author, journalist and television producer best known as the creator of the HBO series The Wire (2002–2008), frequently cited among the greatest television dramas ever made.

Journalism

Simon spent thirteen years as a police reporter for The Baltimore Sun. His nonfiction book Homicide: A Year on the Killing Streets (1991), based on a year shadowing the Baltimore Police Department's homicide unit, became the basis for the NBC series Homicide: Life on the Street, on which he worked as a writer and producer. With former detective Ed Burns he wrote The Corner: A Year in the Life of an Inner-City Neighborhood (1997), adapted as an Emmy-winning HBO miniseries in 2000.

Television

The Wire used Baltimore's drug trade to examine American institutions — police, docks, city government, schools and media — across five seasons. Simon later co-created Treme (2010–2013), set in post-Katrina New Orleans; the miniseries Show Me a Hero (2015) and The Plot Against America (2020); The Deuce (2017–2019), about the New York porn industry; and We Own This City (2022), about the Baltimore Gun Trace Task Force scandal. He received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 2010 and is a vocal critic of the war on drugs and of the decline of American journalism. See also The Wire and Baltimore.
