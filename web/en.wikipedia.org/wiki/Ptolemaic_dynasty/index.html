Ptolemaic dynasty

The Ptolemaic dynasty (Ancient Greek: Πτολεμαῖοι) was a Macedonian Greek royal family that ruled the Ptolemaic Kingdom in Egypt from 305 BC until the death of Cleopatra VII in 30 BC. Founded by Ptolemy I Soter, a general (Diadochus) of Alexander the Great, it was the last dynasty of ancient Egypt.

History

The Ptolemies ruled from Alexandria, which became a centre of learning with its Library and Museum. They adopted Egyptian pharaonic customs, built temples such as Edfu and Philae, practised sibling marriage, and fought the Seleucid Empire in the Syrian Wars. Dynastic strife and growing Roman influence weakened the kingdom in the 2nd–1st centuries BC.

End

Cleopatra VII's alliances with Julius Caesar and Mark Antony ended with defeat at Actium (31 BC) and Roman annexation in 30 BC.
