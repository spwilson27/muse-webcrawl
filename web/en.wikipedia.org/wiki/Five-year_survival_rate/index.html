Five-year survival rate

The five-year survival rate is a type of survival rate used to measure the prognosis of a disease, most commonly cancer. It is the percentage of people in a study or treatment group who are alive five years after their disease was diagnosed or treatment began.

Use and interpretation

Five-year survival rates are compiled by cancer registries such as the SEER programme in the United States and are quoted by cancer type and stage. Relative survival compares observed survival with the expected survival of a comparable cancer-free population. Improvements in five-year survival over time can reflect better treatment, but also earlier diagnosis (lead-time bias) and detection of indolent cases (overdiagnosis), so the statistic must be interpreted carefully.

Examples

Rates vary enormously: localised breast and prostate cancers have five-year relative survival near or above 99% in some registries, while pancreatic cancer remains around 10–13% overall because it is usually detected late.
