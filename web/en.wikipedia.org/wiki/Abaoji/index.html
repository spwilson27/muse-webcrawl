Abaoji (872 – 6 September 926), also known by his Chinese name Yelü Yi, was a Khitan leader and the founding emperor of the Liao dynasty of China. He united the Khitan tribes of Manchuria and eastern Mongolia, proclaimed himself emperor in 916, and laid the institutional foundations of a dynasty that would rule northern China and the steppe for two centuries.

Early life and rise to power

Abaoji was born into the Yila tribe, the leading clan of the Khitan confederation. In 901 he was elected khan (yaolian khan) of the Khitan, succeeding in a system in which leadership was supposed to rotate among tribal chiefs every three years. Abaoji refused to step down after his term, defeating rebellious uncles and rival chiefs, and in 907 he assumed the title of emperor in the Chinese style, abandoning the elective tradition altogether.

Unification and conquests

Between roughly 907 and 926 Abaoji subjugated neighbouring peoples including the Xi, Shiwei and Jurchen tribes, and in 926 conquered the kingdom of Balhae in eastern Manchuria, installing his eldest son Yelü Bei as ruler of the puppet kingdom of Dongdan. His armies campaigned as far west as the Orkhon valley, former heartland of the Uyghur Khaganate.

Reforms

Drawing on Chinese advisers such as Han Yanhui, Abaoji built a dual administration: a northern government for the nomadic tribes and a southern government, modelled on Tang institutions, for the settled population. In 920 he commissioned a large-character Khitan script, and he founded walled towns and promoted agriculture among his people. He adopted the Chinese dynastic name Liao in 916 (the name was formally fixed only later) and the temple name Taizu.

Death and succession

Abaoji died suddenly on 6 September 926 at Fuyu, shortly after the Balhae campaign, aged about 54. His empress Shulü Ping resisted the succession of the eldest son Yelü Bei and engineered the enthronement of the second son, Yelü Deguang, as Emperor Taizong. Abaoji was buried in a mausoleum at the foot of Mount Muye in what is now Bairin Left Banner, Inner Mongolia.
