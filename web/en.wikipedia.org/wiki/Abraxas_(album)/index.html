Abraxas (album)

Abraxas is the second studio album by American rock band Santana, released on 23 September 1970 by Columbia Records. Building on the Latin rock breakthrough of their debut and their celebrated Woodstock performance, it topped the US Billboard 200 and produced the hit singles "Black Magic Woman" and "Oye Cómo Va". It is widely regarded as one of the greatest albums of all time and was selected for preservation in the US National Recording Registry.

Music

The album fuses blues rock, jazz, salsa and Afro-Cuban rhythms. Its best-known tracks are a medley of Fleetwood Mac's "Black Magic Woman" with Gábor Szabó's "Gypsy Queen", which reached number 4 in the US, and a cover of Tito Puente's mambo "Oye Cómo Va", a top-20 hit that popularised Latin dance music for rock audiences. Original compositions include the instrumental "Samba Pa Ti", Carlos Santana's signature slow ballad, and "Incident at Neshabur", showcasing the interplay of Santana's guitar and Gregg Rolie's Hammond organ. The title refers to the Gnostic deity Abraxas, mentioned in Hermann Hesse's novel Demian, a copy of which the band had been reading.

Artwork and legacy

The cover reproduces Mati Klarwein's 1961 painting Annunciation, depicting the angel Gabriel as a nude woman astride a conga player — imagery that attracted controversy and remains iconic. Abraxas has sold millions of copies worldwide, appears on Rolling Stone's list of the 500 greatest albums, and was inducted into the Latin Grammy Hall of Fame; the Library of Congress added it to the National Recording Registry in 2015.
