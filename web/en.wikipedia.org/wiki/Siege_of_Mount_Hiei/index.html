Siege of Mount Hiei

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Mount Hiei (September 1571) was an assault by Oda Nobunaga on the Tendai Buddhist monastery of Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei, northeast of Kyoto. Nobunaga's troops burned the entire temple complex and massacred thousands, in one of the most notorious acts of his wars of unification.

Background

The warrior monks (sōhei) of Enryaku-ji had for centuries been a political and military power in the capital region. In 1570–1571 they sheltered and supplied Nobunaga's enemies, the Asai and Asakura clans, after Nobunaga's retreat from Kanegasaki, and refused his demands and threats. Nobunaga resolved to destroy the monastery's military power permanently.

The massacre

On 29 September 1571 (Japanese calendar), Nobunaga's army surrounded the mountain, advanced up all its paths and set fire to the temples, halls and dwellings of Enryaku-ji and the town of Sakamoto at its foot. Contemporary accounts state that thousands — monks, laymen, women and children — were killed without distinction; traditional figures run to several thousand dead. The great temple was reduced to ashes.

Consequences

The destruction broke the power of the Tendai sōhei forever and served as a warning to other religious armies, though it also branded Nobunaga with the title "Demon King" (Maō) in the eyes of his enemies. Enryaku-ji was rebuilt under Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu, but never regained military significance.
