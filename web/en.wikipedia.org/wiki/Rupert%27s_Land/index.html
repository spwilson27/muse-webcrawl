Rupert's Land, or Prince Rupert's Land, was a territory in British North America comprising the Hudson Bay drainage basin. It was a de facto colony of the Hudson's Bay Company for 200 years, from 1670 to 1870, and was named after Prince Rupert of the Rhine, the first governor of the company.

History

The royal charter granted by Charles II in 1670 gave the Hudson's Bay Company exclusive trading rights over all lands whose waters drained into Hudson Bay — an area of roughly 3.9 million square kilometres, covering much of what is now central and northern Canada and parts of the northern United States. The fur trade conducted from posts such as York Factory was the territory's economic foundation, and it brought the company into long rivalry with French traders and later the North West Company.

Transfer to Canada

Under the Rupert's Land Act 1868, the Hudson's Bay Company surrendered the territory to the British Crown, which transferred it to the Dominion of Canada effective 15 July 1870; the company received £300,000 and retained land around its posts. The transfer, negotiated without consulting the territory's Indigenous peoples and Métis inhabitants, provoked the Red River Resistance of 1869–70, leading to the creation of the province of Manitoba.
