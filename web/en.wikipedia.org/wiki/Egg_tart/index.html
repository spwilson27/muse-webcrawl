Egg tart

TypePastry
Main ingredientsPastry crust, egg custard (egg, milk, sugar)
VariationsHong Kong style, Portuguese pastel de nata, Macanese tart

The egg tart is a kind of custard tart found in Chinese, Portuguese, and other cuisines, consisting of an outer pastry crust filled with a lightly sweetened egg custard and baked until set, sometimes with a caramelised surface.

Chinese egg tarts

In Cantonese cuisine the egg tart (daan taat) is a staple of bakeries and dim sum restaurants, believed to have been introduced to Guangzhou and Hong Kong in the early 20th century, influenced by English custard tarts. Hong Kong egg tarts come in two main crust styles: shortcrust pastry and puff pastry. The filling is a smooth, glossy egg custard, less sweet and less spiced than its European counterparts.

Portuguese pastel de nata

The Portuguese version, pastel de nata, originated with monks at the Jerónimos Monastery in Belém, Lisbon, in the 18th century. It uses a crisp puff pastry shell and a rich egg-yolk custard, baked at high temperature so the top blisters and caramelises, often dusted with cinnamon and powdered sugar. The Macanese egg tart, popularised by Lord Stow's Bakery in Macau, blends Portuguese and English influences and is baked with a caramelised top.

Popularity

Egg tarts are sold across East and Southeast Asia, in Chinatowns worldwide, and in bakeries and cafés internationally. In 2014 the Hong Kong egg tart was recognised as part of the city's intangible cultural heritage.
