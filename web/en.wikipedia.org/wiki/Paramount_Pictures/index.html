Paramount Pictures Corporation, commonly known as Paramount Pictures or simply Paramount, is an American film production and distribution company and the flagship subsidiary of Paramount Skydance. It is the fifth-oldest film studio in the world, the second-oldest in the United States behind Universal Pictures, and the sole member of the "Big Five" film studios still located in Hollywood.

History

Paramount was founded by William Wadsworth Hodkinson in 1912 as a film distribution company; in 1916 it merged with Adolph Zukor's Famous Players Film Company and the Jesse L. Lasky Feature Play Company to form Famous Players–Lasky, with Paramount as its distribution arm. Under Zukor the company became the dominant force of the studio system, building a major theatre chain and signing stars such as Mary Pickford and Douglas Fairbanks.

The studio was a defendant in the landmark antitrust case United States v. Paramount Pictures, Inc. (1948), which forced the majors to divest their theatre chains and ended block booking. Acquired by Gulf+Western in 1966 and later part of Viacom, Paramount produced franchises including The Godfather, Star Trek, Indiana Jones (in partnership), Mission: Impossible, Transformers, and Top Gun. In 1994 it acquired the historic studio lot legacy dating to 1926. Following the 2025 merger of Paramount Global with Skydance Media, the studio became part of Paramount Skydance.

Logo

The Paramount logo — a mountain peak encircled by 22 stars (originally 24, representing its first contracted stars) — is among the most recognisable in cinema. The mountain is often said to be modelled on Ben Lomond in Utah or Artesonraju in Peru.
