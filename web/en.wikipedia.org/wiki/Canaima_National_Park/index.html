Canaima National Park

Canaima National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Canaima) is a 30,000 km² park in Gran Sabana, Bolívar State, Venezuela, established on 12 June 1962. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1994) famed for its flat-topped mountains called tepuis and for Angel Falls, the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall.

Geography

The park covers part of the Guayana Highlands. Its tepuis — including Mount Roraima and Auyán-tepui, from which Angel Falls drops 979 metres — are ancient Precambrian sandstone formations with unique ecosystems on their summits.

Flora and fauna

Savanna, gallery forest and tepui summit habitats host endemic plants (including carnivorous plants), giant otters, jaguars, harpy eagles and many endemic frogs and orchids.

People and tourism

The Pemón Indigenous people live in the park and guide visitors to Canaima Lagoon, Sapo Falls and Angel Falls. Access is mainly by air to Canaima camp. See Angel Falls and Tepui.
