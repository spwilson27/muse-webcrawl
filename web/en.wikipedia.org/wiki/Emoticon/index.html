Emoticon

An emoticon (a portmanteau of "emotion" and "icon") is a pictorial representation of a facial expression using punctuation marks, numbers and letters, usually written to express a person's feelings, mood or reaction in text-based communication. The classic Western emoticons :-) and :-( are read sideways.

History

Typographic approximations of faces appeared in print as early as the nineteenth century. In digital communication, computer scientist Scott Fahlman of Carnegie Mellon University is widely credited with proposing the smiley and frowny symbols on 19 September 1982, in a message to a university bulletin board, to distinguish jokes from serious posts. Emoticons spread through email, Usenet, IRC and SMS culture.

Styles

Western emoticons are read rotated 90 degrees, while Japanese kaomoji such as (^_^) and (>_<) are read upright and emphasise the eyes. From the late 1990s, graphical emoji — standardised in Unicode and popularised by smartphones — largely displaced text emoticons in casual messaging, though emoticons persist in plain-text contexts.
