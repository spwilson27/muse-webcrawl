Treaty of Shaoxing

The Treaty of Shaoxing (紹興和議) was the peace agreement of 1141–42 between the Southern Song dynasty of China and the Jurchen-led Jin dynasty, formally ending the Jin–Song wars' main phase. It fixed the boundary between the two states along the Huai River.

Background

After the Jingkang incident of 1127, in which the Jin captured the Song capital Kaifeng and two emperors, the Song court re-established itself in the south under Emperor Gaozong. Under the influence of chancellor Qin Hui, the court pursued peace despite military recovery under generals such as Yue Fei, whose armies had regained ground in the 1130s. Yue Fei was recalled, imprisoned, and executed in early 1142—an act long attributed to Qin Hui's machinations and to Gaozong's fear that victory might restore the captured former emperor.

Terms

The Song accepted vassal-like status in diplomatic forms, paid an annual indemnity of silk and silver, and ceded territory north of the Huai, including the former capitals. In return the Jin recognized the Southern Song and returned the coffin of the late Emperor Huizong and Gaozong's mother.

Legacy

The treaty bought the Southern Song more than two decades of peace and prosperity, but at the price of permanent division; it remains in Chinese historiography a byword for appeasement, and Yue Fei is revered as a patriotic martyr while Qin Hui's statues kneel in perpetual disgrace at his tomb.
