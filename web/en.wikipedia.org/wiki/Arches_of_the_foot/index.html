Arches of the foot

Arches of the foot
ArchesMedial longitudinal, lateral longitudinal, transverse
BonesTarsals and metatarsals
Key ligamentPlantar calcaneonavicular (spring) ligament
FunctionWeight distribution, shock absorption, leverage

The arches of the foot, formed by the tarsal and metatarsal bones and strengthened by ligaments and tendons, allow the foot to support the weight of the body in the erect posture with the least expenditure of material. They act as springs, absorbing shock during walking and running, distributing body weight across the foot, and providing leverage when the body is propelled forward. The foot is conventionally described as having three arches: two longitudinal (medial and lateral) and one transverse.

Medial longitudinal arch

The medial longitudinal arch is the higher and more clinically important of the two longitudinal arches. It is composed of the calcaneus, talus, navicular, the three cuneiforms, and the first three metatarsals. The head of the talus is its "keystone", supported from below by the plantar calcaneonavicular (spring) ligament and the tendon of tibialis posterior. The arch is visible as the instep on the inner side of the foot.

Lateral longitudinal arch

The lateral longitudinal arch is much flatter and rests on the ground during standing. It is formed by the calcaneus, cuboid, and the fourth and fifth metatarsals, and is maintained by the long plantar ligament and the tendon of fibularis (peroneus) longus.

Transverse arch

The transverse arch runs across the foot at the level of the tarsometatarsal joints, formed by the cuneiforms, cuboid, and the bases of the metatarsals, and is held up chiefly by the tendon of fibularis longus crossing the sole.

Clinical significance

Failure of the supporting structures produces flat feet (pes planus), in which the medial arch collapses; an abnormally high arch is termed pes cavus. Both conditions alter gait and can cause pain in the foot, leg, and back, and are managed with footwear modification, orthotic insoles, physiotherapy, or in severe cases surgery.
