Riad (architecture)

A riad (Arabic: رياض, riyāḍ, "gardens") is a type of traditional Moroccan house or palace built around an interior courtyard garden. The term is applied to the townhouses of the medinas (old cities) of Morocco, particularly Marrakech and Fes. The riad descends from the Roman domus and the courtyard houses of the wider Islamic world, its inward-looking plan providing privacy and protection from the climate.

Layout

Riads are typically multi-storey, with all rooms opening onto the central courtyard rather than to the street; exterior walls are nearly blank. The courtyard is traditionally planted in four sections with citrus or other trees and a central fountain, echoing the chahar bagh garden of Islamic tradition. Decoration is rich around the courtyard: carved cedar wood, stucco plasterwork and colourful zellige tile mosaics. A rooftop terrace commonly overlooks the medina. Related but distinct, a dar has a courtyard without the planted garden.

Modern use

From the late twentieth century, many riads in Marrakech and Fes were bought and restored, often by foreign owners, and converted into boutique hotels and guesthouses. The tourist "riad hotel" has become a hallmark of Moroccan hospitality, though restorations have also raised debates about gentrification and the displacement of medina residents.
