Roger McGuinn (born James Joseph McGuinn III, July 13, 1942) is an American musician best known as the frontman and leader of the rock band the Byrds. His chiming twelve-string Rickenbacker guitar style — the "jingle-jangle" sound — defined the Byrds' records such as "Mr. Tambourine Man" and "Turn! Turn! Turn!" and shaped folk rock and later jangle pop. He is a member of the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of the Byrds.

Early life

McGuinn was born and raised in Chicago. Inspired by Elvis Presley and folk music alike, he studied at Chicago's Old Town School of Folk Music, learning banjo and guitar, and worked as a sideman and arranger for folk acts including the Limeliters and the Chad Mitchell Trio, and as a session musician and songwriter in New York's Brill Building scene, before moving to Los Angeles in 1963.

The Byrds

In 1964 McGuinn formed the Byrds with Gene Clark, David Crosby, Chris Hillman and Michael Clarke. Their 1965 debut single, a rock arrangement of Bob Dylan's "Mr. Tambourine Man", topped the charts in the US and UK and launched folk rock. The band pioneered psychedelic rock with "Eight Miles High" (1966) and country rock with Sweetheart of the Rodeo (1968). As members departed, McGuinn became the sole constant, leading the band until its dissolution in 1973.

Solo career

McGuinn released solo albums in the 1970s, toured with Dylan's Rolling Thunder Revue, and formed McGuinn, Clark & Hillman, whose 1979 debut produced the hit "Don't You Write Her Off". In 1991 he returned with the acclaimed solo album Back from Rio. Since 1995 his Folk Den project has recorded and freely distributed traditional folk songs online. He continues to tour, often performing the Sweetheart of the Rodeo repertoire.
