Domino's Pizza, Inc., commonly referred to as Domino's, is an American multinational pizza restaurant chain founded in 1960. The corporation is headquartered at the Domino's Farms Office Park in Ann Arbor, Michigan, and is one of the world's largest pizza chains, operating thousands of franchised and company-owned stores in more than 90 countries.

History

In 1960, brothers Tom and James Monaghan bought a small pizza shop called DomiNick's in Ypsilanti, Michigan. James soon traded his share for a Volkswagen Beetle; Tom renamed the business Domino's Pizza in 1965 (the logo's three dots originally represented the first three stores) and built the chain around fast delivery, famously guaranteeing delivery in 30 minutes or less — a promise later dropped after safety concerns. Monaghan sold his controlling stake to Bain Capital in 1998, and the company went public in 2004.

Products and model

Domino's built its business on delivery and carryout rather than dine-in service. Its menu centres on pizza with a range of crusts and toppings, supplemented by pasta, chicken wings, sandwiches, and desserts. The company has heavily invested in ordering technology — apps, voice ordering, and delivery tracking — positioning itself as a technology company that sells pizza.
