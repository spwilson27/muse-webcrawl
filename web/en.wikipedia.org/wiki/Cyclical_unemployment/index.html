Cyclical unemployment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cyclical unemployment
TypeUnemployment caused by deficient aggregate demand
Occurs duringRecessions and downturns in the business cycle
Also calledDemand-deficient unemployment, Keynesian unemployment

Cyclical unemployment is a factor of overall unemployment that relates to the cyclical trends in growth and production that occur during the business cycle. When business cycles are at their peak, cyclical unemployment will generally be low because total economic output is being maximized. When economic output falls, as measured by gross domestic product (GDP), the business cycle is low and cyclical unemployment will rise.

Explanation

Cyclical unemployment occurs because not enough jobs exist to employ everyone who wants to work: as consumer demand falls, firms sell less, cut production, and lay off workers, which further reduces spending. This is distinct from frictional unemployment (workers between jobs) and structural unemployment (mismatch of skills or location). When the economy operates at its natural rate of unemployment, cyclical unemployment is zero by definition.

Policy responses

Keynesian economics prescribes expansionary fiscal policy (government spending and tax cuts) and monetary policy (lower interest rates) to raise aggregate demand and reduce cyclical unemployment. Okun's law describes the empirical relationship: unemployment rises roughly half a percentage point for each percentage point of output below potential. Prolonged cyclical unemployment can become structural through skill erosion, a phenomenon called hysteresis.
