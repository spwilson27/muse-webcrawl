Luri (Persian: لری) is a Southwestern Iranian language continuum spoken by the Lur people in the Zagros Mountains of western Iran and by smaller communities in eastern Iraq. It is closely related to Persian, from which it is sometimes considered a group of dialects, though it preserves features of Middle Persian and is generally classified as a distinct language or language cluster. Estimates of native speakers range from about four to five million.

Varieties

Luri is commonly divided geographically into two or three groups: Northern Luri, spoken in Lorestan and parts of Ilam and Hamadan; Southern Luri, spoken in Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad, Mamasani in Fars, and parts of Khuzestan; and the Bakhtiari dialect cluster, spoken by the Bakhtiari people of Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari, eastern Khuzestan and western Isfahan, which some classify with Luri and others treat as transitional to Persian. A related variety, Kumzari, is spoken on the Musandam Peninsula of Oman — the only Iranian language spoken on the Arabian Peninsula proper.

History and status

Luri developed from Middle Persian dialects of the south-west and was spoken in the medieval region known historically as Luristan, whose local dynasties included the Hazaraspids (the Atabaks of Greater Luristan). The language is predominantly oral; it is written in the Perso-Arabic script when written at all, and Persian is the language of education, media and administration throughout the Luri-speaking provinces, so many speakers are bilingual. Luri has a rich tradition of folk poetry and music, including the mourning music performed with the sorna and dohol.
