Marcus Aurelius Antoninus (121–180) was Roman emperor from 161 to 180 and a Stoic philosopher, the last of the rulers traditionally grouped as the Five Good Emperors. His Meditations — private notebooks in Greek, written on campaign along the Danube — urge self-discipline, humility and acceptance of fate, and are among the most read works of Stoicism.

Reign

Adopted by Antoninus Pius as heir, Marcus ruled jointly with Lucius Verus until 169. His reign faced the Parthian and Marcomannic wars, plague (the Antonine Plague), and rising pressure on the northern frontiers. Though persecutions of Christians occurred under his rule, his personal involvement is debated. Breaking the adoptive succession, he named his son Commodus heir — a choice historians count among his few great errors. The Meditations were never intended for publication; their survival is one of literature's accidents.
