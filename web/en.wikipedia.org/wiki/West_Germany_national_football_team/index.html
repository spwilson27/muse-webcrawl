West Germany national football team

The West Germany national football team represented the Federal Republic of Germany in international men's football from 1950 to 1990. Governed by the German Football Association (DFB), it was one of the most successful national teams in history, winning the FIFA World Cup three times (1954, 1974, and 1990) and the UEFA European Championship twice (1972 and 1980).

History

After the Second World War, Germany was divided and three German teams existed: West Germany, East Germany, and the Saarland. West Germany's 1954 World Cup victory over heavily favoured Hungary — the "Miracle of Bern" — became a symbolic moment in the young Federal Republic's post-war identity. The team won the 1974 World Cup at home under captain Franz Beckenbauer, reached further finals in 1966, 1982, and 1986, and won its third World Cup in Italy in 1990, months before German reunification.

Reunification and legacy

Following reunification in October 1990, the East German team and players were absorbed into the DFB, and the united Germany team inherited West Germany's records and honours in FIFA's accounting. Notable West German players include Fritz Walter, Uwe Seeler, Gerd Müller, Franz Beckenbauer, Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, and Lothar Matthäus.
