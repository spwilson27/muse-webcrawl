Tadeusz Kościuszko

Andrzej Tadeusz Bonawentura Kościuszko (1746–1817) was a Polish military engineer, statesman, and revolutionary who fought in both the American Revolutionary War and Poland's struggle against partition by Russia and Prussia. He is a national hero in Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, and the United States.

American Revolution

Trained in Warsaw and Paris, Kościuszko sailed to America in 1776 and was commissioned a colonel of engineers in the Continental Army. He designed the fortifications that contributed to the American victory at Saratoga (1777), built the defences of West Point, and served in the southern campaign; Congress made him a brigadier general and granted him American citizenship. His will famously directed that funds from his American estate be used to free and educate enslaved people, though it was never fully carried out.

Kościuszko Uprising

Returning to Poland, he fought in the war of 1792 against Russia and in 1794 led the Kościuszko Uprising against the partitioning powers, proclaiming peasant emancipation at Połaniec and winning the Battle of Racławice with scythe-armed peasant infantry. Wounded and captured at Maciejowice, he was imprisoned in Russia, then exiled. He spent his final years in Switzerland, refusing to endorse Napoleon's Polish settlement. His remains rest in Wawel Cathedral, Kraków, and the Kościuszko Mound overlooks the city.
