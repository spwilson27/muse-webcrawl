Pedro de la Rosa


Born: 24 February 1971, Barcelona, Spain
Occupation: Racing driver
Formula One: 1999–2012; teams incl. Arrows, Jaguar, McLaren, Sauber, HRT
Best finish: 2nd, 2005 Bahrain Grand Prix

Pedro Martínez de la Rosa (born 24 February 1971) is a Spanish former racing driver who competed in Formula One between 1999 and 2012, and one of the most experienced test and development drivers of his era.

Career

De la Rosa rose through Spanish karting and single-seaters, won the 1990 British Formula Ford and 1992 British Formula Renault titles, and then raced in Japan, winning the 1995 Japanese Formula 3 championship and the 1997 Formula Nippon and All-Japan GT championships — a rare triple crown of Japanese titles.

He entered Formula One as a test driver for Jordan in 1998 and made his race debut with Arrows in 1999, scoring a point in his first race in Australia. He moved to Jaguar Racing for 2001–2002, then became McLaren's long-serving test and reserve driver in 2003. Standing in for the injured Juan Pablo Montoya, he finished second at the 2005 Bahrain Grand Prix — his best result — and raced half of the 2006 season after Montoya's departure.

De la Rosa returned to full-time racing with Sauber in 2010 and finished his career with the Spanish HRT team in 2012. He later served as a development driver for Ferrari and as chairman of the Grand Prix Drivers' Association. He subsequently became a television commentator and, in 2022, an ambassador for the Aston Martin Formula One team.
