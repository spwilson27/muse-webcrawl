The United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD), rebranded in 2024 as UN Trade and Development, is a permanent intergovernmental body of the United Nations that deals with trade, investment, and development issues. It was established in 1964 at the urging of developing countries, which sought a forum in which the development dimension of international trade could be addressed more fully than in the GATT system.

Work

UNCTAD promotes the development-friendly integration of developing countries into the world economy through research and policy analysis, consensus-building, and technical cooperation. Its flagship publications include the Trade and Development Report, the World Investment Report, the Review of Maritime Transport, and the Least Developed Countries Report. It maintains the UNCTADstat database and runs practical programmes such as ASYCUDA, a computerized customs management system used in more than 100 countries, and DMFAS for debt management.

Conferences

The organization's supreme governing body is its quadrennial conference, which sets its work programme. Conferences have been held in Geneva (1964), New Delhi (1968), Santiago (1972), Nairobi (1976 and 2016), Manila (1979), Belgrade (1983), Geneva (1987), Cartagena (1992), Midrand (1996), Bangkok (2000), São Paulo (2004), Accra (2008), Doha (2012), Bridgetown (2021, largely virtual), and Geneva (2025). In its early decades UNCTAD was the principal institutional home of the Group of 77 and of demands for a New International Economic Order, including the Generalized System of Preferences, which it helped bring about.
