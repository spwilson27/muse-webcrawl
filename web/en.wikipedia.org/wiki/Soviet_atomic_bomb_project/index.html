Soviet atomic bomb project

The Soviet atomic bomb project was the classified research and development programme authorized by Joseph Stalin in the Soviet Union to develop nuclear weapons during and after World War II.

Soviet atomic bomb project
Active1941–1949 (first test)
Scientific directorIgor Kurchatov
AdministratorLavrentiy Beria
First testRDS-1, 29 August 1949, Semipalatinsk

Origins

Soviet physicists had pursued fission research in the 1930s. After intelligence reports on the Manhattan Project and the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Stalin in August 1945 gave the programme unlimited priority under Beria, with Kurchatov's Laboratory No. 2 as its scientific centre.

Espionage and development

Intelligence from spies including Klaus Fuchs supplied the design of the American plutonium implosion bomb, which the first Soviet device, RDS-1 ('Joe-1' in the West), closely followed. Plutonium was produced at the Mayak complex in the Urals.

First test and aftermath

RDS-1 was detonated on 29 August 1949 at Semipalatinsk in Kazakhstan, ending the American atomic monopoly and accelerating the Cold War arms race. A thermonuclear device followed in 1953–1955 under Andrei Sakharov and others.
