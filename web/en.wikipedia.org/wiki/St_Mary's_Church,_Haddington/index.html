St Mary's Church, Haddington

St Mary's Parish Church is a large Gothic church in Haddington, East Lothian, Scotland. Standing beside the River Tyne, it is one of the largest parish churches in Scotland and is sometimes described as the "Lamp of Lothian" — a name originally given to an earlier Franciscan church in the town. The church is a Category A listed building and remains in use as a parish church of the Church of Scotland.

History

Building began in the late 14th century and continued into the 15th century, producing a cruciform church with a central tower. The church suffered during the wars of the 16th century: English forces occupied Haddington in 1548–49 during the Rough Wooing, and the building was damaged. After the Reformation, the choir and transepts fell into ruin while the nave continued in use for worship.

Restoration

For centuries only the nave was roofed and used. A major restoration in the 1970s rebuilt the choir, transepts and tower, returning the whole church to use; the work was completed in 1973. The restored eastern arm follows the medieval design, with modern stained glass installed in the east window. The church contains the Lauderdale Aisle, the burial place of the Maitland family, including a notable Renaissance monument to Chancellor John Maitland and his wife.

Architecture

The church is built of red sandstone in the Gothic style, with a rib-vaulted nave and a tall central tower topped by a small spire. Its scale and riverside setting make it one of the landmarks of East Lothian.
