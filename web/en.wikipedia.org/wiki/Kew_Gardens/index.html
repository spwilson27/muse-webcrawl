Kew Gardens

Kew Gardens is a botanic garden in southwest London that houses the "largest and most diverse botanical and mycological collections in the world". Founded in 1759, the site is managed by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, an internationally important botanical research and education institution. Covering 132 hectares (330 acres) at Kew, the gardens contain more than 50,000 living plants and a herbarium of over 8.5 million specimens. In 2003 the gardens were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

The gardens originated in an exotic garden created by Lord Capel John Tewkesbury and were greatly expanded by Princess Augusta, widow of Frederick, Prince of Wales, who founded a nine-acre botanic garden in 1759 with the help of Lord Bute and the architect Sir William Chambers, who built the Great Pagoda in 1762. Under Sir Joseph Banks the collections grew through global expeditions, and in 1840 the gardens were adopted as a national botanic garden. The first director, Sir William Hooker, expanded the site and commissioned the Palm House (1844–1848), designed by Decimus Burton and Richard Turner.

Features

Landmark structures include the Palm House, the Temperate House — the world's largest surviving Victorian glasshouse, reopened in 2018 after restoration — the Princess of Wales Conservatory (1987), the Alpine House, and the Great Pagoda, which was restored with replica dragons in 2018. The Xstrata Treetop Walkway, opened in 2008, carries visitors 18 metres above the arboretum. Kew also holds the Millennium Seed Bank at its Wakehurst site in Sussex, the largest wild plant seed bank in the world, and the Jodrell Laboratory and Marianne North Gallery.

Collections and research

Kew's living collections include around 14,000 trees, extensive orchid and cactus collections, and the world's largest collection of bulbs. Its herbarium and fungarium underpin taxonomic research, and Kew scientists work on plant conservation, crop relatives and climate resilience in more than 100 countries. The gardens receive around two million visitors a year.
