Kahnawake

The Kahnawake Mohawk Territory (Mohawk: Kahnawà:ke, meaning "place of the rapids") is a First Nations reserve of the Mohawks of Kahnawà:ke on the south shore of the Saint Lawrence River in Quebec, Canada, across from Montreal. It is one of several Kahnawake (Caughnawaga) communities and is a member of the Mohawk Nation Council of Chiefs.

History

Kahnawake began as a Jesuit mission village, Sault St. Louis, for Mohawk converts to Catholicism who moved north from the Mohawk Valley in the late 17th century. The community relocated several times before settling at its present site in 1716. Kahnawake men became famous as high-steel ironworkers, helping to build bridges and skyscrapers across North America, including in New York City; a community of Kahnawake families long resided in Brooklyn. In 1990 the neighbouring town of Oka's plans to expand a golf course onto disputed land led to the Oka Crisis, in which Kahnawake residents blockaded the Honoré Mercier Bridge in solidarity with the Mohawks of Kanesatake.

Government and economy

The community is governed by the elected Mohawk Council of Kahnawà:ke, headed by a Grand Chief, alongside traditional Haudenosaunee governance structures. Kahnawake is internationally known for the Kahnawake Gaming Commission, established in 1996, one of the earliest regulators of online gambling, and for Mohawk Internet Technologies, which hosts gaming operations on the territory.

Culture

The community maintains strong Mohawk language and cultural revitalization programs, including immersion schooling through the Kahnawake Survival School. Its Catholic shrine of Saint Kateri Tekakwitha, canonized in 2012, draws pilgrims; she is buried in the mission church of St. Francis Xavier.
