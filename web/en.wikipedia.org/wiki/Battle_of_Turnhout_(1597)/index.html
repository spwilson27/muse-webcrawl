Battle of Turnhout (1597)

The Battle of Turnhout was fought on 24 January 1597 near Turnhout in the Spanish Netherlands, during the Eighty Years' War. A Dutch and English force under Maurice of Nassau surprised and routed a Spanish army under the Count of Varas. It was Maurice's first notable field victory and demonstrated the effectiveness of his reformed, well-drilled army.

The battle

Maurice, learning that a Spanish force was wintering around Turnhout, marched rapidly in mid-winter and caught it as it retreated toward Herentals. The Spanish infantry stood to fight on open heathland; after a brief exchange of fire, the Dutch and English cavalry charged and shattered the Spanish horse, and the foot followed up to destroy the infantry formations. Varas was killed, and Spanish losses were very heavy relative to the Dutch.

Significance

Turnhout launched Maurice's celebrated campaigning sequence of the 1590s, in which his military reforms — smaller, drilled units, volley fire, and professional engineering — turned the Dutch States Army into one of Europe's most effective forces. The battle was widely reported in England, where English troops under Sir Francis Vere played a conspicuous part, and was even commemorated in print and song.
