Duchy of Bavaria

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bavaria as an imperial stem duchy of the Holy Roman Empire

The Duchy of Bavaria (German: Herzogtum Bayern) was a frontier region in the southeastern part of the Merovingian kingdom and, later, one of the stem duchies of the East Frankish kingdom and the Holy Roman Empire. It existed, in changing forms, from the mid-6th century until 1623, when its ruler was raised to electoral dignity, and the duchy was succeeded by the Electorate of Bavaria.

History

The Baiuvarii emerged in the former Roman province of Noricum, and from about 555 the Agilolfing dukes governed Bavaria from Regensburg under Frankish overlordship, codifying the Lex Baiuvariorum. The last Agilolfing, Tassilo III, was deposed by Charlemagne in 788 and Bavaria was incorporated directly into the Carolingian Empire.

Revived as a stem duchy under the Luitpoldings in the early 10th century, Bavaria became the largest of the German stem duchies. In 1156 Emperor Frederick Barbarossa separated Austria as a duchy of its own, and in 1180, after the fall of Henry the Lion, Bavaria was granted to Otto of Wittelsbach. The House of Wittelsbach ruled Bavaria for more than seven centuries, though the duchy was repeatedly partitioned among its branches — most durably into Upper and Lower Bavaria, and from 1506 reunified by the primogeniture decree of Albert IV.

In 1623, during the Thirty Years' War, Duke Maximilian I received the electoral title transferred from the Palatinate, and the duchy became an electorate.
