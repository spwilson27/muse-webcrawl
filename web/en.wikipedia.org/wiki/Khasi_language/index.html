Khasi language

Khasi
Native to: India (Meghalaya), Bangladesh
Speakers: about 1 million
Family: Austroasiatic — Khasic
Script: Latin; formerly Bengali-Assamese

Khasi (Ka Ktien Khasi) is an Austroasiatic language spoken primarily in the state of Meghalaya in north-eastern India, and by smaller communities in Assam and Bangladesh. It is the most widely spoken language of the Khasic branch of Austroasiatic and the principal language of the Khasi people, who are organised in a matrilineal society. Khasi is an associate official language of Meghalaya.

Classification and dialects

Khasi belongs to the Khasic group of the Austroasiatic family, whose other members include Pnar (Jaintia), War and Lyngngam. The standard language is based on the dialect of the Cherrapunji–Shillong area. Khasi is unusual among the languages of its region for its subject–verb–object word order, where neighbouring Indo-Aryan and Tibeto-Burman languages are typically subject–object–verb.

Writing system

In the early nineteenth century Khasi was written in the Bengali-Assamese script in the Sylhet region. The Latin alphabet was introduced by the Welsh missionary Thomas Jones, who arrived in Cherrapunji in 1841 and devised the orthography still in use; a rival system was promoted by other missionaries, but Jones's version prevailed. The alphabet notably includes the letter ï and digraphs such as ng, and marks words with gender markers u (masculine) and ka (feminine) prefixed to nouns.

Grammar

Khasi nouns are classified by gender, with articles agreeing accordingly, and verbs are largely uninflected, tense and aspect being conveyed by particles. The language has a rich system of pronouns and classifiers and extensive derivational morphology typical of Austroasiatic languages of the Mon–Khmer type.
