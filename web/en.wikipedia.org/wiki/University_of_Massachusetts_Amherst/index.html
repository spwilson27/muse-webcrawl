University of Massachusetts Amherst

The University of Massachusetts Amherst (UMass Amherst) is a public land-grant research university in Amherst, Massachusetts. Founded in 1863 as the Massachusetts Agricultural College under the Morrill Land-Grant Act, it is the flagship campus of the University of Massachusetts system and, with more than 30,000 students, the largest public university in New England.

History

The college opened in 1867 with a handful of students and grew through the twentieth century, becoming Massachusetts State College in 1931 and the University of Massachusetts in 1947. The Amherst campus became the system's flagship and adopted the UMass Amherst name in 2003.

Campus and academics

The 1,450-acre campus in the Pioneer Valley is dominated by the 28-storey W. E. B. Du Bois Library, among the tallest academic libraries in the world. UMass Amherst is a member of the Five Colleges consortium with Amherst, Hampshire, Mount Holyoke, and Smith colleges, whose students may cross-register for courses. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" and is known for programmes in computer science, linguistics, food science, and polymer science.
