Chicken paprikash (Hungarian: csirkepaprikás) is a popular dish of Hungarian origin and one of the most famous variants of the paprikas preparations common to Hungarian cuisine. The name is derived from the ample use of paprika, a spice commonly used in Hungarian dishes, combined with sour cream, which tempers and enriches the sauce.

Preparation

The dish is traditionally prepared by braising pieces of chicken — historically an older, tougher bird requiring long cooking — in a sauce built on a base of onions sweated in lard or butter, seasoned generously with sweet paprika (often with a smaller amount of hot paprika). Near the end of cooking, sour cream, sometimes blended with a little flour as a thickener, is stirred into the sauce. Care is taken to temper the sour cream so it does not curdle.

Chicken paprikash is classically served with Hungarian dumplings called nokedli or galuska (similar to German spätzle), though it is also eaten with egg noodles, rice, or bread. A common accompanying side is a Hungarian cucumber salad (uborkasaláta) dressed with vinegar, sugar, and paprika.

Variations

Regional and family variations add tomatoes, green peppers, or garlic. A related preparation, veal paprikash (borjúpaprikás), uses the same sauce. The dish became widely known outside Hungary through Hungarian diaspora communities and appears on menus in regions with large Hungarian-American populations, such as parts of Ohio and Pennsylvania, where it is often served over mashed potatoes or buttered noodles.

In culture

Chicken paprikash is famously the favorite dish of Count Dracula in some adaptations, and it appears in Bram Stoker's novel Dracula, where Jonathan Harker records eating "chicken done up some way with red pepper" (paprika hendl) during his journey through Hungary en route to Transylvania.
