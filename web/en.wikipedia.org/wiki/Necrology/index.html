Necrology
DefinitionA register of deaths, or an obituary notice
EtymologyGreek nekros (corpse) + -logy

A necrology is a register or list of records of the deaths of people, or a notice in a newspaper or periodical reporting a death. Historically, necrologies were registers kept by monasteries, cathedrals and colleges in which the names of the dead — monks, benefactors and members of associated communities — were recorded so that they could be commemorated in prayer on the anniversaries of their deaths.

History

Medieval necrologies, also called obituaries (from Latin obitus, death) or libri memoriales, are important historical sources: entries often note offices held, donations made, and family relationships, making them valuable for prosopography and genealogy. Famous examples include the necrologies of Saint-Germain-des-Prés and of the abbey of Fulda. Communities exchanged rolls of the dead (rotuli mortuorum) carried by messengers between monasteries.

Modern usage

In modern usage the word can mean a published list of notable deaths for a period, or an obituary article itself; academic societies and journals publish necrologies of deceased members summarizing their lives and works.
