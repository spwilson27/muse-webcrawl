Fish oil

Fish oil is oil derived from the tissues of oily fish. It contains the omega-3 fatty acids eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), precursors of eicosanoids that modulate inflammation in the body. Fish do not themselves produce significant amounts of these fatty acids; they accumulate them by eating microalgae, which originally produce EPA and DHA, or other fish that have done so. Major sources include anchovies, sardines, menhaden, herring, and salmon; the largest producer is Peru, whose anchoveta fishery supplies a large share of world output.

Uses

Fish oil is used in dietary supplements and functional foods, in aquaculture feed (the largest single use), and in the manufacture of some prescription omega-3 products. Cod liver oil, taken from the liver rather than the whole body, is additionally rich in vitamins A and D and was historically given to children to prevent rickets. Industrial uses include leather tanning, soaps, and hydrogenated oils.

Health evidence

Prescription-strength omega-3 preparations (such as icosapent ethyl and omega-3 acid ethyl esters) lower triglycerides by roughly 20–45% in people with high levels, and the 2019 REDUCE-IT trial found that high-dose icosapent ethyl reduced cardiovascular events in statin-treated patients with elevated triglycerides. For ordinary over-the-counter supplements, large trials such as VITAL and ASCEND found little or no reduction in major cardiovascular events in general populations, and evidence for benefits in arthritis, depression, and cognition is mixed and inconclusive. Reported adverse effects include fishy aftertaste, gastrointestinal upset, and, at high doses, a possible increase in bleeding tendency and atrial fibrillation risk. The American Heart Association advises that eating one to two servings of oily fish per week is associated with lower risk of coronary heart disease death.

Production and quality

Fish oil is produced by cooking, pressing, and centrifuging fish or fish-processing offcuts, then refining the crude oil to remove impurities and oxidation products. Because oils rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids oxidise readily, products are monitored for peroxide and anisidine values; concerns about heavy metals and persistent organic pollutants in fish are reduced, though not necessarily eliminated, by refining and molecular distillation. Sustainability of forage-fish fisheries is an ongoing issue for the industry.
