One-hit wonder

A one-hit wonder is any entity that achieves mainstream popularity, often for only one piece of work, and becomes known among the general public solely for that momentary success. The term is most commonly used in regard to music performers with only one hit single that overshadows their other work.

Definitions

Definitions vary by chart criteria. Music journalists typically define a one-hit wonder as an artist whose only success is a single song reaching a certain chart position — for example, the top 40 of the Billboard Hot 100 — with no subsequent charting hits. Some artists labelled one-hit wonders in one country have had sustained careers in others, and some have successful careers as songwriters, producers, or in other genres.

Examples

Frequently cited examples include Los del Río with "Macarena" (1996), Lou Bega with "Mambo No. 5" (1999), Vanilla Ice with "Ice Ice Baby" (1990), and Gotye with "Somebody That I Used to Know" (2011) in the United States market. Television channels such as VH1 have produced countdown specials celebrating the phenomenon.

Beyond music

The label has been applied more broadly to authors with a single famous book, inventors, athletes, and film directors, and in sports to teams that win a single unexpected championship.
