Macintosh startup

The Macintosh startup process describes the sequence of events when an Apple Macintosh computer is powered on, from the initial hardware self-test to the loading of macOS. It is traditionally accompanied by a startup chime and, on classic models, by the "Happy Mac" icon indicating that boot software has been found.

Sequence

On startup the firmware (historically Open Firmware, later EFI, and Boot ROM on Apple silicon Macs) performs a power-on self-test, locates a boot volume, loads the boot loader and kernel, and hands control to the operating system. If no bootable disk is found, classic Macs display a flashing question mark on a floppy disk icon.

Startup chime and icons

The startup sound, designed by Jim Reekes, has varied across models; a failed hardware test on early Macs produced the "Chimes of Death". The Happy Mac icon, designed by Susan Kare, became one of the most recognisable symbols of the original Macintosh line.

Startup modes

Key combinations held at startup invoke modes such as Safe Mode, Recovery, Target Disk Mode and verbose mode, used for troubleshooting and maintenance.
