Coptic calendar

The Coptic calendar, also called the Alexandrian calendar, is a liturgical calendar used by the Coptic Orthodox Church and also by the Coptic Catholic Church. It descends from the ancient Egyptian calendar and remains in use for the feasts and fasts of the Coptic Church and, in a modified form, by farmers in Egypt.

Structure

The calendar has thirteen months: twelve of thirty days each, followed by a short intercalary month of five days (six in a leap year). The months — Thout, Paopi, Hathor, Koiak, Tobi, Meshir, Paremhat, Parmouti, Pashons, Paoni, Epip, Mesori and the "little month" — preserve the names of the ancient Egyptian months. The Coptic New Year, Nayrouz, falls on 11 September in the Gregorian calendar (12 September in the year before a Gregorian leap year).

Era of the Martyrs

Years are counted from the Era of the Martyrs (Anno Martyrum), beginning in 284 AD, the year Diocletian became Roman emperor and launched the last and most severe persecution of Christians, which fell especially heavily on Egypt.
