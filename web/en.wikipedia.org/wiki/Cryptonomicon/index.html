Cryptonomicon is a 1999 novel by American author Neal Stephenson. It interweaves two timelines: the Second World War, following Allied codebreakers and soldiers, and the late 1990s, following their descendants as they build a data haven and digital currency in Southeast Asia. The novel is celebrated for its digressions into cryptography, computing history, and finance, and it won the Locus Award for Best Science Fiction Novel.

Plot

In the wartime strand, US Marine Bobby Shaftoe fights across the Pacific; cryptanalyst Lawrence Waterhouse works with Alan Turing and the fictional Detachment 2702, which stages elaborate deceptions so that Allied use of broken Axis codes is not detected; and Japanese engineer Goto Dengo is set to work on a vast buried treasure of looted gold in the Philippines. In the modern strand, programmer Randy Waterhouse (Lawrence's grandson) and the Epiphyte Corporation lay a submarine cable to the fictional Sultanate of Kinakuta to create a data haven and a gold-backed digital currency, while hunting for the same buried gold. The (fictional) title within the story is a classified compendium of cryptologic knowledge.

Themes and reception

The novel explores secrecy, information, and liberty, and includes a real, functional cipher — the "Solitaire" encryption algorithm, designed for the book by Bruce Schneier — in an appendix. Critics praised its ambition and technical richness, though some noted its length (over 900 pages). It is a loose predecessor to Stephenson's Baroque Cycle trilogy (2003–04), which features ancestors of the same families.
