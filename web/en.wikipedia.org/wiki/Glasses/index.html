Glasses

Glasses, also known as eyeglasses, spectacles, or (in some contexts) specs, are vision aids consisting of a pair of glass or hard plastic lenses mounted in a frame that holds them in front of a person's eyes, typically using a bridge over the nose and hinged arms which rest over the ears. Glasses are most commonly worn to correct refractive errors such as myopia (near-sightedness), hyperopia (far-sightedness), astigmatism and presbyopia, but are also used for eye protection (as safety glasses and sunglasses) and for fashion.

History

The earliest wearable spectacles were made in Italy around 1286, probably in Pisa or Florence, consisting of two convex lenses joined by a rivet and balanced on the nose. Over the following centuries designs developed through pince-nez, lorgnettes and scissors-glasses before temple arms, patented in the 18th century, made modern frame designs possible. Bifocal lenses are credited to Benjamin Franklin in the 1780s; trifocals and progressive lenses followed. In the 20th century, plastic lenses (such as CR-39) and later high-index plastics and polycarbonate largely replaced glass for cost, weight and safety reasons.

Types and function

Corrective lenses bend light before it enters the eye so that it focuses on the retina. A lens's power is measured in dioptres. Special-purpose glasses include reading glasses for presbyopia, sunglasses with tinted or polarised lenses, safety glasses and goggles for occupational protection, and 3D glasses for stereoscopic viewing. Smart glasses, such as Google Glass, incorporate head-mounted displays and cameras.

Culture

Glasses have carried varied cultural associations, from symbols of learning and intelligence to stigmatised markers of difference; in the 20th and 21st centuries they also became a major fashion accessory, with designer frames marketed by luxury brands.
