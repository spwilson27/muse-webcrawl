Orlyval is a light automated shuttle service connecting Antony station on the RER B railway line with Paris Orly Airport in France. The line, which opened on 2 October 1991, uses the VAL (Véhicule automatique léger) rubber-tyred driverless technology developed by Matra. The journey between Antony and the airport takes about eight minutes, of which the ride itself is roughly six minutes.

An Orlyval VAL 206 trainset approaching Orly Airport.

Route and stations

The 7.3 km line has three stations: Antony, Orly 1, 2, 3 (serving the combined Orly West/South terminal complex, formerly split into Orly Ouest and Orly Sud stations) and Orly 4. Trains run every few minutes at peak times, and a special Orlyval fare applies between Antony and the airport; travel between the two airport stations is free.

History

Orlyval was promoted as a way to improve surface access to Orly, whose previous rail link required a bus transfer from Pont de Rungis. The project was developed by a private consortium in which Matra was central, but ridership fell far short of forecasts, the company went into bankruptcy, and operation passed to the RATP in 1993. The line has long been criticised for its premium fare and the inconvenience of the change at Antony, and its future was reshaped by the opening of Paris Métro Line 14's extension to Orly Airport in June 2024, which provides a direct, standard-fare rail connection to central Paris.
