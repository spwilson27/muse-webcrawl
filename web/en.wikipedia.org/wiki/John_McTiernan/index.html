John Campbell McTiernan Jr. (born January 8, 1951) is an American filmmaker. He is best known for his action films, especially Predator (1987), Die Hard (1988) and The Hunt for Red October (1990), works that helped define the modern action movie.

Career

McTiernan studied at the Juilliard School and the AFI Conservatory. After the low-budget horror film Nomads (1986), he directed Arnold Schwarzenegger in Predator, then made Die Hard with Bruce Willis, a landmark of the genre. He followed with the submarine thriller The Hunt for Red October, Medicine Man, Last Action Hero (1993), Die Hard with a Vengeance (1995) and the remake of The Thomas Crown Affair (1999).

Legal troubles

In 2006 McTiernan became entangled in the Anthony Pellicano wiretapping scandal; he pleaded guilty to making false statements to the FBI and later to perjury, and served roughly a year in federal prison in 2013–2014, after which his directing career largely stalled.
