Water Margin

Water Margin (Chinese: 水滸傳; pinyin: Shuǐhǔ Zhuàn) is a famous Chinese novel traditionally attributed to Shi Nai'an. Written in vernacular Chinese, it is one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature. Set in the waning Northern Song dynasty, it tells how a group of 108 outlaws — the Stars of Destiny — gather at Liangshan Marsh to form an army, and are eventually granted amnesty and sent on campaigns against foreign invaders and rebel forces.

Story

The novel opens with Marshal Gao Qiu's rise and the release of 108 spirits imprisoned beneath a temple. Individual story cycles introduce the heroes — among them the bandit leader Chao Gai, the resourceful strategist Wu Yong, the monk Lu Zhishen, the tiger-fighting Wu Song, and Lin Chong, driven to outlawry by Gao Qiu's persecution. Under Song Jiang, the band unites at Liangshan, adopts the slogan of "enacting justice on Heaven's behalf", defeats government armies, accepts imperial amnesty, and is destroyed in costly campaigns against the Liao dynasty and the rebel Fang La.

Textual history

The story grew from historical records of Song Jiang's real band of 36 outlaws and centuries of storytelling and drama. Editions vary between 70, 100 and 120 chapters; Jin Shengtan's influential 70-chapter edition ends with the heroes' gathering and a foreboding dream. Pearl S. Buck's translation All Men Are Brothers (1933) introduced the novel widely to English readers.

Influence

Water Margin shaped Chinese ideas of righteous rebellion and brotherhood, influenced Japanese literature (the Suikoden tradition, and later video games), and has been adapted countless times for opera, film, television and comics. Mao Zedong both praised and, during the Cultural Revolution era campaigns, saw it attacked for Song Jiang's "capitulationism".
