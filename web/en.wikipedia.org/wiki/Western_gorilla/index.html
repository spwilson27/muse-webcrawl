Western gorilla

The western gorilla (Gorilla beringei is the eastern species; western: Gorilla gorilla) is a great ape found in west central Africa. It is divided into two subspecies: the western lowland gorilla (G. g. gorilla), the most numerous of all gorillas, and the Cross River gorilla (G. g. diehli), the most endangered great ape, numbering only a few hundred.

Description

Western gorillas are somewhat smaller and lighter in build than eastern gorillas, with brown-grey hair and a distinctive reddish-brown crest in adult males ("silverbacks"). They live in family groups led by a dominant male, feeding mainly on fruit, leaves, stems and occasionally insects in lowland rainforest and swamp forest.

Conservation

The species is listed as Critically Endangered. Populations have declined sharply from poaching for bushmeat, habitat loss to logging and agriculture, and Ebola virus outbreaks that killed thousands of gorillas in the early 2000s. Western lowland gorillas are the gorillas most commonly seen in zoos worldwide.
