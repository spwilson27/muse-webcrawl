Mandalay is the second-largest city in Myanmar, located on the east bank of the Irrawaddy River in the country's central dry zone. Founded in 1857 by King Mindon Min, it was the last royal capital of Burma before British annexation in 1885, and remains the country's cultural and Buddhist heartland.

History

Mindon established the new capital at the foot of Mandalay Hill in fulfilment of a Buddhist prophecy, building the walled Mandalay Palace and convening the Fifth Buddhist Council (1871), which produced the Tripitaka inscribed on 729 marble slabs at Kuthodaw Pagoda — often called "the world's largest book". King Thibaw's defeat in the Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885) ended the Konbaung dynasty; the palace was looted and later largely destroyed by fire during the Second World War fighting of 1945. It was rebuilt in the 1990s.

Geography and culture

Mandalay is a major trading centre, linked by road, rail and river to China and the rest of Myanmar, and a hub for Burmese traditional arts — gold leaf, marble carving, tapestry and puppetry. Nearby former capitals at Amarapura (with the teak U Bein Bridge), Sagaing, Inwa and Mingun form the city's historic hinterland. The city suffered severe damage in a major earthquake in March 2025.
