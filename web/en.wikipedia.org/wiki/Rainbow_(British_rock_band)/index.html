Rainbow are a British rock band formed in 1975 by guitarist Ritchie Blackmore and vocalist Ronnie James Dio. Blackmore had recently left Deep Purple and built the new project around Dio and the remaining members of the American band Elf. The group's music initially fused hard rock with neoclassical influences and fantasy-themed lyrics.

History

1975–1978: Dio era

The debut album Ritchie Blackmore's Rainbow (1975) included the live staple "Man on the Silver Mountain" and the ballad "Catch the Rainbow". The breakthrough came with Rising (1976), featuring "Stargazer" and "A Light in the Black", a landmark of heavy metal. The live album On Stage (1977) and Long Live Rock 'n' Roll (1978) followed, with songs such as "Kill the King" and the title track. Dio left in 1979 over Blackmore's desire for a more commercial, radio-friendly sound.

1979–1984: Commercial success

With vocalist Graham Bonnet, the band scored a UK hit with "Since You Been Gone" from Down to Earth (1979). Joe Lynn Turner took over vocals for Difficult to Cure (1981), Straight Between the Eyes (1982) and Bent Out of Shape (1983), achieving success in the United States. Blackmore disbanded Rainbow in 1984 to reform Deep Purple with the Mark II lineup.

Later revivals

Blackmore revived Rainbow in 1993, recording Stranger in Us All (1995) with vocalist Doogie White before returning to folk-rock with Blackmore's Night. In 2015 he assembled yet another lineup, fronted by Spanish singer Ronnie Romero, for sporadic festival performances in Europe.

Legacy

Rainbow are regarded as pioneers of neoclassical metal and an important influence on power metal. Rising is frequently ranked among the greatest heavy metal albums of all time.
