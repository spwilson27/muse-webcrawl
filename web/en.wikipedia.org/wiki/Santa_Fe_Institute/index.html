The Santa Fe Institute (SFI) is an independent, non-profit theoretical research institute located in Santa Fe, New Mexico, dedicated to the multidisciplinary study of the fundamental principles of complex adaptive systems, including physical, computational, biological and social systems.

History

The institute was founded in 1984 by a group of scientists, many associated with Los Alamos National Laboratory, including chemist George Cowan and physicists Murray Gell-Mann, Philip Anderson and David Pines. Frustrated by the disciplinary compartmentalisation of academic science, they created a centre where researchers could work across boundaries on problems such as emergence, self-organisation and adaptation.

Research

SFI is closely identified with the development of complexity science: agent-based modelling, network theory, scaling laws in biology and cities, the evolution of language, financial-market dynamics and the study of innovation. Its resident faculty is small; much work is done by external faculty and visiting researchers, and its summer schools — notably the Complex Systems Summer School — have trained generations of researchers.

Organisation

The institute is supported by foundation grants, government research funding and private donors. Its presidents have included founder George Cowan, economist W. Brian Arthur (acting), Ellen Goldberg, Robert Eisenstein, Jerry Sabloff, Geoffrey West and David Krakauer.
