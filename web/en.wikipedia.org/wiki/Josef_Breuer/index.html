Josef Breuer

Josef Breuer (January 15, 1842 – June 20, 1925) was a distinguished Austrian physician who made key discoveries in neurophysiology and, working with his patient Bertha Pappenheim — known by the case study pseudonym "Anna O." — laid the groundwork for psychoanalysis through his development of the cathartic method.

Physiology

Breuer first worked under internist Johann Oppolzer at the University of Vienna. He demonstrated the role of the vagus nerve in the reflex control of respiration, described today as the Hering–Breuer reflex, and later researched the semicircular canals' role in the sense of balance (the Mach–Breuer theory of endolymph flow).

Anna O. and the cathartic method

Between 1880 and 1882 Breuer treated Bertha Pappenheim for hysteria. He found that when she narrated the origins of her symptoms under hypnosis — an approach she called the "talking cure" and "chimney sweeping" — the symptoms were relieved. Breuer called the method catharsis.

Collaboration with Freud

Breuer was a mentor and financial supporter of the young Sigmund Freud. Together they published Studies on Hysteria (1895), which included the Anna O. case and Freud's early clinical papers. The two later diverged over the role of sexuality in neurosis, and Breuer withdrew from the psychoanalytic movement while continuing a respected medical practice in Vienna.
