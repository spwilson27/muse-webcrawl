Chiquinha Gonzaga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Francisca Edwiges Neves Gonzaga (17 October 1847 – 28 February 1935), better known as Chiquinha Gonzaga, was a Brazilian composer, pianist and conductor, widely regarded as the first woman composer of Brazil and a pioneering figure in Brazilian popular music.

Biography

Gonzaga was born in Rio de Janeiro, the daughter of a Portuguese father and a mother of African descent. She received a solid education and learned to play the piano from an early age. At sixteen she was married to a naval officer, Jacinto Ribeiro do Amaral, but the marriage was unhappy; she left her husband, a scandalous act for the time, and was repudiated by her family. She supported herself and her children by giving piano lessons and composing music.

Working with the group O Choro Carioca, she became central to the development of choro, one of the earliest forms of urban Brazilian popular music. Her first great success was the polka Atraente (1877). In 1899 she composed "Ó Abre Alas", considered the first carnival marchinha and still performed every year at Brazilian carnival.

Works and legacy

Gonzaga composed more than two thousand songs and 77 stage works, including operettas and musical revues. She was also a committed abolitionist and supporter of the republican movement, using her own money to buy the freedom of enslaved people. She campaigned for composers' copyright and was a founder of the Brazilian Society of Theatrical Authors (SBAT) in 1917. She died in Rio de Janeiro in 1935, on the eve of Carnival.
