Raseborg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Raseborg (Finnish: Raasepori) is a town and municipality in the region of Uusimaa in southern Finland, on the coast of the Gulf of Finland west of Helsinki. It was created on 1 January 2009 by the merger of the towns of Ekenäs and Karis and the municipality of Pohja. The population is around 27,000, a majority of whom are Swedish-speaking, making Raseborg one of Finland's officially bilingual municipalities with a Swedish-speaking majority.

History

The town takes its name from Raseborg Castle, a medieval fortress built in the 1370s, probably by the Swedish royal steward Bo Jonsson Grip, when the shoreline stood at the castle's walls; its ruins remain a local landmark. Ekenäs, the largest population centre, was founded by King Gustav I of Sweden in 1546 and is known for its 18th- and 19th-century wooden old town. The ironworks villages of Fiskars and Billnäs grew up in the area from the 17th century; Fiskars gave its name to the Fiskars company and is now a noted centre for artists and designers.

Geography

Raseborg includes extensive archipelago shoreline, and the Ekenäs Archipelago National Park lies partly within the municipality. The main centres are Ekenäs, Karis (a railway junction on the Helsinki–Turku line) and Pohja. The municipality covers roughly 2,200 square kilometres, much of it water.
