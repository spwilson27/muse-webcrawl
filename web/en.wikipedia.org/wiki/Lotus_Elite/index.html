Lotus Elite

The Lotus Elite name has been used by the British sports car manufacturer Lotus Cars for two distinct models. The original Elite (Type 14), produced from 1957 to 1963, was a lightweight two-seater coupé notable as the first production car with a fibreglass monocoque body. The name was revived for the Type 75/83 Elite of 1974–1982, a four-seater luxury fastback intended to move Lotus upmarket.

Type 14 (1957–1963)

Designed under Colin Chapman, the first Elite was unveiled at the 1957 London Motor Show. Its entire load-bearing structure was moulded fibreglass, giving a weight of only about 510 kg. Power came from a Coventry Climax 1.2-litre all-aluminium overhead-cam engine of about 75 hp, providing lively performance and excellent handling. Around 1,030 were built. Elites achieved class wins at the 24 Hours of Le Mans and success in club racing.

Type 75/83 (1974–1982)

The second Elite, launched in 1974, was the first Lotus with a steel backbone chassis and a glassfibre body in a shooting-brake style, seating four. It introduced the Lotus 907 2.0-litre slant-four engine. The related Eclat fastback and Esprit platform shared its components. See also Lotus Cars and Colin Chapman.
