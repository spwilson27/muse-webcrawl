Uber Technologies, Inc. is an American multinational transportation company that provides ride-hailing services, food delivery through Uber Eats, package delivery, and freight transport. Founded in 2009 by Garrett Camp and Travis Kalanick in San Francisco, Uber operates through a mobile application that matches riders with drivers who use their own vehicles, and it takes a commission on each booking.

An Uber vehicle in service. The company's app matches riders with independent drivers.

History

The company was founded in March 2009 as UberCab, after Garrett Camp conceived of an app to request premium black cars in San Francisco; the service launched in 2010. It expanded rapidly to other cities and internationally, introducing lower-cost options such as UberX in 2012, which allowed drivers to use ordinary personal cars. Uber raised enormous amounts of venture capital and reached a private valuation around $70 billion before its initial public offering in May 2019, which valued it at roughly $82 billion.

In 2017, following a series of controversies over workplace culture and business practices, co-founder Travis Kalanick resigned as CEO and was succeeded by Dara Khosrowshahi. Uber sold or merged several international operations, including in China (to Didi), Southeast Asia (to Grab), and Russia (to Yandex), and acquired food-delivery competitor Postmates in 2020. It achieved its first full year of operating profitability in 2023.

Services and business model

Uber's core mobility business matches passengers with drivers, setting prices algorithmically with reference to time, distance, and demand ("dynamic" or "surge" pricing). Delivery, through Uber Eats, connects customers with restaurants and couriers. Uber Freight matches shippers with carriers. Drivers are generally classified as independent contractors, a classification that has been contested in courts and legislatures in many jurisdictions.

Criticism and legal issues

Uber has faced disputes over driver classification, passenger and driver safety, licensing, and its effects on taxi industries and urban congestion. Regulatory responses have ranged from legalization frameworks to outright bans or suspensions in some cities and countries.
