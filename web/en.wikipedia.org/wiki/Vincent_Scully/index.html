Vincent Joseph Scully Jr. (August 21, 1920 – October 30, 2017) was an American art historian who was a Sterling Professor of the History of Art in Architecture at Yale University, and the author of several books on the subject. Architect Philip Johnson once described Scully as "the most influential architectural teacher ever." His lectures at Yale were known to attract standing-room-only crowds, and filled one of the largest lecture halls at the university.

Scully's early advocacy was critical to the emergence of both Louis Kahn and Robert Venturi as important 20th-century architects. Scully also wrote widely about urban planning, the Shingle Style, and Greek architecture, and was a prominent critic of postwar American urban renewal and suburban development.

Early life and education

Scully was born in New Haven, Connecticut, and grew up in a house on a hill in the Westville section of the city. At the age of 13, he decided he wanted to attend Yale University, where he later enrolled on a scholarship, receiving his bachelor's degree in 1940. During the Second World War he served as an officer in the United States Marine Corps. After the war, he returned to Yale, earning his master's degree in 1947 and his doctorate in 1949. He began teaching at Yale that same year and remained on the faculty, with some interruptions, for more than six decades.

Teaching career

Scully's undergraduate survey course in the history of architecture became legendary at Yale. With dramatic delivery and rapid-fire projection of slides in a darkened hall, he traced the development of architecture from prehistoric times to the present, famously beginning with the Paleolithic cave at Lascaux. His lectures drew auditors from across the university, and a final lecture in 1991 attracted an overflow crowd; he came out of retirement several times to teach again.

Among his students and protégés were the architects Robert Venturi, Charles Moore, and Allan Greenberg. His 1960 monograph on Frank Lloyd Wright and his later books helped shape a generation's understanding of American architecture. Scully championed preservation and humane urbanism, campaigning against the demolition of historic neighborhoods and against modernist redevelopment schemes in New Haven.

Works

Scully's books include The Shingle Style (1955), Frank Lloyd Wright (1960), Modern Architecture: The Architecture of Democracy (1961), The Earth, the Temple, and the Gods: Greek Sacred Architecture (1962), American Architecture and Urbanism (1969), The Shingle Style Today (1974), Pueblo: Mountain, Village, Dance (1975), and Architecture: The Natural and the Manmade (1991).

Death and legacy

Scully died on October 30, 2017, at his home in Lynchburg, Virginia, of complications of Parkinson's disease, aged 97. The Vincent Scully Prize, awarded by the National Building Museum, was established in 1999 to honor his legacy in architectural education and public discourse. In 2004, he received the National Medal of Arts.
