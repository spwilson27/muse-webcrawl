Kondraty Ryleyev

BornSeptember 29, 1795, Batovo, Russian Empire
DiedJuly 25, 1826 (aged 30), St. Petersburg, Russian Empire
OccupationPoet, Decembrist
Notable worksDumy; Voinarovsky; Nalivaiko

Kondraty Fyodorovich Ryleyev (Russian: Кондра́тий Фёдорович Рыле́ев, September 29, 1795 – July 25, 1826) was a Russian poet, publisher, and revolutionary leader. He was one of the five leaders of the Decembrist revolt of December 1825 who were executed by the imperial government.

Life

Ryleyev was born into a family of impoverished gentry and served as an artillery officer in the campaigns against Napoleon, including the advance into France in 1814–1815. After retiring from the army in 1818, he settled in St. Petersburg, married, worked in judicial service and later for the Russian-American Company, and gained fame as a poet. His cycle of historical ballads, the Dumy ("Meditations"), and his narrative poems Voinarovsky and Nalivaiko celebrated Ukrainian and Russian heroes who resisted tyranny. With Alexander Bestuzhev he edited the influential literary almanac Polyarnaya Zvezda ("The Polar Star," 1823–1825).

Decembrist revolt and death

Ryleyev became a leading figure of the Northern Society of Decembrists, advocating constitutional government and the abolition of serfdom, and he helped plan the uprising of December 14 (26), 1825, in Senate Square, St. Petersburg. The revolt failed, and Ryleyev, though ill, accepted responsibility for the plan. Together with Pavel Pestel, Sergey Muravyov-Apostol, Mikhail Bestuzhev-Ryumin, and Pyotr Kakhovsky, he was hanged on July 13 (25), 1826, at the Peter and Paul Fortress. According to legend, when the rope broke during the first attempt, Ryleyev remarked that Russia was a land where they did not even know how to hang a man properly. His works were banned under Nicholas I, but he was later celebrated as a martyr-poet of Russian freedom.
