Cephalic disorder

[Image: MRI scan of an infant brain showing a congenital malformation]

Cephalic disorders are congenital conditions that stem from damage to, or abnormal development of, the developing nervous system — specifically the head and brain. "Cephalic" means "head" or "head end of the body". These disorders are not necessarily caused by a single factor, but may be influenced by hereditary or genetic conditions, nutritional deficiencies, or environmental exposures during pregnancy, such as medication taken by the mother, maternal infection, or exposure to radiation. Many cephalic disorders are caused by a disturbance in the embryonic development of the neural tube.

Types

Among the best known cephalic disorders are:

  • Anencephaly — underdevelopment of the brain and skull following failure of the neural tube to close.
  • Encephalocele — protrusion of brain tissue through an opening in the skull.
  • Microcephaly — abnormally small head circumference due to impaired brain growth.
  • Macrocephaly — abnormally large head circumference.
  • Holoprosencephaly — failure of the forebrain to divide into two hemispheres.
  • Hydrocephalus — build-up of cerebrospinal fluid within the brain (which has many causes, congenital and acquired).
  • Lissencephaly — a "smooth brain" lacking normal folds, caused by defective neuronal migration.

Prognosis and treatment

Prognosis varies widely with the type and severity of the disorder. Some conditions, such as anencephaly, are incompatible with life; others cause intellectual disability, motor problems, seizures or growth disturbances of varying degree. Treatment is generally symptomatic and supportive, though some conditions — notably hydrocephalus — can be treated surgically, for example with a shunt. Folic acid supplementation before and during early pregnancy substantially reduces the risk of neural tube defects.
