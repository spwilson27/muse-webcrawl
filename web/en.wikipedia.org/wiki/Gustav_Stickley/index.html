Gustav Stickley

Gustav Stickley
Born: 9 March 1858, Osceola, Wisconsin
Died: 15 April 1942, Syracuse, New York
Occupation: Furniture designer, architect
Movement: Arts and Crafts / Mission style

Gustav Stickley (March 9, 1858 – April 15, 1942) was an American furniture manufacturer, design leader, publisher, and the chief promoter of the American Arts and Crafts movement. His Craftsman furniture and the magazine The Craftsman popularised the Mission style in the United States.

Career

Stickley began working in a chair factory as a youth and later founded his own furniture company in Syracuse, New York. Influenced by British Arts and Crafts ideals, he introduced simple, sturdy oak furniture emphasising honest construction and exposed joinery.

The Craftsman

From 1901 to 1916 he published The Craftsman magazine, promoting Arts and Crafts philosophy, architecture, and house plans that shaped the American Craftsman bungalow style. His company later went bankrupt in 1915 as tastes changed.

Legacy

Stickley's designs remain icons of American decorative arts, and his home in Syracuse is a National Historic Landmark.
