Rainbow Serpent

The Rainbow Serpent or Rainbow Snake is a major ancestral and creator being in the Dreaming (Tjukurpa) traditions of Aboriginal Australians. Known by many names in different languages — including Witj, Ngarrny, and Almudj — the serpent is associated with water, fertility, rain, and the shaping of the landscape.

Mythology

In Dreaming stories, the Rainbow Serpent travels across the land during the creation period, carving out rivers, gorges, and waterholes, and establishing laws and ceremonies. It is often depicted in rock art in Arnhem Land and elsewhere as a great snake, sometimes with water plants, eggs, or other beings. The serpent can be benevolent or dangerous: it rewards those who respect the law and punishes transgressors, for example by causing floods.

Significance

The Rainbow Serpent is one of the oldest continuing religious beliefs in the world, with rock-art depictions dating back more than 6,000 years. It remains a powerful symbol of Aboriginal identity and of the spiritual connection between people, water, and country, and appears widely in contemporary Aboriginal art.
