Font-de-Gaume

Font-de-Gaume is a cave near Les Eyzies-de-Tayac-Sireuil in the Dordogne department of south-west France. The cave contains prehistoric polychrome cave paintings and engravings, and is one of the few major painted caves in France that remains open to the public. It forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Prehistoric Sites and Decorated Caves of the Vézère Valley", inscribed in 1979.

The cave

The cave is over 120 metres long, with a narrow entrance passage opening into a main gallery. The walls bear more than 200 engravings and paintings of animals, including bison, horses, mammoths, reindeer, and a wolf, as well as abstract signs. Most of the artwork dates to the Magdalenian period, roughly 17,000 years ago, making it broadly contemporary with the famous paintings of Lascaux and Altamira.

Discovery

The paintings were discovered on 12 September 1901 by Denis Peyrony, a local schoolmaster, only days after the nearby Les Combarelles cave came to light. The discovery came at a time when the authenticity of Upper Palaeolithic cave art, long contested, was gaining acceptance among prehistorians.

Conservation

To protect the fragile pigments, visitor numbers are strictly limited and photography inside the cave is prohibited. Font-de-Gaume is managed by the French Centre des monuments nationaux.
