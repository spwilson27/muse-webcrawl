Anne Waldman (born April 2, 1945) is an American poet. Since the 1960s, Waldman has been an active member of the "Outrider" experimental poetry community as a writer, performer, collaborator, professor, editor, scholar, and cultural/political activist. She co-founded the Jack Kerouac School of Disembodied Poetics at Naropa University with Allen Ginsberg in 1974.

Life and career

Waldman was born in Millville, New Jersey, and grew up on MacDougal Street in Greenwich Village, New York City. She graduated from Bennington College in 1966. In the late 1960s she directed the Poetry Project at St. Mark's Church in the Bowery, a central institution of the New York poetry world, and edited the journal The World. She appears in Bob Dylan's film Renaldo and Clara and travelled widely as a performer of her incantatory, chant-based work.

Work

Waldman has published more than forty books of poetry, including Fast Speaking Woman (1975), inspired by Mazatec shaman María Sabina, and her epic The Iovis Trilogy: Colors in the Mechanism of Concealment (2011), which won the PEN Center USA Award for Poetry. A practitioner of Tibetan Buddhism, she has described the poem as a "spiritual technology" and her performance style blends chant, jazz improvisation and feminist ritual.

Recognition

She received a Guggenheim Fellowship in 2013 and was elected a Chancellor of the Academy of American Poets in 2017. A collection of her papers is held at the University of Michigan.
