Imagawayaki

Imagawayaki (今川焼き) is a Japanese dessert made of batter in a special cast-iron pan, filled with sweet azuki bean paste. It is similar to dorayaki but cooked as thick round cakes, and is known by many regional names, including ōban-yaki and taiyaki-like variants in other countries.

History

The sweet is said to have originated in the Kanda area of Tokyo during the Meiji era, taking its name from the nearby Imagawabashi bridge. It became a popular festival and street food throughout Japan.

Variations

Fillings now include custard, chocolate, cheese and sweet potato. Similar snacks include Korea's bungeoppang and Taiwan's wheel cakes (che lun bing), which spread from Japanese influence.
