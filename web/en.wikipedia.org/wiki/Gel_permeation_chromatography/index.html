Gel permeation chromatography

Separation by molecular size in a porous gel column
TypeSize-exclusion chromatography
AnalytesPolymers and macromolecules in organic solvents

Gel permeation chromatography (GPC) is a type of size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) that separates analytes on the basis of their size, typically in organic solvents. The technique is mainly used for the analysis of polymers and their molecular mass distribution.

Principle

The sample is passed through a column packed with a porous gel (commonly cross-linked polystyrene-divinylbenzene beads). Larger molecules cannot enter the pores and so travel quickly through the column and elute first, whereas smaller molecules penetrate the pores, take a longer path, and elute later. Elution volume is therefore correlated with hydrodynamic volume and, by calibration with standards of known molecular weight, with molecular mass.

Instrumentation and analysis

A GPC system consists of a pump delivering a constant flow of solvent (commonly tetrahydrofuran), an injector, one or more columns, and detectors, typically a refractive index detector, often combined with UV, viscometry, or multi-angle light scattering detectors for absolute molecular weight determination. Results are expressed as number-average and weight-average molecular weights (Mn and Mw) and the polydispersity index (Mw/Mn).

Terminology and applications

Historically, "gel permeation chromatography" refers to separations of synthetic polymers in organic solvents, while "gel filtration chromatography" refers to aqueous separations of biological molecules. GPC is a routine quality-control and research tool in the polymer industry, used to monitor polymerizations, degradation, and blending.
