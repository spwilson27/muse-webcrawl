Pachelbel's Canon

ComposerJohann Pachelbel
Full titleCanon and Gigue in D major
Composedc. 1694–1706
ScoringThree violins and basso continuo

Pachelbel's Canon is the common name for the Canon and Gigue in D major by the German Baroque composer Johann Pachelbel. Originally scored for three violins and basso continuo, it is by far the composer's best-known work and one of the most famous pieces of classical music in the world.

Structure

The canon is built on a two-bar ground bass of eight chords — D, A, B, F♯, G, D, G, A — repeated throughout, over which the three violins play a strict canon at the unison, entering two bars apart with increasingly elaborate variations. The famous chord progression has been widely imitated in popular music.

History and popularity

Composed around the turn of the 18th century, the piece was largely forgotten until the 20th century. A 1968 recording by the Jean-François Paillard Chamber Orchestra sparked its modern revival, and it has since become a staple of weddings and ceremonies. Its chord sequence underlies numerous pop songs, from "Basket Case" by Green Day to "Memories" by Maroon 5.
