Pierre Henri Marie Schaeffer (14 August 1910 – 19 August 1995) was a French composer, writer, broadcaster, engineer, musicologist, acoustician and cultural manager. Trained as an electrical engineer, he is best known as the founder of musique concrète — music composed from recorded sounds — and for his theory of the "sound object" (objet sonore) and "reduced listening".

Early life

Schaeffer was born in Nancy, the son of two musicians, and studied at the École Polytechnique, graduating in electrical engineering. He joined the French broadcasting service (later RTF) in 1934, working in Paris on radio drama and sound effects, which turned his attention to the musical potential of recorded noise.

Musique concrète

On 5 October 1948 French radio broadcast Schaeffer's Étude aux chemins de fer ("Study in Trains"), made by editing and looping recordings of locomotives on disc; with four further études it formed the first Cinq études de bruits. In 1950, with the young composer Pierre Henry, he made Symphonie pour un homme seul. Schaeffer founded the Groupe de recherche de musique concrète (1951), which became the Groupe de recherches musicales (GRM), training a generation of electroacoustic composers and developing techniques — tape loops, speed transposition, filtering, the "closed groove" — that anticipate modern sampling.

Theory and later work

His Traité des objets musicaux (1966) set out a systematic theory of listening and sound classification, centred on the sound object and on écoute réduite (reduced listening): attending to a sound's intrinsic qualities apart from its source or meaning. Schaeffer later became sceptical of the musical results of his own revolution, and in his later career directed research at French television and wrote on media and communication.
