East Azerbaijan Province

East Azerbaijan Province (Persian: استان آذربایجان شرقی) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran. It is in the northwest of the country, bordering Armenia and the Republic of Azerbaijan. The capital of the province is Tabriz, historically one of Iran's most important cities and a major centre on the Silk Road. The province covers an area of roughly 45,650 km² and had a population of about 3.9 million at the 2016 census. The majority of the inhabitants are ethnic Azerbaijanis who speak Azerbaijani (Azeri Turkish), with Persian as the language of government and education.

Geography

The province is generally mountainous, with the volcanic cone of Sahand (3,707 m) south of Tabriz and the Sabalan massif shared with neighbouring Ardabil province. The Aras River forms the northern boundary. The climate is continental and Mediterranean-influenced, with cold winters and mild summers; Tabriz is known for its cool summer nights. East Azerbaijan is seismically active, and Tabriz has been destroyed by earthquakes several times in its history.

Economy and culture

Tabriz has long been a commercial and industrial hub, noted for carpets, leather goods, machinery, food processing, and petrochemicals. The Grand Bazaar of Tabriz, one of the largest covered bazaars in the world, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Attractions elsewhere in the province include the rocky village of Kandovan, the Babak Castle, and Saint Stepanos Monastery in the Aras valley, part of the UNESCO-listed Armenian Monastic Ensembles of Iran.
