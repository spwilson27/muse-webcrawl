California Republic

The California Republic (Spanish: La República de California), or the Bear Flag Republic, was an unrecognized breakaway state from Mexico that existed from 14 June 1846 to 9 July 1846, in what is now parts of Northern California. It was proclaimed by American settlers in the Bear Flag Revolt during the Mexican–American War.

Bear Flag Revolt

On 14 June 1846, a party of roughly thirty American settlers seized the town of Sonoma and captured the Mexican commandant of Northern California, General Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo, who was in fact sympathetic to American annexation. The rebels raised a homemade flag bearing a grizzly bear, a star, and the words "California Republic", and proclaimed independence from Mexico. William B. Ide became the republic's only president.

End of the republic

The short-lived republic never exercised control beyond the Sonoma area. On 7 July 1846, American naval forces under Commodore John D. Sloat raised the United States flag at Monterey, and two days later American forces at Sonoma replaced the Bear Flag with the American flag, ending the republic. Its volunteers were absorbed into the California Battalion under John C. Frémont. The Bear Flag later became the basis for the state flag of California, adopted in 1911.
