Clotted cream
RegionDevon and Cornwall, England
Main ingredientUnpasteurised or whole cow's milk
Fat contentminimum 55% (Cornish PDO)
Served withScones, jam — cream tea

Clotted cream (also called scalded, clouted or Devonshire cream) is a thick cream made by indirectly heating full-cream cow's milk using steam or a water bath, then leaving it in shallow pans to cool slowly. During cooling the cream rises to the surface and forms clots or clouts, developing a golden crust. It has a minimum fat content of 55%.

Origins and protection

Clotted cream is strongly associated with south-west England, especially Devon and Cornwall, where a long-running rivalry concerns the proper cream tea: in Devon the cream is spread first, then jam; in Cornwall jam first, then cream on top. "Cornish clotted cream" has held Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) status since 1998, requiring milk produced in Cornwall and a minimum 55% butterfat. Similar creams include kaymak in Turkey and the Balkans and malai in India.

Uses

Beyond the cream tea — served with scones and often alongside crumpets at teatime — clotted cream accompanies desserts such as berries, pies and fudge made with it in Cornwall, and is a traditional partner to strawberries at summer fairs.
