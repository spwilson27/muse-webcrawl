Muriwai

Muriwai, also called Muriwai Beach, is a coastal community on the west coast of the Auckland Region of New Zealand's North Island, about 40 kilometres northwest of Auckland city centre. It lies at the southern end of an unbroken 50-kilometre stretch of black iron-sand beach running up the Tasman Sea coast.

Features

Muriwai is known for its powerful surf, and the beach is patrolled by the Muriwai Volunteer Lifeguard Service during summer. At Ōtakamiro Point, at the southern end of the settlement, a colony of Australasian gannets (tākapu) nests on the headland and offshore stacks from August to March, attracting thousands of visitors to viewing platforms. Much of the surrounding land forms Muriwai Regional Park, managed by Auckland Council, and the area includes the Muriwai Golf Club links course.

History and damage

The area has longstanding significance for Māori of Te Kawerau ā Maki and Ngāti Whātua. In February 2023, Muriwai was severely affected by Cyclone Gabrielle: landslides destroyed homes, and two volunteer firefighters were killed when a house collapsed on Motutara Road.
