The San Francisco War Memorial and Performing Arts Center (SFWMPAC) is a group of performance venues in the Civic Center district of San Francisco, California, across Van Ness Avenue from City Hall. Built as a memorial to the city's veterans of the First World War, it is one of the largest performing arts complexes in the United States.

Venues

The complex's twin Beaux-Arts buildings, both completed in 1932, face each other across a memorial court. The War Memorial Opera House (about 3,146 seats) is home to the San Francisco Opera and the San Francisco Ballet and was the first municipally owned opera house in the United States. Its mirror-image twin, the Veterans Building, contains the Herbst Theatre (about 928 seats), a chamber performance hall, together with offices for veterans' organisations and formerly the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art. The Louise M. Davies Symphony Hall, home of the San Francisco Symphony, opened in 1980 on an adjacent lot. The Zellerbach Rehearsal Hall (2013) provides rehearsal space for the resident companies.

History

The War Memorial buildings were designed in the Beaux-Arts style by architects including Arthur Brown Jr., who also designed City Hall. In April–June 1945 the Opera House and Veterans Building hosted sessions of the United Nations Conference on International Organization, at which the UN Charter was drafted and signed in the Herbst Theatre. The complex is a department of the City and County of San Francisco, managed by a board of trustees. The Opera House underwent a major seismic retrofit in the 1990s, and the Veterans Building was seismically upgraded and renovated, reopening in 2015 with a new performance space, the Taube Atrium Theater.
