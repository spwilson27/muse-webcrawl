Jair Messias Bolsonaro (born 21 March 1955) is a Brazilian politician and former military officer who served as the 38th president of Brazil from 2019 to 2023. A former army captain, he represented Rio de Janeiro in the Chamber of Deputies from 1991 to 2018, sitting with a succession of small parties, before winning the presidency as the candidate of the Social Liberal Party (PSL).

Early life and military career

Bolsonaro was born in Glicério, in the state of São Paulo, into a family of Italian and German descent. He attended the Preparatory School of the Brazilian Army and graduated from the Agulhas Negras Military Academy in 1977, serving as an artillery officer and paratrooper. He rose to public attention in the 1980s after criticising military pay, and was involved in a controversial alleged plot reported by the magazine Veja; he was acquitted by the Superior Military Court but left the army in 1988 with the rank of captain and entered politics, winning a seat on Rio de Janeiro's city council.

Congressional career

Elected to the federal Chamber of Deputies in 1990, Bolsonaro served seven consecutive terms. He became known for defending the legacy of Brazil's 1964–1985 military dictatorship, for socially conservative positions, for opposition to gun control, and for polemical statements about women, Afro-Brazilians, indigenous peoples and LGBT people, which drew repeated criticism and legal complaints.

Presidency (2019–2023)

Bolsonaro ran for president in 2018 on an anti-corruption, anti-crime and socially conservative platform, building a large following on social media. He survived a stabbing attack during a campaign rally in September 2018 and defeated Fernando Haddad of the Workers' Party in the second round with about 55% of the vote.

His government, with Paulo Guedes as economy minister, pursued pension reform, privatisation and deregulation, and passed an economic freedom act. Environmental policy was marked by a sharp rise in Amazon deforestation and conflict with European governments and environmental organisations. His handling of the COVID-19 pandemic — downplaying the virus, opposing lockdowns and promoting unproven treatments — was widely criticised as Brazil suffered one of the world's highest death tolls, and was investigated by a Senate inquiry.

After the presidency

Bolsonaro narrowly lost the 2022 election to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. He did not explicitly concede, and on 8 January 2023 thousands of his supporters stormed the seats of the three branches of government in Brasília. In June 2023 the Superior Electoral Court barred him from holding public office until 2030 for abuse of power and misuse of official media in attacks on the electoral system. He has since faced further criminal proceedings, including charges relating to an alleged coup plot, which he denies.
