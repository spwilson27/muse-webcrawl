Hanke–Henry Permanent Calendar

The Hanke–Henry Permanent Calendar (HHPC) is a calendar reform proposal by economist Steve H. Hanke and physicist Richard Conn Henry of Johns Hopkins University. It is a perennial calendar, meaning the same calendar layout repeats every year, so that each date always falls on the same day of the week.

Design

The calendar has quarters of 30, 30, and 31 days (two months of 30 days followed by one of 31), giving a 91-day quarter and a 364-day year — exactly 52 weeks. To keep the calendar in step with the solar year, an extra "leap week", sometimes called Xtra, is inserted at the end of December every five or six years (in years divisible by 5 having the appropriate remainder). Birthdays, holidays, and anniversaries would fall on the same weekday every year.

Advantages and criticisms

Proponents cite simplified scheduling and financial calculations. Critics object to the disruption of the continuous seven-day week cycle (the leap week contains an extra week rather than a stray day, so the Sabbath cycle is in fact preserved), the reassignment of birthdays for people born in the leap week, and the enormous cost of transition. Hanke and Henry initially proposed adoption on 1 January 2017, a Monday-aligned start date, but this did not occur.
