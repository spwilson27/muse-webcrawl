Extra time

Extra time extends a drawn knockout match in association football

Extra time is an additional period of play used in many sports to break a tie when a winner must be decided and the score is level at the end of regulation time. The term is used differently across sports: in association football it denotes a set extra period played in full, while in North American usage the equivalent period is usually called overtime.

Association football

In knockout association football, if the scores are level after 90 minutes and the competition rules require a result on the day, extra time of 30 minutes is played, in two halves of 15 minutes, with teams changing ends and little or no interval. If the scores remain level, the match is decided by a penalty shoot-out. Variations used historically include the golden goal, under which the first goal in extra time ended the match, and the silver goal, under which a team leading at half-time of extra time won; both were abolished by the IFAB in 2004. Some competitions dispense with extra time entirely and proceed directly to penalties, and two-legged ties may use the away goals rule or its successors before extra time is reached.

Other sports

Rugby league and rugby union employ extra-time periods in knockout matches, field hockey historically used extra time followed by shoot-outs, and ice hockey uses a sudden-death overtime period followed by a shootout in league play, or continuous overtime in playoffs. Gaelic games use two ten-minute halves of extra time. In all cases, extra time is distinct from stoppage (injury) time added at the referee's discretion within regulation play.
