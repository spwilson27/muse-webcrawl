Jacobite rising of 1715

The Jacobite rising of 1715 (Scottish Gaelic: Bliadhna Sheumais, "James's year"), often called "the Fifteen", was an attempt by James Edward Stuart — the "Old Pretender" — to regain the thrones of Great Britain and Ireland for the exiled House of Stuart, after the accession of the Hanoverian George I in 1714.

Background

The death of Queen Anne and the succession of the Elector of Hanover disappointed many Tories and Scottish opponents of the 1707 Union. The Earl of Mar, a former government minister snubbed by George I, returned to Scotland and raised the Jacobite standard at Braemar on 6 September 1715, attracting a substantial Highland army. A simultaneous rising was planned in England, but government arrests largely forestalled it; a smaller English Jacobite force under Thomas Forster joined Scots under William Mackintosh of Borlum who had crossed into England.

Course of the rising

The main Jacobite army fought the government forces of the Duke of Argyll at the inconclusive Battle of Sheriffmuir on 13 November 1715, while the Anglo-Scottish force was defeated at the Battle of Preston two days earlier. Mar failed to exploit his numerical advantage, morale collapsed, and James himself landed at Peterhead in December to find the cause already lost. He and Mar fled back to France in February 1716.

Aftermath

Leading rebels were executed or exiled, estates were forfeited, and the Disarming Act was imposed on the Highlands. The failure of the Fifteen did not end Jacobitism, which rose again in 1745, but it ended serious hopes of French support for a time.
