Neutral mask

A neutral mask is a plain, expressionless mask used in actor training and physical theatre. Free of any fixed expression, character or emotion, it covers the performer's face so that attention shifts to the body: posture, gesture, balance and movement through space.

Origins

The modern neutral mask is closely associated with the French actor and teacher Jacques Copeau, who used a simple cloth mask in training at the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier in the early 20th century, and was developed further by Jacques Lecoq at his school in Paris. Lecoq's mask, sculpted with the mask-maker Amleto Sartori, became a cornerstone of his pedagogy of movement.

Use in training

Wearing the mask, students explore a state of calm availability — a body without tension or personal mannerism, ready to respond to the space and to imagined environments such as landscapes, elements and materials. Exercises in the neutral mask are meant to strip away habitual gesture, heighten physical awareness and prepare the performer for character masks, half-masks and clown work.
