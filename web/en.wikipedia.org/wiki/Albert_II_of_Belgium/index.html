Albert II of Belgium

King Albert II during his reign

Albert II (born 6 June 1934) is a member of the Belgian royal family who reigned as King of the Belgians from 9 August 1993 until his abdication on 21 July 2013. The younger son of King Leopold III and brother of King Baudouin, he succeeded on Baudouin's death and was succeeded by his own son, Philippe. Before his accession he was known as the Prince of Liège and led Belgian trade missions abroad for more than three decades.

Reign

Albert's reign coincided with Belgium's transformation into a fully federal state and with grave national crises, including the Dutroux affair of 1996, in which he met the victims' families and pressed for reform of the police and judiciary. He played a visible mediating role during Belgium's repeated government-formation crises, notably the record 541-day deadlock of 2010–11. On 3 July 2013 he announced his abdication for health reasons, citing his age of 79; he signed the act of abdication on Belgium's National Day, 21 July 2013.

Later life

After his abdication, Albert retained the title of King Albert II. Following a long legal battle, he acknowledged in 2020, after a court-ordered DNA test, that the artist Delphine Boël (now Princess Delphine of Belgium) is his daughter from an extramarital relationship in the 1960s.
