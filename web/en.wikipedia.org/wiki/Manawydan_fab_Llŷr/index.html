Manawydan fab Llŷr

Manawydan fab Llŷr ("Manawydan, son of Llŷr") is the third of the Four Branches of the Mabinogi, a collection of medieval Welsh prose tales. It follows Manawydan, brother of Brân the Blessed, after the disastrous war in Ireland of the second branch, and continues the story of Pryderi of Dyfed and his mother Rhiannon.


Manawydan and Pryderi in Dyfed.

Synopsis

Returning home, Pryderi arranges for Manawydan to marry his widowed mother Rhiannon. During a feast at Arberth, a mist descends after the party sits upon the Gorsedd Arberth mound, and all Dyfed is emptied of people and animals except the four companions — Manawydan, Rhiannon, Pryderi and Pryderi's wife Cigfa. After years living by hunting and craft trades in England, from which jealous guilds repeatedly drive them, the group returns to Dyfed. Pryderi and Rhiannon are trapped by a magical golden bowl in an enchanted fortress and vanish. Manawydan, farming with Cigfa, finally captures a mouse stealing his wheat — in reality the transformed wife of the enchanter Llwyd ap Cil Coed — and, threatening to hang it, negotiates the lifting of the enchantment: Dyfed is restored, and Llwyd confesses the spell was revenge for his friend Gwawl, humiliated by Pwyll in the first branch.

Background

Manawydan is generally equated with the Irish sea god Manannán mac Lir. The tale survives principally in the White Book of Rhydderch and the Red Book of Hergest, and is noted for its measured, humane protagonist — a hero of patience and craft rather than warfare.
