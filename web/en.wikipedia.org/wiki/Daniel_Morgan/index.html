Daniel Morgan (1735/1736 – 6 July 1802) was an American pioneer, soldier and politician from Virginia. One of the most gifted battlefield tacticians of the American Revolutionary War, he commanded riflemen whose victory at the Battle of Cowpens is considered a turning point in the reconquest of the South.

Early life

Of Welsh descent, Morgan was born in New Jersey and settled on the Virginia frontier. He served as a teamster in the French and Indian War — famously receiving 499 lashes after striking a British officer — and as a provincial militiaman.

Revolutionary War

In 1775 Morgan led a company of Virginia riflemen on the gruelling march to Quebec, where he was captured in the failed assault on the city. Exchanged, he commanded the Provisional Rifle Corps ("Morgan's Riflemen"), distinguished at Saratoga in 1777. After a period of retirement due to illness, he returned and on 17 January 1781 destroyed Banastre Tarleton's command at Cowpens with a celebrated double envelopment.

Later life

Morgan helped suppress the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 and served one term in the US House of Representatives (1797–1799) as a Federalist. He died in Winchester, Virginia, in 1802.
