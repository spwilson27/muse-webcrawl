Perfboard

Perfboard is a material for prototyping electronic circuits (also called perforated board, prototyping board, or DOT PCB). It is a thin, rigid sheet with holes pre-drilled at standard intervals across a grid, usually a square grid of 0.1 inches (2.54 mm) spacing. Square copper pads or strips surround each hole to form the electrical connections, which are made by soldering wires and component leads to the pads.

Description

Perfboard is available in several forms. The simplest and most common type has individual copper pads on one side of the board, each pad surrounding a single hole. Wiring is done point-to-point on the underside, typically with the bare leads of through-hole components and short lengths of wire. A related material, stripboard (Veroboard), has copper strips running along one axis of the board, which are cut where connections must be interrupted.

Boards are made from phenolic paper, fibreglass epoxy (FR-4), or other composite materials. Single-sided boards have pads on only one face; double-sided boards have pads on both faces, sometimes with plated-through holes.

Use in prototyping

Before a printed circuit board is manufactured, hobbyists and engineers often build a circuit on perfboard to verify its operation, much as a breadboard is used for temporary experiments. Unlike a breadboard, a perfboard circuit is soldered and therefore permanent and relatively robust, though it lacks the custom routing and ground planes of a purpose-designed PCB.

The standard 0.1-inch hole spacing matches the pin spacing of dual in-line package (DIP) integrated circuits, so chips can be inserted directly or mounted in sockets and wired to surrounding components.
