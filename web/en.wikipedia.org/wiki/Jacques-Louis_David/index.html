Jacques-Louis David (30 August 1748 – 29 December 1825) was a French painter in the Neoclassical style, considered to be the preeminent painter of the era. He became the dominant visual chronicler of the French Revolution and later the court painter of Napoleon.

Early life and career

David studied at the Académie Royale in Paris and won the Prix de Rome in 1774, spending five years in Italy absorbing classical and Renaissance art. His Oath of the Horatii (1784), a severe scene of Roman civic virtue, made him famous and became an emblem of republican values on the eve of revolution.

Revolution and Empire

David threw himself into the Revolution, voting for the execution of Louis XVI and organizing revolutionary festivals. His Death of Marat (1793) is one of the most famous political images ever painted. Imprisoned after the fall of Robespierre, he reinvented himself under Napoleon, producing Napoleon Crossing the Alps and the enormous Coronation of Napoleon (1805–1807).

Exile and legacy

After the Bourbon Restoration he was exiled as a regicide and settled in Brussels, where he continued to paint portraits and mythological scenes until his death. His studio trained a generation of painters, including Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres, and his austere linear style shaped European painting for decades.
