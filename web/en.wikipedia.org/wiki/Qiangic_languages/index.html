Qiangic languages

Qiangic
Family: Sino-Tibetan
Branch: Tibeto-Burman (Qiangic)
Region: Western Sichuan and northwestern Yunnan, China

The Qiangic languages form a branch of the Tibeto-Burman languages spoken in the mountainous borderlands of western Sichuan and northwestern Yunnan in China, along the upper reaches of the Min, Dadu, and Jinsha rivers. The group is named after the Qiang people, an ethnic group recognised in China, though the grouping itself is based on linguistic geography and shared features rather than a proven common ancestor.

Languages

Languages usually classed as Qiangic include Qiang (Northern and Southern), Prinmi (Pumi), Minyak (Muya), Zhaba, Queyu, Ersu, Namuyi, Shixing, Choyo, and others, as well as the extinct Tangut language of the Western Xia empire, which extensive documentation has shown to be closely related to modern Qiangic languages. Many of these languages are notable for complex consonant clusters, elaborate directional prefix systems on verbs, and extensive documentation efforts prompted by their endangered status.

Classification

Whether Qiangic is a genuine genetic node is disputed: some scholars treat it as a linkage or an areal grouping of the "southern corridor" of Tibeto-Burman, while others reconstruct a Proto-Qiangic. The 2008 Sichuan earthquake, whose epicentre lay in the Qiang heartland, devastated speaker communities and accelerated documentation and revitalisation work.
