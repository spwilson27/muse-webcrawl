José Enrique Varela Iglesias (April 17, 1891 – March 24, 1951) was a Spanish general who fought on the Nationalist side in the Spanish Civil War. He was the only Spanish officer of his era to receive the Laureate Cross of Saint Ferdinand twice, and later served as Minister of the Army and as High Commissioner of Spain in Morocco.

Early career

Varela entered the infantry academy and built his reputation as an Africanista officer in the campaigns in Spanish Morocco, winning the Laureate Cross, Spain's highest military decoration, twice—for actions at Muires (1920) and the relief of positions during the Rif War (1921)—as a junior officer. Like Francisco Franco, Emilio Mola, and other future Civil War commanders, he rose rapidly through the Army of Africa's colonial fighting.

Spanish Civil War

At the outbreak of the Civil War in July 1936, Varela joined the Nationalist uprising. Commanding columns of the Army of Africa advancing from Andalusia, he relieved the besieged Alcázar of Toledo in September 1936 (an operation often credited jointly with the forces under his command), fought in the battles for Madrid, and later commanded the Army Corps of Castile and forces in the Levante offensive and the final campaigns of the war in 1939. He was critical of Franco's decision to divert to Toledo, which delayed the assault on Madrid.

Later career

After the Nationalist victory, Varela served as Minister of the Army from 1939 to 1942, resigning after the Begoña incident, in which Falangists attacked a Carlist religious service, amid his disputes with the Falange. From 1945 until his death he was High Commissioner of Spain in Morocco, dying in Tangier in 1951. He was posthumously granted the marquisate of Varela de San Fernando.
