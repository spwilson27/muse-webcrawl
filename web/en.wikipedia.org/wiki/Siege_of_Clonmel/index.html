Siege of Clonmel

The siege of Clonmel, in County Tipperary, Ireland, took place in April–May 1650 during the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland. The town was defended by Hugh Dubh O'Neill and a garrison of Ulster soldiers who had earlier fought in the Irish Confederate Wars. Clonmel's stubborn defence inflicted the heaviest casualties suffered by Oliver Cromwell's New Model Army in any single assault during the campaign, although the town ultimately surrendered on terms.

Background

After storming Drogheda and Wexford in 1649, Cromwell marched into Munster in the spring of 1650. Clonmel, a walled town on the River Suir, was garrisoned by some 1,500–2,000 Ulstermen under Hugh Dubh O'Neill, a veteran of the Spanish service and nephew of Owen Roe O'Neill.

The assault

Cromwell's artillery breached the northern wall after weeks of bombardment. O'Neill had prepared a killing ground inside the breach: a V-shaped earthwork lined with cannon and musketeers, with chains and obstacles. When the Parliamentarian storming party entered on 17 May 1650, it was caught in the trap and cut down; Cromwell committed his reserves in repeated attacks that failed with very heavy losses — estimates of Parliamentarian dead range from several hundred to over 2,000, the bloodiest repulse of Cromwell's career.

Surrender

That night, with his ammunition nearly exhausted, O'Neill slipped out of the town with his garrison. The mayor surrendered Clonmel the next day on generous terms, which Cromwell — unaware the garrison had gone — honoured. The delay and losses at Clonmel contributed to Cromwell's departure from Ireland shortly afterwards, leaving the conquest to his successors.
