Rugby Australia

Rugby Australia
Founded: 1949 (as Australian Rugby Football Union)
Headquarters: Moore Park, Sydney
Affiliation: World Rugby (founding member union)
National teams: Wallabies (men), Wallaroos (women)

Rugby Australia is the governing body for rugby union in Australia. Founded in 1949 as the Australian Rugby Football Union, it administers the game at all levels, including the national teams — the men's Wallabies and the women's Wallaroos — Super Rugby franchises, sevens programmes and grassroots development.

History

Rugby had been organised in Australia by state unions, principally New South Wales and Queensland, since the nineteenth century. A national body was formed in 1949 to coordinate international affairs, and Australia became a founding member of the International Rugby Football Board's expansion. The Wallabies won the Rugby World Cup in 1991 and again in 1999, and Australia hosted the tournament in 2003. The organisation rebranded as Rugby Australia in 2017.

Competitions and challenges

Rugby Australia oversees Australia's Super Rugby Pacific teams (such as the Brumbies, Reds, Waratahs and Western Force) and the domestic Super Rugby Women's competition. The sport competes for athletes and audiences with rugby league and Australian rules football, and the union has faced financial difficulties, including a near-insolvency during the COVID-19 pandemic and the costs surrounding the 2023 World Cup campaign. Australia is scheduled to host the men's Rugby World Cup in 2027 and the women's in 2029, seen as pivotal opportunities for the code.
