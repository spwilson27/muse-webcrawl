The Bishop of Leeds is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Leeds in the Province of York. The diocese was created on 20 April 2014, Easter Day, by the merger of the former dioceses of Bradford, Ripon and Leeds, and Wakefield, forming a single large diocese covering West Yorkshire and parts of neighbouring counties.

History

The creation of the Diocese of Leeds followed the work of the Dioceses Commission, which proposed a reorganisation of Anglican diocesan structures in Yorkshire. The new diocese is unusual in having three cathedrals — at Bradford, Ripon and Wakefield — and in being divided into five episcopal areas, each overseen by an area (suffragan) bishop: Bradford, Huddersfield, Leeds, Ripon and Wakefield.

The first Bishop of Leeds was Nick Baines, formerly Bishop of Bradford, who translated to the new see upon its creation in 2014. The bishop's seat rotates among the three cathedrals, with Wakefield Cathedral often used for diocese-wide occasions, while the pro-cathedral within the city of Leeds is Leeds Minster.

Area bishops

The diocesan bishop is assisted by the suffragan bishops of the five episcopal areas, styled Bishop of Bradford, Bishop of Huddersfield, Bishop of Kirkstall (for the Leeds area), Bishop of Ripon and Bishop of Wakefield.
