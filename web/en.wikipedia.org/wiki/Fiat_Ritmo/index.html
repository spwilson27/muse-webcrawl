The Fiat Ritmo is a small family car produced by the Italian manufacturer Fiat from 1978 to 1988. Launched at the 1978 Turin Motor Show, it was sold in North America as the Fiat Strada and built under licence in Spain by SEAT as the SEAT Ritmo. In total, about 1.79 million units were produced.

Development and design

The Ritmo was notable for being assembled largely by robots—the "Robogate" system at Fiat's Cassino plant—an innovation advertised with the slogan "Hand built by robots". Its distinctive styling, penned at Centro Stile Fiat, featured round headlamps set into a plastic nose panel and unusual circular door handles. The name Ritmo means "rhythm" in Italian; export badging as "Strada" avoided the feminine-hygiene connotations of a similar-sounding English word.

Engines and variants

Petrol engines ranged from a 1.1-litre unit to the twin-cam 2.0-litre of the sporting Ritmo Abarth 130 TC, which produced 130 hp and became a respected hot hatch of the 1980s. A 1.7-litre diesel joined the range in 1980. A facelift in 1982 rationalised the styling, and the Bertone-built Ritmo Cabriolet was sold until 1988.

Legacy

The Ritmo was succeeded by the Fiat Tipo in 1988. The name was revived for a 2007 Australian-market version of the Fiat Bravo, sold as the Fiat Ritmo.
