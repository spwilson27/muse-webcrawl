Violin family

The violin family of musical instruments was developed in Italy in the 16th century. At the time the name of this family of instruments was viole da braccio which was used to distinguish them from the viol family (viole da gamba). The standard modern violin family consists of the violin, viola, cello, and (possibly) double bass.

Instrument names in the violin family are all derived from the root viola, which is itself derived from the medieval Latin word vitula ("stringed instrument"). A violin is a "little viola," a violoncello is a "small violone" while a violone is a "big viola."

The violin and viola are played under the chin; the cello is played upright between the knees, supported by an endpin; and the double bass, the largest and lowest member, is played standing or seated on a high stool. All are normally played with a bow, though players also pluck the strings (pizzicato) and use other techniques such as col legno.

Construction

Instruments of the violin family share a common construction: a hollow body with a spruce top and maple back, ribs, and neck, with four strings stretched over a bridge. Characteristic features include the hourglass outline with C-bouts, f-holes, a carved scroll, and an arched top and back. The soundpost and bass bar inside the body are essential to its acoustics. The earliest makers—Andrea Amati in Cremona and Gasparo da Salò in Brescia—established the form in the mid-1500s, and it was refined by the Amati, Guarneri, and Stradivari workshops.

History

The violin emerged in northern Italy around 1520–1550 from earlier bowed instruments such as the rebec, the vielle, and the lira da braccio. By the 17th century the family was central to European art music, and it remains the core of the modern symphony orchestra's string section. The double bass differs somewhat in shape and tuning (in fourths rather than fifths) and retains some features of the viol family, reflecting its partly separate ancestry. Historical members and variants include the violino piccolo, the tenor violin (viola da spalla and its descendants), and the octobass, an enormous three-string instrument built by Jean-Baptiste Vuillaume in the 19th century.
