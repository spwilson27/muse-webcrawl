Corvette

A corvette is a small warship. It is traditionally the smallest class of vessel considered to be a proper (or "rated") warship. The warship class above the corvette is that of the frigate, while the class below was historically that of the sloop-of-war. The modern roles that a corvette fulfills include coastal patrol, missile boat, anti-submarine warfare, and fast attack craft.

The term "corvette" is derived from the French word corvair and ultimately from the Dutch korf, a kind of small vessel. Modern corvettes typically displace between 500 and 2,000 tonnes, though recent designs may approach frigate size.

History

Corvettes first appeared in the French Navy in the 1670s as small sailing warships larger than sloops but smaller than frigates, typically carrying a single tier of guns on the upper deck. During the Age of Sail, corvettes were used for scouting, dispatch carrying, and convoy escort. In the Royal Navy the equivalent vessels were classed as sloops-of-war.

World Wars

The corvette was revived in the Royal Navy just before the Second World War as a small, cheaply built escort vessel for convoy protection. The Flower-class corvettes, based on a whale-catcher design, became famous for anti-submarine escort work in the Battle of the Atlantic, though they were notorious for their uncomfortable motion in heavy seas. The later Castle-class improved on the design.

Modern corvettes

Modern corvettes are fast and heavily armed for their size, frequently carrying anti-ship missiles, a naval gun, torpedoes, and a helicopter deck. Examples include Russia's Steregushchiy class, Sweden's stealth-designed Visby class, Israel's Sa'ar 5 class, and the United States' littoral combat ships, which are sometimes compared to corvettes. Many navies use corvettes as the backbone of their coastal defence forces.
