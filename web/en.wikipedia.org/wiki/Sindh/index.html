Sindh (Sindhi: سنڌ; Urdu: سندھ) is a province of Pakistan, located in the south-eastern region of the country. It is the third-largest province by area and the second-largest by population after Punjab. The provincial capital is Karachi, Pakistan's largest city and financial centre. Sindh is bordered by Balochistan to the west, Punjab to the north, the Indian states of Gujarat and Rajasthan to the east, and the Arabian Sea to the south. The Indus River flows through the province, whose ancient cities include Mohenjo-daro, a principal centre of the Indus Valley Civilisation.

History

Sindh takes its name from the Sanskrit Sindhu, the Indus. The region hosted one of the world's earliest urban civilisations; Mohenjo-daro flourished around 2600 BCE. The province was conquered by Alexander the Great's forces, ruled by Maurya, Gupta and local dynasties such as the Rai and Brahman, and in 711 CE became the first region of the subcontinent to come under Muslim rule, with the Umayyad general Muhammad ibn al-Qasim's conquest. Later ruled by the Soomra, Samma, Arghun, Tarkhan, Mughal, Kalhora and Talpur dynasties, Sindh was annexed by the British East India Company under Charles Napier in 1843 after the battles of Miani and Hyderabad. At Partition in 1947 Sindh became part of Pakistan and received large numbers of Urdu-speaking Muhajir refugees, which reshaped its demography, especially in Karachi and Hyderabad.

Geography and economy

The province comprises the lower Indus plain, the Kirthar range in the west and the Thar Desert in the east. Agriculture — cotton, wheat, rice and sugarcane — depends on the Indus and its barrage irrigation system, including the Sukkur Barrage (1932). Karachi's ports handle the majority of Pakistan's trade, and the province contributes a large share of national revenue, industry (textiles, cement, steel) and natural gas production.

Culture

Sindh is known for its Sufi heritage — the shrines of Lal Shahbaz Qalandar at Sehwan and Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai at Bhit Shah draw vast gatherings — and for crafts such as ajrak block-printed cloth, Sindhi caps (topi), and ralli quilts. Sindhi is written in a modified Arabic script in Pakistan. The provincial day, Sindhi Culture Day, is celebrated in December.
