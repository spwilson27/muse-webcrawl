Lev Borisovich Kamenev (born Rosenfeld; 18 July 1883 – 25 August 1936) was a Bolshevik revolutionary and a leading figure in the early Soviet Union. A close associate of Lenin, he briefly served as the first head of state of Soviet Russia in November 1917, and after Lenin's death formed the ruling "triumvirate" with Joseph Stalin and Grigory Zinoviev before falling victim to Stalin's purges.

Career

Kamenev joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1901, worked as a Bolshevik organiser and editor, and spent years in exile and Siberian banishment. Returning in 1917, he (with Zinoviev) publicly opposed Lenin's call for the October insurrection, yet after the revolution chaired the Central Executive Committee and later the Moscow Soviet and served as deputy chairman of the Council of People's Commissars. In 1923–24 he allied with Stalin and Zinoviev against Trotsky, then broke with Stalin in 1925 to form the United Opposition; repeatedly expelled, demoted and readmitted, he was arrested after Kirov's assassination.

Trial and death

In August 1936 Kamenev and Zinoviev were the star defendants of the first Moscow show trial, the "Trial of the Sixteen", confessing under duress to fantastic charges of terrorism and treason. Both were shot the day after sentencing. Kamenev was posthumously rehabilitated by the Soviet Supreme Court in 1988.
