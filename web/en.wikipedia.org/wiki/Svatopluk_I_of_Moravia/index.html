Svatopluk I (also Svätopluk, Zwentibold in Frankish sources; died 894) was a Slavic ruler, first duke of Nitra and then the third and greatest ruler of Great Moravia, which he brought to its maximum territorial extent. He was a nephew of Rastislav, whom he helped depose.

Equestrian statue of Svatopluk at Bratislava Castle

Reign

After Rastislav was handed over to the Franks in 870, Svatopluk was himself imprisoned on suspicion of treason but was released, and in 871 he led a successful Slavic uprising that expelled the Frankish overseers. He thereafter balanced submission to and resistance against the East Frankish kingdom, defeating Frankish invasions in the 880s and expanding Moravian power over Bohemia, parts of present-day Slovakia, southern Poland, and eastward territories. He quarrelled with Archbishop Methodius's disciples after the missionary's death in 885 and supported the Latin rite clergy, leading to the expulsion of the Slavonic clergy from Moravia.

Death and legacy

Svatopluk died in 894, reportedly after a hunting accident; legend tells of him dividing his realm among his sons with the parable of the bundle of rods. His sons Mojmír II and Svatopluk II quarrelled, and Great Moravia collapsed under Magyar pressure by 907. Svatopluk remains a major figure in Slovak national tradition, commemorated by the equestrian statue unveiled at Bratislava Castle in 2010.
