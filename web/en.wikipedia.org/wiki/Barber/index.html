Barber

A barber is a person whose occupation is mainly to cut, dress, groom, style, and shave men's and boys' hair and beards. A barber's place of work is known as a barbershop (or barber's shop). Barbershops have historically served as places of social interaction and public discourse as well as grooming. In some jurisdictions barbers are licensed separately from cosmetologists or hairdressers.

History

Barbering is among the oldest professions: razors have been found among relics of the Bronze Age, and barbers held important roles in ancient Egypt and Rome, where the first shave (tonsura) was a rite of passage for young men. In medieval Europe, barbers doubled as surgeons and dentists, performing bloodletting, leeching, and tooth extraction; the traditional red-and-white-striped barber's pole is said to symbolize blood and bandages from this era. The professions were gradually separated, in England by legislation culminating in 1745, when barbers and surgeons formed distinct companies. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, barber colleges and unions standardized training, and barbershops became fixtures of towns and city neighborhoods.

Practice

Barbers work with clippers, scissors, combs, and straight razors. Services typically include haircuts, beard trims, hot-towel shaves, and styling. Training usually involves completing a state-approved barbering program—often around 1,000 to 1,500 hours in the United States—and passing a licensing examination. Since the 2010s, traditional barbershops have enjoyed a revival alongside renewed interest in classic men's grooming.
