Duke of Fife is a title in the Peerage of the United Kingdom, created twice, both times for Alexander Duff, 6th Earl Fife. The first creation in 1889 followed his marriage to Princess Louise, the eldest daughter of the future Edward VII and granddaughter of Queen Victoria.

History

Because the first dukedom could pass only to male heirs, a second creation in 1900 carried a special remainder allowing it to pass to the Duke's daughters and their male issue. On the first Duke's death in 1912 the title passed to his elder daughter, Princess Alexandra, as Duchess of Fife in her own right. Her son Alastair died before her, and the dukedom passed to her nephew James Carnegie.

The current holder is David Carnegie, 4th Duke of Fife. The heir apparent is Charles Duff Carnegie, Earl of Southesk.
