Jelly beans are small bean-shaped sugar candies with soft candy shells and thick gel interiors (the latter in modern versions often made of pectin-based jelly). They come in many flavors and colors and are especially associated in the United States with Easter, when they fill baskets and plastic eggs. The candy's ancestry is usually traced to two older confections: the chewy centers resemble Turkish delight, while the hard sugar coating derives from the dragée and Jordan almond tradition of sugar panning.

History

The precise origin of the jelly bean is uncertain, but by the late 19th century American candy makers were selling "jelly beans" as penny candy. Boston confectioner William Schrafft is often credited with popularizing them during the Civil War era by promoting them as treats to send to Union soldiers. The candies were long sold loose from jars in general stores, with a standard assortment of fruit and spice flavors, and were flavored mostly in the shell, with a relatively plain interior.

Gourmet jelly beans

In 1976 the Herman Goelitz Candy Company (now the Jelly Belly Candy Company) introduced Jelly Belly jelly beans, smaller and flavored throughout — in both shell and center — with natural flavorings where possible. The brand exploded in popularity after it became known that President Ronald Reagan was an enthusiast; Jelly Belly beans were stocked in the Oval Office and aboard Air Force One, and a jar was sent into space aboard the shuttle Challenger in 1983. Gourmet lines eventually grew to dozens of flavors, from buttered popcorn to deliberately eccentric "rotten egg" types in novelty assortments.

Production

Jelly beans are made by a process called starch molding: a sweetened, flavored center mixture is deposited into impressions in trays of cornstarch, allowed to set, then the centers are steamed, coated with successive layers of syrup in rotating pans (sugar panning), colored, flavored, and polished with confectioner's glaze, a cycle that can take one to two weeks. A single traditional jelly bean contains roughly four calories.

In culture

National Jelly Bean Day is observed in the United States on April 22. Estimating the number of jelly beans in a jar is a classic contest and a staple example in statistics and crowd-wisdom discussions; the candy also gave the English language the slang "jellybean" for various small rounded objects and, in computing, codenames for software versions.
