Stop Online Piracy Act

The Stop Online Piracy Act (SOPA) was a proposed United States bill introduced in the House of Representatives on October 26, 2011, by Representative Lamar S. Smith (R-TX). The bill aimed to expand the ability of US law enforcement to combat online copyright infringement and the trafficking of counterfeit goods, including by seeking court orders requiring search engines, payment processors and advertising networks to stop doing business with, and Internet service providers to block access to, websites accused of infringement.

SOPA was supported by the motion picture and recording industries but provoked an unprecedented online backlash. Critics, including technology companies, academics and civil liberties groups, argued it would enable censorship, threaten free speech and break Internet infrastructure such as DNS. On January 18, 2012, the English Wikipedia, Reddit and thousands of other websites blacked out or displayed protest messages; more than 14 million people contacted Congress.

On January 20, 2012, Smith announced that consideration of the bill was postponed indefinitely; its Senate counterpart, the PROTECT IP Act (PIPA), was likewise shelved.
