Library Journal

Library Journal is an American trade publication for librarians. It was founded in 1876 by Melvil Dewey, creator of the Dewey Decimal Classification, and is generally considered the first successful library journal and one of the oldest magazines in the United States devoted to the library profession. It is published by Media Source Inc.

History

Dewey founded the journal in 1876 — the same year he helped found the American Library Association — and it served as the official organ of the association in its early years. Under editors including Frederick Leypoldt and R. R. Bowker, the journal reported library news, published professional articles, and became known for its extensive book-review coverage, which made it a key selection tool for public libraries. In the twenty-first century it developed digital editions, blogs, and events, and its sibling publication School Library Journal covers children's and young-adult librarianship.

Features

The journal is known for annual features such as its "Movers & Shakers" awards recognising emerging leaders in librarianship, its Library of the Year award, salary and placement surveys of library-school graduates, and starred reviews of notable books. Its review section evaluates thousands of titles a year across fiction, nonfiction, and reference categories.
