Vienne, Isère

Vienne (French pronunciation: [vjɛn]) is a commune in southeastern France, located in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, 30 kilometres (19 mi) south of Lyon, on the river Rhône. It is the fourth-largest commune in the Isère department, of which it is a subprefecture. The town has a population of roughly 30,000.

History

Vienne was a major city of Roman Gaul. Before Roman conquest it was the capital of the Gallic Allobroges tribe. Under Augustus it became the Roman colony Colonia Julia Viennensis and prospered as a centre of trade on the Rhône. Numerous Roman monuments survive, including the Temple of Augustus and Livia, the Roman theatre, and the Pyramid — a remnant of the Roman circus. The city was also an important early centre of Christianity in Gaul; according to tradition, its church was founded by Crescens, a disciple of Saint Paul.

In the Middle Ages Vienne was part of the Kingdom of Burgundy and then of the Holy Roman Empire, ruled by its archbishops. The Council of Vienne (1311–1312), convened by Pope Clement V, dissolved the Knights Templar. The town was annexed to France in 1449.

Culture

Since 1981 Vienne has hosted the annual Jazz à Vienne festival, one of France's leading jazz festivals, staged in the ancient Roman theatre. The town is also known for its Roman heritage sites and museums.
