Martin Marietta

Martin Marietta Corporation was an American aerospace and defence company founded in 1961 through the merger of Glenn L. Martin Company and American-Marietta Corporation. It grew into one of the largest defence contractors in the United States, producing aircraft, missiles, launch vehicles and electronics. In 1995 it merged with Lockheed Corporation to form Lockheed Martin. A separate construction-materials company, Martin Marietta Materials, was spun off in 1996 and continues to trade under the Martin Marietta name.

History

The Glenn L. Martin Company, founded by aviation pioneer Glenn L. Martin in 1912, was a major aircraft manufacturer known for bombers such as the B-10 and B-26 Marauder and the Martin Maryland, as well as flying boats. American-Marietta was a diversified chemicals and building products firm. Their 1961 merger combined aerospace manufacturing with cement, chemicals and metallurgy businesses, reflecting a conglomerate strategy common to the era.

Martin Marietta's aerospace work included the Titan family of intercontinental ballistic missiles adapted as space launch vehicles, the Viking landers that reached Mars in 1976, the Magellan Venus probe, the external tank for the Space Shuttle, and electronics for aircraft and missiles. Its Denver, Colorado division was a centre of space systems work.

The Bendix takeover battle

In 1982 Martin Marietta famously fended off a hostile takeover bid by Bendix Corporation using a "Pac-Man defence": it counter-bid for Bendix itself. The episode ended with Bendix absorbed by Allied Corporation, while Martin Marietta survived as an independent company but with substantial debt.

Merger

Following the end of the Cold War, the U.S. defence industry consolidated. In 1993 Martin Marietta acquired General Electric's aerospace business, and in August 1994 it announced a "merger of equals" with Lockheed. The merger closed on March 15, 1995, creating Lockheed Martin, headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland, where Martin Marietta had been based. The aggregates business was retained briefly and spun off as Martin Marietta Materials in 1996.
