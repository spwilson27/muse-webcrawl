Australian Red Ensign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Australian Red Ensign is a defaced Red Ensign — a red field with the Union Jack in the canton and the badge of Australia in the fly. It consists of a red background with the Commonwealth Star and the Southern Cross. Its design mirrors the national flag, the Australian Blue Ensign, except that the background is red rather than blue.

History

Like the blue version, the Red Ensign arose from the 1901 Federal Flag Design Competition and was approved by King Edward VII in 1903. For the first half of the twentieth century the red ensign was the flag most Australians used on land, since the blue ensign was officially restricted to government use; confusion about which to fly persisted until the Flags Act 1953 confirmed the blue ensign as the national flag and the red ensign as the civil ensign for merchant shipping.

Use

Under Australian law the Red Ensign is the proper ensign for Australian-registered merchant and private vessels. It is also flown on Merchant Navy Day, 3 September, to commemorate the service of Australian merchant seamen, and it was carried by Australian troops in some First World War contexts before the blue ensign's status was settled. Some flag-change advocates and protest groups have also adopted the red ensign as a symbol, which has periodically attracted public controversy.
