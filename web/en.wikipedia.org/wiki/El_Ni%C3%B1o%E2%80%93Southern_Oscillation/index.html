El Niño–Southern Oscillation

The El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is an irregular periodic variation in winds and sea surface temperatures over the tropical eastern Pacific Ocean, affecting the climate of much of the tropics and subtropics. It is the most significant source of year-to-year climate variability on Earth.

Phases

ENSO has three phases. In the warm phase, El Niño, the eastern Pacific warms and the trade winds weaken. In the cool phase, La Niña, the eastern Pacific cools and the trades strengthen. Neutral conditions lie between. The "Southern Oscillation" is the accompanying seesaw in atmospheric pressure between the eastern and western Pacific, tracked by the Southern Oscillation Index.

Mechanism

Ocean and atmosphere are coupled through the Walker circulation: pressure differences drive easterly trade winds that pile warm water in the west; changes in sea surface temperature feed back on winds and pressure, allowing the system to oscillate.

Impacts

ENSO influences rainfall, temperature, tropical cyclone activity, agriculture, and ecosystems across the globe, and its state is monitored continuously to produce seasonal climate forecasts.
