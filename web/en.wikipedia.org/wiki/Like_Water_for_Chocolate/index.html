Like Water for Chocolate (Spanish: Como agua para chocolate) is a 1989 novel by Mexican author Laura Esquivel. Written in the style of magical realism, it follows Tita de la Garza, a young woman on a ranch in northern Mexico during the Mexican Revolution, whose emotions are transmitted through the food she cooks.

Plot

Forbidden by family tradition — the youngest daughter must remain unmarried to care for her mother — Tita watches her lover Pedro marry her sister Rosaura instead. The novel is structured in twelve monthly instalments, each opening with a recipe, blending culinary writing with the narrative. The Spanish title refers to water brought almost to boiling point for making hot chocolate, a metaphor for heightened emotion.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a 1992 film directed by Alfonso Arau, which became one of the most successful foreign-language films released in the United States at the time. A stage adaptation and other versions followed, and the book remains one of the best-known works of contemporary Mexican literature.
