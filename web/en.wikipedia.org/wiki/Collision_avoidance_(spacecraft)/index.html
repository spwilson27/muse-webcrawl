Collision avoidance (spacecraft)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Collision avoidance in spacecraft operations is the process of preventing a satellite or crewed spacecraft from colliding with another orbiting object, such as another satellite, a spent rocket stage or a fragment of space debris. It relies on tracking objects in orbit, predicting close approaches (conjunctions), assessing collision probability, and — when the risk is high enough — manoeuvring the spacecraft to a safer trajectory.

Tracking and conjunction assessment

Ground-based radar and optical sensors maintained by organisations such as the United States Space Surveillance Network track tens of thousands of objects in Earth orbit. Operators screen their spacecraft against this catalogue; when a predicted close approach falls within a set threshold, a conjunction data message is generated describing the time of closest approach, miss distance and estimated probability of collision. Satellite operators then decide whether the risk warrants action, balancing the danger of collision against the fuel cost and operational disruption of a manoeuvre.

Manoeuvres

Avoidance manoeuvres are usually small burns that change the spacecraft's orbit slightly — raising or lowering it, or shifting its phase along track — so that the miss distance at the predicted encounter increases. Large constellations, such as Starlink, perform very large numbers of such manoeuvres each year, increasingly automated. The International Space Station carries out debris avoidance manoeuvres roughly once a year on average and can also use docked visiting vehicles to adjust its orbit.

Incidents and context

The 2009 collision between the active Iridium 33 satellite and the defunct Kosmos 2251, which destroyed both and created thousands of debris fragments, demonstrated the consequences of a missed conjunction. The growth of mega-constellations and the general increase in orbital debris have made collision avoidance a routine, central part of satellite operations, and have motivated international work on space traffic management and debris mitigation guidelines.
