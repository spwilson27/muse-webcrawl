Lute song

The lute song (or lute ayre) was a form of vocal music from the late 16th and early 17th centuries, in which a solo voice is accompanied by a lute. It flourished especially in England during the late Renaissance, and related forms appeared in France (the air de cour) and elsewhere in Europe.

English lute songs

The English school began with John Dowland's First Booke of Songes or Ayres (1597), which could be performed by a solo voice with lute, or by four voices with lute accompaniment. Dowland's melancholic songs, such as "Flow my tears" (Lachrimae) and "In darkness let me dwell", became emblematic of the era. Other composers included Thomas Campion, who wrote both words and music for many of his ayres, John Danyel, Robert Jones, and Philip Rosseter.

Style

Lute songs set strophic poetry, often on themes of love and melancholy, with the lute providing either a polyphonic accompaniment in tablature or a simpler chordal support. They were published in books printed so that performers around a table could read their parts, reflecting their domestic, intimate character.
