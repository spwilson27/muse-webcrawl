The Sacramento River is the principal river of Northern California and the largest river in the state by discharge. Rising in the mountains near Mount Shasta, it flows south for about 400 miles through the Sacramento Valley and joins the San Joaquin River in the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta, whence its waters reach San Francisco Bay.

The Sacramento River near Redding, California.

Course and hydrology

The river collects runoff from the southern Cascade Range, the Klamath Mountains, the Coast Ranges and the western Sierra Nevada. Shasta Dam (completed 1945) impounds Shasta Lake and regulates flow for flood control, irrigation and hydroelectric power as part of the federal Central Valley Project. Major tributaries include the Pit, McCloud, Feather and American rivers. Levees line much of the lower river, and the Yolo Bypass carries floodwater around the city of Sacramento.

Ecology and use

The Sacramento historically supported enormous runs of Chinook salmon — four distinct runs are still recognised — and remains critical habitat for salmon, steelhead and the endangered delta smelt in its estuary. Its water irrigates much of the Sacramento Valley's farmland and supplies millions of Californians, making its management a persistent subject of environmental litigation and political debate.
