Seven basic tools of quality

The seven basic tools of quality are a set of graphical techniques identified as most useful for solving quality-related problems. The designation arose in post-war Japan, inspired by the seven famous weapons of the warrior-monk Benkei, and is associated especially with Kaoru Ishikawa, who held that as much as 95 percent of workplace quality problems could be solved with these simple tools.

The tools

ToolPurpose
Cause-and-effect diagram (Ishikawa / fishbone diagram)Identifies and organises the many possible causes of an effect or problem.
Check sheetA structured form for collecting and analysing data systematically.
Control chartGraphs process data over time with control limits, distinguishing common-cause from special-cause variation.
HistogramShows the frequency distribution of measured data, revealing shape, spread, and central tendency.
Pareto chartBar chart ordered by frequency, highlighting the "vital few" causes responsible for most problems (the 80/20 principle).
Scatter diagramPlots pairs of variables to look for relationships or correlation.
Stratification (or flowchart / run chart in some lists)Separates data arising from different sources so patterns become visible; some formulations substitute the flowchart or run chart.

Use

The tools form the practical core of total quality management (TQM) and quality circles, and remain embedded in Six Sigma and lean training, where they are usually taught as part of the DMAIC problem-solving cycle. Their enduring appeal is that they require no advanced statistics, can be taught quickly to front-line workers, and make problems visible by displaying data rather than arguing from impressions.
