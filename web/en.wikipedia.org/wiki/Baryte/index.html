Baryte

FormulaBaSO4
Crystal systemOrthorhombic
Mohs hardness3 – 3.5
Specific gravity4.5
ColourColourless, white, pale shades

Baryte, barite or barytes is a mineral consisting of barium sulfate (BaSO4). It is generally white or colourless and is the main source of the element barium. The baryte group includes baryte, celestine, anglesite and anhydrite. Its name derives from the Greek word βαρύς (barys), meaning "heavy", a reference to its high specific gravity of about 4.5, exceptional for a non-metallic mineral.

Occurrence

Baryte occurs in sedimentary rocks, hydrothermal veins with lead and zinc ores, and as a gangue mineral in metallic ore deposits. Major producers include China, India, Morocco and the United States.

Uses

The great majority of mined baryte is used as a weighting agent in drilling fluids for oil and gas exploration, where its density suppresses high formation pressures. Ground baryte is also used as a filler in paints, plastics and rubber, as a source of barium chemicals, and, because of its opacity to X-rays, in medical imaging as barium meal.

Crystal habit

Baryte crystallises in the orthorhombic system and commonly forms tabular crystals, rosette-like aggregates known as "desert roses", and massive granular deposits.
