Antiquarian

An antiquarian or antiquary is an aficionado or student of antiquities — that is, of past ages and their material remains. More specifically, the term is used for those who study history with particular attention to ancient artefacts, archaeological and historic sites, or historic archives and manuscripts. Antiquarianism, the historical and literary study of the material past, flourished in Europe from the Renaissance through the 18th century and laid foundations for the modern disciplines of archaeology, history, numismatics and epigraphy.

History

Renaissance humanists such as Cyriacus of Ancona collected inscriptions and described monuments. In England, figures like William Camden, John Aubrey and William Stukeley surveyed the landscape's Roman and prehistoric remains, and the Society of Antiquaries of London was founded in 1707. Similar societies arose across Europe. Antiquarians assembled cabinets of curiosities and corresponded in networks that spread knowledge of coins, manuscripts and ruins.

From antiquarianism to archaeology

During the 19th century, antiquarian pursuits professionalised into archaeology and academic history, with greater emphasis on stratigraphy, context and systematic excavation. The word "antiquarian" now often denotes a collector or dealer in old books and objects, as in the antiquarian book trade, while retaining its older scholarly sense.
