Maurice Gibb

Maurice Ernest Gibb CBE (22 December 1949 – 12 January 2003) was a British musician, singer and songwriter. He achieved worldwide fame as a member of the Bee Gees, the pop group formed with his brothers Barry and Robin Gibb. Although his elder brother Barry and twin brother Robin took most of the lead vocals, Maurice was the group's principal instrumentalist and arranger, playing bass guitar, keyboards and guitar, and singing the close harmonies that defined the Bee Gees' sound.

Early life

Maurice was born on the Isle of Man to English parents, the fraternal twin of Robin Gibb — Maurice was born second, 35 minutes after Robin. The family moved to Manchester and then emigrated to Brisbane, Australia, in 1958, where the brothers began performing and signed their first recording contract.

Career

The Bee Gees returned to England in 1967 and broke through internationally with songs such as "New York Mining Disaster 1941", "To Love Somebody" and "Massachusetts". After a brief split, the group reinvented itself in the mid-1970s with a falsetto-led R&B and disco style. Maurice's bass playing anchored hits including "Jive Talkin'", "Stayin' Alive", "How Deep Is Your Love" and "Night Fever", several of them from the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack (1977), one of the best-selling albums of all time.

Maurice also worked as a producer and songwriter for other artists and recorded a small amount of solo material. He was briefly married to the singer Lulu (1969–1973), and later to Yvonne Spenceley, with whom he had two children.

Death and legacy

Maurice died suddenly in Miami Beach, Florida, in January 2003, aged 53, from complications arising from a twisted intestine (volvulus). His brothers retired the Bee Gees name for a time in his honour. Maurice is remembered as the group's musical backbone — the multi-instrumentalist whose arrangements and harmonies held the Bee Gees' sound together across five decades.
