Major League Lacrosse

Sport: Field lacrosse

Founded: 1999

First season: 2001

Ceased: 2020 (merged into the Premier Lacrosse League)

Country: United States

Most titles: Chesapeake Bayhawks (6, incl. Baltimore era)

Major League Lacrosse (MLL) was a men's field lacrosse league in the United States. Founded in 1999 by Jake Steinfeld, Dave Morrow, and Tim Robertson, the league played its inaugural season in 2001 and operated through the 2020 season, after which it merged with the Premier Lacrosse League.

History

The league was conceived as a professional outdoor alternative to the indoor National Lacrosse League. Its first season in 2001 featured six teams, and the Long Island Lizards won the inaugural Steinfeld Cup championship. Over two decades the league expanded and contracted repeatedly, at its peak fielding ten teams in 2009 before financial pressures reduced its footprint.

MLL distinguished itself with rule variations from college lacrosse, most notably a two-point goal line 15 yards from the goal and a 60-second shot clock. The league served as a showcase for many of the sport's best-known players, including Casey Powell, John Grant Jr., Paul Rabil, and Rob Pannell.

Merger

On December 16, 2020, MLL announced it would merge its operations into the Premier Lacrosse League, with MLL players entering a PLL expansion draft. The Boston Cannons were carried over as the Cannons Lacrosse Club, preserving a direct link between the two leagues.

Championships

The Steinfeld Cup was awarded to the league champion. The Chesapeake Bayhawks were the most successful franchise, winning titles in 2002, 2005, 2010, 2012, 2013, and 2019. Other multiple champions included the Philadelphia Barrage and the Denver Outlaws.
