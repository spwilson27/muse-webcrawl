Emily, Countess Cowper, later Viscountess Palmerston

Emily Mary Lamb, Countess Cowper (21 April 1787 – 11 October 1869), later Viscountess Palmerston, was a leading figure of Regency and early Victorian London high society, a renowned political hostess, and one of the patronesses of Almack's assembly rooms.

Life

Emily Lamb was the daughter of Peniston Lamb, 1st Viscount Melbourne, and Elizabeth Lamb, Viscountess Melbourne, and sister of William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne, the Whig prime minister. There has long been speculation that her biological father was in fact her mother's lover, Lord Egremont. In 1805 she married Peter Clavering-Cowper, 5th Earl Cowper, with whom she had five children.

As Countess Cowper she became one of the most influential hostesses in London, was a celebrated patroness of Almack's — the exclusive social club whose vouchers were coveted by the ton — and was known for her beauty, wit and lavish entertaining. She conducted a long affair with Henry John Temple, 3rd Viscount Palmerston, whom she married in 1839, two years after Lord Cowper's death. As Lady Palmerston she ran one of the great political salons of the mid-19th century from Cambridge Square and Broadlands, supporting her husband's career as Foreign Secretary and Prime Minister. Palmerston's death in 1865 left her widowed a second time; she died in 1869.
