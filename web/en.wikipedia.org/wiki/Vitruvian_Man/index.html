Vitruvian Man

The Vitruvian Man (Italian: L'uomo vitruviano) is a drawing by the Italian Renaissance artist Leonardo da Vinci, dated to about 1490. Inspired by the Roman architect Vitruvius, the drawing depicts a nude man in two superimposed positions with his arms and legs apart, inscribed in a circle and a square. Written notes based on Vitruvius's De architectura accompany the image.

Description

The work illustrates Vitruvius's theory that the ideal human body fits geometrically into both circle and square and can serve as the model for harmonious architecture. Leonardo lists proportions in the accompanying text: a palm is four fingers, a foot is four palms, a cubit is six palms, a man's height is four cubits, and the span of his arms equals his height. The drawing merges art and science and is often held up as a symbol of the Renaissance ideal of proportion and of humanism.

History

Kept in the Gallerie dell'Accademia in Venice, the drawing is fragile and only rarely exhibited. It is one of the most reproduced images in the world, appearing on the Italian one-euro coin, textbooks, logos and spacesuits.
