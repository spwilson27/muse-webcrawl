Watkins Glen International, nicknamed "The Glen", is an automobile race track located in the town of Dix just southwest of the village of Watkins Glen, New York, at the southern tip of Seneca Lake. It is best known as the home of the United States Grand Prix from 1961 to 1980 and today hosts NASCAR Cup Series and IMSA sports car racing, including the Six Hours of The Glen.

History

Road racing in Watkins Glen began in 1948 on a circuit of public roads organised by Cameron Argetsinger, an event often credited with reviving road racing in the United States after the Second World War. After a fatal accident in 1952 the races moved first to a temporary course and then, in 1956, to a permanent 2.35-mile circuit on the current site.

From 1961 to 1980 The Glen hosted the United States Grand Prix, the longest continuous run of any American Formula One venue; winners included Jim Clark, Graham Hill, Jackie Stewart, Emerson Fittipaldi and Gilles Villeneuve. Financial difficulties forced the track into bankruptcy in 1981, but it was revived and later purchased by International Speedway Corporation (now part of NASCAR).

Layout

The full Grand Prix course is 3.400 miles (5.472 km) with 11 turns, including the fast uphill Esses and the "Boot" loop added in 1971. NASCAR uses a shorter 2.454-mile version that bypasses the Boot. The circuit is regarded as a classic natural-terrain road course, with significant elevation change and little run-off in its older sections.
