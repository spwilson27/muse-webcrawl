Irena Sendler (née Krzyżanowska; 15 February 1910 – 12 May 2008), also known by her wartime code name Jolanta, was a Polish social worker and humanitarian who served in the Polish Underground resistance in German-occupied Warsaw during the Second World War. As head of the children's section of Żegota, the Polish Council to Aid Jews, she and her network smuggled approximately 2,500 Jewish children out of the Warsaw Ghetto and placed them with Polish families, orphanages and convents under false identities.

Rescue work

Sendler, employed by Warsaw's Social Welfare Department, obtained a permit to enter the ghetto ostensibly to inspect for typhus. She recruited a network of about thirty collaborators, mostly women, who carried children out in ambulances, sacks, toolboxes and through the courthouse and sewers adjoining the ghetto. Determined that the children might one day reclaim their identities, she recorded their real names and hiding places on slips of paper buried in jars beneath an apple tree. In October 1943 she was arrested by the Gestapo, tortured and sentenced to death, but Żegota bribed guards to free her; she lived under an alias for the rest of the war.

Recognition

Israel's Yad Vashem recognised Sendler as Righteous Among the Nations in 1965. Her story remained little known outside Poland until 1999, when Kansas school students created the play Life in a Jar, which brought her international attention. She received Poland's highest honour, the Order of the White Eagle, in 2003 and was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 2007. She died in Warsaw in 2008, aged 98.
