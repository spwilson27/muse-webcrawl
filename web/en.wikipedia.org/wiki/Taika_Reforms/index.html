Taika Reforms

The Taika Reforms (大化の改新, Taika no Kaishin) were a series of political and administrative reforms enacted in Japan beginning in 645 CE, following the Isshi Incident, in which Prince Naka no Ōe (later Emperor Tenji) and Nakatomi no Kamatari overthrew the dominant Soga clan. Named for the Taika era proclaimed in 645 — the first Japanese era name (nengō) — the reforms reshaped the Japanese state on the model of Tang-dynasty China.

Content

The reform edicts of 646 declared all land and people public (under the emperor), abolishing private estates in principle; introduced a capital and provincial administrative system; ordered household registers and land surveys as the basis for taxation and corvée; and established a centralized bureaucracy. The reforms laid the groundwork for the ritsuryō legal-administrative system codified in the Taihō Code of 701.
