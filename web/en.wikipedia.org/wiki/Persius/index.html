Persius

Aulus Persius Flaccus (34 – 62 AD) was a Roman poet and satirist of Etruscan origin, known for his six Satires written in a dense Stoic style.

Persius
Born: 4 Dec 34 AD, Volterra
Died: 24 Nov 62 AD
Occupation: Satirist
Philosophy: Stoicism

Life

Born to a wealthy equestrian family at Volterra, Persius was educated in Rome, studied under the Stoic philosopher Lucius Annaeus Cornutus, and befriended Lucan and Seneca's circle. He died of a stomach illness at 27, leaving his satires to be edited posthumously by Cornutus and published by Caesius Bassus.

Work

His six hexameter satires (about 650 lines total) attack literary pretension, religious superstition, ambition and moral laziness, advocating Stoic self-examination. Notoriously compressed and allusive, they were admired in antiquity and the Middle Ages, influenced Juvenal, and were translated by John Dryden.
