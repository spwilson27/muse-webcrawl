Frank Gardiner

Francis Gardiner (1830–1882) was a Scottish-born Australian bushranger. The leader of a gang responsible for a series of robberies in New South Wales in the early 1860s, Gardiner is best known for leading the 1862 robbery of the gold escort at Eugowra, then the largest gold robbery in Australian history.

Early life

Gardiner was born in Rosshire, Scotland, in 1830 and emigrated to Australia with his parents in 1834. He grew up in the Boro district near Goulburn, New South Wales. As a young man he worked as a stockman and horse dealer. In 1850 he was convicted of horse stealing and sentenced to five years' hard labour at Cockatoo Island in Sydney Harbour. Released on ticket of leave, he was soon in trouble again and spent further time in prison for cattle theft.

Bushranging career

By 1861 Gardiner had taken to bushranging in the Lachlan district, operating around Lambing Flat and the Abercrombie Ranges. Adopting the alias Frank Christie at various times, he gathered a gang that included John Gilbert, Ben Hall, Dan Charters and others who would go on to lead notorious gangs of their own.

On 15 June 1862, Gardiner's gang held up the gold escort coach near Eugowra, making off with banknotes and gold worth about £14,000 — the largest gold robbery in Australia at the time. The robbery made Gardiner the most wanted man in the colony, though he escaped the ensuing police hunt and fled to Queensland, where he lived under the name Frank Christie and ran a store at Apis Creek near Rockhampton.

Capture and imprisonment

In 1864 Gardiner was recognised, arrested and brought back to Sydney. He was tried and sentenced to 32 years' hard labour. His case attracted public sympathy; petitions for clemency circulated widely, and in 1874, after serving about ten years, he was released on condition that he leave the colony.

Later life and death

Gardiner travelled to San Francisco, where he reportedly ran a saloon. He died there in 1882. His release had caused a political controversy in New South Wales, contributing to the fall of the government of the day.

Legacy

Gardiner is remembered as the "king of the road" and a central figure in the bushranging era that also produced Ned Kelly. The Eugowra robbery remains a staple of Australian folklore.
