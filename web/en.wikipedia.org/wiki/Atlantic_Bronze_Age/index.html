Atlantic Bronze Age

The Atlantic Bronze Age is a cultural complex of the Bronze Age period in prehistoric Europe, roughly 1300–700 BC, encompassing the coastal regions of Portugal, Galicia, western France (Brittany), southern Ireland, Cornwall, Wales and southern Scotland.

Characteristics

The complex is defined less by a single unified culture than by intense maritime trade networks exchanging metalwork — including distinctive swords of the "carp's tongue" type, bronze cauldrons, and gold ornaments — along the Atlantic façade. Large hoards of bronze objects, many deposited in rivers and bogs, are a hallmark of the period.

Interpretation

Archaeologists debate how far the Atlantic Bronze Age represents a shared cultural identity, possibly associated with early Celtic languages, as opposed to a trade network linking distinct regional groups.
