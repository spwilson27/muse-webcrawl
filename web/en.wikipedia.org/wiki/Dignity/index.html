Dignity

Dignity is the right of a person to be valued and respected for their own sake, and to be treated ethically. It is a central concept in morality, ethics, law, and politics, denoting the intrinsic worth of human beings simply by virtue of being human.

Philosophical accounts

In Kantian ethics, dignity (Würde) is the incomparable worth of rational beings as ends in themselves, which must never be treated merely as means. Earlier traditions grounded dignity in the image of God in humanity or in social rank; modern thought, influenced by thinkers such as Pico della Mirandola, universalised it.

In law

Human dignity is proclaimed in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) and is a foundational value of several constitutions, notably Germany's Basic Law, whose first article declares human dignity inviolable. It plays a major role in bioethics, debates about the treatment of prisoners, and end-of-life care.
