Formal group

Formal group
FieldAlgebraic geometry, number theory
Key examplesAdditive and multiplicative formal groups; formal groups of elliptic curves
RelatedLie algebra, Hopf algebra, complex cobordism

In mathematics, a formal group law is a power series that behaves like the product operation of a Lie group, but is defined purely algebraically, without any notion of convergence. A one-dimensional formal group law over a commutative ring R is a power series F(x, y) in two variables with coefficients in R satisfying identities that mimic the group axioms: F(x, 0) = x, F(0, y) = y, and associativity F(F(x, y), z) = F(x, F(y, z)). A formal group is the geometric object — a formal scheme — corresponding to such a law.

Examples

The additive formal group law is F(x, y) = x + y, and the multiplicative formal group law is F(x, y) = x + y + xy. A rich source of examples comes from algebraic groups: completing an algebraic group at its identity element yields a formal group; for an elliptic curve this construction is fundamental in arithmetic geometry.

Logarithms and Lie algebras

Over a ring containing the rationals, every one-dimensional formal group law has a logarithm — a power series converting it to the additive law — and formal group theory mirrors the theory of Lie algebras. In characteristic p the situation is richer: formal groups are classified in part by their height, an invariant central to chromatic homotopy theory and the study of formal groups arising from elliptic curves and abelian varieties.

Applications

Formal groups appear in algebraic topology, where the formal group law of a complex-oriented cohomology theory encodes deep structure (as in Quillen's theorem relating complex cobordism to the universal formal group law), and in number theory, through Lubin–Tate formal groups used in local class field theory.
