Media conglomerate

Media conglomerate
TypeCorporate structure
Related fieldsMedia economics, communications policy
[Image: a diagram of a media group's divisions]

A media conglomerate (or media group) is a company that owns interests across multiple mass-media businesses — television, film, publishing, radio, streaming and digital media. Conglomerates grew through 20th-century consolidation; major examples include Disney, Comcast, Warner Bros. Discovery, Paramount and News Corp, and their scale is a recurring subject of media-policy debate.

History

Early press barons built chains of newspapers; broadcasting and film added new arms. Deregulation, notably the US Telecommunications Act of 1996, accelerated mergers, producing giants that combined content production with distribution.

Major groups

The largest groups span film studios, TV networks, cable channels, streaming services and publishing. Family-controlled groups (News Corp, Bertelsmann) coexist with publicly traded ones, and technology companies (Apple, Amazon, Alphabet) have become media owners through streaming and platforms.

Criticism and regulation

Critics argue concentration threatens viewpoint diversity and local news; regulators impose ownership caps and review mergers. Supporters cite the investment scale global content now requires.

Local news decline

In newspapers, chain ownership and hedge-fund ownership of chains are widely linked to newsroom cuts — a central concern of journalism-policy research.
