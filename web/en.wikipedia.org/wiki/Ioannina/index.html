Ioannina

Ioannina (Greek: Ιωάννινα), often called Jannena within Greece, is the capital and largest city of the Ioannina regional unit and of Epirus, an administrative region in north-western Greece. The city has a population of roughly 113,000 in its municipal unit and lies on the western shore of Lake Pamvotida at an elevation of about 480 metres.

History

Although the area was inhabited in antiquity, the city itself is first securely attested in the Byzantine period; tradition links its foundation to the emperor Justinian I in the 6th century. After the Fourth Crusade it became the capital of the Despotate of Epirus. Ioannina was conquered by the Ottoman Empire in 1430 and remained under Ottoman rule for nearly five centuries.

The city is especially associated with Ali Pasha of Yanina, the Albanian Ottoman ruler who governed much of western Greece from Ioannina between 1788 and his death in 1822, maintaining a famously splendid and brutal court visited by Lord Byron. Ioannina was incorporated into the Greek state in 1913 during the Balkan Wars.

Landmarks

The walled old town (the Kastro) encloses the Its Kale acropolis, where the Fethiye Mosque and the tomb of Ali Pasha stand, and the Aslan Pasha Mosque, now a municipal museum. On the island in Lake Pamvotida (Nisi) stand several Byzantine monasteries and the house where Ali Pasha was killed, now a museum. Ioannina was historically renowned for its silversmiths, and the craft survives in its jewellery shops today.

Modern city

The University of Ioannina, founded in 1964, is one of Greece's major universities with a noted medical school. The city's Jewish Romaniote community, present for centuries, was largely destroyed in 1944 during the Holocaust. Ioannina is served by Ioannina National Airport "King Pyrrhus" and the Egnatia Odos motorway.
