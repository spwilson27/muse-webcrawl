Advanced Placement (AP) is a program in the United States and Canada created by the College Board that offers college-level curricula and examinations to high school students. Colleges and universities may grant placement and course credit to students who obtain high scores on the examinations, which are graded on a scale of 1 to 5.

History

The program emerged from studies in the early 1950s — funded by the Ford Foundation — that sought to close the gap between secondary school and college teaching. The first AP examinations were administered in 1954 to students at a group of pilot schools, and the College Board took over administration in 1955. Participation has since grown to millions of students taking several million exams each year across roughly forty subjects.

Courses and exams

AP courses span the arts, English, history and social sciences, mathematics and computer science, sciences, and world languages; well-known examples include AP Calculus, AP Biology, AP United States History and AP English Literature. Exams, given over two weeks each May, typically combine multiple-choice questions with free-response sections. Scores of 3 ("qualified") or above are considered passing by most institutions, though selective colleges often require 4 or 5 for credit.

Criticism

Debate around AP concerns the cost of exams, disparities in course availability between wealthy and less affluent schools, and some colleges' decisions to reduce or stop granting credit. In 2023–24 the College Board also began moving many exams to digital formats.
