Swedish Pomerania

Swedish Pomerania
Swedish: Svenska Pommern
German: Schwedisch-Pommern
Period: 1630–1815
Capital: Stettin (Szczecin), later Stralsund and Greifswald

Swedish Pomerania (Swedish: Svenska Pommern; German: Schwedisch-Pommern) was a dominion under the Swedish Crown from 1630 to 1815, situated on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea in present-day Germany and Poland. It comprised part of the former Duchy of Pomerania, including the islands of Rügen, Usedom and Wolin, with the Oder estuary and the ports of Stettin, Stralsund, Greifswald and Wismar at various times.

History

Swedish forces under Gustavus Adolphus landed in Pomerania in 1630 during the Thirty Years' War. Under the Peace of Westphalia in 1648, Sweden received western Pomerania with Rügen, the mouth of the Oder, and rights in eastern Pomerania. As a holder of an imperial fief, the Swedish king thereby became a prince of the Holy Roman Empire with a seat in the Imperial Diet.

By the Treaty of Stockholm in 1720, following the Great Northern War, Sweden ceded Stettin and the lands south of the Peene to Prussia. The remaining territory north of the Peene, with Stralsund and Rügen, stayed Swedish until 1815, when Sweden ceded it to Prussia under the Congress of Vienna settlement; in exchange Sweden received compensation connected to its union with Norway.

Administration

The territory was governed by a governor-general acting for the Swedish Crown. It retained German law and its estates (nobility, towns and clergy), and the University of Greifswald, founded in 1456, became Sweden's oldest university on what is now German soil. Wismar was administered separately and was formally returned in 1903, though Sweden had effectively lost control much earlier.

Legacy

Swedish rule left visible traces in the region, including fortifications, administrative buildings and the incorporation of towns such as Stralsund into Sweden's trading sphere. The town of Barth, Greifswald and Stralsund preserve notable Swedish-era architecture.
