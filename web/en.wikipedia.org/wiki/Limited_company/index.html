Limited company

A limited company is a form of business organisation in which the liabilities of the company's owners or subscribers are limited to what they have invested or guaranteed to the company. Limited companies may be set up as public or private, and the concept is found in most common-law and many civil-law jurisdictions, under local names such as Ltd, GmbH, BV, SARL, and PTY Ltd.

Types in the United Kingdom

Under UK company law, principally the Companies Act 2006, the main forms are: the private company limited by shares ("Limited" or "Ltd"), whose shares may not be offered to the public; the public limited company ("plc"), which may offer shares to the public and must have a minimum allotted share capital; the private company limited by guarantee, commonly used by charities and clubs, whose members guarantee a nominal sum on winding up; and the unlimited company, a rarer form.

Limited liability

The company is a separate legal person from its members, a principle settled in the landmark case Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897]. Accordingly, shareholders risk only their investment and any unpaid amount on their shares; their personal assets are generally beyond the reach of the company's creditors, except in cases such as fraud or wrongful trading where courts may pierce the corporate veil.

Formation and administration

In the UK, companies are formed by registering documents, including articles of association, with Companies House. Directors manage the company, must file annual accounts and confirmation statements, and owe statutory duties to the company.
