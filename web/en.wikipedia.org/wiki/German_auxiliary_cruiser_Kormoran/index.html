The auxiliary cruiser Kormoran

The German auxiliary cruiser Kormoran (HSK-8) was a Kriegsmarine merchant raider of World War II. Originally the merchant vessel Steiermark, she was converted into a commerce raider disguised as a merchantman. Kormoran was the largest of Germany's auxiliary cruisers and operated in the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific Oceans under the command of Fregattenkapitän Theodor Detmers.

Career

Commissioned in October 1940, Kormoran sank or captured eleven merchant ships totalling about 68,000 gross register tons. She replenished U-boats and other raiders and laid mines off Indian ports.

Battle with HMAS Sydney

On 19 November 1941, off the coast of Western Australia, Kormoran encountered the Australian light cruiser HMAS Sydney. Disguised as the Dutch freighter Straat Malakka, the raider opened fire at close range when challenged. In the ensuing battle both ships were fatally damaged: Sydney sank with all 645 hands, the only Australian warship ever lost with her entire crew, while Kormoran was scuttled; most of her crew survived and were taken prisoner.

Discovery

The wrecks of both ships were located in March 2008 by the Finding Sydney Foundation, ending a 66-year mystery. A formal inquiry in 2009 confirmed the German accounts of the battle.
