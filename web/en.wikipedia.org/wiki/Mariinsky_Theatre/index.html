Mariinsky Theatre

The Mariinsky Theatre is a historic theatre of opera and ballet in Saint Petersburg, Russia. Opened in 1860, it became the pre-eminent music theatre of late-19th-century Russia, where many of the stage masterpieces of Tchaikovsky, Mussorgsky and Rimsky-Korsakov received their premieres. It is home to the Mariinsky Ballet, the Mariinsky Opera and the Mariinsky Orchestra.

History

The theatre was named after Empress Maria Alexandrovna, wife of Tsar Alexander II. Under ballet master Marius Petipa, the company premiered such classics as The Sleeping Beauty and the revived Swan Lake. In the Soviet era the theatre was renamed the Kirov Theatre (1935), after the assassinated Leningrad party leader Sergei Kirov, a name the ballet company still uses when touring abroad. The historic name Mariinsky was restored in 1992.

The theatre today

Since 1988 the theatre’s artistic director has been the conductor Valery Gergiev. A modern second stage, Mariinsky II, designed by Diamond Schmitt Architects, opened across the Kryukov Canal in 2013, alongside the historic Mariinsky I building.
