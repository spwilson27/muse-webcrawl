The champion 1936 Green Bay Packers

The 1936 NFL season was the 17th regular season of the National Football League. It was the first season in which every team played the same number of games, and the league held its first college draft — the 1936 NFL draft, an idea championed by future commissioner Bert Bell to promote competitive balance. The season concluded with the Green Bay Packers defeating the Boston Redskins 21–6 in the NFL Championship Game.

Major changes

The inaugural NFL draft was held on 8 February 1936 in Philadelphia; Jay Berwanger of the University of Chicago was the first player ever selected, by the Philadelphia Eagles, though he never played professional football. The Pittsburgh Pirates (now Steelers) signed the first drafted player to actually play, William Shakespeare of Notre Dame. The Boston Redskins played their final season in Boston before moving to Washington in 1937.

Division races

In the Western Division, Curly Lambeau's Packers, powered by the passing of Arnie Herber to Don Hutson and the running of Johnny "Blood" McNally and Clarke Hinkle, finished 10–1–1. In the East, the Boston Redskins won the division on the final weekend, edging out Pittsburgh. Boston owner George Preston Marshall, angered by poor fan support, moved the championship game to the neutral Polo Grounds in New York.

Championship game

On 13 December 1936, before 29,545 spectators at the Polo Grounds, the Packers beat the Redskins 21–6 on touchdown passes from Herber to Hutson and Milt Gantenbein and a Hank Gremminger-era ground score, securing Green Bay's fourth NFL championship and its first since the championship game was instituted.
