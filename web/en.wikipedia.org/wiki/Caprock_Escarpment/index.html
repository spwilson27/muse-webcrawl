Caprock Escarpment

The Caprock Escarpment is a term used in West Texas and Eastern New Mexico for the escarpment that forms the boundary between the level high plains of the Llano Estacado and the surrounding rolling terrain. It is a prominent geological feature, dropping several hundred feet over a rugged, eroded slope.

Geology

The escarpment is held up by the caliche-rich "caprock" layer of the Ogallala Formation — hard calcareous sandstone and conglomerate that resists erosion while softer underlying Triassic and Permian red beds are worn away. Streams cutting back into the plateau have created canyons such as Palo Duro Canyon and Caprock Canyons, now protected as Texas state parks.

Extent

The escarpment runs roughly north–south for hundreds of miles from the Panhandle near Amarillo southward past Lubbock and Big Spring. Historically it marked a formidable travel barrier; the city of Lubbock sits atop it, while towns like Post lie at its foot.
