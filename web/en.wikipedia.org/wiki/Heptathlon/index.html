Heptathlon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A heptathlon is a combined track and field event made up of seven events. The name derives from the Greek hepta (seven) and áthlon (contest). The women's heptathlon is the senior combined event for women at the Olympic Games and World Championships, having replaced the pentathlon in 1984; there is also a men's heptathlon contested indoors.

Women's heptathlon

The women's outdoor heptathlon is contested over two consecutive days:

Day 1Day 2
100 metres hurdlesLong jump
High jumpJavelin throw
Shot put800 metres
200 metres

Performances in each event are converted to points using scoring tables. The world record of 7,291 points was set by American Jackie Joyner-Kersee at the 1988 Seoul Olympics and remains one of the oldest records in women's athletics. Other celebrated champions include Carolina Klüft of Sweden, Jessica Ennis-Hill of Great Britain, and Nafissatou Thiam of Belgium.

Men's indoor heptathlon

The men's heptathlon is held indoors and consists of the 60 metres, long jump, shot put, and high jump on day one, and the 60 metres hurdles, pole vault, and 1000 metres on day two.
