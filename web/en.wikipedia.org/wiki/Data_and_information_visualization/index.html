Data and information visualization

Data and information visualization (often abbreviated data viz or info viz) is the practice of designing and creating easy-to-communicate and easy-to-understand graphic or visual representations of a large amount of complex quantitative and qualitative data and information, with the aim of helping viewers understand patterns, trends, and insights in the data.

History

Early landmarks include William Playfair's invention of the line, bar, and pie charts in the late 18th century; Charles Joseph Minard's celebrated flow map of Napoleon's 1812 Russian campaign; and Florence Nightingale's polar area diagrams of mortality in the Crimean War. In the 20th century, Jacques Bertin's Sémiologie Graphique (1967) systematized the use of visual variables, John Tukey championed exploratory data analysis, and Edward Tufte's The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983) set enduring standards for clarity and data-ink efficiency.

Types and techniques

Common visual forms include bar charts, histograms, scatter plots, heat maps, choropleth maps, tree maps, network diagrams, and dashboards. Interactive visualization, enabled by software libraries such as D3.js and tools such as Tableau, allows users to filter, zoom, and drill into data. Research in the field studies perception, cognition, and the effectiveness of visual encodings, drawing on the work of Cleveland and McGill on graphical perception.

Applications

Data visualization is used in science, business intelligence, journalism, public health, and government. The field overlaps with information design, infographics, scientific visualization, and visual analytics.
