Photo manipulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A digitally composited image.

Photo manipulation involves the transformation or alteration of a photograph using various methods and techniques to achieve desired results. Some photo manipulations are considered to be skillful artwork, while others are considered to be unethical practices, especially when used to deceive the public.

Manipulation of photographs began almost as soon as photography itself. In the nineteenth century, "spirit photographers" produced images purporting to show ghosts, and combination printing — making a single print from multiple negatives — was used by art photographers such as Oscar Gustave Rejlander and Henry Peach Robinson. In the twentieth century, state propaganda made notorious use of retouching: officials who fell from favour in the Soviet Union were airbrushed out of official photographs.

Digital era

The release of software such as Adobe Photoshop (1990) made sophisticated manipulation accessible to anyone with a computer, and the verb "to photoshop" entered common usage. Digital techniques include cloning, compositing, colour correction, blemish removal, object removal, and generative fill driven by machine learning.

Ethics and journalism

Photojournalism codes of ethics generally prohibit manipulation that alters the content of a news image beyond minimal cropping and tonal adjustment. Several scandals — including altered war photographs and magazine covers — have led to firings and to the development of content-authenticity standards such as C2PA provenance metadata. Manipulation remains routine and accepted in advertising, fashion, and fine-art contexts, though fashion retouching is increasingly regulated in some countries.
