Hairy woodpecker
Scientific nameLeuconotopicus villosus
FamilyPicidae
RangeNorth and Central America
ConservationLeast Concern

The hairy woodpecker (Leuconotopicus villosus, formerly Dryobates villosus or Picoides villosus) is a medium-sized woodpecker found across North and Central America, from Canada and Alaska to Panama. It is a common and widespread species of forests, woodlots and wooded suburbs.

Description

Adults are mainly black and white: a white back, black wings with white spotting, a white underside and bold head stripes; males have a small red patch on the back of the head. It is almost identical in plumage to the smaller downy woodpecker, from which it is best distinguished by its larger size, longer, chisel-like bill and clean white outer tail feathers.

Behaviour

Hairy woodpeckers forage on tree trunks and large branches, hammering and probing bark for insects, especially larvae of wood-boring beetles, and also eat seeds and suet at feeders. They nest in cavities excavated in dead trees, laying about four white eggs.
