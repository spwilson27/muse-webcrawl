Police rank

Police ranks are a system of hierarchical relationships in police organisations. Most police forces organise their personnel into ranked structures broadly modelled on military hierarchy, with titles and insignia varying by country and force. Rank determines authority, responsibility, and pay, and is usually displayed on uniform epaulettes or sleeves.

Rank insignia are typically worn on epaulettes.

United Kingdom

Territorial police forces in England and Wales use a ladder running constable, sergeant, inspector, chief inspector, superintendent, chief superintendent, then chief officer ranks (assistant chief constable, deputy chief constable, chief constable). The Metropolitan Police and City of London Police use commander and commissioner titles at the top. Detectives carry the same ranks with the prefix "detective".

United States

US ranks vary widely by department but commonly include officer/deputy, corporal, sergeant, lieutenant, captain, major, deputy chief, and chief of police (or commissioner/superintendent in large cities such as New York and Chicago). Federal agencies use special agent grades and supervisory titles rather than uniform ranks.

Other countries

India's police use ranks from constable through sub-inspector, deputy superintendent, and Indian Police Service grades up to Director General of Police. Many European forces employ commissioner-based systems, while Japan's ranks run from police officer to Superintendent General of the Tokyo Metropolitan Police.
