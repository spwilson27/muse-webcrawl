Śnieżka

Śnieżka (Czech: Sněžka; German: Schneekoppe) is a mountain on the border between Poland and the Czech Republic, in the Karkonosze (Giant Mountains) range of the Sudetes. At 1,603 metres, it is the highest point of the Czech Republic and of the Silesian region, and the most prominent peak in northern Europe east of the Alps.

Geography and climate

The summit is a broad granite pyramid rising above the alpine tundra of the Karkonosze National Park. Its weather is famously severe: fog covers the peak for much of the year, winds are strong, and average temperatures are near freezing. A Polish meteorological observatory — a striking building of three interconnected discs, built in the 1970s — stands on the summit, along with the 17th-century Chapel of St. Lawrence and a Czech post office.

History

The mountain was mined and prospected in the Middle Ages and became one of the earliest tourist summits in Europe; Goethe and numerous other travellers climbed it in the 18th and 19th centuries. Before 1945 the mountain lay in German Silesia and was known as the Schneekoppe. A chairlift from the Czech town of Pec pod Sněžkou and marked trails from Karpacz in Poland carry large numbers of hikers to the top each year.
