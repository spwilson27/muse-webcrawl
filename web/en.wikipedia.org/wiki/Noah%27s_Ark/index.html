Noah's Ark

Noah's Ark is the vessel in the Genesis flood narrative in the Bible, through which God saves Noah, his family, and examples of all the world's animals from a global deluge. According to the Book of Genesis, God instructed Noah to build the ark so that life could continue after the Flood.

The biblical account

Genesis describes the ark as made of gopher wood, pitched inside and out, measuring 300 cubits long, 50 cubits wide, and 30 cubits high, with a roof, a window, and a door in its side. Noah took aboard his three sons and their wives, and pairs of every animal — seven pairs of clean animals — before rain fell for forty days and nights. The waters prevailed for months before receding, and the ark came to rest "upon the mountains of Ararat".

Parallels and traditions

The story closely parallels earlier Mesopotamian flood myths, including those of Ziusudra, Atrahasis, and Utnapishtim in the Epic of Gilgamesh. The ark also appears in the Qur'an, where Nuh's vessel is a major sign. Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions have generated extensive commentary on its construction and meaning.

Searches and interpretations

Mount Ararat in modern Turkey has long been a focus of expeditions searching for remains of the ark, none of which have produced accepted evidence. Scholars generally treat the narrative as mythic, while many believers understand it literally; full-scale replica arks have been built in the Netherlands and the United States.
