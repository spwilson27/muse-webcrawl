Leônidas da Silva (6 September 1913 – 24 January 2004) was a Brazilian footballer who played as a forward. Nicknamed the "Black Diamond," he was one of the most celebrated players of the pre-war era and is widely credited with popularising the bicycle kick.

Career

Leônidas starred for Brazilian clubs including Vasco da Gama, Botafogo, Flamengo, and São Paulo. At the 1938 FIFA World Cup in France he was the tournament's top scorer with seven goals, including a famous performance against Poland, despite Brazil's semi-final defeat. His acrobatic overhead kicks, which he had performed since the early 1930s, made him a national idol.

Legacy

After retiring, Leônidas became a popular radio commentator. He is remembered as one of Brazil's first great football superstars and a pioneer whose fame helped establish the sport's central place in Brazilian culture.
