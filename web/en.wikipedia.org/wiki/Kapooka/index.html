Kapooka is a suburb of Wagga Wagga in the Riverina region of New South Wales, Australia. Situated about 12 kilometres south-west of the city centre, Kapooka is best known as the location of Blamey Barracks, home to the Australian Army's 1st Recruit Training Battalion — the Army Recruit Training Centre (ARTC), through which nearly all Australian Army enlisted recruits pass.

History

The site was established as an army camp during the Second World War and named after a nearby property. In 1942, an explosion during a training exercise with live explosives killed trainees and instructors; a memorial at Kapooka commemorates the incident. Blamey Barracks, named after Field Marshal Sir Thomas Blamey, developed into the Army's principal recruit training establishment.

Recruit training

Recruits at Kapooka undertake the Army Recruit Course, an intensive programme of drill, field training, weapons handling, and physical fitness lasting several weeks. The name "Kapooka" is widely recognised within the Australian Defence Force, and the centre's marching-out parades are a Wagga Wagga institution.

Geography

Kapooka lies near the Olympic Highway and the Main Southern railway line, amid low hills and farmland south-west of Wagga Wagga. Part of the training area is preserved bushland that also serves conservation purposes.
