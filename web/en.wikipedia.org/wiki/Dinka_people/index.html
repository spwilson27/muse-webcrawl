Dinka people

The Dinka people (Dinka: Jiɛ̈ɛ̈ŋ) are a Nilotic ethnic group native to South Sudan, where they are the largest ethnic group, numbering several million. They live chiefly along the White Nile and in the vast swamp region of the Sudd, in the modern states of Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, and Jonglei.

Dinka society is traditionally organized around agro-pastoralism: cattle are the centre of economic and spiritual life, providing milk, status, bridewealth, and sacrifice, while millet and sorghum are cultivated in permanent settlements. Social organization is by clan and age-set, without a centralized kingship; spiritual leaders known as "spear-masters" mediate with the divine, centred on the high god Nhialic.

The Dinka language belongs to the Western Nilotic branch of the Nilo-Saharan family and is written in a Latin-based alphabet with distinctive vowel-length and tone markings. During Sudan's second civil war (1983–2005) the Dinka suffered heavily; the displacement of the "Lost Boys of Sudan" drew international attention. Prominent Dinka include South Sudan's first president Salva Kiir, the late SPLM leader John Garang, and basketball player Manute Bol, among the tallest players in NBA history.
