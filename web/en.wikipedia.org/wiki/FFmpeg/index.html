FFmpeg is a free and open-source software project consisting of a suite of libraries and programs for handling video, audio, and other multimedia files and streams. At its core is the command-line ffmpeg tool itself, designed for processing video and audio files. It is widely used for format transcoding, basic editing such as trimming and concatenation, video scaling and post-production effects.

History

The project was started in 2000 by Fabrice Bellard; the name is derived from the MPEG video standards group together with "FF" for "fast forward". Michael Niedermayer led the project for many years after Bellard's departure. In 2011, disagreement among developers produced a fork, Libav, which was later abandoned as development consolidated again around FFmpeg. The libraries of FFmpeg power a large share of the world's multimedia software, including VLC, mpv, HandBrake, Blender, and the media pipelines of major web browsers and streaming services.

Components

  • ffmpeg — a command-line tool to decode, encode, transcode, mux, demux, stream, filter and play media.
  • ffplay — a simple media player based on SDL and the FFmpeg libraries.
  • ffprobe — a command-line tool to display media information.
  • libavcodec — a library containing decoders and encoders for audio and video codecs.
  • libavformat — a library containing demuxers and muxers for multimedia container formats.
  • libavfilter, libswscale, libswresample, libavutil — filtering, scaling, resampling and utility libraries.

Supported formats

FFmpeg supports hundreds of codecs and container formats, including H.264, H.265/HEVC, AV1, VP9, MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 video; AAC, MP3, Opus, Vorbis and FLAC audio; and containers such as MP4, Matroska, WebM, AVI and MPEG transport streams. Hardware acceleration is available through platform APIs on supported systems.
