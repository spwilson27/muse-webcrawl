Steppe polecat

The steppe polecat (Mustela eversmanii), also known as the white ferret, masked polecat, or simply polecat in parts of its range, is a species of mustelid native to Central and Eastern Europe and Central Asia. It is listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List because of its wide distribution, presumed large population, and tolerance of a degree of habitat modification.

Description

The steppe polecat is a medium-sized mustelid, generally larger than the European polecat (Mustela putorius), to which it is closely related. Males weigh between roughly 1 and 2 kg, while females are smaller. The body is elongated with short legs, and the head bears a distinctive dark mask across the eyes. The pelage is pale yellowish to sandy above, with a dark brown to blackish tail tip, legs, and facial markings, giving the animal a striking two-toned appearance. The underfur is pale, and guard hairs have dark tips, producing a grizzled effect.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges across the steppes and semi-deserts of Eurasia, from Central Europe (including Austria, Hungary, and Romania) eastward through Ukraine, southern Russia, Kazakhstan, and Mongolia to northern China. As its name suggests, it inhabits open grassland, steppe, and semi-arid environments, avoiding dense forest and deep snow. It frequently occupies the burrow systems of ground squirrels and other rodents on which it preys.

Behaviour and ecology

The steppe polecat is primarily nocturnal and solitary. It is a specialist predator of rodents, especially ground squirrels (sousliks), hamsters, pikas, and voles, which it pursues into their burrows. It also takes birds, reptiles, and insects opportunistically. The animal is known to cache surplus prey in its burrow. Mating occurs in early spring, and after a gestation of about 40 days the female gives birth to a litter of typically four to six kits, though litters of up to 18 have been recorded. Delayed implantation does not occur in this species, unlike in some other mustelids.

Relationship with the domestic ferret

The steppe polecat has been implicated, along with the European polecat, in the ancestry of the domestic ferret (Mustela putorius furo). Genetic studies suggest the domestic ferret was domesticated primarily from the European polecat, though hybridisation with steppe polecats occurs where their ranges overlap and feral ferrets are present.

Conservation

Although widespread and still common in parts of its range, the steppe polecat has declined in Europe, largely because of declines in ground squirrel populations, poisoning campaigns against rodents, road mortality, and habitat conversion to intensive agriculture. It is protected in several countries, and its European populations are of conservation concern even though the species as a whole is classed as Least Concern globally.
