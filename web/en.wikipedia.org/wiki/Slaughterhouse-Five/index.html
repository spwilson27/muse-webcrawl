Slaughterhouse-Five, or, The Children's Crusade: A Duty-Dance with Death is a 1969 semi-autobiographical science fiction-infused anti-war novel by Kurt Vonnegut. It follows the life and experiences of Billy Pilgrim, from his early years, to his time as an American soldier and chaplain's assistant during World War II, to the postwar years. Interspersed are episodes in which Billy is abducted by aliens from the planet Tralfamadore and kept in a zoo.

Plot summary

Billy Pilgrim has become "unstuck in time": he drifts uncontrollably between episodes of his life. Captured at the Battle of the Bulge, he survives the firebombing of Dresden while held with other prisoners in an underground slaughterhouse — Slaughterhouse Five — an experience drawn from Vonnegut's own time as a prisoner of war. The Tralfamadorians, who see all moments of time simultaneously, teach Billy their fatalistic philosophy: "So it goes" is repeated after every mention of death. The novel's non-linear structure and metafictional first chapter frame the war narrative as the only way Vonnegut could write about Dresden.

Reception and censorship

The novel was nominated for Nebula and Hugo awards and is widely ranked among the greatest English-language novels of the 20th century. It has also been a frequent target of book challenges and bans in American schools and libraries because of its profanity, sexual references and irreverent tone; it featured prominently in the Supreme Court-adjacent debates symbolised by the 1982 Island Trees school board case.

Adaptations

George Roy Hill directed a well-regarded film adaptation in 1972. The novel has also been adapted for the stage, radio and as a graphic novel (2020, by Ryan North and Albert Monteys).
