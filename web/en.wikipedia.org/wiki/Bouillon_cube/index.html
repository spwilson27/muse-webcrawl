Bouillon cube

A bouillon cube (also called a stock cube) is dehydrated broth or stock formed into a small cube, typically about 13 mm wide. It is made by dehydrating vegetables, meat stock, a small portion of fat, salt, and seasonings and shaping them into a small cube. The cube is dissolved in hot water to produce a ready broth.

Bouillon cube
Type: Dehydrated stock
Main ingredients: Salt, fat, stock extract, seasonings
Major brands: Maggi, Knorr, Oxo

History

Portable soups existed since the 18th century, but the modern cube followed Justus von Liebig's meat extract of the 1840s. The Oxo cube was introduced in 1910 by Liebig's Extract of Meat Company, and Julius Maggi's company launched its cube in 1908. Mass production made the bouillon cube a staple of home cooking and military rations throughout the 20th century.

Composition and use

A typical cube contains mostly salt, with hydrogenated fat, flavour enhancers such as monosodium glutamate, dehydrated meat or vegetable extracts, sugar, and colouring. One cube generally seasons about 500 ml of water. In West African cooking, cubes — especially the Maggi cube — are used not only as stock but as a general-purpose seasoning crumbled directly into dishes.

Alternatives

Bouillon is also sold as powder, granules, paste, and liquid concentrate in sachets. Vegetarian and low-sodium versions are widely available.
