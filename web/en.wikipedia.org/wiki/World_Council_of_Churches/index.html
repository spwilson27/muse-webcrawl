The World Council of Churches (WCC) is a worldwide inter-church organisation founded in 1948 to work for Christian unity. Its members include nearly all of the world's Orthodox churches and many mainline Protestant denominations — Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed, Methodist, Baptist, and others — representing more than half a billion Christians. The Roman Catholic Church is not a member but cooperates through a joint working group and sends observers.

History

The WCC grew out of the early 20th-century ecumenical movement — the 1910 Edinburgh World Missionary Conference, and the Faith and Order and Life and Work movements. Plans made in the 1930s were delayed by the Second World War; the founding assembly met in Amsterdam in 1948 under the theme "Man's Disorder and God's Design", with Dutch theologian Willem Visser 't Hooft as first general secretary. Subsequent assemblies have met roughly every seven years, in cities including Evanston, New Delhi, Uppsala, Nairobi, Vancouver, Harare, Porto Alegre, Busan, and Karlsruhe (2022).

Work

The WCC's work includes theological convergence (its Faith and Order Commission produced the influential 1982 document Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry), interreligious dialogue, humanitarian aid through partners such as the ACT Alliance, advocacy on climate change and peace, and its controversial Programme to Combat Racism, which in the 1970s made grants to liberation movements in southern Africa. It promotes the Week of Prayer for Christian Unity each January.

Structure

The Assembly, meeting about every eight years, is the supreme governing body; between assemblies a 150-member Central Committee and Executive Committee direct the work, led by the general secretary from the Ecumenical Centre in Geneva.
