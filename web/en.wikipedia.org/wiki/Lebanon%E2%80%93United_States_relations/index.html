Lebanon–United States relations

Lebanon–United States relations are the bilateral relations between Lebanon and the United States. Formal diplomatic relations were established in 1944, following Lebanon's independence from France. The United States maintains an embassy in Awkar, near Beirut, and Lebanon maintains an embassy in Washington, D.C.

History

American ties to Lebanon date to the nineteenth century, when American missionaries founded schools including the institution that became the American University of Beirut (1866). The United States recognized Lebanon in 1944 and supported its independence. During the 1958 Lebanon crisis, President Eisenhower sent U.S. Marines to Beirut at the request of President Camille Chamoun — the first application of the Eisenhower Doctrine.

Civil war era

During the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), the United States participated in the Multinational Force in Lebanon in 1982–1984. In 1983, the U.S. embassy and the Marine barracks in Beirut were bombed, the barracks attack killing 241 American servicemen. The United States later supported the Taif Agreement that ended the war.

Recent relations

After the 2005 Cedar Revolution and Syria's withdrawal, Washington increased aid to Lebanon, and the United States has been a major provider of humanitarian assistance and support to the Lebanese Armed Forces, particularly after the Syrian refugee crisis and the 2020 Beirut port explosion.
