Camera matrix

The pinhole camera model: the camera matrix maps 3D world points to 2D image points.

In computer vision, the camera matrix (or camera projection matrix) is a 3×4 matrix that describes the mapping of a pinhole camera from 3D points in the world to 2D points in an image. If x is a homogeneous 3D point and y its homogeneous image projection, then y ≃ Px, where P is the camera matrix and ≃ denotes equality up to scale.

Decomposition

The camera matrix is conventionally factored as P = K [R | t], where:

  • K is the 3×3 calibration matrix of intrinsic parameters: focal lengths in pixel units (fx, fy), the principal point (cx, cy), and a skew parameter.
  • R is a 3×3 rotation matrix giving the camera's orientation.
  • t is a translation vector giving the camera's position; together R and t are the extrinsic parameters.

Properties

The camera matrix has 11 degrees of freedom. Its right null space is the camera centre — the 3D point through which all projection rays pass, which projects to no finite image point. A camera matrix is called finite if its left 3×3 submatrix is non-singular. Special forms arise for affine cameras, whose last row has the form (0, 0, 0, 1) and which model weak-perspective projection.

Estimation

Given correspondences between known 3D points and their images, the camera matrix can be estimated by the direct linear transformation (DLT) algorithm, followed by non-linear refinement that minimises reprojection error. This procedure, called camera resectioning, is a basic step in photogrammetry, augmented reality and structure-from-motion pipelines.
