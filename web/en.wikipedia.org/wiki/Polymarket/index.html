Polymarket

Polymarket is a decentralized prediction market platform that allows users to speculate on the outcome of future events by trading shares in event contracts. Founded in 2020 by Shayne Coplan, the platform is built on the Polygon blockchain and uses the USDC stablecoin for transactions. It became widely known during the 2024 United States presidential election, when its markets drew billions of dollars in trading volume and were frequently cited by news media as an alternative gauge of electoral sentiment.

History

Polymarket was founded in 2020 by Shayne Coplan, a New York-based entrepreneur who had previously worked on cryptocurrency projects. The platform launched publicly in mid-2020, initially attracting attention with markets on COVID-19 outcomes and the 2020 US presidential election. In 2021, the company raised funding from investors including Founders Fund; a subsequent round in 2024, led by Founders Fund with participation from Vitalik Buterin among others, brought total funding to roughly $200 million.

In January 2022, the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) charged Polymarket with offering off-exchange event-based binary options and failing to register as a designated contract market or swap execution facility. The company paid a $1.4 million civil penalty and agreed to block United States users from trading on the platform, although US users could still view market prices.

How it works

Polymarket lists binary event markets, typically phrased as yes-or-no questions such as "Will candidate X win the election?" Shares trade between $0.00 and $1.00, with the price interpreted as the market's implied probability of the outcome. Shares in the winning outcome pay out $1.00 each at resolution, while losing shares become worthless. Trading is peer-to-peer through an automated order book, and markets are collateralized in USDC. Polymarket does not charge trading fees on most markets, instead profiting through other means.

2024 US presidential election

During the 2024 election cycle, Polymarket's presidential market accumulated more than $3 billion in volume, making it by far the largest prediction market in the world. Its odds were displayed on television broadcasts and financial terminals and were cited by major outlets including Bloomberg, CNN, and The Wall Street Journal. The market consistently favored Donald Trump over Kamala Harris in the final weeks; a single French trader, widely reported under the pseudonym "Théo," placed bets exceeding $30 million on Trump and reportedly profited by roughly $48 million after Trump's victory. The accuracy of Polymarket's odds relative to traditional polling prompted renewed debate about the informational value of prediction markets.

Regulation and controversies

Despite geoblocking US users following the 2022 CFTC settlement, reporting suggested some Americans accessed the platform through VPNs. In November 2024, shortly after the election, FBI agents searched Coplan's Manhattan home and seized electronic devices as part of an investigation whose precise focus was not publicly confirmed. Critics have raised concerns about market manipulation by large traders ("whales"), the potential for insider trading on news-sensitive markets, and the use of prediction markets for events such as military conflicts and celebrity trials. Supporters argue that markets aggregate dispersed information more efficiently than polls or punditry.
