Hernán Cortés

Hernán Cortés de Monroy y Pizarro Altamirano, 1st Marquis of the Valley of Oaxaca (1485 – December 2, 1547) was a Spanish conquistador who led an expedition that caused the fall of the Aztec Empire and brought large portions of what is now mainland Mexico under the rule of the King of Castile in the early 16th century. Cortés was part of the generation of Spanish explorers and conquistadors who began the first phase of the Spanish colonisation of the Americas.

Early life

Born in Medellín, Extremadura, Cortés studied briefly at the University of Salamanca before sailing for Hispaniola in 1504. He took part in the conquest of Cuba (1511) under Diego Velázquez, whose secretary he became, and received an encomienda and the office of mayor of Santiago de Cuba.

Conquest of Mexico

In 1519, defying Velázquez's order recalling him, Cortés sailed for the Mexican mainland with about 500 men, 11 ships and 16 horses. He founded Veracruz, famously scuttled his ships to prevent retreat, and allied with the Tlaxcalans and other peoples hostile to Aztec rule. Entering Tenochtitlan in November 1519, he took the emperor Moctezuma II captive. After a disastrous retreat during the Noche Triste (June 1520) and a smallpox epidemic that devastated the indigenous population, Cortés besieged and destroyed Tenochtitlan in August 1521, building Mexico City on its ruins.

Later years

Cortés governed New Spain until rivals and royal officials curtailed his power; he was made Marquis of the Valley of Oaxaca but never viceroy. He explored Honduras and the Pacific coast of Mexico, returned to Spain in 1540, and died near Seville in 1547, embittered and largely forgotten at court. His reputation remains deeply contested: a brilliant and ruthless commander to some, the destroyer of a civilisation to others.
