Abū al-Qāsim Aḥmad ibn al-Mustanṣir (1074–1101), better known by his regnal name al-Mustaʿlī Billāh, was the ninth Fatimid caliph, reigning in Egypt from 1094 to 1101, and the 19th imam of Musta'li Isma'ilism.

Succession dispute

On the death of his father, al-Mustansir Billah, in 1094, the vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah installed the younger son al-Musta'li as caliph, passing over the elder son Nizar. Nizar's supporters rejected the succession, producing the great Nizari–Musta'li schism within Isma'ili Shi'ism that persists to this day.

Reign

Real power lay with the vizier al-Afdal. Al-Musta'li's reign saw the arrival of the First Crusade: Jerusalem fell to the Crusaders in 1099 after being taken from Fatimid control. He died in 1101 and was succeeded by his son al-Amir.
