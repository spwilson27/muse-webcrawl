Heterochromia

Heterochromia is a variation in pigmentation in which an individual has differently coloured irises (heterochromia iridum), or one iris with multiple colours (sectoral heterochromia), or a ring of contrasting colour around the pupil (central heterochromia). It may be inherited, or caused by genetic mosaicism, chimerism, disease or injury.

Classification

Complete heterochromia is a difference in colour between the two eyes. Sectoral (partial) heterochromia occurs when part of one iris differs in colour from the rest. Central heterochromia is an eye condition where the iris has two colours in concentric rings. Heterochromia is also common in some domestic animals, including cats, dogs and horses.

Causes

Congenital heterochromia is usually harmless and often inherited as an autosomal dominant trait, but it can be associated with syndromes such as Waardenburg syndrome and congenital Horner's syndrome. Acquired heterochromia may follow trauma, inflammation, tumours of the iris, or the use of certain prostaglandin-analogue eye drops for glaucoma, and warrants medical evaluation.
