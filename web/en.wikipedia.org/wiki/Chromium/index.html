Chromium is the chemical element with the symbol Cr and atomic number 24. It is a hard, steely-grey transition metal whose colourful compounds (Greek chroma, colour) gave it its name, and whose addition to steel creates stainless steel.

Chromium's salts span green, violet, orange and yellow; chrome pigments (chrome yellow) coloured school buses and paintings, while hexavalent chromium compounds are toxic and carcinogenic, making chrome plating and leather tanning regulated industries. Chromium(III) is a trace nutrient, and thin chrome plating gives the classic mirror shine on taps and car trim.

History

Louis Vauquelin discovered chromium in 1797 in the Siberian mineral crocoite (lead chromate) and isolated the metal the next year. Its oxide gives emeralds and rubies their colour — ruby is chromium-doped corundum.

Oxidation states

+3 and +6 dominate: Cr(III) forms green/violet complexes and Cr₂O₃ pigment; Cr(VI) (chromate, dichromate) is a strong oxidiser used in volumetric analysis and wood preservation.
