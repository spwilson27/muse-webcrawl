The Pickwick Papers

Author: Charles Dickens
Published: 1836–1837 (serial)
Genre: Comic novel

The Posthumous Papers of the Pickwick Club, commonly known as The Pickwick Papers, is the first novel by English author Charles Dickens, published in monthly installments from March 1836 to November 1837 under the pseudonym "Boz." It became a publishing phenomenon and made Dickens famous at the age of 24.

Plot

The novel follows the amiable, retired seminarian-turned-traveller Samuel Pickwick and his companions — the poetic Nathaniel Winkle, the amorous Augustus Snodgrass, and the elderly Tracy Tupman — as they tour the English countryside recording their adventures. Pickwick hires the cockney manservant Sam Weller, whose wit and loyalty became the book's most popular feature. The story culminates in the breach-of-promise lawsuit Bardell v. Pickwick, after which Pickwick, refusing to pay damages on principle, is imprisoned in the Fleet debtors' prison, an experience that darkens the novel's tone before a happy resolution.

Publication history

The project began as text to accompany sporting illustrations by Robert Seymour; after Seymour's death, Hablot Knight Browne ("Phiz") took over the plates. Sales grew from a few hundred to some 40,000 copies per number, and the success spawned Pickwick merchandise, songs, and stage adaptations.
