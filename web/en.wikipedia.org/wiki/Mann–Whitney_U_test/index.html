Mann–Whitney U test

The Mann–Whitney U test (also called the Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, or Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test) is a nonparametric statistical test of the null hypothesis that, for randomly selected values X and Y from two populations, the probability of X being greater than Y is equal to the probability of Y being greater than X. Unlike the t-test, it does not require the assumption of normal distributions.

Assumptions and interpretation

The test requires two independent samples measured on at least an ordinal scale. Under the additional assumption that the two distributions have the same shape, the test can be interpreted as a test of the equality of medians; otherwise it is best interpreted as a test of stochastic dominance.

Calculations

All observations from both samples are ranked together, with ties receiving average ranks. If R1 is the sum of ranks for sample 1 of size n1, then U1 = R1 − n1(n1 + 1)/2, and U2 = n1n2 − U1. The smaller of U1 and U2 is compared against tabulated critical values, or, for larger samples, a normal approximation with a correction for ties is used. Equivalently, U counts the number of pairwise comparisons in which an observation from one sample exceeds an observation from the other.

History

The statistic was proposed by Frank Wilcoxon in 1945 for equal sample sizes and extended to arbitrary sizes by Henry Mann and Donald Whitney in 1947. It is among the most widely used nonparametric tests in medicine, psychology, and the social sciences.
