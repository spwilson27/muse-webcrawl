Throughput

Throughput is the rate at which something is processed or transferred. In telecommunications and computing, network throughput is the rate of successful message delivery over a communication channel, usually measured in bits per second (bit/s). In other fields it describes the number of items passing through a system per unit time, such as passengers through an airport or units through a factory.

Networking

Throughput differs from bandwidth: bandwidth is the theoretical maximum rate of a link, whereas throughput is the actual achieved rate, reduced by protocol overhead, latency, retransmissions, and competing traffic. Goodput measures only the useful application-level data delivered.

Manufacturing and logistics

In operations management, throughput is the rate at which a system generates units of output; according to the theory of constraints, maximising the throughput of the bottleneck resource maximises the throughput of the whole system. Little's law relates throughput, inventory, and lead time in a stable system.
