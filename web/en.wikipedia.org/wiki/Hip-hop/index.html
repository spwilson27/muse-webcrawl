Hip-hop

Hip-hop
Stylistic originsFunk, disco, soul, reggae, dub, spoken word
Cultural origins1970s, the Bronx, New York City, US
ElementsMCing (rapping), DJing, breakdancing, graffiti

Hip-hop (also spelled hip hop) is a cultural movement and music genre that originated in African-American and Latino communities in the Bronx, New York City, in the 1970s. Its birth is conventionally dated to 11 August 1973, when DJ Kool Herc hosted a back-to-school party at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue and pioneered the technique of extending percussion "breaks" using two turntables.

Elements

Afrika Bambaataa is credited with identifying the core elements of hip-hop culture: MCing (rapping), DJing and turntablism, B-boying/B-girling (breakdance), and graffiti art; a fifth element, "knowledge," is sometimes added. Hip-hop music itself centres on rhythmic, rhyming speech (rap) delivered over beats built from samples, drum machines and beatboxing.

History

Early commercial successes included the Sugarhill Gang's "Rapper's Delight" (1979). In the 1980s the genre diversified with artists such as Run-DMC, LL Cool J and the Beastie Boys, while "golden age" acts like Public Enemy, De La Soul and A Tribe Called Quest expanded its lyrical and sonic range. Gangsta rap (N.W.A, Tupac Shakur, the Notorious B.I.G.) dominated the 1990s amid an East Coast–West Coast rivalry. In the 2000s and 2010s hip-hop became the most commercially successful popular music in the United States, with stars including Jay-Z, Eminem, Kanye West, Kendrick Lamar, Drake and Nicki Minaj, and subgenres such as trap and drill shaping global popular culture.
