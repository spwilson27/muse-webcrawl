Maria Theresa thaler

The Maria Theresa thaler (MTT) is a silver bullion coin that has been struck continuously since the death of Empress Maria Theresa of Austria in 1780. One of the most widely used trade coins in history, it circulated for over two centuries in Arabia, Africa, and India, and was still produced by the Austrian Mint in the twenty-first century. All restrikes bear the date 1780 regardless of the year of manufacture.

Description

The coin is 41 mm in diameter and 2.5 mm thick, weighs 28.0668 grams, and contains 23.389 grams (0.752 troy ounces) of fine silver at a fineness of 833 thousandths. The obverse shows a bust of Maria Theresa in widow's veil, with the Latin legend M. THERESIA. D. G. R. IMP. HU. BO. REG. ("Maria Theresa, by the Grace of God, Empress of the Romans, Queen of Hungary and Bohemia"). The reverse bears the imperial double-headed eagle with the arms of Austria and Burgundy, and the motto AVST. DVX. BVRG. CO. TYR. 1780. X.

History and circulation

The thaler was first struck in 1741 during Maria Theresa's reign. After her death in 1780 it was restruck without interruption, always with the 1780 date, because its users demanded exactly the same coin. It became the de facto currency of large parts of the Red Sea region, Arabia (where it was known as the riyal), the Horn of Africa, Ethiopia, and British colonies in East Africa. In Ethiopia it remained in use into the twentieth century, and Maria Theresa thalers were legal tender in several countries well after the Second World War.

Coins were minted in Vienna, Hall, Günzburg, Kremnica, Milan, Venice, and also in London, Paris, Brussels, Bombay, and Rome under various agreements. It is estimated that several hundred million Maria Theresa thalers were struck between 1780 and 2000.

Modern issues

The Austrian Mint continues to strike the Maria Theresa thaler as a bullion and collector coin, using the same design. Counterfeits and local imitations are common in older circulation pieces.
