Bathurst 1000

The Bathurst 1000 is an annual 1,000-kilometre (621 mi) touring car race held at the Mount Panorama Circuit in Bathurst, New South Wales, Australia. First run in 1960 at Phillip Island as a 500-mile event, it moved to Bathurst in 1963 and is widely regarded as the pinnacle of Australian motorsport — nicknamed "The Great Race".

The circuit

Mount Panorama is a public-road circuit of 6.213 km with a dramatic elevation change: cars climb from Pit Straight up through The Cutting to Skyline and plunge down through the Esses and Conrod Straight, reaching speeds near 300 km/h. Two drivers share each car, and the race typically lasts six to seven hours.

History and culture

The race began as a test of showroom production cars and fuelled the Ford–Holden rivalry that defined Australian touring car racing for decades. Peter Brock, "The King of the Mountain", won nine times, and the winners now receive the Peter Brock Trophy, introduced after his death in 2006. Memorable moments include Dick Johnson's collision with a rock in 1980, Jim Richards's rain-soaked 1992 victory, and Chaz Mostert and Paul Morris's last-to-first win in 2014. The event anchors the Bathurst enduro season of the Supercars Championship.
