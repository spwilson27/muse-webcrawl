Diabetic gastroparesis

Diabetic gastroparesis is a form of gastroparesis — delayed emptying of the stomach in the absence of mechanical obstruction — that occurs as a complication of diabetes mellitus. It results from damage to the vagus nerve and the stomach's pacemaker cells caused by long-standing high blood glucose.

Symptoms

Symptoms include early satiety, nausea, vomiting of undigested food, bloating, upper abdominal pain and unpredictable fluctuations in blood glucose, since food absorption no longer matches insulin dosing.

Diagnosis

The standard test is gastric emptying scintigraphy, in which the transit of a radiolabelled meal is measured over several hours. Other methods include wireless motility capsules and breath tests.

Management

Treatment focuses on glycaemic control, dietary modification (small, low-fat, low-fibre meals), antiemetic and prokinetic medications such as metoclopramide or erythromycin, and in severe cases gastric electrical stimulation or enteral feeding.
