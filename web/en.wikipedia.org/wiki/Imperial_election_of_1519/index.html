Imperial election of 1519

The imperial election of 1519 was an imperial election held to select the emperor of the Holy Roman Empire. It took place in Frankfurt on 28 June 1519, following the death of Emperor Maximilian I on 12 January 1519, and resulted in the election of Charles of Habsburg—King Charles I of Spain—as Emperor Charles V. It was one of the most expensive and hard-fought elections in the Empire's history, shaped by an unprecedented scale of bribery financed by international banking houses.

Background

The seven prince-electors summoned to Frankfurt were the archbishops of Mainz, Cologne, and Trier; the King of Bohemia; the Count Palatine of the Rhine; the Duke of Saxony; and the Margrave of Brandenburg. The principal candidates were Charles of Spain, the late emperor's grandson, and King Francis I of France; Henry VIII of England was briefly floated, and Elector Frederick III of Saxony emerged as a compromise candidate favoured by some electors and by Pope Leo X, who feared both a Habsburg encirclement of Italy and a French one.

The campaign

Both camps poured money into Frankfurt. Charles's campaign, managed by his aunt Margaret of Austria and his advisers, spent roughly 850,000 florins, much of it borrowed from the Fugger and Welser banking families of Augsburg—Jakob Fugger later famously reminded Charles of the debt in a letter of 1523. The French spent lavishly too, but relied more on promises; the Habsburgs paid in cash and in pledges of pensions, and could back their offer with troops massing near the city under the Swabian League.

Result

On 28 June 1519 the electors voted unanimously for Charles, who was crowned King of the Romans at Aachen on 23 October 1520 and as emperor by the Pope at Bologna in 1530. In return he signed an electoral capitulation (Wahlkapitulation)—the first of its kind—promising, among other things, to employ Germans in imperial administration and not to bring foreign troops into the Empire. The election set up two generations of Habsburg–Valois rivalry for dominance in Italy and Europe.
