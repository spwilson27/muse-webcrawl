Fidel Alejandro Castro Ruz (13 August 1926 – 25 November 2016) was a Cuban revolutionary and politician who led Cuba from 1959 to 2008, first as prime minister and then as president. Ideologically a Marxist–Leninist and Cuban nationalist, he also served as first secretary of the Communist Party of Cuba from 1965 until 2011, converting Cuba into a one-party communist state and implementing socialist reforms across society.

Revolution

Born in Birán, the son of a wealthy Spanish farmer, Castro studied law at the University of Havana and became a critic of the government of Fulgencio Batista. His 1953 assault on the Moncada Barracks failed and he was imprisoned, but after release he founded the 26th of July Movement. Landing in Cuba aboard the yacht Granma in December 1956, he waged a guerrilla campaign from the Sierra Maestra with his brother Raúl Castro and Ernesto "Che" Guevara. Batista fled on 1 January 1959 and Castro seized power.

Rule

Castro's government nationalised industry and businesses, implemented land reform, and greatly expanded healthcare and education. His alignment with the Soviet Union led to the failed US-backed Bay of Pigs invasion in 1961 and the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962. Cuba supported revolutionary movements abroad, notably in Angola. After the Soviet collapse Cuba entered the severe economic hardship of the "Special Period".

Later life and legacy

Ill health forced Castro to transfer power to Raúl in 2006, and he formally resigned the presidency in 2008. He died in Havana on 25 November 2016. Supporters praise his advances in literacy, healthcare and anti-imperialism; critics condemn human rights abuses, suppression of dissent and Cuba's economic record under his leadership.
