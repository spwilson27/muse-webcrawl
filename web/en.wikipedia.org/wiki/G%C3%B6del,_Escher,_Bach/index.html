Gödel, Escher, Bach

Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid (1979), often abbreviated GEB, is a book by Douglas Hofstadter exploring how meaning and consciousness can arise from self-referential formal systems, through the intertwined work of logician Kurt Gödel, artist M. C. Escher and composer Johann Sebastian Bach.

Gödel, Escher, Bach
Author: Douglas Hofstadter
Published: 1979
Award: Pulitzer Prize (1980)

Content

Chapters alternate between dialogues between Achilles and the Tortoise (in homage to Lewis Carroll) and essays on Gödel's incompleteness theorems, recursion, formal systems, artificial intelligence, genetics and cognition.

Reception

The book won the Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction and the National Book Award for science in 1980, and has remained influential in discussions of mind and computation. Hofstadter later wrote a sequel, I Am a Strange Loop (2007).
