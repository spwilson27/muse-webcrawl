The Australian Labor Party (ALP), commonly known as Labor (spelled without a "u"), is a centre-left political party in Australia and one of the two major parties in Australian politics, along with the Liberal Party and its Coalition partners. It is the oldest political party in Australia, tracing its origins to the labour movement and trade unions of the 1890s.

History

Labor emerged from the defeated strikes of the early 1890s, when unions turned to parliamentary representation; the first Labor government in the world, under Anderson Dawson in Queensland (1899), lasted a week, but in 1904 Chris Watson formed the first national Labor government. The party has since governed federally under leaders including Andrew Fisher, John Curtin, Ben Chifley, Gough Whitlam, Bob Hawke, Paul Keating, Kevin Rudd, Julia Gillard and Anthony Albanese.

Platform and organisation

Labor's historic objectives, restated in modernised form, are the democratic socialisation of industry in a manner compatible with social democracy: a mixed economy, a strong welfare state, universal healthcare (Medicare), public education, and workers' rights. The party is organised into state and territory branches and organised factions, and affiliated trade unions hold significant voting weight at party conferences. Internally it operates through a national executive, a federal parliamentary caucus that elects the leader, and a national conference that sets the party platform.
