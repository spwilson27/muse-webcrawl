Madhava of Sangamagrama
Bornc. 1340, Sangamagrama (Irinjalakuda), Kerala, India
Diedc. 1425
Known forInfinite series for π and trigonometric functions; founding the Kerala school

Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340 – c. 1425) was an Indian mathematician and astronomer from the town of Sangamagrama (modern Irinjalakuda) in present-day Kerala. He is regarded as the founder of the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, and is recognised as one of the pioneers of mathematical analysis: he developed infinite series expansions for π and for the trigonometric functions sine, cosine and arctangent, more than two centuries before similar work in Europe.

Contributions

Madhava's original works are largely lost, but his results survive through the writings of his successors, notably Nilakantha Somayaji and Jyeshthadeva's Yuktibhasa. He derived the series now written as π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + ..., known in Europe as the Leibniz series, together with sophisticated correction terms that greatly accelerated its convergence and allowed π to be computed to 11 correct decimal places. He also gave power series for sine and cosine equivalent to the Taylor series, and accurate tables of sines.

Kerala school

The lineage of teacher–student transmission that Madhava founded continued in Kerala for nearly two centuries, producing advances in infinite series, calculus-like techniques and astronomy, documented in works such as the Tantrasamgraha and the Yuktibhasa.

Legacy

Scholarly attention to Madhava's work grew in the 20th century through the researches of historians of Indian mathematics; he is now widely credited as a forerunner of the calculus tradition and of the theory of infinite series.
