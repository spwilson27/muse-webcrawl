Hodonín

Hodonín (German: Göding) is a town in the South Moravian Region of the Czech Republic, situated on the Morava River near the border with Slovakia. It has a population of roughly 24,000 and is the administrative seat of Hodonín District. The town is best known as the birthplace of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, the founder and first president of Czechoslovakia.

History

A Slavic fortified settlement existed at the site in the Great Moravian period, and Hodonín castle is first mentioned in the eleventh century. The town developed as a border fortress between Bohemia and Hungary and was repeatedly damaged in wars, including the Hussite Wars and the Thirty Years' War. In the nineteenth century it industrialised, with sugar refining, brickworks and lignite mining nearby. Masaryk was born here in 1850; a museum in the town is dedicated to him. On 24 June 2021 a powerful tornado struck Hodonín and neighbouring villages in the Břeclav and Hodonín districts, killing several people and causing extensive damage — one of the strongest tornadoes recorded in the Czech lands.

Economy and culture

The surrounding Slovácko region is known for wine growing, folk traditions and vernacular architecture. Local industry has included power generation (the Hodonín power station), food processing and engineering. The town has a small zoo and lies on cycling routes along the Morava and the Baťa Canal.

Notable people

  • Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk (1850–1937), first President of Czechoslovakia
