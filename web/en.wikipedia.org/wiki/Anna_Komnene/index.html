Anna Komnene

Anna Komnene (Greek: Ἄννα Κομνηνή; 1 or 2 December 1083 – c. 1153) was a Byzantine Greek princess and historian. The eldest daughter of Emperor Alexios I Komnenos and Irene Doukaina, she wrote the Alexiad, an account of her father's reign that is a principal source for Byzantine political and military history of the late 11th and early 12th centuries, including the First Crusade.

Life

Born in the purple chamber of the imperial palace, Anna received an unusually broad education in Greek literature, history, philosophy, medicine and astronomy. Betrothed as a child to Constantine Doukas, she later married the general and historian Nikephoros Bryennios. Anna resented the accession of her younger brother John II in 1118 and was implicated in a plot to place her husband on the throne; after its failure she eventually withdrew to the convent of Kecharitomene, founded by her mother, where she spent her last decades writing and where she ran what amounted to a circle of scholars.

The Alexiad

Completed around 1148, the Alexiad narrates in fifteen books the reign of Alexios I (1081–1118): wars against the Normans under Robert Guiscard and Bohemond, the Seljuk Turks and the Pechenegs, and the passage of the First Crusade, whose Latin leaders Anna portrays with a mixture of admiration and deep suspicion. Though openly partial to her father, the work is prized for its detail and for its author's classical learning.
