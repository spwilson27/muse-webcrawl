Grandstand

A grandstand is a large, usually roofed, permanent structure for seating spectators at a stadium, racetrack, or other sporting venue. Grandstands typically provide tiered rows of seats, often with the best views of the field of play, and may include premium facilities, press boxes, and hospitality suites.

Design

Grandstands are usually built of concrete and steel and arranged so that each row is elevated above the one in front, giving spectators an unobstructed view. The main grandstand at a stadium often faces the finish line at a racetrack or the halfway line at a football ground.

History

The term dates to the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, when temporary stands were erected for prominent spectators at race meetings. Many historic grandstands are architecturally notable, such as those at Lord's Cricket Ground and the Melbourne Cricket Ground.

Temporary stands

Smaller, temporary or demountable grandstands — often called bleachers in North America — are used for parades, school sports, and one-off events.
