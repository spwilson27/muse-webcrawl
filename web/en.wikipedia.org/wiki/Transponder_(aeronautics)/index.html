A transponder (short for transmitter-responder) is an electronic device in an aircraft that produces a response when it receives a radio-frequency interrogation from a ground secondary surveillance radar. Transponders help air traffic control identify aircraft on radar displays and supply the traffic collision avoidance system (TCAS) with data.

Modes and codes

Mode A transponders reply with a four-digit identity code ("squawk") assigned by ATC, chosen from octal digits 0–7 giving 4,096 codes. Mode C adds pressure-altitude reporting from the aircraft's altimeter. Mode S transponders have a unique 24-bit ICAO address, reply selectively to reduce garble, and can exchange data — they form the basis of ADS-B Out broadcasts, which continuously transmit GPS position and are now mandated in much controlled airspace. Reserved codes include 7500 (unlawful interference), 7600 (radio failure) and 7700 (general emergency).

History

The transponder descends from Second World War identification friend or foe (IFF) systems. Civil secondary radar and Mode A/C spread through the post-war decades, and Mode S was developed to overcome the limitations of the earlier modes in dense traffic. Flight recorders and open ADS-B tracking networks such as ADS-B Exchange have made transponder data widely visible to the public.
