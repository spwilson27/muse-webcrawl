Bardsey Island

Bardsey Island (Ynys Enlli)
Location: Irish Sea, off the Llŷn Peninsula, Gwynedd, Wales
Area: about 1.8 km²
Highest point: Mynydd Enlli, 167 m
Known for: pilgrimage site; bird observatory; International Dark Sky Sanctuary

Bardsey Island (Welsh: Ynys Enlli), known as the legendary "Island of 20,000 saints," lies about 3 km off the Llŷn Peninsula in Gwynedd, Wales. It has been an important site of pilgrimage since the early Middle Ages and is now a National Nature Reserve, a Site of Special Scientific Interest, and home to the Bardsey Bird and Field Observatory.

History

Saint Cadfan founded a monastery on Bardsey in the sixth century, and the island became a major destination for pilgrims; three pilgrimages to Bardsey were said to equal one to Rome. The Augustinian Abbey of St Mary was established in the twelfth or thirteenth century, and the tower of its church survives. Tradition holds that 20,000 saints are buried on the island, and it was claimed as the burial place of King Arthur in some Welsh legends, as well as a candidate for the mystical Isle of Avalon. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries a small farming and fishing community lived there, complete with a self-declared "King of Bardsey" (Brenin Enlli), the last of whom, Love Pritchard, died in 1926.

Natural history

Bardsey is famous for its Manx shearwaters, with one of the world's largest breeding colonies—tens of thousands of pairs nest in burrows and return to the island at night. Grey seals haul out on its shores, and the island records many rare migrant birds at its observatory, founded in 1953. In 2023 Bardsey was designated an International Dark Sky Sanctuary, the first in Europe, reflecting its exceptionally dark, light-pollution-free skies.

Access

Boats to the island run from Porth Meudwy near Aberdaron, weather permitting. The island is owned and managed by the Bardsey Island Trust, and has a small resident population of wardens, farmers, and observatory staff.
