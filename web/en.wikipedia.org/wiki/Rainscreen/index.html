Rainscreen

A rainscreen is an exterior wall cladding system in which the outer layer — the rainscreen — stands off from the moisture-resistant surface of the main wall, creating a ventilated air gap. The system is designed on the principle that wind-driven rain is managed in two stages: the cladding deflects most water, and any moisture that penetrates drains or evaporates from the cavity.

How it works

A typical assembly, from outside to inside, consists of cladding panels, a ventilated and drained cavity, insulation, an air and weather-resistive barrier, and the structural wall. In pressure-equalized rainscreens, the cavity is compartmentalized so that its air pressure matches the exterior, reducing the pressure difference that drives rain through joints.

History and use

The principles were formalized in the 1960s from Norwegian research on open-jointed timber cladding and Canadian research into rain penetration. Rainscreen cladding in metal, terracotta, fibre cement, stone, and high-pressure laminate is now common on commercial and institutional buildings, and has drawn scrutiny in fire-safety reviews after cladding fires, prompting stricter regulation of materials and cavity barriers.
