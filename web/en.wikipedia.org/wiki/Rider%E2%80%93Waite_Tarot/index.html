Rider–Waite Tarot

DesignerArthur Edward Waite; illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith
PublisherRider & Co.

The Rider–Waite Tarot is a widely popular deck for tarot card reading. It is also known as the Waite–Smith, Rider–Waite–Smith, or Rider Tarot deck. It was published in 1909 by the Rider Company and was drawn by artist Pamela Colman Smith, under the direction of academic and mystic A. E. Waite, and was accompanied by Waite's text The Pictorial Key to the Tarot.

Innovations

The deck was notable for being one of the first to feature fully illustrated scenes on all 56 Minor Arcana cards, rather than simple arrangements of suit symbols; Smith drew on the imagery of the 15th-century Italian Sola Busca tarot for inspiration. Waite also switched the positions of the Strength and Justice cards (to VIII and XI respectively) to fit his astrological correspondences, a change adopted by most subsequent English-language decks.

Influence

The Rider–Waite deck became the best-selling and most influential tarot deck in the English-speaking world, and its imagery underlies the vast majority of modern tarot decks and instructional books. Pamela Colman Smith's role was long under-credited, leading many publishers and readers to prefer the name "Rider–Waite–Smith" or "Waite–Smith". The original artwork entered the public domain in many jurisdictions in the 2010s, spurring numerous facsimile editions.
