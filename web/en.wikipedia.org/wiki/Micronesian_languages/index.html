Micronesian languages

The Micronesian languages are a group of about twenty languages forming a branch of the Oceanic subgroup of the Austronesian language family, spoken across Micronesia in the western Pacific.

Members

The "Nuclear Micronesian" languages, which share a common ancestor (Proto-Micronesian), include Marshallese, Gilbertese (Kiribati), Kosraean, Chuukese, Pohnpeian, Carolinian, Mortlockese, Mokilese, Pingelapese, Ulithian, Woleaian and Sonsorolese, among others, spoken in the Marshall Islands, Kiribati, the Federated States of Micronesia and the Northern Mariana Islands. Nauruan is often classified as Micronesian but its position is debated. The languages of Palau (Palauan) and the Marianas (Chamorro) are Western Malayo-Polynesian rather than Micronesian proper, while Yapese is an Oceanic outlier of uncertain affiliation.

Features

Micronesian languages are notable for consonant gemination used grammatically, extensive reduplication, and possessive classifier systems distinguishing, for example, general, edible and drinkable possessions. Many distinguish inclusive and exclusive first-person plural pronouns and have numeral classifier systems. Colonial history layered Spanish, German, Japanese and English influences over the region; today English is the main language of government and secondary education in much of Micronesia, and several of the smaller languages are endangered.
