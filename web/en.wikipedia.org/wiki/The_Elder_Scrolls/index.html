The Elder Scrolls is a series of action role-playing video games developed primarily by Bethesda Game Studios and published by Bethesda Softworks. The series is known for its large open worlds, emphasis on player freedom, and rich in-world lore, and is set on the continent of Tamriel in the fictional world of Nirn. The main series has sold more than 60 million copies.

Main series

The Elder Scrolls: Arena (1994) introduced the whole of Tamriel as a procedurally-augmented arena; Daggerfall (1996) expanded scale dramatically with a vast region of High Rock and Hammerfell. Morrowind (2002), set on the island of Vvardenfell, marked the series' move to fully 3D, handcrafted worlds and console releases. Oblivion (2006), set in Cyrodiil during the Oblivion Crisis, was a landmark Xbox 360 title, and Skyrim (2011), set 200 years later among dragons and civil war, became one of the best-selling games in history, with over 60 million copies sold across its many re-releases.

Spin-offs and world

The massively multiplayer The Elder Scrolls Online (2014), developed by ZeniMax Online Studios, covers the Second Era. The universe's factions — the Empire, the Aldmeri Dominion, guilds of fighters and mages, and the shadowy Dark Brotherhood — and its Daedric Princes give the series a mythic depth sometimes compared with classic literary worlds such as those in Asimov's Robot novels. A sixth main entry was announced in 2018.
