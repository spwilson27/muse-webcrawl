Electional astrology

Electional astrology is a branch of astrology concerned with choosing ("electing") the most favourable moment to begin an undertaking — such as a wedding, a journey, a business venture or the founding of a building or city — in the belief that the astrological configuration at the start of an enterprise influences its outcome.

Principles

The electional astrologer examines the positions of the Sun, Moon and planets at candidate times, seeking configurations judged harmonious for the matter at hand. Particular attention is paid to the Moon's sign, phase and aspects, to avoiding Mercury retrograde for communications and contracts, and to strengthening the ruler of the ascendant and the planet signifying the enterprise. The moment chosen is sometimes called an "election", and the chart cast for it is treated analogously to a birth chart for the undertaking.

History

Electional astrology developed within Hellenistic astrology alongside horary astrology; the first-century astrologer Dorotheus of Sidon devoted the fifth book of his Carmen Astrologicum to elections. The practice flourished in medieval Islamic and European astrology and was a standard service of court astrologers, used for coronations, battles and foundations. It continues to be practised today, particularly in India, where the related discipline of muhurta selects auspicious times for ceremonies.
