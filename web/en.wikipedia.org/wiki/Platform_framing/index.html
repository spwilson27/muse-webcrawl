Platform framing

Platform framing, also called western framing or stick framing, is the dominant method of light wood-frame construction in North America. In this system, each floor is built as a platform on top of the floor below: wall studs are only one storey high, and the floor joists and subfloor of the next level rest on the top plates of the walls beneath, forming a working platform for the next storey.

Comparison with balloon framing

Platform framing largely replaced balloon framing, in which studs run continuously for two storeys, during the mid-20th century. Platform framing uses shorter, cheaper lumber, is easier to erect in stages, and provides a natural fire stop at each floor level, since the open wall cavities of balloon framing — which could act as chimneys in a fire — are interrupted at every platform.

Materials

Typical materials include dimension lumber (such as 2×4 and 2×6 studs), engineered wood joists, and plywood or oriented strand board sheathing, joined mostly with nails and metal connectors.
