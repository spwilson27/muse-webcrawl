The Mayor of the District of Columbia is the head of the executive branch of the government of the District of Columbia, the capital city of the United States. The mayor has the duty to enforce district laws, and the power to approve or veto bills passed by the Council of the District of Columbia. In addition, the mayor oversees all district services, public property, and police and fire protection; most executive power in the district is vested in the mayor.

History

Washington had an elected mayor as early as 1802, when Thomas Jefferson appointed Robert Brent as the first mayor and Congress provided for election by the city council, then by popular vote from 1812. Congress revoked the city's territorial government in 1874 and governed the district through appointed commissioners for a century. The District of Columbia Home Rule Act of 1973 restored an elected mayor and council; Walter Washington, previously the appointed mayor-commissioner, won the first election in 1974 and took office in 1975.

Notable mayors

Marion Barry served from 1979 to 1991 and again from 1995 to 1999, a tenure marked both by social programmes and by scandal and federal financial oversight. Anthony A. Williams (1999–2007) is credited with restoring the city's finances. Adrian Fenty (2007–2011) pursued school reform. Vincent C. Gray served 2011–2015. Muriel Bowser took office on 2 January 2015 and was re-elected in 2018 and 2022.

Powers

The mayor prepares the district budget (subject to congressional review), appoints department heads, and commands the DC National Guard's local functions under presidential authority. Because the district is not a state, Congress retains ultimate legislative authority over it.
