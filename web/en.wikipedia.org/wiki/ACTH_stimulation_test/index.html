The ACTH stimulation test is a medical test usually requested by endocrinologists to assess the functioning of the adrenal glands' stress response by measuring the adrenal response to adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH). A synthetic analogue of ACTH, cosyntropin (tetracosactide; brand name Synacthen), is administered and blood cortisol is measured before and after.

Procedure

In the standard high-dose test, 250 µg of cosyntropin is given intravenously or intramuscularly; serum cortisol is sampled at baseline and at 30 and 60 minutes. In a normal response, cortisol rises above a threshold (commonly about 18–20 µg/dL, assay-dependent). A low-dose (1 µg) version is used in some centres because it may be more sensitive to mild secondary adrenal insufficiency.

Interpretation

An inadequate cortisol rise indicates adrenal insufficiency. A blunted response occurs in primary adrenal failure (such as Addison's disease), where the glands themselves cannot respond, and in chronic secondary or tertiary insufficiency, where prolonged ACTH deficiency has caused adrenal atrophy. A normal result does not fully exclude recent-onset secondary insufficiency, and further dynamic testing may be required.

Uses

Besides diagnosing adrenal insufficiency, the test is used in the work-up of congenital adrenal hyperplasia (measuring 17-hydroxyprogesterone responses) and in evaluating patients tapering from long-term glucocorticoid therapy.
