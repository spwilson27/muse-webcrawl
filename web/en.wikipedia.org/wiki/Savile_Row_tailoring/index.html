Savile Row tailoring refers to the tradition of bespoke men's tailoring centred on Savile Row, a street in Mayfair, central London. The term "bespoke" is said to have originated there: cloth was said to "be spoken for" by a customer. Savile Row tailors are widely regarded as the standard-bearers of hand-made suiting, and the street has clothed royalty, heads of state, military officers, and film stars for more than two centuries.

History

Savile Row was built between 1731 and 1735 as part of the Burlington Estate, and was named after Lady Dorothy Savile, wife of the 3rd Earl of Burlington. Tailors began moving into the area in the late 18th century, initially serving military officers. By the 19th century the street had become synonymous with fine tailoring. Henry Poole & Co, founded in 1806, is often credited with creating the dinner jacket (tuxedo) in 1860 for the Prince of Wales, later Edward VII. Other famous houses include Huntsman (founded 1849), Gieves & Hawkes (1771), Anderson & Sheppard (1906), and Kilgour.

The bespoke process

A bespoke suit from Savile Row typically requires several fittings over a period of weeks or months. The process includes:

  • An initial consultation and selection of cloth, often from thousands of samples
  • Individual paper pattern drafting by a cutter
  • A "baste" fitting, where the garment is loosely stitched together
  • One or more forward fittings to refine fit and shape
  • Hand finishing, including hand-rolled lapels, hand-sewn buttonholes, and a floating canvas chest piece

A true bespoke garment may involve more than 50 hours of hand work. The hallmark Savile Row silhouette features a structured shoulder, suppressed waist, and high armhole for ease of movement.

Savile Row Bespoke Association

In 2004, the Savile Row Bespoke Association was founded to protect and promote the street's tailoring standards. Members must meet strict criteria, including a minimum number of hours of hand work per garment and completing the bulk of production on or near the premises. The association also runs an apprenticeship scheme to train new cutters and tailors.

In popular culture

The street is referenced in the Beatles' Apple Corps headquarters at 3 Savile Row, where the band played its famous rooftop concert on 30 January 1969. The phrase "Savile Row suit" remains a byword for the highest standard of men's tailoring worldwide.
