Rorschach (comics)

Rorschach (real name Walter Joseph Kovacs) is a fictional antihero in the graphic novel Watchmen, created by Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons and published by DC Comics in 1986–87. A ruthless vigilante wearing a mask printed with shifting inkblots, he is the character who drives the investigation that opens the series.

Character

Kovacs, the abused son of a prostitute, became a crimefighter in 1965, first as a relatively conventional hero; after failing to save a kidnapped girl, Blair Roche, whom he found murdered in 1975, his psychology fractured and his methods became brutal and uncompromising. When costumed adventurers were outlawed by the Keene Act, Rorschach alone refused to retire. He sees the world in absolute black-and-white moral terms — a trait reflected in his mask, made of a fabric with heat- and pressure-sensitive fluids between layers of latex.

Role in Watchmen

Investigating the murder of the Comedian, Rorschach uncovers Adrian Veidt's plot to stage an alien attack on New York. At the climax he refuses to accept the moral compromise the revelation demands — "Never compromise. Not even in the face of Armageddon" — and is killed by Doctor Manhattan. His journal, sent to a newspaper before his death, threatens to expose the truth. The character has been widely acclaimed and influential, and was portrayed by Jackie Earle Haley in the 2009 film adaptation and (in a successor form) in later sequels such as the comic Rorschach (2020).
