Sagitta is a small but distinctive constellation in the northern sky. Its name is Latin for "the arrow", and it should not be confused with the much larger constellation Sagittarius, the archer. Sagitta is the third-smallest constellation by area. It was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Features

The constellation's brightest star, Gamma Sagittae, is an orange giant of magnitude about 3.5. The arrow shape is formed by a handful of fourth-magnitude stars lying within the Milky Way, just north of the bright star Altair in Aquila. Notable deep-sky objects include the globular cluster M71, once thought to be a dense open cluster, which lies between Gamma and Delta Sagittae. Sagitta also contains several interesting variable stars and the planetary nebula M27 (the Dumbbell Nebula) lies just across the border in neighbouring Vulpecula.

Mythology

Several myths attach to the Arrow. In Greek tradition it was variously identified as the arrow with which Apollo slew the Cyclopes, the arrow of Eros (Cupid), or the arrow Hercules used to kill the eagle Aquila that tormented Prometheus. Sagitta was sometimes depicted as having been shot by Sagittarius or by Hercules. In Babylonian astronomy, its stars were associated with "the Arrow", the forerunner of the Greek figure.

Location

Sagitta lies inside the Summer Triangle, between Aquila, Vulpecula, Hercules and Delphinus, and is best seen in the late summer and autumn skies of the northern hemisphere. Despite its small size, its compact arrow asterism makes it easy to pick out under dark skies.
