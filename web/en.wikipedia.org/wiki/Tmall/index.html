Tmall

Tmall (Chinese: 天猫; pinyin: Tiānmāo, "Sky Cat"), formerly known as Taobao Mall, is a Chinese-language business-to-consumer (B2C) online retail platform operated by Alibaba Group. It is the largest B2C retail platform in China and one of the most visited e-commerce websites in the world, hosting official flagship stores for tens of thousands of Chinese and international brands.

History

Alibaba launched the platform in April 2008 as Taobao Mall (Taobao Shangcheng), a complement to its consumer-to-consumer marketplace Taobao: while Taobao hosted individual sellers, the Mall offered verified brand owners and authorised distributors, addressing buyers' concerns about counterfeits. In November 2010 it received its own domain, and in January 2012 it was renamed Tmall, adopting a black cat as its mascot. Tmall Global, launched in 2014, allows overseas brands to sell cross-border into China without a Chinese business licence, fulfilling orders from bonded warehouses.

Business model

Merchants pay a refundable deposit, annual service fees, and commissions typically of 0.5–5 percent depending on category, and must meet service and authenticity standards enforced by Alibaba. Tmall is host to flagship stores of brands such as Apple, Nike, L'Oréal, and Unilever, and it integrates with Alibaba's Alipay payments and Cainiao logistics network. Together with Taobao, it organises the annual Singles' Day (Double 11) shopping festival, which Tmall's predecessor helped popularise from 2009; the event's gross merchandise volume across Alibaba's platforms reached hundreds of billions of yuan at its early-2020s peak. Tmall has also moved into "new retail", with supermarket and offline-store formats such as Tmall Supermarket and Freshippo integration.
