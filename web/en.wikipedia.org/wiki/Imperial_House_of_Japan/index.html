The Imperial House of Japan (皇室, Kōshitsu), also referred to as the Imperial Family or the Yamato Dynasty, comprises those members of the extended family of the reigning Emperor of Japan who undertake official and public duties. Under the present Constitution of Japan, the Emperor is "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the people". The Japanese imperial family is the oldest continuous hereditary monarchy in the world, traditionally tracing its origin to Emperor Jimmu in 660 BC.

History

According to the eighth-century chronicles Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, the imperial line descends from the sun goddess Amaterasu. Historically verifiable emperors begin around the sixth century AD. For much of Japanese history, real political power was exercised by regents (the Fujiwara clan), retired emperors, or military governments (shogunates), while the emperor retained religious and legitimating authority. The Meiji Restoration of 1868 restored direct imperial rule in name, and the 1889 constitution made the emperor sovereign and sacred. After the Second World War, Emperor Hirohito renounced the claim of divinity, and the 1947 constitution redefined the emperor as a symbol without political power.

Succession

The Imperial House Law of 1947 restricts succession to male offspring in the male line. Female members who marry commoners leave the Imperial Family. The shrinking size of the family — Emperor Naruhito, who acceded in 2019 after the abdication of his father Akihito, has one daughter — has prompted continuing debate in Japan about reforming the succession rules, including proposals to allow female emperors or to restore former branch families abolished in 1947.

Members and duties

Members of the Imperial Family perform ceremonial and diplomatic functions, attend state events, and support charitable and cultural activities. The family's affairs are administered by the Imperial Household Agency. Unlike most monarchies, the Japanese imperial house has no family name, and emperors are referred to after death by the name of their era.
