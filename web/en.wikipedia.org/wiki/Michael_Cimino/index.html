Michael Cimino (3 February 1939 – 2 July 2016) was an American film director, screenwriter and producer. He won the Academy Award for Best Director for The Deer Hunter (1978), and became a cautionary figure in Hollywood history after the commercial failure of Heaven's Gate (1980) effectively ended the era of director-driven "New Hollywood" filmmaking.

Career

Cimino studied architecture and painting at Yale before working in television commercials and co-writing Silent Running (1972) and the Dirty Harry sequel Magnum Force (1973). His directing debut was the Clint Eastwood heist film Thunderbolt and Lightfoot (1974). The Deer Hunter, a Vietnam War drama starring Robert De Niro, Christopher Walken and Meryl Streep, won five Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Director.

Given extraordinary freedom by United Artists, Cimino's western epic Heaven's Gate ballooned far over budget and was released to savage reviews and disastrous box office, contributing to the sale of United Artists. Although later films such as Year of the Dragon (1985), The Sicilian (1987) and Desperate Hours (1990) were admired by some critics, he never regained his former standing. A restored, longer cut of Heaven's Gate in 2012 prompted significant critical re-evaluation of the film.
