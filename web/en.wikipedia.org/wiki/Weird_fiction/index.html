Weird fiction is a subgenre of speculative fiction originating in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It is distinguished from conventional horror by its focus on an awe-filled dread of the truly alien or incomprehensible — entities and phenomena that exceed both scientific explanation and traditional supernaturalism. H. P. Lovecraft popularised the term, defining the weird tale as one whose atmosphere of "breathless, unexplainable dread of outer, unknown forces" dominates.

History

The genre grew out of Gothic fiction and the works of writers such as Edgar Allan Poe, Sheridan Le Fanu, Ambrose Bierce, Arthur Machen, Algernon Blackwood and Lord Dunsany. The American magazine Weird Tales (founded 1923) gave the mode a home and a name, publishing Lovecraft, Robert E. Howard, Clark Ashton Smith and others.

The New Weird

From the 1990s, authors including China Miéville revived and recast the tradition as the "New Weird", blending weird horror with fantasy and science fiction. Jeff VanderMeer's Annihilation (2014) is a prominent contemporary example. Critics debate the genre's boundaries, but its emphasis on cosmic indifference and the limits of human knowledge remains recognisable.
