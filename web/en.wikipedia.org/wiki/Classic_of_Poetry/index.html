Classic of Poetry

The Classic of Poetry, also Shijing or the Book of Songs, is the oldest existing collection of Chinese poetry, comprising 305 works dating from the 11th to 7th centuries BC. It is one of the Five Classics of Confucianism.

Shijing 詩經
Poems: 305
Date: c. 11th–7th century BC
Sections: Guofeng, Xiaoya, Daya, Song

Contents

The poems divide into Airs of the States (Guofeng, folk songs), Lesser and Greater Court Hymns (Xiaoya, Daya), and Eulogies (Song, temple hymns of Zhou, Lu and Shang). Themes range from love and courtship to agriculture, war, ritual and dynastic legend. The first poem, "Guan ju" (Ospreys), became one of the most famous in Chinese literature.

Transmission

Tradition credits Confucius with selecting the poems from a larger corpus. The received text derives from the Mao school; fragments of rival Lu, Qi and Han schools survive. Commentaries, notably the Mao prefaces and Zheng Xuan's notes, read many love songs as political allegories.

Influence

The Shijing shaped Chinese poetic diction, allusion and the fu and later traditions for two millennia. See Chinese poetry and Five Classics.
