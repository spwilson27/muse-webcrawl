The National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) is the agency of the South African government responsible for state prosecutions. Established under the Constitution of South Africa and the National Prosecuting Authority Act of 1998, it is headed by the National Director of Public Prosecutions (NDPP), who is appointed by the President.

Structure

The NPA comprises the National Director, Directors of Public Prosecutions in the provinces, and several specialised units. These have included the Directorate of Special Operations (the "Scorpions", disbanded in 2009), the Asset Forfeiture Unit, the Specialised Commercial Crimes Unit, and the Investigating Directorate established in 2019 to focus on serious corruption.

Role and independence

Section 179 of the Constitution vests the prosecuting authority in a single national structure, which must exercise its functions without fear, favour or prejudice. The NPA decides whether to institute criminal proceedings on behalf of the state and conducts those proceedings in the courts of South Africa. Its independence has been a recurring subject of political debate, particularly during the state capture era examined by the Zondo Commission.
