Handover of Hong Kong

The handover of Hong Kong from the United Kingdom to the People's Republic of China took place at midnight on 1 July 1997, ending 156 years of British rule. Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region of China under the principle of "one country, two systems", following the terms of the Sino-British Joint Declaration signed in 1984.

Background

Hong Kong Island was ceded in perpetuity by the Treaty of Nanking (1842) after the First Opium War, and Kowloon was ceded by the Convention of Peking (1860). The New Territories, however — the great majority of the colony's land area — were leased for 99 years under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory (1898), with the lease expiring in 1997. Because the ceded and leased areas were not viable separately, Britain and China negotiated the territory's future; the Joint Declaration of 19 December 1984 provided for the transfer of the whole of Hong Kong and guaranteed its capitalist system and way of life for 50 years, until 2047, under the Basic Law.

The ceremony

The transfer ceremony was held at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre on the night of 30 June – 1 July 1997, attended by the Prince of Wales (representing Queen Elizabeth II), Chinese President Jiang Zemin, British Prime Minister Tony Blair and Chinese Premier Li Peng. The last British governor, Chris Patten, departed aboard the royal yacht Britannia. Tung Chee-hwa became the first Chief Executive of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.
