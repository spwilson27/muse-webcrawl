Ralph Allen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ralph Allen (1693 – 29 June 1764) was a British postmaster, merchant and philanthropist who became one of the most prominent figures in Georgian Bath. He reformed the British postal system through a network of cross-posts, made a second fortune from the Bath stone quarries, and used his wealth to shape the architecture and charitable institutions of the city.

Postal reforms

Born in Cornwall, Allen went to work at the Bath post office as a young man and became postmaster at 19. From 1720 he contracted to run the "cross" and "bye" posts — routes that bypassed London — and by reorganising them he greatly increased postal revenue and efficiency, earning a large fortune and continuing the contract for the rest of his life.

Bath stone and building

Allen bought the stone quarries at Combe Down and, working with the architect John Wood the Elder, promoted the use of Bath stone throughout the city's Georgian building boom. He built the mansion of Prior Park outside Bath, landscaped with celebrated gardens, and the Sham Castle folly on the hill overlooking the city. He supplied stone for many of Bath's landmarks and for buildings across Britain.

Philanthropy and legacy

Allen was a notable benefactor, funding much of the Mineral Water Hospital in Bath, and was host to writers and artists including Alexander Pope and Henry Fielding, who partly modelled Squire Allworthy in Tom Jones on him. He served as mayor of Bath in 1742. On his death he left substantial charitable bequests.
