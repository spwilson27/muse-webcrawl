Daina (Latvia)

A daina is a traditional form of music or poetry in Latvia, consisting of very short folk songs, usually of one or two stanzas of four trochaic lines. Dainas are a central part of Latvian cultural identity and of the Song Festival tradition, and more than 1.2 million texts have been catalogued.

Content and form

Dainas address the cycle of human life — birth, childhood, courtship, marriage, work, old age and death — as well as mythology, nature and the seasons. Mythological dainas feature deities such as Dievs (God), Laima (fate), Saule (the sun) and Māra. The verse is unrhymed, built on trochaic or dactylic metre, and the songs are traditionally sung a cappella, sometimes with drone or instrumental accompaniment.

Collection

The folklorist Krišjānis Barons (1835–1923) collected and published the monumental Latvju dainas (1894–1915), organising hundreds of thousands of songs contributed by singers across Latvia. His famous Cabinet of Folk Songs (Dainu skapis) is inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register. Lithuanian dainos are a closely related tradition.
