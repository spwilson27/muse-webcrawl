Metapolitefsi
Country: Greece
Date: 1974
Key figure: Konstantinos Karamanlis
Outcome: Third Hellenic Republic

The Metapolitefsi (Greek: Μεταπολίτευση, "regime change") was the transition to democratic rule in Greece that followed the fall of the military junta (the "Regime of the Colonels") in 1974. It is generally dated from 24 July 1974, when the exiled former prime minister Konstantinos Karamanlis returned to Athens to head a national unity government.

Background

Greece had been ruled by a military dictatorship since the coup of 21 April 1967. The junta's position collapsed in July 1974 after it backed a coup against President Makarios in Cyprus, which triggered a Turkish invasion of the island. Unable to manage the resulting crisis or a possible war with Turkey, the armed forces handed power to civilians.

Transition

Karamanlis legalised the Communist Party of Greece for the first time since the civil war, held free elections in November 1974 — won by his New Democracy party — and organised a referendum in December in which Greeks voted to abolish the monarchy and establish a republic. A new constitution followed in 1975, founding the Third Hellenic Republic.

Legacy

Metapolitefsi also names the broader political era of the restored democracy. The transition is widely regarded as a model of peaceful democratisation and laid the groundwork for Greece's accession to the European Community in 1981. Officials of the junta were tried and imprisoned, and the armed forces were permanently removed from government.
