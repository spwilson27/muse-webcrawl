D-ration

The D-ration was a military ration developed by the United States Army during World War II, consisting of a specially formulated chocolate bar designed to serve as an emergency survival food. Unlike ordinary candy bars, the D-ration bar was deliberately made to be only mildly palatable — described in Army specifications as tasting "a little better than a boiled potato" — so that soldiers would not consume it casually and would save it for genuine emergencies.

Development

In 1937, Colonel Paul Logan of the Quartermaster Corps approached the Hershey Chocolate Corporation with a request for a survival ration bar. Logan's requirements were exacting: the bar had to weigh four ounces, provide high food energy, withstand high temperatures without melting, and taste poor enough to discourage casual eating. Hershey's chief chemist, Sam Hinkle, developed a blend of chocolate liquor, sugar, skim milk powder, cocoa butter, oat flour, and vanilla flavoring. The oat flour raised the melting point and gave the bar its notably dense, hard texture and unappealing flavor.

Production and use

The first production run of 90,000 bars was delivered in 1937. During World War II, millions of D-ration bars were produced and issued to American troops, typically packed three to a box as a day's emergency supply. The bars were famously difficult to eat — soldiers nicknamed them "Hitler's secret weapon" — and often had to be shaved into slivers with a knife or softened in hot water. A tropical variant, the Tropical Bar, was developed for the Pacific theater with an improved formula and better taste.

Legacy

The D-ration was eventually replaced by other survival rations, but it remains a well-known example of purpose-designed military food. The later K-ration and C-ration incorporated lessons learned from its design, and Hershey's wartime production earned the company Army-Navy "E" awards for excellence.
