Article 370 of the Constitution of India

Jammu and Kashmir, whose special status Article 370 defined

Article 370 of the Constitution of India granted special status to the state of Jammu and Kashmir, allowing it its own constitution and limiting the Indian Parliament's power to make laws for the state. Drafted as a temporary provision in 1949, it gave effect to the terms of the Instrument of Accession signed in 1947, under which the princely state acceded to India only in defence, foreign affairs and communications.

Provisions

Under Article 370, provisions of the Indian Constitution other than Article 1 and Article 370 itself applied to Jammu and Kashmir only with the concurrence or consultation of the state government. A related provision, Article 35A (inserted by a 1954 Presidential Order), empowered the state legislature to define "permanent residents" and reserve for them rights in property, employment and settlement.

Revocation in 2019

On 5 August 2019 the Government of India issued presidential orders that made the entire Indian Constitution applicable to Jammu and Kashmir, effectively rendering Article 370 inoperative, and Parliament passed the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, dividing the state into the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh from 31 October 2019. In December 2023 a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court of India unanimously upheld the abrogation, holding that Article 370 was a temporary provision and that Jammu and Kashmir retained no element of internal sovereignty after accession.
