Itelmen language

Native to: Russia (Kamchatka Peninsula)
Ethnicity: Itelmens
Speakers: Critically endangered; only a small number of elderly speakers
Family: Chukotko-Kamchatkan (Kamchatkan branch)

Itelmen (autonym: itənmən) is a language spoken by the Itelmen people on the western coast of the Kamchatka Peninsula in far-eastern Russia. It is the sole surviving member of the Kamchatkan branch of the Chukotko-Kamchatkan language family; its southern and northern sister languages died out earlier.

Status

Once spoken across southern Kamchatka, Itelmen declined sharply under Russian colonisation and Soviet-era language shift to Russian. Today only a handful of fluent first-language speakers remain, all elderly, and the language is classified as critically endangered. Documentation and revitalisation efforts include dictionaries, grammars and teaching materials produced with the remaining speakers.

Features

Itelmen is polysynthetic with complex consonant clusters, vowel harmony of a limited type, and a consonant inventory including ejectives and uvulars.
