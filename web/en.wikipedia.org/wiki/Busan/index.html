Busan (Korean: 부산; pronounced [pusan]), officially Busan Metropolitan City, is South Korea's second-most populous city after Seoul, with a population of roughly 3.4 million. Located on the southeastern tip of the Korean Peninsula, it is the country's largest port city and a major centre of shipping, logistics, finance, and tourism.

History

The area was known in early records as Geochilsan-guk and later Dongnae. During the Joseon dynasty, Busan became Korea's principal trading port with Japan, hosting a Japanese trading settlement (waegwan). In the late 19th century it was among the first Korean ports opened to foreign trade. During the Korean War (1950–53), Busan was one of only two cities never captured by North Korean forces; it served as the provisional capital of the Republic of Korea and the anchor of the Pusan Perimeter, swelling with refugees. The United Nations Memorial Cemetery in Korea is located in the city.

Geography and climate

Busan sits where the Nakdong River meets the Korea Strait, ringed by low mountains and indented bays. It has a humid subtropical climate, milder in winter than most of Korea because of the warm Tsushima current, with a monsoon season in midsummer and occasional typhoons in late summer. Its beaches — Haeundae, Gwangalli, and Songjeong — are among the most popular in South Korea.

Economy

The Port of Busan is the busiest container port in South Korea and consistently ranks among the ten busiest in the world, handling the great majority of the country's container traffic. The city is also home to the Busan International Film Festival, one of Asia's most significant film festivals, held each autumn since 1996, and to BEXCO, a major convention venue. Marine, financial, and film industries anchor the modern economy.

Districts and landmarks

Busan is divided into 15 districts (gu) and one county (Gijang-gun). Notable landmarks include the Gamcheon Culture Village, the colourful hillside neighbourhood famous for its painted houses; Jagalchi Market, Korea's largest seafood market; the Buddhist temple Beomeosa on Mount Geumjeong; Haedong Yonggungsa temple on the coast; the Diamond Bridge (Gwangandaegyo); and the 118-storey Lotte World Tower Busan under development in the old port district.

Transport

Busan is served by Gimhae International Airport, a two-line subway plus light rail, and KTX high-speed trains that reach Seoul in about two and a half hours. Ferries connect the city with Japanese ports including Fukuoka, Osaka, and Shimonoseki.
