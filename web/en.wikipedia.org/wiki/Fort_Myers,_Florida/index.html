Fort Myers is a city in and the county seat of Lee County, Florida, United States, on the Gulf coast of south-west Florida along the Caloosahatchee River. It is a major tourist destination and the principal city of the Cape Coral–Fort Myers metropolitan area.

History

The site was first established as Fort Harvie during the Seminole Wars and then in 1850 as Fort Myers, named for Colonel Abraham Myers, chief quartermaster of Florida. Abandoned after the war, the fort was reoccupied during the Civil War and was the scene of the Battle of Fort Myers in February 1865, the southernmost land battle of the war. The town was platted in 1866 and incorporated in 1885, after which cattle ranching, fishing and winter tourism drove growth.

Edison and Ford

In 1885 inventor Thomas Edison visited and built his winter estate, Seminole Lodge, along the Caloosahatchee; his friend Henry Ford later bought the neighbouring estate, The Mangoes. The Edison and Ford Winter Estates, with Edison's botanical gardens and laboratory, are now among the city's best-known attractions. Edison's influence is also remembered in the royal palms he planted along McGregor Boulevard, which gave the city its "City of Palms" identity.

Modern city

Fort Myers hosts spring training for the Boston Red Sox (at JetBlue Park) and the Minnesota Twins. In September 2022 Hurricane Ian devastated the surrounding coast, particularly Fort Myers Beach and Sanibel Island, causing catastrophic storm surge damage.
