Mammoth Lakes is a town in Mono County, California, located in the Sierra Nevada mountains. It is home to Mammoth Mountain Ski Area, one of the largest and most popular ski resorts in the United States, and serves as a gateway to the Mammoth Lakes Basin and the eastern Sierra.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Mono people. European settlers arrived in the 1870s during a brief gold rush, when the Mammoth Mining Company was formed in 1877; the mines were short-lived. The settlement grew slowly as a summer recreation destination before developer Dave McCoy founded the ski area in the 1950s, transforming the town into a year-round resort community. Mammoth Lakes was incorporated in 1984.

Geography and geology

Sitting at an elevation of about 2,402 metres (7,880 ft), the town lies within the Long Valley Caldera, a large volcanic depression formed by a massive eruption about 760,000 years ago. The region remains geologically active, with hot springs, the earthquake faults of the eastern Sierra, and nearby features such as Devils Postpile National Monument and the columns of Crowley Lake.

Recreation and economy

Tourism drives the local economy. In winter, Mammoth Mountain offers skiing and snowboarding on over 3,500 acres of terrain with an average snowfall among the highest in California. In summer, visitors come for fishing in the lakes basin, mountain biking, hiking and access to Yosemite National Park via Tioga Pass.
