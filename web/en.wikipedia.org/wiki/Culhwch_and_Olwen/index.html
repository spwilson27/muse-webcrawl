Culhwch and Olwen

Culhwch and Olwen
Welsh: Culhwch ac Olwen
Language: Middle Welsh
Date: c. 1100
Sources: White Book of Rhydderch, Red Book of Hergest

Culhwch and Olwen is a Welsh tale, the oldest Arthurian prose story and one of the tales later associated with the Mabinogion. It tells how Culhwch, with King Arthur's help, wins Olwen, daughter of the giant Ysbaddaden, by completing a series of impossible tasks.

Plot

Cursed by his stepmother to marry only Olwen, Culhwch seeks his cousin Arthur. Ysbaddaden demands some forty anoethau (impossible tasks), knowing he will die when his daughter marries. Arthur's warband — including Cai, Bedwyr, Gwalchmai and Menw — performs the tasks, culminating in the hunt for the great boar Twrch Trwyth across Wales, Ireland and Cornwall. Ysbaddaden is shaved, flayed and beheaded, and the marriage takes place.

Significance

The tale preserves early Welsh Arthurian tradition, place-name lore and a catalogue of heroes; its manuscript witnesses date to the 14th century, though the language suggests composition around 1100.
