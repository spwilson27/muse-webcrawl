Sallen–Key topology

Sallen–Key topology
Type: Active electronic filter topology
Designed by: R. P. Sallen & E. L. Key (MIT Lincoln Laboratory)
Published: 1955
Order: Second-order sections (cascadable)

The Sallen–Key topology is an electronic filter topology used to implement second-order active filters, described by R. P. Sallen and E. L. Key of MIT Lincoln Laboratory in 1955. It is a form of voltage-controlled voltage-source (VCVS) filter consisting of a non-inverting operational amplifier and a network of two resistors and two capacitors.

Configurations

By choosing which components are resistors and capacitors, the same basic circuit realises low-pass, high-pass and (with variants) band-pass responses. The popular unity-gain version with equal resistors and capacitors gives a Butterworth-like response set by the component ratio, while applying gain allows control of the quality factor Q.

Applications

Sallen–Key stages are widely used in audio equipment, anti-aliasing filters for data converters and instrumentation, valued for their simplicity, low component count and good performance at modest Q values.
