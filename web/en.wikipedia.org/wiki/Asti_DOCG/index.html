Asti (also known as Asti Spumante and in the United States as Asti wine) is a sparkling white Italian wine that is produced throughout southeastern Piedmont but is particularly associated with the town of Asti in the province of Asti. Since 1993 the wine has been classified as a Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita (DOCG) and as of 2014 is the largest producing wine DOCG in Italy.

Made from the Moscato Bianco grape, it is sweet and low in alcohol, and often served with dessert. Unlike Champagne, Asti is not made sparkling through the use of secondary fermentation in the bottle, but rather through a single tank fermentation utilizing the Charmat method. It retains its sweetness through a process of filtration prior to bottling.

History

Spumante production in the Asti region dates to the 19th century, when Carlo Gancia produced the first Italian sparkling wine in nearby Canelli from Moscato grapes in 1865 using the méthode champenoise. The single-tank (charmat) method that defines modern Asti was developed in the 20th century, allowing the wine's characteristic fresh, grapey aroma to be preserved.

Styles

Asti DOCG covers several styles including the classic fully sparkling spumante, the lightly sparkling frizzante known as Moscato d'Asti (a separate DOCG within the same area, more lightly sparkling and lower in alcohol), and more recently extra-dry and metodo classico versions.
