Dirge

Dirge
Type: Funeral song / lament
Context: Funerals, memorials
Related: Elegy, Requiem, Threnody

A dirge is a somber song or lament expressing mourning or grief, often performed at funerals or memorials. The word derives from Latin dirige, 'direct', from the antiphon 'Dirige, Domine' in the Office of the Dead.

Traditions

Funeral laments exist worldwide: ancient Greek threnodies, Irish keening, and New Orleans jazz funeral dirges played slowly on the way to the cemetery before turning to upbeat music afterward.

In literature and music

Shakespeare included dirges in plays such as Cymbeline ('Fear no more the heat o' the sun'). Composers have written instrumental dirges, and the term is loosely applied to any mournful elegiac piece. See Elegy and Requiem.
