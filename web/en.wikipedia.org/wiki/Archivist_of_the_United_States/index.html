The Archivist of the United States is the head of the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA), the agency responsible for preserving and providing public access to the records of the United States federal government. The Archivist is appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

History

The office was created with the National Archives Establishment in 1934; Robert Digges Wimberly Connor became the first Archivist. In 1985 NARA became an independent federal agency, strengthening the Archivist's statutory autonomy.

Duties

The Archivist oversees the National Archives, the presidential libraries, and the Federal Register. The office has a constitutional role in the ratification of amendments: under Article V, proposed amendments are submitted to the Archivist, who publishes them and issues a certificate when the required three-fourths of states have ratified. The Archivist also certifies the votes of the Electoral College.
