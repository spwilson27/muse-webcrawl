The International Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Trans and Intersex Association, commonly known as ILGA World, is an international organisation bringing together more than 1,900 LGBTI groups from over 160 countries and territories. It campaigns for lesbian, gay, bisexual, trans and intersex human rights and engages with the United Nations and other international institutions.

Delegates at an ILGA World conference, the organisation's biennial global assembly.

History

ILGA was founded in Coventry, England, in August 1978 as the International Gay Association (IGA) during a conference held alongside the annual meeting of the Campaign for Homosexual Equality. It became the International Lesbian and Gay Association in the 1980s, and later expanded its name to be explicitly inclusive of bisexual, trans, and intersex people.

The organisation lost its UN consultative status in 1994 amid controversy, and regained ECOSOC consultative status in 2011. Its secretariat is based in Geneva, and it operates through six regional branches: ILGA Africa, ILGA Asia, ILGA Europe, ILGA Latin America and the Caribbean (ILGALAC), ILGA North America and the Caribbean, and ILGA Oceania.

Work

ILGA World publishes the widely cited State-Sponsored Homophobia report and global legal mapping of sexual orientation and gender identity laws, convenes a biennial world conference, and supports member organisations in engaging UN mechanisms such as the Universal Periodic Review and treaty bodies.
