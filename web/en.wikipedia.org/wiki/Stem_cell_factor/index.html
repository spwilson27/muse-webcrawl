Stem cell factor

Stem cell factor (SCF), also known as KIT ligand, steel factor or mast cell growth factor, is a cytokine that binds to the c-KIT receptor (CD117), a receptor tyrosine kinase. It is encoded by the KITLG gene in humans.

Function

SCF plays an essential role in the survival, proliferation and differentiation of haematopoietic stem cells and progenitor cells. Together with other growth factors it supports the formation of blood cells, and it is also important for melanocytes (skin pigmentation), germ cells (fertility) and mast cells.

Clinical significance

Mutations that reduce SCF or c-KIT signalling cause piebaldism and defects in blood-cell production, pigmentation and fertility; activating mutations in c-KIT are found in gastrointestinal stromal tumours and some leukaemias. Recombinant SCF has been used experimentally to mobilise stem cells for transplantation.
