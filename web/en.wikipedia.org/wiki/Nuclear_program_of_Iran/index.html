Nuclear program of Iran

The nuclear program of Iran comprises the country's nuclear facilities, uranium enrichment activities and related research, and has been a major subject of international diplomacy since the early 2000s. Iran states that the programme is for peaceful purposes such as electricity generation and medical isotope production; several Western governments and the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) have expressed concern about possible military dimensions.

History

Iran's nuclear programme began in the 1950s under Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi with assistance from the United States under the Atoms for Peace programme, and the Tehran Research Reactor was supplied in 1967. Work on the Bushehr power plant was begun by German contractors in the 1970s, halted after the 1979 Islamic Revolution, and eventually completed with Russian assistance, with the first unit connected to the grid in 2011. In 2002, previously undeclared enrichment facilities at Natanz and a heavy-water plant at Arak were publicly revealed, triggering a prolonged confrontation with the IAEA and the UN Security Council, which imposed sanctions.

JCPOA and aftermath

In 2015 Iran and the P5+1 (the five permanent Security Council members plus Germany) concluded the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), under which Iran accepted limits on enrichment levels and stockpiles, redesign of the Arak reactor and enhanced inspections in return for sanctions relief. The United States withdrew from the agreement in 2018 and reimposed sanctions; Iran subsequently reduced its compliance, expanding enrichment up to 60 percent purity. Key facilities in the programme include the enrichment plants at Natanz and Fordow, the Isfahan conversion facility, the Bushehr power station and the Arak heavy-water reactor.
