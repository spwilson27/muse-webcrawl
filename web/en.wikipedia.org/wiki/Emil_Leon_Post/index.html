Emil Leon Post (1897–1954) was a Polish-American mathematician and logician, a founder of recursion (computability) theory. Born in Augustów to a Jewish family that emigrated to New York, he studied at City College and Columbia.

Contributions

Post's 1921 doctoral work proved completeness and consistency results for propositional logic of Principia Mathematica and introduced truth tables systematically (in parallel with Wittgenstein). In 1936 he independently described a universal computing device (the "Post machine"/"worker" model) equivalent to Alan Turing's machine, and formulated the thesis that effective calculability is captured by such models. He proved undecidability related results, introduced the Post correspondence problem (1946, undecidable), Post's lattice of Boolean clones, and Post's theorem and inversion problem on degrees of unsolvability. He anticipated aspects of Gödel's incompleteness theorems but did not publish in time.

Later life

Post taught for many years at City College of New York despite severe bipolar disorder that interrupted his research. He died in 1954 following electroshock treatment.
