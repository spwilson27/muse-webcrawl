Soybean
Scientific nameGlycine max (L.) Merr.
FamilyFabaceae (legumes)
OriginEast Asia

The soybean, or soya bean (Glycine max), is a species of legume native to East Asia, widely grown for its edible bean, which has numerous uses. Domesticated in China thousands of years ago from the wild Glycine soja, it is one of the world's most important crops.

Cultivation

Soybeans are annual, nitrogen-fixing plants (via Bradyrhizobium root nodules) growing 20 cm to 2 m tall, with trifoliate leaves and hairy pods containing 2–4 seeds. The United States, Brazil and Argentina dominate production; most of the harvest is crushed for soybean meal (animal feed) and oil, and the majority of the US and Brazilian crop is genetically modified for herbicide tolerance.

Uses and nutrition

Traditional foods include tofu, soy sauce, miso, tempeh, soy milk, edamame and natto. Soybeans are unusually protein-rich for a plant (about 36–40% protein, containing all essential amino acids) and rich in isoflavones, whose health effects are debated. Industrial uses include biodiesel, inks and plastics. Environmental concerns link expanding South American cultivation to deforestation.
