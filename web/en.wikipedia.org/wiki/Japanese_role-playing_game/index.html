Japanese role-playing game

A classic turn-based battle screen from a Japanese role-playing game

A Japanese role-playing game (JRPG) is a genre of role-playing video game traditionally developed in Japan, distinguished by turn-based or menu-driven combat, pre-written narratives with defined characters, and party-based adventuring. The genre emerged in the 1980s on Japanese home computers and consoles, drawing on Western titles such as Wizardry and Ultima but adapting them to console hardware and Japanese storytelling conventions.

History

Early Japanese RPGs such as Black Onyx (1984) and Hydlide (1984) localized Western ideas for domestic audiences. The breakthrough came with Dragon Quest (1986), designed by Yuji Horii with art by Akira Toriyama and music by Koichi Sugiyama, which simplified RPG mechanics for the Famicom and defined the genre's template: a hero's party traveling an overworld map, visiting towns, and fighting random encounters in turn-based battles. Final Fantasy (1987) followed, and franchies such as Shin Megami Tensei, Fire Emblem, and later Pokémon and Persona expanded the genre's range.

Characteristics

Typical JRPG features include a linear or semi-linear story, a fixed cast of protagonists with scripted character development, experience points and level progression, and combat resolved through menus rather than direct action. Many use anime-influenced art styles and orchestral or rock soundtracks. Subgenres include tactical RPGs played on grid maps, action RPGs with real-time combat, and monster-collecting games.

Influence

During the 1990s, JRPGs were among the best-selling console games worldwide; Final Fantasy VII (1997) is widely credited with popularizing the genre in North America and Europe. In the 2000s Western open-world RPGs gained prominence, but Japanese series such as Dragon Quest XI, Persona 5, and Xenoblade Chronicles have sustained global popularity, and independent developers worldwide now produce games explicitly modeled on classic JRPGs.
