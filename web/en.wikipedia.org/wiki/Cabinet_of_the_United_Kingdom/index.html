Cabinet of the United Kingdom

The Cabinet of the United Kingdom is the senior decision-making body of His Majesty's Government. Chaired by the Prime Minister, it is composed of the most senior government ministers, mostly heads of departments (Secretaries of State), chosen by the Prime Minister. Cabinet ministers are bound by collective ministerial responsibility.

Composition and meetings

The Cabinet typically has around twenty members, including the Chancellor of the Exchequer, Foreign Secretary, and Home Secretary — with the Prime Minister, the Great Offices of State. It meets weekly, usually on Tuesday mornings, and much of its work is done through cabinet committees. The Cabinet Secretary heads the Cabinet Office supporting it.

History

The cabinet evolved from the Privy Council in the 17th and 18th centuries; its modern form took shape under Robert Walpole, often regarded as the first prime minister. See also British royal family and Cabinet of India.
