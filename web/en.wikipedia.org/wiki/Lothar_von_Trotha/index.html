Lothar von Trotha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lothar von Trotha Born: 3 July 1848, Magdeburg, Prussia
Died: 31 March 1920, Bonn, Germany
Service: Imperial German Army
Known for: Herero and Nama genocide

Adrian Dietrich Lothar von Trotha (3 July 1848 – 31 March 1920) was a German military commander who served as supreme commander of the colonial forces in German South West Africa (present-day Namibia). He is chiefly remembered for issuing the extermination order (Vernichtungsbefehl) of October 1904 against the Herero people, an action that began the Herero and Nama genocide, widely regarded as the first genocide of the twentieth century.

Early career

Trotha joined the Prussian Army in 1865 and fought in the Austro-Prussian and Franco-Prussian wars. He later served in colonial campaigns in German East Africa, where he took part in suppressing the Wahehe rebellion, and in China during the Boxer Rebellion of 1900–01, gaining a reputation for severity in colonial warfare.

German South West Africa

In 1904 Kaiser Wilhelm II appointed Trotha to replace Theodor Leutwein as commander in South West Africa, where the Herero had risen against German rule. After defeating the Herero at the Battle of Waterberg in August 1904, Trotha drove the survivors into the Omaheke desert and on 2 October issued his proclamation that "every Herero, with or without a gun, with or without cattle, will be shot". Tens of thousands of Herero and, later, Nama people perished from shooting, thirst, starvation and imprisonment in concentration camps such as Shark Island. The Berlin government eventually countermanded the order, and Trotha was recalled in 1905.

Later life

Trotha retired to Bonn and died in 1920. In 2004 the German government formally apologised for the genocide, and in 2021 Germany officially recognised the atrocities as genocide and agreed a reconciliation programme with Namibia.
