The Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II marked the 25th anniversary of the accession of Queen Elizabeth II to the thrones of the United Kingdom and the other Commonwealth realms, following the death of her father, George VI, on 6 February 1952. Celebrations were held throughout 1977 across the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth.

Celebrations

The Queen and the Duke of Edinburgh undertook extensive tours, travelling some 56,000 miles and visiting 36 counties in the United Kingdom as well as numerous Commonwealth countries. The festivities culminated in London in June 1977: on 6 June the Queen lit a beacon at Windsor, the first of a chain of jubilee beacons across the country, and on 7 June enormous crowds watched the state procession to St Paul's Cathedral for a service of thanksgiving, followed by a luncheon at the Guildhall. An estimated 500 million people watched the events on television, and thousands of street parties were held across Britain.

Legacy

The Jubilee line of the London Underground — opened in 1979 and named in honour of the jubilee — commemorates the occasion, and the Silver Jubilee Walkway in London was created as a permanent memorial. The celebrations were widely seen as demonstrating the monarchy's continuing popularity.
