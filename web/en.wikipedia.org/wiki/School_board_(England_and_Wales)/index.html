School board (England and Wales)

School boards were public bodies in England and Wales established under the Elementary Education Act 1870 to provide elementary schooling in districts where existing voluntary provision was insufficient. They were the first directly elected bodies with a dedicated tax base in British local government, and women were eligible both to vote in school board elections and to stand for election — decades before women won the parliamentary franchise.

History

The 1870 Act, associated with W. E. Forster, divided the country into school districts and required elections of school boards where provision was inadequate. Boards could levy a rate, build schools, hire teachers, and make attendance by-laws; the London School Board was the largest and most influential, building hundreds of the tall "board schools" that became a feature of Victorian cities.

Notable members

Prominent board members included Elizabeth Garrett Anderson and Emily Davies, elected to the London School Board in 1870, and the socialist Annie Besant. Boards often split between denominational (Church) and undenominational factions over religious teaching and funding of church schools.

Abolition

The Education Act 1902 abolished school boards — some 2,568 in England and Wales — transferring their functions to local education authorities (county and county borough councils) in 1904.
