Black Panther is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer-editor Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, the character first appeared in Fantastic Four #52 (cover-dated July 1966) in the Silver Age of Comic Books. Black Panther's real name is T'Challa, king and protector of the fictional African nation of Wakanda.

Concept and creation

Black Panther is the first Black superhero in mainstream American comics, debuting several years before characters such as Luke Cage and the African-American Green Lantern John Stewart. The Panther is a hereditary title held by the ruler of Wakanda, enhanced by consuming the heart-shaped herb, which grants superhuman senses, strength, speed, and agility, and by a suit woven with the metal vibranium, found only in Wakanda.

Fictional character biography

T'Challa succeeded his father T'Chaka, who was murdered by the adventurer Ulysses Klaw. Educated at Oxford and trained to peak physical condition, T'Challa tested himself against the Fantastic Four to judge their worth as allies. He has been a long-standing member of the Avengers, married (and later separated from) the X-Men's Storm, and led Wakanda through invasions and political upheaval. After T'Challa's periods of incapacity, his sister Shuri also held the Black Panther mantle in comics.

In other media

Chadwick Boseman portrayed T'Challa in the Marvel Cinematic Universe films Captain America: Civil War (2016), Black Panther (2018), Avengers: Infinity War (2018), and Avengers: Endgame (2019). Black Panther grossed over $1.3 billion and became the first superhero film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Following Boseman's death in 2020, Marvel chose not to recast the role; Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022) centred on Shuri assuming the mantle.
