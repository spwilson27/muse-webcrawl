Veneration of Mary in the Catholic Church


A crowned statue of the Virgin Mary

The veneration of Mary in the Catholic Church encompasses the devotional beliefs and practices directed to Mary, the mother of Jesus, within Roman Catholic teaching. Catholic theology distinguishes the worship (latria) owed to God alone from the veneration (dulia) given to saints, with a special, higher veneration termed hyperdulia reserved for Mary because of her unique role in salvation history.

Doctrinal foundations

Marian devotion rests on dogmas defined by the Church: Mary as Theotokos ("God-bearer", affirmed at the Council of Ephesus in 431), her perpetual virginity, the Immaculate Conception (defined in 1854: Mary was conceived free from original sin), and the Assumption (defined in 1950: Mary was taken up body and soul into heaven). Catholics also honour titles such as Queen of Heaven, Mother of the Church, and Mediatrix, and believe Mary intercedes with her son on behalf of the faithful.

Devotional practices

Prayer

The best-known Marian prayers are the Hail Mary, the Rosary — a meditative cycle of prayers on the mysteries of Christ's and Mary's lives — the Angelus, the Memorare, and litanies such as the Litany of Loreto. The Hail Holy Queen (Salve Regina) closes the traditional night office.

Feasts and images

The liturgical calendar includes feasts such as the Immaculate Conception (8 December), the Assumption (15 August), and Mary, Mother of God (1 January). May and October are traditionally Marian months. Veneration of statues and icons, pilgrimages to shrines such as Lourdes, Fátima, Guadalupe, and Czestochowa, and consecrations to Mary are widespread. Reported Marian apparitions, once investigated and approved by the Church, often become major centres of devotion.

History

Devotion to Mary grew from the early centuries of Christianity, flourishing in medieval monasticism and popular piety. The Counter-Reformation strengthened Marian practice as a marker of Catholic identity in contrast to Protestant critiques. In the modern era, popes from Pius IX to John Paul II — whose motto was Totus Tuus ("totally yours", addressed to Mary) — promoted Marian devotion, while the Second Vatican Council's Lumen gentium presented it within an explicitly Christ-centred framework.

Ecumenical perspectives

Protestant traditions generally reject or sharply limit Marian veneration, regarding it as unbiblical or distracting from Christ; Orthodox Christianity shares much Marian devotion with Catholicism, though it does not accept the Immaculate Conception as defined dogma. Dialogue continues on Mary's role in the communion of saints.
