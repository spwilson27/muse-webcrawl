Tiradito

Tiradito
CourseAppetiser / main
Place of originPeru (Nikkei cuisine)
Main ingredientsRaw fish, leche de tigre, ají peppers
Similar dishesCeviche, sashimi, crudo

Tiradito is a Peruvian dish of raw fish, cut in the shape of sashimi and covered in a spicy sauce. It reflects the influence of Japanese immigrants on Peruvian cuisine—Nikkei cuisine—and is similar to ceviche.

Preparation

Unlike ceviche, whose fish is cubed and marinated, tiradito fish—commonly sole, sea bass or tuna—is sliced into long thin strips and dressed at the moment of serving with leche de tigre (lime juice, fish stock and ají chilli), so the fish remains essentially raw. Sauces range from classic lime and ají amarillo to rocoto, olive, passionfruit or Nikkei soy-based versions, sometimes 'nikkei-style' with a brief sear (tiradito tataki).

Accompaniments

It is typically served with sweet potato, choclo (large-kernel Andean corn), cancha corn and red onion, sharing ceviche's garnishes.
