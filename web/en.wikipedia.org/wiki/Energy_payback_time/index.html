Energy payback time

Applies toEnergy generation systems
Related metricEROEI

The energy payback time (EPBT) of an energy-generating system is the period of operation required for the system to produce as much energy as was consumed in manufacturing, transporting, installing, and decommissioning it. It is a standard metric in life-cycle assessment, particularly for photovoltaic systems and wind turbines.

Values

For modern solar photovoltaic systems, payback times are commonly on the order of one to a few years depending on technology and local solar resource, against operating lifetimes of 25–30 years or more. Wind turbines typically repay their embodied energy within months to about a year. Payback times have fallen steadily as manufacturing efficiency improves.

Interpretation

EPBT complements the energy return on energy invested (EROEI): a short payback time implies a high EROEI over the system lifetime. Critics note that boundary choices in life-cycle inventories — which inputs are counted — can shift results considerably.
