Adena culture

The Adena culture was a Pre-Columbian Native American culture that existed from roughly 500 BCE to 100 CE, in a time known as the Early Woodland period, in the Ohio River valley region of North America.

Characteristics

The Adena are known for large conical burial mounds, ceremonial earthworks and enclosures, finely made stone tools, copper ornaments, and engraved stone tablets. They practised horticulture with native seed crops alongside hunting and gathering.

Sites

The type site is the Adena Mound near Chillicothe, Ohio. Other major sites include Grave Creek Mound in West Virginia and Serpent Mound (attribution debated). The Adena preceded and influenced the Hopewell culture.
