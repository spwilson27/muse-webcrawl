Sociative case

The sociative case is a grammatical case that expresses accompaniment — the meaning "with" or "together with" a person or thing. It is sometimes grouped with or distinguished from the comitative case.

Usage

The sociative case appears in languages such as Quechua, where the suffix -wan marks accompaniment, and in various other languages that use case endings rather than prepositions to express the notion. In Hungarian, the comitative-instrumental case serves similar functions.

Related cases

Related notions include the comitative case (accompaniment) and the instrumental case (the means by which an action is performed), which some languages merge into a single case.
