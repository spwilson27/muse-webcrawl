Điện Biên Phủ (Vietnamese: [ɗîəˀn ʔɓīən fû]) is a city in northwestern Vietnam, the capital of Điện Biên province. It lies in the Mường Thanh valley, some 35 kilometres long and 6 kilometres wide, close to the border with Laos, and is historically famed as the site of the decisive Battle of Dien Bien Phu in 1954.

History

The valley was long inhabited principally by Tai peoples and was a trading point on routes between Vietnam, Laos, and China. Its modern importance dates to the First Indochina War: in November 1953 French Union forces under Operation Castor parachuted into the valley and built a fortified camp, intending to lure the Viet Minh into a set-piece battle. Instead, General Võ Nguyên Giáp's forces hauled artillery through jungle onto the surrounding hills, dug in around the perimeter, and after a siege of 57 days overran the garrison on 7 May 1954. The defeat precipitated the Geneva Accords and the end of French Indochina.

The modern city

Today Dien Bien Phu is a growing provincial city serving an agricultural region of rice, tea, and coffee, with its economy boosted by battlefield tourism. Sites preserved or erected around the city include the A1 Hill (Eliane) strongpoint, the command bunker of Colonel Christian de Castries, the Viet Minh trenches, war cemeteries, the Dien Bien Phu Victory Museum, and a monumental victory statue on D1 Hill erected for the battle's fiftieth anniversary in 2004. The city is served by Dien Bien Phu Airport.
