Dallas Fort Worth International Airport

Dallas Fort Worth International Airport (IATA: DFW), also known as DFW Airport, is the primary international airport serving the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex in Texas. Located roughly midway between the two cities, it is one of the largest airports in the world by land area — covering about 17,000 acres (69 km²) — and one of the busiest by passenger traffic and aircraft movements.

History

For decades Dallas (Love Field) and Fort Worth (Meacham) operated rival airports, and federal pressure for a shared regional facility led to a joint project agreed in the 1960s. DFW opened for commercial service in January 1974. It became the largest hub of American Airlines, whose headquarters stands nearby in Fort Worth; American accounts for the great majority of DFW's flights. The Skylink automated people-mover, opened in 2005, links the five terminals airside, and international Terminal D opened the same year.

Operations

The airport serves over 250 destinations and handles roughly 80 million passengers annually in recent years, ranking among the top three busiest airports worldwide by aircraft movements. It is also a major cargo facility and the site of a large maintenance base for American Airlines.
