Treasury Solicitor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treasury Solicitor is the head of the Government Legal Department of the United Kingdom and the government's principal legal adviser in civil matters — in effect the solicitor to the Crown. The officeholder is the head of the Government Legal Profession and, ex officio, also holds the office of King's Proctor (Queen's Proctor when the monarch is female). Despite the title, the post is not part of HM Treasury: the name is historical, surviving from the period when the office's functions centred on the Treasury.

Functions

The Treasury Solicitor leads the Government Legal Department (formerly the Treasury Solicitor's Department, rebranded in 2015), which provides legal services to most central government departments and agencies: litigation before domestic courts and tribunals, employment, commercial and property work, and the drafting support given to Parliamentary Counsel. The department employs over two thousand lawyers and staff, making it one of the largest legal practices in the country. As King's Proctor, the officeholder administers bona vacantia in England and Wales — the estates of people who die intestate without entitled relatives, and the assets of dissolved companies — through the Bona Vacantia Division, and may intervene in matrimonial and probate proceedings on behalf of the Crown.

History

The office developed out of the Treasury's use of outside solicitors in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; by the nineteenth century the Treasury Solicitor had become the Crown's standing solicitor, famously conducting prosecutions (before the creation of the Director of Public Prosecutions, and for a time combining that role). Notable historical work included prosecuting major criminal cases and representing the government in landmark public-law litigation. The department's headquarters are in London, with offices in other cities including Leeds, Bristol, and Croydon. The Treasury Solicitor is appointed through open competition and is formally the permanent head of the department, accountable to the Attorney General for England and Wales in professional matters.
