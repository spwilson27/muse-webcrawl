ATR 72

The ATR 72 is a twin-engine turboprop, short-haul regional airliner developed and produced in France and Italy by aircraft manufacturer ATR. It was developed from the smaller ATR 42 by stretching the fuselage to seat up to 78 passengers. The ATR 72 first flew on 27 October 1988 and entered service in 1989; it has become the best-selling regional turboprop airliner of its era, with well over a thousand delivered.

Design

The ATR 72 is a high-wing aircraft with a T-tail, powered by two Pratt & Whitney Canada PW127-series turboprops driving six-bladed Hamilton Standard propellers. Compared with the ATR 42, it is stretched by 4.5 m, with a larger wing and greater fuel capacity. Successive variants — the -200, -210, -500 (ATR 72-500) and the current -600 series with a glass cockpit — progressively improved performance, comfort and avionics. Military variants include maritime patrol versions, and a freighter version is flown by cargo operators.

Operations

The type is used by regional airlines worldwide for short sectors where its turboprop fuel efficiency is advantageous. Operators have included Air New Zealand, IndiGo, Azul Brazilian Airlines, Bangkok Airways and Aer Lingus Regional. The aircraft's safety record includes the 1994 crash of American Eagle Flight 4184 in icing conditions, which led to changes in de-icing procedures and certification for turboprops.
