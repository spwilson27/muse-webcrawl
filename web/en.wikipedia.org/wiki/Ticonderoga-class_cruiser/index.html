The Ticonderoga class of guided-missile cruisers is a class of warships in the United States Navy, first ordered and authorized in the 1978 fiscal year. The class is also known as the CG-47 class after the hull number of the lead ship, USS Ticonderoga. The ships are built around the Aegis Combat System and the SPY-1 electronically scanned array radar, which allow them to track and engage large numbers of airborne targets simultaneously.

The Ticonderoga class was the first class of U.S. Navy vessels to incorporate the Aegis system. The first five ships were armed with twin-arm Mk 26 missile launchers; beginning with USS Bunker Hill (CG-52), the ships received the Mk 41 vertical launching system (VLS), which greatly increased their magazine capacity and flexibility. A total of 27 ships were completed, all built by Ingalls Shipbuilding in Pascagoula, Mississippi, and Bath Iron Works in Bath, Maine.

Design and development

The class was originally envisioned as an enlarged version of the Virginia-class cruiser design with the Aegis system, but was ultimately built on the hull of the Spruance-class destroyer. This allowed the ships to use proven propulsion and hull machinery while accommodating the large SPY-1 radar arrays on the superstructure faces. The Aegis system integrates the AN/SPY-1 radar with computer systems to detect, track, and engage targets, originally optimized for defense of carrier battle groups against saturation attacks by Soviet anti-ship missiles and aircraft.

Service history

Ticonderoga-class cruisers have served in numerous operations, including the Persian Gulf, the Balkans, and the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, providing air defense and launching Tomahawk cruise missile strikes. In 1988, USS Vincennes (CG-49) shot down Iran Air Flight 655 over the Strait of Hormuz, killing all 290 people aboard, an incident the United States attributed to misidentification during a surface engagement. The class has also been central to the Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System, with several ships modified to intercept ballistic missiles; in 2008, USS Lake Erie (CG-70) destroyed a malfunctioning U.S. spy satellite in orbit.

Retirement

Because of the high cost of modernizing the hulls, the Navy began retiring the class earlier than originally planned. The five non-VLS ships were decommissioned in 2004–2005. Beginning in the 2020s, the Navy accelerated retirements, and proposals have envisioned retiring the remaining cruisers over the course of the decade as Arleigh Burke-class destroyers and other platforms assume their roles.

Ships in class
