Overview

Mount Tai (Chinese: 泰山; pinyin: Tài Shān) is a mountain of historical and cultural significance located north of the city of Tai'an, in Shandong province, China. Its tallest peak, the Jade Emperor Peak, rises about 1,545 metres (5,069 ft) above sea level. In Chinese tradition the sun is said to begin its journey in the east at Mount Tai, and the mountain is the foremost of the Five Great Mountains of China.

Religious and imperial significance

For more than two thousand years Mount Tai was the pre-eminent sacred mountain of imperial China. Emperors of successive dynasties ascended the mountain to perform the Feng and Shan sacrifices, ceremonies by which a ruler reported to Heaven on the state of the realm. The First Emperor of Qin, Qin Shi Huang, is traditionally said to have climbed the mountain, and later emperors including Han Wudi, Tang Gaozong, Song Zhenzong and the Kangxi and Qianlong emperors of the Qing all made the pilgrimage.

The mountain is closely associated with the cult of the God of Mount Tai (Taishan Fujun) and with Bixia Yuanjun, the Goddess of Dawn, whose temple stands near the summit. Pilgrims — historically numbered in the millions — climbed the mountain seeking blessings, especially for fertility and safe childbirth.

Features

The main pilgrim route from Tai'an climbs some 7,200 stone steps. Along the way are temples, pavilions, and hundreds of inscribed cliff faces and stelae recording imperial visits and poems. The Dai Temple at the foot of the mountain in Tai'an is one of the largest intact ancient building complexes in China. Watching the sunrise from the Jade Emperor Peak or the Sunrise Viewing Peak is a celebrated experience, one of the so-called Four Wonders of Mount Tai along with the Sea of Clouds, the Golden Belt of the Yellow River, and the colourful sunset glow.

World Heritage

UNESCO inscribed Mount Tai on the World Heritage List in 1987 as both a cultural and a natural property, recognising its combination of spectacular scenery, geological value, and an unusually complete record of Chinese religious and artistic history spanning three millennia.
