Active management

Active management (also called active investing) is an investment strategy in which a portfolio manager or investor makes specific investments with the goal of outperforming an investment benchmark index, such as the S&P 500. It contrasts with passive management (indexing), which seeks to replicate the performance of a benchmark by holding its constituent securities.

[Image: Fund manager reviewing portfolio data]

Approach

Active managers rely on analytical research, forecasts, and their own judgement and experience in deciding which securities to buy, hold and sell. Methods include fundamental analysis of companies, macroeconomic timing, quantitative models, and technical analysis. Portfolio managers may deviate substantially from benchmark weightings in pursuit of excess return, known as alpha.

Performance debate

Active funds charge higher fees than passive funds, and a large body of research — including the SPIVA scorecards — shows that the majority of actively managed funds underperform their benchmarks over long horizons after fees, though some managers outperform in particular markets or periods. The debate turns on market efficiency: proponents argue that skilled managers can exploit mispricing, while critics, invoking the efficient-market hypothesis, hold that persistent outperformance is rare and difficult to identify in advance.
