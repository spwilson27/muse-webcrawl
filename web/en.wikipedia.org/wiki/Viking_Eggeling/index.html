Viking Eggeling

Viking Eggeling (21 October 1880 – 19 May 1925) was a Swedish artist and filmmaker. He was a pioneer of abstract film and is best known for his experimental work Symphonie diagonale (1924), one of the first fully abstract films.

Life

Eggeling was born in Lund, Sweden. He emigrated to Germany in his youth, studied art and music, and worked as an art teacher. After the First World War he lived in Paris, where he associated with avant-garde artists including Hans Arp and Tristan Tzara, and became involved with the Dada movement.

Seeking a universal language of visual form, Eggeling developed long scroll drawings of abstract shapes that evolved and repeated in counterpoint, influenced by musical structure. From 1919 he collaborated with the German artist Hans Richter on scrolls such as Preludium, and the two attempted to translate the scroll concept into film.

Symphonie diagonale

Working largely alone, Eggeling created Symphonie diagonale between 1921 and 1924 by photographing cut-out tin foil and paper shapes frame by frame. The film presents slowly transforming diagonal compositions of lines and geometric figures, intended as "visual music". It was first publicly screened in Berlin in May 1925 at the landmark "Der absolute Film" matinee, shortly before Eggeling's death in Berlin at age 44.

Eggeling's work influenced later abstract animators, including Oskar Fischinger, and is recognised as a foundational contribution to absolute film and visual music.
