Harrisburg is the capital city of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania and the seat of Dauphin County. It lies on the east bank of the Susquehanna River, about 105 miles (169 km) west of Philadelphia. With a population of 50,099 at the 2020 census, it is the tenth-largest city in the state, while its metropolitan area — the Harrisburg–Carlisle area — is among the most populous in Pennsylvania.

History

The site was long inhabited by Native American peoples, including the Susquehannock and Shawnee. Welsh trader John Harris established a trading post and ferry here in the early eighteenth century; his son, John Harris Jr., laid out the town in 1785 and named it Harrisburg. The town became a borough in 1791, and in 1812 it was chosen as the state capital, replacing Philadelphia. The Pennsylvania State Capitol, completed in 1906 and crowned by a dome modelled on St Peter's Basilica, dominates the city centre.

Harrisburg was an important transportation and industrial hub, served by canals, the Pennsylvania Railroad, and later interstate highways. The city is also known nationally for the 1979 Three Mile Island accident at the nuclear plant about ten miles downstream. After decades of decline, the city entered state receivership in 2011 over crushing debt largely tied to a failed trash-incinerator project, emerging from receivership in 2014.

Geography

Harrisburg sits in the Great Valley region of the Appalachian Mountains, with the wide, shallow Susquehanna River to its west crossed by several bridges, including the Harvey Taylor and Market Street bridges. The city itself covers about 11.4 square miles, of which roughly 8.1 square miles is land.

Government

As the state capital, Harrisburg hosts the Capitol Complex and numerous state agencies, which together with the federal government and regional health systems are the area's largest employers. The city is governed by a mayor and a seven-member city council.
