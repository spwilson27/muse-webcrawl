Reference work

A reference work is a text — such as a book, periodical, or electronic publication — to which one can refer for information. The information is intended to be found quickly when needed. Reference works are usually not read cover to cover, but consulted selectively; they are typically designed with an organisational structure (alphabetical, chronological, or topical) that allows a specific fact to be located rapidly.

Reference works are consulted for specific facts rather than read in full. [Image placeholder]

Types

  • Encyclopedias — summaries of knowledge across subjects or within a field.
  • Dictionaries and glossaries — definitions and usage of words and terms.
  • Thesauruses — synonyms, antonyms, and related words.
  • Almanacs and yearbooks — annual compilations of facts, statistics, and events.
  • Atlases and gazetteers — geographic information and maps.
  • Handbooks and manuals — concise practical guidance on a subject.
  • Directories — listings of people, organisations, or services.
  • Bibliographies and indexes — guides to other publications.
  • Biographical dictionaries — lives of notable people.

In libraries

Libraries traditionally keep reference works in a separate reference section, often for use only within the building, supported by reference librarians who assist readers in finding information. With digitisation, many standard reference works have moved online, and general web reference sources such as Wikipedia have become first stops for quick facts, supplemented by publisher databases and digital libraries.
