Henry Grattan (1746–1820) was an Irish politician and orator, a member of both the Irish House of Commons and, after the Union, the British Parliament at Westminster. He led the movement that won legislative independence for Ireland in 1782 — the parliament of 1782–1800 is remembered as "Grattan's Parliament" — and he campaigned, unsuccessfully, against the Act of Union that abolished it.

Early life and career

Grattan was born in Dublin, educated at Trinity College Dublin and called to the Irish bar in 1772, though he practised little law. Entering the Irish Parliament in 1775 as member for Charlemont, he quickly became the leading voice of the Patriot party, using celebrated oratory to demand free trade and the repeal of the laws subordinating the Irish legislature to London.

Legislative independence, 1782

Backed by the armed Irish Volunteers and Britain's difficulties in the American war, Grattan's campaign culminated in 1782 when Westminster repealed the Declaratory Act of 1720 and conceded that only the King, Lords and Commons of Ireland could legislate for Ireland. Grattan was voted a grant of £50,000 by a grateful parliament. He subsequently pressed for parliamentary reform and Catholic relief, supporting the Catholic Relief Acts, though the parliament he led remained an exclusively Protestant body dominated by the Ascendancy.

The Union and later years

After the rebellion of 1798, Grattan fiercely opposed Pitt's proposal for a legislative union, in a celebrated series of speeches in 1800, but the Act of Union passed and the Irish Parliament was dissolved. Grattan took his seat at Westminster in 1805 and spent his remaining years advocating Catholic emancipation. He died in London in 1820 and was buried in Westminster Abbey, near Pitt and Fox.

Legacy

Grattan is remembered as one of the greatest parliamentary orators of the eighteenth century and a symbol of constitutional Irish nationalism. His statue stands outside the old Parliament House in College Green, Dublin, and Grattan Bridge and Grattan Street commemorate him in the capital.
