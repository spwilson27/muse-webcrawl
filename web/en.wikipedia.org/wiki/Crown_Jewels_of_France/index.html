Crown Jewels of France

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Crown Jewels of France (Joyaux de la Couronne de France) consist of the crowns, orb, sceptres, diadems and jewels that were symbols of Royal power between 752 and 1825. Most of the collection was broken up and sold by the Third Republic in 1887; the surviving pieces are displayed in the Galerie d'Apollon of the Louvre Museum in Paris.

History

The regalia were traditionally kept at the Basilica of Saint-Denis, the burial place of French kings. The collection grew over the centuries through gifts and purchases, reaching its height under the Bourbon monarchy. Famous items included the crown of Louis XV (1722), the Regent Diamond, the Sancy diamond, and the Hortensia diamond. During the French Revolution the jewels were seized by the state; in 1792 the royal storehouse was famously burgled and many pieces were stolen, though several were later recovered.

The 1887 sale

Fearing a monarchist restoration, the government of the Third Republic decided to disperse the collection. A law of 1887 authorised the sale, and most of the jewels were broken up and auctioned in Paris, with the gems sold loose. A small number of pieces of particular historical or artistic value were retained, including the Regent Diamond, the crown of Louis XV, and the brooch reliquary of Empress Eugénie. In more recent decades the Louvre has reacquired a few dispersed items, such as the tiara of Empress Eugénie, damaged during its theft from the museum in October 2025.

Surviving collection

Today the remaining jewels — crowns of Louis XV and Napoleon, the Regent, Hortensia and Mazarin diamonds, and various items of Empresses Marie-Amélie and Eugénie — are exhibited in the Louvre's Apollo Gallery.
