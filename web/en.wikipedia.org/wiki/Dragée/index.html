Dragée

A dragée is a bite-sized confectionery with a hard outer shell. The term most often refers to sugar-coated almonds (Jordan almonds), but also covers other coated sweets and even the sugar-coated form of pills and other items. Dragées are traditionally given at weddings, baptisms, and other celebrations in many cultures.

Production

Dragées are made in a process called panning, in which centres such as nuts, chocolate, or liquorice are tumbled in a rotating pan while syrup is gradually applied, building up a smooth, hard sugar shell layer by layer. Colourings are added to the final layers; metallic dragées are coated with edible silver or gold.

Wedding tradition

In Italy, sugared almonds called confetti are given to wedding guests, traditionally five at a time to symbolise health, wealth, happiness, fertility, and longevity. Similar traditions exist in France, Greece, and the Middle East.
