The Battle of Fontenoy, fought on 11 May 1745 near Tournai in the Austrian Netherlands, was a major French victory of the War of the Austrian Succession. The French army under Marshal Maurice de Saxe, with King Louis XV observing the field, repelled the attack of the Pragmatic Army under the Duke of Cumberland, securing France's conquest of Flanders.

Background

In 1745 Saxe besieged Tournai, a key barrier fortress. The Allied Pragmatic Army — British, Hanoverian, Dutch and Austrian troops — advanced to relieve it. Saxe, though so ill he directed the battle from a wicker litter, chose a strong defensive ridge around Fontenoy, fortifying the village, anchoring his flanks on woods and redoubts, and inviting attack.

The battle

On 11 May the Allies assaulted the French flanks: the Dutch attacked Fontenoy village and were repulsed, while the British and Hanoverian infantry, in one of the most famous episodes of the war, formed a single massive column and marched steadily up the slope, driving in successive French lines — the occasion of the apocryphal exchange, "Gentlemen of the French Guards, fire first." The column, its flanks exposed, was then battered by artillery and counterattacked by French cavalry and the Irish Brigade in French service, whose charge helped break it. The Allies retreated in good order, leaving about 7,500 casualties on the field; French losses were comparable at roughly 7,200.

Aftermath

Tournai fell, and French armies overran the Austrian Netherlands in the following campaigns. Fontenoy remains celebrated in Ireland for the Irish Brigade's role ("Remember Limerick and Saxon perfidy") and in France as one of the great set-piece victories of the ancien régime.
