Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation

Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), commonly known as bone marrow transplantation, is the transplantation of multipotent hematopoietic stem cells, usually derived from bone marrow, peripheral blood, or umbilical cord blood, in order to replicate inside a recipient and produce additional normal blood cells. It is most often performed for patients with cancers of the blood or bone marrow, such as leukaemia and lymphoma, and for certain inherited blood and immune disorders.

Procedure

Before transplant the patient receives a conditioning regimen of chemotherapy, sometimes with total-body irradiation, intended to eradicate malignant cells and suppress the immune system. Donor cells are collected by bone-marrow harvest under anaesthesia or, more commonly today, by apheresis of peripheral blood after mobilization with granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF). The stem cells are infused intravenously and home to the marrow, where engraftment — recovery of blood counts — typically takes two to four weeks.

Allogeneic versus autologous

In autologous transplant, the patient's own cells are collected in advance, permitting high-dose chemotherapy that would otherwise be irreversibly myelosuppressive; there is no graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), but also no graft-versus-tumour effect, and reinfused cells may harbour residual disease. In allogeneic transplant, donor cells provide both a new immune system and an immunological attack on residual cancer, at the cost of GVHD — in which donor T cells attack the recipient's skin, gut, and liver — and greater treatment-related mortality. Donors are matched by HLA typing; registries such as Be The Match coordinate unrelated donors worldwide, and haploidentical (half-matched family) transplants have expanded access.

History

The field grew from post-war research on radiation injury. E. Donnall Thomas performed the first successful allogeneic transplants in the late 1950s–1970s and received the Nobel Prize in 1990 for work on organ and cell transplantation. More than a million transplants have now been performed worldwide.
