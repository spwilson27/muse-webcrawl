Manga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A Tokyo bookshop's manga wall: floor-to-ceiling shelves of tankōbon volumes, spines a mosaic of colour and type]

Manga (Japanese: 漫画) are comics or graphic novels originating from Japan, characterised by a distinctive visual language — large eyes and expressive line from its post-war codifiers, cinematic panel flow read right-to-left, and speed of production through weekly magazine serialisation. Most manga conform to a style developed in the late 19th century and revolutionised by Osamu Tezuka after 1945, though its roots run to medieval picture scrolls and Hokusai's sketchbooks (he used the word 'manga' for them).

Manga is a mass medium of a scale unmatched elsewhere: weekly anthologies like Weekly Shōnen Jump serialise the hits (Dragon Ball, One Piece — history's best-selling series — Naruto, Demon Slayer), collected volumes (tankōbon) fill bookshops, and demographics (shōnen for boys, shōjo for girls, seinen and josei for adults) segment an industry read by all of Japan and, since the 2000s, an enormous and growing world audience.

Tezuka and the system

Osamu Tezuka, the 'god of manga', fused Disney and cinema into the post-war form (Astro Boy, 1952; Phoenix) and built the studio system of assistants, deadlines and magazine serialisation that still runs the industry — at legendary cost to its artists' health (Tezuka died at 60; the weekly pace he set is the industry's glory and its scandal). Alternative manga (the gekiga of Yoshihiro Tatsumi, the avant-garde of Garo magazine) shadowed the mainstream from the 1950s.

Global manga

Manga became world culture through anime's gateway: book-format manga now outsells domestic comics in the United States and France (the world's second manga market), doujinshi (self-published) culture culminates in Comic Market, the world's largest fan convention, and Korean manhwa and webtoons adapt the system vertically for the phone. The form's grammar — decompressed action, emotional close-up, the panel as heartbeat — is now every young cartoonist's mother tongue.
