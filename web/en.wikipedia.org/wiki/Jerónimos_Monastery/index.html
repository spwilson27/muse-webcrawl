Jerónimos Monastery, Lisbon

The Jerónimos Monastery (Portuguese: Mosteiro dos Jerónimos) is a former monastery of the Order of Saint Jerome near the Tagus river in Belém, Lisbon, Portugal. It is one of the most prominent examples of the Portuguese Manueline style of architecture.

History

Commissioned by King Manuel I around 1501 and funded by a tax on the spice trade, the monastery was built on the site where Vasco da Gama prayed before his voyage to India. Its church houses the tombs of Vasco da Gama and the poet Luís de Camões.

Heritage

Secularised in the nineteenth century, the monastery is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, together with the nearby Tower of Belém, and houses museums including the National Archaeology Museum. The Treaty of Lisbon was signed there in 2007. See also Lisbon.
