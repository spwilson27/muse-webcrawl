Biblical narrative

Jael (Hebrew: יָעֵל Yāʿēl, "ibex") is a heroine of the Hebrew Bible, wife of Heber the Kenite. In Judges 4, after Deborah and Barak defeat the Canaanite army of King Jabin at the Kishon, the fleeing general Sisera seeks refuge in Jael's tent, her husband's clan being at peace with Jabin. Jael welcomes him, covers him with a rug, and gives him milk when he asks for water (in the Song of Deborah, curds "in a lordly bowl"). As he sleeps, she drives a tent peg through his temple with a hammer, killing him. When Barak arrives in pursuit, Jael shows him the corpse: "Come, and I will show you the man whom you seek."

The Song of Deborah

The archaic Song of Deborah (Judges 5), among the oldest texts in the Bible, singles Jael out for blessing above all women: "Blessed above women shall Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite be, blessed shall she be above women in the tent." The song gloats over Sisera's mother waiting vainly at the window for her son's return.

Interpretation

Commentators have long debated Jael's violation of hospitality — a sacred duty in the ancient Near East — with readings ranging from divinely sanctioned deliverance of Israel to an ambivalent portrait of a woman exploiting the weapons and spaces (tent, milk, peg) of the female domestic sphere. Feminist scholarship reads Jael alongside Deborah and the "mother in Israel" theme, while her act is often paired typologically with Judith's beheading of Holofernes.

In art

Jael became a favourite subject of Renaissance and Baroque art — Artemisia Gentileschi painted her (c. 1620) with characteristic force — typically shown in the moment of the killing or displaying the peg, an iconography that made her one of the standard "strong women" of the biblical cycle.
