Louis, Grand Condé

Born8 September 1621, Paris
Died11 December 1686, Fontainebleau
AllegianceKingdom of France
Known forPrince of Condé; general of Louis XIV

Louis de Bourbon, Prince of Condé (1621–1686), known as the Great Condé (le Grand Condé), was a French general and the most celebrated member of the House of Condé, a cadet branch of the French royal family. Regarded in his lifetime as one of the great captains of the age, he won fame while still in his twenties.

Early victories

As Duke of Enghien, Condé won a brilliant victory over the Spanish at Rocroi in 1643, aged 21, ending the reputation of the Spanish tercios for invincibility. He followed this with successes at Freiburg, Nördlingen (1645) and Lens (1648), playing a major part in the final campaigns of the Thirty Years' War.

Fronde and later career

During the civil wars of the Fronde, Condé turned against the regency of Anne of Austria and Cardinal Mazarin, was imprisoned, and eventually defected to Spain, fighting against France before being pardoned under the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659). Restored to favour, he conquered Franche-Comté in 1668 and campaigned in the Dutch War, winning his last battle at Seneffe in 1674, before retiring to Chantilly, which he embellished with gardens by André Le Nôtre.
