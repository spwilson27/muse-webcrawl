San Millán de la Cogolla is a small village in the province and autonomous community of La Rioja, Spain, famous for its twin monasteries of Suso ("upper") and Yuso ("lower"), inscribed together on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997.

History

The community traces its origin to the 6th-century hermit Saint Emilian (San Millán), whose cave hermitage became the monastery of Suso. The larger monastery of Yuso was built below it from the 11th century onward, in Romanesque, Gothic and Baroque styles, and became a major pilgrimage and monastic centre.

Language significance

San Millán is celebrated as a cradle of the Spanish language: the Glosas Emilianenses, marginal glosses written in the monastery and once dated to the 11th century, were long regarded as the earliest written Spanish (and include the earliest known text in Basque), though their dating and status are now debated by scholars.
