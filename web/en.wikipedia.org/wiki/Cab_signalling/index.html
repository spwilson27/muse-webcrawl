Cab signalling

Cab signalling is a railway safety system that communicates track status and condition information to the cab, crew compartment or driver's compartment of a locomotive, railcar or multiple unit. The information is continually updated, giving an easy-to-read display to the train driver or engineer.

Operation

Track circuits or lineside equipment encode signal aspects and speed restrictions, which are transmitted through the rails (often as coded pulses or audio-frequency signals) or by radio and picked up by antennas on the train. The cab display shows the current aspect and authorized speed; if the driver fails to respond to a more restrictive indication, automatic enforcement can sound an alarm or apply the brakes.

Systems

Examples include the Pennsylvania Railroad's pulse-code cab signals in the United States, the British AWS and TPWS, and the transmission-based components of ETCS. Cab signalling improves safety in poor visibility and is a prerequisite for high-speed operation, where lineside signals cannot be read reliably; it forms a step toward automatic train operation.
