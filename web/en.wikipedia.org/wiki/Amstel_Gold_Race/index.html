The Amstel Gold Race is an annual one-day road bicycle race held in the province of Limburg in the Netherlands. Traditionally run in mid-April, it is the first of the three Ardennes classics held in the week that also includes La Flèche Wallonne and Liège–Bastogne–Liège, although the Amstel Gold Race itself takes place in Dutch Limburg rather than the Belgian Ardennes. The race is named after its sponsor, the Amstel brewery.

History

The race was first held in 1966, organised by Dutch sports promoters Ton Vissers and Herman Krott. Early editions started in Breda and later in Heerlen and Maastricht; since 1998 the start has been in Maastricht. For many years the finish was on top of the Cauberg climb in Valkenburg, one of the most famous hills in Dutch cycling. From 2013 the finish was moved a short distance beyond the summit of the Cauberg, and later editions have finished in Berg en Terblijt after a final circuit.

Dutch rider Jan Raas holds the record with five victories (1977, 1978, 1979, 1980 and 1982). Other multiple winners include Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, Philippe Gilbert and Michał Kwiatkowski. A women's race, the Amstel Gold Race Ladies Edition, has been held since 2001, with a break between 2004 and 2016.

Course

The course winds through the rolling hill country of South Limburg, repeatedly crossing short, steep climbs known locally as hellingen, including the Cauberg, Keutenberg, Eyserbosweg and Bemelerberg. The route typically exceeds 250 kilometres for the men's race and features more than thirty categorised climbs, making it a selective race suited to punchy climbers and classics specialists.
