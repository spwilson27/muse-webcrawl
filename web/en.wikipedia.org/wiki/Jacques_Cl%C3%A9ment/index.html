Jacques Clément

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Engraving of Jacques Clément, assassin of Henry III of France

Jacques Clément (1567 – 1 August 1589) was a French Dominican friar and Catholic fanatic who assassinated King Henry III of France, the last Valois king, during the French Wars of Religion.

The assassination

Clément, a member of the Catholic League who blamed the king for the murder of the Duke of Guise, gained an audience with Henry III at Saint-Cloud on 1 August 1589 under the pretext of delivering confidential letters. As the king read them, Clément stabbed him in the abdomen with a knife. The friar was killed immediately by the royal guards; Henry died the following day, naming the Protestant Henry of Navarre (Henry IV) as his successor.

Aftermath

Clément's body was drawn and quartered and burned. League supporters celebrated him as a martyr and avenger; Pope Sixtus V reportedly compared the deed to the Incarnation in its benefit to France, though this was never formal praise. The assassination, following that of Henry IV by Ravaillac in 1610, became a touchstone in debates about tyrannicide.
