Treaty of Amritsar (1846)

The Treaty of Amritsar of 16 March 1846 was an agreement between the British East India Company and Raja Gulab Singh of Jammu, concluded days after the Treaty of Lahore that ended the First Anglo-Sikh War. It created the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir.

Terms

In the Treaty of Lahore, the defeated Sikh Empire had ceded the hill territories between the Beas and Indus rivers, including Kashmir and Hazara, to the British, and owed a large war indemnity. Unable to pay in full, the Lahore durbar surrendered the territories in lieu. By the Amritsar treaty, the British transferred these lands to Gulab Singh—the powerful Dogra raja who had served the Sikh court—"in independent possession," in return for a payment of 75 lakh (7.5 million) rupees. Gulab Singh acknowledged British supremacy and agreed to present annually a token tribute of one horse, twelve shawl goats, and three pairs of Kashmiri shawls.

Consequences

The sale of Kashmir to Gulab Singh founded the Dogra dynasty that ruled Jammu and Kashmir until 1947. The treaty's status and the circumstances of the "sale" of a people became central to later criticism and to the historiography of the Kashmir conflict.
