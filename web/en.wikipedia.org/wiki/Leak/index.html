Leak

A leak is a way (usually an opening) for fluid to escape a container or fluid-containing system, such as a tank or a ship's hull, through which the contents of the container can escape or outside matter can enter the container. Leaks are usually unintended and therefore undesired. The word leak usually refers to a gradual loss; a sudden loss is usually called a spill.

Types

Leaks may be of liquid or gas, visible or invisible, and range from slow drips to large flows. In addition to openings such as punctures, cracks, defective seals, and failed joints, fluid can escape through porous materials by permeation. Common examples include leaking pipes and roofs, fuel and refrigerant leaks in machinery, and air leaks in vacuum systems, where gas flows inward.

Detection

Leak detection methods depend on the fluid involved: pressure-decay and bubble testing for simple systems; soapy water on pressurised gas joints; ultrasonic listening; tracer gases such as helium measured with a mass spectrometer for high-vacuum equipment; and dye penetrants for liquid systems. In industry, leak detection systems on pipelines monitor flow and pressure balances to identify and localise ruptures.

Other meanings

By extension, "leak" describes the unauthorised release of confidential information (a news leak, or leaked documents), the unintentional disclosure of answers or content before an official release, and, in electronics and software, phenomena such as current leakage and memory leaks.
