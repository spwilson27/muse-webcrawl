Via Egnatia

Builtc. 146 BC
Length≈ 1,120 km (696 mi)
RouteDyrrachium (Durrës) to Byzantium, via Thessalonica

The Via Egnatia was a Roman road constructed around 146 BC by the proconsul Gnaeus Egnatius, crossing the Balkan Peninsula from the Adriatic Sea to Byzantium. It was the principal overland route linking Rome's eastern provinces with Italy and one of the great military and commercial highways of the ancient world.

Route

The road began at the Adriatic ports of Dyrrachium and Apollonia (in modern Albania) and ran east through the Candavian mountains and past Lake Ohrid to Heraclea Lyncestis and Edessa, reaching Thessalonica on the Aegean. From there it continued through Philippi, Amphipolis and Adrianople across Thrace to Byzantium (later Constantinople, modern Istanbul). Its total length was about 1,120 km, paved and marked with milestones, and it formed the continuation across the Adriatic of the Via Appia from Rome to Brundisium.

History

The Via Egnatia carried Roman armies east — it was used in the civil wars of the late Republic, including by the forces of Caesar and of Brutus and Cassius before Philippi — and later served Byzantine and Ottoman communications for nearly two millennia. The Apostle Paul is traditionally described as travelling along it between Philippi and Thessalonica. A modern motorway in northern Greece, the Egnatia Odos, follows much of the same corridor.
