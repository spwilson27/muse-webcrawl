Olana is a historic house museum and landscape in Greenport, near Hudson, New York, created by the Hudson River School painter Frederic Edwin Church. The centrepiece is a richly ornamented villa in an eclectic Persian-Moorish style, designed by Church with architect Calvert Vaux and built in 1870–1872 on a hilltop overlooking the Hudson River and the Catskill Mountains. It is preserved as the Olana State Historic Site, administered by the New York State Office of Parks in partnership with The Olana Partnership.

History and design

Church, then America's most famous landscape painter, bought the first parcels of the property in 1860, planting thousands of trees and shaping carriage drives, a lake and meadows into a composed landscape he considered among his greatest works. After travel in the Middle East in 1867–68, he named the estate Olana — after a fortress in ancient Persia — and filled the house with stencilled ornament, carved screens, Middle Eastern tiles and his global collections of art and furnishings. He added a studio wing in 1888–1891. Church lived and painted at Olana until his death in 1900; the property stayed in the family until its threatened dispersal in the 1960s prompted a preservation campaign that led New York State to acquire it in 1966.

Today

The house, studio and 250-acre designed landscape are open to the public, with guided tours, exhibitions drawn from Church's work, and restored views across the Hudson Valley that Church composed as "paintings" in land. Olana is regarded as one of the most complete surviving artist's homes and a masterpiece of 19th-century American art and landscape design.
