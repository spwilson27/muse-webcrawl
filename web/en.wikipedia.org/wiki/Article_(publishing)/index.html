Article (publishing)

An article is a written work published in a print or electronic medium, such as a newspaper, magazine, journal, newsletter or website. It may be written for the purpose of delivering news, research results, analysis, description or narrative, and is usually a self-contained piece forming one part of a larger publication. The author of an article is sometimes credited in a byline.

Types

Common types of article include:

  • News articles, reporting current events, conventionally written in an inverted pyramid structure with the most important facts first;
  • Feature articles, longer, more discursive pieces exploring a subject in depth;
  • Scholarly or scientific articles, presenting original research or reviewing a field, published in academic journals after peer review;
  • Opinion articles (op-eds, columns and editorials), expressing the writer's or publication's viewpoint;
  • Encyclopedia articles, summarising established knowledge on a subject, of which this page is itself an example of the genre;
  • Blog posts and online articles, published on websites and digital platforms.

Structure

A typical article consists of a headline, a lead (or lede) paragraph summarising the essential points, a body developing the subject with quotations, evidence and background, and a conclusion. Journalistic articles traditionally answer the "five Ws" — who, what, when, where and why. Scholarly articles instead follow the structure of abstract, introduction, methods, results and discussion.

History

The article emerged as a distinct form with the growth of periodical publishing in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries — essays in journals such as The Tatler and The Spectator, newspaper reports, and scientific papers in the Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society (from 1665). Mass-circulation newspapers and magazines in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries made the article a dominant form of public writing, a position it retains in digital media.
