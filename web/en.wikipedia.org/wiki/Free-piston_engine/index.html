Free-piston engine

A free-piston engine is a linear internal combustion engine in which the motion of the piston is not controlled by a crankshaft but is determined by the balance of gas pressure, combustion, and rebound forces. The design eliminates the crank mechanism, reducing friction and allowing a variable compression ratio.

Applications

Free-piston engines have been used as air compressors (notably the Pescara type), as gas generators feeding turbines, and in hydraulic pumps used in mining equipment. Since the 2000s, interest has revived in the free-piston linear generator, in which magnets on the piston move through coils to produce electricity directly, proposed as a compact range extender for electric vehicles and as a portable power unit.

Advantages and challenges

Potential advantages include mechanical simplicity, high part-load efficiency, and multi-fuel capability. The main challenges are controlling piston motion and stroke precisely, starting the engine, and managing emissions, which require sophisticated electronic control of fuel injection and load.
