Duval County is a county in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Florida. As of the 2020 census its population was about 995,000, making it one of Florida's most populous counties. Its county seat is Jacksonville, with which it has formed a consolidated city–county government since 1968.

History

Duval County was created in 1822, shortly after Florida became a U.S. territory, and was named for William Pope Duval, the first territorial governor of Florida. The county spans the lower course of the St. Johns River, including the historic Cowford settlement that became Jacksonville.

Consolidated government

On 1 October 1968, the governments of Duval County and the city of Jacksonville merged, creating one of the largest cities in the United States by land area. The consolidation was approved by voters in 1967 amid concerns over corruption and inefficient services. The independent municipalities of Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, Atlantic Beach and Baldwin did not fully merge and retain their own governments, though their residents vote in consolidated elections.

Geography

The county covers roughly 918 square miles, including Atlantic coastline, barrier islands and extensive wetlands along the St. Johns River, which flows north through Jacksonville to the Atlantic Ocean. Naval Air Station Jacksonville and Naval Station Mayport give the county a significant military presence.
