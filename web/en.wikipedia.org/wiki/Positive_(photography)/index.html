Positive (photography)

A positive, in photography, is an image in which the tonal values are the same as those of the original scene: light areas appear light and dark areas appear dark. It is the counterpart of a negative, in which tonal values are reversed.

Production

In traditional film photography, a positive print is usually made by projecting or contact-printing a negative onto photographic paper and developing it. Transparency (slide) films such as Kodachrome and Ektachrome are reversal films that yield a positive image directly on the film itself after processing. Some historical processes, including the daguerreotype and tintype, produced a unique direct positive with no negative.

Digital photography

In digital imaging the concept carries over: a processed image file intended for viewing is a "positive," while a raw sensor readout with inverted or unprocessed values is sometimes loosely compared to a negative. Film scanners likewise convert negatives into positive digital images by inverting the tones and removing the orange mask of colour negative film.
