Wine tourism

Wine tourism
FocusTasting, purchasing and experiencing wine where it is produced

Wine tourism, also known as enotourism, is tourism whose purpose is the tasting, consumption or purchase of wine, often at or near the source of production. It typically includes visits to wineries, vineyards and wine festivals, and in many regions has become a significant source of income for wine producers, supplementing or even exceeding revenue from cellar-door wine sales.

Wine tourists at a vineyard tasting room

History

Wine tourism has a relatively long history in Europe, where travellers on the Grand Tour visited the wine regions of France, Italy and the German Rhine valley. The modern industry developed in the United States during the 1970s, particularly in California's Napa Valley, where wineries began marketing tastings and tours directly to consumers. The practice spread to Australia, South Africa, Chile, Argentina and New Zealand during the 1980s and 1990s as producers in the "New World" adopted direct-to-consumer marketing.

Activities

Wine tourism commonly includes guided tours of vineyards and production facilities, structured tastings of a producer's range, food and wine pairing experiences, harvest participation, and stays in vineyard accommodation. Many regions have developed signposted "wine roads", such as the Alsace Wine Route in France and the German Wine Route, to channel visitors between producers. Wine festivals, including harvest festivals and new-wine celebrations such as Beaujolais Nouveau releases, anchor seasonal tourism.

Economic impact

For many small producers, cellar-door sales to tourists carry higher margins than wholesale distribution. Wine tourism also supports local hospitality businesses. Critics note potential negative effects, including increased traffic, drunk driving, and the conversion of agricultural regions into leisure landscapes.
