Fixed income analysis

Fixed income analysis is the process of evaluating fixed income securities such as bonds, and determining their valuation, risk, and suitability for a portfolio. Practitioners assess expected cash flows, discount them at appropriate rates, and measure the sensitivity of prices to changes in interest rates and credit conditions.

Valuation

The fundamental valuation approach discounts a bond's contractual cash flows — coupon payments and principal repayment — at a yield reflecting the risk-free rate plus a credit spread. Key measures include yield to maturity, current yield, and option-adjusted spread for bonds with embedded options such as call provisions.

Risk measures

  • Duration — measures a bond's price sensitivity to interest-rate changes; Macaulay and modified duration are the standard forms.
  • Convexity — captures the curvature of the price–yield relationship, refining duration-based estimates.
  • Credit analysis — evaluation of the issuer's ability to pay, often informed by credit ratings, financial ratios, and covenant structures.
  • Yield curve analysis — the term structure of interest rates is modelled and used for relative-value trading and risk management.

Applications

Fixed income analysis underlies bond portfolio management, liability-driven investing by pension funds and insurers, and the pricing of interest-rate derivatives. Analysts also use measures such as DV01 (dollar value of a basis point) to hedge portfolios.
