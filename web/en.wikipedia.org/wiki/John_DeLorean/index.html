John Zachary DeLorean (January 6, 1925 – March 19, 2005) was an American automobile engineer and executive, widely known for his work at General Motors — where he is credited with creating the first muscle car, the Pontiac GTO — and as the founder of the DeLorean Motor Company, whose stainless-steel, gull-winged DMC DeLorean became famous as the time machine in the Back to the Future films.

General Motors career

DeLorean joined GM's Pontiac division in 1956 and rose rapidly, becoming Pontiac's chief engineer and then general manager in 1965 — the youngest division head in GM history. At Pontiac he oversaw the GTO (1964), the Grand Prix, and the Firebird; he then ran Chevrolet (1969) and became a GM vice president in 1972 before resigning in 1973, frustrated with corporate bureaucracy.

DeLorean Motor Company

In 1975 DeLorean founded his own company to build a futuristic "ethical" sports car. Backed by celebrity investors and roughly $175 million in British government loans, he built a factory at Dunmurry, Northern Ireland. The DMC-12 (later simply the DeLorean) entered production in 1981 with a stainless-steel body and gull-wing doors designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro, but suffered quality problems, a weak engine, and a collapsing market. The company went into receivership in February 1982 after producing roughly 9,000 cars.

Arrest and acquittal

In October 1982 DeLorean was arrested in Los Angeles and charged with conspiring to smuggle cocaine worth some $24 million to rescue his company. At trial in 1984 he was acquitted on grounds of police entrapment — the operation had been set up by government informants. Subsequent litigation over the company's finances dogged him for years; he declared bankruptcy in 1999. He died in 2005.
