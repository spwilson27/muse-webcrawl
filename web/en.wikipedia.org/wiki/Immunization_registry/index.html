Immunization registry

Registries consolidate vaccination records from many providers

An immunization registry or immunization information system (IIS) is a confidential, population-based, computerized database that records all immunization doses administered by participating providers to persons residing within a given geopolitical area. Registries consolidate records that would otherwise be scattered across many clinics, pharmacies, and schools.

Functions

A fully functioning registry can record new vaccinations, generate reminder and recall notices for doses that are due or overdue, produce official vaccination records for school and childcare entry, and compute vaccination coverage rates for a population. At the point of care, a clinician can query the registry to determine which vaccines a patient still needs, avoiding missed opportunities and duplicate doses.

Development

In the United States, state and city immunization information systems developed from the 1990s onward with support from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, which sets functional standards for IIS. Interoperability standards such as HL7 messaging allow electronic health records to exchange data with registries. Many other countries operate national or regional vaccination registers with similar aims, including Australia, whose Australian Immunisation Register covers the whole population.

Privacy

Because registries hold identifiable health information, they operate under strict confidentiality rules: access is limited to authorized users such as healthcare providers and public health officials, and parents or patients may in some jurisdictions review or correct their records.
