Consecrated virgin

A consecrated virgin is a woman in the Catholic Church who, having resolved to live in perpetual virginity for the sake of the Kingdom of Heaven, is consecrated to God by her diocesan bishop according to the approved liturgical rite, and is mystically betrothed to Christ. Consecrated virgins living in the world form the Ordo Virginum (Order of Virgins).

History

The Order of Virgins is among the earliest forms of consecrated life in the Church, attested from apostolic times; many virgin martyrs of the early Church, such as Agnes and Cecilia, were held as its exemplars. The rite fell into relative disuse as communal monastic life predominated, but was restored for women living in the world by Pope Paul VI in 1970 following the Second Vatican Council. Canon 604 of the 1983 Code of Canon Law recognises the order.

Life

Consecrated virgins remain in their ordinary circumstances, supporting themselves by their own work, and devote themselves to prayer, works of penance, the service of their brethren and the apostolate according to their gifts.
