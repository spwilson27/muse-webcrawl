Two-person rule

The two-person rule, also known as the two-man rule, is a control mechanism designed to achieve a high level of security for critical operations, requiring the presence and agreement of two authorised persons at all times. It is most famously applied to the handling and launch of nuclear weapons, where no single individual may act alone.

Nuclear weapons

In the United States, launch procedures require two officers in a missile launch control centre to turn keys simultaneously, in facilities separated so that one person cannot turn both. Comparable rules govern access to nuclear weapons storage (the "no-lone zone") and the authentication of emergency action messages. Other nuclear-armed states employ analogous arrangements, such as the two-key and permissive action link systems.

Other applications

The principle is used wherever a lone operator poses an unacceptable risk: two pilots on an airliner flight deck, dual control of bank vaults and safety deposit boxes, paired verification in cryptography and certificate authorities, two-person integrity in intelligence facilities, and buddy systems in hazardous work such as diving, confined spaces and electrical maintenance. In aviation, after the Germanwings Flight 9525 crash in 2015, many airlines temporarily mandated two crew members in the cockpit at all times.

Related concepts

The two-person rule is a form of separation of duties and an application of the four-eyes principle: sensitive actions require independent confirmation, reducing the risk of error, coercion or malicious action by a single individual.
