Charles Dickens

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Charles Dickens in later life: full beard, intense gaze, seated at his writing desk at Gad's Hill]

Charles John Huffam Dickens (7 February 1812 – 9 June 1870) was an English novelist, journalist and social critic. He created some of the world's best-known fictional characters and is regarded by many as the greatest novelist of the Victorian era. His works enjoyed unprecedented popularity during his lifetime and, by the 20th century, critics and scholars had recognised him as a literary genius.

Born in Portsmouth, Dickens was forced at 12 to leave school and work in a boot-blacking factory while his father was in debtors' prison — an experience that haunted his fiction. He became a parliamentary reporter and then a novelist with The Pickwick Papers (1836). Most of his novels appeared in monthly or weekly instalments, a format that let him shape plots to public reaction.

Major novels

His fifteen novels include Oliver Twist, Nicholas Nickleby, The Old Curiosity Shop, A Christmas Carol (1843, a novella that defined the modern Christmas), David Copperfield, Bleak House, A Tale of Two Cities and Great Expectations (1861). His characters — Scrooge, Oliver, Miss Havisham, Mr Micawber — have passed into the language.
