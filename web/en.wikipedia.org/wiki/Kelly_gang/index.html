Kelly gang

The Kelly gang was an Australian bushranging gang active in north-eastern Victoria in the late 1870s. The gang consisted of Ned Kelly, his brother Dan Kelly, and their associates Joe Byrne and Steve Hart. Between 1878 and 1880 the gang robbed banks, killed three police officers, and became the most wanted outlaws in the colony of Victoria.

Formation

The gang formed in the aftermath of the Fitzpatrick incident of April 1878, in which Constable Alexander Fitzpatrick accused members of the Kelly family of attempted murder. Ned and Dan Kelly went into hiding in the Wombat Ranges, where they were joined by Byrne and Hart. A police party sent to arrest them was ambushed at Stringybark Creek on 26 October 1878, where Ned Kelly shot dead three policemen: Sergeant Michael Kennedy and Constables Michael Scanlan and Thomas Lonigan. The gang was subsequently declared outlawed under the Felons Apprehension Act, meaning they could be shot on sight.

Bank robberies

In December 1878 the gang robbed the National Bank at Euroa, taking over £2,000. In February 1879 they crossed into New South Wales and robbed the Bank of New South Wales at Jerilderie, where Ned Kelly dictated the famous Jerilderie Letter, a lengthy statement justifying his actions and denouncing police persecution of his family and of poor Irish selectors.

Glenrowan and the end of the gang

In June 1880, after the gang murdered police informer Aaron Sherritt, the Kellys planned to derail a police train at Glenrowan and ambush the survivors. The plan failed when schoolteacher Thomas Curnow warned the train. In the ensuing siege at the Glenrowan Inn on 28 June 1880, Joe Byrne, Dan Kelly and Steve Hart were killed, and Ned Kelly — wearing his famous suit of homemade plate armour — was captured after being shot in the legs. Ned Kelly was tried in Melbourne, convicted of murdering Constable Lonigan, and hanged at the Old Melbourne Gaol on 11 November 1880.

Legacy

The Kelly gang remains a central element of Australian folklore. Ned Kelly in particular has been portrayed variously as a murderous criminal and as a folk hero who resisted unjust authority, and the gang's story has inspired numerous books, films, and artworks.
