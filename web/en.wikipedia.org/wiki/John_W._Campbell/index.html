John Wood Campbell Jr. (8 June 1910 – 11 July 1971) was an American science fiction writer and editor. He was editor of Astounding Science Fiction (later Analog Science Fiction and Fact) from late 1937 until his death, and is generally credited with shaping the so-called Golden Age of Science Fiction. Isaac Asimov called him "the most powerful force in science fiction ever" and said the first ten years of his editorship made Astounding dominant in the field.

Early writing career

Campbell studied physics at MIT and Duke University. He began publishing science fiction while still a teenager, writing space opera in the style of E. E. Smith, then, under the pseudonym Don A. Stuart, a series of more atmospheric stories including "Twilight" (1934) and "Who Goes There?" (1938), the novella later adapted as the film The Thing from Another World (1951) and John Carpenter's The Thing (1982).

Editor of Astounding

Taking over Astounding Stories in 1937, Campbell demanded rigorous, idea-driven fiction about plausible futures and the human consequences of technology. He discovered or developed a generation of writers including Isaac Asimov, Robert A. Heinlein, L. Sprague de Camp, Lester del Rey, Theodore Sturgeon, A. E. van Vogt and Arthur C. Clarke. Stories he assigned or shaped — Asimov's Foundation and Robot series among them — defined science fiction for decades.

Later controversies

Campbell's forceful editorial views, his enthusiasms for pseudoscientific causes such as Dianetics (which he promoted in 1950), psionics and the Dean drive, and his provocative editorials on race and society alienated many writers and readers in the 1950s and 1960s. The field's centre of gravity shifted toward newer magazines and voices, though he edited Analog until his sudden death in 1971.

Legacy

The John W. Campbell Award for Best New Writer (now the Astounding Award) and the John W. Campbell Memorial Award for Best Science Fiction Novel were named for him; in 1996 he was inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame. Modern assessments credit his talent-spotting and story sense while also critically examining his prejudices, a reassessment that led to the renaming of the new-writer award in 2019.
