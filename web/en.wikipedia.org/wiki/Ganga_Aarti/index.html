Ganga Aarti

Ganga Aarti
Type: Hindu ritual of worship
Deity: Ganga
Performed: Daily at sunset
Famous locations: Varanasi, Haridwar, Rishikesh

Ganga Aarti is a Hindu ritual ceremony in which light from fire (offered in lamps, usually lit with ghee or camphor) is offered to the goddess Ganga, the personification of the Ganges river. The ceremony takes place every evening at dusk on the ghats (riverfront steps) of the Ganges.


Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat, Varanasi

Ceremony

At Varanasi's Dashashwamedh Ghat, a group of priests in identical saffron dress perform the aarti in synchronised movements, raising large fire lamps, incense and conch shells to the accompaniment of hymns and bells, watched by thousands of devotees from the steps and from boats on the river. Similar ceremonies are performed at Har Ki Pauri in Haridwar and at Triveni Ghat in Rishikesh.

Significance

The ritual thanks the goddess for the river's life-giving water and seeks her blessing and purification. Aarti (also spelled arti) is a general Hindu rite of offering light, performed for many deities; the Ganga Aarti is among the most celebrated, drawing pilgrims and tourists year-round.
