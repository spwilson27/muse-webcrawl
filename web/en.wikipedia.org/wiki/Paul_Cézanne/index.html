Paul Cézanne

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Mont Sainte-Victoire seen from a Provençal hillside: the mountain built from patches of ochre, green and blue]

Paul Cézanne (19 January 1839 – 22 October 1906) was a French Post-Impressionist painter whose work introduced new modes of representation and influenced avant-garde artistic movements of the early 20th century. He sought to make of Impressionism 'something solid and durable, like the art of museums', treating nature by 'the cylinder, the sphere, the cone'.

Working largely in isolation in Aix-en-Provence, Cézanne painted Mont Sainte-Victoire dozens of times, along with still lifes of apples and oranges, card players (the five versions of The Card Players) and the late Large Bathers. Picasso and Matisse are both reported to have called him 'the father of us all'; his influence on Cubism and abstract art is fundamental.
