4179 Toutatis

Radar model of Toutatis, an elongated contact-binary-like body about 4.5 km long.

4179 Toutatis, provisional designation 1989 AC, is an elongated, stony asteroid and slow tumbler, classified as a near-Earth object and potentially hazardous asteroid of the Apollo group, approximately 2.5 kilometers in average diameter. It was discovered on 4 January 1989 by French astronomer Christian Pollas at the Caussols site of the Côte d'Azur Observatory.

Toutatis is named after the Celtic god Toutatis, familiar to many readers from the Asterix comics, in which the Gauls swear "By Toutatis!". The asteroid became famous for its extremely chaotic, non-principal-axis rotation and for a series of close approaches to Earth that allowed detailed radar imaging.

Orbit and classification

Toutatis orbits the Sun at a distance of roughly 0.94–4.1 AU once every four years, in a 1:4 resonance with Earth and a 4:1 resonance with Jupiter. Its orbit brings it close to Earth about every four years; on 29 September 2004 it passed within 0.0104 AU (about four lunar distances), the closest known approach of an asteroid of its size at the time until 2069. Because of repeated planetary perturbations, its orbit cannot be predicted reliably more than a few centuries into the future.

Physical characteristics

Radar observations from Goldstone and Arecibo revealed an irregular, elongated body roughly 4.5 × 2.4 × 1.9 km, possibly two formerly separate bodies in contact. The surface bears craters, including a large depression about 800 meters wide. Toutatis rotates in a tumbling state: it spins about its long axis with a period of about 5.4 days while that axis precesses with a period of about 7.4 days, a consequence of torques from close planetary encounters acting on its irregular shape.

Exploration

On 13 December 2012 the Chinese lunar probe Chang'e 2, retasked after its Moon mission, flew past Toutatis at a distance of under 100 kilometers and returned images showing a rocky, cratered surface, making China the fourth space power to visit an asteroid.
