Dark tetrad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The dark tetrad is a set of four related socially aversive personality traits: narcissism, Machiavellianism, subclinical psychopathy, and everyday sadism. It extends the earlier "dark triad" model (narcissism, Machiavellianism and psychopathy) proposed by Delroy Paulhus and Kevin Williams in 2002, with sadism added on the strength of research showing it predicts antisocial behaviour beyond the other three.

The four traits

Narcissism is characterised by grandiosity, entitlement, dominance and a sense of superiority. Machiavellianism involves cynical manipulation and exploitation of others, deceit and a lack of morality. Subclinical psychopathy is marked by impulsivity, callousness, thrill-seeking and low empathy. Everyday sadism is the tendency to derive pleasure from cruelty to others, including "vicarious" sadism such as enjoying violent entertainment.

Measurement and correlates

The traits are typically measured by self-report instruments such as the Short Dark Triad (SD3), the Dirty Dozen, and the Short Sadistic Impulse Scale. All four traits correlate with a common core of callousness and dishonesty (the "dark core" or D factor), and with outcomes including bullying, trolling, workplace misconduct and criminal behaviour. Critics note substantial overlap between Machiavellianism and psychopathy measures and question whether they are truly distinct. See also Dark triad, Narcissism, and Psychopathy.
