The Battle of Cowpens was a land battle of the American Revolutionary War fought on 17 January 1781 at the Cowpens, a frontier pasture in northwestern South Carolina. Brigadier General Daniel Morgan, commanding a mixed force of Continentals, militia, and cavalry, inflicted a crushing defeat on the British force of Lieutenant Colonel Banastre Tarleton. The battle is frequently cited as a tactical masterpiece and a turning point of the war in the South.

Background

After the American victory at Kings Mountain and the replacement of Horatio Gates by Nathanael Greene, Greene divided his small army, sending Morgan west to threaten the British post at Ninety Six. Lord Cornwallis detached Tarleton with about 1,150 men — legion cavalry and infantry, the 17th Light Dragoons, the 7th Foot, and a battalion of the 71st Highlanders — to destroy Morgan's command.

The battle

Morgan chose ground at the Cowpens with the Broad River behind him, discouraging militia flight, and arranged his men in three lines: sharpshooters, militia instructed to fire two volleys and retire, and his Continental regulars on a low rise, with William Washington's cavalry in reserve. Tarleton attacked at dawn after a night march. The militia's planned withdrawal was mistaken by the British for a rout; when the British rushed forward in disorder, Morgan's Continentals about-faced and delivered a volley at close range, then charged with the bayonet while the returning militia and Washington's cavalry struck both flanks — a rare double envelopment in the warfare of the period.

Outcome

Tarleton's force was effectively destroyed: over 100 killed, more than 200 wounded, and some 500–600 captured, out of about 1,150 engaged; American losses were fewer than 100. Tarleton escaped with a remnant of cavalry. The loss of this elite striking force crippled Cornwallis's southern army and began the sequence of operations that ended with his surrender at Yorktown in October 1781. The battlefield is preserved as Cowpens National Battlefield.
