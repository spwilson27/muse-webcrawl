Barney Clark (patient)

Barney Bailey Clark (January 21, 1921 – March 23, 1983) was an American retired dentist who became the first person to receive a permanent artificial heart. On December 2, 1982, surgeons at the University of Utah, led by William DeVries, implanted the Jarvik-7 device in Clark, who was dying of cardiomyopathy and ineligible for a transplant. He survived 112 days on the device.

The operation

The Jarvik-7, designed by Robert Jarvik, replaced both ventricles of Clark's heart and was driven by an external pneumatic console connected by air lines through his chest. Clark never left hospital, suffering seizures, kidney problems, and episodes of confusion, but remained lucid for stretches and gave interviews.

Legacy

Clark's case provoked worldwide attention and intense ethical debate about experimental treatment, quality of life, and informed consent. He died of multi-organ failure after 112 days; the Jarvik-7 was still functioning. Later recipients survived longer, and total artificial hearts remain in use as a bridge to transplantation.
