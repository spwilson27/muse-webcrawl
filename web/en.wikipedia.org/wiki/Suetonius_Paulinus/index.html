Gaius Suetonius Paulinus (fl. 1st century AD) was a Roman general best known as the commander who defeated the British rebellion of Boudica, queen of the Iceni tribe, in AD 60 or 61. He was governor of Roman Britain from AD 58 to 62.

Early career

Paulinus first appears in history in AD 42, when as praetor he served in Mauretania, suppressing a revolt. He became the first Roman recorded to have crossed the Atlas Mountains, and wrote a description of the geography beyond them that was later used by Pliny the Elder. He was suffect consul, probably in AD 66.

Governor of Britain

Appointed governor of Britain in AD 58, Paulinus pursued an aggressive policy of subduing the tribes of Wales. In AD 60 or 61 he led an assault on the island of Mona (Anglesey), a stronghold of the druids, massacring the druids and destroying their sacred groves.

Boudica's revolt

While Paulinus was campaigning in Wales, the Iceni and their neighbours rose in revolt under Boudica, sacking Camulodunum (Colchester), Londinium (London), and Verulamium (St Albans). Paulinus rushed back with the Fourteenth and part of the Twentieth legions, and despite being heavily outnumbered, defeated the rebels decisively at the Battle of Watling Street, reportedly killing tens of thousands. His harsh punitive measures afterwards, however, contributed to his replacement by Publius Petronius Turpilianus in AD 62.

Later career

In AD 69, during the Year of the Four Emperors, Paulinus was a senior general and adviser on the side of Otho. After Otho's defeat at Bedriacum, Paulinus was captured by Vitellius's forces but obtained his pardon, pleading that he had deliberately lost the battle. His subsequent fate is unknown.
