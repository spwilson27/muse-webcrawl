Leonhard Seppala

Leonhard Seppala
Born: 14 September 1877, Skibotn, Norway
Died: 28 January 1967, Seattle, Washington
Known for: 1925 serum run to Nome

Leonhard Seppala (September 14, 1877 – January 28, 1967) was a Norwegian-born American musher who became one of the most celebrated dog sled racers of his era. He is best remembered for driving the longest and most dangerous leg of the 1925 serum run to Nome, Alaska, which delivered diphtheria antitoxin to the isolated town.

Early life and racing

Born in northern Norway to a family of Kven and Sami descent, Seppala emigrated to Nome during the Alaska gold rush and worked for the Pioneer Mining Company. After a disastrous first race in 1914, he dominated the All Alaska Sweepstakes in 1915, 1916 and 1917 with his small, fast Siberian dogs, establishing his reputation and kennel.

The serum run

In January–February 1925 Seppala, with lead dog Togo, covered the longest single leg of the relay — some 260 miles (420 km) round trip in whiteout conditions and extreme cold, including a perilous shortcut across the breaking ice of Norton Sound. Although the final driver, Gunnar Kaasen with Balto, received much of the publicity, historians generally credit Seppala and Togo with the run's greatest feat.

Later life

Seppala toured the United States with his team, helping to popularise the Siberian Husky breed; his dogs founded bloodlines central to both the racing "Seppala Siberian Sleddog" strain and the modern Siberian Husky. He later raced in New England and competed at the 1932 Winter Olympics demonstration event. He is commemorated by the Iditarod's Leonhard Seppala Humanitarian Award.
