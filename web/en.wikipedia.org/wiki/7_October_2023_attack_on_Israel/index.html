The 7 October 2023 attack on Israel, referred to by Hamas as Operation Al-Aqsa Flood, was a coordinated assault launched from the Gaza Strip into southern Israel on the Jewish holiday of Simchat Torah. It was the deadliest single day in Israel's history and the trigger for the ensuing Gaza war.

The attack

In the early morning of 7 October 2023, Palestinian armed groups led by Hamas fired several thousand rockets into Israel while some 3,000 militants crossed the Gaza–Israel barrier at dozens of points, using bulldozers, paragliders, boats and motorbikes. Fighters attacked military bases and civilian communities in the "Gaza envelope", including the kibbutzim Be'eri, Kfar Aza and Nir Oz, the city of Sderot, and an outdoor music festival near Re'im, where 364 people were killed.

Casualties and hostages

Around 1,200 people were killed in Israel, including civilians, soldiers and foreign nationals, and more than 3,400 were wounded. Attackers seized 251 hostages and took them into Gaza, among them women, children and elderly people. The Israeli government formally declared war the following day — its first formal declaration since 1973 — and began a large-scale air and, later, ground campaign in Gaza.

Aftermath

The attack prompted mass mobilisation of Israeli reservists, the evacuation of communities near Gaza and the Lebanon border, and widespread international reaction. Subsequent ceasefires and exchanges in late 2023 and 2025 freed many hostages in return for Palestinian prisoners. The events of 7 October are commemorated in Israel as a national trauma comparable, in public discourse, to a "second Holocaust" for some survivors' families, while the ensuing war reshaped the politics of the region.
