Acetylcysteine, also known as N-acetylcysteine (NAC), is a medication and dietary supplement derived from the amino acid cysteine. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. Its best-established uses are as an antidote to paracetamol (acetaminophen) poisoning and as a mucolytic agent that loosens thick mucus in respiratory disease.

Medical uses

Paracetamol overdose

In overdose, paracetamol is metabolised to the toxic compound NAPQI, which is normally detoxified by glutathione. When glutathione stores are exhausted, NAPQI damages liver cells. Acetylcysteine replenishes cysteine for glutathione synthesis and can also bind NAPQI directly. Given intravenously or orally within about eight hours of an overdose it is highly protective against liver failure; effectiveness is assessed using the Rumack–Matthew nomogram.

Mucolytic

Inhaled or oral acetylcysteine breaks disulfide bonds in mucoproteins, reducing mucus viscosity. It is used in chronic bronchitis, cystic fibrosis and other conditions with viscid secretions, though evidence for long-term benefit in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is mixed.

Other uses

NAC has been investigated for preventing contrast-induced kidney injury (with largely negative trial results), for psychiatric conditions including schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and obsessive–compulsive-related behaviours, and as an antioxidant supplement. These uses remain off-label or investigational.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting and a rotten-egg odour and taste from its sulfur content. Intravenous administration can cause anaphylactoid reactions — flushing, itching, rash and occasionally bronchospasm or low blood pressure — usually related to the infusion rate. Inhaled preparations may provoke bronchospasm in people with asthma.

Mechanism and chemistry

Acetylcysteine is the N-acetyl derivative of L-cysteine. After administration it is deacetylated to cysteine, the rate-limiting precursor of glutathione, and it also acts directly as a free-radical scavenger through its thiol group.
