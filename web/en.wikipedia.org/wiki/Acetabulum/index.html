Acetabulum

Part ofPelvis, hip joint
ArticulationsHead of femur
Formed fromIlium, ischium, pubis

The acetabulum (plural: acetabula) is the concave, cup-shaped surface of the pelvis with which the head of the femur articulates to form the hip joint. Its name comes from the Latin for a small vinegar cup.

Structure

All three bones of the os coxae — the ilium, ischium, and pubis — contribute to the acetabulum, meeting at the triradiate cartilage in children. The articular surface, the lunate surface, is a horseshoe-shaped band of cartilage-covered bone surrounding the non-articular acetabular fossa, which contains fat and the ligament of the head of the femur. The acetabular notch at the bottom is bridged by the transverse acetabular ligament.

Clinical significance

Acetabular fractures, usually from high-energy trauma such as road traffic collisions, can disrupt the hip joint and require surgical fixation. The shape and depth of the acetabulum are central to total hip replacement, in which an artificial cup is implanted, and to conditions such as hip dysplasia and femoroacetabular impingement.
