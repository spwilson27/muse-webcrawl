Lake Assal (Arabic: بحيرة عسل‎) is a crater lake in the Danakil/Afar Depression of central Djibouti, at the northern end of the Great Rift Valley. Its surface lies about 155 metres (509 ft) below sea level, making it the lowest point in Africa and the third-lowest point on Earth after the Dead Sea and Lake Baikal's regional depressions.

Geography

The lake occupies a volcanic depression surrounded by dormant volcanoes and black basalt lava fields. It has no outlet; water is supplied by underground springs connected to the nearby Gulf of Tadjoura and is lost only by evaporation in one of the hottest and driest climates on Earth, where daytime temperatures regularly exceed 40 °C.

Salinity

Lake Assal's salinity of roughly 348 grams per litre exceeds that of ocean water tenfold and rivals the Dead Sea. Thick white salt crusts cover its shores, and salt has been harvested for centuries by the Afar people, who transported it by camel caravan into the Ethiopian highlands — a trade that continues today, alongside modern industrial salt extraction.

Significance

The lake and its surrounding volcanic landscape are protected as part of Djibouti's national heritage and are a candidate UNESCO World Heritage landscape. The region sits atop the Afar Triple Junction, where three tectonic plates are pulling apart, making it a major site for geological research into continental rifting.
