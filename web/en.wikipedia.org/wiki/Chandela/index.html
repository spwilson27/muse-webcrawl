The Chandelas were a Rajput dynasty of central India that ruled the region of Bundelkhand (historically known as Jejakabhukti) from the 9th to the 14th century CE. Initially feudatories of the Gurjara-Pratiharas, they became an independent power in the 10th century and resisted invasions by the Ghaznavids and later the Delhi Sultanate. The Chandelas are best remembered for building the celebrated temples of Khajuraho, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Origins and rise

The dynasty was founded by Nannuka (c. 831–845 CE). Later Chandela legends claim descent from the legendary lunar dynasty (Chandravansha) through the sage Chandratreya, while some bardic traditions connect them with the local Gond population. The early Chandelas ruled as vassals of the Gurjara-Pratihara empire. Yashodharman (c. 915–950) expanded their power, and his son Dhanga (c. 950–999) asserted full independence, extending the kingdom and patronising the great temples at Khajuraho.

Peak and invasions

Under Vidyadhara (c. 1003–1035), the Chandela kingdom reached its greatest extent. Vidyadhara resisted the invasions of Mahmud of Ghazni in 1019 and 1022; the encounters ended without a decisive Ghaznavid conquest of the stronghold of Kalinjar. The dynasty weakened during the 11th and 12th centuries under pressure from neighbouring powers such as the Kalachuris and Paramaras. The Chandela king Paramardi was defeated by Prithviraj Chauhan in 1182 and by Qutb al-Din Aibak in 1202–1203, who captured Kalinjar and Mahoba.

Decline

The Chandelas recovered Kalinjar and continued to rule a reduced territory into the early 14th century. The last known Chandela ruler, Hammiravarman (c. 1288–1311), was defeated by Alauddin Khalji's forces, and the dynasty disappears from the record by about 1315. Collateral branches and claimed descendants survived as local chiefs in Bundelkhand.

Architecture

Chandela patronage produced some of the finest examples of Nagara-style temple architecture. At Khajuraho, the dynasty built dozens of Hindu and Jain temples between roughly 950 and 1050 CE, of which about 25 survive, famed for their sculptural decoration. Other major Chandela monuments include the fortress of Kalinjar, the fort at Ajaigarh, and temples at Mahoba and Dudhai. The Chandelas also built numerous lakes, stepwells, and irrigation works across Bundelkhand.
