Leading edge

Leading edge
Aerodynamics termFront edge of an airfoil or wing
OppositeTrailing edge
Devices fitted thereSlats, Krueger flaps, leading-edge extensions, de-icing boots

The leading edge is the part of the wing that first contacts the air; equivalently, it can be defined as the foremost edge of an airfoil section. Defined perpendicular to the airflow, it lies on the line tangent to the forward-most points of the wing's mean camber line. A wing may have a straight or swept leading edge, and its radius and shaping strongly influence the wing's stall behaviour and high-speed performance.

At the leading edge the airflow divides at the stagnation point, passing above and below the wing. Sharp leading edges suit thin supersonic airfoils but stall abruptly at high angles of attack; rounded leading edges give gentler stall characteristics typical of subsonic aircraft.

Leading-edge devices

High-lift devices fitted at the leading edge include slats, which open a slot delaying separation, Krueger flaps, slots and leading-edge root extensions (LERX), which generate vortices over the wing at high incidence on fighter aircraft. The leading edge often houses pneumatic de-icing boots or bleed-air anti-icing ducts, and on the Space Shuttle orbiters it was protected by reinforced carbon–carbon panels against re-entry heating.

Other uses

The term is also used in surfboard and hydrofoil design, in fan and propeller blades, and metaphorically in business and technology writing ("leading edge" or "bleeding edge" technology).
