Nathan "Nearest" Green

Nathan "Nearest" Green (c. 1820 – unknown) was an American head stiller, commonly referred to as a master distiller, who was an enslaved African-American man later credited as the first African-American master distiller on record in the United States. He is best known as the teacher and mentor of a young Jack Daniel, founder of the Jack Daniel's Tennessee whiskey distillery, and is now regarded as a foundational figure in American whiskey history.

Life

Green was enslaved by Landis & Green, a Tennessee firm that leased out his distilling services. In the 1850s he was placed on the Lynchburg-area farm and distillery of preacher Dan Call, where he instructed the young Jasper Newton "Jack" Daniel in distilling — including the charcoal-mellowing "Lincoln County Process" that defines Tennessee whiskey, a technique with roots in West African charcoal-filtering practices. After emancipation, Green continued to work for Daniel as a free man and is believed to have served as the first master distiller of the Jack Daniel Distillery when it was established in 1866; his sons also worked at the distillery.

Recognition

Although Green's role circulated in oral tradition around Lynchburg, the Jack Daniel's company itself did not prominently acknowledge it for many decades. In 2016–2017, reporting and company statements formally recognised Green as Daniel's mentor and first master distiller. In 2017 entrepreneur Fawn Weaver launched Uncle Nearest Premium Whiskey, named in his honour, and founded the Nearest Green Foundation, which funds scholarships for his descendants. Green was inducted into the Tennessee Whiskey Hall of Fame, and in 2021 the distillery's master distiller suite in Lynchburg was named after him.
