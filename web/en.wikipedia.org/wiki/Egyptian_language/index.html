Egyptian language

The Egyptian language is an extinct branch of the Afro-Asiatic languages spoken in ancient Egypt. It has the longest documented history of any language, from c. 3400 BC to the 17th century AD in its Coptic form, now surviving only as the liturgical language of the Coptic Church.

Egyptian
Family: Afro-Asiatic
Stages: Old, Middle, Late, Demotic, Coptic
Scripts: Hieroglyphs, hieratic, demotic, Coptic

Stages

Old Egyptian (to c. 2000 BC, Pyramid Texts), Middle Egyptian (classical literary standard), Late Egyptian (vernacular of the New Kingdom), Demotic (from c. 650 BC), and Coptic (written in a Greek-based alphabet, from the 3rd century AD). Typologically it shows VSO to analytic shifts, consonantal roots, and grammatical gender.

Study

Decipherment after the Rosetta Stone opened Egyptology; Coptic preserved vowels and helped reconstruct pronunciation.
