Valentinian I

Valentinian I
Reign: 364 – 375 AD
Born: 321, Cibalae, Pannonia
Died: 17 November 375, Brigetio
Successor: Gratian and Valentinian II (West), Valens (East)

Valentinian I (Latin: Flavius Valentinianus; 321 – 17 November 375), also called Valentinian the Great, was Roman emperor from 364 to 375. Born at Cibalae in Pannonia, the son of the officer Gratianus Funarius, he rose through the army and was proclaimed Augustus at Nicaea on 26 February 364, following the sudden death of the emperor Jovian. He immediately divided the empire with his brother Valens, taking the West himself and giving Valens the East — the last effective division of the empire between co-emperors of equal standing.

Reign

Valentinian spent most of his reign campaigning on the Rhine and Danube frontiers. He rebuilt the fortifications along both rivers, constructing a chain of forts and watchtowers, campaigned against the Alamanni in Gaul — winning the hard-fought Battle of Solicinium in 368 — and later fought the Quadi and Sarmatians on the Danube. His reign saw the defence of Roman Britain coordinated by Count Theodosius after the "Great Conspiracy" of 367. In religious matters he was a Nicene Christian but broadly tolerant toward pagans and rival Christian factions, and he issued measures on education, taxation and the legal defence of the poor (the defensores plebis). He made Trier and then Milan his principal western residences.

Death and legacy

Valentinian died at Brigetio on 17 November 375, traditionally said to have burst a blood vessel in a rage during an audience with envoys of the Quadi. He was succeeded in the West by his sons Gratian and the child Valentinian II. Historians, following Ammianus Marcellinus, judge him a stern, sometimes brutal ruler but an energetic and competent soldier-emperor who stabilised the western frontiers for a generation. He founded the Valentinianic dynasty, which ruled until 455.
