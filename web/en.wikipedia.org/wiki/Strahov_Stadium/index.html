Strahov Stadium

The Great Strahov Stadium (Czech: Velký strahovský stadion) is a stadium in the Strahov district of Prague, Czech Republic. It was built for displays of synchronized gymnastics on a massive scale, with a field three times as long and three times as wide as the standard Association football field. At its completion it was the largest stadium in the world, with a capacity of around 250,000 spectators.

Construction

Construction began in 1926 for the Sokol slet (gymnastics festival) and the stadium was expanded over the following decades, reaching its greatest extent in the 1948–1975 period, when it hosted the mass Spartakiad displays of communist Czechoslovakia. The playing field measures about 310 by 200 metres.

Later use

After the fall of communism in 1989, the stadium fell into disuse and disrepair. It has occasionally hosted major concerts — including performances by the Rolling Stones, Guns N' Roses, U2, and Pink Floyd — and is currently used as a training centre for the football club AC Sparta Prague, with eight football pitches inside. Plans for its demolition or redevelopment have been discussed periodically.
