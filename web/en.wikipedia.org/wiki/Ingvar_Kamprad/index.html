Ingvar Kamprad

Feodor Ingvar Kamprad was a Swedish business magnate best known as the founder of IKEA, the world's largest furniture retailer. A legendary entrepreneur in Sweden, he built his fortune on flat-pack furniture sold at low prices, while cultivating a personal image of extreme frugality.

Early life

Kamprad grew up on the Elmtaryd farm near Agunnaryd in Småland, a poor, stony region whose thrift shaped his outlook. As a boy he sold matches, fish, seeds and Christmas decorations; at 17, in 1943, he used a reward from his father to register IKEA — an acronym of his initials, the farm and his village.

Building IKEA

IKEA sold furniture from 1948; when Swedish manufacturers boycotted him, Kamprad turned to Polish suppliers and leaned into self-assembly flat packs after a table's legs were removed to fit a car in 1956. The first showroom-store opened in Älmhult in 1958, and international expansion began in the 1960s–70s. In 1982 he placed IKEA into a Dutch foundation structure (Stichting INGKA) that still governs it, and he lived for decades in Switzerland before returning to Sweden in 2014.

Frugality and controversies

Kamprad flew economy, drove an old Volvo, and furnished his home with his own products — habits he preached in his internal manifesto The Testament of a Furniture Dealer (1976). His reputation was complicated by revelations of youthful involvement with Swedish fascist circles in the 1940s, for which he apologised, and by scrutiny of IKEA's complex tax arrangements. He formally retired from all board roles in 2013 and died at home in Småland in 2018.
