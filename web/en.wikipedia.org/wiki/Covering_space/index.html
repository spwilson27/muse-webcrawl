Covering space

In topology, a covering space is a topological space that maps onto another space in a way that locally looks like a disjoint union of identical copies. Formally, a covering map is a continuous surjection p : X̃ → X such that every point of X has a neighbourhood U whose preimage in X̃ is a disjoint union of open sets, each of which is mapped homeomorphically onto U by p. The neighbourhood U is called evenly covered, and the space X̃ is a covering space of X.

Examples

The classic example is the map p : ℝ → S¹ given by p(t) = (cos 2πt, sin 2πt), which wraps the real line around the circle infinitely many times; the real line is the universal cover of the circle. Similarly, the exponential map covers the punctured complex plane by the whole complex plane, and the n-sheeted maps z ↦ zⁿ cover the circle by itself. The surface of a sphere covers the real projective plane in a two-sheeted covering.

Properties

Covering maps have the homotopy lifting property: paths and homotopies in the base space lift uniquely to the covering space once a starting point is chosen. This makes covering spaces a powerful tool for computing fundamental groups. A covering space is universal if it is simply connected; every connected, locally path-connected, semi-locally simply connected space has a universal cover, unique up to isomorphism.

Galois theory of coverings

Connected covering spaces of a space X correspond to subgroups of its fundamental group π₁(X), with normal subgroups corresponding to regular (Galois) coverings. The group of deck transformations of the universal cover is isomorphic to π₁(X) itself. This correspondence is analogous to the fundamental theorem of Galois theory in algebra.
