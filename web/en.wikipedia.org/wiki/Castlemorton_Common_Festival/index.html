Castlemorton Common Festival

The Castlemorton Common Festival was a week-long free festival and rave held in May 1992 on Castlemorton Common in the Malvern Hills near Malvern, Worcestershire, England. Attended by an estimated 20,000 to 40,000 people, it was the largest illegal rave in British history and a landmark in the free party and traveller movements.

The event

Beginning on 22 May 1992, New Age travellers, already in the area for the Avon Free Festival, converged on the common along with rave sound systems including Spiral Tribe, Bedlam, Circus Warp, and DIY. Conflicts with locals over noise, sanitation, and livestock, plus saturation media coverage, overwhelmed the police, who were unable to disperse the gathering; the festival eventually wound down after about a week.

Aftermath

The scale of Castlemorton galvanized the Conservative government to act against the free festival and rave scene. It directly contributed to the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, which gave police new powers against trespassory assemblies and famously targeted gatherings featuring music "wholly or predominantly characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats." Several Spiral Tribe members were prosecuted in a lengthy trial but acquitted.

Legacy

Castlemorton is remembered as the high-water mark of the UK free party movement and a turning point that pushed rave culture into licensed commercial venues.
