Ranks and insignia of the Nazi Party

Ranks and insignia of the Nazi Party were paramilitary titles used by the National Socialist German Workers' Party (NSDAP) from the early 1920s until the fall of Nazi Germany in 1945. The party developed an elaborate hierarchy both for its political leadership corps and for its paramilitary formations, such as the SA (Sturmabteilung) and SS (Schutzstaffel).

Political leaders

By the late 1930s the political leader ranks, worn with distinctive collar patches and armbands on the brown uniform, ran from junior grades such as Mitarbeiter and Helfer up through Arbeitsleiter, Bereitschaftsleiter, Einsatzleiter, Hauptbereitschaftsleiter and beyond, to the territorial leaders: Ortsgruppenleiter (local group leader), Kreisleiter (district leader), Gauleiter (regional leader) and Reichsleiter (Reich leader, the highest party rank below the Führer himself, Adolf Hitler).

Paramilitary ranks

The SA and SS each developed their own rank systems, many titles derived from First World War German military usage: Sturmmann, Rottenführer, Scharführer, Truppführer, Sturmführer, Sturmhauptführer, Sturmbannführer, Standartenführer, Oberführer, Brigadeführer, Gruppenführer, Obergruppenführer and, in the SS, Reichsführer-SS. The Hitler Youth, NSKK, NSFK and other party organisations likewise had their own ranks and insignia.

Legacy

The display of Nazi insignia is restricted by law in Germany and several other countries, with exceptions for educational, artistic and historical purposes.
