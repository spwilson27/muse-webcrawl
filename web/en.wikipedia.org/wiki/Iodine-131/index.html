Iodine-131

Iodine-131 (131I) is an important radioisotope of iodine discovered by Glenn Seaborg and John Livingood in 1938 at the University of California, Berkeley. It has a radioactive decay half-life of about eight days and decays by beta emission to stable xenon-131.

Medical use

Because iodine is concentrated by the thyroid gland, iodine-131 is widely used in nuclear medicine: in small doses for diagnostic uptake studies and imaging, and in larger doses to treat hyperthyroidism (Graves' disease) and differentiated thyroid cancer by destroying thyroid tissue.

Fission product and hazards

Iodine-131 is a significant product of uranium and plutonium fission and was a major component of fallout from atmospheric weapons tests and reactor accidents such as Chernobyl and Fukushima. Released iodine-131 can accumulate in the thyroid, particularly in children; potassium iodide tablets are distributed as a countermeasure to block uptake.
