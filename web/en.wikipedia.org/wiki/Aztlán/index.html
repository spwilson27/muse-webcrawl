Aztlán

Aztlán (Nahuatl: aztlan, "place of whiteness" or "place of herons") is the ancestral homeland of the Aztec peoples in Mexica legend. According to Nahua migration accounts, the Mexica departed Aztlán — often depicted as an island in a lake — on a long migration that ended with the founding of Tenochtitlan in the Valley of Mexico in the 14th century.

In Aztec legend

Colonial-era sources such as the Codex Boturini and the works of chroniclers including Chimalpahin and Tezozómoc describe Aztlán as the origin point of several Nahua groups, who left together and later diverged. The Mexica, guided by their god Huitzilopochtli, travelled for generations before reaching the island site where they saw an eagle on a cactus — the sign for their promised city.

The historical location of Aztlán, if it existed, is unknown. Proposed identifications range across northwestern Mexico, and the question is generally regarded as belonging to mythic geography rather than recoverable history.

Chicano movement

In the 1960s and 1970s the Chicano movement adopted "Aztlán" as the name for the southwestern United States — territory Mexico lost to the United States in 1848 — which activists framed as the Chicano homeland. The concept was central to El Plan Espiritual de Aztlán, a manifesto adopted at the 1969 Chicano Youth Liberation Conference in Denver, and remains an enduring cultural and political symbol.
