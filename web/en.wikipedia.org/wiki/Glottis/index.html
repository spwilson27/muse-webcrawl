Glottis

The glottis is the opening between the vocal folds in the larynx, together with the folds themselves and the space (the rima glottidis) between them. It is the part of the airway principally responsible for producing the voice.

Anatomy and function

The vocal folds are attached anteriorly to the thyroid cartilage and posteriorly to the arytenoid cartilages, whose movement opens and closes the glottis. During quiet breathing the glottis is open; for phonation the folds are brought together and set vibrating by airflow from the lungs. Opening the glottis widens the airway for deep breathing, while complete closure occurs during swallowing and effort closure.

Phonetics

Sounds produced with the glottis as primary articulator are called glottal sounds, including the glottal stop [ʔ] and the fricatives [h] and [ɦ]. The state of the glottis distinguishes voiced from voiceless sounds.
