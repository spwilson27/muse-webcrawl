Earthing system

An earthing system (UK and IEC) or grounding system (US) in an electrical installation connects specific parts of that installation to the Earth's conductive surface for safety and functional purposes. The point of reference is the Earth's conductive surface. The choice of earthing system can affect the safety and electromagnetic compatibility of the installation; regulations for earthing systems vary widely among countries, though most follow the recommendations of the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).


A typical earthing arrangement with earth electrode and bonding

IEC terminology

IEC 60364 classifies earthing systems using two-letter codes. The first letter describes the connection of the power source to earth: T (direct connection to earth, from French terre) or I (isolated, or connected through a high impedance). The second letter describes the connection of exposed conductive parts in the installation: T (direct local connection to earth) or N (connected to the supply's earthed neutral). Common arrangements include TN (with variants TN-S, TN-C, and TN-C-S), TT, and IT systems.

Purposes

Earthing provides a low-impedance path for fault currents so that protective devices (fuses, circuit breakers, or residual-current devices) disconnect the supply quickly; it limits touch voltages on exposed metalwork; it stabilises system voltages with respect to earth; and it provides a path for lightning and surge currents. An earth electrode — such as a rod, plate, or foundation electrode — makes the connection to the soil, and equipotential bonding ties together metallic services to minimise dangerous potential differences.
