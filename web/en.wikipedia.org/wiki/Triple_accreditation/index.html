Triple accreditation

Triple accreditation, informally the "triple crown", is the accreditation of a business school by all three of the leading international bodies:

  • AACSB — the Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business (United States);
  • EQUIS — the EFMD Quality Improvement System (Europe);
  • AMBA — the Association of MBAs (United Kingdom), which accredits MBA programmes.

Only a small fraction — around one percent — of the world's business schools hold all three accreditations simultaneously; the number of triple-accredited schools is on the order of one hundred-plus worldwide. The status is widely used in marketing and rankings competition, as it certifies that a school's teaching, research, governance, and MBA provision meet three independent quality standards, each with different emphasis: AACSB on academic quality and continuous improvement, EQUIS on internationalisation and corporate connections, and AMBA on the MBA portfolio specifically.

Significance

Triple accreditation is treated by applicants, employers, and partner institutions as a shorthand for elite status in management education, and schools typically publicise membership prominently. Critics note the cost and administrative burden of maintaining three accreditations and question how far accreditation measures educational outcomes rather than inputs and processes.
