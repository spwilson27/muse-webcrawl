Recovery (metallurgy)

In materials science, recovery is a process by which the deformed grains of a metal can reduce their stored energy by the removal or rearrangement of defects in their crystal structure. These defects, primarily dislocations, are introduced by plastic deformation of the material and act to increase the yield strength of the metal.

Mechanism

During recovery, dislocations of opposite sign annihilate one another and the remainder rearrange into lower-energy configurations such as dislocation walls, forming subgrains. Because recovery involves only the motion of existing dislocations, it reduces internal stresses and restores some electrical and thermal conductivity without significantly changing the grain structure or hardness of the material.

Relationship to recrystallization

Recovery precedes recrystallization during annealing. At higher temperatures, new strain-free grains nucleate and grow, producing the large drop in strength and increase in ductility characteristic of recrystallization. Recovery is favored in high stacking-fault-energy metals such as aluminium and ferritic iron.
