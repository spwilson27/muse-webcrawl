Kharosthi

Type: Abugida
Period: c. 3rd century BCE – 3rd century CE
Region: Gandhara, northwestern South Asia
Direction: Right-to-left

The Kharosthi script (also Kharoshthi) was an ancient abugida used in the Gandhara region of present-day northwestern Pakistan and eastern Afghanistan to write Gandhari Prakrit and Sanskrit. Unlike most Indic scripts, it was written from right to left.

History

Kharosthi derived in part from the Aramaic alphabet under Achaemenid influence. It appears on the rock edicts of Ashoka in the northwest (c. 3rd century BCE) and on coins, inscriptions and Buddhist manuscripts — including some of the oldest surviving Buddhist texts, written on birch bark. It fell out of use around the 3rd century CE.

Decipherment

The script was deciphered in the 19th century by James Prinsep and others using bilingual Indo-Greek coins bearing Greek on one side and Kharosthi on the other.
