Fish processing is the process of preparing fish and fish products between the time fish are caught or harvested and the time the final product is delivered to the customer. Although the term technically applies to all fish, in practice it is usually applied to wild-caught fish, and increasingly to fish produced by aquaculture.

The term fish processing also encompasses the handling of fish aboard fishing vessels — including gutting, bleeding and chilling — as well as the work done in shore-based processing plants, where fish are graded, filleted, frozen, canned, smoked or otherwise preserved.

Handling and preservation

Fish is an extremely perishable food. Microbial spoilage begins almost immediately after death, so the temperature of the fish must be lowered as quickly as possible. Chilling on ice at about 0 °C is the most common short-term method; freezing at −18 °C or below extends storage life to months. Traditional methods include salting, drying, smoking and pickling, while canning — in which fish is sealed in airtight containers and sterilised by heat — allows storage for years.

Processing operations

Typical operations in a processing plant include washing, grading by size and species, deheading, gutting, skinning and filleting. White fish such as cod and haddock are commonly sold as skinless, boneless fillets, while oily fish such as herring and mackerel are often canned, smoked or pickled. By-products — heads, frames, trimmings and viscera — are used to produce fish meal and fish oil, important ingredients in animal feed and aquaculture diets.

Surimi, a paste made from mechanically deboned fish flesh, is washed and stabilised with cryoprotectants and forms the basis of imitation crab and other formed seafood products.

Quality and safety

Processing is governed by food safety systems such as HACCP (hazard analysis and critical control points). Hazards include histamine formation in scombroid fish (such as tuna and mackerel), parasites such as Anisakis in raw fish, and bacterial contamination. Many jurisdictions require freezing of fish intended to be eaten raw in order to kill parasites.
