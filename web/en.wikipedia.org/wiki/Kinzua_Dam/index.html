Kinzua Dam

Kinzua Dam Location: Warren County, Pennsylvania, United States
River: Allegheny River
Construction: 1960–1965
Height: 179 ft (55 m)
Reservoir: Allegheny Reservoir (Lake Perfidy)
Power capacity: about 451 MW (pumped storage)

The Kinzua Dam is a large earthfill embankment dam on the Allegheny River in Warren County, Pennsylvania, built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers between 1960 and 1965 for flood control, hydroelectric power and recreation. The dam impounds the Allegheny Reservoir, which stretches some 24 miles upstream into New York State.

Seneca controversy

The reservoir flooded roughly 10,000 acres of land reserved to the Seneca Nation by the Treaty of Canandaigua of 1794, including the Cornplanter Tract in Pennsylvania. More than 600 Seneca families were forced to relocate. The taking, upheld by the courts despite the treaty's guarantee that Seneca land would not be taken, became a landmark case in Native American land rights and was protested by the American Indian Movement; Johnny Cash recorded the song "As Long as the Grass Shall Grow" about the flooding. Because of the broken treaty, the reservoir is sometimes called "Lake Perfidy."

Power generation

The associated Seneca Pumped Storage Hydroelectric Plant, with a capacity of about 451 megawatts, pumps water to an upper reservoir on the plateau above the dam during periods of low demand and generates electricity during peak demand.

The dam provides flood protection for Pittsburgh and the upper Ohio Valley and is a popular recreation area within the Allegheny National Forest.
