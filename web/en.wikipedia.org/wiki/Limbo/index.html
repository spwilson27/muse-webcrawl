Limbo

In Catholic theology, Limbo (Latin: limbus, meaning "edge" or "border", referring to the edge of Hell) is the afterlife condition of those who die in original sin without being assigned to the Hell of the Damned. Medieval theologians of Western Europe described the underworld ("hell", "hades", "infernum") as divided into four distinct parts: Hell of the Damned, Purgatory, Limbo of the Fathers or Patriarchs, and Limbo of the Infants.

Limbo was a speculative solution to a theological problem: how God could be both merciful and just toward those — above all infants who died unbaptised — who bore original sin but had committed no personal sin. The Limbo of Infants was never an officially defined doctrine of the Catholic Church, and in 2007 the International Theological Commission published a document concluding there are strong grounds for hope that unbaptised infants will be saved, greatly weakening the hypothesis.

Limbo of the Patriarchs

The Limbo of the Fathers (limbus patrum) was understood as the temporary state of the righteous who died before the Resurrection of Jesus, and who could not enter Heaven until Christ's redemptive work was complete. It is associated with the Bosom of Abraham of Luke 16 and with the Harrowing of Hell, in which Christ descended to free them.

Limbo of Infants

Thomas Aquinas taught that infants in limbo suffer no pain and enjoy a natural happiness, knowing and loving God without the beatific vision, of whose absence they are unaware. Augustine of Hippo had earlier held a harsher view, assigning unbaptised infants to hell, albeit with the mildest of punishments.

In Dante

In Dante's Inferno, Limbo is the first circle of Hell, a place of sighs rather than torment, resembling a noble castle in a green meadow where the virtuous pagans — Homer, Virgil, Aristotle, Socrates, Saladin — dwell in honour but without hope of Heaven.

Modern developments

The Catechism of the Catholic Church (1992) does not mention limbo and instead states that the Church can only entrust unbaptised infants to the mercy of God, with strong reasons for hope in their salvation. The word "limbo" has passed into common usage for any intermediate, neglected or unresolved state.
