Principal dancer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A principal dancer (often shortened to principal) is a dancer at the highest rank within a professional dance company, particularly a ballet company. A principal may be male or female. Unlike the prima ballerina of past centuries, the term is used broadly and is not reserved for a single outstanding artist in a company.

Principals are hired by — or promoted from within — the company to regularly perform the most prominent roles in a ballet, such as the leads in full-length productions like Swan Lake, The Nutcracker, and Giselle. In most companies the rank structure, from highest to lowest, runs: principal dancer, soloist, demi-soloist, and corps de ballet (with variations in terminology between companies and traditions).

Comparable titles

At the Paris Opera Ballet, the equivalent highest rank is étoile. At La Scala, the title is primo ballerino or prima ballerina. Some companies also maintain the rank of principal character dancer, for senior artists who perform primarily mime and character roles rather than the classical leads.

Career path

Dancers typically reach principal rank only after years progressing through the corps de ballet and soloist ranks, and promotion is at the discretion of the artistic director. Principal contracts are often held for the peak of a dancer's career; many principals later become ballet masters, choreographers, répétiteurs, or artistic directors.
