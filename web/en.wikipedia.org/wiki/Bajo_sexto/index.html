The bajo sexto (Spanish for "sixth bass") is a Mexican twelve-string guitar-like instrument with six courses of doubled strings, used primarily in norteño and Tejano (conjunto) music, where it provides bass lines and rhythmic chordal accompaniment alongside the accordion and bajo quinto.

Description

The bajo sexto has a large, deep body with a cutaway, a short scale and heavy-gauge strings tuned in fourths (from low to high, commonly E–A–D–G–C–F in doubled courses). Played with a pick, it historically supplied both the bass line and chords before the tololoche (upright bass) and later electric bass took over the bottom end, freeing the bajo sexto for melodic runs and fills.

History

The instrument developed in northern Mexico and south Texas in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, becoming the rhythmic backbone of conjunto music as recorded by pioneers such as Narciso Martínez. The five-course bajo quinto variant has largely superseded it in modern norteño. Noted luthiers include the Macías family of San Antonio.
