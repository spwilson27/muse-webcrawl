Haupia

Haupia
TypePudding
Place of originHawaii
Main ingredientsCoconut milk, cornstarch (traditionally pia starch)
Serving temperatureChilled

Haupia is a traditional Hawaiian coconut milk-based dessert, historically made by mixing coconut milk with ground pia (Polynesian arrowroot, Tacca leontopetaloides) and cooking the mixture until it thickened and set. Modern versions generally use cornstarch as the thickener. It is usually served chilled, cut into small cubes, and has a firm, gelatin-like consistency with a smooth, sweet coconut flavour.

Preparation

Coconut milk is heated with sugar and a starch slurry; the mixture is cooked, stirring constantly, until glossy and thick, then poured into a flat pan, chilled until set, and cut into squares. Because the dessert contains no eggs or dairy, it is naturally vegan.

Cultural significance

Haupia is a staple at Hawaiian lūʻau and is one of the most recognisable desserts in Hawaiian cuisine. It appears in many modern adaptations: as the filling of haupia pie (often layered over chocolate or sweet potato), as a topping for cakes, in haupia-flavoured malasadas, and in combinations with tropical fruits such as lilikoi (passion fruit), mango, and pineapple.

Variations

A popular variant is chocolate haupia pie, with a bottom layer of chocolate custard and a top layer of haupia in a pie crust, widely sold in Hawaiian bakeries. Sweet potato haupia pie uses a purple Okinawan sweet potato base. Haupia is also used as a sauce or glaze and blended into drinks.
