Second Battle of Fort Fisher

The Second Battle of Fort Fisher was a successful assault by the Union Army, Navy and Marine Corps against Fort Fisher, a Confederate fort at the entrance to the Cape Fear River in North Carolina. Fought from 13 to 15 January 1865 during the American Civil War, the battle closed Wilmington, the last major port open to Confederate blockade runners, and is regarded as one of the decisive Union victories of the war's final months.

Background

Fort Fisher, an enormous earthwork fortification nicknamed the "Gibraltar of the South," guarded New Inlet and the approaches to Wilmington, North Carolina, through which supplies from Europe sustained Robert E. Lee's army around Richmond. A first expedition in December 1864 under Benjamin Butler and Admiral David Dixon Porter failed after a botched attempt to explode a powder boat near the fort and a half-hearted landing. Ulysses S. Grant replaced Butler with Major General Alfred Terry and ordered a second attempt.

The battle

Porter's fleet of some sixty warships — the largest the United States Navy had yet assembled — opened a methodical bombardment on 13 January, deliberately targeting the fort's guns rather than merely its walls, and silenced most of the Confederate artillery. About 8,000 Union troops under Terry landed and entrenched across the peninsula, facing the expected relief force of Robert Hoke to the north while preparing the assault. On 15 January a naval landing party of sailors and Marines attacked the fort's northeast bastion and was bloodily repulsed, but the diversion drew defenders away from the landward wall, where Terry's infantry fought traverse by traverse through the fort in hours of hand-to-hand combat. The garrison's commander, Colonel William Lamb, and district commander W. H. C. Whiting were both wounded, and the fort surrendered that evening.

Aftermath

Roughly 1,900 Confederates were captured. The fall of Fort Fisher sealed the Confederacy's last maritime lifeline; Wilmington itself fell the following month, and Lee's position at Petersburg became untenable. The battle was notable for the close cooperation between the army and navy. Thirteen Medals of Honor were awarded for the assault.
