Turán graph

Turán graph T(n, r)
Named after: Pál Turán
Type: Complete multipartite graph
Field: Extremal graph theory

The Turán graph, denoted T(n, r), is the complete multipartite graph formed by partitioning a set of n vertices into r subsets as equal in size as possible, and connecting two vertices by an edge whenever they belong to different subsets. It is named after the Hungarian mathematician Pál Turán.

Turán's theorem

Turán's theorem (1941) states that among all graphs on n vertices containing no (r+1)-clique, the Turán graph T(n, r) has the maximum possible number of edges. This result founded the field of extremal graph theory. Special cases include Mantel's theorem (1907), where the triangle-free extremal graph is the complete bipartite graph with parts as equal as possible.

Properties

The Turán graph is the complement of a disjoint union of equal-sized cliques, and every regular complete multipartite graph of this balanced form is a Turán graph. The number of edges of T(n, r) approaches (1 − 1/r)·n²/2 for large n.
