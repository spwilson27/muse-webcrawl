Nicolas Bourriaud

Nicolas Bourriaud
Born: 1965, France
Occupation: Curator, art critic, theorist
Known for: Relational Aesthetics (1998), Postproduction, Altermodern

Nicolas Bourriaud (born 1965) is a French curator and art theorist, best known for coining the concept of relational aesthetics.

Career

Bourriaud co-founded the Palais de Tokyo in Paris and served as its co-director (1999–2006), was Gulbenkian Curator at Tate Britain, curated the 2009 Tate Triennial Altermodern, directed the École Nationale Supérieure des Beaux-Arts in Paris (2011–2015), and later founded Montpellier Contemporain (MoCo). He has curated numerous biennials including Lyon, Moscow and Taipei.

Ideas

In Relational Aesthetics (1998, French 1998) he argued that 1990s artists such as Rirkrit Tiravanija, Philippe Parreno, Pierre Huyghe and Liam Gillick created social situations and encounters as art. Postproduction (2002) described artists reusing existing cultural material like DJs and programmers. The Radicant (2009) and the altermodern concept addressed art in globalisation.

Criticism

Claire Bishop and others criticised relational aesthetics for privileging conviviality over political antagonism and quality of social relations.
