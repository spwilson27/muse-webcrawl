Intermittent river

An intermittent river is a river that temporarily stops flowing, or dries out, for part of the year. Intermittent rivers are distinguished from perennial rivers, which flow year-round, and from ephemeral streams, which flow only in direct response to precipitation. Such rivers are common in arid and semi-arid regions and in Mediterranean and seasonal climates worldwide.

Terminology

Regional names include wadi in North Africa and Arabia, arroyo in the southwestern United States, winterbourne for chalk streams in England that flow in winter, and torrente in Italy. Scientists increasingly group them under the term "temporary streams" or "non-perennial rivers".

Ecology

Despite their dry phases, intermittent rivers support distinctive ecosystems. Aquatic organisms survive dry periods in refuges such as hyporheic zones and residual pools, or as dormant eggs and seeds. Dry riverbeds are used by terrestrial animals and contribute to nutrient cycling when flow resumes. Research suggests that more than half of the global river network is non-perennial.

Management

Intermittent rivers are often excluded from legal protections designed for permanent waters, and their flow regimes are sensitive to groundwater extraction and climate change, leading to growing scientific and policy attention.
