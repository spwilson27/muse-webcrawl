Beliebers is the name given to the fan community of Canadian singer Justin Bieber. The word is a portmanteau of "Bieber" and "believers". One of the earliest and most prominent social-media fandoms, Beliebers are credited with helping propel Bieber from YouTube discovery to global stardom and with pioneering forms of organised online fan activity.

History and activities

The fandom formed around 2009–2010 as Bieber's debut single "One Time" and album My World found a young audience on YouTube and the then-new Twitter. Beliebers trended hashtags, coordinated streaming and voting campaigns for fan-choice awards, and created vast amounts of fan fiction, art and tribute accounts. Bieber has acknowledged the fandom in songs, tours and the concert film Justin Bieber: Never Say Never (2011), and the fandom's birthday is informally marked by fan communities each year.

Characteristics and reputation

The Beliebers were stereotypically depicted, especially in the early 2010s, as predominantly teenage girls exhibiting intense devotion ("Bieber fever"), a characterisation scholars of fan studies have used to examine gendered attitudes toward young women's fandom. The community is also known for charity fundraising campaigns and for online activism, including trending-topic campaigns both celebratory and critical of Bieber himself.

In fan studies

Alongside, for example, the "Directioners" (One Direction) and later the "ARMY" (BTS), the Beliebers are frequently cited in academic and journalistic analyses of participatory culture, celebrity–fan relations on social media, and the commercial organisation of modern pop fandoms.
