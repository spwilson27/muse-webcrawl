Thomas Michael Bond (13 January 1926 – 27 June 2017) was an English author best known for creating the children's character Paddington Bear. He wrote more than 200 books, including 29 Paddington titles, the Olga da Polga guinea-pig stories, the Parsley the Lion series, and the adult comic novels featuring Monsieur Pamplemousse, a former Paris detective turned restaurant inspector.

Life and career

Born in Newbury, Berkshire, Bond served in the Royal Air Force and the British Army during the Second World War, and afterwards worked for the BBC, first as a cameraman. He began writing in his spare time; A Bear Called Paddington was published in 1958 by Collins. The inspiration came from a lone toy bear Bond bought for his wife from a London department store shelf on Christmas Eve 1956 — named Paddington because the couple lived near Paddington Station — combined with memories of child evacuees and refugees arriving in wartime Britain with labels around their necks.

Paddington

Paddington, a polite, marmalade-loving bear from "darkest Peru" adopted by the Brown family of 32 Windsor Gardens, became one of the most beloved figures in children's literature, translated into more than 40 languages and adapted for television and for the acclaimed feature films Paddington (2014) and Paddington 2 (2017). Bond continued writing Paddington stories into his nineties; Paddington at St Paul's was published posthumously in 2018.

Honours

Bond was appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) in 1997 and a Commander (CBE) in 2015 for services to children's literature. He died in London at the age of 91, on the day Paddington author tributes noted as the character's birthday on-screen.
