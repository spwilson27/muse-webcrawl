Abhay Charanaravinda Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada (1 September 1896 – 14 November 1977), born Abhay Charan De and commonly known as Srila Prabhupada, was an Indian Hindu spiritual teacher and the founder-acharya of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), commonly known as the Hare Krishna movement. He is credited with bringing the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition of bhakti yoga to the Western world.

Early life

Abhay Charan De was born in Calcutta (now Kolkata) into a Vaishnava family. He studied at Scottish Church College and was influenced by the Indian independence movement before meeting his spiritual master, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, in 1922. Bhaktisiddhanta instructed him to preach Krishna consciousness in the English language, a commission he worked toward for decades while running a pharmaceutical business and, from 1944, publishing the magazine Back to Godhead. He took formal sannyasa (renounced orders) in 1959, receiving the name Bhaktivedanta Swami.

Journey to America and ISKCON

In 1965, at the age of 69, he sailed from India to New York City aboard the cargo ship Jaladuta, arriving with little money and a trunk of his translated scriptures. Preaching in the Lower East Side of Manhattan, he attracted young followers and incorporated ISKCON in July 1966. Under his direction the movement grew rapidly, establishing temples, farm communities, and restaurants around the world, and popularising public chanting of the Hare Krishna mantra, vegetarian food distribution, and festivals such as Ratha Yatra in Western cities.

Writings and legacy

Prabhupada translated and commented on major Vaishnava scriptures, producing more than eighty volumes, including Bhagavad-gītā As It Is (1968), a multi-volume translation of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, and the Caitanya-caritāmṛta. His books have been translated into dozens of languages and are distributed by the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust, which he founded in 1972. He died in Vrindavan in 1977, having appointed a Governing Body Commission to lead ISKCON after him.
