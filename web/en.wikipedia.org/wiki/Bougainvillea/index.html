Bougainvillea

Bougainvillea is a genus of thorny ornamental vines, bushes and trees belonging to the four o'clock family, Nyctaginaceae, native to eastern South America from Brazil to Peru and south to southern Argentina. The plants, first documented by Europeans during the 1768 voyage of Admiral Louis Antoine de Bougainville (after whom the genus is named), are famous for the brilliant colour of their blooms — though the colour comes not from the small, inconspicuous true flowers but from the large papery bracts that surround them.

Description

Bougainvilleas are evergreen in rainy climates and deciduous where a dry season occurs. The woody, thorny stems climb by means of hooked thorns and can reach 1–12 metres. Bracts occur in shades of magenta, purple, red, orange, pink, white and yellow. The widely grown garden forms derive mainly from three species — Bougainvillea glabra, B. spectabilis and B. peruviana — and their many hybrids and cultivars, of which hundreds have been named.

Cultivation

The plants require full sun, warmth and well-drained soil, and are drought-tolerant once established. They are grown throughout the tropics and warm-temperate zones as hedges, climbers, bonsai and container plants, but are frost-sensitive and in cooler climates are kept under glass. Bougainvillea is the official flower of several cities, and the bracts are used in some traditional medicine and as a natural colourant.
