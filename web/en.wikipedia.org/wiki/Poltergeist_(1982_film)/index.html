Poltergeist (1982 film)

Directed by: Tobe Hooper

Story by: Steven Spielberg

Starring: JoBeth Williams, Craig T. Nelson, Heather O'Rourke

Released: 1982

Poltergeist is a 1982 American supernatural horror film directed by Tobe Hooper from a story by Steven Spielberg, who also produced it. It is the first instalment in the Poltergeist trilogy and one of the defining suburban horror films of its era. The line "They're here" is among the most famous in horror cinema.

Plot

The Freeling family — Steven, Diane, and their children Dana, Robbie and five-year-old Carol Anne — live in the planned community of Cuesta Verde, California. The youngest child begins communicating with unseen presences through a television tuned to static, announcing "They're here." Strange phenomena escalate: furniture moves, a tree attacks Robbie, and Carol Anne is pulled into a spectral dimension, her voice heard through the TV. Parapsychologists confirm a poltergeist haunting, and the medium Tangina Barrons explains that Carol Anne is being held by a malevolent entity, "the Beast". Diane enters the spirit world to rescue her daughter. The family learns that Cuesta Verde was built over a cemetery whose headstones were moved but whose bodies were left behind; in the climax, corpses erupt from the ground and the house implodes into the spirit realm as the family flees.

Production

Spielberg, contractually barred from directing another film while making E.T., hired Hooper after his work on The Texas Chain Saw Massacre. The extent of Spielberg's on-set creative involvement has long been debated. The film's special effects, by Industrial Light & Magic, were groundbreaking.

Reception and legacy

Poltergeist was a major critical and commercial success and was nominated for three Academy Awards. Two sequels followed (1986 and 1988), plus a 2015 remake. The deaths of several cast members, including Heather O'Rourke at age 12, gave rise to a persistent "Poltergeist curse" legend.
