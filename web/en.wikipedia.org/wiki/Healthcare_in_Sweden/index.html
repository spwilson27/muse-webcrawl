Healthcare in Sweden

Healthcare in Sweden is a publicly funded, decentralised universal system. Responsibility rests mainly with Sweden's 21 regions (formerly county councils), which own and operate most hospitals and primary care centres and levy income taxes to fund care. The national government sets overall policy and legislation through the Ministry of Health and Social Affairs.

Organisation

Primary care is the first point of contact, delivered through local health centres (vårdcentraler). Regional hospitals provide specialist and emergency care, and seven university hospitals provide highly specialised care and research. Municipalities are responsible for elderly care and home healthcare.

Funding and access

Care is funded predominantly through regional taxation. Patients pay modest capped fees for consultations and prescriptions, with annual high-cost ceilings; care is free for children. Private providers operate within the system under public contracts, and patients have statutory rights to choice of provider and to care within guaranteed waiting times.

Outcomes

Sweden consistently ranks among the world's best health systems for outcomes such as life expectancy and infant mortality, though waiting times for elective surgery and regional disparities remain recurring policy concerns.
