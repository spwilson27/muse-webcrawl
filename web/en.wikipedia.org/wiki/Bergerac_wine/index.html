Bergerac is a wine-growing region in South West France centred on the town of Bergerac in the Dordogne department, along the Dordogne river east of Bordeaux. The region produces red, white (dry and sweet), and rosé wines under a family of appellations headed by the Bergerac and Côtes de Bergerac AOCs.

Appellations

The generic Bergerac AOC covers red, rosé, and dry white wines from the wider district, while Côtes de Bergerac is reserved for more concentrated reds and moelleux whites from lower yields. Within the area lie several specialised appellations: Monbazillac, famous for sweet botrytised whites from Sémillon, Sauvignon blanc, and Muscadelle; Pécharmant, producing structured reds north-east of Bergerac; Saussignac, for sweet whites; Montravel and Côtes de Montravel to the west; and the small sweet-white appellation Rosette.

History

Viticulture around Bergerac dates to the Roman era and flourished in the Middle Ages, when wines were shipped down the Dordogne to Bordeaux and exported to England and the Low Countries. In the 13th–16th centuries Bergerac's largely Protestant merchants enjoyed privileges that allowed them to sell their wines ahead of Bordeaux's, a source of lasting rivalry. The appellation system recognised Bergerac in 1936, among the earliest AOC decrees.

Styles

The reds are based on the Bordeaux varieties Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Merlot, and Malbec (locally Côt), and are generally lighter and earlier-drinking than Bordeaux. Dry whites are led by Sauvignon blanc and Sémillon. The sweet wines of Monbazillac and Saussignac are made from grapes affected by noble rot (Botrytis cinerea).
