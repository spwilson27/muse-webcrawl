Amadís de Gaula (Spanish: "Amadis of Gaul") is a landmark prose chivalric romance and one of the most famous works of Spanish literature. First printed in 1508 in the version by Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo, who reworked a lost primitive text dating to the late 13th or 14th century, it became the archetype of the Spanish libros de caballerías (books of chivalry) and a European best-seller, imitated and continued in dozens of sequels.

Plot

Amadís is the secret son of King Perión of Gaul and Princess Elisena of England, set adrift at birth and raised in Scotland as the "Child of the Sea". He becomes the perfect knight — invincible in arms, humble, and utterly devoted to his lady, Oriana, heiress of King Lisuarte of Great Britain. Across four books Amadís rescues enchanted kingdoms, defeats giants and monsters such as the Endriago, serves and quarrels with Lisuarte, and ultimately wins Oriana. Montalvo's continuation introduces Amadís's son Esplandián, whose adventures fill the sequel, Las sergas de Esplandián (1510).

Influence

The book's popularity spanned Europe: it was translated into French, Italian, German, Dutch, English and Hebrew, and the Amadis cycle grew to some two dozen volumes in Spanish and more in French. The name "California" derives from the mythical island of Queen Calafia in Las sergas de Esplandián. Miguel de Cervantes made Amadís the model of chivalry for Don Quixote: in the scrutiny of Don Quixote's library, the priest spares Amadís de Gaula from the flames as the best book of its kind, even as Cervantes' novel parodies the genre it inspired.

Montalvo's fourth book also introduced more didactic, courtly and religious elements and criticism of pure knight-errantry, reflecting the values of Catholic Monarchs' Spain.
