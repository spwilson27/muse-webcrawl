Cayuga Lake

Cayuga Lake is the longest of central New York's glacial Finger Lakes, and is the second largest in surface area (slightly smaller than Seneca Lake) and second longest in length. It is located in the Finger Lakes region of upstate New York, within Cayuga, Seneca, and Tompkins counties. The city of Ithaca, home to Cornell University and Ithaca College, is located at the southern end of the lake.

Geography

The lake is approximately 38.1 miles (61.3 km) long and has an average width of 1.7 miles (2.7 km), with a maximum width of about 3.5 miles near Aurora. Its surface area is roughly 66.4 square miles (172 km²), and its maximum depth is 435 feet (133 m), making it one of the deepest lakes in the United States relative to its size. The lake's mean depth is about 182 feet (55 m). Its outlet at the northern end flows through the Seneca River system toward Lake Ontario.

History

The lake takes its name from the Cayuga people, one of the nations of the Iroquois Confederacy, whose villages lined its shores. The Sullivan Expedition of 1779 during the American Revolutionary War destroyed Cayuga settlements along the lake. After the war, the lakeshore was opened to settlement, and villages such as Aurora, Union Springs, and Cayuga grew along its banks.

Ecology and recreation

Cayuga Lake supports fisheries for lake trout, rainbow trout, smallmouth bass, and Atlantic salmon, and is used extensively for boating, sailing, and swimming. State parks along its shores include Taughannock Falls State Park, Allan H. Treman State Marine Park, and Long Point State Park. The lake also serves as a source of drinking water and as cooling water for lakeshore facilities.
