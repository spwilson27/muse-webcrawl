Sports science

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sports science (also sports sciences or exercise science) is an interdisciplinary field that applies scientific principles to sport, exercise, and physical activity in order to improve athletic performance, health, and recovery. It draws on physiology, biomechanics, psychology, nutrition, and motor learning, and is practised both in academic research and in applied work with athletes and teams.

History

The scientific study of exercise traces to early twentieth-century laboratories such as the Harvard Fatigue Laboratory (founded 1927) and the physiology departments of Scandinavia, where researchers including August Krogh and, later, Bengt Saltin and Erik Hohwü Christensen studied human work capacity. After the Second World War, national institutes of sport were established in many countries, and the field professionalised from the 1970s onward. The modern era has added sports analytics, wearable monitoring, and large performance databases.

Subdisciplines

  • Exercise physiology — cardiovascular, muscular, and metabolic responses and adaptations to exercise, including VO2 max and lactate threshold testing.
  • Biomechanics — analysis of technique, forces, and movement efficiency, increasingly using motion capture and force plates.
  • Sports psychology — motivation, mental skills, anxiety management, and team dynamics.
  • Sports nutrition — fuelling, hydration, and supplementation strategies.
  • Strength and conditioning — design of training programmes to develop strength, power, speed, and endurance.
  • Sports medicine and rehabilitation — injury prevention, diagnosis, and return-to-play protocols.
  • Performance analysis — video and data analysis of competition.

Applications

Applied sports scientists monitor training load, design periodised programmes, test fitness, advise on recovery and sleep, and help prevent injuries. The same knowledge base supports public-health exercise prescription and occupational and military physiology. Professional accreditation bodies, such as the British Association of Sport and Exercise Sciences, regulate standards for practitioners.
