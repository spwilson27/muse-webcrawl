Free silver

Free silver was a major economic policy issue in the United States in the late 19th century, centered on the proposal that the government should mint silver coins at a fixed ratio to gold (16 to 1), with unlimited coinage of silver brought to the mints.

Background

After the Coinage Act of 1873 ended the minting of standard silver dollars — denounced by silver supporters as the "Crime of '73" — farmers and debtors, hurt by deflation, demanded free silver to expand the money supply, while creditors and the financial establishment defended the gold standard.

Political movement

The cause was championed by the Populist Party and by William Jennings Bryan, whose "Cross of Gold" speech won him the Democratic nomination in 1896; he lost to William McKinley, and the Gold Standard Act of 1900 settled the issue.
