The Varieties of Religious Experience

Author: William James

Published: 1902

Subtitle: A Study in Human Nature

Origin: Gifford Lectures, University of Edinburgh, 1901–02

The Varieties of Religious Experience: A Study in Human Nature is a book by the American philosopher and psychologist William James, published in 1902. It comprises the Gifford Lectures on natural theology delivered at the University of Edinburgh in 1901 and 1902. It remains a classic of the psychology of religion.

Content

James focused not on institutions or theology but on the personal religious experiences of individuals, drawing on autobiographies, letters and testimonies. He distinguished the "healthy-minded" temperament from the "sick soul", analysed conversion, saintliness and mysticism, and described the mystical state by four marks: ineffability, noetic quality, transiency and passivity. He proposed that religious experiences be judged pragmatically — "by their fruits, not by their roots" — rather than by their origins, and speculated about a "subconscious self" through which such experiences might connect the individual to a wider spiritual reality, his tentative "over-belief".

Influence

The book helped found the academic psychology of religion and influenced figures as diverse as Carl Jung and Ludwig Wittgenstein. James's account of conversion shaped the early Alcoholics Anonymous movement; co-founder Bill Wilson credited the book with a role in his own spiritual awakening. It is regularly listed among the most important works of non-fiction of the twentieth century.
