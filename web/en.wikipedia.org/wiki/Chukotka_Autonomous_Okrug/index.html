Chukotka Autonomous Okrug
Country: Russia
Capital: Anadyr
Area: About 737,700 km²
Population: About 48,000
Location: Northeasternmost federal subject, opposite Alaska across the Bering Strait

Chukotka Autonomous Okrug is a federal subject of Russia in the far northeast of Asia. It is the easternmost region of Russia, separated from Alaska by the Bering Strait, and the only part of Russia lying in the Western Hemisphere (east of 180° longitude).

Geography

The terrain is largely tundra and mountains, with a severe Arctic climate. The region includes Wrangel Island, a UNESCO site noted for polar bears and mammoth remains, and Cape Dezhnev, the easternmost point of mainland Asia.

People and economy

Indigenous peoples include the Chukchi, Yupik, Even and Yukaghir, historically reindeer herders, hunters and fishers. The population fell sharply after the Soviet collapse. Economy centres on mining (gold, coal, tin), reindeer husbandry and fishing. Roman Abramovich served as governor from 2000 to 2008, investing heavily in infrastructure. There are no roads connecting Chukotka to the rest of Russia; transport is by air and sea.
