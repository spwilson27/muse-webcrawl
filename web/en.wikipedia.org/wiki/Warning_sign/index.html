Warning sign

A warning sign is a type of sign that indicates a potential hazard, obstacle or condition requiring special attention. Warning signs use distinctive shapes and colours to be recognisable at a glance: in much of Europe, triangular signs with red borders; in the Americas, yellow diamond-shaped signs; for workplace and product hazards, standardised pictograms under systems such as the Globally Harmonized System (GHS).

The radiation trefoil (ISO 361), the biohazard symbol (designed in 1966 by Charles Baldwin), the skull-and-crossbones for poisons, and the lightning-flash symbol for electrical hazards are among the most widely recognised warning symbols. Their design principle is communication without words, across languages and literacy levels.
