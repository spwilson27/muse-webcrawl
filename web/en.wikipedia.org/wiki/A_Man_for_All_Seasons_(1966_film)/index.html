A Man for All Seasons (1966 film)

A Man for All Seasons is a 1966 British historical drama film directed by Fred Zinnemann and adapted by Robert Bolt from his own play of the same name. It depicts the final years of Sir Thomas More, Lord Chancellor of England, whose refusal to swear the oath acknowledging King Henry VIII as head of the Church of England led to his trial and execution in 1535. The film stars Paul Scofield, who had originated the role on stage, alongside Wendy Hiller, Robert Shaw as Henry VIII, Leo McKern, Orson Welles and Susannah York.

Plot

More, a respected lawyer and devout Catholic, rises to Lord Chancellor but declines to endorse Henry VIII's divorce from Catherine of Aragon and break with Rome. Pressured by the King, Thomas Cromwell and others to sign the oath of supremacy, More relies on silence and the law to protect him. Betrayed by perjured testimony from Richard Rich, he is convicted of treason and beheaded, declaring himself "the King's good servant, but God's first".

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success and won six Academy Awards from eight nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor for Scofield, and Best Adapted Screenplay. It also won the Golden Globe and BAFTA awards for Best Film, and is consistently ranked among the finest British films.
