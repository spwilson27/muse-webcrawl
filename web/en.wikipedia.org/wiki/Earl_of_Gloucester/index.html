Earl of Gloucester was a title in the Peerage of England, created several times between the twelfth and fourteenth centuries. The earldom was associated with the county and city of Gloucester and was held by some of the most powerful magnates of medieval England, including members of the de Clare family and a son of King Edward III.

First creation (1121/1122)

The title was first created for Robert of Caen (c. 1090–1147), an illegitimate son of King Henry I. Robert of Gloucester became the chief supporter of his half-sister, the Empress Matilda, during the civil war known as the Anarchy, and was the principal military commander of her party against King Stephen. His son William FitzRobert, 2nd Earl of Gloucester, died in 1183, and the earldom passed through his daughters; one of them, Isabel, married the future King John.

The de Clare earls

In the thirteenth century the earldom came to the de Clare family, earls of Hertford, who were also great lords in the Welsh Marches. The most famous holder was Gilbert de Clare, 7th Earl of Gloucester (1243–1295), known as "Gilbert the Red", builder of Caerphilly Castle and a central figure in the baronial politics of Henry III's and Edward I's reigns. His son, Gilbert de Clare, 8th Earl of Gloucester, was killed at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314, after which the de Clare inheritance was divided among his three sisters.

Later creations

The title was created again in 1337 for Hugh de Audley, and in 1397 for Thomas le Despenser, who was executed in 1400 and attainted, extinguishing his creation. The earldom is not to be confused with the later dukedom of Gloucester, created for royal princes. The title Earl of Gloucester has not been recreated in modern times.
