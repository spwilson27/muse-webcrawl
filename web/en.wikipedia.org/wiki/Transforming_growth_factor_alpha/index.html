Transforming growth factor alpha

Transforming growth factor alpha (TGF-α) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TGFA gene. As a member of the epidermal growth factor (EGF) family, TGF-α is a mitogenic polypeptide: it binds the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and drives cell proliferation, differentiation, and development.

Function

TGF-α is synthesized as a membrane-anchored precursor of about 160 amino acids that is cleaved by metalloproteinases such as TACE/ADAM17 to release the mature 50-amino-acid growth factor. It is produced by macrophages, brain cells (keratinocytes and neurons among them), and various epithelial tissues. Functions include wound healing, stimulation of gastric acid suppression via prostaglandin pathways, hair follicle development, and neural cell proliferation. Despite its name, it is structurally and functionally unrelated to transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β).

Clinical significance

Overexpression of TGF-α is observed in many tumors, including lung, breast, pancreatic, and head and neck cancers, where autocrine EGFR signaling promotes growth; it is a target of interest in EGFR-directed therapies. Knockout mice lacking TGF-α show wavy hair, curly whiskers, and eye abnormalities. TGF-α in the suprachiasmatic nucleus has also been implicated in regulating circadian activity rhythms.
