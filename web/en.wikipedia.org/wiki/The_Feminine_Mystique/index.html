The Feminine Mystique
Author: Betty Friedan
Published: 1963
Subject: Women's roles, second-wave feminism
Famous phrase: "the problem that has no name"

The Feminine Mystique is a 1963 book by Betty Friedan, widely credited with igniting second-wave feminism in the United States. Based on surveys of her Smith College classmates and research into women's magazines and psychology, Friedan argued that postwar suburban housewives were unfulfilled by a culture insisting fulfilment lay only in domesticity and motherhood.

Argument

The "feminine mystique" was the idealised image of feminine domestic bliss that left educated women with "the problem that has no name" — a pervasive dissatisfaction Friedan traced to lack of meaningful work and identity beyond husband and children.

Impact

A bestseller, it helped galvanise the women's movement and Friedan co-founded the National Organization for Women (NOW) in 1966; later critics noted its focus on white, middle-class women.
