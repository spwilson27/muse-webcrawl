Position weight matrix

A position weight matrix (PWM), also known as a position-specific weight matrix (PSWM) or position-specific scoring matrix (PSSM), is a commonly used representation of motifs (patterns) in biological sequences. A PWM is derived from a set of aligned sequences that share a functional site — for example, the binding sites of a transcription factor — and records how likely each base or amino acid is at each position of the motif.

Construction

Starting from a multiple sequence alignment, the observed counts of each symbol at each position are converted to frequencies, usually with pseudocounts added to avoid zeros. Frequencies are then compared with a background model to give log-odds scores: the entry for symbol s at position j is typically log(ps,j / ps), where ps is the background frequency of s.

Scoring

A candidate sequence is scored by summing the matrix entries for its symbols at each position. High-scoring windows are predicted to match the motif. The standard model assumes that positions within a motif are statistically independent; extensions such as dinucleotide weight matrices relax this assumption.

Applications

PWMs are used to predict transcription factor binding sites, splice sites, translation initiation sites and other sequence signals, and are central to motif databases such as JASPAR. They are often visualised as sequence logos, in which the height of each letter reflects its information content at that position.
