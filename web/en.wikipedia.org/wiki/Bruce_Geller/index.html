Bruce Geller (13 October 1930 – 21 May 1978) was an American lyricist, screenwriter, director and television producer. He is best remembered as the creator of the television series Mission: Impossible (1966–1973) and co-creator of Mannix (1967–1975).

Career

Geller graduated from Yale University and began in New York, writing for variety and anthology programmes and musicals before moving into television drama in Los Angeles. He wrote and produced for series including Rawhide, The Dick Powell Show and Dr. Kildare. His Mission: Impossible, produced at Desilu/Paramount, became a signature spy series, and Geller won an Emmy Award for Outstanding Dramatic Series and for writing its pilot. He later created or produced series such as Mannix (with Richard Link and William Levinson), Bronk and The Interns, and directed the feature film Harry in Your Pocket (1973). The Mission: Impossible premise was revived for the long-running film franchise beginning in 1996.

Death

Geller died on 21 May 1978 when the Cessna Skymaster he was piloting crashed near Santa Barbara, California.
