Electronic System for Travel Authorization

The Electronic System for Travel Authorization (ESTA) is an automated system operated by U.S. Customs and Border Protection that determines the eligibility of visitors to travel to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program (VWP). Travellers from VWP countries must obtain an approved ESTA before boarding a carrier to the United States by air or sea.

Operation

Applicants complete an online form with biographic, passport and eligibility information and pay a fee; most applications are decided within minutes, though travellers are advised to apply at least 72 hours before departure. An approved ESTA is valid for two years and for multiple entries, but it does not guarantee admission: the final decision rests with the CBP officer at the port of entry, and each stay is limited to 90 days for business or tourism.

History

ESTA was introduced under the Implementing Recommendations of the 9/11 Commission Act of 2007 to screen VWP travellers before departure. Eligibility questions and data requirements have been expanded over time, and travellers who have visited certain countries, or who hold dual nationality with certain states, may be required to obtain a full visa instead.
