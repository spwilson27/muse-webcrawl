East Falkland (Spanish: Isla Soledad) is the largest island of the Falkland Islands in the South Atlantic, with an area of 6,605 km2. It contains the capital, Stanley, and most of the archipelago's population. The island is separated from West Falkland by the Falkland Sound.

Geography

The island has two main land masses: the northern part, dominated by the Wickham Heights range whose highest point is Mount Usborne (705 m), and the southern peninsula of Lafonia, joined to the north by a narrow isthmus at Darwin and Goose Green. The coastline is deeply indented, with Berkeley Sound, Port William and Choiseul Sound among its major inlets.

History

The first settlement on the Falklands, Port Louis, was founded by the French in 1764 on Berkeley Sound. East Falkland saw heavy fighting during the Falklands War of 1982, including the battles of Goose Green, Mount Longdon, Two Sisters, Wireless Ridge and Mount Tumbledown around Stanley. Argentine forces surrendered at Stanley on 14 June 1982.

Economy and settlements

Sheep farming dominates the rural economy of the "camp" (countryside), supplemented by fishing licences, tourism and the military garrison at RAF Mount Pleasant, which is also the islands' international airport. Settlements outside Stanley include Goose Green, Darwin, Port Louis, Johnson's Harbour and Fitzroy.
