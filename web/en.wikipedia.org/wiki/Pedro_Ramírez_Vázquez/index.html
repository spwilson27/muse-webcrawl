Pedro Ramírez Vázquez

Born: 16 April 1919, Mexico City, Mexico
Died: 16 April 2013, Mexico City, Mexico
Occupation: Architect

Pedro Ramírez Vázquez (16 April 1919 – 16 April 2013) was a Mexican architect and designer, one of the most influential figures of twentieth-century Mexican architecture. His best-known work is the National Museum of Anthropology in Mexico City (1964), famous for its vast courtyard covered by a single monumental concrete umbrella fountain.

Works

Other major projects include the Azteca Stadium (1966), built for the 1968 Olympics and the 1970 FIFA World Cup; the Museum of Modern Art in Mexico City; the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe (with others); and the Mexican pavilion at several world expositions. He also designed the iconic identity and graphic system for the 1968 Mexico City Olympic Games, working with Lance Wyman.

Public life

Ramírez Vázquez served as president of the organizing committee of the 1968 Olympic Games and later as Secretary of Human Settlements and Public Works. He was a member of the International Olympic Committee. He died in Mexico City on his 94th birthday.
