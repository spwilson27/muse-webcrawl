Battle of Ravenna (1512)

The Battle of Ravenna, fought on 11 April 1512 (Easter Sunday), was a major engagement of the War of the League of Cambrai, between the forces of France and Ferrara under Gaston de Foix, Duke of Nemours, and the Spanish-Papal army of the Holy League under Ramón de Cardona. The French won the battle but Gaston de Foix was killed, and the victory proved strategically fruitless: within months the French were driven from Italy. Ravenna was among the bloodiest battles of the Italian Wars and is noted for its large-scale artillery duel.

The battle

The Spanish army, entrenched behind a ditch and wagons by the River Ronco, was bombarded by French and Ferrarese artillery — including guns of Alfonso d'Este fired into the Spanish flank — in one of the first great artillery duels in European warfare. After hours of bombardment, cavalry and infantry attacks broke the Spanish position, though the Spanish infantry withdrew in good order. Gaston de Foix was killed leading a charge against retreating Spanish troops.

Aftermath

Ravenna city was sacked. Despite the victory, the loss of Gaston de Foix and the arrival of Swiss forces for the League reversed the campaign; by August 1512 the French had evacuated Milan and most of their gains in Italy.
