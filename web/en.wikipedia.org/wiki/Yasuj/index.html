Yasuj (Persian: یاسوج) is a city in the Central District of Boyer-Ahmad County, Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad province, Iran, serving as capital of the province, the county, and the district. The city lies in the Zagros Mountains of southwestern Iran, in a mountainous region known for its oak forests, waterfalls, and nomadic Lur heritage.

History

The area around Yasuj has been inhabited since ancient times, and the region formed part of the territory of the Elamites and later the Persian empires. The modern city grew from a small settlement after it was designated the provincial capital in the 20th century, expanding rapidly as administrative offices, a university, and services were established there.

Geography and climate

Yasuj sits at an elevation of about 1,831 metres in a valley of the Zagros range. It has a Mediterranean-influenced continental climate with cold, wet winters — snowfall is common — and warm, dry summers. The surrounding mountains are covered in oak woodland, and nearby attractions include the Yasuj waterfall and the Tang-e Mehrian gorge.

Demographics

At the 2016 census, Yasuj had a population of 108,505. Most inhabitants are Lurs, and the Luri language is widely spoken alongside Persian.

Economy

The local economy is based on government services, agriculture, and livestock raising by settled and semi-nomadic communities. The region produces apples, walnuts, and other mountain crops, and Yasuj serves as a market centre for the province's rural hinterland.
