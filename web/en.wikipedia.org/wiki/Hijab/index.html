Hijab

A hijab (Arabic: حِجَاب) is a head covering worn by some Muslim women in the presence of males outside their immediate family. In modern usage it generally refers to a headscarf covering the head and neck while leaving the face visible, though the term can also denote the broader Islamic practice of modest dress and conduct.

Religious basis

The practice is associated with verses of the Quran, particularly in Surah An-Nur (24:31) and Surah Al-Ahzab (33:59), which instruct women to draw their coverings over themselves. Interpretations of these verses and of the hadith literature vary among scholars and communities, and practices differ widely across the Muslim world.

Styles and variation

Related garments include the niqab, which covers the face, the chador worn in Iran, and the burqa, which covers the whole body. The wearing of hijab is legally required for women in some countries, discouraged or restricted in certain public contexts in others, and is a matter of personal choice elsewhere. It has been the subject of significant public and legal debate, particularly in Europe.
