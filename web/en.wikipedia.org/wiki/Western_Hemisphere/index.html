Western Hemisphere

The Western Hemisphere, also called the Western hemisphere or the hemisphere west of Greenwich, is the half of Earth west of the prime meridian (which passes through Greenwich, London) and east of the antimeridian. The other half is called the Eastern Hemisphere. In geopolitical terms, the Western Hemisphere is often used as a synonym for the Americas (the "New World"), though strictly it also includes portions of Europe, Africa, Antarctica and Oceania that fall west of the prime meridian.

Geography

The Western Hemisphere consists primarily of the continents of North America and South America, together with surrounding oceans: the western Atlantic, the eastern Pacific and parts of the Arctic and Southern oceans. Countries lying partly in both hemispheres include the United Kingdom, France, Spain, Algeria, Mali, Burkina Faso, Ghana, Fiji and Russia.

Usage

The term has political resonance in the Americas, notably through the Monroe Doctrine of 1823 and the Organisation of American States, whose member states are sometimes collectively described as the Western Hemisphere.
