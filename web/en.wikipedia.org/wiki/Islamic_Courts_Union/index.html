Islamic Courts Union

Active: 2000–2007

Country: Somalia

Headquarters: Mogadishu

Leaders: Sharif Sheikh Ahmed (chairman of the executive council); Hassan Dahir Aweys (head of the shura council)

Ideology: Islamism, Somali nationalism

The Islamic Courts Union (ICU; Somali: Midowga Maxkamadaha Islaamiga) was a legal and political organisation formed from a federation of Sharia courts in Mogadishu, Somalia. In 2006 it briefly became the most powerful force in the country, controlling the capital and much of southern Somalia before being driven from power by an Ethiopian military intervention in support of the Transitional Federal Government (TFG).

Origins

After the collapse of Somalia's central government in 1991, clan-based Sharia courts emerged in Mogadishu to provide basic law and order in the absence of a state. The courts, funded largely by the business community, ran police forces and prisons and suppressed the banditry and warlord checkpoints that plagued the city. In 2000 the courts united into a loose federation, the Islamic Courts Union, and in 2004 eleven courts formalised the union with a joint military force.

Takeover of Mogadishu

In early 2006 the ICU fought a coalition of warlords known as the Alliance for the Restoration of Peace and Counter-Terrorism, which was widely reported to receive funding from the United States. After months of fighting, the ICU captured Mogadishu in June 2006 — the first time the capital had been under a single authority since 1991. It went on to take Jowhar, Kismayo and much of the south, reopening Mogadishu's seaport and international airport, removing militia roadblocks and restoring a degree of security that many residents welcomed. The union was internally divided between moderates led by Sharif Sheikh Ahmed and hardliners associated with Hassan Dahir Aweys; its youth militia, al-Shabaab, became its most radical armed wing.

War and collapse

Negotiations in Khartoum between the ICU and the TFG failed as both sides prepared for war. In December 2006 Ethiopian forces, backed by the United States, intervened decisively; the ICU's forces collapsed, Mogadishu fell on 28 December, and the last stronghold at Ras Kamboni was taken in January 2007. ICU leaders fled or went into exile in Eritrea, where they later formed the Alliance for the Re-liberation of Somalia. Sharif Sheikh Ahmed went on to become president of Somalia in 2009, while al-Shabaab continued as an insurgent movement and later affiliated with al-Qaeda.
