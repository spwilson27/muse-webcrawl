George Halas

George Stanley Halas Sr.

Born: 2 February 1895, Chicago, Illinois

Died: 31 October 1983, Chicago, Illinois

Teams: Chicago Bears (player, coach, owner)

NFL championships (as coach): 6

George Stanley Halas Sr. (2 February 1895 – 31 October 1983), nicknamed "Papa Bear" and "Mr. Everything", was an American professional football player, coach and team owner. He was the founder, owner and long-time head coach of the Chicago Bears of the National Football League (NFL), and one of the founders of the league itself in 1920.

Career

Halas played end for the Decatur Staleys, the franchise that became the Bears, and took over the team in 1921, moving it to Chicago. As coach in four separate stints spanning 40 seasons, he won six NFL championships, including the famous 73–0 victory over Washington in the 1940 NFL Championship Game, in which his modernized T-formation with a man in motion revolutionized offensive football.

Innovations and legacy

Halas pioneered many practices now standard in professional football, including daily practice sessions, film analysis, placing assistant coaches in the press box, and broadcasting games on radio. He was a charter inductee into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1963. The NFC Championship trophy is named the George Halas Trophy, and the Bears wear his initials, "GSH", on their jersey sleeves.
