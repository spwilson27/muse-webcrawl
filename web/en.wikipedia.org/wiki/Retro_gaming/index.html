Retro gaming

Retro gaming is the playing and collecting of older personal computer, console and arcade video games, typically those of the 1970s through the early 2000s. It is also known as classic gaming or old-school gaming. Enthusiasts play on original hardware, through emulation, or via modern re-releases and mini consoles.

Culture and collecting

Retro gaming grew from nostalgia among players who grew up with systems such as the Atari 2600, Nintendo Entertainment System, Sega Mega Drive, Super Nintendo and the original PlayStation, and from an interest in preserving gaming history. A collector market exists for cartridges, discs, arcade cabinets and memorabilia, with rare graded items selling for very high prices. Events such as retro expos, speedrunning communities, and new games developed in period styles for old hardware ("homebrew") keep the culture active.

Ways of playing

Besides original hardware, players use software emulators, FPGA-based systems that replicate hardware behaviour, official download services, plug-and-play and "mini" consoles, and subscription libraries. Legal and ethical questions surround the distribution of game ROM images, and preservationists argue for exemptions to keep out-of-print software accessible; museums and archives have increasingly collected video games as cultural artefacts.
