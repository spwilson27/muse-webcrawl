Inchoate offence

An inchoate offence (in American English, inchoate offense) is a type of crime that is committed in preparation for, or in the process of attempting, another crime. The principal inchoate offences in common law jurisdictions are attempt, conspiracy and incitement (solicitation).

Main categories

Attempt involves an act that goes beyond mere preparation towards committing a substantive offence, done with intent to commit it. Conspiracy is an agreement between two or more people to commit an unlawful act. Incitement or solicitation consists of encouraging, requesting or commanding another person to commit a crime. Aiding and abetting a completed crime is generally treated as secondary liability rather than an inchoate offence, though the categories overlap in some jurisdictions.

Rationale

Inchoate liability allows the law to intervene before harm occurs and to punish those whose culpability is established even though the completed crime did not take place. Typically an inchoate offence merges into the completed offence if the crime is carried out, so a defendant cannot be convicted of both the attempt and the full crime for the same conduct.
