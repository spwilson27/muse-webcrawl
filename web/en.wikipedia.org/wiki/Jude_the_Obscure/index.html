Jude the Obscure is a novel by Thomas Hardy, first published in book form in 1895. It is Hardy's last completed novel. Its protagonist, Jude Fawley, is a working-class stonemason whose ambition to become a scholar at the university city of Christminster (Oxford) is thwarted by class prejudice, while his unconventional relationship with his cousin Sue Bridehead collides with the marriage laws and morality of Victorian England.

Plot

Jude Fawley, an orphan in the Wessex village of Marygreen, teaches himself Latin and Greek while working as a stonemason, dreaming of study at Christminster. Tricked into a loveless marriage with the earthy Arabella Donn, he is abandoned by her and moves to Christminster, where he works restoring colleges he cannot enter. There he falls in love with his free-thinking cousin Sue Bridehead, who is married to the schoolmaster Richard Phillotson. Sue leaves Phillotson and lives with Jude; both obtain divorces, but refuse to marry legally, fearing the contract would kill their love. Society ostracises them. Jude's son by Arabella, the melancholy child nicknamed Father Time, arrives to live with them; destitute and despairing, the boy kills Sue's two children and himself, leaving a note: "Done because we are too menny." Sue, crazed by grief, returns in penance to Phillotson; Jude, tricked back into marriage with Arabella, dies alone in Christminster during Remembrance Day festivities.

Themes

The novel attacks the institutions of marriage, religion and the class-bound university system. Hardy explores the conflict between intellect and instinct, the new woman represented by Sue, and the cruelty of social convention toward natural human desire. The tragedy is driven as much by institutional barriers and bad luck as by character.

Reception and legacy

Serialised in bowdlerised form in Harper's New Monthly Magazine from December 1894, the novel was denounced on publication — one critic dubbed it "Jude the Obscene", and the Bishop of Wakefield claimed to have burned his copy. The hostile reception contributed to Hardy's decision to abandon novel-writing and devote himself to poetry. The book is now recognised as one of the great English novels of the nineteenth century, frequently adapted for stage and screen, including the 1996 film Jude starring Christopher Eccleston and Kate Winslet.
