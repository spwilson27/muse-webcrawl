Som tam

Som tam (ส้มตำ)
Type: Salad
Place of origin: Isan (northeastern Thailand) and Laos
Main ingredients: Shredded unripe papaya, lime, fish sauce, palm sugar, chillies, peanuts
[Image: A plate of som tam — shredded green papaya salad with tomatoes, long beans and crushed peanuts, served with sticky rice]

Som tam (Thai: ส้มตำ, pronounced [sôm tām]), also known as tam maak hoong in Lao, is a spicy salad made from shredded unripe papaya. It originated in the Isan region of northeastern Thailand and in Laos, where it is a staple, and has become one of Thailand's most popular dishes nationwide and internationally. The name combines som ("sour") and tam ("pounded"), after the mortar and pestle used to bruise the ingredients together.

Preparation

Green papaya is shredded into matchsticks and pounded in a clay mortar with garlic, chillies, palm sugar, lime juice and fish sauce, together with long beans, cherry tomatoes and roasted peanuts; the balance of hot, sour, salty and sweet is adjusted to the eater's order. Variants include tam Thai (the central Thai style, with dried shrimp and peanuts), tam pu (with preserved crab) and tam pla ra (with fermented fish sauce, favoured in Isan and Laos). It is typically eaten with sticky rice, grilled chicken (gai yang) and raw vegetables.

Origins

Papaya itself is an American fruit that reached Southeast Asia via the Columbian exchange, and the enzyme papain in the unripe fruit gives the salad part of its character. The dish's modern form developed in the Lao-speaking world, blending the introduced fruit and chillies with long-established fermented-fish flavourings.
