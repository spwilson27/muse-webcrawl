San Fermín

LocationPamplona, Navarre, Spain
Dates6–14 July
HonoursSaint Fermín, first bishop of Pamplona
Famous forRunning of the bulls (encierro)

The festival of San Fermín (Sanfermines) is a week-long, historically rooted celebration held annually in Pamplona, Spain, in honour of Saint Fermín, co-patron of Navarre. It runs from noon on 6 July, opened by the chupinazo rocket fired from the city hall balcony, until midnight on 14 July, closing with the song "Pobre de mí".

Running of the bulls

The festival's most famous event is the encierro, the running of the bulls, held at 8 a.m. each morning from 7 to 14 July. Six fighting bulls and a group of steers are released to run 825 metres through the old town's streets to the bullring, accompanied by hundreds of runners dressed in white with red scarves and sashes. The run lasts about three minutes; injuries, mainly from falls and gorings, occur most years, and deaths have been recorded since 1910.

Other traditions

The festival combines religious observance — the procession of Saint Fermín on 7 July — with street music, parades of giants and big-heads (gigantes y cabezudos), fireworks competitions, rural sports and the afternoon bullfights at the Plaza de Toros. The red-and-white dress of participants is a defining visual feature.

History and fame

The festival merged religious feasts for Saint Fermín with commercial fairs and bull festivals over the centuries. Its global fame owes much to Ernest Hemingway, whose 1926 novel The Sun Also Rises is set largely during the Sanfermines; Hemingway returned repeatedly, and the festival now draws well over a million visitors.
