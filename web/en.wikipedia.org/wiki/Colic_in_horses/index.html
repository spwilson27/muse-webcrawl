Colic in horses

Colic in horses
Type: Abdominal pain syndrome
Common causes: Gas, impaction, displacement, torsion, strangulation
Specialty: Equine veterinary medicine

Colic in horses is defined as abdominal pain, but rather than a single disease it is a clinical sign of many possible gastrointestinal and other disorders. Colic is one of the most common emergencies in equine practice and a leading cause of death in horses, with outcomes ranging from mild, self-limiting episodes to life-threatening surgical emergencies.

Causes

Causes include gas accumulation, feed impactions, sand enteropathy, intestinal displacements, twists (volvulus) and torsions, strangulating lesions such as lipomas, enteroliths, parasite burdens including tapeworms and large strongyles, gastric ulcers, and conditions outside the gut such as laminitis. Risk factors include sudden feed changes, dental problems, restricted water intake, and stall confinement.

Signs

Signs include pawing, flank watching, rolling, sweating, inappetence, reduced gut sounds, kicking at the abdomen, and repeated lying down and getting up. Severity of pain does not always correlate with severity of disease, so veterinary assessment is important.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment includes heart rate, mucous membrane colour, gut sounds, rectal examination, nasogastric intubation, blood tests, ultrasound, and abdominocentesis. Many cases resolve with analgesia, fluids, and walking; others require hospitalisation or abdominal surgery. Early referral improves survival in surgical cases.

Prevention

Consistent feeding routines, forage-based diets, constant access to water, regular dental care, parasite control, and daily turnout reduce colic risk.
