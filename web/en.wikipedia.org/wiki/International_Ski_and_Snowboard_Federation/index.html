The International Ski and Snowboard Federation, commonly known by its French initials FIS (Fédération Internationale de Ski et de Snowboard), is the highest international governing body for skiing and snowboarding. Founded on 2 February 1924 in Chamonix, France, during the first Winter Olympic Games, it is headquartered at Oberhofen am Thunersee, Switzerland, and has more than 130 affiliated national ski associations.

Disciplines

FIS governs the Olympic disciplines of alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, ski jumping, Nordic combined, freestyle skiing and snowboarding, as well as non-Olympic disciplines including telemark skiing, speed skiing, grass skiing and masters competition. It sets competition rules, maintains world ranking points systems, and sanctions the annual World Cup circuits and the biennial FIS World Ski Championships in each discipline.

History

The federation grew out of the International Ski Commission founded in Christiania (Oslo) in 1910. Fourteen delegates formally established the FIS at Chamonix in 1924. Snowboarding, originally governed by a separate federation, was brought under the FIS umbrella in the 1990s ahead of its Olympic debut at Nagano in 1998. In 2022, at its congress in Milan, the federation changed its official name from the International Ski Federation to the International Ski and Snowboard Federation to reflect snowboarding's equal status.

Governance

The FIS is led by a president and council elected by its congress of member associations. Gian Franco Kasper served as president from 1998 until 2021, when Johan Eliasch was elected. The federation has faced debate over calendar expansion, environmental impacts of ski racing, athlete safety on speed courses, and the awarding of events amid human-rights and climate concerns.
