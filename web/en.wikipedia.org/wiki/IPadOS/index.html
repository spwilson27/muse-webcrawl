iPadOS is a mobile operating system developed by Apple Inc. for its iPad line of tablet computers. It was announced at the company's Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC) in June 2019 as a rebranding of iOS on iPad, reflecting the growing divergence of features between the iPhone and iPad lines, and was released as iPadOS 13 on September 24, 2019.

History

The iPad originally ran iOS, sharing nearly all features with the iPhone. As the iPad gained larger screens, Apple Pencil support and keyboard accessories, Apple added tablet-specific features such as Split View and Slide Over multitasking. With iPadOS the split became formal: the iPad received its own release train, a more desktop-like Safari browsing experience, support for external storage and mouse/trackpad input, and home-screen widgets.

Features

Subsequent versions added Stage Manager windowed multitasking (iPadOS 16), a redesigned lock screen, pro apps such as Final Cut Pro and Logic Pro for iPad, and — in iPadOS 18 — a Calculator app for the first time, Apple Intelligence features, and greater home-screen customisation. iPadOS continues to share its core with iOS and macOS, all derived from the Darwin foundation.

Reception

Reviewers have generally praised iPadOS for making the iPad more capable as a productivity device, while noting that its multitasking model remains more constrained than a conventional desktop operating system.
