Square du Temple

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Square du Temple in the 3rd arrondissement

The Square du Temple is a garden and public square in the 3rd arrondissement of Paris, France, in the Marais district. Covering about 7,700 square metres, it occupies part of the site of the former Temple, the fortified enclosure of the Knights Templar from which the surrounding Temple quarter takes its name.

History

The medieval Temple fortress contained the tower in which Louis XVI and his family were imprisoned during the French Revolution; the young Louis XVII died there in 1795. The great tower was demolished under Napoleon, and the rest of the complex was progressively cleared in the 19th century. The garden was laid out in the English style in 1857 under the direction of Jean-Charles Adolphe Alphand, who directed the creation of parks for Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris.

Features

The square contains an artificial lake with a small waterfall fed by water from the Canal Saint-Martin, lawns, and a bandstand. It is planted with a notable variety of trees, including exotic specimens such as a ginkgo, a Turkish hazel, and American honey locusts. A statue commemorates the chansonnier Pierre-Jean de Béranger, who lived nearby. The garden is surrounded by the town hall of the 3rd arrondissement and the streets of the old Temple quarter, including the Rue de Bretagne with its Marché des Enfants Rouges.

Access

The square is served by the Temple and Arts et Métiers stations of the Paris Métro. It is open during the day and is a popular resting place for residents of the upper Marais.
