Bogatyr
TypeEpic hero of East Slavic folklore
SourceByliny (oral epic poems)
Famous examplesIlya Muromets, Dobrynya Nikitich, Alyosha Popovich
SettingKievan Rus'

A bogatyr (Russian: богатырь) is a stock character in medieval East Slavic legends and byliny — heroic knights of Kievan Rus' endowed with extraordinary strength, courage and loyalty, who defend the Russian land against enemies such as steppe nomads, dragons and sorcerers.

Famous bogatyrs

The three best-known are Ilya Muromets, the mighty peasant hero who served Prince Vladimir of Kiev after being healed of paralysis; Dobrynya Nikitich, the dragon-slayer and diplomat; and Alyosha Popovich, the clever youngest of the trio. They are depicted together in Viktor Vasnetsov's famous painting Bogatyrs (1898).

Characteristics

Bogatyrs often possess semi-historical prototypes — Ilya and Dobrynya may be based on real figures — and their tales mix pagan and Christian elements, recorded by folklorists from oral tradition in the 18th and 19th centuries. The word derives from a Turkic-Mongolic root meaning "hero" or "valiant warrior".
