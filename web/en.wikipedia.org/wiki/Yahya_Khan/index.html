Yahya Khan

Agha Muhammad Yahya Khan
Born: 4 February 1917, Chakwal, British India
Died: 10 August 1980, Rawalpindi, Pakistan
Office: President of Pakistan (1969–1971); Commander-in-Chief of the Pakistan Army (1966–1971)

Agha Muhammad Yahya Khan (4 February 1917 – 10 August 1980) was a Pakistani military officer and statesman who served as the third president of Pakistan from 1969 until the country's defeat and breakup in the 1971 war. A career soldier commissioned into the British Indian Army, he fought in the Second World War and rose to become Commander-in-Chief of the Pakistan Army in 1966.

Presidency

Yahya took power on 25 March 1969 when President Ayub Khan resigned amid mass protests, imposing martial law. He promised and held Pakistan's first free general elections in December 1970, intended as a path back to civilian rule. The elections produced a split verdict: Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's Awami League won an outright majority based in East Pakistan, while Zulfikar Ali Bhutto's Pakistan Peoples Party dominated in the West. Deadlock over transferring power led Yahya's regime to launch a military crackdown in East Pakistan in March 1971 (Operation Searchlight), triggering the Bangladesh Liberation War.

Fall

After India's intervention and Pakistan's surrender in Dhaka on 16 December 1971, Yahya resigned on 20 December and handed power to Bhutto. He spent his later years under house arrest and in obscurity, dying in Rawalpindi in 1980.
