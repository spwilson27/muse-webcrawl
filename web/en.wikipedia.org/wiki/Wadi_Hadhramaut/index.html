Wadi Hadhramaut

Wadi Hadhramaut (also spelled Hadramaut or Hadramawt) is the largest wadi (valley) in the Arabian Peninsula, located in the Hadhramaut Governorate of eastern Yemen. Running roughly parallel to the coast of the Gulf of Aden for about 160 kilometres, it is the heartland of the historic Hadhramaut region and is famous for its ancient mudbrick tower towns.

Geography

The wadi is an intermittently flowing river valley cut into a high plateau, with cliffs rising up to several hundred metres on either side. Rain falling on the plateau feeds tributaries that join the main valley; the wadi eventually continues as Wadi Masila toward the Arabian Sea. Agriculture in the valley depends on floodwater irrigation (spate irrigation) and wells.

Towns

The valley's principal towns include Shibam, Say'un, and Tarim. Shibam, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is famous for its densely packed mudbrick high-rise houses, sometimes nicknamed the "Manhattan of the desert". Say'un hosts the former palace of the Kathiri sultan, and Tarim is a renowned centre of Islamic learning with hundreds of mosques.

History

In antiquity the region was part of the Hadhramaut kingdom, a centre of the frankincense trade. Hadhrami merchants and scholars later established a wide diaspora across East Africa, India, and Southeast Asia.
