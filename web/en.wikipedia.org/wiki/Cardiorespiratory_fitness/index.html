Cardiorespiratory fitness

Cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF), also called cardiovascular or aerobic fitness, is the ability of the circulatory and respiratory systems to supply oxygen to the skeletal muscles during sustained physical activity. It is most often quantified as VO2 max — the maximum rate of oxygen consumption during incremental exercise — and is regarded by the American Heart Association as a clinical vital sign, since low CRF is a powerful predictor of cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality.

Physiology

Oxygen delivery depends on cardiac output (heart rate × stroke volume), the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood, and the ability of working muscle to extract oxygen. Training increases stroke volume, capillary density and mitochondrial content, raising VO2 max typically by 15–20% in sedentary adults; genetics accounts for a large share of both baseline fitness and trainability, as shown by the HERITAGE family study. VO2 max declines with age by roughly 10% per decade after early adulthood.

Measurement

The reference method is a maximal cardiopulmonary exercise test on a treadmill or cycle ergometer with breath-by-breath gas analysis. Because maximal testing is impractical in many settings, submaximal and field tests estimate fitness from heart-rate responses or performance: the Cooper test (distance run in 12 minutes), the multi-stage fitness ("beep") test, the Rockport one-mile walk test, and wearable-device estimates based on heart rate during exercise.

Health significance

Landmark cohort studies, including the Aerobics Center Longitudinal Study, found that low CRF is associated with substantially higher risk of death from all causes — in several analyses a stronger predictor than smoking, hypertension or diabetes. Each 1-MET improvement in fitness is associated with roughly a 10–25% reduction in mortality risk. Regular moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise — the basis of public-health guidelines recommending at least 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly — improves CRF along with insulin sensitivity, blood pressure and mental health.
