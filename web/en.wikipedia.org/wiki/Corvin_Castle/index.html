Corvin Castle, also known as Hunyadi Castle, is a Gothic-Renaissance castle in Hunedoara, in Transylvania, Romania. It is considered one of the largest castles in Europe and is frequently listed among the most impressive medieval fortresses in the country. The castle was laid out in 1446 by John Hunyadi, Voivode of Transylvania, on the site of an earlier keep donated to his father by King Sigismund of Hungary in 1409.

The fortress was built in stages, with subsequent work under Matthias Corvinus and later owners adding Renaissance and Baroque elements. Its features include a long drawbridge, massive defensive towers, the Knights' Hall and the Diet Hall, an inner courtyard, and a chapel. A well in the courtyard is surrounded by a legend that it was dug by Turkish prisoners who were promised freedom when they struck water.

The castle is popularly associated with Vlad the Impaler, who is said in legend to have been imprisoned there by Hunyadi, though the historical basis for the story is disputed. After a major fire in 1854 and periods of neglect, the castle was restored and now operates as a museum and tourist attraction, and has served as a filming location for numerous productions.
