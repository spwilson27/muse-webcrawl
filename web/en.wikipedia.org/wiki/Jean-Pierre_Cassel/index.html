Jean-Pierre Cassel

Image placeholder: Jean-Pierre Cassel

Jean-Pierre Cassel (born Jean-Pierre Crochon; 27 October 1932 – 19 April 2007) was a French actor and dancer. He appeared in over 200 films and television productions between 1953 and 2007, and became known in the 1960s for his light-hearted comedies and his work with directors of the French New Wave.

Career

Cassel was born in Paris and began his career as a dancer and extra, appearing uncredited in Gene Kelly's The Happy Road (1957). He achieved fame in comedies by Philippe de Broca, including The Love Game (1960) and Male Companion (1964), and starred in Claude Chabrol's The Rooster in the Wine and in Les Jeux de la comtesse Dolingen de Gratz. Internationally he is remembered as King Louis XIII in Richard Lester's The Three Musketeers (1973) and The Four Musketeers (1974), and for roles in Murder on the Orient Express (1974) and Luis Buñuel's The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie (1972).

Also an accomplished stage actor, singer, and dancer, Cassel toured in a tribute show to Serge Gainsbourg in later life and played the narrator in productions honouring Fred Astaire, whom he admired.

Personal life

Cassel was the father of actors Vincent Cassel and Cécile Cassel, and of Mathias Cassel, a rapper known as Rockin' Squat. He died of cancer in Paris in 2007, aged 74.
