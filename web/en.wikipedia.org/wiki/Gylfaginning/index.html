Gylfaginning

Part ofProse Edda
AuthorAttributed to Snorri Sturluson
DateEarly 13th century
LanguageOld Norse

Gylfaginning (Old Norse: "the tricking of Gylfi") is the first major part of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, following the Prologue. It is the principal systematic source for Norse mythology, presenting the creation of the world, the deeds of the gods, and the destruction and rebirth of the cosmos at Ragnarök in dialogue form.

Gylfi questioning Hárr, Jafnhárr and Þriði

Frame and content

In the frame narrative, Gylfi, a legendary Swedish king, travels in disguise under the name Gangleri to Asgard to test the wisdom of the Æsir. He is received in a great hall by three enthroned figures — Hárr (High), Jafnhárr (Just-as-High) and Þriði (Third), aspects of Odin — who answer his questions about the origin of the world, Ymir and the first humans Ask and Embla, the world-tree Yggdrasill, the gods and their dwellings, and the coming of Ragnarök and the world renewed. The account weaves in numerous myths, including the binding of Fenrir, Thor's fishing for the World Serpent, and the death of Baldr. At the dialogue's end the hall vanishes, and Gylfi returns home to spread the tales — a device allowing the Christian Snorri to present pagan lore as an illusion staged by the Æsir.

Together with the Poetic Edda, Gylfaginning shapes nearly all modern knowledge of Norse myth, and many of its stories are known from no other source.
