Coventry Climax

Coventry Climax was a British manufacturer of forklift trucks, fire pumps and, most famously, lightweight racing engines. Founded in Coventry, England, the company became one of the most successful engine suppliers in Formula One history, powering cars to multiple World Championships in the late 1950s and 1960s.

History

The company originated in the early 20th century as an engine manufacturer and was renamed Coventry Climax in 1917. Its breakthrough product was the FW ("Feather Weight") engine of 1950, a small all-aluminium overhead-camshaft unit originally designed for a portable fire pump for the Auxiliary Fire Service. Because it was compact, light and powerful for its size, racing car builders quickly adopted it.

Racing engines

The FWA racing derivative of the fire pump engine dominated the 750 cc and 1100 cc classes, and the FPF four-cylinder engine powered Cooper to Formula One World Championships in 1959 and 1960 with Jack Brabham. For the 1.5-litre formula of 1961–1965, Coventry Climax developed the FWMV V8, which powered championship-winning cars of Lotus and Jim Clark, as well as Brabham and Cooper. Climax engines won 40 Formula One Grands Prix in total. When the 3-litre formula arrived in 1966, Climax withdrew from Grand Prix racing rather than fund a new engine, and the business was later absorbed into British Leyland in 1969.

Other products

Climax engines also powered road cars, including the Lotus Elite (FWE engine), and the firm's fork lift truck division was a major part of its later business, trading as Climax Conveyancer.
