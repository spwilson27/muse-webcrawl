Judiciary of the Netherlands

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The Hoge Raad (Supreme Court) building in The Hague.

The judiciary of the Netherlands is the system of courts administering justice in the Netherlands. It is a civil-law system in which judges are career professionals appointed for life, courts do not engage in judicial review of Acts of Parliament against the constitution, and — a distinctive feature — there is no jury trial.

Structure

At first instance, eleven district courts (rechtbanken) hear civil, criminal and administrative cases, with subdistrict judges handling minor matters. Appeals lie to four courts of appeal (gerechtshoven). At the apex sits the Supreme Court (Hoge Raad der Nederlanden) in The Hague, which reviews questions of law through cassation rather than re-trying facts.

Administrative justice is divided among specialized bodies: the Administrative Jurisdiction Division of the Council of State (Raad van State), the highest general administrative court, plus the Central Appeals Tribunal for social security and civil-service matters and the Trade and Industry Appeals Tribunal for economic administrative law.

Features

Article 120 of the Dutch Constitution forbids courts from testing Acts of Parliament and treaties against the constitution, though courts routinely review legislation against directly effective international law, notably the European Convention on Human Rights. Prosecutions are brought by the Public Prosecution Service (Openbaar Ministerie), which can also decide not to prosecute under the expediency principle. Judges are appointed by the Crown for life (with retirement at 70) and are administered, in budgetary and organizational terms, by the Council for the Judiciary, established in 2002 to insulate court administration from the Ministry of Justice.

The Netherlands is also host to numerous international courts in The Hague, including the International Court of Justice and the International Criminal Court, though these are not part of the Dutch judiciary itself.
