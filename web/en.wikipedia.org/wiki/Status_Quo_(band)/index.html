Status Quo are an English rock band formed in London in 1962 by schoolfriends Francis Rossi and Alan Lancaster, beginning as the Scorpions and later the Spectres and Traffic Jam before adopting their best-known name in 1969. The band first found fame with the psychedelic single "Pictures of Matchstick Men" (1968) but achieved enduring success in the 1970s with a stripped-down boogie-rock sound built on driving twelve-bar grooves and Rossi and Rick Parfitt's riffing Telecasters, worn slung low with heads nodding in unison.

Career

Albums such as Piledriver (1972), Hello! (1973), Quo (1974), and Blue for You (1976) made them one of Britain's biggest rock acts, with singles including "Caroline" (1973), "Down Down" (their only UK number one, 1974), and "Rockin' All Over the World" (1977), a John Fogerty cover that became their signature song. In 1985 they opened the Live Aid concert at Wembley with that song. In 1986 they scored a UK number two with "In the Army Now." Rossi and Parfitt fronted the band for over five decades through numerous lineup changes; bassist Lancaster left in 1985 and drummer John Coghlan in 1981, and legal disputes followed over the name.

Parfitt died on 24 December 2016; Rossi continued the band with guitarist Richie Malone. Status Quo are among the most commercially successful British groups of all time, with more than 60 UK chart hits — more than any other rock band — and record album sales estimated at over 118 million worldwide. Their famous denim-clad, head-shaking image has made them both critically derided for their simplicity and beloved as a British institution; they hold honorary doctorates and appeared on Coronation Street in 2005.
