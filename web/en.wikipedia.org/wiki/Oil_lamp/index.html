Oil lamp

An oil lamp is a lamp used to produce light continuously for a period of time using an oil-based fuel source. The use of oil lamps began thousands of years ago and continued until the widespread introduction of electric lighting; they remain in use for religious ceremonies and in regions without reliable electricity.

History

Stone lamps burning animal fat date to the Upper Paleolithic. Clay and bronze lamps were common throughout the ancient Mediterranean world, typically burning olive oil with a linen or papyrus wick. In the 18th and 19th centuries, improved designs such as the Argand lamp and later kerosene lamps greatly increased brightness and safety.

Fuel and design

Traditional fuels include olive oil, sesame oil, ghee, and fish oil; later lamps burned kerosene (paraffin). A typical lamp consists of a fuel reservoir, a wick that draws fuel upward by capillary action, and sometimes a glass chimney to protect the flame and improve airflow.

Cultural significance

Oil lamps feature prominently in festivals such as Diwali, in which rows of small earthen lamps (diyas) are lit, and in the Hanukkah menorah, which commemorates the rededication of the Temple in Jerusalem.
