Gross motor skill

Gross motor skills are the abilities usually acquired during childhood as part of a child's motor learning. By the time they reach two years of age, almost all children are able to stand up, walk and run, walk up stairs, etc. These skills are built upon, improved and better controlled throughout early childhood, and continue in refinement throughout most of an individual's life. Gross motor skills involve the large muscles of the body and whole-body movements, in contrast to fine motor skills, which involve small, precise movements of the hands and fingers.

Development

Gross motor development follows a roughly cephalocaudal sequence: head control, rolling, sitting, crawling, standing, walking (typically around 12 months), running, climbing, hopping, and skipping. Milestones are monitored in paediatrics; standardized tools such as the Denver Developmental Screening Test and the ages-and-stages questionnaires help identify delay. Development depends on both neuromuscular maturation and opportunity to practise.

Types of gross motor activity

Gross motor activities are often grouped into locomotor skills (walking, running, jumping, galloping), non-locomotor or stability skills (balancing, twisting, bending), and object-control or manipulative skills (throwing, catching, kicking, striking). Postural control underlies all three.

Assessment and disorders

Physiotherapists and occupational therapists assess gross motor function using instruments such as the Gross Motor Function Measure (GMFM), widely used with children with cerebral palsy. Developmental coordination disorder, muscular dystrophy, and cerebral palsy can all impair gross motor skill acquisition; early intervention programmes aim to improve strength, balance, and coordination through play-based practice.
