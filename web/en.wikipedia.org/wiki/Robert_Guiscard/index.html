Robert Guiscard (c. 1015 – 17 July 1085), born Robert de Hauteville, was a Norman adventurer remembered for the conquest of southern Italy and Sicily. His nickname, in contemporary Latin Viscardus and Old French Viscart, is often rendered "the Resourceful", "the Cunning" or "the Fox". He was Count of Apulia and Calabria from 1057 to 1059, when Pope Nicholas II invested him as Duke, and he was also made Duke of Sicily — an island he had yet to conquer with his brother Roger.

Early life in Italy

Robert arrived in Italy around 1047 with a handful of followers and initially lived as a highwayman in Calabria. At the Battle of Civitate (1053) the Normans defeated a papal coalition and captured Pope Leo IX; Robert distinguished himself in the fighting. He married Alberada of Buonalbergo, then annulled the marriage and married the Lombard princess Sikelgaita, sealing an alliance with Salerno.

Conquests

By the Treaty of Melfi (1059) the papacy recognised Norman gains. Bari, the last Byzantine stronghold in Italy, fell to Guiscard in 1071; Palermo was taken in 1072 in the joint campaign with Roger. The Lombard principality of Salerno fell in 1076–77, leaving Guiscard master of mainland southern Italy.

Wars with Byzantium

In 1081 Guiscard invaded the Byzantine Empire, defeating Emperor Alexios I Komnenos at the Battle of Dyrrhachium. Recalled to Italy in 1082 to defend Pope Gregory VII against Emperor Henry IV, his forces rescued the pope in 1084 but sacked Rome in the process. He returned to Greece in 1084–85, winning a naval victory off Corfu.

Death and legacy

Guiscard died of fever at Cephalonia on 17 July 1085, mid-campaign. His son Bohemond became a leader of the First Crusade and Prince of Antioch. Guiscard's conquests laid the groundwork for the Kingdom of Sicily created by his nephew Roger II. Dante placed him in Paradise among the warrior spirits in the Heaven of Mars.
