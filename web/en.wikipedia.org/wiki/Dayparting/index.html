Dayparting

In advertising, dayparting is the practice of dividing the broadcast day into several parts, during which different types of programming or audiences are targeted, and advertisers choose the segments that best fit their products. In digital media buying, the term has been extended to scheduling online advertisements — also called ad scheduling — so that campaigns run only at certain hours of the day or days of the week.

Broadcast dayparts

Radio and television stations price advertising according to audience size in each daypart. Typical United States television dayparts include:

DaypartApproximate hours
Early morning6:00 a.m. – 9:00 a.m.
Daytime9:00 a.m. – 4:30 p.m.
Early fringe4:30 p.m. – 7:30 p.m.
Prime access7:30 p.m. – 8:00 p.m.
Prime time8:00 p.m. – 11:00 p.m.
Late news / late night11:00 p.m. – 2:00 a.m.
Overnight2:00 a.m. – 6:00 a.m.

In radio, morning and afternoon "drive time" dayparts command premium rates because of commuter audiences. Prime time commands the highest television rates, as it draws the largest and broadest audience.

Digital ad scheduling

Online advertising platforms allow advertisers to set schedules specifying the hours and days their adverts appear and to adjust bids by time period. Dayparting is used to concentrate spending when conversions are most likely — for example, a restaurant advertising near meal times, or a business-to-business advertiser pausing campaigns overnight and at weekends when prospects are not working. Analytic tools track conversion rates by hour to inform these schedules.
