Bundle of His

The bundle of His (BH) or His bundle (HB), also known as the atrioventricular bundle, is a collection of heart muscle cells specialised for electrical conduction. As part of the electrical conduction system of the heart, it transmits the electrical impulses from the atrioventricular node (located between the atria and the ventricles) to the point of the apex of the fascicular branches via the bundle branches. The fascicular branches then lead to the Purkinje fibres, which provide electrical conduction to the ventricles, causing the cardiac muscle of the ventricles to contract at a paced interval.

Anatomy and function

The bundle is named after Swiss cardiologist Wilhelm His Jr., who discovered it in 1893. It is the only normal electrical pathway between the atria and ventricles, since the fibrous skeleton of the heart otherwise insulates them. After leaving the AV node, the impulse travels down the bundle, which penetrates the central fibrous body and divides into right and left bundle branches running in the interventricular septum. Conduction through the bundle is rapid, so that the delay introduced at the AV node — which allows the atria to empty into the ventricles first — is not compounded, and both ventricles contract nearly simultaneously from apex to base.

Clinical significance

Disease of the bundle and its branches produces heart block and bundle branch blocks, visible on the electrocardiogram as characteristic widening of the QRS complex. Block within the His bundle itself can be demonstrated by an intracardiac electrogram recorded with a catheter (the His bundle electrogram). Since 2017, His bundle pacing — placing a pacemaker lead directly at the bundle — has been developed as a physiological alternative to conventional right-ventricular pacing, preserving the heart's natural activation pattern.
