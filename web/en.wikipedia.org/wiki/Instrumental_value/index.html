Instrumental value

Instrumental value is the value that something has as a means to a desired or valuable end. It contrasts with intrinsic value, which is valuable in and for itself. Money is a classic example of an instrumentally valuable thing: it is prized not for its own sake but for what it can buy.

In ethics and economics

Philosophers distinguish instrumental goods, which derive their worth from other goods, from final goods valued as ends. In environmental ethics, a central debate concerns whether nature has only instrumental value for humans or intrinsic value independent of human use.
