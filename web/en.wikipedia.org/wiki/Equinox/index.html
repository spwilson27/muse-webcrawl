Overview

An equinox is the moment when the Sun crosses the celestial equator, passing from one hemisphere to the other. It happens twice a year, around 20 March and 22–23 September. On those days the Sun rises almost exactly in the east and sets in the west, stands overhead at noon on the Equator, and day and night are nearly equal everywhere on Earth — hence the name, from Latin for 'equal night'.

In practice nights are slightly shorter than days at the equinoxes, because the Sun is a disc rather than a point and atmospheric refraction lifts its image: the truly equal day comes a few days before the March equinox and after the September one.

Seasons

Equinoxes and solstices divide the astronomical seasons. The March equinox traditionally begins northern spring and southern autumn; the September equinox begins northern autumn and southern spring. At the poles, each equinox ends six months of daylight or darkness in a single long twilight.
