Lifeboat (shipboard)

Shipboard lifeboat
TypeSurvival craft
RegulationSOLAS Convention, LSA Code
LaunchingDavit-launched or free-fall

A lifeboat is a small, rigid or inflatable boat carried for emergency evacuation from a ship. Modern shipboard lifeboats are usually totally enclosed, motorised survival craft designed to carry all persons assigned to them safely away from a stricken vessel.

Types

Davit-launched lifeboats are lowered from gravity davits along the ship's side; free-fall lifeboats, common on cargo ships, slide down a ramp at the stern and dive away from the ship. Partially enclosed boats remain permitted on some existing vessels. Lifeboats must be capable of launching with the ship listing 20 degrees and underway speed of 5 knots.

Regulation

The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS), first adopted in response to the sinking of the Titanic in 1912, requires passenger ships to carry enough lifeboat capacity for all aboard, with boats on each side able to accommodate at least half the total complement. The International Life-Saving Appliance (LSA) Code sets construction, equipment and testing standards, including rations, water, and a motor capable of 6 knots.

Equipment

Standard equipment includes oars or a motor, compass, sea anchor, bilge pump, rations and water, first aid kit, signalling equipment such as rockets and a torch, and thermal protective aids.
