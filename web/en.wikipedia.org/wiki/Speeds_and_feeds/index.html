Speeds and feeds

The phrase speeds and feeds in machine shop and manufacturing contexts refers to two separate velocities used together on machine tools such as lathes and milling machines: the cutting speed (or surface speed) and the feed rate. Choosing them correctly balances material removal rate, tool life, surface finish and part accuracy.

Cutting speed

Cutting speed is the speed at which the cutting edge passes over the workpiece surface, usually expressed in surface feet per minute (SFM) or metres per minute. For rotating tools it depends on tool diameter and spindle speed (RPM). Suitable values depend on the workpiece material, the tool material (high-speed steel, carbide, ceramic) and the use of cutting fluid.

Feed rate

Feed rate is the velocity at which the tool advances into or along the workpiece, expressed in millimetres per minute or inches per minute, and often derived from the desired chip load per tooth. Excessive feed can chip or break tools, while insufficient feed can cause rubbing, work hardening and premature wear.

Calculation

Machinists consult charts, tooling manufacturers' recommendations, and formulas — for example spindle speed = (cutting speed × 1000) / (π × diameter in mm) — and adjust them empirically for machine rigidity, depth of cut and coolant. CNC control software and machining calculators automate these computations.
