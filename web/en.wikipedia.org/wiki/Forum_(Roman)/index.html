Forum (Roman)

A forum (Latin forum, plural fora) was a public square in a Roman municipium or city, the centre of politics, commerce, law, religion and social life — the Roman equivalent of the Greek agora. Every Roman city had a forum, usually located at the intersection of the main streets, the cardo and decumanus.

Forum
Type: Public square / civic centre
Typical buildings: Basilica, curia, temples, market halls, rostra
Famous example: Forum Romanum, Rome

Features

Fora were typically paved rectangular plazas surrounded by porticoes, containing a temple (often to Jupiter or the imperial cult), a basilica for law courts and business, the curia or council house, a market, triumphal arches and speaking platforms. As cities grew, emperors added specialised fora — the Imperial Fora in Rome of Caesar, Augustus, Vespasian, Nerva and Trajan.

Function

Citizens gathered there for elections, trials, speeches, processions, commerce and announcements. The word survives in “forensic” and modern senses of a place of public discussion.
