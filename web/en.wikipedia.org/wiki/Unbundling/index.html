Unbundling

Unbundling is the process of separating a product, service, or company operation into distinct components that can be offered, priced, or regulated separately. It is the opposite of bundling, in which multiple offerings are combined into a single package.

Applications

In regulated network industries such as electricity, natural gas, and telecommunications, unbundling refers to the legal or functional separation of monopoly network activities (transmission and distribution) from competitive activities (generation and retail supply), so that competitors can access the network on equal terms. European Union energy directives, for example, required the unbundling of transmission system operators from generation and supply interests. In telecommunications, local loop unbundling gives competing providers access to the incumbent's last-mile copper lines.

In marketing and technology, unbundling describes how a comprehensive product is broken into specialized offerings — for instance, how functions of a full-service platform or department store are taken over by focused startups or apps. In scholarly publishing, the "big deal" journal bundles faced pressure toward unbundled, article-level access.
