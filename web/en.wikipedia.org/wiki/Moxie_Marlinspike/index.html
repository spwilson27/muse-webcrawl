Moxie Marlinspike

Born: Matthew Rosenfeld
Occupation: Cryptographer, computer security researcher
Known for: Signal Protocol; co-founding Signal; SSL research

Moxie Marlinspike is the pseudonym of Matthew Rosenfeld, an American entrepreneur, cryptographer, and computer security researcher. He is best known as the founder of Open Whisper Systems and co-creator of the Signal messaging app and the Signal Protocol, the end-to-end encryption system now used by billions of people in Signal, WhatsApp, and other messengers. Earlier, he was known for influential research on weaknesses in SSL/TLS certificate validation.

Career

Marlinspike worked in enterprise security in San Francisco before becoming a prominent independent researcher, presenting at Black Hat and DEF CON. His 2002-era sslsniff tool and his 2009 null-prefix certificate attack demonstrated practical man-in-the-middle attacks on HTTPS; he also introduced "convergence" as an alternative to the certificate authority system. He co-founded Whisper Systems in 2010, which produced the encrypted voice app RedPhone and the encrypted texting app TextSecure; Twitter acquired the company in 2011, and the apps were later released as open source.

Signal

In 2013 Marlinspike founded Open Whisper Systems to continue developing the tools, unifying them as Signal. He co-designed the Signal Protocol (built on the double ratchet algorithm, prekeys, and the X3DH key agreement) with Trevor Perrin. In 2018 he co-founded the Signal Foundation with WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton and served as Signal Messenger's CEO until stepping down in January 2022, when he was succeeded on an interim basis by Acton. Marlinspike is also known for an unconventional lifestyle, including living aboard a sailboat and solo ocean crossings, and for experiments in anonymity such as a 2021 non-fungible token prank highlighting inconsistencies in NFT rendering.
