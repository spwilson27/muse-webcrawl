Saudi Arabian–led intervention in Yemen

The Saudi Arabian–led intervention in Yemen began on 26 March 2015, when a coalition of Arab states led by Saudi Arabia launched Operation Decisive Storm, a campaign of airstrikes and a naval blockade against Houthi forces in Yemen. The intervention supported the internationally recognised government of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, who had fled the capital Sanaa after the Houthis (Ansar Allah) seized it in 2014–15. The United Nations and aid agencies described the resulting conflict as one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Background

The Houthi movement, rooted in Yemen's Zaydi Shia north, rose against the government amid the turmoil following the 2011 uprising and the transition that brought Hadi to power. In September 2014 Houthi forces, allied with former president Ali Abdullah Saleh, took Sanaa; by March 2015 they were advancing on Aden.

Campaign

Coalition aircraft struck Houthi military targets and infrastructure across the country, while Emirati and government ground forces, supported by coalition special forces, retook Aden and much of the south in 2015. The war then settled into stalemate around Marib, Hodeidah and Taiz. The Houthis responded with ballistic-missile and drone attacks into Saudi Arabia and, later, against shipping in the Red Sea. Coalition airstrikes repeatedly hit civilian gatherings, markets, hospitals and a school bus (2018), drawing international condemnation; a UN-brokered truce in April 2022 sharply reduced fighting, though a formal peace remained elusive.

Humanitarian impact

The war displaced millions and pushed Yemen to the brink of famine; the UNDP estimated some 377,000 direct and indirect deaths by the end of 2021, with children under five accounting for a large share. Cholera, economic collapse and the blockade of Hodeidah port compounded the crisis.
