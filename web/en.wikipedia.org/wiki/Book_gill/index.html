Book gill

[Image placeholder: Book gill]

A book gill is a type of respiratory organ found in horseshoe crabs and some extinct chelicerates. It consists of numerous thin, page-like membranous lamellae attached to a base plate on the appendages of the opisthosoma, whose flapping movements circulate water over the membranes for gas exchange.

Structure and function

Each book gill contains over a hundred delicate, chitin-surfaced sheets stacked like the pages of a book, greatly increasing surface area. Rhythmic beating of the gill-bearing appendages ventilates them. Book gills are thought to be homologous with the book lungs of arachnids such as spiders and scorpions, supporting the hypothesis that those air-breathing organs evolved from aquatic book gills in terrestrialising ancestors.
