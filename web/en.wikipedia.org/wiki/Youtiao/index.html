Youtiao

Place of originChina
Main ingredientsWheat flour dough, salt, leavening
ServingBreakfast, often with soy milk or congee
Other namesChinese cruller, Chinese oil stick; yau ja gwai (Cantonese)

Youtiao is a long, golden-brown, deep-fried strip of dough widely eaten in China and across East and Southeast Asia. Lightly salted and puffy inside, it is typically made by joining two strips of leavened dough and stretching them before frying — the pair symbolising, in folklore, the Song-dynasty traitor Qin Hui and his wife.

Preparation and consumption

Two thin strips of dough are pressed together and pulled long, then deep-fried until they balloon into a crisp, airy stick. Youtiao is a classic breakfast item eaten plain, dipped in soy milk, wrapped in rice rolls (ci fan tuan), or served with congee; it also appears sliced in soups and hot pots.

Regional variants

Cantonese cuisine calls it yau ja gwai ("oil-fried devil"), and variants spread with the Chinese diaspora to Vietnam (quẩy), Indonesia (cakwe), Malaysia, Thailand, and the Philippines (bicho-bicho/shakoy relatives).
