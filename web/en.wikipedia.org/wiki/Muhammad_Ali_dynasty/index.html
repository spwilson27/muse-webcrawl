The Muhammad Ali dynasty was the ruling dynasty of Egypt and Sudan from the 19th to the mid-20th century. It is named after its progenitor, Muhammad Ali Pasha, an Ottoman Albanian commander regarded as the founder of modern Egypt. The dynasty ruled in various forms — as Ottoman viceroys (walis), hereditary khedives, sultans and finally kings — from 1805 until the abolition of the monarchy in 1953.

History

Muhammad Ali seized power in Cairo in 1805 amid the chaos following the French occupation of Egypt, destroyed the Mamluk elite in the Citadel massacre of 1811, and built a modern army, navy and centralised administration. His armies conquered Sudan and Arabia and twice threatened the Ottoman Empire itself before European intervention confined his hereditary rule to Egypt. His grandson Isma'il Pasha obtained the title of Khedive in 1867, presided over the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869, and plunged the country into debt, leading to British occupation in 1882.

Egypt was declared a British protectorate in 1914, and under Sultan (later King) Fuad I it became nominally independent in 1922. King Farouk, who reigned from 1936, was overthrown in the Egyptian revolution of 1952; his infant son Fuad II was formally deposed when the republic was proclaimed in June 1953, ending the dynasty after 148 years.
