Argentine Patriotic League

Members of the Argentine Patriotic League, early 1920s

The Argentine Patriotic League (Spanish: Liga Patriótica Argentina) was a nationalist paramilitary organisation in Argentina, formed in the aftermath of the Tragic Week of January 1919. Drawing its membership from the upper and middle classes, the League organised armed civilian groups to break strikes, attack workers' organisations and persecute left-wing, anarchist and Jewish communities, with the tolerance and support of police, military and government figures.

Formation and the Tragic Week

The League crystallised during the Semana Trágica of January 1919, when a general strike in Buenos Aires was suppressed with extensive violence. Civilian groups drawn from elite social clubs organised themselves into "brigades" to defend order, patrolling neighbourhoods and raiding union halls, left-wing newspapers and the Jewish neighbourhood of Once, in what is often described as the only pogrom in the Americas. The organisation was formally founded shortly afterwards, with the naval officer Manuel Domecq García among its leading figures.

Activities

Throughout the 1920s the League functioned as a nationwide strikebreaking and anti-communist militia. Its brigades provided armed escorts, informants and propaganda for employers and the state, and it campaigned against "foreign agitators" and for patriotic education. Historians regard it as an early expression of the nationalist right in Argentina, foreshadowing later movements that drew on Catholic, anti-communist and anti-liberal ideas.

The League declined in the later 1920s as the labour movement weakened and its role was absorbed by other nationalist organisations and by state repression, but its networks and ideas fed into the nationalist politics that culminated in the 1930 military coup.
