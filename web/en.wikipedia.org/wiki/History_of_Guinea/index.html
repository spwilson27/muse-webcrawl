History of Guinea

The history of Guinea in West Africa includes the medieval empires of Ghana and Mali, the Imamate of Futa Jallon, French colonisation, and independence in 1958.

Pre-colonial and colonial periods

The region was successively influenced by the Ghana and Mali empires and by Songhai power. In the 18th century Fulani Muslims established a theocratic imamate in Futa Jallon. France built up the colony of French Guinea in the late 19th century, resisted by Samori Touré until his capture in 1898.

Independence

Guinea voted "No" in France's 1958 referendum and became independent under Ahmed Sékou Touré, whose socialist rule lasted until 1984. A military regime under Lansana Conté followed; Guinea held its first democratic presidential election in 2010, and a further coup occurred in 2021.
