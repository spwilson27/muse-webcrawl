Concordance (mathematics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Two knots are concordant if they bound an embedded cylinder in four dimensions

In mathematics, specifically in knot theory, a concordance is an equivalence relation on knots defined using smooth structures of 4-manifolds. Two knots K1 and K2 are said to be concordant if there exists an embedded cylinder (a smooth annulus) joining them inside the product of the 3-sphere with an interval.

Definition and properties

Equivalently, two knots are concordant if they together bound a smoothly embedded annulus in S3 × [0,1]. A knot that is concordant to the unknot is called a slice knot, because it bounds a smoothly embedded disk in the 4-ball. Every ribbon knot is slice, but whether every slice knot is ribbon — the slice-ribbon conjecture — remains an open problem.

Under the operation of connected sum, equivalence classes of knots modulo concordance form an abelian group called the (algebraic or topological) knot concordance group, with the mirror image of a knot serving as its inverse. The group is known to be infinitely generated and contains both a large free abelian part and 2-torsion.

Invariants

Many classical knot invariants are concordance invariants, including the knot signature and the Alexander polynomial conditions given by the Fox–Milnor theorem: if a knot is slice, its Alexander polynomial factors as f(t)f(t−1) for some polynomial f. Modern tools from Heegaard Floer homology and Khovanov homology, such as the tau invariant and Rasmussen's s-invariant, give powerful obstructions to sliceness and detect elements of the concordance group invisible to classical invariants.
