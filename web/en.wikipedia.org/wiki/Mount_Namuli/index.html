Mount Namuli

Mount Namuli is the second highest mountain in Mozambique and the highest peak in Zambezia Province, rising to 2,419 metres (7,936 ft). It is a granite inselberg — an isolated dome of rock rising abruptly from the surrounding plains — located northeast of the town of Gurué, amid one of Mozambique's main tea-growing districts.

Environment

Namuli is the centrepiece of the Namuli massif, a mosaic of montane forest, grassland and miombo woodland that forms part of the Eastern Afromontane biodiversity hotspot and an important area of endemism in south-eastern Africa. Its moist montane forests harbour numerous species found nowhere else, including the Namuli apalis (a small warbler discovered in 1932 and not seen again for decades), the Thyolo alethe, pygmy chameleons, and endemic plants. Scientific expeditions in the 2000s and 2010s, supported by conservation organisations, documented many undescribed species.

Cultural significance

The mountain is sacred to the local Lomwe (Macua) people, whose traditions hold that their ancestors originated on Namuli; access to the summit area is traditionally restricted and ceremonies are held there. The massif's slopes and surroundings are densely farmed, and the forests face pressure from smallholder clearing, fire, and logging, making Namuli a conservation priority.
