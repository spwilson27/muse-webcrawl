Categories (Aristotle)

The Categories (Greek: Κατηγορίαι, Katēgoriai) is a text from Aristotle's Organon that enumerates all the possible kinds of things that can be subjects or predicates of a proposition. It is often regarded as the starting point of Western ontology and classification.

The ten categories

Aristotle lists ten categories: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, posture, possession, action, and passion (being acted upon). Primary substances — individual men, horses — are fundamental; secondary substances are species and genera.

Influence

Through Porphyry's Isagoge and Boethius's Latin translations and commentaries, the Categories became a foundational text of medieval Scholasticism and logic teaching for over a millennium.
