Dhammapada

The Dhammapada (Pali: धम्मपद; Sanskrit: धर्मपद, Dharmapada) is a collection of sayings of the Buddha in verse form and one of the most widely read and best-known Buddhist scriptures. The original version is part of the Khuddaka Nikaya of the Sutta Pitaka in the Pali Canon.

Contents

The Pali Dhammapada contains 423 verses arranged in 26 chapters (vagga) organised by theme, including the Pairs (Yamaka), Heedfulness (Appamada), the Mind (Citta), Flowers (Puppha), the Fool (Bala), the Wise (Pandita), the Arahant, the Elephant (Naga), Craving (Tanha) and the Brahmana. Its famous opening declares that mind precedes all mental states: suffering follows an impure mind as the wheel follows the ox's foot, while happiness follows a pure mind like a never-departing shadow.

Versions and parallels

Parallel recensions survive in Gandhari Prakrit (the Gandhari Dharmapada), in Sanskrit (the Udanavarga, much larger, with some 1,050 verses), and in Chinese and Tibetan translation, indicating the collection's wide circulation in early Buddhism.

Translations and influence

The Dhammapada was among the first Buddhist texts translated into European languages (F. Max Müller's 1881 Sacred Books of the East edition) and remains a popular introduction to Buddhist ethics, emphasising non-violence, self-mastery, vigilance and generosity.
