Land degradation

Land degradation is a process in which the value of the biophysical environment is diminished by human activities, reducing the land's capacity to support ecosystems, agriculture, and livelihoods. It encompasses soil erosion, salinization, nutrient depletion, compaction, desertification, deforestation, and loss of soil organic matter and biodiversity.

Causes

Major drivers include unsustainable agriculture (overcropping, over-irrigation, excessive tillage), overgrazing, deforestation, urbanization, mining, industrial pollution, and climate change, which intensifies drought and extreme rainfall in many regions. The UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) estimates that up to 40% of the world's land is degraded, affecting some 3.2 billion people, and that land degradation costs the global economy trillions of dollars annually in lost ecosystem services.

Consequences and responses

Degraded land yields less food, stores less carbon, holds less water, and supports less biodiversity, contributing to food insecurity, migration, and conflict. Responses include sustainable land management (terracing, agroforestry, conservation agriculture, rotational grazing), restoration programmes such as Africa's Great Green Wall and the UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration, and the goal of Land Degradation Neutrality under the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 15.3), adopted by the UNCCD in 2015.
