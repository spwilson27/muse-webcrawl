Government spending

[Image: construction of a publicly funded bridge, an example of government investment spending]

Government spending (public expenditure) is the money spent by governments — central, regional and local — on goods, services, investment and transfer payments. In advanced economies it totals roughly 35–55% of GDP (the United States toward the lower end, France and the Nordic countries at the top), financed by Taxation and borrowing.

Composition and role

The largest categories are social protection (pensions and benefits), health, education, defence, debt interest, and infrastructure. Economists distinguish government consumption and investment — which enter GDP directly — from transfers, which redistribute purchasing power. Spending is a lever of Fiscal policy: automatic stabilisers (unemployment benefits, progressive taxes) cushion recessions without new decisions, while discretionary stimulus or austerity is a deliberate choice, its effects governed by fiscal multipliers debated since John Maynard Keynes. Wagner's law observed that the state's share tends to grow with development; ageing populations and health costs keep upward pressure on budgets, making spending control a permanent theme of Public finance.
