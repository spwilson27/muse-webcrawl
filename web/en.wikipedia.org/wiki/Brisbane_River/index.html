The Brisbane River (Turrbal: Maiwar) is the longest river in South East Queensland, Australia, flowing 344 kilometres from Mount Stanley through the city of Brisbane to Bramble Bay in Moreton Bay. It is a major waterway of the region, crossed in the city by numerous bridges, used by the CityCat ferry service, and lined by landmarks including South Bank, Kangaroo Point, and the Story Bridge. The river has flooded catastrophically several times, most notably in 1893, 1974, 2011, and 2022.

Course

The river rises in the hills near Mount Stanley east of Kingaroy and flows south, joined by tributaries including the Stanley River, before being impounded by Wivenhoe Dam, built after the 1974 floods for flood mitigation and water supply. Downstream it passes Ipswich — where it is joined by the Bremer River — and meanders through Brisbane's suburbs in a series of reaches, before widening into its estuary and emptying into Moreton Bay.

History

The river sustained the Turrbal and Jagera peoples for millennia. Explorer John Oxley named it in 1823 after Sir Thomas Brisbane, Governor of New South Wales, and the Moreton Bay penal settlement was established on its banks in 1824–25. The river served as Brisbane's port and transport artery; extensive dredging straightened and deepened the channel, and the Port of Brisbane now operates near the mouth at Fisherman Island.

Flooding

The 1893 floods destroyed the Victoria Bridge and inundated much of the city. On Australia Day 1974, floodwaters peaked at 5.45 metres in the city, inundating some 13,000 buildings. In January 2011 a peak of 4.46 metres flooded tens of thousands of homes across Brisbane and Ipswich. Further major flooding in February 2022 again inundated riverside suburbs, prompting continuing debate about flood mitigation and development on the floodplain.

Bridges and transport

Major crossings include the Story Bridge (1940), the Captain Cook Bridge, the Goodwill Bridge, the William Jolly Bridge, the Gateway (Sir Leo Hielscher) Bridges, and the Clem Jones Tunnel. CityCat and Cross River ferries operate passenger services along the city reaches, and the Brisbane Riverwalk provides a floating pedestrian and cycle path along the northern bank.
