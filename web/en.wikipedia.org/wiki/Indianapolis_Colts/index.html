Indianapolis Colts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indianapolis Colts are a professional American football team based in Indianapolis, Indiana. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) South division. The team plays its home games at Lucas Oil Stadium. Founded in Baltimore in 1953 as the Baltimore Colts, the franchise relocated to Indianapolis in 1984 in a famous overnight move.

Baltimore era

The Baltimore Colts won NFL championships in 1958 and 1959 behind quarterback Johnny Unitas; the 1958 championship game against the New York Giants, won 23–17 in sudden death, is often called "The Greatest Game Ever Played". The Colts won Super Bowl V in the 1970 season. In March 1984, owner Robert Irsay moved the team to Indianapolis overnight using Mayflower moving vans, a relocation that remains controversial in Baltimore.

Indianapolis era

After years of mediocrity, the Colts drafted quarterback Peyton Manning first overall in 1998. Under Manning and head coach Tony Dungy, the Colts became perennial contenders and defeated the Chicago Bears in Super Bowl XLI in February 2007, the franchise's first title in Indianapolis. They returned to the Super Bowl in the 2009 season, losing to the New Orleans Saints in Super Bowl XLIV. Manning departed in 2012 and was succeeded by Andrew Luck, the first overall pick that year; Luck retired abruptly in 2019, and the team has cycled through quarterbacks since, drafting Anthony Richardson in 2023.

Rivalries and identity

The Colts' most notable modern rivalry is with the New England Patriots, forged through repeated Manning–Brady playoff meetings in the 2000s. The team's horseshoe logo and blue-and-white uniforms are direct continuations of the Baltimore era.
