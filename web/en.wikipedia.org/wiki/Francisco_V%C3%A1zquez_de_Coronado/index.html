Francisco Vázquez de Coronado

Born1510, Salamanca, Spain
Died22 September 1554, Mexico City
OccupationConquistador, explorer
Known forExpedition to the Seven Cities of Cíbola, 1540–1542

Francisco Vázquez de Coronado (1510 – 22 September 1554) was a Spanish conquistador and explorer who led a large expedition from what is now Mexico to present-day Kansas through parts of the southwestern United States between 1540 and 1542. The expedition was prompted by reports of the Seven Cities of Gold, or Cíbola, relayed by Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca and Friar Marcos de Niza.

The expedition

Coronado's party included some 350 Spanish soldiers and many more indigenous allies and servants. They captured the Zuni pueblo of Hawikuh in 1540, wintered at Tiguex near modern Albuquerque — fighting the Tiguex War against the Rio Grande pueblos — and in 1541 followed reports of Quivira onto the Great Plains, reaching central Kansas. Finding no golden cities, the expedition returned to Mexico in 1542.

Discoveries

Members of the expedition were the first Europeans to see the Grand Canyon (under García López de Cárdenas) and the Colorado River, and they encountered the bison herds of the Llano Estacado.

Later life

Coronado faced investigations into the expedition's conduct and finances, lost the governorship of Nueva Galicia, and died in Mexico City in 1554.
