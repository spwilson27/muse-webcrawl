The Iranian languages are a branch of the Indo-Iranian languages within the Indo-European language family, spoken natively by the Iranian peoples. They are spoken across the Iranian plateau and beyond — from eastern Turkey and the Caucasus through Iran, Afghanistan and Central Asia to western China (Xinjiang) — by roughly 150 to 200 million people.

History

The Old Iranian period is attested by Avestan, the liturgical language of Zoroastrianism, and Old Persian, written in cuneiform and known from Achaemenid inscriptions such as Behistun. Middle Iranian languages (roughly 300 BC–AD 800) include Middle Persian (Pahlavi), Parthian, Bactrian, Sogdian and Khwarezmian, as well as the steppe language of the Scythians and Sarmatians, whose principal modern descendant is Ossetian. The New Iranian period, from the Islamic conquest onward, is dominated by New Persian (Farsi, Dari and Tajik), alongside Pashto, Kurdish varieties (Kurmanji, Sorani, Southern Kurdish), Balochi, Gilaki, Mazandarani, Luri, Talysh and many smaller languages.

Classification

Iranian languages are conventionally divided into a Western group (including Persian, Kurdish and Balochi) and an Eastern group (including Pashto, Ossetian, the Pamir languages and the extinct Sogdian and Bactrian). The division reflects early isoglosses in the treatment of certain consonants. Most modern Iranian languages are written in Perso-Arabic script, while Tajik uses Cyrillic and Ossetian uses Cyrillic (and historically Georgian and Latin) scripts.
