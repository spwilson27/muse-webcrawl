Neuf-Brisach

Neuf-Brisach (German: Neubreisach) is a commune in the Haut-Rhin department in Alsace in north-eastern France. It is a fortified town founded in 1697, designed by the military engineer Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban, and is considered his masterpiece: an octagonal star fort with a regular grid of streets, built from scratch on the plain west of the Rhine.

History

After the Treaty of Ryswick (1697) obliged France to return Breisach, on the east bank of the Rhine, to the Empire, Louis XIV ordered a replacement fortress on the French bank. Vauban laid out an ideal fortified town: an octagon of bastioned ramparts surrounded by a second outer defensive ring, with straight streets on a grid pattern meeting at a central square, the Place d'Armes, flanked by the church of Saint-Louis and the town hall.

Later history

The fortress saw little action for most of its existence; it was besieged in 1870 during the Franco-Prussian War, when it was bombarded and surrendered after 33 days. The town suffered damage again in 1945 during the fighting for the Colmar Pocket. Since 2008 the fortifications have been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Fortifications of Vauban.

Layout

The octagonal plan, broad ramparts, and four monumental gates survive almost intact, and the town within retains Vauban's ordered grid. Neuf-Brisach is frequently cited as the purest example of the "third system" of Vauban's fortification design.
