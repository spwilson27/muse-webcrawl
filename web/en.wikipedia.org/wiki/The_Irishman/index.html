The Irishman (subtitled onscreen as I Heard You Paint Houses) is a 2019 American epic crime film directed by Martin Scorsese and written by Steven Zaillian, based on Charles Brandt's 2004 memoir of Frank Sheeran. It stars Robert De Niro as Sheeran, a truck driver turned hitman for the Bufalino crime family, alongside Al Pacino as union leader Jimmy Hoffa and Joe Pesci as crime boss Russell Bufalino.

Plot

Framed as the recollections of an elderly Sheeran in a nursing home, the film traces his rise through the Philadelphia underworld, his introduction to Hoffa, and his entanglement in the Teamsters' battles with the Kennedy administration, culminating in Hoffa's disappearance in 1975 — presented, following Sheeran's confession, as a killing he carried out himself. The final act dwells on old age, guilt and abandonment.

Production

After years of development at Paramount, Netflix financed the film's unusually large budget, driven partly by the digital de-aging visual effects used to let its septuagenarian stars play characters across five decades. At 209 minutes it is Scorsese's longest film.

Reception

The film premiered at the New York Film Festival in September 2019 to near-universal acclaim, received ten Academy Award nominations, and is regarded as a valedictory summation of Scorsese's gangster films. Historians continue to dispute Sheeran's claims about Hoffa's fate.
