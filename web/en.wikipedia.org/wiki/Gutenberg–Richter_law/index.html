Gutenberg–Richter law

Gutenberg–Richter law
Field: Seismology
Proposed by: Beno Gutenberg, Charles Richter
Relation: log N = a − bM

The Gutenberg–Richter law in seismology expresses the relationship between the magnitude and the total number of earthquakes in a region over time: the frequency of earthquakes decreases exponentially with increasing magnitude.

Formulation

The law is written log10 N = a − bM, where N is the number of events with magnitude greater than or equal to M, a describes the overall seismicity rate, and b (the b-value) is typically near 1, meaning ten times fewer events for each unit increase in magnitude.

Significance

The law holds over wide magnitude ranges and is fundamental to seismic hazard assessment, earthquake forecasting, and understanding the self-organised criticality of fault systems. Deviations in b-value can signal changes in stress or material properties.
