Hyperbolic functions

In mathematics, hyperbolic functions are analogues of the ordinary trigonometric functions, but defined using the hyperbola rather than the circle. Just as the points (cos t, sin t) form a circle with a unit radius, the points (cosh t, sinh t) form the right half of the unit hyperbola. The hyperbolic functions take a real argument called a hyperbolic angle, which measures the area of a hyperbolic sector.

Definitions

The hyperbolic sine and cosine are defined in terms of the exponential function: sinh x = (ex − e−x)/2 and cosh x = (ex + e−x)/2. From these, the hyperbolic tangent tanh x = sinh x / cosh x, and the hyperbolic cosecant, secant and cotangent are derived analogously to the trigonometric functions. They satisfy the fundamental identity cosh2 x − sinh2 x = 1.

History

Hyperbolic functions were introduced in the 1760s independently by Vincenzo Riccati and Johann Heinrich Lambert. Lambert adopted the names "hyperbolic sine" and "hyperbolic cosine", and the notation sinh and cosh became standard. The functions arise in the solution of the catenary problem — the shape of a hanging chain — and in the description of the pseudosphere and hyperbolic geometry.

Applications

Hyperbolic functions occur in many areas of physics and engineering: the profile of a hanging cable (catenary), the equations of special relativity (where rapidity is a hyperbolic angle), Laplace's equation in certain coordinate systems, the calculation of hanging structures and suspension bridges, and the activation functions of some neural network architectures use tanh.

Inverse hyperbolic functions

The inverse hyperbolic functions, arsinh, arcosh and artanh, can be expressed in terms of logarithms; for example arsinh x = ln(x + √(x² + 1)).
