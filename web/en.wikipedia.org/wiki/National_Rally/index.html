The National Rally (French: Rassemblement national, RN), known until 2018 as the National Front (Front national, FN), is a French nationalist and right-wing populist political party. Founded in 1972 by Jean-Marie Le Pen, it grew from the fringes of French politics into one of the country's largest parties, built around opposition to immigration, national preference in welfare and employment, law-and-order policies, euroscepticism and economic protectionism.

History

Jean-Marie Le Pen led the party for nearly four decades, shocking France by reaching the run-off of the 2002 presidential election, where he was defeated heavily by Jacques Chirac after a republican front formed against him. His daughter Marine Le Pen succeeded him in 2011 and pursued a strategy of dédiabolisation — "de-demonisation" — softening the party's image, expelling her father in 2015 after he repeated Holocaust-minimising remarks, and rebranding it as the National Rally in 2018. She reached the presidential run-off in 2017 and again in 2022, losing to Emmanuel Macron both times but raising her vote share to over 41% in 2022.

Recent position

In the 2022 legislative elections the RN won 89 seats, becoming the largest single opposition party in the National Assembly. Under party president Jordan Bardella it finished first in France's 2024 European Parliament election, prompting Macron to dissolve the Assembly; in the ensuing snap legislative election the RN and its allies led the first round but finished third in seats after tactical voting in the second round, while becoming the largest party by votes. Long excluded from power by a cordon sanitaire of rival parties, it remains a central — and contested — force in French politics.
