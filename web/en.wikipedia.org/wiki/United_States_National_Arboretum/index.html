U.S. National Arboretum
LocationNortheast Washington, D.C.
Area446 acres (180 ha)
Established1927
Operated byAgricultural Research Service, USDA

The United States National Arboretum is a major centre for botanical research and public gardens in Northeast Washington, D.C. Established by an Act of Congress in 1927, its 446 acres are administered by the Agricultural Research Service of the U.S. Department of Agriculture.

The arboretum is known for plant breeding that has produced popular cultivars of trees, shrubs, and flowers, as well as for beloved public collections including the National Bonsai & Penjing Museum, the National Herb Garden, and extensive azalea collections.

Features

A prominent landmark is the National Capitol Columns: 22 Corinthian columns that once stood at the east portico of the U.S. Capitol, removed during the Capitol's 1958 extension and re-erected in an open meadow at the arboretum in the 1980s. The grounds include nine miles of roadways winding through themed gardens and natural woodlands along the Anacostia River.
