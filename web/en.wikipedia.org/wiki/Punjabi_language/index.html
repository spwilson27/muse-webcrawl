Punjabi (Gurmukhi: ਪੰਜਾਬੀ; Shahmukhi: پنجابی) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by the Punjabi people in the Punjab region of Pakistan and India and by a worldwide diaspora. With roughly 125 million native speakers it is among the most widely spoken languages in the world — the majority of its speakers live in Pakistani Punjab, though the language has official status only on the Indian side of the border. Punjabi is unusual among Indo-Aryan languages in being tonal: it developed lexical tone from the loss of voiced aspirated consonants.

History

Punjabi emerged from the northwestern Prakrits (through the stage linguists call Vrachada Apabhramsha) by around the 11th century CE. Its earliest literature includes the verses of the Sufi poet Baba Farid (12th–13th century), whose hymns entered the Sikh scripture. The Janamsakhis (hagiographies of Guru Nanak), the poetry of Shah Hussain and Bulleh Shah, and the epic romance Heer of Waris Shah (1766) form the classical canon. The language was standardised in the 19th and 20th centuries, in India around the Majhi dialect of Amritsar–Lahore region.

Scripts

In India, Punjabi is written in Gurmukhi, a Brahmic script traditionally associated with Guru Angad, the second Sikh Guru, and the script of the Guru Granth Sahib. In Pakistan it is written in Shahmukhi, a Perso-Arabic script closely related to that of Urdu. The 1947 Partition of Punjab split the language's literary culture along scriptal and religious lines.

Phonology

Punjabi has three tones — low, mid (default) and high — arising historically where words began with voiced aspirates or contained h: for example, ਘੋੜਾ (kòṛā, "horse", low tone, from earlier ghoṛā) contrasts with ਕੋੜਾ (koṛā, "whip"). It retains a full series of retroflex consonants and has ten vowel phonemes.

Status and diaspora

Punjabi is the official language of the Indian state of Punjab and a scheduled language of the Indian constitution; in Pakistan, where most speakers live, it is not used officially in government or education, where Urdu predominates. Large diaspora communities in Canada, the United Kingdom, the United States and Australia sustain Punjabi media, music (including the global bhangra and Punjabi pop industries) and Gurdwara-based language teaching. It is one of the most spoken immigrant languages in Canada and the UK.
