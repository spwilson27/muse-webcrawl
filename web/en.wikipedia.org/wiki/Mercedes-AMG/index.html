Mercedes-AMG

Mercedes-AMG GmbH, commonly known as AMG, is the high-performance subsidiary of Mercedes-Benz AG, headquartered in Affalterbach, Germany. AMG was originally an independent engineering firm specialising in performance improvements for Mercedes-Benz vehicles. Its cars are hand-built, traditionally following the "one man, one engine" philosophy in which a single master technician assembles each engine and signs it with a plaque.

History

AMG was founded in 1967 by former Mercedes engineers Hans Werner Aufrecht and Erhard Melcher; the initials stand for Aufrecht, Melcher, and Großaspach (Aufrecht's birthplace). The firm gained fame in 1971 when its heavily modified 300 SEL 6.8 "Red Pig" finished first in class at the 24 Hours of Spa. Daimler-Benz progressively acquired the company between 1999 and 2005, making it a wholly owned subsidiary. Standalone AMG models include the SLS AMG (2009) and the AMG GT (2014), while the AMG One hybrid hypercar brought Formula One-derived technology to the road.

Motorsport

AMG is deeply involved in motorsport: it supplies cars for GT racing and has been the works engine and chassis partner of the Mercedes team in Formula One, based at Brackley and Brixworth in England, winning eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014 to 2021.
