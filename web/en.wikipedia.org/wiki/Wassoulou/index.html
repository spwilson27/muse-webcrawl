Wassoulou

Wassoulou (also spelled Wassulu or Ouassoulou) is a historic and cultural region of West Africa centred on the upper Niger River basin, straddling the present-day borders of southern Mali, north-eastern Guinea and northern Ivory Coast. Its name is associated with the Wassoulou Empire founded in the 19th century by Samori Touré, who resisted French colonial expansion from the 1880s until his capture in 1898.

Music

The region is internationally known for Wassoulou music, a style rooted in the traditions of Mandé hunters' societies and their music — the pentatonic melodies of the donso ngoni hunter's harp and the kamale ngoni — blended with modern instrumentation. In its contemporary form, Wassoulou music is notable for its prominent women singers, whose songs address social issues including marriage, women's rights and everyday life. Its best-known exponents include Oumou Sangaré, whose 1989 album Moussolou ("Women") brought the style to a world audience, and Nahawa Doumbia.

Geography and people

The region is inhabited mainly by Mandé-speaking peoples. Its historic towns include Bissandougou, Samori's capital, and Yanfolila in Mali. Agriculture and gold mining have long shaped its economy.
