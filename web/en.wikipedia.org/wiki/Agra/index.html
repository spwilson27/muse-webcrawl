Agra (Hindi: आगरा) is a city on the banks of the Yamuna River in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, about 230 km south-east of New Delhi. It is one of India's most visited cities, famed for the Taj Mahal, the white-marble mausoleum built by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan, and for the Agra Fort and other monuments of the Mughal era. Together with nearby Fatehpur Sikri, Agra holds three UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

History

Agra was founded in its modern form by Sikandar Lodi, Sultan of Delhi, who made it his capital in 1504. Under the Mughals from Babur onward it rose to become the empire's principal capital: Akbar rebuilt the fort and briefly founded Fatehpur Sikri nearby; Jahangir and Shah Jahan embellished the city, and Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal (1632–1653) in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal before moving the capital to Delhi in 1648. After the Mughal decline the city passed among Jats, Marathas and finally the British (1803), and it was a scene of fighting in the Indian Rebellion of 1857.

Economy and sights

Beyond the Taj Mahal and the red-sandstone Agra Fort, visitors seek the tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah (the "Baby Taj"), the Mehtab Bagh gardens across the river, and Akbar's tomb at Sikandra. Agra's economy depends heavily on tourism, alongside leather goods and footwear manufacturing, marble inlay work (pietra dura), and the sweet petha for which the city is known across India.
