Wilpena Pound


Location Flinders Ranges, South Australia
Highest point St Mary Peak (1,171 m)
Protected area Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park

Wilpena Pound, known to the Adnyamathanha people as Ikara ("meeting place"), is a natural amphitheatre of mountains in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia, within the Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park.

Geography

The Pound is a large, roughly elliptical basin about 17 km long and 8 km wide, ringed by the rugged quartzite ridges of the Heysen Range and Elder Range. The rim's highest point is St Mary Peak (Ngarri Mukuna), at 1,171 metres. The interior is largely flat and drains through Wilpena Creek, which exits through a narrow gorge. From the air the formation resembles a natural stock enclosure, hence the name "pound".

History

The Adnyamathanha people have lived in the region for tens of thousands of years, and Ikara is a significant ceremonial meeting place in their tradition. Europeans settled the Pound for wheat farming and grazing in the mid-19th century; the Hills family homestead in the interior dates from this period. Farming proved marginal because of drought and flooding, and the area was progressively protected, becoming a centrepiece of the national park.

Tourism

Wilpena Pound is one of South Australia's best-known natural attractions, with a resort and campground at its edge, walking trails to lookouts and to St Mary Peak, and scenic flights over the amphitheatre.
