The 1996 Formula One World Championship was the 50th season of FIA Formula One motor racing. It comprised sixteen Grands Prix and was won by Damon Hill driving for Williams-Renault. Hill's title made him, with his father Graham Hill, the first father-and-son pair of Formula One world champions. Williams also won the Constructors' Championship, their eighth.

The Williams FW18 dominated the 1996 season, winning 12 of 16 races.

Season summary

Defending champion Michael Schumacher left Benetton for Ferrari, while Hill's new teammate was the Canadian rookie Jacques Villeneuve, the reigning CART champion, who won on only his fourth start at the Nürburgring and pushed Hill for the title until the final round at Suzuka. Hill won eight races to clinch the championship in Japan, despite having already been dropped by Williams for 1997 in favour of Heinz-Harald Frentzen. Other winners were Schumacher, who took three victories for a rebuilding Ferrari including a celebrated wet-weather triumph in Spain, and Olivier Panis, whose victory at Monaco — in a race only three cars finished — was the last win for the Ligier team.

Legacy

The season is remembered for the introduction of a new qualifying format, the stricter 107% rule, and the continuing technical dominance of Adrian Newey's Williams designs. Hill moved to Arrows for 1997, and Villeneuve succeeded him as champion that year.
