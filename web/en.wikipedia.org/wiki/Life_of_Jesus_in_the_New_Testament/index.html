Life of Jesus in the New Testament

The life of Jesus in the New Testament is primarily outlined in the four canonical gospels, which include his genealogy and nativity, public ministry, passion, prophecy, resurrection and ascension. Other parts of the New Testament — such as the Pauline epistles, which were likely written within 20 to 30 years of each other, and which include references to key episodes in the life of Jesus such as the Last Supper — also provide information about his life. The Acts of the Apostles recounts the ascension of Jesus and the spread of his message.

Genealogy and nativity

Matthew and Luke provide genealogies tracing Jesus through Joseph back to Abraham (Matthew) or Adam (Luke), with differing lists. The nativity accounts describe his birth in Bethlehem to Mary, betrothed to Joseph; Matthew recounts the visit of the Magi, the flight into Egypt and Herod's massacre of the innocents, while Luke describes the annunciation, the census of Quirinius, the manger and the announcement to shepherds. Neither Mark nor John includes a birth narrative.

Baptism and temptation

All four gospels place the beginning of Jesus' ministry after his baptism by John the Baptist in the Jordan River, when the heavens opened and a voice declared him God's Son. The synoptic gospels then describe his forty days of fasting and temptation by Satan in the wilderness.

Public ministry

Jesus travelled through Galilee preaching the coming of the Kingdom of God, gathering twelve apostles, healing the sick, casting out demons and performing miracles such as walking on water and feeding the multitude. His teaching, delivered in parables and in discourses such as the Sermon on the Mount, emphasized love of God and neighbour, forgiveness, and care for the poor. His ministry also brought conflict with religious authorities over Sabbath observance, purity laws and his association with outcasts.

Passion

During Passover in Jerusalem, Jesus shared a final meal with his disciples, was betrayed by Judas Iscariot, arrested in the Garden of Gethsemane, tried before the Sanhedrin and Pontius Pilate, and crucified at Golgotha. He was buried in a tomb provided by Joseph of Arimathea.

Resurrection and ascension

The gospels report that women followers found the tomb empty on the first day of the week, and that Jesus appeared to his disciples over the following days. Luke and Acts describe his ascension into heaven forty days after the resurrection, followed by the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost.

Chronology

Most scholars place Jesus' birth between 6 and 4 BC, the start of his ministry around AD 27–29, and his crucifixion around AD 30 or 33, during the governorship of Pontius Pilate under Emperor Tiberius.
