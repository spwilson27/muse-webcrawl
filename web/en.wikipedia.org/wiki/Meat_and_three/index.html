Meat and three is a popular restaurant format and meal in the cuisine of the Southern United States, particularly associated with Nashville, Tennessee. The customer picks one meat from a daily selection of three to six choices (such as fried chicken, country ham, roast beef, meatloaf or catfish) and three side dishes from a rotating list of a dozen or more vegetables and starches, traditionally served with cornbread or a roll and iced tea.

Sides

The side-dish list reflects Southern home cooking: macaroni and cheese, collard or turnip greens, black-eyed peas, fried okra, green beans, mashed potatoes, pinto beans, squash casserole, creamed corn and fried green tomatoes. In the classic joke of the genre, macaroni and cheese counts as a vegetable. Desserts such as banana pudding and chess pie are usually offered à la carte.

History

The format grew out of early-20th-century Southern cafeterias and boarding-house "blue plate" lunches and became a Nashville institution; the city is often said to be its capital. Well-known meat-and-three restaurants include Arnold's Country Kitchen (a James Beard Foundation America's Classic), the Loveless Cafe, Swett's, and Monell's. Although some classic venues have closed, the format remains a staple of Southern lunch counters and has spread nationwide.
