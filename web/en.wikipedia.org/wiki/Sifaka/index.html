Sifaka

Sifakas are lemurs of the genus Propithecus in the family Indriidae, found only on the island of Madagascar. They are medium-sized primates with long, silky fur, long tails, and powerful hind legs adapted for their distinctive vertical clinging and leaping locomotion through the trees.

Description and behaviour

Sifakas measure roughly 40–55 cm in body length with tails of similar length and weigh about 3–6 kg. On the ground they move by an unmistakable bipedal sideways hopping, arms held aloft for balance. They are diurnal and social, living in groups of up to about thirteen individuals, and feed mainly on leaves, supplemented by fruit, flowers, and bark. Their name imitates their distinctive alarm call, "si-fak".

Species and conservation

Nine species are generally recognised, including Verreaux's sifaka, the diademed sifaka, the silky sifaka, and Coquerel's sifaka. Nearly all are threatened — several critically endangered — because of habitat loss from slash-and-burn agriculture and logging, as well as hunting, in Madagascar's forests.
