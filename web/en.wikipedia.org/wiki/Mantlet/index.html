Mantlet

A mantlet was a large shield or portable shelter used for stopping projectiles in medieval warfare. It could be a portable, freestanding wooden screen — sometimes mounted on wheels — behind which archers, crossbowmen, sappers or siege engineers could shelter while approaching or undermining a fortification.

Use

Mantlets were typically constructed of wood planks, wicker or hides, sometimes covered with earth or raw hides to resist fire. In sieges they protected men filling moats, mining walls or operating siege engines. Fixed mantlets also formed protective screens on battlements, and similar devices were used in trench warfare in later centuries, including sniper shields. Closely related devices include the pavise, a large body shield used by crossbowmen, and the gabion.

Terminology

The term derives from the French mantelet. In modern armoured vehicle terminology, "gun mantlet" refers to the armoured plate surrounding the point where a tank's main gun passes through the turret front — a distinct usage, though sharing the idea of a protective covering.
