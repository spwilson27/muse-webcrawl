Peshawar

Country: Pakistan

Province: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Population (2017): approx. 1.97 million (city)

Languages: Pashto, Hindko, Urdu

Peshawar (Pashto: پېښور; Hindko: پشور) is the capital and largest city of the Pakistani province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Situated near the eastern end of the Khyber Pass, close to the border with Afghanistan, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in South Asia and the cultural and economic hub of Pakistan's Pashtun-majority northwest.

History

Known in antiquity as Purushapura, the city was a capital of the Kushan emperor Kanishka in the 2nd century CE and a great centre of Mahayana Buddhism; the Kanishka stupa, among the tallest buildings of the ancient world, stood here. Peshawar lay on the Silk Road and passed through the hands of the Gandharan, Hindu Shahi, Ghaznavid, Mughal, Durrani and Sikh empires before being annexed by the British in 1849 after the Second Anglo-Sikh War.

Renowned as the "City of Flowers", Peshawar's old walled city contains landmarks such as the Bala Hissar Fort, Mahabat Khan Mosque (1630), the Qissa Khwani Bazaar ("Storytellers' Bazaar") and Chowk Yadgar. The city figured prominently in the Khudai Khidmatgar non-violent movement led by Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan; the Qissa Khwani massacre of 1930 occurred here.

Modern era

During the Soviet–Afghan War of the 1980s Peshawar became a centre for Afghan refugees and mujahideen logistics, and it has since absorbed large Afghan refugee populations. The city suffered numerous militant attacks during the 2000s–2010s, most infamously the 2014 Army Public School massacre, in which over 140 people, mostly schoolchildren, were killed. Peshawar is served by Bacha Khan International Airport and is the terminus of the historic Khyber railway. The Peshawar Zalmi cricket franchise represents the city in the Pakistan Super League.
