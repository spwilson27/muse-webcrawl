Gardner Francis Fox (1911–1986) was an American writer known best for his prolific work in comic books for DC Comics. A trained lawyer, Fox wrote an estimated 4,000 comics stories and more than 100 novels across science fiction, fantasy, western and other genres.

Career

Fox co-created or developed many foundational DC characters and concepts. In the Golden Age he wrote the original Flash (Jay Garrick), Hawkman, Doctor Fate, the Sandman, and Starman, and scripted the first team-up of superheroes, the Justice Society of America (1941). In the Silver Age he was central to DC's revival: he co-created the Barry Allen Flash and the modern Hawkman, wrote Adam Strange, and co-created the Justice League of America in The Brave and the Bold #28 (1960). His 1961 story "Flash of Two Worlds!" introduced the concept of the DC Multiverse. Fox also created the Barbara Gordon Batgirl with Carmine Infantino in 1967.

Fox fought for creators' rights late in his career and was dismissed by DC in 1968 after joining demands for health benefits. He continued writing novels, including the Kothar and Kyrik sword-and-sorcery series. He was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame in 1998.
