The London Eye, or Millennium Wheel, is a cantilevered observation wheel on the South Bank of the River Thames in London. It is Europe's tallest cantilevered observation wheel and is the most popular paid tourist attraction in the United Kingdom, drawing over three million visitors a year. Opened to celebrate the new millennium, it was the world's tallest Ferris wheel from 1999 until 2006 and remains the defining landmark of the South Bank skyline.

Design and construction

The wheel was designed by husband-and-wife architects David Marks and Julia Barfield of Marks Barfield Architects, who won a 1993 competition for a millennium landmark. Standing 135 metres (443 ft) tall with a diameter of 120 metres, it is supported by an A-frame on one side only—unlike most giant wheels—so it is described by its makers as a cantilevered wheel. Its rim, built from sections floated up the Thames on barges and assembled flat on a platform in the river before being raised, carries 32 sealed, air-conditioned ovoid capsules, one for each London borough, each holding up to 25 passengers. A full rotation takes about 30 minutes; the wheel moves slowly enough (about 26 cm per second) that passengers board without it stopping. On a clear day the view extends some 40 kilometres, taking in the Houses of Parliament across the river, St Paul's Cathedral, and Windsor Castle.

History

The Eye was formally opened by Prime Minister Tony Blair on 31 December 1999 and opened to the public in March 2000. Originally intended as a temporary structure with a five-year lease, it proved so popular that it was made permanent. It has been sponsored at various times by British Airways, EDF Energy, Coca-Cola, and lastminute.com, whose name it has carried since 2020. The wheel is lit in different colours for occasions such as royal events, Pride, and New Year's Eve, when it forms the centrepiece of London's fireworks display.
