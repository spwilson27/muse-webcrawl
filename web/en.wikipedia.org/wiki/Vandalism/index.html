Vandalism

Vandalism is the action of deliberately damaging or defacing public or private property. The term derives from the Vandals, a Germanic tribe that sacked Rome in 455 AD, and was applied to destructive acts from the French Revolution onward, when the Abbé Grégoire coined vandalisme in 1794 to describe the destruction of artworks and monuments.

Forms and motives

Vandalism ranges from graffiti and broken windows to the destruction of cultural heritage. Criminologists distinguish types by motive, including acquisitive vandalism (damage in the course of theft), tactical vandalism (to achieve another goal), ideological vandalism, vindictive vandalism, play vandalism by juveniles, and malicious vandalism driven by frustration or rage. The broken windows theory proposed that visible disorder encourages further crime and neglect.

Law

Most jurisdictions criminalise vandalism under offences such as criminal damage or malicious mischief, with penalties scaled to the value of the damage. The boundary between vandalism and art is contested: unauthorised street art may be prosecuted as vandalism in one context and celebrated in another. In online contexts, "vandalism" also denotes deliberate destructive editing of collaborative works such as wikis.
