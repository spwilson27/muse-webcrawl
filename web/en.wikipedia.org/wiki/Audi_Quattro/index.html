The Audi Quattro is a road and rally car, produced by the German automobile manufacturer Audi, part of the Volkswagen Group. It was first shown at the 1980 Geneva Motor Show. The word quattro is derived from the Italian word for "four" and refers to the car's four-wheel drive system.

An Audi Quattro in rally trim.

History

The Quattro was the first production car to combine a turbocharged engine with permanent four-wheel drive in a passenger car at scale. Production ran from 1980 to 1991, with 11,452 examples built. Later variants included the shorter-wheelbase Sport Quattro of 1984, built for Group B rally homologation.

Rallying

The Quattro revolutionised rallying. Introduced to the World Rally Championship in 1981, it quickly dominated: Audi won the manufacturers' championship in 1982 and 1984, and drivers' titles with Hannu Mikkola (1983) and Stig Blomqvist (1984). Michèle Mouton became the first woman to win a WRC event in a Quattro at the 1981 San Remo Rally. The extreme Group B-era Sport Quattro S1 E2, with its large wings and roughly 500 hp, became one of the most iconic rally cars of all time; Walter Röhrl also won the 1987 Pikes Peak hill climb in a Quattro. The name has since been applied to Audi's all-wheel drive system across its range (styled quattro).
