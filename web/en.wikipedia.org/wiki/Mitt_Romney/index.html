Willard Mitt Romney (born 12 March 1947) is an American politician, businessman, and lawyer who served as a United States senator from Utah from 2019 to 2025. A member of the Republican Party, he previously served as the 70th governor of Massachusetts from 2003 to 2007 and was the Republican Party's nominee for president of the United States in the 2012 election, losing to incumbent Barack Obama. He is the son of George W. Romney, a former governor of Michigan.

Early life and business career

Romney was born in Detroit and raised in Bloomfield Hills, Michigan. He attended the Cranbrook School and, as a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, served a 30-month mission in France. He graduated from Brigham Young University in 1971 and earned a joint Juris Doctor and Master of Business Administration from Harvard in 1975, graduating as a Baker Scholar.

Romney joined the management consultancy Bain & Company and in 1984 co-founded the spin-off private equity firm Bain Capital, which he led for many years and which made him wealthy. In 1994 he ran unsuccessfully for the U.S. Senate in Massachusetts against incumbent Ted Kennedy. In 1999 he took over as president and CEO of the Salt Lake Organizing Committee for the 2002 Winter Olympics, which were mired in a bribery scandal and budget shortfalls; he is widely credited with turning the Games around, and they ended with a surplus.

Governor of Massachusetts

Romney was elected governor of Massachusetts in 2002 and served one term, declining to seek re-election in 2006. Working with a heavily Democratic legislature, he eliminated a projected budget deficit and in 2006 signed into law the Massachusetts health care reform law ("Romneycare"), which required nearly all residents to obtain health insurance and later served as a partial model for the federal Affordable Care Act.

Presidential campaigns

Romney first sought the Republican presidential nomination in 2008, losing to John McCain. In 2012 he won the Republican nomination and selected Congressman Paul Ryan as his running mate. He lost the general election to President Barack Obama, winning 47.2% of the popular vote and 206 electoral votes to Obama's 332.

U.S. Senate

After moving to Utah, Romney was elected to the U.S. Senate in 2018, succeeding Orrin Hatch. In February 2020 he became the first senator in U.S. history to vote to convict a president of his own party in an impeachment trial, voting guilty on the abuse-of-power article against President Donald Trump; he voted to convict again in Trump's second trial in 2021. In September 2023 Romney announced he would not seek re-election, citing his age and a desire for a new generation of leaders. He retired from the Senate in January 2025 and was succeeded by John Curtis.

Personal life

Romney married Ann Davies in 1969; they have five sons. Ann Romney was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis in 1998. Mitt Romney has been active in the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, serving as a bishop and stake president in the Boston area.
