Formula Three, abbreviated F3, is a third-tier class of open-wheel single-seater formula racing. The various championships held in Europe, Australia, South America and Asia form an important part of the FIA's career ladder for young drivers aiming to reach Formula One, sitting above Formula 4 and below Formula 2.

History

Formula Three originated in the post-war 500 cc motorcycle-engined racing cars of the late 1940s. The formula was revived in the 1960s and grew into the premier junior category of the 1970s and 1980s, with the British championship in particular launching the careers of champions such as Ayrton Senna, Mika Häkkinen, David Coulthard and Jenson Button. The end-of-season Macau Grand Prix became F3's unofficial world cup.

Cars

Modern Formula Three cars are lightweight monocoque single-seaters with slick tyres and significant aerodynamic downforce, powered by production-based four-cylinder engines of about 2 litres. Chassis have been dominated for decades by the Italian manufacturer Dallara. In 2019 the FIA merged the European and GP3 series into the international FIA Formula 3 Championship, which runs on the Formula One support bill.
