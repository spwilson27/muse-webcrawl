Vitamin D receptor

Gene: VDR (human)
Type: Nuclear receptor, NR1I1
Ligand: Calcitriol (1,25-(OH)2D3)
Chromosome: 12q13.11

The vitamin D receptor (VDR), also known as the calcitriol receptor and by its nuclear receptor designation NR1I1, is a member of the nuclear receptor superfamily of transcription factors. Upon activation by its ligand calcitriol — the active hormonal form of vitamin D — the VDR forms a heterodimer with the retinoid X receptor and binds to hormone response elements in DNA, regulating the expression of target genes involved in mineral metabolism, immunity and cell growth.

Function

The VDR mediates most of the biological actions of vitamin D. In the intestine it induces proteins such as calbindin and the epithelial calcium channel TRPV6 that enable dietary calcium absorption. In bone, kidney and parathyroid tissue it helps regulate calcium and phosphate homeostasis and suppresses parathyroid hormone synthesis. The receptor is expressed in many additional tissues, including immune cells, skin, brain and many cancers, where vitamin D signalling modulates proliferation and differentiation.

Mechanism of action

Calcitriol binding induces a conformational change in the VDR's ligand-binding domain, promoting heterodimerisation with RXR and recruitment of coactivator complexes. The complex binds vitamin D response elements (VDREs) in promoter regions, typically direct repeats of hexameric motifs separated by three nucleotides. Corepressor and coactivator exchange controls transcriptional activation or repression of genes such as osteocalcin, CYP24A1 and the calcium transport machinery.

Clinical significance

Inactivating mutations in the VDR gene cause hereditary vitamin D-resistant rickets (HVDRR), characterised by early-onset rickets, hypocalcaemia and, in many cases, alopecia. Common VDR polymorphisms — including FokI, BsmI, ApaI and TaqI variants — have been studied extensively for associations with bone mineral density, fracture risk, cancer and autoimmune disease, with mixed results. Synthetic VDR agonists such as calcipotriol are used clinically, for example in the treatment of psoriasis, with reduced calcaemic side effects.
