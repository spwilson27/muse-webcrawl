Nagar kirtan

Nagar kirtan (Punjabi: ਨਗਰ ਕੀਰਤਨ, "town hymn-singing") is a Sikh tradition in which a congregation processes through the streets of a town or city singing sacred hymns (shabad kirtan) from the Guru Granth Sahib, the Sikh scripture. The custom takes the singing of the divine word out of the gurdwara and into the public space of the whole community.

Form of the procession

The Guru Granth Sahib is carried in honour on a decorated float or vehicle, attended by the Panj Pyare (the Five Beloved Ones) in saffron and blue dress, often bearing swords. Ahead of the scripture, devotees may sweep the road as an act of humility. Ragi musicians lead the singing of hymns accompanied by harmonium and tabla, and the congregation follows, chanting responses such as "Satnam Waheguru". Martial displays of gatka, banners, and the distribution of free food and drink (seva) commonly accompany the procession, and onlookers of all faiths are welcomed and fed.

Occasions

Nagar kirtans are held above all for Vaisakhi, the spring festival commemorating Guru Gobind Singh's founding of the Khalsa in 1699, and for gurpurabs — anniversaries connected with the Sikh Gurus, especially the birth of Guru Nanak. Large processions take place in Punjab and in diaspora cities: the Khalsa Day parades of Toronto and Vancouver, the Vaisakhi processions of Southall and Birmingham in London and Birmingham, and nagar kirtans in California are among the largest Sikh gatherings outside India.

Significance

The practice expresses core Sikh values: devotion expressed through music, equality (the whole town, not just initiates, is included), and selfless service. By moving through shared streets, the procession also publicly marks the Sikh presence in a locality, and in the diaspora it functions as both worship and a festival of community identity.
