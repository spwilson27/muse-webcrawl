Artemis Accords

Signing of the Artemis Accords in October 2020

The Artemis Accords are a set of non-binding international agreements, first signed on 13 October 2020, establishing principles for cooperation in the civil exploration and use of the Moon, Mars, comets, and asteroids. They are associated with NASA's Artemis programme and build on the 1967 Outer Space Treaty.

Principles

The Accords affirm peaceful purposes, transparency, interoperability, emergency assistance, registration of space objects, release of scientific data, preservation of outer space heritage, deconfliction of activities through "safety zones", and mitigation of orbital debris. They also endorse the extraction and utilisation of space resources as consistent with the Outer Space Treaty.

Signatories

The eight founding signatories were the United States, Australia, Canada, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, the United Arab Emirates, and the United Kingdom; dozens of additional countries, including France, India, and South Korea, joined in subsequent years. China and Russia are not signatories and have pursued a separate International Lunar Research Station partnership.
