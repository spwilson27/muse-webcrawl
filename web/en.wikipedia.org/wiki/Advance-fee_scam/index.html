Advance-fee scam

TypeConfidence trick, fraud
MethodPromise of large sum in exchange for an upfront fee
Famous variant"Nigerian prince" / 419 scam

An advance-fee scam is a form of fraud and a common confidence trick: the victim is persuaded to advance a relatively small sum of money in the hope of obtaining a much larger sum. The scammer invents a pretext — an inheritance, a frozen bank account, a lottery win, or funds that must be smuggled out of a country — and asks the victim to pay taxes, legal fees, bribes, or processing charges first. Once the fee is paid, the promised fortune never arrives, and further pretexts may extract more payments.

History

The scheme predates the internet: an early form was the "Spanish Prisoner" letter of the late nineteenth century, in which a correspondent sought money to bribe officials to free a wealthy prisoner who would supposedly share his fortune. By fax, letter, and later email, versions associated with Nigeria became so prominent that they are called "419 scams," after the section of the Nigerian Criminal Code dealing with fraud. The stereotypical letter purports to come from a deposed prince or official needing help moving millions of dollars abroad.

Variants and prevention

Modern variants include fake lottery and sweepstakes notices, bogus inheritance claims, romance and investment frauds, and fraudulent loan or job offers requiring "processing fees." Law enforcement agencies advise that any unsolicited request for money to unlock a larger payment is a hallmark of the scam, and that funds sent by wire transfer are rarely recoverable. Victims are often targeted repeatedly once they appear on "sucker lists."
