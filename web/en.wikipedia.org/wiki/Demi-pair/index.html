Demi-pair

A demi-pair is a person, typically an overseas student, who lives with a host family and provides part-time childcare and light housework — commonly around 15 to 20 hours per week — in exchange for accommodation and meals. The arrangement is a reduced-hours version of the au pair programme.

Arrangement

Demi-pair placements are especially popular in Australia and New Zealand, where they are combined with enrolment in a language school. The demi pair studies part-time during the day and helps the host family mainly in the mornings and evenings: preparing children for school, supervising homework, babysitting and light household tasks. Pocket money may or may not be paid, depending on the agency and country.

Comparison with au pair

While au pairs typically work 25–45 hours per week and receive a stipend, demi pairs work fewer hours and are usually students first and helpers second. Demi pairs generally must meet the host country's student visa and working-holiday visa requirements, including limits on paid work.

Regulation

Because demi pairing is informal in many countries, agencies typically screen host families and participants and set written expectations about hours, duties and free time to avoid the arrangement becoming disguised full-time domestic work.
