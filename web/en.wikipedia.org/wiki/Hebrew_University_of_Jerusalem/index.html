Hebrew University of Jerusalem

The Hebrew University of Jerusalem (Hebrew: Ha-Universita ha-Ivrit bi-Yerushalayim) is Israel's oldest university and a leading public research institution. Founded during the British Mandate, it opened on Mount Scopus on 1 April 1925 with an inauguration attended by Lord Balfour and addressed by its leading advocates, Chaim Weizmann and Albert Einstein — who served on its first board of governors and bequeathed his papers to it.

History

The idea of a Jewish university in Jerusalem dated to the early Zionist congresses; cornerstones were laid in July 1918. Early institutes covered Jewish studies, chemistry, microbiology, and mathematics–physics. After the 1948 war, the Mount Scopus campus became an Israeli enclave inside Jordanian-held territory, accessible only by fortnightly convoy, and the university rebuilt itself at Givat Ram in west Jerusalem. Following the 1967 war, Mount Scopus was rebuilt and reoccupied as the principal campus for the humanities and social sciences.

Campuses and institutions

Mount Scopus hosts humanities, social sciences, law, and education; Givat Ram (the Edmond J. Safra campus) hosts the sciences; Ein Kerem hosts medicine and dentistry with the Hadassah Medical Center; and Rehovot hosts agriculture, food, and environment. The Jewish National and University Library, on Givat Ram, is Israel's national library and holds the Albert Einstein Archives and the world's largest collection of Hebraica and Judaica manuscripts.

People

Faculty and alumni have included numerous Nobel laureates — including Daniel Kahneman, Robert Aumann, Aaron Ciechanover, Avram Hershko, David Gross, and Ada Yonath — as well as Israeli presidents, prime ministers, and Supreme Court justices. The university consistently ranks among the world's top 100–200 universities and first in Israel in most rankings.
