InSight (Interior Exploration using Seismic Investigations, Geodesy and Heat Transport) was a NASA robotic lander mission designed to study the deep interior of Mars. It was the first mission dedicated to looking beneath the Martian surface, measuring the planet's "vital signs": its pulse (seismology), temperature (heat flow) and reflexes (precision tracking of its wobble).

Mission

InSight launched on 5 May 2018 from Vandenberg Air Force Base — the first interplanetary mission from the US West Coast — and landed on 26 November 2018 in the flat plain of Elysium Planitia, near the Martian equator, chosen so its solar panels would receive ample sunlight. The lander used a robotic arm to place two instruments directly on the surface: the SEIS seismometer, built by the French space agency CNES, sheltered under a wind and thermal shield, and the German-built HP3 heat-flow probe, a self-hammering "mole" intended to burrow 5 metres underground.

Results

SEIS detected more than 1,300 marsquakes, including quakes caused by meteoroid impacts, allowing scientists to determine that Mars's crust is thinner and more layered than expected, its liquid core is larger and less dense than predicted, and the planet lacks a distinct lower mantle boundary of the terrestrial kind. The HP3 mole, however, could not gain friction in the unexpectedly clumpy soil and never reached its target depth; its mission was ended in January 2021. Dust accumulating on the solar arrays steadily reduced power, and NASA declared the mission over on 21 December 2022 after two consecutive missed communication sessions.
