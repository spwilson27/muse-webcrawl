Materials science

[Image: The materials science tetrahedron: processing, structure, properties, performance]

Overview

Materials science is an interdisciplinary field of researching and designing materials, drawing on physics, chemistry and engineering. It studies how a material's structure — from atomic bonding to microstructure — determines its properties, and how synthesis and processing can tailor them. The classic framing is the "materials tetrahedron": processing, structure, properties and performance.

Scope and history

The field encompasses metals, ceramics, polymers, semiconductors, composites, biomaterials and nanomaterials. Human ages are named for materials (Stone, Bronze, Iron), and the modern discipline crystallised after the Second World War (the first materials science department was founded at Northwestern University in 1955 by merging metallurgy with solid-state physics). Landmark products of the field include semiconductors and the transistor, superalloys for jet engines, carbon-fibre composites, lithium-ion battery materials, shape-memory alloys and high-temperature superconductors; characterisation tools (electron microscopy, X-ray diffraction) and computational materials design (CALPHAD, materials informatics, the Materials Genome Initiative) drive its progress.
