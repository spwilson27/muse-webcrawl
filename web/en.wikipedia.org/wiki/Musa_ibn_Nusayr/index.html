Mūsā ibn Nuṣayr (c. 640–716) was an Arab general and governor who served under the Umayyad Caliphate. As governor of Ifriqiya (North Africa) he completed the Muslim conquest of the Maghreb and directed the conquest of Visigothic Hispania beginning in 711, one of the most consequential campaigns of the early Islamic expansion.

Career in North Africa

Musa was appointed governor of Ifriqiya around 698–703 by the caliph Abd al-Malik. He subdued the remaining Berber resistance, secured the North African coast, and organised naval raids into the western Mediterranean, including expeditions against Sardinia, the Balearic Islands, and Sicily. He is credited with converting many Berber tribes to Islam and recruiting them into his armies.

Conquest of Hispania

In 711 Musa dispatched his Berber client and general Tariq ibn Ziyad across the strait to Hispania, where Tariq defeated King Roderic at the Battle of Guadalete. Musa himself crossed in 712 with a larger army, capturing Seville, Mérida, and other strongholds, and meeting Tariq at Toledo. Within a few years most of the Iberian Peninsula was under Muslim rule as the province of al-Andalus.

Recall and death

In 714 Musa was recalled to Damascus by Caliph al-Walid I. He famously arrived with a great procession of captives and treasure, but after al-Walid's death he fell from favour under Sulayman, was heavily fined, and died in 716, reportedly in poverty and obscurity at Wadi al-Qura in the Hejaz.
