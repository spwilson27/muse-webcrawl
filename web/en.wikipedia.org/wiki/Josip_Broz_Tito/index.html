Josip Broz Tito

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Marshal Tito in his white uniform reviewing troops in Belgrade]
Marshal Tito in his white uniform reviewing troops in Belgrade

Josip Broz, known as Tito (7 May 1892 – 4 May 1980), was a Yugoslav communist revolutionary and statesman who led the Yugoslav Partisans to victory in the Second World War and ruled Yugoslavia from 1944 until his death — as prime minister, then President (for life from 1974) of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia. He defied Stalin in the 1948 split, built a distinct 'self-management' socialism, and co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement.

Partisan leader

Born to a Croat father and Slovene mother in Kumrovec, Tito was a metalworker, Austro-Hungarian soldier, Russian prisoner and Bolshevik recruit before becoming a Comintern agent and, in 1937, head of the outlawed Communist Party of Yugoslavia. After the Axis invasion of 1941 he built the Partisans into Europe's most effective resistance — a multinational liberation army that largely freed its own country — winning Allied recognition over the royalist Chetniks by 1943.

Tito's Yugoslavia

His federation of six republics suppressed ethnic nationalism under 'Brotherhood and Unity' while repressing dissent (the Goli otok camp held Cominformists after 1948). After the split with Stalin, Yugoslavia charted a third course: workers' self-management, market elements, open borders unusual in the socialist world, and non-alignment abroad alongside Nehru, Nasser and Sukarno (Brioni, 1956; Belgrade conference, 1961). Living standards and personal freedoms exceeded the Soviet bloc's, on Western credits that later turned to debt.

Death and aftermath

Tito died in Ljubljana on 4 May 1980; his funeral drew representatives of 128 states, among the largest state funerals in history. The collective presidency designed to succeed him could not hold the federation's centrifugal forces; within a decade of his death Yugoslavia dissolved into the Yugoslav Wars. Assessments of Tito remain polarised between nostalgia ('Yugonostalgia') and condemnation of his authoritarianism.
