The 9K38 Igla (Russian: Игла́, "needle"; NATO reporting name SA-18 Grouse) is a Soviet/Russian man-portable infrared-guided surface-to-air missile (MANPADS) system. It entered service in 1981 as the successor to the Strela series (9K32 Strela-2 and 9K34 Strela-3), offering a more capable seeker, better resistance to countermeasures and a slightly larger warhead.

Design

The system consists of the 9M39 missile in a disposable launch tube, a reusable gripstock and an IFF interrogator. Its two-colour infrared seeker helps distinguish a target aircraft's exhaust from decoy flares. The missile can engage targets out to roughly 5 km and at altitudes up to about 3.5 km. A refined variant, the Igla-S (SA-24 Grinch), with a larger warhead and proximity fuze, appeared in the 2000s.

Service

The Igla has been exported widely and used in numerous conflicts from the Gulf War onward, credited with several coalition aircraft losses in 1991. Because MANPADS can threaten civilian aircraft, their proliferation has been a persistent arms-control concern, prompting international export-control arrangements.
