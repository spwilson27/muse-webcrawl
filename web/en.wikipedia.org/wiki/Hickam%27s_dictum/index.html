Hickam's dictum

Hickam's dictum is a counter-aphorism to Occam's razor in medicine, commonly stated as "Patients can have as many diseases as they damn well please" (variants use "he" or "they"). It cautions diagnosticians against assuming that all of a patient's findings must be explained by a single unifying diagnosis.

Origin

The saying is attributed to John Hickam, an American physician at Indiana University and Grady Memorial Hospital in Atlanta, who reportedly offered it in response to colleagues' insistence on parsimony in diagnosis, as taught by William Osler's reading of Occam's razor. In practice, especially in older or multimorbid patients, multiple concurrent diseases are common, and Hickam's dictum reminds clinicians to keep considering additional diagnoses even after one has been found.
