The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, commonly called the Toller, is a medium-sized gundog breed developed in Nova Scotia, Canada, in the 19th century. It is the smallest of the retrievers and is unique among gundogs for "tolling" — luring waterfowl within gunshot range before retrieving them.

Tolling

"Tolling" (from Middle English tollen, to entice) imitates the behaviour of a fox: the dog plays and frolics along the shoreline with a stick or ball thrown by the hidden hunter, its waving white-tipped tail and red coat attracting curious ducks and geese, which swim closer to investigate. When the birds are within range the hunter rises, shoots, and the dog retrieves the shot birds from land or water. Tolling dogs of this type were worked in the Little River and Yarmouth districts of southwestern Nova Scotia, and the breed was refined from crosses of retrievers, spaniels, setters and possibly farm collies.

Appearance

Tollers are compact, powerful and slightly longer than tall, with a wedge-shaped head, triangular drop ears, and amber-to-brown eyes whose expression is alert and friendly. The double coat is water-repellent with a soft dense undercoat; colour ranges from red to orange, usually with white markings on the feet, chest, blaze and tail tip. The tail is heavily feathered and carried in a wide wag when the dog is working — essential to the tolling action.

Temperament and health

Intelligent, energetic and somewhat more reserved with strangers than other retrievers, Tollers are keen workers in hunting, obedience, agility and dock diving, and are known for a high-pitched "scream" of excitement when anticipating work. Genetic conditions in the breed include progressive retinal atrophy, collie eye anomaly, autoimmune disorders (notably steroid-responsive meningitis-arteritis and Addison's disease) and a juvenile form of Addison's; responsible breeders screen breeding stock. The Canadian Kennel Club recognised the breed in 1945; it became Nova Scotia's provincial dog.
