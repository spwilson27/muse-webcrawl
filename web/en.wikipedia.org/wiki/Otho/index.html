Marcus Otho Caesar Augustus (born Marcus Salvius Otho; 28 April 32 – 16 April 69) was Roman emperor, reigning for three months from 15 January to 16 April 69. He was the second emperor of the Year of the Four Emperors. A former companion of Nero, Otho had been exiled as governor of Lusitania after Nero took Otho's wife Poppaea Sabina. He joined Galba's revolt against Nero in 68, expecting to be named heir; when Galba adopted Piso Licinianus instead, Otho bribed the Praetorian Guard to murder Galba and Piso and proclaim him emperor.

Reign and downfall

Despite a reputation for youthful extravagance, Otho governed energetically and moderately, confirming privileges and attempting to win over Nero's former supporters. Almost immediately, the legions of Germania Inferior proclaimed their commander Vitellius emperor and marched on Italy. Otho's forces won an initial skirmish near Cremona but were defeated at the First Battle of Bedriacum on 14 April 69. Although he still had loyal troops and reinforcements coming from Dalmatia, Otho chose suicide — reportedly declaring that it was far more just to perish one for all than many for one — and stabbed himself on 16 April. His death was widely admired as a noble end that spared Rome further civil bloodshed; Tacitus and Suetonius both treat his final act with respect.
