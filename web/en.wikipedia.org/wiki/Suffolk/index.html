Suffolk is a ceremonial county in the East of England, on the North Sea coast. It borders Norfolk to the north, Cambridgeshire to the west, and Essex to the south. The county town is Ipswich; other notable settlements include Lowestoft, Bury St Edmunds, Newmarket, Felixstowe, and Sudbury. The name Suffolk derives from the Old English Suðfolc, "southern folk", distinguishing the southern Angles of East Anglia from the "northern folk" of Norfolk.

Geography

Suffolk is predominantly low-lying, built on chalk and crag deposits, with a coastline of estuaries, shingle spits, and eroding soft cliffs along the North Sea. The principal rivers are the Stour (which forms much of the Essex border and was immortalised in the paintings of John Constable in "Constable Country" around Dedham Vale), the Orwell, the Deben, the Alde, and the Waveney on the Norfolk border. The Broads national park extends into northern Suffolk around Oulton Broad and the Waveney valley. The county's eastern edge includes the long shingle spit of Orford Ness, a nature reserve and former military testing site.

History

Suffolk formed part of the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of East Anglia; the great ship burial at Sutton Hoo, near Woodbridge, uncovered in 1939, is believed to be the grave of the early 7th-century king Rædwald. In the medieval period the county grew rich on the wool and cloth trade, and wealthy merchants built the great "wool churches" and the half-timbered guildhalls of towns such as Lavenham and Long Melford. Bury St Edmunds was one of England's most important abbeys, and the shrine of St Edmund, martyred king of East Anglia. During the Second World War Suffolk hosted numerous American airfields — the county was dotted with USAAF bomber stations, whose legacy remains in museums and memorials across the county.

Economy

Agriculture remains significant, with arable farming, pig and poultry production, and the famous Suffolk Punch heavy horse bred in the county. Felixstowe is the United Kingdom's largest container port. Ipswich has insurance and service industries, British Telecom's research centre at Adastral Park lies near Martlesham, and Sizewell on the coast hosts nuclear power stations. Newmarket is the historic home of British horse racing, housing thousands of racehorses and two racecourses. Tourism centres on the coast — Southwold, Aldeburgh (home of the Aldeburgh Festival founded by Benjamin Britten) — and on Constable Country.
