Galveston, Texas


Galveston is a coastal city on Galveston Island, Texas, county seat of Galveston County.

Galveston (locally /ˈɡælvɪstən/) is a coastal resort city and port on Galveston Island and Pelican Island in the U.S. state of Texas. It is the county seat of Galveston County and lies on the southeastern Texas coast of the Gulf of Mexico, about 45 miles southeast of Houston.

History

The island was named for Spanish military leader Bernardo de Gálvez. During the 19th century Galveston became Texas's chief port and one of the busiest in the Gulf, a major center for immigration and the cotton trade, and briefly a center of the Republic of Texas's navy. Its commercial primacy was shattered by the Galveston hurricane of 1900, the deadliest natural disaster in U.S. history, which killed an estimated 6,000–8,000 people and prompted construction of the Galveston Seawall and a remarkable grade-raising of the city. Trade shifted to Houston, reached by its dredged ship channel.

Economy and attractions

Today Galveston is a popular tourist destination known for its historic Strand district, Victorian architecture, pleasure pier, and the Moody Gardens attractions. The Port of Galveston is a major cruise ship terminal. The city is home to Texas A&M University at Galveston and the University of Texas Medical Branch. In 2008, Hurricane Ike again caused extensive flooding and damage. The population was about 53,000 at the 2020 census.
