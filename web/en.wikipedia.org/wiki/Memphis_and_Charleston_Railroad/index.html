Memphis and Charleston Railroad

The Memphis and Charleston Railroad, completed in 1857, was the first railroad in the United States to link the Atlantic Ocean with the Mississippi River. It ran from Memphis, Tennessee, to Stevenson, Alabama, where it connected with other lines to Chattanooga and onward to Charleston, South Carolina, a distance of about 311 miles (501 km).

History

The Memphis and Charleston Railroad was chartered in Tennessee in 1846, with additional charters in Alabama and Mississippi. Construction was completed on April 27, 1857, when the line reached Stevenson, Alabama, connecting with the Nashville and Chattanooga Railroad.

American Civil War

Because it formed the only direct east–west rail route through the Confederacy's heartland — the "great artery of the Confederacy" — the line was of immense strategic importance during the American Civil War. Control of the railroad, particularly at the junction at Corinth, Mississippi, was a major objective of Union operations in the Western Theater. The Battle of Shiloh (April 1862) was fought in part over control of this rail corridor, and Corinth fell to Union forces in May 1862. The line suffered extensive damage during the war but was rebuilt afterward.

Later years

After the Civil War the Memphis and Charleston was reorganized and in 1877 came under the control of the East Tennessee, Virginia and Georgia Railroad. It eventually became part of the Southern Railway system in 1894. The route remains in use today as part of Norfolk Southern Railway.
