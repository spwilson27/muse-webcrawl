Baroque architecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
St Peter's Square in Rome: Bernini's sweeping colonnades embracing the Egyptian obelisk, with the basilica dome behind

Baroque architecture is a style that emerged in late 16th-century Italy and spread across Europe and Latin America during the 17th and 18th centuries. It used grandeur, drama, movement and tension — sweeping curves, rich ornament, bold contrasts of light and shadow and illusionistic ceiling painting — to evoke emotional states, serving first the Counter-Reformation Catholic Church and then absolutist monarchies.

Major Baroque architects include Gian Lorenzo Bernini and Francesco Borromini in Rome (St Peter's Square, San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane), and the style culminated in palaces such as Versailles under Louis XIV. Regional flowerings include the exuberant Spanish and Latin American Churrigueresque and the English Baroque of Christopher Wren and Nicholas Hawksmoor.
