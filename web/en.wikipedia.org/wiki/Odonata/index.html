OrderOdonata (Fabricius, 1793)

Odonata is an order of predatory flying insects that includes dragonflies (suborder Anisoptera) and damselflies (suborder Zygoptera). Around 6,400 species are known. Odonates are characterised by large compound eyes, two pairs of strong membranous wings, and elongated abdomens; their aquatic nymphs breathe through gills and are fierce predators of other invertebrates and small vertebrates.

Evolution

The order is ancient: giant griffinflies of the related order Meganisoptera, with wingspans up to about 70 cm, flew in the Carboniferous forests some 300 million years ago. True odonates appeared by the Late Permian and diversified through the Mesozoic.

Biology

Adults are aerial hunters, catching flying insects on the wing with spiny legs held like a basket; their independently controllable wings give exceptional manoeuvrability, including hovering and backward flight. Males of many species defend territories around ponds and streams. Females lay eggs in or near water, and nymphs develop underwater for months to years before climbing out to moult into adults. Odonates are valued as indicators of freshwater ecosystem health and as controllers of mosquitoes.

Classification

The two principal living suborders are Anisoptera (dragonflies, stout-bodied with wings held flat at rest) and Zygoptera (damselflies, slender with wings usually folded together). A small third group, Anisozygoptera, survives in a few Asian relict species.
