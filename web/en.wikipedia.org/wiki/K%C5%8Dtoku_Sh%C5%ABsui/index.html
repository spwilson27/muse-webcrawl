Kōtoku Shūsui

Kōtoku Shūsui (1871–1911) was a Japanese socialist and anarchist journalist who played a leading role in introducing socialist ideas to Japan. He was executed for treason in the High Treason Incident of 1910.

Life

Born in Tosa Province, Kōtoku moved to Tokyo and became a journalist. Initially a member of Japan's early socialist movement, he co-founded the anti-war newspaper Heimin Shimbun during the Russo-Japanese War and later moved toward anarcho-syndicalism.

High Treason Incident

In 1910 Kōtoku was arrested amid allegations of a plot to assassinate the Emperor Meiji. He was tried in camera, sentenced to death, and hanged in January 1911 along with eleven others, in a case widely regarded by historians as a frame-up used to crush the left.
