Hungarian Reform Era

The Hungarian Reform Era (Hungarian: reformkor) was a period in the history of the Kingdom of Hungary, conventionally dated from 1825 to 1848, characterised by movements for political, economic and cultural modernisation within the Habsburg monarchy. It culminated in the Hungarian Revolution of 1848.

Beginnings

The era is usually dated from the Diet of 1825, when Count István Széchenyi offered a year's income of his estates to found the Hungarian Academy of Sciences. Széchenyi, "the Greatest Hungarian", advocated gradual modernisation: credit reform, infrastructure such as the Széchenyi Chain Bridge and Danube regulation, and economic development.

Language and culture

The reform movement promoted the Hungarian language in place of Latin in administration and education; the language reform (nyelvújítás) modernised Hungarian vocabulary, and in 1844 Hungarian became the official language of the kingdom.

Radicalisation

In the 1840s Lajos Kossuth emerged as leader of a more radical liberal opposition demanding abolition of serfdom, equality before the law, and representative government. The reform diets of 1832–36, 1839–40 and 1843–44 passed incremental measures.

Outcome

News of revolution in Paris and Vienna in March 1848 triggered the Hungarian revolution; the Diet enacted the April Laws, creating a responsible Hungarian government and abolishing feudal privileges, before the ensuing war of independence was crushed in 1849.
