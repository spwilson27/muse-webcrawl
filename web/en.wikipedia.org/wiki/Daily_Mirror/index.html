The Daily Mirror is a British national daily tabloid newspaper founded in 1903 and published by Reach plc. Long the voice of Labour-supporting working-class readers, it was Britain's best-selling paper in the mid-20th century under editors including Hugh Cudlipp, and remains known for campaigning journalism, sport and the Pride of Britain Awards.

Founding and rise

Alfred Harmsworth (Lord Northcliffe) launched the Mirror in 1903, initially aimed at women; after a faltering start it was relaunched as a picture paper and became a mass success.

The Cudlipp era

Under Hugh Cudlipp (editorial director from the 1950s), the Mirror backed Labour, campaigned on social issues and reached circulations above five million, pioneering a literate tabloid style — 'the intelligent tabloid' in later branding.

Maxwell and after

Robert Maxwell bought the Mirror Group in 1984; his death in 1991 exposed the looting of the company's pension funds. The group later became Trinity Mirror and then Reach plc (2018), publisher of the Express and Star titles too.

The modern Mirror

The Mirror campaigns on the cost of living and public services, founded the Pride of Britain Awards (1999), and publishes Mirror Online as one of the UK's largest news sites.
