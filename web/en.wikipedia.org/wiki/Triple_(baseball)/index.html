Triple (baseball)

In baseball, a triple is a hit in which the batter reaches third base safely on the same play, without the benefit of a fielding error and without another runner being put out on a fielder's choice. A triple is denoted by the abbreviation 3B in box scores and is one of the standard categories of extra-base hit, along with the double and the home run.

Characteristics

Triples are the rarest of the common hit types in modern baseball. They generally require a ball hit into a gap or down a line and past the outfielders, combined with above-average running speed; most triples are hit by fast runners to deep right-center or left-center field, or down the right-field line. Ballpark design strongly affects their frequency: spacious outfields and unusual features, such as the deep center field of certain parks, produce more triples.

Records

Sam Crawford holds the Major League Baseball career record with 309 triples, a mark set largely in the dead-ball era when triples were far more common. The single-season record is 36, by Chief Wilson in 1912. Hitting a single, double, triple, and home run in the same game constitutes hitting for the cycle, with the triple usually the hardest component to obtain.
