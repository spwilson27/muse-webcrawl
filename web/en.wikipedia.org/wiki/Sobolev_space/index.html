Sobolev space

In mathematics, a Sobolev space is a vector space of functions equipped with a norm that is a combination of Lp-norms of the function itself and its derivatives up to a given order. The derivatives are understood in a weak sense, so that the space is complete — a Banach space, and for p = 2 a Hilbert space.

Sobolev spaces are named after the Russian mathematician Sergei Sobolev, who introduced them in the 1930s. They are fundamental to the modern theory of partial differential equations, because weak solutions of PDEs are naturally sought in Sobolev spaces, and to the calculus of variations, where minimising sequences converge in these norms.

Definition

For a domain Ω ⊂ ℝn, the space Wk,p(Ω) consists of functions in Lp(Ω) all of whose weak derivatives up to order k are also in Lp(Ω). The Hilbert case Hk = Wk,2 is especially important. H10 denotes the closure of smooth compactly supported functions and encodes zero boundary conditions.

Embedding theorems

Sobolev embedding theorems state that when the order of differentiability exceeds n/p, functions in Wk,p are in fact continuous (or Hölder continuous), and the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem gives compact embeddings into lower-order spaces — key tools for proving existence of solutions.
