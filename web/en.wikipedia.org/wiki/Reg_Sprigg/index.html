Reginald Claude Sprigg AO (1 March 1919 – 2 December 1994) was an Australian geologist and conservationist. He is best known for discovering, in 1946, the Ediacara biota — the fossilised remains of some of the earliest known complex multicellular organisms — in the Ediacara Hills of the Flinders Ranges in South Australia.

Life and career

Sprigg studied geology at the University of Adelaide, where he was a student of Sir Douglas Mawson, and served with the Royal Australian Engineers during the Second World War. Working for the South Australian Geological Survey, he found the Ediacara fossils while examining old mine workings in 1946. His 1947 paper describing the fossils was initially dismissed — the fossils were thought to be Cambrian or even inorganic — but they were later recognised as a Precambrian (Ediacaran) assemblage of global significance; the jellyfish-like Ediacaria and other taxa were named for the site, and the genus Spriggina was named in his honour.

Later work

Sprigg went on to a career in petroleum and mineral exploration, founding companies including Santos's predecessor interests and Beach Petroleum (1961), and undertaking oceanographic and geological expeditions. A committed conservationist, he bought the Arkaroola pastoral lease in the northern Flinders Ranges and established it as a wildlife sanctuary and wilderness tourism venture. He was appointed an Officer of the Order of Australia in 1983.
