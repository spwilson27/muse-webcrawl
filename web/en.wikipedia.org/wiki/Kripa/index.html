Kripa

Kripa as preceptor of the Kuru princes (image placeholder)

Kripa (Sanskrit: कृप, Kṛpa), also known as Kripacharya, is a figure of the Hindu epic Mahabharata. A Brahmin warrior-sage, he was the royal preceptor (kulaguru) of the Kuru court at Hastinapura and the first teacher of archery to the Pandava and Kaurava princes before Drona. In the Kurukshetra War he fought on the Kaurava side, was one of the few survivors of the Kaurava army, and afterwards became the teacher of Parikshit, the grandson of Arjuna. Kripa is numbered among the seven Chiranjivis, the immortals said to live until the end of the present age (Kali Yuga).

Birth and upbringing

According to the Adi Parva, Kripa and his twin sister Kripi were the children of the sage Sharadvan, who, distracted in his austerities by the sight of the apsara Janapadi, let fall his seed on a clump of reeds (śara), from which the twins were born. King Shantanu's hunters found the infants — with a deerskin, a sacred thread and a bow beside them, signs of Brahmin-warrior parentage — and brought them to court. Shantanu raised them out of compassion (kṛpā), whence Kripa's name. Sharadvan later revealed their origin and instructed Kripa in the Vedas and the science of arms, in which he became so accomplished that he was appointed preceptor to the Kuru princes. His sister Kripi married Drona, and their son was Ashvatthama.

Teacher of the princes

Kripa taught the young princes of Hastinapura the fundamentals of warfare before Bhishma engaged Drona as their principal martial teacher; Kripa remained a senior counsellor of the court, sitting in Dhritarashtra's assembly. At moments of crisis he counselled moderation: he advised Duryodhana against the humiliation of Draupadi and urged peace negotiations before the war, though as a dependant of the Kuru throne he regarded himself as bound in loyalty to fight for Hastinapura.

The Kurukshetra War

Kripa fought as one of the eleven commanders of the Kaurava army. He duelled various Pandava warriors during the eighteen days of battle. On the final night, he accompanied Ashvatthama and Kritavarma to the Pandava camp, where he stood guard at the entrance while Ashvatthama slew the sleeping Panchala warriors and the five Upapandavas — an act whose morality the epic leaves troublingly shaded. Kripa was one of only three Kaurava warriors (with Ashvatthama and Kritavarma) to survive the war.

Aftermath and immortality

After the war Kripa was reconciled with Yudhishthira and appointed preceptor to Parikshit, Arjuna's grandson and heir to the throne. In later tradition Kripa is counted among the seven Chiranjivis — Ashvatthama, Bali, Vyasa, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Kripa and Parashurama — who remain alive on earth until the end of the Kali Yuga, remembered in the morning prayer verse listing their names as a remedy against disease and untimely death.
