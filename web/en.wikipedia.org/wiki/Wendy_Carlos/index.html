Wendy Carlos


Carlos's recordings were made on early Moog modular synthesizers.

Wendy Carlos (born November 14, 1939) is an American musician and composer best known for her electronic music and film scores. Her 1968 album Switched-On Bach, which presented works by Johann Sebastian Bach performed on a Moog synthesizer, was a commercial and critical breakthrough that popularised the synthesizer and won three Grammy Awards.

Career

Carlos studied music and physics at Brown University and composition at Columbia University, where she encountered electronic music pioneer Vladimir Ussachevsky. Working closely with Robert Moog, she provided feedback that influenced the development of his instruments. After the success of Switched-On Bach, she released further electronic albums including The Well-Tempered Synthesizer and Sonic Seasonings, an early example of ambient music.

Carlos composed the scores for Stanley Kubrick's A Clockwork Orange (1971) and The Shining (1980), and for the film Tron (1982), which combined synthesizer, orchestra, and chorus. In the 1980s she turned to digital synthesis and explored alternative tuning systems on albums such as Digital Moonscapes and Beauty in the Beast. She was also among the first notable public figures to speak openly about gender transition, discussing her experience in a 1979 interview.
