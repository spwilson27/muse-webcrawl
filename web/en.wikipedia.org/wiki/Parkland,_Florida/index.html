Parkland, Florida

Parkland is a city in northern Broward County, Florida, United States. Founded in 1963 and named for its park-like character, it long maintained a rural, equestrian atmosphere with zoning that restricted shops and traffic lights. Its population grew rapidly in recent decades, reaching roughly 35,000 by the 2020 census.

History

Parkland was incorporated on 10 July 1963. Through the late twentieth century it remained a small, semi-rural community; suburban development accelerated in the 1990s and 2000s as metropolitan South Florida expanded westward toward the Everglades.

Stoneman Douglas High School shooting

On 14 February 2018, a gunman opened fire at Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School in Parkland, killing seventeen students and staff and injuring seventeen others. The shooting prompted nationwide protests and the student-led March for Our Lives movement advocating gun control legislation.

Geography

The city lies at the northwestern edge of Broward County, bordering Palm Beach County and the Everglades Water Conservation Area to the west.
