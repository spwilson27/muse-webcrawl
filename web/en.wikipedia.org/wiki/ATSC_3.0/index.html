ATSC 3.0

ATSC 3.0 is a major version of the ATSC standards for terrestrial television broadcasting, marketed in the United States as NextGen TV. Unlike its predecessor, it is based on Internet Protocol transport and uses OFDM modulation with LDPC error correction, supporting 4K video with HEVC compression, high dynamic range, immersive audio (Dolby AC-4), interactive services, and targeted emergency alerting.

Deployment

South Korea adopted ATSC 3.0 and launched UHD broadcasts in 2017 ahead of the 2018 Winter Olympics. In the United States, the FCC authorized voluntary deployment in 2017; stations began launching services in 2020 sharing "lighthouse" transmitters while continuing ATSC 1.0 simulcasts. By the mid-2020s, NextGen TV signals reached the majority of US households, though receiver adoption and debate over a mandatory transition timeline — and over the system's use of DRM encryption — continued.

Technical features

The system is layered: a bootstrap signal enables discovery, the physical layer can be configured for robust mobile reception or high-capacity fixed reception, and services are delivered over ROUTE/DASH and MMTP protocols. It also supports broadcast–broadband hybrid services and datacasting applications.
