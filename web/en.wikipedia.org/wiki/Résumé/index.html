A résumé (also spelled resume) is a document created and used by a person to present their background, skills, and accomplishments, most often when applying for a job. A résumé typically summarises relevant work experience and education. The French word résumé means "summary". In much of Europe, South America and elsewhere, the equivalent document is called a curriculum vitae (CV), while in North America a CV usually denotes a longer, detailed document used mainly in academia, medicine and research.

History

The résumé is sometimes traced to Leonardo da Vinci, whose 1482 letter to Ludovico Sforza listed his skills and experience in seeking employment in Milan. Formal résumés became common in the 20th century as professional hiring standardised, and the format has been repeatedly reshaped by technology — typewriters, word processors, email, online job boards and professional networking sites such as LinkedIn, as well as applicant tracking systems (ATS) that scan and rank documents automatically.

Organisation and styles

The three most common formats are:

  • Reverse chronological — work history listed from most recent to earliest; the most common format.
  • Functional — emphasises skills and abilities rather than dated work history; sometimes viewed with suspicion by employers as it can conceal gaps.
  • Combination (hybrid) — leads with a skills summary followed by a chronological history.

Common sections include contact information, a summary or objective, work experience, education, skills, and optional sections for certifications, languages, publications and volunteer work. In North America résumés usually omit photographs, date of birth and marital status, both by convention and because of anti-discrimination law; in some other countries such details remain customary.
