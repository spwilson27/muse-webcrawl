Enhanced recovery after surgery

Enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) is a programme of perioperative care designed to help patients recover more quickly after major surgery, reduce complications and shorten hospital stays. Sometimes described as "fast-track surgery," ERAS uses a multimodal, evidence-based pathway that spans the preoperative, intraoperative and postoperative periods, coordinated by a multidisciplinary team of surgeons, anaesthetists, nurses, physiotherapists and dietitians.

History

The concept was pioneered in the 1990s by the Danish surgeon Henrik Kehlet and colleagues, who questioned many traditional practices in colorectal surgery — such as prolonged fasting, routine bowel preparation and extended bed rest — and showed that a coordinated package of interventions could reduce the body's stress response to surgery. The ERAS Society was founded in 2010 to develop and disseminate standardised guidelines, which now exist for many surgical specialties including colorectal, orthopaedic, gynaecological, urological, thoracic and pancreatic surgery.

Elements of the pathway

Typical ERAS elements include preoperative counselling and patient education; avoidance of prolonged fasting, with clear fluids and carbohydrate drinks allowed until shortly before surgery; omission of routine mechanical bowel preparation in most procedures; antibiotic and thrombosis prophylaxis; anaesthetic techniques that minimise opioids, such as regional blocks and epidural analgesia; minimally invasive surgical techniques where appropriate; maintenance of normal body temperature and careful fluid management; avoidance of routine drains and nasogastric tubes; early oral feeding after surgery; and early mobilisation, with patients encouraged to get out of bed on the day of surgery or the day after.

Outcomes

Studies and audits of ERAS programmes consistently show reduced complication rates and shorter hospital stays — often by several days after major colorectal surgery — without increased readmission rates, along with cost savings for health systems. Compliance with the full bundle of elements is associated with the best outcomes, and audit of compliance is a core part of ERAS implementation.
