Chūgoku region

The Chūgoku region (中国地方, Chūgoku-chihō, "middle country") is the westernmost region of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. It consists of the prefectures of Hiroshima, Okayama, Shimane, Tottori, and Yamaguchi, and is traditionally divided by the Chūgoku Mountains into the sunny Seto Inland Sea side (San'yō) and the quieter, snowier Sea of Japan side (San'in).

Geography and history

Hiroshima is the region's principal city and industrial centre, known worldwide as the first city attacked with an atomic bomb, in 1945. Okayama and Kurashiki serve as eastern gateways, while the San'in coast holds the ancient shrine of Izumo Taisha and the sand dunes of Tottori, Japan's largest. Yamaguchi, at the western tip, faces Kyushu across the Kanmon Straits at Shimonoseki.
