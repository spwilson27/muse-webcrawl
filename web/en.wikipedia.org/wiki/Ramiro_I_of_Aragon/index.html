Ramiro I of Aragon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ramiro I (before 1007 – 8 May 1063) was the first King of Aragon, ruling from 1035 until his death. The illegitimate son of Sancho III of Pamplona, he inherited the county of Aragon on his father's death and expanded it into a kingdom, laying the foundations of the medieval Aragonese monarchy.

Reign

When Sancho III divided his realms among his sons in 1035, Ramiro received Aragon, initially as a county under the suzerainty of his half-brother García Sánchez III of Pamplona. Ramiro soon asserted his independence and took the royal title. He also acquired the counties of Sobrarbe and Ribagorza after the death of his half-brother Gonzalo in 1045.

Ramiro campaigned against the Muslim taifa kingdoms to the south, seizing frontier fortresses and exacting tribute (parias). He fought against his half-brother García in Navarre and was allied at times with his other half-brother, Ferdinand I of León and Castile.

Death and succession

Ramiro was killed in 1063 at the Battle of Graus while besieging the town, which was defended by forces of the taifa of Zaragoza aided by Castilian troops under the young Sancho II and, according to tradition, Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, later known as El Cid. He was succeeded by his son Sancho Ramírez, who continued the expansion of Aragon.
