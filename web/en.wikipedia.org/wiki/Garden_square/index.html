Garden square

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A garden square is a type of communal garden enclosed by, and arranged at the centre of, a square of residential buildings. Garden squares are most strongly associated with London, where hundreds were laid out from the 17th century onwards as part of the speculative development of the great estates, but examples are found in many other British and Irish cities, including Edinburgh, Bath, Bristol, Glasgow and Dublin.

Description

A garden square consists of a railed green space — lawns, paths, mature trees and planting — surrounded on some or all sides by terraced houses or mansion blocks whose residents historically held keys to the garden. The garden provided a private, semi-rural amenity in the middle of the city: a place for promenading, children's play and the enjoyment of clean air. Unlike a public park, access was restricted to the surrounding householders, who paid a garden rate for its upkeep; many squares remain private or key-holder-only today, although a number have been opened to the public at least part of the time.

History

The form developed from the residential squares of 17th-century London, such as Covent Garden and Lincoln's Inn Fields, where the central space was at first a simple gravelled area. During the 18th and early 19th centuries, as districts such as Bloomsbury, Marylebone, Belgravia, Kensington and Bayswater were built up, the centre of the square was increasingly landscaped as a planted garden for residents. Gardeners such as Humphry Repton influenced their design.

In the 20th century, many squares were threatened by neglect, wartime requisitioning (for allotments and air-raid shelters) and development. The London Squares Preservation Act 1931 gave statutory protection to 461 squares in the capital, and further protection followed through listing, conservation areas and the work of the London Parks & Gardens Trust. The annual Open Garden Squares Weekend (now London Open Gardens) opens many normally private squares to visitors.

Notable examples

  • Russell Square, Bloomsbury
  • Belgrave Square, Belgravia
  • Eccleston Square, Pimlico
  • Merrion Square, Dublin
