Hezbollah (Arabic: حزب الله, romanized: Ḥizb Allāh, lit. 'Party of God') is a Lebanese Shia Islamist political party and paramilitary organization. It was founded in the midst of the Lebanese Civil War, with support from Iran, in response to the Israeli invasion of Lebanon in 1982. Hezbollah's paramilitary wing, the Jihad Council, is regarded as substantially stronger than the Lebanese Armed Forces.

Hezbollah has been a significant political force in Lebanon, holding seats in the Lebanese Parliament and participating in coalition governments, while also running an extensive network of social services, schools, and hospitals. Its long-time secretary-general, Hassan Nasrallah, led the organization from 1992 until his death in an Israeli airstrike in September 2024.

History

The organization coalesced in the early 1980s among Lebanese Shia clerics inspired by the Iranian Revolution and trained by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. It announced itself publicly with an "Open Letter" manifesto in 1985, calling for the expulsion of Western forces from Lebanon and the destruction of Israel. During the 1980s and 1990s, Hezbollah guerrillas fought Israeli forces occupying southern Lebanon until Israel's withdrawal in 2000.

2006 Lebanon War

In July 2006, a cross-border raid by Hezbollah that killed and captured Israeli soldiers triggered a 34-day war. Israeli airstrikes and a ground offensive devastated parts of southern Lebanon and Beirut's southern suburbs, while Hezbollah fired thousands of rockets into northern Israel. The conflict ended with UN Security Council Resolution 1701 and an expanded UNIFIL presence in the south.

Political role

Hezbollah first participated in Lebanese elections in 1992 and became a central actor in national politics, at times holding veto power within the cabinet through the "blocking third." Its alliance with the Free Patriotic Movement and the Amal Movement shaped Lebanon's post-2005 political landscape.

Foreign involvement

From 2012 onwards, Hezbollah fighters intervened extensively in the Syrian civil war in support of the government of Bashar al-Assad, an involvement that strained Lebanon's policy of dissociation from regional conflicts. The organization has also been accused of operations abroad, and its military wing was designated a terrorist organization by the European Union in 2013; several states, including the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia, designate the organization in its entirety.
