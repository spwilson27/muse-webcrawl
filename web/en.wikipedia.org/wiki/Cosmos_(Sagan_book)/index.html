Cosmos (Sagan book)

[Image: Cosmos (Sagan book)]

Cosmos is a 1980 popular science book by astronomer and astrophysicist Carl Sagan. Its 13 illustrated chapters, corresponding to the 13 episodes of the Cosmos television series, explore topics including the origins of life, the history of astronomy, the nature of the universe, and humanity's place within it.

Background

The book was published by Random House in 1980 to accompany the PBS television series Cosmos: A Personal Voyage, which Sagan co-wrote with Ann Druyan and Steven Soter. It spent 70 weeks on the New York Times bestseller list and was for a time the best-selling science book ever published in English.

Contents

Chapters range from "The Shores of the Cosmic Ocean", introducing the scale of the universe and the Cosmic Calendar, through the history of astronomy from Eratosthenes and Kepler, the search for extraterrestrial life, the lives of stars, and the future of human civilisation. The book is illustrated with hundreds of photographs and artworks.

Legacy

Cosmos has been credited with inspiring a generation of scientists and science communicators. A sequel television series, Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey (2014), hosted by Neil deGrasse Tyson, renewed interest in the book. Sagan followed it with Pale Blue Dot (1994), a further exploration of humanity's future in space.
