Banshee

A banshee (from Irish bean sí, "woman of the fairy mound") is a female spirit in Irish folklore who heralds the death of a family member, usually by keening, wailing, shrieking, or a combination of these. Her cry is said to be heard at night before a death in certain old Irish families.

OriginIrish folklore
TypeFemale spirit / fairy
SignKeening foretells a death

Description

Accounts vary: she may appear as a beautiful young woman, a stately matron, or a ragged old hag, often with long flowing hair she combs with a silver comb — an object folklore warns against picking up. She is commonly dressed in white or grey with a cloak. In some traditions she washes the blood-stained clothes of those about to die, similar to the Scottish bean nighe.

Tradition

Banshees were attached to particular families, especially those with surnames beginning with Ó or Mac, and her keening — related to the professional mourning practice of caoineadh — could be heard for miles. Comparable figures appear in Scottish, Welsh (the cyhyraeth and Gwrach y Rhibyn), and Breton tradition. The banshee has become a staple of Gothic literature, horror films, and fantasy games.
