Central Government War Headquarters

The Central Government War Headquarters (CGWHQ) was a vast underground complex at Corsham in Wiltshire, England, built in the late 1950s as the United Kingdom's emergency seat of government in the event of nuclear war. Known by codenames including Burlington, the facility occupied a former stone quarry, Spring Quarry, which had already been developed underground during the Second World War.

History

Spring Quarry was requisitioned in the 1940s and used for underground aircraft engine production and ammunition storage. After the war, as the threat of nuclear attack on London grew, the site was redeveloped from about 1957 into a self-contained underground headquarters intended to accommodate the Prime Minister, senior ministers, civil servants, and military staff — a population of several thousand people — for a period of weeks following a nuclear strike.

Facilities

The complex covered roughly 35 acres underground and contained offices, dormitories, a canteen and kitchen facilities, a hospital, telephone exchanges, a BBC studio from which emergency broadcasts could be made, and a room connecting to the national Government Communications Headquarters network. A reservoir and lake within the quarry provided water, and generators and fuel stores allowed independent operation. An underground railway connected parts of the site, and a route linked the complex to the main London–Bristol railway line near Box Tunnel.

Later history

The headquarters was maintained on a care-and-maintenance basis through the Cold War, with its readiness declining after the late 1980s. It was formally decommissioned in the early 2000s; the site's existence, long an open secret locally, was publicly acknowledged by the government in 2004. Parts of the former quarry have since been used for wine storage and secure document storage.
