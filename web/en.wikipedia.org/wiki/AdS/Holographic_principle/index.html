The holographic principle and anti-de Sitter space

Bulk information encoded on a boundary, as in a hologram

The holographic principle is a conjectured property of quantum gravity holding that the description of a volume of space can be encoded on a lower-dimensional boundary to that region — much as a hologram encodes a three-dimensional image on a two-dimensional plate. In anti-de Sitter space (AdS), the principle is made concrete by the AdS/CFT correspondence: a gravitational theory in the AdS bulk is fully equivalent to a non-gravitational conformal field theory on its boundary. AdS is so far the only setting in which holography has been realised explicitly and studied in quantitative detail.

Origins

The principle emerged from black hole thermodynamics. Jacob Bekenstein and Stephen Hawking showed in the 1970s that a black hole's entropy is proportional not to its volume but to the area of its event horizon (in Planck units), suggesting that the maximum information content of any region scales with its surface area rather than its volume. Gerard 't Hooft (1993) and Leonard Susskind (1995) generalised this "Bekenstein bound" into the holographic principle, arguing that a region of space containing gravity cannot hold more degrees of freedom than can be represented on its boundary.

Realisation in AdS

Maldacena's AdS/CFT correspondence (1997) identified the boundary theory precisely: for string theory on AdS5 × S5, the boundary theory is four-dimensional N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory. In this framework, every bulk field corresponds to a boundary operator, radial position in AdS corresponds to energy scale in the field theory, and a black hole in the bulk corresponds to a hot thermal state on the boundary. The principle has since been extended conceptually by the Ryu–Takayanagi prescription (2006), which computes boundary entanglement entropy from minimal surfaces in the bulk and suggests that spacetime itself is stitched together by quantum entanglement — an idea sharpened in the "ER = EPR" conjecture and the interpretation of the bulk as a quantum error-correcting code.

Significance and limits

Holography in AdS provides the sharpest available evidence that spacetime and gravity are emergent, that black hole evaporation must be unitary, and that apparently distinct physical theories can be exact reformulations of one another. Its principal limitation is cosmological: the observable universe has a positive cosmological constant and resembles de Sitter space, for which a comparably explicit holographic theory ("dS/CFT") remains incomplete. Extending the lessons of AdS holography — the subject of quantum gravity in AdS more broadly — to realistic cosmologies is a central open problem of theoretical physics.
