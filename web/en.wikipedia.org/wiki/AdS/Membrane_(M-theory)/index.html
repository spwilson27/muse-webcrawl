Membrane (M-theory)

Membrane (M2-brane)
Type: Brane in M-theory
Spatial dimensions: 2
Dual: M5-brane

In theoretical physics, a membrane, also called an M2-brane, is a spatially extended mathematical object — a two-dimensional membrane sweeping out a three-dimensional worldvolume — that arises in M-theory, the proposed unifying theory of the five superstring theories. Membranes are charged under the three-form field of eleven-dimensional supergravity.

Context

M-theory was proposed by Edward Witten in 1995. In its low-energy limit, eleven-dimensional supergravity, the fundamental extended objects are the M2-brane and its magnetic dual, the M5-brane. When M-theory is compactified on a circle, the M2-brane wrapping the circle becomes the fundamental string of type IIA string theory, connecting membranes to the duality web used in AdS/CFT constructions involving anti-de Sitter backgrounds.

Properties

Multiple coincident M2-branes are described by theories such as the Bagger–Lambert–Gustavsson model and the ABJM theory, the latter describing M2-branes in an AdS4 × S7/Zk background and central to three-dimensional holography.
