General relativity

General relativity
Introduced: 1915, Albert Einstein
Field: Gravitation / spacetime geometry

General relativity is a theory of gravitation developed by Albert Einstein between 1907 and 1915. It describes gravity not as a force but as a consequence of the curvature of spacetime caused by the uneven distribution of mass and energy. The Einstein field equations relate spacetime curvature to the stress–energy tensor.

This page is reproduced in the context of anti-de Sitter space: anti-de Sitter (AdS) space is a solution of general relativity with a negative cosmological constant, central to the AdS/CFT correspondence proposed by Juan Maldacena.

History

Einstein presented the field equations to the Prussian Academy of Sciences in November 1915. The theory explained the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion and predicted the bending of light by the Sun, confirmed by Arthur Eddington's 1919 eclipse expedition.

Consequences

Predictions include gravitational time dilation, gravitational redshift, black holes emitting Hawking radiation, gravitational waves (directly detected in 2015), frame-dragging, and the expansion of the universe. Solutions include Schwarzschild, Kerr, Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker, de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spaces — see De Sitter space.

Modern status

General relativity has passed many precision tests and underlies GPS corrections and modern cosmology, though reconciling it with quantum mechanics remains an open problem addressed by string theory and other approaches to quantum gravity.
