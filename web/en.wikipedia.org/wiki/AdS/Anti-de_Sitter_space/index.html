Anti-de Sitter Space

Anti-de Sitter space (AdS) is a maximally symmetric Lorentzian manifold with constant negative scalar curvature. It is the Lorentzian analogue of hyperbolic space and arises in general relativity as a vacuum solution of Einstein's field equations with a negative cosmological constant. AdS plays a central role in theoretical physics through the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Definition

Anti-de Sitter space AdSn can be defined as a hyperboloid embedded in a flat space with two timelike dimensions: the set of points satisfying −X0² − Xn² + ΣXi² = −L², where L is the AdS radius, related to the cosmological constant by Λ = −(n−1)(n−2)/(2L²). Identifying points to remove closed timelike curves gives the universal cover, which is the space usually meant in physics.

Properties

Although AdS is spatially infinite, light can reach its conformal boundary and return in finite coordinate time, so the boundary — conformal to Minkowski space times a sphere — plays an essential role: boundary conditions are part of the definition of physics in AdS. Geodesics diverge exponentially, a reflection of the negative curvature, and the space acts rather like a confining box for fields within it.

In physics

Freund–Rubin compactifications of supergravity yield AdS4 × S7 and AdS7 × S4 solutions in M-theory, and stacks of D3-branes give AdS5 × S5 in type IIB string theory. Since 1997, AdS has been the gravitational side of the AdS/CFT duality, linking quantum gravity in the bulk to conformal field theories on the boundary.
