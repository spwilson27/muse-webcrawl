De Sitter space

De Sitter space
Type: Maximally symmetric Lorentzian manifold
Cosmological constant: Positive

In mathematical physics, de Sitter space is a maximally symmetric Lorentzian manifold with constant positive scalar curvature. It is the Lorentzian analogue of a sphere and is named after Willem de Sitter. It models a universe with a positive cosmological constant and no matter, expanding exponentially.

De Sitter space is a solution of Einstein's field equations in general relativity — see AdS/General relativity in this set — with positive cosmological constant. Our universe appears to be asymptotically de Sitter in the far future if dark energy is a cosmological constant.

Definition

De Sitter space in n dimensions can be defined as a hyperboloid embedded in Minkowski space of one higher dimension. Its isometry group is the Lorentz group O(1,n).

Coordinates

Static coordinates cover the region accessible to a single observer and exhibit a cosmological horizon with an associated temperature, analogous to Hawking radiation for black holes. Global, flat (Poincaré), and closed slicings describe the space in expanding forms used in inflationary cosmology.

Applications

De Sitter space underlies models of cosmic inflation in the early universe and the current accelerated expansion. The dS/CFT correspondence is a conjectured analogue of the AdS/CFT correspondence proposed by Juan Maldacena and others for anti-de Sitter space.
