Black hole information paradox in AdS/CFT

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The black hole information paradox arises from the apparent conflict between quantum mechanics, which requires that the evolution of a closed system be unitary (information-preserving and reversible), and Stephen Hawking's 1976 calculation that a black hole evaporates by emitting thermal radiation, seemingly destroying the information about everything that fell in. The AdS/CFT correspondence provides the sharpest setting in which the paradox has been addressed: in anti-de Sitter space, gravity in the bulk is equivalent to an ordinary quantum field theory — with no gravity at all — living on the boundary, and that theory is manifestly unitary.

The AdS/CFT argument

If a black hole forms and evaporates in AdS space, the dual description is a thermal state of the boundary conformal field theory heating up and cooling down. Because the boundary theory is an ordinary unitary quantum system, its evolution cannot lose information; by the duality, the bulk gravitational process cannot lose information either. This persuaded many physicists, including Hawking himself (who conceded a famous bet to John Preskill in 2004), that black hole evaporation is unitary and that information escapes, encoded subtly in correlations among the Hawking quanta. The Page curve — the expected rise and fall of the radiation's entanglement entropy over the evaporation, derived by Don Page in 1993 — is the benchmark any unitary account must reproduce.

Firewalls and later developments

In 2012 Almheiri, Marolf, Polchinski and Sully (AMPS) sharpened the paradox, arguing that unitarity, the equivalence principle at the horizon, and ordinary effective field theory outside the hole cannot all hold, and proposing a high-energy "firewall" at the horizon. Responses include ER = EPR (the proposal by Maldacena and Susskind that entangled particles are connected by microscopic wormholes). From 2019, semiclassical gravity calculations using "islands" — regions of the black hole interior included in the radiation's entanglement wedge — reproduced the Page curve within gravitational path integrals, strengthening the consensus that the paradox is resolved in favour of unitarity, though a complete microscopic account in realistic spacetimes remains an open problem.
