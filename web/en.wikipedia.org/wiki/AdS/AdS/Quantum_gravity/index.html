Quantum Gravity in Anti-de Sitter Space

Quantum gravity in anti-de Sitter (AdS) space refers to the study of gravitational physics at the quantum level in spacetimes with a negative cosmological constant. Because of the AdS/CFT correspondence, quantum gravity in AdS is uniquely tractable: it is defined non-perturbatively by an ordinary conformal field theory on the AdS boundary, making AdS the best-understood setting for quantum gravity in general.

Holography defines AdS quantum gravity through a non-gravitational boundary theory.

The holographic definition

Unlike quantum gravity in flat or de Sitter space, which still lacks a complete non-perturbative formulation, quantum gravity in AdS inherits a definition from the dual CFT: every bulk observable maps to a boundary observable, and the boundary theory's unitary quantum mechanics guarantees a consistent bulk theory. Semiclassical Einstein gravity emerges in the limit of large central charge and strong coupling.

Emergent spacetime

Work since 2010 has emphasized that bulk spacetime geometry is encoded in the pattern of entanglement of the boundary theory. The Ryu–Takayanagi formula, tensor-network models, and the interpretation of the bulk as a quantum error-correcting code have made AdS a laboratory for the idea that spacetime itself is emergent from quantum information.

Black holes and information

AdS black holes correspond to thermal states of the CFT, so questions about Hawking radiation and information loss can be posed in a unitary framework. Replica-wormhole calculations of the Page curve, first carried out in AdS-like settings in 2019, showed that semiclassical gravity itself reproduces the information-preserving answer expected from unitarity.
