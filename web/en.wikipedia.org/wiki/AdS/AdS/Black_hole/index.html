Black Holes in Anti-de Sitter Space

A black hole in anti-de Sitter (AdS) space — most simply the Schwarzschild–AdS solution — is a black hole whose far-away geometry approaches AdS rather than flat space. AdS black holes are thermodynamically richer than their flat-space cousins and, through the AdS/CFT correspondence, are dual to thermal states of a conformal field theory, making them a central tool in quantum gravity.

Thermodynamics

Unlike Schwarzschild black holes in flat space, large AdS black holes have positive specific heat: the AdS "box" reflects Hawking radiation back, allowing stable equilibrium between the hole and its own radiation. Hawking and Page showed in 1983 that above a critical temperature the dominant phase is a large black hole, while below it thermal AdS (no black hole) dominates — a first-order phase transition.

Holographic interpretation

Edward Witten interpreted the Hawking–Page transition as the confinement–deconfinement transition of the dual gauge theory: thermal AdS corresponds to the confined phase and the AdS black hole to a deconfined quark–gluon plasma. Black hole entropy, quasinormal modes, and transport coefficients computed in the bulk translate into thermalization and hydrodynamic properties of the boundary plasma.

Variants and instabilities

Charged (Reissner–Nordström–AdS) and rotating (Kerr–AdS) holes display phase diagrams resembling liquid–gas systems. Small AdS black holes can be unstable to superradiance, and hairy and extremal AdS holes underlie holographic models of superconductors and strange metals.
