Quantum Field Theory and Anti-de Sitter Space

Quantum field theory (QFT) in and around anti-de Sitter (AdS) space takes two related forms: quantum fields propagating on a fixed AdS background, and — more consequentially — the conformal field theories (CFTs) that live on the AdS boundary and, via the AdS/CFT correspondence, define quantum gravity in the bulk. Together they make quantum field theory the language in which AdS physics is both formulated and solved.

Bulk fields in AdS are dual to operators of the boundary conformal field theory.

Fields in AdS

Because AdS has a timelike boundary, quantum fields there require boundary conditions; in global AdS the spectrum of a free field is discrete, like that of a particle in a box. A bulk scalar of mass m corresponds to a boundary operator of dimension Δ satisfying m²L² = Δ(Δ − d), and for masses in the Breitenlohner–Freedman window two different quantizations (two boundary conditions, two values of Δ) are both consistent.

The holographic dictionary

The Gubser–Klebanov–Polyakov–Witten prescription equates the bulk partition function, evaluated with boundary value φ₀, to the CFT generating functional with φ₀ as the source for the dual operator. Correlation functions, renormalization-group flows (realized as bulk domain walls), and anomalies all have matched descriptions on the two sides. Large-N expansion on the QFT side corresponds to the classical (small gs) expansion of bulk string theory.

Applications

Treating strongly coupled QFTs through weakly coupled AdS gravity has produced results for quark–gluon plasma transport, holographic superconductors, and non-Fermi liquids, while bootstrap and lattice studies of CFTs feed back into constraints on which quantum gravities in AdS can exist at all.
