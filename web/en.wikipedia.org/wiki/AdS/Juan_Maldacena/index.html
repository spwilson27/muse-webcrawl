Juan Maldacena

Juan Martín Maldacena
Born: 10 September 1968, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Known for: AdS/CFT correspondence
Fields: String theory, quantum gravity

Juan Martín Maldacena (born 10 September 1968) is an Argentine theoretical physicist and the Carl P. Feinberg Professor in the School of Natural Sciences at the Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton. He is best known for proposing the AdS/CFT correspondence in 1997, the most successful concrete realization of the holographic principle.

Career

Maldacena studied at the Universidad Nacional de Cuyo and Instituto Balseiro, receiving his doctorate in 1996. After postdoctoral work at Rutgers and Harvard, he became a professor at Harvard before moving to Princeton in 2001.

AdS/CFT correspondence

His 1997 paper conjectured an equivalence between string theory / supergravity on anti-de Sitter space and a conformal field theory — specifically supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory — on its boundary. The paper became one of the most cited in high-energy physics, linking general relativity, black holes and Hawking radiation to gauge theory.

Awards

Awards include the Dirac Medal, the Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics (2012), the Dannie Heineman Prize, and membership in the U.S. National Academy of Sciences.
