Supergravity in anti-de Sitter space

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Supergravity is a class of field theories that combine general relativity with supersymmetry, so that the graviton is accompanied by a spin-3/2 partner, the gravitino, and matter fields by superpartners. Its consistent vacua with a negative cosmological constant — anti-de Sitter (AdS) space — occupy a central place in string theory because of the AdS/CFT correspondence: the most studied examples of holographic duality have, on the gravitational side, a supergravity theory on an AdS background.

AdS vacua

Eleven-dimensional supergravity (the low-energy limit of M-theory) admits the Freund–Rubin solutions AdS4 × S7 and AdS7 × S4, in which a four-form flux threads one factor of the geometry. Type IIB supergravity in ten dimensions admits AdS5 × S5, supported by five-form flux; this is the geometry generated by a stack of D3-branes, and the background of Maldacena's original duality with 𝒩 = 4 super Yang–Mills theory. In the regime where the AdS radius is large compared with the string and Planck lengths, string theory on these backgrounds reduces to classical supergravity, which is the regime dual to strongly coupled, large-N gauge theory.

Gauged supergravity

Dimensional reduction of the higher-dimensional theories on spheres yields gauged supergravities in four, five and seven dimensions, in which some supersymmetries are preserved and the scalar potential has AdS critical points. These lower-dimensional theories are workhorses of applied holography: their black holes, domain walls and time-dependent solutions model thermalisation, phase transitions and transport in strongly coupled quantum matter, including holographic models of superconductors and strange metals.
