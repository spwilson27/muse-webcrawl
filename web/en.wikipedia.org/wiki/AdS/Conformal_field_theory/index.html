Conformal field theory — the boundary side of AdS/CFT

A conformal field theory (CFT) is a quantum field theory invariant under conformal transformations — transformations that preserve angles but not necessarily distances. In the context of anti-de Sitter space, a CFT living on the boundary of AdS is conjectured to be fully equivalent to a theory of gravity in the bulk: the AdS/CFT correspondence proposed by Juan Maldacena in 1997.

Key features

CFTs are characterised by operator scaling dimensions, operator product expansions, and correlation functions strongly constrained by symmetry. They describe critical points of statistical systems and the worldsheet theory of strings. The best-understood example is maximally supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory in four dimensions, dual to type IIB string theory on AdS5×S5.

The holographic dictionary

Bulk fields correspond to boundary operators; bulk masses fix operator dimensions; the generating functional of the CFT equals the gravitational partition function. Because the boundary theory has no gravity and evolves unitarily, the duality bears on the black hole information paradox and quantum gravity generally.

Methods

The conformal bootstrap, conformal blocks, and the study of entanglement entropy (Ryu–Takayanagi formula) are central tools for extracting CFT data and testing holography.
