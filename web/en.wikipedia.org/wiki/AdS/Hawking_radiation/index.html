Hawking radiation

Hawking radiation
Proposed: 1974, Stephen Hawking
Effect: Black holes emit thermal radiation and evaporate

Hawking radiation is theoretical radiation predicted to be released outside a black hole's event horizon because of relativistic quantum effects. It was first proposed in 1974 by Stephen Hawking. Because this radiation reduces the mass and rotational energy of black holes, they are predicted to shrink and eventually evaporate.

Overview

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime predicts that near the event horizon, particle–antiparticle fluctuations can lead to one partner falling in while the other escapes, carrying away energy. The resulting spectrum is thermal, with a temperature inversely proportional to the black hole's mass. In anti-de Sitter space, Hawking radiation figures in the thermodynamics of AdS black holes and the Hawking–Page transition, important in Maldacena's AdS/CFT correspondence and in general relativity.

Information paradox

If Hawking radiation is purely thermal, information about matter falling in appears lost, conflicting with quantum unitarity. This black hole information paradox has motivated research in holography, firewalls, complementarity, and replica wormholes.

Detection

Astronomical Hawking radiation is far too faint to observe for stellar-mass black holes. Analogue systems in fluids, optics, and condensates have reported phenomena analogous to Hawking emission.
