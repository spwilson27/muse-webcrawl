Dog grooming refers to both the hygienic care and cleaning of a dog, as well as a process by which a dog's physical appearance is enhanced for showing or other types of competition. A dog groomer (or simply "groomer") is a person who earns their living grooming dogs.

Reasons for grooming

Grooming is an important part of dog care. Depending on the breed, age, and health of the dog, grooming may need to be done daily or far less frequently. Regular grooming helps to ensure the dog is healthy and comfortable, provides an opportunity to check for parasites, skin problems, lumps, and other health issues, and strengthens the bond between dog and owner.

Processes

Typical grooming services include bathing and drying, brushing and de-matting, haircuts or clipping to breed-specific styles, hand stripping of wire coats, nail trimming, ear cleaning, and expression of the anal glands. Tools of the trade include brushes, combs, clippers, stripping knives, grooming tables, and dryers. Show grooming for breeds such as the Poodle and Bichon Frise can be highly elaborate and time-consuming.

Health considerations

Excessive bathing can strip natural oils from the coat; groomers use shampoos formulated for dogs, whose skin has a different pH from human skin. Long nails can cause discomfort and gait problems, so regular trimming is considered essential maintenance rather than a cosmetic extra.
