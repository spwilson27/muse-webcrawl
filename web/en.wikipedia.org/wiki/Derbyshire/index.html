Derbyshire is a ceremonial county in the East Midlands of England. It is bordered by Greater Manchester, West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, Nottinghamshire, Leicestershire, Staffordshire, and Cheshire. The county town is Matlock and the largest settlement is the city of Derby, a unitary authority within the ceremonial county.

Geography

Much of the county is upland. The southern Pennines occupy the north, including most of the Peak District National Park — the first national park in Britain, designated in 1951. The Dark Peak, of gritstone moorland, includes Kinder Scout, the county's highest point at 636 metres, while the White Peak to the south is a limestone plateau dissected by dales such as Dovedale and Lathkill Dale. The south of the county is lower-lying, centred on the Trent valley.

History and economy

Derbyshire has a long industrial heritage: lead mining since Roman times, coal mining in the east and north-east, and cotton textiles in the Derwent valley, where the mills of Arkwright and the Strutts — now a UNESCO World Heritage Site — pioneered the factory system. Derby became a centre of railway engineering and is home to Rolls-Royce's aero-engine works; Toyota manufactures cars at Burnaston. Quarrying of limestone and aggregates remains important, and tourism in the Peak District is a major employer.

Settlements and attractions

Towns include Chesterfield, Buxton (a Georgian spa town), Matlock, Ilkeston, Glossop, Ashbourne, and Bakewell, origin of the Bakewell pudding. Great houses include Chatsworth House, seat of the Duke of Devonshire, Haddon Hall, Hardwick Hall, and Kedleston Hall.
