Nobiin language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Nile valley in Nubia, where Nobiin is spoken

Nobiin (also called Mahas or Fadicca) is a Northern Nubian language of the Nilo-Saharan family, spoken by the Nubian people along the Nile in southern Egypt and northern Sudan, from around Aswan downstream of the First Cataract to the area of Dongola. It is the largest Nubian language by number of speakers, with several hundred thousand users, though it is under pressure from Arabic.

History

Nobiin descends from Old Nubian, the language of the medieval Christian kingdoms of Nubia, which was written in a modified Coptic alphabet and is attested in texts from the 8th to 15th centuries — making the Nubian languages among the oldest documented in sub-Saharan Africa. Modern Nobiin was unwritten for most of its recent history; a Latin-based orthography (and adapted Arabic script) has been developed in the 20th and 21st centuries, and teaching materials now exist.

Linguistic features

Nobiin is a tonal, agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order and postpositions, features that have been cited in debates over the classification of the Nilo-Saharan phylum. It has vowel length distinctions, two tones, and an elaborate system of derivational suffixes. The language lost speakers after the building of the Aswan High Dam in the 1960s flooded Nubian lands and displaced communities to resettlement villages, accelerating shift to Arabic; revival efforts include cultural associations, music, and social-media initiatives in the Nubian diaspora.
