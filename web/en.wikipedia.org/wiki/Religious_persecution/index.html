Religious persecution

Religious persecution is the systematic mistreatment of an individual or group of individuals as a response to their religious beliefs or affiliations or their lack thereof. The tendency of societies or groups within societies to alienate or repress different subcultures is a recurrent theme in human history. Moreover, because a person's religion often determines their sense of morality, worldview, self-image, attitudes towards others, and overall personal identity, religious differences can be significant cultural, personal, and social factors.

Definition

Religious persecution can be triggered by religious or anti-religious stances — a person can be subject to oppression or violence by a group that either disagrees with their particular beliefs or finds their lack of adherence to religious dogma objectionable. Persecution ranges from social discrimination and legal disabilities to forced conversion, expulsion, violence, and mass killing.

Forms

  • Legal disabilities: restrictions on worship, building places of worship, holding office, or on dress and practice.
  • Forced conversion: compelling members of a faith to renounce it, sometimes under threat of death.
  • Expulsion: driving a religious community from a territory, as in the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492.
  • Violence: pogroms, massacres, and campaigns of extermination directed at religious groups.

History

Examples span antiquity to the present: the Roman persecution of Christians; medieval persecutions of heretics and Jews; the early modern wars of religion and the Inquisitions; the persecution of Baháʼís in Iran; of Christians, Yazidis, and others in parts of the Middle East; of Falun Gong practitioners and Uyghur Muslims in China; and of Rohingya Muslims in Myanmar. Regimes that promote state atheism have also persecuted believers, notably in the Soviet Union and in China during the Cultural Revolution.

International law

Freedom of religion is protected by Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. The 1981 UN Declaration on the Elimination of All Forms of Intolerance and of Discrimination Based on Religion or Belief addresses persecution directly, and international reports such as those of the U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom monitor violations.
