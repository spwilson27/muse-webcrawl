Mongolian writing systems

Many alphabets and writing systems have been devised for the Mongolian language over the centuries. The most widely used historically is the classical Mongolian script, created at the time of Genghis Khan in the early 13th century, and still the standard script of the Mongolian language in Inner Mongolia, China. In Mongolia itself, the Cyrillic alphabet, adopted in the 1940s, is the principal script, though the classical script is taught in schools and is being restored to wider official use.

The classical script

The traditional Mongolian alphabet was adapted from the Old Uyghur alphabet, itself derived from Sogdian and ultimately Aramaic. It is written vertically, in columns running from left to right, the letters joining along a central spine with forms varying by position in the word. It was used to record the great works of Mongolian literature and administration, including the Secret History of the Mongols in its surviving transcribed form.

Other scripts

  • 'Phags-pa script – created in 1269 by the Tibetan lama 'Phags-pa for Kublai Khan as a universal script for the Yuan dynasty; it fell out of use after 1368.
  • Clear script (Todo) – devised in 1648 by the Oirat lama Zaya Pandita to write the Oirat dialect more precisely; still used by Kalmyks and Oirats.
  • Galik script – an extension of the classical alphabet for transcribing Sanskrit and Tibetan religious terms.
  • Soyombo script – created in 1686 by Zanabazar for religious use.
  • Cyrillic alphabet – introduced in the Mongolian People's Republic in the 1940s, with the additional letters ө and ү.
  • Latin alphabet – briefly promoted in the 1930s and now used informally in electronic communication.
