Music of Belize

The music of Belize reflects the country's diverse ethnic makeup—Creole, Garifuna, Maya, Mestizo, and East Indian communities—and its Caribbean and Central American setting. Belizean music ranges from Garifuna punta and punta rock to Creole brukdown, Maya harp music, and modern soca, reggae, and dancehall-influenced styles.

Garifuna music

The Garifuna, descendants of West African, Central African, Island Carib, and Arawak people, have a rich tradition centered on drumming, call-and-response singing, and genres such as punta, paranda, chumba, and wanaragua (John Canoe). Garifuna music and language were proclaimed a UNESCO Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2001. Punta rock, created by Pen Cayetano in the early 1980s, electrified punta rhythms and became Belize's most popular dance music. The late Andy Palacio, with his acclaimed album Wátina (2007), brought Garifuna music to world attention.

Brukdown

Brukdown is a Creole genre that developed in the mahogany camps of the 19th and early 20th centuries, featuring accordion, banjo, harmonica, jawbone, and grater. Its most famous exponent was Wilfred Peters and his Boom and Chime Band; Peters's "Shush and Yuh No Hear" is a national favourite.

Maya and Mestizo music

Maya communities maintain harp-and-violin music, marimba traditions, and ceremonial music tied to Catholic feast days. Mestizo communities in the north share marimba, cumbia, and ranchera influences with neighbouring Mexico and Guatemala.

Popular music

Contemporary Belizean artists perform soca, reggae, dancehall, hip-hop, and R&B. Punta rock bands such as the Garifuna Collective and musicians like Supa G and Aurelio Martínez (from Honduras) are regional stars.
