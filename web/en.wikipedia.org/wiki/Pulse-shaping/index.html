Pulse shaping

In electronics and telecommunications, pulse shaping is the process of changing the waveform of transmitted pulses to optimize the signal for its intended purpose or the communication channel. After a signal is generated, it is passed through a transmission medium, which unavoidably modifies it; pulse shaping aims to control this modification so that, for example, the signal's bandwidth is limited and intersymbol interference is minimized.

Nyquist criterion and common filters

The Nyquist ISI criterion states the conditions under which a pulse's sampled response has zero intersymbol interference. The most widely used pulse-shaping filters satisfying or approximating this criterion are the raised-cosine filter and its square-root form, the root-raised-cosine filter, which is split between transmitter and receiver. Other schemes include Gaussian filtering, used in GMSK modulation.

Applications

Pulse shaping is standard practice in digital radio systems, modems, and wired standards such as DSL. In optics, "pulse shaping" also refers to techniques, often using spatial light modulators, that sculpt the temporal profile of ultrashort laser pulses for spectroscopy, microscopy, and coherent control.
