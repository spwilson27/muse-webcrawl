George Pérez (9 June 1954 – 6 May 2022) was an American comic book artist and writer, whose titles include The Avengers, The New Teen Titans and Crisis on Infinite Earths. Renowned for his detailed, dynamic crowd scenes and clean storytelling, he was one of the most popular and influential super hero artists of his generation.

Career

Born in the South Bronx to Puerto Rican parents, Pérez broke into comics at Marvel in 1974 and became the regular artist on The Avengers. In 1980 he moved to DC Comics and, with writer Marv Wolfman, launched The New Teen Titans, one of the best-selling series of the decade. The pair then created Crisis on Infinite Earths (1985), the landmark 12-issue series that reorganised the DC Universe; Pérez drew every major DC character in its pages.

In 1987 Pérez relaunched Wonder Woman as both artist and writer, redefining the character with a mythology-rooted approach that influenced all later versions. He later returned to Marvel for a celebrated run on The Avengers with Kurt Busiek (1998) and drew the intercompany crossover JLA/Avengers (2003).

Later life

Pérez announced his retirement in 2019 owing to ill health. In December 2021 he disclosed a diagnosis of terminal pancreatic cancer and declined further treatment; the comics community held numerous tributes to him. He died on 6 May 2022, during Free Comic Book Day weekend, aged 67.
