Masatoshi Koshiba

Masatoshi Koshiba
Born: September 19, 1926, Toyohashi, Aichi, Japan
Died: November 12, 2020 (aged 94), Tokyo, Japan
Nationality: Japanese
Fields: Physics (neutrino astronomy)
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (2002)

Masatoshi Koshiba (September 19, 1926 – November 12, 2020) was a Japanese physicist who pioneered neutrino astronomy and was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2002 for his contributions to the detection of cosmic neutrinos.

Career

Koshiba studied at the University of Tokyo and the University of Rochester, and worked at the University of Chicago before returning to Tokyo. In the 1970s he designed the Kamiokande detector, a 3,000-ton water Cherenkov detector built in a mine near Kamioka, Japan, originally to search for proton decay.

Neutrino detections

Kamiokande detected neutrinos from the supernova SN 1987A in 1987 — the first observation of neutrinos from outside the solar system — and recorded atmospheric and solar neutrinos. Its successor, Super-Kamiokande, provided the decisive evidence in 1998 that neutrinos have mass and oscillate between flavours, resolving the long-standing solar neutrino problem.

Nobel Prize

Koshiba shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Physics with Raymond Davis Jr. "for pioneering contributions to astrophysics, in particular for the detection of cosmic neutrinos." He later helped found the Institute for Cosmic Ray Research at the University of Tokyo and served as its director.
