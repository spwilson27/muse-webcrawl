Operation Ten-Go

Date7 April 1945
LocationEast China Sea, between Kyushu and Okinawa
ResultAmerican victory; sinking of the battleship Yamato
BelligerentsEmpire of Japan; United States

Operation Ten-Go (天号作戦, Ten-gō Sakusen), also known in English as Operation Heaven One, was the last major Japanese naval operation in the Pacific Theater of World War II. The plan, approved by Emperor Hirohito, consisted of a suicide attack by the battleship Yamato and nine other warships on the Allied fleet supporting the invasion of Okinawa.

The operation

On 6 April 1945, the surface special attack force under Vice Admiral Seiichi Itō — the battleship Yamato, the light cruiser Yahagi, and eight destroyers — sailed from Tokuyama with only enough fuel for a one-way trip. The plan was for the force to fight its way to Okinawa, beach Yamato, and use her guns in support of the island's defenders until she was destroyed.

The force was sighted by American submarines and reconnaissance aircraft, and on 7 April it came under massive attack by some 380 carrier aircraft from Task Force 58. Yamato, Yahagi, and four destroyers were sunk; Yamato capsized and exploded after taking numerous bomb and torpedo hits, with the loss of over 3,000 of her crew. American losses were ten aircraft and twelve airmen. The sinking of Yamato effectively ended the Imperial Japanese Navy as an organised fighting force.
