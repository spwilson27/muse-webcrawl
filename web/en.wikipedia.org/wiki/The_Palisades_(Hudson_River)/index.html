The Palisades (Hudson River)

The Palisades, also called the New Jersey Palisades or Hudson River Palisades, are a line of steep cliffs along the west side of the lower Hudson River in northeastern New Jersey and southeastern New York, rising nearly vertically from near the water's edge to about 90 metres (300 feet) in places.

LocationHudson River, New Jersey and New York

Geology

The cliffs are the exposed edge of a thick diabase sill intruded into sedimentary rock about 200 million years ago, part of the Central Atlantic magmatic province associated with the breakup of Pangaea. Glacial action and erosion exposed the columnar rock face that reminded early European observers of a wooden palisade fence.

Preservation

Heavy quarrying in the late 19th century threatened the cliffs, prompting a preservation campaign led by women's clubs and governors Theodore Roosevelt and others; the Palisades Interstate Park Commission was created in 1900. The cliffs are now largely protected parkland and a US National Natural Landmark.
