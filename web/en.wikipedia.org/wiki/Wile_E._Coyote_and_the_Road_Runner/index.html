Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner

Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner are a duo of cartoon characters from the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series of animated cartoons, first appearing in Fast and Furry-ous (1949). In each episode, the cunning, devious, and constantly hungry coyote repeatedly attempts to catch and subsequently eat the Road Runner, a fast-running ground bird, but is never successful.

Wile E. Coyote with his trademark Acme products.

Premise

Instead of his natural instincts, the Coyote relies on absurdly complex contraptions, purchased by mail-order from the Acme Corporation, to try to catch his prey — catapults, rocket sleds, jet engines, giant magnets, painted tunnels, and suits of springs — all of which invariably backfire, leaving the Coyote flattened, scorched, or falling from a great height. The Road Runner, who communicates only with a signature "beep beep", remains serenely oblivious, escaping through luck and the cartoon's unique laws of physics.

Creation and rules

The characters were created by animation director Chuck Jones and writer Michael Maltese, as a parody of the traditional chase cartoons such as Tom and Jerry. Jones later codified the series' strict internal rules, including that the Road Runner must stay on the road, that the Coyote is stopped only by his own devices and gravity, that no dialogue is allowed beyond "beep beep", and that the Coyote's humiliation is always self-inflicted — "the Coyote could stop anytime, if he weren't a fanatic".

Legacy

The pair starred in 49 classic theatrical shorts between 1949 and 1966, plus later shorts, television appearances, and video games. Wile E. Coyote also appears, speaking at length, in several cartoons opposite Bugs Bunny. The characters remain icons of slapstick animation and of futile persistence.
