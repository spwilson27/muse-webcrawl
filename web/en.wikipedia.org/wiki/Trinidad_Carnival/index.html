Trinidad Carnival

The Trinidad and Tobago Carnival is an annual festival held on the Monday and Tuesday before Ash Wednesday in Trinidad and Tobago. Often described as "the greatest show on Earth," it is famous for its colourful costumes, calypso and soca music, steelpan competitions and exuberant street parades.

History

The festival has roots in the pre-Lenten masquerade balls of French Catholic settlers who arrived in Spanish Trinidad in the late 18th century, combined with the Canboulay celebrations of emancipated Africans, commemorating the abolition of slavery (1834/1838) with torchlit processions, drumming and stick-fighting. British colonial authorities repeatedly tried to suppress the African elements, provoking the Canboulay riots of 1881, but the festival absorbed them and grew.

Events

Celebrations build through a season of fetes and competitions: the Panorama steelband contest, the Calypso Monarch and Soca Monarch competitions, and the crowning of the King and Queen of the Bands for the most spectacular costumes. The street festival opens with J'ouvert before dawn on Carnival Monday, when revellers covered in mud, oil and paint dance through the streets, followed by two days of costumed "mas bands" parading in "pretty mas" before judges and crowds.

Influence

Trinidadian migrants carried the carnival abroad, inspiring festivals such as London's Notting Hill Carnival, Toronto Caribbean Carnival and Brooklyn's Labor Day Parade.
