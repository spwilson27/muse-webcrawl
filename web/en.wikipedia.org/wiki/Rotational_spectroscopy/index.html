Rotational spectroscopy

Rotational spectroscopy is concerned with the measurement of the energies of transitions between quantized rotational states of molecules in the gas phase. The spectra occur in the microwave region and are often called microwave spectroscopy.

Theory

For a rigid rotor, rotational energy levels are given by E = BJ(J+1), where J is the rotational quantum number and B the rotational constant, inversely proportional to the moment of inertia. Selection rules require a permanent dipole moment and ΔJ = ±1 for absorption.

Applications

Measurements yield precise bond lengths and angles. Rotational spectroscopy is a major tool in radio astronomy for identifying molecules in interstellar space, and in atmospheric sensing. Techniques include Stark spectroscopy and Fourier-transform microwave spectroscopy.
