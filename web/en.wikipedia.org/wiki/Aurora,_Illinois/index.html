Aurora is a city in the Chicago metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Illinois. Located on the Fox River some 40 miles west of Chicago, it is the second-most populous city in Illinois, with around 180,000 residents at the 2020 census. Aurora is known as the "City of Lights" because in 1881 it became one of the first cities in the United States to implement an all-electric street lighting system.

History

The first settler, Joseph McCarty, arrived in 1834 and built a mill on the Fox River; the settlement grew on both banks, which developed as separate communities before uniting as one city in 1857. Aurora became an important manufacturing and railroad centre, home to workshops of the Chicago, Burlington and Quincy Railroad, and its factories produced heavy machinery, textiles and silverplate. The downtown Paramount Theatre, an Art Deco landmark opened in 1931, remains a major performing arts venue.

Geography and demographics

The city straddles the Fox River and extends into four counties: Kane, DuPage, Kendall and Will. Aurora is notably diverse, with a large Hispanic and Latino population and long-established African American and immigrant communities. The city forms the western anchor of the Illinois Technology and Research Corridor along Interstate 88.

Transportation

Aurora is the western terminus of Metra's BNSF Line, one of the busiest commuter rail lines in the Chicago region, running to Chicago Union Station. Interstate 88 passes through the city's north side, and the Aurora Transportation Center serves both Metra and Amtrak trains.
