Flying Finn
A nickname originally given to Finnish distance runners, later applied to Finnish racing drivers and other athletes.

"The Flying Finn" (Finnish: Lentävä suomalainen) is a nickname given to several Finnish athletes who were noted for their speed. Originally, it was given to the Finnish long distance runner Hannes Kolehmainen in the 1910s, but was later also used for Paavo Nurmi, other Finnish endurance athletes, and Finnish racing drivers, in which context it remains most famous today.

Origin: distance running

Hannes Kolehmainen was the first athlete dubbed "Flying Finn." At the 1912 Stockholm Olympics he won gold in the 5,000 m, 10,000 m, and individual cross country, setting world records, and in 1920 he won the Olympic marathon at Antwerp. His dominance introduced Finland to the international sporting world.

The title was later applied most prominently to Paavo Nurmi, Kolehmainen's successor, who won nine Olympic gold medals and set numerous world records during the 1920s, including five gold medals at the 1924 Paris Olympics. Other Finnish endurance athletes described as Flying Finns include Ville Ritola, who won five Olympic golds, and, in a later generation, Lasse Virén, winner of the 5,000 m and 10,000 m double at both the 1972 and 1976 Olympics.

Motorsport

From the 1960s onward the nickname transferred to Finnish rally and racing drivers, reflecting Finland's outsized success in international rallying. Drivers who have been called Flying Finns include:

  • Rauno Aaltonen, Timo Mäkinen, and Hannu Mikkola, pioneers of Finnish rally success
  • World Rally Champions Juha Kankkunen, Tommi Mäkinen, and Marcus Grönholm
  • Mika Häkkinen, two-time Formula One World Champion, for whom the nickname is especially common
  • Keke Rosberg, Finland's first Formula One World Champion (1982), and later drivers such as Kimi Räikkönen and Valtteri Bottas

The phrase even appeared in aviation: "Flying Finn" was used for Finnish motorcycle racer Jarno Saarinen, and for the record-breaking motorcyclist and other sportsmen, while in snowboarding and ski jumping the epithet has been applied to Finnish athletes as well. The nickname also inspired the name of Finland's Athletics Championships mascot and various popular uses in Finnish media.

Other uses

"Flying Finn" has also been used as the name of a low-cost airline (Flying Finn, 2003–2004), a Finnish train service brand, and an express passenger train that formerly operated between London and the continent. In the Second World War era the term was applied in the press to Finnish ski troops and pilots.
