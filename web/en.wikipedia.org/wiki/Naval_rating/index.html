Naval rating

Ratings of the Royal Navy

A naval rating is an enlisted member of a navy — a sailor who is not a commissioned officer. The term originates in the Royal Navy, where sailors were "rated" according to their skills and the berths they filled on a ship's muster book. In Commonwealth navies, personnel below officer rank are collectively called ratings; the United States Navy uses the term similarly for its enlisted occupational specialties.

United Kingdom

Royal Navy ratings progress through the rates of Ordinary Rate, Able Rate, Leading Rate, Petty Officer, Chief Petty Officer, and Warrant Officer. Ratings belong to branches such as warfare, engineering, logistics, medical and aviation, wearing branch badges alongside rate insignia. Historically, naval ratings signed on for a ship's commission and the crew was "paid off" at its end; the expression "the lower deck" refers collectively to the ratings.

United States

In the U.S. Navy, a "rating" is the occupational specialty of an enlisted sailor — such as Boatswain's Mate, Machinist's Mate, or Hospital Corpsman — combined with a pay grade (rate) from Seaman Recruit to Master Chief Petty Officer. Sailors are formally addressed by a combination of rating and rank, for example "Petty Officer First Class" plus their rating abbreviation.
