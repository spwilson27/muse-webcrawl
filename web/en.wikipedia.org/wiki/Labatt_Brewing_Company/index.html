The Labatt Brewing Company Limited (French: La Brasserie Labatt Limitée) is a Canadian brewery headquartered in Toronto, Ontario. Founded in London, Ontario, in 1847 by John Kinder Labatt, it is one of Canada's oldest and largest brewing companies. Since 1995 Labatt has been owned by the Belgian brewer Interbrew, which later merged into Anheuser-Busch InBev, the world's largest brewing group.

History

John Kinder Labatt, an Irish immigrant, purchased the Simcoe Street Brewery in London in 1847 in partnership with Samuel Eccles, buying out his partner in 1853. The company grew under his son John Labatt Jr. and grandson Hugh Labatt, surviving Prohibition by selling soft drinks and near beer while quietly supplying the export market. In the 20th century Labatt expanded across Canada through acquisitions, and its Labatt Blue, launched in 1951 and named for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers sponsorship, became one of Canada's best-selling beers.

Brands and operations

Labatt's principal brands include Labatt Blue, Labatt 50, Budweiser (brewed under licence in Canada), Alexander Keith's, and Kokanee. It operates breweries in several provinces, including London and Toronto in Ontario, Edmonton, and Creston. The company's original London brewery remains a landmark in that city.
