Popular piety

Popular piety (Latin: pietas popularis) refers to the religious devotions, practices and expressions of faith that arise among the people of a culture, distinct from — though ideally in harmony with — the official liturgy of the Church. The term is used especially within Roman Catholicism, where popular piety includes such practices as praying the rosary, processions, pilgrimages, novenas, veneration of relics and images, and devotion to Mary and the saints.

Characteristics

Popular piety is shaped by the culture, history and temperament of a people, and is often transmitted within families and local communities rather than through formal instruction. It tends to be more affective and expressive than official worship, engaging the senses through music, images, festivals and movement.

Church teaching

The Second Vatican Council's constitution on the liturgy, Sacrosanctum Concilium, encouraged popular devotions while insisting they should be ordered toward the liturgy and derive from it. Subsequent magisterial documents, notably the Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy (2001), set out principles for evaluating and renewing these practices. Pope Francis's apostolic exhortation Evangelii Gaudium praised popular piety as a genuine expression of the faith of the poor and as a force for evangelisation.

Examples

  • Processions during Holy Week
  • Pilgrimages to shrines such as Lourdes, Fátima and Guadalupe
  • The rosary and other Marian devotions
  • Feast-day celebrations of patron saints
  • Votive offerings and devotional candles
