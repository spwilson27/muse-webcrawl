Xinyang (Chinese: 信阳) is a prefecture-level city in southern Henan province, China, bordering Anhui and Hubei provinces. It is the southernmost city of Henan and sits astride the Huai River basin, giving it a climate and rice-growing culture that feel transitional between northern and southern China. The urban population is around one million, while the whole prefecture-level city had about 6.2 million residents at the 2020 census.

Tea

Xinyang is famous across China for Xinyang Maojian tea, a green tea ranked among China's ten most famous teas. Grown in the hills around the city, including near Mount Jigong and the Nanwan reservoir, the tea is noted for its fine, hairy buds and fresh, chestnut-like aroma. An annual tea festival is held in the spring.

Geography and transport

The city is framed by the Dabie Mountains to the south and the Tongbai Mountains to the west. Major transport links include the Beijing–Guangzhou and Nanjing–Xi'an railways, the Beijing–Hong Kong high-speed railway, and several national expressways, making Xinyang a regional hub between the Central Plains and the Yangtze region. Attractions include Mount Jigong (Jigongshan), one of China's historic summer resorts, Nanwan Lake, and the Tang dynasty Kaiyuan Temple.

History

The area has been inhabited since antiquity and lay near the ancient states of Chu and Shen; the name Xinyang dates to the Song dynasty. During the Second Sino-Japanese War the city saw fighting along the Dabie front in 1938.
