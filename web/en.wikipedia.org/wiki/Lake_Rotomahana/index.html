Lake Rotomahana

Lake Rotomahana is an 800-hectare lake in northern New Zealand, about 20 kilometres southeast of Rotorua. The present lake is much larger than the original lake of the same name and was formed by the 1886 eruption of Mount Tarawera.

Eruption of 1886

Before the eruption, the smaller Lake Rotomahana was famed for the Pink and White Terraces — silica sinter terraces widely regarded as a natural wonder of the world. The Tarawera eruption destroyed the terraces, excavated a great crater, and created the modern lake, which is the largest hot spring system in New Zealand, with steaming cliffs and the Frying Pan Lake nearby in the Waimangu Volcanic Rift Valley.
