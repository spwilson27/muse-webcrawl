England and Wales

England and Wales within the United Kingdom

England and Wales (Welsh: Cymru a Lloegr) is one of the three legal jurisdictions of the United Kingdom, covering the constituent countries of England and Wales. It forms a single legal jurisdiction because, under the Laws in Wales Acts of 1535 and 1542, Wales was fully incorporated into the Kingdom of England and its legal system; Scotland and Northern Ireland retain separate jurisdictions.

The legal jurisdiction

The courts of England and Wales — headed by the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom, with the Court of Appeal and High Court below it — administer a single common-law system. Bodies such as the Legal Aid Agency, HM Courts & Tribunals Service, the Charity Commission and the Land Registry operate for England and Wales jointly, as do statistics in many official series (for example crime and demography from the Office for National Statistics).

Devolution

Since 1999 Wales has had its own devolved legislature, the Senedd (Welsh Parliament), and government, which can pass primary legislation in devolved areas such as health and education. As a result, Welsh law and the law of England have begun to diverge, and there is an ongoing debate about whether Wales should become a separate legal jurisdiction — a change recommended by the Commission on Justice in Wales (the Thomas Commission) in 2019. England, by contrast, has no devolved parliament and is governed directly by the UK Parliament.

Geography and population

Together England and Wales cover about 151,000 square kilometres and contain roughly 59 million people — about 89 percent of the UK's population — including the capital cities London (of both England and the UK) and Cardiff (of Wales).
