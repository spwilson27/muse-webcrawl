The 1966 FIFA World Cup Final was played on 30 July 1966 at Wembley Stadium, London, between hosts England and West Germany. England won 4–2 after extra time, securing their first — and to date only — World Cup title.

Match summary

Helmut Haller put West Germany ahead before Geoff Hurst equalised and Martin Peters made it 2–1 to England. Wolfgang Weber's last-minute equaliser forced extra time, in which Hurst scored twice more to complete the first hat-trick in a World Cup final. His second goal, awarded after the ball struck the crossbar and bounced down near the line, remains one of football's most debated decisions.

Legacy

Alf Ramsey's "wingless wonders" were received by Queen Elizabeth II, who presented the Jules Rimet Trophy to captain Bobby Moore. The match is a cornerstone of English sporting culture.
