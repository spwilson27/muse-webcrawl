Amitabh Bachchan (born 11 October 1942) is an Indian actor who works in Hindi cinema. With a career spanning more than five decades and over 200 films, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential actors in the history of Indian cinema, and is often referred to as the Shahenshah of Bollywood and as "Big B".

Early life

Bachchan was born in Allahabad (now Prayagraj) to the Hindi poet Harivansh Rai Bachchan and social activist Teji Bachchan. He was educated at Sherwood College, Nainital, and Kirori Mal College, University of Delhi. He worked briefly as a freight broker in Calcutta before moving to Bombay to pursue acting.

Career

After a debut as a voice narrator in Bhuvan Shome (1969) and a series of minor roles, Bachchan achieved stardom with Zanjeer (1973), which established his "angry young man" persona. A string of landmark films followed, including Deewaar (1975), Sholay (1975) — one of the highest-grossing Indian films of all time — Amar Akbar Anthony (1977) and Don (1978). He was seriously injured during filming of Coolie in 1982, an event that drew national attention.

After a brief political career as a Member of Parliament (1984–1987) and a commercial setback with his production company ABCL in the 1990s, Bachchan revived his career in 2000 as host of the television quiz show Kaun Banega Crorepati and with acclaimed roles in Mohabbatein (2000), Black (2005), Paa (2009) and Piku (2015). He has won numerous National Film Awards and Filmfare Awards, and was named "Star of the Millennium" in a 1999 BBC online poll.
