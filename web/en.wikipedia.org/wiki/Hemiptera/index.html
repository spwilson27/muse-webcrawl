PhylumArthropoda
ClassInsecta
OrderHemiptera
Species50,000–80,000 described

Hemiptera is an order of insects, commonly called true bugs, comprising over 50,000 and possibly as many as 80,000 species, including the cicadas, leafhoppers, planthoppers, aphids, whiteflies, scale insects, shield bugs, assassin bugs, and bed bugs. The name derives from the Greek hemi (half) and pteron (wing), referring to the forewings of heteropterans, whose basal half is hardened and apical half membranous.

Characteristics

The defining feature of the order is the piercing-sucking mouthparts: the mandibles and maxillae form stylets enclosed in a segmented labium, the rostrum or "beak", used to pierce tissues and suck fluids — plant sap in most species, but blood or body fluids of prey in predatory and parasitic groups. Development is hemimetabolous, with nymphs resembling small, wingless adults.

Suborders

The order is conventionally divided into the suborders Heteroptera (the typical bugs, with hemelytra), Auchenorrhyncha (cicadas, leafhoppers, planthoppers), Sternorrhyncha (aphids, whiteflies, scale insects), and Coleorrhyncha (moss bugs, a small relict group).

Ecology and importance

Most hemipterans are herbivores, and many — notably aphids and whiteflies — are serious agricultural pests, damaging crops both by feeding and by transmitting plant viruses. Others, such as predatory assassin bugs and anthocorids, are valued in biological control. Blood-feeding species include the bed bugs and the triatomine "kissing bugs" that transmit Chagas disease. Some groups are economically valuable: cochineal and shellac are produced from scale insects.
