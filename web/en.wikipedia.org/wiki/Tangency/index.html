Tangency

FieldGeometry
Related conceptsTangent line, tangent space, osculating circle

In geometry, tangency is the property of two curves, or a curve and a surface, that meet at a single point while sharing a common direction there — informally, they "just touch" without crossing. The point at which they meet is called the point of tangency.

Circles and lines

A line is tangent to a circle if it intersects the circle at exactly one point; the radius drawn to that point is perpendicular to the line. Two circles are tangent to one another if they meet at exactly one point, being internally tangent when one lies inside the other and externally tangent otherwise.

General curves

For a smooth curve, the tangent line at a point is the limiting position of secant lines through nearby points, and its slope is given by the derivative. Tangency underlies the differential geometry of curves and surfaces, optimization problems involving tangent planes, and the economics concept of tangency between budget lines and indifference curves.
