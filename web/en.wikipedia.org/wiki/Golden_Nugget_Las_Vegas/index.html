Golden Nugget Las Vegas

The Golden Nugget Las Vegas is a luxury hotel and casino located in downtown Las Vegas, Nevada, on the Fremont Street Experience. It is owned and operated by Golden Nugget, Inc. and Landry's, Inc. It is the largest casino in the downtown area, with more than 2,400 rooms, and has received the AAA Four Diamond Award consecutively since 1977.

History

The Golden Nugget was built in 1946 by Guy McAfee and opened on August 30, 1946, making it one of the oldest casinos in Las Vegas. Casino developer Steve Wynn acquired a controlling interest in 1973, beginning his rise in the Las Vegas gaming industry, and built the hotel's first tower in 1977. Wynn's company sold the property in 2000; after a brief period under other ownership, it was acquired in 2005 by Landry's, Inc., controlled by Tilman Fertitta, which undertook major renovations, adding the Rush Tower in 2009.

Features

The property is known for the Hand of Faith, a 61-pound 11-ounce gold nugget — the largest gold nugget still in existence — discovered in Australia in 1980 and displayed at the casino. Its pool complex, The Tank, features a three-storey waterslide passing through a shark aquarium. The name "Golden Nugget" was later extended to sister properties in Laughlin, Atlantic City, Biloxi, and Lake Charles.
