Littlewood–Richardson rule

In mathematics, the Littlewood–Richardson rule is a combinatorial description of the coefficients that arise when the product of two Schur polynomials is expanded as a sum of Schur polynomials. These Littlewood–Richardson coefficients also count tensor product multiplicities of irreducible representations of the general linear group and appear in Schubert calculus as intersection numbers on Grassmannians.

Statement

The product of Schur polynomials sλ sμ equals the sum over partitions ν of cνλμ sν. The rule computes cνλμ as the number of Littlewood–Richardson tableaux: semistandard skew tableaux of shape ν/λ and weight μ whose reading word is a lattice (Yamanouchi) word — at every prefix, the number of i's read is at least the number of (i+1)'s.

History

The rule was stated by Dudley Littlewood and Archibald Richardson in 1934, but their proof contained gaps; the first complete proofs came in the 1970s through the work of Schützenberger and others using the combinatorics of Young tableaux and the Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence.

Applications

Beyond representation theory, the coefficients appear in algebraic geometry (Schubert calculus), algebraic combinatorics, and the Horn problem on eigenvalues of sums of Hermitian matrices, where they connect to honeycombs and puzzles.
