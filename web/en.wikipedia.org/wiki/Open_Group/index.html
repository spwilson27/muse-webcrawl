The Open Group is a global consortium that seeks to "enable the achievement of business objectives" by developing open, vendor-neutral technology standards and certifications. It was formed in 1996 by the merger of X/Open and the Open Software Foundation (OSF), two consortia that had been rivals during the "Unix wars" of the late 1980s and early 1990s.

Standards and certifications

The Open Group owns the UNIX trademark and publishes the Single UNIX Specification, against which operating systems — including macOS and various commercial Unix systems — are certified. It is also responsible for TOGAF, a widely used enterprise architecture framework; ArchiMate, an enterprise architecture modelling language; and the FACE (Future Airborne Capability Environment) and SOSA technical standards used in defence systems.

Organisation

The consortium's members include technology vendors, large enterprise customers, government agencies, and universities. Its work is organised into forums (such as the Architecture Forum and the Open Platform 3.0 Forum) and work groups that develop standards through a consensus process, complemented by professional certification programmes for architects and other specialists.

History

X/Open, founded in 1984 by European Unix vendors, worked to consolidate Unix specifications, while the US-based OSF, founded in 1988, developed technologies including the Motif toolkit and the DCE distributed computing environment. Their 1996 merger unified stewardship of the Unix specification under The Open Group.
