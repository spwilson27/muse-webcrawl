Einstein@Home
LaunchedFebruary 19, 2005
PlatformBOINC volunteer computing
FieldsGravitational waves, pulsars, gamma-ray astronomy
Lead institutionsUniversity of Wisconsin–Milwaukee, Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics

Einstein@Home is a volunteer computing project that searches for signals from spinning neutron stars using data from LIGO gravitational-wave detectors, the Arecibo and Parkes radio telescopes, and the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope. It runs on the BOINC platform and was launched during the World Year of Physics 2005.

History

Directed by Bruce Allen, the project harnesses idle computing power from hundreds of thousands of volunteers' computers and phones, achieving computing power comparable to major supercomputers.

Discoveries

Einstein@Home volunteers are credited with discovering dozens of new pulsars, including radio pulsars in Arecibo data and gamma-ray pulsars in Fermi data — some found in binary systems of interest for testing general relativity.

Science

Continuous gravitational waves from deformed neutron stars are extremely weak, requiring coherent searches over long data spans. The project distributes data segments and collects candidate results for follow-up on clusters.
