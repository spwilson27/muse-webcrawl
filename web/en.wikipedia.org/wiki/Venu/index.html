A bamboo venu

The venu (Sanskrit: वेणु, veṇu) is an ancient transverse flute made of bamboo, originating in the Indian subcontinent. It is one of the oldest musical instruments of India, mentioned in Vedic literature, and is iconically associated with the Hindu god Krishna, who is almost always depicted playing it. In South Indian Carnatic music the venu (also called kuzhal or pullanguzhal) is a key melodic instrument; its Hindustani counterpart is the bansuri.

Construction

The venu is fashioned from a single piece of bamboo with one embouchure hole and finger holes — traditionally eight in the Carnatic form refined by Sharaba Shastri and popularised in the twentieth century, and six-plus-one in the Hindustani bansuri. The player's breath and cross-fingering produce the microtonal ornamentation (gamaka) essential to raga music. Venu are made in different lengths and pitches to suit the performer's chosen tonic.

Mythology and symbolism

In the Bhagavata Purana, Krishna's flute music enchants the gopis of Vrindavan, the peacocks, and all of nature; the sound of the venu symbolises the divine call to the soul. Flute-playing Krishna (Venugopala) is a major theme of Indian painting and sculpture.

Notable performers

Leading exponents have included T. R. Mahalingam ("Mali"), who revolutionised Carnatic flute playing, N. Ramani, and Shashank Subramanyam; in the Hindustani tradition Hariprasad Chaurasia brought the bansuri to concert stages worldwide.
