Chengdu is the capital of Sichuan province in southwestern China and one of the most populous cities in the country. Founded over two thousand years ago, it has been a major political, economic, and cultural centre of the Chengdu Plain, a fertile agricultural region watered by the Min River. Chengdu is internationally famous as the home of the giant panda and for its fiery Sichuan cuisine.

History

The region around Chengdu was inhabited by the ancient Shu kingdom, whose remarkable bronze artefacts are preserved at the Sanxingdui and Jinsha museums. The city was founded around the 4th century BC and became the capital of the Shu Han kingdom during the Three Kingdoms period. The Dujiangyan irrigation system, built around 256 BC by Li Bing, still waters the plain today and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Chengdu was long a centre of brocade weaving — its name is associated with the famous Shu brocade — and of tea culture and printing; paper currency (jiaozi) was first issued here in the Song dynasty.

Geography and climate

Chengdu sits on the western edge of the Sichuan Basin, backed by the Longmen and Qionglai mountains. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild, foggy winters; the basin's persistent cloud cover gives the city relatively few sunny days. The Min River and its branches flow through the metropolitan area.

Economy

Modern Chengdu is a hub for electronics, information technology, pharmaceuticals, and finance in western China. Major technology firms operate research and manufacturing centres there, and the city is a leading centre for the video game and software industries. It is also a major transport node, with two international airports and rail links including the Chengdu–Europe freight railway.

Culture

Chengdu is celebrated for a relaxed lifestyle centred on tea houses, mahjong, and its spicy hot pot. The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding attracts millions of visitors. Landmarks include Wuhou Shrine, Du Fu's Thatched Cottage, the Wide and Narrow Alleys (Kuanzhai Xiangzi), and Jinli Street. Sichuan opera, with its face-changing (bian lian) art, is performed in local theatres.
