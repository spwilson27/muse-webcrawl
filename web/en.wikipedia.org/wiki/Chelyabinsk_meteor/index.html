Chelyabinsk meteor

The Chelyabinsk meteor was a superbolide that entered Earth's atmosphere over the southern Ural region of Russia on 15 February 2013 at about 09:20 local time. Travelling at roughly 18–19 km/s, the roughly 20-metre, 12,000-tonne asteroid exploded in an air burst at an altitude of about 23–30 km above Chelyabinsk, releasing energy estimated at around 400–500 kilotons of TNT.

Effects

The shock wave from the explosion arrived at ground level a minute or two after the flash, blowing out windows across Chelyabinsk and neighbouring towns. About 1,500 people sought medical treatment, mainly for cuts from flying glass and concussions; remarkably, no deaths were reported. The event was extensively recorded by dashboard cameras and security cameras, making it the best-documented meteor air burst in history.

The meteorite

Fragments of the ordinary chondrite (LL5) meteorite fell over a wide area. In October 2013 divers recovered a roughly 540 kg fragment from Lake Chebarkul, where it had punched through the ice. The parent asteroid had not been detected before impact, and the event occurred only hours before the unrelated close approach of the asteroid 367943 Duende.
