The Kremasta Dam is a large earth-fill embankment dam on the Achelous River in western Greece, near the borders of the regional units of Aetolia-Acarnania and Evrytania. Completed in 1965, it created Lake Kremasta, the largest artificial lake in Greece, and its hydroelectric power station, with an installed capacity of about 437 megawatts, was for many years the largest in the country.

Construction and the 1966 earthquake

Construction by the Public Power Corporation began in 1961. Impoundment of the reservoir was associated with increased local seismicity: on 5 February 1966, shortly after first filling, a magnitude 6.2 earthquake struck the area near the dam, damaging hundreds of houses in nearby villages — a widely cited case of reservoir-induced seismicity. The dam itself survived undamaged.

Power station and reservoir

The power station at the dam's toe houses four Francis turbine-generator units. Lake Kremasta covers about 80 km² and stores several billion cubic metres of water fed by the Achelous and its tributaries (Agrafiotis, Tavropos and Trikeriotis). The lake supports irrigation, water supply and a growing local tourism economy of lakeside villages.
