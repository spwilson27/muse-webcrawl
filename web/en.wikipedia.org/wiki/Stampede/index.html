Stampede

A stampede is a situation in which a group of large animals — most famously cattle — suddenly start running in the same direction, especially because they are excited or frightened. The word comes from Mexican Spanish estampida ("stampede, uproar"), from Spanish estampar, "to stamp". The term is also applied to sudden rushes of people, with dangerous and sometimes fatal consequences when crowds panic or are crushed.

Cattle stampedes

On the cattle trails of the nineteenth-century American West, stampedes were among the most feared dangers of the drive. Herds bedded down for the night could be startled by a thunderstorm, a predator, or an unexpected noise, and thousands of longhorn cattle might bolt in darkness, trampling anything in their path. Cowboys on night guard sang quietly to calm the herd, and if a stampede began, riders attempted to ride alongside the leaders and turn them into a circle — "milling" — until the animals tired and stopped. The folklore of the stampede entered Western literature and film, and Charles Goodnight and other trail bosses left detailed accounts of the phenomenon.

Human stampedes and crowd crushes

By extension, "stampede" is used for sudden collective rushes by crowds, as at religious festivals, sporting events, concerts and sales. Modern research into crowd disasters notes that most such tragedies are more precisely crowd crushes: when density exceeds about six or seven people per square metre, pressure waves can propagate through the crowd, and victims die of compressive asphyxiation while still standing, without any "stampeding" behaviour at all. The term "stampede" is therefore criticised by disaster scientists and by victims' families, since it can wrongly imply irrational or selfish behaviour by the crowd rather than failures of planning, design and crowd management. Notable deadly incidents include the Hillsborough disaster (1989, later ruled a crush caused by failures of policing), the Love Parade disaster in Duisburg (2010), the Itaewon crowd crush in Seoul (2022) and repeated incidents during the Hajj pilgrimage in Mecca, including in 2015.

In popular culture

The annual Calgary Stampede in Alberta, Canada — one of the world's largest rodeos, founded in 1912 — takes its name from the phenomenon. Stampedes also feature in film, notably the wildebeest stampede in Disney's The Lion King (1994).
