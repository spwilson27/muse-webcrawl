Kenneth Baker, Baron Baker of Dorking

Kenneth Wilfred Baker, Baron Baker of Dorking, CH, PC (born 3 November 1934) is a British politician and a former cabinet minister of the Conservative Party. He served in the governments of Margaret Thatcher and John Major, holding several senior posts including Secretary of State for Education and Home Secretary.

Early life and career

Baker was educated at Hampton Grammar School, St Paul's School, London, and Magdalen College, Oxford, where he read history and law. He worked in publishing and at Shell before entering politics, and was also a London borough councillor. He was elected to Parliament in a 1968 by-election for Acton, later representing St Marylebone (1970–1983) and Mole Valley (1983–1997).

Cabinet career

Baker served as Minister for Information Technology in the early 1980s, promoting the microcomputer in British schools. He became Secretary of State for the Environment (1985–1986) and then Secretary of State for Education and Science (1986–1989), where he introduced the National Curriculum and grant-maintained schools through the Education Reform Act 1988 — reforms sometimes known as the "Baker Act". He served as Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster and Chairman of the Conservative Party (1989–1990), and as Home Secretary (1990–1992) under John Major, overseeing among other matters the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 and prison reforms following the Strangeways riot.

Later life

Baker retired from the House of Commons in 1997 and was raised to the peerage as Baron Baker of Dorking. He has written extensively, including memoirs, political history, and poetry anthologies, and has campaigned for technical and vocational education, founding the Baker Dearing Educational Trust to promote university technical colleges.
