Wallpaper (computing)

A wallpaper or background (also known as a desktop background, desktop picture or desktop image on computers) is a digital image (photo, drawing etc.) used as a decorative background of a graphical user interface on the screen of a computer, smartphone or other electronic device.

History

The practice of using a picture behind the windows of a graphical desktop began with early window systems in the 1970s and 1980s, such as the Xerox Office System and later the X Window System, which allowed users to set patterned or solid root-window backgrounds. Microsoft Windows included standard wallpapers from version 3.0 (1990) onward; the famous "Bliss" photograph of a green hill in Sonoma County, California, shipped with Windows XP (2001) and is often described as the most viewed photograph in history.

Formats and use

Wallpapers are commonly supplied in common resolutions matching display sizes, and modern operating systems can crop, stretch, tile, or centre an image to fit screens of any aspect ratio. Many users download wallpapers from websites or use personal photographs, and mobile devices commonly allow separate images for home and lock screens. Animated and live wallpapers, which use video or interactive graphics, are supported on some platforms at the cost of additional battery and processing use.
