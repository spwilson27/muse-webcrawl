Anwar Nasser Abdulla al-Awlaki (22 April 1971 – 30 September 2011) was a Yemeni-American Islamic scholar and lecturer who became a leading propagandist and operational figure of al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP). His English-language sermons, circulated widely online, were linked by investigators to a number of terrorist plots and attackers in the West. He was killed in a US drone strike in Yemen in 2011, in a decision that provoked significant debate because he was a United States citizen who was killed without trial.

Life

Al-Awlaki was born in New Mexico to Yemeni parents, spent his early childhood in Yemen, and returned to the United States for university studies in civil engineering and education. He served as an imam in Denver, San Diego, and at the Dar Al-Hijrah mosque in Falls Church, Virginia, where two of the future 9/11 hijackers worshipped, though investigators later disputed the extent of his involvement with them at the time. After leaving the US in 2002 he spent time in the United Kingdom and then settled in Yemen in 2004, where he was imprisoned in 2006–2007 and afterwards moved fully into al-Qaeda militancy.

Al-Qaeda role and death

US officials described him as a regional commander of AQAP involved in directing plots, including the 2009 attempt by Umar Farouk Abdulmutallab to bomb a US airliner, and he was in contact with Nidal Hasan before the Fort Hood shooting. In 2010 President Barack Obama authorised his targeted killing. On 30 September 2011 a US drone strike killed al-Awlaki and the AQAP propagandist Samir Khan in Yemen's Al Jawf governorate; his teenage son Abdulrahman was killed in a separate strike two weeks later. The killings raised enduring legal controversy over due process and targeted killings of US citizens.
