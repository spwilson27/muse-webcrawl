Maurrassisme
Ideology of Charles Maurras and Action Française
Key concepts: Integral nationalism, monarchism, "Politique d'abord"

Maurrassisme is the political doctrine developed by the French writer and political theorist Charles Maurras (1868–1952) and embodied by the movement and newspaper Action Française. Rooted in the counter-revolutionary and anti-Dreyfusard currents of the Third Republic, it combined integral nationalism with monarchism: Maurras argued that France's decline stemmed from the Revolution and its democratic, individualist legacy, and that a hereditary monarchy would restore order, authority and national unity.

Principles

The doctrine's rallying formula "Politique d'abord" ("politics first") subordinated religion and morality to political necessity; Maurras, himself an agnostic, valued Catholicism chiefly as a pillar of social order — a position that led to the Vatican's condemnation of Action Française in 1926. Maurrassisme was anti-democratic, anti-liberal, anti-communist and marked by antisemitism directed against what Maurras termed the "Anti-France."

Influence

The doctrine influenced far-right movements across Europe and Latin America, including currents in Franco's Spain and Salazar's Portugal, and some Maurrassians collaborated with the Vichy regime; Maurras himself was sentenced to life imprisonment in 1945 for collaboration. Its intellectual legacy continues in French nationalist thought.
