Bandleader

A bandleader is the leader of a band of musicians — the person who organizes, directs, and serves as the public identity of the ensemble. In jazz and popular music especially, the bandleader traditionally hires the players, chooses and commissions the arrangements, sets the tempo and cues the sections in performance, negotiates engagements and recording contracts, and lends the band its name.

Role

The bandleader may be a featured soloist (as trumpeters Louis Armstrong and Dizzy Gillespie were), a conductor standing in front of the group, or a musician leading from within — pianists Duke Ellington and Count Basie both directed their orchestras from the keyboard with minimal gestures. Some bandleaders were primarily arrangers or businessmen whose musical contribution was organizational; others, such as Benny Goodman, the "King of Swing," were star instrumentalists whose name sold the band.

History

The bandleader emerged as a central figure in the dance-band era of the 1920s–1940s, when names like Paul Whiteman, Glenn Miller, Tommy Dorsey, and Artie Shaw topped record charts and radio broadcasts. As the big-band business contracted after the Second World War, smaller combos and, later, rock groups distributed leadership differently, but the figure persisted in salsa (Tito Puente), Latin jazz, television orchestras (Doc Severinsen, Paul Shaffer), and contemporary jazz ensembles. In modern usage the term may apply to any musician who fronts and administers an ensemble, including leaders of brass bands, wedding bands, and studio groups.
