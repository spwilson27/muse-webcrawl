Relative risk

Relative risk
SynonymRisk ratio
FieldStatistics, epidemiology

The relative risk (RR) or risk ratio is the ratio of the probability of an outcome in an exposed group to the probability of an outcome in an unexposed group. Together with risk difference and odds ratio, relative risk measures the association between the exposure and the outcome.

If the probability of an event in the exposed group is pE and in the unexposed group pU, then the relative risk is RR = pE / pU. An RR of 1 means the exposure is not associated with the outcome; an RR above 1 indicates increased risk in the exposed group, and below 1 indicates a protective effect.

Use and interpretation

Relative risk is used in the statistical analysis of data from experimental, cohort and cross-sectional studies. It is more intuitive than the odds ratio, with which it is often confused; the two coincide approximately when the outcome is rare. A common criticism is that relative risk, reported alone, can exaggerate the practical importance of an effect when the absolute risk is small, so it should be reported alongside absolute measures such as the risk difference or number needed to treat.

Statistical inference

The sampling distribution of the log relative risk is approximately normal, allowing confidence intervals to be computed from the counts in a 2×2 contingency table.
