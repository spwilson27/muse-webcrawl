Law Society of England and Wales

The Law Society of England and Wales (officially The Law Society) is the professional association that represents and governs solicitors in England and Wales. It provides services and support to practising and training solicitors, and serves as a sounding board for law reform. Its headquarters, the Law Society's Hall, is at 113 Chancery Lane, London.

History

The Society was founded in 1825 as the Society of Gentlemen Practisers, and received its first royal charter in 1831; a new charter in 1903 changed its name to "The Law Society". It long acted as both the representative and regulatory body for solicitors, but following the Legal Services Act 2007, its regulatory functions were transferred to the independent Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), leaving the Law Society as the profession's representative body.

Functions

The Law Society campaigns on issues affecting the profession and access to justice, publishes practice guidance, operates accreditation schemes, and supports members through services such as its library and professional publications. It is governed by a Council elected by solicitors, and led by a President who serves a one-year term.
