Snape Maltings

Snape Maltings is an arts complex on the banks of the River Alde at Snape, Suffolk, England. Built in converted Victorian maltings, it is best known as the home of the Aldeburgh Festival and its concert hall, one of the principal classical music venues in the United Kingdom.

History

The maltings were built in the mid-19th century for malting barley, much of it shipped from the quay at Snape to London and Europe. Malting ceased in the 1960s. The composer Benjamin Britten and the tenor Peter Pears, who had founded the Aldeburgh Festival in 1948, acquired the site, and the largest malthouse was converted into a concert hall designed for the festival.

Snape Maltings Concert Hall was opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 2 June 1967, at the start of the 20th Aldeburgh Festival. The hall burned down on the opening night of the 1969 festival but was rebuilt in time for the following year's festival.

Today

The site is now managed by Britten Pears Arts and hosts year-round concerts, the Aldeburgh Festival each June, residencies for young musicians, shops, galleries and restaurants. The concert hall seats about 830 and is praised for its acoustics. Nearby, the Hoffmann Building and other converted structures house rehearsal and performance spaces.
