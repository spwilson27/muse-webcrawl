Block-matching algorithm

A block-matching algorithm is a method for estimating motion between two images or video frames by dividing the current frame into blocks (typically macroblocks of 16×16 pixels) and finding, for each block, the best-matching block of the same size in a reference frame within a limited search area. The displacement between the positions of the two blocks is recorded as a motion vector. Block matching is a core technique in motion estimation and is used for temporal compression in video coding standards such as MPEG-1, MPEG-2, H.264/AVC and HEVC, as well as in image registration and stereo vision.

Matching criteria

The quality of a candidate match is measured by a distortion criterion, commonly the sum of absolute differences (SAD) or mean squared error (MSE) between the pixel values of the current block and the candidate block. SAD is popular in hardware because it requires no multiplications.

Search strategies

An exhaustive ("full") search evaluates every position in the search window and guarantees the best match under the chosen criterion, but is computationally expensive. Many faster strategies sacrifice optimality for speed: the three-step search, four-step search, diamond search and hexagon-based search examine a pattern of candidate points, progressively refining around the best match found so far. Sub-pixel motion estimation refines the vector to half-pixel or quarter-pixel accuracy by interpolation.

Applications

Beyond video compression, block matching is used in frame-rate conversion, video stabilisation, medical image registration and in the block-matching and 3D filtering (BM3D) algorithm for image denoising.
