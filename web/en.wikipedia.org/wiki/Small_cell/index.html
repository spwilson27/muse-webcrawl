Small cell

Small cells are low-powered cellular radio base stations that operate in licensed and unlicensed spectrum and have a range of from about ten metres to a few kilometres. They include femtocells, picocells, microcells and metrocells, in increasing order of size, and are used to add capacity and coverage — especially indoors and in dense urban areas — to mobile networks.

Small cells became central to 4G LTE densification and are a key building block of 5G networks, particularly on millimetre-wave frequencies whose signals travel only short distances. They connect to the operator's core network via broadband backhaul, which may be wired or wireless.

Types

Femtocells serve homes and small offices; picocells serve larger buildings; microcells serve city blocks; and metrocells serve busy urban outdoor areas.
