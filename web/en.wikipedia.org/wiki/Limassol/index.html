Limassol (Greek: Λεμεσός, Lemesós) is a city on the southern coast of Cyprus and the capital of the district of the same name. It is the island's second-largest urban area and its principal seaport, a major centre for tourism, shipping and offshore business.

History

The city developed between the ancient Greek city-kingdoms of Amathus to the east and Kourion to the west, whose archaeological sites remain major attractions. In 1191 Richard the Lionheart married Berengaria of Navarre in Limassol and was crowned King of England there, after seizing Cyprus during the Third Crusade. The medieval Limassol Castle, rebuilt under Ottoman rule, stands near the old harbour.

Economy

Limassol's port is the busiest in Cyprus and a significant transshipment and passenger hub in the eastern Mediterranean. The city is the heart of the Cypriot wine industry — the great wineries of the island are based around it — and hosts the annual Limassol Wine Festival each autumn, as well as one of the Mediterranean's best-known carnival celebrations.

Modern development

A long seafront promenade (the Molos), a modern marina opened in 2014, and a growing skyline of residential towers have reshaped the coastline. Inland, the Troodos foothills and the wine villages of the Commandaria region rise above the city.
