Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport


Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (IATA: PHX) is a civil–military public airport in Phoenix, Arizona, one of the largest commercial airports in the United States.

Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (IATA: PHX, ICAO: KPHX) is the principal airport serving Phoenix, Arizona. Located about 3 miles (5 km) southeast of downtown Phoenix, it is among the busiest airports in the United States, handling over 48 million passengers in a record year (2023).

History

The airfield's origins date to the late 1920s; the City of Phoenix purchased the airport in 1935 for $100,000. Its nickname "Sky Harbor" dates to the airport's early years. It grew steadily after the Second World War, with Terminal 2 opening in 1962, Terminal 3 in 1979, and the large Terminal 4 — named for Senator Barry M. Goldwater — in 1990. The 200-foot air traffic control tower opened in 2007, and the PHX Sky Train automated people mover began service in 2013.

Operations

Sky Harbor has three parallel runways and two passenger terminals in use (Terminals 3 and 4; Terminal 2 closed in 2020). It is a hub for American Airlines, which inherited the operation from US Airways and America West Airlines, and a major base for Southwest Airlines; it also hosts Frontier Airlines and others. The airport offers nonstop service to more than 140 destinations, including international flights to Mexico, Canada and London Heathrow. The Arizona Air National Guard's 161st Air Refueling Wing is based on the airport's south side.
