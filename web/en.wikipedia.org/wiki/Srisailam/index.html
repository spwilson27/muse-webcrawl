Srisailam

Srisailam is a census town in Nandyal district of the Indian state of Andhra Pradesh, set in the Nallamala Hills on the banks of the Krishna River. It is best known as the site of the Mallikarjuna Temple, one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines of Shiva, and as the location of the Srisailam Dam, one of India's largest hydroelectric projects.

Mallikarjuna Temple

The temple, dedicated to Shiva as Mallikarjuna and to the goddess as Bhramaramba, is both one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the eighteen Shakti Peethas — a rare combination. The complex, much of it built under the Vijayanagara empire and the Reddy kings, is surrounded by fort-like walls with richly carved gopurams. The site is associated with the saint-poet Akka Mahadevi and was visited by Adi Shankaracharya, who is said to have composed the Sivananda Lahari here.

Dam and surroundings

The Srisailam Dam, completed in 1981, spans the Krishna River in a deep gorge and holds one of the largest reservoirs in India, serving irrigation and power generation for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. The surrounding forests form part of the Nagarjunsagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve, one of the largest tiger reserves in India, home to tigers, leopards and the endangered fauna of the Eastern Ghats.
