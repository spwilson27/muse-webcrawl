Warren Edward Buffett (born August 30, 1930) is an American investor and philanthropist who is the chairman and chief executive officer of Berkshire Hathaway. Widely regarded as one of the greatest investors of all time, he is known as the "Oracle of Omaha" for his Omaha roots and his record of long-term value investing. As of the mid-2020s he is consistently ranked among the wealthiest people in the world, with a net worth well in excess of one hundred billion dollars, the great majority of which he has pledged to give away.

Early life and education

Buffett was born in Omaha, Nebraska, the son of Leila (née Stahl) and Howard Buffett, a stockbroker who later served four terms in the U.S. House of Representatives. He showed an early aptitude for business: he sold chewing gum, Coca-Cola bottles and weekly magazines door to door, bought his first shares at age eleven, and by his high school years owned a small pinball-machine business and a 40-acre farm worked by a tenant farmer. After graduating from the University of Nebraska in 1950, Buffett studied at Columbia Business School under Benjamin Graham, the father of value investing, earning a master's degree in economics in 1951. He later worked for Graham's partnership, Graham-Newman Corp., in New York.

Career

Returning to Omaha, Buffett formed Buffett Partnership Ltd. in 1956. Over the following decade the partnership substantially outperformed the market. In 1962 he began buying stock in Berkshire Hathaway, a struggling New England textile manufacturer, took control in 1965, and eventually closed the partnership to run Berkshire as his main investment vehicle. Together with his long-time partner Charlie Munger (1924–2023), Buffett transformed Berkshire into a sprawling conglomerate holding wholly owned businesses such as GEICO, BNSF Railway, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, Duracell and See's Candies, alongside large stock positions in companies including Apple, Bank of America, American Express and Coca-Cola. Under Buffett, Berkshire's per-share market value grew at a compounded annual rate of roughly 20 percent over nearly six decades.

In May 2025, at Berkshire's annual meeting, Buffett announced that he would step down as chief executive at the end of 2025, with Greg Abel succeeding him, while Buffett remained chairman.

Investment philosophy

Buffett advocates buying shares in "wonderful companies at fair prices" rather than fair companies at wonderful prices — a refinement, credited to Munger, of Graham's original "cigar butt" approach. He emphasises businesses with durable competitive advantages ("economic moats"), honest and able management, and sensible capital allocation, and he holds investments for long periods, famously remarking that his favourite holding period is "forever". His annual letters to shareholders and the question-and-answer sessions at Berkshire's annual meetings in Omaha are widely read by investors.

Personal life and philanthropy

Buffett is known for a notably frugal lifestyle relative to his wealth: he has lived in the same Omaha house since purchasing it in 1958. In 2006 he announced that he would gradually give away the bulk of his fortune to philanthropic foundations, principally through annual gifts to the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation and foundations run by his children. In 2010, with Bill and Melinda French Gates, he launched The Giving Pledge, encouraging other billionaires to commit at least half their wealth to charitable causes.
