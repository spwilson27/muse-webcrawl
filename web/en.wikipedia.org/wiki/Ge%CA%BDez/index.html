Geʻez (ግእዝ) is an ancient South Semitic language that originated in the region of modern-day Eritrea and northern Ethiopia. It became the official language of the Kingdom of Aksum and the Ethiopian imperial court, and, although it ceased to be spoken as a vernacular by around the 10th–12th centuries, it remains the liturgical language of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church, Ethiopian Catholic Church and the Beta Israel Jewish community.

History

The earliest inscriptions in Geʻez date from the pre-Aksumite period in the first millennium BC, written in the South Arabian musnad script; the language later adopted its own abugida, in which each consonant is modified to indicate the following vowel. Geʻez literature flourished from the 13th to 17th centuries, including royal chronicles, hagiographies and the epic Kebra Nagast.

Modern descendants

Geʻez is the ancestor of the modern Ethiosemitic vernaculars Tigrinya and Tigre, and its script is still used to write Amharic and other Ethiopian languages.
