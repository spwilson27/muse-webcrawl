Parian marble

SourceIsland of Paros, Cyclades, Greece
QuarriesMarathi valley, Mount Marpissa
ColourPure white, translucent, fine-grained
Used forClassical Greek sculpture; the Venus de Milo, Nike of Samothrace, Hermes of Praxiteles
Ancient nameLychnites (said to be quarried by lamplight)

Parian marble is a fine-grained, semi-translucent pure-white marble quarried on the Greek island of Paros in the Cyclades during classical antiquity. It was among the most prized sculptural stones of the ancient world: its fine, even grain, lack of veining, and slight translucency — allowing light to penetrate a few millimetres and giving carved flesh a soft, lifelike glow — made it the preferred medium for masterworks of Greek sculpture.

Parian marble's translucency gives sculpted surfaces a distinctive luminous quality.

Quarries and history

The principal quarries lay in the Marathi valley of Paros, on the slopes of Mount Marpissa, where underground galleries were worked from the Archaic period (6th century BC) onward. The Roman author Pliny the Elder records that the stone was called lychnites because it was quarried in underground tunnels by the light of lamps (lychnoi). Paros's wealth in the Archaic and Classical periods rested substantially on the marble trade, and Parian sculptors and workshops were renowned throughout the Greek world. Quarrying continued, on a reduced scale, into the Roman period and intermittently thereafter.

Famous works

  • The Venus de Milo (Aphrodite of Milos), Louvre
  • The Nike of Samothrace (the figure; the ship's prow is of Lartian marble), Louvre
  • The Hermes and the Infant Dionysus attributed to Praxiteles, Olympia
  • The Peplos Kore and other Archaic korai of the Athenian Acropolis
  • The Medici Venus

Identification

Because many Greek marbles are visually similar, provenance studies use isotopic analysis (stable carbon and oxygen isotopes) and other petrographic methods to distinguish Parian marble from Pentelic, Thasian, Naxian, and Carrara marbles — a matter of practical importance in authentication and in the study of ancient trade.
