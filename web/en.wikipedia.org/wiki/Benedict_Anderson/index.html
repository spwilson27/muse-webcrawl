Benedict Anderson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Benedict Richard O'Gorman Anderson (26 August 1936 – 13 December 2015) was an Irish political scientist and historian of nationalism, best known for his book Imagined Communities: Reflections on the Origin and Spread of Nationalism (1983), one of the most influential works in the study of nations and nationalism.

Anderson defined the nation as “an imagined political community — and imagined as both inherently limited and sovereign”: imagined because members never meet most of their compatriots yet carry an image of communion; limited because every nation has boundaries beyond which lie other nations; sovereign because nations aspire to freedom under their own state. He explained nationalism's rise through “print-capitalism” — the convergence of printing, markets and vernacular languages — which allowed strangers to imagine themselves moving through the same homogeneous, empty time, an effect deepened by the census, the map and the museum in colonial states.

Life and work

Born in Kunming, China, to an Irish father in the Chinese Maritime Customs and raised in Ireland and California, Anderson was educated at Cambridge and Cornell, where he became a leading Southeast Asia specialist. His analysis of the 1965 events in Indonesia (the “Cornell Paper”) led to his expulsion by the Suharto regime. He spent his later career at Cornell and died in Batu, Indonesia, in 2015. Imagined Communities appeared in a revised edition in 1991 and has been translated worldwide.
