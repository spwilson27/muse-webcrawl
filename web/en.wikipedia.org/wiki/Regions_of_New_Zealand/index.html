Regions of New Zealand

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

New Zealand is divided into sixteen regions for the purposes of local government. Eleven are administered by regional councils, and five are administered by unitary authorities, which combine the functions of regional councils and territorial authorities. The Chatham Islands Territory is not part of any region, although its council has some functions of a regional council under the Resource Management Act 1991. The regions were established in their modern form in 1989, when around 850 local bodies were consolidated in a major reform of local government.

Regional councils are principally responsible for environmental resource management, including water quality, biosecurity, regional transport planning, and civil defence.

History

New Zealand's provinces, established in the 1850s, were abolished in 1876 and replaced by a system of counties and boroughs. The 1989 reforms created the modern regional councils, largely on the basis of water catchment boundaries, alongside 74 territorial authorities. Auckland Council was formed in 2010 by amalgamating the Auckland Regional Council with seven territorial authorities, creating a "super city".
