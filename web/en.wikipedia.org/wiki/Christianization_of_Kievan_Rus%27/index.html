Christianization of Kievan Rus'

The Christianization of Kievan Rus' refers to the conversion of the East Slavic state of Kievan Rus' to Orthodox Christianity, traditionally dated to 988, when Grand Prince Vladimir of Kiev was baptised and ordered the mass baptism of his people in the Dnieper River.

Background

Christianity had reached the Rus' earlier: Princess Olga, Vladimir's grandmother, was baptised in Constantinople in the 950s, and Varangian and merchant communities included Christians. According to the Primary Chronicle, Vladimir investigated Islam, Judaism, Latin Christianity and Byzantine Christianity before choosing the Byzantine rite.

The baptism of 988

Vladimir's conversion was bound up with his marriage to Anna, sister of the Byzantine emperors Basil II and Constantine VIII, and his capture of the Byzantine city of Chersonesus. The baptism of Kiev's population in 988 is considered the symbolic founding moment of the Russian and Ukrainian Orthodox churches.

Aftermath

Christianization proceeded gradually, especially in the northern lands, where pagan resistance persisted into the twelfth century, but it integrated Rus' into the Byzantine cultural sphere and spurred church building, literacy and art.
