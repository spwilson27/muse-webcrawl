Heroic legend

Heroic legend
GenreLegendary narrative; heroic poetry
Typical subjectsHeroes, kings, battles, feuds, quests, downfall of peoples
ExamplesNibelungenlied, Völsunga saga, Beowulf, heroic lays of the Poetic Edda

Heroic legend is a body of legendary narrative — in verse or prose — centred on heroes of a distant, usually migration-age or early-medieval past. Heroic legends differ from both history and pure myth: their heroes are presented as human kings and warriors, often with names that correspond to known historical figures, but their deeds are embellished, recombined and reshaped by oral tradition over centuries. The term is used especially of the Germanic and Old Norse traditions, though analogous bodies of heroic narrative exist in Greek, Irish, South Slavic, Persian and many other literatures.

Characteristics

Heroic legend typically grows around a historical kernel — a battle, the fall of a kingdom, a famous king such as Theodoric the Great or Attila — around which stories accumulate across generations and peoples, frequently contradicting chronology so that figures separated by a century meet as contemporaries. The stories are usually tragic in shape: they dwell on loyalty and betrayal, gift-giving and vengeance, kin-slaying and the destruction of whole kindreds, expressed in the conventions of heroic poetry — feasting in the hall, boasting, single combat and defiant last stands.

Germanic and Norse tradition

The richest corpus concerns the Gothic and Burgundian worlds and the fall of the Burgundians (437) under the Huns. Its central figure is Sigurd (Siegfried), slayer of the dragon Fafnir, whose story is told in the heroic lays of the Poetic Edda, the Völsunga saga, and — transposed to a courtly Christian setting — the Middle High German Nibelungenlied. Theodoric the Great appears as Dietrich von Bern in a large cycle of German epics, and the English poem Beowulf embeds episodes of the same legendary history. Scholars such as Hermann Schneider and later Jan de Vries systematised the study of this material as Heldensage, "heroic legend".

Other traditions

Comparable corpora include the Greek heroic myths set in the age of the Trojan War, the Irish Ulster Cycle around Cú Chulainn, the Serbian epic songs of the Battle of Kosovo, and the Persian Shahnameh's accounts of Rostam — all blending remembered persons and events with supernatural and formulaic elements.
