Anisotropic etching

FieldMicrofabrication, semiconductor processing
Common etchantsKOH, TMAH, EDP
ContrastIsotropic etching

Anisotropic etching is a microfabrication process in which a material is etched at different rates in different crystallographic directions, producing structures with characteristic sloped sidewalls. It is widely used in the manufacture of MEMS devices and silicon microstructures.

Silicon wet etching

In single-crystal silicon, alkaline etchants such as potassium hydroxide (KOH) and tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAH) etch the {100} and {110} planes much faster than the close-packed {111} planes. On a standard (100) wafer this produces V-shaped grooves and pyramidal pits bounded by {111} planes meeting the surface at an angle of about 54.74°.

Applications

Anisotropic etching is used to form pressure-sensor diaphragms, ink-jet nozzles, accelerometers, and microfluidic channels. Dry processes such as deep reactive-ion etching offer an alternative when vertical walls independent of crystal orientation are required.
