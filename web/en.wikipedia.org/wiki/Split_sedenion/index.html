Split-sedenion

In abstract algebra, the split-sedenions form a 16-dimensional noncommutative, nonassociative algebra over the real numbers, obtained by applying the Cayley–Dickson construction to the split-octonions. As with the ordinary sedenions, they extend the sequence real numbers → complex numbers → quaternions → octonions into a "split" branch in which the quadratic form is indefinite.

Construction

The Cayley–Dickson construction builds an algebra of twice the dimension from an algebra with conjugation. Applying it with a sign choice that yields an indefinite norm gives the split-complex numbers, split-quaternions, split-octonions, and finally the split-sedenions. The split-sedenions have basis elements e0 = 1 together with fifteen imaginary units, some squaring to +1 and others to −1, reflecting the indefinite signature of their norm.

Properties

Like the sedenions, the split-sedenions are neither commutative nor associative, and they are not alternative. They possess zero divisors — nonzero elements whose product is zero — so they do not form a division algebra. Their automorphism group is related to that of the split-octonions, the exceptional Lie group G2 in its split form, extended by additional structure. The split algebras in the Cayley–Dickson sequence are studied in connection with indefinite orthogonal groups, spinors, and certain constructions in mathematical physics.
