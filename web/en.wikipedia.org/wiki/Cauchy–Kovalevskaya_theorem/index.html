Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem

Sofia Kovalevskaya (1850–1891)

In mathematics, the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem (also written Cauchy–Kowalevski theorem) is the main local existence and uniqueness theorem for analytic partial differential equations associated with Cauchy initial value problems. A special case was proved by Augustin-Louis Cauchy in 1842, and the full result by Sofia Kovalevskaya in 1875.

Statement

Roughly stated: consider a system of partial differential equations that can be solved for the highest time-derivative of each unknown function, with coefficients and initial data that are real-analytic near the initial surface. Then there exists a unique real-analytic solution in a neighbourhood of the initial surface. The model example is the heat equation with analytic initial data, which locally has an analytic solution; by contrast, for initial data that is merely smooth, the problem may have no solution at all in the classical sense.

Significance and limitations

The theorem guarantees a local analytic solution but says nothing about global existence, and its analyticity hypotheses are essential. Hans Lewy's famous 1957 example showed that there exist smooth (non-analytic) linear partial differential equations with smooth right-hand sides that admit no solutions whatsoever, even locally, underscoring that the analytic setting of Cauchy–Kovalevskaya cannot simply be relaxed. The theorem remains foundational in the analytic theory of PDEs; Holmgren's uniqueness theorem extends the uniqueness part to broader classes of solutions.
