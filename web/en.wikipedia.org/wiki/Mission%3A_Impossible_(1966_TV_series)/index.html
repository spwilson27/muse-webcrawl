Mission: Impossible is an American espionage television series created by Bruce Geller and broadcast on CBS from 1966 to 1973. It follows the Impossible Missions Force (IMF), a small team of secret agents who carry out covert operations against dictators, crime syndicates, and hostile powers, using elaborate deception, disguises, and technology rather than force.

Format

Episodes famously open with the team leader receiving instructions on a self-destructing tape recording — "Your mission, should you choose to accept it..." — followed by the selection of agents from a dossier portfolio. The mission then unfolds as an intricate confidence trick, with the audience watching the plan succeed (or nearly fail) step by step. Steven Hill played team leader Dan Briggs in the first season; Peter Graves, as Jim Phelps, led the team for the rest of the run.

Music and legacy

Lalo Schifrin's theme, written in 5/4 time, became one of the most recognisable in television. The series won multiple Emmy Awards, including for stars Martin Landau and Barbara Bain. A revival series aired in 1988–1990, and the concept was adapted into the long-running Mission: Impossible film series beginning in 1996.
