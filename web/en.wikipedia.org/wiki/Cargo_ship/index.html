Cargo ship

A cargo ship or freighter is a merchant vessel that carries cargo, goods and materials from one port to another. Thousands of cargo carriers ply the world's seas and oceans each year, handling the bulk of international trade.

Types

Cargo ships are categorised by the type of cargo they carry. Container ships carry standardised intermodal containers; bulk carriers transport unpackaged goods such as grain, coal, and ore; tankers carry liquids including crude oil and liquefied natural gas; roll-on/roll-off ships carry wheeled vehicles; and general cargo ships carry break-bulk goods.

History

The earliest cargo vessels were sailing ships such as the Roman grain freighters that fed the empire and the clipper ships of the 19th century. Steam propulsion, steel hulls, and the standardised shipping container introduced by Malcom McLean in 1956 transformed the industry, dramatically lowering freight costs and enabling modern global supply chains.

Size

Modern cargo ships range from small coastal freighters to ultra-large container vessels over 400 metres long carrying more than 20,000 TEU. Size is usually measured in deadweight tonnage or, for container ships, twenty-foot equivalent units.
