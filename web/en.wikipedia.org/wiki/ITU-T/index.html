The ITU Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) is one of the three sectors of the International Telecommunication Union, a specialised agency of the United Nations. It coordinates standards for telecommunications and information and communication technologies, publishing them as ITU-T Recommendations.

The ITU headquarters in Geneva, home of the Telecommunication Standardization Bureau.

History

ITU-T's work descends from the International Telegraph and Telephone Consultative Committee (CCITT), created in 1956, and ultimately from the ITU itself, founded in 1865. In the 1993 restructuring of the ITU, CCITT became ITU-T. Its study groups bring together member states, private-sector members, and academia to draft standards by consensus.

Notable standards

  • G series — transmission systems, including G.711 audio coding, DSL (G.992), and passive optical networks (G.984 GPON, G.9807 XGS-PON)
  • H series — audiovisual systems: H.264/MPEG-4 AVC, H.265 HEVC, H.323
  • V series — data communication over the telephone network, including the classic modem standards (V.34, V.90)
  • X series — data networks and security, including X.509 public-key certificates and X.25
  • E series — telephone numbering, including E.164 international numbers
  • Y series — global information infrastructure and next-generation networks

Organisation

Technical work is done in numbered study groups overseen by the World Telecommunication Standardization Assembly (WTSA), held every four years. The Telecommunication Standardization Bureau (TSB) in Geneva provides the secretariat. Since 2007 most ITU-T Recommendations have been freely available online.
