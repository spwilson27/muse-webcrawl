United States Secretary of Energy

The United States Secretary of Energy is the head of the United States Department of Energy, a member of the Cabinet of the United States, and fifteenth in the presidential line of succession. The position was created on October 1, 1977, when President Jimmy Carter signed the Department of Energy Organization Act, amid the energy crises of the 1970s.

Responsibilities

The secretary oversees a department whose responsibilities include the nation's nuclear weapons program, nuclear reactor production for the United States Navy, energy conservation, energy-related research, development of renewable energy, radioactive waste disposal, and domestic energy production. The department operates a system of seventeen national laboratories, including Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, and Oak Ridge national laboratories, and manages the Strategic Petroleum Reserve.

Notable secretaries

  • James R. Schlesinger (1977–79) — first secretary, formerly Secretary of Defense.
  • Hazel O'Leary (1993–97) — first woman and first African American to hold the post.
  • Bill Richardson (1998–2001) — former governor of New Mexico and ambassador to the United Nations.
  • Steven Chu (2009–13) — Nobel laureate in physics.
  • Ernest Moniz (2013–17) — nuclear physicist who helped negotiate the Iran nuclear agreement's scientific framework.
  • Rick Perry (2017–19) — former governor of Texas.
  • Jennifer Granholm (2021–25) — former governor of Michigan.

Line of succession

Within the Department of Energy, the deputy secretary is first in line to act as secretary, followed by the under secretaries. The secretary is a statutory member of the National Security Council's orbit on energy matters and plays a key role in the "nuclear football" chain of custody for nuclear command decisions.
