Berry Gordy Jr. (born 28 November 1929) is an American retired record executive, record producer, songwriter and film producer, best known as the founder of the Motown record label and its subsidiaries, which became the highest-earning African-American business for several decades.

Motown

A former boxer and autoworker, Gordy began as a songwriter (co-writing hits for Jackie Wilson) before borrowing $800 from his family to found Tamla Records in 1959, soon Motown, based at "Hitsville U.S.A." in Detroit. Applying assembly-line principles to artist development, he built a roster including the Supremes, the Temptations, Marvin Gaye, Stevie Wonder, the Jackson 5 and Smokey Robinson and the Miracles, and a house band, the Funk Brothers.

Later career

Gordy moved Motown to Los Angeles in 1972, produced films including Lady Sings the Blues, and sold Motown Records to MCA in 1988. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame and received the National Medal of Arts.
