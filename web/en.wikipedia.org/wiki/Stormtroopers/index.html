Stormtroopers

Stormtroopers (German: Sturmtruppen or Stoßtruppen) were specialised assault infantry developed by the Imperial German Army in the latter part of the First World War to break the deadlock of trench warfare.

Beginning with experimental units such as Captain Willy Rohr's assault battalion in 1915, stormtroopers were organised in small, self-contained squads trained to infiltrate enemy lines at weak points, using cover, camouflage and combined arms — machine guns, trench mortars, flamethrowers, grenades and later the MP 18 submachine gun — instead of advancing in rigid waves after long artillery bombardments. Their infiltration tactics were employed decisively in the March 1918 Operation Michael offensive, which achieved the deepest advances on the Western Front since 1914 but could not be sustained logistically.

Stormtrooper methods influenced interwar infantry doctrine in many armies and formed part of the tactical background to later German mobile warfare. The term was subsequently adopted by the Nazi Party's Sturmabteilung.
