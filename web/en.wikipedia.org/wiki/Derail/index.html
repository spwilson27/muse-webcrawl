Derail

A derail or derailer is a device used to prevent fouling (blocking or compromising) of a rail track by unauthorised movements of trains or unattended rolling stock. It works by derailing any vehicle that passes over it, causing it to drop off the track in a controlled location rather than continue into a protected area.

Design and use

The most common form is a wedge-shaped cast steel block that fits over the head of one rail. When a wheel encounters it, the wheel is guided up and off the rail to the outside of the track. Derails are installed on sidings, industrial spurs, and yard tracks near their junction with main lines, and on drawbridge approaches. They may be manually operated, linked to a signal system, or powered and interlocked so that a route cannot be cleared until the derail is removed.

Regulation

Operating rules in many countries require derails to be applied whenever rolling stock is left standing on a siding that could roll toward a main track. In the United States, the Federal Railroad Administration regulates their use; portable derails are also carried for temporary protection of work zones. A derail is considered a fail-safe device: a runaway car stopped by derailment is far less dangerous than one that rolls onto a main line into the path of a train.

Related devices

Related protective devices include catch points and trap points, used especially in the United Kingdom, which employ a short set of points to divert runaway vehicles away from running lines, and wheel stops and buffer stops at the ends of tracks.
