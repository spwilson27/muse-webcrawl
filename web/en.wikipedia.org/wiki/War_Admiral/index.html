War Admiral

War Admiral (May 2, 1934 – October 30, 1959) was a champion American Thoroughbred racehorse who is the fourth winner of the American Triple Crown. He was also the 1937 Horse of the Year and is best remembered for his famous match race against Seabiscuit in 1938, the "Match Race of the Century," which Seabiscuit won. War Admiral was sired by the great Man o' War; his owner, Samuel D. Riddle, also owned Man o' War.

Racing career

War Admiral won three of four starts as a two-year-old in 1936. In 1937 he was undefeated in eight starts, sweeping the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes and Belmont Stakes to become the fourth horse to win the American Triple Crown. In the Belmont he stumbled at the start, injuring his foot, yet still won in a time that equalled the track record. He was unanimously voted American Horse of the Year.

As a four-year-old, War Admiral won most of his races but was beaten by Seabiscuit in their celebrated match race at Pimlico Race Course on November 1, 1938, before a crowd of some 40,000 and a huge radio audience. He made one start as a five-year-old before injury ended his career, finishing with 21 wins from 26 starts and earnings of $273,240.

Stud record and legacy

At stud, War Admiral was the leading sire in North America in 1945 and the leading broodmare sire in 1962 and 1964. His offspring included the champion filly Busher. He died in 1959 and was buried at Faraway Farm in Kentucky near Man o' War; both were later reinterred at the Kentucky Horse Park. War Admiral was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1958 and ranked number 13 in The Blood-Horse magazine's list of the top 100 American racehorses of the 20th century. He features prominently in Laura Hillenbrand's book Seabiscuit: An American Legend and its 2003 film adaptation.
