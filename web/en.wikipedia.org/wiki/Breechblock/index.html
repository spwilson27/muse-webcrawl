Breechblock

A breechblock (or breech block) is the part of a breech-loading firearm that blocks the rear opening of the chamber while the propellant burns, ensuring the expanding gases propel the projectile forward rather than escaping rearward. It must withstand enormous pressure and is a key component in the action of rifles, pistols, and artillery.

Types

Designs include the rotating bolt (used in most modern bolt-action and many semi-automatic rifles), the tilting block, the falling or sliding block, the rolling block, and the interrupted-screw breech used in large artillery pieces. In many modern self-loading firearms the equivalent component is called the bolt.

Function

The breechblock typically houses the firing pin and extractor, locks securely to the barrel extension or receiver during firing, and is withdrawn to eject the spent case and chamber a fresh round.
