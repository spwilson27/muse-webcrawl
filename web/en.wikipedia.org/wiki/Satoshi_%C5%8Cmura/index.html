Satoshi Ōmura (大村 智, born 12 July 1935) is a Japanese biochemist, known for the discovery and development of various pharmaceuticals originally occurring in microorganisms. In 2015 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with William C. Campbell and Tu Youyou for discoveries concerning a novel therapy against infections caused by roundworm parasites.

Career

Ōmura earned degrees in pharmaceutical science at the University of Tokyo and in chemistry at Tokyo University of Science. At the Kitasato Institute he developed systematic methods for culturing and screening microorganisms for bioactive compounds, building a large library of natural products.

Avermectin and ivermectin

In the 1970s Ōmura isolated a strain of the soil bacterium Streptomyces avermitilis near a golf course in Shizuoka Prefecture. Working with Merck, the strain yielded the avermectins, a class of compounds with potent antiparasitic activity. The derivative ivermectin became a hugely successful treatment for onchocerciasis (river blindness) and lymphatic filariasis in humans, and for parasites in livestock, dramatically reducing the burden of these diseases worldwide.

Other work

Ōmura's group discovered more than 500 new bioactive compounds, many of which became drugs or research tools. He has received numerous honours, including the Gairdner Global Health Award, and has served as director of the Kitasato Institute.
