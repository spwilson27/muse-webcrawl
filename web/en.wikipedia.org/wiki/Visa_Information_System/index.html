Visa Information System

Fingerprint enrolment for the VIS at a Schengen consulate

The Visa Information System (VIS) is a large-scale information technology system of the European Union that stores data on applications for short-stay (Schengen) visas. It allows Schengen states to exchange visa data, verify the identity of visa holders at external borders, and identify persons who may not fulfil, or may no longer fulfil, the conditions for entry or stay.

History and rollout

The legal basis for the VIS is Regulation (EC) No 767/2008, and the system began operations on 11 October 2011 in the first region of deployment, North Africa. Deployment proceeded region by region through consulates worldwide and was completed on 20 November 2015; rollout at the external border crossing points followed. The system is operated by eu-LISA, the EU agency for the operational management of large-scale IT systems. A reform adopted in 2021 (Regulation (EU) 2021/1134) extended the VIS to long-stay visas and residence permits, lowered the fingerprinting age for children from twelve to six, and added interoperability with other EU systems such as the Entry/Exit System and ETIAS.

Data stored

For each applicant the VIS records alphanumeric application data, a facial photograph, and the fingerprints of all ten fingers, together with decisions taken on the application (issue, refusal, annulment, revocation or extension) and links between applications of family members and travel groups. Biographical and biometric data are generally retained for five years. Border guards can search the system by the visa sticker number and verify it against the holder's fingerprints, which is intended to combat visa fraud and "visa shopping" — lodging multiple applications in different member states after a refusal.

Access and privacy

Access is granted to visa, border, immigration and asylum authorities of the Schengen states; under strict conditions, law-enforcement authorities and Europol may consult the VIS for the prevention, detection and investigation of terrorist offences and other serious crime. Data protection supervision is shared between the European Data Protection Supervisor and national supervisory authorities, and data subjects have rights of access and correction.
