Eminönü is a district of the Fatih municipality in Istanbul, Turkey, occupying the historic commercial heart of the old city where the Golden Horn meets the Bosphorus and the Sea of Marmara. Until 2008 it was a separate district; it was then merged into Fatih. The name derives from the Ottoman emin (customs official) and önü ("in front of"), recalling the customs houses of the port.

Landmarks

Eminönü is dominated by the New Mosque (Yeni Cami, completed 1665) and the adjacent Egyptian or Spice Bazaar (Mısır Çarşısı). The Galata Bridge crosses the Golden Horn from Eminönü to Karaköy, and the district's piers serve ferries to Üsküdar, Kadıköy and the Bosphorus villages. Sirkeci station, the historic terminus of the Orient Express, and the Topkapı Palace and Hagia Sophia on the Seraglio point lie within or beside the quarter.

Character

By day Eminönü is one of Istanbul's busiest commercial districts, crowded with shoppers, street vendors selling grilled fish sandwiches (balık ekmek) from boats moored at the quay, and passengers thronging the ferries; its resident population is small and the streets largely empty at night.
