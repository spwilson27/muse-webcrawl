Basic fighter maneuvers

Fundamental maneuvers used in air combat

Basic fighter maneuvers (BFM) are tactical movements performed by fighter aircraft during air combat maneuvering, commonly called dogfighting, to gain a positional advantage over an opponent. BFM combines the fundamentals of aerodynamics and energy management with the geometry of pursuit.

Principles

A fighter pilot manages two forms of energy — airspeed (kinetic) and altitude (potential) — trading one for the other as the fight demands. Three pursuit curves describe the attacker's path relative to the defender: lead pursuit, pure pursuit, and lag pursuit. Turning performance is characterized by turn rate and turn radius, and a pilot seeks a position in the defender's rear hemisphere within weapons parameters.

Offensive maneuvers

  • High Yo-Yo — the attacker climbs and rolls to reduce overshoot and preserve energy while staying in phase with a turning defender.
  • Low Yo-Yo — the attacker descends briefly to gain speed and cut across the defender's turn circle.
  • Lag displacement roll — a rolling maneuver used to reduce angle-off while maintaining position behind the defender.
  • Lead turn — turning early toward where the opponent will be, to gain angles before the merge.

Defensive maneuvers

A defender under attack typically turns into the attack to maximize angle-off and closure problems, employing break turns, jinks, and the scissors — a series of turn reversals intended to force the attacker to overshoot. The last-ditch defense combines a hard turn with a reduction in the attacker's tracking ability.

Neutral maneuvers

When neither aircraft holds an advantage, pilots maneuver for position after the initial merge, using one-circle and two-circle fight geometries depending on each aircraft's turn radius and rate performance.
