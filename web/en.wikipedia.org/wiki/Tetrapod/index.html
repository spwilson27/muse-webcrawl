Tetrapod

[Image: Tiktaalik, a transitional form between fish and tetrapods]

Overview

Tetrapods are four-limbed vertebrate animals constituting the superclass Tetrapoda — amphibians, reptiles (including birds) and mammals. The group evolved from lobe-finned fish (Sarcopterygii) in the Devonian period, about 370 million years ago, through transitional forms such as Tiktaalik (a "fishapod" with limb-like fins) and the first digit-bearing tetrapods Acanthostega and Ichthyostega.

Evolution and diversity

Although "tetrapod" means four-footed, snakes, caecilians and whales descend from four-limbed ancestors and remain tetrapods by descent; the earliest tetrapods were aquatic, and terrestrialisation (with the amniotic egg for amniotes) followed. Living tetrapods number over 30,000 species. The limb's basic plan — one bone, two bones, many bones, digits — is a textbook case of homologous structures and evo-devo (Hox gene patterning). Fossil trackways in Poland (Zachełmie, ~395 Ma) complicate the timeline, suggesting tetrapod-grade animals earlier than body fossils record.
