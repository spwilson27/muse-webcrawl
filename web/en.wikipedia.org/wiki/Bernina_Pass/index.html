Bernina Pass

Elevation2,330 m (7,644 ft)
Traversed byRoad and Bernina railway line
LocationGraubünden, Switzerland
RangeBernina Range, Alps

The Bernina Pass (Italian: Passo del Bernina) is a high mountain pass in the Bernina Range of the Alps, in the canton of Graubünden (Grisons) in eastern Switzerland. At an elevation of 2,330 metres above sea level, it connects the Engadin valley in the north with the Italian-speaking Val Poschiavo in the south, continuing onward to Tirano in Italy.

Geography

The pass road passes the Lago Bianco ("White Lake"), a reservoir whose waters drain both north toward the Inn and Danube and south toward the Po. The Bernina railway line, part of the Rhaetian Railway and a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Albula/Bernina lines, crosses near the pass and reaches its summit at Ospizio Bernina station, 2,253 metres above sea level, the highest rail crossing in Europe open year-round.

History

The route over the Bernina has been used since the Middle Ages as a trade route between Switzerland and Italy. The modern road was completed in the 1860s, and the Bernina railway opened in 1910. The pass is a popular scenic drive and a gateway to the Piz Bernina massif, the highest summit in the Eastern Alps.
