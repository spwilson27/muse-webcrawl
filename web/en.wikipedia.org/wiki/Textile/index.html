Textile

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Weaving is one of the principal methods of textile production.

A textile is a flexible material made by creating an interlocking bundle of yarns or threads, which are produced by spinning raw fibres from either natural or synthetic sources into long and twisted lengths. Textiles are then formed by weaving, knitting, crocheting, knotting, tatting, felting, bonding, or braiding these yarns together.

Fibres

Textile fibres may be natural or synthetic. Natural plant fibres include cotton, flax (linen), hemp, and jute; animal fibres include wool and silk. Synthetic fibres, developed largely in the 20th century, include nylon, polyester, acrylic, and spandex, all derived principally from petroleum.

History

Textiles are among the oldest human technologies: fragments of woven flax from the Neolithic period survive, and the production of silk in China and cotton in the Indus Valley dates back thousands of years. Textile manufacture was the leading industry of the Industrial Revolution, driven by inventions such as the spinning jenny, the power loom, and the cotton gin, which shifted production from cottages to factories and mills.

Uses

Textiles are used for clothing, household furnishings such as carpets, bedding and towels, and an expanding range of technical applications — including medical implants, geotextiles, protective clothing, and composite reinforcement — collectively known as technical textiles.

Environmental impact

The textile and fashion industries are major sources of water use, chemical pollution, microplastic shedding from synthetic fabrics, and landfill waste, issues that have driven interest in recycling and sustainable fibres.
