War of the Second Coalition

The War of the Second Coalition (1798–1802) was the second war fought between Revolutionary France and a coalition of European powers, principally Great Britain, Austria and Russia, joined at various times by the Ottoman Empire, Portugal, Naples and others. It formed part of the French Revolutionary Wars.

Course of the war

The coalition was assembled after France's expansion into Italy, Switzerland and the Netherlands and Napoleon's expedition to Egypt in 1798. Initial coalition successes in 1799—including Suvorov's campaign in Italy, the Anglo-Russian invasion of Holland, and British naval victories—reversed many French gains.

The turning point came after Napoleon returned from Egypt and seized power in the coup of 18 Brumaire. His victory at Marengo (June 1800) and Moreau's at Hohenlinden (December 1800) forced Austria to make peace by the Treaty of Lunéville (1801). Russia had already withdrawn under Tsar Paul I. Britain, left alone, signed the Treaty of Amiens in 1802, briefly ending hostilities.

Consequences

The war confirmed French dominance on the continent west of the Rhine, dissolved the coalition, and set the stage for the Napoleonic Wars when fighting resumed in 1803.
