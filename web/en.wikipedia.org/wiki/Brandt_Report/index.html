Brandt Report

Brandt Report
Full title: North-South: A Programme for Survival
Published: 1980
Commission chair: Willy Brandt
The Brandt Line, separating North from South at about 30°N

The Brandt Report is the report of the Independent Commission on International Development Issues, chaired by the former West German chancellor Willy Brandt and published in 1980 under the title North-South: A Programme for Survival. It examined the widening gap between the wealthy industrialised countries and the developing world and proposed urgent reforms in trade, aid, food and energy.

The Brandt Line

The report popularised the division of the world into a rich North and a poor South, illustrated by the "Brandt Line", a boundary drawn around the mid-latitudes (roughly 30°N) that loops south to include Australia and New Zealand in the North and excludes them from the poorer bloc. The terms Global North and Global South descend from this usage.

Recommendations and legacy

The commission called for large-scale transfers of resources to developing countries, stabilisation of commodity prices, an emergency programme against hunger, reform of the International Monetary Fund and World Bank, and progress toward the target of official development assistance of 0.7% of gross national product. A follow-up report, Common Crisis, appeared in 1983. Although few of its proposals were enacted amid the economic downturn and changing politics of the early 1980s, the report remains a landmark of development thinking and led to the North-South Summit at Cancún in 1981.
