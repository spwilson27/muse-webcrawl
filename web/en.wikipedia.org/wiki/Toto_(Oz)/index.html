Toto is a fictional dog created by L. Frank Baum, the beloved pet of Dorothy Gale in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900) and its sequels. Baum described Toto as "a little black dog, with long silky hair and small black eyes that twinkled merrily on either side of his funny, wee nose." Toto accompanies Dorothy on the cyclone to the Land of Oz and remains at her side through nearly all of her adventures.

Role in the stories

Though he cannot speak in the first book, Toto plays a pivotal role in the plot: it is Toto who knocks over the screen hiding the Wizard, revealing him as an ordinary man. In later Oz books, as Dorothy settles in Oz, Toto — like other animals brought there — gains the power of speech, though he often chooses not to use it much. Baum's later books give him a larger role, and in Tik-Tok of Oz and other sequels his loyalty and instincts repeatedly help the travellers. The illustrator W. W. Denslow drew Toto as a Boston terrier, while John R. Neill later depicted him differently.

In the 1939 film

In MGM's The Wizard of Oz (1939), Toto was played by a female Cairn terrier named Terry (later renamed Toto), trained by Carl Spitz. Terry performed many of her own stunts and was paid more per week than many of the film's human supporting players. In the film it is Toto who escapes from the Wicked Witch's castle and leads the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Cowardly Lion to Dorothy's rescue, and Toto who pulls back the curtain on the Wizard. Toto remains one of the most famous animal characters in cinema; Terry's memoir and descendants are still celebrated by fans, and a memorial to her stands at Hollywood Forever Cemetery.
