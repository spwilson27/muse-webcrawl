Montgomery County, locally known as Montco, is a county in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. It is the third-most populous county in Pennsylvania and part of the Philadelphia–Camden–Wilmington metropolitan area, bordering the city of Philadelphia to the northwest.

History

The county was created on September 10, 1784, out of land originally part of Philadelphia County. It is generally believed to have been named for Richard Montgomery, an American Revolutionary War general killed at the Battle of Quebec in 1775. Valley Forge National Historical Park, in the county's southeastern corner, preserves the site of the Continental Army's winter encampment of 1777–78.

Geography and economy

The Schuylkill River forms much of the county's southwestern border. Montgomery County is one of the wealthiest counties in Pennsylvania, with major employment centres including the pharmaceutical and technology corridor around King of Prussia, home to one of the largest shopping malls in the United States.

Communities

Principal communities include Norristown, Pottstown, Lansdale, Lower Merion, Abington, and Upper Dublin.
