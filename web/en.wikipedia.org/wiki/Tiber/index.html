The Tiber (Italian: Tevere; Latin: Tiberis) is the third-longest river in Italy, 406 kilometres long, rising in the Apennine mountains of Emilia-Romagna and flowing generally south through Tuscany, Umbria and Lazio to the Tyrrhenian Sea. It is famous above all as the river of Rome: the city was founded on its eastern banks, and Roman history, myth and identity are woven around it — the infant Romulus and Remus were said to have been set adrift on its waters, and the river god Tiberinus was venerated as a protector of the city.

Course

The Tiber rises on Mount Fumaiolo, at about 1,268 metres, and passes the towns of Pieve Santo Stefano, Città di Castello and Umbertide before flowing south past Perugia's territory and Todi. In Lazio it is joined by the Nera near Orte and by the Aniene just north of Rome. Within Rome the river makes a broad bend past the Vatican, the historic centre and Trastevere, wrapping around Tiber Island. Below the city it reaches the sea in two branches — the navigable Fiumara Grande at Ostia and the Fiumicino canal — enclosing the Isola Sacra.

History

In antiquity the Tiber was Rome's commercial artery: barges carried grain, wine, oil and building stone up from the port of Ostia to wharves such as the Emporium below the Aventine, whose discarded amphorae accumulated into the artificial hill of Monte Testaccio. The river was also a recurring danger. Floods inundated the low-lying Campus Martius and Forum Boarium throughout antiquity, the Middle Ages and the early modern period; after the catastrophic flood of Christmas 1870, the young Italian state walled the urban Tiber between high travertine embankments (the muraglioni), completed over the following decades, which ended the great inundations while hiding the river from the city's streets.

Bridges

Rome's bridges span two millennia: the Pons Fabricius (62 BC) still carries pedestrians to Tiber Island and is the city's oldest bridge in original use; the Pons Aemilius survives as the broken Ponte Rotto; the Ponte Sant'Angelo, built under Hadrian, is lined with Bernini's angels; and modern crossings include the Ponte Sisto and Ponte Umberto I. South of Rome the river is also dammed for power and crossed by motorway bridges.
