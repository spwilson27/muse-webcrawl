Rebel Without a Cause is a 1955 American coming-of-age melodrama directed by Nicholas Ray, starring James Dean, Natalie Wood, and Sal Mineo. The film portrays emotionally confused suburban teenagers and the generational conflict between them and their parents, becoming a landmark depiction of American youth alienation and a defining part of Dean's legend: he died in a car crash a month before its release. In 1990 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.

James Dean's second starring role made him the emblem of teenage rebellion.

Plot

Jim Stark (Dean), arrested for drunkenness in a new town, meets Judy (Wood), ignored by her father, and Plato (Mineo), a lonely boy who idolises Jim. Bullied by a gang led by Buzz Gunderson, Jim is drawn into a "chickie run" — a stolen-car race toward a cliff edge — in which Buzz dies. Hiding with Judy and Plato in an abandoned mansion, Jim forms an improvised family with them, but the night ends in tragedy when police shoot Plato, who has obtained a gun. In the aftermath, Jim's parents begin to understand their son.

Production

The title derives from psychiatrist Robert M. Lindner's 1944 book Rebel Without a Cause: The Hypnoanalysis of a Criminal Psychopath, though the film's story, by Ray and Stewart Stern, is unrelated in detail. Warner Bros. shot the film in CinemaScope and colour — a mid-production switch from black and white — at locations including the Griffith Observatory, whose planetarium sequence is one of its most famous scenes. The knife fight at the observatory and the chickie run remain iconic set pieces.

Legacy

Released weeks after Dean's death at 24, the film cemented his image as the symbol of restless youth; Wood and Mineo also died young, adding to its mythology. It received three Academy Award nominations, including for Wood and Mineo, and its influence runs through decades of teen cinema.
