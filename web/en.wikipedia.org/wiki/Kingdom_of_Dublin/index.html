Kingdom of Dublin

Years: 853 – 1170

Capital: Dublin

First king: Olaf the White (Amlaíb Conung)

Last king: Ascall mac Ragnaill

The Kingdom of Dublin (Old Norse: Dyflin) was a Norse kingdom in Ireland that lasted from about 853 to 1170, ruled by the Norse-Gaelic (Hiberno-Norse) dynasty of the Uí Ímair. It was the first and longest-lasting Norse kingdom in Ireland, founded out of the Viking longphort established at Dublin in 841.

History

In 853 Olaf the White (Amlaíb Conung) arrived and made himself king of the Dublin Vikings; his brother Ivar the Boneless (Ímar) founded a dynasty that would also produce kings of York and the Isles. Dublin grew into a major slave-trading and commercial port linking Ireland to Scandinavia, Britain and Iceland. The Norse were expelled by the Irish in 902 but returned in 917 under Sitric Cáech, refounding the town. Dublin's kings fought at the Battle of Clontarf (1014) on the losing side against Brian Boru, yet the kingdom survived as a wealthy city-state, increasingly Gaelicised; its king Sigtrygg Silkbeard founded Christ Church Cathedral and issued Ireland's first coinage around 995.

End

In 1170 the exiled Irish king Diarmait Mac Murchada returned with Norman allies under Richard de Clare ("Strongbow") and captured Dublin; its last king, Ascall mac Ragnaill, fled and was killed attempting a counter-attack in 1171. The kingdom was absorbed into the Norman Lordship of Ireland.
