Amerigo Vespucci is a tall ship of the Italian Navy (Marina Militare), named after the explorer Amerigo Vespucci, after whom the Americas are named. She is a full-rigged three-masted steel hull sailing ship with an auxiliary engine, launched in 1931 at the Royal Naval Shipyard of Castellammare di Stabia, Naples. Her main role is the training of officer cadets of the Italian Naval Academy.

Design

The ship was designed by Lieutenant Colonel Francesco Rotundi of the Italian Navy Engineering Corps, inspired by the style of large late-18th-century 74-cannon ships of the line. She is painted black with two white stripes along the hull, in the style of the ships of the line of her era, and carries a figurehead of Amerigo Vespucci. Her sister ship is the Cristoforo Colombo.

Service

Except during the Second World War, Amerigo Vespucci has been continually active. Most years she embarks on a summer training cruise for first-year cadets of the Livorno Naval Academy, visiting ports across Europe and, in some years, North and South America. She is frequently described as one of the most beautiful sailing ships in the world; a famous exchange in 1962 had the American carrier USS Independence signal "Who are you?" and receive the reply "Training ship Amerigo Vespucci, Italian Navy" — answered by "You are the most beautiful ship in the world."

World tour

In 2023–2025 the ship undertook a two-year world tour, visiting ports on five continents as an ambassador for Italy.
