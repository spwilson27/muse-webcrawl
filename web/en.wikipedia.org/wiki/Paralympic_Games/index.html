Paralympic Games

The Paralympic Games are a periodic series of international multisport events involving athletes with a range of physical disabilities, including impaired muscle power, limb deficiency, leg length difference, short stature, hypertonia, ataxia, athetosis, vision impairment, and intellectual impairment. There are Winter and Summer Paralympic Games, held since 1988 in the same cities and venues as the Olympic Games.

History

The movement began at Stoke Mandeville Hospital in England, where neurologist Sir Ludwig Guttmann organised sports competitions for Second World War veterans with spinal cord injuries; the first Stoke Mandeville Games were held on 29 July 1948, the opening day of the London Olympics. The first official Paralympic Games took place in Rome in 1960, with 400 athletes from 23 countries. The first Winter Paralympics were held in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden, in 1976. The International Paralympic Committee was founded in 1989 and is headquartered in Bonn, Germany.

Sports and classification

The Summer Games include sports such as athletics, swimming, wheelchair basketball, wheelchair rugby, goalball (a sport unique to the Paralympics, for visually impaired athletes), para cycling, and sitting volleyball; the Winter Games include para alpine skiing, para ice hockey, wheelchair curling, and para cross-country skiing. Athletes are grouped by classification — a sport-specific assessment of how an impairment affects performance — so that competition rewards skill and training rather than degree of impairment.

Scale

The Games have grown into one of the world's largest sporting events: the Tokyo 2020 Summer Paralympics (held in 2021) featured about 4,400 athletes from 162 delegations competing in 539 medal events across 22 sports, and London 2012 is credited with transforming public perceptions of disability sport in the host country.
