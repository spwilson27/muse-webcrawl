Afro-Peruvian music

Afro-Peruvian music is a genre of music created by the descendants of enslaved Africans brought to Peru during the colonial period. Rooted in West African rhythms and blended with Spanish and Andean influences, it flourished along Peru's central coast, in towns such as Chincha and Cañete and in Lima's Barrios Altos.

Instruments and genres

The emblematic instruments are the cajón, a wooden box drum played by sitting on it and striking its face; the cajita, a small lidded box tapped rhythmically; and the quijada, a donkey's jawbone whose loose teeth rattle when struck. Principal genres include the festejo, a fast celebratory dance; the landó; the zamacueca; and the vals criollo tradition shared with broader Peruvian creole music.

Revival

After long marginalization, Afro-Peruvian music was revived in the mid-20th century by figures such as Nicomedes Santa Cruz, who collected and popularized décimas and songs, and later carried worldwide by performers including Susana Baca, Eva Ayllón, and the group Perú Negro. The cajón has since been adopted internationally, notably in flamenco.
