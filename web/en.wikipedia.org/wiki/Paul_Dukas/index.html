Paul Dukas

Paul Abraham Dukas (1 October 1865 – 17 May 1935) was a French composer, critic, scholar, and teacher. A perfectionist who destroyed many of his manuscripts, he left a small catalogue of works of exceptional craftsmanship, of which the orchestral scherzo The Sorcerer's Apprentice (1897) — immortalized by Walt Disney's film Fantasia (1940) — is by far the best known.

Life and work

Dukas entered the Paris Conservatoire in 1881, studying with Georges Mathias and Ernest Guiraud alongside Claude Debussy, with whom he formed a lasting friendship. His overture Polyeucte (1891) and his Symphony in C (1896) established his reputation. L'apprenti sorcier (1897), a symphonic poem after Goethe's ballad about a magician's apprentice who loses control of an enchanted broom, displayed his mastery of orchestration and became an international success.

His opera Ariane et Barbe-bleue (1907), to a libretto by Maurice Maeterlinck, is regarded as a major work of French lyric theatre. Other major scores include the monumental Piano Sonata in E-flat minor (1900), the Villanelle for horn and piano, and the ballet La Péri (1912). From 1910 he taught orchestration and later composition at the Conservatoire, where his students included Olivier Messiaen, Joaquín Rodrigo, and Maurice Duruflé. He also wrote extensively as a music critic and edited editions of Rameau and Beethoven.
