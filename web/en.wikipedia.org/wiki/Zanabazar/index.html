Zanabazar

Öndör Gegeen Zanabazar (1635–1723), born Eshidorji, was the first Jebtsundamba Khutuktu, spiritual head of Tibetan Buddhism in Khalkha Mongolia, and a renowned sculptor, artist and scholar. Son of Tüsheet Khan Gombodorj, he was recognised as a reincarnation of Taranatha.

Life and work

Zanabazar created the Soyombo script (1686), still a symbol of Mongolia, and cast exquisite gilt-bronze statues including the Green and White Taras. He founded monasteries that became the nucleus of Ulaanbaatar. Politically he helped bring Khalkha Mongolia under Qing rule in 1691 to resist the Dzungars. His works are central to Buddhism in Mongolia, and the Zanabazar Museum of Fine Arts in Ulaanbaatar bears his name.
