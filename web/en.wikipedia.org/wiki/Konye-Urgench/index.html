Konye-Urgench (Turkmen: Köneürgenç; from Persian Kuhna Urganj, "Old Gurganj"), also known as Kunya-Urgench or simply Urgench in older sources, is a town of about 30,000 inhabitants in north-eastern Turkmenistan, near the border with Uzbekistan. It is the site of the ancient city of Gurganj (Urgench), capital of Khwarazm, whose largely unrestored ruins include some of the finest monuments of Central Asian Islamic architecture.

History

Gurganj was a flourishing city of the Achaemenid satrapy of Chorasmia, later incorporated into the Samanid and then the Khwarazmian Empire. Under the Khwarazmshahs in the 12th and early 13th centuries it became one of the greatest cities of the Islamic world, rivalling Baghdad and Bukhara, a centre of trade on the routes between the Volga and Persia and of learning — the physician-philosopher Ibn Sina (Avicenna) and the scholar al-Biruni worked at its court around the year 1000.

In 1221 Genghis Khan's Mongols destroyed the city and massacred its population, reportedly diverting the Amu Darya to flood the ruins. Revived under the Golden Horde, Gurganj again prospered in the 14th century under Qutlugh Timur, until Timur (Tamerlane) sacked it repeatedly in the 1380s and ordered its destruction. The shifting course of the Amu Darya in the 16th century finally forced abandonment; the population moved to New Urgench in present-day Uzbekistan. The modern Turkmen town grew up beside the ruins.

Monuments

Surviving monuments include the Kutlug Timur Minaret (c. 1320–30), at about 60 m the tallest medieval minaret in Central Asia; the Mausoleum of Turabek Khanum; the Il-Arslan and Tekesh mausolea of the Khwarazmshahs; and the Najm al-Din Kubra mausoleum. In 2005 UNESCO inscribed "Kunya-Urgench" on the World Heritage List.
