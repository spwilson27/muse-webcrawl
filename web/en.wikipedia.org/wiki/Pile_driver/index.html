Pile driver

A pile driver is a device used to drive piles into soil to provide foundation support for bridges, buildings, and other structures. The term is also used in reference to members of the construction crew that work with pile-driving rigs.

One type of pile driver is a heavy weight placed between guides so that it is able to freely slide up and down in a single line. It is placed upon a pile. The weight is raised, which may involve the use of hydraulics, steam, diesel, or manual labour. When the weight reaches its highest point, it is released and falls using gravity, striking the pile and driving it into the ground.

History

Pile-driving machines have been used since ancient times. The Romans built an early pile driver resembling the modern design, and drawings of such devices appear in medieval manuscripts. Steam-powered pile drivers were developed in the 19th century and greatly increased the speed and force of pile installation.

Types

Diesel hammer

A diesel hammer is a modern pile-driving device similar to a vertical two-stroke diesel engine. The piston is the hammer, and the cylinder combusts diesel fuel at the moment of impact, adding driving energy and rebounding the hammer.

Hydraulic hammer

A hydraulic hammer uses an external power source to lift the hammer and drop it onto the pile. These are generally quieter and produce less vibration than diesel hammers.

Drop hammer

The drop hammer is the oldest and simplest type: a heavy weight is raised by a winch and dropped onto the pile.

Vibratory pile driver

Vibratory drivers use rotating counterweights to generate vibration that loosens the soil, allowing the pile to be pushed or pulled. They are often used for sheet piles and in noise-sensitive areas.

Environmental concerns

Underwater pile driving produces intense sound pressure waves that can injure or kill fish and marine mammals. Mitigation measures include bubble curtains, isolation casings, and restrictions on driving during sensitive seasons.
