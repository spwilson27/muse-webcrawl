Noise rock (sometimes called noise punk) is a noise-oriented style of experimental rock that spun off from punk rock in the 1980s. Drawing on bands such as the Velvet Underground, the Stooges, and the no-wave scene, it uses the traditional rock instrumentation and structures while incorporating atonality, dissonance, heavy distortion, feedback, and abrasive textures.

Characteristics

Noise rock bands favour harsh, distorted guitar sounds, often with alternate tunings, prepared guitars, and effects pedals; rhythms may be pounding and repetitive, and vocals range from deadpan to screaming. Despite the name, most noise rock is more structured than free-form noise music, retaining riffs, songs, and choruses beneath the abrasion.

History

Precursors include the Velvet Underground's White Light/White Heat (1968) and the work of Glenn Branca and Rhys Chatham with massed detuned guitars. New York's no-wave movement of the late 1970s, and bands such as Sonic Youth — who became the genre's most famous exponents — defined its 1980s form. Other key acts include Big Black, Swans, the Jesus Lizard, Butthole Surfers, Pussy Galore, and later Lightning Bolt, Melt-Banana, and HEALTH. The style fed into alternative rock, industrial, and the 1990s underground, and its techniques became a standard part of indie rock's vocabulary.
