CYP2E1

Gene: CYP2E1 (chromosome 10q26.3 in humans)
Enzyme family: Cytochrome P450
Also known as: Cytochrome P450 2E1

Cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1) is a member of the cytochrome P450 mixed-function oxidase system, which is involved in the metabolism of xenobiotics in the body. In humans, the CYP2E1 enzyme is encoded by the CYP2E1 gene. It is expressed mainly in the liver, where it constitutes a significant portion of hepatic P450 content, and is strongly inducible by ethanol.

Function

CYP2E1 metabolizes small, polar molecules including ethanol, acetone, benzene, and halogenated anesthetics, and it activates several procarcinogens and toxins. It is responsible for the conversion of acetaminophen (paracetamol) to the hepatotoxic metabolite NAPQI, a pathway that becomes important in acetaminophen overdose and in chronic alcohol use. Ethanol is oxidized by CYP2E1 as part of the microsomal ethanol-oxidizing system (MEOS), generating reactive oxygen species that contribute to alcoholic liver injury.

Because its activity is induced by chronic ethanol consumption, fasting, and diabetes, and inhibited by compounds such as disulfiram, CYP2E1 plays a role in drug interactions and susceptibility to chemical-induced toxicity.
