The medial frontal gyrus is the portion of the superior frontal gyrus that lies on the medial surface of the frontal lobe of the brain. It is continuous with the superior frontal gyrus around the medial border of the hemisphere and extends from the frontal pole posteriorly to the paracentral lobule.

Anatomy

The medial frontal gyrus is bounded inferiorly by the cingulate sulcus, which separates it from the cingulate gyrus below, and posteriorly it merges with the paracentral lobule surrounding the central sulcus. Its anterior portion corresponds largely to medial prefrontal cortex, overlapping Brodmann areas 9 and 10, while its posterior portion approaches the supplementary motor area (Brodmann area 6) near the paracentral lobule.

Function

The anterior medial frontal gyrus, as part of the medial prefrontal cortex, participates in self-referential thought, social cognition, and the default mode network. More posterior regions contribute to motor planning; stimulation or lesions near the supplementary motor region can produce characteristic motor and speech effects. The gyrus receives its blood supply chiefly from branches of the anterior cerebral artery, so strokes in that territory can damage it.
