Fallingwater is a house designed in 1935 by the renowned American architect Frank Lloyd Wright (1867–1959) for the family of Liliane and Edgar J. Kaufmann, owners of Kaufmann's Department Store in Pittsburgh. It is located in the mountains of Southwestern Pennsylvania, in the Laurel Highlands, near the village of Mill Run. The house is built partly over a waterfall on Bear Run creek, in the Allegheny Mountains.

The Kaufmanns commissioned Wright to design a country house facing their waterfall, but Wright instead placed the house directly above the falls, integrating the building with the site — a defining statement of his philosophy of organic architecture. The house was designated a National Historic Landmark and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1966. In 1991, members of the American Institute of Architects named Fallingwater the "best all-time work of American architecture," and in 2007 it was ranked 29th on the AIA's list of America's Favorite Architecture.

Design

The main house is organised around a central vertical core of local sandstone, from which reinforced-concrete terraces cantilever dramatically over the stream. Horizontal trays of pale concrete contrast with the rough stone and with cornerless window bands of steel casements painted in Wright's signature Cherokee red. Inside, the living room features a hearth built around an exposed boulder that rises through the floor — reportedly the Kaufmanns' favourite picnic spot — and a suspended stair descending from the living room to the stream below. Floors are waxed flagstone, and built-in furniture throughout was designed by Wright.

A guest house on the hill above, connected by a canopied walkway, followed in 1939. The completed cost, including furnishings and fees, was about $155,000 — far over the original budget.

Construction and structural problems

Construction began in 1936 under apprentice Robert Mosher. Wright's calculations for the cantilevers were disputed by the Kaufmanns' consulting engineers, who secretly doubled the reinforcing steel; the terraces nevertheless deflected significantly, sagging several inches over the decades. A major restoration by the Western Pennsylvania Conservancy, led in 2001–02 by engineers Robert Silman Associates, stabilised the structure with post-tensioning.

Museum

In 1963, Edgar Kaufmann Jr. donated Fallingwater and its surrounding 1,543 acres of land to the Western Pennsylvania Conservancy, and the house opened as a museum in 1964. It receives well over 100,000 visitors in a typical year and has inspired countless imitations; Wright's son Lloyd Wright's Wayfarers Chapel and his own Taliesin are among the related works of the period. The house appeared on the cover of Time magazine in 1938 and remains one of the most widely recognised buildings in the world.
