Potter Stewart

Potter Stewart (January 23, 1915 – December 7, 1985) was an American lawyer and judge who served as an associate justice of the United States Supreme Court from 1958 to 1981. A moderate and a swing vote on the Warren and Burger Courts, he is perhaps most famous for his phrase in the obscenity case Jacobellis v. Ohio (1964): "I know it when I see it."

Career

Stewart graduated from Yale Law School, served in the U.S. Naval Reserve in the Second World War, practised law in Cincinnati, and sat on Cincinnati's city council before President Eisenhower nominated him to the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals in 1954. In 1958 Eisenhower gave him a recess appointment to the Supreme Court, and the Senate confirmed him in 1959.

Jurisprudence

Stewart wrote important opinions in criminal procedure, access to the courts, and property rights, including Katz v. United States (1967), where his concurrence argued the Fourth Amendment protects people, not places. He dissented in Griswold v. Connecticut and generally resisted novel substantive due process claims, while supporting strong First Amendment and criminal-procedure protections. He retired in 1981 and was succeeded by Sandra Day O'Connor.
