The Virée de Galerne was a military campaign of the War in the Vendée during the French Revolution, lasting from 18 October to 23 December 1793. Defeated at Cholet, the Catholic and Royal Army of the Vendée — accompanied by tens of thousands of civilians — crossed the Loire and marched north through Brittany and Normandy in the hope of capturing a Channel port and receiving British support; "Galerne" refers to the north-west wind.

Course of the campaign

After taking Laval and other towns, the Vendéans failed to storm Granville on the Normandy coast in November. Turning back toward the Loire, they were repulsed at Angers, defeated at Le Mans on 12–13 December — where thousands of soldiers and camp followers were massacred — and finally annihilated at the Battle of Savenay on 23 December 1793, which ended the Vendée as an organised field army.

Aftermath

Of the perhaps 60,000–80,000 people who had crossed the Loire, only a few thousand returned home. The disaster was followed by the brutal repression of the Vendée under General Turreau's "infernal columns" in early 1794. The campaign takes its name from the word used by the Vendéans for the bitter north-west wind that accompanied their march.
