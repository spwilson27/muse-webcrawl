Cordell Hull


Image placeholder: Cordell Hull

Cordell Hull (October 2, 1871 – July 23, 1955) was an American politician who served as the United States Secretary of State from 1933 to 1944 under President Franklin D. Roosevelt. He is the longest-serving Secretary of State in U.S. history, holding the post for 11 years, and played a central role in American foreign policy during the buildup to and early years of World War II.

Early life and career

Hull was born in Olympus, Pickett County, Tennessee. He studied law and was admitted to the bar in 1891. He served in the Tennessee House of Representatives and in the Spanish–American War, and represented Tennessee in the U.S. House of Representatives for many years, later serving in the U.S. Senate.

Secretary of State

As Secretary of State, Hull championed the Good Neighbor Policy toward Latin America and reciprocal trade agreements that lowered tariffs. He was a leading advocate of international cooperation and is often called the "father of the United Nations" for his work in planning the organization; he received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1945 for that role.
