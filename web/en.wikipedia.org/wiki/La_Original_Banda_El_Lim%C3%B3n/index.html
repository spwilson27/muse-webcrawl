La Original Banda El Limón

La Original Banda El Limón de Salvador Lizárraga is a Mexican banda group from Mazatlán, Sinaloa. Founded in 1965 in the village of El Limón de los Peraza, Sinaloa, the band is one of the pioneers of the Sinaloan banda genre and a reference point for the modern brass banda sound.

History

The band was created by Salvador Lizárraga, a clarinettist who remained its director for decades. In 1977 a split among the musicians led some members to form a rival ensemble, La Banda El Limón, prompting Lizárraga's group to adopt the name "La Original Banda El Limón" to assert its continuity with the original band. The resulting naming dispute between the two ensembles persisted for decades and became one of the best-known controversies in regional Mexican music.

Music and recognition

The band has recorded dozens of albums of rancheras, corridos, cumbias, and romantic ballads, with hits such as "El Sinaloense" in its repertoire and songs including "Que Me Vas a Dar", "Derecho de Antigüedad", and "Diablito Pachanguero". Well-known vocalists, among them Julio Preciado, began their careers with the band. La Original Banda El Limón has won Latin Grammy Awards for Best Banda Album, including for En Vivo (2009) and subsequent releases, and continues to tour in Mexico and the United States. Salvador Lizárraga died in March 2021 in Mazatlán.
