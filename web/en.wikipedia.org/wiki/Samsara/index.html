Samsara is the cycle of birth, death and rebirth to which beings are bound in the Indian religions, driven by Karma and ignorance, and characterised by suffering. In Buddhism the wheel of life (bhavacakra) depicts six realms of rebirth — gods, demigods, humans, animals, hungry ghosts and hells — with the human realm prized as the one from which awakening is possible. Liberation from samsara is the goal: Moksha for Hindus, Jains and Sikhs; Nirvana for Buddhists.

Origins

The doctrine appears in the early Upanishads, fused with karma doctrine, and was shared — competitively reworked — by the sramana movements of the 6th–5th centuries BC, including Buddhism and Jainism, distinguishing Indic religions from the linear eschatologies of the Abrahamic traditions.
