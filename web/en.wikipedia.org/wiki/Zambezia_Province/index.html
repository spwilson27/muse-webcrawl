Zambezia (Portuguese: Zambézia) is the second-largest and most populous of Mozambique's ten provinces. It lies in the central part of the country, straddling the lower course of the Zambezi River from which it takes its name, and has a coastline on the Mozambique Channel of the Indian Ocean. Its capital is the port city of Quelimane, near the mouth of the Rio dos Bons Sinais.

Geography

Zambezia borders Malawi and the provinces of Niassa and Nampula to the north, Tete to the west, and Sofala to the south, from which it is separated in part by the Zambezi delta — one of Africa's great wetland systems. The interior rises to uplands around Gurúè and the massif of Mount Namuli (2,419 m), among Mozambique's highest peaks and a centre of endemism. The province's many rivers include the Zambezi, Shire, Licungo and Ligonha.

History

The Portuguese established trading posts on the coast from the 16th century, and the lower Zambezi valley was organised under the prazo system of crown estates, later leased to companies such as the Zambezia Company and Sena Sugar Estates. The province suffered heavily during the Mozambican Civil War (1977–1992); Quelimane and the tea estates around Gurúè were repeatedly contested. In March 2023 Cyclone Freddy made its second Mozambican landfall in Zambezia, causing severe flooding.

Economy

Agriculture dominates: Zambezia produces much of Mozambique's tea (grown around Gurúè, among the largest tea estates in Africa), coconuts and copra from vast coastal palm groves, cashew nuts, sugar at the delta, and rice. The estuary port of Quelimane and the Zambezi delta shrimp fishery are significant, while the interior holds timber and heavy-mineral sands. Despite its resources the province remains one of Mozambique's poorest, with limited road and rail infrastructure.

Demographics

The principal peoples include the Macua-Lomwe, Sena and Chuabo; Portuguese is the official language, widely spoken alongside Elomwe, Echuwabo and Cisena. The population is predominantly Christian (Catholic and various Protestant and Zionist churches), with a Muslim minority concentrated near the coast.
