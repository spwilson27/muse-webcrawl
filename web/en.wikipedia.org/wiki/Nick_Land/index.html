Nick Land

Nick Land (born 17 January 1962) is an English philosopher and writer. A lecturer at the University of Warwick in the 1990s, he co-founded the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU), an experimental collective whose work on accelerationism, cybernetics, and "hyperstition" influenced later speculative philosophy and internet culture. After leaving academia he moved to Shanghai, and in the 2010s he became a prominent theorist of the neoreactionary movement.

Warwick and the CCRU

Land's early writings, collected in Fanged Noumena (2011), combined readings of Kant, Nietzsche, Bataille and Deleuze and Guattari with an intense, fragmentary style. With Sadie Plant and others he formed the CCRU around 1995; the unit, joined by figures including Mark Fisher and Reza Negarestani, explored the self-accelerating dynamics of capitalism, technology and the occult. Land's concept of accelerationism — pushing capitalist processes to their dissolution rather than resisting them — was later developed, in divergent left and right directions, by other theorists.

Neoreaction

Writing from Shanghai, Land published The Dark Enlightenment (2012), a foundational text of neoreaction (NRx), which rejects egalitarianism and democracy in favour of formalist, hierarchical governance, and blogged as a leading voice of the movement. His later work has focused on Bitcoin, "crypto-capital", and commentary on Chinese development.
