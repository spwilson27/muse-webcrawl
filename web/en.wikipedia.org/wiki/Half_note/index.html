Half note

In musical notation, a half note (American) or minim (British) is a note played for half the duration of a whole note (or semibreve) and twice the duration of a quarter note (or crotchet). It is written as a hollow oval note head with a straight stem and no beam or flag; like other stemmed notes, the stem points upward when the note is below the middle line of the staff and downward when above it.

Description

In time signatures such as 4/4, a half note receives two beats, so two half notes fill a bar. The corresponding silence is the half rest, drawn as a thick horizontal line sitting on top of the middle staff line — the opposite orientation to a whole rest, which hangs from the line above. The minim's name and form derive from the minima, the shortest note value in medieval mensural notation.
