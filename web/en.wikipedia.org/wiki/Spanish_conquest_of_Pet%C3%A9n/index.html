Spanish conquest of Petén

The Spanish conquest of Petén was a prolonged campaign during the Spanish colonisation of the Americas, culminating in the capture of Nojpetén, capital of the Itza Maya kingdom, on 13 March 1697. Petén, in the lowlands of northern Guatemala, was the last independent Maya region to fall to the Spanish.

Background

The Itza, who had migrated from Yucatán, ruled a kingdom centred on Lake Petén Itzá. Hernán Cortés passed through in 1525 and met the Itza king, but no conquest was attempted. Franciscan missionaries visited Nojpetén in the early 17th century but were rebuffed or killed.

Final campaign

In the 1690s Spain built a road south from Campeche toward the lake. In March 1697, Martín de Urzúa y Arismendi launched an assault on Nojpetén using a purpose-built galeota (small warship) assembled on the lakeshore. The Itza defenders fled or were killed, and the city fell, ending organised Maya resistance in the region.

Aftermath

The conquest opened Petén to Spanish administration and missionary activity, though Spanish control of the remote lowlands remained weak for decades.
