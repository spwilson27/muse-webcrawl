FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives

The FBI Ten Most Wanted Fugitives is a most wanted list maintained by the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI). The list came into existence on 14 March 1950 at the direction of FBI director J. Edgar Hoover, after a reporter for the International News Service asked the Bureau for the names and descriptions of the "toughest guys" it was trying to capture; the resulting story generated so much publicity that Hoover institutionalized the list.

Criteria and outcomes

Fugitives are nominated by the FBI's 56 field offices and reviewed by the Criminal Investigative Division and the Office of Public Affairs. Candidates must have a lengthy record of committing serious crimes or be considered particularly dangerous, and the Bureau must believe that nationwide publicity will assist in their capture. Fugitives are removed when captured, killed, charges are dropped, or they no longer fit the criteria. Since 1950, more than 530 fugitives have been listed, and the great majority have been located, with over 160 found as a direct result of citizen assistance. A minimum reward of US$250,000 is offered for information leading directly to the arrest of a listed fugitive (the amount was raised over the years from an initial $10,000 in some cases).

Notable fugitives

Long-running members of the list included Boston mobster James "Whitey" Bulger, listed from 1999 until his capture in Santa Monica in 2011, and Osama bin Laden, added after the 1998 embassy bombings and remaining on the list until his death in 2011. The only woman addition era includes figures such as Shauntay Henderson; as of the 2020s only a small number of women have ever been listed. The FBI also maintains companion lists, including the Ten Most Wanted Fugitives' historical archive and lists for terrorists and kidnappings/missing persons.
