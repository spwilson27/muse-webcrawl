μ-opioid receptor

GeneOPRM1
TypeG protein-coupled receptor
Endogenous ligandsβ-endorphin, endomorphins
Exogenous ligandsMorphine, fentanyl, oxycodone

The μ-opioid receptor (MOR) is a protein encoded in humans by the OPRM1 gene. It is the principal target of most opioid analgesics, including morphine, and mediates both their pain-relieving effects and their adverse effects such as respiratory depression, euphoria, sedation, and dependence.

Function

MOR is a G protein-coupled receptor activated by endogenous peptides such as β-endorphin and the endomorphins. Activation inhibits adenylyl cyclase, opens potassium channels, and closes calcium channels, reducing neuronal excitability and neurotransmitter release in pain pathways.

Splice variants

The OPRM1 gene produces several splice variants, historically classified as μ1, μ2, and μ3 subtypes, though these reflect alternative splicing of a single gene rather than separate genes.

Clinical significance

Agonists at MOR are mainstays of pain management but carry risks of tolerance, dependence, and overdose. Antagonists such as naloxone and naltrexone block the receptor and are used to reverse overdose and treat opioid use disorder. Genetic variation in OPRM1, such as the A118G polymorphism, can influence pain sensitivity and drug response.
