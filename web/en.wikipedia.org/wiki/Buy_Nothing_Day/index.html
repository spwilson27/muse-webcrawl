Buy Nothing Day

Buy Nothing Day is an international day of protest against consumerism, observed annually: in North America it is held on the day after US Thanksgiving — the retail event known as Black Friday — and elsewhere typically on the following Saturday (the last Saturday of November). Participants are encouraged to abstain from shopping for 24 hours and to take part in actions highlighting overconsumption and its environmental and social costs.

History

Buy Nothing Day was founded in Vancouver, Canada, by artist Ted Dave in September 1992. It was subsequently taken up by the Vancouver-based magazine and media foundation Adbusters, founded by Kalle Lasn, which promoted it internationally. The campaign deliberately moved the North American date to coincide with Black Friday, the busiest shopping day of the US year, to contrast the protest with peak consumption. Adbusters famously sought television advertising time for its "Uncommercial" spots, which most major networks refused to air.

Activities

Typical actions include simply not buying anything, credit-card cut-up ceremonies, "Whirl-mart" performances (lines of activists pushing empty shopping carts through stores), zombie walks in malls, and free-exchange events. From 2007, Buy Nothing Day organisers in some places paired the protest with "Buy Nothing Christmas" campaigns.

Criticism

Critics argue that a one-day boycott merely displaces spending, that abstention is a privilege of the affluent, and that the protest targets shoppers rather than the structures of production. Supporters respond that the day is primarily symbolic and educational, intended to prompt reflection rather than measurable economic impact.
