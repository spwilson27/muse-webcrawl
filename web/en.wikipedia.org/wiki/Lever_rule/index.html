Lever rule

The lever rule is a rule used in phase diagrams in materials science and thermodynamics to determine the mole fraction (or mass fraction) of each phase of a binary equilibrium phase diagram.

Explanation

For a composition lying inside a two-phase region, a horizontal tie line is drawn at the temperature of interest. The lever rule states that the fraction of one phase is proportional to the length of the opposite segment of the tie line, measured from the overall composition point — analogous to the balance point of a lever or seesaw.

Derivation

The rule follows from a simple mass balance on the components in the two phases. It can be applied to binary alloy systems, liquid–vapour equilibria and other two-phase mixtures to find the relative amounts of each phase at equilibrium.
