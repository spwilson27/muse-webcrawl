Kannada script

The Kannada script (Kannada: ಕನ್ನಡ ಲಿಪಿ, Kannaḍa lipi) is an abugida of the Brahmic family used to write the Kannada language, the official language of the Indian state of Karnataka. It is also used for Tulu, Kodava, Konkani and other languages of the region. Kannada script is closely related to, and largely mutually intelligible in glyph repertoire with, the Telugu script.

History

The script descends from Brahmi through the Kadamba script of the 4th–6th centuries CE. The Halmidi inscription (c. 450 CE) is the earliest known full-length Kannada inscription. By the Rashtrakuta and Chalukya periods, a Kannada–Telugu script had crystallised; the two scripts diverged into their modern rounded forms in the later medieval and early modern periods, partly under the influence of writing on palm leaves and of printing.

Structure

Like other Brahmic scripts, Kannada is an abugida: each consonant letter carries an inherent vowel /a/, modified by dependent vowel signs (matras). The traditional inventory comprises 49 letters — 13 vowels (svara), 2 part-vowels (yogavāhaka: anusvara and visarga), and 34 consonants (vyañjana) — arranged in the pan-Indian phonetic order. Consonant clusters are written with subjoined "vattu" forms, and the virama (halanta) cancels the inherent vowel. Kannada letterforms are notably curvilinear, with few straight lines.

Usage

Kannada script is the sole official script of Karnataka and is used in government, education, literature and media for the roughly 44 million native speakers of Kannada. It has been encoded in Unicode since version 1.0.
