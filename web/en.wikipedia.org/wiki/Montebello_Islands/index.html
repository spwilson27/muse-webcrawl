Montebello Islands

LocationPilbara coast, Western Australia
Number of islandsapprox. 174
Major islandsTrimouille, Alpha, Hermite
StatusMarine park and conservation reserve

The Montebello Islands are an archipelago of around 174 small islands (about 22 of them named) lying about 130 km off the Pilbara coast of north-western Western Australia, and some 20 km north of Barrow Island. The islands form the Montebello Islands Marine Park and are a conservation reserve noted for their marine life.

History

The islands were visited by European navigators from the early 17th century; the English ship Tryall, the earliest known shipwreck in Australian waters, was lost on Tryal Rocks near the islands in 1622. The archipelago was named in 1801 by the French explorer Nicolas Baudin.

Nuclear tests

The islands are best known as the site of British nuclear weapons tests in the 1950s. Under Operation Hurricane, Britain detonated its first atomic bomb aboard HMS Plym in Main Bay off Trimouille Island on 3 October 1952. Two further tests followed under Operation Mosaic in 1956 on Trimouille and Alpha Islands, the second of which, at about 98 kilotons, was the largest nuclear device ever tested in Australia. Residual contamination, mainly on Alpha and Trimouille islands, means visits to those areas are still restricted and monitored.

Environment

The surrounding waters support coral reefs, mangroves, dugongs, sea turtles, and humpback whales. Introduced rats and feral cats severely damaged native fauna, including the rufous hare-wallaby and golden bandicoot populations translocated from Barrow Island; eradication programs have since removed the predators, and the islands are an important site for rewilding endangered species.
