Jazz dance is a performance dance and style that arose in the United States from African-American vernacular dance traditions in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It developed alongside jazz music and is characterised by syncopated rhythms, grounded movement, isolations of individual body parts, improvisation, and a strong sense of individual expression.

Jazz dance encompasses a wide range of styles, from the vernacular dances of the jazz age such as the Charleston and the Lindy Hop, to the highly theatrical, ballet-influenced style codified by choreographers such as Jack Cole, Bob Fosse and Jerome Robbins for Broadway and film.

History

The roots of jazz dance lie in the dances brought to the Americas by enslaved Africans, which blended with European social dance forms. In New Orleans and other cities, dances such as the cakewalk, the black bottom and the Charleston became national crazes during the 1910s and 1920s. Ballrooms such as Harlem's Savoy Ballroom were centres of innovation, where the Lindy Hop emerged in the late 1920s.

From the 1930s onward, jazz dance moved into Hollywood musicals and onto the Broadway stage. Jack Cole, often called the father of theatrical jazz dance, combined ballet, modern dance and ethnic dance forms into a technique taught to film stars. Later choreographers including Bob Fosse developed a distinctive, stylised vocabulary — turned-in knees, jazz hands, isolated hip and shoulder movements — that became synonymous with musical theatre dance.

Technique

Jazz dance technique borrows from ballet (turns, leaps, extension) and modern dance, but is danced with a lower centre of gravity, bent knees and an emphasis on rhythmic attack. Key elements include isolations, contractions, syncopation, and improvisation. Classes typically begin with a warm-up of isolations and stretches, progress across the floor with kicks, turns and leaps, and end with a combination.

Styles

  • Classical jazz – the traditional style associated with Matt Mattox and Luigi.
  • Theatrical jazz – the Broadway style of Fosse and Robbins.
  • Contemporary/lyrical jazz – a fusion with contemporary dance.
  • Street jazz / jazz-funk – a commercial style influenced by hip-hop, seen in music videos.
