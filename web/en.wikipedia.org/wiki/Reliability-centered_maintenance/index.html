Reliability-centered maintenance

OriginCommercial aviation, 1960s–1970s
Key figuresF. Stanley Nowlan, Howard F. Heap
FocusMaintenance program optimization

Reliability-centered maintenance (RCM) is a process used to determine what must be done to ensure that physical assets continue to do what their users want in their present operating context. It is used to achieve improvements in fields such as the establishment of safe minimum levels of maintenance, changes to operating procedures, and the establishment of capital replacement plans.

History

RCM originated in the commercial airline industry in the 1960s and 1970s, when studies for the Boeing 747 maintenance program showed that traditional time-based overhauls did little to improve reliability for many components. The approach was codified by F. Stanley Nowlan and Howard F. Heap of United Airlines in a 1978 report prepared for the U.S. Department of Defense.

Method

The classical RCM process asks seven questions about each asset: its functions, functional failures, failure modes, failure effects, failure consequences, proactive tasks, and default actions. Tasks are selected based on the consequences of failure — safety, environmental, operational, or economic — rather than on fixed intervals alone.
