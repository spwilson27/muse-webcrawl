Hero

A hero (feminine: heroine) is a real person or a fictional character who, in the face of danger, combats adversity through feats of ingenuity, courage or strength. The original hero type of classical epics did such things for the sake of glory and honour; modern heroes may act instead for the greater good, out of selflessness or moral conviction. The word comes from the Greek hērōs (ἥρως), a term for a demigod or a mortal granted heroic cult after death.

In antiquity

In Greek religion, heroes such as Heracles, Achilles and Theseus stood between gods and men: mortals of exceptional deeds who were venerated at shrines after death. The epics of Homer and the tragedies of Athens explored heroic virtues — prowess, honour, loyalty — along with heroic flaws such as Achilles' wrath or hubris.

The hero in literature

The hero is the central figure of epic poetry, romance, folklore and the novel; scholars such as Joseph Campbell described the "hero's journey" or monomyth — departure, initiation and return — as a pattern recurring across world mythology. Variants include the tragic hero, whose downfall follows from a fatal flaw, the Byronic hero, and the antihero, who lacks conventional heroic qualities yet occupies the protagonist's role.

In modern usage

Today "hero" is applied to rescuers, soldiers, emergency workers and others who display exceptional courage, as well as to fictional superheroes of comics and film. The term is also used loosely for the main character of a story and, in some regions, for a type of sandwich.
