Breitscheidplatz, dominated by the ruined tower of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.

Breitscheidplatz is a major public square in Berlin, Germany, in the district of Charlottenburg. Together with the Kurfürstendamm boulevard, which begins here, it formed the commercial centre of West Berlin during the city's division. The square is overlooked by the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, whose bomb-damaged spire was preserved as a memorial after the Second World War, and by the Europa-Center tower.

History

The square was laid out in the late 19th century and originally named Auguste-Viktoria-Platz. It was renamed in 1947 for Rudolf Breitscheid, a Social Democratic politician who died in Buchenwald concentration camp. On 19 December 2016, the square was the site of a truck attack on its Christmas market, in which 13 people were killed; a memorial now marks the site.
