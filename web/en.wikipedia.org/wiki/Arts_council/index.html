Arts council

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Arts councils fund and support the arts at arm's length from government

An arts council is a government or private, non-profit organization dedicated to promoting the arts and mainly to funding the arts, often through grants to individual artists, arts organizations, and educational programmes. Arts councils typically operate on the arm's length principle, under which funding decisions are made by peer review panels rather than directly by politicians, in order to insulate artistic judgement from political interference.

History

The modern arts council model began in the United Kingdom with the Council for the Encouragement of Music and the Arts (1940), which became the Arts Council of Great Britain in 1946, championed by the economist John Maynard Keynes, its first chairman. The model spread to Commonwealth countries and beyond: the Canada Council for the Arts was founded in 1957, the Australia Council in 1968, and similar bodies exist in Ireland, New Zealand, and many other countries, as well as at regional and local levels.

Functions

Arts councils distribute public funds, advise governments on cultural policy, support touring and international exchange, and collect data on the cultural sector. In England the role is held by Arts Council England; in Scotland by Creative Scotland and in Wales by the Arts Council of Wales.
