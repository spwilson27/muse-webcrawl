Method of least squares

The method of least squares is a standard approach in regression analysis to approximate the solution of overdetermined systems of equations. It fits a model to data by minimising the sum of the squares of the residuals — the differences between the observed values and the values predicted by the model.

Formulation

For a linear model with parameters β, data matrix X and observations y, the least-squares estimate is the β that minimises ||y − Xβ||². Setting the gradient to zero yields the normal equations XTXβ = XTy, whose solution, when XTX is invertible, is β = (XTX)−1XTy. Numerically, the problem is usually solved with QR decomposition or the singular value decomposition rather than by forming the normal equations explicitly.

History

Adrien-Marie Legendre published the method in 1805 for fitting comet orbits; Carl Friedrich Gauss claimed to have used it since 1795 and in 1809 connected it with the normal distribution of errors, showing that least squares gives the most probable estimates under Gaussian errors. A priority dispute followed. The method was famously applied to relocate the asteroid Ceres.

Properties and extensions

By the Gauss–Markov theorem, among linear unbiased estimators the ordinary least squares estimator has the smallest variance when errors are uncorrelated with equal variance. Variants include weighted least squares, generalised least squares, total least squares, ridge regression (regularised least squares), and non-linear least squares, solved iteratively by methods such as Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt.
