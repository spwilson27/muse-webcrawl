Intel 8088

The Intel 8088 is a microprocessor introduced by Intel in 1979. Externally it has an 8-bit data bus, but internally it is a fully 16-bit processor identical in software to the Intel 8086. The narrower bus made systems cheaper because 8-bit support chips and memory were less expensive.

Use in the IBM PC

The 8088 is best known as the processor of the original IBM Personal Computer (model 5150), released in 1981 and running at 4.77 MHz. IBM's choice of the chip, together with the open architecture of the PC, helped establish the x86 family as the standard for personal computers.

Variants

The NEC V20 was a popular pin-compatible clone offering slightly better performance. Later embedded versions, such as the 80C88, were used in portable computers and industrial equipment for many years.
