Timeline of medicine and medical technology

This is a timeline of the history of medicine and medical technology, from early surgical practices and herbal medicine through to modern imaging, genetics, and biotechnology.

Ancient and medieval

Early entries include trepanation in prehistory, the medical papyri of ancient Egypt, Hippocratic writings in Greece, Galen's anatomical work in Rome, and the hospitals and medical encyclopedias of the Islamic Golden Age, such as Avicenna's Canon of Medicine.

16th–19th centuries

Vesalius founded modern anatomy in 1543; Harvey described the circulation of the blood in 1628. Jenner's smallpox vaccine (1796), the introduction of anaesthesia (1846), antiseptic surgery, Pasteur's germ theory, and Koch's identification of disease-causing bacteria transformed medicine in the 19th century, along with Röntgen's discovery of X-rays in 1895.

20th century and beyond

Twentieth-century milestones include insulin therapy, penicillin and the antibiotic era, the structure of DNA, organ transplantation, the polio vaccine, CT and MRI scanning, and the decoding of the human genome. The 21st century adds robotic surgery, monoclonal antibodies, mRNA vaccines, and CRISPR-based therapies.
