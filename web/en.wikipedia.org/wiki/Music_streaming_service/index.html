Music streaming service

A music streaming service is a type of online service that provides legal access to a large catalogue of recorded music over the internet, typically on demand, in exchange for a subscription fee or supported by advertising. Rather than purchasing downloads or physical media, subscribers access music hosted on remote servers and delivered via streaming media technology.

History

Early services such as Rhapsody (2001) offered subscription listening, while the peer-to-peer file sharing of Napster (1999–2001) demonstrated mass demand for digital music accessed over the internet. Spotify, launched in Sweden in 2008, popularised the "freemium" model — free, advertising-supported access alongside paid tiers — and became the largest service worldwide, with hundreds of millions of users. Competitors include Apple Music (2015, successor to the Beats Music service), Amazon Music, YouTube Music, Tidal (noted for high-fidelity audio and artist ownership at launch), Deezer, and regional services such as Tencent Music's QQ Music in China.

Business model and royalties

Services license recordings from labels and compositions from publishers, paying royalties based largely on a pro-rata share of total streams. Per-stream payouts to rights holders are typically estimated in the range of a fraction of a cent, a model debated by artists and songwriters; some services and proposals advocate user-centric payment systems that allocate each subscriber's fee only to the artists they listen to. Streaming became the recorded music industry's largest revenue source in the mid-2010s, reversing two decades of decline driven by piracy and falling CD sales.

Features

Services compete on catalogue size, audio quality (including lossless and spatial audio tiers), personalised playlists and algorithmic recommendation (such as Spotify's Discover Weekly), podcasts and audiobooks, lyrics, social features, and integration with smart speakers and car systems.

Criticism

Criticism has focused on low per-stream rates for artists, the dominance of major-label catalogues, algorithmic promotion, and the environmental cost of data centres. Nonetheless, streaming is credited with sharply reducing music piracy and expanding global access to music.
