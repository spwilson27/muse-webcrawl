White's

White's
Type: Gentlemen's club
Founded: 1693
Location: St James's Street, London

White's is a gentlemen's club in St James's, London, the oldest and most famous of the London clubs. It was founded in 1693 as a chocolate house by Francesco Bianco ("Francis White") and moved into the world of private club life as its clientele of aristocrats and politicians made it their social base.

History

White's began at 4 Chesterfield Street as Mrs White's Chocolate House. It became notorious for high-stakes gambling; wagers recorded in its famous betting book included bets on battles, elections, and the survival of public figures. In 1778 the club moved to 37–38 St James's Street, where it remains. The bow window, added in 1811, became a famous vantage point from which the dandy Beau Brummell and his circle held court.

Character

White's has historically been associated with the Tory party and the aristocracy, in contrast to the Whig associations of Brooks's. It remains a private members' club.
