Low emissivity

Low emissivity (abbreviated low-e) refers to a surface condition that emits low levels of radiant thermal (heat) energy. All materials absorb, reflect and emit radiant energy according to Planck's law, but here the primary concern is a special wavelength interval: thermal radiation at temperatures near room temperature, roughly 3–30 micrometres. A low-e coating is a microscopically thin layer, usually of metal or metallic oxide, applied to glass or other surfaces to reduce the emission and absorption of infrared radiation, and so to reduce heat transfer through windows and other building elements.

Principle

By Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation, a surface that absorbs little infrared at a given wavelength also emits little. Shiny metals such as silver have very low emissivity in the thermal infrared while remaining transparent in thin layers in visible light. Placing a low-e surface facing the cavity of a double-glazed unit suppresses the radiative component of heat transfer across the cavity, significantly lowering the window's overall heat transfer coefficient (U-value) without reducing daylight much.

Types of coating

Hard-coat (pyrolytic) low-e coatings, usually tin oxide, are applied during glass manufacture while the glass is hot; they are durable and can be exposed, but have higher emissivity (about 0.15–0.20). Soft-coat (sputtered) coatings use one or more thin silver layers deposited in a vacuum; they achieve emissivities as low as 0.02–0.04 but are delicate and must be sealed inside an insulating glazing unit. Products are tuned either for cold climates — high solar gain, keeping winter sun and indoor heat — or for hot climates, where low solar gain coatings reject solar near-infrared while admitting visible light.

Other applications

Low-emissivity materials are also used in radiant barriers in roofs, insulating window films, emergency space blankets, and, in specialised form, on spacecraft and cryogenic equipment, where controlling radiative heat transfer is critical. Low-e treatments are a standard element of passive house and low-energy house glazing.
