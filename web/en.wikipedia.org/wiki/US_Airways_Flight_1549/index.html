US Airways Flight 1549 was a regularly scheduled US Airways flight from New York City's LaGuardia Airport to Charlotte and onward to Seattle, operated on January 15, 2009, by an Airbus A320. About three minutes into the flight, at an altitude of roughly 2,818 feet (859 m), the aircraft struck a flock of Canada geese, losing nearly all engine power. Unable to reach any airport for an emergency landing, pilots Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger and Jeffrey Skiles glided the plane to a ditching in the Hudson River off Midtown Manhattan. All 155 people on board were rescued by nearby boats, with only a few serious injuries. The event became known as the "Miracle on the Hudson".

Background

The flight was operated by US Airways using an Airbus A320-214 registered N106US. Captain Chesley Sullenberger, a former U.S. Air Force fighter pilot and glider pilot with about 20,000 hours of flight experience, was in command. First Officer Jeffrey Skiles had about 15,000 hours of experience, although the flight was only his second on the A320. There were 150 passengers and three flight attendants aboard.

Accident

Flight 1549 departed LaGuardia's Runway 4 at 3:25 p.m. EST. About two minutes after takeoff, at an altitude of approximately 2,818 feet, the aircraft struck a flock of Canada geese. Both engines ingested birds and lost thrust almost completely. Sullenberger took control while Skiles worked the emergency checklist for an engine restart.

After assessing the options, Sullenberger informed air traffic control that returning to LaGuardia or diverting to Teterboro Airport in New Jersey was impossible, telling the controller: "We're gonna be in the Hudson." The aircraft descended, passing over the George Washington Bridge at about 900 feet, and touched down on the Hudson River near West 48th Street at about 3:31 p.m.

Evacuation and rescue

The ditching was controlled enough that the fuselage remained intact and afloat. Passengers evacuated onto the wings and into inflatable slides and rafts. Ferries of NY Waterway were the first to arrive, followed by Coast Guard and New York City Fire Department vessels. All occupants were rescued within about 24 minutes of the ditching. Injuries were mostly minor, though five people were seriously injured, including a flight attendant who suffered a deep leg laceration.

Investigation and aftermath

The National Transportation Safety Board investigated the accident. Simul­ations showed that an immediate return to LaGuardia might have been possible only with no delay for decision-making; the board credited the crew's decisions and the performance of the aircraft's systems and structure. The aircraft was recovered from the river and is preserved at the Sullenberger Aviation Museum (formerly the Carolinas Aviation Museum) in Charlotte, North Carolina. The event was dramatized in the 2016 film Sully, directed by Clint Eastwood and starring Tom Hanks.
