Train wreck

A train wreck, or train crash, is a type of disaster involving one or more trains. Train wrecks include collisions between trains, derailments, and collisions with obstacles at level crossings. Despite their dramatic nature, rail travel is statistically among the safest forms of transport; serious wrecks are rare and are typically investigated by independent bodies to establish causes and prevent recurrence.

Causes

Major causes of train wrecks include human error — such as a driver passing a signal at danger or speeding on a curve — track and equipment failures, broken rails, bridge failures, sabotage, and obstacles on the line. The great majority of incidents are minor derailments in yards at low speed; catastrophic wrecks usually involve a combination of failures.

Notable wrecks

Among the deadliest accidents in history are the 2004 Sri Lanka tsunami train wreck, in which a train was swept away by the Indian Ocean tsunami, killing around 1,700 people; the 1917 Saint-Michel-de-Maurienne derailment in France; and the 1989 Ufa train disaster in the Soviet Union, caused by a gas pipeline explosion. Famous individual accidents include the 1895 Montparnasse derailment in Paris, photographed with the locomotive hanging from the station front, and the staged head-on collision arranged at Crush, Texas, in 1896 as a publicity spectacle, which killed spectators when the boilers exploded.

Investigation and prevention

Modern safety systems — automatic train protection, continuous signalling, event recorders ("black boxes"), and rigorous track inspection — have steadily reduced wreck frequency. Investigation agencies such as the UK's Rail Accident Investigation Branch and the US National Transportation Safety Board publish findings and safety recommendations.

In popular culture

The phrase "train wreck" is widely used metaphorically for any spectacular failure or chaotic situation. Deliberately staged wrecks were once a fairground attraction in the United States, and wreck scenes remain a staple of action cinema.
