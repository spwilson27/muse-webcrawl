Flag of Canada

Use: National flag and ensign
Proportion: 1:2
Adopted: February 15, 1965
Design: A vertical triband of red, white and red with a red maple leaf in the centre

The national flag of Canada, popularly referred to in Canadian English as the Canadian flag or, unofficially, as the maple leaf flag or l'Unifolié (French for "the one-leafed"), consists of a red field with a white square at its centre in the ratio of 1:2:1, in which is featured a stylized, red, 11-pointed maple leaf charged in the centre. It is the first flag to have been adopted by both houses of Parliament and formally proclaimed by the country's monarch as the official national flag.

The flag was adopted in 1965 to replace the Union Flag and the Canadian Red Ensign. Various designs were considered, following a parliamentary debate known as the Great Canadian Flag Debate. The design by George Stanley, Dean of Arts at the Royal Military College of Canada, was inspired by the college's flag. It was proclaimed by Queen Elizabeth II on January 28, 1965, and took effect on February 15 of that year — a date now celebrated as National Flag of Canada Day.

Design

The flag is twice as long as it is wide. The white square in the centre, called the Canadian pale, is flanked by two red vertical bands. The maple leaf, drawn by Jacques Saint-Cyr, has 11 points and is coloured in a shade officially described as "Canada red". The maple leaf had emerged as a national symbol of Canada by the 19th century, appearing on coins, military insignia during both world wars, and the coat of arms of 1921.

History

Before 1965, Canada used the Canadian Red Ensign, a British Red Ensign defaced with the Canadian coat of arms, in several versions from the 1870s onward. Calls for a distinctive national flag grew after the Second World War. In 1964, Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson committed his government to adopting a new flag in time for Canada's centennial in 1967, despite strong opposition from supporters of the Red Ensign. After months of debate and a filibuster, the House of Commons approved the maple leaf design on December 15, 1964; the Senate followed two days later.

Protocol

There is no law in Canada governing the use of the national flag by private citizens, but federal guidelines govern its use by government institutions. The flag is flown at federal buildings, airports, and military installations, and takes precedence over all other flags in Canada except the personal standards of the sovereign and the governor general.
