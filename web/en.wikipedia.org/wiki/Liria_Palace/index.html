Liria Palace

The Liria Palace (Spanish: Palacio de Liria) is a neoclassical palace in Madrid, Spain. It is the Madrid residence of the Dukes of Alba and contains a notable private art collection. The palace is located at 20–22 Calle de la Princesa, in the centre of the city.

History

The palace was built around 1770 to designs by the French architect Louis Guilbert for James Fitz-James Stuart, 3rd Duke of Berwick, who was also Duke of Liria and Jérica. In the early 19th century it passed by inheritance to the House of Alba. Eugénie de Montijo, the last Empress consort of France, died in exile at the palace in 1920.

Most of the palace was destroyed by fire during the Spanish Civil War, with the exception of its exterior facades. It was subsequently rebuilt by Jacobo Fitz-James Stuart, 17th Duke of Alba, with the collaboration of his daughter Cayetana Fitz-James Stuart, 18th Duchess of Alba, who was its main resident until her death in 2014. The rebuilding designs were by the British architect Sir Edwin Lutyens, who had been commissioned by the 17th Duke in 1921 to design part of the interior; his plans were realized after his death by the Spanish architect Manuel Cabanyes.

Collections

The palace houses a major private collection of European art, assembled by the House of Alba over centuries. It includes paintings by artists such as Fra Angelico, Titian, Rubens, Rembrandt, Goya, Renoir, and Chagall, as well as sculpture, tapestries, furniture, and a large library and archive containing historical documents, including letters of Christopher Columbus and the so-called Alba Bible of 1430.

Paintings and other works from the collection were traditionally not lent or exhibited outside the palace, but since the 2010s the family has allowed major exhibitions of the Alba collection in museums. In 2019 the palace opened to public tours for the first time.
