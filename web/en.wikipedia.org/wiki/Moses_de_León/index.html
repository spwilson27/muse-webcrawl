Moses de León (c. 1240 – 1305), in Hebrew Moshe ben Shem-Tov de León, was a Spanish rabbi and kabbalist. He is best known for circulating, in the 1280s and 1290s, the book that became the central text of Kabbalah: the Sefer ha-Zohar ("Book of Splendour"), which he attributed to the 2nd-century sage Shimon bar Yochai.

Life

Little is known of his biography. Born in León, he lived for much of his life in Guadalajara and Valladolid, moving in circles of Castilian kabbalists including Joseph Gikatilla, whose works he knew and admired. He earned a precarious living copying and selling manuscripts, and died in Arévalo in 1305 while travelling home.

The Zohar question

Moses presented the Zohar as an ancient midrash by the tanna Shimon bar Yochai, composed in an archaic Aramaic. Doubts about its antiquity were already voiced in his lifetime — the kabbalist Isaac of Acre investigated and reported conflicting testimony — but the work was broadly accepted for centuries and printed in the 16th century. Modern scholarship, led by Gershom Scholem, concluded from the Zohar's Aramaic (which contains medieval features and echoes of Moses's own Hebrew writings), its knowledge of medieval Spain, and its parallels with Moses's other books, that Moses de León himself was the principal author of the main body of the Zohar, working in a state of creative pseudepigraphy common in the period. He also wrote undisputed Hebrew kabbalistic works, including Sefer ha-Rimmon ("Book of the Pomegranate").

Legacy

Whatever its authorship, the Zohar became, with the Bible and Talmud, one of the canonical texts of Judaism, shaping Jewish mysticism, law, and liturgy for centuries.
