Weevils are beetles in the superfamily Curculionoidea, generally small, herbivorous insects recognised by their elongated snouts (rostra). With about 83,000 described species in over 6,800 genera, they form one of the largest groups of animals.

Description

Most weevils are under 6 mm long. The rostrum bears the mouthparts at its tip and elbowed, clubbed antennae partway along it. Females use the snout to bore holes in plant tissue in which to lay eggs. Many species feign death when disturbed.

Life cycle

Larvae are legless, grub-like and usually develop inside seeds, nuts, stems or roots, feeding within the host plant. Pupation also often occurs inside the plant material, and adults emerge by chewing their way out.

As pests

Several weevils are major agricultural and stored-product pests. The boll weevil devastated the American cotton industry; the granary and rice weevils infest stored grain worldwide; the black vine weevil damages ornamentals. Conversely, some weevils are deliberately released as biological control agents against invasive weeds.

Notable species

The giraffe weevil of Madagascar has an extraordinarily long neck. Palm weevils are pests of palms but their larvae are eaten as food in parts of Asia and Africa. Bark beetles, now classified among the weevils, kill vast areas of conifer forest.
