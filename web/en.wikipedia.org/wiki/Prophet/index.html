Prophet

In religion, a prophet is an individual who is regarded as being in contact with a divine being and is said to speak on behalf of that being, serving as an intermediary with humanity by delivering messages or teachings from the supernatural source to other people. The message delivered by a prophet may include the revelation of divine will, inspired teaching, or — in the sense most familiar in popular usage — a prediction of future events, a prophecy. The word derives from the Greek prophētēs, "one who speaks forth," itself a rendering of the Hebrew navi.

In the Hebrew Bible

In ancient Israel, prophets (nevi'im) were figures who received the word of God, often in ecstatic experience, and proclaimed it to kings and people. The Hebrew Bible's second division is named Nevi'im (Prophets) and includes both narrative books about early prophets such as Samuel, Elijah, and Elisha and the collected oracles of the "writing prophets" — Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the twelve minor prophets. Their messages emphasised covenant fidelity, social justice, and the consequences of disobedience more than fortune-telling, though prediction and symbolic acts also feature prominently.

In Islam

In Islam, prophets (Arabic: anbiyāʾ) are men chosen by God to convey His message. The Qur'an names twenty-five, including Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus. Muhammad is regarded as the final prophet — the "Seal of the Prophets" — so mainstream Islam rejects the possibility of later prophets. A distinction is often drawn between a nabi (prophet) and a rasul (messenger), the latter bringing a revealed scripture or law.

In Christianity

Christianity inherits the Hebrew prophets and applies the title to John the Baptist; Jesus is also described as a prophet, though Christian doctrine holds him to be far more. The New Testament lists prophecy among the gifts of the Spirit, and prophetic movements have recurred throughout Christian history, from Montanism in the second century to modern Pentecostal and charismatic churches, where prophecy is widely practised.

Other traditions

Prophet-like figures appear in many religions: Zoroaster in Zoroastrianism, the Sikh Gurus, Joseph Smith in the Latter Day Saint movement, and Baháʼu'lláh in the Baháʼí Faith, whose theology speaks of progressive revelation through successive "Manifestations of God." Comparative scholars caution that the category is shaped by the Abrahamic model and fits other traditions only loosely.
