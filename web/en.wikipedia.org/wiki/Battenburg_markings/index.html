Battenburg markings

Battenburg markings are high-visibility vehicle livery patterns used on emergency vehicles in the United Kingdom and several other European countries. The pattern consists of alternating rectangles of two colours — typically fluorescent yellow-green and blue for police, green and yellow for ambulances, and red and yellow for fire services — arranged along the side of the vehicle.

Development

The markings were developed in the 1990s by the Police Scientific Development Branch of the UK Home Office, named after the resemblance to the cross-section of Battenberg cake. Research showed the large blocks of colour greatly improved the visibility and recognition of emergency vehicles in both daylight and night conditions compared with earlier liveries.

Use

Battenburg markings are retro-reflective and are combined with fluorescent colours for daytime conspicuity. A half-Battenburg variant uses a single row of blocks. The pattern is standard on British police traffic cars, ambulances, and fire appliances, and has been adopted in Ireland, Belgium, Sweden, and elsewhere.
