Fort Philip Kearny was a United States Army outpost in north-eastern Wyoming, established in 1866 along the Bozeman Trail during Red Cloud's War. Named for Civil War general Philip Kearny, it was the largest of three forts built to protect emigrants travelling to the Montana goldfields through the Powder River Country, land guaranteed to the Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho by the Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1851.

Red Cloud's War

The fort's garrison was besieged almost continuously by Lakota and Cheyenne warriors. On 21 December 1866, a detachment under Captain William J. Fetterman was lured beyond Lodge Trail Ridge and annihilated by a force led by warriors including Crazy Horse — the Fetterman Fight, the Army's worst defeat on the Northern Plains until Little Bighorn. In August 1867 the Wagon Box Fight and Hayfield Fight saw soldiers with new breech-loading rifles repel attacks near the fort.

Abandonment

Under the Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1868, the United States agreed to abandon the Bozeman Trail forts. Troops withdrew in August 1868, and Cheyenne warriors burned the empty fort to the ground shortly afterwards. The site is now a Wyoming state historic site, with a reconstructed stockade and interpretive centre.
