Hilde Mangold

Hilde Mangold (20 October 1898 – 4 September 1924), née Proescholdt, was a German embryologist whose doctoral experiments in Hans Spemann's laboratory in Freiburg led to the discovery of the embryonic organizer — a region of the embryo that directs the development of surrounding tissues. The work earned Spemann the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1935; Mangold herself died before she could share in the recognition.

The organizer experiment

For her dissertation, Mangold performed delicate transplantations between embryos of two newt species with differently coloured tissues. She grafted the dorsal lip of the blastopore from one gastrula into another host embryo. The graft caused the host to develop a second body axis, producing a conjoined twin larva largely formed from host tissue — demonstrating that the grafted region had "organized" the surrounding cells' development.

Life and death

Mangold published the results with Spemann in 1924, in a paper that became one of the most influential in developmental biology. In September that year she died at age 25 from severe burns after a gas water-heater explosion in her home. Modern commentary has frequently noted that, had she lived, she plausibly would have shared Spemann's Nobel Prize; the organizer region is still often called the Spemann–Mangold organizer.
