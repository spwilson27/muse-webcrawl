Marine propulsion

Marine propulsion is the mechanism or system used to generate thrust to move a ship or boat across water. While paddles and sails were the first forms of propulsion, virtually all modern vessels use mechanical systems consisting of an electric motor or internal combustion engine driving a propeller, or less frequently, in pump-jets, an impeller. Steam propulsion was the mainstay of marine transport in the nineteenth century, giving way to diesel engines in the twentieth.

Engines

Large commercial ships are usually powered by slow-speed two-stroke diesel engines that burn heavy fuel oil and drive a single large fixed-pitch propeller directly. Medium-speed four-stroke diesels, often coupled through gearboxes or driving generators in diesel-electric arrangements, are common on ferries, cruise ships and offshore vessels. Gas turbines are used where high power-to-weight ratio matters, particularly in naval vessels, and steam turbines survive mainly in nuclear-powered ships and liquefied natural gas carriers.

Propulsors

  • Fixed-pitch propellers — simple and robust, standard on most cargo ships.
  • Controllable-pitch propellers — blade angle can be varied to reverse thrust without reversing the engine.
  • Azipods — electric propulsion units housed in steerable pods beneath the hull, giving high manoeuvrability.
  • Waterjets — used on fast ferries and patrol craft, drawing water in and expelling it at high speed.
  • Voith Schneider propellers — cycloidal drives used on tugs and ferries requiring precise thrust control.

Alternative fuels and future developments

Pressure to reduce greenhouse gas emissions has revived interest in wind assistance, including rotor sails and rigid wings, and in alternative fuels such as liquefied natural gas, methanol, ammonia and hydrogen, as well as battery-electric propulsion for short routes. Nuclear propulsion remains confined largely to military submarines and aircraft carriers, and a small number of icebreakers.

Marine propulsion is an essential complement to the work of marines and navies, and to the ecosystems studied in marine biology.
