Amitābha

Sanskritअमिताभ ("Infinite Light")
RealmSukhāvatī, the Western Pure Land
Venerated inMahayana Buddhism; Pure Land Buddhism

Amitābha (Sanskrit for "Infinite Light"), also known as Amitāyus ("Infinite Life"), is a celestial Buddha described in the scriptures of Mahayana Buddhism. He is the principal Buddha of Pure Land Buddhism and presides over Sukhāvatī, the Western Pure Land.

Doctrine

According to the Larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life, Amitābha was once a monk named Dharmākara who made forty-eight vows, promising to establish a pure land where beings could be reborn through faith and the recitation of his name, and there attain enlightenment. In East Asian Pure Land practice, devotees chant the nembutsu — "Namo Amitābha Buddha" in Sanskrit, Namu Amida Butsu in Japanese.

Iconography

Amitābha is usually depicted in meditation or with the vitarka or karuṇa mudrā, often flanked by the bodhisattvas Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Famous images include the Great Buddha of Kamakura.
