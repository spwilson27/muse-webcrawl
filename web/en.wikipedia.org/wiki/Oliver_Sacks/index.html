Oliver Sacks

Oliver Wolf Sacks (9 July 1933 – 30 August 2015) was a British neurologist, naturalist, historian of science, and writer. Born in London, he spent most of his career in New York City. He is best known for his popular books of case studies of people with neurological disorders, including Awakenings (1973) and The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Hat (1985).

Life and career

Sacks studied medicine at Oxford and moved to the United States in the 1960s. At Beth Abraham Hospital in the Bronx, he treated survivors of the encephalitis lethargica epidemic with the new drug L-DOPA, an experience described in Awakenings, later adapted into a film starring Robin Williams and Robert De Niro. He taught at Albert Einstein College of Medicine and later at New York University School of Medicine.

Writing

Sacks' case histories combined clinical observation with empathy and literary narrative, exploring conditions such as Tourette syndrome, autism, prosopagnosia, and musical hallucinations. His other books include An Anthropologist on Mars, Musicophilia, The Mind's Eye, and the memoir On the Move. He was a frequent contributor to The New Yorker and The New York Review of Books.

Death and legacy

Sacks died of metastatic melanoma in 2015, having announced his terminal diagnosis in a widely read essay. He was appointed a CBE in 2008. His work is credited with humanising neurology and inspiring generations of clinicians and writers.
