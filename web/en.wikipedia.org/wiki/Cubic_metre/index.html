Cubic metre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

One cubic metre is the volume of a cube with edges one metre long.

The cubic metre (symbol: m3) is the SI derived unit of volume. It is defined as the volume of a cube whose edges measure one metre. The cubic metre is the standard unit for measuring large volumes of liquids, gases, and bulk materials in science, engineering, construction, and trade; in the United States the spelling "cubic meter" is used.

Conversions

  • 1 m3 = 1,000 litres
  • 1 m3 = 1,000,000 cubic centimetres (cm3)
  • 1 m3 ≈ 35.31 cubic feet
  • 1 m3 ≈ 1.308 cubic yards
  • 1 m3 ≈ 264.17 US gallons
  • One cubic metre of pure water at its maximum density (about 3.98 °C) has a mass of almost exactly one tonne (1,000 kg).

Usage

Cubic metres are used for water and natural gas consumption billing, concrete and earthworks quantities, shipping and storage capacity, and the displacement of vessels. Multiples and submultiples follow the usual SI prefixes, though in practice the cubic centimetre (also called a millilitre for liquids) and the cubic millimetre are the most common submultiples, while very large volumes such as reservoir capacities are often expressed in millions of cubic metres or cubic kilometres.

A related traditional unit is the stere, one cubic metre of stacked firewood, still used in parts of Europe. In oceanography and climatology, the sverdrup — one million cubic metres per second — measures ocean current transport.
