The Blues Brothers are an American blues and soul revivalist band founded in 1976 by comedians Dan Aykroyd and John Belushi as part of a musical sketch on Saturday Night Live. Performing as brothers "Joliet" Jake and Elwood Blues — in black suits, hats and sunglasses — they fronted a band of distinguished session and soul musicians, achieved chart success, and starred in the 1980 film The Blues Brothers.

Formation

Aykroyd, a blues enthusiast, developed the characters with Belushi after the two jammed with the house band at a bar Aykroyd rented in New York. With musical director Paul Shaffer, they recruited members of the Saturday Night Live band and Stax Records veterans: guitarists Steve Cropper and Matt "Guitar" Murphy, bassist Donald "Duck" Dunn, drummer Willie Hall, keyboardist Murphy Dunne, and the horn section of Lou Marini, Alan Rubin and Tom Malone. The act debuted on Saturday Night Live in 1978, warming up the studio audience before appearing on air.

Recording success

Their debut album, Briefcase Full of Blues (1978), recorded live, reached number one on the Billboard 200 and included hit versions of "Soul Man" and "Rubber Biscuit". The 1980 film soundtrack and Made in America followed, with songs such as "Gimme Some Lovin'" charting. The band toured widely and helped revive interest in blues, soul and R&B artists including Ray Charles, Aretha Franklin, James Brown and Cab Calloway, all of whom appeared in the film.

After Belushi

John Belushi died in 1982, ending the original partnership. Aykroyd later revived the act with other vocalists, including John Goodman and Jim Belushi, for the film Blues Brothers 2000 (1998) and the House of Blues franchise, which Aykroyd co-founded. A line-up billed as the Blues Brothers Band, featuring original members such as Cropper and Marini, has continued to tour internationally.
