Children of Dune

Children of Dune is a 1976 science fiction novel by American author Frank Herbert, the third in his Dune series of six novels. Initially selling well, it was the first hardcover best-seller in the history of the science fiction genre. The novel follows the nine-year-old twins Leto II and Ghanima Atreides, the children of Paul Atreides, as they contend for the future of the desert planet Arrakis against their aunt, the regent Alia, who is succumbing to possession by the ancestral memory of Baron Vladimir Harkonnen.

Plot summary

Nine years after the events of Dune Messiah, Paul Atreides has vanished into the desert, blind, and his sister Alia rules the Imperium as regent for Paul's twin children, Leto II and Ghanima. Both twins are "pre-born", possessing the memories of their ancestors from birth, and both are threatened by Alia's descent into Abomination — possession by the persona of her grandfather, the Baron Harkonnen. Meanwhile, the exiled House Corrino, led by Princess Wensicia, plots to assassinate the twins using trained Laza tigers.

The twins allow the assassination attempt to play out as cover for Leto to disappear into the desert, where he encounters the mysterious Preacher — rumoured to be Paul himself — and undergoes a transformation, binding sandtrout to his skin and beginning his metamorphosis into a human-sandworm hybrid. Leto returns to seize the throne from Alia, who dies by her own hand, and embarks on the Golden Path that will dominate the next 3,500 years of human history, setting up the events of God Emperor of Dune.

Adaptation

The novel, together with Dune Messiah, was adapted as the Emmy Award-winning 2003 Sci Fi Channel miniseries Frank Herbert's Children of Dune, starring Alec Newman as Paul and James McAvoy as Leto II.
