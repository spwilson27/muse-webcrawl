Debir

Debir (Hebrew: דְּבִיר) is a name occurring in the Hebrew Bible, referring both to ancient towns in the land of Canaan and to a king of Eglon. The best-known Debir was a Canaanite city in the hill country of Judah, also called Kiriath-Sepher ("City of the Book") and Kiriath-Sannah.

In the Bible

According to the Book of Joshua, Debir was captured during the Israelite conquest of Canaan. The narrative in Joshua 15 and Judges 1 relates that Caleb offered his daughter Achsah in marriage to whoever would capture the city; his nephew (or younger kinsman) Othniel took it and married her. Othniel later became the first of the biblical judges. Debir is listed among the cities allotted to the tribe of Judah and later assigned to the priests (the sons of Aaron).

Another Debir is mentioned east of the Jordan near the territory of Gad, and a third appears as a boundary point in the northern territory of Judah.

Identification

The Debir of Judah is commonly identified with the archaeological site of Khirbet Rabud, southwest of Hebron, though Tell Beit Mirsim was proposed in earlier scholarship. The name Kiriath-Sepher, "City of the Book," has prompted speculation that the town was a center of writing or record-keeping in the Late Bronze Age.
