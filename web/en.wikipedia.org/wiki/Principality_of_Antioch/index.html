The Principality of Antioch was one of the crusader states created during the First Crusade, encompassing territory around the city of Antioch in the Levant (modern southern Turkey and north-western Syria). Founded in 1098 by Bohemond of Taranto of the Hauteville family, it endured, with interruptions, until the Mamluk sultan Baibars destroyed Antioch in 1268.

History

After a gruelling eight-month siege, the crusaders captured Antioch in June 1098 and then withstood a counter-siege by Kerbogha of Mosul. Bohemond, who had sworn to return former Byzantine territory to Emperor Alexios I, kept the city for himself, creating lasting friction with Byzantium. His nephew Tancred acted as regent during Bohemond's captivity and after his departure for Europe.

The principality's history was one of constant warfare with Muslim powers — Aleppo, the Artuqids, and later Nur ad-Din and Saladin — and of alternating submission to and defiance of Byzantine emperors; in 1138 and 1159 emperors John II and Manuel I entered Antioch as overlords. A disastrous defeat at the Battle of Inab in 1149 cost Prince Raymond of Poitiers his life. The principality's longest-serving ruler, Bohemond III, maintained it through the era of the Third Crusade.

Fall

Weakened by dynastic disputes with Armenia and the loss of territory, the principality became a shadow centred on Antioch itself. In May 1268 the Mamluk sultan Baibars stormed the city; the massacre and destruction were so complete that Antioch never recovered its former significance, ending the principality's 170-year existence.
