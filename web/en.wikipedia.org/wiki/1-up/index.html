1-up
A 1-up grants the player an extra life

A 1-up (pronounced "one-up") is an item in a video game that grants the player character an extra life, allowing play to continue after the character would otherwise have been defeated. The term is most often associated with arcade games and platform games of the 1980s and 1990s, and is exemplified by the green 1-Up Mushroom of the Mario series.

Terminology

The related display term "1UP" originated on pinball machines and arcade cabinets, where it labelled the first player's score ("player one up", i.e. player one is up to play). In arcade games the "1UP" and "2UP" markers blinked to indicate whose turn it was. The phrase passed into general gaming vocabulary, and "1-up" came to denote the extra-life item or event itself. The verb "to one-up" someone — to outdo a rival — predates video games and has a separate history.

Use in games

1-ups are typically awarded for collecting a special item, accumulating a threshold score, or collecting a set number of common items, such as 100 coins or rings. In Super Mario Bros. (1985) the green-spotted 1-Up Mushroom became the most recognisable form of the item. Because extra lives were a scarce resource in games designed around limited attempts, 1-ups were often hidden in secret areas as a reward for exploration.

As game design moved away from finite lives toward checkpoint and respawn systems, the 1-up became less common, surviving mainly in genres that retain a lives system and in retro-styled games.
