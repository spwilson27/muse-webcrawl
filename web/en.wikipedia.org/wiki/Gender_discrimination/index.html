Gender discrimination

[Image placeholder: Equal rights campaign]

Gender discrimination is unequal or disadvantaging treatment of an individual or group based on their gender or sex. It encompasses practices in employment, education, healthcare, law, and politics that systematically advantage one gender over another. Sexism — prejudice or discrimination based on a person's sex or gender — is the ideology underlying much of this treatment.

Forms

In employment, gender discrimination appears in hiring, promotion, pay (the gender pay gap), and occupational segregation, as well as in sexual harassment. Historically, legal discrimination included women's exclusion from voting, property ownership, higher education, and many professions; most explicit legal barriers in Western countries were dismantled during the 20th century, but de facto disparities persist. Men can also face gender-based disadvantage, for example in family courts, military conscription, and higher workplace fatality rates.

Law and policy

International law addresses gender discrimination through instruments such as the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW, 1979). National measures include the U.S. Civil Rights Act of 1964 (Title VII) and Equal Pay Act of 1963, the UK Equality Act 2010, and similar statutes worldwide. Enforcement agencies and courts continue to adjudicate questions about pay equity, pregnancy discrimination, and the treatment of transgender employees.
