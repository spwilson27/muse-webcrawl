Districts of Hong Kong

The 18 districts of Hong Kong.

Hong Kong is divided into 18 districts, each administered by a District Council. The districts were established in 1982 under the District Administration Scheme introduced by Governor Murray MacLehose, to improve co-ordination of government services and encourage public participation in local affairs. They are grouped into three regions: Hong Kong Island (4 districts), Kowloon (5 districts) and the New Territories (9 districts).

List of districts

Hong Kong Island comprises Central and Western, Eastern, Southern and Wan Chai districts. Kowloon comprises Kowloon City, Kwun Tong, Sham Shui Po, Wong Tai Sin and Yau Tsim Mong. The New Territories comprise Islands, Kwai Tsing, North, Sai Kung, Sha Tin, Tai Po, Tsuen Wan, Tuen Mun and Yuen Long districts. Population is unevenly distributed: Kwun Tong is among the most densely populated, while the Islands District, which includes Lantau, is the largest by area.

District Councils

District Councils advise the government on local matters and oversee community facilities. Their composition and method of election have changed several times; reforms in 2023 reduced the proportion of directly elected seats and introduced appointed and ex-officio members. Each district is also served by a District Office of the Home Affairs Department, headed by a District Officer.
