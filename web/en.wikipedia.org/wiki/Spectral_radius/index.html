Spectral radius

In mathematics, the spectral radius of a square matrix is the maximum of the absolute values of its eigenvalues. More generally, the spectral radius of a bounded linear operator on a Banach space is the supremum of the absolute values of the elements of its spectrum. It is commonly denoted by ρ(·).

Definition for matrices

Let λ1, …, λn be the (real or complex) eigenvalues of a matrix A ∈ Cn×n. Then its spectral radius is defined as

ρ(A) = max { |λ1|, …, |λn| }.

The spectral radius is a kind of measure of the magnitude of a matrix. For a normal matrix, the spectral radius equals the largest singular value; in general it is less than or equal to any matrix norm of A.

Gelfand's formula

Gelfand's formula, named after Israel Gelfand, expresses the spectral radius as a limit involving any matrix norm ‖·‖:

ρ(A) = limk→∞ ‖Ak‖1/k.

An important consequence is that the powers Ak converge to zero as k → ∞ if and only if ρ(A) < 1. This criterion underlies the convergence theory of iterative methods for solving linear systems, such as the Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel methods, and the stability analysis of discrete-time linear dynamical systems: a discrete system is asymptotically stable exactly when the spectral radius of its transition matrix is less than one.

Bounded operators

For a bounded linear operator T on a complex Banach space, the spectrum σ(T) is a non-empty compact subset of the complex plane, and the spectral radius is the radius of the smallest closed disc centred at the origin containing σ(T). Gelfand's formula extends to this setting, and the spectral radius formula is a key part of the Gelfand theory of Banach algebras.

Graphs

The spectral radius of a finite graph is defined as the spectral radius of its adjacency matrix; by the Perron–Frobenius theorem, for a connected graph it is a simple eigenvalue with a positive eigenvector, and it is bounded between the average degree and the maximum degree of the graph.
