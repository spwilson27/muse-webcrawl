Gosplan

Gosplan (Russian: Госплан) was the committee responsible for economic planning in the Soviet Union. The name is an abbreviation of Gosudarstvenny planovy komitet, the State Planning Committee. It was established in February 1921 and operated until the dissolution of the USSR in 1991.

Gosplan's most famous role was in devising and administering the series of five-year plans, the first of which was launched in 1928 under Stalin. Working with the central ministries and the statistical administration, Gosplan set production targets for every sector of the economy, allocating resources by the method of "material balances".

The institution grew into a vast bureaucracy employing thousands of economists and planners. Its headquarters in Moscow — a monumental building on Okhotny Ryad — later housed the Russian State Duma. Critics of Soviet planning argued that Gosplan's rigid targets encouraged waste and misreporting, while defenders credited it with coordinating rapid industrialisation. After 1991, Gosplan's functions were absorbed by successor bodies in the Russian government.
