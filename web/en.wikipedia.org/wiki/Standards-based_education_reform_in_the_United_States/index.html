Standards-based education reform in the United States

Standards-based education reform in the United States is a movement to improve schools by setting explicit academic standards for what students should know and be able to do, aligning curriculum and assessment to those standards, and holding schools accountable for results.

History

The movement gained momentum after the 1983 report A Nation at Risk and was embodied in federal laws such as Goals 2000, the No Child Left Behind Act of 2001, and the Every Student Succeeds Act of 2015. States adopted standards in mathematics and English language arts, most prominently the Common Core State Standards from 2010.

Criticism

Supporters credit the reforms with transparency and attention to achievement gaps, while critics argue they encouraged teaching to the test, narrowed curricula, and imposed top-down control on classrooms.
