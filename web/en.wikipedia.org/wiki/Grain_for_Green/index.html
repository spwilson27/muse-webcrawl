Grain for Green

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Former sloping cropland converted to terraces and woodland on the Loess Plateau.

Grain for Green (Chinese: 退耕还林, Tuìgēng Huánlín, "Returning Farmland to Forest") is a large-scale ecological restoration programme launched by the government of China in 1999. It pays farmers to convert steep, erosion-prone marginal cropland into forest or grassland, compensating them with grain, cash subsidies, and free seedlings. It is among the largest payments-for-ecosystem-services schemes ever undertaken.

Background and scope

The programme followed the devastating Yangtze floods of 1998, which were blamed in part on deforestation and cultivation of steep slopes, and severe dust storms and siltation of the Yellow River from erosion on the Loess Plateau. Pilots began in 1999 in Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu, and the programme expanded nationally from 2002. By the end of its first phase, tens of millions of hectares of cropland and barren land had been enrolled and hundreds of millions of rural residents reached.

Effects

Studies credit Grain for Green with significantly increased vegetation cover — contributing to the widely reported "greening" of the Loess Plateau — sharply reduced soil erosion and sediment loads in the Yellow River, and expanded forest carbon stocks. Effects on farmers were mixed: many households shifted labour into off-farm work and orchards, and incomes generally rose, though researchers have raised concerns about the permanence of conversions when subsidies end, the planting of monoculture tree stands with high water demand, and localised impacts on food production.
