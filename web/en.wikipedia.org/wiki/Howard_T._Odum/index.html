Howard T. Odum

Howard Thomas Odum (September 1, 1924 – September 11, 2002) was an American ecologist known for his pioneering work in ecosystem ecology. He developed the concept of emergy (embodied energy), the energy systems language, and influential ideas about maximum power and ecological engineering.

Life and career

Odum was born in Durham, North Carolina, the son of sociologist Howard W. Odum. He earned his PhD at Yale University in 1951 under G. Evelyn Hutchinson. With his brother Eugene Odum, he studied the coral reef ecosystem at Eniwetok Atoll, demonstrating how ecosystems could be analysed through energy flows. He spent most of his career at the University of Florida, where he founded the Center for Environmental Policy and the Center for Wetlands.

Contributions

Odum's book Environment, Power, and Society (1971) and his emergy synthesis attempted to express all ecological and economic processes in common energy units (solar emjoules). His energy circuit language became an early form of systems modelling. With his brother Eugene he received the Crafoord Prize in 1987.
