Fade margin

In telecommunications, the fade margin (also called fading margin) is the difference, in decibels, between the normal received signal power and the minimum power level at which a receiver can still operate correctly — for example, maintaining a specified bit error rate. It is an allowance built into a link budget to account for the statistical variability of signal strength caused by fading, ensuring that the link remains usable for a desired percentage of time.

Definition

Formally, fade margin F = Pr − Pmin, where Pr is the predicted (unfaded) received power and Pmin is the receiver sensitivity threshold. A link with a 20 dB fade margin can tolerate a 20 dB fade — a hundredfold drop in received power — before service is lost.

Sources of fading

  • Multipath fading — interference between signals arriving over multiple paths, prominent in mobile and urban environments (Rayleigh or Rician statistics).
  • Rain fade — attenuation by precipitation, which grows severe at Ku- and Ka-band satellite frequencies.
  • Atmospheric ducting, scintillation, and absorption — refraction and turbulence effects on terrestrial microwave links.
  • Shadowing — obstruction by terrain, buildings, or vegetation.

Design practice

Engineers choose a fade margin based on the required link availability (for example, 99.99% of the year) and empirical fading models for the frequency band, path length, and climate. Terrestrial microwave links commonly use fade margins of 20–40 dB; satellite links in rain-prone regions at high frequencies may need large margins or compensating techniques such as uplink power control, adaptive coding and modulation, and site diversity. Excessive margin wastes power and capacity, so margin selection is a trade-off between cost and reliability.

Related concepts

Fade margin is one component of a complete link budget, alongside free-space path loss, antenna gains, cable losses, and system noise. Closely related terms include link margin (the total margin above threshold), rain margin (the fade margin allocated specifically to precipitation), and Eb/N0 margin used in digital system analysis.
