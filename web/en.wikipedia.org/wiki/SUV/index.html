A sport utility vehicle (SUV) is a category of car combining elements of road-going passenger automobiles with features from off-road vehicles, such as raised ground clearance, a tall upright body and often four-wheel drive. Traditional SUVs were built on light-truck chassis with body-on-frame construction, whereas the related "crossover" SUVs that dominate current sales use car-like unibody platforms.

History

The type descends from military and commercial off-road vehicles such as the Second World War Jeep and the early Land Rover, and from civilian derivatives like the Jeep Wagoneer (1963), the Range Rover (1970) and the Toyota Land Cruiser. SUVs grew rapidly in popularity in the United States during the 1990s and 2000s — helped by the Ford Explorer and Jeep Grand Cherokee — and by the late 2010s they and their crossover relatives accounted for around half of new car sales in the US and a large and growing share worldwide.

Criticism

SUVs have been criticised for their higher fuel consumption and carbon emissions relative to ordinary cars, for their greater danger to pedestrians and occupants of smaller vehicles in collisions due to their mass and height, and for contributing to congestion and tyre and brake particulate pollution. The International Energy Agency has noted that the growth of SUVs was one of the largest contributors to rising global CO2 emissions in the 2010s. Electric SUVs have proliferated in the 2020s, reducing tailpipe emissions though not the size-related concerns.

Types

The market is segmented into mini, subcompact, compact, mid-size and full-size SUVs, ranging from small urban crossovers to large three-row vehicles, with coupé-styled and luxury variants at premium price points.
