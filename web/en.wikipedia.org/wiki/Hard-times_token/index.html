Hard-times token

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hard-times tokens are large-cent-sized copper tokens struck privately in the United States from about 1832 to 1844. They circulated as small change during the coin shortage that followed the Panic of 1837 and served both as emergency currency and as vehicles for advertising and political satire.

Background

President Andrew Jackson's destruction of the Second Bank of the United States, the Specie Circular of 1836 and the Panic of 1837 led banks to suspend specie payments in May 1837. Federal coins were hoarded and vanished from circulation, so merchants and manufacturers issued their own copper tokens, roughly the size of the large cent, which passed at face value in local trade.

Designs

Many tokens were political, mocking Jackson and his successor Martin Van Buren: famous types show a running jackass with the legend "I follow in the steps of my illustrious predecessor", a ship named Experiment wrecked on rocks, or a phoenix rising from flames. Others simply advertised businesses such as hat sellers, hardware dealers and taverns. The standard reference catalogues, begun by Lyman Low, organise the series by "Low numbers".

End of the series

As federal coinage returned to circulation and laws against private token coinage took effect in the 1840s and 1850s, hard-times tokens disappeared from everyday use. They remain popular with collectors of American exonumia for their variety and their vivid political commentary on the Jacksonian era.
