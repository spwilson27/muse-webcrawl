Afghan Civil War (1992–1996)
Date: 1992–1996
Location: Afghanistan
Result: Taliban capture of Kabul, 1996
Belligerents: Islamic State of Afghanistan; Hezb-e Islami; Taliban (from 1994); various militias

The Afghan Civil War of 1992–1996 was a conflict that followed the collapse of the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan in April 1992, when mujahideen factions that had fought the Soviet-backed government turned on one another for control of the country.

Course of the war

The Peshawar Accord created an Islamic State of Afghanistan under Burhanuddin Rabbani, with Ahmad Shah Massoud as defence minister, but Gulbuddin Hekmatyar's Hezb-e Islami and other factions contested Kabul. Years of rocket attacks and shifting alliances devastated the capital and killed tens of thousands of civilians. The warlord Abdul Rashid Dostum's forces changed sides repeatedly.

Rise of the Taliban

In late 1994 the Taliban movement emerged from Kandahar, promising order, and advanced rapidly with support from Pakistan. They captured Kabul in September 1996, ending this phase of the war, though fighting against the Northern Alliance continued.
