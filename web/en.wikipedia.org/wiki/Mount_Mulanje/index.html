Mount Mulanje, also called the Mulanje Massif, is a great granite inselberg in southern Malawi, rising abruptly from the plains near Blantyre some 65 km east of the city. Covering about 650 km², it is the highest ground in south-central Africa, its summit, Sapitwa Peak (3,002 m), being Malawi's highest point; its name means "do not go there" in Chewa.

Geology and ecology

The massif consists of syenite and granite intruded into surrounding Precambrian gneisses about 130 million years ago, weathered into dramatic sheer-sided peaks separated by deep valleys. Its altitude generates a wet montane climate that supports Afromontane forest, including the endemic Mulanje cedar (Widdringtonia whytei), Malawi's national tree, along with numerous endemic plants, the Mulanje chameleon and other unique fauna. The area is designated a forest reserve and an Important Bird Area, and has been proposed as a UNESCO World Heritage site for its cultural landscape.

Human use

Local Lomwe and Mang'anja traditions hold the mountain sacred, abode of ancestral spirits. It is a popular trekking and climbing destination served by a network of mountain huts, while its foothills are famous for tea estates around Mulanje town. The cedar forests, badly depleted by logging, are the object of continuing conservation and replanting efforts.
