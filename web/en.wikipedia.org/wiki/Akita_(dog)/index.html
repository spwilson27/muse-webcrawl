The Akita (秋田犬, Akita-inu or Akita-ken) is a Japanese dog breed of large size. Originating from the mountains of northern Japan, the Akita has a short double coat similar to that of many other northern spitz breeds. Historically, the breed was used for hunting in packs, able to track and hold at bay large game such as bear, wild boar and deer.

The Akita is a powerful, dominant and loyal breed, commonly aloof with strangers but highly affectionate and loyal with family members. Two varieties are recognised: the Japanese Akita and the American Akita, the latter developed from dogs brought to the United States after the Second World War. The breed is perhaps best known internationally through the story of Hachikō, a dog celebrated for his remarkable loyalty to his deceased owner.

History

The breed takes its name from Akita Prefecture in northern Japan, where it is believed to have originated. The ancestors of the modern Akita, known as Matagi dogs, were used by the Matagi hunters of the region to track large game in deep snow. Over time the dogs were also used in dog fighting, and crosses with larger breeds such as the Tosa and mastiffs increased their size. In the early twentieth century, Japanese breeders worked to restore the breed to its original spitz type, and in 1931 the Akita was officially recognised as a natural monument of Japan.

Helen Keller is credited with bringing the first Akitas to the United States in 1937. After the Second World War, American servicemen returning from Japan brought more dogs home, and these formed the basis of the American Akita, which developed along somewhat different lines from the Japanese type.

Characteristics

The Akita is a large, powerful dog with a heavy bone structure, a broad head forming a blunt triangle, small triangular erect ears, and a tail that curls over the back. The double coat consists of a dense, soft undercoat and a straight, harsh outer coat. Recognised colours include red fawn, sesame, brindle and white. The breed is known for its dignity, courage and loyalty, but it requires firm, consistent training and socialisation, and is often intolerant of other dogs, particularly of the same sex.

Health

Akitas are generally healthy but are prone to certain conditions, including hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism, progressive retinal atrophy, and autoimmune disorders such as sebaceous adenitis and Vogt–Koyanagi–Harada-like syndrome. Gastric dilation-volvulus (bloat) is also a concern in the breed.

Hachikō

Hachikō (1923–1935) was an Akita who waited at Shibuya Station in Tokyo every day for nearly ten years following the death of his owner, Professor Hidesaburō Ueno. His story became a national symbol of loyalty in Japan, and a statue of Hachikō stands outside Shibuya Station today.
