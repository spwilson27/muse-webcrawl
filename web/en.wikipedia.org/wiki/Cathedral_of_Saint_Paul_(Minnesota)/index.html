Cathedral of Saint Paul (Minnesota)

The Cathedral of Saint Paul is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Saint Paul, Minnesota. It is the co-cathedral of the Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis, along with the Basilica of Saint Mary in Minneapolis, and a National Shrine of the Apostle Paul.

Cathedral of Saint Paul
Location: Saint Paul, Minnesota
Denomination: Roman Catholic
Architect: Emmanuel Louis Masqueray
Style: Beaux-Arts / Renaissance Revival
Groundbreaking: 1907; Opened: 1915
Height: 306 ft (93 m)

History and architecture

The present cathedral, the fourth to bear the name, was designed by French Beaux-Arts architect Emmanuel Louis Masqueray under Archbishop John Ireland. Built of granite and travertine, its copper-clad dome rises 306 feet. The interior seats about 3,000 and features stained glass, statues of the four evangelists, and a Shrine of the Nations honouring immigrant communities.

Significance

Overlooking downtown Saint Paul from Summit Hill, it is one of the most recognisable landmarks in Minnesota and was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1974.
