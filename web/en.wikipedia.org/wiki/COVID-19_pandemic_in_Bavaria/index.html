COVID-19 pandemic in Bavaria

Disease: COVID-19
Virus strain: SARS-CoV-2
Location: Bavaria, Germany
First confirmed case: 27 January 2020, near Munich

The COVID-19 pandemic in Bavaria was part of the worldwide pandemic of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by SARS-CoV-2. Bavaria was the first German state in which a case of the virus was confirmed: on 27 January 2020 an employee of the automotive supplier Webasto in the district of Starnberg, near Munich, tested positive after contact with a colleague visiting from China — one of the first documented person-to-person transmissions outside China.

Course of the pandemic

After the initial Webasto cluster was contained, cases rose sharply in March 2020, driven in part by returning ski holidaymakers and carnival events. On 16 March the Bavarian government under Minister-President Markus Söder declared a state of disaster (Katastrophenfall), and on 20 March Bavaria became the first German state to impose a general curfew and stay-at-home order. The Oktoberfest was cancelled in both 2020 and 2021 — the first cancellations since the Second World War.

Response and measures

Bavaria frequently adopted stricter and earlier measures than other German states, including border checks with Austria, mandatory FFP2 masks in shops and public transport (from January 2021), and the introduction of the "2G" and "3G" rules restricting access to venues for the vaccinated, recovered or tested. The state set up vaccination centres from late December 2020, beginning with the BioNTech/Pfizer vaccine. By the time the state of disaster was finally lifted, Bavaria had recorded millions of confirmed infections and tens of thousands of deaths attributed to the virus.
