Transnet

Transnet SOC Ltd is a South African state-owned company that is the custodian of the country's freight rail network, national ports, and pipelines. Headquartered in Johannesburg, it is the largest logistics company in Africa, responsible among other things for hauling coal, iron ore, and containers between mines, factories, and ports.

Operations

Transnet's principal divisions are Transnet Freight Rail, the heavy-haul rail operator of some 20,000 km of track; Transnet National Ports Authority, which manages eight commercial ports including Durban, Cape Town, and Richards Bay; Transnet Port Terminals; Transnet Pipelines, carrying petroleum products and gas; and Transnet Engineering, which manufactures and maintains rolling stock. The company traces its origins to the South African Railways and Harbours Administration, becoming Transnet in 1990 as it was corporatised.

Challenges

During the state capture era, Transnet was at the center of major corruption scandals, including the controversial procurement of 1,064 locomotives from Chinese and other suppliers, which became a focus of the Zondo Commission. In the 2020s the company faced severe operational decline — cable theft, locomotive shortages, and derailments drove freight volumes down and forced commodity exporters onto road transport — prompting government reform plans to open the rail network to private operators.
