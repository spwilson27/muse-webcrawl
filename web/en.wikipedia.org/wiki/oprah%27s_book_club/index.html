Oprah's Book Club is a book discussion club segment of the American talk show The Oprah Winfrey Show, hosted by Oprah Winfrey. It debuted in 1996 and became one of the most powerful forces in publishing, with selected titles routinely jumping to the top of bestseller lists.

History

Winfrey introduced the segment in September 1996, choosing Jacquelyn Mitchard's novel The Deep End of the Ocean as the first selection. Selections were typically announced monthly, and featured authors appeared on the show to discuss their work. The club was credited with introducing millions of viewers to literary fiction.

The original club ended in 2011 with the conclusion of The Oprah Winfrey Show. A revived version, Oprah's Book Club 2.0, launched in 2012 in partnership with the Oprah Winfrey Network, and in 2019 the club moved to Apple TV+ as a streaming series.

Notable selections

Selections have included East of Eden by John Steinbeck, Love in the Time of Cholera by Gabriel García Márquez, A Million Little Pieces by James Frey, and Wild by Cheryl Strayed. The Frey selection became controversial in 2006 when the memoir was revealed to contain fabrications, prompting a public confrontation on the show.

Impact

The "Oprah effect" on book sales was dramatic; publishers reported print runs swelling by hundreds of thousands of copies after a selection was announced. Scholars credit the club with sustaining a mass readership for literary fiction in the United States.
