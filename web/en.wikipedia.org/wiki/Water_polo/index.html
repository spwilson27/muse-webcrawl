Water polo is a competitive team sport played in water between two teams of seven players, who attempt to score goals by throwing a ball into the opposing team's goal. Players may not touch the bottom of the pool and must tread water or swim throughout the game, making it one of the most physically demanding team sports. A match consists of four periods, and the team with the most goals at the end wins.

History

The game originated in Great Britain in the 1870s as a form of "water rugby" played in rivers and lakes, initially with considerable violence — players could hold opponents underwater and wrestle for the ball. Standardised rules emerged in Scotland under William Wilson, and the sport spread through clubs in England and the United States. Men's water polo was among the first team sports introduced at the modern Olympic Games, appearing at Paris 1900; women's water polo was added a century later at Sydney 2000.

Rules of play

Each team fields six field players and a goalkeeper. Field players may handle the ball with one hand only, except the goalkeeper, who may use both hands within the 5-metre area. Fouls are divided into ordinary fouls (resulting in a free throw) and major fouls; three major fouls, or a brutality foul, lead to exclusion. A 30-second shot clock limits the time a team may hold possession before attempting a shot. Players advance the ball by passing or swimming with it (dribbling), and defend with a mix of zone and pressing systems.

Major competitions

Besides the Olympics, the sport's premier events are the World Aquatics Championships, the FINA/World Aquatics World Cup, and the World League. Hungary is historically the most successful men's Olympic nation, while Italy, Serbia, Croatia, and Spain are also perennial contenders; the United States has dominated the women's game in recent decades. Strong domestic leagues exist in Hungary, Italy, Spain, Greece, and Croatia.
