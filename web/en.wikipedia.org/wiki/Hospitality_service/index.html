Hospitality service

SectorServices
IndustriesHotels and lodging, food and beverage, travel and tourism, events

Hospitality service refers to the relationship between a host and a guest, and more broadly to the work of the hospitality industry: the broad category of fields within the service industry that includes lodging, food and drink service, event planning, theme parks, travel, and tourism. Providing hospitality service involves receiving guests warmly and meeting their needs for accommodation, food, comfort, and entertainment, typically in exchange for payment.

Scope

The hospitality sector is commonly divided into segments: accommodation (hotels, resorts, hostels, and serviced apartments), food and beverage (restaurants, bars, catering, and room service), travel and tourism services, and meetings and events. Service quality in these segments is assessed through dimensions such as reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and the quality of physical facilities — the "tangibles" — a model formalized in the SERVQUAL framework of service-quality research.

History

Formal hospitality has ancient roots: caravanserais along trade routes, monastic guesthouses, inns on Roman and later coaching routes, and the tea houses and ryokan of East Asia all offered lodging and food to travellers. The modern hotel industry developed in the 19th century with grand railway hotels and innovators such as César Ritz and Auguste Escoffier, who professionalized hotel and kitchen management. In the 20th century, franchised motel and hotel chains standardized hospitality service across large networks, and the rise of online travel agencies and home-sharing platforms reshaped distribution once again.

Service culture

Hospitality businesses emphasize guest satisfaction, service recovery when problems occur, and increasingly personalization and experience design. Training, standard operating procedures, and customer feedback systems such as guest-satisfaction surveys and review platforms are central management tools. The industry is labour-intensive and characterized by a large front-line workforce in roles including reception, housekeeping, food service, and concierge services.
