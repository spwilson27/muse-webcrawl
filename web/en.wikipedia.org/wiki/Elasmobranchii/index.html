Elasmobranchii

Elasmobranchii
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Chondrichthyes
Subclass: Elasmobranchii
Includes: Sharks, rays, skates, sawfish

Elasmobranchii is a subclass of Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fish) comprising the sharks and the rays, skates, and sawfish (collectively the batoids). The name comes from the Greek elasmo- ("plate") and branchia ("gill"), referring to the broad, plate-like gills of these animals.

Characteristics

Elasmobranchs possess five to seven pairs of gill slits opening separately to the exterior, rigid dorsal fins, and small placoid scales (dermal denticles) that make the skin feel like sandpaper. Unlike bony fish they have no swim bladder, relying instead on a large oil-rich liver and dynamic lift from their heterocercal tails for buoyancy. They have electroreceptive organs (the ampullae of Lorenzini) and a keen sense of smell. Most species are marine predators; fertilisation is internal, and reproduction ranges from egg-laying (oviparity) to live birth (viviparity).

Classification and evolution

The subclass is divided into the Selachimorpha (sharks, some 500 species) and the Batoidea (rays and skates, over 600 species). Definitive elasmobranch fossils appear in the Early Devonian, roughly 400 million years ago, and the group radiated widely despite suffering in several mass extinctions. Many modern species are threatened by overfishing, finning, and bycatch, and elasmobranchs' slow growth and low fecundity make populations slow to recover.
