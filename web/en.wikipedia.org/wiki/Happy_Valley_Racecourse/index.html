Happy Valley Racecourse

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Happy Valley Racecourse is one of the two race tracks for horse racing in Hong Kong, the other being Sha Tin Racecourse. Located in Happy Valley on Hong Kong Island, it is surrounded by high-rise residential buildings and is operated by the Hong Kong Jockey Club. Racing is typically held on Wednesday evenings.

History

The racecourse was built in 1845 on reclaimed marshland, making it one of the oldest sporting venues in Hong Kong. In 1918 a fire in a temporary grandstand killed at least 590 people, one of the worst disasters in Hong Kong's history. The course was repeatedly rebuilt and modernized; the present seven-storey grandstand, completed in the 1990s, can accommodate over 50,000 spectators.

Features

The turf track is approximately 1,450 metres in circumference. The venue also houses the Hong Kong Racing Museum. Night races under floodlights, backed by the illuminated skyline, are an iconic image of the city, and the "Happy Wednesday" meetings combine racing with live entertainment and food stalls.
