Separatism

Separatism is the advocacy of cultural, ethnic, tribal, religious, racial, governmental or gender separation from the larger group. Separatist groups often seek greater autonomy or full political independence for a territory or people they consider oppressed within an existing state.

Motivations

Motivations include ethnic or religious differences, economic inequality, historical grievances, linguistic distinctiveness, and perceived political marginalisation. Separatist movements range from peaceful political campaigns, such as those for Scottish or Catalan independence, to armed insurgencies.

Types

Ethnic separatism rests on ethnic differences; religious separatism on doctrinal or communal divides; and racial separatism on claims of racial distinctness. Secession is a formal withdrawal from a political entity, while irredentism seeks to join a neighbouring state.

Responses

Governments respond variously with devolution, federalism, autonomy arrangements, referendums, or suppression. International law generally favours territorial integrity, recognising external self-determination mainly in colonial contexts or cases of grave oppression.
