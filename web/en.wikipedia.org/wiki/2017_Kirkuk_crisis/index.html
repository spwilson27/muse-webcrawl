The 2017 Kirkuk crisis was a confrontation between the Iraqi federal government and the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) in October 2017, following the Kurdish independence referendum of 25 September 2017. Iraqi forces, supported by the Popular Mobilization Forces, retook the oil-rich city of Kirkuk and most of the disputed territories that Kurdish Peshmerga had held since 2014.

Background

Kurdish forces had taken control of Kirkuk in June 2014 when the Iraqi army fled the advance of Islamic State, and the KRG thereafter exported oil from its fields independently. The independence referendum — in which over 92% voted yes on a high turnout — was held in the KRG and the disputed areas including Kirkuk, despite opposition from Baghdad, Turkey, Iran and the United States, which called it illegitimate and destabilising.

The operation

On 16 October 2017 Iraqi counter-terrorism units and federal police advanced into Kirkuk. Peshmerga of the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) largely withdrew under an apparent understanding with Baghdad, while KDP Peshmerga also pulled back; resistance was limited and the city fell within a day. Iraqi forces then took Sinjar, Makhmour and other disputed districts; brief clashes followed near Altun Kupri. Tens of thousands of Kurdish residents fled Kirkuk temporarily.

Aftermath

The loss of Kirkuk cost the KRG roughly half its oil revenue and ended the immediate independence bid. KRG president Masoud Barzani announced his resignation effective 1 November 2017, and Erbil offered to "freeze" the referendum result and negotiate with Baghdad.
