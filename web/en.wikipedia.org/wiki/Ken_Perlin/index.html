Kenneth H. Perlin is an American computer scientist and professor of computer science at New York University, best known for inventing Perlin noise, a procedural texture primitive used extensively in computer graphics, film visual effects, and video games.

Perlin noise

Perlin developed his gradient noise algorithm in the early 1980s while working on the film Tron at Mathematical Applications Group, Inc. Dissatisfied with the machine-like appearance of contemporary computer graphics, he created a pseudo-random, band-limited noise function that could synthesise natural-looking textures such as clouds, fire, marble, and terrain. He presented the work at SIGGRAPH in 1985 and received an Academy Award for Technical Achievement in 1997 for the development of Turbulence and Perlin noise.

Career

Perlin founded NYU's Media Research Laboratory and was a founding director of the Games for Learning Institute. His research extends to interactive animation, user interfaces, and augmented reality; he directed the Future Reality Lab at NYU. His procedural character animation work also influenced film and game production.
