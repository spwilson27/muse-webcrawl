Sir Henri Charles Wilfrid Laurier (20 November 1841 – 17 February 1919) was a Canadian lawyer, statesman, and politician who served as the seventh prime minister of Canada from 1896 to 1911. The first francophone prime minister of Canada, he led the Liberal Party for nearly 32 years (1887–1919), a record in Canadian politics, and presided over a period of rapid immigration, settlement of the West, and economic growth. His portrait appears on the Canadian five-dollar bill.

Prime ministership

Laurier, a lawyer from Quebec educated at McGill, became Liberal leader in 1887 and won the 1896 election. His government settled the Manitoba Schools Question by compromise, promoted massive immigration under Clifford Sifton to settle the Prairies, created the provinces of Alberta and Saskatchewan in 1905, supported the construction of new transcontinental railways, founded the Department of External Affairs and the Royal Canadian Navy (1910), and negotiated a reciprocity (free trade) agreement with the United States. The naval question and reciprocity contributed to his defeat in the 1911 election by Robert Borden's Conservatives.

Later life and legacy

Laurier remained opposition leader through the First World War, but his party split over the conscription crisis of 1917; he died in office as party leader in 1919. Renowned as an orator in both French and English — his famous dictum held that "the 20th century shall be the century of Canada" — Laurier is consistently ranked among Canada's greatest prime ministers. Wilfrid Laurier University in Waterloo, Ontario, is named for him.
