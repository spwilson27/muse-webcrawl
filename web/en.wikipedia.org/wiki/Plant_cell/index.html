Plant cell

DomainEukaryota
KingdomPlantae
Distinctive structuresCell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuole

Plant cells are the eukaryotic cells of green plants within the kingdom Plantae. Their distinctive features include a rigid cell wall composed mainly of cellulose, chloroplasts for photosynthesis, and a large central vacuole that maintains turgor pressure against the cell wall.

Characteristics

Like other eukaryotic cells, plant cells contain a membrane-bound nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and cytoskeleton. Unlike animal cells, they are typically fixed in shape, lack lysosomes and centrioles in most cases, and communicate through channels called plasmodesmata that cross the cell walls.

Types of plant cells

The three basic tissue systems of plants are built from cell types including parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma cells. Parenchyma cells perform most metabolic functions including photosynthesis and storage; xylem vessels and phloem sieve elements conduct water and nutrients through the plant body.

Photosynthesis

Within chloroplasts, thylakoid membranes host the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis, while the Calvin cycle fixes carbon dioxide in the stroma. This process underpins nearly all life on Earth by producing oxygen and organic compounds.
