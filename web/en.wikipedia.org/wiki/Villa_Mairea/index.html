Villa Mairea

Villa Mairea is a country house and studio in Noormarkku, Finland, designed by the Finnish architect Alvar Aalto for Harry and Maire Gullichsen and built in 1938–1939. It is regarded as one of Aalto's masterpieces and one of the most significant private houses of 20th-century architecture, synthesising modernist planning with Finnish vernacular materials and nature.

Design

The Gullichsens, patrons of modern art and part of the Ahlström industrial family, commissioned Aalto and his wife Aino to design a house that combined living quarters, an art studio, and space to display their collection. The L-shaped plan wraps around a courtyard with a kidney-shaped swimming pool and a sauna wing.

Inside, Aalto used a rich collage of materials — pine panelling, teak, stone, and free-form wooden slat screens suggesting a forest — and designed much of the furniture and lighting himself. The studio, with its undulating wall, sits above the entrance. The house marks Aalto's transition from the strict functionalism of the Paimio Sanatorium toward a warmer, "organic" modernism.

Today

Villa Mairea remains in the care of the Mairea Foundation, is a protected building, and is open to visitors by guided tour. An original design prototype exists in Aalto's experimental architecture.
