Edward II (25 April 1284 – 21 September 1327), also known as Edward of Caernarfon, was King of England from 1307 until he was deposed in January 1327. The fourth son of Edward I, he became heir apparent after his elder brothers died.

Early life

Edward was born at Caernarfon Castle in north Wales. In 1301 he became the first English prince to be invested as Prince of Wales. He formed a close — and controversial — relationship with the Gascon knight Piers Gaveston, whom his father exiled.

Reign

On succeeding his father, Edward recalled Gaveston and made him Earl of Cornwall, angering the barons. The Ordainers imposed the Ordinances of 1311, limiting royal power and exiling Gaveston; his execution by barons in 1312 sparked conflict. Edward's campaign against Scotland ended in disastrous defeat by Robert the Bruce at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314.

Later Edward relied on the Despenser family, Hugh Despenser the Younger especially, whose greed alienated the nobility and his queen, Isabella of France. In 1326 Isabella and her lover Roger Mortimer invaded from France; Edward was captured, forced to abdicate in favour of his son Edward III, and died at Berkeley Castle — traditionally said to have been murdered.

Assessment

Contemporary chroniclers criticised Edward's favouritism and military failures. Modern historians debate his sexuality, competence, and the manner of his death. He was canonised in popular devotion at Gloucester Cathedral, where he is buried, though never officially.
