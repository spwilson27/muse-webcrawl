The inferior colliculus (IC) is the principal midbrain nucleus of the auditory pathway and an obligatory relay for information ascending from the lower brainstem to the medial geniculate nucleus of the thalamus and auditory cortex. It forms the lower of the two pairs of bumps (the corpora quadrigemina) on the dorsal surface of the midbrain, below the visual superior colliculus.

Structure and function

The IC consists of a central nucleus, a dorsal cortex, and external (lateral) subdivisions. The central nucleus has a laminated, tonotopic organisation — sheets of neurons each tuned to a band of sound frequencies — and receives converging input from many brainstem auditory nuclei, including the cochlear nuclei, superior olivary complex, and nuclei of the lateral lemniscus. In addition to relaying sound information, the inferior colliculus integrates binaural cues for sound localisation, receives descending input from the auditory cortex and cross-modal input, and contributes to auditory reflexes such as the acoustic startle pathway via connections with the superior colliculus and pontine reticular formation. Nearly all ascending auditory fibres synapse in the IC, making it a major computational hub rather than a passive relay.
