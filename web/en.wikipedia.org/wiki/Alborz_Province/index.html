Alborz Province (Persian: استان البرز) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, centred on the city of Karaj, about 20 km west of Tehran. Created on 23 June 2010 by the division of Tehran Province, it is the smallest province in the country by area and one of the most densely populated, named after the Alborz mountain range whose foothills dominate its northern half.

Geography

The province stretches from the high peaks of the central Alborz — ski resorts such as Dizin lie just beyond its northern border in the same range — south to the plains of the Karaj River basin and the salt desert margins near Eshtehard. The Amir Kabir (Karaj) Dam on the Karaj River supplies water and recreation and is a well-known landmark on the road to the Caspian coast via the Chalus valley. The climate ranges from cold mountain conditions in Taleqan to semi-arid steppe in the south.

Demographics and economy

Karaj, the provincial capital, has grown into one of Iran's largest cities as a commuter and industrial satellite of Tehran, housing a diverse population of Persians, Azerbaijanis, Kurds, Lurs, and others who migrated from across Iran. The province hosts major industrial estates, pharmaceutical and food production, and the agricultural research institutes around Karaj, as well as Payame Noor and Islamic Azad university campuses and Kharazmi University. The Tehran–Karaj metro line and motorways tie it closely to the capital.

Tourism

Attractions include the Taleqan valley and its dam lake, the villages and waterfalls of the Chalus Road corridor, historic caravanserais and the Pearl Palace (Kakh-e Morvarid) in Karaj, built in the late Pahlavi era.
