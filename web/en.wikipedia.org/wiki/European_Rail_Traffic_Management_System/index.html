European Rail Traffic Management System

The European Rail Traffic Management System (ERTMS) is a European Union-backed initiative to create a single standard for railway signalling and train control across Europe, replacing the patchwork of more than twenty incompatible national systems. It is intended to make cross-border rail traffic seamless, increase capacity and safety, and open the market for signalling equipment to competition.

Components

ERTMS has two main components. The European Train Control System (ETCS) is the automatic train protection and signalling component: trackside equipment communicates movement authorities to the train, and an on-board computer continuously supervises speed. GSM-R, a railway-specific variant of GSM, provides the voice and data radio link between trains and control centres. A further component, ETML, concerned traffic management layers. ETCS is deployed in application levels: Level 1 overlays existing signals with balise transmission, while Level 2 uses continuous radio communication and can dispense with lineside signals; Level 3, with moving-block operation, remains largely conceptual.

Deployment

Deployment is coordinated through European corridors and national plans, supervised by the European Union Agency for Railways. High-speed and conventional lines equipped with ETCS operate in Spain, Italy, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Belgium and elsewhere; Switzerland has equipped much of its standard-gauge network. Progress has repeatedly been slower and more expensive than planned, owing to evolving specifications, software baselines and the cost of retrofitting rolling stock. The system has also been adopted outside Europe, including in China (as a basis of CTCS), India, Saudi Arabia, South Korea and Australia.
