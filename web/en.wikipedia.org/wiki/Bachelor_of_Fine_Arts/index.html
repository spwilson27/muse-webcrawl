The Bachelor of Fine Arts (BFA) is a standard undergraduate degree for students pursuing a professional education in the visual or performing arts. It differs from a Bachelor of Arts (BA) in fine arts in the intensity of its practice-based curriculum: typically, about two-thirds of a BFA programme consists of studio or performance coursework in the major, versus roughly one-third for a BA, which includes broader liberal-arts requirements.

Curriculum

BFA programmes are offered in painting, drawing, sculpture, printmaking, photography, ceramics, graphic and industrial design, animation, film and television production, theatre (acting, design, directing), dance, musical theatre and creative writing. Admission frequently requires a portfolio or audition in addition to academic qualifications. Students usually complete foundation courses in drawing, design and art history, progress through concentrated studio sequences with regular critiques, and finish with a senior exhibition, thesis show or recital. In the United States, professional accreditation is overseen by bodies such as the National Association of Schools of Art and Design (NASAD).

By country

The BFA is most characteristic of the United States and Canada, where it is the standard professional bachelor's in the arts and the usual prerequisite for the Master of Fine Arts, considered the terminal degree for practising artists and college art teachers. Similar practice-intensive bachelor's degrees exist elsewhere: the UK awards BA (Hons) degrees in fine art subjects of three years' duration, while Australia, India and the Philippines also grant BFAs, the Philippine version typically running four to five years.
