Box set

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A box set or boxed set is a set of items — typically books, recorded music, or films and television programmes — packaged together in a single box. Box sets are often marketed as collectors' editions, frequently including material not available separately, such as previously unreleased recordings, remastered editions, extensive liner notes, and commemorative packaging.

Music

In the music industry, a box set usually comprises three or more discs collecting an artist's studio albums, live recordings, rarities, demos, and out-takes. Box sets became prominent in the LP era — notable early examples include collections of classical recordings — and flourished on compact disc from the late 1980s onward, when labels mined their back catalogues for comprehensive career retrospectives. Lavish classical box sets, collecting for instance the complete symphonies of a composer or the complete recordings of a celebrated performer, remain a significant niche of the classical market.

Books

Book box sets bundle a series of related volumes — the novels of a single author, or a multi-volume work such as an encyclopedia — often in a slipcase. They are common for children's series and for popular fiction franchises, and are frequently sold as gifts.

Film and television

For film and television, box sets collect a complete series, a season, or a group of related films on DVD or Blu-ray, often with bonus features such as commentaries, documentaries, and deleted scenes. Although physical box sets have declined with the rise of streaming, complete-series sets remain popular collectors' items.
