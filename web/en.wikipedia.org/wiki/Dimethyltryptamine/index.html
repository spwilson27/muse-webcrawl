N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT or N,N-DMT) is a substituted tryptamine that occurs in many plants and animals, including humans, and is a potent psychedelic drug. It is historically and traditionally used by many Amazonian cultures in the preparation of the brew ayahuasca, typically in combination with plants containing monoamine oxidase inhibitors that render it orally active. When smoked or injected, DMT produces a short but intense psychedelic experience, usually lasting only a few minutes, characterised by vivid visual imagery and reports of encounters with seemingly autonomous entities — experiences popularised in the 1990s by researcher Rick Strassman, who called DMT "the spirit molecule".

History

DMT was first synthesised in 1931 by the Canadian chemist Richard Manske. Its psychoactive properties were recognised in 1956 by the Hungarian chemist and psychiatrist Stephen Szára, who extracted it from Mimosa tenuiflora and self-administered it intramuscularly. Amazonian peoples had used DMT-containing preparations — ayahuasca, yopo and vilca snuffs — for centuries before Western science identified the active compound.

Pharmacology

DMT acts principally as an agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, with activity at other serotonergic and trace-amine-associated receptors. It is rapidly broken down by monoamine oxidase A, which explains its very short duration when taken without an MAOI. Trace amounts are produced endogenously in the human body: the enzyme INMT can methylate tryptamine to form DMT, and the compound has been detected in cerebrospinal fluid. The significance of endogenous DMT remains unknown.

Legal status

DMT is controlled internationally under the 1971 UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances and is a Schedule I substance in the United States and a Class A drug in the United Kingdom. Some religious use of ayahuasca is protected in a few jurisdictions: in Brazil ayahuasca use by religious groups such as the União do Vegetal is legal, and the U.S. Supreme Court upheld a religious exemption for the UDV in Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal (2006).
