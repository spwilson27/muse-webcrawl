Landfill

A landfill is a site for the disposal of waste materials by burial. It is the oldest and most common form of waste disposal worldwide. Modern engineered ("sanitary") landfills are carefully designed structures built into or on top of the ground, with liners to isolate waste from groundwater, systems to collect leachate and landfill gas, and daily covering of waste — a stark contrast to uncontrolled open dumps, which remain widespread in the developing world.

Operation

Waste is delivered by truck, weighed at the gatehouse, and spread in a working cell by bulldozers and compactors, which crush and densify it to save space. At day's end the waste is covered with soil or alternative daily cover to reduce odour, litter, and vermin. Cells are built over a composite liner — typically compacted clay plus a high-density polyethylene geomembrane — above a drainage layer that collects leachate for treatment. Wells drilled into the waste extract landfill gas, roughly half methane and half carbon dioxide, which may be flared or burned to generate electricity. When a landfill reaches capacity it is capped with an impermeable cover, graded, vegetated, and monitored for decades; many closed landfills become parks or golf courses, such as New York's Freshkills Park, built over what was the world's largest landfill.

Environmental issues

Landfills are a major anthropogenic source of methane, a potent greenhouse gas, though gas capture reduces emissions. Leachate can contaminate groundwater if liners fail. Poorly managed sites attract disease vectors, generate odours, and have historically been sited disproportionately near low-income communities, an environmental-justice concern. Landfill fires and, in the developing world, slope collapses of open dumps have caused many deaths among waste pickers.

Regulation and alternatives

In the United States, municipal solid waste landfills are regulated under Subtitle D of the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (criteria set in 1991); hazardous waste landfills fall under the stricter Subtitle C. The EU Landfill Directive (1999) requires diversion of biodegradable waste and has pushed member states toward recycling, composting, anaerobic digestion, and incineration with energy recovery. Waste-management hierarchies generally rank landfill last, after prevention, reuse, and recycling.
