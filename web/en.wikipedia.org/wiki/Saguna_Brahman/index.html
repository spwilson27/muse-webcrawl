Saguna Brahman

Saguna Brahman (Sanskrit: सगुण ब्रह्मन्, saguṇa brahman, "Brahman with attributes") is a concept in Vedanta philosophy denoting the Absolute conceived as possessing qualities, form, and personality, in contrast to Nirguna Brahman, the Absolute without attributes. In saguna Brahman, the ultimate reality is identified with a personal God — commonly Vishnu, Shiva, or the Goddess (Devi) — who can be worshipped, loved, and approached through devotion (bhakti).

Saguna Brahman: the Absolute with attributes, object of devotional worship.

Concept

The distinction between saguna and nirguna Brahman arises from the Upanishadic descriptions of Brahman both as "not this, not this" (neti neti) and as the lord and creator of the universe. Saguna Brahman is Brahman associated with maya and the three gunas (qualities) of nature, appearing as Ishvara, the personal God who creates, sustains, and dissolves the cosmos. Devotional traditions hold that for most seekers the formless Absolute is inaccessible, so worship of Brahman with attributes provides a concrete focus for meditation and love of God.

In the schools of Vedanta

In Advaita Vedanta, saguna Brahman is regarded as ultimately belonging to the empirical level of reality (vyavaharika), while nirguna Brahman alone is absolutely real; nevertheless, worship of saguna Brahman is accepted as a valid means of purifying the mind and preparing for knowledge of the formless Absolute. The theistic schools — Vishishtadvaita of Ramanuja and Dvaita of Madhva — affirm that Brahman eternally possesses auspicious attributes and that the personal God is the highest truth itself, not a provisional appearance.
