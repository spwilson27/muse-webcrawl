Three Shire Heads is a point on the River Dane in the Peak District of England where the boundaries of three counties — Cheshire, Derbyshire, and Staffordshire — meet. The site is known for its picturesque waterfalls, plunge pools, and an old packhorse bridge across the river.

History

The bridge and paved causeway formed part of a packhorse route across the moorland, used for carrying goods such as silk, salt, and coal between the surrounding towns. The meeting of the three county boundaries also made the spot a traditional place for prize fights and other gatherings in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, since constables of one county could be evaded by stepping into another.

Today

Three Shire Heads is a popular walking destination on the moorland near the Cat and Fiddle road, and the pools below the falls are a well-known wild swimming spot.
