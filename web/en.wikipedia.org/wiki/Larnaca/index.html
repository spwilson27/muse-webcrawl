Larnaca

Country: Cyprus
District: Larnaca District
Population: ~85,000 (urban area, approx.)
Known for: Finikoudes promenade, Hala Sultan Tekke, Larnaca International Airport

Larnaca (Greek: Λάρνακα) is a city on the south-east coast of Cyprus and the capital of the district of the same name. It is built on the site of the ancient city-kingdom of Kition, and is one of the island's oldest continuously inhabited places. Larnaca is known for its palm-tree seafront promenade, Finikoudes, its salt lake and flamingos, and Larnaca International Airport, the island's largest airport, which lies just south of the city.

History

Ancient Kition was founded by Mycenaean Greeks and later settled by Phoenicians, becoming an important trading port and a major centre of copper trade. The philosopher Zeno of Citium, founder of Stoicism, was born here. According to tradition, Saint Lazarus lived in Larnaca after his resurrection and became its first bishop; the Church of Saint Lazarus, built in the 9th century over his reputed tomb, remains a landmark. The city passed through Byzantine, Lusignan, Venetian, and Ottoman rule — the Hala Sultan Tekke mosque on the Salt Lake, one of the holiest shrines in Islam, dates to the Ottoman period — and then British rule until Cypriot independence in 1960. After the Turkish invasion of 1974, Larnaca received many Greek Cypriot refugees and grew rapidly, while its airport replaced Nicosia International Airport as the island's main gateway.

Sights

Landmarks include the Larnaca Salt Lake, home in winter to thousands of pink flamingos; the Kamares Aqueduct (1747); the medieval Larnaca Fort on the seafront; the Zenobia wreck, a famous dive site just off the coast; and the Pierides Museum of antiquities. Mackenzie Beach and Finikoudes are the city's main leisure beaches.

Transport

Larnaca International Airport serves as the main international gateway to Cyprus. The city also has a commercial port and a large marina, and is linked by motorway to Nicosia, Limassol, and Famagusta district.
