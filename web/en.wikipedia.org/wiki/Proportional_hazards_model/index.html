Proportional hazards model

A proportional hazards model is a class of survival models in statistics. It relates the time that passes before an event occurs to covariates that may be associated with that quantity of time, assuming that a covariate's effect multiplies the hazard by a constant.

Cox model

The most famous example is the Cox proportional hazards model, proposed by David Cox in 1972. It decomposes the hazard function into a baseline hazard depending only on time and a factor depending only on the covariates, estimated by partial likelihood without specifying the baseline hazard.

Assumptions and use

The key assumption is that hazard ratios between groups remain constant over time; violations can be detected with methods such as Schoenfeld residuals. The model is widely used in medical research to analyse survival data.
