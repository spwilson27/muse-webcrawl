The Eurasian reed warbler (Acrocephalus scirpaceus) is an Old World warbler in the genus Acrocephalus. It breeds across Europe into the temperate Middle East and western Asia and is migratory, wintering in sub-Saharan Africa.

Description

It is a medium-sized warbler, 12.5 cm long, with unstreaked brown upperparts, buff-white underparts, a whitish throat, and a faint supercilium. The sexes are identical in appearance. Its song is a slow, chattering series of repeated phrases delivered from within reedbeds.

Behaviour

The species is almost always found in reed beds, where it feeds on insects and weaves its deep cup nest around upright reed stems. The reed warbler is one of the principal hosts of the common cuckoo, and the evolutionary arms race between the two — egg mimicry and chick rejection — is a classic subject of behavioural ecology.
