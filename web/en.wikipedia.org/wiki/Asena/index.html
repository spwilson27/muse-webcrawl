Asena

Asena is the name of a she-wolf in Turkic mythology, central to the origin legends of the Göktürks. In the best-known version of the myth, a young boy — the sole survivor of a massacre of his people — is rescued and nursed by a she-wolf; she later bears him ten sons, from whom the Ashina clan, the ruling house of the Göktürk Khaganate, is said to descend.

The legend

Versions of the tale appear in Chinese dynastic histories describing the Turks, including the Book of Zhou and Book of Sui. In another variant, the wolf leads the remnant of the people to safety in a hidden valley — identified with Ergüneqon in related Mongolian tradition. The wolf became an enduring symbol of Turkic identity: the grey wolf appears in the folklore and nationalist symbolism of Turkey and other Turkic peoples, and the figure of Asena is sometimes personified as a female warrior ancestor in modern retellings.

Modern usage

Asena is also used as a Turkish female given name, and the she-wolf motif recurs in art, literature and political symbolism across the Turkic world.
