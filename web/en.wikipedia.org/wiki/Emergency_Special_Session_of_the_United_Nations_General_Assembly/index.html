Emergency special session of the United Nations General Assembly

An emergency special session is an unscheduled meeting of the United Nations General Assembly to make urgent (though non-binding) recommendations on a particular issue. Sessions may be called under the "Uniting for Peace" procedure (General Assembly resolution 377) when the Security Council, because of lack of unanimity among its permanent members, fails to act in a case of a threat to the peace, breach of the peace, or act of aggression.

Procedure

A session can be requested by the Security Council on the vote of any nine members, or by a majority of the Member States of the United Nations. The Assembly must meet within 24 hours of the request. Emergency special sessions are distinct from special sessions, which are convened on a broader timetable for scheduled topics.

List of sessions

Eleven emergency special sessions have been convened. The first (1956) addressed the Suez Crisis; others concerned Hungary (1956), Lebanon (1958), the Congo (1960), the Six-Day War (1967), Bangladesh (1971), Afghanistan (1980), Palestine (1980), Namibia (1981), and the Israeli occupation (1982). The tenth session, on Israeli actions in occupied East Jerusalem and the Palestinian territories, was first convened in 1997 and has been repeatedly resumed. The eleventh session was convened in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine; it adopted resolutions demanding Russian withdrawal and suspending Russia from the Human Rights Council.
