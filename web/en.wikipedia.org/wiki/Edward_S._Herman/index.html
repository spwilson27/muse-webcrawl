Edward Samuel Herman (April 7, 1925 – November 11, 2017) was an American economist, media scholar and political activist. He was professor emeritus of finance at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania and a leading critic of corporate power and United States foreign policy.

The propaganda model

With Noam Chomsky, Herman wrote Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media (1988), which presented the propaganda model: five filters — ownership, advertising, sourcing, flak, and anti-communist (later "anti-terror"/fear) ideology — that shape news content in a market system without overt censorship. The book's case studies compared media treatment of "worthy" and "unworthy" victims in enemy and client states.

Other work

Herman wrote extensively on corporate control and on US interventions abroad, including The Washington Connection and Third World Fascism (1979, with Chomsky) and The Real Terror Network (1982). His concept of "worthy versus unworthy victims" and his "Srebrenica revisionism" — contested articles questioning aspects of the consensus account of the Bosnian genocide — made him controversial; critics accused him of genocide denial, which he rejected.
