Animals in war

Animals in war have been used by human militaries throughout history in a wide variety of roles: as transport, messengers, scouts, guards, mascots, and in some cases as weapons or weapons platforms. Horses, elephants, camels, mules, oxen, dogs, pigeons, dolphins and many other species have served — and died — in conflicts from ancient battles to modern wars.

History

War elephants were used by Indian, Persian and Hellenistic armies; Hannibal famously marched elephants across the Alps against Rome. Cavalry horses formed the backbone of mounted warfare for millennia. In the First World War, some eight million horses, donkeys and mules are estimated to have died, hauling guns and supplies through the mud of the Western Front, while carrier pigeons such as Cher Ami carried messages when other communications failed.

Roles

  • Transport: horses, mules, camels and elephants moving troops and matériel.
  • Communication: homing pigeons and messenger dogs.
  • Detection: dogs detecting mines and explosives; marine mammals locating sea mines.
  • Combat: cavalry charges, war elephants, and experimental uses such as incendiary bats and anti-tank dogs.
  • Morale: regimental mascots, from goats to bears such as Wojtek, the Polish artillery bear of the Second World War.

Recognition

The PDSA Dickin Medal, instituted in 1943, honours animals displaying devotion to duty in wartime. Memorials such as the Animals in War Memorial in London commemorate their service.
