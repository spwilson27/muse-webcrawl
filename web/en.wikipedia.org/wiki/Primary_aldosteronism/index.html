Primary aldosteronism

Primary aldosteronism
Synonym: Conn's syndrome (unilateral form)
Specialty: Endocrinology
Symptoms: Hypertension, low potassium, fatigue
Causes: Adrenal adenoma, bilateral adrenal hyperplasia

Primary aldosteronism, also known as primary hyperaldosteronism, is the excess production of the hormone aldosterone by the adrenal glands independent of the renin–angiotensin system, resulting in hypertension and often low blood potassium. When caused by a single aldosterone-producing adrenal adenoma, it is called Conn's syndrome, after Jerome Conn, who described it in 1955.

Causes and prevalence

The most common causes are bilateral idiopathic adrenal hyperplasia (about 60% of cases) and a unilateral aldosterone-producing adenoma (about 35%); rare causes include familial hyperaldosteronism and adrenal carcinoma. Once thought rare, primary aldosteronism is now estimated to account for 5–10% of all hypertension and a higher share of resistant hypertension, making it the most common curable cause of secondary hypertension.

Signs and symptoms

Many patients have only treatment-resistant hypertension. Excess aldosterone causes renal sodium retention and potassium and hydrogen ion loss, so some patients develop hypokalemia with muscle weakness, fatigue, cramps, and metabolic alkalosis. Beyond blood pressure, excess aldosterone increases cardiovascular and renal damage relative to equivalent primary hypertension.

Diagnosis and treatment

Screening uses the plasma aldosterone-to-renin ratio, followed by confirmatory suppression testing. Adrenal CT and adrenal vein sampling distinguish unilateral from bilateral disease. Unilateral disease is treated by laparoscopic adrenalectomy, often curing or greatly improving hypertension; bilateral disease is treated with mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists such as spironolactone or eplerenone.
