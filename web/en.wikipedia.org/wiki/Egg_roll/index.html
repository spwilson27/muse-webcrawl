Egg roll

Egg roll
CourseAppetizer, side dish
Place of originChina / United States (Chinese-American)
Main ingredientsWheat flour wrapper, cabbage, pork, vegetables

An egg roll is a fried appetizer served in American Chinese restaurants. It consists of a thick, egg-based wheat flour wrapper filled with shredded cabbage, chopped pork and other fillings, rolled into a cylindrical shape and deep-fried until crisp.

History

The egg roll is closely related to, but distinct from, the Chinese spring roll. Its modern American form developed in Chinese-American restaurants in the early 20th century, notably in New York City. Unlike the thin rice or wheat wrappers of spring rolls, egg roll wrappers contain egg, giving a thicker, blistering crust when fried.

Preparation

Fillings are typically pre-cooked and seasoned with soy sauce, ginger and garlic, wrapped tightly, sealed, and deep-fried. Common variations include shrimp, chicken and vegetarian egg rolls. They are served hot with duck sauce, sweet and sour sauce, plum sauce or hot mustard.

Variations

Similar dishes include Vietnamese chả giò, Filipino lumpia, and the spring roll. In Britain, a large spring roll is sometimes called an egg roll in Chinese takeaways.
