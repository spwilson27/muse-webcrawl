C. F. Martin & Company

Industry: Musical instruments
Founded: 1833
Founder: Christian Frederick Martin
Headquarters: Nazareth, Pennsylvania, United States
Products: Acoustic guitars, strings, ukuleles

C. F. Martin & Company (often referred to simply as Martin) is an American guitar manufacturer established in 1833 by Christian Frederick Martin. It is highly regarded by musicians and collectors and is widely credited with inventing many of the features that define the modern acoustic guitar, including the dreadnought body shape and X-bracing of the soundboard.

History

Christian Frederick Martin, born in Markneukirchen, Germany, in 1796, apprenticed in Vienna with the noted guitar maker Johann Georg Stauffer before emigrating to the United States in 1833. He opened a shop in New York City and in 1839 moved to Nazareth, Pennsylvania, where the company's factory remains today. The company has been owned and managed by the Martin family for six generations; the headstock signature "C. F. Martin" and the slogan "America's guitar company" are long-standing trademarks.

Innovations

Martin developed the X-brace pattern for the underside of the guitar top in the 1840s and 1850s, which allowed larger-bodied guitars to withstand the tension of steel strings. In 1916 Martin began building large "dreadnought" guitars — named after the British battleship class — initially for the Oliver Ditson Company and from 1931 under its own name; the D-18 and D-28 became archetypal American flat-top guitars. Martin also built influential archtops, ukuleles, mandolins and, in the 1960s, electric guitars, though acoustics remain its core business.

Instruments and players

Martin guitars have been played by generations of folk, country, bluegrass and rock musicians, including Hank Williams, Johnny Cash, Elvis Presley, Bob Dylan, Neil Young, John Mayer and Ed Sheeran, the latter in collaboration on a signature line. Vintage pre-war Martin dreadnoughts are among the most valuable production acoustic guitars in the collector market.
