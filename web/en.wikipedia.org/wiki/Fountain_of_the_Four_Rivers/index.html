The Fountain of the Four Rivers (Italian: Fontana dei Quattro Fiumi) is a fountain in the Piazza Navona in Rome, Italy. It was designed in 1651 by Gian Lorenzo Bernini for Pope Innocent X, whose family palace, the Palazzo Pamphili, faced onto the piazza.

Design

The fountain supports an ancient Egyptian obelisk over a hollowed travertine basin. Around it recline four river gods representing the four major rivers of the four continents then known: the Nile (Africa), the Danube (Europe), the Ganges (Asia), and the Río de la Plata (the Americas), each identified by the flora, animals, and attributes carved beside them. The Nile's head is veiled, traditionally said to symbolize that its source was then unknown.

History

The fountain was unveiled in June 1651 to great public spectacle. It forms the centerpiece of Piazza Navona, flanked by Bernini's Fountain of the Moor and the Fountain of Neptune, and faces Borromini's church of Sant'Agnese in Agone — a juxtaposition long embellished by legends of rivalry between the two Baroque masters.
