Clermont-Ferrand (Auvergnat: Clarmont-Ferrand) is a city and commune of France, in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, with a population of about 147,000. It is the prefecture (capital) of the Puy-de-Dôme department, and lies on the edge of the Limagne plain in the Massif Central, overlooked by the volcanic Chaîne des Puys.

History

The city unites two ancient towns: Clermont, seat of the bishops of Auvergne and site of the Roman Augustonemetum, and Montferrand, founded by the counts of Auvergne in the 12th century; they were formally merged in the 17th century. At the Council of Clermont in 1095, Pope Urban II preached the call that launched the First Crusade. Blaise Pascal, born in Clermont in 1623, conducted his famous barometric experiments on the nearby Puy de Dôme.

Economy

Clermont-Ferrand is the home of Michelin, founded there in 1889, which remains the city's dominant employer and one of the world's largest tyre manufacturers. Food processing, metallurgy and pharmaceuticals are also significant, and the city hosts the University of Clermont Auvergne.

Landmarks

The Gothic cathedral of Notre-Dame de l'Assomption, built of black volcanic stone, dominates the old town, as does the Romanesque basilica of Notre-Dame du Port, a UNESCO World Heritage site on the Routes of Santiago de Compostela. The city hosts the Clermont-Ferrand International Short Film Festival, the world's leading festival for short films. The nearby Puy de Dôme, a dormant volcano, is one of France's most visited natural sites.
