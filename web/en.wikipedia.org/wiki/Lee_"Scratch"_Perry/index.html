Lee "Scratch" Perry (born Rainford Hugh Perry; 20 March 1936 – 29 August 2021) was a Jamaican record producer, composer and singer noted for his innovative studio techniques and production approach. He was among the pioneers in the development of dub music in the 1970s with his early adoption of remix and studio effects to create new musical versions of existing reggae tracks. He worked with and produced for a wide variety of artists, including Bob Marley and the Wailers, Junior Murvin, The Congos, Adrian Sherwood, Mad Professor, and the Beastie Boys.

Early career

Perry was born in Kendal, Hanover Parish, Jamaica. He left school early and worked various jobs before moving to Kingston in the late 1950s, where he found employment at Clement "Coxsone" Dodd's sound system and Studio One label, working as a gofer, talent scout and uncredited songwriter and arranger. After falling out with Dodd, he moved to Joe Gibbs's Amalgamated Records, and in 1968 formed his own label, Upsetter Records, named after his 1968 hit "I Am the Upsetter", aimed at Gibbs.

The Upsetters and the Black Ark

Perry's house band, The Upsetters, recorded extensively with him. His production of the Wailers' early 1970s material, including the albums Soul Rebels and Soul Revolution, is widely regarded as some of the finest of the era. In 1973 he built the Black Ark studio behind his family home in Washington Gardens, Kingston, where, using only basic four-track equipment, he produced landmark dub albums such as Super Ape (1976) and Junior Murvin's Police and Thieves (1977), and The Congos' Heart of the Congos (1977). The studio was destroyed by fire in the early 1980s, in circumstances Perry variously described.

Later life and work

Perry spent much of his later life in Switzerland with his family, continuing to record and perform prolifically, and collaborating with producers such as Adrian Sherwood and Mad Professor. He won a Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album in 2003 for Jamaican E.T. His eccentric public persona — mirrored costumes, mystical pronouncements and ritualistic performances — became as famous as his music. Perry died on 29 August 2021 at Noel Holmes Hospital in Lucea, Jamaica, aged 85.
