Tollens' reagent (chemical formula Ag(NH₃)₂OH) is a chemical reagent used to distinguish aldehydes from ketones and to detect reducing sugars. It consists of a solution of silver nitrate and ammonia, forming the diamminesilver(I) complex ion. It is named after the German chemist Bernhard Tollens.

The silver mirror test

When an aldehyde is added to freshly prepared Tollens' reagent and warmed, the aldehyde is oxidised to a carboxylic acid (as its ammonium salt) while the silver ions are reduced to metallic silver. If the test tube is clean, the silver deposits as a bright mirror on the glass — the famous silver mirror test. Ordinary ketones do not react (alpha-hydroxy ketones are an exception), so the test confirms the presence of an aldehyde group. It is also positive for formic acid and reducing sugars such as glucose.

Safety and preparation

The reagent must be freshly prepared and never stored: on standing, it can form explosive silver nitride ("fulminating silver"). After use, residues are destroyed with dilute acid. Tollens' reagent is also used in the chemical silvering of mirrors and in analytical chemistry.
