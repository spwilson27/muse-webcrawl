The International Maritime Organization (IMO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for measures to improve the safety and security of international shipping and to prevent marine pollution from ships. Its mission, summed up in its own formulation, is to promote "safe, secure, environmentally sound, efficient and sustainable shipping through cooperation". The IMO is the global standard-setting authority for shipping; the standards it adopts are given effect through international conventions ratified by member states.

The IMO headquarters on the Albert Embankment in London, its only headquarters worldwide.

History

The IMO Convention was adopted at a conference in Geneva in 1948 and entered into force in 1958; the organisation first met in 1959. It was originally named the Inter-Governmental Maritime Consultative Organization (IMCO); the name was changed to IMO in 1982. The organisation has 175 member states and three associate members, and its secretariat is based in London.

Conventions

The IMO has developed a comprehensive body of international law, of which the most important instruments include:

  • SOLAS — International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (1914, in response to the Titanic disaster; modern version 1974), covering ship construction, fire protection, life-saving appliances and safety management.
  • MARPOL 73/78 — International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships, covering oil, noxious substances, sewage, garbage and air emissions.
  • STCW — Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers (1978).
  • COLREGs — Convention on the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (1972).

Structure and work

The IMO Assembly, comprising all member states, meets every two years. Between sessions a Council of 40 states acts as governing body, while technical work is carried out by committees—notably the Maritime Safety Committee (MSC), the Marine Environment Protection Committee (MEPC), the Legal Committee and the Facilitation Committee—and their sub-committees. In recent decades the IMO has focused increasingly on decarbonisation: its 2023 GHG Strategy aims for net-zero greenhouse-gas emissions from international shipping "by or around" 2050.
