Line of sight

Line of sight
FieldsOptics, telecommunications, surveying, gaming
Related conceptsFresnel zone, radio horizon, visibility

The line of sight (LOS) is the straight line along which an observer has unobstructed vision of an object, or along which electromagnetic radiation travels between a transmitter and a receiver. A target within line of sight can be seen or contacted directly; one beyond terrain or the horizon cannot.

In telecommunications

Radio waves at VHF and higher frequencies travel essentially in straight lines, so microwave links, satellite ground stations and many wireless systems require a clear line of sight, including clearance of the Fresnel zone around the direct path. Obstructions cause reflection, diffraction and absorption; lower-frequency signals can propagate beyond the horizon by ground wave or ionospheric reflection. The radio horizon extends slightly beyond the geometric horizon because of atmospheric refraction.

In other fields

In surveying and astronomy, line of sight determines intervisibility between points and the apparent position of celestial objects. In architecture and urban planning, sightlines are protected for landmarks. In games and military contexts, line of sight governs whether a unit can see or engage a target, computed by ray casting through terrain and obstacles.
