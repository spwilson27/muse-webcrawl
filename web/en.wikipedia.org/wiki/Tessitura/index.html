Tessitura

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In music, tessitura (Italian for "texture"; plural tessiture) is the most acceptable and comfortable vocal range for a given singer or, less commonly, the range in which the notes of a melodic line predominantly lie for a musical instrument. It differs from the total range of a voice or part: a singer may be capable of extreme high and low notes, but the tessitura is where most of the singing sits and where the voice sounds best and can be sustained without fatigue.

Vocal tessitura

Composers writing songs or operatic roles shape them around a particular tessitura suited to a voice type (soprano, tenor, baritone, and so on). A role with a high tessitura — such as the Queen of the Night in Mozart's The Magic Flute, whose part repeatedly sits around high coloratura passages — can be within a singer's range yet exhausting if the bulk of the notes lie near an extreme. Singers and teachers assess repertoire by tessitura more than by absolute range when judging suitability.

Instrumental tessitura

The term is also applied to instruments: a passage may lie in a high or low tessitura of a clarinet or violin, affecting its timbre and difficulty even where it is technically playable. Melodic tessitura is sometimes analysed statistically, for example by the median pitch of a melody.

Etymology

The word derives from the Italian tessitura, "weaving/texture", reflecting the idea of the fabric in which the notes lie.
