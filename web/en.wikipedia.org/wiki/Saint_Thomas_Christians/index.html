The Saint Thomas Christians, also called Nasrani or Malayalam-speaking Syrian Christians, are an ethno-religious community of Christians in Kerala, India, who traditionally trace their origins to the evangelistic activity of Thomas the Apostle in the 1st century AD. For centuries they formed a single church in communion with the Church of the East, worshipping in the East Syriac liturgical tradition.

The Persian (Saint Thomas) cross of carved stone, a symbol of the community's ancient East Syriac heritage.

Origins

According to tradition, Thomas arrived at Muziris (Kodungallur) in AD 52, preached the Gospel, founded churches and was martyred at Mylapore near Chennai. Historians consider it certain that Christianity reached the Malabar Coast early—likely through trade links with the Middle East—and by the 4th–6th centuries the church in India was firmly part of the Persian Church of the East, receiving bishops and Syriac liturgy from Persia. Copper-plate charters record privileges granted to the community by Hindu rulers, and the Christians enjoyed high social status.

Portuguese period and division

After the Portuguese arrived in 1498, they sought to bring the Saint Thomas Christians under Latin jurisdiction. The Synod of Diamper (1599) imposed Latin practices and burned Syriac books, provoking the Coonan Cross Oath of 1653, in which many Christians swore never to submit to Jesuit rule. The community split: one part remained in communion with Rome (today's Syro-Malabar Church), while the other aligned with the Syriac Orthodox (West Syriac) tradition. Over subsequent centuries, further divisions—driven by theology, colonial influence and internal disputes—produced today's family of churches.

Present-day churches

Descendant denominations include the Syro-Malabar Church (Eastern Catholic, the largest), the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, the Jacobite Syrian Christian Church, the Syro-Malankara Catholic Church, the Malabar Independent Syrian Church, the Chaldean Syrian Church, and the Mar Thoma Syrian Church and St. Thomas Evangelical Church (Reformed in character), together with Pentecostal offshoots. Together they number roughly six million adherents, concentrated in Kerala with a large global diaspora.
