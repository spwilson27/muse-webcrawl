Hermann von Helmholtz

Born31 August 1821, Potsdam, Prussia
Died8 September 1894, Berlin
FieldsPhysics, physiology
Known forConservation of energy, ophthalmoscope, Helmholtz resonance, colour vision

Hermann Ludwig Ferdinand von Helmholtz (1821–1894) was a German physicist and physician who made fundamental contributions to energy conservation, optics, acoustics and electromagnetism.

Energy

His 1847 paper Über die Erhaltung der Kraft gave a mathematical formulation of the conservation of energy based on the impossibility of perpetual motion.

Physiology and optics

Helmholtz measured the speed of nerve conduction, invented the ophthalmoscope (1851), developed the Young–Helmholtz theory of trichromatic colour vision, and wrote the monumental Handbook of Physiological Optics and On the Sensations of Tone (1863) on acoustics and music perception.

Physics

He worked on fluid vortices, electrodynamics, thermodynamics (Helmholtz free energy) and mentored Heinrich Hertz. The Helmholtz Association of German research centres bears his name.
