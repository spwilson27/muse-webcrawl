Duan Qirui

Duan Qirui (Chinese: 段祺瑞; 1865–1936) was a Chinese warlord and statesman of the early Republic of China. A leading figure of the Beiyang Army and leader of the Anhui clique, he served repeatedly as Premier of the Republic of China between 1916 and 1918 and as Chief Executive (head of state) from 1924 to 1926.

Career

Trained in artillery in Germany, Duan rose under Yuan Shikai. As premier he pressed for China's entry into the First World War against Germany, sought Japanese loans (the Nishihara Loans) and built the War Participation Army. Rivalry with Zhili clique leader Cao Kun and defeats in the Zhili–Anhui War of 1920 ended his dominance, though he briefly returned as chief executive after the Beijing Coup of 1924.

Later life

His government's responsibility for the March 18 Massacre of student protesters in 1926 damaged his reputation. He retired to Tianjin and Shanghai, devoted himself to Buddhism and weiqi, and died in Shanghai in 1936.
