Stable homotopy groups of spheres

In algebraic topology, the stable homotopy groups of spheres are a fundamental invariant describing, roughly, how spheres of different dimensions can wrap around one another in a stable range. They are defined as the limits of the ordinary homotopy groups of spheres under the suspension homomorphism, and are denoted πnS.

Schematic of suspension maps between homotopy groups of spheres

Definition

By Freudenthal's suspension theorem, the suspension map πn+k(Sk) → πn+k+1(Sk+1) is an isomorphism once k is large compared with n. The stable homotopy group πnS is the value of this group in the stable range. Together the stable groups form a graded ring under composition, and they are also the homotopy groups of the sphere spectrum.

Known values

The first stable groups are π0S ≅ Z, generated by the identity; π1S ≅ Z/2, generated by the Hopf map η; π2S ≅ Z/2, generated by η²; and π3S ≅ Z/24, generated by the Hopf invariant one element ν. In general the groups πnS are finite abelian groups for n > 0, a theorem of Serre, but their complete structure is known only in a finite range of degrees and their computation is one of the hardest problems in topology.

Computation

The principal tools are spectral sequences, beginning with the Adams spectral sequence, which computes the stable groups from Ext groups over the Steenrod algebra. More recent work uses motivic and equivariant methods; computer calculations by several authors have pushed the known range beyond the 60th stem. The stable homotopy groups also appear in geometry through the classification of exotic spheres and the Kervaire invariant problem.
