Intelligence Bureau (India)

Intelligence Bureau
Formed: 23 December 1887
Headquarters: New Delhi, India
Parent: Ministry of Home Affairs
Function: Domestic intelligence and internal security

The Intelligence Bureau (IB) is India's internal security and counter-intelligence agency, operating under the Ministry of Home Affairs. Regarded as one of the world's oldest intelligence agencies still in existence, it traces its origin to the Central Special Branch created in 1887 by the British Indian Army.

History

The IB was established on 23 December 1887, following the recommendations of Major General Charles MacGregor, and was initially staffed by military officers. After Indian independence in 1947 it was reorganized under the Home Ministry. Failures surrounding the Sino-Indian War of 1962 contributed to the creation of the external agency, the Research and Analysis Wing (R&AW), in 1968, leaving the IB focused on domestic intelligence.

Functions

The Bureau collects intelligence within India on terrorism, insurgency, communal violence, smuggling, and threats to public order, and performs counter-intelligence, VIP security assessments, and vetting. Its officers include personnel drawn from the Indian Police Service and its own cadre recruited through examinations such as the Assistant Central Intelligence Officer exam.

Criticism

The IB operates without a statutory charter or parliamentary oversight mechanism specific to it, a feature it shares with other Indian intelligence agencies and which has drawn criticism and reform proposals, particularly after intelligence failures preceding the 2008 Mumbai attacks.
