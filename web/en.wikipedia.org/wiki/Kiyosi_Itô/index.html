Kiyosi Itô (伊藤 清, Itō Kiyoshi, 7 September 1915 – 10 November 2008) was a Japanese mathematician who developed the foundations of stochastic calculus — the Itô calculus — which provides a rigorous way to integrate and differentiate functions of random processes such as Brownian motion. His stochastic integral, stochastic differential equations, and the formula known as Itô's lemma became indispensable tools in probability theory and, from the 1970s, in mathematical finance, where they underlie the Black–Scholes option-pricing model. He is often called the father of the modern theory of stochastic processes.

Life and career

Itô was born in Hokusei (now Inabe), Mie Prefecture, and studied mathematics at the Imperial University of Tokyo, graduating in 1938. From 1939 to 1942 he worked at the Government Statistical Bureau, where, struck by Kolmogorov's and Lévy's work on probability, he developed his ideas in relative isolation during the war years. His landmark paper "On Stochastic Processes" (1942) introduced the stochastic integral and laid out the theory of stochastic differential equations; a 1944 paper gave the change-of-variables formula now called Itô's lemma (published in fuller form in 1951).

He received his doctorate in 1945 and became a professor at Kyoto University in 1952. Itô spent periods abroad at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, Cornell University (where he was professor 1969–1975), and Aarhus University, before returning to Kyoto and later becoming director of the Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences. He retired in 1979 and continued publishing into old age.

Work

Itô's calculus extends ordinary calculus to paths of Brownian motion, which are nowhere differentiable: his integral defines the integral of a random process against Brownian motion as a limit in probability, and his formula gives the differential of a function of a diffusion process, including the characteristic extra second-order term. He also founded the modern theory of stochastic differential equations and diffusion processes (Itô diffusions), classified by their drift and diffusion coefficients, and with Henry McKean wrote the influential text Diffusion Processes and Their Sample Paths (1965). Work in stochastic analysis by Itô and his school shaped probability worldwide.

Honours

Itô received the Asahi Prize (1977), the Wolf Prize in Mathematics (1987, shared with Peter Lax), the Kyoto Prize (1998), and in 2006 the inaugural Carl Friedrich Gauss Prize for Applications of Mathematics, whose citation emphasised the impact of his work on finance, engineering, and biology. He was a member of the Japan Academy and a foreign member of the French and US academies of sciences.
