The Battle of Rarańcza was fought on 15–16 February 1918 during the First World War, when soldiers of the Polish Auxiliary Corps (the former Polish Legions), commanded by Colonel Józef Haller, fought their way through Austro-Hungarian lines near Rarańcza in Bukovina in protest against the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk between the Central Powers and Ukraine, which assigned Polish-inhabited Chełm Land to Ukraine.

Background

The Polish Legions had been raised to fight alongside the Central Powers against Russia in hope of Polish independence. The "oath crisis" of 1917 and the terms of the Brest-Litovsk treaty of 9 February 1918 convinced many legionaries that Austria-Hungary and Germany were betraying the Polish cause.

The battle

On the night of 15–16 February, Haller's troops broke through the Austro-Hungarian front at Rarańcza after sharp fighting, and crossed into the Russian side of the former front to join Polish forces forming in Russia. Not all units got through: the corps' artillery and trains were stopped, and many soldiers were interned; some officers were later tried for mutiny in the process at Máramarossziget (Sighet).

Aftermath

The units that crossed later formed the Polish II Corps in Russia, which fought the Germans at Kaniów in May 1918. Rarańcza became a celebrated episode in the Polish independence struggle, symbolising the Legions' refusal to serve foreign interests.
