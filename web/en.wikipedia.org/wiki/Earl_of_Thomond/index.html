The title Earl of Thomond was an Irish peerage title held by the O'Brien family, descendants of the High King Brian Boru, who had ruled the Gaelic kingdom of Thomond (in the modern counties of Clare and Limerick) for centuries. In 1543, under Henry VIII's policy of "surrender and regrant", Murrough O'Brien surrendered his Gaelic kingship and was created Earl of Thomond and Baron Inchiquin, while his nephew Donough O'Brien received the barony of Ibrickane, with the earldom to pass according to arrangements that caused later disputes.

History

Donough O'Brien became 2nd Earl in 1553. His successors navigated the Elizabethan wars and the upheavals of the seventeenth century: Connor O'Brien, 3rd Earl, and Donough O'Brien, 4th Earl (died 1624), who was raised partly at the English court; Henry O'Brien, 7th Earl (died 1691); and Henry O'Brien, 8th Earl (1688–1741), the last of the direct line, on whose death the earldom of the first creation became extinct. The family's other titles, including the barony of Inchiquin, continued through another branch, while the marquessate of Thomond was later created (1801) for Murrough O'Brien of the Inchiquin line and became extinct in 1855.

The Thomond name — from the Irish Tuadh-Mhumha, "North Munster" — survives in place names and institutions in County Clare, and the O'Briens remain among the most notable of Ireland's historic Gaelic dynasties.
