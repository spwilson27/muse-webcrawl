Gary K. Wolf (born January 24, 1941) is an American author best known as the creator of Roger Rabbit and the world of Toontown. His 1981 novel Who Censored Roger Rabbit? was adapted into the acclaimed 1988 film Who Framed Roger Rabbit.

Early life and career

Wolf was born and raised in Earlville, Illinois. He earned a degree in advertising from the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign and served as a captain in the United States Air Force, flying missions during the Vietnam War. After leaving the service he worked in advertising in San Francisco while writing fiction.

Roger Rabbit

In Who Censored Roger Rabbit? (1981), Wolf created a hardboiled detective story set in an alternate world where cartoon characters — "Toons" — live alongside humans. The novel's private eye, Eddie Valiant, is hired by comic-strip star Roger Rabbit to investigate his employers and then to solve Roger's murder. The book differs substantially from the film: its Toons appear in comic strips rather than animated cartoons and communicate in word balloons.

Disney's 1988 film adaptation, directed by Robert Zemeckis, combined live action and animation and became a major critical and commercial success, winning Academy Awards for its visual effects, editing and sound editing. Wolf subsequently negotiated rights and royalties relating to the Roger Rabbit characters, including litigation with Disney over merchandising and sequel revenues that was settled in the 2000s.

Sequels and other works

Wolf returned to the characters in Who P-P-P-Plugged Roger Rabbit? (1991), which retconned the series to follow the film's continuity, and in Who Wacked Roger Rabbit? (2013), set in 1947 amid the filming of a fictional Gary Cooper picture. Among his other novels are the science-fiction works Killerbowl (1975), A Generation Removed (1977) and The Resurrectionist (1979). His extensive collection of Roger Rabbit memorabilia has been exhibited and documented in histories of the franchise.
