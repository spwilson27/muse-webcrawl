Capote is a 2005 American biographical drama film directed by Bennett Miller and written by Dan Futterman, based on Gerald Clarke's biography of Truman Capote. It stars Philip Seymour Hoffman as Capote during the research and writing of his pioneering non-fiction novel In Cold Blood (1966), about the 1959 murders of the Clutter family in Kansas.

Plot

Reading of the murders in a newspaper, Capote travels to Kansas with his friend Harper Lee (Catherine Keener), intending an article for The New Yorker. He forms a complicated bond with one of the killers, Perry Smith, mining his life for the book while Smith awaits execution on death row. The film portrays Capote's manipulation of his subjects, his growing fame, and the emotional damage the six-year project inflicts on him: he finishes the book only after Smith and Dick Hickock are hanged in 1965, and never completes another.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at the Telluride Film Festival and was widely acclaimed. Hoffman won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his performance; the film received Oscar nominations for Best Picture, Best Director, Best Supporting Actress (Keener) and Best Adapted Screenplay.
