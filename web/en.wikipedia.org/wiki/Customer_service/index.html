Customer service

Customer service is the assistance and advice provided by a company to those people who buy or use its products or services. Each industry requires different levels of customer service, but towards the end, the idea of a well-performed service is that of increasing revenues. The perception of success of the customer service interactions is dependent on employees "who can adjust themselves to the personality of the customer". Customer service is often seen as an important aspect of what a business can provide.

History

The concept of customer service has its roots in the pre-industrial relationship between merchants and their regular customers, where personal reputation governed trade. With the rise of mail-order catalogues and department stores in the nineteenth century, businesses began formalising return policies and complaint handling. The spread of the telephone in the early twentieth century created the first dedicated service desks, and by the 1970s and 1980s large companies operated call centres staffed by agents working from scripts. The growth of the internet in the 1990s added email support, live chat, and self-service knowledge bases, while social media later made service interactions publicly visible.

Customer support

Customer support is a range of services intended to help customers make cost-effective and correct use of a product. It includes assistance in planning, installation, training, troubleshooting, maintenance, upgrading, and disposal. Support may be delivered in person, by telephone, by email, through live chat, or via social media channels. Many organisations tier their support, with front-line agents resolving common issues and specialists handling escalated cases.

Automation

Automation has repeatedly reshaped customer service. Interactive voice response systems route callers and resolve simple queries without an agent. Chatbots and virtual assistants handle routine questions on websites and messaging platforms, while customer relationship management (CRM) software gives agents a unified view of a customer's history. Self-service portals and community forums allow customers to find answers themselves, reserving human contact for complex problems.

Feedback and satisfaction

Organisations measure service quality through customer satisfaction surveys, the Net Promoter Score, first-contact resolution rates, and average handling time. Feedback is used to identify recurring product faults, retrain staff, and redesign processes. Research in service marketing links high satisfaction with customer loyalty, repeat purchases, and positive word of mouth, while poor service is a leading cause of customer churn.
