Posse Comitatus (organization)

Overview
Founded:1969
Founder:Henry L. Beach
Ideology:Christian Identity, anti-government, white supremacist
Region:United States

The Posse Comitatus is a loosely organized American far-right extremist social movement that began in the late 1960s. Its members spread a conspiracy-minded, anti-government, and antisemitic ideology, asserting that the legitimate form of government in the United States is at the county level, embodied in the county sheriff, and that no authority above the sheriff is lawful. The movement is frequently associated with the Christian Identity movement and with white supremacist beliefs.

History

The movement was founded around 1969 by Henry Lamont "Mike" Beach, a former member of the Silver Shirts, a 1930s American fascist organization. William Potter Gale, a retired Army colonel and Christian Identity figure, is also credited with shaping the movement's ideology in the 1970s. Posse Comitatus chapters spread through the Midwest and West during the farm crisis of the late 1970s and 1980s.

Activities and influence

Members refused to pay taxes, obtain driver's licenses, or recognize federal authority, and some resorted to violence; in 1983, member Gordon Kahl killed two U.S. marshals in North Dakota and was himself killed in Arkansas. The movement's ideas about the sheriff as the highest lawful authority and its "paper terrorism" tactics influenced the later sovereign citizen movement and the modern American militia movement.
