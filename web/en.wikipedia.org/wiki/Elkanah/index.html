Elkanah

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Elkanah (Hebrew: אֱלְקָנָה, ʾElqānā, "God has purchased" or "God has created") is a biblical figure in the First Book of Samuel, best known as the husband of Hannah and the father of the prophet Samuel. He was an Ephraimite living at Ramathaim-Zophim in the hill country of Ephraim.

Biblical narrative

According to 1 Samuel, Elkanah had two wives: Hannah, whom he loved, and Peninnah, who had children while Hannah was barren. Each year Elkanah travelled with his household to worship and sacrifice at the sanctuary of Shiloh, where he gave portions of the offering to Peninnah and her children and a choice portion to Hannah, who was taunted by Peninnah for her childlessness. Elkanah comforted Hannah — "Am I not more to you than ten sons?" — and after Hannah's fervent prayer at Shiloh and her vow to dedicate the child to God, she conceived and bore Samuel.

True to the vow, Elkanah went up with Hannah to present the young Samuel at Shiloh, where the boy was left to serve under the priest Eli. Elkanah and Hannah subsequently had further children, and they visited Samuel annually, Hannah bringing him a little robe she had made.

Genealogy

Elkanah's lineage is given in 1 Samuel 1:1 as son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, an Ephraimite. A genealogy in 1 Chronicles 6 traces the Levitical line of Samuel's descendants, including the musician Heman, through an Elkanah; several other men named Elkanah appear elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible, including a Korahite ancestor and an official of King Ahaz.

In tradition

Elkanah is portrayed in rabbinic tradition as a pious man whose annual pilgrimages helped revive public worship at Shiloh. His story frames the opening of the Books of Samuel, setting the stage for the birth of Samuel and the transition from the period of the judges to the monarchy in Israel.
