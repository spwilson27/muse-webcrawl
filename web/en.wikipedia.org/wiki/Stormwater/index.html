Stormwater

Stormwater, also written storm water, is water that originates from precipitation (storm), including heavy rain and meltwater from hail and snow. Stormwater can soak into the soil (infiltrate) and become groundwater, be stored on depressed land and surface water, or evaporate. The remainder, which flows overland or through pipes, is known as surface runoff. In most cases stormwater recovered prior to becoming polluted, owing to its contact with closed land surfaces (greenfield runoff) and collection structures like channels, can be a useful resource, while stormwater that has flowed over urban surfaces picks up pollutants and is often treated as a waste.

Quantity and impervious surfaces

Natural catchments infiltrate most rainfall, but urbanisation replaces soil and vegetation with impervious surfaces — roofs, roads, car parks — that shed water rapidly. Runoff volume and peak flow therefore increase with urban development, raising flood risk, eroding stream channels, and reducing groundwater recharge. Engineers estimate flows with methods such as the rational method and design storm sewers, culverts and detention basins for storms of chosen intensity and recurrence interval.

Pollution

Urban stormwater carries sediment, nutrients, heavy metals, hydrocarbons, road salt, litter and pathogens washed from streets and roofs. In cities with combined sewers, heavy rain can overwhelm the system and cause combined sewer overflows of diluted but untreated sewage into waterways. Stormwater is now recognised in many countries as a leading cause of impairment of urban rivers and estuaries.

Management

Traditional management conveyed stormwater away as quickly as possible through gutters, pipes and storm drains. Modern approaches — known as low-impact development (LID) in the United States, sustainable drainage systems (SuDS) in the United Kingdom, and water-sensitive urban design in Australia — aim to restore natural water balance using green infrastructure:

  • Rain gardens and bioswales that filter and infiltrate runoff
  • Permeable pavements that let water soak through
  • Green roofs and rainwater harvesting tanks
  • Constructed wetlands and retention ponds that settle sediment and treat pollutants

Under the U.S. Clean Water Act, stormwater discharges from municipal separate storm sewer systems (MS4s) and industrial and construction sites are regulated through permits requiring management plans and best management practices.
