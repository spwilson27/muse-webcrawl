Minoan chronology

Minoan chronology is the dating framework for the Bronze Age Minoan civilization of Crete. Because Minoan writing (Linear A) remains undeciphered, dating relies on stratigraphy, pottery sequences, synchronisms with historically dated Egyptian and Near Eastern objects, and scientific methods such as radiocarbon dating and dendrochronology.

Evans's scheme

Sir Arthur Evans, excavator of Knossos, divided the Minoan Bronze Age into Early, Middle, and Late Minoan periods (EM, MM, LM), each subdivided into I, II, and III and further into A and B phases, based largely on pottery styles. A complementary scheme classifies the culture by palace history: Prepalatial (c. 3100–1925 BC), Protopalatial or Old Palace (c. 1925–1750 BC), Neopalatial or New Palace (c. 1750–1490 BC), and Postpalatial periods.

Dating debates

The eruption of Thera (Santorini), which buried the Minoan town of Akrotiri, anchors the chronology. Traditional archaeological dating places it around 1525–1500 BC, while radiocarbon and ice-core evidence suggests a date in the late 17th century BC, around 1628 BC — the core of the long-running "high" versus "low" chronology controversy in Aegean prehistory.
