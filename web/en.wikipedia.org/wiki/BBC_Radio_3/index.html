BBC Radio 3 is a British national radio station owned and operated by the BBC. It replaced the BBC Third Programme in 1967 and broadcasts classical music and opera, jazz, world music, new music, arts programmes and drama. By Baroque standards it is unusual among classical broadcasters for the amount of live and specially recorded performance it airs, including every concert of the BBC Proms season each summer.

History

The station's predecessor, the Third Programme, launched on 29 September 1946 as the third pillar of the BBC's post-war radio output alongside the Home Service and Light Programme, devoted to high culture: serious music, drama, talks and poetry. When the BBC reorganised its national networks in 1967, the Third Programme's frequencies became Radio 3. Under controllers including William Glock, John Drummond, Nicholas Kenyon and Roger Wright, the station balanced its minority remit with occasional audience-building initiatives; Kenyon's tenure saw the controversial introduction of weekday breakfast programming in a lighter style modelled on commercial rival Classic FM, founded in 1992.

Programming

Radio 3 sustains much of the BBC's performing infrastructure: it is closely associated with the BBC Symphony Orchestra, BBC Singers and the other BBC orchestras and choirs, and broadcasts from venues such as the Royal Albert Hall and Wigmore Hall. Long-running strands include Composer of the Week, Through the Night, Jazz Record Requests, Words and Music and the essay and documentary slot The Essay. The station also commissions new music extensively and runs talent schemes such as the New Generation Artists programme for young performers.

Reception

Although its audience share is small — around one per cent of UK radio listening — Radio 3 is regarded as a flagship of the BBC's public-service remit and has repeatedly won Station of the Year at the industry Radio Academy Awards.
