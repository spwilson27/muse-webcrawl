Tachypnea

SpecialtyRespirology, internal medicine
SymptomsAbnormally rapid breathing
CausesFever, hypoxia, pneumonia, heart failure, metabolic acidosis, anxiety
Differential diagnosisDyspnea, hyperventilation, tachycardia

Tachypnea (from the Greek tachy-, meaning fast, and pnoia, meaning breath) is abnormally rapid breathing. In adult humans at rest, any respiratory rate between 12 and 20 breaths per minute is considered clinically normal, while tachypnea is generally defined as a rate above 20 breaths per minute. Resting respiratory rates are higher in children than in adults, and the thresholds for tachypnea are correspondingly adjusted by age: the World Health Organization defines tachypnea in infants under two months as more than 60 breaths per minute, in infants two to twelve months as more than 50 breaths per minute, and in children one to five years as more than 40 breaths per minute.

Causes

Tachypnea may be a physiological response to increased metabolic demand, as during exercise or fever, or a sign of underlying disease. Common pathological causes include pneumonia and other causes of impaired gas exchange in the lungs, congestive heart failure, pulmonary embolism, asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, sepsis, metabolic acidosis (where rapid breathing helps expel carbon dioxide and compensate for excess acid), carbon monoxide poisoning, and central nervous system disorders. Anxiety and panic attacks can also produce a transiently elevated respiratory rate.

Related terms

Tachypnea is distinguished from hyperventilation, in which the increased rate of breathing leads to abnormally decreased levels of carbon dioxide in the blood; tachypnea may occur without such a change. It is also distinct from dyspnea, the subjective sensation of breathlessness, although the two frequently occur together. The opposite condition, abnormally slow breathing, is called bradypnea, while the complete cessation of breathing is apnea.

Clinical significance

Because the respiratory rate is one of the principal vital signs, tachypnea is an important early warning marker of clinical deterioration. It forms part of several early-warning scoring systems used in hospitals, and in infants and young children it is a key sign used in the assessment of possible pneumonia in resource-limited settings.
