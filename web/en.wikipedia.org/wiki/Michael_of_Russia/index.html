Michael I (Russian: Михаи́л Фёдорович Рома́нов, romanized: Mikhaíl Fyódorovich Románov; 22 July 1596 – 22/23 July 1645) was the first tsar of Russia from the House of Romanov. He reigned from 1613 to 1645, having been elected to the throne by the Zemsky Sobor (assembly of the land) in February 1613, an event that brought an end to the chaotic period known as the Time of Troubles.

Early life

Michael was born in Moscow, the son of Feodor Nikitich Romanov, a boyar who later became Patriarch Filaret of Moscow, and Kseniya Ivanovna Shestova. His family was related by marriage to the last tsar of the Rurik dynasty, Feodor I, whose wife Irina Godunova was Michael's great-aunt. During the reign of Boris Godunov the Romanovs fell from favour: Feodor Nikitich was forcibly tonsured as the monk Filaret, and the young Michael was exiled with his mother to the remote village of Klin.

Election to the throne

After the Polish forces were expelled from Moscow in 1612 by the volunteer army of Kuzma Minin and Dmitry Pozharsky, a national assembly, the Zemsky Sobor, convened in January 1613 to elect a new tsar. On 21 February 1613 the assembly chose the sixteen-year-old Michael Romanov, who was then living at the Ipatiev Monastery in Kostroma. According to tradition, he and his mother at first refused the crown; Michael was crowned in the Dormition Cathedral of the Moscow Kremlin on 22 July 1613.

Reign

Michael's reign was dominated by the task of restoring a country devastated by war, famine and foreign intervention. The Treaty of Stolbovo (1617) ended the war with Sweden, while the Truce of Deulino (1618) concluded hostilities with the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, though at the cost of Smolensk and other territories. From 1619 until his death in 1633, Michael's father, Patriarch Filaret, effectively ruled as co-sovereign, bearing the title of Great Sovereign.

The Smolensk War (1632–1634) failed to recover the territories lost to Poland, ending with the Treaty of Polyanovka, under which Władysław IV renounced his claim to the Russian throne. Domestically, Michael's government strengthened central authority, reorganised the military with the help of foreign officers, expanded into Siberia, and fortified the southern frontier against Crimean Tatar raids.

Family and death

Michael married Maria Dolgorukova in 1624; she died a few months later. In 1626 he married Eudoxia Streshneva, with whom he had ten children, including his successor Alexis I and Tsarevna Irina. Michael died in Moscow in July 1645 and was buried in the Archangel Cathedral of the Kremlin. The Romanov dynasty he founded ruled Russia until 1917.
