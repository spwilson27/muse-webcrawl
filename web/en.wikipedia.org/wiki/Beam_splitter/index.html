Beam splitter

Type: Optical device
Function: Divides a beam of light into two or more beams

A beam splitter or beamsplitter is an optical device that splits a beam of light into a transmitted and a reflected beam. It is a crucial part of many optical experimental and measurement systems, such as interferometers, and is also widely used in fibre-optic telecommunications and in the viewfinding systems of cameras.

Designs

In its most common form, a cube, a beam splitter is made from two triangular glass prisms glued together with a partly reflective coating (often a thin metallic or dielectric layer) at the interface, so that half the incident light is reflected and half transmitted, giving a 50:50 split ratio. Plate beam splitters use a thin partly-silvered mirror; dichroic beam splitters separate light by wavelength; and polarising beam splitters separate it by polarisation. Fibre-optic couplers perform the same role for guided light.

Phase shifts and quantum optics

For a lossless symmetric beam splitter, unitarity requires a definite phase relationship between the reflected and transmitted amplitudes — reflection from one side carries a phase shift of π/2 relative to the other. This property underlies interference experiments such as the Hong–Ou–Mandel effect, where two identical photons entering the two input ports of a 50:50 beam splitter always leave together from the same output port, a cornerstone demonstration of quantum optics.

Applications

Beam splitters are used in interferometers (Michelson, Mach–Zehnder), autocollimators, single-lens reflex and rangefinder cameras, laser systems, optical coherence tomography, quantum information experiments, and fibre-optic networks.
