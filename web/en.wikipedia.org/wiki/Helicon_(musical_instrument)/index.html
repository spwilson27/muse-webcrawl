Helicon (musical instrument)

The helicon is a brass musical instrument in the tuba family. Its distinguishing characteristic is its circular, coiled tubing, which allows the instrument to be carried over the player's shoulder while playing, with the large bell pointing forward or upward. Helicons are most commonly built in E-flat and B-flat.

A helicon, worn around the body

History

The helicon was invented by Ignaz Stowasser in Vienna around 1845 and was patented in 1848. It was designed for use in military bands, where the wrap-around shape made it practical for marching and for cavalry musicians. The instrument spread through European and Russian military bands in the later 19th century and remains popular in Eastern European and Balkan brass bands.

Relationship to the sousaphone

The American sousaphone, developed in the 1890s by J. W. Pepper and popularised by John Philip Sousa's band, was derived from the helicon, adding a large, detachable, forward- or upward-facing bell. In modern usage the helicon generally keeps the bell integrated with the coil and pointing forward, while the sousaphone's bell points up or forward above the player's head.
