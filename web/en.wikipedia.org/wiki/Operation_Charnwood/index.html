Operation Charnwood

Operation Charnwood was an Anglo-Canadian offensive that took place from 8 to 9 July 1944, during the Battle for Caen, part of the larger Battle of Normandy in the Second World War. The operation was intended to capture the German-occupied city of Caen, a key D-Day objective that had remained in German hands for over a month, and to forestall a German counter-attack against the Allied beachhead.

Prelude

Caen was a vital road hub and an objective assigned to the British 3rd Infantry Division on D-Day (6 June 1944), but strong German defences, including the 21st Panzer Division, held the city. Subsequent operations — Perch, Epsom and Windsor — failed to take Caen itself, though they drew in German armoured reserves.

The operation

On the evening of 7 July, RAF Bomber Command dropped over 2,000 tons of bombs on the northern part of the city, causing heavy destruction and civilian casualties. On 8 July, I Corps attacked with the 3rd and 59th (Staffordshire) Infantry Divisions and the 33rd Armoured Brigade, supported by artillery and specialised armour. By 9 July the Allies had captured Caen north of the River Orne, while German forces withdrew to the southern suburbs across the river.

Aftermath

Charnwood achieved its limited objective and further eroded German strength around Caen. The battle for the whole city was completed during Operation Goodwood and Operation Atlantic later in July. The bombing and fighting left much of Caen in ruins.
