Robert James Keeshan (June 27, 1927 – January 23, 2004) was an American television producer and actor. He created and played the title role in the children's television program Captain Kangaroo, which ran on CBS from 1955 to 1984 — one of the longest-running nationally broadcast children's shows. He had earlier originated the role of Clarabell the Clown on the Howdy Doody show.

Early life

Keeshan was born in Lynbrook, New York. In 1945, on his 18th birthday, he enlisted in the United States Marine Corps Reserve; because the war ended shortly afterwards he did not see combat — a fact later garbled into a persistent (and false) urban legend that he had fought at Iwo Jima alongside actor Lee Marvin. After the war he worked as an NBC page while studying, and joined the Howdy Doody production.

Captain Kangaroo

Frustrated with the noisy, frenetic style of early children's television, Keeshan proposed a gentler programme in which a friendly grandfather figure — named for the big pockets of his jacket, like a kangaroo's pouch — hosted stories, visitors, crafts and cartoon segments from the "Treasure House". Captain Kangaroo premiered on 3 October 1955 and ran for nearly 30 years, with characters including Mr. Green Jeans (Hugh "Lumpy" Brannum), Dancing Bear, Bunny Rabbit and Mr. Moose, whose knock-knock jokes reliably triggered a shower of ping-pong balls. The show won multiple Emmy and Peabody awards and influenced generations of children's programming.

Later life and advocacy

After the show ended, Keeshan hosted segments on CBS and became a prominent child advocate, speaking against violence in children's media and testifying before Congress. He wrote books including the memoir Growing Up Happy (1995) and children's titles. He was inducted into the Television Hall of Fame in 1998. Keeshan died in Vermont in 2004 after a long cardiac illness.
