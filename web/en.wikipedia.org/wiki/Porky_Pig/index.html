Porky Pig

Porky Pig is an animated cartoon character in the Warner Bros. Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series of cartoons. He was the first character created by the studio to draw audiences based on his star power, and the animators created many critically acclaimed cartoons around him. Porky is known for his stutter and for ending cartoons by bursting through a drum and stammering "Th-th-th-that's all, folks!"

History

Porky debuted in the 1935 Merrie Melodies cartoon I Haven't Got a Hat, directed by Friz Freleng, as a shy schoolboy pig in a talent show. Originally voiced by Joe Dougherty, whose natural stutter made recording slow and expensive, the role passed in 1937 to Mel Blanc, who refined the stutter into a comic trademark — swapping words mid-sentence when a word proved too difficult ("I had a h-h-h... a d-d-d... a tough time").

Role in the cartoons

In the studio's early black-and-white Looney Tunes, Porky was the lead character. As Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck rose in popularity, Porky settled into the role of the earnest, good-natured straight man — most famously in director Chuck Jones's hunting trilogy (Rabbit Fire, Rabbit Seasoning, Duck! Rabbit, Duck!) and in Bob Clampett's surreal cartoons such as Porky in Wackyland (1938). He also starred opposite Daffy Duck in parodies including Duck Dodgers in the 24½th Century (1953), as the eager young space cadet.

Signature elements

Porky wears a blue jacket, a red bow tie and no trousers. His closing line, "That's all, folks!", delivered over the Merrie Melodies theme, became one of the most famous sign-offs in animation. The character has continued to appear in later Warner Bros. productions, including The Looney Tunes Show and New Looney Tunes.
