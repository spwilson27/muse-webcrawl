P&O (in full the Peninsular and Oriental Steam Navigation Company) was a British shipping and logistics company dating from the early 19th century. It became one of the world's largest shipping companies, pioneering mail routes to Iberia, Egypt, India, and the Far East, and giving its name to successor brands including P&O Ferries and P&O Cruises.

History

The company began in 1837 with a mail contract to the Iberian Peninsula, and in 1840 merged services eastward to become the Peninsular and Oriental Steam Navigation Company. P&O ships carried mail, passengers, and cargo to India via Egypt (the "overland route") and later through the Suez Canal after 1869, and on to Singapore, Hong Kong, and Australia.

Later development

In the 20th century P&O diversified into container shipping (through Overseas Containers), ferries, ports, and property. In 2006 the group was acquired by Dubai-based DP World in a £3.9 billion deal. The P&O name survives in ferry operations in the UK and in cruise brands operated by Carnival Corporation.
