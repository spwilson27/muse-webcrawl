Lincoln County Process

The Lincoln County Process is a step used in producing Tennessee whiskey in which the distilled spirit is filtered through stacks of sugar maple charcoal before being aged in barrels. The process is named for Lincoln County, Tennessee, where the Jack Daniel's distillery was originally located (it is now in neighbouring Moore County).

Method

Newly distilled spirit, typically at around 140 proof, is slowly dripped or trickled through vats packed with roughly ten feet of sugar maple charcoal pellets, a process that can take days. The charcoal mellowing removes impurities and harsh flavours, giving the whiskey a smoother character. The charcoal is made by burning sugar maple wood that has been soaked in the whiskey.

Legal status

Since 2013, Tennessee law has required that whiskey labelled "Tennessee whiskey" be made with the Lincoln County Process, with an exception written in for the Benjamin Prichard's distillery. The requirement distinguishes Tennessee whiskey from bourbon, although the two are otherwise made to similar standards from a corn-based mash.
