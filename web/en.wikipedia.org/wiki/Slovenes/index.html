Slovenes

The Slovenes are a South Slavic ethnic group native to Slovenia and adjacent regions of Italy, Austria, Hungary and Croatia. They speak Slovene, a South Slavic language written in the Latin alphabet, and number roughly two and a half million worldwide, including a significant diaspora in the United States, Canada, Argentina and Australia.

Slavic settlers arrived in the eastern Alps in the 6th century. For most of their history Slovenes lived under Habsburg rule; a national movement developed in the 19th century, and after the First World War most Slovenes joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia). Slovenia became independent in 1991.

Culture

Slovene culture features a strong literary tradition beginning with Primož Trubar's first printed Slovene books in 1550, Alpine folk music, and distinctive regional costumes and cuisine.
