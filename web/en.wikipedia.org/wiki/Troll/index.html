Troll

A troll is a creature of Norse mythology and Scandinavian folklore, typically depicted as a large, dim-witted, and often hostile being living in remote mountains, caves, or forests, sometimes beneath bridges. The word derives from Old Norse troll, a term applied to various supernatural beings, giants, and malevolent creatures.

Folklore

In later Scandinavian folktales, trolls are huge, ugly, and strong, with a taste for human flesh, and are turned to stone if exposed to sunlight — a motif explained by local rock formations. They feature prominently in collections by Asbjørnsen and Moe and in Henrik Ibsen's Peer Gynt. Trolls range from the terrifying ogres of early tales to the more whimsical, nature-loving figures of later children's literature.

In modern culture

Tolkien's trolls in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings — large, brutish, and petrified by sunlight — fixed the image for modern fantasy, where trolls are staple monsters in literature, film, and role-playing games; in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series a mountain troll menaces the school in the first book. Norway's landscape retains many troll place names, such as Trolltunga ("the troll's tongue") and the Trollstigen road. In internet slang, a "troll" is a person who posts inflammatory content to provoke others — a usage drawing partly on the fishing term "trolling" and partly on the mythical creature.
