Central Tibetan Administration

The Central Tibetan Administration (CTA) is the Tibetan exile administration based in Dharamshala, Himachal Pradesh, India. It describes itself as the legitimate governing authority of Tibet and the Tibetan people, and it administers the welfare, education, health, and cultural affairs of the Tibetan refugee community in exile. Its headquarters are at Gangchen Kyishong in McLeod Ganj, Dharamshala.

History

The CTA was established after the 14th Dalai Lama fled Tibet in 1959 following the Lhasa uprising and the incorporation of Tibet into the People's Republic of China. The Dalai Lama re-established the Kashag (cabinet) in exile in 1960, first at Mussoorie and then at Dharamshala. In 1990–1991 the administration underwent democratization, with the Tibetan Parliament-in-Exile becoming directly elected. In 2011 the Dalai Lama devolved his political authority to the elected leadership; the head of government, formerly titled Kalon Tripa, became known as the Sikyong.

Structure

The administration comprises a cabinet (Kashag) headed by the Sikyong, a legislature (the Tibetan Parliament-in-Exile, with 45 members representing the traditional provinces of Tibet and religious schools), and a judiciary (the Tibetan Supreme Justice Commission). Departments cover finance, home affairs, education, health, religion and culture, security, and information and international relations. The CTA maintains Offices of Tibet in several countries that function as de facto representative offices.

Activities

The CTA runs schools for Tibetan refugee children together with organizations such as the Tibetan Children's Villages, administers settlements and health services across India and Nepal, and advocates the "Middle Way Approach" — genuine autonomy for Tibet within the framework of the People's Republic of China rather than full independence. It is not recognized as a government by any country, though it receives funding and political support from several states, including India and the United States.
