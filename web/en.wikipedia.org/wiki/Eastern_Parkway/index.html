Eastern Parkway is a major boulevard in the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, it is generally credited as the world's first parkway, conceived as a landscaped route connecting Prospect Park with the proposed — but never built — parkland to the east. It runs for roughly two miles from Grand Army Plaza eastward through the neighbourhoods of Crown Heights and Brownsville.

Design and history

Olmsted and Vaux proposed the parkway in their 1866 report on Prospect Park, envisioning a broad, tree-lined avenue with a central roadway, service roads, pedestrian malls and rows of shade trees. Construction took place between 1870 and 1874. The parkway's design, with a wide planted median and separate local and through roadways, influenced later boulevards such as Ocean Parkway in Brooklyn and parkway systems across the United States.

The boulevard passes major institutions including the Brooklyn Museum, the Brooklyn Public Library's central branch and the Brooklyn Botanic Garden near its western end. The building of the Eastern Parkway branch of the New York City Subway (the IRT Eastern Parkway Line) beneath it in the 1910s spurred dense apartment development.

Culture

Eastern Parkway is the route of the annual West Indian Day Parade, held each Labor Day, one of the largest carnival celebrations in the United States. The Crown Heights section is also the world headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch Hasidic movement at 770 Eastern Parkway, a building replicated by Chabad centres around the world.
