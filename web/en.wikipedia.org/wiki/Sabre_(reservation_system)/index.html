Sabre (reservation system)

Sabre (pronounced SAY-bər), legally known as Sabre Global Distribution System, is a travel reservation system used by travel agents and companies to search, price, book, and ticket travel services provided by airlines, hotels, car rental companies, rail providers, and tour operators. The network is operated by the Sabre Corporation.

Sabre is a global distribution system (GDS) — a computerised network that enables transactions between travel industry service providers, mainly airlines, hotels, car rental companies, and travel agencies. The system also stores data that is made available as part of the travel supply chain, such as schedules, fares, and availability.

History

The system was developed by American Airlines and IBM beginning in the 1950s; the name derived from "Semi-Automated Business Research Environment". The first Sabre system, running on two IBM 7090 mainframes, went online in 1960 and by 1964 handled the bookings of the entire airline. It was one of the first large-scale real-time transaction processing systems, and its design influenced IBM's subsequent commercial systems.

In 1976 Sabre terminals were installed in travel agencies, transforming the distribution of airline tickets. American's parent AMR spun off Sabre as an independent public company in 2000. The system was an important early example of electronic commerce and of the competitive issues raised when a dominant carrier owned the reservation system used by its rivals — concerns that led to government display regulations.
