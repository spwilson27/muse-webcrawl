Chester Arthur Burnett (June 10, 1910 – January 10, 1976), better known by his stage name Howlin' Wolf, was an American blues singer and guitarist. With a booming voice and imposing physical presence, he is one of the best-known Chicago blues artists, ranked among the greatest bluesmen and among the greatest artists of all time by Rolling Stone.

Career

Burnett learned guitar from Charley Patton and harmonica from Sonny Boy Williamson II in the Mississippi Delta. After recording in Memphis for Sam Phillips in 1951, he moved to Chicago and signed with Chess Records, where producer and songwriter Willie Dixon supplied many of his best-known songs, including "Spoonful", "Back Door Man", "Little Red Rooster", "I Ain't Superstitious", and "Killing Floor". His guitarist Hubert Sumlin's jagged playing defined the band's sound.

Legacy

Howlin' Wolf's records profoundly influenced British and American rock musicians, including the Rolling Stones, Led Zeppelin, Eric Clapton, and the Doors. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1991.
