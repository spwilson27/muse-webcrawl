Heineken Cup

Sport: Rugby union
Founded: 1995
Ceased: 2014
Region: Europe
Most titles: Leinster and Toulouse (4 each at the time of rebranding)

The Heineken Cup was an annual rugby union tournament involving leading club, regional and provincial teams from England, France, Ireland, Scotland, Wales and Italy. It was one of the most prestigious trophies in club rugby, and was the top-tier competition of European club rugby from its inauguration in 1995 until its replacement by the European Rugby Champions Cup in 2014.

The tournament was sponsored by the Dutch brewer Heineken, which gave the competition its name; in France, for legal reasons relating to alcohol advertising, it was known simply as the H Cup.

History

The competition was launched in the summer of 1995, shortly after rugby union turned professional, with twelve teams from five countries. Toulouse won the first final, beating Cardiff after extra time in January 1996. The tournament expanded to twenty and then twenty-four teams, structured in a pool stage followed by knockout rounds culminating in a final at a major stadium.

Irish province Leinster won three titles in four years between 2009 and 2012, while Munster's victories in 2006 and 2008 were celebrated for their enormous travelling support. Following disputes between the English and French clubs and the tournament organisers over governance and revenue, the competition was replaced in 2014 by the European Rugby Champions Cup, organised by European Professional Club Rugby; Heineken later returned as title sponsor of the successor competition from 2018.

Format

In its mature form, 24 teams were drawn into six pools of four, playing home-and-away fixtures. The six pool winners and two best runners-up advanced to the quarter-finals, with semi-finals and a final at a predetermined venue.
