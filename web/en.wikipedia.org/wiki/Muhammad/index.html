Muhammad (c. 570 – 8 June 632 CE) was an Arab religious and political leader and the founder of Islam. According to Islam, he was a prophet who was divinely inspired to preach and confirm the monotheistic teachings of Adam, Abraham, Moses, Jesus and other prophets; he is believed to be the Seal of the Prophets (final prophet) in Islam. Muslims believe the Quran, the central text of Islam, was revealed to him by God through the angel Jibreel (Gabriel) over the last 22 years of his life.

Born in Mecca into the Quraysh tribe, orphaned young, Muhammad worked as a merchant and married Khadija, a wealthy businesswoman 15 years his senior. Around 610 CE, meditating in a cave on Mount Hira, he received (Muslims believe) the first revelation. Opposition in Mecca led to the Hijra — his emigration to Medina in 622, the start of the Islamic calendar — where he united the city under the Constitution of Medina, and after several conflicts returned to Mecca in 630, largely peacefully. By his death in 632 most of Arabia had embraced Islam; his Farewell Sermon at Arafat summarised his message. Non-Muslim depictions of Muhammad are restricted in most Islamic traditions, a sensitivity rooted in the prohibition of idolatry.
