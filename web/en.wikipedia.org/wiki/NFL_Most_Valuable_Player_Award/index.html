SportAmerican football
LeagueNational Football League (NFL)

The NFL Most Valuable Player Award is an annual honour given to the player judged most valuable in the National Football League (NFL) for a given season. Several organisations have presented MVP awards, but the Associated Press NFL MVP award, voted on by a panel of sportswriters at the end of the regular season and announced at the NFL Honors ceremony, is considered the most authoritative.

History and voting

The Associated Press has awarded its MVP since 1957. Quarterbacks dominate the award, reflecting the position's importance; running backs occasionally win, while defensive players have won only twice (Alan Page in 1971 and Lawrence Taylor in 1986), and no wide receiver has won the AP award. Peyton Manning holds the record with five AP MVP awards, followed by Aaron Rodgers with four. The Pro Football Writers of America and the Newspaper Enterprise Association also selected MVPs historically, and the Joe F. Carr Trophy was the league's official award from 1938 to 1946.

Selection process

Fifty AP voters rank their choices; since 2022 the ballot uses a weighted point system for the top five selections. The winner is announced the night before the Super Bowl.
