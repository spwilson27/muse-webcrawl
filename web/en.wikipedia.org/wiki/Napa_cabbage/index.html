Napa cabbage

Napa cabbage (Brassica rapa subsp. pekinensis), also called Chinese cabbage, celery cabbage or wombok, is a type of leafy vegetable originating in China near the Beijing region, widely used in East Asian cuisines. The name "napa" derives from the Japanese word nappa (菜っ葉), meaning greens or leaves.

Description and cultivation

The plant forms an oblong head of tightly packed, crinkly leaves that are pale green to nearly white, with broad white midribs. It is a cool-season annual, usually sown in late summer for autumn harvest, and is susceptible to bolting in heat. China is the largest producer, and the vegetable is grown commercially on every continent.

Culinary use

Napa cabbage is the principal ingredient of Korean baechu-kimchi, the most common form of kimchi. In China it is stir-fried, stewed (as in the classic pork and glass-noodle braise zhurou dun fencai) and used in dumplings and hot pot; in Japan it appears as hakusai in nabemono and pickles. Its mild, sweet flavour and tender-crisp texture suit it equally to salads, slaws and wraps. It is a good source of vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and calcium.
