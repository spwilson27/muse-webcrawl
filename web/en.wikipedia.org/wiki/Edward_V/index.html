Edward V

Edward V (2 November 1470 – c. mid-1483) was King of England from 9 April to 25 June 1483. He succeeded his father Edward IV but was never crowned, and was deposed by his uncle Richard III. Along with his brother Richard of Shrewsbury, he is known as one of the Princes in the Tower.

Edward V
Reign: 9 April – 25 June 1483
Born: Westminster, 1470
Died: c. 1483, Tower of London (presumed)
House: York

Reign

Edward was Prince of Wales and governed from Ludlow under his uncle Anthony Woodville. After Edward IV's death, the protectorship went to Richard, Duke of Gloucester, who intercepted the king, arrested Woodville, and lodged Edward in the Tower — then normal pre-coronation residence.

Deposition and fate

Parliament declared Edward illegitimate via Titulus Regius, claiming Edward IV's marriage was invalid; Richard became king. The princes disappeared; their fate remains one of English history's most debated mysteries. Bones found in the Tower in 1674 were reburied in Westminster Abbey as theirs.
