Animal welfare in the United Kingdom

Animal welfare in the United Kingdom is governed by some of the oldest animal protection legislation in the world. The UK was the first country to enact animal welfare law—Martin's Act of 1822, against cruelty to cattle—and the first to found an animal welfare charity, the Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (1824), which became the RSPCA. Modern protection centres on the Animal Welfare Act 2006 in England and Wales, with parallel Acts in Scotland (2006) and Northern Ireland (2011).

Legislation

The 2006 Act consolidated earlier law and introduced a "duty of care": it became an offence to fail to meet an animal's needs, not merely to cause suffering. Keepers must provide a suitable environment, diet, housing, and protection from pain, suffering, injury, and disease. Later measures include the Animal Welfare (Sentencing) Act 2021, which raised maximum penalties for the worst cruelty offences to five years' imprisonment, and the Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022, which formally recognises vertebrates—and, following review, cephalopods and decapod crustaceans—as sentient in policy-making.

Key issues

Major areas of regulation and campaigning include farm animal welfare (with phase-outs of battery cages and sow stalls), live animal exports, the use of animals in research under the Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986, hunting with dogs (banned by the Hunting Act 2004 in England and Wales), compulsory microchipping of dogs, and bans on wild animals in circuses and on ivory sales. Following Brexit, the UK diverged from some EU rules while introducing its own trade and welfare standards.

Organisations

Enforcement involves local authorities, the police, and prosecution by charities such as the RSPCA and SSPCA. Policy advice comes from bodies including the Animal Welfare Committee, while assurance schemes such as RSPCA Assured set standards above the legal minimum.
