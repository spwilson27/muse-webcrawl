Communist Party of Czechoslovakia

The Communist Party of Czechoslovakia (Czech and Slovak: Komunistická strana Československa, KSČ) was a communist and Marxist–Leninist political party in Czechoslovakia that existed between 1921 and 1992. It governed the country from 1948 to 1989 as the ruling party of a one-party state, holding a constitutionally guaranteed "leading role" in society.

Founding and interwar period

The KSČ was founded in May 1921 from the left wing of the Czechoslovak Social Democratic Workers' Party. Unlike communists in many countries, it became a mass party and one of the strongest in the Communist International, polling over 13 percent in the 1925 elections and retaining significant support through the interwar republic. Led from 1929 by Klement Gottwald, the party was banned in 1938 after the Munich Agreement and operated in exile and underground during the Second World War.

Takeover and rule

After 1945 the KSČ emerged as the largest party, winning about 38 percent in the 1946 elections, and Gottwald became prime minister of a coalition government. In February 1948, in the crisis known as the Czechoslovak coup d'état, the communists seized full power with Soviet backing, establishing a socialist state aligned with the USSR. The regime carried out nationalisation, collectivisation, and political purges — including the Slánský show trial of 1952 — while Gottwald's successors Antonín Novotný and others maintained orthodox Stalinist policies.

Prague Spring and normalisation

In 1968 reformist leader Alexander Dubček attempted "socialism with a human face" during the Prague Spring; the experiment was crushed by the Warsaw Pact invasion of August 1968. Dubček was replaced by Gustáv Husák, whose era of "normalisation" reversed reforms and restored hardline control for two decades.

Fall and dissolution

The Velvet Revolution of November–December 1989 ended communist rule peacefully. The party lost power, reformed itself as a federal party of two republican organisations, and, with the dissolution of Czechoslovakia at the end of 1992, ceased to exist; its successor in the Czech lands is the Communist Party of Bohemia and Moravia (KSČM), and in Slovakia the Communist Party of Slovakia (KSS).
