Cominform

The Cominform — the Communist Information Bureau, formally the Information Bureau of the Communist and Workers' Parties — was an organisation of European communist parties founded in September 1947 under Soviet leadership, often regarded as the successor to the dissolved Comintern.

History

Convened at Szklarska Poręba, Poland, the bureau united the parties of the USSR, Bulgaria, Romania, Hungary, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, France and Italy. Andrei Zhdanov's 'two camps' doctrine proclaimed the Cold War division of the world; the Cominform coordinated campaigns against the Marshall Plan. In June 1948 it expelled Tito's Yugoslavia for defying Stalin, moving headquarters from Belgrade to Bucharest and unleashing purges of suspected 'Titoists' across Eastern Europe.

Dissolution

Rendered obsolete by de-Stalinisation and Khrushchev's reconciliation with Yugoslavia, the Cominform was dissolved in April 1956; its newspaper, For a Lasting Peace, for a People's Democracy!, ceased with it.
