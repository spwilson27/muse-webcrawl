Ludwig Otto Hesse (22 April 1811 – 4 August 1874) was a German mathematician. He is known for the Hessian matrix, the Hesse normal form of a plane, the Hesse configuration, and the Hessian group.

Life and work

Hesse was born in Königsberg, Prussia, and studied at the University of Königsberg under Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi, whose influence shaped his research on algebraic forms and invariants. He taught at Königsberg for many years while also instructing at the local trade school, and later held professorships at Heidelberg (from 1856) and at the Polytechnic School in Munich (from 1868), where he died.

His name attaches to several fundamental concepts: the Hessian determinant and matrix of second partial derivatives, used to classify critical points and in the theory of algebraic curves; the Hesse normal form for the equation of a line or plane in terms of its perpendicular distance from the origin; and the Hesse configuration of points and lines in projective geometry. His treatise on analytic geometry and his posthumously published lectures were widely used.
