Falkland, Fife

Falkland Palace in the village centre

Falkland is a village, parish and former royal burgh in Fife, Scotland, at the foot of the Lomond Hills. It is best known for Falkland Palace, a Renaissance palace of the Stewart kings. The village has a population of roughly 1,200.

History

Falkland Castle was a hunting seat of the earls of Fife and later of the Scottish Crown. James IV and James V rebuilt it as a Renaissance palace in the French style in the early 16th century; the palace's real tennis court, built in 1539, is the oldest surviving in Britain and still in use. James V died at Falkland in December 1542. Mary, Queen of Scots frequently visited, and the palace remained a royal residence into the 17th century before passing eventually to the Tyndall Bruce family, whose 19th-century restoration saved the buildings.

The village became a royal burgh in 1458. In the 18th and 19th centuries it was a centre of handloom linen weaving. Its well-preserved historic streetscape of stone houses makes it a popular filming location; it stood in for 1940s Inverness in the television series Outlander.

Geography

Falkland lies in the Howe of Fife, overlooked by East Lomond (also called Falkland Hill, 448 m) and West Lomond (522 m), the highest points of Fife. The Maspie Burn runs through the village.
