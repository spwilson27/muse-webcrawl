Stagg Bowl

Stagg Bowl Sport: College football
Governing body: NCAA Division III
First played: 1973
Named for: Amos Alonzo Stagg
Long-time host: Salem, Virginia (1993–2017 era)
Trophy: NCAA Division III national championship

The Stagg Bowl is the name given to the NCAA Division III football championship game, the final of the Division III playoffs in American college football. It is named after Amos Alonzo Stagg, the pioneering coach associated with the University of Chicago and the University of the Pacific.

The Stagg Bowl decides the NCAA Division III national champion

History

Division III established its football championship in 1973, and the title game soon took the Stagg Bowl name. The game was hosted for many years in Salem, Virginia, which branded itself "Virginia's Championship City" after hosting numerous NCAA finals. More recent editions have rotated among venues including Shenandoah, Texas; Canton, Ohio; Annapolis, Maryland; and Houston, Texas.

Notable dynasties

Mount Union (Ohio) and Wisconsin–Whitewater dominated the game in the 2000s and 2010s, meeting in the Stagg Bowl nine consecutive times (2005–2013) in one of college football's great rivalries. Other frequent champions include Ithaca, Augustana (Illinois) and North Central (Illinois).
