Pierre François Verhulst, originator of the logistic growth model.

Pierre François Verhulst (28 October 1804 – 15 February 1849) was a Belgian mathematician and demographer. In 1838 he published the logistic function, the first mathematical model of population growth to include a carrying capacity, correcting the unbounded exponential growth of Malthus.

Early life and career

Verhulst studied mathematics at the University of Ghent, obtaining his doctorate in 1825. He worked in Brussels under the influence of the statistician Adolphe Quetelet, whose faith in quantitative social science shaped Verhulst's approach to demography. After a period of political exile following a pamphlet on Italian unification, he became professor of mathematics at the École Militaire in Brussels and a member of the Royal Academy of Belgium.

The logistic equation

In his Notice sur la loi que la population suit dans son accroissement (1838) and a fuller memoir of 1845, Verhulst proposed that population growth is proportional both to the population itself and to the room remaining before an upper limit is reached:

dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K)

where N is population, r the growth rate and K the saturation level. The solution is the S-shaped logistic curve. He named the curve "logistique" and tested it against census data for Belgium, France and other countries.

Reception

Verhulst's work was largely forgotten until the 1920s, when Raymond Pearl and Lowell Reed rediscovered and promoted the logistic curve in the United States. The model became fundamental in ecology, epidemiology (the logistic map and SIR models), chemistry, and, in discrete form, the logistic map studied in chaos theory. Verhulst also made contributions to the theory of elliptic functions and number theory.
