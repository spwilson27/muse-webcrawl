BlackBerry is a discontinued brand of mobile phones and services, originally designed and marketed by the Canadian company Research In Motion (RIM, later renamed BlackBerry Limited) and later licensed. The BlackBerry line was known for its physical QWERTY keyboards, secure "push" email, and the BlackBerry Messenger (BBM) service, becoming a status symbol for business users in the 2000s — the devices were nicknamed "CrackBerries" for their addictiveness.

History

The first BlackBerry device, an email pager (the BlackBerry 850), was introduced in 1999 in Munich. Converged smartphones followed from 2002, adding phone calls, text messaging, web browsing and the thumb-operated keyboards that defined the brand. At its peak in the late 2000s and early 2010s, BlackBerry accounted for roughly a fifth of global smartphone sales, with over 80 million active subscribers at its height.

Decline

The launch of the Apple iPhone (2007) and the rise of Android touchscreen phones eroded BlackBerry's market share. BlackBerry 10, launched in 2013 with the Z10 and Q10, failed to reverse the decline; the company exited hardware design in 2016, licensing the brand to TCL and other manufacturers for Android-based devices such as the KeyOne and Key2. Legacy BlackBerry services for BlackBerry OS and BlackBerry 10 were shut down on January 4, 2022, ending support for the classic devices, while BlackBerry Limited continues as a software and cybersecurity company.
