Jack Mathieu Émile Lang (born 2 September 1939) is a French politician of the Socialist Party, best known as Minister of Culture under President François Mitterrand, where he dramatically expanded state support for the arts and created popular festivals.

Early life

Born in Mirecourt in the Vosges, Lang studied law and theatre, founded the Nancy World Theatre Festival, and taught law before entering politics as a close adviser to Mitterrand.

Minister of Culture

From 1981 Lang doubled the culture budget, championed the "grands travaux" (Grand Louvre pyramid, Opéra Bastille, Bibliothèque nationale), created the Fête de la Musique (21 June, now celebrated worldwide), the Fête du Cinéma, and France's fixed book-price law (the "loi Lang", 1981). He promoted the doctrine of cultural exception in trade negotiations and defended French-language culture.

Later career

Lang served as Minister of Education, mayor of Blois (1989–2000) and deputy for Pas-de-Calais. In 2013 he became president of the Institut du Monde Arabe in Paris, a post he held until resigning in February 2026 amid scrutiny following release of files relating to Jeffrey Epstein, whose contacts with Lang were reported in the press; Lang denied wrongdoing in connection with Epstein's crimes.
