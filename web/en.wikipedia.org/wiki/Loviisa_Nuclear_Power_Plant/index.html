The Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant (Finnish: Loviisan ydinvoimalaitos) is a nuclear power plant located on Hästholmen island near the town of Loviisa on Finland's southern coast. It is operated by Fortum and has two pressurised-water reactors of the Soviet-designed VVER-440 type, modified with Western safety and control systems.

History

Unit 1 entered service in 1977 and Unit 2 in 1980. They were unusual Cold War hybrid projects: Soviet reactors and turbines combined with German containment structures and American instrumentation, earning Loviisa the nickname "Eastinghouse" (a play on Westinghouse). The plant has consistently achieved high capacity factors.

Operation and life extension

Together the two units generate roughly 10% of Finland's electricity. Their operating licences were repeatedly extended with safety upgrades, and in 2023 the Finnish government granted an extension allowing operation until the end of 2050. A low- and intermediate-level waste repository operates on the site.

A proposed third unit (Loviisa 3) was rejected by the Finnish parliament in 2010.
