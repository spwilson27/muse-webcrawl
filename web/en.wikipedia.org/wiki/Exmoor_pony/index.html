Exmoor pony

The Exmoor pony is a British breed of pony originating on Exmoor in Devon and Somerset. It is one of Britain's native mountain and moorland breeds, semi-feral herds still graze the moor, and it is listed as endangered by the Rare Breeds Survival Trust.

Exmoor pony
Country: England
Height: approx. 11.1–12.3 hands
Colour: Bay, brown, dun with mealy markings
Status: Endangered

Characteristics

Stocky and hardy, with a thick winter coat, "toad eye", hooded eyes, and pale mealy markings around eyes, muzzle and belly — pangaré coloration thought to be ancient. Mares stand about 12 hands.

History

Ponies have lived on Exmoor since ancient times; some claim direct descent from wild prehistoric horses, though genetic studies show shared domestic ancestry. Numbers collapsed after the Second World War, when many were taken for meat, leaving around 50; conservation herds have since rebuilt the breed.

Uses

Used for riding, driving, showing and conservation grazing.
