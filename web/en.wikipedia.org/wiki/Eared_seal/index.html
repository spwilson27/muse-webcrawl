Eared seal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The California sea lion, an otariid, showing the external ear flap (pinna)

An eared seal, or otariid, is any member of the marine mammal family Otariidae, one of three groups of pinnipeds together with the true seals (Phocidae) and the walrus (Odobenidae). The family comprises 15 extant species: the fur seals and the sea lions. They are distinguished from true seals by the presence of external ear flaps (pinnae), larger fore flippers, and the ability to rotate their hind flippers forward to move comparatively well on land.

Anatomy and behaviour

Otariids propel themselves mainly with their long fore flippers, steering with the hind flippers, whereas phocids swim chiefly with the hind flippers. Sea lions have short, coarse hair, while fur seals possess a dense underfur formerly prized by the fur trade. Otariids are generally highly social and polygynous: males establish territories or harems on breeding beaches (rookeries), and females return to natal sites to pup and mate. They feed on fish, squid, crustaceans and occasionally penguins or other seals, and are preyed upon by sharks and killer whales.

Distribution

Eared seals are concentrated in the subpolar, temperate and equatorial waters of the Pacific and Southern Oceans, with several species in the Atlantic; no otariids breed in the North Atlantic. Species include the Steller sea lion, California sea lion, Galápagos sea lion, northern fur seal, Antarctic fur seal and South American fur seal. Several populations were driven close to extinction by commercial sealing in the 18th and 19th centuries and have since partially recovered under protection.
