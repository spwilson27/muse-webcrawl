Chromatic homotopy theory

Chromatic homotopy theory is a branch of algebraic topology, founded in work of Dennis Ravenel and others in the 1970s–80s, that studies stable homotopy theory by filtering spectra according to "chromatic" layers associated with formal group laws of increasing height.

Overview

The central insight is that the stable homotopy category can be assembled from simpler pieces indexed by a prime p and a natural number n (the "height"). Each layer is governed by a Morava K-theory K(n) and its associated Morava E-theory, and a spectrum can be recovered from its localizations at these layers, much as white light splits into colours — hence "chromatic." The chromatic spectral sequence organizes computations of the stable homotopy groups of spheres by height.

Key results

Major landmarks include the nilpotence and periodicity theorems of Devinatz, Hopkins, and Smith; the Ravenel conjectures, largely resolved in the 1980s; and the resolution of the Kervaire invariant problem by Hill, Hopkins, and Ravenel (2009, except in dimension 126). The telescope conjecture, long a guiding open problem, was disproved in 2023 by Burklund, Hahn, Levy, and Schlank for heights n ≥ 2.
