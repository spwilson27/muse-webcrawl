International Plant Protection Convention

The International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC) is a multilateral treaty overseen by the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) that aims to secure coordinated, effective action to prevent and control the introduction and spread of pests of plants and plant products.

History and membership

The IPPC was adopted in 1951 and entered into force in 1952; a substantially revised text was adopted in 1997 and took effect in 2005 to align the convention with the WTO Agreement on Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures. More than 180 countries are contracting parties.

Standards

The convention's governing body, the Commission on Phytosanitary Measures, adopts International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures (ISPMs). The best known, ISPM 15, requires wood packaging material in international trade to be heat-treated or fumigated and stamped, to prevent the spread of forest pests. The IPPC also coordinates the ePhyto system for electronic phytosanitary certificates.

Implementation

Each party maintains a National Plant Protection Organization responsible for pest surveillance, import regulation, and export certification. 2020 was proclaimed the International Year of Plant Health to raise awareness of the convention's goals.
