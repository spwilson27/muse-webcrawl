The Battle of Cromdale took place on 30 April and 1 May 1690 near Cromdale in Speyside, Scotland. Government troops under Sir Thomas Livingstone surprised and routed a Jacobite force, effectively ending the first Jacobite rising in the Highlands.

Background

After the death of Viscount Dundee at Killiecrankie in 1689, the Jacobite cause in Scotland was carried on by Colonel Alexander Cannon and later General Thomas Buchan. In spring 1690 Buchan gathered a force of some 1,200 men on Speyside.

The battle

Livingstone marched from Inverness through the night and fell on the Jacobite camp at dawn. Many Jacobites were still asleep or drunk; hundreds were killed or captured, and the rest scattered. Government losses were light.

Legacy

Cromdale ended organised Jacobite resistance in the rising of 1689–90. The battle is remembered in the folk song "The Haughs of Cromdale", which inaccurately makes it a Jacobite victory.
