Five-Power Treaty

SignedFebruary 6, 1922; Washington, D.C.
EffectiveAugust 17, 1923
SignatoriesUnited States, British Empire, Japan, France, Italy
SubjectNaval disarmament

The Five-Power Treaty, commonly known as the Washington Naval Treaty, was a treaty signed in 1922 among the victors of the First World War that limited naval armaments and averted a battleship-building race. It was concluded at the Washington Naval Conference convened by U.S. President Warren G. Harding.

Terms

The treaty fixed capital-ship tonnage ratios for the five powers at 5:5:3:1.75:1.75 for the United States, the British Empire, Japan, France and Italy respectively. It capped individual battleships at 35,000 tons with guns no larger than 16 inches, imposed a ten-year "holiday" on new capital-ship construction, required the scrapping of numerous existing and planned warships, and limited aircraft carrier tonnage. The signatories also agreed not to fortify certain Pacific island possessions.

Aftermath

The treaty system was extended by the London Naval Treaty of 1930, but Japan gave notice of withdrawal in 1934 and the limitations lapsed at the end of 1936, after which the naval arms race resumed in the run-up to the Second World War.
