Cauberg

The Cauberg is a hill in Valkenburg aan de Geul in the municipality of Valkenburg aan de Geul in the province of Limburg, in the southern Netherlands. It is one of the most famous climbs in Dutch professional road cycling and a recurring feature of spring classic races and world championship courses.

Cycling

The Cauberg rises from the centre of Valkenburg to the top of the plateau above the town, gaining roughly 69 metres of elevation over a distance of about 1,200 metres, with an average gradient of around 5.8 percent and a maximum gradient of about 12 percent. Its position in the closing kilometres of a race has made it a decisive launch pad for attacks.

From 2003 until 2016 the climb formed the finish of the Amstel Gold Race, with the finish line moved to just beyond the summit in 2013. The Cauberg has also featured in the Tour de France and in the UCI Road World Championships, which were held in Valkenburg in 1938, 1948, 1979, 1998 and 2012, with the Cauberg as the central climb of the circuit.

The climb

The ascent begins beside the Geul river in the old town and passes the municipal caves and the Holland Casino before curving up through a series of bends lined with cafés and grandstands during major races. At the summit lies the village plateau and the road continues towards Berg en Terblijt.
