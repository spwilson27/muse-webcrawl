Pavane (Fauré)

The Pavane in F-sharp minor, Op. 50, is a short work by the French composer Gabriel Fauré written in 1887. Originally a piano piece, it is best known in Fauré's orchestral version and is one of his most popular compositions.

ComposerGabriel Fauré
OpusOp. 50
Composed1887
KeyF-sharp minor

Music

Named after the slow processional Spanish court dance, the Pavane unfolds a graceful, melancholy flute melody over a gently rocking accompaniment. Fauré later added an optional chorus singing a text by Robert de Montesquiou, and the piece was also choreographed within his incidental music Masques et bergamasques.

Reception

Though Fauré himself dismissed it as elegant but insignificant, the Pavane has remained a concert favourite and has been widely arranged and recorded.
