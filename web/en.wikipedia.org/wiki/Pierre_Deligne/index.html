Pierre René, Viscount Deligne (born 3 October 1944) is a Belgian mathematician. He is best known for his work on the Weil conjectures, leading to a complete proof in 1973. He is the winner of the 2013 Abel Prize, the 2008 Wolf Prize, the 1978 Fields Medal, and the 1988 Crafoord Prize.

Early life and education

Deligne was born in Etterbeek, near Brussels, and attended school in Brussels. He studied at the Université libre de Bruxelles, where his interest in mathematics was encouraged by Jacques Tits. After graduating, he went to Paris to work with Alexander Grothendieck at the Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques (IHÉS). He received his doctorate in 1968.

Career

Deligne worked with Grothendieck on the theory of schemes and étale cohomology. In 1970 he joined the permanent staff of IHÉS, and in 1984 he moved to the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey, where he later became professor emeritus.

Work

Deligne's most celebrated result is his proof of the last and deepest of the Weil conjectures, now often called Deligne's theorem. He also worked on Hodge theory, developing the theory of mixed Hodge structures with Phillip Griffiths, John Morgan, and Dennis Sullivan. Other concepts bearing his name include Deligne cohomology and Deligne–Mumford stacks.
