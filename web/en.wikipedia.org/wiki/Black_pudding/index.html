Black pudding is a distinct regional type of blood sausage originating in the United Kingdom and Ireland. It is made from pork or beef blood, with pork fat or beef suet, and a cereal, usually oatmeal, oat groats, or barley groats. The high proportion of cereal, along with the use of certain herbs such as pennyroyal, distinguishes it from blood sausages eaten elsewhere in the world.

History

Blood sausages are among the oldest forms of sausage; a blood pudding is mentioned in Homer's Odyssey. Black pudding became closely associated with certain towns, notably Bury in Lancashire and Stornoway in the Outer Hebrides. Stornoway black pudding was granted Protected Geographical Indication status by the European Union in 2013. In 2016 black pudding enjoyed a fashion as a supposed "superfood," widely reported in the press.

Preparation and serving

The mixture is stuffed into a casing and boiled or simmered; fresh puddings are typically sliced and fried or grilled. Black pudding is a classic component of the full breakfast in much of Britain and Ireland, though it is also served in salads, starters, and even ice cream in modern restaurants. Lancashire tradition holds that rival black puddings were hurled between Lancashire and Yorkshire partisans, a legend commemorated in the annual World Black Pudding Throwing Championships at Ramsbottom.

Regional variations

Irish black pudding, such as that from Clonakilty, often includes onion and distinctive spicing. Scottish versions may use oatmeal and spices including coriander. Related products include white pudding, which omits the blood, and red pudding, found in parts of Scotland.
