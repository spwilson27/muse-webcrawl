Berkson's paradox

Berkson's paradox, also known as Berkson's bias or Berkson's fallacy, is a result in conditional probability and statistics in which two independent variables appear negatively correlated when samples are selected conditionally on a collider.

Explanation

If hospital patients are sampled, diseases that independently increase admission can appear negatively associated in the sample, because admission is a common effect of both. Joseph Berkson described the effect in 1946.

Applications

The paradox matters in epidemiology, observational studies and machine learning, where selection bias can create spurious correlations.
