Single Form

Single Form is a monumental bronze sculpture by the British artist Barbara Hepworth (1903–1975). Completed in 1964, it stands outside the headquarters of the United Nations in New York City as a memorial to Dag Hammarskjöld, the second Secretary-General of the United Nations, who was a friend of the artist and died in a plane crash in 1961.

Description

The sculpture rises about 6.4 metres (21 feet) on a granite base and takes the form of a tall, pierced vertical monolith with a circular opening near its top. Hepworth described the work as arising from her long-standing exploration of the upright single form in her sculpture, from carvings of standing figures to the memorial to Hammarskjöld. A smaller version, Single Form (September), commemorates the month of his death.

History

After Hammarskjöld's death, Hepworth offered to create a sculpture in his memory for the United Nations. The work was cast in bronze at the Morris Singer foundry in London and unveiled on 18 June 1964. It stands in the plaza in front of the Secretariat Building.

Related works

Hepworth returned to the theme in Single Form (Chûn Quoit) and other late bronzes. A smaller cast of the UN sculpture's form stands in Battersea Park, London.
