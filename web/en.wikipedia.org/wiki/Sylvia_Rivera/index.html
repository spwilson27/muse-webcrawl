Sylvia Rivera (1951–2002) was an American gay liberation and transgender rights activist of Puerto Rican and Venezuelan descent. A veteran of the 1969 Stonewall uprising in New York, she spent her life advocating for those she saw as left behind by the mainstream gay rights movement — drag queens, transgender people, homeless queer youth, sex workers and people of colour.

STAR

In 1970 Rivera co-founded, with her close friend Marsha P. Johnson, the Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries (STAR), which provided housing and support to homeless queer and trans youth and sex workers in New York, funded largely by the founders' own street work. STAR House, a rented tenement on East 2nd Street, is considered an early landmark of mutual aid in the queer community.

Later life and legacy

Rivera's famous 1973 speech at the Christopher Street Liberation Day rally — in which she seized the microphone to denounce the movement's exclusion of trans people and prisoners — became legendary. Her activism revived in the 1990s as she fought for transgender inclusion in New York's gay rights bills. The Sylvia Rivera Law Project, founded in 2002, carries her name, and she is honoured in murals, pride histories and the Stonewall National Monument visitor narratives.
