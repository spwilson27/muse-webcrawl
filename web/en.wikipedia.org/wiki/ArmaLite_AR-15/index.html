The ArmaLite AR-15 is a lightweight, magazine-fed, gas-operated rifle designed by Eugene Stoner at the ArmaLite company in the 1950s. It is the progenitor of the M16 rifle and M4 carbine used by the United States military, and of a wide family of civilian "AR-15 style" rifles.

History

ArmaLite, a division of Fairchild Engine and Airplane Corporation, developed the AR-15 in 1956–1957 as a small-caliber, high-velocity infantry rifle scaled down from Stoner's AR-10. In 1959, financial difficulties led ArmaLite to sell the design to Colt's Manufacturing Company. Colt marketed the rifle to military customers; after modifications, it was adopted by the United States Air Force and Army as the M16 during the Vietnam War.

Design

The AR-15 uses a direct impingement gas system in which propellant gas is routed back to act directly on the bolt carrier. Extensive use of aluminium alloys and synthetic materials made the rifle notably light for its era. It fires the intermediate .223 Remington cartridge (later standardised as 5.56×45mm NATO) from detachable box magazines.

Trademark and modern usage

"AR-15" remains a trademark of Colt, but the term is widely used generically for rifles patterned on the design made by many manufacturers; AR-15 style rifles are among the most popular firearms in the United States civilian market. The "AR" in AR-15 stands for "ArmaLite Rifle", not "assault rifle" or "automatic rifle".
