Rhinoplasty

TypePlastic surgery
SpecialtyPlastic surgery; otolaryngology
PurposesCosmetic reshaping; functional reconstruction

Rhinoplasty (commonly called a nose job) is a plastic surgery procedure for altering and reconstructing the nose. It may be performed for cosmetic reasons or to restore form and function after trauma, disease, or congenital defects.

History

Nasal reconstruction is described in the ancient Indian text Sushruta Samhita, and forehead-flap techniques spread to Europe in the Renaissance. Modern rhinoplasty developed with Jacques Joseph in Berlin in the late 19th century and was refined through the 20th century.

Techniques

Surgeons use open (external) or closed (endonasal) approaches to modify nasal bone and cartilage — reducing a dorsal hump, refining the tip, narrowing the bridge — while preserving or improving breathing. Revision rates are notable, and healing and final results may take a year to settle.

Risks

Complications include bleeding, infection, septal perforation, breathing difficulty, and dissatisfaction with appearance. Rhinoplasty remains among the most commonly performed cosmetic surgeries worldwide.
