Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Incorporated (ΑΚΑ) is an international service organisation and the first intercollegiate historically African American sorority. It was founded on 15 January 1908 at Howard University in Washington, D.C., by a group of sixteen students led by Ethel Hedgeman Lyle. It is a member of the National Pan-Hellenic Council, the umbrella body of the nine historically Black Greek-letter organisations known as the "Divine Nine."

History

The sorority emerged a generation after the first Black fraternity, Alpha Phi Alpha (1906), when Black women at Howard sought a sisterhood devoted to scholarship, leadership, and service. Hedgeman (later Lyle) conceived the idea and, with eight fellow sophomores and seven juniors invited in 1909, formed the founding group remembered as the "Twenty Pearls." Alpha Kappa Alpha was incorporated in 1913 — the first Black Greek-letter organisation to take that legal step, protecting its name and founding governance.

Chapters spread nationally and internationally; the sorority supported anti-lynching campaigns, Mississippi health projects in the 1930s, voter registration, and the 1938 founding of the American Council on Human Rights. Its Educational Advancement Foundation (1980) funds scholarships and community programmes.

Membership and programmes

Membership, organised in undergraduate and graduate chapters across the United States and several other countries, is by invitation and has included several hundred thousand women. The sorority's programmes run under successive international administrations' initiatives, typically covering empowerment of families, economic well-being, environmental sustainability, social justice advocacy, and strengthening local communities, alongside longstanding support for historically Black colleges and universities.

Notable members

Members have included civil-rights leader Coretta Scott King, author Maya Angelou, astronaut Mae Jemison, and Kamala Harris, the first woman and first Black and South Asian American Vice President of the United States, who joined the Alpha chapter at Howard.
