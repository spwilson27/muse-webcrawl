Andronikos Doukas (Greek: Ἀνδρόνικος Δούκας; died 14 October 1077) was a Byzantine general and a member of the powerful Doukas family. He is chiefly remembered for his role at the Battle of Manzikert in 1071, where he commanded the rearguard of Emperor Romanos IV Diogenes' army. His withdrawal — or, as hostile sources claim, deliberate betrayal — during the battle contributed to the Roman defeat and the Emperor's capture by the Seljuk Turks.

Career

Andronikos was the son of the Caesar John Doukas, brother of Emperor Constantine X, and thus a cousin of Emperor Michael VII. The Doukas family resented Romanos IV, an outsider raised to the throne by their kinswoman Eudokia Makrembolitissa, and relations between the court faction and the Emperor were poisonous throughout his reign.

In 1071 Andronikos accompanied Romanos on the campaign that culminated at Manzikert, holding the senior field command of Domestic of the Schools and leading the reserve division. When the Byzantine centre came under pressure late in the day, Andronikos marched his troops off the field, spreading the rumour that the Emperor was dead or defeated. The army's cohesion collapsed, Romanos was surrounded and captured, and Anatolia was opened to Turkish settlement in the following decade.

Later life

After Manzikert, Andronikos served the regime of his cousin Michael VII. In 1073 he was sent against the revolt of the Norman mercenary Roussel de Bailleul, was defeated and captured by him, then released. In 1077 he was wounded in a campaign against Roussel and died at Edessa on 14 October of that year.

Assessment

Whether his action at Manzikert was treason, panic or a misunderstanding remains disputed. Contemporary historians such as Michael Attaleiates, an eyewitness, treated it as betrayal driven by the Doukas family's hostility to Romanos; modern scholars are divided.
