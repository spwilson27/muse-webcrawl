United States Naval Academy

USNA
Established: 10 October 1845
Location: Annapolis, Maryland
Motto: Ex Scientia Tridens ("From Knowledge, Seapower")

The United States Naval Academy (USNA, Annapolis, or Navy) is an undergraduate college in Annapolis, Maryland, with the mission of educating and commissioning officers for the United States Navy and Marine Corps. It was founded in 1845 by Secretary of the Navy George Bancroft.

Campus

The 338-acre campus, known as the Yard, sits at the mouth of the Severn River. Bancroft Hall, the largest dormitory in the United States, houses the entire Brigade of Midshipmen, some 4,500 strong. The Naval Academy Chapel, with its distinctive dome, and the crypt of John Paul Jones are notable landmarks.

Midshipman life

Students, called midshipmen, enter after a competitive appointment process, traditionally requiring a congressional nomination. The four-year program begins with Plebe Summer and combines academic majors with professional naval training and athletics. Graduates earn a Bachelor of Science degree and commissions as ensigns or second lieutenants.

Traditions

Traditions include the Herndon Monument climb, in which plebes scale a greased obelisk to cap their first year, and the intense Army–Navy football rivalry, dating to 1890.
