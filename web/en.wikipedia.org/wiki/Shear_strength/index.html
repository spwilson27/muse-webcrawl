Shear strength

The shear strength of a material or component is its yield or failure strength when subjected to shear loading, that is, forces that act parallel to a surface and tend to make internal layers slide past one another.

In materials engineering

For metals, shear yield strength is often estimated from tensile properties; von Mises theory predicts shear yield at about 0.577 of the tensile yield strength. Engineers measure shear strength with torsion tests, punch tests and direct shear tests. As a rough rule, ultimate shear strength of many steels is about 60 percent of ultimate tensile strength.

In soil mechanics

In geotechnical engineering, soil shear strength is described by the Mohr-Coulomb criterion, combining cohesion and internal friction. It governs the stability of slopes, foundations and retaining walls, and is measured with direct shear and triaxial tests.
