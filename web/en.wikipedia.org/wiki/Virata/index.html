Virata (Sanskrit: विराट, Virāṭa) is a king in the Hindu epic Mahabharata, ruler of the Matsya kingdom. He is best known for unknowingly sheltering the Pandavas during the thirteenth year of their exile (ajñātavāsa), the "year of concealment" that forms the subject of the epic's fourth book, the Virata Parva.

The court of King Virata, where the Pandavas spent their year in disguise.

In the Mahabharata

Under the terms of their exile, the Pandavas had to spend one year undiscovered or repeat twelve more years in the forest. Each took a disguised role at Virata's court: Yudhishthira as the brahmin gambler Kanka, Bhima as the cook Ballava, Arjuna as the dance teacher Brihannala, Nakula and Sahadeva as horse and cattle keepers, and Draupadi as Queen Sudeshna's maid Sairandhri. When the queen's brother Kichaka harassed Draupadi, Bhima killed him, and suspicion fell on the kingdom. The Kauravas and the Trigartas then attacked Matsya to seize its cattle; Arjuna, revealing himself to Virata's son Prince Uttara, defeated the Kaurava host single-handedly, and the incognito year ended with the Pandavas' identities revealed.

Aftermath

Virata offered his daughter Uttarā in marriage; she wed Arjuna's son Abhimanyu. In the Kurukshetra War, Virata fought for the Pandavas and was killed by Drona (in some recensions by Bhishma's forces) along with his sons. The modern Indian city of Bairat (ancient Viratnagar) in Rajasthan is traditionally identified with his capital.
