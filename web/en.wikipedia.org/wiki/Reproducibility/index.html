Reproducibility

Reproducibility is a core principle of the scientific method: an experiment or study is reproducible if its results can be obtained again — by the same procedure ("repeatability"), by a different team using the same data and methods ("reproducibility" in the computational sense), or independently with new data ("replicability"). Terminology varies between fields and has itself been a source of confusion; a 2019 U.S. National Academies report standardised several of these definitions.

The replication crisis

Beginning in the 2010s, large-scale attempts to repeat published findings produced strikingly low success rates in some fields — most famously the Open Science Collaboration's 2015 effort, which replicated only about a third of 100 psychology studies with the same statistical conclusion — fuelling talk of a replication crisis. Causes identified include publication bias toward positive results, low statistical power, "p-hacking" and other questionable research practices, and weak methods reporting.

Responses

Reforms include pre-registration of hypotheses and analyses, open data and code sharing, registered reports (peer review of protocols before results are known), larger sample sizes, and journals and funders requiring reproducibility checklists. In computational research, reproducible pipelines use version control, containerised environments and workflow tools so that analyses can be rerun exactly.
