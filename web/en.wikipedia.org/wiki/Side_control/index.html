Side control

Side control, also known as side mount or cross side, is a dominant ground position in grappling martial arts such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo, and mixed martial arts, in which the top practitioner lies perpendicular across the opponent's chest, pinning the opponent's shoulders and hips to the mat while remaining free to strike, advance position, or apply submissions. In judo pinning terminology it corresponds broadly to variants of yoko-shiho-gatame (side four-quarters hold).

Technique

The classic side control position has the top player sprawled chest-to-chest at right angles to the opponent, with one arm under the opponent's head, the other controlling the far hip or arm, knees drawn tight to the opponent's side, and weight distributed through the chest and hips to maximize pressure and minimize space. Control of the opponent's near arm and prevention of the bottom player turning to their knees or inserting a knee shield are central details. Variants include the traditional position, kesa gatame (scarf hold), reverse kesa, north–south, and the knee-on-belly transition position.

Attacks and escapes

From side control the top player commonly threatens the kimura and Americana shoulder locks, straight armbars, the arm-triangle choke, and lapel and Ezekiel chokes in gi grappling, or transitions to the mount or back control. In MMA, side control also offers strong ground-and-pound with elbows and knees. Escapes for the bottom player centre on framing against the neck and hip, bridging and shrimping to recover guard, or turning away to a turtle position and wrestling up — movement made difficult precisely because side control isolates the shoulders from the hips.

Scoring

In Brazilian jiu-jitsu competition, passing the guard to a stable side control earns three points; in judo, holding the pin for the prescribed time wins the match by ippon under osaekomi rules.
