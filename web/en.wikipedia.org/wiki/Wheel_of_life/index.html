Wheel of life

The wheel of life (Sanskrit: bhavachakra) is a symbolic representation of samsara, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth in Buddhism. It is commonly depicted in Tibetan Buddhist art as a wheel held in the clutches of Yama, the lord of death.

Structure

The wheel has several concentric elements:

  • Hub: three animals — a pig, a rooster and a snake — symbolising the three poisons of ignorance, attachment and aversion, which drive the wheel.
  • Second circle: a half-circle of light and a half-circle of dark, showing beings rising toward liberation or falling into lower realms according to their karma.
  • Third circle: the six realms of existence — gods, demigods, humans, animals, hungry ghosts and hell beings.
  • Outer rim: twelve links of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), from ignorance through birth, ageing and death, showing how suffering is perpetuated.

A Buddha figure outside the wheel points toward the moon, symbolising liberation from the cycle. The image serves as a teaching device: recognising the wheel's workings is the first step toward escaping it.

Other uses

"Wheel of life" is also the name of a coaching tool in which a circle is divided into segments representing areas of life — career, health, relationships, and so on — and each is rated for satisfaction, producing a visual picture of balance or imbalance.
