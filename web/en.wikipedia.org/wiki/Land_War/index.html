Land War

The Land War (Irish: Cogadh na Talún) was a period of agrarian agitation in Ireland, roughly 1879–1882, organised by the Irish National Land League to campaign for the 'three Fs' — fair rent, fixity of tenure and free sale — for tenant farmers.

Date1879–1882 (later phases to 1909)
LocationIreland
LeadersMichael Davitt, Charles Stewart Parnell
ResultLand Acts; tenant purchase

Course

Founded in 1879 by Michael Davitt, with Charles Stewart Parnell as president, the Land League combined constitutional politics with mass protest amid agricultural depression and threatened famine. Tactics included rent strikes and the boycott — named after Captain Charles Boycott, an ostracised land agent in County Mayo in 1880. The government responded with coercion acts and the Land Law (Ireland) Act 1881, which conceded the three Fs in principle. The Kilmainham Treaty of 1882 ended the first phase.

Legacy

Later campaigns — the Plan of Campaign (1886–1891) and the work of the United Irish League — led to land purchase acts culminating in the Wyndham Act of 1903, which largely transferred Irish land from landlords to tenants and transformed Irish society. See also Land reform.
