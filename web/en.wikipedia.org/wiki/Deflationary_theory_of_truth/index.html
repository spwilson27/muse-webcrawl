Deflationary theory of truth

Deflationary theories of truth hold that saying a statement is true does not attribute a substantial property to it; 'it is true that p' adds nothing beyond asserting p itself.

Main versions

Redundancy, prosentential, minimalist and disquotational theories differ in how they explain truth-talk, but share a deflationary spirit.

Motivation

Deflationists argue truth is a logical or expressive device for generalisation and endorsement, not a deep metaphysical property needing analysis.

Criticism

Critics argue deflationism struggles to explain truth's normative role, its connection to meaning, or its use in theories of success and reference.
