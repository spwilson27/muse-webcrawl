Heat treating

Heat treating (or heat treatment) is a group of industrial, thermal and metalworking processes used to alter the physical, and sometimes chemical, properties of a material, most commonly steel. By controlled heating and cooling, metallurgists adjust hardness, strength, toughness, ductility and wear resistance.

Processes

Annealing heats metal and cools it slowly to soften it and relieve internal stresses. Quenching cools heated metal rapidly in water, oil or air to harden it, and is often followed by tempering, a lower-temperature reheat that reduces brittleness. Case hardening processes such as carburizing and nitriding harden only the surface. Normalizing and precipitation hardening (age hardening) are used for steels and aluminium alloys respectively.

Physical basis

In steels, the effects arise from phase transformations between ferrite, austenite, pearlite, bainite and martensite, governed by the iron–carbon phase diagram and time–temperature–transformation diagrams.
