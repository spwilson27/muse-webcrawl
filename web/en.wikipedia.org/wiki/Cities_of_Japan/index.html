Cities of Japan

A city (市, shi) is a local administrative unit in Japan created under the Local Autonomy Law.

A city (市, shi) is a local administrative unit in Japan. Cities are ranked on the same level as towns (町, machi) and villages (村, mura), with the difference that they are not a component of districts (郡, gun). Like other contemporary administrative units, they are defined by the Local Autonomy Law of 1947.

Definition

Article 8 of the Local Autonomy Law sets the following conditions for a municipality to be designated as a city:

  • The population must generally be 50,000 or greater (原則として人口5万人以上)
  • At least 60% of households must be built up in a central urban area
  • At least 60% of the population must be employed in commerce, industry or other urban occupations
  • Any other conditions in the prefectural ordinances must be satisfied

The designation is approved by the prefectural governor and the Minister for Internal Affairs and Communications. A city can theoretically be demoted to a town or village when it fails to meet any of these conditions, but such a demotion has not happened to date. The least-populated city, Utashinai in Hokkaidō, has a population of merely three thousand, while the town in the same prefecture, Otofuke, has nearly forty thousand.

Special categories

Under the Local Autonomy Law, larger cities may be granted additional autonomy:

CategoryMinimum populationNotes
Designated city (seirei shitei toshi)500,000Divided into wards (ku); performs many prefectural functions
Core city (chūkakushi)200,000Delegated functions in health, environment, and urban planning
Special city (tokureishi)200,000 (historical)Category abolished in 2015; functions merged into core cities

Tokyo's 23 special wards occupy a unique position: the metropolis (to) is a prefecture-level unit, and the wards are treated as city-equivalent municipalities.

Largest cities

The largest cities by population include Tokyo (special wards), Yokohama, Osaka, Nagoya, Sapporo, Fukuoka, Kobe, Kyoto, Kawasaki, and Saitama. As of the 2020s Japan has nearly 800 cities.
