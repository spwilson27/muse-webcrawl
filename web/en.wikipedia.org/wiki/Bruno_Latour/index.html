Bruno Latour

Bruno Latour (22 June 1947 – 9 October 2022) was a French philosopher, anthropologist and sociologist. He was especially known for his work in the field of science and technology studies (STS). After teaching at the École des Mines de Paris (Centre de Sociologie de l'Innovation) from 1982 to 2006, he became Professor at Sciences Po Paris (2006–2017), where he was the scientific director of the Sciences Po Medialab. He retired from several university activities in 2017. He was also a Centennial Professor at the London School of Economics.

Latour is best known for his books We Have Never Been Modern (1991; English translation, 1993), Laboratory Life (with Steve Woolgar, 1979) and Science in Action (1987). Although his studies of scientific practice were at one time associated with social constructionist approaches to the philosophy of science, Latour diverged significantly from such approaches, embracing a more pragmatic and non-modernist stance. He is known for developing the actor–network theory (ANT) approach to science and technology studies in collaboration with Michel Callon, Madeleine Akrich, John Law and others.

Biography

Latour was born in Beaune, Burgundy, into a family of wine producers. He studied philosophy under Michel Serres and was influenced by his work. Latour received a PhD in philosophical theology from the University of Tours in 1975; his dissertation focused on exegesis and religious discourse. He developed an interest in anthropology and undertook fieldwork in Côte d'Ivoire. Latour's first book, Laboratory Life: The Social Construction of Scientific Facts (1979), written with Steve Woolgar, presented an ethnographic study of scientists at the Salk Institute.

Actor–network theory

Latour was one of the key developers of actor–network theory, a constructionist approach influenced by the work of ethnomethodologist Harold Garfinkel, philosopher Michel Serres, and semiotics. ANT treats human and non-human entities (such as laboratory instruments, microbes and texts) symmetrically as "actants" whose interactions form networks. Latour later distanced himself from the term, arguing that "there are four things that do not work with actor-network theory: the word actor, the word network, the word theory and the hyphen!"

Major works

  • The Pasteurization of France (1984)
  • Science in Action (1987)
  • We Have Never Been Modern (1991)
  • Aramis, or the Love of Technology (1992)
  • Pandora's Hope (1999)
  • Politics of Nature (1999)

  • Facing Gaia (2015 lectures; published 2017)
  • Down to Earth (2017)

Later life and death

In his later years Latour turned increasingly to questions of ecology and climate change, curating exhibitions such as Iconoclash (2002), Making Things Public (2005) and Critical Zones (2020) at ZKM Karlsruhe. He died of pancreatic cancer in Paris on 9 October 2022, at the age of 75.
