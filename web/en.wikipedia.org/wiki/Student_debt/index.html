Student debt

Student debt is a form of debt that is owed by an attending, formerly attending, or graduated student to a lending institution, or to a financial institution. The amount loaned is often in the tens of thousands of dollars. Student debt is most prominent in the United States, where total outstanding student loan debt has exceeded $1.6 trillion in the 2020s, held by more than 40 million borrowers; significant student debt also exists in countries including the United Kingdom, Australia and Canada, though the financing systems differ.

United States

Most American student loans are issued or guaranteed by the federal government through programmes such as Direct Loans (Stafford Loans) and PLUS Loans, with a smaller share from private lenders. Federal student loans are unusually difficult to discharge in bankruptcy. Concerns about student debt include its effects on home ownership, household formation, career choice, and retirement saving, as well as disproportionate burdens on borrowers who did not complete a degree.

Repayment options include standard ten-year repayment, income-driven repayment plans that cap payments at a share of discretionary income with forgiveness after 20–25 years, and the Public Service Loan Forgiveness programme. Student debt relief has been a major political issue: in 2022 President Joe Biden announced a plan for broad cancellation of up to $10,000–$20,000 per borrower, which the Supreme Court struck down in Biden v. Nebraska (2023).

Other countries

In England, students borrow from the Student Loans Company and repay through the tax system at a rate above an income threshold, with unpaid balances written off after a fixed term. Australia's HECS-HELP system similarly collects repayments through taxation. Many other countries fund higher education largely from general taxation, resulting in little or no individual student debt.

Effects and debate

Economists debate whether student debt drags on economic growth; borrowers, on average, earn a substantial college wage premium, but default and delinquency are concentrated among those with small balances who left school without credentials.
