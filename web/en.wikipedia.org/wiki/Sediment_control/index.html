Sediment control

Sediment control is the practice of preventing soil, rock, and debris eroded from a site — most often a construction site — from polluting nearby waterways, storm drains, and properties. It is one half of the standard erosion and sediment control (E&SC) discipline: erosion controls stop soil from detaching in the first place, while sediment controls capture soil that has already been mobilised before it leaves the site.

Methods

  • Silt fences — geotextile fabric trenched into the ground along a site's downhill perimeter, ponding runoff so sediment settles out.
  • Sediment basins and ponds — excavated traps that detain stormwater and let suspended soil settle before discharge.
  • Check dams — small dams of rock or fibre rolls placed across swales and ditches to slow flow and drop sediment.
  • Inlet protection — filters around storm drain inlets.
  • Fibre rolls (wattles) and compost socks — placed on slopes and perimeters as barriers.
  • Stabilised site entrances — gravel pads and wheel washes that keep mud from being tracked onto roads.

Regulation

In the United States, construction sites disturbing an acre or more generally require coverage under the Clean Water Act's NPDES stormwater programme, with a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) specifying sediment controls, inspection schedules, and maintenance. Similar requirements exist in Canada, the EU, Australia, and elsewhere. Because sediment is the largest pollutant of surface waters by volume — and carries nutrients, metals, and hydrocarbons with it — controls must be installed before grading begins and maintained until the site is stabilised with vegetation or pavement.
