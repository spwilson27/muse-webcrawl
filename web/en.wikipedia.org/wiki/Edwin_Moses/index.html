Edwin Moses

Edwin Moses

Born: August 31, 1955, Dayton, Ohio, U.S.

Event: 400 metres hurdles

Olympic medals: Gold 1976, Gold 1984, Bronze 1988

Personal best: 47.02 s (1983)

Edwin Corley Moses (born August 31, 1955) is an American former track and field athlete who won gold medals in the 400 m hurdles at the 1976 and 1984 Olympic Games. Between 1977 and 1987, Moses won 107 consecutive finals (122 consecutive races) and set the world record in the event four times, a streak widely regarded as one of the most dominant in athletics history.

Early life

Moses was born in Dayton, Ohio, the son of teachers, and attended Morehouse College in Atlanta on an academic scholarship — the college had no track team — studying physics and industrial engineering while training largely on his own.

Career

Moses burst onto the international scene in 1976, winning the Montreal Olympics in a world record 47.64 seconds in only his first season of international competition. He was prevented from defending his title in 1980 by the U.S. boycott of the Moscow Games, but won gold again at Los Angeles in 1984 and bronze at Seoul in 1988. His world record of 47.02, set in Koblenz in 1983, stood for nine years. He was famous for taking 13 strides between hurdles, a feat few others could sustain.

Sports administration

After retiring from competition, Moses became a leading figure in sports administration and anti-doping reform, chairing the Laureus World Sports Academy and serving in roles connected with the United States Anti-Doping Agency and the World Anti-Doping Agency. He also competed briefly in bobsleigh at the start of the 1990s.
