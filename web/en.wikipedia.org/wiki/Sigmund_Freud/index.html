Sigmund Freud

Sigmund Freud (1856–1939) was an Austrian neurologist and the founder of psychoanalysis, a clinical method for treating psychopathology through dialogue, and one of the most influential — and contested — thinkers of the 20th century.

Theory

Freud proposed the unconscious mind driving behaviour, the structural model of id, ego and superego (1923), psychosexual stages, the Oedipus complex, and dream interpretation (The Interpretation of Dreams, 1899 — see Dream). Techniques included free association and analysis of transference and resistance.

Influence and criticism

Psychoanalysis dominated Western Psychiatry for decades and saturated literary and film culture. But its scientific standing is disputed: Karl Popper argued it is unfalsifiable, and modern psychology accepts selected ideas (unconscious processing, defence mechanisms, talk therapy's value) while rejecting much of the framework. Freud fled Nazi Vienna for London in 1938, dying there the next year.
