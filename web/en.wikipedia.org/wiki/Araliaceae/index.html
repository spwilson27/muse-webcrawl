Araliaceae

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Clade:Angiosperms
Clade:Eudicots
Clade:Asterids
Order:Apiales
Family:Araliaceae Juss.
Genera:About 55
Species:About 1,500

The Araliaceae are a family of flowering plants composed of about 43 to 55 genera and approximately 1,500 species consisting of primarily woody plants and some herbaceous plants commonly called the aralia family or ivy family. The morphology of Araliaceae varies widely, but it is predominantly recognizable by its woody habit, tropical distribution, and the presence of simple umbels.

The family includes well-known ornamental and economically important plants such as Hedera helix (English ivy), Schefflera species (umbrella plants), and Panax ginseng, whose roots are widely used in traditional medicine. Fatsia, Polyscias, and various species of Aralia are cultivated as houseplants and garden ornamentals throughout temperate and tropical regions.

Distribution and habitat

Araliaceae are distributed mainly in the tropics, with centers of diversity in the Indomalayan region, tropical America, and Australasia. A smaller number of species occur in temperate zones of North America, East Asia, and New Zealand. Most members are trees, shrubs, or lianas of humid forests, though a few are herbaceous perennials of temperate woodlands.

Description

Leaves are usually alternate, often palmately compound or palmately lobed, and sometimes very large. Flowers are small, actinomorphic, and typically arranged in umbels that may themselves be grouped into panicles. The ovary is inferior, and the fruit is usually a drupe or berry, often black or dark purple at maturity, dispersed by birds.

Taxonomy

The family is placed in the order Apiales, closely related to Apiaceae and Pittosporaceae. Two subfamilies are generally recognized: Aralioideae, containing the great majority of genera, and Hydrocotyloideae, which includes the former pennyworts such as Hydrocotyle and Trachymene, transferred from Apiaceae on molecular evidence.

Uses

Beyond ornamentals, several species have long histories of use. Ginseng (Panax ginseng and Panax quinquefolius) is among the most valuable medicinal plants in the world. Rice-paper plant (Tetrapanax papyrifer) yields the pith used to make rice paper, and the wood of some tropical genera is used locally for light construction.
