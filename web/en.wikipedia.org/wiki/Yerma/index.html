Yerma

Yerma is a play by Spanish dramatist Federico García Lorca, written in 1934 and first performed that year. Together with Blood Wedding and The House of Bernarda Alba, it forms Lorca's "rural trilogy."

Yerma
Playwright: Federico García Lorca
Premiere: 29 December 1934, Teatro Español, Madrid
Genre: Tragedy
Setting: Rural Andalusia, Spain

Plot

Yerma ("barren") is a young peasant woman trapped in a loveless, childless marriage to Juan, a husband chosen by her father who cares more for honour and work than for her longing for a child. Despite pilgrimages and prayers, she remains childless — the sterility may be Juan's — and refuses to betray honour by taking her former sweetheart Víctor. In despair, she finally strangles Juan, killing her only hope of a child, and accepts her fate.

Themes

The play explores fertility and sterility, honour, repression, and women's confinement in traditional rural Spain, told in poetic prose and verse with chorus-like washerwomen.

Reception

Yerma has been widely translated, adapted into opera and film, and remains a staple of world theatre.
