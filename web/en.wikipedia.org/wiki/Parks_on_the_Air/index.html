Parks on the Air

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parks on the Air (POTA) is an amateur radio operating programme that encourages radio amateurs to operate portable stations from public parks and other publicly owned lands. Participants set up temporary stations in designated parks ("activators") and make contacts with other operators ("hunters") who may be at home or in other parks. Activators upload logs to the programme's website, and both activators and hunters earn awards based on the parks activated and contacted.

History

POTA grew out of the United States National Park Service centennial "National Parks on the Air" event run by the ARRL in 2016. After that year-long event ended, volunteers continued the idea as an ongoing programme, initially in the United States and Canada, and it has since expanded to parks in many countries around the world. The programme catalogues tens of thousands of park entities, each identified by a reference code.

Operation

An activation requires the operator and all equipment to be within the park boundary and on public land. Activators typically use battery-powered transceivers, wire antennas strung in trees, and operate on HF bands, using SSB, CW or digital modes such as FT8. Spotting networks and the POTA website let hunters find activations in progress. A valid activation generally requires at least ten contacts. Park-to-park contacts, where both stations are in registered parks, are credited to both operators.

Awards and community

The programme issues certificates for milestones such as numbers of parks activated or hunted, late-shift and winter operations, and repeated activations of the same park. Annual events and plaque programmes encourage participation. POTA is frequently cited as a way to combine amateur radio with outdoor recreation and to practise emergency-style portable operating.
