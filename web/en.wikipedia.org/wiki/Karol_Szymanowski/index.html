Karol Szymanowski

Karol Maciej Szymanowski (3 October 1882 – 29 March 1937) was a Polish composer and pianist, regarded as the most important Polish composer of the early 20th century and, after Chopin, the central figure of Polish art music. His work passed through distinct phases: a late-Romantic, Straussian early period; a luxuriant, exotic middle period influenced by Scriabin, Debussy, Mediterranean travel and Islamic and ancient Greek culture; and a final period drawing on Polish folk music, especially that of the Tatra highlanders.

Died29 March 1937, Lausanne, Switzerland
OccupationComposer, pianist

Life

Szymanowski was born into a wealthy landed family at Tymoszówka in present-day Ukraine and studied in Warsaw, where he joined the circle known as Young Poland in Music, alongside figures such as Grzegorz Fitelberg and Artur Rubinstein, who championed his music abroad. Travels to Italy, Sicily and North Africa in 1914 transformed his style. After the First World War he settled in Warsaw, becoming director of the Warsaw Conservatory, and spent much time in Zakopane in the Tatras, where he absorbed highlander folk music.

Works

His opera Król Roger (King Roger, 1926), set in Norman Sicily, is his most famous stage work. Other major compositions include the Third Symphony "Song of the Night" (to texts by Rumi), the Stabat Mater (1926), two violin concertos, the ballet Harnasie, the piano cycles Masques and Métopes, and the violin pieces Mythes, written with violinist Paweł Kochański. He also wrote a novel, Efebos, mostly lost. Szymanowski died of tuberculosis in a Lausanne sanatorium in 1937 and is buried in the crypt of honour at Skałka in Kraków.
