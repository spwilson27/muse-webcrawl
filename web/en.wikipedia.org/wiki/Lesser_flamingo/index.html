The lesser flamingo (Phoeniconaias minor) is a species of flamingo occurring in sub-Saharan Africa and northwestern India. Birds are occasionally reported well outside their normal range, such as in southern Europe. It is the smallest and, by number, the most abundant flamingo species, with a population estimated in the millions — flocks on the alkaline lakes of the East African Rift Valley can exceed one million birds and form one of the great wildlife spectacles of the world.

Description

The lesser flamingo stands 80–90 cm tall with a wingspan of 90–105 cm and weighs 1.2–2.7 kg. Its plumage is mostly pinkish white, deepened to crimson on the wing coverts during the breeding season, with black flight feathers. It is readily distinguished from the greater flamingo by its much darker, deep-red bill with a black tip (the greater's bill is pink with a black tip), its smaller size, and its yellow-orange eyes surrounded by a maroon eye-ring. Its deep-keeled bill is specialised for filtering minute food items.

Feeding

The species feeds almost exclusively on microscopic blue-green algae, principally Spirulina platensis (Arthrospira), and on benthic diatoms, which flourish in the warm, highly alkaline soda lakes it inhabits. It feeds with its head immersed and bill upside down, walking slowly and sweeping its head from side to side, filtering organisms as small as a twentieth of a millimetre. The carotenoid pigments in its food give the plumage its pink colour, which fades without them. A large flock can consume many tonnes of algae daily and is a key component of the soda-lake ecosystem.

Distribution and breeding

The main population breeds and feeds on the soda lakes of Kenya and Tanzania — including Lake Nakuru, Lake Bogoria, and Lake Natron — with other populations in Namibia and Botswana (Etosha Pan, Makgadikgadi Pan), Mauritania, and northwestern India. Lake Natron in Tanzania is the most important breeding site: the birds nest on vast mudflats in the caustic lake, building conical mud mounds and laying a single chalky-white egg. The species is highly nomadic, moving between lakes — mostly at night — in response to algal blooms and water levels.

Conservation

The lesser flamingo is listed as Near Threatened by the IUCN. Its dependence on a handful of breeding sites makes it vulnerable: threats include proposed soda-ash extraction at Lake Natron, water abstraction and pollution at feeding lakes, disturbance, and predation when falling water levels give predators access to nesting colonies. Mass die-offs have periodically occurred at Kenyan lakes, linked to toxins and changes in algal communities.
