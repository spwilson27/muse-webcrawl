The River Murray is a river in south-eastern Australia. It is Australia's longest river at 2,508 km (1,558 mi). Its tributaries include five of the next six longest rivers of Australia (the Murrumbidgee, Darling, Lachlan, Warrego and Lower Darling). Together with the Darling, it forms the Murray–Darling river system, whose basin covers about one seventh of the area of Australia and supports a large share of the nation's irrigated agriculture.

Course

The Murray rises in the Australian Alps, draining the western side of Australia's highest mountains, and meanders northwest across the inland plains, forming the border between New South Wales and Victoria. It passes Albury-Wodonga, Echuca, Swan Hill, Mildura and Renmark, and enters South Australia, where it turns south through the Murray Bridge to Lake Alexandrina and the Murray Mouth near Goolwa, reaching the Southern Ocean.

At Echuca, the river's historic paddle-steamer port, the Murray is joined by the Goulburn River; further downstream it receives the Murrumbidgee at Boundary Bend and the Darling — its longest tributary — at Wentworth.

History

The Murray has been home to Aboriginal peoples for at least 40,000 years, and its floodplains and billabongs are rich in cultural sites. Europeans first reached the river in 1824 (Hume and Hovell) and it was named by Captain Charles Sturt in 1830 in honour of Sir George Murray, the British Secretary of State for the Colonies. From the 1850s, paddle steamers made the river a major trade route for wool and supplies, and river ports such as Echuca flourished.

Water management

The river's flow is heavily regulated by dams, weirs and 13 locks built for navigation and irrigation. Over-allocation and drought, notably during the Millennium Drought, caused severe ecological stress, including the closure of the Murray Mouth. The Murray–Darling Basin Plan (2012) set sustainable diversion limits and recovered environmental water for the system.
