The abdominal aorta is the largest artery in the abdominal cavity. As part of the aorta, it is a direct continuation of the descending (thoracic) aorta.

Structure

The abdominal aorta begins at the level of the diaphragm, crossing it via the aortic hiatus at the vertebral level of T12. It descends slightly to the left of the midline and terminates at the level of L4 by bifurcating into the left and right common iliac arteries. Along its course it gives off the celiac trunk, the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries, the renal arteries, gonadal arteries, and paired lumbar arteries.

Clinical significance

Dilation of the abdominal aorta, known as an abdominal aortic aneurysm, can rupture with life-threatening hemorrhage. The pulse of the abdominal aorta can be palpated in the midline of the abdomen.
