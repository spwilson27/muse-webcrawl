Duke of Leinster is a title in the Peerage of Ireland, created in 1766 for James FitzGerald, 20th Earl of Kildare. It is the premier dukedom in the Irish peerage, and the FitzGeralds were for centuries among the most powerful families in Ireland. The family's Dublin residence, Leinster House, is now the seat of the Oireachtas, the Irish parliament.

History

The FitzGeralds, of Norman origin, arrived in Ireland in the 12th century and produced the Earls of Kildare and Desmond. Lord Edward FitzGerald, a younger son of the 1st Duke, was a leader of the United Irishmen and died of wounds after his arrest in 1798. The 2nd Duke's seat was Carton House in County Kildare.

The current holder is Maurice FitzGerald, 9th Duke of Leinster. The heir apparent is Thomas FitzGerald, Earl of Offaly.
