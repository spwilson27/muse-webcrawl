Dependant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A dependent (also spelled dependant in British English) is a person who relies on another, usually a family member, for financial support. In law, the status of a dependent matters in fields such as taxation, immigration, social security, military benefits, and insurance, and each legal system defines the term differently for its own purposes.

Tax law

In United States federal tax law, a dependent is a person other than the taxpayer or spouse who entitles the taxpayer to claim a dependency-related benefit. The Internal Revenue Code distinguishes qualifying children — who must meet relationship, age, residency and support tests — from qualifying relatives, whose gross income must fall below an exemption threshold and to whom the taxpayer provides more than half of support. Historically, dependents generated personal exemptions; since the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, the exemption amount has been zero, but dependent status still affects credits such as the child tax credit and the credit for other dependents.

Immigration and visas

Many visa categories allow the principal holder's spouse and minor children to accompany them as dependents — for example, H-4 dependents of H-1B workers in the United States and PBS dependants in the United Kingdom. Dependents may or may not be permitted to work, depending on the category.

Benefits and other contexts

Military and employer benefit systems cover service members' dependents for health care and housing allowances. In workers' compensation and wrongful-death law, dependents of a deceased worker or victim may be entitled to compensation based on their dependency on the deceased's income. Universities and financial-aid systems similarly classify students as dependent or independent to determine parental income reporting.
