Claude Debussy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Claude Debussy: high forehead and dark beard, photographed in his Paris study]

Achille-Claude Debussy (22 August 1862 – 25 March 1918) was a French composer. He is sometimes seen as the first Impressionist composer, although he vigorously rejected the term. He was among the most influential composers of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Debussy's music loosened the rules of functional harmony, using whole-tone and pentatonic scales, unresolved chords and orchestral colour as ends in themselves. His works include the orchestral Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune (1894), La mer (1905) and Images, the opera Pelléas et Mélisande (1902), and piano pieces such as the two books of Préludes and the beloved 'Clair de lune' from the Suite bergamasque.
