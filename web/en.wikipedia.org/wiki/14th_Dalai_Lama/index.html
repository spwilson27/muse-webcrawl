The 14th Dalai Lama (born Lhamo Thondup, 6 July 1935), known in religion as Tenzin Gyatso, is the current Dalai Lama, the highest spiritual leader of Tibetan Buddhism and, until 2011, the political leader of the Tibetan people. He was recognised as the reincarnation of the 13th Dalai Lama at the age of two and was enthroned in Lhasa in 1940. Following the Chinese incorporation of Tibet and the failure of the 1959 Tibetan uprising, he fled to India, where he has lived in exile in Dharamshala, Himachal Pradesh, ever since.

Early life and recognition

Tenzin Gyatso was born Lhamo Thondup in the village of Taktser in the Amdo region of north-eastern Tibet, into a farming family of Tibetan ethnicity. After the death of the 13th Dalai Lama, Thubten Gyatso, in 1933, a search party of senior lamas was dispatched to find his reincarnation. Following signs and visions — including those seen in the oracle lake Lhamo La-tso — the party arrived at the boy's home, where the child reportedly identified possessions belonging to the previous Dalai Lama, saying "It's mine, it's mine." He was taken to Lhasa and formally enthroned on 22 February 1940 in the Potala Palace, receiving the religious name Tenzin Gyatso.

Political leadership and exile

In 1950, at the age of fifteen, the Dalai Lama was invested with full political power just as the People's Liberation Army entered Tibet. He negotiated with the Chinese government under the Seventeen Point Agreement of 1951, but relations deteriorated through the decade. In March 1959, fearing an attempt on his life, he fled Lhasa disguised as a soldier and crossed the Himalayas into India, where Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru granted him asylum. In exile he established the Central Tibetan Administration, often described as the Tibetan government-in-exile, with its seat at McLeod Ganj, Dharamshala.

Teachings and international role

The Dalai Lama has travelled extensively, teaching on compassion, nonviolence, interreligious harmony and the dialogue between Buddhism and modern science. His books, including The Art of Happiness (with Howard Cutler), have reached a worldwide audience. In 1989 he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for his non-violent struggle for the liberation of Tibet and his advocacy of peaceful solutions based on tolerance and mutual respect. He has consistently advocated the "Middle Way Approach", seeking genuine autonomy for Tibetans within the framework of the People's Republic of China rather than full independence.

Political devolution

In 2011 the Dalai Lama announced his retirement from political leadership, devolving his formal political authority to the democratically elected Sikyong and the leadership of the Central Tibetan Administration, ending the centuries-old tradition of the Dalai Lamas serving as both spiritual and temporal heads of Tibet. He has described himself thereafter as a "simple Buddhist monk". Questions concerning his succession — including statements that his reincarnation should be found in a "free country" and Chinese government claims to control the process — remain a significant geopolitical issue.
