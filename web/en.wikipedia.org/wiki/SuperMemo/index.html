SuperMemo

SuperMemo is a learning method and software package developed in Poland by Piotr Woźniak and collaborators, first released for DOS in 1987. It pioneered the practical application of spaced repetition, scheduling reviews of material at expanding intervals computed to minimize the total time needed to maintain knowledge in long-term memory. SuperMemo's algorithms (SM-0 through SM-18 and beyond) estimate the retrievability of each item and schedule repetitions just before the material is predicted to be forgotten.

SuperMemo schedules flashcard repetitions using algorithmic estimates of forgetting.

History

Woźniak began developing the method in the early 1980s while a student, computing repetition schedules on paper; the first computer implementation appeared in 1987. SuperMemo for Windows followed in the 1990s, and the method popularized ideas that later influenced open-source programs such as Anki and Mnemosyne. Woźniak also developed incremental reading, a technique for processing large volumes of text within the spaced repetition framework.

Algorithm

The SuperMemo algorithms model the forgetting curve for each item based on grades the learner assigns after each repetition (typically on a 0–5 scale). Early versions used a fixed matrix of interval multipliers; later versions compute an item's difficulty and stability to predict the probability of recall on a given day, aiming to keep retrievability above a chosen threshold while minimizing repetitions.

Influence

SuperMemo is credited with founding the modern spaced repetition software movement. Its research on memory — including the "two components of long-term memory" model (stability and retrievability) — has influenced both commercial products and academic study of the spacing effect.
