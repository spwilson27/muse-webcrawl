Aperol Spritz

Type: Cocktail
Primary alcohol: Prosecco, Aperol
Standard garnish: Orange slice
IBA specified: 6 cl Prosecco, 4 cl Aperol, splash of soda

An Aperol Spritz is an Italian wine-based cocktail, commonly served as an aperitif across Italy, especially in the Veneto region. It consists of Prosecco, Aperol and soda water, served over ice in a large wine glass with a slice of orange. The drink's bright orange colour and bittersweet flavour come from Aperol, a gentian- and rhubarb-based aperitivo created in Padua in 1919 by the Barbieri brothers.

History

The spritz itself descends from the practice, in the 19th-century Veneto under Austrian rule, of diluting wine with a splash ("spritzen" in German) of soda water. Aperol was launched at the Padua International Fair in 1919 but the Aperol Spritz only became widely popular in the 1950s, and its modern international fame dates from the 2000s and 2010s, when the Campari Group (which acquired Aperol in 2003) marketed it aggressively worldwide. It is often summarised by the "3-2-1" recipe: three parts Prosecco, two parts Aperol, one part soda.

Preparation

The International Bartenders Association recipe calls for building the drink over ice in a wine glass: 6 cl (2 oz) Prosecco, 4 cl (1¼ oz) Aperol and a splash of soda water, garnished with an orange slice. Aperol has an alcohol content of 11% (22 proof), so the finished spritz is relatively light, typically around 8–9% alcohol.

Popularity

The Aperol Spritz became one of the world's best-selling cocktails in the 2010s and 2020s, emblematic of the Italian aperitivo hour. Its popularity has spawned many variations using other bitter aperitivi, such as the Campari Spritz and the Hugo.
