French Florida

French Florida (French: Floride française) was a colonial territory established by French Huguenot colonists in what is now Florida and the southeastern United States between 1562 and 1565. The French effort, led by Jean Ribault and René Goulaine de Laudonnière, founded Charlesfort on Parris Island in present-day South Carolina and then Fort Caroline near the mouth of the St. Johns River in present-day Jacksonville, Florida. The colony was destroyed by Spanish forces under Pedro Menéndez de Avilés in 1565.

Background

France sought a foothold in the Americas and a refuge for persecuted Huguenots, as well as a base from which to threaten Spanish treasure fleets. Admiral Gaspard de Coligny sponsored the expeditions. Ribault's first voyage in 1562 established Charlesfort, which was abandoned within a year after supplies ran short.

Fort Caroline

Laudonnière returned in 1564 and built Fort Caroline, named for King Charles IX. The colonists traded with the local Timucua peoples, notably the Saturiwa chiefdom, but suffered from food shortages, mutinies and desertion. The artist Jacques le Moyne de Morgues, a member of the expedition, produced some of the earliest European images of Native Americans in the region; his surviving work, later engraved by Theodor de Bry, became famous in Europe.

Destruction

Spain regarded the French settlement as an intrusion and a Protestant threat. Menéndez founded St. Augustine in September 1565 and marched overland to capture Fort Caroline, killing most of its defenders. Ribault's relief fleet was wrecked by a storm, and Menéndez executed most of the shipwrecked Frenchmen at an inlet later named Matanzas ("slaughters"). France never again attempted to colonise Florida, though a French force under Dominique de Gourgues avenged the colonists in 1568 by attacking a Spanish fort on the St. Johns River.

Legacy

The site of Fort Caroline is commemorated by the Fort Caroline National Memorial, part of the Timucuan Ecological and Historic Preserve. The episode remains a notable episode of early European colonisation and confessional conflict in North America.
