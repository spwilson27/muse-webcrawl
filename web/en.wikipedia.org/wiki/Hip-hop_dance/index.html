Hip-hop dance

Hip-hop dance is a range of street dance styles primarily performed to hip-hop music, which developed as part of hip-hop culture in New York City during the 1970s. It includes breaking (breakdance), locking, and popping — the latter two originating in California funk scenes — as well as later social and choreography styles.

Breaking

Breaking, performed by b-boys and b-girls, was pioneered in the Bronx by Puerto Rican and African-American youth at DJ Kool Herc's parties. It comprises four elements: toprock (standing steps), downrock (footwork on the floor), power moves (acrobatic spins such as headspins and windmills), and freezes. Dancers compete in battles, individually or in crews, judged in cyphers. Breaking gained worldwide attention in the early 1980s through films such as Wild Style and Flashdance, and made its Olympic debut at the Paris 2024 Games.

Funk styles and new style

Locking was created by Don Campbell in Los Angeles, and popping by Boogaloo Sam; both were popularised on the television show Soul Train. From the 1990s, commercial "new style" and lyrical hip-hop choreography developed in studios and music videos, differing from the improvisational street forms; competitions and television shows such as America's Best Dance Crew and World of Dance further popularised the dance globally.
