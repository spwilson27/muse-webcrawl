Royal Danish Theatre

The Royal Danish Theatre (Danish: Det Kongelige Teater) is Denmark's national theatre institution, founded in 1748 and based in Copenhagen. It is unusual among national theatres in encompassing four art forms under one administration: drama, opera, ballet and orchestral music, performed by the Royal Danish Playhouse company, the Royal Danish Opera, the Royal Danish Ballet and the Royal Danish Orchestra. Its historic home since 1874 is the theatre building on Kongens Nytorv in central Copenhagen.

History

The theatre was established by royal decree of Frederick V, and its first permanent building opened on Kongens Nytorv in 1748. Drama had previously been performed in Danish by Ludvig Holberg's company at the Lille Grønnegade theatre from 1722, and Holberg's comedies remained central to the repertoire. The present main building, designed by Vilhelm Dahlerup and Ove Petersen, opened in 1874 with a main stage (Gamle Scene, the Old Stage) seating about 1,600. Over the centuries the theatre became the cradle of Danish performing arts: the Royal Danish Ballet, founded in 1748 and shaped in the 19th century by August Bournonville, is one of the world's oldest ballet companies, and the Royal Danish Orchestra traces its origins to a court ensemble of 1448.

Venues

Since the early 21st century the institution has expanded beyond Kongens Nytorv. The Copenhagen Opera House (Operaen) on Holmen, donated by the A.P. Møller Foundation and opened in 2005, is the home of the Royal Danish Opera. The Royal Danish Playhouse (Skuespilhuset), designed by Lundgaard & Tranberg and opened in 2008 on the harbourfront at Kvæsthusbroen, houses the drama department with three stages. The Old Stage continues to host ballet and opera. The institution is funded by the Danish state under the Ministry of Culture and tours productions nationally.
