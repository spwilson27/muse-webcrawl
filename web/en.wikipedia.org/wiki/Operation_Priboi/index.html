Operation Priboi

Operation Priboi ("Coastal Surf") was the code name for the Soviet mass deportation from the Baltic states on 25–28 March 1949. The operation, also known as the March deportation, was carried out by the Soviet authorities in the Estonian SSR, Latvian SSR and Lithuanian SSR. More than 94,000 people — overwhelmingly women, children and the elderly — were deported to remote regions of Siberia and other parts of the Soviet Union.

Background

After the Soviet re-occupation of the Baltic states in 1944, armed resistance by the "Forest Brothers" continued, and the authorities moved to eliminate the support base of the partisans and to force the collectivization of agriculture. Families designated as "kulaks", nationalists, and relatives of resistance fighters were listed for deportation.

The operation

The deportations were planned in secrecy and executed by MGB troops and local officials in a coordinated sweep beginning in the early hours of 25 March 1949. Deportees were given little time to pack, transported in freight wagons, and resettled in areas including Irkutsk, Omsk, Tomsk and Amur oblasts and Krasnoyarsk Krai, where many were assigned to collective farms and forestry work. A significant number died from the harsh conditions.

Legacy

The March deportation broke the back of armed resistance and accelerated collectivization in the Baltic states. Many deportees were permitted to return only after Stalin's death and the partial amnesties of the 1950s. The events are commemorated annually in Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, and have been recognized as a crime against humanity by Baltic courts and the European Court of Human Rights.
