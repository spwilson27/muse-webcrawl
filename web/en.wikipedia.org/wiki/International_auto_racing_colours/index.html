International auto racing colours

International auto racing colours were national colour schemes applied to racing cars in international motor sport from the early 1900s until the late 1960s, when sponsorship liveries took over. Under the scheme administered first by the AIACR and later the FIA, a car's colour identified its country of origin.

Famous colours

The best-known schemes are British racing green for the United Kingdom, rosso corsa (racing red) for Italy, silver — later the Silver Arrows — for Germany, blue for France (associated with Bugatti), white with red for Japan, and the United States' white with blue stripes or blue with white stripes. Belgium raced in yellow and the Netherlands in orange.

Origins

The system began with the Gordon Bennett Cup races of 1900–1905, where national teams were distinguished by colour. German cars were originally white; the famous silver arose in 1934 when Mercedes-Benz stripped the paint from its W25 to meet a weight limit, exposing the aluminium bodywork.

Decline

The FIA relaxed the rules in 1968, allowing sponsor colours, and the Gold Leaf Lotus of that year inaugurated the modern era of commercial livery. National colours survive in occasional retro liveries and in the identity of teams such as Ferrari, which still races in red.
