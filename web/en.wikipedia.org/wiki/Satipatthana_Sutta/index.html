Satipatthana Sutta

Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta
CanonPali Canon (Theravada)
CollectionMajjhima Nikāya, sutta 10
Expanded versionMahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (Digha Nikāya 22)
SettingKammāsadamma, in the Kuru country
SubjectThe four foundations of mindfulness

The Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta is a discourse (sutta) of the Pali Canon, found as the tenth sutta of the Majjhima Nikāya, in which the Buddha sets out the practice of satipaṭṭhāna — the four foundations of mindfulness. It is one of the most influential meditation texts in Buddhism and the principal scriptural basis of modern insight (vipassanā) meditation. An expanded version, the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, appears as sutta 22 of the Digha Nikāya.

The sutta opens by declaring the four foundations "the direct path" to the purification of beings and the realisation of nibbāna.

Contents

After a refrain verse describing how the practitioner dwells "ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful", the sutta proceeds through the four foundations. The section on the body covers mindfulness of breathing, the four postures, clear comprehension of activities, reflection on the body's parts and elements, and cemetery contemplations of a decaying corpse. Feelings and mind are contemplated according to their qualities — pleasant, painful or neutral; lustful, hating, deluded, concentrated and so on. The section on mental phenomena (dhammas) treats the five hindrances, the five aggregates, the six sense bases and the seven factors of awakening; the Digha version adds an exposition of the Four Noble Truths. The sutta closes with the promise that sustained practice can lead to the highest stages of awakening within a period of from seven years down to seven days.

Influence

Commentarial tradition treats the sutta as the Buddha's definitive teaching on meditation. In the 20th century it became the foundation text of the Burmese vipassanā revival associated with teachers such as Mahasi Sayadaw and U Ba Khin, and through their students a major source for the global spread of mindfulness practice.
