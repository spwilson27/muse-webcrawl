Ihsan "Sammy" Farha (born 1959 in Beirut, Lebanon) is a Lebanese-American professional poker player, best known as the runner-up of the 2003 World Series of Poker Main Event, in which he lost heads-up to amateur Chris Moneymaker — a televised moment widely credited with igniting the 2000s "poker boom."

Career

Farha moved to the United States in 1977 to study at the University of Kansas, later settling in Houston, Texas, where he ran businesses before turning to poker professionally in the 1990s. An aggressive cash-game specialist, particularly in pot-limit Omaha, he has won three WSOP bracelets, all in Omaha events (1996, 2006 and 2010). He was a regular on televised cash games such as High Stakes Poker and Poker After Dark, and is instantly recognisable at the table by the unlit cigarette he habitually holds in his mouth, having given up smoking.

The 2003 Main Event

Farha's duel with Moneymaker became poker's most famous hand matchup: on the final table, Moneymaker's celebrated bluff with king-high against Farha's top pair helped swing momentum before the amateur won the title. Because Farha was the consummate professional beaten by an internet qualifier, the story became central to ESPN's coverage and to the popular narrative of the poker boom. Farha co-authored the book Farha on Omaha (2007).
