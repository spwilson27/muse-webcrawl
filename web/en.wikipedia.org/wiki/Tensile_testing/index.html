Tensile testing

Tensile testing, also known as tension testing, is a fundamental materials science and engineering test in which a sample is subjected to a controlled uniaxial tension until failure. The test measures properties such as ultimate tensile strength, breaking strength, maximum elongation, and reduction in area, from which qualities like Young's modulus, yield strength, and strain-hardening characteristics can be determined.

Procedure

A standardized specimen, often machined into a "dog-bone" shape with a reduced central section, is mounted in the grips of a universal testing machine. The machine pulls the specimen at a controlled rate while load cells and extensometers record the applied force and the elongation of the gauge length.

Stress–strain curve

The results are typically plotted as a stress–strain curve. The initial linear region gives the elastic modulus; the yield point marks the onset of permanent plastic deformation; and the peak of the curve is the ultimate tensile strength. Ductile materials exhibit necking before fracture, whereas brittle materials fail with little plastic deformation.

Standards

Tensile testing of metals is governed by standards such as ASTM E8/E8M and ISO 6892, while plastics and composites have their own standards (for example ASTM D638 and ASTM D3039). Testing is used in quality control, materials selection, and research and development across many industries.
