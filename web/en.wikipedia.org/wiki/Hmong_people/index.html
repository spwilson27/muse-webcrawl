Hmong people

Hmong
Total population: est. 4–5 million
Regions: China, Vietnam, Laos, Thailand, United States
Languages: Hmong (Hmongic)
Religions: Animism/Shamanism, Buddhism, Christianity

The Hmong people (Hmong: Hmoob) are an ethnic group of East and Southeast Asia who speak Hmongic languages of the Hmong–Mien family. They originate from southern China, where the majority still live, with significant populations in northern Vietnam, Laos and Thailand and a large diaspora in the United States, France and Australia.

History

Hmong oral tradition traces origins to the Yellow River region of China. Over centuries, conflicts with the expanding Han Chinese state pushed Hmong groups southward into Guizhou, Yunnan and Sichuan, and from the early 19th century into the mountains of mainland Southeast Asia. In China, Hmong are classified within the Miao nationality, a grouping that includes related peoples.

Laos and the diaspora

During the Laotian Civil War (1959–1975), many Hmong in Laos were recruited by the CIA to fight communist forces in the "Secret War", under leaders such as General Vang Pao. After the communist victory in 1975, tens of thousands fled to refugee camps in Thailand and were resettled abroad, especially in the United States, where large communities exist in Minnesota, Wisconsin and California's Central Valley.

Culture

Hmong society is organised by patrilineal clans sharing surnames such as Vang, Xiong, Lee and Yang. Traditional culture features shamanic healing, elaborate embroidery (paj ntaub, "flower cloth") and story cloths, silver jewellery, and the New Year festival Tsab Pob. The Hmong language was given a widely used Latin-based script, the Romanized Popular Alphabet, in the 1950s. Major subgroups include White Hmong (Hmoob Dawb) and Green/Blue Hmong (Moob Leeg/Ntsuab).
