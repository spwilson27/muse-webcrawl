The Graduate Record Examinations (GRE) is a standardized test administered by the Educational Testing Service (ETS) that is an admissions requirement for many graduate schools in the United States, Canada, and other countries. Created in 1936 by the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching, the GRE aims to measure skills — verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, analytical writing, and critical thinking — acquired over a long period of learning.

Structure

The GRE General Test is section-level adaptive: performance on the first section of verbal or quantitative questions determines the difficulty of the second. Since September 2023 the test has been shortened to under two hours, comprising an Analytical Writing section with a single essay task, two Verbal Reasoning sections, and two Quantitative Reasoning sections. Verbal and Quantitative sections are each scored on a 130–170 scale in one-point increments; Analytical Writing is scored from 0 to 6.

Separate GRE Subject Tests exist in a small number of disciplines, such as mathematics, physics, and psychology, most of them now delivered in computer format.

Use and criticism

Scores are valid for five years. Graduate programs weigh GRE scores alongside undergraduate records and letters of recommendation, but many programs made the test optional or dropped it entirely during and after the COVID-19 pandemic. Critics have questioned its predictive value for graduate success and its correlation with demographic factors; ETS and supporters argue it provides a common, objective measure across applicants from diverse institutions.
