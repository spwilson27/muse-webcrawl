Common Riding

Common Riding
RegionScottish Borders
WhenSummer (June–August)
TownsHawick, Selkirk, Galashiels, Peebles, Langholm and others

Common Riding is an annual equestrian tradition of the Scottish Borders, in which townspeople ride out on horseback to inspect the boundaries (marches) of the town's common land, commemorating the era when riding the bounds protected the community's territory from encroachment and raiders.

Origins

The custom dates to the centuries of Anglo-Scottish border warfare, when burghs appointed riders to patrol their marches. Towns such as Hawick trace their ridings to events of the early sixteenth century; the Selkirk Common Riding also commemorates the town's sole survivor of the Battle of Flodden in 1513.

Modern festivals

Today each Border town holds its own festival, led by an elected principal rider — Hawick's Cornet, for example — with ride-outs, ceremonies at historic monuments, and the casting of town colours. The season of ridings is a major focus of local identity and tourism.
