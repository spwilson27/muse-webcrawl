Maurice Rupert Bishop (29 May 1944 – 19 October 1983) was a Grenadian revolutionary leader who served as Prime Minister of Grenada from 1979 to 1983. A British-trained lawyer, he led the New Jewel Movement (NJM) in overthrowing the government of Eric Gairy in March 1979, establishing the People's Revolutionary Government.

Early life

Bishop was born in Aruba to Grenadian parents and raised in Grenada before studying law in London, where he was influenced by Black Power and Marxist thought. Returning home, he co-founded the NJM in 1973, merging his Movement for Assemblies of the People with the Joint Endeavour for Welfare, Education and Liberation.

People's Revolutionary Government

The 1979 coup, launched while Gairy was abroad, was nearly bloodless and initially popular. Bishop's government expanded literacy and health programmes, built a new international airport with Cuban assistance, and aligned Grenada with Cuba and the Soviet bloc while keeping a mixed economy. It postponed elections, however, and restricted opposition media, drawing criticism.

Downfall and death

In 1983 an internal NJM power struggle led by Deputy Prime Minister Bernard Coard placed Bishop under house arrest. Freed by supporters on 19 October 1983, Bishop and several ministers were recaptured by the army and executed at Fort Rupert. Days later the United States invaded Grenada in Operation Urgent Fury. Bishop remains a revered figure for many Grenadians; the island's international airport now bears his name.
