Computer anxiety

Computer anxiety is a phenomenon experienced by many users of computers, in which a person feels apprehensive, fearful, or stressed when using a computer or anticipating its use. It has been studied in psychology and human–computer interaction since the widespread adoption of personal computers in the 1980s.

Description

Computer anxiety can manifest as reluctance to use computers, avoidance of tasks involving them, negative thoughts about one's ability to use them, and physiological symptoms of stress. It is related to, but distinct from, low computer self-efficacy: an anxious user may be competent but still dread the experience, fearing they might break something or lose data.

Causes and factors

Research associates computer anxiety with limited prior experience, negative early encounters with technology, perceptions that computers are complex or unforgiving, and organizational pressures to adopt new systems. Some studies have found correlations with age and with certain personality traits, though experience and training are generally stronger predictors.

Effects

Higher levels of computer anxiety are associated with lower usage, poorer performance on computer-based tasks, reduced job satisfaction in technology-heavy roles, and resistance to new systems in workplaces and education. In educational settings it can affect students' willingness to engage with online learning.

Mitigation

Interventions include supportive training that allows practice in a low-stakes environment, user-friendly interface design, gradual exposure, and technical support that reduces the fear of making irreversible mistakes.
