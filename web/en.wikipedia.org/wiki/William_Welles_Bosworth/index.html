William Welles Bosworth


Born May 8, 1869, Marietta, Ohio, U.S.
Died May 3, 1966 (aged 96), Short Hills, New Jersey
Occupation Architect

William Welles Bosworth (May 8, 1869 – May 3, 1966) was an American architect known for his Beaux-Arts designs for wealthy patrons, including the Kykuit estate of John D. Rockefeller Jr.

Education and career

Bosworth studied architecture at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, where he absorbed the French classical tradition. Returning to New York, he became a favourite architect of the Rockefeller family and of other Gilded Age patrons.

Major works

His most famous commission is Kykuit (1913), the Rockefeller estate at Pocantico Hills, New York, whose gardens and classical mansion are landmarks of American country-house design. He also designed buildings and the campus plan for MIT's move to Cambridge, Massachusetts, including the Maclaurin Buildings around Killian Court, and Letchworth Village in New York.

France

After the First World War Bosworth moved to France, where he undertook restorations of historic properties, including work at Versailles and the Château de Bagatelle, and was made a commander of the Legion of Honour. He spent his later years between France and the United States, dying in 1966 just short of his 97th birthday.
