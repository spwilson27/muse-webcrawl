National Government (United Kingdom)

Period1931–1940
Prime MinistersRamsay MacDonald (1931–35), Stanley Baldwin (1935–37), Neville Chamberlain (1937–40)
CompositionConservative-dominated coalition with National Labour and National Liberal elements

In British politics, the National Government was a coalition of parties formed in August 1931 to deal with the financial crisis of the Great Depression, and it governed the United Kingdom — increasingly as a Conservative administration under a "National" label — until it was replaced by Churchill's wartime coalition in May 1940.

Formation and the 1931 crisis

The Labour government of Ramsay MacDonald split over proposed cuts to unemployment benefit demanded to defend the pound and balance the budget. On 24 August 1931 MacDonald formed an emergency all-party government with the Conservatives under Stanley Baldwin and the Liberals. The Labour Party expelled him, and he led a breakaway "National Labour" group. Britain left the gold standard within weeks, but the government won a landslide "doctor's mandate" in the October 1931 election, winning over 550 seats, the great majority of them Conservative.

Government of the 1930s

MacDonald remained Prime Minister until ill health forced him to hand over to Baldwin in 1935; Neville Chamberlain succeeded Baldwin in 1937. The government pursued protectionism through Imperial Preference, managed gradual economic recovery as rearmament began, steered through the abdication crisis of Edward VIII in 1936, and under Chamberlain became identified with the appeasement of Nazi Germany, culminating in the Munich Agreement of 1938. After the outbreak of the Second World War and the disastrous Norway debate, Chamberlain resigned and Winston Churchill formed a genuine all-party coalition in May 1940, ending the National Government.
