John D. Lawson

John David Lawson FRS (4 April 1923 – 15 January 2008) was a British engineer and physicist best known for formulating the Lawson criterion, a key condition for achieving net energy gain in nuclear fusion.

Career

Born in Coventry, Lawson studied mechanical sciences at the University of Cambridge and worked on radar during the Second World War at the Telecommunications Research Establishment, Malvern. He subsequently worked at the Atomic Energy Research Establishment at Harwell and later at the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory, where he pursued research on particle accelerators, plasma physics, and free-electron lasers.

Lawson criterion

In a classified 1955 paper (published in 1957), Lawson analysed the power balance of a fusion reactor and showed that a plasma must simultaneously achieve sufficient density, temperature, and energy confinement time — often summarised by the "triple product" — for fusion to be practical. The Lawson criterion remains a standard benchmark for fusion experiments such as tokamaks.

Lawson was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1983 and wrote a widely read textbook, The Physics of Charged-Particle Beams (1977).
