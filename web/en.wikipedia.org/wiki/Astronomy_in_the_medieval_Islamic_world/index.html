Astronomy in the medieval Islamic world

Astronomy in the medieval Islamic world refers to the astronomical advances made between roughly the 8th and 16th centuries, during the Islamic Golden Age, by scholars working in Arabic and Persian across the Middle East, North Africa, al-Andalus and Central Asia. Building on Greek, Persian and Indian sources, Islamic astronomers refined observations, instruments and mathematical models, and their work was later transmitted to Europe, where it influenced Copernicus and the Scientific Revolution.

Origins and translation

Under the Abbasid caliphs of Baghdad, Greek scientific works — above all Ptolemy's Almagest — were translated into Arabic, alongside Persian and Sanskrit texts. Astronomy (ʿilm al-hayʾa) served practical religious needs: determining prayer times, the qibla (direction of Mecca), and the lunar calendar, as well as astrology, navigation and timekeeping.

Observations and instruments

Major observatories were built at Baghdad, Damascus, Maragha (under Nasir al-Din al-Tusi), Samarkand (under Ulugh Beg) and Istanbul (under Taqi ad-Din). Astronomers compiled star catalogues and astronomical tables (zijes), such as those of al-Battani and Ulugh Beg, and improved instruments including the astrolabe, quadrant and armillary sphere. Many star names used today — Betelgeuse, Rigel, Aldebaran, Deneb — are of Arabic origin, as are terms such as azimuth, nadir and zenith.

Models and critique

Scholars such as Ibn al-Haytham criticised inconsistencies in Ptolemy's models. Astronomers of the Maragha school, including al-Tusi (inventor of the Tusi couple) and Ibn al-Shatir of Damascus, developed new geometrical devices to reconcile planetary models with uniform circular motion. Strikingly similar devices appear in Copernicus's De revolutionibus, suggesting transmission of these techniques to Renaissance Europe.

Legacy

Islamic astronomy preserved and extended ancient astronomy through a period of many centuries, improved the accuracy of planetary tables, and contributed instruments, terminology and mathematical methods that became part of the global scientific heritage.
