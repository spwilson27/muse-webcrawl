Refurbishment (electronics)

Refurbishment is the distribution of products, usually electronics and electricals, that have been previously returned to a manufacturer or vendor for various reasons, like not being sold in a market or a new design of a product coming to market. Refurbished products are normally tested for functionality and defects before they are sold to the public. They are repaired by the original equipment manufacturer and resold, usually at a discount and often with a warranty.

Process

Returned or traded-in devices are inspected, data-wiped, repaired with replacement parts where necessary, cleaned, and tested against specifications. Cosmetic grades (such as "excellent", "very good", or "good") describe exterior wear. Batteries in phones and laptops are frequently replaced or certified to retain a minimum proportion of original capacity. Refurbished units are then repackaged, sometimes with new accessories.

Market and environmental role

Manufacturer-certified programmes (such as Apple's Refurbished store) coexist with third-party refurbishers and resale platforms. Refurbishment extends product lifetimes, reduces electronic waste, and lowers the embodied carbon of a purchase compared with buying new; it forms part of the circular economy and supports right-to-repair initiatives. Consumer law in many jurisdictions treats refurbished goods distinctly from second-hand goods, requiring clear labelling and, for manufacturer-refurbished items, warranty coverage.
