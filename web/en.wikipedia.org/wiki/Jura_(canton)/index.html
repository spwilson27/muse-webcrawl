The Republic and Canton of Jura (French: République et canton du Jura) is the newest of the 26 cantons of Switzerland, officially created on 1 January 1979. Located in the northwestern part of the country, in the Jura Mountains, it is predominantly French-speaking and Roman Catholic, with its capital at Delémont.

History

The region belonged for centuries to the Prince-Bishopric of Basel. After the Napoleonic Wars, the Congress of Vienna (1815) assigned it to the canton of Bern, whose government was largely German-speaking and Protestant. Over the following century, grievances over language, religion and economic neglect fed a separatist movement — the Question jurassienne.

After the Moeckli affair of 1947, in which a French-speaking politician was denied a government post partly on linguistic grounds, separatist organisations such as the Rassemblement jurassien campaigned for a separate canton. A series of plebiscites in 1974–1975 saw the northern, Catholic, French-speaking districts vote to form the new canton, while the southern districts (the Bernese Jura) voted to remain with Bern. Federal voters approved the new canton in a 1978 referendum, and it was inaugurated on 1 January 1979.

The Moutier question

The town of Moutier, which had remained Bernese, voted in 2017 and again in 2021 to transfer to Jura — the 2021 vote being accepted after the earlier one was annulled — with the transfer scheduled for 1 January 2026.

Geography and economy

The canton comprises three districts: Delémont, Porrentruy (in the Ajoie) and the Franches-Montagnes plateau, famous for its horses. The economy combines watchmaking and precision engineering around Delémont and Porrentruy with agriculture and tourism. The canton is known for the damassine plum spirit, Tête de Moine cheese (from nearby Bellelay), and the St-Ursanne old town on the river Doubs, part of which forms the border with France.
