City God (China)

The City God (Chinese: 城隍; pinyin: Chénghuáng, literally "god of the moat and the walls") is a tutelary deity in Chinese folk religion and Taoism who protects the inhabitants and affairs of a city and its surrounding territory. Nearly every major city in historical China had a City God temple (Chenghuangmiao), and the deity was regarded as a divine magistrate mirroring the imperial bureaucracy.

Role and worship

The City God was believed to guard the city against disasters such as floods, droughts, and epidemics, and to judge the dead of the locality, recording their deeds and presiding over their passage to the afterlife. Incoming local officials traditionally reported to the City God temple upon taking office. The deity's birthday, commonly the 15th day of the fifth lunar month in many regions, was celebrated with temple fairs and processions.

History

Sacrifices to city walls and moats are recorded from ancient times, and the cult developed during the Tang and Song dynasties. The Hongwu Emperor of the Ming dynasty systematised the cult in the 14th century, granting City Gods official ranks corresponding to the administrative rank of their cities — first, second, or third rank for prefectural and county gods. Many City Gods were deified historical officials or generals famed for integrity.

Temples

Famous City God temples include those in Shanghai, Xi'an, and Pingyao. The cult spread with Chinese diaspora communities to Taiwan, Vietnam (where the god is known as Thành hoàng), Korea, Malaysia, and Singapore.
