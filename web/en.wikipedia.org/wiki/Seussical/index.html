Seussical

Music: Stephen Flaherty
Lyrics: Lynn Ahrens
Book: Lynn Ahrens and Stephen Flaherty
Conceived by: Ahrens, Flaherty, and Eric Idle
Basis: The works of Dr. Seuss
Premiere: 30 November 2000, Richard Rodgers Theatre, Broadway

Seussical, sometimes Seussical the Musical, is a musical with music by Stephen Flaherty, lyrics by Lynn Ahrens, and a book by Ahrens and Flaherty. It is based on the children's stories of Dr. Seuss, weaving together primarily Horton Hears a Who!, Horton Hatches the Egg, and Gertrude McFuzz, with appearances by many other Seuss characters, narrated by the Cat in the Hat.

Productions

The musical premiered in Boston before opening on Broadway at the Richard Rodgers Theatre on 30 November 2000. The Broadway production received mixed reviews and closed in May 2001 after 198 performances, losing much of its investment. A substantially revised version toured North America, and the show — especially in its one-act "Theatre for Young Audiences" version — became one of the most frequently produced musicals in American schools and community theatres.

Synopsis

The Cat in the Hat guides the audience into the Jungle of Nool, where Horton the Elephant discovers a speck of dust containing the tiny world of Who-ville and resolves to protect it, even as the other animals mock him and the Wickersham Brothers steal the clover on which the Whos live. Interwoven is the story of Gertrude McFuzz, who longs for a grander tail and for Horton's notice, and of JoJo, a young Who whose "thinks" get him into trouble. Horton is put on trial, but Gertrude's persistence and the Whos' collective cry — "We are here!" — save their world. Well-known songs include "Oh, the Thinks You Can Think!", "Alone in the Universe", "The One Feather Tail of Miss Gertrude McFuzz", "Amazing Mayzie", "Notice Me, Horton", and "Green Eggs and Ham".

Reception and legacy

Although the original Broadway run disappointed commercially, the show's modest-cast touring revision found a durable afterlife in educational and regional theatre, where its themes of imagination, loyalty, and the worth of every person — "a person's a person, no matter how small" — made it a staple family musical.
