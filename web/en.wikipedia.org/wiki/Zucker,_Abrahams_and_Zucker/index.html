Zucker, Abrahams and Zucker

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zucker, Abrahams and Zucker (abbreviated ZAZ) is an American comedy filmmaking trio consisting of Jim Abrahams and brothers David Zucker and Jerry Zucker. They are known for their parody films, particularly Airplane! (1980), Top Secret! (1984), and the Naked Gun series, and are credited with popularizing the spoof movie genre built on rapid-fire sight gags, puns, and deadpan delivery.

Career

The three grew up together in Wisconsin and founded the Kentucky Fried Theater sketch-comedy group in Madison in 1969. Their first film as writers was The Kentucky Fried Movie (1977), directed by John Landis. Airplane!, a parody of disaster films, was a major commercial and critical success and is frequently ranked among the greatest comedy films ever made. ZAZ went on to create the short-lived television series Police Squad! (1982), which later spawned The Naked Gun films starring Leslie Nielsen.

Later work

The members later pursued separate projects: Jerry Zucker directed the hit drama Ghost (1990), while David Zucker directed installments of the Scary Movie franchise. Their collaborative style—casting dramatic actors to play absurd material completely straight—became a template for decades of parody filmmaking.
