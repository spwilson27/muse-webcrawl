Jonathan Wild

Jonathan Wild (c. 1683 – 24 May 1725) was a London underworld figure who operated as both a criminal gang leader and a self-styled "Thief-Taker General", ostensibly recovering stolen goods and bringing criminals to justice while running the very networks that stole them.

Wild's double game made him one of the most powerful criminals in Georgian London; he betrayed associates to the gallows when convenient and profited from rewards and blackmail. Arrested under a law aimed at receivers who took rewards without prosecuting thieves, he was hanged at Tyburn in 1725. His life inspired Henry Fielding's satirical novel The Life and Death of Jonathan Wild, the Great (1743) and a character in John Gay's The Beggar's Opera.
