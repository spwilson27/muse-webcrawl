George IV (George Augustus Frederick; 12 August 1762 – 26 June 1830) was King of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland and King of Hanover from the death of his father, George III, in 1820 until his own death. He had served as Prince Regent since 1811 because of his father's mental illness, and the period of his regency and reign is known for the Regency style in architecture, literature and fashion.

Prince of Wales and Regency

As Prince of Wales, George led an extravagant life that strained relations with his frugal father. He secretly married the Catholic widow Maria Fitzherbert in 1785 — a union invalid under the Royal Marriages Act — and in 1795 reluctantly married his cousin Caroline of Brunswick; the couple separated after the birth of their only child, Princess Charlotte, and his attempt to divorce Caroline in 1820 caused a public scandal. As Regent he presided over the final years of the Napoleonic Wars, ending with victory at Waterloo in 1815.

Reign

George's lavish coronation in 1821 and expensive building projects — including John Nash's remodelling of Buckingham Palace, the Royal Pavilion at Brighton, and the development of Regent's Park and Regent Street — made him a byword for extravagance, though they left a lasting architectural legacy. His reign saw Catholic emancipation in 1829, conceded reluctantly. Obese and in poor health, he died at Windsor in 1830; his only legitimate child having predeceased him, he was succeeded by his brother William IV.
