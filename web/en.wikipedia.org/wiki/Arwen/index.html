Arwen Undómiel is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, appearing in The Lord of the Rings. She is the daughter of Elrond, Lord of Rivendell, and is said to be the most beautiful of the last generation of High Elves, bearing a strong resemblance to Lúthien, the Elven heroine of the First Age, whose story hers partly mirrors.

Biography

Arwen was born in the Third Age, in the year 241. As one of the Half-elven, she shared the choice offered to her father's family: to live as an Elf with immortality, or as a mortal. She first met Aragorn, the heir of Isildur, in Rivendell, and they later plighted their troth on the hill of Cerin Amroth in Lothlórien. Elrond required that Aragorn first become king of Gondor and Arnor before he could marry her. After the War of the Ring, Arwen wed the crowned King Elessar (Aragorn), becoming Queen of the Reunited Kingdom and renouncing her Elven immortality. She bore him a son, Eldarion, and daughters. After Aragorn's death in the year 120 of the Fourth Age, Arwen departed to a fading Lothlórien and died of grief upon Cerin Amroth within a year.

Role in the narrative

Arwen appears only briefly in the main text of The Lord of the Rings; her full story is told in "The Tale of Aragorn and Arwen" in Appendix A. She presents Aragorn with a banner she has woven — the standard of Elendil — and later gives Frodo a white gem, the Evenstar, in token of her place sailing West being yielded to him. Tolkien considered her story an essential part of the work, remarking that it was "part of the essential story" of Aragorn's motivation.

Adaptations

In Peter Jackson's film trilogy (2001–2003), Arwen is played by Liv Tyler and given a substantially expanded, more active role, including rescuing Frodo by riding with him to the Ford of Bruinen — a scene performed by Glorfindel in the novel.
