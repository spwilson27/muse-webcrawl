South African Rugby Union

Founded: 1992 (unified body; predecessors from 1889)

Headquarters: Cape Town, South Africa

Affiliation: World Rugby

National team: Springboks (men), Springbok Women

President: Mark Alexander (from 2016)

The South African Rugby Union (SA Rugby; formerly the South African Rugby Football Union, SARFU) is the governing body for rugby union in South Africa. It administers the national teams — most famously the Springboks — and the country's domestic competitions, including the Currie Cup, first played in 1892 and among the oldest rugby competitions in the world.

History

Rugby was introduced to South Africa in the 19th century, and the white South African Rugby Board was founded in 1889. Under apartheid, rugby was racially segregated, with separate bodies for black, coloured and white players, and the Springboks' international participation became a focus of the sports boycott of South Africa. In 1992, with the end of apartheid under way, the rival bodies merged into a single non-racial union, the SARFU, and South Africa was readmitted to international rugby. The country hosted and won the 1995 Rugby World Cup, an event of great symbolic importance in the new "Rainbow Nation".

Competitions and teams

SA Rugby oversees provincial unions and franchises. South African teams competed in Super Rugby until 2020, after which the leading franchises (Stormers, Bulls, Sharks and Lions) joined the European-based United Rugby Championship, while the Currie Cup remains the premier domestic trophy. The Springboks, four-time Rugby World Cup champions (1995, 2007, 2019, 2023), are the union's flagship team; SA Rugby also runs the Blitzboks sevens team and women's programmes.
