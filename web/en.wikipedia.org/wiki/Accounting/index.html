Accounting

[Image: a ledger book, calculator and financial statements spread on a desk]

Accounting is the process of recording, classifying, summarising and reporting the financial transactions of an organisation so that its performance and position can be judged by managers, investors, lenders and regulators. It has been called 'the language of business'.

Practice and standards

Its foundation is double-entry bookkeeping — every transaction recorded as equal debits and credits — described by Luca Pacioli in 1494 and underpinning the standard financial statements: the balance sheet (assets, liabilities, equity), the income statement (profit or loss) and the cash-flow statement. Financial accounting serves external users under rules such as IFRS (issued by the IASB, used in most countries) or US GAAP; management accounting serves internal decisions — budgets, costs, pricing; and independent auditing verifies that statements give a true and fair view. Large-scale failures of accounting and audit — Enron (2001), WorldCom, Carillion, Wirecard — have repeatedly driven reform of standards and of the corporate reporting system, while tax accounting and forensic accounting form further specialisms.
