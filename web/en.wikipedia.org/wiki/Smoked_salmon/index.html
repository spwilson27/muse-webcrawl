Smoked salmon

Smoked salmon is a preparation of salmon, typically a fillet that has been cured and hot- or cold-smoked. It is a popular ingredient in haute cuisine and is widely eaten in Northern and Eastern Europe, North America and Jewish delicatessen cuisine, where it is classically served on a bagel with cream cheese.

Production

Salmon fillets are first cured in salt or brine, which draws out moisture and seasons the fish; sugar and spices may be added. In cold smoking, the fish is exposed to smoke at roughly 20–30 °C for many hours or days; the salmon is not cooked and retains a silky, raw-like texture. In hot smoking, the fish is smoked at temperatures above about 50 °C so that it cooks, giving a flaky texture. Salmon used is usually Atlantic salmon, much of it farmed in Norway, Scotland, Chile and Canada, though wild Pacific species are also smoked.

Styles

Lox is salmon cured in a salt brine but traditionally not smoked; "Nova" lox originated with Nova Scotia salmon and is milder. Gravlax is the Scandinavian preparation cured with salt, sugar and dill but not smoked. Smoked salmon may also be flavoured with pastrami-style spice rubs, whisky, or beet cure.

Food safety

Cold-smoked salmon carries a small risk of Listeria contamination and parasites such as Anisakis, so commercial production involves strict temperature control and prior freezing; pregnant and immunocompromised people are often advised to avoid it.
