Will (law)

Will
FieldProbate / succession law
MakerTestator
Typical formalitiesIn writing, signed, witnessed

A will, or testament, is a legal document by which a person (the testator) expresses how their property is to be distributed at death and names one or more executors to manage the estate until distribution. If a person dies without a valid will, they are said to die intestate, and their property passes according to the intestacy rules of the applicable jurisdiction.

Requirements

Most jurisdictions require the testator to have legal capacity (typically being of adult age and sound mind), and that the will be in writing, signed by the testator, and attested by witnesses. Some jurisdictions recognise holographic (entirely handwritten) wills. A later amendment is called a codicil. Wills may be revoked by a subsequent will, by marriage in some jurisdictions, or by destruction.

Probate

After death, the will is submitted to a probate court to verify its validity; the executor then collects the assets, pays debts and taxes, and distributes the remainder to beneficiaries. Freedom of testation is broad in common-law countries but limited in many civil-law systems by forced-heirship rules reserving shares for close family.
