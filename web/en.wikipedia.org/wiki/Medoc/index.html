The Médoc is a region of France, well known as a wine growing region, located in the department of Gironde, on the left bank of the Gironde estuary, north of Bordeaux. Its name comes from the Latin in medio aquae — "in the middle of the waters" — referring to its position between the Atlantic Ocean and the Gironde estuary.

The Médoc peninsula extends northwest from Bordeaux between the Atlantic coast and the estuary. The northern part, the Bas-Médoc, is lower and marshier, while the southern Haut-Médoc contains the gravel terraces that produce the region's most celebrated wines.

Wine

The Médoc is one of the most famous red wine regions in the world. The appellations of the Haut-Médoc include Margaux, Saint-Julien, Pauillac, and Saint-Estèphe, home to many of the châteaux classified in the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855, including Château Lafite Rothschild, Château Latour, Château Margaux, and Château Mouton Rothschild. Cabernet Sauvignon predominates on the gravel soils of the left bank, blended with Merlot, Cabernet Franc, and Petit Verdot.

Geography

The peninsula's Atlantic coast is lined with sandy beaches and pine forest (the Landes forest extends into its western part), and the coastal lakes include Lac d'Hourtin-Carcans, the largest freshwater lake entirely in France. The Cordouan lighthouse stands at the mouth of the estuary.
