The Brazilian Armed Forces (Portuguese: Forças Armadas Brasileiras) are the unified military forces of the Federative Republic of Brazil. They consist of three service branches: the Brazilian Army, the Brazilian Navy (which includes the Marine Corps and Naval Aviation), and the Brazilian Air Force. They are the largest armed forces in Latin America and among the largest in the world by number of personnel.

History

The armed forces trace their origins to the wars of independence from Portugal in the 1820s. Brazil's military fought in the Cisplatine War, the Paraguayan War (1864–1870) — the largest conflict in South American history — and contributed an expeditionary force and naval and air units to the Allied cause in the Second World War, fighting in Italy in 1944–45. The armed forces ruled Brazil directly during the military dictatorship of 1964–1985; since democratisation they have been formally subordinated to civilian authority through the Ministry of Defence, created in 1999.

Roles and deployments

Besides external defence, the forces are tasked with guaranteeing law and order at the request of constitutional authorities, border patrol in the Amazon, support to elections, disaster relief, and civic-action programmes such as health care in remote regions. Brazil has been a major contributor to United Nations peacekeeping, notably commanding the military component of MINUSTAH in Haiti (2004–2017) and the UN maritime task force off Lebanon.

Conscription

Military service is compulsory for males; conscripts typically serve 9 to 12 months, though the great majority of eligible young men are not selected because volunteers exceed requirements.
