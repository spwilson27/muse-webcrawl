Chloé Zhao

Chloé Zhao (Chinese: 赵婷, Zhào Tíng; born 31 March 1982) is a Chinese-born filmmaker. She is known for her naturalistic independent films set in the American West, and became the second woman — and the first woman of colour — to win the Academy Award for Best Director, for Nomadland (2020), which also won Best Picture.

Career

Born in Beijing, Zhao was educated in China, England, and the United States, studying politics at Mount Holyoke College before turning to film at New York University. Her debut feature, Songs My Brothers Taught Me (2015), was filmed on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation. The Rider (2017), starring non-professional actors playing versions of themselves, won wide critical acclaim.

Nomadland (2020), starring Frances McDormand alongside real-life nomads, won the Golden Lion at Venice and three Academy Awards. Zhao then directed the Marvel film Eternals (2021). Her later work includes Hamnet (2025), adapted from Maggie O'Farrell's novel about Shakespeare's family, co-written with O'Farrell.
