Carboplatin

Trade name: Paraplatin
Type: Platinum-based chemotherapy agent
Formula: C₆H₁₂N₂O₄Pt
Administration: Intravenous
First approved: 1989 (US)

Carboplatin, sold under the trade name Paraplatin among others, is a chemotherapy medication used to treat a number of forms of cancer, including ovarian cancer, lung cancer, head and neck cancer, brain cancer and neuroblastoma. It is a platinum-based alkylating-like agent related to cisplatin, developed to retain cisplatin's antitumour activity while reducing its kidney, nerve and gastrointestinal toxicity. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism of action

Like cisplatin, carboplatin binds to DNA, forming intra- and interstrand crosslinks that distort the double helix, block replication and transcription, and trigger cell death in rapidly dividing cells. Its cyclobutanedicarboxylate leaving group reacts much more slowly than cisplatin's chlorides, which accounts for its different side-effect profile and the need for substantially higher doses.

Medical use and dosing

Carboplatin is given by intravenous infusion, usually in combination regimens — for example with paclitaxel in ovarian and non-small-cell lung cancer. Unusually among chemotherapy drugs, its dose is calculated not by body surface area but by target exposure (area under the concentration–time curve, AUC) using the Calvert formula, which takes into account the patient's kidney function as measured by glomerular filtration rate.

Side effects

The principal dose-limiting toxicity is myelosuppression, especially thrombocytopenia (low platelets) and neutropenia, which increase the risk of bleeding and infection. Compared with cisplatin, carboplatin causes much less nausea and vomiting (though it is still emetogenic), kidney damage and peripheral nerve damage, but more bone-marrow suppression. Allergic reactions can occur, particularly after many cycles.
