Singular value decomposition

Singular value decomposition
FieldLinear algebra
StatementAny real or complex matrix M factors as M = UΣV*
FactorsU, V unitary; Σ diagonal with non-negative entries (singular values)
Key theoremEckart–Young: truncated SVD is the best low-rank approximation
ApplicationsPrincipal component analysis, pseudoinverse, image compression, recommender systems, signal processing

In linear algebra, the singular value decomposition (SVD) is a factorisation of a real or complex matrix into a rotation, followed by a scaling, followed by another rotation. Specifically, any m×n matrix M can be written as M = UΣV*, where U and V are unitary (orthogonal, in the real case) matrices and Σ is a diagonal matrix whose non-negative entries σ1 ≥ σ2 ≥ … are the singular values of M. The columns of U and V are the left- and right-singular vectors.

The SVD pictured geometrically: a unit circle is rotated, stretched along the axes by the singular values, and rotated again.

Properties and intuition

Geometrically, the SVD says that any linear transformation maps the unit sphere to an ellipsoid whose semi-axis lengths are the singular values. The number of non-zero singular values equals the rank of M, and the singular values are the square roots of the eigenvalues of M*M. Unlike the eigendecomposition, the SVD exists for every matrix, including rectangular and singular ones, making it the workhorse of applied linear algebra.

Applications

Truncating the SVD to the k largest singular values gives, by the Eckart–Young theorem, the best rank-k approximation to M in both the Frobenius and spectral norms — the basis of image compression and latent semantic analysis of text. The SVD yields the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse for solving least-squares problems, underlies principal component analysis in statistics, and is used in signal processing, control theory, recommender systems and the computation of matrix condition numbers.
