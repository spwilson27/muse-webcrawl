Funny Girl is a musical with music by Jule Styne, lyrics by Bob Merrill and a book by Isobel Lennart. The semi-biographical plot is based on the life and career of Broadway and film star Fanny Brice, and her stormy relationship with entrepreneur and gambler Nicky Arnstein. The original 1964 Broadway production starred Barbra Streisand in the role that made her a star.

Productions

Produced by Ray Stark — Brice's son-in-law — the show opened on Broadway at the Winter Garden Theatre on 26 March 1964, after a troubled tryout period, and ran for 1,348 performances. Streisand's performance earned nightly standing ovations, though the Tony Award for Best Actress went to Carol Channing for Hello, Dolly!; the production received eight Tony nominations but won none. Streisand reprised the role in the 1966 London production.

A major London revival at the Menier Chocolate Factory in 2015, starring Sheridan Smith, transferred to the Savoy Theatre in the West End. The first Broadway revival opened at the August Wilson Theatre in April 2022 starring Beanie Feldstein, who was succeeded by Lea Michele in September 2022 to wide acclaim; the production ran until September 2023 and toured North America.

Synopsis

The musical follows Fanny Brice from her beginnings as an awkward but determined teenage performer in New York vaudeville, through her rise as a star of Florenz Ziegfeld's Follies, to her troubled marriage to the charming gambler Nick Arnstein, whose financial schemes and imprisonment destroy the relationship even as Fanny's career flourishes.

Songs

The score includes "People", "Don't Rain on My Parade", "I'm the Greatest Star", "I'd Rather Be Blue over You", "Roller Skate Rag", "His Love Makes Me Beautiful", "Sadie, Sadie" and "Who Are You Now?". "People" became one of Streisand's signature songs and a standard.

Film adaptation

The 1968 film adaptation, directed by William Wyler, starred Streisand (winning the Academy Award for Best Actress, in a tie with Katharine Hepburn) and Omar Sharif as Arnstein. A sequel, Funny Lady, followed in 1975.
