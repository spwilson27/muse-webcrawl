Stack interchange

A stack interchange is a type of grade-separated highway interchange in which left-turning movements are handled by semi-directional ramps that cross over or under the main carriageways. The most common form is the four-level stack, where crossing left-turn ramps pass at different heights between the two through roads.

Design

Stack interchanges avoid the weaving movements of cloverleaf interchanges and can handle high traffic volumes in all directions. Their disadvantages are a large footprint, high construction cost, and the height of the structures, which can exceed 20 metres in a four-level stack.

Examples

Famous examples include the Judge Harry Pregerson Interchange in Los Angeles and the Kennedy Interchange in Louisville, Kentucky, nicknamed "Spaghetti Junction". Three-level and partial variants are used where land or funds are limited.
