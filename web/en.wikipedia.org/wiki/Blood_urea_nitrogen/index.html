Blood urea nitrogen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Blood urea nitrogen
Other namesBUN, serum urea nitrogen
Reference rangeabout 7–20 mg/dL (2.5–7.1 mmol/L urea)
PurposeAssessment of kidney function

Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is a medical test that measures the amount of urea nitrogen in the blood. Urea is produced in the liver as the end product of protein metabolism and is excreted by the kidneys, so the BUN test is used as a rough marker of renal function alongside creatinine.

Interpretation

An elevated BUN (azotemia) may result from kidney failure, dehydration, heart failure, high protein intake or gastrointestinal bleeding. A low BUN can occur in liver failure, malnutrition or pregnancy. The BUN-to-creatinine ratio helps distinguish prerenal causes (ratio above about 20:1) from intrinsic kidney disease.
