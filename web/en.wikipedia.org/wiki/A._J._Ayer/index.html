Sir Alfred Jules "Freddie" Ayer (29 October 1910 – 27 June 1989) was an English philosopher known for his promotion of logical positivism, particularly through his books Language, Truth and Logic (1936) and The Problem of Knowledge (1956).

Life and career

Ayer was educated at Eton and Christ Church, Oxford. After a period in Vienna in 1932–33, where he absorbed the ideas of the Vienna Circle, he returned to Oxford and published Language, Truth and Logic at the age of 24, introducing logical positivism to the English-speaking world. He served in the Welsh Guards and SOE during the Second World War, then held chairs at University College London (1946–1959) and as Wykeham Professor of Logic at Oxford (1959–1978). He was knighted in 1970.

Philosophy

Ayer championed the verification principle: a statement is meaningful only if it is either analytic or empirically verifiable. On this basis he argued that most metaphysical and theological claims are literally nonsensical. In ethics he defended emotivism, holding that moral judgements express feelings rather than stating facts. He also worked extensively on epistemology, the problem of induction and the philosophy of perception, engaging in a celebrated debate with J. L. Austin.

Public life

A flamboyant public intellectual, Ayer was a committed atheist and humanist, a supporter of left-wing causes, and a frequent broadcaster. In 1988 he reported a near-death experience after choking, which he discussed with characteristic caution in print without abandoning his atheism.
