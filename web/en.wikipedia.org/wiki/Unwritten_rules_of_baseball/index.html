Unwritten rules of baseball

Baseball's code of conduct governs player behaviour beyond the official rulebook

The unwritten rules of baseball are a set of conventions and codes of conduct that players, managers, and coaches in baseball are expected to follow, even though they do not appear in the official rulebook. Enforcement is typically carried out by the players themselves, most famously through retaliatory pitches — a pitcher deliberately throwing at or near a batter who has violated the code.

Examples

Commonly cited unwritten rules include: do not bunt to break up a no-hitter; do not steal bases or swing at a 3–0 pitch when your team holds a large lead late in a game; do not admire a home run or flip your bat excessively; do not walk across the pitcher's mound when returning to the dugout; and do not talk about a no-hitter in progress. Players who violate these norms may face bench-clearing confrontations or internal discipline from their own teammates and managers.

Debate

The unwritten rules have been much debated in recent decades. Critics argue they suppress self-expression and fun, particularly for younger players and players from Latin American baseball cultures where celebratory play is customary. Defenders argue the code preserves respect for opponents and the game's traditions. Incidents such as Fernando Tatís Jr.'s grand slam on a 3–0 count in 2020 reignited the discussion.
