Curriculum

[Image: A classroom timetable and textbooks illustrating a school curriculum]

In education, a curriculum (plural: curricula) is the totality of student experiences that occur in the educational process: the subjects taught, their content, the sequence in which they are presented, the learning activities planned, and the standards or outcomes students are expected to reach. The word derives from the Latin curriculum, a running course or race track.

Definitions and dimensions

Curriculum theorists distinguish the intended curriculum (official documents and standards), the implemented curriculum (what teachers actually teach), and the attained curriculum (what students learn). The hidden curriculum refers to the unwritten norms, values and beliefs transmitted by school life. Ralph Tyler's 1949 rationale — objectives, experiences, organisation, evaluation — remains an influential framework for curriculum design.

National curricula

Many countries prescribe a national curriculum: England's National Curriculum, introduced in 1988, sets programmes of study for maintained schools; other systems, such as those in the United States, rely on state standards like the Common Core. University curricula define degree requirements, while professional curricula in fields such as medicine are shaped by accreditation bodies.

Curriculum studies

Curriculum studies is an academic field concerned with what should be taught and why, drawing on philosophy, sociology, and history. Thinkers such as John Dewey emphasised experience and the child's interests, while later theorists critiqued curricula as instruments of cultural reproduction and social selection.
