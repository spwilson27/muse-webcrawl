The four young envoys of the Tenshō embassy

The Tenshō embassy (Japanese: 天正の使節, named after the Tenshō era) was an embassy sent by the Japanese Christian daimyō Ōtomo Sōrin to the Pope and the kings of Europe in 1582, organised by the Jesuit missionary Alessandro Valignano. It was the first Japanese diplomatic mission to Europe.

The mission

Valignano selected four Christian Japanese teenagers, around thirteen years old, as ambassadors: Itō Mancio (representing Ōtomo Sōrin), Chijiiwa Miguel, Hara Martinho and Nakaura Julião. Accompanied by Jesuits, they sailed from Nagasaki in February 1582, travelling via Macau, Malacca, Goa and around the Cape of Good Hope to Lisbon, which they reached in August 1584. They were received by King Philip II of Spain in Madrid and then travelled to Rome.

Audiences in Rome

On 23 March 1585 the envoys were received in audience by Pope Gregory XIII in a celebrated public consistory; after his death weeks later, they also attended the coronation of Pope Sixtus V, an honour extended to few. They toured Italian cities including Venice, Milan and Florence, were fêted by nobles, and were presented with gifts — among them a printing press and musical instruments — to carry back to Japan.

Return and aftermath

The embassy returned to Nagasaki in July 1590, bringing the first movable-type printing press to Japan, used by the Jesuit mission press. By then the political climate had changed: Toyotomi Hideyoshi had issued an edict expelling missionaries in 1587. The four envoys were ordained as the first Japanese Jesuit priests in 1608. Nakaura Julião was later martyred in 1633 during the Tokugawa persecution of Christians; he was beatified in 2008.
