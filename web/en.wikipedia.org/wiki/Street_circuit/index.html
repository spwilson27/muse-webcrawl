Street circuit

Racing on a temporary street circuit, with concrete barriers and catch fencing lining public roads.

A street circuit is a motorsport racing circuit composed of temporarily closed-off public roads of a city, town or village, used in motor races such as those of Formula One, IndyCar, Formula E, and touring car series. Races held on such circuits are often called street races. The circuit's corners, straights and chicanes follow the existing street layout, though the route is sometimes designed with racing in mind, and temporary facilities — pit buildings, grandstands, barriers and debris fencing — are erected for the event and removed afterward.

Characteristics

Street circuits are typically narrow, with little run-off area: concrete walls line the track, so mistakes are punished heavily, and overtaking is often difficult. Surfaces are bumpier and offer less grip than purpose-built tracks, and may include manhole covers, painted lines, and changes of camber. Because the layout is constrained by the street grid, many street circuits feature slow, tight corners and 90-degree turns.

Promoters favor street circuits because they bring racing to city-centre audiences and tourist landmarks, generate economic impact for the host city, and do not require a permanent facility. Critics point to disruption for residents, high setup costs, and processional racing.

Notable street circuits

  • Circuit de Monaco — home of the Monaco Grand Prix since 1929; the most famous street circuit in the world.
  • Marina Bay Street Circuit — Singapore Grand Prix, Formula One's first night race (2008).
  • Circuit Gilles Villeneuve (Montreal) and Albert Park (Melbourne) — semi-permanent parkland circuits with street-circuit character.
  • Long Beach Street Circuit — in use since 1975, now an IndyCar venue.
  • Adelaide Street Circuit — hosted the Australian Grand Prix 1985–1995.
  • Macau Guia Circuit — venue of the Macau Grand Prix since 1954.
  • Baku City Circuit — Azerbaijan Grand Prix since 2016, noted for its very long main straight and narrow castle section.
  • Las Vegas Strip Circuit and the Miami International Autodrome — additions of the 2020s.
  • Formula E has raced extensively on street circuits in cities such as Berlin (Tempelhof), London, Rome, and Diriyah.

Safety

Modern street circuits are built to the same FIA grading standards as permanent tracks, using concrete block walls topped with debris fencing, temporary kerbs, and carefully placed marshal posts and medical access. Drainage covers must be secured, and road furniture removed or protected.
