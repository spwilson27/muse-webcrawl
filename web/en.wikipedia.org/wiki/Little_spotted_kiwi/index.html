Little spotted kiwi

The little spotted kiwi (Apteryx owenii), also known as the little grey kiwi or kiwi pukupuku, is the smallest species of kiwi. It is a flightless ratite endemic to New Zealand. Once widespread on both main islands, it survived extinction largely because a small population was established on Kapiti Island in the early 20th century.

Description

Little spotted kiwi weigh between about 0.9 and 1.9 kg and stand roughly 35–45 cm tall, with females larger than males. Their plumage is pale greyish brown, finely mottled or barred with white, giving the "spotted" appearance. Like other kiwi they are nocturnal, flightless, and have nostrils at the tip of a long bill used to probe soil and leaf litter for invertebrates.

Distribution and habitat

By about 1900 the species had disappeared from the North Island and survived in the South Island only in dwindling numbers, threatened by introduced stoats, dogs and other predators. Between 1912 and 1923 a handful of birds were released on Kapiti Island, a predator-free sanctuary off the Wellington coast; from as few as five founders the population grew to around 1,200 or more and became the species' stronghold. Birds have since been reintroduced to other predator-free islands and fenced mainland sanctuaries, including Zealandia in Wellington, Anchor Island, Chalky Island and the Marlborough Sounds.

Behaviour and breeding

The species is monogamous and territorial. The female lays one or two very large eggs in a burrow or hollow log; unusually among kiwi, both parents share incubation, which lasts around two to two and a half months. Chicks are precocial and leave the burrow within about a week. Total population is estimated at roughly 1,500–2,000 birds and slowly increasing thanks to translocations.
