The Cambodian–Vietnamese War was an armed conflict between the Socialist Republic of Vietnam and Democratic Kampuchea, the Cambodian state ruled by the Khmer Rouge under Pol Pot. Following years of border clashes and Khmer Rouge massacres of Vietnamese civilians, Vietnam launched a full-scale invasion on 25 December 1978, captured Phnom Penh on 7 January 1979, and installed a friendly government, the People's Republic of Kampuchea. Vietnamese forces then occupied Cambodia for a decade, fighting a guerrilla insurgency by the Khmer Rouge and its coalition partners, before withdrawing in 1989.

Background

Relations between the Vietnamese communists and the Khmer Rouge, uneasy allies during the Vietnam War, deteriorated rapidly after 1975. Democratic Kampuchea pursued territorial claims in the Mekong Delta (which it called Kampuchea Krom), and Khmer Rouge forces repeatedly attacked Vietnamese border villages — most notoriously at Ba Chúc in April 1978, where some 3,000 civilians were massacred. Vietnam, backed by a 1978 treaty with the Soviet Union, prepared a decisive response, while Cambodia relied on support from China.

The invasion

On 25 December 1978, roughly 150,000 Vietnamese troops, accompanied by the Cambodian United Front for National Salvation organised under Heng Samrin, invaded Cambodia. The Khmer Rouge army collapsed within two weeks; Phnom Penh fell on 7 January 1979, and Pol Pot's forces fled to the Thai border. The invasion ended the Cambodian genocide, which had killed an estimated 1.5 to 2 million people.

Occupation and insurgency

Vietnam and the new People's Republic of Kampuchea were diplomatically isolated: most of the world continued to recognise the Khmer Rouge-led Coalition Government of Democratic Kampuchea as Cambodia's legitimate government at the United Nations. China attacked northern Vietnam in the brief Sino-Vietnamese War of February–March 1979 in retaliation. Through the 1980s, Vietnam maintained over 100,000 troops in Cambodia against a guerrilla coalition supported by China, the United States, and ASEAN states, with refugee camps along the Thai border becoming centres of resistance.

Withdrawal and settlement

Facing economic exhaustion and Soviet retrenchment, Vietnam withdrew its last forces in September 1989. The Paris Peace Accords of October 1991 established a UN transitional authority (UNTAC), leading to elections in 1993 and the restoration of the Cambodian monarchy.
