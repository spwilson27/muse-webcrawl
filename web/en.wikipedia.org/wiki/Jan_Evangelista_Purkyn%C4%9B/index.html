Jan Evangelista Purkyně

Born17 December 1787, Libochovice, Bohemia
Died28 July 1869, Prague
FieldsPhysiology, anatomy, histology
Known forPurkinje cells, Purkinje fibres, protoplasm

Jan Evangelista Purkyně (also written Purkinje; 17 December 1787 – 28 July 1869) was a Czech anatomist and physiologist, regarded as one of the founders of modern physiology and histology. In 1839 he founded the world's first department of physiology at the University of Breslau (Wrocław) in Prussia.

Discoveries

Purkyně's name is attached to several structures he described: Purkinje cells, the large neurons of the cerebellar cortex (1837); Purkinje fibres, the specialised conducting tissue of the heart (1839); and the Purkinje effect, the shift in the eye's sensitivity toward blue at low light. He coined the term "protoplasm" for the living substance of cells, recognised the individuality of fingerprints, and published pioneering work on vision, vertigo and the effects of drugs such as belladonna and digitalis, often experimenting on himself.

Later life

From 1850 he held the chair of physiology in Prague, where he also promoted Czech national culture and science writing in Czech. He is buried at Vyšehrad Cemetery.
