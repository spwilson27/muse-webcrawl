Clavier-Übung

Clavier-Übung ("Keyboard Practice") is the collective title given to a series of four collections of keyboard music composed by Johann Sebastian Bach, published between 1726 and 1741. The title, in Bach's day, simply indicated keyboard exercises, following the precedent of earlier composers such as Johann Kuhnau.

The four parts

Part I comprises the six Partitas, BWV 825–830 (1726–1730). Part II (1735) contains the Italian Concerto and the French Overture. Part III (1739), for organ, includes the Prelude and Fugue in E-flat major and the chorale preludes of the so-called German Organ Mass. Part IV (1741) is the Goldberg Variations, BWV 988.

Significance

Together the collections survey the principal keyboard genres of the Baroque era — suite, concerto, overture, liturgical organ music and aria with variations — and rank among Bach's most important published works.
