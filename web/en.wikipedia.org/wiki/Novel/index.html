Novel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A tall stack of hardback novels of many sizes and colours beside a reading lamp]

A novel is a long work of narrative fiction, typically written in prose and published as a book. It is one of the most popular forms of literature, characterised by extended exploration of character, plot and setting, and is distinguished from the novella and short story mainly by length.

Precursors include ancient works such as The Tale of Genji (11th-century Japan) and Miguel de Cervantes' Don Quixote (1605/1615), often called the first modern novel. The form rose in 18th-century England with Defoe, Richardson and Fielding, and reached extraordinary range in the 19th century with Jane Austen, the Brontës, Charles Dickens, Leo Tolstoy and Fyodor Dostoevsky.
