Zeebrugge Raid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zeebrugge Raid

Date: 23 April 1918

Location: Port of Zeebrugge, Belgium

Combatants: United Kingdom vs German Empire

Commander: Vice-Admiral Roger Keyes

Result: German port temporarily obstructed; limited strategic effect

The Zeebrugge Raid (also called the Battle of Zeebrugge) was a British Royal Navy operation on St George's Day, 23 April 1918, during the First World War. Its objective was to neutralize the German-held Belgian port of Zeebrugge — used as a base for U-boats and destroyers raiding the English Channel — by sinking blockships in the entrance to the Bruges ship canal.

The operation

Planned by Vice-Admiral Roger Keyes of the Dover Patrol, the raid involved some 75 vessels. The obsolete cruiser HMS Vindictive, supported by the ferries Iris II and Daffodil, stormed the harbor mole under cover of a smoke screen to land Royal Marines and silence the German guns, while the old cruisers Thetis, Intrepid, and Iphigenia, filled with concrete, were to be scuttled in the canal mouth. An old submarine packed with explosives was blown up beneath the viaduct connecting the mole to the shore. A simultaneous attack was made on Ostend.

Outcome

Two of the blockships were sunk in the canal entrance and the third grounded short of it, partially obstructing the channel; German forces dredged a passage around them within a few weeks, so the raid's practical effect on U-boat operations was limited. British casualties were heavy — more than 200 killed and some 400 wounded — but the raid was celebrated in Britain as a great feat of arms, and eight Victoria Crosses were awarded.
