Bill Clinton

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

William Jefferson Clinton (born William Jefferson Blythe III, 19 August 1946) is an American politician who served as the 42nd president of the United States (1993–2001). A Democrat from Arkansas, where he was governor for most of 1979–1992, he defeated incumbent George H. W. Bush in 1992 on a “New Democrat” programme of welfare reform, free trade (NAFTA) and fiscal discipline, and presided over the longest peacetime economic expansion in American history, ending his term with budget surpluses and high approval ratings.

His presidency was defined politically by conflict with a Republican Congress after 1994 (including a government shutdown and welfare reform in 1996) and by scandal: the House impeached him in December 1998 on charges of perjury and obstruction of justice arising from his denial, in a deposition and testimony, of an affair with White House intern Monica Lewinsky; the Senate acquitted him in February 1999 (votes of 45–55 and 50–50, short of the two-thirds required). After office he founded the Clinton Foundation and became an elder statesman of his party; his wife Hillary Clinton served as Secretary of State and was the Democratic presidential nominee in 2016.

Foreign and domestic record

Clinton's foreign policy combined trade globalisation, NATO enlargement, intervention in the Balkans (Bosnia 1995, Kosovo 1999) and an unsuccessful attempt at a final Israeli–Palestinian settlement (Camp David, 2000). Domestic legislation included the Brady Bill and assault-weapons ban (1994), the crime bill of 1994 — later criticised for contributing to mass incarceration — and the repeal (1999) of Glass–Steagall banking separation, debated in accounts of the 2008 financial crisis.
