Human foot

The human foot is a highly developed, biomechanically complex structure that serves as the foundation of the human body in standing, walking, running and jumping. Each foot contains 26 bones — 52 in both feet, about a quarter of all the bones in the body — together with 33 joints and more than a hundred muscles, tendons and ligaments.

Structure

The bones are grouped into the seven tarsals of the hindfoot and midfoot (including the talus and the calcaneus, or heel bone), the five metatarsals of the forefoot, and fourteen phalanges in the toes. The foot's arches — the medial and lateral longitudinal arches and the transverse arch — act as springs, absorbing shock and storing elastic energy during gait, supported by the plantar fascia and the intrinsic muscles of the sole. The big toe (hallux) bears much of the load in push-off; its loss markedly impairs balance and walking.

Function and evolution

The arched, rigid-lever foot is a distinctive human adaptation to habitual bipedalism, contrasting with the grasping, flat feet of other apes. During walking the foot alternates between a flexible shock-absorbing adapter at heel strike and a stiff lever at toe-off. Common conditions affecting the foot include plantar fasciitis, bunions (hallux valgus), flat feet (pes planus), stress fractures and diabetic foot ulcers. Footwear, prosthetics and orthotics are designed around the foot's anatomy, and footprint and gait analysis are used in biomechanics and forensics.
