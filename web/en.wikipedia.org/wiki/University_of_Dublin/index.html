University of Dublin

The University of Dublin (Irish: Ollscoil Átha Cliath), founded in 1592, is the degree-awarding body for Trinity College Dublin, its sole constituent college. It is Ireland's oldest university and was modelled on Oxford and Cambridge.

University of Dublin
Established: 1592
Location: Dublin, Ireland
Constituent college: Trinity College Dublin
Chancellor: head of the university

History

Trinity College was founded by charter of Queen Elizabeth I in 1592. The university formally is the corporation of scholars; its Senate confers degrees. Graduates of the university elect three members of Seanad Éireann, the Irish Senate.

Degrees and governance

The Chancellor is the head of the university, elected by the Senate. Degrees follow the Latin naming of Oxbridge, including MA granted by standing. The university's alumni include Jonathan Swift, Oscar Wilde, Bram Stoker, Samuel Beckett, and Ernest Walton.
