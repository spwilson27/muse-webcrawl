Omar Nelson Bradley (February 12, 1893 – April 8, 1981) was a senior officer of the United States Army during and after World War II, rising to the rank of General of the Army. Known to his troops as "the soldier's general" for his modest, caring style, he commanded the largest body of American soldiers ever to serve under one field commander.

Early life

Born in rural Missouri, Bradley graduated from West Point in 1915 in the famous "class the stars fell on". He served on the Mexican border and in the interwar army, studying at the Infantry School and the Command and General Staff School and teaching at West Point, before commanding the Officer Candidate School at Fort Benning early in World War II.

World War II

Bradley took command of II Corps in Tunisia in 1943, led it in the invasion of Sicily, and then commanded the U.S. First Army in the Normandy landings of June 1944. From August 1944 he led the 12th Army Group — over 1.3 million men — through the breakout from Normandy, the liberation of Paris, the Battle of the Bulge and the advance into Germany.

Postwar career

After heading the Veterans Administration, Bradley became Chief of Staff of the Army in 1948 and the first Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff in 1949, serving through the Korean War. In 1950 he was promoted to General of the Army, the last American officer to hold five-star rank. His memoir A Soldier's Story (1951) is a classic of the war literature.
