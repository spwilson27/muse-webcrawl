The Hales Trophy is a large silver trophy awarded to the ship with the fastest transatlantic crossing. It was created in 1934 by British politician Harold Keates Hales to give the informal contest for the Blue Riband of the Atlantic a formal, tangible prize.

History

For decades, shipping lines had competed unofficially for the Blue Riband, recognition as the fastest liner on the North Atlantic route. Dissatisfied with the lack of a formal award, Hales commissioned a four-foot, nearly 100-pound sterling silver trophy surmounted by figures representing Speed and the Atlantic. The rules required the fastest average speed in both directions under commercial conditions.

Holders

The Italian liner Rex was the first recipient. The trophy then passed to the French Normandie, the Cunard liner Queen Mary, and in 1952 to the American liner SS United States, which crossed in 3 days, 10 hours and 40 minutes at an average of 35.59 knots. Her record for a commercial passenger liner has never been broken, and the United States retained the trophy permanently, as no subsequent passenger liner has surpassed her speed. The trophy itself was displayed aboard the ship and later held by maritime institutions.
