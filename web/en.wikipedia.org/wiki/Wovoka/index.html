Wovoka (c. 1856 – 1932), also known as Jack Wilson, was a Northern Paiute religious leader who founded the Ghost Dance movement of 1889–1890, a spiritual revival that spread rapidly among Native American peoples of the western United States.

Early life

Wovoka was born in the Smith Valley area of western Nevada, the son of a Paiute medicine man. As a young man he worked on the ranch of David Wilson, taking the name Jack Wilson and gaining exposure to Christian teachings, which blended with Paiute shamanic traditions in his later preaching.

The revelation

On 1 January 1889, during a solar eclipse and while ill with fever, Wovoka reported a vision: he had died, traveled to heaven, and seen God and the resurrected dead living in happiness. He returned with a message that if Native people lived in peace, worked, abstained from lying and fighting, and performed a round dance at intervals, the dead would return, the buffalo would come back, and the world would be renewed.

Spread of the movement

Delegations from many tribes traveled to Nevada to hear Wovoka, and emissaries carried the Ghost Dance across the Plains. Among the Lakota, the dance took on a more urgent, sometimes militant character, and its spread alarmed U.S. authorities, contributing to the tensions that culminated in the killing of Sitting Bull and the Wounded Knee Massacre of December 1890. Wovoka himself preached peace and was dismayed by the violence.

Later life

Wovoka lived quietly in Nevada after the movement faded, working occasionally and continuing to receive visitors and letters from followers. He died in 1932 at Schurz, Nevada, and is buried in the Paiute cemetery there.
