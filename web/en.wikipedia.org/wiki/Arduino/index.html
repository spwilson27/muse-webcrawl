Arduino is an Italian open-source hardware and software company, project, and user community that designs and manufactures single-board microcontrollers and microcontroller kits for building digital devices. Its products are licensed under open licenses, and its hardware reference designs are distributed under a Creative Commons license, so anyone may manufacture compatible boards.

Arduino boards read inputs — light on a sensor, a finger on a button, or a message — and turn them into outputs such as activating a motor, switching on an LED, or publishing data online. The platform is programmed through the Arduino Integrated Development Environment (IDE) using a simplified dialect of C++ based on the Wiring framework.

History

The Arduino project began in 2005 at the Interaction Design Institute Ivrea in Italy, as a tool for students without a background in electronics and programming. The name comes from the Bar di Re Arduino in Ivrea, named after Arduin of Ivrea, a medieval king of Italy, where the founders met. The first board was assembled in 2005; over the following years Arduino became one of the most influential platforms in the maker movement.

In 2015 a trademark dispute between two factions of the company led to a split — Arduino LLC and Arduino SRL — and the temporary use of the "Genuino" brand outside the United States. The dispute was settled in 2016 and the parties were reunified.

Hardware

The most widely known board is the Arduino Uno, based on the ATmega328P microcontroller, with 14 digital input/output pins, 6 analogue inputs, and a USB connection. Other boards include the Nano, Mega, Leonardo, Due, and the Portenta family. The standard programming interface uses a bootloader preloaded on the chip, allowing sketches to be uploaded over USB without an external programmer.

Software

The Arduino IDE supports the languages C and C++ using special rules of code organisation: a sketch requires only two functions, setup() and loop(). A large ecosystem of libraries written by the community extends the platform to displays, motors, sensors, networking, and the Internet of Things. Arduino later introduced a cloud platform, a command-line interface, and a professional IDE (IDE 2.0).
