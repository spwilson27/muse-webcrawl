Bodhi (Sanskrit and Pali: बोधि, "awakening") is the understanding and wisdom possessed by a Buddha — the insight into the true nature of reality that liberates one from saṃsāra, the cycle of rebirth and suffering. In English it is commonly translated as "enlightenment." According to Buddhist tradition, Siddhartha Gautama attained bodhi while meditating beneath a sacred fig tree at Bodh Gaya, thereafter known as the Bodhi Tree.

Meaning

The word derives from the Sanskrit root budh-, "to wake, to know" — the same root as "Buddha," "the Awakened One." Bodhi is not mere intellectual knowledge but a direct, transformative realisation of the Four Noble Truths, dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), impermanence (anicca), and non-self (anattā), accompanied by the destruction of the defilements (greed, hatred, and delusion) and the attainment of nirvana.

Levels of awakening

Traditions distinguish degrees and kinds of awakening: the awakening of the śrāvaka (disciple, an arhat), of the pratyekabuddha (solitary buddha), and the perfect awakening (samyak-saṃbodhi) of a fully enlightened Buddha. Mahayana Buddhism adds the bodhisattva path, in which the aspiration to awakening (bodhicitta) is cultivated over many lifetimes. The event of the Buddha's awakening is commemorated on Bodhi Day, traditionally observed on 8 December in Japan and on the full moon of Vesak elsewhere.

Bodhi tree

The Bodhi Tree at the Mahabodhi Temple in Bodh Gaya, Bihar, marks the spot of the Buddha's awakening; the present tree is said to descend from the original, and cuttings of its Sri Lankan offshoot — the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi at Anuradhapura, planted in 288 BCE — make it among the oldest documented trees in the world.
