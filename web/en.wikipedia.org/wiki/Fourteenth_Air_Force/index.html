Fourteenth Air Force

The Fourteenth Air Force was a numbered air force of the United States Air Force and later a component of the United States Space Force. It was famous during the Second World War as the successor to the China Air Task Force and the American Volunteer Group ("Flying Tigers"), operating in the China Burma India Theater under Major General Claire Lee Chennault.

Second World War

The Fourteenth Air Force was activated in China on 5 March 1943, under Chennault's command. Building on the legend of the Flying Tigers, its fighters and bombers attacked Japanese shipping, airfields, and supply lines across occupied China and Indochina, and defended the Chinese end of the "Hump" airlift route over the Himalayas from India. Despite chronic supply shortages, by 1945 the Fourteenth had achieved air superiority over much of China and claimed the destruction of thousands of Japanese aircraft, bridges, locomotives and river craft.

Post-war and space role

Inactivated after the war, the Fourteenth Air Force was repeatedly reactivated, and from the 1970s onward it became the Air Force's principal space operations command, headquartered at Vandenberg Air Force Base, California, overseeing missile warning, space surveillance, satellite command and control, and spacelift. On 20 December 2019 its headquarters and mission transferred to the newly established United States Space Force, where the organisation was redesignated as the Space Operations Command (SpOC).
