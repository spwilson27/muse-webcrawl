Nalayira Divya Prabandham

The Nalayira Divya Prabandham (Tamil: நாலாயிரத் திவ்வியப் பிரபந்தம், "Four Thousand Divine Hymns") is a collection of 4,000 Tamil devotional verses composed by the twelve Alvars, the poet-saints of the Vaishnava tradition, roughly between the sixth and ninth centuries CE. It is the foundational scripture of the Sri Vaishnava tradition, sometimes called the "Tamil Veda".

Compilation

The hymns, sung in temples and set to music, were gradually lost from common use until the theologian Nathamuni (c. ninth–tenth century) is said to have recovered and arranged them, setting them to music and instituting their recitation at Srirangam. The collection is organised into four parts: the Mudhal Ayiram, Periya Tirumoli portion of the second thousand, Iyarppa, and the Tiruvaymoli of Nammalvar, which forms the final thousand and is regarded as the essence of the Vedas in Tamil.

Content and significance

The poems express intense devotional love (bhakti) for Vishnu and his avatars, especially Krishna and Rama, and celebrate the 108 Divya Desam sacred temples. Tirumangai Alvar is the most prolific contributor, while Andal, the only female Alvar, composed the beloved Tiruppavai. The Prabandham remains central to Sri Vaishnava liturgy, recited daily in temples and homes in Tamil Nadu and beyond.
