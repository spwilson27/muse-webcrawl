Snettisham

Snettisham is a village and civil parish in the English county of Norfolk. It lies on the west coast of Norfolk near The Wash, about 8 km (5 miles) south of Hunstanton and some 17 km (11 miles) north of King's Lynn. The village is built partly of the local ginger-coloured carrstone, giving many buildings a distinctive appearance.

History

Snettisham is famous for the Snettisham Hoard, a series of Iron Age hoards discovered from 1948 onwards containing gold, silver, and bronze torcs and other jewellery associated with the Iceni tribe. The finest find, the Great Torc of twisted gold alloy found in 1950, is now in the British Museum; the hoard is regarded as one of the most important assemblages of Iron Age metalwork in Britain. The village's parish church, St Mary's, is a large fourteenth-century building with a prominent spire, sometimes called a "mini-cathedral."

RSPB Snettisham

The RSPB Snettisham nature reserve, west of the village on the shore of The Wash, comprises lagoons (former gravel pits) and saltmarsh. It is internationally important for wintering wading birds: on high tides, vast flocks of red knot and other waders are pushed off the mudflats onto the lagoons in spectacular displays known as the "Snettisham Spectacular," and tens of thousands of pink-footed geese roost there in winter.

Geography

The village stands near the River Ingol. The coast here is low-lying and protected by sea defences; the coastline of The Wash is designated under multiple conservation schemes for its birdlife. The Ken Hill estate near the village is known for rewilding projects including free-roaming livestock and beaver enclosures.
