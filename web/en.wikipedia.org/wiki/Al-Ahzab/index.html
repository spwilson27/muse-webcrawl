Al-Aḥzāb (Arabic: الأحزاب, "The Combined Forces") is the 33rd chapter (surah) of the Quran, with 73 verses. It is a Medinan surah, named after the confederate armies — the "combined forces" of Meccan Quraysh and their allies — who besieged Medina at the Battle of the Trench in 627 CE.

Content

The surah recalls the siege of Medina and God's deliverance of the believers, then addresses matters arising in the Prophet Muhammad's household and community: the abolition of the pre-Islamic adoption fiction in verse 5 and subsequent verses concerning Zayd ibn Harithah and Zaynab bint Jahsh; rulings on the Prophet's marriages; etiquette of entering the Prophet's houses; the verse of hijab (33:59); and the "verse of purification" (33:33), significant in both Sunni and Shia theology. Verse 56, calling on believers to bless the Prophet, is among the most frequently recited verses.

Historical setting

Revelation is traditionally dated to 5 AH, in the aftermath of the Battle of the Trench and the affair of Banu Qurayza. The surah blends narrative, exhortation and legislation for the early Muslim community.
