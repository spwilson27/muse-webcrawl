The Witch-king of Angmar is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's fantasy novel The Lord of the Rings. He is the Lord of the Nazgûl (Ringwraiths), the chief servant of the Dark Lord Sauron. In origin a king of Men, he was corrupted by one of the nine Rings of Power given to Men, becoming an invisible wraith bound to Sauron's will. During the Third Age he founded the realm of Angmar in the far north of Middle-earth and waged war against the successor kingdoms of Arnor, destroying them over several centuries.

History within the legendarium

The Witch-king's mortal identity is never given by Tolkien. As Lord of Angmar from about T.A. 1300, he destroyed the divided realms of Rhudaur, Cardolan, and finally Arthedain in T.A. 1974. His forces were then defeated at the Battle of Fornost by an army of Gondor under Eärnur, and the Witch-king fled Middle-earth's north; Glorfindel prophesied that "not by the hand of man will he fall." The Witch-king later occupied Minas Ithil, renaming it Minas Morgul, and led the siege of Minas Tirith during the War of the Ring, breaking the gates of the city with the battering ram Grond.

Death

At the Battle of the Pelennor Fields, the Witch-king mortally wounded King Théoden of Rohan. He was confronted by Éowyn, Théoden's niece, disguised as the Rider Dernhelm. The hobbit Meriadoc Brandybuck stabbed the Witch-king behind the knee with a barrow-blade of Westernesse, breaking the spell that knit his sinews and making him vulnerable. Éowyn then drove her sword into the space between his crown and mantle, killing him and fulfilling the prophecy — he was slain by a woman and a hobbit, not by "the hand of man." Critics have noted the parallel with Macbeth, in which the tyrant who cannot be killed "of woman born" is slain by a man delivered by Caesarean birth; Tolkien acknowledged the inspiration but altered it.

Analysis

Scholars have described the Witch-king as among the most frightening figures in the novel, a presence defined by absence — an empty black shape, invisible but for his crown, with a voice like the grating of metal. His role as Sauron's mightiest captain and as a twisted counterpart to Aragorn, the returning king of the line he destroyed, has also been discussed. In Peter Jackson's film adaptation The Return of the King (2003), the Witch-king is depicted with a distinctive crowned helmet and a huge flail, and his duel with Éowyn is one of the film's set pieces.
