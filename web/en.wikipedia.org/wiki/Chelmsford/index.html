Chelmsford

Chelmsford is the county town of Essex, England. It is located in the London commuter belt, approximately 31.6 miles (50.8 km) northeast of central London. Chelmsford is also the name of the local government district administered by Chelmsford City Council, which covers a wider area than the town itself. In December 2011, Chelmsford was granted city status as part of the Diamond Jubilee celebrations of Queen Elizabeth II.

History

The area around Chelmsford has been inhabited since the Neolithic period. A Roman town, Caesaromagus, was built at the site, and many Roman remains have been found there. The modern town's name comes from Ceolmaer's ford, a ford in the River Can. In the Domesday Book of 1086 the town was recorded as "Celmeresfort". Chelmsford grew substantially during the 19th century with the arrival of the railway in 1843.

Chelmsford is widely regarded as the birthplace of radio: in 1899 Guglielmo Marconi opened the world's first radio factory in the town, and in 1920 the first British radio broadcast was made from Chelmsford by Dame Nellie Melba. The town was also the home of the UK's first electrical engineering works, founded by Rookes Crompton.

Geography

Chelmsford lies at the confluence of the River Can and the River Chelmer, just east of the town centre. The city is surrounded by rolling countryside and the commuter villages of the Chelmsford district. The A12 trunk road passes close to the town, linking it to London and Colchester.

Governance

Chelmsford City Council administers the district. The parliamentary constituency of Chelmsford elects one Member of Parliament to the House of Commons. City status was formally conferred in 2012, making Chelmsford one of three cities in Essex alongside Southend-on-Sea and Colchester.

Education

Anglia Ruskin University has a campus in Chelmsford, developed on the site of a former British Rail yard. The town is home to two grammar schools, King Edward VI Grammar School and Chelmsford County High School for Girls, as well as a number of comprehensive schools.

Transport

Chelmsford railway station is on the Great Eastern Main Line, with services to London Liverpool Street taking around 35 minutes. The station is one of the busiest in the East of England outside London. The A12 and A130 provide road links, and Stansted Airport is around 30 minutes away by road.
