Grigor Tatevatsi

Saint Grigor Tatevatsi
Bornc. 1346, Tmkaberd or Vayots Dzor, Armenia
DiedDecember 27, 1409, Tatev Monastery
Venerated inArmenian Apostolic Church

Grigor Tatevatsi (Armenian: Գրիգոր Տաթևացի; c. 1346–1409), also Gregory of Tatev, was an Armenian philosopher, theologian, pedagogue and saint of the Armenian Apostolic Church. He was the last great scholastic of medieval Armenia and the rector of the University of Tatev.

Life

Grigor studied under Hovhannes Vorotnetsi at the universities of Gladzor and Tatev, succeeding his teacher as head of the school at Tatev Monastery in the province of Syunik. Under his leadership Tatev became a major centre of learning, teaching philosophy, theology, grammar, music and manuscript illumination to hundreds of students in a period when Armenia was suffering repeated invasions.

Works

Tatevatsi wrote extensive commentaries on the Bible, Aristotle and other classical authors, as well as original treatises. His most famous works include the Book of Questions (Girkʿ Harcʿmancʿ), an encyclopaedic summa of theology and philosophy; the Book of Sermons, divided into winter and summer volumes; and the Book of Virtues. He also wrote against the union of the Armenian Church with Rome and against the Latinizing tendencies of the time, defending Armenian doctrinal and liturgical tradition.

Legacy

Grigor Tatevatsi is venerated as a saint in the Armenian Church and is regarded as the culminating figure of the Armenian scholastic tradition. He is buried at Tatev Monastery, which remains a place of pilgrimage.
