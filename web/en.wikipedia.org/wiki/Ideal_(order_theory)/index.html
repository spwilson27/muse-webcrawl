Ideal (order theory)

In mathematical order theory, an ideal is a special subset of a partially ordered set (poset). Although the term historically grew out of ring theory, in order theory an ideal is a directed lower set: a non-empty subset I of a poset P such that for every x, y in I there is some z in I with x ≤ z and y ≤ z (directedness), and such that whenever x is in I and y ≤ x, then y is also in I (being a lower set).

Definitions

A principal ideal is an ideal of the form {x | x ≤ a} for some element a, written ↓a. The dual notion of an ideal is a filter, which is an upper set closed under finite meets.

Applications

Ideals play a central role in domain theory, where the ideals of a poset ordered by inclusion form its ideal completion, used to model computation. The concept also underlies the theory of Scott topology and appears in lattice theory, pointfree topology and the study of Boolean algebras, where maximal ideals correspond to ultrafilters.

Prime ideals

In a lattice, an ideal is prime if whenever x ∧ y is in the ideal, then x or y is in the ideal. Prime ideals are key to representation theorems for distributive lattices.
