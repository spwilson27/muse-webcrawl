Jaeger-LeCoultre

Jaeger-LeCoultre is a Swiss luxury watch and clock manufacturer founded by Antoine LeCoultre in 1833 and based in Le Sentier, Switzerland, in the Vallée de Joux. Since 2000 it has been a subsidiary of the Swiss luxury group Richemont. The company is known for producing hundreds of calibres entirely in-house and for iconic models such as the Reverso.

History

Antoine LeCoultre founded a workshop in Le Sentier in 1833, inventing machines to cut watch pinions and, in 1844, the Millionomètre, then the most precise measuring instrument in the world. In 1903, Paris watchmaker Edmond Jaeger challenged Swiss makers to produce ultra-thin movements he had designed; LeCoultre accepted, and the partnership culminated in the formal Jaeger-LeCoultre brand in 1937.

Notable products

The Reverso, introduced in 1931, features a case that flips over to protect the dial, originally designed for polo players in British India, and is an icon of Art Deco design. Other landmarks include the Memovox alarm watch (1950), the Atmos clock, powered by tiny changes in atmospheric temperature and pressure, and the tiny Calibre 101 (1929), long the world's smallest mechanical movement. The manufacture has created over a thousand different calibres and holds hundreds of patents.
