The Nguyễn lords (Vietnamese: Chúa Nguyễn) were a succession of rulers who governed southern Vietnam (Đàng Trong, or Cochinchina) from 1558 to 1777, nominally as subjects of the Lê emperors but in practice as an independent dynasty. Their rivals, the Trịnh lords, ruled the north, and the two sides fought the Trịnh–Nguyễn Civil War across the seventeenth century.

History

Nguyễn Hoàng, sent south in 1558 to govern the frontier provinces, built a power base at Phú Xuân (modern Huế). His successors fortified the Đồng Hới walls against Trịnh invasions, expanded steadily southward at the expense of Champa and the Khmer — the Nam tiến that gave Vietnam its modern shape — and ran a thriving foreign trade through the port of Hội An.

Fall

Corruption and heavy taxation provoked the Tây Sơn rebellion of 1771; by 1777 the Tây Sơn had destroyed the Nguyễn regime, though the surviving prince Nguyễn Ánh later returned to found the Nguyễn dynasty in 1802.
