Solomon's Temple, also known as the First Temple, was a Jewish temple in Jerusalem that, according to the Hebrew Bible, was built by King Solomon and dedicated to Yahweh. It is said to have stood on the Temple Mount until its destruction by the Neo-Babylonian Empire under Nebuchadnezzar II in 587 BCE.

Biblical account

According to the Books of Kings, Solomon built the Temple on Mount Moriah using cedar from Lebanon supplied by Hiram I of Tyre and labour organised throughout Israel. The structure measured about 60 cubits long, 20 cubits wide and 30 cubits high, and contained the Holy of Holies, which housed the Ark of the Covenant.

Destruction and legacy

After a siege of Jerusalem, the Babylonians looted and burned the Temple in 587 BCE, deporting much of the population of Judah to Babylon. A Second Temple was later built on the site after the Persian conquest. Because excavation on the Temple Mount is not possible, no archaeological remains of Solomon's Temple have been directly confirmed, and historians debate its scale and historicity.
