Amniocentesis

Amniocentesis
FieldObstetrics, medical genetics
PurposePrenatal diagnosis of chromosomal and genetic disorders
TimingUsually 15–20 weeks of gestation
SampleAmniotic fluid (about 20 mL)

Amniocentesis is a prenatal diagnostic procedure in which a small sample of amniotic fluid surrounding the fetus is withdrawn from the uterus for testing. The fluid contains fetal cells and substances that can be analysed to detect chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome, genetic disorders, and certain infections.

Procedure

Amniocentesis is usually performed between the 15th and 20th weeks of pregnancy. Under continuous ultrasound guidance, a thin needle is passed through the mother's abdomen and uterine wall into the amniotic sac, avoiding the fetus and placenta, and about 20 millilitres of fluid is aspirated. Fetal cells in the fluid are cultured and examined by karyotyping, fluorescence in situ hybridisation, chromosomal microarray or DNA testing.

Indications and risks

The test is offered when screening results, maternal age, family history or ultrasound findings indicate an increased risk of a fetal abnormality, and it provides a definitive diagnosis rather than a probability estimate. The procedure carries a small risk of miscarriage, generally estimated at roughly 0.1–0.3% above background in modern practice, as well as small risks of infection, fluid leakage and needle injury. Non-invasive prenatal testing on maternal blood has reduced the number of amniocenteses performed, but amniocentesis remains the diagnostic standard when confirmation is needed.

Other uses

Later in pregnancy, amniocentesis may be used to assess fetal lung maturity in some circumstances, to test for intrauterine infection, or to drain excess fluid (amnioreduction) in polyhydramnios.
