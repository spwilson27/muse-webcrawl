Saint Titus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Titus]
Titus, bishop of Gortyn

Titus (Greek: Τίτος, Títos) was an early Christian missionary and church leader, a companion and associate of the Apostle Paul, and the recipient of the Epistle to Titus in the New Testament. A Greek Gentile convert, he became the first bishop of Crete according to tradition and is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and other churches. His feast day is 26 January, shared with Timothy, in the Roman calendar.

Life

Though not mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles, Titus appears repeatedly in Paul's letters. Paul brought him to Jerusalem around 49 AD as a test case in the controversy over circumcision: Titus, a Greek, was not compelled to be circumcised, a precedent for the inclusion of Gentiles. He served as Paul's trusted agent in Corinth, carrying letters and organising the collection for the Jerusalem church, and Paul affectionately calls him "my true son in our common faith."

The Epistle and Crete

The Epistle to Titus, one of the Pastoral Epistles, instructs Titus in organising the churches of Crete, where Paul had left him "to put in order what remained." Tradition holds that Titus served as bishop of Gortyn in Crete and died there at an advanced age — 94, according to some accounts. His skull is venerated as a relic in the church of Saint Titus in Heraklion, having been returned from Venice in 1966. Titus is the patron saint of Crete and of the United States Army Chaplain Corps, whose highest award bears his name.
