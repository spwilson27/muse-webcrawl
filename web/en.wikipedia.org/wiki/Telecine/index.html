Telecine

Telecine is the process of transferring motion picture film to video or digital formats. The term also denotes the equipment used: a film scanner or projector combined with a video camera or sensor that converts each film frame into electronic form.

Frame-rate conversion

Because film is usually shot at 24 frames per second while television standards used 25 fps (PAL) or 30 fps (NTSC), telecine must reconcile the rates. For NTSC, the classic "3:2 pulldown" repeats fields so that four film frames become five video frames; PAL transfers typically simply run the film about 4% faster. Modern digital workflows scan film frame-by-frame at high resolution (2K, 4K and above) for restoration and distribution.

Piracy usage

In file-sharing slang, "telecine" (TC) labels a bootleg copy of a film made by transferring the cinema print directly, producing better quality than a camera recording (CAM) but usually below retail releases.
