Accord on Fire and Building Safety in Bangladesh

The Accord on Fire and Building Safety in Bangladesh (the Bangladesh Accord) is a legally binding agreement between global brands and trade unions, signed on 15 May 2013, to improve safety in the Bangladeshi ready-made garment industry. It was created in the aftermath of the Rana Plaza collapse of 24 April 2013, which killed more than 1,100 garment workers and ranked as the deadliest garment-factory disaster in history.

Provisions

The Accord covers more than 1,600 factories supplying over 200 signatory brands. Its core features, which distinguish it from voluntary corporate programs, include independent safety inspections of electrical, fire, and structural hazards with publicly released reports; binding commitments by brands to ensure remediation is financially feasible; worker participation through health and safety committees and a confidential complaints mechanism; and governance shared between brand representatives and the global unions IndustriALL and UNI, with the International Labour Organization chairing its steering committee.

Remediation and transition

Inspections identified tens of thousands of hazards, the large majority of which were verified as corrected over the Accord's first five-year term. In 2018 a Transition Accord extended the work, and from 2020 the RMG Sustainability Council (RSC), a tripartite body of industry, brands, and unions in Bangladesh, took over operations in the country. In 2021 the parties signed the International Accord for Health and Safety in the Textile and Garment Industry, expanding the model beyond Bangladesh, with Pakistan the first additional country program (launched 2023).

Reception

Labor advocates cite the Accord as a landmark because it is enforceable — two arbitration cases brought by unions against brands were settled with payments toward remediation. Some factory owners and the rival, non-binding Alliance for Bangladesh Worker Safety (organized by North American retailers, which wound up in 2018) criticized it as intrusive or costly, and progress on remediation financing and boiler safety remained contested issues.
