Salk Institute for Biological Studies
Founded: 1960/1963, by Jonas Salk
Location: La Jolla, San Diego, California
Architect: Louis Kahn

The Salk Institute for Biological Studies is an independent, nonprofit research institute in La Jolla, California, founded by Jonas Salk, developer of the polio vaccine, with support from the March of Dimes. It was established in 1960 and its laboratories opened in 1963.

Salk wanted a place where science and humanities would meet — a "crucible of creativity" — and worked closely with architect Louis Kahn on the design. The resulting complex, two mirror-image laboratory blocks of concrete and teak flanking a travertine plaza bisected by a narrow stream of water aligned to the sunset, is considered a masterpiece of modern architecture and is a designated historic landmark.

The institute's faculty work in genetics, neuroscience, plant biology, cancer and ageing, and its ranks have included several Nobel laureates, among them Francis Crick, who spent the second half of his career there.
