Dovercourt

Dovercourt is a small seaside town and former civil parish in the Tendring district of Essex, England. It adjoins Harwich, with which it forms a single built-up area, and lies on the North Sea coast near the mouth of the River Stour.

History

Dovercourt developed as a Victorian seaside resort after the arrival of the railway in the 19th century, when the developer John Bagshaw promoted it as a genteel resort with marine villas, a promenade and pleasure gardens. Its two cast-iron lighthouses, built in 1863 to guide shipping into Harwich harbour, are a well-known local landmark; they were replaced by lightships in the late 1910s.

Kindertransport

In 1938–39 Dovercourt hosted a transit camp for Jewish refugee children brought to Britain by the Kindertransport from Nazi Germany and Austria. Several hundred children stayed at the holiday camp at Dovercourt Bay before being placed with foster families; the episode was dramatised in later accounts and memorials.

Today

Dovercourt has a sandy beach, a boating lake and a promenade, and its railway station on the Mayflower line links it to Harwich and Manningtree. The town centre serves as a local shopping area for the combined Harwich–Dovercourt community.
