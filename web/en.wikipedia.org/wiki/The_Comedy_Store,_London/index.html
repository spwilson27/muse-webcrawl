The Comedy Store is a comedy club in London, England, regarded as the birthplace of the British alternative comedy scene of the 1980s. It was founded in 1979 by businessman Don Ward, who took inspiration — and, with permission, the name — from the Comedy Store in Los Angeles. Its first home was in rooms above a strip club in Soho.

History

The club became a launching pad for the generation of performers who reshaped British comedy, including Alexei Sayle (its first MC), Rik Mayall and Adrian Edmondson, French and Saunders, Ben Elton, and Keith Allen. In 1993 the club moved to purpose-built premises at 1a Oxendon Street, between Piccadilly Circus and Leicester Square. A Manchester branch, on Deansgate Locks, opened in 2000.

The Comedy Store Players

Since 1985 the club has been home to the Comedy Store Players, an improvisational troupe performing on Wednesday and Sunday nights with games in the style later popularized by Whose Line Is It Anyway? Long-serving members have included Josie Lawrence, Paul Merton, Neil Mullarkey, Richard Vranch, and Mike Myers, with guest appearances by Eddie Izzard and others. The Players hold a Guinness World Record for the longest-running comedy show with a permanent cast. The club's weekly programming — topical show The Cutting Edge and the New Material nights — keeps it central to British stand-up, sustained much like the city institutions chronicled by urban writers such as Jane Jacobs chronicled street life elsewhere.
