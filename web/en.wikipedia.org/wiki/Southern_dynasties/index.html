Southern dynasties

The Southern dynasties were a succession of four Chinese dynasties that ruled south China from 420 to 589, during the period of division known as the Northern and Southern dynasties. In order they were the Liu Song (420–479), Southern Qi (479–502), Liang (502–557) and Chen (557–589), all with their capital at Jiankang (modern Nanjing).

Background

The southern regimes descended from the Eastern Jin, which had been founded by northern aristocratic refugees fleeing the conquest of north China. Southern politics was dominated by great émigré clans and by military strongmen who repeatedly seized the throne, producing short dynasties and frequent imperial assassinations, especially under the Liu Song and Southern Qi.

Culture and economy

The south, long less developed, grew rapidly as population migrated to the Yangtze delta and beyond, expanding rice agriculture and commerce. The period saw major achievements in literature — landscape poetry, literary criticism such as Liu Xie's Wenxin diaolong, and historical compilation — and the flourishing of Buddhism under imperial patrons, above all Emperor Wu of Liang. In 589 the Sui dynasty conquered the Chen and reunified China.
