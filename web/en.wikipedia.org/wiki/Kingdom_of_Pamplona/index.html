The Kingdom of Pamplona (Basque: Iruñeko Erresuma) was a medieval kingdom in the western Pyrenees, centred on the city of Pamplona, and is regarded as the early form of the later Kingdom of Navarre. It emerged in the early 9th century as a Basque polity resisting both Frankish and Muslim expansion.

Origins

Tradition dates the kingdom's foundation to around 824, when Íñigo Arista, leader of the Basque Íñiguez family and ally of the Banu Qasi of the Ebro valley, was proclaimed king in Pamplona following the second Battle of Roncevaux Pass. His successors consolidated control over the surrounding valleys and forged ties with the Christian kingdom of Asturias.

Expansion under the Jiménez dynasty

In 905 Sancho Garcés I inaugurated the Jiménez dynasty and expanded the kingdom at Muslim expense toward the Ebro. His descendant Sancho III "the Great" (1004–1035) brought Pamplona to its greatest extent, ruling directly or indirectly over much of Christian Iberia, including Castile, Aragon and León. On his death the realm was divided among his sons, giving rise to the separate kingdoms of Navarre, Aragon and Castile.

From Pamplona to Navarre

The title "King of Pamplona" gradually gave way to "King of Navarre" during the 12th century; Sancho VI, who reigned from 1150, was the first to style himself King of Navarre consistently, and by 1162 the kingdom is generally referred to as Navarre.
