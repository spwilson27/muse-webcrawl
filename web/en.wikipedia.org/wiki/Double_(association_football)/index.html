Double (association football)

The Double, in association football, is the achievement of winning a country's top-tier league championship and its primary domestic cup competition in the same season. It is considered one of the most prestigious accomplishments in club football. Winning additional major trophies in the same season extends the feat into a treble or beyond.

History

The first recognised Double in English football was achieved by Preston North End in the inaugural Football League season of 1888–89, when they also went unbeaten in the league. The feat remained rare in England during the 20th century: Tottenham Hotspur (1960–61), Arsenal (1970–71, 1997–98, 2001–02), Liverpool (1985–86), Manchester United (1993–94, 1995–96, 1998–99) and Manchester City (2018–19, when they won every domestic trophy) are among the clubs to have achieved it.

Notable doubles

In Scotland, Rangers and Celtic have won the league and Scottish Cup double many times. Across Europe, clubs such as Ajax, PSV Eindhoven, Bayern Munich, Barcelona, Real Madrid and Porto have repeatedly won domestic doubles. The term is also applied in other footballing countries — for example in Ireland, Turkey, and Japan — wherever a league title and main cup are won together.

League and cup doubles worldwide

The prestige of a Double varies with the strength of the domestic cup: in countries where the cup has lost prominence, the achievement may be valued less than in earlier eras, but it remains a standard benchmark of a dominant season.
