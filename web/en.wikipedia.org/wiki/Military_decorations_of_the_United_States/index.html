Military decorations of the United States

U.S. military awards and decorations are worn in a prescribed order of precedence.

The military decorations of the United States are awards and badges issued by the United States Armed Forces to recognize heroism, meritorious service, achievement, and participation in campaigns. They include personal decorations, unit citations, service medals, campaign medals, and qualification badges, worn on the uniform in an official order of precedence.

Highest decorations

The highest military decoration is the Medal of Honor, awarded by the President in the name of Congress for conspicuous gallantry and intrepidity at the risk of life above and beyond the call of duty. Below it rank the service crosses — the Distinguished Service Cross (Army), Navy Cross, and Air Force Cross — followed by the Distinguished Service Medals, the Silver Star, the Legion of Merit, the Distinguished Flying Cross, and the Bronze Star Medal. The Purple Heart is awarded to those wounded or killed in action.

History

The tradition dates to the Badge of Military Merit created by George Washington in 1782, the ancestor of the Purple Heart. The Civil War saw the creation of the Medal of Honor, and the two World Wars produced the extensive system of campaign medals still in use. Modern awards include the Afghanistan and Iraq Campaign Medals and the Global War on Terrorism Service Medal.
