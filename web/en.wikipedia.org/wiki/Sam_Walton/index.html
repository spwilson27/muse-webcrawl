Samuel Moore Walton was an American business magnate best known for founding Walmart and Sam's Club, the world's largest retailer and private employer. By his death he was among the richest people in the United States, and his heirs — the Walton family — remain among the wealthiest families in the world.

Early life and first stores

Walton grew up during the Great Depression in Oklahoma and Missouri, worked a paper route and other jobs, studied economics at the University of Missouri, and served in the U.S. Army intelligence corps during the Second World War. In 1945 he bought a Ben Franklin variety store in Newport, Arkansas; losing that store's lease in 1950, he opened Walton's 5 & 10 in Bentonville, the town that remains Walmart's home.

Founding Walmart

On 2 July 1962 Walton opened the first store named Wal-Mart in Rogers, Arkansas, built on a formula of deep discounting in small towns underserved by retailers, relentless cost control, and sophisticated logistics. The company went public in 1970 and expanded across the Sun Belt and then the nation, passing Kmart and Sears to become America's largest retailer by 1990. Walton launched the Sam's Club warehouse format in 1983 and the first Supercenter in 1988.

Famously frugal — he drove an old pickup truck and flew coach — Walton was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom shortly before his death from multiple myeloma in 1992. His autobiography, Sam Walton: Made in America, appeared the same year.
