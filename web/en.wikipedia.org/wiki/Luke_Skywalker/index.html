Luke Skywalker is a fictional character and the main protagonist of the original film trilogy in the Star Wars franchise created by George Lucas. Portrayed by Mark Hamill, Luke first appears in Star Wars (1977) and returns in The Empire Strikes Back (1980) and Return of the Jedi (1983), with Hamill reprising the role in the sequel trilogy films The Force Awakens (2015), The Last Jedi (2017) and briefly The Rise of Skywalker (2019).

Appearances

Raised by his aunt and uncle on the desert planet Tatooine, Luke is drawn into the galactic civil war after purchasing the droids R2-D2 and C-3PO. Mentored by Obi-Wan Kenobi, he joins the Rebel Alliance, uses the Force to destroy the Death Star, trains as a Jedi under Yoda on Dagobah, and learns that the Sith Lord Darth Vader is his father, Anakin Skywalker. In Return of the Jedi he redeems his father, whose act destroys the Emperor. In the sequel trilogy, Luke is found in self-imposed exile on Ahch-To after his nephew Ben Solo's fall to the dark side; he trains Rey, then projects himself across the galaxy to face Kylo Ren on Crait, dying as he becomes one with the Force.

Characterisation and impact

Luke is widely read as an embodiment of Joseph Campbell's "hero's journey" monomyth, which Lucas consciously drew upon. The character has become one of the most recognisable figures in popular culture, appearing across films, television series, novels, comics and video games in both the current canon and the former Expanded Universe ("Legends"), where he founds a New Jedi Order.
