Naive Bayes classifier

ClassSupervised learning / statistical classification
BasisBayes' theorem
AssumptionConditional independence of features
VariantsGaussian, multinomial, Bernoulli, complement

In statistics, naive Bayes classifiers are a family of simple probabilistic classifiers based on applying Bayes' theorem with strong (naive) independence assumptions between the features. They are among the simplest Bayesian network models, yet combined with kernel density estimation they can achieve respectable accuracy, and they are a popular baseline method in machine learning.

Principle

For a document or observation with features x1...xn, the classifier computes the posterior probability of each class using Bayes' theorem. The "naive" assumption that every feature is conditionally independent of every other feature given the class greatly simplifies the computation, allowing the likelihood to be written as a product over features. The predicted class is the one with the highest posterior probability (the maximum a posteriori decision rule).

Variants and applications

Different event models give the common variants: Gaussian naive Bayes assumes normally distributed numeric features; multinomial naive Bayes suits count data such as word frequencies in text; Bernoulli naive Bayes uses binary occurrence data; and complement naive Bayes was designed to correct the skewed estimates of the multinomial model on imbalanced text data. Naive Bayes is best known for document classification and spam filtering, where it was used in early Bayesian email filters, and it remains useful because it trains quickly, requires little data and scales to very large feature spaces. Despite the crudeness of the independence assumption, its classification decisions are often surprisingly accurate, partly because correct ranking of class probabilities matters more than exact probability estimates.
