The Lockheed Martin F-35B Lightning II is the short take-off and vertical landing (STOVL) variant of the F-35 family of single-seat, single-engine, stealth multirole strike fighters. It is designed to operate from amphibious assault ships, small aircraft carriers with ski-jumps, and austere forward bases, giving its operators a supersonic stealth jet that does not need a long runway or catapults. The F-35B was the first F-35 variant to reach initial operational capability, with the United States Marine Corps in July 2015.

Lift system

The F-35B's vertical-lift capability comes from the Rolls-Royce LiftSystem, integrated with its Pratt & Whitney F135 engine. In STOVL mode a shaft-driven lift fan behind the cockpit provides vertical thrust, a three-bearing swivel duct directs the main engine exhaust downward, and roll posts in the wings provide balance and control. This arrangement—descended from the X-35 demonstrator that won the Joint Strike Fighter competition in 2001—replaced the separate lift engines of earlier designs such as the Yak-38 and the direct-lift Pegasus engine of the Harrier. The lift fan occupies volume used for fuel in other variants, giving the F-35B somewhat shorter range, and it cannot carry a gun internally, using an external pod instead.

Service

Besides the US Marine Corps, the F-35B is operated by the United Kingdom—flown jointly by the Royal Air Force and Royal Navy from the Queen Elizabeth-class carriers—by Italy's Navy and Air Force, and by Japan, Singapore, and other customers. British F-35Bs first deployed operationally aboard HMS Queen Elizabeth in 2021, and USMC aircraft have flown combat strikes from amphibious assault ships. The type is the direct successor to the Harrier "jump jet" in US, British, and Italian service.
