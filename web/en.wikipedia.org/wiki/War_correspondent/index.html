War correspondent

[Image: A correspondent in flak jacket filing beside an armoured vehicle]

A war correspondent is a journalist who reports from armed conflicts, often embedded with military units or working unilaterally. The profession is conventionally dated to William Howard Russell's Crimean War dispatches for The Times (1854–56), which helped bring down a government and reshape public knowledge of war.

Figures and risks

From Ernie Pyle and Martha Gellhorn to modern correspondents, war reporters have combined witnessing with advocacy debates. Modern conflict reporting is extraordinarily dangerous: press-freedom groups record dozens of journalists killed in wars each year, with the Gaza war among the deadliest ever documented for media workers.
