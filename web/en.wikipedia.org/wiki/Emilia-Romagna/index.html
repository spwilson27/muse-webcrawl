Emilia-Romagna is one of the twenty regions of Italy, in the north of the country, with its capital at Bologna. It comprises the historical regions of Emilia and Romagna, stretching from the Po plain south to the Apennines and east to the Adriatic Sea. With around 4.4 million inhabitants, it is one of Italy's wealthiest and most developed regions.

Geography and history

The region takes its name in part from the Via Aemilia, the Roman road from Piacenza to Rimini along which most of its historic cities lie. In the Middle Ages and Renaissance its cities — Bologna, Ferrara, Modena, Parma, Ravenna — flourished as communes and lordships under families such as the Este and Farnese. Bologna is home to the University of Bologna, founded in 1088 and the oldest university in continuous operation in the world. Ravenna, once the capital of the Western Roman Empire, preserves famed Byzantine mosaics.

Economy and culture

Emilia-Romagna is renowned for its agriculture and food industry — Parmigiano Reggiano, Parma ham, balsamic vinegar of Modena, and fresh pasta such as tortellini and tagliatelle originate here — and for engineering: Ferrari, Lamborghini, Maserati and Ducati form part of its "Motor Valley". Packaging machinery, ceramics and biomedical industries are also major employers. The Adriatic resorts of the Romagna coast, including Rimini, are popular tourist destinations.
