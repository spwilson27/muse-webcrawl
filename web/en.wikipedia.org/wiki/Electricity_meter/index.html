Electricity meter

An electricity meter, also called an electric meter or energy meter, is a device that measures the amount of electric energy consumed by a residence, business, or electrically powered device. Utilities use meters to bill customers, usually in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

Types

The classic electromechanical induction meter, invented in the late nineteenth century (the Ferraris/Shallenberger induction principle), uses an aluminium disc rotating at a speed proportional to power, driving a mechanical counter. Electronic meters measure voltage and current digitally and can record consumption by time of day, enabling time-of-use tariffs. Smart meters add two-way communication, sending readings automatically to the utility and supporting remote connection and detailed feedback to consumers.

Operation and accuracy

Meters are classified by accuracy class and by the service they measure (single-phase or three-phase). They are calibrated and sealed by regulation, and utilities periodically test samples in service. Tampering and meter bypass are common forms of electricity theft.

Smart metering programmes

Many countries have undertaken nationwide smart meter rollouts, justified by reduced meter-reading costs, faster outage detection and support for demand management, though costs, privacy and health concerns have generated debate.
