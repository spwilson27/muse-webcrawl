Swiss Standard German

Swiss Standard German (German: Schweizer Hochdeutsch) is the standard variety of the German language used in Switzerland as a written language and, in more formal contexts, as a spoken language. It is one of the four national languages of Switzerland alongside French, Italian and Romansh, and is the standard form taught in schools in German-speaking Switzerland — although everyday speech there is in Swiss German dialects (Schweizerdeutsch), a classic case of diglossia.

Characteristics

Swiss Standard German differs from the standard German of Germany and Austria in vocabulary, pronunciation and some spellings. Most conspicuously, it does not use the letter ß (eszett), writing ss instead (e.g., Strasse). Typical Helvetisms include Velo for bicycle, Billett for ticket, Trottoir for pavement, parkieren for to park, and Grüezi as a greeting in speech. Vocabulary borrows from French and Italian more readily than in Germany. Swiss spelling also tends to retain French-influenced forms in loanwords. In pronunciation, Swiss speakers typically lack the glottal stop, use a rolled or uvular r, and pronounce final -ig as [ɪk] less commonly than in northern Germany.
