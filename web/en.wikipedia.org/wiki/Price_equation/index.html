Price equation

The Price equation (also known as Price's equation or Price's theorem) is a mathematical identity in evolutionary biology that describes how the average value of a trait in a population changes between generations. It was derived by George R. Price in 1970 while working on kin selection.

Statement

The equation partitions evolutionary change into two components: the covariance between a trait and fitness (the effect of natural selection) and the expected value of the change in the trait during transmission (transmission bias, including mutation and recombination effects). In compact form, the change in mean trait equals the sum of these two terms divided by mean fitness.

Applications

Because it is an identity rather than a dynamical model, the Price equation applies to any system of entities that reproduce with heritable traits. It underlies derivations of Hamilton's rule for kin selection and has been used in multilevel (group) selection theory, epidemiology, and cultural evolution.
