Polish National Opera

The Grand Theatre in Warsaw, home of the Polish National Opera.

The Polish National Opera (Polish: Opera Narodowa) is Poland's national opera company, based at the Grand Theatre (Teatr Wielki) in Warsaw. It is one of the largest opera institutions in Europe, with a main stage seating over 1,700 people, and it shares its home with the Polish National Ballet.

History

Opera in Poland dates to the court of King Władysław IV in the 1630s. The national opera tradition is usually traced to 1765, when King Stanisław August Poniatowski established a public opera stage in Warsaw. The Grand Theatre building, designed by Antonio Corazzi, opened in 1833 with a production of Rossini's The Barber of Seville.

The theatre was destroyed during the Second World War, in the Warsaw Uprising and its aftermath, and was rebuilt after the war, reopening in 1965. The Polish composer Stanisław Moniuszko, whose operas Halka and The Haunted Manor are central to the national repertoire, served as the opera's conductor in the nineteenth century.

Repertoire and activity

The company performs the standard international repertoire alongside Polish works, and has premiered operas by composers including Karol Szymanowski and Krzysztof Penderecki. In addition to opera productions and ballet, the institution runs education programmes and tours internationally.
