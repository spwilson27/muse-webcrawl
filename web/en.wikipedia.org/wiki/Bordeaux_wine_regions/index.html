Bordeaux wine regions

The wine regions of Bordeaux, in south-western France, form one of the world's largest and most prestigious fine-wine areas, divided by the Gironde estuary and its tributaries the Garonne and Dordogne into three broad zones: the Left Bank, the Right Bank and the area between the rivers, Entre-Deux-Mers.

Left Bank

West of the Gironde and Garonne lie the Médoc — including the communes of Pauillac, Margaux, Saint-Julien and Saint-Estèphe — and Graves, home to Pessac-Léognan. Left Bank reds are dominated by Cabernet Sauvignon grown on gravel soils; the region's most famous estates were ranked in the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855. Sauternes and Barsac, in the south, produce celebrated botrytised sweet white wines.

Right Bank and Entre-Deux-Mers

East of the Dordogne, the Right Bank appellations of Saint-Émilion and Pomerol favour Merlot on clay and limestone. Between the Garonne and Dordogne, Entre-Deux-Mers is best known for dry white wines from Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon. Bordeaux's red blends typically combine Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Petit Verdot and Malbec.
