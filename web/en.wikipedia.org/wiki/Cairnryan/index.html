Cairnryan (Scottish Gaelic: Machair an Sgithich) is a small village and major ferry port in Dumfries and Galloway, south-west Scotland. It lies on the eastern shore of Loch Ryan, about 9 kilometres (6 miles) north of Stranraer, and is one of the principal gateways between Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

History

Cairnryan developed as a port during the Second World War, when it was built as a military port (known as No. 2 Military Port) to provide an alternative west-coast harbour should the ports of the Clyde and Mersey be bombed. Floating “Whale” roadway sections used in the Mulberry harbours of the Normandy landings were constructed and shipped from the area.

After the war, Cairnryan became a major ship-breaking yard, where famous vessels including the aircraft carrier HMS Centaur and the battleship HMS Warspite's contemporaries were dismantled. Regular ferry services across the North Channel to Larne began in the post-war period, and in 2011 Stena Line transferred its services from Stranraer to a new terminal at Loch Ryan Port in Cairnryan, operating to Belfast. P&O Ferries also sails from Cairnryan to Larne.

Transport

The village is served by the A75 road, which links the port with Stranraer, Dumfries and the motorway network at Gretna, and by the B734. The short sea crossing of about 22 miles to Northern Ireland is the shortest between mainland Britain and Ireland after the Stranraer route it replaced.
