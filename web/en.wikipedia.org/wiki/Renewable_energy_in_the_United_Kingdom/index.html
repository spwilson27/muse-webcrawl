Renewable energy in the United Kingdom

Renewable energy in the United Kingdom contributes a growing share of the country's electricity, heat, and transport energy. In the early 2020s, renewables supplied roughly 40–50% of UK electricity generation in some years, led by wind power, followed by biomass, solar photovoltaics, and hydroelectricity. The UK became a world leader in offshore wind capacity.

History

Support schemes have included the Renewables Obligation (2002), feed-in tariffs for small installations (2010), and Contracts for Difference (from 2014), which became the main support mechanism for large-scale low-carbon generation. Coal power was phased out, with the last coal plant closing in 2024.

Sources

  • Wind — the largest renewable source, with major onshore resources in Scotland and large offshore arrays in the North Sea and Irish Sea.
  • Solar — grew rapidly in the 2010s under feed-in tariffs.
  • Biomass — including converted coal units such as Drax power station.
  • Hydro and marine — long-established hydroelectricity in Scotland and Wales; tidal stream projects under development.
