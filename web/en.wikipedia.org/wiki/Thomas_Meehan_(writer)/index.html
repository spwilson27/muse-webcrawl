Thomas Meehan (writer)

BornAugust 1, 1929, Ossining, New York, U.S.
DiedAugust 22, 2017 (aged 88), New York City, U.S.
OccupationWriter, librettist
Notable worksAnnie, The Producers, Hairspray

Thomas Edward Meehan (August 1, 1929 – August 22, 2017) was an American writer best known for his work on Broadway musicals. He won three Tony Awards for Best Book of a Musical, for Annie (1977), The Producers (2001), and Hairspray (2003) — a feat unmatched by any other librettist.

Career

Meehan began his career writing for The New Yorker, contributing humorous pieces. His leap to fame came with Annie, adapted from the comic strip Little Orphan Annie, with music by Charles Strouse and lyrics by Martin Charnin; it ran for nearly six years on Broadway and won seven Tony Awards. He returned to Broadway triumphs by writing the books for Mel Brooks's The Producers, which won a record twelve Tony Awards, and for Hairspray, based on the John Waters film.

Other work

His other credits include the musicals Young Frankenstein, Cry-Baby, Elf, and Rocky, and contributions to screen work including Brooks's film Spaceballs and the film version of To Be or Not to Be. He collaborated with Charnin again on sequels to Annie. Meehan died in New York City in 2017 at the age of 88.
