Glee (TV series)

Glee is an American musical comedy-drama television series that aired on Fox from May 19, 2009, to March 20, 2015. It was created by Ryan Murphy, Brad Falchuk and Ian Brennan. The series centres on the glee club at the fictional William McKinley High School in Lima, Ohio, and its director, Spanish teacher Will Schuester (Matthew Morrison), as the club competes in show choir competitions.

Cast and characters

The ensemble cast included Lea Michele as Rachel Berry, Cory Monteith as Finn Hudson, Chris Colfer as Kurt Hummel, Amber Riley as Mercedes Jones, Dianna Agron as Quinn Fabray, Mark Salling as Noah "Puck" Puckerman, Naya Rivera as Santana Lopez, Heather Morris as Brittany Pierce, Kevin McHale as Artie Abrams, Jenna Ushkowitz as Tina Cohen-Chang, and Jane Lynch as cheerleading coach Sue Sylvester.

Music

Each episode featured several musical performances, mostly cover versions, released as singles and compilation albums. The cast's recordings sold tens of millions of copies; their cover of "Don't Stop Believin'" became a signature hit, and the Glee cast surpassed Elvis Presley for the most entries on the Billboard Hot 100 by an act at the time.

Reception

The series won numerous awards, including six Primetime Emmy Awards and four Golden Globe Awards. It was noted for addressing social issues such as sexuality, bullying, race and disability within a mainstream musical format.
