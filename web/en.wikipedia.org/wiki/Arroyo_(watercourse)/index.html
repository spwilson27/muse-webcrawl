Arroyo (watercourse)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A dry arroyo in the southwestern United States

An arroyo (Spanish for "brook"), also called a wash, is a dry creek, stream bed or gulch that temporarily or seasonally fills and flows after sufficient rain. Flash floods are common in arroyos following thunderstorms.

The term arroyo is used interchangeably with wash in the dry desert landscapes of the southwestern United States, and the terms rambla and wadi describe similar features in Spain and North Africa and the Middle East respectively.

Formation and processes

Arroyos can be natural fluvial landforms or constructed flood control channels. The term usually applies to a sloped or mountainous terrain in xeric and desert climates. In addition, in many rural communities arroyos are also the principal roads in the community and are subject to flash flood and the loss of road access. The alluvium on which many arroyos lie is typically coarse, loose sediment.

In the United States, the desert pavement and arroyo systems of the Southwest have been studied extensively. Arroyos provide a water source to desert animals.

Flash flood danger

Arroyos are dangerous during storms because of their propensity to flash flood. A thunderstorm hundreds of metres or a kilometre away can cause flooding in an arroyo, making it essentially un-crossable for vehicles and treacherous on foot. Signs posted at many arroyos warn of the danger.
