The Hai River (Chinese: 海河; pinyin: Hǎi Hé), also known as the Peiho or Hai Ho, is a Chinese river that connects Beijing to Tianjin and the Bohai Sea. It is formed in Tianjin by the confluence of five rivers — the Southern Canal, Ziya River, Daqing River, Yongding River and the Northern Canal — and flows about 70 kilometres from Tianjin to the sea.

Basin

The greater Hai River basin covers much of Hebei province as well as Beijing, Tianjin, and parts of Shanxi, Shandong, Henan and Inner Mongolia, draining an area of over 300,000 km² with a population well above 100 million. The Grand Canal links the Hai system to the Yellow and Yangtze rivers.

Flooding and management

The basin's flat plain and intense summer rains made it historically prone to devastating floods, notably in 1939 and during the catastrophic 1963 Hai River flood, after which a major flood-control programme dug new channels to the sea. Chronic water scarcity and pollution are now the basin's principal challenges.
