Chittorgarh Fort

Chittorgarh Fort
Location: Chittorgarh, Rajasthan, India
Type: Hill fort
Area: About 280 hectares
UNESCO: Hill Forts of Rajasthan, World Heritage Site (2013)

Chittorgarh Fort (Hindi: Chittorgarh Durg) is one of the largest forts in India, perched on a 180-metre hill near the Gambhiri River in Rajasthan. It was the capital of Mewar and a symbol of Rajput resistance, valour and sacrifice.

History

Tradition dates the fort to the 7th century. It was attacked three times by Muslim invaders: Alauddin Khalji in 1303, Bahadur Shah of Gujarat in 1535, and the Mughal emperor Akbar in 1568. Each siege is associated in Rajput memory with jauhar — mass self-immolation by women of the fort — and saka, a final charge by the men. The story of Rani Padmini and Alauddin Khalji became famous through Malik Muhammad Jayasi's epic poem Padmavat.

Architecture

The fort has seven massive gates, numerous palaces, temples and reservoirs. Its landmarks include the nine-storey Vijay Stambha (Tower of Victory, built 1448 by Rana Kumbha) and the Kirti Stambha dedicated to Jain tradition. Rana Kumbha Palace and Padmini's Palace stand near a lotus pool.

Legacy

Chittorgarh remains an emblem of Rajput pride and is part of the UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan. See also Mewar and Udaipur, founded by Udai Singh II after the 1568 siege.
