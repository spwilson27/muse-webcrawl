Streamlines, streaklines, and pathlines

Streamlines, streaklines, and pathlines are three types of field lines used to visualise fluid flow. They coincide in steady flow but generally differ in unsteady flow.

Definitions

Streamlines are a family of curves that are instantaneously tangent everywhere to the velocity vector of the flow at a given moment. They show the direction a massless fluid element would travel at any point in time.

Pathlines are the trajectories that individual fluid particles follow over time — the Lagrangian history of a marked particle, equivalent to the path recorded in a long-exposure photograph of a single tracer particle.

Streaklines are the loci of all fluid particles that have passed through a particular fixed point in the flow, as produced by continuously injecting dye or smoke at that point.

Relationships

In steady flow, streamlines, streaklines, and pathlines are identical. In unsteady flow all three differ. A timeline — a line of particles marked simultaneously across the flow — is a fourth visualisation device often discussed alongside them.

Applications

Streamline visualisation is central to wind-tunnel testing, weather charts (where isobars approximate streamlines of geostrophic flow), and computational fluid dynamics post-processing.
