Pageboy

The pageboy or page boy is a hairstyle named after what was believed to be the haircut of a late medieval page boy. It has straight hair hanging to below the ear, where it usually turns under. There is often a fringe (bangs) in the front. The pageboy haircut was fashionable for men in the late 16th century and later became a popular women's hairstyle.

History

The pageboy look emerged in the 1950s, when New York hairstylist M. Lewis popularised a short, under-curled style. In the 1970s and 1980s the style was widely worn, aided by public figures such as British socialite Joanna Lumley, whose "Purdey" cut in the television series The New Avengers sparked demand, and by Princess Diana, whose early 1980s hairstyle was a variant of the pageboy. Dorothy Hamill's similar "wedge" cut during the 1976 Winter Olympics is often compared with it.

Men have also worn the pageboy: members of British and American pop groups of the 1960s, including the Beatles in their early years and the Rolling Stones, sported longer versions of the style.

Description

The pageboy is a variation of the bob cut, typically longer, reaching the shoulders or just below, with the ends turned under. It is usually worn with a fringe and requires blow-drying with a round brush to maintain the characteristic under-curl.
