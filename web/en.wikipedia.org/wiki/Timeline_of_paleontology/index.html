Timeline of paleontology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fossils provided the earliest evidence of ancient life central to the history of paleontology.

This timeline of paleontology records significant discoveries, events and ideas in the history of the study of fossils and ancient life, from early interpretations of fossils as curiosities to the modern science of evolutionary history.

Antiquity to the 17th century

  • 6th century BC – The Greek philosopher Xenophanes recognises fossil shells found far inland as evidence that land was once covered by sea.
  • 1027 – The Persian scholar Avicenna discusses the petrification of organisms in The Book of Healing.
  • 1669 – Nicolaus Steno publishes Prodromus, establishing principles of stratigraphy and arguing that shark teeth fossils ("tongue stones") came from real sharks.

18th century

  • 1780s – The first Mosasaur fossils are discovered in the Netherlands.
  • 1796 – Georges Cuvier presents his work on elephant anatomy, demonstrating that mammoths were a separate, extinct species — a founding argument for the reality of extinction.

19th century

  • 1824 – William Buckland formally describes Megalosaurus, the first dinosaur named in scientific literature.
  • 1825 – Gideon Mantell describes Iguanodon.
  • 1842 – Richard Owen coins the term Dinosauria.
  • 1859 – Charles Darwin publishes On the Origin of Species, transforming the interpretation of the fossil record.
  • 1861 – The first Archaeopteryx skeleton is described, a transitional form between dinosaurs and birds.
  • 1870s – The "Bone Wars" rivalry between Edward Drinker Cope and Othniel Charles Marsh drives rapid dinosaur discovery in the American West.

20th century

  • 1909 – Charles Walcott discovers the Burgess Shale fauna in Canada, revealing the Cambrian explosion.
  • 1912 – Alfred Wegener proposes continental drift, later supported by fossil distributions.
  • 1924 – The Taung Child (Australopithecus africanus) is described by Raymond Dart.
  • 1974 – "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis) is discovered in Ethiopia.
  • 1980 – Luis and Walter Alvarez propose an asteroid impact caused the end-Cretaceous mass extinction.

21st century

  • 2004 – Tiktaalik roseae, a transitional fish–tetrapod fossil, is described from Arctic Canada.
  • 2009 – "Ardi" (Ardipithecus ramidus) is formally described.
  • 2015 – Homo naledi is announced from the Rising Star Cave system in South Africa.
