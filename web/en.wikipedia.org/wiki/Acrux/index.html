Acrux (Alpha Crucis, α Crucis) is the brightest star in the southern constellation of Crux, the Southern Cross, and the southernmost first-magnitude star in the night sky. Its name is a contraction of "Alpha" and "Crux". Acrux lies at the foot of the cross asterism and, together with Mimosa, Gacrux, and Imai, defines the constellation's distinctive shape.

Properties

Acrux is not a single star but a multiple system about 320 light-years from the Sun. The two brightest components, α¹ Crucis and α² Crucis, are separated by a few arcseconds, appear at magnitudes of roughly 1.4 and 1.9, and are themselves close spectroscopic binaries, so the system contains at least five and possibly more massive, hot, blue-white B-type stars. Their combined light gives Acrux an apparent magnitude of 0.76, making it the 13th-brightest star in the sky.

Significance

As the brightest star of Crux, Acrux features on the national flags of several Southern Hemisphere countries, including Australia, New Zealand, Brazil, and Papua New Guinea. Because of its far southern declination, about −63°, it is invisible from most of the northern temperate zone and is circumpolar for observers south of roughly 27° S latitude.
