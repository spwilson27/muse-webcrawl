The Tempi train crash occurred on 28 February 2023 near the village of Evangelismos in the Tempi Valley, Thessaly, Greece, when an InterCity passenger train travelling from Athens to Thessaloniki collided head-on with a freight train travelling in the opposite direction on the same track. Fifty-seven people were killed, most of them young passengers and students returning from the carnival weekend, and at least 85 were injured. It is the deadliest railway accident in Greek history.

The collision

InterCity 62, carrying about 342 passengers and 10 crew, departed Athens in the evening. Near Larissa station, through a combination of signalling failures, manual routing errors, and miscommunication between the stationmaster and the train driver, the passenger train was directed onto the southbound track used by an approaching freight train carrying steel plate. Neither crew was warned in time. The trains collided at a combined speed estimated above 160 km/h shortly before midnight; the front carriages of the passenger train were destroyed and a fire broke out, intensified by flammable liquids — the nature and origin of a large fireball seen in video footage became a major point of public controversy.

Causes and aftermath

Investigations found that the Athens–Thessaloniki line was operating without functioning automatic signalling and train protection systems, despite contracts — notably the repeatedly delayed "Contract 717" for signalling and ETCS installation — dating back years. The Larissa stationmaster on duty, who had limited experience, was arrested and charged, but official inquiries and expert reports pointed to systemic failures in the Greek railway's management, staffing, and safety culture. The transport minister Kostas Karamanlis resigned the day after the crash.

Public response

The disaster provoked widespread anger and mass protests across Greece, with demonstrators accusing the state of chronic neglect; the slogan "Pare me otan ftaseis" ("Call me when you arrive") — from the last message of a victim's mother — became emblematic. Protests continued on anniversaries, with enormous demonstrations in February 2025 demanding accountability, amid criticism of the official investigation, including the rapid concreting-over of the crash site and disputed findings about the freight train's cargo. The crash became a defining political issue in Greece and prompted European-level scrutiny of Greek rail safety.
