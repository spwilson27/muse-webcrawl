Ethylene glycol

FormulaC2H6O2
Molar mass62.07 g/mol
Melting point−12.9 °C
Boiling point197.3 °C
AppearanceColourless, viscous liquid

Ethylene glycol (IUPAC name: ethane-1,2-diol) is an organic compound with the formula (CH2OH)2. It is mainly used for two purposes: as a raw material in the manufacture of polyester fibres and for antifreeze formulations. It is an odourless, colourless, sweet-tasting, viscous liquid.

Production

Ethylene glycol is produced from ethylene via the intermediate ethylene oxide, which reacts with water to give the glycol. Most production uses a thermal process with excess water; a catalytic process developed commercially recycles ethylene carbonate intermediates to achieve higher selectivity. Global production amounts to tens of millions of tonnes per year.

Uses

The major end uses are as a precursor to polyester resins and fibres (through reaction with terephthalic acid) and as an antifreeze and coolant in automobiles, where it depresses the freezing point and raises the boiling point of water. It is also used in hydraulic fluids, inks, and as a dehydrating agent in natural gas processing.

Toxicity

Ethylene glycol is poisonous. Because of its sweet taste it is attractive to children and animals, and accidental or deliberate ingestion can be fatal: the body metabolises it to glycolic and oxalic acids, causing metabolic acidosis, calcium oxalate crystal deposition and kidney failure. Treatment involves antidotes such as fomepizole or ethanol, which block its metabolism, often combined with haemodialysis. The less toxic propylene glycol is substituted in some applications for this reason.
