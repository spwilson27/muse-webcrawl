Karl Jaspers

Karl Theodor Jaspers (23 February 1883 – 26 February 1969) was a German-Swiss psychiatrist and philosopher who had a strong influence on modern theology, psychiatry and philosophy. A founder of existential philosophy, he developed the concepts of the Axial Age, boundary situations (Grenzsituationen) and the encompassing (das Umgreifende).

Life and work

Born in Oldenburg, Jaspers studied medicine and worked at Heidelberg's psychiatric clinic, where his General Psychopathology (1913) introduced phenomenological method into psychiatry. He moved to philosophy, holding a chair at Heidelberg from 1921. Dismissed under the Nazis because his wife Gertrud was Jewish, he survived in internal exile and returned after the war, publishing The Question of German Guilt (1946). In 1948 he moved to the University of Basel and took Swiss citizenship in 1967.

Philosophy

Jaspers distinguished mere existence from Existenz, the authentic self realised in communication and in "boundary situations" such as death, suffering and guilt. His history of religion coined the term "Axial Age" for the period around 800–200 BCE when parallel spiritual revolutions occurred in China, India, Persia, Israel and Greece. He was a close correspondent of Hannah Arendt and Martin Heidegger, though he later criticised Heidegger's politics.
