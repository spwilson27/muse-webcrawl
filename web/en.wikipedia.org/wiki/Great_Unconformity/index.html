Great Unconformity

In the Grand Canyon, the Cambrian Tapeats Sandstone rests directly on the ancient Vishnu Schist.

The Great Unconformity is a major gap in the geological record — an unconformity — in which sedimentary rocks of Cambrian age rest directly on much older Precambrian basement rocks, with hundreds of millions to more than a billion years of Earth history missing at the contact. The term was popularised by Clarence Dutton for the exposures in the Grand Canyon of Arizona, where the flat-lying Tapeats Sandstone (about 525 million years old) lies on the Vishnu Schist and Zoroaster Granite, rocks up to about 1.8 billion years old.

Extent

The unconformity is not confined to the Grand Canyon. Comparable sub-Cambrian erosion surfaces occur across North America (Laurentia) and have been reported on other continents, leading some geologists to regard it as a near-global feature of the late Precambrian Earth. At any single locality, between roughly 200 million and 1.3 billion years can be missing.

Proposed causes

Several mechanisms have been proposed, probably acting in combination:

  • Rodinia breakup — erosion associated with the rifting apart of the supercontinent Rodinia from about 750 million years ago uplifted and stripped kilometres of rock along new continental margins.
  • Snowball Earth glaciation — the great Sturtian and Marinoan glaciations (about 717–635 million years ago) may have planed down continental surfaces, with glacial erosion removing several kilometres of material; this "glacial erosion" hypothesis was advanced in a widely discussed 2019 study.
  • Tectonic uplift and plate collisions — mountain-building episodes such as the Grenville orogeny and later transcontinental events exposed deep crustal rocks to erosion for immense spans of time.

Significance

The Great Unconformity has attracted attention because the rock record resumes just before the Cambrian explosion, the rapid diversification of animal life. Some researchers suggest a causal link: enhanced erosion delivered nutrients such as phosphorus to the oceans and changed seawater chemistry, possibly promoting biomineralisation and complex ecosystems. Others caution that much of the missing interval simply reflects a long period of non-deposition and erosion on a stable continent, and that the apparent coincidence may be an artefact of preservation.

History of study

John Wesley Powell noted the canyon's angular unconformities during his 1869 expedition, and Dutton named the "Great Unconformity" in his 1882 monograph on the Grand Canyon. Modern thermochronology — dating the cooling history of minerals such as hematite, zircon and apatite — is used to reconstruct when the basement rocks were exhumed, and has yielded evidence for multiple episodes of burial and stripping rather than a single event.
