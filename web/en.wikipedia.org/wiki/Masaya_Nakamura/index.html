Masaya Nakamura

Masaya Nakamura (Japanese: 中村 雅哉, 24 December 1925 – 22 January 2017) was a Japanese businessman and founder of Namco, the video game company responsible for Pac-Man and many other arcade classics. He is often called the "father of Pac-Man" for commissioning and championing the game, which was designed by Toru Iwatani.

Early life and founding of Nakamura Seisakusho

Nakamura was born in Tokyo. After wartime service training and a period running his father's shotgun repair business, in 1955 he founded Nakamura Seisakusho, later Nakamura Manufacturing Company — abbreviated "Namco" — installing two mechanical rocking horses on the roof of a Yokohama department store. The company expanded into amusement rides and coin-operated games.

Namco and the video game era

In 1974 Nakamura bought the Japanese subsidiary of Atari, giving Namco entry into video arcade distribution. Namco's own titles Gee Bee (1978), Galaxian (1979), and Pac-Man (1980) made it a leading developer; Pac-Man became the best-selling arcade game of all time. Nakamura guided Namco's expansion into home consoles, theme parks (Namco Wonder Eggs), and film, and oversaw the 2005 merger with Bandai that formed Bandai Namco Holdings, serving as its honorary chairman.

Honours and death

Nakamura was decorated by the Japanese government with the Order of the Rising Sun and recognised by the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences. He died in January 2017 aged 91; Bandai Namco announced his death on 30 January at the family's request.
