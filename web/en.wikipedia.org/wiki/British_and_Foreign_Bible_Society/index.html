The British and Foreign Bible Society, often simply called the Bible Society, is a British charity founded in 1804 to translate, publish and distribute the Bible worldwide. It was the first and for a century the largest of the Bible societies, and its model — distributing scriptures "without note or comment" — was copied across Europe, America and the missionary world.

Founding

The Society was founded in London on 7 March 1804, inspired in part by the story of Mary Jones, a Welsh teenager who had walked 26 miles in 1800 to buy a Welsh Bible from the minister Thomas Charles. Its founders, including the abolitionist William Wilberforce and the Clapham Sect circle, intended to supply Bibles in the languages of Britain and throughout the world. Early disputes over whether to include the Apocrypha, and later over Baptists' translation preferences, led to secessions including the Trinitarian Bible Society (1831).

Work

Through the 19th century the Society financed translations into hundreds of languages, printed Bibles cheaply by exploiting stereotype printing, and distributed them through colporteurs and missionaries on every continent. Its London headquarters, Bible House, moved from Ludgate Hill to Queen Victoria Street and later to Swindon. The Society is a member of the United Bible Societies fellowship and continues to fund translation work and promote Bible engagement in England and Wales.
