Chicharrón

Chicharrón (Spanish: [tʃitʃaˈron]) is a dish of fried pork belly or fried pork rinds, originating in Spain and spread throughout the Spanish-speaking world and former Spanish colonies, including Latin America and the Philippines. The name is also applied by extension to similar preparations of chicken, beef or other meats.

Preparation

Pork skin, with or without attached meat and fat, is salted, often simmered and dried, then deep-fried in its own rendered fat until it puffs and blisters into a light, crisp texture. Chicharrones may be eaten as a snack, crumbled over dishes, or stewed in sauces such as Mexican chicharrón en salsa verde, which softens them.

Regional varieties

In Mexico, chicharrón is sold in markets in large crisp sheets and used in tacos and gorditas; in Colombia, chicharrón is a meaty pork-belly staple of the bandeja paisa; in Peru it is served with sweet potato and salsa criolla as a sandwich; in the Philippines, chicharon (often dipped in spiced vinegar) is a classic beer snack, with famous versions from Bulacan. Bolivia's chicharrón is a festival dish of seasoned pork chunks cooked in their own fat.
