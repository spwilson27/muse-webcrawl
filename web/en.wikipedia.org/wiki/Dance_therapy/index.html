Dance therapy

Field: Psychotherapy, expressive arts therapy
Pioneer: Marian Chace
Professional body: American Dance Therapy Association (founded 1966)
Aliases: Dance/movement therapy (DMT)

Dance/movement therapy (DMT), or dance therapy, is the psychotherapeutic use of movement and dance to support intellectual, emotional and motor functions of the body. It is based on the premise that body and mind are inseparable, and that movement can express and help regulate emotion, cognition and social interaction.

History

The field emerged in the United States in the 1940s, when dancer Marian Chace began working with psychiatric patients at St. Elizabeths Hospital in Washington, D.C., finding that patients who could not communicate verbally could express themselves through rhythm and movement. The American Dance Therapy Association was founded in 1966, and training standards and registration (R-DMT and BC-DMT credentials) followed.

Practice

Therapists work in psychiatric hospitals, schools, rehabilitation and elder-care settings, using techniques such as mirroring, rhythmic group activity and improvisation. Evidence suggests benefits for depression, anxiety, Parkinson's disease and quality of life in dementia, though research quality varies and DMT is usually one component of a broader care plan.
