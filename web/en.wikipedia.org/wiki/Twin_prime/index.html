Twin prime

Twin prime
FieldNumber theory
DefinitionA prime p such that p + 2 or p − 2 is also prime
First pairs(3, 5), (5, 7), (11, 13), (17, 19), (29, 31)
ConjectureTwin prime conjecture: infinitely many such pairs

A twin prime is a prime number that is either 2 less or 2 more than another prime number — for example, either member of the twin prime pair (41, 43). In other words, a twin prime is a prime that has a prime gap of two. Sometimes the term twin prime is used for a pair of twin primes; an alternative name is prime twin or prime pair.

Properties

The first twin prime pairs are (3, 5), (5, 7), (11, 13), (17, 19), (29, 31), (41, 43), and the pair (5, 7) is the only pair where one prime belongs to two pairs, since every twin prime pair other than (3, 5) is of the form (6n − 1, 6n + 1). The sum of a twin prime pair (except (3, 5)) is divisible by 12.

Twin prime conjecture

Whether there are infinitely many twin primes has been asked since antiquity and remains one of the great open problems of number theory. Brun's theorem (1919) shows that the sum of the reciprocals of twin primes converges — a value known as Brun's constant, about 1.90216 — in contrast to the divergence for all primes. In 2013 Yitang Zhang proved that there are infinitely many prime pairs with a gap of at most 70 million; subsequent work by the Polymath project reduced the bound to 246, a landmark step toward the twin prime conjecture itself.
