Factory Acts

The Factory Acts were a series of acts passed by the Parliament of the United Kingdom beginning in 1802 to regulate the conditions of industrial employment, particularly the working hours and conditions of women and children in textile mills and other factories.

Child labour in textile mills prompted the first Factory Acts.

Early acts

The Health and Morals of Apprentices Act 1802 was the first factory legislation, limiting apprentices in cotton mills to twelve-hour days and prohibiting night work. The Factory Act 1833 created a professional factory inspectorate and limited children aged 9–13 to eight hours a day. The Factory Act 1844 restricted women's hours and required machinery to be fenced, while the Ten Hours Act 1847 limited the working day of women and young persons to ten hours.

Later development

Subsequent acts extended regulation to more industries, raised minimum ages, and consolidated the law, notably in the Factory and Workshop Act 1878 and the Factories Act 1937 and 1961. The Factory Acts were foundational to modern British health and safety and employment law, much of which is now governed by later legislation such as the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974.
