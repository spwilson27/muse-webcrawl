Battle of Huế

The Battle of Huế (31 January – 2 March 1968) was one of the longest and bloodiest battles of the Vietnam War, fought during the Tet Offensive. Forces of the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and the Viet Cong seized most of the city of Huế, the former imperial capital of Vietnam, and held it for over three weeks against counterattacks by the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN) and the United States Marine Corps.

Background

Huế straddled Highway 1 and the Perfume River, close to the Demilitarized Zone. Lightly defended because of the Tet holiday truce, the city fell quickly when PAVN and Viet Cong forces attacked on the night of 30–31 January 1968, raising their flag over the Citadel.

The battle

Marines from Phu Bai fought their way into the southern city, then crossed the river to assault the Citadel in brutal house-to-house fighting, hampered initially by restrictions on artillery and air support intended to spare the historic city. The last enemy positions in the Citadel were cleared on 24–25 February, and operations in the surrounding area ended on 2 March.

Massacre and aftermath

During the occupation, PAVN and Viet Cong cadres executed thousands of civilians — officials, teachers, religious figures and foreigners — whose bodies were later found in mass graves; estimates of the victims of the Massacre at Huế range from about 2,800 to 6,000. The ancient city was devastated. Although a military defeat for the attackers, the battle's ferocity shocked the American public and contributed to the Tet Offensive's strategic effect on US opinion.
