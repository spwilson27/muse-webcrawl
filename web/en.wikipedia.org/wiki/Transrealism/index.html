Transrealism

Transrealism
Genre: Science fiction / literary fiction
Term coined by: Rudy Rucker, 1983 manifesto
Notable authors: Rudy Rucker, Philip K. Dick (retrospectively)

Transrealism is a mode of science fiction writing, named by mathematician and author Rudy Rucker in his 1983 essay 'A Transrealist Manifesto', which blends autobiographical, recognisably real characters and settings with fantastic, surreal or science-fictional transformations.

Principles

Rucker proposed writing about one's immediate life and perceptions, but with reality 'transfigured' — the author and people they know appear thinly disguised, while the familiar world bends under higher-dimensional, time-travelling or mind-altering phenomena. He identified Philip K. Dick's novels such as A Scanner Darkly as transrealist before the term existed. His own novels, including White Light and the Ware Tetralogy, exemplify the approach.

Related uses

The word is also used, separately, in poetry circles and for a Latin American literary tendency seeking to transcend magical realism, with different meanings. See Rudy Rucker and Cyberpunk.
