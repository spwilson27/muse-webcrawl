South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology

South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology
German: Südtiroler Archäologiemuseum
Italian: Museo archeologico dell'Alto Adige
Location: Bolzano, South Tyrol, Italy
Opened: 1998

The South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology (German: Südtiroler Archäologiemuseum; Italian: Museo archeologico dell'Alto Adige) is an archaeological museum in the city of Bolzano, South Tyrol, Italy. It is best known as the home of the glacier mummy Ötzi the Iceman, a naturally mummified man from the Copper Age (c. 3300 BCE) discovered in the Ötztal Alps in 1991.

The Ötzi exhibition

The museum opened in its present location in 1998 in a former bank building in Bolzano's centre. Ötzi is kept in a specially developed refrigerated cell at −6 °C and about 98 percent humidity, reproducing glacier conditions; visitors view him through a small window. His equipment — including a copper axe, a flint dagger, a longbow, a quiver of arrows, and a birch-bark container — is displayed on the same floor, and the exhibition presents the scientific research into his life, clothing, tools, and violent death.

Other exhibitions

Further floors trace the archaeology of South Tyrol from the end of the last Ice Age and the Palaeolithic and Mesolithic periods, through the Neolithic, Copper, Bronze, and Iron Ages, to Roman times and the early Middle Ages. The permanent exhibition is presented in German, Italian, and English. The museum typically receives several hundred thousand visitors a year and is one of the most visited museums in Italy.
