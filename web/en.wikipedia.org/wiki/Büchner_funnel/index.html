Büchner funnel

TypeLaboratory filtration equipment
Named afterErnst Büchner
MaterialsPorcelain, glass, polypropylene
Used withBüchner flask, vacuum source, filter paper

A Büchner funnel is a piece of laboratory equipment used in filtration. It is traditionally made of porcelain, but glass and plastic funnels are also available. The funnel is used together with a vacuum source to separate solids from liquids far more quickly than gravity filtration alone.

Design

The upper part of the funnel is a cylindrical chamber whose base is a flat, perforated plate. A disc of filter paper, cut to fit and moistened with solvent, is laid over the perforations. The funnel stem passes through a rubber adapter (a Büchner ring or stopper) into a heavy-walled filter flask, often called a Büchner flask or side-arm flask, whose side arm is connected by vacuum tubing to a water aspirator or vacuum pump. When the vacuum is applied, the filtrate is drawn through the paper while the solid remains on the paper surface.

Use

Büchner funnels are routine tools in organic chemistry for collecting precipitates and the products of recrystallisation, and in gravimetric and preparative work generally. The related Hirsch funnel has sloped sides and a smaller plate, suiting it to filtering on a smaller scale, while sintered-glass funnels have a fused porous glass disc in place of filter paper.

History

The funnel is named after the German industrial chemist Ernst Büchner (1850–1925). A common misconception credits Eduard Buchner, the Nobel laureate in chemistry, but the filtration apparatus is associated with Ernst Büchner, who patented the design in the late 19th century.
