La Raza Unida

Partido Nacional de La Raza Unida (National United Peoples Party; commonly La Raza Unida, or LRU) was a Hispanic political party centered in the southwestern United States. It was founded in early 1970 to give a political alternative to Mexican Americans who felt the two-party system, and especially the Democratic Party in Texas, had neglected their interests; it became the most prominent electoral expression of the Chicano Movement.

La Raza Unida's 1972 national convention in El Paso

Origins

La Raza Unida grew out of the Mexican American Youth Organization (MAYO) and local struggles in South Texas, notably the 1969 school walkouts in Crystal City, Texas, where Chicano candidates backed by the movement swept school board and city council elections in 1970. Activists including José Ángel Gutiérrez helped build the party, which spread to other states with varying ideologies, from Texas's electoral pragmatism to Colorado's more nationalist current under Rodolfo "Corky" Gonzales.

Elections and decline

The party's peak came in 1972, when Ramsey Muñiz ran for governor of Texas and won about 6.3 percent of the vote, and La Raza Unida held its first national convention in El Paso. LRU candidates won local offices in South Texas counties, including county judgeships and school boards. Internal disputes, legal challenges to ballot access, pressure from the two major parties, and the co-optation of some leaders weakened the party through the late 1970s; Muñiz's later arrests further damaged it. The Texas party was effectively defunct by the early 1980s, though groups using the name have occasionally re-formed, and the party's campaigns trained a generation of Latino political leaders.
