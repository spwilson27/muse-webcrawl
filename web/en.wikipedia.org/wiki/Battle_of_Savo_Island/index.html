Battle of Savo Island

Date8–9 August 1942
LocationNear Savo Island, Solomon Islands
ResultJapanese victory
BelligerentsUnited States and Australia vs. Empire of Japan
CommandersVictor Crutchley; Richmond Kelly Turner vs. Gunichi Mikawa

The Battle of Savo Island, also known as the First Battle of Savo Island, was a night naval battle of the Pacific War fought on 8–9 August 1942 between the Imperial Japanese Navy and Allied naval forces around Savo Island near Guadalcanal in the Solomon Islands. It was among the worst defeats in United States naval history: four Allied heavy cruisers were sunk for negligible Japanese loss.

Background

On 7 August 1942 Allied forces landed on Guadalcanal and Tulagi, the opening of the Guadalcanal campaign. Vice Admiral Gunichi Mikawa sortied from Rabaul with seven cruisers and a destroyer to attack the invasion shipping by night — a form of warfare in which the Japanese, with superior torpedoes, optics and training, excelled. The Allied screening force was divided into three groups and fatally compromised by poor communications, fatigue, and the absence of its overall commander, Rear Admiral Crutchley, called away to a conference.

The battle

Mikawa's column slipped past the radar picket destroyers undetected and, shortly after 01:30 on 9 August, opened fire with guns and Long Lance torpedoes. The southern group was smashed first — HMAS Canberra fatally damaged and USS Chicago torpedoed — then the northern group was caught in turn: USS Astoria, Quincy and Vincennes were all sunk. More than 1,000 Allied sailors died. Mikawa, fearing daylight air attack and unaware that the American carriers had withdrawn, retired without attacking the defenceless transports.

Aftermath

The surviving Allied warships and transports withdrew, leaving the Marines on Guadalcanal short of supplies. The disaster prompted reforms in US Navy night-fighting doctrine, radar use and communications. The waters north of Guadalcanal, site of many further sinkings, became known as "Ironbottom Sound".
