Charmides (dialogue)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

AuthorPlato
SubjectSophrosyne (temperance, self-knowledge)
InterlocutorsSocrates, Charmides, Critias, Chaerephon

The Charmides is a dialogue by Plato in which Socrates discusses the virtue of sophrosyne - temperance or moderation - with the young Charmides and his guardian Critias.

Summary

Fresh from the battle of Potidaea, Socrates visits a palaestra and, pretending to have a cure for Charmides' headache, steers conversation to the soul. Definitions of temperance are proposed - quietness, modesty, doing one's own business, self-knowledge, and a "knowledge of knowledge" - and each is refuted, ending in aporia.

Themes

The dialogue explores self-knowledge (linked to the Delphic "know thyself"), the relation of knowledge to benefit, and foreshadows the later careers of Charmides and Critias, both later among the Thirty Tyrants.
