Zakat al-Fitr (Arabic: زكاة الفطر, "the zakat of breaking the fast"), also called Sadaqat al-Fitr, is an obligatory charity given by Muslims at the end of the fasting month of Ramadan, before the festival of Eid al-Fitr on 1 Shawwal. Unlike the annual zakat on wealth, Zakat al-Fitr is a flat per-person levy owed by every Muslim who has food in excess of their family's needs, paid also on behalf of dependents.

Purpose

According to a hadith of Ibn Abbas, the Prophet Muhammad prescribed the charity "as a purification for the fasting person from idle talk and obscenities, and as food for the poor." Its twin aims are thus spiritual — expiating lapses during the fast — and social: ensuring that the poor have enough to eat and can share in the joy of Eid rather than begging on the festival day.

Amount and form

The classical measure is one ṣāʿ — a volume measure equivalent to about 2.5 to 3 kilograms — of a local staple food (historically dates, barley, raisins, or dried cheese; today commonly rice, wheat, or flour) for each member of the household. The majority of classical jurists require payment in food; the Hanafi school and many contemporary fatwa bodies permit paying the cash value, and mosques and charities in many countries publish a per-person monetary figure each Ramadan and distribute it on donors' behalf.

Timing and recipients

The obligation falls due at sunset on the last day of Ramadan (or by the morning of Eid, in differing views), and it should be handed over before the Eid prayer; paying a day or two early is accepted by most schools, while delaying past the prayer without excuse is sinful though still owed. Recipients are the categories of zakat recipients in Qur'an 9:60, in practice chiefly the poor and needy.
