Regional natural parks protect inhabited rural landscapes of natural and cultural value.

The regional natural parks of France (French: parcs naturels régionaux, PNR) are a category of protected area created by a decree of 1 March 1967. Unlike France's national parks, which protect largely uninhabited wilderness under strict regulation, regional natural parks are inhabited rural territories recognised for the quality and fragility of their natural and cultural heritage, whose development is organised around a negotiated charter of protection and sustainable development.

Organisation

A regional natural park is created on the initiative of the regional council and the communes of the territory, which voluntarily adhere to a charter (charte) — a contract setting out objectives for protecting landscapes and heritage, managing the environment, supporting local economic and social life, and welcoming visitors. The state classifies the park by decree for a period of 15 years, renewable after revision of the charter. Each park is administered by a joint syndicate (syndicat mixte) of the member local authorities, and the parks are linked nationally by the Fédération des parcs naturels régionaux de France.

Examples

There are more than 55 regional natural parks, from the first — the Scarpe-Escaut park near the Belgian border, created in 1968 — to recent additions. Well-known examples include the Camargue, with its wetlands and flamingos; the Gorges du Verdon; the volcanic landscapes of the Auvergne; the Morvan in Burgundy; the Ballons des Vosges; the Luberon; and Armorique in Brittany. Together they cover a substantial share of metropolitan France and include thousands of communes and several million inhabitants. A related category, the regional natural parks' model of landscape-scale, negotiated protection has influenced protected-area policy elsewhere in Europe.
