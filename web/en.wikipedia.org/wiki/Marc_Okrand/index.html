Marc Okrand

Marc Okrand (born July 3, 1948) is an American linguist, best known as the creator of the Klingon language, the fictional language of the Klingon species in the Star Trek franchise.

Okrand earned a bachelor's degree in linguistics from the University of California, Santa Cruz, in 1972 and a PhD in linguistics from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1977, with a dissertation on the Mutsun language, an extinct Ohlone (Costanoan) language of California. He taught at UC Santa Barbara before joining the National Captioning Institute, where he worked on closed captioning for television.

Klingon

Okrand was hired by Paramount Pictures to develop the Klingon dialogue for Star Trek III: The Search for Spock (1984), expanding on a few phrases devised by actor James Doohan for the first film. He went on to create the language's full grammar and vocabulary, published in The Klingon Dictionary (1985), and later works including The Klingon Way (1996) and Klingon for the Galactic Traveler (1997). He also created the Atlantean language for Disney's Atlantis: The Lost Empire (2001) and provided Vulcan and Romulan dialogue for later Star Trek productions.
