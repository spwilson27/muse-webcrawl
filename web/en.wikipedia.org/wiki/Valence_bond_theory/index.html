Valence bond theory

In chemistry, valence bond (VB) theory is one of two foundational theories, along with molecular orbital theory, developed to explain chemical bonding using quantum mechanics. It was introduced in 1927 by Walter Heitler and Fritz London for the hydrogen molecule and was generalised by Linus Pauling in the 1930s.

Principles

Valence bond theory describes a covalent bond as forming when half-filled atomic orbitals on two atoms overlap and the electrons pair their spins, so that electron density accumulates between the nuclei. Key extensions include hybridisation — the mixing of atomic orbitals into sp, sp² and sp³ hybrids that explains molecular geometry — and resonance, in which a molecule such as benzene is described as a superposition of several Lewis structures.

Comparison

Unlike molecular orbital theory, which delocalises electrons over the whole molecule, VB theory keeps electrons localised in bonds between pairs of atoms, making it intuitively close to classical structural chemistry. Modern computational versions (such as generalised valence bond and spin-coupled theory) remain in use alongside MO methods.
