Big Audio Dynamite (later also BAD II and Big Audio) were an English band formed in London in 1984 by ex-Clash guitarist and vocalist Mick Jones and filmmaker Don Letts. The group pioneered a fusion of rock, punk, reggae, funk, hip hop and film dialogue samples, using drum machines and sampling ahead of most of their contemporaries.

History

Their debut album This Is Big Audio Dynamite (1985) included "E=MC²" and "The Bottom Line". The follow-up No. 10, Upping St. (1986) was co-written and co-produced with Jones's former Clash bandmate Joe Strummer and included "C'mon Every Beatbox" and "Sightsee M.C!". After personnel changes, the band re-formed as BAD II and scored a US number-one modern rock hit with "Rush" (1991). Later albums appeared under the name Big Audio before the band split in the late 1990s. The original line-up reunited for tours and festival appearances in 2011, including Coachella.

Legacy

Big Audio Dynamite are credited with anticipating the sample-driven dance-rock of the 1990s and influencing acts from the alternative-dance and big beat scenes. Their use of film dialogue — especially from the films of Nicolas Roeg — remains a signature.
