Huun-Huur-Tu (Tuvan: Хүүн-Хүүр-Ту, Khün Khürtü) is a music group from Tuva, a republic of Russia situated on the border with Mongolia. The group performs traditional Tuvan music, centred on throat singing (khöömei), in which a single vocalist produces two or more pitches simultaneously. Their name translates as "sun propeller", for the vertical rays of light seen at dawn and dusk on the Tuvan steppe.

History

The group was founded in 1992 by Kaigal-ool Khovalyg, Alexander Bapa, Sayan Bapa and Albert Kuvezin, originally under the name Kungurtuk ("saddle bells"). Their debut album 60 Horses in My Herd was released in 1993, and they began touring internationally, helping popularise Tuvan music in the West. Kuvezin left to form the rock-influenced Yat-Kha; he was succeeded by Radik Tyulyush. Founding member Alexander Bapa died in 2006.

Music

Huun-Huur-Tu perform in several throat-singing styles including khöömei, sygyt (with a clear whistling overtone melody) and kargyraa (a deep, growling style). They accompany themselves on traditional instruments such as the igil (horse-head fiddle), doshpuluur (lute), byzaanchy, shamanic drum and khomus (jew's harp). Songs traditionally evoke the natural world — horses, rivers, mountains and steppe. The group has also collaborated extensively with Western musicians and producers, including work with the Bulgarian women's choir Angelite, the Moscow Art Trio, and electronic and remix projects.
