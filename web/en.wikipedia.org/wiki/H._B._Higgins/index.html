Henry Bournes Higgins (30 June 1851 – 13 January 1929) was an Australian lawyer, politician and judge. He served as a member of the Victorian Legislative Assembly and later of the federal Parliament, and as Attorney-General of Australia in 1904. In 1906 he was appointed a justice of the High Court of Australia, and from 1907 he was President of the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration.

Early life

Higgins was born in Newtownards, County Down, Ireland, the son of a Wesleyan minister. The family emigrated to Australia in 1870. Higgins studied at the University of Melbourne, graduating in arts and law, and was admitted to the Victorian Bar in 1876, building a successful practice as a barrister.

Political career

Higgins was elected to the Victorian Legislative Assembly in 1894 as the member for Geelong. He was a leading supporter of Federation and was elected to the Australasian Federal Convention of 1897–98, where he championed the inclusion of provisions allowing the federal parliament to legislate on conciliation and arbitration of industrial disputes. At the first federal election in 1901 he won the seat of North Melbourne, and served briefly as Attorney-General in the Reid government of 1904–05.

Judicial career

Appointed to the High Court in 1906, Higgins became best known for his work on the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration. In the Harvester case of 1907 (Ex parte H.V. McKay), he established the concept of a "fair and reasonable" basic wage for an unskilled labourer, sufficient to support a man, his wife and three children in "frugal comfort". The "Harvester judgment" became the foundation of Australia's centralised wage-fixing system for much of the twentieth century.

Death and legacy

Higgins died at Dromana, Victoria, in 1929. The federal electoral Division of Higgins in Melbourne is named in his honour.
