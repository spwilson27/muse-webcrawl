Icing (food)

Icing, or frosting in the United States, is a sweet, often creamy glaze, made of sugar with a liquid such as water or milk, that is enriched with ingredients like butter, egg whites, cream cheese or flavourings. It is used to coat or decorate baked goods, such as cakes, pastries and cookies. When used between layers of cake it is called a filling.

Types

Common types include buttercream (butter or shortening beaten with icing sugar), royal icing (egg white and icing sugar, which hardens on drying and is used for piping and gingerbread houses), fondant, which is rolled out into sheets for a smooth finish, ganache (chocolate and cream), glacé icing (a simple sugar-and-water glaze) and cream cheese frosting. "Icing" in British usage corresponds broadly to American "frosting", though icing can also denote thinner glazes.

History

Sugar frostings appeared in the 17th century as refined sugar became available; the earliest printed recipe for icing dates to 1655. Iced cakes were initially a luxury, and the term "frosting" is recorded in American English from the mid-18th century. Industrial production of rolled fondant and modern buttercreams developed in the 20th century.

Application

Icing is spread with a palette knife or piped through a piping bag with shaped nozzles to create borders, flowers, writing and other decoration. Colourings and flavourings such as vanilla, lemon, coffee and cocoa are commonly added.
