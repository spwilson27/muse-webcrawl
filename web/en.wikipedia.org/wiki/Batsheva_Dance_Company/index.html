Batsheva Dance Company

The Batsheva Dance Company is a renowned modern dance company based in Tel Aviv, Israel. It was founded in 1964 by the Baroness Batsheva de Rothschild, who named it after herself, with Martha Graham as its first artistic adviser. Since 1990 the company has been shaped by choreographer Ohad Naharin, creator of the Gaga movement language.

History

Batsheva was Israel's first repertory modern dance company, initially performing works by Graham and other American choreographers. Its early repertoire introduced Israeli audiences to modern dance, and Batsheva de Rothschild also founded the sister Bat-Dor company. The company struggled through leadership changes in the 1970s and 1980s before Naharin's appointment transformed it.

Naharin and Gaga

Under Ohad Naharin, Batsheva developed a distinctive, explosive physical style and works such as Kyr, Anaphaza, Decadance, and Mamootot. Naharin's Gaga movement language — improvisational training exploring sensation, momentum, and availability for movement — became the company's daily practice and spread worldwide through open classes. The Batsheva Ensemble serves as its junior company for young dancers. Batsheva tours extensively and is regarded as one of the world's leading contemporary dance companies.
