Beep test

Participants run 20-metre shuttles in time with recorded beeps.

The beep test, also known as the multi-stage fitness test (MSFT), the 20-metre shuttle run test or the bleep test, is a running test used to estimate an athlete's aerobic capacity (VO2 max). It was developed in the early 1980s by Luc Léger at the University of Montreal and is widely used by schools, police and military organisations and sports teams.

Procedure

Participants run back and forth between two lines 20 metres apart, keeping pace with audio beeps from a recording. The required speed starts slowly and increases by stages — typically each minute a new "level" begins, with the time between beeps shortening. A participant who fails to reach the line before the beep receives a warning; after a further failure they are withdrawn, and their score is recorded as the last level and shuttle completed.

Interpretation

The final level correlates with maximal oxygen uptake; conversion tables translate the score into an estimated VO2 max. Elite endurance athletes may reach levels above 20, while minimum scores are prescribed for entry to various police, fire and armed services. Critics note that motivation and turning technique affect scores, and that the test measures aerobic power better than sport-specific fitness.
