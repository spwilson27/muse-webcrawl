Oradour-sur-Glane
Commune in Haute-Vienne, Nouvelle-Aquitaine, France
Known for: Massacre of 10 June 1944

Oradour-sur-Glane is a commune in the Haute-Vienne department of west-central France. It is remembered worldwide for the massacre of its inhabitants on 10 June 1944, when troops of the 2nd SS Panzer Division "Das Reich" murdered 643 civilians, including women and children, and burned the village.

The massacre

Four days after the Normandy landings, SS troops sealed off the village, assembled the population on the fairground, shot the men in barns, and locked the women and children in the church, which was set on fire. Only a handful of people survived. The atrocity became a symbol of Nazi brutality in occupied France.

The memorial village

After the war, President Charles de Gaulle ordered that the ruins be preserved as a permanent memorial. A new village was built nearby, and the destroyed original — with its rusted cars, tram tracks and ruined houses — remains as it was left. A memorial centre, the Centre de la mémoire, opened in 1999.
