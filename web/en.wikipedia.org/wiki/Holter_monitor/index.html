Holter monitor

A Holter monitor (often simply Holter) is a battery-operated portable device that measures and records the heart's electrical activity (ECG) continuously for 24 to 48 hours or longer during daily activities. It is named after physicist Norman J. Holter, who invented telemetric cardiac monitoring in 1949.

Holter monitor
Purpose: Ambulatory ECG recording
Duration: 24–48 hours (extended: up to 14 days)
Inventor: Norman J. Holter
Detects: Arrhythmias, silent ischemia, palpitation causes

Use

Electrodes attached to the chest connect to a small recorder worn on a belt or strap; patients keep a diary of symptoms and activities. After return, software scans the recording for pauses, tachycardias, bradycardias, atrial fibrillation, ectopic beats and ST changes, correlating them with symptoms — useful when palpitations, syncope or suspected arrhythmia do not appear on a resting ECG.

Variants

Extended patch monitors (e.g. single-lead adhesive devices worn 7–14 days), event recorders triggered by patients, mobile cardiac telemetry with real-time transmission, and implantable loop recorders extend monitoring to weeks or years with higher diagnostic yield for infrequent events.

Limitations

Skin irritation, artefact from electrode detachment, and low yield if symptoms are rare are common issues. A normal Holter during no symptoms does not exclude arrhythmia.
