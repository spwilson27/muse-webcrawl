Uncle Milton Industries

Uncle Milton Industries, Inc. is an American toy company best known for the Ant Farm, the see-through formicarium sold by mail order from 1956 onwards. Founded in 1946 in California by Milton Levine (1913–2011), with his brother-in-law E. Joseph Cossman, the company built its name on science-and-nature toys sold as educational novelties.

The Ant Farm

Levine said the idea came from watching ants at a Fourth of July picnic in 1956. The original Ant Farm consisted of two panes of plastic separated by a thin layer of sand, in a frame, sold for $2.98 through magazine advertisements; buyers received a coupon to be sent away for a vial of live red harvester ants (Pogonomyrmex). The product became a classic of mail-order Americana, and the phrase "ant farm" entered general usage — something Levine never trademarked successfully. Over the decades more than 20 million ant farms were sold. The design was later updated with coloured gel habitats, and the product was named to various toy halls of fame lists.

Other products and later history

The company expanded into other live-animal and science kits, including butterfly gardens, frog habitats, telescopes, planetariums and Star Wars-branded science toys such as a lightsaber room light and a Force Trainer. Joseph Cossman, a celebrated mail-order marketer, helped build the business before selling his interest. Levine remained active in the company into his nineties; the business was later sold and the brand continued under new ownership, with production partly moved overseas.

Ants by mail

Because harvesting ants is seasonal and the insects cannot be shipped in freezing weather, Uncle Milton's ant supply operations became famous in their own right: ants are collected in the Mojave Desert and mailed in vials timed to arrive alive at the buyer's home. State laws restrict importing some species, so the company has long used native red harvester ants for its farms.
