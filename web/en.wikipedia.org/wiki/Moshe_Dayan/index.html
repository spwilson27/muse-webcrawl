Moshe Dayan (May 20, 1915 – October 16, 1981) was an Israeli military leader and politician. He was the second child born on the first kibbutz, Degania, in Ottoman-ruled Palestine. As the fourth Chief of Staff of the Israel Defense Forces (1953–1958), he became a fighting symbol to the world of the new nation of Israel. As Defence Minister he played a key role in the Six-Day War of 1967, and a controversial one in the Yom Kippur War of 1973. As Foreign Minister under Menachem Begin he was instrumental in negotiating the peace treaty with Egypt.

Early life and the eye

Dayan grew up at Nahalal, joined the Haganah at fourteen, and served in Orde Wingate's Special Night Squads during the Arab revolt. In 1939 he was arrested by the British and imprisoned at Acre. In June 1941, serving with the Australian 7th Division in the invasion of Vichy-held Lebanon, he was shot by a sniper while using binoculars on a rooftop in Vichy territory; the bullet shattered the binoculars and destroyed his left eye. Unable to tolerate a glass eye, he wore the black eyepatch that became his trademark.

Military career

In the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Dayan commanded forces in the Jordan Valley and around Lod and Ramle. In 1949 he led the Israeli delegation in armistice talks with Jordan on Rhodes. Appointed Chief of Staff in 1953, he built the IDF's doctrine of offensive reprisal and, in the 1956 Sinai Campaign, commanded the conquest of Sinai. After conflict with David Ben-Gurion he left the army in 1958.

Political career

Dayan served as Minister of Agriculture (1959–1964) and was brought back as Defence Minister on the eve of the Six-Day War, winning enormous fame for the victory and for his administration of the captured territories. He was blamed for Israel's unpreparedness in October 1973 and resigned in 1974. In 1977, though elected on the Labour list, he accepted the foreign ministry from Likud's Begin, conducting the secret negotiations in Morocco that led to the Camp David Accords and the Egypt–Israel peace treaty of 1979. He resigned in 1979, founded the Telem party, and died of colon cancer in 1981. His memoirs include Diary of the Sinai Campaign and the autobiography Story of My Life.
