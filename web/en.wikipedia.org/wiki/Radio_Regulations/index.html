The Radio Regulations (RR) is an intergovernmental treaty published by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) that governs the use of the radio-frequency spectrum and the geostationary and non-geostationary satellite orbits worldwide. Together with the ITU Constitution and Convention, it has the status of an international treaty binding on ITU member states.

Contents

The Radio Regulations comprise four volumes:

  • Volume 1 — Articles: definitions of radiocommunication services, the international Table of Frequency Allocations, and rules on interference, licensing and identification of stations.
  • Volume 2 — Appendices: technical appendices concerning emissions, distress and safety communications, and notification procedures.
  • Volume 3 — Resolutions and Recommendations adopted by world radiocommunication conferences.
  • Volume 4 — ITU-R Recommendations incorporated by reference.

Frequency allocation

The centrepiece of the Regulations is Article 5, the Table of Frequency Allocations, which divides the spectrum from about 8.3 kHz to 275 GHz among more than 40 radiocommunication services (fixed, mobile, broadcasting, radionavigation, amateur, satellite services and others) in the three ITU Regions. National regulators base their domestic allocation tables on the international table.

Revision process

The Regulations are revised by World Radiocommunication Conferences (WRC), held every three to four years; recent editions followed WRC-15 (2016 edition), WRC-19 (2020 edition) and WRC-23. Notable provisions include the distress and safety rules underpinning the Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS) and the coordination procedures for satellite networks administered by the Radiocommunication Bureau.
