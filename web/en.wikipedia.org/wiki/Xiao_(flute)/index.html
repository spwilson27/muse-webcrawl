Xiao (flute)

The xiao (Chinese: 簫; pinyin: xiāo) is a long Chinese vertical end-blown flute, traditionally made of bamboo. It produces a soft, mellow, and breathy tone and is associated with scholarly, meditative music. The xiao is related to but distinct from the transverse flute, the dizi, and from the shorter dongxiao of Fujian.

Description

A typical xiao is a single tube of bamboo, often dark purple-black bamboo (zizhu), with a closed natural node at the top pierced by a blowing hole and usually five finger holes on the front and one thumb hole at the back. Instruments commonly range from about 60 to 80 cm in length. Varieties include the longer dongxiao and the short, thick qin xiao, used to accompany the guqin.

History and repertoire

End-blown flutes of bone and bamboo are among China's oldest instruments. The xiao became a favorite of literati and is classically paired with the guqin in duet repertoire that values subtlety and restraint. It is also used in Jiangnan sizhu ensemble music and in Cantonese music. Its quiet, introspective character is often compared to the sound of wind through bamboo, and it has a long association with Daoist and Confucian ideals of harmony.
