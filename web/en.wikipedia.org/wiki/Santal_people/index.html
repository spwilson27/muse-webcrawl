Santal people

The Santal people (also spelled Santhal) are an Austroasiatic-speaking Munda ethnic group of the Indian subcontinent, the largest tribal community in the Indian states of Jharkhand and West Bengal, with significant populations in Odisha, Bihar, Assam, and in northern Bangladesh and Nepal. Their population is estimated at over six million.

Language and script

The Santali language belongs to the Munda branch of the Austroasiatic family and is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India. In 1925 Pandit Raghunath Murmu created the Ol Chiki script specifically for Santali, and 30 December is celebrated as Santali Language Day; Santali entered the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution in 2003.

History

Santal society was historically centred on agriculture and forest life. In 1855–56 the Santal rebellion (Hul), led by Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu, rose against British colonial authorities and exploitative moneylenders and landlords; it was suppressed but led to the creation of the Santhal Pargana district with protections for tribal land. Santals played a noted role in later movements, including the Jharkhand statehood movement, achieved in 2000.

Culture and religion

Traditional Santal religion, known as Sarna, venerates nature spirits and deities such as Marang Buru (the Great Mountain) and Jaher Ayo, with sacred groves (jaher) in villages. Festivals include Sohrai (harvest), Baha (flower festival), and Karam. Music and dance, featuring drums such as the tamak and tumdak, are central to community life. A substantial minority of Santals are Christian or Hindu.
