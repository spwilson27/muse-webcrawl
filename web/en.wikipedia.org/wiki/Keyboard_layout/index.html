Keyboard layout

[Image placeholder: diagram of the QWERTY keyboard layout]

A keyboard layout is any specific physical, visual, or functional arrangement of the keys, legends, or key-meaning associations of a computer keyboard, mobile phone, or other typographic device. Physical layout is the actual positioning of keys; visual layout is the arrangement of the labels printed on them; functional layout is the mapping of key presses to characters or actions determined by software.

QWERTY and its variants

The dominant layout for Latin-script alphabets is QWERTY, named for the first six letters of its top letter row. It was designed in the 1870s by Christopher Latham Sholes for early typewriters, partly to reduce jamming of typebars by separating common letter pairs. National variants include QWERTZ in German-speaking Europe and AZERTY in France and Belgium, which rearrange letters and punctuation to suit local languages.

Alternative layouts

The Dvorak layout, patented in 1936 by August Dvorak and William Dealey, places the most common letters on the home row to increase speed and comfort; despite its advocates, it never displaced QWERTY. The Colemak layout (2006) modifies QWERTY more conservatively, keeping common shortcut positions while improving ergonomics. Other layouts serve non-Latin scripts, such as JCUKEN for Cyrillic, and specialised layouts exist for Chinese and Japanese input methods.

Physical layouts

Standard physical standards include the ANSI layout common in the United States and the ISO layout used in Europe, which differ in the shape of the Enter key and the number of keys. Laptop, compact, and ergonomic keyboards rearrange or omit keys while generally preserving the functional layout.
