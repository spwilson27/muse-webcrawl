Alan Irwin Menken (born 22 July 1949) is an American composer and songwriter best known for his scores and songs for Walt Disney Animation Studios films. With eight Academy Awards — more than any other living person — he is one of the most decorated composers in film history and one of the few people to achieve EGOT status (Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony). His work with lyricist Howard Ashman on The Little Mermaid (1989), Beauty and the Beast (1991) and Aladdin (1992) powered the Disney Renaissance.

Early career

Menken grew up in New Rochelle, New York, in a family of dentists, and studied music at New York University, originally intending a career as a singer-songwriter. With Howard Ashman he wrote the off-Broadway musical Little Shop of Horrors (1982), a hit later filmed in 1986, whose success brought the pair to Disney.

Disney Renaissance

For The Little Mermaid Menken and Ashman structured the film as a Broadway musical, winning Oscars for the score and for "Under the Sea". Beauty and the Beast won the same two awards ("Beauty and the Beast"); Ashman died of AIDS-related illness before its release, and the film was dedicated to him. With Tim Rice completing Aladdin after Ashman's death, Menken won two further Oscars (score and "A Whole New World"). He went on to score Pocahontas (1995, winning for "Colors of the Wind"), The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1996), Hercules (1997), Enchanted (2007) and Tangled (2010).

Later work

Menken composed for the stage versions of his Disney films and for new musicals including Newsies (Tony Award for Best Score), Sister Act and Leap of Faith. He returned for Disney's live-action remakes — Beauty and the Beast (2017), Aladdin (2019) and The Little Mermaid (2023, with Lin-Manuel Miranda) — and achieved EGOT status in 2020 with a Daytime Emmy for the series Rapunzel's Tangled Adventure.
