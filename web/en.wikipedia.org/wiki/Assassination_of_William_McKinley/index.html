Assassination of William McKinley

William McKinley, the 25th president of the United States, was shot and fatally wounded on 6 September 1901, inside the Temple of Music on the grounds of the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo, New York. His assassin was Leon Czolgosz, an American anarchist. McKinley died eight days later, on 14 September, of gangrene caused by his wounds. He was the third American president to be assassinated, following Abraham Lincoln in 1865 and James A. Garfield in 1881.

The shooting

McKinley had travelled to Buffalo to address the Pan-American Exposition and was holding a public reception at the Temple of Music. Czolgosz, who had concealed a .32 calibre revolver beneath a handkerchief wrapped around his hand, joined the receiving line and, when he reached the president, fired twice at close range. One bullet grazed McKinley; the other entered his abdomen, passed through the stomach, and lodged in the muscles of his back, where it was never found. Bystanders and security subdued Czolgosz, whom McKinley reportedly asked them not to harm.

Death and succession

Surgeons at the exposition's hospital operated but could not locate the bullet, and no X-ray machine was used despite one being exhibited nearby. McKinley appeared to recover but took a turn for the worse as gangrene set in around the wound, and he died in the early hours of 14 September. Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was sworn in as president later that day.

Trial and legacy

Czolgosz was tried in state court, convicted of first-degree murder after a brief trial, and executed in the electric chair at Auburn Prison on 29 October 1901. The assassination led Congress to formally charge the Secret Service with presidential protection, and prompted a nationwide crackdown on anarchists, including the exclusion of anarchists under the Immigration Act of 1903.
