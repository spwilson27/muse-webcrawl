Judith Jarvis Thomson

Judith Jarvis Thomson (October 4, 1929 – November 20, 2020) was an American philosopher known for her work in ethics and metaphysics. She taught at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology for most of her career.

Life and career

Thomson was born in New York City and educated at Barnard College, Cambridge University, and Columbia University, where she received her PhD in 1959. She joined the MIT faculty in 1963 and became a leading figure in analytic moral philosophy.

Work

Her 1971 essay "A Defense of Abortion," with its famous "violinist" thought experiment, argued that even granting fetal personhood, abortion can be morally permissible. In "The Trolley Problem" (1985) she developed Philippa Foot's problem into a central topic of moral theory, later refining her views in "Turning the Trolley" (2008). She also wrote extensively on rights, including the influential paper "Killing, Letting Die, and the Trolley Problem" and the book The Realm of Rights (1990), and on metaphysics, including a critique of David Lewis's four-dimensionalism.

Legacy

Thomson was a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and served as president of the American Philosophical Association. Her clear, example-driven style reshaped how analytic philosophers argue about moral questions.
