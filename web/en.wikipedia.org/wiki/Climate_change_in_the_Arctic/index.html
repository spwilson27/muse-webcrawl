Climate change in the Arctic

RegionArctic
Key effectsSea ice loss, permafrost thaw, warming amplification

Climate change in the Arctic refers to the effects of global warming in the Arctic region, which has been warming several times faster than the global average — a phenomenon known as Arctic (polar) amplification.

Sea ice

Arctic sea ice extent and thickness have declined substantially since satellite records began in the late 1970s, with summer ice shrinking markedly. The loss of bright ice lowers the region's albedo, reinforcing further warming.

Other effects

Permafrost is thawing, affecting infrastructure and releasing carbon; the Greenland ice sheet is losing mass; and ecosystems and Indigenous communities face shifting wildlife, erosion and changing hunting conditions. Declining ice is also opening Arctic shipping routes.
