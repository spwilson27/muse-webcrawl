Lidl is a German international discount supermarket chain that operates over 12,000 stores across Europe and the United States. It is part of the Schwarz Gruppe, the world's fourth-largest retailer by revenue, which also owns the hypermarket chain Kaufland. Lidl is a major rival of fellow German discounter Aldi in many markets.

History

The Schwarz family's wholesale business, Lidl & Schwarz, was founded in 1930. The modern Lidl discount concept was launched in 1973, when Dieter Schwarz opened the first Lidl discount store in Ludwigshafen, modelled on the Aldi concept. By the late 1980s Lidl had expanded beyond Germany, opening stores in France in 1989, and it reached the United Kingdom in 1994 and other European markets through the 1990s and 2000s. In June 2017, Lidl entered the United States market, opening its first stores on the East Coast.

Business model

Lidl follows the hard-discount model: a limited assortment of roughly 2,000 products per store (about 80 percent of them own-brand), high-volume purchasing, no-frills store layouts in which goods are often displayed in their shipping cartons, and weekly rotating non-food special offers — famously including tools, clothing, and kitchen appliances in the "middle of Lidl." Stores are typically staffed by small teams, and customers bag their own purchases.

Operations

Lidl competes on price with national supermarket chains and has taken significant market share in the UK, Ireland, and much of continental Europe. The company has also moved upmarket in selected ranges, expanding fresh bakery sections, organic lines, and award-winning own-label wines to attract a broader range of shoppers.
