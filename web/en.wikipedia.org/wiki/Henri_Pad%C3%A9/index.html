Henri Padé

Henri Eugène Padé (17 December 1863 – 9 July 1953) was a French mathematician best known for the Padé approximant, a rational-function approximation to a given function that often converges far beyond the radius of the function's Taylor series. His doctoral dissertation of 1892, written under Charles Hermite, laid the foundations of the subject.

Life and career

Padé studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris and spent a year in Leipzig and Göttingen, where he encountered the work of Felix Klein and Hermann Schwarz. Returning to Paris, he completed his thesis Sur la représentation approchée d'une fonction par des fractions rationnelles in 1892. He taught at the University of Lille, succeeding Émile Borel, and later held chairs at Poitiers and Besançon, becoming rector of the Academy of Besançon and finally of Dijon before retiring to Aix-en-Provence.

Padé approximants

Given a formal power series, the [L/M] Padé approximant is the rational function whose numerator and denominator have degrees L and M and whose Taylor expansion agrees with the series to the highest possible order. Padé organised the approximants into the "Padé table" and studied its structure for the exponential function in detail. The method was revived in the mid-20th century and is now a standard tool in numerical analysis, signal processing and theoretical physics for summing divergent series and continuing functions analytically beyond their natural boundaries.
