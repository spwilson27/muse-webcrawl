Common Security and Defence Policy

[Image: European Union flag in Brussels]

The Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP) is the European Union's (EU) course of action in the fields of defence and crisis management, and is a main component of the EU's Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP).

The CSDP involves the deployment of military or civilian missions to preserve peace, prevent conflict and strengthen international security in accordance with the principles of the United Nations Charter. Military missions are carried out by EU forces established with contributions from member states' armed forces. The CSDP also entails collective self-defence amongst member states as well as a Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO) in which 26 of the 27 national armed forces pursue structural integration.

History

The post-war movement for European integration first sought to create a European Defence Community in the early 1950s, but the plan failed when the French National Assembly refused to ratify the treaty in 1954. Defence cooperation subsequently developed largely through NATO and the Western European Union. The Maastricht Treaty of 1992 established the CFSP, and the Amsterdam Treaty of 1997 introduced the position of High Representative. Following the Franco-British Saint-Malo declaration of 1998, which declared that the Union must have "the capacity for autonomous action, backed up by credible military forces", the European Security and Defence Policy was launched at the Cologne European Council in 1999. It was renamed the Common Security and Defence Policy by the Treaty of Lisbon, which entered into force in 2009 and introduced a mutual defence clause (Article 42.7 TEU).

Structure

The CSDP is directed by the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, who also serves as a Vice-President of the European Commission. Political control and strategic direction of crisis-management operations are exercised by the Political and Security Committee (PSC), composed of member states' ambassadors. The European Union Military Committee (EUMC), composed of the chiefs of defence of the member states, provides military advice, while the European Union Military Staff (EUMS) plans and monitors operations. The European Defence Agency (EDA), established in 2004, supports the development of defence capabilities.

Operations

Since 2003 the EU has launched more than thirty CSDP missions and operations on three continents. The first was the EU Police Mission in Bosnia and Herzegovina, followed by the military Operation Concordia in North Macedonia. Notable operations include Operation Althea in Bosnia and Herzegovina, EU NAVFOR Atalanta counter-piracy operation off Somalia, EULEX Kosovo rule-of-law mission, and training missions in Mali, Somalia and the Central African Republic.

Permanent Structured Cooperation

PESCO was activated in December 2017 by 25 member states (Denmark joined in 2023 after abolishing its defence opt-out), committing participants to jointly develop defence capabilities, invest in shared projects and enhance the operational readiness of their armed forces.
