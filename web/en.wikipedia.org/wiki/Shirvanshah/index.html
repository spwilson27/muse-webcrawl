Shirvanshah

Shirvanshah (Persian: شروانشاه) was the title of the rulers of Shirvan, a historical region in the eastern Caucasus, in present-day Azerbaijan. A succession of dynasties bearing this title governed Shirvan, with varying degrees of independence, from the early Islamic period until the 16th century — one of the longest-lasting dynastic traditions in the Islamic world.

History

The title pre-dates Islam, deriving from Sasanian-era provincial governorships. After the Arab conquest, local Arab-descended dynasties such as the Yazidids ruled Shirvan, at times as vassals of caliphs, Seljuks, the Eldiguzids, Mongols, Timurids and the Qara Qoyunlu and Aq Qoyunlu confederations. In the 14th–15th centuries the Derbendid line made Baku their capital, where the Palace of the Shirvanshahs — a celebrated complex of palace, mosque, mausoleum and baths — was built.

End of the state

The Shirvanshah state was absorbed by the rising Safavid Empire: Shah Ismail I defeated the last effective Shirvanshah in 1500–1501, and although the family briefly retained local authority, Shirvan was fully incorporated into Safavid Persia in the following decades.
