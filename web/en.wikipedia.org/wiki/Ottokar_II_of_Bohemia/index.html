Ottokar II (Czech: Přemysl Otakar II.; c. 1233 – 26 August 1278), known as "the Iron and Golden King", was King of Bohemia from 1253 until his death, and also ruled Austria, Styria, Carinthia and Carniola, assembling the largest Přemyslid realm, stretching from the Baltic to the Adriatic.

Rise

The second son of Wenceslaus I, Ottokar became Margrave of Moravia and was acclaimed Duke of Austria in 1251, marrying Margaret of Austria to bolster his claim. He defeated Béla IV of Hungary at the Battle of Kressenbrunn (1260), gaining Styria, later added Carinthia and Carniola, and joined the Prussian Crusades — the city of Königsberg ("king's mountain") was named in his honour in 1255.

Imperial ambitions and fall

Ottokar sought the imperial crown but the electors chose the lesser-known Rudolf of Habsburg in 1273, fearing Ottokar's power. Refusing to recognise Rudolf or surrender his Austrian lands, Ottokar was placed under the imperial ban, lost his southern territories in 1276, and was killed at the Battle on the Marchfeld (Dürnkrut) on 26 August 1278.

Legacy

Ottokar founded numerous towns and castles, encouraged German settlement and mining in Bohemia, and is a major figure of Czech historical memory — celebrated by chroniclers and later dramatised by Grillparzer — even as his death opened the way for Habsburg rule in Austria.
