Cy-près doctrine

Cy-près allows a court to redirect a charitable gift "as near as possible" to the donor's intent.

The cy-près doctrine (from Norman French cy près comme possible, "as near as possible") is a legal doctrine in the law of trusts, particularly charitable trusts. When a charitable trust's original purpose becomes impossible, impracticable, or illegal to fulfil, a court may direct the trust's assets to a purpose as near as possible to the settlor's original charitable intention, rather than allowing the trust to fail.

Application

Traditionally, cy-près required a finding of general charitable intent on the part of the donor: if the donor intended only one specific purpose and that purpose failed, the gift would lapse and result back to the estate. Modern statutes in many jurisdictions have relaxed these requirements, allowing cy-près where the original purpose has been fulfilled, has ceased to be charitable, or where the funds exceed what the purpose requires.

Class actions

In the United States, the doctrine has also been applied in class-action settlements: where settlement funds cannot practicably be distributed to all class members — for example because individual shares are trivially small or members cannot be identified — courts may award the residue to charities whose work approximates the interests of the class. This use of cy-près in class actions has been both influential and controversial, attracting scrutiny from appellate courts.
