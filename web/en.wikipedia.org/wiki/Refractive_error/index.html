Refractive error

Refractive error
SpecialtyOphthalmology, optometry
SymptomsBlurred vision, double vision, headaches, eye strain
CausesShape of the eye, cornea or lens
TreatmentGlasses, contact lenses, refractive surgery

Refractive error is a problem with focusing light accurately on the retina due to the shape of the eye and/or the cornea. The most common types of refractive error are near-sightedness (myopia), far-sightedness (hyperopia), astigmatism, and presbyopia.

Near-sightedness results in far-away objects being blurry, far-sightedness results in close objects being blurry, astigmatism causes objects to appear stretched or blurry at all distances, and presbyopia results in a reduced ability to focus on close objects. Other symptoms may include double vision, headaches, and eye strain.

Causes

Near-sightedness is due to the length of the eyeball being too long, far-sightedness the eyeball being too short, astigmatism the cornea being the wrong shape, and presbyopia aging of the lens of the eye such that it cannot change shape sufficiently. Some refractive errors occur more often among those whose parents are affected. Diagnosis is by eye examination.

Treatment

Refractive errors are corrected with eyeglasses, contact lenses, or surgery. Eyeglasses are the easiest and safest method of correction. Contact lenses can provide a wider field of vision; however they are associated with a risk of infection. Refractive surgery permanently changes the shape of the cornea.

Epidemiology

The number of people globally with refractive errors has been estimated at one to two billion. Rates vary between regions of the world with about 25% of Europeans and 80% of Asians affected. Near-sightedness is the most common disorder. Refractive errors are the first common cause of visual impairment and second most common cause of visual loss.
