Impact investing

Impact investing is investment made with the intention to generate positive, measurable social and environmental impact alongside a financial return. The term was coined in 2007 at a Rockefeller Foundation gathering, and the field is coordinated in part by the Global Impact Investing Network (GIIN), founded in 2009. Impact investments target enterprises addressing challenges in sectors such as renewable energy, affordable housing, healthcare, education, microfinance, and sustainable agriculture.

Impact capital often finances clean energy, housing, and financial inclusion.

Characteristics

The GIIN identifies four core characteristics: intentionality — the investor intends to achieve social or environmental impact; return expectations — investments are expected to return at least the capital, and may target below-market or market-rate returns; measurement — impact is tracked against metrics, commonly using the IRIS+ catalog and increasingly aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals; and range of asset classes, from cash and fixed income to private equity and venture capital.

History and size

Impact investing grew out of older traditions of socially responsible investing, community development finance, and microfinance. The GIIN estimated the market at about US$715 billion in 2020, rising to over US$1 trillion in its 2022 sizing report. Major participants include foundations deploying program-related investments, development finance institutions, pension funds, and specialized fund managers such as LeapFrog Investments and responsAbility.

Criticism

Skeptics question whether claimed impacts are additional to what ordinary investment would achieve, whether impact measurement is rigorous or largely self-reported, and whether the label is used for "impact washing" — marketing conventional funds as transformative. Some critics also argue that expecting market returns from serving the poorest populations is unrealistic, and that subsidy or philanthropy remains necessary for the deepest-impact work.
