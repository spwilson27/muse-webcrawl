The Province of Maryland was an English, later British, colony in North America from 1634 until 1776, when it joined the other Thirteen Colonies in rebellion and became the U.S. state of Maryland. It was founded under a charter granted by Charles I to Cecil Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore, whose family ruled it as Lords Proprietor for most of its history.

Founding

The charter of 1632 granted the Calverts the territory between the Potomac River and the 40th parallel. The first settlers, a mix of Catholic gentry and Protestant labourers, sailed on the Ark and the Dove and landed in March 1634, founding St. Mary's City. Maryland became notable as a haven for English Catholics and, in 1649, passed a Toleration Act guaranteeing freedom of worship to Trinitarian Christians.

Government and society

Proprietary rule was interrupted several times: by Puritan settlers in the 1650s, and after the Glorious Revolution, when Maryland became a royal colony (1692–1715) before being restored to the Calverts, who had converted to Anglicanism. The economy rested on tobacco, cultivated first by indentured servants and increasingly by enslaved Africans. The capital moved to Annapolis in 1695.

Revolution

Maryland resisted the Stamp Act and hosted heated disputes over proprietor fees, but moved cautiously toward independence. In 1776 the last proprietary governor, Robert Eden, departed peacefully, and a state convention drafted Maryland's first constitution.
