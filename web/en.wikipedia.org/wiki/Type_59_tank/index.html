The Type 59 (Chinese: 59式; pinyin: Wǔ jiǔ shì; industrial designation: WZ120) main battle tank is a Chinese-produced version of the Soviet T-54A tank, an early model of the ubiquitous T-54/55 series. The first vehicles were produced in 1958 and it entered service in 1959, with serial production beginning in 1963. About 9,500 of the tanks were produced by the time production ended in 1985, and roughly 5,500 remain in service with the People's Liberation Army.

Development

After the signing of the Sino-Soviet Treaty of Friendship, Alliance and Mutual Assistance in 1950, the Soviet Union agreed to assist China in building a tank factory. In 1956, Factory 617 (now the Inner Mongolia First Machinery Group Corporation) was established in Baotou, Inner Mongolia. Initially the factory produced Soviet-supplied components assembled in China; the design was essentially a copy of the T-54A, incorporating simplifications suited to Chinese production, such as the deletion of the gun stabilizer and night-vision equipment on the first production vehicles.

Variants

Numerous variants were produced, including the Type 59-I with an improved 100 mm gun and simplified fire-control system, the Type 59-II with a 105 mm rifled gun based on the Royal Ordnance L7, and the Type 59D series of the 1990s with explosive reactive armour, a new engine, and improved fire control. Export variants were widely sold, and the type saw service with numerous countries, including Pakistan, Bangladesh, Iran, Iraq, North Korea, Vietnam, and several African states. Upgraded derivatives such as the Bangladeshi Durjoy and the Sudanese Al-Zubair illustrate the design's longevity.
