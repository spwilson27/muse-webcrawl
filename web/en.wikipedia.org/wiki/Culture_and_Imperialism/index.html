Culture and Imperialism


First edition cover of Culture and Imperialism (1993)

Culture and Imperialism is a 1993 book by Edward Said, the Palestinian-American literary theorist, published as a sequel and companion to his landmark Orientalism (1978). In it, Said examines how the culture of the imperial West — above all the nineteenth- and twentieth-century novel — both reflected and reinforced European and American imperial domination, and how colonised peoples resisted and answered back through their own literatures.

Content

Said develops the method of "contrapuntal reading": canonical works such as Jane Austen's Mansfield Park, Joseph Conrad's Heart of Darkness, Rudyard Kipling's Kim, Albert Camus's The Stranger, and Verdi's opera Aida are read together with the colonial histories (Antiguan slave plantations, the Congo, British India, French Algeria, Egypt) that form their usually unacknowledged background. The book then turns to resistance literature and criticism — Yeats, Fanon, C. L. R. James, and the literatures of decolonisation — and to the contemporary American empire and media culture of the Gulf War era.

Reception

The book was widely read and debated, consolidating Said's role as a founder of postcolonial studies. Admirers praised its erudition and its insistence that culture and empire be studied together; critics charged that Said's readings were selective or reductive, exaggerated literature's complicity with empire, or neglected economic and political causes. It remains a standard text in postcolonial literary studies.
