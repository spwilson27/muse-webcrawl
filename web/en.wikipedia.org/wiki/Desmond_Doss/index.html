Desmond Doss

Born7 February 1919, Lynchburg, Virginia, U.S.
Died23 March 2006 (age 87)
AllegianceUnited States Army
AwardsMedal of Honor, Bronze Star (2), Purple Heart (3)

Desmond Thomas Doss (1919–2006) was a United States Army corporal who served as a combat medic in World War II. A Seventh-day Adventist conscientious objector who refused to carry or use a weapon, he became the first conscientious objector to receive the Medal of Honor.

[Image: images/desmond-doss-medal.jpg — Desmond Doss receiving the Medal of Honor from President Truman, 1945]

Service

Doss enlisted in 1942 and, despite ridicule and attempts to discharge him, served as a medic with the 77th Infantry Division at Guam, Leyte and Okinawa. During the assault on the Maeda Escarpment (Hacksaw Ridge) in May 1945, under heavy fire he rescued an estimated 75 wounded men, lowering them one by one down the cliff. He was wounded several times.

Later life and portrayal

President Harry S. Truman presented him the Medal of Honor on 12 October 1945. Ill health from his wounds shaped his post-war life in Georgia and Alabama. His story was told in the documentary The Conscientious Objector (2004) and the feature film Hacksaw Ridge (2016), starring Andrew Garfield.
