Matthew K. Franklin

Matthew Keith Franklin is an American cryptographer and computer scientist, known for fundamental contributions to public-key cryptography, including pairing-based cryptography. He is a professor of computer science at the University of California, Davis, and previously worked at Bellcore (later Telcordia Technologies) and at Xerox PARC.

Education and career

Franklin received his bachelor's degree from Pomona College and his Ph.D. in computer science from Columbia University in 1994, under the supervision of Zvi Galil and Moti Yung. His research has addressed the theory and practice of cryptography, including secure communication, electronic voting, and cryptographic protocol design.

Boneh–Franklin identity-based encryption

Franklin is best known as co-author, with Dan Boneh, of the Boneh–Franklin scheme (2001), the first practical and fully functional identity-based encryption system. The scheme uses bilinear pairings on elliptic curves — specifically the Weil pairing — to turn an arbitrary string, such as an email address, into a public key, realising a concept proposed by Adi Shamir in 1984. The paper received the Gödel Prize in 2016, awarded to Boneh, Franklin, and (for related independent work) Antoine Joux, and is among the most cited works in modern cryptography.

Other work

With Michael K. Reiter, Franklin designed one of the first secure electronic voting protocols based on threshold cryptography. He also introduced, with Moti Yung, influential models of fault-tolerant secure computation against mobile adversaries ("proactive security"). Franklin has served as editor-in-chief of the Journal of Cryptology and is a fellow of the Association for Computing Machinery.
