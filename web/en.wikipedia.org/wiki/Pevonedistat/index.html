Pevonedistat (development codes MLN4924 and TAK-924) is an investigational anticancer drug developed by Millennium Pharmaceuticals, a subsidiary of Takeda. It is a first-in-class inhibitor of the NEDD8-activating enzyme (NAE), the enzyme that initiates neddylation, a post-translational modification pathway related to ubiquitination.

Mechanism of action

Neddylation activates the cullin-RING ubiquitin ligases (CRLs), which tag many regulatory proteins for degradation by the proteasome. By binding NAE and forming a covalent NEDD8–pevonedistat adduct that the enzyme cannot process, pevonedistat shuts down CRL activity. Substrates that would normally be destroyed — including cell-cycle regulators such as CDT1 — accumulate. The accumulation of CDT1 causes DNA re-replication, DNA damage and, in cancer cells, apoptosis. Because malignant cells divide rapidly and are especially dependent on protein turnover, they are preferentially affected.

Clinical development

Pevonedistat was studied primarily in haematological malignancies, including acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) and higher-risk myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), often in combination with the hypomethylating agent azacitidine. The phase 3 PANTHER trial evaluated pevonedistat plus azacitidine as first-line treatment for higher-risk MDS, chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia and low-blast AML. In 2021 Takeda announced that the trial did not meet its primary endpoint of event-free survival at a preplanned interim analysis. Development continued in other settings and combinations, including solid tumours.

Adverse effects

Reported toxicities include fatigue, nausea, myelosuppression (anaemia, neutropenia and thrombocytopenia), febrile neutropenia, and elevations in liver enzymes. Early trials also observed hepatic adverse events that prompted dose and schedule adjustments.
