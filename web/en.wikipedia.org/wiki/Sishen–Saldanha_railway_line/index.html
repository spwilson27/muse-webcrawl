The Sishen–Saldanha railway line, also known as the Ore Export Line (OREX), is an 861-kilometre heavy-haul railway in South Africa linking the iron-ore mines at Sishen in the Northern Cape to the port of Saldanha Bay on the Atlantic coast. Operated by Transnet Freight Rail, it runs some of the longest and heaviest trains in the world.

Construction

The line was built by the state steel company ISCOR in the 1970s to export high-grade hematite ore from Sishen, then the largest open-pit iron mine in the world. It opened in 1976, together with the deep-water bulk terminal at Saldanha, the only natural deep-water port on South Africa's west coast.

Operations

Ore trains are about 4 kilometres long, with roughly 340 wagons hauled by multiple locomotives, carrying some 40,000 tonnes of ore per trip. The line is electrified at 50 kV AC, the highest railway electrification voltage in general use.

Economy

The line is a critical export artery for South African iron ore, moving tens of millions of tonnes per year, primarily to steel mills in Asia and Europe.
