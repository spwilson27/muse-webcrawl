Boudin

TypeSausage
RegionsFrance, Louisiana (Cajun country)
Main ingredientsPork, rice, onions, peppers, seasoning
VarietiesBoudin blanc, boudin noir, Cajun boudin

Boudin (pronounced "boo-DAN") refers to several types of sausage found in French and French-influenced cuisines, most famously in the Cajun cuisine of Louisiana, where it consists of pork, cooked rice, onions, green peppers, and seasonings stuffed into a casing.

Cajun boudin

In southwest Louisiana, boudin is a staple snack food sold at gas stations, grocery stores, and specialty meat shops, often eaten hot by squeezing the filling from the casing. It traditionally uses pork shoulder and a portion of pork liver, mixed with rice — a thrifty way to extend meat — plus the "holy trinity" of Cajun cooking: onion, bell pepper, and celery. Variations include seafood boudin made with crawfish or shrimp, and boudin balls, in which the filling is breaded and deep-fried.

French varieties

In France, boudin blanc is a white sausage of finely ground pork, chicken, or veal with milk and breadcrumbs, traditionally served at Christmas. Boudin noir is a blood sausage made with pork blood, fat, and onions, akin to black pudding.

Culture

The town of Scott, Louisiana, has declared itself the "Boudin Capital of the World," and annual festivals in the region celebrate the sausage with cook-offs and boudin-eating contests.
