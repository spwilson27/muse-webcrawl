Trivia

Trivia is information and data that are considered of little value or importance — "useless" facts valued mainly for entertainment. The word derives from the Latin trivium, the lower division of the medieval liberal arts (grammar, logic, and rhetoric), and from trivia, "crossroads" places where ordinary, commonplace talk occurred; it acquired its modern sense of insignificant detail in the early 20th century.

Trivia as entertainment

The pursuit of trivia became a popular pastime with the rise of quiz games and competitions. The board game Trivial Pursuit (created 1979, released 1981) sold over 100 million copies and defined the modern trivia game. Pub quizzes, radio and television quiz shows such as Jeopardy!, and trivia nights in bars built a durable culture around competitive recall of facts. Logan Pearsall Smith's 1902 book Trivia, a collection of aphorisms, helped shift the word's literary meaning toward delightful minutiae.
