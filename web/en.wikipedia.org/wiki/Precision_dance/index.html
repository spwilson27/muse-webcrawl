Precision dance

Precision dance is a form of dance in which a group of dancers perform a sequence of steps in exact unison, emphasizing synchronized timing, spacing, and formation changes. It is closely associated with precision dance teams, drill teams, and competitive kick lines, and is distinguished from other ensemble dance forms by the degree to which every performer's movement must match every other performer's movement in angle, height, and timing.

History

Precision dance developed in the United States in the early twentieth century, drawing on military drill, chorus-line entertainment, and collegiate marching traditions. The Rockettes of Radio City Music Hall, founded in 1925 as the Missouri Rockets and later based in New York, became the best-known precision dance troupe, famous for their eye-high kick line performed in perfect unison. Through the mid-twentieth century, high school and university drill teams spread the form across the country, particularly in Texas and the Midwest.

Technique

Precision routines typically feature kick lines, pinwheels, ripple effects, and geometric formation changes executed on counts. Dancers train to standardize posture, arm placement, head direction, and kick height so that the line reads as a single unit. Judging criteria in competition include synchronization, execution, choreography, and presentation.

Precision dance teams

School-based precision teams, often called drill teams or dance teams, perform at sporting events and compete at regional and national championships. In Texas, large high school drill teams such as the Kilgore College Rangerettes (founded 1940) helped define the genre, combining precision kick routines with pom and jazz styles.
