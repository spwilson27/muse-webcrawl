Big Pit National Coal Museum

Big Pit National Coal Museum (Welsh: Pwll Mawr Amgueddfa Lofaol Cymru) is an industrial heritage museum in Blaenavon, Torfaen, Wales, forming part of Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales. Housed in a working coal mine that closed in 1980, it opened to visitors in 1983 and is renowned for its underground tours led by former miners.

The winding gear of Big Pit, Blaenavon.

History

Big Pit was sunk in the 1860s–1880s and was one of the coal mines of the Blaenavon iron and coal district. At its peak it employed well over a thousand men. After closure in 1980 it was preserved as a museum, retaining its winding engine, blacksmith's forge, stables and miners' baths. In 2000 the surrounding Blaenavon Industrial Landscape was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The museum

Visitors descend about 90 metres (300 feet) in the cage to tour the underground workings with ex-miner guides, wearing helmets, lamps and battery belts. Surface attractions include the Pithead Baths exhibition, the winding engine house and a recreated coal dramway. The museum interprets the social history of the South Wales coalfield as well as mining technology and safety.
