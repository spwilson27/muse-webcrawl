Mont Aiguille

Mont Aiguille is a distinctive flat-topped mountain in the Vercors massif of the French Prealps, in the commune of Chichilianne in the Isère department. Rising to 2,086 metres (6,844 ft), it is a limestone "inverted ship's prow" of rock separated from the main Vercors plateau by erosion.

First ascent

Mont Aiguille is celebrated in the history of mountaineering: on 26 June 1492, Antoine de Ville, lord of Dompjulien, climbed it on the orders of King Charles VIII of France, using ladders and ropes. The ascent is often regarded as the birth of mountaineering as an activity undertaken for its own sake, and the mountain was long considered one of the marvels of the Dauphiné.
