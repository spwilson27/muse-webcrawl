The Discovery Program is a series of Solar System exploration missions funded by the United States National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) through its Planetary Missions Program Office. The cost of each mission is capped at a lower level than missions from NASA's New Frontiers or Flagship programs. As a result, Discovery missions tend to be more focused on a specific scientific goal rather than serving a general purpose.

The program was founded in 1992 under NASA Administrator Daniel Goldin, embodying his "faster, better, cheaper" philosophy. Discovery missions are proposed by teams led by a principal investigator (PI) and are selected through open competition, unlike flagship missions whose targets are directed by NASA.

Program philosophy

Discovery missions are cost-capped (roughly US$500 million in recent rounds, excluding launch vehicle and operations) and must be led by a single principal investigator from a university, NASA center, or industry. The competitive selection model is credited with producing scientifically productive missions at modest cost, and with broadening participation in planetary science beyond NASA's own centers.
