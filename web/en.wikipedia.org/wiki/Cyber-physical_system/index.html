Cyber-physical system

Placeholder image: Cyber-physical system

A cyber-physical system (CPS) is a mechanism controlled or monitored by computer-based algorithms, tightly integrated with the Internet and its users. In cyber-physical systems, physical and software components are deeply intertwined, each operating on different spatial and temporal scales, exhibiting multiple distinct behavioral modalities, and interacting with each other in ways that change with context.

Overview

Examples of CPS include smart grid, autonomous automobile systems, medical monitoring, industrial control systems (ICS), robotics systems, recycling systems, and automatic pilot avionics. CPS involves transdisciplinary approaches, merging theories of cybernetics, mechatronics, design, and process science. The term was coined around 2006, in connection with workshops and programs of the U.S. National Science Foundation.

Relation to embedded systems and IoT

Cyber-physical systems build on embedded systems but emphasize the networking and feedback-loop coupling between computation and the physical world, often with real-time constraints and safety-critical requirements. The concept overlaps with the Internet of things (IoT), though CPS typically stresses coordinated control and computation rather than connectivity alone. In manufacturing, the related concept underlies "Industry 4.0" and the Industrial Internet of Things.

Challenges

Key research challenges include designing for safety and security (since attacks can have physical consequences, as demonstrated by incidents such as Stuxnet), verification and validation of hybrid continuous-discrete behavior, timing predictability, energy efficiency, and resilience to faults and uncertainty.

Applications

  • Transportation: autonomous vehicles, intelligent traffic management, fly-by-wire aircraft
  • Energy: smart grids, renewable integration, demand response
  • Healthcare: medical devices, remote patient monitoring, robotic surgery
  • Infrastructure: smart buildings, water distribution, structural monitoring
