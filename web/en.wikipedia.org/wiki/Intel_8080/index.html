Intel 8080

Intel 8080
Launched: April 1974
Designer: Intel (Federico Faggin, Masatoshi Shima)
Word size: 8-bit
Successor: Intel 8085; influenced x86 via 8086

The Intel 8080 is an 8-bit microprocessor released by Intel in April 1974. It was a greatly expanded and more usable successor to the 8008, and is widely credited with launching the personal computer revolution: the Altair 8800 (1975) was built around it.

Design

The 8080 had a 16-bit address bus supporting 64 KB of memory, seven 8-bit registers, a 16-bit stack pointer and program counter, and a richer instruction set than its predecessor, in a 40-pin DIP package. Unlike the 8086 that followed, it was not source-compatible with x86, though its design strongly influenced it through the work of its designers.

Use

Beyond the Altair, the 8080 powered the IMSAI 8080, many CP/M machines, arcade games such as Space Invaders, and early word processors and terminals. The Zilog Z80 was a compatible and improved competitor that shared much of its market.
