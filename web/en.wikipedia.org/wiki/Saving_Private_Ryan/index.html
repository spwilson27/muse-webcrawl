Saving Private Ryan is a 1998 American epic war film directed by Steven Spielberg and written by Robert Rodat. Set during and after the Normandy landings of the Second World War, it follows Captain John Miller (Tom Hanks) and a squad of U.S. Army Rangers ordered to find and bring home Private James Francis Ryan (Matt Damon), the last surviving of four brothers, three of whom have been killed in action. The film is renowned for its graphic, realistic portrayal of combat, particularly its 23-minute depiction of the Omaha Beach assault.

The Omaha Beach sequence is widely regarded as the most realistic battle depiction in cinema.

Plot

After surviving the D-Day landing at Omaha Beach, Captain Miller is assigned a squad to locate Private Ryan, a paratrooper of the 101st Airborne dropped behind enemy lines whose three brothers have died within days of one another. Journeying through war-torn Normandy, the squad questions the value of risking eight lives for one; they eventually find Ryan defending a strategically vital bridge in the town of Ramelle and join him in a costly last stand against German armour.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing $482 million worldwide and winning five Academy Awards, including Best Director for Spielberg, though it controversially lost Best Picture to Shakespeare in Love. Veterans praised its authenticity, and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs issued advisories for viewers with PTSD. It is consistently ranked among the greatest war films ever made and helped revive popular interest in the Second World War, preceding the series Band of Brothers. The story was loosely inspired by the real Niland brothers and the Sole Survivor Policy.
