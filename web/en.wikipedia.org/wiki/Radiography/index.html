Radiography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Radiography is an imaging technique using X-rays, gamma rays or similar ionising radiation to view the internal structure of an object. The radiation passes through the object and is captured by a detector — film or a digital sensor — producing a two-dimensional image in which denser materials appear lighter.

Medical radiography

Discovered in effect by Wilhelm Röntgen in 1895, the X-ray image became the first great tool of medical imaging. Radiographs are used to examine bones for fractures, the chest for pneumonia and heart failure, the teeth, and — with contrast agents — parts of the digestive and vascular systems. Mammography is a specialised low-dose application for breast screening. Although CT and MRI have taken over many tasks, plain radiography remains the most common imaging examination worldwide because it is fast, cheap and low-dose.

Industrial radiography

Radiography is also a non-destructive testing method: welds, castings, pipelines and aircraft components are radiographed to reveal internal cracks and voids, and cargo and baggage are screened at ports and airports.

Safety

Ionising radiation carries a small stochastic risk, so exposures are justified clinically and kept as low as reasonably achievable (ALARA), with shielding and dose monitoring for staff.
