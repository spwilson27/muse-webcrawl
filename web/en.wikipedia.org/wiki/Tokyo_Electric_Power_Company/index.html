The Tokyo Electric Power Company Holdings, Incorporated (東京電力ホールディングス株式会社), also known as TEPCO, is a Japanese electric utility holding company servicing Japan's Kantō region, Yamanashi Prefecture, and the eastern portion of Shizuoka Prefecture. This area includes Tokyo. Its headquarters are located in Uchisaiwaicho, Chiyoda, Tokyo; international branch offices exist in Washington, D.C., and London. It is a founding member of strategic consortiums related to energy innovation and research, such as JERA.

History

TEPCO was established on 1 May 1951 during the American occupation's reorganization of Japan's electric industry, inheriting the assets of the Kantō distribution company of the wartime state monopoly. It grew with Japan's postwar economy to become the country's largest power utility and one of the largest in the world, operating a large fleet of fossil-fuel, hydroelectric, and nuclear plants, including the Kashiwazaki-Kariwa Nuclear Power Plant, the world's largest nuclear generating station by capacity.

Fukushima Daiichi disaster

On 11 March 2011, the Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami cut external power to TEPCO's Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant and flooded its backup generators, causing loss of cooling, meltdowns in three reactors, and hydrogen explosions — the most severe nuclear accident since Chernobyl (1986). TEPCO was heavily criticized for its preparedness and handling of the crisis. In 2012, facing enormous compensation and decommissioning costs, the company was effectively nationalized through a government-backed fund taking a majority voting stake. Decommissioning Fukushima Daiichi is projected to take decades, and TEPCO remains responsible for compensation to evacuees and for the treated-water discharge programme that began in 2023.

Restructuring

In April 2016, TEPCO adopted a holding-company structure, spinning its fuel and thermal generation, power transmission and distribution, and retail businesses into separate subsidiaries (TEPCO Fuel & Power, TEPCO Power Grid, and TEPCO Energy Partner). The company has sought to restart reactors at Kashiwazaki-Kariwa amid regulatory review and local debate.
