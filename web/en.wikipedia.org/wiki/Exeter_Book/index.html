Exeter Book

The Exeter Book (Exeter Cathedral Library MS 3501) is a tenth-century anthology of Old English poetry, one of the four major poetic codices of Anglo-Saxon literature, together with the Junius manuscript, the Vercelli Book, and the Nowell Codex (which contains Beowulf). It has been held at Exeter Cathedral since it was donated by Bishop Leofric in the 11th century.

Contents

The book contains some 30 poems and nearly 100 riddles. Notable poems include The Wanderer, The Seafarer, Widsith, Deor, The Wife's Lament, Wulf and Eadwacer, and religious works such as Christ I–III, Guthlac, and The Phoenix. No titles or author names are given in the manuscript; all poems are anonymous.

History and condition

The manuscript was copied by a single scribe, probably around 960–990. Its survival is remarkable given visible damage: burn marks (apparently from use as a stand or board), knife cuts, and staining from a liquid vessel set on it. It was recognized as an important poetic collection in the 19th century and first published in 1842 by Benjamin Thorpe.
