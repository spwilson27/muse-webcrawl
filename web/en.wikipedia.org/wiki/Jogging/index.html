Jogging is a form of running at a slow or leisurely pace, undertaken mainly for exercise and fitness rather than competition. The boundary between jogging and running is not precisely defined, but jogging is generally taken to be slower than about 9.7 kilometres per hour (6 mph) and is characterised by a shorter stride and an upright posture intended to be sustainable over long distances.

History

Running for health had advocates as early as the seventeenth century, but the modern jogging movement began in New Zealand in the early 1960s, when coach Arthur Lydiard founded a jogging club in Auckland and promoted the activity for middle-aged former athletes. Oregon coach Bill Bowerman encountered Lydiard's methods in New Zealand and popularized them in the United States with his 1966 booklet Jogging, which sold over a million copies. Jim Fixx's The Complete Book of Running (1977) helped spark the 1970s running boom, and jogging became a mass-participation activity in many countries.

Health effects

Jogging is a moderate-intensity aerobic exercise associated with improved cardiovascular fitness and reduced risk of mortality; large cohort studies have found that even modest amounts of regular jogging correlate with gains in life expectancy. Like other forms of running it can stress the knees, ankles and feet, and beginners are advised to build up distance gradually. Jogging can be done almost anywhere, alone or in groups, and is the basis of community events such as weekly timed 5 km parkruns.
