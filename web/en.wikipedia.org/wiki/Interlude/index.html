Interlude

An interlude, in theatre and music, is a short piece performed between the parts of a longer work. The term comes from the Latin interludium, "a play between".

An entr'acte or interlude fills the interval between acts.

In theatre

In English theatre history, interludes were short comic or moral plays performed at court or in great households during the early Tudor period, often between the courses of a banquet or between longer performances. Writers such as John Heywood composed interludes in the early 16th century, and the form bridged medieval morality plays and Elizabethan drama. In modern usage, an interlude may be a brief scene or entertainment inserted between the acts of a play, related to the entr'acte in French theatre.

In music

In music, an interlude is a connecting passage between sections of a larger composition: an instrumental piece between verses of a hymn or psalm, a short organ piece between liturgical items (as in the French organ tradition's interludes), or bridging material in an opera or album. In popular music albums, an interlude is a short track, often instrumental or spoken, that links longer songs.

Other uses

By extension, "interlude" denotes any brief interruption or pause in a longer sequence of events, such as a peaceful interval between wars.
