Lion Rampant

The Lion Rampant is the Royal Banner of Scotland, a heraldic flag depicting a red lion rampant on a gold field within a red double border decorated with fleurs-de-lis. It is the banner of the monarchs of Scotland, traditionally associated with William the Lion in the 12th century, and its use is legally restricted under Scots heraldic law.

History

The lion rampant arms are attributed to William I ("the Lion"), who reigned 1165–1214, though the earliest surviving depiction is on the seal of Alexander II in 1222. The flag forms the second quarter of the Royal Standard of the United Kingdom used in Scotland, and the first and fourth quarters in the version used elsewhere. Because it is the sovereign's personal banner, flying it without authority is technically an offence administered by the Court of the Lord Lyon, though it is widely flown informally by the public, notably at Scottish sporting events.

Design

The banner is blazoned: Or, a lion rampant Gules armed and langued Azure within a double tressure flory-counter-flory of the second.
