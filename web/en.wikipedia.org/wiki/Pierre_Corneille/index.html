Pierre Corneille

Pierre Corneille (6 June 1606 – 1 October 1684) was a French tragedian, generally regarded as the founder of classical French tragedy and one of the three great seventeenth-century French dramatists, alongside Molière and Jean Racine. His best-known play is Le Cid (1637), whose success — and the controversy over its observance of the classical unities — helped shape the rules of French drama.

Life

Corneille was born in Rouen into a prosperous bourgeois family and educated by the Jesuits. He studied law and practised as a civil servant in Rouen while writing his first plays, beginning with the comedy Mélite (1629). Early successes in comedy and tragicomedy, including Clitandre and La Place Royale, brought him the patronage of Cardinal Richelieu, who employed him among the "Five Poets" who versified the Cardinal's plots — a collaboration that ended in disagreement.

Le Cid and the quarrel

Le Cid, first performed in late 1636 and published in 1637, was an enormous popular triumph. Adapted from Guillén de Castro's Las mocedades del Cid, it tells of Rodrigue, torn between love for Chimène and his duty to avenge his father's honour by killing hers. Critics objected that it violated the unities and decorum, prompting the Querelle du Cid; the newly founded Académie française, at Richelieu's urging, issued a judgment censuring the play's irregularities while acknowledging its beauties. Corneille withdrew for several years, returning with a sequence of classical tragedies.

Major tragedies

Between 1640 and 1643 Corneille wrote his great Roman and classical tragedies: Horace, Cinna (on clemency), and Polyeucte martyr (a Christian tragedy). Later works include Rodogune (1644), Nicomède (1651), and the comedy Le Menteur. After the failure of Pertharite (1652) he retired from the stage for seven years, then returned under the patronage of Fouquet and later Louis XIV, though his later plays were overshadowed by those of Racine. He was elected to the Académie française in 1647. His younger brother Thomas Corneille was also a successful dramatist.

Style and themes

Corneille's heroes are driven by will and reason, choosing duty, honour and gloire over passion — a contrast often drawn with Racine, whose characters are governed by passion. His verse, in rhyming alexandrine couplets, is noted for its rhetorical power and sententious maxims.
