Johann Nepomuk Maelzel

Born: 15 August 1772, Regensburg

Died: 21 July 1838 (aged 65), at sea near La Guaira, Venezuela

Occupation: Inventor, engineer, showman

Known for: Metronome; exhibiting the Mechanical Turk

Johann Nepomuk Maelzel (or Mälzel; 15 August 1772 – 21 July 1838) was a German inventor, engineer and showman, best known for patenting the metronome and for exhibiting the chess-playing automaton known as the Mechanical Turk.

Inventions

Maelzel built musical automatons, including the panharmonicon, a mechanical orchestra for which Beethoven composed Wellington's Victory in 1813, and devised an ear trumpet for the increasingly deaf composer. Their friendship collapsed in a dispute over the proceeds of the work's performances. In 1815 Maelzel patented a pyramid-shaped clockwork metronome based on designs by Dietrich Nikolaus Winkel, adding the tempo scale still known by his name ("M.M." = Maelzel's metronome).

The Turk and later years

Maelzel acquired Wolfgang von Kempelen's chess automaton, the Turk, and toured with it through Europe and the Americas for two decades, dying aboard ship off Venezuela in 1838 while returning from Cuba.
