Deepfake

Deepfakes (a portmanteau of "deep learning" and "fake") are synthetic media that depict a person in an image, video, or audio recording doing or saying something they did not actually do or say, generated using artificial intelligence techniques such as generative adversarial networks (GANs) and, more recently, diffusion models. The term originated in 2017 on Reddit, where a user posted face-swap videos superimposing celebrities' faces onto pornographic performers.

Technology

Classic deepfake creation trains autoencoder or GAN architectures on images of a target's face, then decodes one person's facial movements with another's identity. Later methods include face reenactment (driving a target's expressions with a source actor), lip-sync models, neural rendering, and voice cloning from short audio samples. Tools such as FaceSwap, DeepFaceLab, and commercial apps progressively lowered the skill and data needed, and text-to-video and audio models have widened deepfakes beyond face swapping entirely.

Uses and harms

Benign uses include film production (de-aging and digital doubles), satire, education, and accessibility. Documented harms include non-consensual intimate imagery — which studies have found constitutes the overwhelming majority of deepfake videos online — fraud (such as a 2024 Hong Kong case in which an employee was tricked by a video conference of deepfake executives into transferring about US$25 million), political disinformation, and impersonation of public figures. The existence of deepfakes also produces a "liar's dividend": genuine recordings can be dismissed as fakes.

Detection and responses

Detection methods analyze visual artifacts, physiological signals (e.g., blinking, pulse-related color changes), and audio inconsistencies, but detectors typically lag generator quality. Provenance approaches, such as the C2PA standard and content credentials, cryptographically sign media at capture. Legislatures in the United States, the European Union, China, South Korea, and elsewhere have enacted laws addressing non-consensual imagery, election deepfakes, and labeling of AI-generated content.
