C*-algebra

A C*-algebra (pronounced "C-star") is a Banach algebra together with an involution satisfying the C* identity. C*-algebras were first considered by Gelfand and Naimark in 1943 and are a central tool in functional analysis and mathematical physics.

Definition

A C*-algebra is a complex Banach algebra A with an involution * such that for all elements x, ||x*x|| = ||x||². The Gelfand–Naimark theorem states that every C*-algebra is isometrically *-isomorphic to an algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space; commutative C*-algebras correspond, via Gelfand duality, to spaces of continuous functions on locally compact Hausdorff spaces.

Applications

C*-algebras provide the mathematical framework for quantum mechanics, where observables are modelled by self-adjoint operators, and are important in representation theory, noncommutative geometry and quantum field theory.
