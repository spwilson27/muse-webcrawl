Emagic was a German music software and hardware company based in Rellingen near Hamburg, best known for developing the Logic digital audio workstation. Founded in 1992 by former employees of C-Lab — the company behind Creator and Notator for the Atari ST — Emagic built Logic into one of the leading MIDI sequencers and audio production programs before being acquired by Apple in 2002, after which Logic became the Apple product Logic Pro.

History

Emagic's founders, including Gerhard Lengeling and Chris Adam, had created Notator, the pattern-based sequencer used by many professional musicians on the Atari ST. With Emagic they launched Logic in 1993 for Atari, Macintosh, and later Windows, adding digital audio recording, virtual instruments, and effects plug-ins such as the EXS24 sampler and ES1/ES2 synthesizers. The company also made audio and MIDI hardware, including the Audiowerk cards and the Unitor and AMT MIDI interfaces.

Apple acquisition

On 1 July 2002 Apple acquired Emagic for a reported sum of around US$30 million. Apple promptly discontinued the Windows version of Logic — at the time used by an estimated large minority of its customer base — causing considerable controversy among professional users, many of whom migrated to the Mac platform or to rival workstations. Under Apple, Logic's technology formed the basis of GarageBand, and Emagic's team in Germany continued to develop Logic Pro and MainStage as an Apple division for many years.
