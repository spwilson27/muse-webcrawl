Thomas R. Perrotta (born August 13, 1961) is an American novelist and screenwriter best known for the novels Election (1998) and Little Children (2004), both adapted into acclaimed films, and The Leftovers (2011), which he adapted with Damon Lindelof into the HBO television series of the same name.

Early life and education

Perrotta was born in Newark, New Jersey, and raised in nearby Garwood, the son of Italian-American parents. He graduated from Yale University in 1983 and earned a master's degree in English/creative writing from Syracuse University, where he studied with Tobias Wolff. His story collection Bad Haircut: Stories of the Seventies was published in 1994.

Career

Perrotta's early novels include The Wishbones (1997), about a New Jersey wedding band guitarist, and Election (1998), a satire of a high school student-council election narrated by multiple characters; Alexander Payne's 1999 film adaptation, starring Reese Witherspoon and Matthew Broderick, became a modern classic. Joe College (2000) drew on his Yale years. Little Children (2004), a darkly comic portrait of suburban parents, was adapted into a 2006 film directed by Todd Field, with Perrotta co-writing the screenplay, earning an Academy Award nomination for Best Adapted Screenplay. Later novels include The Abstinence Teacher (2007), The Leftovers (2011)—about a community coping after 2% of humanity vanishes—which became the HBO series (2014–2017) he co-created, Mrs. Fletcher (2017), adapted as an HBO limited series in 2019, and Tracy Flick Can't Win (2022), a sequel to Election.

Themes

Perrotta's fiction is known for its compassionate satire of American suburban and middle-class life, multiple-perspective narration, and interest in religion, desire, and moral compromise. Critics frequently compare his work to that of Richard Yates and John Updike.
