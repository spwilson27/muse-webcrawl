Battle of Surigao Strait

Date25 October 1944
LocationSurigao Strait, Philippines
ResultDecisive American victory
BelligerentsUnited States and Australia vs. Empire of Japan
CommandersJesse B. Oldendorf vs. Shōji Nishimura (killed); Kiyohide Shima

The Battle of Surigao Strait, fought on the night of 24–25 October 1944, was one of the four engagements of the Battle of Leyte Gulf, the largest naval battle of the Second World War. A Japanese "Southern Force" attempting to force Surigao Strait and attack the Leyte invasion shipping was annihilated by the US Seventh Fleet's support force under Rear Admiral Jesse B. Oldendorf. It was the last battleship-versus-battleship action in history.

Background

Under Japan's Shō-Gō plan to destroy the Leyte landings, Vice Admiral Shōji Nishimura's force — the battleships Fusō and Yamashiro, the heavy cruiser Mogami and four destroyers — was to transit Surigao Strait at night, followed by Vice Admiral Shima's cruisers from the south. Oldendorf barred the strait's northern exit with six battleships (five of them Pearl Harbor survivors), eight cruisers, 28 destroyers and 39 PT boats, deployed with radar-directed gunfire.

The battle

Japanese ships advancing up the narrow strait were harassed by PT boats, then struck by destroyer torpedo attacks from both flanks, which sank Fusō and three destroyers and crippled Yamashiro. Around 03:50 Oldendorf's battle line, having executed the classic manoeuvre of "crossing the T", opened fire; Yamashiro was sunk with Nishimura aboard, and Mogami was wrecked (sunk the next day by aircraft). Shima, seeing the wreckage, fired torpedoes and withdrew, colliding with Mogami in the process. Only the destroyer Shigure survived of Nishimura's force.

Aftermath

The Southern Force ceased to exist and never threatened the Leyte anchorages. Together with the actions off Samar, at Cape Engaño and in the Sibuyan Sea, Leyte Gulf broke the Imperial Japanese Navy as an organised fleet. Surigao Strait remains the textbook example of a prepared night ambush with radar-controlled gunfire and layered torpedo attack.
