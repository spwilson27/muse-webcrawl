Lowell is a city in Massachusetts, United States. The county seat of Middlesex County, it had a population of 115,554 at the 2020 census, making it the fifth most populous city in the state. Along with Cambridge, Lowell is one of two traditional seats of Middlesex County. With an estimated population of 118,000 in 2023, it is the second most populous city in the Boston metropolitan statistical area's North sub-region.

Named after Francis Cabot Lowell, a figure in the industrialization of the United States, Lowell was planned in the 1820s as a mill town for the manufacture of textiles. The city became known as the cradle of the American Industrial Revolution because of its textile mills and factories. Many of Lowell's historic manufacturing sites were later preserved by the National Park Service to create Lowell National Historical Park, established in 1978.

History

The area was originally inhabited by Pennacook peoples. Lowell was founded by Boston businessmen who sought to replicate the textile mill system of Lancashire, England, on American soil. Using the water power of the Merrimack River's Pawtucket Falls — a drop of 32 feet over about a mile — engineers built an extensive canal system through the 1820s and 1830s. By the 1850s Lowell had the largest industrial complex in the United States, employing thousands of workers.

The workforce drew heavily on young women from New England farms, known as "Lowell mill girls," who lived in company boarding houses and published the Lowell Offering, a literary magazine. Later waves of immigration — Irish, French Canadian, Greek, Polish, Portuguese, and later Southeast Asian communities — reshaped the city. By the mid-20th century many mills had closed or moved south, but Lowell revived in the later decades through education, healthcare, and heritage tourism.

Geography

Lowell lies on the Merrimack River about 25 miles (40 km) northwest of Boston, near the New Hampshire border. The Concord River joins the Merrimack within the city. Lowell's neighborhoods include Belvidere, Pawtucketville, the Acre, the Highlands, and South Lowell.

Demographics

Lowell is one of the most ethnically diverse cities in New England, with sizable Cambodian, Dominican, Puerto Rican, Brazilian, and African communities. The Cambodian-American population is among the largest in the United States, giving the city the nickname "Little Cambodia."

Education

The city is home to the University of Massachusetts Lowell (UMass Lowell), a public research university, and Middlesex Community College. Notable people from Lowell include the writer Jack Kerouac, born and raised in the city.
