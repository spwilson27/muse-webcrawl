Consultation chess

Consultation chess is a form of chess in which two or more players on each side consult with one another before making their moves, effectively playing as a team against another team. It was especially popular in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, when clubs, cities and newspapers staged consultation games and matches.

History

Famous consultation games include the 1899 game in which a team including Harry Nelson Pillsbury faced Emanuel Lasker, and consultation matches played between chess clubs of different cities, sometimes by telegraph or correspondence. Strong individual players such as Alexander Alekhine and José Raúl Capablanca also took part in consultation games as exhibitions. The format declined as tournament chess professionalised, but team consultation survives in online "vote chess", where large groups vote on each move.
