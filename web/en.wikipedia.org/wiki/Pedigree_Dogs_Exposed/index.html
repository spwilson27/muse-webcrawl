Pedigree Dogs Exposed is a British investigative documentary, produced by Jemima Harrison's Passionate Productions, that was first broadcast on BBC One on 19 August 2008. The programme examined the health and welfare problems of pedigree dogs in the United Kingdom, arguing that selective breeding for appearance to meet Kennel Club breed standards had produced inherited diseases and exaggerated physical features in many breeds.

Content

The documentary featured Cavalier King Charles spaniels suffering from syringomyelia, a painful condition linked to skull conformation; boxers with epilepsy and cancer; pugs and other brachycephalic breeds with breathing difficulties; and a German shepherd show dog with a severely sloped back. It compared modern show dogs with photographs of the same breeds a century earlier and interviewed geneticists who criticised inbreeding within closed stud books, including the use of popular sires and mother-to-son matings then permitted by the Kennel Club.

Reception and impact

The programme attracted millions of viewers and extensive press coverage. The RSPCA and Dogs Trust withdrew support from Crufts, and sponsors including Pedigree Petfoods ended their association with the show. The BBC, which had broadcast Crufts since 1966, suspended its coverage and did not renew it. The Kennel Club announced changes to breed standards and banned mother–son, father–daughter and brother–sister matings, and commissioned health initiatives; an independent inquiry chaired by Patrick Bateson reported in 2010. The Kennel Club complained to Ofcom about unfair editing; the regulator upheld some elements of the complaint while the programme's makers stood by its central claims. A follow-up, Pedigree Dogs Exposed: Three Years On, aired on BBC Four in 2012 and assessed progress. See also Crufts.
