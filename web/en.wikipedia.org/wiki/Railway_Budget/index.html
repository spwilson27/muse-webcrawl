Railway Budget

The Railway Budget was the annual financial statement of Indian Railways, presented separately in the Parliament of India by the Minister of Railways from 1924 until 2016. From the budget of 2017–18 it was merged into the Union Budget of India, ending a 92-year practice.

History

A separate Railway Budget was instituted following the recommendations of the Acworth Committee (1921), reflecting the railways' dominant share of public finances — at the time, railway revenue was a very large part of government income. The presentation became a major annual political event, with announcements of new trains, fare changes and projects.

Merger

In 2016 the government decided to merge the Railway Budget with the general budget from 2017, following a committee recommendation chaired by Bibek Debroy. The merger relieved Indian Railways of paying an annual dividend to general revenues and was intended to improve capital planning; railways allocations are now a part of the Union Budget documents.

Legacy

The separate budget had been criticised for encouraging populist announcements of unviable trains and projects, distorting investment in the network.
