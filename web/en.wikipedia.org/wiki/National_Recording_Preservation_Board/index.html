The National Recording Preservation Board is an advisory body of the Library of Congress in the United States. It was established by the National Recording Preservation Act of 2000 to advise the Librarian of Congress on the selection of sound recordings for the National Recording Registry and on matters of recorded sound preservation.

Functions

Each year the board reviews nominations of recordings that are "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" and at least ten years old. The Librarian of Congress, advised by the board and its appointed members drawn from the music industry, archives, academia and professional organisations, announces the new selections annually. By the mid-2020s the Registry held more than 600 titles, ranging from Edison-era cylinders to landmark albums and broadcasts.

The board also promoted a national plan for recorded sound preservation, published by the Library of Congress in 2010, and supported studies on the condition and longevity of audio formats.
