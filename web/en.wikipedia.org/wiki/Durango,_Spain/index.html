Durango is a town and municipality in the province of Biscay, in the Basque Country of northern Spain. It is the main town of the comarca of Durangaldea and lies in a valley surrounded by mountains, including the Urkiola range to the south. The town's name was carried to Durango in Mexico and subsequently to Durango, Colorado.

History

Durango grew in importance during the Middle Ages as a stopping point on trade routes between the Castilian plateau and the Biscay coast. It was the seat of the historic Merindad de Durango, a district with its own fueros (customary laws) administered from the Casa de Juntas. The town suffered a notorious episode of religious persecution in the 15th century, the so-called Durango heresy trials.

On 31 March 1937, during the Spanish Civil War, Durango was bombed by Italian aircraft of the Aviazione Legionaria supporting the Nationalist forces. The bombing, which killed hundreds of civilians including worshippers at Mass, occurred weeks before the more famous bombing of Guernica and is commemorated annually in the town.

Landmarks

Notable buildings include the church of Santa María de Uribarri, with its large carved wooden porch, the church of Santa Ana and the adjacent Arco de Santa Ana, the only surviving gate of the old town walls, and the Kurutziaga Cross, an ornately carved 15th-century wayside cross now housed in the town museum.
