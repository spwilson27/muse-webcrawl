Crown (dentistry)

A dental crown is a cap that completely covers or encircles a tooth or implant.

In dentistry, a crown is a restoration that mostly or completely caps or encircles a tooth or dental implant. A crown may be needed when a large dental cavity threatens the health of a tooth, after root canal treatment, to hold together a cracked tooth, or to cover a tooth that is badly shaped or discoloured. Crowns are made of many materials, including porcelain, ceramic, zirconia, metal alloys, and porcelain fused to metal.

Indications

Crowns are commonly used to restore teeth that are heavily filled or weakened, to protect teeth that have undergone root canal therapy (which leaves them more brittle), to anchor a dental bridge, to cover a dental implant, and for cosmetic improvement of severely stained or misshapen teeth.

Procedure

The tooth is first reshaped so the crown can fit over it. An impression or digital scan is taken and sent to a dental laboratory, which fabricates the crown; a temporary crown protects the prepared tooth in the meantime. At a second appointment the crown is checked for fit and colour and cemented in place. Modern CAD/CAM systems allow some practices to design and mill a ceramic crown in a single visit. With good oral hygiene, crowns typically last many years, though they can chip, loosen, or develop decay at the margin.
