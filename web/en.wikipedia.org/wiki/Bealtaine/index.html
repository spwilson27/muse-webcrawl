Bealtaine

Also called: Beltane, Lá Bealtaine, Bealltainn
Date: 1 May (or the evening of 30 April)
Type: Gaelic seasonal festival

Bealtaine (Irish pronunciation roughly "byowl-tin-ye"; also spelled Beltane) is the Gaelic May Day festival, traditionally held on 1 May, or about halfway between the spring equinox and summer solstice. Historically it was widely observed in Ireland, Scotland and the Isle of Man, and it has been revived in modern times, including by Celtic neopagans.

Historical customs

Bealtaine marked the beginning of summer, when cattle were driven out to summer pastures. Central to the festival were bonfires whose flames, smoke and ashes were believed to have protective powers: household fires were doused and re-lit from the communal Bealtaine fire, cattle were driven between two bonfires, and people jumped flames for luck. Food offerings were made to the aos sí (the spirits or fairies), doors and livestock were decorated with yellow flowers such as primrose and rowan, and "May bushes" were set up. It was one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals, together with Samhain, Imbolc and Lughnasadh.

Etymology

The name is commonly explained as "bright fire" or "fire of Bel", from Old Irish bel ("bright, shining") and tene ("fire"). In Irish, the month of May itself is called Bealtaine (Mí na Bealtaine).

Modern observance

The Beltane Fire Festival in Edinburgh, founded in 1988, draws large crowds to Calton Hill. Wiccans and other neopagans celebrate Beltane as one of the eight sabbats of the Wheel of the Year.
