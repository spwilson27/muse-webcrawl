Terence Chi-Shen Tao (born 17 July 1975) is an Australian-American mathematician who is a professor at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). Widely regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of his generation, he has made fundamental contributions across an unusually broad range of fields, including harmonic analysis, nonlinear partial differential equations, analytic number theory, additive combinatorics, random matrix theory, and compressed sensing. He was awarded the Fields Medal in 2006.

Early life and education

Tao was born in Adelaide to immigrant parents from Hong Kong. A child prodigy, he competed in the International Mathematical Olympiad at ages 10, 11, and 12, winning bronze, silver, and then gold medals — he remains the youngest gold medallist in the competition's history. He completed a bachelor's and master's degree at Flinders University by age 16 and earned his PhD from Princeton University in 1996 at 21, under Elias Stein. He joined UCLA the same year and became, at 24, one of the youngest full professors in the university's history.

Work

Among Tao's best-known results is the Green–Tao theorem (2004), proved with Ben Green, which states that the prime numbers contain arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length. With Emmanuel Candès and others he helped create the field of compressed sensing, showing that signals can be reconstructed exactly from far fewer measurements than classical sampling theory suggests, with applications in imaging and signal processing. He has also obtained major results on the Kakeya problem, the Erdős discrepancy problem (solved in 2015), wave maps and other nonlinear equations, and the Navier–Stokes regularity problem, where he constructed an averaged version exhibiting finite-time blow-up.

Tao is a prolific author and an influential communicator of mathematics, known for his blog What's New and for collaborative "polymath" projects conducted online. He received the Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics in 2015 and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society.
