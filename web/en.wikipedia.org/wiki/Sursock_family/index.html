The Sursock family (Arabic: سرسق) is a prominent Greek Orthodox Christian family of Beirut, Lebanon, long among the city's wealthiest and most influential landowning dynasties. Of Byzantine Greek origin, the family rose in the Ottoman period through tax farming, banking, trade and vast agricultural estates in Lebanon, Syria, Egypt, Turkey and — most consequentially in modern historiography — Palestine.

The Sursock Purchases

In the 1870s the family acquired extensive lands in northern Palestine, including much of the Jezreel Valley (Marj ibn Amir) and areas around the Sea of Galilee. Between 1901 and the 1920s the family sold large tracts to the Jewish Colonisation Association and the Jewish National Fund — transactions collectively known as the Sursock Purchases. The largest, the Jezreel Valley purchase completed in the early 1920s, transferred roughly 240,000 dunams and required the relocation of Arab tenant farmers (fellahin) from more than twenty villages, making the sales a lasting point of controversy in the history of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and an emblem, for critics, of absentee landlordism.

In Beirut

The family's Beirut palaces in the Achrafieh district — notably the Sursock Palace and Nicolas Sursock's villa, bequeathed to the city and opened in 1961 as the Sursock Museum of modern art — remain landmarks. Lady Yvonne Sursock Cochrane (1922–2020) was a leading patron of Lebanese art and heritage; she died of injuries sustained in the Beirut port explosion of August 2020, which also badly damaged the family palace and museum.
