Concord, Massachusetts

Concord (/ˈkɒŋkərd/) is a town in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States. In the 2020 census, the town population was 18,491. The town is famous as the site of the first battle of the American Revolutionary War and for its literary heritage as a home of the transcendentalists.

History

Concord was established in 1635 by English settlers on land purchased from local Native American peoples, and was one of the first inland settlements in the Massachusetts Bay Colony. On April 19, 1775, the Battles of Lexington and Concord opened the Revolutionary War; at Concord's North Bridge, colonial militiamen fired on British regulars in what Ralph Waldo Emerson later called "the shot heard round the world."

Literary heritage

In the nineteenth century Concord became a center of American letters. Ralph Waldo Emerson, Henry David Thoreau, Nathaniel Hawthorne, and Louisa May Alcott all lived there. Thoreau's stay at Walden Pond produced Walden; Alcott's home, Orchard House, was the setting for Little Women. Sleepy Hollow Cemetery's "Authors Ridge" holds their graves.

Geography

Concord lies about 20 miles (32 km) northwest of Boston, at the confluence of the Sudbury and Assabet rivers, which form the Concord River. Parts of the town are protected within Minute Man National Historical Park and Great Meadows National Wildlife Refuge.
