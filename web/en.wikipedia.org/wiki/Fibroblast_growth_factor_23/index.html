Fibroblast growth factor 23

FGF23 regulates phosphate and vitamin D metabolism
SymbolFGF23
Gene locationChromosome 12p13.32 (human)
Produced byOsteocytes and osteoblasts (bone)
ReceptorFGFR1 with α-Klotho co-receptor

Fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) is a protein hormone encoded by the FGF23 gene in humans. It is secreted mainly by osteocytes and osteoblasts in bone and acts as a central regulator of phosphate homeostasis and vitamin D metabolism.

Function

FGF23 acts on the kidneys, where it binds fibroblast growth factor receptors in complex with the co-receptor α-Klotho. It decreases the reabsorption of phosphate in the proximal tubule, promoting urinary phosphate excretion, and suppresses the production of calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D), thereby reducing intestinal absorption of phosphate and calcium. It also suppresses parathyroid hormone secretion.

Clinical significance

Excess FGF23 causes hypophosphataemic disorders including X-linked hypophosphataemia (caused by mutations in PHEX that raise FGF23 levels), autosomal dominant hypophosphataemic rickets (caused by activating mutations in FGF23 itself), and tumour-induced osteomalacia, in which a tumour secretes FGF23. Inactivating mutations cause familial hyperphosphataemic tumoral calcinosis. FGF23 concentrations rise early in chronic kidney disease and are associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes. The monoclonal antibody burosumab, which blocks FGF23, is used to treat X-linked hypophosphataemia and tumour-induced osteomalacia.
