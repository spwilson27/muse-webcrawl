Bob Weir

Robert Hall Weir (born October 16, 1947) is an American musician and songwriter best known as a founding member of the rock band Grateful Dead. After the group disbanded in 1995, Weir performed with the Other Ones, later known as the Dead, together with other former members of the Grateful Dead. Weir also founded and played in several other groups, including Kingfish, the Bob Weir Band, Bobby and the Midnites, Scaring the Children and RatDog. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of the Grateful Dead in 1994. In 2015, Weir joined John Mayer and Grateful Dead drummers Bill Kreutzmann and Mickey Hart to form the band Dead & Company.

Early life

Weir was born in San Francisco and raised by adoptive parents in the suburb of Atherton. He began playing guitar at age thirteen after stints with the clarinet, piano and trumpet. On New Year's Eve 1963, the sixteen-year-old Weir and a friend met Jerry Garcia at a music store in Palo Alto; they formed a jug band, Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions, which evolved into the Grateful Dead.

Grateful Dead

Weir played rhythm guitar and sang lead vocals on many Dead songs, including "Sugar Magnolia", "Playing in the Band", "Jack Straw", "Estimated Prophet" and "Truckin' ", which he co-wrote. His distinctive rhythm guitar style and songwriting partnership with lyricist John Perry Barlow were central to the band's sound across its thirty-year career.

Later projects

Following Garcia's death in 1995, Weir toured extensively with RatDog, and in 2009 formed Furthur with bassist Phil Lesh. In 2015 he co-founded Dead & Company with John Mayer, which became one of the most successful touring acts in the United States, playing stadium residencies including the Las Vegas Sphere. Weir released the solo album Blue Mountain in 2016 and has fronted the trio Wolf Bros since 2018.
