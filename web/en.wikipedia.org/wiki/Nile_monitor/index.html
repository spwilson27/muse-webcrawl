Nile monitor

Scientific nameVaranus niloticus
FamilyVaranidae
RangeSub-Saharan Africa; introduced to Florida
Lengthtypically 120–220 cm

The Nile monitor (Varanus niloticus) is a large member of the monitor lizard family (Varanidae) found throughout most of sub-Saharan Africa, especially along river systems including the Nile. It is one of the largest lizards in Africa and is a capable swimmer and climber.

Description

Adults are greyish-brown or olive with darker cross-bands and yellowish spots; juveniles are more strikingly patterned in black and yellow. The body is muscular, with strong limbs ending in sharp claws, a long laterally compressed tail used in swimming, and nostrils placed high on the snout. Like other monitors, it has a deeply forked tongue used to sample chemical cues.

Behaviour and diet

Nile monitors are opportunistic predators and scavengers. They feed on fish, frogs, rodents, birds and their eggs, young crocodiles, crustaceans, molluscs, and carrion, and are known egg predators at crocodile nesting sites. Females often lay their eggs inside active termite mounds, whose stable warmth incubates the clutch of 20 to 60 eggs. When threatened, monitors inflate the throat, hiss, lash the tail, and bite.

Introduced populations

Released or escaped pets have established breeding populations in Florida, notably around Cape Coral, where the species is regarded as invasive and a threat to native burrowing owls, sea turtles, and other wildlife.
