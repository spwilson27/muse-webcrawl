The Hostage Rescue Team (HRT) is the FBI's elite tactical unit, the principal federal counter-terrorist and hostage-rescue force of the United States. Founded in 1983, it provides a national-level capability to respond to major terrorist incidents, hostage situations and high-risk arrests that exceed the capacity of local FBI SWAT teams.

History

The HRT was created in the build-up to the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, when officials sought a civilian federal team able to conduct military-style counter-terrorism operations within the United States; its first commander was Danny Coulson, and early members trained with the Army's Delta Force and foreign units such as the British SAS and German GSG 9. The team was originally conceived of about 50 operators and has since grown. It became part of the FBI's Critical Incident Response Group (CIRG) when that division was formed in 1994.

Selection and organisation

Candidates are experienced FBI special agents who pass a demanding selection course assessing fitness, marksmanship, judgement and teamwork, followed by a New Operator Training School of several months. Operators are organised into assault and sniper/observer elements, supported by negotiators, tactical helicopter and maritime capabilities, and specialists in breaching and communications. The HRT trains full-time, deploys on hundreds of operations and training missions each year, and also provides enhanced support to FBI field divisions and assistance to allied units.

Notable operations and controversies

HRT operators participated in the sieges at Ruby Ridge (1992) and Waco (1993), events that led to lasting scrutiny of federal tactical rules of engagement and of the team's role; a sniper's shots at Ruby Ridge were extensively investigated. The team has also conducted many hostage rescues and arrests without casualties, deployed to protect major events, and supported operations overseas in coordination with the military. In 2013 two HRT members died in a training accident during a maritime exercise off Virginia.
