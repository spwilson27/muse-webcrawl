Cycle Messenger World Championships

Riders compete in simulated messenger work races at the CMWC.

The Cycle Messenger World Championships (CMWC) is an annual international championship event for bicycle messengers, first held in Berlin in 1993. The championships rotate among host cities around the world and are organised by the International Federation of Bike Messenger Associations (IFBMA) together with local messenger communities.

Events

The centrepiece is the main race, a multi-hour event on a closed course in which riders simulate a working messenger's day: collecting and delivering packages between checkpoints according to a manifest, with the winner being the rider who completes the most work correctly in the time allowed. Other events include sprint races, cargo bike races, track stands, skids, footdown, and the bike polo tournament held in association with the championships. The programme also includes cultural events, parties, and an open forum on messenger industry issues.

History

The first CMWC grew out of the European messenger scene of the early 1990s and drew couriers from across Europe and North America. Subsequent editions have been held in cities including London, Toronto, San Francisco, Copenhagen, Tokyo, Chicago, Melbourne, and Paris, making it both a sporting event and the principal reunion of the global messenger subculture. Regional championships — European, North American, and others — follow a similar format.
