1923 in Japan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Events in the year 1923 in Japan. It corresponds to Taishō 12 (大正12年) in the Japanese calendar. Emperor Taishō reigned, though Crown Prince Hirohito served as regent owing to the emperor's illness.

Events

The year was dominated by the Great Kantō earthquake of 1 September 1923, a magnitude 7.9 quake that struck the Kantō Plain at noon, devastating Tokyo and Yokohama and igniting firestorms that killed the majority of the estimated 105,000–143,000 victims. The disaster flattened much of the capital and triggered martial law, as well as rumour-driven massacres of Koreans and attacks on socialists — including the Kameido incident and the Amakasu incident, in which anarchist Ōsugi Sakae was murdered by military police.

The government of Prime Minister Yamamoto Gonnohyōe, appointed amid the emergency, resigned at year's end after the Toranomon incident of 27 December, an assassination attempt on Regent Hirohito by the communist Namba Daisuke.

Births and deaths

Notable deaths included industrialist and political figures affected by the quake, while the year's chaos accelerated Tokyo's reconstruction into a more modern, concrete capital in the later 1920s.
