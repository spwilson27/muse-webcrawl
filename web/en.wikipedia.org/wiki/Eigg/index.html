Eigg

Eigg (Scottish Gaelic: Eige) is one of the Small Isles in the Scottish Inner Hebrides. It lies to the south of the Isle of Skye and to the north of the Ardnamurchan peninsula. Eigg is 9 kilometres (5.6 mi) long from north to south and 5 kilometres (3.1 mi) east to west. With an area of just over 3,000 hectares (7,400 acres), it is the second largest of the Small Isles after Rùm. Eigg is notable for being run almost entirely from renewable energy sources, and for its community buy-out in 1997, which ended decades of absentee landlord ownership.

Geology and geography

The dominant geological feature is An Sgurr, a dramatic pitchstone ridge rising to 393 metres (1,289 ft), formed from a lava flow of the Palaeogene period around 58 million years ago. The island is largely a plateau of moorland, with fertile coastal ground in the south-west. The main settlements are Cleadale in the north and Galmisdale in the south, where the ferry pier is located.

History

Eigg has been inhabited since prehistoric times. The island was associated with the early Christian church; St Donnán of Eigg, an Irish missionary, was martyred there in 617, according to tradition, along with 52 monks. In the Norse period the island formed part of the Kingdom of the Isles. The Massacre Cave near Grulin is traditionally associated with a notorious clan massacre of the island's MacDonald inhabitants by MacLeods of Skye in the late 16th century. In 1997, after a fundraising campaign, the Isle of Eigg Heritage Trust — a partnership of residents, the Highland Council and the Scottish Wildlife Trust — purchased the island, bringing it into community ownership.

Renewable energy

In 2008 Eigg launched an electricity grid powered by wind, hydro and solar generation, with battery storage and a small diesel backup. Before the scheme, residents had no mains electricity and relied on individual generators. The system, often cited as a model of community renewables, supplies nearly all of the island's electricity needs, with each property subject to a 5 kW consumption cap.

Transport

A Caledonian MacBrayne ferry from Mallaig serves Galmisdale, calling at the other Small Isles of Muck, Rùm and Canna. There is no public transport on the island and few cars.
