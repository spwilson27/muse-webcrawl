Hadrians Wall

The Roman frontier fortification built across northern Britain from AD 122 on the orders of the emperor Hadrian is properly known as Hadrian's Wall. It ran for 73 miles (80 Roman miles) from Wallsend on the River Tyne in the east to Bowness-on-Solway in the west, marking the northern limit of the Roman Empire in Britain.

Construction

The wall was built by the three Roman legions stationed in Britain, largely of stone in the east and turf in the west (later rebuilt in stone). Along it stood milecastles at every Roman mile, pairs of turrets between them, and larger forts at intervals. A great earthwork, the Vallum, ran to the south. The garrison numbered perhaps 9,000–15,000 auxiliary troops.

Later history

The wall remained the frontier, with interruptions, until Roman rule in Britain ended in the early 5th century. Much stone was later robbed for buildings and roads, but long stretches survive, notably along the crags of the Whin Sill. Hadrian's Wall was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987 as part of the Frontiers of the Roman Empire, and the Hadrian's Wall Path national trail follows its course.
