Rudolph Giuliani

Rudolph William Louis Giuliani
Born: 28 May 1944, Brooklyn, New York, USA
Occupation: Lawyer, politician
Offices: Mayor of New York City (1994–2001); US Attorney for the Southern District of New York (1983–1989)

Rudolph William Louis Giuliani (born 1944) is an American lawyer and politician who served as the 107th Mayor of New York City from 1994 to 2001. He previously served as United States Attorney for the Southern District of New York, where he prosecuted high-profile Mafia and white-collar crime cases.

Prosecutor and mayor

As US Attorney, Giuliani led the Mafia Commission Trial (1985–86), which convicted the heads of New York's Five Families under the RICO statute, and prosecuted insider-trading cases on Wall Street. Elected mayor in 1993, he is credited by supporters with reducing crime through "broken windows" policing and CompStat, and with cleaning up areas such as Times Square, though his tenure also drew criticism over police conduct and civil liberties.

Giuliani was in office during the September 11 attacks of 2001; his visible leadership in the aftermath earned him the nickname "America's Mayor", Time magazine's Person of the Year for 2001, and an honorary knighthood from Queen Elizabeth II.

Later career

Giuliani ran unsuccessfully for the Republican presidential nomination in 2008 and founded a security consultancy, Giuliani Partners. He became a prominent supporter and personal lawyer of Donald Trump. His efforts to challenge the results of the 2020 presidential election led to the suspension and eventual loss of his law licences in New York and Washington, DC, adverse court findings, and a large defamation judgment against him in favour of two Georgia election workers, after which he filed for bankruptcy in 2023.
