Matsusaka, Mie

Matsusaka (松阪市, Matsusaka-shi) is a city located in Mie Prefecture, Japan, on the island of Honshu. It lies on Ise Bay in the central part of the prefecture. As of recent estimates, the city has a population of roughly 155,000–160,000 residents.

History

Matsusaka developed as a castle town after Gamō Ujisato built Matsusaka Castle in 1588. During the Edo period it prospered as a commercial center; its merchants, the Ise merchants, were renowned throughout Japan. The city was also home to Motoori Norinaga (1730–1801), the celebrated kokugaku scholar, whose residence and museum remain local landmarks. The modern city was founded in 1933 and expanded through mergers with surrounding towns and villages in 2005.

Economy and culture

Matsusaka is nationally famous for Matsusaka beef, one of the "three great wagyu" brands of Japanese premium beef, prized for its intense marbling. The city is also known for Matsusaka cotton, a traditional indigo-striped textile. Attractions include the ruins of Matsusaka Castle, the Motoori Norinaga Memorial Museum, and the Gokasho district of preserved merchant houses.

Transportation

Matsusaka Station is served by the Kintetsu Yamada Line and JR lines (Kisei Main Line and Meishō Line), connecting the city with Nagoya, Tsu, and the Kii Peninsula.
