Mykola Leontovych

Born: 13 December 1877, Monastyrok, Podolia, Russian Empire
Died: 23 January 1921, Markivka, Ukraine
Occupation: Composer, conductor, ethnomusicologist
Known for: "Shchedryk" ("Carol of the Bells")

Mykola Dmytrovych Leontovych (13 December 1877 – 23 January 1921) was a Ukrainian composer, conductor, ethnomusicologist and teacher. His music was inspired by Ukrainian folk traditions, and he specialised in a cappella choral works, arranging hundreds of folk songs.

"Shchedryk"

Leontovych is best known internationally for "Shchedryk", a choral miniature based on a Ukrainian New Year folk chant, which he worked on between 1901 and 1919. It was first performed in Kyiv in 1916 and introduced to American audiences by the Ukrainian National Chorus in 1922. In 1936 Peter Wilhousky adapted its melody with new English lyrics as the Christmas song "Carol of the Bells".

Life and death

Leontovych studied at the Kamianets-Podilskyi theological seminary and later in St Petersburg and Kyiv. He taught music in schools across Podolia and collected folk songs. In January 1921, during the turmoil after the Russian Revolution, he was shot and killed at his parents' home by a Cheka agent.
