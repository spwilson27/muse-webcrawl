Grabar (Classical Armenian)
Native toArmenian Highlands
Era5th century AD onward; liturgical and literary use to the present
Language familyIndo-European → Armenian
Writing systemArmenian alphabet

Grabar (Armenian: գրաբար), also known as Classical Armenian, is the oldest attested form of the Armenian language. It was first written down in the early 5th century AD, after the creation of the Armenian alphabet by Mesrop Mashtots around 405 AD, and remained the standard literary language of Armenians until roughly the 18th-19th centuries, when modern literary standards (Ashkharhabar) developed.

History

The creation of the alphabet was followed by an intense period of translation, beginning with the Bible, and by original works of theology, history, and philosophy — the "Golden Age" of Armenian literature, whose authors include Movses Khorenatsi, Eznik of Kolb, and David the Invincible. Grabar continued in use for centuries after the spoken language had diverged, functioning much like Latin did in medieval Europe.

Present use

Grabar remains the liturgical language of the Armenian Apostolic Church and the Armenian Catholic Church, and is still studied by scholars and clergy. Its vocabulary and forms heavily influenced the modern Eastern and Western Armenian literary standards.
