Ryukyu Islands

Native name琉球諸島 (Ryūkyū-shotō)

The Ryukyu Islands (琉球諸島, Ryūkyū-shotō), also known as the Nansei Islands, are a chain of volcanic islands stretching about 1,200 km southwest from Kyushu toward Taiwan, between the East China Sea and the Philippine Sea. They include the Ōsumi, Tokara, Amami, Okinawa, Sakishima (Miyako and Yaeyama), and Senkaku groups.

History

The islands were once ruled by the Ryukyu Kingdom, an independent maritime trading state centred on Shuri Castle in Okinawa that flourished from the 15th to the 19th century, paying tribute to Ming and Qing China while trading across East and Southeast Asia. In 1609 the Satsuma domain of Japan invaded and placed the kingdom under its control; in 1879 Japan formally annexed the islands as Okinawa Prefecture.

During the Second World War the Battle of Okinawa (1945) devastated the islands. Afterwards the United States administered the Ryukyus until reversion to Japan in 1972; a large US military presence remains on Okinawa and is a continuing source of local controversy.

Culture and environment

The indigenous Ryukyuan languages, related to Japanese but mutually unintelligible with it, are endangered. Ryukyuan culture is noted for distinct music (the sanshin), dance, karate (which originated in Okinawa), textiles, and cuisine. The islands' subtropical forests — including the Iriomote cat's habitat — and coral reefs are of major conservation importance; parts of Amami Ōshima, Tokunoshima, northern Okinawa, and Iriomote were inscribed as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site in 2021.
