Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Hebrew: שניאור זלמן מליאדי; 1745–1812) was an influential Lithuanian Jewish rabbi and the founder and first Rebbe of Chabad, a branch of Hasidic Judaism, then based in the town of Liadi in the Russian Empire. He is known by many names, including the Alter Rebbe ("Old Rabbi"), the Baal HaTanya ("author of the Tanya"), and the Rav.

Life

A prodigy in Talmudic study, Shneur Zalman became a leading disciple of Dov Ber, the Maggid of Mezeritch, successor of the Baal Shem Tov. Settling in Liadi, he developed a distinctive intellectual approach to Hasidism — Chabad, an acronym for Chochmah (wisdom), Binah (understanding), and Da'at (knowledge) — teaching that the mind must rule and refine the heart. His opposition to Napoleon's invasion of Russia and conflicts with rival Hasidic and rabbinic opponents led to two imprisonments in St. Petersburg on charges of treason, from which he was released.

Works

His masterwork, the Tanya (Likkutei Amarim, published 1796), is regarded as the foundational text of Chabad philosophy, mapping the psychology of the soul and the struggle between the divine and animal souls. He also compiled the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, a systematic code of Jewish law, and the Siddur Torah Or prayer book.

Legacy

Through his son and grandson and the town of Lubavitch (Lyubavichi), his movement became one of the largest in Hasidic Judaism; the Chabad-Lubavitch movement today maintains thousands of centres worldwide.
