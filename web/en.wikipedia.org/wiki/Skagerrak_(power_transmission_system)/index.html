Skagerrak (power transmission system)

Skagerrak is the name of a series of high-voltage direct current (HVDC) submarine power cables between Tjele in Denmark and Kristiansand in Norway, crossing the Skagerrak strait. The system links the Norwegian hydroelectric-dominated grid with the Danish grid, which has a large share of wind power.

Type: Submarine HVDC interconnector
Countries: Norway – Denmark
From: Kristiansand, Norway
To: Tjele, Denmark
Operators: Statnett and Energinet

History

The first two poles, Skagerrak 1 and 2, entered service in 1976–1977 with a combined capacity of about 500 MW. Skagerrak 3 followed in 1993, adding about 440 MW, and Skagerrak 4 was commissioned in 2014 with 700 MW, bringing the total transfer capacity to roughly 1,700 MW.

Technology

The original lines used line-commutated converters; Skagerrak 4 uses voltage-source converter (VSC) technology. The cables cross roughly 240 km of water, among the longest submarine power cable routes in the world at the time of construction.

Significance

The link allows Norway to export hydropower when prices are high and import surplus Danish wind power when wind generation exceeds Danish demand, improving security of supply in both countries.
