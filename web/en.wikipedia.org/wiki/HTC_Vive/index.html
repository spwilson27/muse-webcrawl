The HTC Vive is a virtual reality headset developed by HTC and Valve, first released on 5 April 2016. Developed from Valve's prototypes (including the "Room" demonstrations shown at Steam Dev Days 2014), the Vive was unveiled at Mobile World Congress in March 2015 and became the first commercially released headset to offer room-scale VR, allowing users to walk around a tracked play area.

Technology

The headset uses two 1080×1200 OLED displays refreshing at 90 Hz with a roughly 110-degree field of view. Positional tracking is provided by Valve's Lighthouse system: two base stations sweep the room with laser light, and photodiodes on the headset and its two wand controllers compute their positions with sub-millimetre precision. A front-facing camera enables a "Chaperone" boundary overlay warning users of the edges of the play space.

Reception and successors

The Vive launched alongside the Oculus Rift as a flagship of the modern VR era and was praised for its tracking and motion controllers, though criticised for its price ($799 at launch) and setup complexity. HTC followed it with the Vive Pro (2018), the standalone Vive Focus line, the eye-tracking Vive Pro Eye, the modular Vive Cosmos series, and the compact Vive XR Elite. Valve itself released the Valve Index in 2019. Vive hardware is widely used in location-based entertainment, industry, healthcare and research.
