Helmut Jahn

Born4 January 1940, Zirndorf, Germany
Died8 May 2021 (aged 81), Campton Hills, Illinois, US
OccupationArchitect
FirmJAHN (formerly Murphy/Jahn)

Helmut Jahn (January 4, 1940 – May 8, 2021) was a German-American architect, known for designs of high-rise buildings in the postmodern and high-tech styles. Among his best-known works are the Sony Center in Berlin, the Messeturm in Frankfurt, One Liberty Place in Philadelphia, and the United Airlines Terminal 1 at Chicago O'Hare International Airport.

Early life and career

Jahn was born in Zirndorf near Nuremberg and studied architecture at the Technical University of Munich, graduating in 1965. He emigrated to the United States in 1966 to study at the Illinois Institute of Technology in Chicago — without completing the degree — and joined the firm of C. F. Murphy Associates in 1967, working under Gene Summers. Taking control of the design side of the firm in the 1970s, it became Murphy/Jahn in 1981 and eventually simply JAHN.

Major works

His early Kemper Arena in Kansas City (1974) announced an interest in exposed structure. In Chicago he designed the James R. Thompson Center (1985), a controversial glass state office building, and the United Airlines terminal (1987) with its vaulted, steel-and-glass concourses recalling historic train sheds. Postmodern landmarks followed: One Liberty Place (1987), Philadelphia's first building to exceed the height of City Hall's William Penn statue; the 257-metre Messeturm (1990) in Frankfurt; and in Berlin, the Sony Center (2000) at Potsdamer Platz with its tent-like Forum roof. In Asia he designed Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi Airport passenger terminal, notable for its vast cable-stayed glass roof.

Death and legacy

Jahn died on May 8, 2021, when he was struck by a car while riding his bicycle near his home in Campton Hills, Illinois. He was a fellow of the American Institute of Architects and received numerous honours, including a retrospective of his engineering-driven approach to design.
