Devorguilla of Galloway (c. 1210 – 28 January 1290) was a Scottish noblewoman, heiress of the lordship of Galloway and mother of John Balliol, who became King of Scots in 1292. Her name is also rendered Dervorguilla, Dearbhforgail, or Devorgilla.

Life

Devorguilla was a daughter of Alan, Lord of Galloway, and Margaret of Huntingdon, a descendant of King David I of Scotland. She married John de Balliol of Barnard Castle, a powerful Anglo-Norman baron. On her father's death in 1234 she inherited a share of the Galloway lordship and extensive lands in Scotland and northern England.

Foundations

After her husband's death in 1268, Devorguilla famously had his heart embalmed and kept in an ivory casket, which she carried with her and set a place for at meals. When she founded a Cistercian abbey in Galloway in 1273 she named it Dulce Cor — "Sweet Heart" — in his memory; it is still known as Sweetheart Abbey. She also confirmed and endowed her husband's foundation of Balliol College, Oxford, issuing its statutes in 1282; the college's scholars are sometimes called the "Balliol Scholars" founded by "Dervorguilla". Devorguilla Bridge in Dumfries is named for her. She died in 1290 and was buried at Sweetheart Abbey beside her husband's heart.
