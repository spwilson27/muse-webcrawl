Eli (biblical figure)

Eli (Hebrew: עֵלִי, ʿĒlî) was, according to the Books of Samuel, a High Priest of Shiloh and one of the last Israelite Judges before the rule of kings in ancient Israel. He assessed a childless Hannah as drunk after seeing her silently praying in the temple; when she explained herself, he blessed her. The promised child, Samuel, was entrusted to Eli's care and became his assistant in the shrine at Shiloh.

The fall of Eli's house

Eli's sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were corrupt priests who abused their office, taking sacrificial meat by force and lying with women at the entrance of the tent of meeting. Eli rebuked them only mildly, and a man of God prophesied the destruction of his priestly house: both sons would die on the same day, and the priesthood would pass to a faithful line. The prophecy was repeated to the young Samuel in the famous night-time call narrative, in which Samuel at first mistook God's voice for Eli's.

When the Israelites fought the Philistines at Aphek, the Ark of the Covenant was taken into battle and captured, and Hophni and Phinehas were killed. Eli, then ninety-eight years old, blind and heavy, sat by the road watching for news; when a messenger reported the Ark's capture, Eli fell backward from his seat, broke his neck, and died, having judged Israel forty years. His daughter-in-law, dying in childbirth on hearing the news, named her son Ichabod—"the glory has departed from Israel".

Legacy

The descendants of Eli, including Abiathar, were later deposed from the high priesthood by Solomon in favour of Zadok, which the biblical text presents as the fulfilment of the prophecy against Eli's house. In rabbinic tradition Eli is generally treated kindly as a righteous but indulgent father whose failure was one of discipline rather than personal wickedness.
