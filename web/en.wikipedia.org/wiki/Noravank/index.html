Noravank

Noravank (Armenian: Նորավանք, "new monastery") is a 13th-century Armenian monastery complex located 122 km from Yerevan, in a narrow gorge made by the Amaghu River near the town of Yeghegnadzor in Vayots Dzor Province, Armenia. The gorge is known for its tall, sheer, brick-red cliffs rising directly across from the monastery.

Noravank
Location: Vayots Dzor Province, Armenia
Founded: 1205 (church of St. Karapet)
Architect: Momik
Denomination: Armenian Apostolic Church

History

Noravank was founded in 1205 by Bishop Hovhannes, former abbot of Vahanavank. Under the patronage of the Orbelian princes, it became a major religious and cultural center of medieval Armenia, closely connected with the university of Gladzor. The master architect and miniaturist Momik worked here in the early 14th century.

The complex

The principal church, Surb Karapet (St. John the Baptist, 1216–1227), is fronted by a gavit (narthex) whose western portal carries a celebrated relief of God the Father. Beside it stands the two-story Surb Astvatsatsin (Holy Mother of God, 1339), built by Prince Burtel Orbelian, with narrow cantilevered stairs rising to its upper chapel — one of the most photographed facades in Armenia. The complex also includes the chapel of St. Gregory (1275) and numerous carved khachkars, including Momik's masterpiece of 1308.
