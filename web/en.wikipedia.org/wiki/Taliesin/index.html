Taliesin

Taliesin (c. 534 – c. 599) was an early Brittonic poet of Sub-Roman Britain whose work has survived in the Middle Welsh manuscript the Book of Taliesin. He was a court bard to at least three kings of the Brittonic north, including Urien of Rheged, and is one of the best-known early Welsh poets, alongside Aneirin.

Life and poems

Twelve poems in the Book of Taliesin are generally accepted as plausibly 6th-century, praise poems to Urien, Owain mab Urien and Gwallog. The rest of the collection accumulated legendary and prophetic material. Taliesin's name means "radiant brow".

Legend

In the later prose tale Hanes Taliesin, the boy Gwion Bach, set to stir the witch Ceridwen's cauldron of poetic inspiration (awen), tastes three drops, gains all knowledge, and flees through shape-shifting transformations before being swallowed by Ceridwen and reborn as Taliesin, who is set adrift and found by Elffin. This myth made Taliesin the archetypal bard of Welsh tradition, in the language discussed at Welsh language.
