Eugenio Beltrami

Eugenio Beltrami (16 November 1835 – 18 February 1900) was an Italian mathematician notable for his work on non-Euclidean geometry, continuum mechanics and mathematical physics. His 1868 model of hyperbolic geometry provided the first proof that Euclid's parallel postulate cannot be derived from the other axioms of Euclidean geometry.

Life

Beltrami was born in Cremona, then part of the Austrian Empire. He studied mathematics at the University of Pavia under Francesco Brioschi, but financial difficulties forced him to leave and work as a railway secretary. He returned to academia, holding professorships at Bologna, Pisa, Pavia and finally Rome, where he died in 1900.

Work

In his Essay on the Interpretation of Non-Euclidean Geometry (1868), Beltrami constructed a model of Lobachevskian geometry within Euclidean geometry, using the surface of constant negative curvature he called the pseudosphere. This established the relative consistency of hyperbolic geometry: if Euclidean geometry is consistent, so is the non-Euclidean kind. With his contemporary Felix Klein he is credited with the Beltrami–Klein model of the hyperbolic plane. Beltrami also made important contributions to differential geometry (the Laplace–Beltrami operator is named for him), elasticity, hydrodynamics, thermodynamics and potential theory, and his 1873 work on singular potentials anticipated later developments in vector analysis.
