Telemundo is an American Spanish-language terrestrial television network owned by NBCUniversal, a division of Comcast. It is the second-largest provider of Spanish-language content in the United States, after Univision, reaching the great majority of Hispanic households through owned stations and affiliates.

History

The network traces its origins to WKAQ-TV in San Juan, Puerto Rico, founded in 1954 by Ángel Ramos, who coined the name "Telemundo" ("World TV"). On the US mainland it began in 1984 as NetSpan, a network of Spanish-language stations, and adopted the Telemundo name in 1987 after its acquisition by Reliance Capital. Sony and Liberty Media bought the network in the late 1990s, and NBC acquired it in 2002 for about $2.7 billion. In 2018 the network moved into Telemundo Center in the Miami area, one of the largest television production facilities in the United States.

Programming

Telemundo produces most of its programming in-house, notably its prime-time super series and telenovelas filmed in Miami, Mexico and Colombia — hits include La Reina del Sur and El Señor de los Cielos. Its schedule also includes the morning show Hoy Día, the national newscast Noticias Telemundo, reality formats, and major sports rights: it holds US Spanish-language rights to the FIFA World Cup and broadcasts Premier League football.
