Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab), located just outside Batavia, Illinois, is a United States Department of Energy national laboratory specialising in high-energy particle physics. Founded in 1967 and named after Enrico Fermi, the laboratory discovered the bottom quark (1977) and the top quark (1995) at its Tevatron collider, the world's highest-energy accelerator until the Large Hadron Collider surpassed it. Since the Tevatron's shutdown in 2011, Fermilab has centred its programme on neutrino physics, muon experiments, and cosmology.

History

The site — 6,800 acres of Illinois prairie — was chosen in 1966, and founding director Robert R. Wilson built the laboratory ahead of schedule and under budget, opening its Main Ring in 1972. The superconducting Tevatron began operations in 1983, colliding protons and antiprotons at nearly 2 TeV; the CDF and DØ experiments there discovered the top quark in 1995. Leon Lederman, the second director, founded the laboratory's education programmes and its famous herd of American bison.

Current programme

Fermilab produces the world's most intense neutrino beams. Its flagship project is the international Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) with the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility, sending neutrinos 1,300 km from Fermilab to detectors deep underground in South Dakota. Other major efforts include the Muon g-2 experiment, which reported measurements of the muon's anomalous magnetic moment in tension with theoretical prediction, the Mu2e experiment, the PIP-II superconducting linac, and roles in the Dark Energy Survey and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST.
