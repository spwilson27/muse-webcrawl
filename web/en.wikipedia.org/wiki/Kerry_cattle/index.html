Kerry cattle

Placeholder image: a Kerry cow.

Kerry cattle (Irish: Bó Chiarraí) are a rare dairy breed native to Ireland, particularly associated with County Kerry. The breed is considered one of the oldest in Europe, traditionally believed to descend from the cattle of Neolithic settlers, and is valued for producing rich milk on poor pasture, including the rough grazing of the Irish hills.

Characteristics

Kerry cattle are small, fine-boned and predominantly black, occasionally with white markings on the udder. Cows weigh around 350–400 kg and produce milk with a high butterfat content suited to butter and cheese making. They are hardy, long-lived, and good mothers, able to thrive where larger improved breeds struggle.

History and conservation

The Kerry was once the main dairy cow of small farms in western Ireland but declined sharply in the 20th century as farmers adopted higher-yielding breeds such as the Holstein-Friesian. It is now classified as a rare breed; a herd book was established in the early 20th century, and conservation efforts by the Kerry Cattle Society and Irish government agri-environment schemes support remaining breeders. The related, smaller Dexter breed is thought to have partly derived from Kerry stock.
