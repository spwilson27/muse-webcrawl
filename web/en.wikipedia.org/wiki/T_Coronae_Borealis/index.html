T Coronae Borealis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

T Coronae Borealis (T CrB), nicknamed the Blaze Star, is a recurrent nova in the constellation Corona Borealis. It is a binary star system comprising a white dwarf and a red giant that orbit each other every 228 days, at a distance of roughly 3,000 light-years from Earth. It was first discovered in outburst in 1866 by John Birmingham.

Eruptions

Material shed by the red giant is accreted onto the white dwarf. Roughly every 80 years the accumulated hydrogen layer on the white dwarf's surface becomes dense and hot enough to ignite in a runaway thermonuclear reaction, ejecting the layer and brightening the system by thousands of times for a few days. Recorded eruptions occurred in 1866 and 1946, when the star rose from about magnitude 10 to about magnitude 2 — easily visible to the naked eye; a possible earlier eruption was reported in 1217, and another may have been seen in 1787.

Anticipated eruption

Before the 1946 eruption the star showed a characteristic brightening and then a dip in brightness, and a similar dip was observed beginning in 2023, leading astronomers to predict a further eruption in the mid-2020s. The event is expected to make T CrB temporarily one of the brighter stars in its part of the sky, and both professional and amateur astronomers monitor it closely.

System properties

The red giant is an M-type giant that fills a substantial fraction of its Roche lobe, funnelling gas through the inner Lagrange point and through its wind onto the massive white dwarf, whose mass is estimated near the upper end of the white dwarf range. Between eruptions the system shows flickering typical of accretion, and is classified as a symbiotic star.
