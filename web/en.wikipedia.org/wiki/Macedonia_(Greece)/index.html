Macedonia (Greece)

Macedonia (Greek: Μακεδονία, Makedonía) is a geographic and administrative region of Greece, and the country's largest and second-most populous region. Together with Western Thrace it forms part of northern Greece. Its capital and largest city is Thessaloniki. The region corresponds roughly to the southern part of the ancient kingdom of Macedon, homeland of Alexander the Great.

Geography

Greek Macedonia is bounded by Albania, North Macedonia and Bulgaria to the north, the Aegean Sea to the south, Thessaly and Epirus to the southwest, and Thrace to the east. It is a largely mountainous region, containing Mount Olympus — the highest mountain in Greece and mythological home of the Olympian gods — as well as Mount Athos, an autonomous monastic community. Major rivers include the Haliacmon, Axios (Vardar), Strymon and Nestos.

History

The ancient kingdom of Macedon rose to prominence under Philip II and his son Alexander the Great in the 4th century BC. After Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman rule — the region remained under the Ottoman Empire until the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 — Macedonia was divided among Greece, Serbia and Bulgaria, with Greece receiving the Aegean (southern) portion. The name dispute with the neighbouring republic, which called itself "Macedonia" after 1991, was resolved by the 2018 Prespa Agreement, under which the neighbour became North Macedonia and Greece recognised its language and identity with qualifiers.

Administration

For administrative purposes Greek Macedonia is divided into three regions (peripheries): Western Macedonia, Central Macedonia, and the Macedonian regional units of the Region of Eastern Macedonia and Thrace. Thessaloniki is the dominant economic and cultural centre, a major port and university city.
