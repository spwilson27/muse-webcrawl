National Tsunami Warning Center

NTWC
Location: Palmer, Alaska, USA
Parent: NOAA / National Weather Service
Former name: West Coast and Alaska Tsunami Warning Center

The National Tsunami Warning Center (NTWC), located in Palmer, Alaska, is a United States tsunami warning center operated by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) as part of the National Weather Service. It issues tsunami warnings, watches, and advisories for the continental United States, Alaska, and Canada's Pacific and Atlantic coasts.

History

The center was established in Palmer in 1967–1968, following the devastating 1964 Alaska earthquake and tsunami, as the Palmer Observatory. Renamed the West Coast and Alaska Tsunami Warning Center, it long served the U.S. West Coast, Alaska, and British Columbia. In 2013 it was renamed the National Tsunami Warning Center after its area of responsibility expanded to include the U.S. Atlantic and Gulf coasts and eastern Canada; the Pacific Tsunami Warning Center in Hawaii covers Hawaii, U.S. Pacific territories, and international partners.

Operations

Staffed around the clock, the center analyses seismic data and sea-level observations from coastal tide gauges and DART deep-ocean buoys to detect tsunamis and forecast their arrival times and heights, issuing messages to emergency managers, the media, and the public, including via the Emergency Alert System and NOAA Weather Radio.
