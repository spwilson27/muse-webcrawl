TypeVehicle

A cart is a vehicle designed for transport, using two wheels and normally pulled by one or a pair of draught animals, though hand-pushed and hand-pulled carts are also common. The two-wheeled cart is one of the oldest forms of wheeled transport, dating to the early history of the wheel itself.

History

Carts appear in the archaeological record from the 4th millennium BC in Mesopotamia and Europe, following the invention of the wheel. Two-wheeled carts were used in warfare as chariots, in agriculture, and for hauling goods; the Roman carpentum and the humble farm cart were both carts by this definition. Because a cart's load is balanced over a single axle, a draught animal can pull substantial weight, resting between two shafts.

Types

Common types include the ox cart and bullock cart of Asia and Africa, the horse-drawn dog cart and governess cart, the Irish jaunting car, the two-wheeled tipping cart for building and farm work, and hand carts such as the rickshaw and the gardener's handcart. A person who drives or makes carts is historically a carter or cartwright.

Terminology

By contrast, a four-wheeled vehicle for loads is a wagon, and a four-wheeled passenger vehicle is a carriage. The word "cart" is also applied figuratively and to light modern vehicles such as shopping carts, golf carts, and go-karts.
