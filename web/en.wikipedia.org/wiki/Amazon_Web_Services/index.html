Amazon Web Services, Inc. (AWS) is a subsidiary of Amazon that provides on-demand cloud computing platforms and APIs to individuals, companies and governments on a metered pay-as-you-go basis. Launched in 2006 with the Simple Storage Service (S3) and Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2), AWS essentially created the modern cloud infrastructure market and remains its largest provider, ahead of Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud. Despite being far smaller than Amazon's retail business in revenue, AWS generates the majority of the company's operating profit.

History

AWS grew out of Amazon's internal infrastructure work in the early 2000s. The 2006 launch of S3 in March and EC2 in August let startups rent storage and servers by the hour instead of buying hardware; early customers such as Netflix built their businesses on it. Under long-time CEO Andy Jassy (who became Amazon's CEO in 2021) AWS expanded to hundreds of services and dozens of geographic "Regions", each with multiple Availability Zone data centers. Adam Selipsky led AWS from 2021 to 2024, succeeded by Matt Garman.

Services

Flagship services include EC2 (virtual servers), S3 (object storage), Lambda (serverless computing), RDS and DynamoDB (databases), CloudFront (content delivery) and SageMaker and Bedrock (machine learning and generative AI). Major customers include large enterprises, governments and much of the consumer internet; major outages in its oldest region, US-East-1 in Northern Virginia, have periodically taken down many unrelated websites, underlining its centrality to the web.

Market position

Analysts typically estimate AWS holds roughly thirty percent of the worldwide cloud infrastructure market, with Microsoft Azure second and Google Cloud third. In the mid-2020s all three have invested heavily in AI data centers, with AWS offering its own Trainium and Inferentia chips alongside Nvidia GPUs.
