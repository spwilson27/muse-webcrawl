Dispute resolution

Dispute resolution or dispute settlement is the process of resolving disputes between parties. The term encompasses a wide range of processes, including judicial litigation, arbitration, mediation, negotiated settlement, and collaborative law.

FieldLaw, conflict studies

Methods

Litigation resolves disputes through courts, ending in a binding judgment. Arbitration submits the dispute to a neutral arbitrator whose award is usually binding. Mediation uses a neutral facilitator to help the parties reach a voluntary agreement; the mediator does not impose an outcome. Negotiation proceeds directly between the parties without a third party.

Alternative dispute resolution

Processes other than litigation are collectively called alternative dispute resolution (ADR). ADR is often promoted as faster, cheaper, and more private than court proceedings, and it can preserve ongoing business relationships by producing negotiated rather than imposed settlements.

Online dispute resolution

Online dispute resolution applies technology to facilitate or automate the resolution of disputes, particularly e-commerce disagreements handled by marketplaces and payment processors handling large volumes of low-value claims.
