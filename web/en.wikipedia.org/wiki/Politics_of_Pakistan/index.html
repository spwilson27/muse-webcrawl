Politics of Pakistan

The politics of Pakistan take place within a federal parliamentary republic established by the Constitution of 1973. The President is head of state, the Prime Minister is head of government, and the bicameral Parliament (Majlis-e-Shoora) consists of the National Assembly and the Senate. Pakistan has four provinces: Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan.

Civilian and military rule

Pakistan's history alternates between elected civilian governments and military rule. The armed forces seized power in 1958 (Ayub Khan), 1977 (Zia-ul-Haq) and 1999 (Pervez Musharraf), and the military retains a powerful behind-the-scenes role — the so-called "establishment" — in foreign, security and even domestic policy. No elected prime minister has ever completed a full five-year term.

Parties

Major parties include the Pakistan Muslim League (Nawaz), the Pakistan Peoples Party founded by Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, and Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf, founded by cricketer Imran Khan, who became prime minister in 2018 and was removed by a no-confidence vote in 2022. Regional and religious parties also win representation. Elections are administered by the Election Commission of Pakistan and are frequently accompanied by allegations of rigging and pre-poll engineering.

Judiciary and politics

The superior judiciary has often intervened in politics, validating coups under the "doctrine of necessity" but also disqualifying prime ministers, as in the 2017 Panama Papers case against Nawaz Sharif. See also Prime Minister of Pakistan.
