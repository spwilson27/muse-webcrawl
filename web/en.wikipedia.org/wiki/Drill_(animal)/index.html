Drill (animal)

The drill (Mandrillus leucophaeus) is a primate of the family Cercopithecidae (Old World monkeys), related to baboons and even more closely to mandrills. It is found in Cross River State in Nigeria, southwestern Cameroon, and on Bioko Island in Equatorial Guinea. One of the largest monkeys in the world, it is also one of Africa's most endangered mammals.

Description

Drills are large, sexually dimorphic monkeys. Males can weigh up to about 32 kg (70 lb), roughly twice the weight of females. They have dark grey-brown fur, a black face rimmed by a white ruff, and distinctive ridged, bony protrusions along the nose. Males develop a lilac-coloured chin and brightly coloured rumps. The tail is very short.

Behaviour

Drills live in multi-male, multi-female groups of around 20–30 individuals and can aggregate into "super groups" of over 100. They are semi-terrestrial, foraging on the ground for fruit, roots, invertebrates and small animals, and sleeping in trees at night.

Conservation

Hunted for bushmeat and threatened by habitat loss, the drill is listed as Endangered. Conservation efforts include the Drill Ranch in Nigeria, founded by the Pandrillus Foundation.
