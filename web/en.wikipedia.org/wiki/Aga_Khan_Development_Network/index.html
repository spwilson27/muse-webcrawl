The Aga Khan Development Network (AKDN) is a group of private, international, non-denominational development agencies founded by the Aga Khan, the Imam of the Nizari Ismaili Muslims. Its agencies work to improve living conditions and opportunities in the developing world, principally in sub-Saharan Africa, Central and South Asia, and the Middle East, employing some 90,000 people, most of them in the countries served.

Structure and agencies

The network's agencies include:

  • Aga Khan Foundation (1967) — rural development, health, education, and civil society.
  • Aga Khan Fund for Economic Development (AKFED, 1984) — for-profit investments in industry, tourism, financial services, and infrastructure, whose surpluses support development work.
  • Aga Khan University (1983) — chartered in Pakistan, with campuses and programmes across East Africa and Central Asia, including major teaching hospitals in Karachi and Nairobi.
  • University of Central Asia (2000) — founded by treaty between the Aga Khan and Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and Tajikistan, with mountain campuses in all three countries.
  • Aga Khan Trust for Culture — runs the Aga Khan Award for Architecture, historic-city revitalisation (such as Cairo's al-Azhar Park), and music and museum programmes.
  • Aga Khan Academies, Aga Khan Education Services, Aga Khan Health Services, Aga Khan Agency for Microfinance, and the Aga Khan Agency for Habitat.

Approach

The AKDN describes its mandate as deriving from the Ismaili Imamat's ethical framework while serving people of all faiths. Its signature approach is long-term, multi-input area development: combining schools, clinics, water and sanitation, rural finance, and enterprise support in defined regions such as northern Pakistan, the Tajik Pamirs, and coastal Kenya, in partnership with governments and international donors.
