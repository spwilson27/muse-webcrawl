Hyundai Motor Company (Korean: 현대자동차), often abbreviated to Hyundai Motors, is a South Korean multinational automotive manufacturer headquartered in Seoul, South Korea. Founded in 1967 by Chung Ju-yung, it is the world's third-largest carmaker by production volume together with its affiliate Kia, in which it holds a major stake; both form part of the Hyundai Motor Group. The company operates the world's largest integrated automobile manufacturing facility in Ulsan, with an annual production capacity of about 1.6 million units.

History

Hyundai's first model, the Cortina, was built in cooperation with Ford Motor Company in 1968. Its first independently designed car was the Pony of 1975, styled by Giorgetto Giugiaro's Italdesign and exported widely, including to Canada and later the United States (as the Excel). After surviving the 1997 Asian financial crisis — acquiring Kia in 1998 — the company focused heavily on quality, design, and global expansion, building plants in the United States, India, China, Turkey, the Czech Republic, and elsewhere. In 2015 it launched Genesis as a standalone luxury brand, and in 2020 revived the Ioniq name as a dedicated electric vehicle sub-brand, whose Ioniq 5 and Ioniq 6 models have won major international awards.

Products and technology

Hyundai sells a full range of passenger cars and SUVs — including the Elantra, Sonata, Tucson, and Santa Fe — under the Hyundai marque, alongside Genesis luxury models and the Ioniq battery-electric line. It is a leading developer of hydrogen fuel cell vehicles, marketing the Nexo SUV, and it invests in electrification, autonomous driving, robotics (through Boston Dynamics), and urban air mobility. The company also competes in top-level motorsport, including the World Rally Championship.
