Onychomycosis

Onychomycosis, also called tinea unguium, is a fungal infection of the nail. It is the most common disease of the nails, accounting for roughly half of all nail abnormalities. Toenails are affected much more often than fingernails.

Signs and symptoms

Infected nails typically become thickened, discolored (white, yellow, brown, or black), brittle, and crumbly, and may separate from the nail bed (onycholysis). Debris collects beneath the nail plate. The infection usually causes little pain at first, though severe cases can make wearing shoes uncomfortable and affect quality of life, particularly in people with diabetes or weakened immunity.

Causes

Most cases are caused by dermatophyte fungi, especially Trichophyton rubrum and Trichophyton mentagrophytes. Yeasts such as Candida species and non-dermatophyte molds cause a minority of infections. Risk factors include older age, athlete's foot, nail injury, diabetes, peripheral vascular disease, psoriasis, and exposure to warm, moist communal environments.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is ideally confirmed by microscopy, culture, or histology of nail clippings, since many other conditions mimic fungal nails. Treatment options include oral antifungal medications such as terbinafine and itraconazole, which are the most effective; topical agents such as ciclopirox or efinaconazole for mild cases; and laser or surgical approaches in selected patients. Treatment courses are long because nails grow slowly, and recurrence is common.
