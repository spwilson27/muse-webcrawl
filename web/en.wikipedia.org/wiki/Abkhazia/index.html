Abkhazia (Abkhaz: Аҧсны, Apsny; Georgian: აფხაზეთი) is a partially recognised state on the eastern coast of the Black Sea, in the South Caucasus. It considers itself the independent Republic of Abkhazia; Georgia and most of the international community regard it as Georgia's Autonomous Republic of Abkhazia under Russian occupation. The territory covers about 8,665 square kilometres between the Psou River (border with Russia) and the Enguri River (ceasefire line with Georgia proper).

History

Abkhazia was the site of Greek colonies on the Black Sea coast and of a medieval Abkhazian kingdom that helped unify Georgia in the tenth century. It later fell under Ottoman influence and was annexed by the Russian Empire in 1864, prompting mass emigration (muhajirism) of Muslim Abkhaz to the Ottoman Empire. In the Soviet period Abkhazia was first a union republic (SSR Abkhazia, 1921–1931) in treaty association with Georgia, then demoted to an autonomous republic within the Georgian SSR — a grievance sharpened by Georgianisation policies under Stalin and Beria.

War and secession

As the USSR dissolved, Abkhaz fears of subordination in an independent Georgia led to war in 1992–1993. Abkhaz separatists, aided by North Caucasian volunteers and elements of the Russian military, defeated Georgian forces; the fall of Sukhumi in September 1993 was followed by the flight or expulsion of some 200,000–250,000 ethnic Georgians, an ethnic cleansing recognised as such by the OSCE. A further clash in 1998 displaced Georgians again from the Gali district, though many later returned there.

Recognition and politics

During the August 2008 Russo-Georgian War, Russian and Abkhaz forces took the Kodori Gorge, the last Georgian-held part of Abkhazia. Russia recognised Abkhazia's independence on 26 August 2008, followed by Nicaragua, Venezuela, Nauru and (in 2018) Syria; Vanuatu and Tuvalu briefly recognised it before withdrawing recognition. Russia maintains military bases and border guards in the territory and supplies much of its budget. Abkhazia's politics are lively and competitive by regional standards, with street protests repeatedly forcing presidents from office (notably in 2014 and 2020 and again amid an energy and investment-law crisis in late 2024, when President Aslan Bzhania resigned). Georgia's policy of non-recognition, supported by the EU and US, treats Abkhazia and South Ossetia as occupied territories.

Geography and economy

The subtropical coast — Soviet-era resorts at Gagra, Pitsunda and New Athos, Lake Ritsa and the cave monastery of New Athos — once drew millions of tourists; tourism (largely Russian) remains the mainstay of a fragile economy alongside agriculture (tea, citrus, hazelnuts) and Russian subsidies.
