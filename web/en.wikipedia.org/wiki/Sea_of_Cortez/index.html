The Gulf of California (Spanish: Golfo de California), also known as the Sea of Cortés (Mar de Cortés) or Sea of Cortez, is a marginal sea of the Pacific Ocean that separates the Baja California Peninsula from the Mexican mainland. It is bordered by the Mexican states of Baja California, Baja California Sur, Sonora, Sinaloa and Nayarit.

Geology and geography

The gulf formed about 5.3 million years ago as the Baja California Peninsula rifted away from mainland Mexico along the East Pacific Rise and the San Andreas–Gulf of California fault system; the peninsula continues to move northwest at several centimetres per year. The gulf is about 1,126 km long and 48–241 km wide, with numerous islands including Ángel de la Guarda and Tiburón, the largest island in Mexico. Strong tidal ranges in the northern gulf can exceed 9 metres.

Ecology

The gulf is one of the most biologically diverse seas on Earth, home to more than 5,000 species of micro-invertebrates, rich fisheries, and marine mammals including the critically endangered vaquita, the world's smallest cetacean, found only in the northern gulf. Jacques Cousteau famously called it "the aquarium of the world". The islands and protected areas of the gulf are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
