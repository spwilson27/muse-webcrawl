Xerophyte

A xerophyte (from Greek xeros, "dry", and phyton, "plant") is a species of plant that has adaptations to survive in an environment with little liquid water, such as a desert or an ice- or snow-covered region (where liquid water is unavailable because it is frozen). Xerophytes are familiar as cacti, succulents and desert shrubs, but the category also includes many epiphytes and alpine plants. Popular examples include cacti, the pineapple, agaves and the resurrection plants that can revive after near-total desiccation.

Adaptations

Xerophytes reduce water loss and maximise water storage and uptake through a variety of mechanisms:

  • Water storage: succulent stems or leaves store water in parenchyma tissue, as in cacti, aloes and euphorbias.
  • Reduced surface area: small, needle-like or scale-like leaves, or spines in place of leaves, cut transpiration; photosynthesis moves into the green stem.
  • Thick cuticles and sunken stomata: waxy coatings, hairs, and stomata recessed in pits (often open only at night) limit evaporation.
  • CAM photosynthesis: many xerophytes use crassulacean acid metabolism, opening their stomata at night to fix CO2 and closing them during the hot day.
  • Root strategies: either very deep taproots reaching groundwater (phreatophytes such as mesquite) or wide, shallow root mats that exploit brief rains.
  • Life-cycle tricks: desert ephemerals complete their whole life cycle in the few weeks after rain and survive drought as seeds; poikilohydric "resurrection plants" such as Selaginella lepidophylla tolerate desiccation of their tissues themselves.

Classification

Ecologists contrast xerophytes with hydrophytes (aquatic plants) and mesophytes (plants of moderate moisture), and subdivide them into drought escapers (ephemerals), drought avoiders (succulents that store water and shut down) and drought tolerators (plants that withstand very low tissue water content). Xerophytes dominate the vegetation of deserts, steppes, Mediterranean scrub such as maquis, and dry rock and canopy habitats.
