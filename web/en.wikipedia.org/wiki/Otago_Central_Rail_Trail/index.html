Otago Central Rail Trail

The trail in the Poolburn Gorge

Length: 150 km (93 mi)

Location: Otago, New Zealand

Trailheads: Clyde, Middlemarch

Use: Cycling, walking, horse riding

Opened: 2000

The Otago Central Rail Trail is a 150-kilometre walking, cycling and horse-riding track in the South Island of New Zealand. It follows the former route of the Otago Central Railway between Clyde and Middlemarch, passing through the Maniototo plain and the Ida Valley in Central Otago.

The railway line was closed in 1990, and most of the track was removed. The Department of Conservation developed the corridor as a recreational trail, opening it in full in 2000. It was New Zealand's first major rail trail and became the model — and catalyst — for the New Zealand Cycle Trail network.

Route

From Clyde the trail passes through Alexandra, Galloway, Chatto Creek, Omakau, Lauder, Oturehua, Wedderburn, Ranfurly, Waipiata, Kokonga, Tiroiti and Hyde before reaching Middlemarch near Dunedin. Highlights include the Poolburn Gorge tunnels and viaduct, the curved Manuherikia Bridge, and the Prices Creek viaduct near Hyde. The trail's highest point, near Wedderburn, is 618 metres above sea level. Most riders complete the trail in three to five days, staying in small towns along the way that have developed accommodation, cafes and bike-hire services serving trail users.
