English Football Hall of Fame

English Football Hall of Fame
Established: 2002
Location: National Football Museum, Manchester
Operator: National Football Museum

The English Football Hall of Fame honours players, managers and teams who have made an outstanding contribution to association football in England. It is housed at the National Football Museum — originally in Preston and since 2012 in Manchester — and was established in 2002 with an initial class of inductees chosen by a panel of football historians and former players.

Induction

Inductees are normally chosen annually, with players required to be retired and to have played or managed in England for a substantial part of their careers; both English and foreign figures are eligible. A separate Premier League Hall of Fame, launched in 2021, honours players of the Premier League era and is distinct from the museum's hall.

Notable inductees

The inaugural 2002 class included Gordon Banks, George Best, Bobby Charlton, Kenny Dalglish, Dixie Dean, Peter Doherty, Duncan Edwards, Tom Finney, Trevor Francis, Jimmy Greaves, Billy Wright and managers Matt Busby, Brian Clough, Bob Paisley, Alf Ramsey and Bill Shankly. Later inductees have included the 1966 World Cup-winning England team, women's football pioneers such as Lily Parr, and modern figures including Eric Cantona, Thierry Henry and Alex Ferguson.

Selection panel

Selections are made by a panel convened by the National Football Museum, drawing on existing hall members, historians and journalists. Inductions are marked by a ceremony and by the addition of the inductee's memorabilia and profile to the museum's displays.
