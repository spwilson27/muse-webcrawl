The Spruance class was a class of 31 destroyers built for the United States Navy in the 1970s and named for Admiral Raymond A. Spruance. Designed primarily for anti-submarine warfare (ASW) in support of carrier battle groups, they were notable for introducing gas-turbine propulsion and extensive automation to American destroyers, and for their large, air-conditioned, and relatively quiet hulls.

Design

Powered by four LM2500 gas turbines driving two shafts, the ships exceeded 30 knots and were far quieter than their steam-driven predecessors — a key advantage for ASW. Armament originally centred on the ASROC anti-submarine rocket, an eight-cell Sea Sparrow launcher, two 5-inch guns, and helicopters operated from a large flight deck and hangar. In the 1980s and 1990s most ships received Tomahawk capability: armoured box launchers and, in many units, a 61-cell Vertical Launching System replacing the ASROC launcher.

Service and fate

The class served throughout the late Cold War and in operations including the Tanker War and the 1991 Gulf War. The related Kidd class, built originally for Iran, used the same hull with enhanced air defences. All Spruance-class ships were decommissioned by 2005 — relatively early, a decision debated in naval circles — and most were sunk as targets, providing valuable ship-survivability data.
