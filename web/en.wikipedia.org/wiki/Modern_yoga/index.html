Modern yoga is a broad range of yoga practices with differing purposes, encompassing in its various forms yoga philosophy derived from the Vedas and later sources, meditation, and spirituality; first and foremost, however, it is yoga as exercise, a physical activity consisting mainly of postures called asanas, connected by transitional sequences sometimes accompanied by the breathing exercises of pranayama, and usually ending with a period of relaxation or meditation. It became popular in the Western world beginning in the mid-20th century, drawing millions of practitioners.

History

Modern yoga as exercise emerged in the early 20th century, in part through the teachings of Tirumalai Krishnamacharya, often called the father of modern yoga. His students included B. K. S. Iyengar, K. Pattabhi Jois, and Indra Devi, who carried distinctive styles to worldwide audiences.

Earlier influences included the physical culture movements of Europe and America, Scandinavian gymnastics, and the harmonial movements of the 19th century, which blended with Indian traditions as yoga was presented to Western seekers. Swami Vivekananda's appearance at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago in 1893 is often taken as a starting point for yoga's global spread.

Styles

Major styles of modern yoga include Iyengar yoga, with its emphasis on alignment and props; Ashtanga vinyasa yoga, a flowing sequence of postures; Bikram or hot yoga, practised in heated rooms; Yin yoga, with long-held passive postures; and power yoga, a vigorous fitness-oriented approach.

Many classes combine postures (asanas), breath control (pranayama), and meditation, though the balance between physical exercise and spiritual content varies widely between teachers and traditions.

Health effects and research

Research suggests yoga practice may reduce stress, improve flexibility, strength, and balance, and offer modest benefits for back pain and anxiety, though the quality of evidence varies. As with any physical activity, injuries can occur, particularly with forceful adjustments or extreme postures.

Modern yoga is practised by tens of millions of people worldwide and supports a large industry of studios, teacher training, clothing, and retreats.
