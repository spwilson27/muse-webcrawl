Protected areas of South Africa

The protected areas of South Africa include national parks managed by South African National Parks (SANParks), provincial nature reserves, marine protected areas, and privately owned game reserves. Together they conserve the country's exceptional biodiversity across biomes from fynbos to savanna.

National parks

There are 19 national parks, covering about 4 per cent of the land area. The largest and best known is Kruger National Park, established in 1926 and covering nearly 20,000 km² in the northeast. Others include Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park (shared with Botswana), Table Mountain National Park in Cape Town, Addo Elephant National Park, and iSimangaliso Wetland Park, a World Heritage Site.

Legal framework

The system is governed by the National Environmental Management: Protected Areas Act of 2003, which sets categories including special nature reserves, national parks, nature reserves and protected environments. Marine protected areas were greatly expanded in 2019, when 20 new areas brought about 5 per cent of the ocean territory under protection.

Conservation challenges

Major challenges include rhino poaching, invasive alien species, climate change, and funding. Transfrontier conservation areas, such as the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park linking Kruger with parks in Mozambique and Zimbabwe, aim to restore historic migration routes.
