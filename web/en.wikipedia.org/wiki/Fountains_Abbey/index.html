Fountains Abbey

LocationNear Ripon, North Yorkshire, England
OrderCistercian
Founded1132
Dissolved1539
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (with Studley Royal Park)

Fountains Abbey is one of the largest and best preserved ruined Cistercian monasteries in England. It is located approximately 3 miles (5 km) south-west of Ripon in North Yorkshire, near the village of Aldfield. Founded in 1132 by monks who had left St Mary's Abbey, York, in protest at its laxity, and admitted into the Cistercian order, the abbey operated for over 400 years, becoming one of the wealthiest monasteries in England through wool production and extensive estates.

History

The community survived attack, fire and plague, and its surviving church — with the great Cellarium and the later Tower of Abbot Huby — shows building campaigns from the 12th to the early 16th century. In 1539 the abbey was suppressed in Henry VIII's Dissolution of the Monasteries, surrendered by Abbot Marmaduke Bradley; the estate was sold by the Crown.

In the 18th century the ruins were incorporated into the landscaped park of Studley Royal, with its celebrated water garden. Fountains Abbey and Studley Royal Park together were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986 and are cared for by the National Trust and English Heritage.
