Rangoli

Rangoli is an art form originating in the Indian subcontinent, in which patterns are created on the floor or a tabletop using materials such as powdered lime stone, red ochre, dry rice flour, coloured sand, quartz powder, flower petals and coloured rocks. It is an everyday practice in many Hindu households, and elaborate rangoli are made during festivals such as Diwali, Onam, Pongal and Tihar to welcome deities and bring good fortune.

A Diwali rangoli with diyas (oil lamps)

Regional names and styles

The art is known by many regional names: kolam in Tamil Nadu, muggu in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, rangavalli in Karnataka, alpona in Bengal, aipan in Uttarakhand, madana in Rajasthan and pookkalam (floral rangoli) in Kerala. Traditional designs are geometric — dots arranged in grids joined by looping lines, lotus motifs, peacocks, footprints of the goddess Lakshmi — though contemporary rangoli include freehand, figurative and portrait designs, and competitions are held during festivals.

Materials and technique

Designs are drawn freehand, with the powder trickled between thumb and forefinger, or by joining a lattice of dots (pulli kolam). Traditionally, rice flour served a charitable purpose, feeding ants, birds and other small creatures — a daily act of generosity toward other beings. Colours historically came from natural sources such as turmeric, indigo, kumkum and ground leaves; today synthetic colours are common. Rangoli may also be decorated with floating candles or diyas, and materials are swept away after the festival, reflecting the impermanence central to the tradition.
