Charles I (early 1226/1227 – 7 January 1285), commonly called Charles of Anjou, was a French prince, the youngest son of King Louis VIII of France and Blanche of Castile, and a younger brother of King Louis IX. Count of Anjou and Maine by inheritance and Count of Provence and Forcalquier by marriage, he conquered the Kingdom of Sicily in 1266 and founded the Capetian House of Anjou.

Rise to power

Charles accompanied his brother Louis IX on the Seventh Crusade to Egypt in 1248 and was captured at the Battle of Fariskur in 1250. After his release he consolidated his rule in Provence, suppressing revolts in Marseille and other cities.

Conquest of Sicily

With the support of Pope Clement IV, who sought to displace the Hohenstaufen dynasty, Charles invaded southern Italy and defeated Manfred of Sicily at the Battle of Benevento in 1266, where Manfred was killed. He defeated the last Hohenstaufen claimant, Conradin, at the Battle of Tagliacozzo in 1268 and had him executed in Naples.

Sicilian Vespers and death

Charles's harsh rule and heavy taxation provoked the revolt of the Sicilian Vespers in 1282, which cost him the island of Sicily to Peter III of Aragon. Retaining only the mainland kingdom centered on Naples, he died at Foggia in 1285 while preparing a counterattack.
