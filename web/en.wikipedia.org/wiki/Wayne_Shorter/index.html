Wayne Shorter (August 25, 1933 – March 2, 2023) was an American jazz saxophonist and composer. Shorter came to prominence in the late 1950s as a member of, and eventually primary composer for, Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers. In the 1960s he joined Miles Davis's Second Great Quintet, and from there he co-founded the jazz fusion band Weather Report. He recorded more than 20 albums as a bandleader in jazz and is widely regarded as one of the most important composers in the genre.

Early life

Shorter was born in Newark, New Jersey, and attended Newark Arts High School. Encouraged by his parents, he took up the clarinet and later the tenor saxophone. After graduating from New York University with a degree in music education in 1956, he served two years in the United States Army, during which he played briefly with Horace Silver.

Career

After leaving the army, Shorter joined Maynard Ferguson's band briefly before being recruited by Art Blakey in 1959. With the Jazz Messengers he became the band's musical director and wrote compositions such as "Lester Left Town" and "One by One." In 1964 he joined Miles Davis's quintet alongside Herbie Hancock, Ron Carter, and Tony Williams, contributing standards including "E.S.P.," "Footprints," and "Nefertiti."

As a leader for Blue Note Records, Shorter recorded a string of acclaimed albums including Night Dreamer (1964), JuJu (1964), Speak No Evil (1965), and Adam's Apple (1966). In 1970 he co-founded Weather Report with Joe Zawinul; the band became one of the most commercially successful jazz fusion groups of the 1970s, releasing albums such as Heavy Weather (1977).

Later work and legacy

From 2000, Shorter led an acoustic quartet with pianist Danilo Pérez, bassist John Patitucci, and drummer Brian Blade, earning wide acclaim for live albums including Footprints Live! and Without a Net. He won 12 Grammy Awards over his career and was named an NEA Jazz Master in 1998. His opera Iphigenia, created with Esperanza Spalding, premiered in 2021. Shorter died in Los Angeles on March 2, 2023, at the age of 89.
