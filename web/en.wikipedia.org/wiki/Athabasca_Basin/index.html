Athabasca Basin

The Athabasca Basin is a region in the Canadian Shield of northern Saskatchewan and Alberta, Canada. It is best known as the source of the world's highest-grade uranium mines: the basin hosts unconformity-associated uranium deposits of exceptional grade, including Cigar Lake and McArthur River, and supplies a substantial share of global uranium production.

Geology

The basin consists of flat-lying, unmetamorphosed sandstones and conglomerates of Paleoproterozoic age (about 1.7 billion years old) resting unconformably on deformed Archean and Paleoproterozoic basement rocks. Uranium mineralisation occurs at and near this unconformity, where oxidised basinal fluids met reducing basement rocks, producing deposits with grades tens or hundreds of times higher than typical uranium ores elsewhere.

Mining

Uranium exploration began in the 1940s–50s around Uranium City on Lake Athabasca. Major discoveries at Rabbit Lake (1968), Key Lake (1975–76), Cigar Lake (1981), and McArthur River (1988) established the basin as the world's premier uranium district. Operators such as Cameco and Orano mine the high-grade ore with specialised methods, including remote-controlled raise boring and ground freezing at Cigar Lake. The basin has at times accounted for roughly 15–20 percent of world uranium production.
