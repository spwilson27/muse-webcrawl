Heerenveen

Heerenveen (West Frisian: It Hearrenfean) is a town and municipality in the province of Friesland in the north of the Netherlands. Founded in 1551 as a centre for the digging of peat (veen), it is best known internationally as a centre of speed skating, home to the Thialf arena, and for its football club, SC Heerenveen.

History

Heerenveen was established in 1551 by three lords (heeren) — Van Dekema, Cuyck and Foeyts — who bought the surrounding moorland to exploit its peat, giving the settlement its name, "the lords' peat." The Heeresloot and Schoterlandse Compagnonsvaart canals were dug for transporting peat. Because it was a peat colony rather than a chartered city, Heerenveen never received city rights, yet it grew into one of the largest towns in Friesland.

Sport

The Thialf ice stadium, rebuilt as an indoor arena in 1986, is one of the fastest low-lying long-track speed skating venues in the world and hosts World Cup and World Championship events; Dutch skating greats such as Sven Kramer trained there. SC Heerenveen plays at the Abe Lenstra Stadion, named after the Frisian footballer Abe Lenstra, and is noted for its Frisian regional identity, including playing the Frisian anthem before home matches.

Transport

Heerenveen railway station lies on the line between Leeuwarden and Zwolle, and the A7 and A32 motorways meet at the town, making it a junction for the northern Netherlands.
