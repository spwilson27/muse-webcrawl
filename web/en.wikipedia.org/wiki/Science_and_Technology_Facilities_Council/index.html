The Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC) is a United Kingdom government agency and one of the nine councils of UK Research and Innovation. It funds research in astronomy, particle physics, nuclear physics and space science, and operates large national research facilities used by university scientists and industry.

History

STFC was formed on 1 April 2007 by the merger of the Particle Physics and Astronomy Research Council (PPARC) with the Council for the Central Laboratory of the Research Councils (CCLRC). In 2018 it became part of the newly created UK Research and Innovation.

Facilities

At the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory in Oxfordshire, STFC runs the ISIS neutron and muon source, the Diamond Light Source synchrotron (as majority shareholder), the Central Laser Facility and the RAL Space department. It also operates the Daresbury Laboratory in Cheshire — home to the Hartree Centre for supercomputing — the UK Astronomy Technology Centre in Edinburgh, and the observatories on La Palma and Hawaii, and it manages the UK subscriptions to international organisations including CERN, the European Southern Observatory and the European Space Agency's science programme.

Funding

The council provides research grants to UK universities and funds PhD training, public engagement and technology development, with an annual budget on the order of £900 million in recent years.
