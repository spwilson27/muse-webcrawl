Xiamen, historically known as Amoy, is a sub-provincial city in southeastern Fujian province, China, facing the Taiwan Strait. It consists of Xiamen Island, Gulangyu Island and a larger mainland area, and is one of China's original four Special Economic Zones established in 1980. The city is a major port and tourist destination, with a population of about 5.3 million.

History

Xiamen grew as a trading port under the Ming and Qing dynasties and was a centre of the tea trade. It was one of the five treaty ports opened to foreign trade by the Treaty of Nanjing in 1842. Gulangyu, a small island off the city, became an international settlement; its colonial-era architecture earned UNESCO World Heritage status in 2017. Xiamen also played a key role in relations with Taiwan, facing the Taiwan-administered Kinmen islands only a few kilometres offshore.

Economy and landmarks

The city hosts Xiamen University, founded in 1921, and landmarks including Nanputuo Temple and the Hulishan Fortress. Its mild climate, beaches and historic Shacha noodles and seafood cuisine make it one of China's most popular domestic tourist destinations.
