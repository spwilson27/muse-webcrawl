Embolization

Embolization refers to the passage and lodging of an embolus within the bloodstream. In therapeutic use, it is a minimally invasive procedure, performed by interventional radiologists, in which embolic agents are delivered through a catheter into a blood vessel to block blood flow deliberately for medical benefit.

Uses

Therapeutic embolization is used to control bleeding (for example, gastrointestinal or traumatic haemorrhage), to treat vascular malformations and aneurysms, and to starve tumours of their blood supply. Specific applications include uterine artery embolization for fibroids, chemoembolization of liver tumours (in which chemotherapy drugs are delivered with embolic particles), radioembolization with radioactive microspheres, and preoperative devascularisation of tumours.

Agents and technique

Under imaging guidance, a catheter is advanced from a puncture site — commonly the femoral or radial artery — to the target vessel. Embolic materials include metal coils, detachable balloons, polyvinyl alcohol particles, calibrated microspheres, gelatin sponge, and liquid agents such as n-butyl cyanoacrylate glue or ethylene-vinyl alcohol copolymer. Agents may be temporary or permanent. Risks include non-target embolization, vessel injury, infection, and post-embolization syndrome of pain, fever, and nausea.
