Garage house

Garage house (also called New York house or simply garage) is a style of electronic dance music that developed in New York City in the early to mid-1980s, named after the Paradise Garage nightclub where resident DJ Larry Levan developed his distinctive, soulful approach to mixing disco, funk, R&B and early house records. Garage house is characterised by gospel-influenced piano chords, rich orchestration, emotive vocals and a warmer, more musical sensibility than the Chicago house sound from which it partly diverged.

History

The Paradise Garage (1977–1987) at 84 King Street became the crucible of the style: Levan's marathon sets, the club's exceptional sound system (designed by Richard Long), and its membership-based, largely Black and Latino gay crowd shaped a tradition of dance music as spiritual and communal experience. As recording caught up with DJ culture, producers such as Boyd Jarvis, Timmy Regisford, and the production team behind labels like Prelude and later Strictly Rhythm codified the garage sound, overlapping with what became known more broadly as deep house and soulful house.

Musical characteristics

Garage house typically uses a four-on-the-floor kick at around 118–128 BPM, swung hi-hats and percussion, prominent piano or organ riffs derived from gospel and disco, and diva-style lead vocals — artists such as Loleatta Holloway (whose sampled voice anchored Black Box's "Ride on Time") and Jocelyn Brown became signature voices. Compared with Chicago house, garage tracks favour live-sounding basslines and arrangements closer to disco and boogie.

Legacy

The term's shortened form, "garage", was later borrowed in Britain, where UK garage developed in the 1990s from sped-up, chopped New York garage imports played in London clubs — a lineage that led onward to 2-step, dubstep and grime. Soulful house continues the original New York tradition.
