2005 Kashmir earthquake

Date: 8 October 2005

Time: 08:50:39 PKT (UTC+5)

Magnitude: 7.6 Mw

Epicentre: Near Muzaffarabad, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, Pakistan

Deaths: about 86,000–87,351

Injured: about 69,000–75,266

The 2005 Kashmir earthquake occurred at 08:50:39 Pakistan Standard Time on 8 October 2005 in Azad Jammu and Kashmir, a territory administered by Pakistan. It was centred near the city of Muzaffarabad, and also affected nearby Balakot in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and some areas of Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir. It registered a moment magnitude of 7.6 and had a maximum Mercalli intensity of XI (Extreme).

Damage and casualties

The earthquake was the deadliest ever recorded in South Asia up to that time. The official death toll in Pakistan was about 73,276 to 87,350, with some estimates putting it at over 100,000; about 1,350 people were killed in Indian-administered Kashmir. Nearly 3.5 million people were rendered homeless, and more than 690,000 buildings were damaged or destroyed, most of them in rural areas where construction was of poor quality. The towns of Muzaffarabad and Balakot were devastated; in Balakot about 90 percent of the buildings were destroyed.

Geology

The earthquake resulted from the ongoing collision between the Indian Plate and the Eurasian Plate, the same tectonic process that built the Himalayas. Rupture occurred on a previously unmapped thrust fault. Landslides were widespread and blocked roads, severely hampering rescue efforts in the mountainous terrain.

Response

The disaster prompted a large international relief effort. Because the quake struck shortly before winter in a high-altitude region, millions of survivors urgently needed shelter, food, and medical care. Reconstruction was coordinated by Pakistan's Earthquake Reconstruction and Rehabilitation Authority (ERRA), with extensive foreign assistance. The anniversary is observed in Pakistan as a day of remembrance.
