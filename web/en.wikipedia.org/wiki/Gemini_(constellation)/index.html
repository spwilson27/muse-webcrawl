Gemini is one of the constellations of the zodiac, lying between Taurus to the west and Cancer to the east in the northern celestial hemisphere. Its name is Latin for "twins", and it is associated in Greek mythology with the Dioscuri, Castor and Pollux, whose names mark its two brightest stars. It was one of the 48 constellations described by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Stars and objects

Pollux (Beta Geminorum) is the brighter of the pair, an orange giant about 34 light-years away with a confirmed exoplanet. Castor (Alpha Geminorum) appears as a bright white star but is a sextuple system of six stars about 52 light-years away. Other notable stars include Alhena, Wasat, Mebsuta and the red dwarf–hosting Tejat. The constellation contains the open cluster Messier 35, the planetary Eskimo Nebula (NGC 2392), the Medusa Nebula, and the supernova remnant IC 443.

Meteor shower and mythology

Gemini is the radiant of the Geminids, one of the richest annual meteor showers, peaking around 13–14 December and originating from the asteroid 3200 Phaethon. In Greek myth, Castor was mortal and Pollux divine; when Castor died, Pollux begged Zeus to share his immortality, and the two were placed together in the sky, inseparable. The Sun passes through Gemini in June and July; in astrology the sign of Gemini covers approximately 21 May to 21 June owing to the precession of the equinoxes.
