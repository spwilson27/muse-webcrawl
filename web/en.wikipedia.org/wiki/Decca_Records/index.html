Decca Records is a British record label established in 1929 by Edward Lewis. Its US label was established in late 1934 by Lewis, Jack Kapp and Milton Rackmil; in 1937, following Nazi propaganda broadcasts to the United States, Lewis sold American Decca. In the late 1990s, American Decca and British Decca were both owned by Universal Music Group, and their catalogues and artist rosters were merged. Around 1970, the label was revitalised with the introduction of a series of new initiatives under Tony Hall.

History

The name dates back to a portable gramophone called the "Decca Dulcephone" patented in 1914 by musical instrument makers Barnett Samuel and Sons. That company was eventually renamed the Decca Gramophone Company and then sold to former stockbroker Edward Lewis in 1929. Within years, Decca Records Ltd was the second largest record label in the world, calling itself "The Supreme Record Company".

Decca became a major player in the American market in 1934, emphasising lower prices, a roster of popular artists including Bing Crosby, and a distribution network that made it a serious competitor to RCA Victor and Columbia Records. In 1939, British Decca sold its interest in the American company, which continued independently for decades.

The label was known for technical innovation, including the development of full frequency range recording (ffrr) during the Second World War, originally devised for detecting enemy submarines. After the war Decca became one of the pioneering labels in long-playing records and stereo recording, and its classical catalogue, produced by figures such as John Culshaw, included landmark recordings of Wagner's Ring cycle conducted by Georg Solti.

Classical music

Decca's classical division built a reputation for exceptionally high engineering standards, with producers and engineers developing the famous "Decca tree" microphone arrangement used in orchestral recording. Artists associated with the label include Ernest Ansermet, Claudio Abbado, Vladimir Ashkenazy, Joan Sutherland, Luciano Pavarotti and Renata Tebaldi.

Popular music

In popular music Decca released records by artists as diverse as Louis Armstrong, Count Basie, Judy Garland, Bill Haley, Buddy Holly (on the Coral subsidiary in the United States) and the Rolling Stones. The label famously declined to sign the Beatles after their 1962 audition, a decision often cited as one of the greatest mistakes in music business history, though it did sign Brian Poole and the Tremeloes from the same round of auditions.

Legacy

Today the Decca name continues within Universal Music Group, used principally for classical and crossover releases, while its historic catalogue remains one of the most important in recorded music.
