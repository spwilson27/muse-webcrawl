1969 Santa Barbara oil spill

LocationSanta Barbara Channel, California
Date28 January 1969
CauseBlowout at Union Oil Platform A
Volume~80,000–100,000 barrels

The 1969 Santa Barbara oil spill occurred in January and February 1969 in the Santa Barbara Channel, near the city of Santa Barbara in Southern California. It was the largest oil spill in United States waters at the time and remains the third-largest after the 2010 Deepwater Horizon and 1989 Exxon Valdez spills.

Blowout

On 28 January 1969, a blowout occurred during drilling operations at Union Oil's Platform A, about six miles offshore. Workers had been pulling a drill pipe out of a well when a surge of pressure released natural gas, oil, and mud. The blowout cracked the seafloor, and crude oil escaped through fissures for days.

Impact

An oil slick spread over hundreds of square miles, coating some 35 miles of coastline with thick crude. Thousands of seabirds and marine animals, including dolphins and elephant seals, died. Beaches in Santa Barbara, Ventura, and Carpinteria were closed and coated with tar-like oil.

Aftermath

The spill galvanized the modern environmental movement in the United States. It helped inspire the first Earth Day in 1970, the National Environmental Policy Act, and the creation of the Environmental Protection Agency. California halted new offshore drilling leases, and federal offshore leasing was sharply restricted for years afterward.
