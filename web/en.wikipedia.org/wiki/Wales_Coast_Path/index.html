Wales Coast Path

The Wales Coast Path (Welsh: Llwybr Arfordir Cymru) is a long-distance footpath following the entire coastline of Wales. Opened in May 2012, it runs for about 870 miles (1,400 km) from Chepstow in the south-east to Queensferry in the north, making Wales the first country in the world to provide a dedicated footpath along its whole coast.

Route

The path links existing routes, including the Pembrokeshire Coast Path, the Ceredigion Coast Path and the Llŷn Coastal Path, together with new sections created for the project. It passes through the Gower Peninsula — Britain's first Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty — the cities of Swansea, Cardiff and Newport, the cliffs of Pembrokeshire Coast National Park, and the estuaries and mountains of North Wales, where it meets Offa's Dyke Path at each end to form a circuit of Wales. The route is managed by Natural Resources Wales together with local authorities and is waymarked with a distinctive dragon-and-shell logo.

Use and impact

The path attracts walkers from around the world and is estimated to bring significant tourism revenue to coastal communities. Sections vary from paved urban promenades to rugged, remote cliff paths, and large parts are accessible to walkers of differing abilities.
