Lothlórien, often simply Lórien, is a fictional Elven realm in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth, appearing in The Lord of the Rings. A forest of golden mallorn trees lying between the river Anduin and the Misty Mountains, east of Moria, it is ruled in the Third Age by Galadriel and Celeborn and is regarded as the fairest remaining dwelling of the Elves in Middle-earth.

Description

Lothlórien — Sindarin for "Dream-flower" — was also called Laurelindórenan, "Valley of Singing Gold", and by the Rohirrim Dwimordene, "phantom wood". Its giant mallorn trees, with silver bark and gold leaves, were said to have been grown from nuts brought from the West. The Elves, the Galadhrim, lived on platforms (flets or telain) in the trees; the chief city, Caras Galadhon, stood near the confluence of the Celebrant and Anduin. Through the power of Galadriel's ring, Nenya, the realm was preserved from decay and hidden from the Enemy, and time seemed to pass differently within its borders.

History in the legendarium

Elves settled the forest in the Second Age, and it became a refuge after the fall of Eregion. In the War of the Ring, Lothlórien sheltered the Fellowship after their escape from Moria in January 3019; there Frodo offered Galadriel the One Ring, which she refused, and she gave the company gifts, boats, and the Phial of Galadriel before they departed down the Anduin. Lothlórien repelled three assaults from Dol Guldur during the war; after Sauron's fall its Elves destroyed Dol Guldur. In the Fourth Age Galadriel departed over the Sea, and the realm gradually faded and was abandoned.

Themes and reception

Scholars read Lothlórien as a place of enchantment and arrested time, a "Faërie" enclave where the quest is suspended and tested; its mirror of Galadriel and its blend of beauty and peril embody Tolkien's idea of the Elvish imagination. The Lothlórien chapters are often considered among the finest in the book, and the realm has been widely depicted in illustration and film adaptation.
