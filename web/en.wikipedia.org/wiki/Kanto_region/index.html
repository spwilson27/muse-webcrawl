Kantō region

The Kantō region (関東地方, Kantō-chihō) is a geographical region of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. In a common definition, the region includes the Greater Tokyo Area and encompasses seven prefectures: Chiba, Gunma, Ibaraki, Kanagawa, Saitama, Tochigi and Tokyo. More than half of the region is the Kantō Plain, the largest lowland in Japan.

History

During the feudal era Kantō was the centre of power of the Tokugawa shogunate, whose seat was Edo (modern Tokyo), while the imperial court remained in Kyoto in Kansai. After the Meiji Restoration, Tokyo became the capital and the region grew into Japan's political, economic and cultural centre.

Demographics and economy

Kantō is the most populous region of Japan, with over 43 million inhabitants — roughly a third of the national population — concentrated in the Tokyo–Yokohama metropolitan corridor. It hosts the headquarters of most major Japanese corporations, the Keihin and Keiyō industrial zones, and the ports of Tokyo, Yokohama and Chiba.

Geography

The region is bounded by the Pacific Ocean and Tokyo Bay to the south and east, and by mountain ranges to the north and west. The Tone River, Japan's second-longest river, drains the plain. On 1 September 1923 the region was devastated by the Great Kantō earthquake, which destroyed much of Tokyo and Yokohama.
