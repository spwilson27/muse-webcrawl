Tolkien's legendarium
Creator: J. R. R. Tolkien
Setting: Arda / Middle-earth
Core works: The Silmarillion; The Hobbit; The Lord of the Rings

Tolkien's legendarium is the body of mythopoeic work developed by J. R. R. Tolkien over more than fifty years, concerning the fictional world of Arda and its best-known continent, Middle-earth. Tolkien considered much of it a single connected mythology — a "legendarium" — written as if it were the recorded history of an ancient past of the real Earth.

Development

Tolkien began the mythology during the First World War with tales later published as The Book of Lost Tales. Driven first by his invented Elvish languages, the legendarium grew to encompass the creation myth of the Ainulindalë, the Quenta Silmarillion, and the histories of the Elves, Men, Dwarves and Hobbits across three Ages, culminating in the events of The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings.

Publication

After Tolkien's death in 1973, his son Christopher Tolkien edited and published The Silmarillion (1977), Unfinished Tales (1980) and the twelve volumes of The History of Middle-earth, documenting the legendarium's evolution in extraordinary detail.
