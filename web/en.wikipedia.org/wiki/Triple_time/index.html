Triple time

Triple time (also called triple metre) is a musical metre characterised by a primary division of the bar into three beats, most commonly indicated by time signatures such as 3/4, 3/2, or 3/8, and in compound form 9/8. The first beat of each bar is accented relative to the following two.

Characteristics

In 3/4, the characteristic metre of the waltz and the minuet, a bar contains three quarter-note beats. Triple metres contrast with duple metres (2/4, 4/4) and quadruple metres. In medieval and Renaissance mensural notation, triple division (tempus perfectum) was symbolised by a complete circle and carried theological associations with the Trinity.

Examples

Dances in triple time include the waltz, minuet, mazurka, polonaise, and sarabande. Compound triple time, 9/8, subdivides each of the three beats into three eighth notes and appears in pieces such as the first movement of Beethoven's "Moonlight" Sonata (12/8-adjacent forms aside) and in many Baroque gigues.
