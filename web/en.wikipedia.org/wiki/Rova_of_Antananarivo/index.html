Overview

The Rova of Antananarivo is a royal palace complex that served as the home of sovereigns of the Kingdom of Imerina in the 17th and 18th centuries and of the Kingdom of Madagascar in the 19th. It occupies the highest point of Analamanga hill in the capital. Its most prominent building was Manjakamiadana, the Queen's Palace, built of stone for Queen Ranavalona I (1839–41) by the missionary-craftsman Jean Laborde.

Fire and rebirth

On the night of 6 November 1995, fire — widely suspected to be arson amid political tensions — destroyed the Rova's buildings and nearly all its contents, including the royal tombs' remains were rescued only in part (the silver coffins of queens were saved). Reconstruction of Manjakamiadana's stone shell proceeded slowly with state and diaspora support; the palace reopened as a museum in 2023, displaying returned and surviving artefacts. The Rova remains the sacred heart (with the royal tombs and the sacred houses) of Merina identity.
