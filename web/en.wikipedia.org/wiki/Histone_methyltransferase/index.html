Histone methyltransferase

Histone methyltransferases (HMTs) are enzymes that catalyse the transfer of one, two, or three methyl groups to lysine and arginine residues of histone proteins. They use S-adenosyl methionine as the methyl donor. Histone methylation is a major epigenetic modification affecting chromatin structure and gene expression.

Types

Lysine methyltransferases mostly contain a SET domain (named after Su(var)3-9, Enhancer of zeste, and Trithorax), while the protein DOT1 is a notable non-SET lysine methyltransferase. Arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs) produce monomethylarginine and symmetric or asymmetric dimethylarginine.

Function

Depending on the residue modified and the degree of methylation, histone methylation can activate or repress transcription: for example, H3K4 trimethylation is associated with active promoters, whereas H3K9 and H3K27 methylation are associated with repression. Misregulation of histone methyltransferases such as EZH2 is implicated in cancer, and inhibitors of these enzymes have entered clinical use.
