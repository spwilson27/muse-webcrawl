British American Tobacco p.l.c. (BAT) is a British multinational tobacco company headquartered in London, England. It is one of the world's largest tobacco manufacturers, selling cigarettes and newer nicotine products in more than 180 markets. Its best-known cigarette brands include Dunhill, Kent, Lucky Strike, Pall Mall and Rothmans, and it also markets the Vuse vapour brand, the glo heated-tobacco system and Velo oral nicotine pouches.

History

BAT was founded in 1902 as a joint venture between the United Kingdom's Imperial Tobacco Company and the American Tobacco Company of the United States, created to trade outside the partners' home markets. The arrangement dissolved in 1911, and BAT subsequently expanded worldwide, particularly across the British Empire and later in Asia, Africa, Latin America and continental Europe.

During the twentieth century the company diversified into retailing, paper, cosmetics and financial services, but in the 1990s it refocused on tobacco, demerging its financial-services businesses (which became part of Zurich Financial Services) in 1998. Major acquisitions included Rothmans International (1999) and, in 2017, the remaining stake in the American company Reynolds American for about US$49 billion, making BAT the largest listed tobacco company in the world by revenue at the time.

Operations and products

BAT reports its business across regions including the United States, the Americas and Europe, and Asia-Pacific and the Middle East. Alongside combustible cigarettes, the company has invested heavily in what it calls "new categories": vapour products under the Vuse brand, heated products under glo, and modern oral nicotine pouches under Velo and Lyft. The company has stated an ambition to build a majority of its revenue from non-combustible products in the long term.

Criticism and litigation

Like other major tobacco companies, BAT has faced extensive litigation and criticism over the health effects of smoking, marketing practices, smuggling allegations and operations in high-risk markets. It has been investigated by the UK Serious Fraud Office and has paid penalties in the United States relating to sanctions violations connected with North Korea. The company maintains that its newer products are substantially less risky than cigarettes, a claim that remains debated among public-health researchers.
