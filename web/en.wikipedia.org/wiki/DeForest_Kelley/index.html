Jackson DeForest Kelley (January 20, 1920 – June 11, 1999) was an American actor, screenwriter, poet and singer known for his roles in Westerns and as Dr Leonard "Bones" McCoy of the USS Enterprise in the television and film series Star Trek (1966–1991).

Early life

Kelley was born in Toccoa, Georgia, the son of a Baptist minister, and grew up in Conyers and Decatur, singing as a boy soprano in his father's church and on Atlanta radio. He moved to California intending on a singing or stage career, served in the US Army Air Forces during the Second World War — appearing in training films — and was spotted by a Paramount talent scout while acting with the Long Beach Theater Group. He married Carolyn Dowling in 1945, a marriage that lasted until his death.

Westerns

After an early lead in the film noir Fear in the Night (1947), Kelley built a steady career playing — usually — villains in Westerns, including Gunfight at the O.K. Corral (1957), The Law and Jake Wade (1958), Warlock (1959) and Apache Uprising (1966), and in dozens of television series such as Bonanza, Gunsmoke and The Lone Ranger. His brooding heavies made him an unlikely candidate for a kindly country doctor — the role creator Gene Roddenberry had tried to cast him in, in earlier projects and pilots, before Star Trek.

Star Trek

Cast in 1966 as the irascible, humane chief medical officer Leonard McCoy, Kelley formed with William Shatner's Kirk and Leonard Nimoy's Spock the emotional triad at the centre of Star Trek. McCoy's catchphrases — "I'm a doctor, not a…" and "He's dead, Jim" — entered popular culture. Kelley reprised the role in Star Trek: The Animated Series and the first six Star Trek feature films (1979–1991), with a cameo as a 137-year-old Admiral McCoy in the 1987 premiere of Star Trek: The Next Generation. Many doctors and medical students have cited McCoy as an inspiration, and Kelley, who had briefly considered medicine, took pride in fan letters from healthcare workers.

Later life and death

Semi-retired after the films, Kelley wrote poetry, appeared at conventions, and voiced McCoy in video games. He died of stomach cancer on 11 June 1999 at the Motion Picture & Television Country House and Hospital in Woodland Hills — the first of the original series' principal cast to die. He has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, awarded in 1991.
