Tarquinia

Tarquinia is a town and comune in the province of Viterbo, Lazio, central Italy, known chiefly for the Etruscan necropolis of Monterozzi, whose painted tombs are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It stands on the site of, or close to, the ancient Etruscan city of Tarquinii, one of the twelve cities of the Etruscan League and legendary home of the Tarquin kings of Rome.

History

Ancient Tarquinii flourished from the early Iron Age and traded widely across the Mediterranean. Roman tradition made it the home of Lucius Tarquinius Priscus and Tarquinius Superbus, the last king of Rome. After long wars the city came under Roman control in the 4th–3rd centuries BC. The medieval town, long known as Corneto, revived on a neighbouring hill and resumed the ancient name Tarquinia in the 20th century.

Necropolis

The Monterozzi necropolis contains some 6,000 graves cut into the rock, about 200 of them with wall paintings — including the famous Tombs of the Leopards, the Augurs, the Lionesses and the Bacchus — which form the largest surviving picture gallery of the ancient world from before the Roman Empire and reveal much about Etruscan daily life, religion and myth. Finds from the site, including the terracotta Winged Horses, are displayed in the town's National Museum in Palazzo Vitelleschi.
