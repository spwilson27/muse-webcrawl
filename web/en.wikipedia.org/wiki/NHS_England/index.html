NHS England is an executive non-departmental public body of the Department of Health and Social Care that oversees the National Health Service in England. Established under the Health and Social Care Act 2012 — first as the NHS Commissioning Board in October 2012, and taking full statutory powers on 1 April 2013 under the name NHS England — it commissions health services, allocates the NHS budget in England, and sets strategy and standards for the service.

Functions

NHS England directly commissions primary care (GPs, dentists, optometrists, pharmacists) and specialised services, while since the Health and Care Act 2022, 42 integrated care boards (ICBs) commission most hospital and community care for their populations. NHS England publishes the NHS Long Term Plan (2019) and operating frameworks, negotiates the GP contract and the pharmaceutical price schemes, and leads national programmes on digital transformation, cancer, mental health and elective recovery.

History and reform

The body was created in Andrew Lansley's 2012 reforms to put commissioning at arm's length from ministers. In 2019 it was effectively merged in management with NHS Improvement. In March 2025 the government announced that NHS England would be abolished and its functions brought back into the Department of Health and Social Care over about two years, in order to cut duplication.
