The Chongchon River (Korean: 청천강) is a river of North Korea, flowing from the Rangrim Mountains north of the country southwestwards to empty into the Yellow Sea at Sinanju, near Anju. It is about 217 kilometres (135 mi) long and drains a basin of about 9,553 square kilometres.

Korean War

The river is best known internationally for the Battle of the Ch'ongch'on River in November–December 1950, a major engagement of the Korean War. After the United Nations offensive towards the Yalu River, Chinese People's Volunteer Army forces counter-attacked in their Second Phase Offensive, striking UN forces along the Chongchon valley. The battle was a decisive Chinese victory that forced the US Eighth Army into a long retreat — the longest in American military history — and ended expectations of a rapid end to the war.

Geography

The river passes through the city of Anju in South Pyongan Province. Its valley forms an important agricultural and transport corridor, and hydroelectric installations have been built along its course.
