Tully–Fisher relation

Tully–Fisher relation
Proposed: 1977
By: R. Brent Tully and J. Richard Fisher
Field: Extragalactic astronomy

The Tully–Fisher relation is an empirical relationship between the mass or intrinsic luminosity of a spiral galaxy and its asymptotic rotation velocity. It was first published in 1977 by astronomers R. Brent Tully and J. Richard Fisher. The luminosity is roughly proportional to the fourth power of the rotation speed.

Use as a distance indicator

Because rotation velocity can be measured from the width of the galaxy's 21-cm hydrogen line (corrected for inclination) independently of distance, while apparent brightness depends on distance, the relation allows astronomers to estimate distances to spiral galaxies well beyond the reach of Cepheid variable stars. It has been an important rung on the cosmic distance ladder and was used in studies of peculiar velocities and large-scale flows of galaxies.

Variants

The baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, relating total baryonic mass (stars plus gas) to rotation velocity, shows remarkably small scatter and is a key test of galaxy-formation models and modified-gravity theories such as MOND. The analogous relation for elliptical galaxies is the Faber–Jackson relation.
