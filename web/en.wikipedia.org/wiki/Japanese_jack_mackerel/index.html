The Japanese jack mackerel (Trachurus japonicus) is a species of jack mackerel in the family Carangidae. It is known in Japan as ma-aji (真鯵) and is one of the most common and popular food fishes in the country, eaten raw as sashimi, fried, grilled, or dried.

Description

The fish has a streamlined, silvery body with a row of bony scutes along the lateral line, a characteristic of the genus Trachurus. It reaches a maximum length of about 50 cm, though most fish caught are considerably smaller. It inhabits coastal waters of the north-west Pacific, ranging from Japan and the Korean Peninsula to the East China Sea and Taiwan.

Fishery

Japanese jack mackerel are caught throughout the year, with the greatest landings in the warmer months. They are taken by purse seine, trawl, and set nets. In Japan the species supports a large domestic fishery, and fish caught around the country are marketed under regional brands, the best known being the "Seki aji" of Ōita Prefecture.

Culinary use

Ma-aji is prized for its delicate flavour and is prepared in many ways: as aji no sashimi, as aji fry (breaded and deep-fried), as tataki (seared and chopped with condiments), and as himono (dried fish). It is also a traditional fish for kan-aji, the prized winter catch.
