Paracompact space

In mathematics, a paracompact space is a topological space in which every open cover admits an open refinement that is locally finite. The notion was introduced by Jean Dieudonné in 1944. Paracompactness is a generalization of compactness that retains many of its useful consequences, most notably the existence of partitions of unity.

Definition

A collection of subsets of a space is called locally finite if every point has a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many members of the collection. A space is paracompact if every open cover has a locally finite open refinement.

Examples and properties

Every compact space is paracompact, and every metric space is paracompact (a theorem of A. H. Stone). Every paracompact Hausdorff space is normal. Paracompactness is the key hypothesis guaranteeing that a smooth manifold admits Riemannian metrics and partitions of unity.
