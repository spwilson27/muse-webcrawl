Caliper

A caliper (also spelled calliper) is a precision measuring instrument used to measure distances between two opposite sides of an object, such as internal and external diameters and depths. Calipers range from simple compass-like tools to digital instruments accurate to hundredths of a millimetre.

Types

Common types include the Vernier caliper, which reads measurements from a sliding scale invented by Pierre Vernier; the dial caliper, which uses a rack-and-pinion dial; and the digital caliper, which displays readings on an electronic screen. Spring and divider calipers are used with a separate ruler.

Use

Calipers are used in metalworking, woodworking, engineering, and science. Measurements can be read in metric or imperial units, and proper technique — avoiding excessive force and keeping jaws perpendicular — is essential for accuracy.
