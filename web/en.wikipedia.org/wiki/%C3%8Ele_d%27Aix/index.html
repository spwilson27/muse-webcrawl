Île d'Aix

Île d'Aix is a small island and commune in the Charente-Maritime department of western France, in the Atlantic Ocean off the coast between the Île d'Oléron and the mainland. Only about 3 kilometres long and 600 metres wide, it is reached by ferry from Fouras and is largely car-free. The island is historically notable as the last French soil Napoleon Bonaparte stood on before his exile to Saint Helena in 1815.

Geography

The crescent-shaped island lies in the Pertuis d'Antioche strait, protected offshore by Fort Enet and, to the south, the famous Fort Boyard. Its village clusters around a small port; the rest of the island is fields, hollyhocks, and pine woods.

History

The island was fortified under Louis XIV and Vauban's successors as part of the defences of the Rochefort naval arsenal. In 1809 British forces attacked the French fleet in the nearby Basque Roads. Napoleon, after his defeat at Waterloo, spent his last days in France on Île d'Aix in July 1815 before surrendering to HMS Bellerophon. The house where he stayed is now the Musée Napoléonien.

Tourism

Visitors tour the island on foot or by bicycle. Attractions include the Napoleon museum, the African Museum, oyster farming, and views of Fort Boyard. Cars are essentially absent, giving the island a reputation for tranquillity.
