Buddhism in Mongolia

Buddhism in Mongolia derives largely from the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism. It first spread under the Mongol Empire, was revived in the 16th century after Altan Khan met Sonam Gyatso (granting the title Dalai Lama), and became Mongolia's dominant religion.

History

Figures such as Zanabazar shaped a distinct Mongolian Buddhism blending shamanic elements. Under Qing rule hundreds of monasteries and a large monk population existed. Communist purges in the 1930s destroyed most monasteries and killed many lamas; after the 1921 revolution's successor regime fell in 1990, Buddhism revived, with Gandantegchinlen Monastery in Ulaanbaatar a centre. Today a majority of Mongolians identify as Buddhist.
