Blues rock
Stylistic originsElectric blues, rock and roll, British blues
Cultural originsEarly to mid-1960s, United Kingdom and United States
Derivative formsHard rock, heavy metal, Southern rock, boogie rock
Key artistsCream, Led Zeppelin, Jimi Hendrix, Johnny Winter, Stevie Ray Vaughan

Blues rock is a fusion music genre that combines elements of blues and rock music. It is characterized by bluesy improvisation, extended electric guitar solos, twelve-bar blues structures, and the heavy amplification and driving rhythms of rock. The genre emerged in the mid-1960s in England and the United States as blues musicians adopted rock instrumentation and rock musicians embraced the blues repertoire.

History

British groups such as the Yardbirds, the Rolling Stones, and John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers popularized electric blues covers, while in America the Paul Butterfield Blues Band and Canned Heat did the same. Cream, Led Zeppelin, and the Jimi Hendrix Experience pushed the style toward hard rock by the end of the decade. In the 1970s, ZZ Top, Johnny Winter, and George Thorogood sustained it; Stevie Ray Vaughan led a blues-rock revival in the 1980s. Later practitioners include the Black Keys, Gary Clark Jr., and Joe Bonamassa.

Characteristics

Blues rock centers on the electric guitar, typically played with distortion through valve amplifiers, supported by electric bass and drums, and often a Hammond organ or harmonica. Songs commonly use the twelve-bar blues progression and call-and-response vocals, but with the louder, faster attack of rock.
