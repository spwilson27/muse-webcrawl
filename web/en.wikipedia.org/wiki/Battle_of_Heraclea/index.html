Battle of Heraclea

The Battle of Heraclea was fought in 280 BC between the Roman Republic, led by consul Publius Valerius Laevinus, and the combined forces of the Greek city of Tarentum and King Pyrrhus of Epirus. It was the first major battle of the Pyrrhic War and the first time Roman legions faced the Macedonian-style phalanx and war elephants.

Background

Tarentum, fearing Roman expansion after incidents including attacks on Roman ships, invited Pyrrhus to defend the Greek cities of Magna Graecia. Pyrrhus landed in Italy in 280 BC with some 25,000 men and 20 elephants. Laevinus marched into Lucania to meet him near the Greek colony of Heraclea, on the river Siris.

The battle

The Romans forded the Siris and engaged Pyrrhus's phalanx. The legions repeatedly threw back the phalanx in close fighting, and Pyrrhus, who exchanged armour with a companion to avoid assassination and was nearly killed, committed his elephants against the Roman cavalry. The Roman horses panicked at the unfamiliar sight and smell of the elephants; the Roman army broke and fled across the river. Roman losses are given at around 7,000 (some sources say far more), while Pyrrhus lost about 4,000 — losses among his best troops that he could ill afford.

Aftermath

Heraclea encouraged Rome's Italian allies to waver and brought Pyrrhus reinforcements, but Rome refused to negotiate while he remained in Italy. The pattern of the war was set: tactical victories that cost Pyrrhus more than Rome.
