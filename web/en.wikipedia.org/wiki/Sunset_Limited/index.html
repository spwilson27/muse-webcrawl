Sunset Limited

The Sunset Limited is a long-distance passenger train operated by Amtrak on a 1,995-mile (3,211 km) route between New Orleans, Louisiana, and Los Angeles, California, with major stops in Houston, San Antonio, El Paso, Phoenix (via Maricopa), and Tucson. It is the oldest continuously operating named train in the United States, tracing its origins to the Southern Pacific Railroad, which introduced the service in 1894.

History

The Southern Pacific inaugurated the Sunset Limited in November 1894 as its premier train on the "Sunset Route" between New Orleans and San Francisco; the service was later cut back to Los Angeles. Amtrak took over operation on May 1, 1971. From 1993 to 2005 the train continued east of New Orleans to Florida, at its peak running coast-to-coast between Los Angeles and Miami — the first transcontinental passenger train service in American history. Hurricane Katrina in 2005 severed the Gulf Coast route, and service east of New Orleans was suspended indefinitely.

Operations

The train runs three times weekly in each direction, using Superliner equipment with sleeping cars, a dining car, and a Sightseer Lounge. Between Los Angeles and San Antonio it operates in conjunction with the Texas Eagle, with through cars exchanged at San Antonio. The 1993 derailment of the train near Mobile, Alabama, caused by sabotage of a bridge, remains the deadliest accident in Amtrak history, killing 47 people.
