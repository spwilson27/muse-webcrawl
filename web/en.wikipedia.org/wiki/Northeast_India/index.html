Northeast India (officially the North Eastern Region, NER) is the easternmost region of India, comprising eight states — Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura (the "Seven Sisters") and the Himalayan state of Sikkim. It is connected to the rest of India only by the narrow Siliguri Corridor (the "Chicken's Neck"), some 22 km wide, and shares over 5,000 km of international border with China, Myanmar, Bangladesh, Bhutan and Nepal — far more than its connection to mainland India.

Geography

The region spans the Brahmaputra and Barak river valleys, the Patkai–Naga and Mizo hills, the Meghalaya plateau — one of the wettest places on Earth, home to Mawsynram and Cherrapunji — and the eastern Himalaya in Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim (Kangchenjunga, the world's third-highest mountain, stands on Sikkim's border). It is a global biodiversity hotspot, with Kaziranga National Park's one-horned rhinoceroses among its best-known wildlife.

History

The Ahom kingdom ruled Assam for nearly 600 years from 1228. Much of the region was annexed by the British after the First Anglo-Burmese War (Treaty of Yandabo, 1826) and administered as part of Bengal and then Assam Province. At independence in 1947 the predominantly Muslim district of Sylhet joined East Pakistan, severing the region's sea access. Border disputes persist: China claims most of Arunachal Pradesh (as "South Tibet"), fought over in the Sino-Indian War of 1962, and several states have long-running boundary disputes among themselves.

Politics and society

The region is ethnically and linguistically one of the most diverse in Asia, with hundreds of tribal groups speaking Tibeto-Burman and Austroasiatic languages alongside Indo-Aryan Assamese and Bengali. Since the 1950s several states have experienced separatist insurgencies (Naga, Mizo, Assamese/ULFA, Manipuri and Tripuri movements); the Mizo accord of 1986 is often cited as India's most successful peace settlement, while negotiations with Naga groups continue. The Sixth Schedule of the Indian Constitution grants autonomous district councils to tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. The North Eastern Council (est. 1972) coordinates development, and the region is central to India's "Act East" policy linking it to Southeast Asia.

Economy

Assam produces over half of India's tea and much of its crude oil and natural gas. Other important products include bamboo, rubber, handloom textiles and tourism. Poor connectivity has historically constrained development, prompting major bridge, rail and highway programmes across the Brahmaputra in recent decades.
