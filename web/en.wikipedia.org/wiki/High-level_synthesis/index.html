High-level synthesis

High-level synthesis (HLS), also called algorithmic or behavioural synthesis, is an automated design process that converts an algorithmic description in a language such as C, C++ or SystemC into a register-transfer level (RTL) hardware design for an FPGA or ASIC.

Process

The tool performs allocation, scheduling and binding: operations are assigned to clock cycles and to functional units, registers and interconnect are generated, and interface protocols are synthesised. Designer pragmas control loop unrolling, pipelining and memory partitioning, trading area against latency and throughput.

Tools

Commercial examples include AMD Vitis HLS and Siemens Catapult; HLS raises productivity for accelerators in signal processing, networking and machine learning, though hand-written RTL can still beat it on efficiency.

References: Coussy et al., High-Level Synthesis: From Algorithm to Digital Circuit.
