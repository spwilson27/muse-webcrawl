The Turtle Mountain Indian Reservation is a Native American reservation in northern North Dakota, United States, belonging to the Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa Indians. The main part of the reservation lies in Rolette County, in the Turtle Mountains along the Canadian border, with headquarters at Belcourt. The tribe's members are primarily of Ojibwe (Chippewa) and Métis heritage.

History

The reservation was established by executive order in December 1882, initially covering a much larger area that was greatly reduced in 1884. The 1892 McCumber Agreement — remembered by the tribe as the "Ten Cent Treaty" because it paid roughly ten cents per acre for some ten million acres of ceded land — was ratified only in 1904 after years of dispute. Many tribal members who could not obtain allotments on the reduced reservation took allotments elsewhere, and the tribe today holds trust lands in Montana and South Dakota as well.

Government and community

The Turtle Mountain Band is governed by an elected tribal council under a constitution adopted in the 20th century. Enrolled membership numbers over 30,000, with roughly 13,000–17,000 residents living on or near the reservation. Belcourt hosts Turtle Mountain Community College, founded in 1972, and the Sky Dancer Casino. The community is known for its Métis fiddle music and a distinctive Michif-speaking heritage.
