Case study

A case study is an in-depth, detailed examination of a particular case — a person, group, organisation, event, or phenomenon — within its real-world context. Case studies are a common research design in social science, business, law, medicine, and education, and are used to explore, describe, or explain complex issues where the boundaries between the phenomenon and its context are not clear.

Methodology

Case study research typically draws on multiple sources of evidence: interviews, documents, archival records, direct observation, and artefacts. Methodologists such as Robert K. Yin and Robert Stake distinguish explanatory, exploratory, and descriptive case studies, and emphasise the importance of a research protocol, triangulation of evidence, and an explicit chain of evidence linking conclusions to data.

Uses

In business education, case studies of companies form the core of the case method pioneered by Harvard Business School. In medicine, a case report describes the symptoms and treatment of an individual patient. Critics note the difficulty of generalising from a single case, while defenders argue that a well-chosen case can falsify a theory or reveal mechanisms invisible to large-sample studies.
