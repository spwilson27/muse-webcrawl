Bahia is one of the 26 states of Brazil, located in the Northeast Region. Its capital, Salvador, was Brazil's first capital (1549–1763) and remains one of the country's great cultural centers, renowned for Afro-Brazilian heritage, music, and cuisine. Bahia is Brazil's fourth-most populous and fourth-largest state by area.

History

Portuguese explorer Pedro Álvares Cabral landed in 1500 near Porto Seguro. Salvador was founded in 1549 as the seat of colonial government and became the principal port of entry for enslaved Africans, whose descendants shaped Bahian culture profoundly—Candomblé religion, capoeira, samba de roda, and dishes like acarajé and moqueca. Independence fighting continued in Bahia until 2 July 1823, still celebrated as Bahian Independence Day.

Geography

Bahia spans Atlantic beaches, the fertile Recôncavo around the Bay of All Saints, and the semi-arid sertão interior. Chapada Diamantina National Park protects dramatic tablelands and waterfalls. The São Francisco River forms much of its northern border. The state's wildlife includes Atlantic Forest reserves where species such as the jaguar persist.

Culture

Salvador's carnival, powered by trio elétrico trucks and axé music, is among the world's largest street parties. Writers Jorge Amado and musicians Gilberto Gil, Caetano Veloso, and Dorival Caymmi all hail from Bahia. Like Brazil's other great state capitals—compare Rio de Janeiro—Salvador anchors a distinctive regional identity within the federation.
