Alan Moore (born 18 November 1953) is an English author known primarily for his work in comic books, including the acclaimed series Watchmen, V for Vendetta, The Ballad of Halo Jones, and From Hell. Frequently described as the best graphic-novel writer in history, Moore is widely credited with transforming the perception of comics as a serious literary medium.

Early life

Moore was born in Northampton into a working-class family and has lived there his entire life. Expelled from school for dealing LSD, he spent the 1970s in various jobs while contributing cartoons and strips to music magazines and underground publications.

Career

Moore first gained attention in British comics such as 2000 AD and Warrior, where he created V for Vendetta and Marvelman (later Miracleman). Recruited by DC Comics in 1983, he revitalised the horror title Swamp Thing, then wrote Watchmen (1986–87) with artist Dave Gibbons — a deconstruction of the superhero genre that became the only graphic novel on Time magazine's list of the 100 best English-language novels since 1923.

His later work includes From Hell, a sprawling account of the Jack the Ripper murders with artist Eddie Campbell; The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen with Kevin O'Neill; and the prose novels Voice of the Fire (1996) and Jerusalem (2016). Moore has repeatedly fallen out with publishers over ownership and film adaptations, and has asked for his name to be removed from most screen versions of his work, donating his share of fees to collaborators.

Personal life and beliefs

A self-described ceremonial magician since the early 1990s, Moore worships the Roman snake-god Glycon and incorporates occult themes into works such as Promethea. He is a vegetarian, an anarchist, and a prominent supporter of his hometown of Northampton.
