A moment of silence (also referred to as a minute's silence or two minutes' silence) is a period of silent contemplation, prayer, reflection, or meditation. Similar to flying a flag at half-mast, a moment of silence is often a gesture of respect, particularly in mourning for those who have died recently or as part of a tragic historical event, such as Remembrance Day.

History

The practice became widespread after the First World War. The two-minute silence observed at 11 a.m. on 11 November (Armistice Day, now Remembrance Sunday in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth) was instituted in 1919 and is often credited to proposals by the South African statesman Sir Percy FitzPatrick, drawing on a wartime custom in Cape Town.

Moments of silence are now observed worldwide at public ceremonies, sporting events, schools, and legislatures, and after disasters and acts of violence.

Religious and legal aspects

Because it is silent, a moment of silence can be observed by people of any faith or none, which has made it a common alternative to public prayer in secular institutions. In the United States, the Supreme Court struck down a statute requiring a moment of silence 'for meditation or voluntary prayer' in Wallace v. Jaffree (1985), finding its purpose was to promote religion, though many states maintain neutral moment-of-silence laws.
