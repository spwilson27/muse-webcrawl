KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
ClassActinopterygii
OrderAcipenseriformes
FamiliesAcipenseridae (sturgeons); Polyodontidae (paddlefish)

Acipenseriformes is an order of basal ray-finned fishes that includes the sturgeons (family Acipenseridae) and paddlefishes (family Polyodontidae), together with several extinct families. The group dates back to the Early Jurassic, about 200 million years ago, and is often described as a lineage of "living fossils".

Characteristics

Members of the order have largely cartilaginous skeletons, a heterocercal tail fin, a spiral-valve intestine, and a protrusible mouth with barbels in sturgeons. Sturgeons bear rows of bony scutes instead of typical scales. Most species are anadromous, migrating from the sea into rivers to spawn.

Conservation

Sturgeons are among the most threatened groups of animals in the world, heavily overfished for their roe — caviar — and blocked by river dams. The Chinese paddlefish was declared extinct in the early 21st century, leaving the American paddlefish as the sole surviving paddlefish species.
