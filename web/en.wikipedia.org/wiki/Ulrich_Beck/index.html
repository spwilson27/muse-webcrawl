Ulrich Beck

Ulrich Beck (15 May 1944 – 1 January 2015) was a German sociologist, and one of the most cited social scientists in the world during his lifetime. His work focused on questions of modernization, ecological problems, individualization and globalization. He is particularly well known for introducing the concepts of the "risk society" and "second modernity" or "reflexive modernization", and for popularizing the term "cosmopolitanism".

Born: 15 May 1944, Stolp, Germany (now Słupsk, Poland)
Died: 1 January 2015, Munich, Germany
Known for: Risk society, reflexive modernization, individualization
Key work: Risk Society (1986)

Risk society

In Risikogesellschaft (1986), published in the year of the Chernobyl disaster and translated into English as Risk Society: Towards a New Modernity (1992), Beck argued that advanced industrial society increasingly organizes itself around the distribution of "bads" — manufactured risks such as pollution, radiation and financial hazards — rather than the distribution of wealth. Such risks are often invisible, global, knowledge-dependent and capable of a "boomerang effect" that eventually affects those who produced them.

Other concepts

With Elisabeth Beck-Gernsheim, Beck analysed the individualization of life courses: people are compelled to construct their own biographies as traditional institutions loosen. Later works extended risk analysis to World Risk Society (1999), the cosmopolitan vision, and, with Edgar Grande, a European cosmopolitanism. He also debated with Anthony Giddens and Scott Lash in Reflexive Modernization (1994).

Career and legacy

Beck studied in Freiburg and Munich, held posts at the University of Münster and Bamberg, and from 1992 was professor of sociology at Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich, with visiting roles including at the London School of Economics. He died of a heart attack on 1 January 2015. His notion of risk reshaped environmental sociology, political science and public debate about technology and climate change.
