Chamber music

A string quartet in performance

Chamber music is a form of classical music composed for a small group of instruments — traditionally a group that could fit in a palace chamber or a large room. Most broadly, it includes any art music performed by a small number of performers, normally one player to a part, in contrast to orchestral music. Because of its intimate character, chamber music has been described as "the music of friends"; Goethe famously described the string quartet as "four rational people conversing".

Common ensembles

The most important chamber ensemble is the string quartet (two violins, viola, and cello), codified by Joseph Haydn in the 18th century. Other standard groupings include the piano trio (piano and two stringed instruments), piano quintet, string quintet, piano quartet, clarinet quintet, and wind ensembles such as the wind quintet; works are also named by size — duet, trio, quartet, quintet, sextet, septet, octet, nonet.

History

Chamber music grew out of Baroque trio sonatas and court consort music. Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven raised the string quartet to a central genre; the 19th century added masterpieces by Schubert, Mendelssohn, Schumann, Brahms, and Dvořák, often written for domestic amateur performance as pianos entered middle-class homes. In the 20th century, composers such as Debussy, Bartók (whose six quartets are landmarks), Shostakovich, and Elliott Carter extended the medium's expressive range and technical demands, and professional touring quartets became fixtures of concert life.
