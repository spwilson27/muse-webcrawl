Do-it-yourself biology

Do-it-yourself biology
Also known as: DIYbio, biohacking
Origin: 2000s, United States
Related: Citizen science, synthetic biology

Do-it-yourself biology (DIY biology, DIY bio) is a growing biotechnological social movement in which individuals, communities, and small organizations study and experiment with biology and life science using methods similar to those of traditional research institutions, but outside professional laboratories.

History

The term gained currency around 2008 with the founding of the DIYbio.org network. Community labs such as Genspace in New York City (opened 2010) and BioCurious in California provided shared wet-lab space, courses, and equipment to the public.

Activities

Projects include DNA extraction, genetic testing, bacterial culture, CRISPR experiments, open-source laboratory hardware, and environmental monitoring. Many participants identify as biohackers; a smaller subset pursue body modification such as grinding.

Safety and regulation

DIY biology has raised biosafety and biosecurity concerns among regulators including the FBI, which has also engaged in outreach to community labs. Practitioners emphasize codes of ethics, transparency, and avoiding pathogens. See also synthetic biology and citizen science.
