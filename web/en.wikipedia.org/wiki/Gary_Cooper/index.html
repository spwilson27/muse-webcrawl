Gary Cooper

Gary Cooper (born Frank James Cooper; May 7, 1901 – May 13, 1961) was an American actor known for his strong, quiet screen persona and understated acting style. He won the Academy Award for Best Actor twice and had a third nomination, and received an Academy Honorary Award in 1961 for his career achievements. He was one of the top money-making stars of Hollywood for more than three decades, appearing in more than 100 films.

Career

Raised in Montana and educated in England and Montana, Cooper began in Hollywood as a stunt rider and extra in silent Westerns before winning leading roles in the late 1920s. He became a major star with films such as A Farewell to Arms (1932), Mr. Deeds Goes to Town (1936), and Meet John Doe (1941).

He won Best Actor Oscars for portraying First World War hero Alvin York in Sergeant York (1941) and for the Western High Noon (1952), whose Marshal Will Kane became one of the defining roles of his career. He was also nominated for playing Lou Gehrig in The Pride of the Yankees (1942). Other notable films include Beau Geste (1939), For Whom the Bell Tolls (1943), and Friendly Persuasion (1956).

Legacy

The American Film Institute ranked Cooper 11th on its list of the greatest male screen legends of classic American cinema. His laconic, morally steadfast screen image came to personify the archetypal American hero.
