Westminster Hall

Westminster Hall is the oldest surviving part of the Palace of Westminster in London. Built in 1097–1099 under William II (William Rufus), it was for centuries the largest hall in Europe—about 73 m (240 ft) long and 20 m (65 ft) wide—and remains one of the finest examples of medieval secular architecture. Its celebrated hammerbeam roof, commissioned by Richard II in the 1390s, is the largest clear-span medieval roof in England.

History

The hall hosted coronation banquets, royal feasts, and the great state trials of English history, including those of William Wallace, Thomas More, Guy Fawkes, and Charles I—who was condemned to death here in 1649. For centuries the royal law courts (the King's Bench and Chancery) sat in the hall, giving rise to the phrase "the courts at Westminster".

Modern uses

Westminster Hall survived the fire of 1834 that destroyed most of the old palace. Today it hosts lying-in-state for monarchs and eminent figures (most recently Queen Elizabeth II in 2022), addresses by visiting heads of state to both Houses of Parliament, and ceremonial receptions. It is part of the Westminster UNESCO World Heritage Site.
