Foveaux Strait

Foveaux Strait (Māori: Te Ara a Kiwa, "the path of Kiwa") separates Stewart Island from the South Island of New Zealand. The strait runs about 130 kilometres (81 mi) from Ruapuke Island in the northeast to Little Solander Island in the southwest, and narrows from about 46 km wide at its northeastern end to about 14 km at its narrowest. It is known for rough seas and strong tidal currents.

History

The strait is named after Joseph Foveaux, lieutenant-governor of New South Wales, whose name was given to it following the sealing expeditions of the early 19th century. European sealers and whalers worked the strait from the first decade of the 1800s, and the port of Bluff, on the northern shore, became New Zealand's southernmost deep-water port.

Features and use

The strait contains several islands, including Ruapuke Island, the Titi (Muttonbird) Islands, and the Solander Islands. A passenger and vehicle ferry crosses between Bluff and Oban on Stewart Island. The waters are famed for the Bluff oyster fishery, harvested from oyster beds in the strait since the 1860s, and are also used for paua, crayfish, and blue cod fishing. Because of its exposure to Southern Ocean swells, the strait is considered one of New Zealand's most dangerous stretches of water.
