USS Samuel B. Roberts (DE-413) was a John C. Butler-class destroyer escort of the United States Navy, named for Coxswain Samuel Booker Roberts Jr., who was posthumously awarded the Navy Cross for his actions at Guadalcanal. The ship is remembered as "the destroyer escort that fought like a battleship" for her role in the Battle off Samar on 25 October 1944, part of the larger Battle of Leyte Gulf. In June 2022 her wreck was identified at a depth of 22,621 feet (6,895 m), making her the deepest shipwreck ever located.

Battle off Samar

Part of the escort group "Taffy 3", Samuel B. Roberts was confronted at dawn on 25 October 1944 by the Japanese Center Force, including the battleship Yamato and several heavy cruisers. Her commanding officer, Lieutenant Commander Robert W. Copeland, announced to the crew that they were entering "a fight against overwhelming odds from which survival could not be expected". The little ship closed to within 4,000 yards of the heavy cruiser Chōkai, launching three torpedoes that blew off her bow, and then dueled with cruisers for nearly an hour with her two 5-inch guns, setting the cruiser Chikuma ablaze. Struck by three 14-inch shells from the battleship Kongō, she sank with the loss of 90 of her crew of 224.

Legacy

Samuel B. Roberts received one battle star and was part of the Presidential Unit Citation awarded to Taffy 3. Two later US Navy ships — a destroyer and an Oliver Hazard Perry-class frigate famous for surviving a mine strike in 1988 — have carried the name.
