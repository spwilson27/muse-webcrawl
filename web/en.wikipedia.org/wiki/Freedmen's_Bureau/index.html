Freedmen's Bureau

A school established with Freedmen's Bureau support.

The Freedmen's Bureau, formally the Bureau of Refugees, Freedmen, and Abandoned Lands, was a U.S. federal government agency established in 1865 to aid freedmen (newly emancipated enslaved people) and poor whites in the South during the Reconstruction era following the American Civil War. Established by an act of Congress on March 3, 1865, it was headed by General Oliver Otis Howard.

Activities

The Bureau provided food, housing, and medical aid; established schools; helped freedpeople negotiate labour contracts; and offered legal assistance. It operated courts to resolve disputes, supervised abandoned and confiscated lands, and issued millions of rations to destitute people of both races. In education, it helped found thousands of schools and supported institutions such as Howard University and Fisk University, enrolling hundreds of thousands of African American students.

Opposition and dissolution

The Bureau faced violent opposition from white Southerners, chronic underfunding, and resistance from President Andrew Johnson, who vetoed bills to extend its life (Congress overrode one veto in 1866). Its operations were wound down by 1869, and it was formally disbanded in 1872. Despite its limitations and the failure of land redistribution, historians regard its educational work as a lasting achievement of Reconstruction.
