Carlo Lorenzini (24 November 1826 – 26 October 1890), better known by the pen name Carlo Collodi, was an Italian author, journalist, and humble civil servant, best known for his children's novel The Adventures of Pinocchio (1883). His pen name derives from Collodi, the Tuscan village where his mother was born.

Life

Lorenzini was born in Florence to a cook and a peasant woman. Thanks to the patronage of the wealthy Ginori family, he was educated at seminary and trained as a bookseller. During the Italian wars of independence (1848 and 1860) he served as a volunteer. He founded and edited satirical newspapers, including Il Lampione, worked as a censor and civil servant for the Florentine prefecture, and wrote plays, novels, and short stories for adults.

From 1875 he turned to children's literature, translating French fairy tales and writing the school-book series about the boy Giannettino, intended to teach the children of the newly unified Italy.

Pinocchio

In 1881 Collodi began serialising La storia di un burattino ("The Story of a Puppet") in the Giornale per i bambini. The tale of the wooden puppet Pinocchio, carved by Geppetto, whose nose grows when he lies and who longs to become a real boy, was published in book form as Le avventure di Pinocchio in 1883. It became one of the most translated and widely read books in the world, adapted countless times for stage and screen, most famously in Disney's 1940 animated film.

Collodi died suddenly in Florence in 1890 and is buried in the Cimitero delle Porte Sante.
