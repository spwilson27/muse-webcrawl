Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty

The Gurjara-Pratihara dynasty was an imperial dynasty that ruled much of northern India from the mid-8th to the 11th century CE. It ruled first at Ujjain and later at Kannauj (Kanyakubja), and at its height, under Mihira Bhoja (c. 836–885), it controlled a vast empire stretching from Gujarat and Rajasthan in the west to Bihar in the east, and from the Himalayan foothills in the north to the Vindhyas in the south.

History

The dynasty's founder is usually taken to be Nagabhata I (c. 730–760), who is credited with resisting Arab incursions from Sindh into western India. His successors Vatsaraja (c. 780–800) and Nagabhata II (c. 800–833) expanded Pratihara power and captured Kannauj, making it the imperial capital. This expansion drew the Pratiharas into the celebrated tripartite struggle with the Palas of Bengal and the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan for control of Kannauj and the Gangetic plain.

The dynasty reached its zenith under Mihira Bhoja I, a devotee of Vishnu who took the title Adivaraha, and under his son Mahendrapala I (c. 885–910), whose court included the poet Rajashekhara. Arab travellers such as Sulaiman described the Pratihara kingdom as wealthy and well administered, with a powerful cavalry, and hostile to Islam's westernmost expansion.

Decline

From the early 10th century the empire weakened. The Rashtrakuta ruler Indra III sacked Kannauj around 916–918, and feudatories such as the Chandelas of Jejakabhukti, the Paramaras of Malwa, the Chauhans and the Guhilas asserted independence. The last significant ruler, Rajyapala, submitted to Mahmud of Ghazni in 1018; the dynasty finally ended around 1036 when Kannauj passed to the Gahadavalas. Although politically fragmented, Pratihara-era temple architecture — seen at sites such as Osian, Gwalior (Teli ka Mandir) and the early temples of central India — left a lasting legacy on North Indian Nagara architecture.
