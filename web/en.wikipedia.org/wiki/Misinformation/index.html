Misinformation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Misinformation is false or inaccurate information that is spread regardless of the intent of those sharing it. It is distinct from disinformation, which is false information spread deliberately to deceive, and from malinformation, which is genuine information shared to cause harm. People may spread misinformation because they believe it, find it entertaining, or wish to signal group identity.

Causes and spread

Misinformation arises from honest errors, rumours, satire taken at face value, outdated reporting, and the distortions of propaganda. Research shows false news can spread faster and further than true news on social media, partly because novel and emotionally charged content attracts engagement. Cognitive factors such as confirmation bias, illusory truth (repetition making claims feel true), and motivated reasoning make misinformation persistent; corrections often fail to fully undo its influence, a phenomenon known as the continued influence effect.

Effects and responses

Misinformation affects public health (for example, vaccine hesitancy), elections, financial markets and communal tensions. Responses include professional fact-checking, media literacy education, "prebunking" or inoculation messages that warn people about manipulation techniques in advance, platform labelling and moderation, and regulatory measures. Researchers caution that the scale and real-world impact of misinformation remain debated, and that heavy-handed countermeasures can raise free-expression concerns.
