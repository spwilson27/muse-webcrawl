Sandpiper

FamilyScolopacidae
OrderCharadriiformes
HabitatWetlands, coasts, tundra, grasslands worldwide
DietInvertebrates, small crustaceans, worms

Sandpipers are a large family of waders, Scolopacidae, in the order Charadriiformes. The family includes the "true" sandpipers (such as the dunlin, knots, sanderling and ruff), as well as curlews, godwits, snipes, woodcocks, dowitchers, turnstones, phalaropes and shanks. Most species feed on small invertebrates picked or probed from mud, sand or shallow water.

Description

Scolopacids range from the tiny least sandpiper (about 11 cm long) to the Far Eastern curlew, among the largest shorebirds. Their bills vary dramatically with feeding ecology: short bills for surface picking (plover-like species), medium bills for probing mud, very long downcurved bills in curlews for extracting deep prey, and upturned bills in godwits. Many species have cryptic brown, grey or black breeding plumage that becomes plainer in winter, and legs ranging from short to extremely long. Sandpipers have flexible bill tips (rhynchokinesis) allowing them to seize buried prey, and many locate food by touch rather than sight.

Distribution and migration

Sandpipers breed from high Arctic tundra to temperate wetlands and migrate over some of the longest distances of any birds: the bar-tailed godwit makes non-stop trans-Pacific flights exceeding 11,000 km, tracked by satellite. Major staging areas such as the Yellow Sea mudflats and Delaware Bay are critical refuelling sites, and habitat loss at these stopovers is a principal conservation concern for species such as the red knot and spoon-billed sandpiper, the latter critically endangered.

Breeding

Most sandpipers nest on the ground, laying typically four camouflaged eggs in a scrape. Mating systems are unusually varied: monogamy is common, but the ruff has elaborate lek mating with three male morphs, phalaropes are polyandrous with reversed sex roles (bright females, incubating males), and some species practise double-clutching in which each parent raises a separate nest.
