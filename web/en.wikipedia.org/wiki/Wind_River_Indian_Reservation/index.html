Wind River Indian Reservation

The Wind River Indian Reservation is a federally recognized reservation located in the central part of the U.S. state of Wyoming, in Fremont and Hot Springs counties. It is shared by two tribes: the Eastern Shoshone and the Northern Arapaho. At more than 2.2 million acres (about 3,500 square miles), it is one of the largest reservations in the United States and the only reservation in Wyoming.

History

The reservation was established for the Eastern Shoshone by the Treaty of Fort Bridger in 1863, originally encompassing some 44 million acres; an 1868 treaty reduced it to about 3 million acres in the Wind River valley. The Shoshone leader Washakie, an ally of the United States, is commemorated at Fort Washakie, the reservation's agency town.

In 1878 the Northern Arapaho — traditional rivals of the Shoshone — were placed on the reservation "temporarily" pending their own lands; they never left, and in 1937 a federal court recognized their equal interest in the reservation. A large northern portion was ceded to the United States in 1906 (the Riverton reclamation area), though boundary litigation in the 2010s (the Wyoming v. EPA dispute) centred on whether the city of Riverton remains within the reservation under the 1863 treaty.

Geography and communities

The reservation spans the Wind River Basin between the Wind River Range and the Owl Creek Mountains, irrigated by the Wind River and its tributaries. Communities include Fort Washakie, Ethete, Arapahoe, and Crowheart. Sacajawea, the Lemhi Shoshone guide of the Lewis and Clark Expedition, is traditionally said to be buried at Fort Washakie.

Government and economy

The two tribes govern separately through the Eastern Shoshone Tribal Council and the Northern Arapaho Business Council, and jointly through the Wind River Inter-Tribal Council. Oil and gas production, ranching, and the Wind River Casino are significant economic activities.
