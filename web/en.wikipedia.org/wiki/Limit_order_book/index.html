A limit order book is the record, maintained by a securities exchange, of all outstanding limit orders for a traded instrument. A limit order specifies the maximum price a buyer is willing to pay (a bid) or the minimum price a seller is willing to accept (an ask), together with a quantity. Orders that cannot be executed immediately rest in the book.

Structure

The book has two sides: bids, sorted from highest to lowest price, and asks, sorted from lowest to highest. The highest bid and lowest ask define the best bid and offer; the difference between them is the bid–ask spread, and their midpoint is often quoted as the market price. Depth of book data ("Level 2" data) shows quantities available at successive price levels.

Matching

When an incoming order can be filled against resting orders, the exchange's matching engine executes trades following price–time priority: the best-priced orders trade first, and among orders at the same price, the earliest submitted trade first. A market order executes immediately against the best prices in the book, walking through further price levels if its size exceeds the available quantity.

Uses and issues

Order book data underlie market microstructure research and high-frequency trading strategies. Regulators and exchanges monitor books for abusive practices such as spoofing — placing orders with no intent to execute in order to mislead other participants.
