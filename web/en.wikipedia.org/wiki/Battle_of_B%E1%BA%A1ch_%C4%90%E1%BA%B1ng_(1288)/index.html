The Battle of Bạch Đằng of 1288 was a naval battle on the Bạch Đằng River in northern Vietnam in which forces of the Trần dynasty under Trần Hưng Đạo destroyed a Yuan dynasty invasion fleet, ending the third Mongol invasion of Đại Việt.

Background

In 1287–88 the Yuan court of Kublai Khan launched a third invasion of Đại Việt. After initial Mongol successes, Vietnamese forces cut their supply lines, and the Yuan commander ordered a withdrawal by sea.

The battle

Learning the fleet's route, Trần Hưng Đạo planted iron-tipped wooden stakes in the riverbed at Bạch Đằng, hidden at high tide — the same tactic used at the same river in 938. Vietnamese boats lured the Yuan fleet in at high tide; as the tide fell, the heavy Mongol ships were impaled on the stakes and destroyed by fire rafts and attack craft.

Aftermath

The Yuan fleet was annihilated and its commander captured. The defeat ended Mongol attempts to conquer Đại Việt, which thereafter sent nominal tribute but remained independent.
