Understanding Comics

Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art is a 1993 non-fiction work by American cartoonist Scott McCloud, itself written as a comic book, that explores the medium of comics — its definition, history, vocabulary, and techniques.

AuthorScott McCloud
Published1993
GenreNon-fiction comic, media theory

Contents

McCloud defines comics as "juxtaposed pictorial and other images in deliberate sequence." Key concepts include closure (readers mentally completing action between panels in the gutter), the masking effect (iconic characters against realistic backgrounds), the picture plane triangle of reality–language–abstraction, time mapped to space, and the six types of panel transitions. The book surveys comics history from pre-Columbian art and Bayeux tapestry to manga and Will Eisner.

Reception and sequels

Praised by figures including Alan Moore, Art Spiegelman and Neil Gaiman, it became a standard text in comics studies and design courses. Sequels are Reinventing Comics (2000), on digital comics, and Making Comics (2006), on craft.
