Bawdsey Manor is a large country house at Bawdsey in Suffolk, England, standing at the mouth of the River Deben. It is celebrated as the birthplace of radar: in the 1930s the manor became the Royal Air Force's research station where the first operational radar system was developed.

The house

The manor was built in 1886 for Sir William Cuthbert Quilter, a wealthy stockbroker, art collector and MP, and was greatly enlarged in subsequent decades in a fanciful mixture of Tudor, Jacobean and French styles, with extensive gardens running down to the Deben estuary. A private chain ferry connected the estate to Felixstowe across the river.

Radar at Bawdsey

In 1936 the Air Ministry purchased the manor as a research station. Here a team under Robert Watson-Watt and Arnold Wilkins turned the results of the Daventry experiment into the Chain Home system: the first transmitting and receiving towers were erected at Bawdsey, and in 1937 RAF Bawdsey became the first operational radar station, the prototype for the chain that gave Fighter Command early warning during the Battle of Britain. Research moved inland after the outbreak of war in 1939, but the station continued in air-defence use, later hosting Bloodhound surface-to-air missiles, until the RAF left in the early 1990s.

Later use

The manor subsequently served as a school and is now used as a residential activity centre. The Bawdsey Radar Trust preserves the original transmitter block as a museum telling the story of radar's development.
