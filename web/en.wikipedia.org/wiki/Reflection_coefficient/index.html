Reflection coefficient


A wave reflected at a discontinuity in impedance.

In physics and electrical engineering, the reflection coefficient is a parameter that describes how much of a wave is reflected by an impedance discontinuity in the transmission medium. It is equal to the ratio of the amplitude of the reflected wave to the incident wave, with each expressed as phasors.

Transmission lines

For a transmission line terminated in a load impedance ZL, with characteristic impedance Z0, the voltage reflection coefficient is Γ = (ZL − Z0) / (ZL + Z0). When the load matches the line (ZL = Z0), Γ = 0 and no energy is reflected. An open circuit gives Γ = +1 and a short circuit Γ = −1. The magnitude of Γ is related to the standing wave ratio by SWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 − |Γ|).

Optics and acoustics

In optics, the corresponding amplitude ratios for reflected electric fields are given by the Fresnel equations; the squared magnitude gives the reflectance (reflected power fraction). In acoustics and seismology, reflection coefficients at an interface depend on the contrast in acoustic impedance between layers, and are central to reflection seismology.
