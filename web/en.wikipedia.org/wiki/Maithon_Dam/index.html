The Maithon Dam is located at Maithon, about 48 km from Dhanbad, in the state of Jharkhand, India. It is one of the dams built under the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC), modelled on the Tennessee Valley Authority of the United States. The dam was specially designed for flood control and generates 60 MW of electric power.

Description

The dam is built across the Barakar River, a tributary of the Damodar. It is 4,789 metres long and about 50 metres high, with a concrete spillway section and earthen flanks. Its reservoir, covering some 65 square kilometres, is the largest in the DVC system and a popular tourist destination, with an island and deer park.

Maithon has a unique feature: its powerhouse is located underground, one of the first such underground power stations in Asia, built into the hill on the left bank.

Purpose

The Damodar valley was historically prone to devastating floods — the Damodar was known as the "River of Sorrows". Maithon, together with the Tilaiya, Panchet and other DVC dams, moderates floods, supplies irrigation water, and provides power and industrial water supply to the coal and steel belt of Jharkhand and West Bengal.

Tourism

The lake and its surroundings attract visitors from Dhanbad, Asansol and Kolkata, especially in winter, for boating and picnics.
