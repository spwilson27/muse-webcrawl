Bamboo

Bamboos are a diverse group of perennial evergreen flowering plants in the subfamily Bambusoideae of the grass family Poaceae. They are among the fastest-growing plants in the world, with some species recorded growing over 90 centimetres in a single day. Bamboos are native to every continent except Europe and Antarctica, with the greatest diversity in Asia and South America.

Growth and life cycle

Bamboo culms emerge from underground rhizomes and reach their full height in a single growing season; unlike trees, they have no secondary growth and do not thicken with age. Many species flower only at intervals of decades in a phenomenon called gregarious flowering, after which the plants die, producing vast quantities of seed that can trigger rodent population booms.

Uses

Bamboo is used as a building material, in scaffolding, furniture, paper, and textiles, and its young shoots are eaten in many Asian cuisines. It is an important food source for animals including the giant panda, and is cultivated as an ornamental plant worldwide. Because of its rapid growth and carbon uptake, bamboo is promoted as a sustainable resource.

Cultural significance

In Chinese culture bamboo is one of the "Four Gentlemen" of art, symbolising resilience and integrity. It holds similar symbolic roles in Japan, Vietnam, and India.
