Registan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The three madrasas of the Registan in Samarkand, turquoise domes and tiled facades
The three madrasas of the Registan in Samarkand, turquoise domes and tiled facades

The Registan was the public square of ancient Samarkand, Uzbekistan — the heart of the Silk Road city and one of the most celebrated ensembles of Islamic architecture. Three madrasas frame it: the Ulugh Beg Madrasa (1417–20, built by the astronomer-king), the Tilya-Kori Madrasa (1646–60, 'gilded') and the Sher-Dor Madrasa (1619–36, whose facade bears the famous tiger-and-sun mosaics defying the aniconic convention).

History

Under Timur (Tamerlane) Samarkand became an imperial capital; his great-grandson Ulugh Beg made the Registan its scholarly face, teaching astronomy in his own madrasa. The square served as market, mustering ground and scaffold — proclamations read, executions staged — under successive Shaybanid and later rulers; Soviet restoration rebuilt its ruined domes, and the ensemble anchors UNESCO's listing of Samarkand (2001).

Architecture

Iwan-framed courtyards behind towering pishtaq facades, ribbed turquoise domes on cylindrical drums, and majolica tilework in geometric and calligraphic programmes make the Registan the textbook image of Central Asian Timurid and post-Timurid style — copied in spirit from Bukhara to Mughal India.
