Member states of the Visegrád Group

The Visegrád Group (also known as the Visegrád Four or the V4) is a cultural and political alliance of four Central European countries: the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, and Slovakia. The group takes its name from the Hungarian town of Visegrád, where the leaders of Czechoslovakia, Hungary and Poland met on 15 February 1991.

History

The alliance was formed as the member states sought to cooperate on joining NATO and the European Union following the fall of communism. After Czechoslovakia split in 1993, the group continued with four members. All four joined the European Union on 1 May 2004.

Cooperation

The V4 countries hold regular summits of heads of government and coordinate positions within EU institutions, particularly on energy, infrastructure and migration policy. The International Visegrád Fund, based in Bratislava, supports cultural, scientific and educational exchanges.
