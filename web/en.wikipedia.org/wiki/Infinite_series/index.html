Infinite series

An infinite series is the sum of the terms of an infinite sequence. Given a sequence (a1, a2, a3, …), the series is written a1 + a2 + a3 + ⋯, and its value is defined as the limit of its partial sums, if that limit exists.

Σn=1..∞ an = limN→∞ Σn=1..N an

Convergence

A series is convergent if its sequence of partial sums converges to a finite limit, and divergent otherwise. A series converges absolutely if the series of absolute values of its terms converges; absolute convergence implies convergence. Standard tests for convergence include the comparison test, the ratio test, the root test, the integral test and the alternating series test. The harmonic series 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + ⋯ is the classic example of a divergent series whose terms tend to zero — showing that terms tending to zero is necessary but not sufficient for convergence.

Important series

Geometric series converge when the common ratio has absolute value less than one. Power series, such as the Taylor and Maclaurin series of the exponential, sine and cosine functions, are central to analysis and are used to define many functions. Fourier series decompose periodic functions into sums of sines and cosines. The study of series was placed on a rigorous footing in the nineteenth century by Cauchy, Abel and others, and remains fundamental to mathematics, physics and engineering.
