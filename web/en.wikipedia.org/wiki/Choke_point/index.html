Choke point

The world's principal maritime choke points

In military strategy, a choke point (or chokepoint) is a geographical feature on land such as a valley, defile, or bridge, or a maritime passage through a strait, which an armed force is forced to pass through to reach its objective, giving a defender an advantage. The term also applies to congestion points in transportation networks, shipping routes, computer networks, and other systems where flow is constricted.

Maritime choke points

Several narrow waterways are critical to global trade and energy supply: the Strait of Malacca, the Strait of Hormuz (through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil passes), the Bab el-Mandeb, the Suez Canal, the Panama Canal, the Turkish Straits, and the Taiwan Strait. Blockage of any of these—by conflict, accident, or piracy—can have outsized effects on world commerce, as illustrated by the 2021 Suez Canal obstruction by the container ship Ever Given.

Land and other uses

On land, mountain passes such as Thermopylae and the Khyber Pass, bridges, and tunnels have historically served as choke points where small forces could hold off larger ones. In economics and logistics, the term describes points of vulnerability in supply chains; in computing, checkpoints and narrow interfaces in networks are likewise called choke points.
