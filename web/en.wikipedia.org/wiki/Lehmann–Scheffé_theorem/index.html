Lehmann–Scheffé theorem

In statistics, the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem states that if a statistic is unbiased, complete and sufficient for some parameter, then it is the best possible estimator for that parameter: it is the unique minimum-variance unbiased estimator (UMVUE). More precisely, if T is a complete sufficient statistic and δ is any unbiased estimator of a function of the parameter, then the conditional expectation of δ given T is an unbiased estimator with variance no larger than that of δ, and it does not depend on the particular δ chosen.

Background

The theorem, published by Erich Leo Lehmann and Henry Scheffé in 1950, builds on the Rao–Blackwell theorem, which shows that conditioning an unbiased estimator on a sufficient statistic never increases its variance. Completeness of the sufficient statistic guarantees that the resulting estimator is unique, hence genuinely "best" among unbiased estimators. If no complete sufficient statistic exists, a UMVUE may fail to exist at all.

Example

For a sample from a normal distribution with unknown mean and variance, the sample mean and sample variance are based on a complete sufficient statistic, and the theorem underlies the classical result that the usual sample statistics are optimal unbiased estimators.
