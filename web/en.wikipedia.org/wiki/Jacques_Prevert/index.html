Jacques Prévert

Born4 February 1900, Neuilly-sur-Seine, France
Died11 April 1977, Omonville-la-Petite, France
OccupationPoet, screenwriter
Notable worksParoles (1946); Les Enfants du paradis (1945)

Jacques Prévert (4 February 1900 – 11 April 1977) was a French poet and screenwriter. His best-known poetry collection, Paroles (1946), is among the most widely read volumes of French verse of the twentieth century, and he wrote the scripts of several classics of French cinema, above all Marcel Carné's Les Enfants du paradis (1945).

Life and work

Prévert was associated in his youth with the surrealists before breaking with André Breton, and in the 1930s he wrote for the agitprop October Group. His collaboration with Carné produced Drôle de drame (1937), Le Quai des brumes (1938), Le jour se lève (1939) and Les Enfants du paradis, often cited as the greatest French film. His poems, written in a direct, colloquial style mixing humour, wordplay and anti-authoritarian feeling, were set to music by Joseph Kosma — most famously "Les Feuilles mortes", known in English as "Autumn Leaves" — and sung by Yves Montand and others.

Legacy

Prévert's poetry entered French school curricula and popular culture to an unusual degree; poems such as "Barbara" and "Déjeuner du matin" remain widely memorised and quoted.
