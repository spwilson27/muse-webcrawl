Aboutness

Aboutness is the property by which a word, phrase, sentence, book, or mental state is about something — directed at a subject matter or object. It is closely related to the philosophical notion of intentionality, Franz Brentano's term for the directedness of mental phenomena toward objects, real or imagined.

In philosophy of language and mind

Philosophers ask what makes a representation about one thing rather than another, and how a representation can be about something that does not exist, such as a unicorn. Theories of content include causal, informational, teleological, and conceptual-role accounts. Aboutness also underlies puzzles about propositional attitudes: Lois Lane believes Superman can fly but not that Clark Kent can, although the names co-refer.

In information science

In library and information science, "aboutness" names the subject content of a document — what a text is about — which cataloguers and indexers must identify to assign subject headings and support retrieval. Approaches range from author-stated topics to reader-oriented and domain-analytic views of subject. The concept is central to subject indexing, classification theory, and document summarisation.

Related concepts

Aboutness differs from reference, which picks out an object, and from meaning in general. Two documents may refer to the same entities yet differ in aboutness because they treat different aspects of them. Students of logic study aboutness through subject matter and topic-sensitive logics, in which valid inference may require shared content, not truth preservation alone.
