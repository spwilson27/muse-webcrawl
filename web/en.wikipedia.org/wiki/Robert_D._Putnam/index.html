Robert D. Putnam

Robert David Putnam
Born: 9 January 1941, Rochester, New York
Institution: Harvard University
Known for: Bowling Alone, social capital theory

Robert David Putnam (born 1941) is an American political scientist and professor at the Harvard Kennedy School. He is best known for his work on social capital and for the book Bowling Alone (2000), which argued that civic engagement in the United States has declined since the 1960s.

Work

His earlier Italian study, Making Democracy Work (1993), linked regional government performance to traditions of civic community. In Bowling Alone — expanded from a 1995 essay — Putnam documented declines in club membership, volunteering and informal sociability, attributing them to generational change, television, suburbanisation and work pressures, and distinguishing "bonding" from "bridging" social capital.

Later books

With David Campbell he wrote American Grace (2010) on religion in US public life; Our Kids (2015) examined the growing opportunity gap between rich and poor American children, drawing on his hometown of Port Clinton, Ohio; and The Upswing (2020, with Shaylyn Romney Garrett) traced a century-long arc of American solidarity and its reversal.
