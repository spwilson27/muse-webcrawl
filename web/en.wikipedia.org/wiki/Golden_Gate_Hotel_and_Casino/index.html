Golden Gate Hotel and Casino

The Golden Gate Hotel and Casino is a hotel and casino located at One Fremont Street in downtown Las Vegas, Nevada, at the western end of the Fremont Street Experience. Opened in 1906 as the Hotel Nevada, it is the oldest hotel and casino in Las Vegas still in operation, and occupies the address assigned the city's very first telephone number: number 1.

History

The Hotel Nevada opened in 1906, a year after Las Vegas was founded, offering rooms for $1 a night with electric lighting, steam heat and indoor plumbing. Gambling was legalised in Nevada in 1931, and the property — renamed Sal Sagev ("Las Vegas" spelled backwards) for a period — expanded under that name before becoming the Golden Gate in 1955, a name taken from San Francisco's Golden Gate and suggested by partners including members of an Italian-American group from the Bay Area. In 1959 the Golden Gate introduced a 50-cent shrimp cocktail that became a famous Las Vegas loss-leader tradition, served for decades and raising in price only after many years.

Recent years

Brothers Derek and Greg Stevens took a controlling interest in 2016, aligning the Golden Gate with their nearby properties The D and Circa. A 2017 renovation expanded the casino floor into the former La Bayou space, added a modern sportsbook presence and refreshed the exterior while preserving the hotel's historic character; the original 1906 rooms remain in use as "Original 10" rooms. At only 122 rooms, it is one of the smallest casinos on Fremont Street, marketed on its vintage atmosphere and history.
