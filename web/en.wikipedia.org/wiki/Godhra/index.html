Godhra is a city and municipality in the Panchmahal district of Gujarat, India, of which it is the administrative headquarters. It lies in eastern Gujarat, roughly midway between Ahmedabad and the Madhya Pradesh border, and is an important railway junction on the Delhi–Mumbai line. The name is traditionally derived from gou (cow) and dhara (land) — "land of cows".

History

The area was ruled successively by the Chalukyas, the Gujarat Sultanate, the Mughals, the Marathas and the British, under whom Godhra became a district town of the Bombay Presidency. During the freedom struggle the town and district figured in tribal (Bhil) and peasant movements.

2002 train burning and riots

Godhra became nationally known on 27 February 2002, when a coach of the Sabarmati Express carrying Hindu pilgrims returning from Ayodhya caught fire at the station, killing 59 people. The cause of the fire was fiercely disputed — official commissions and courts treated it as an attack by a mob, while some inquiries suggested an accident. The incident triggered the 2002 Gujarat riots, in which over a thousand people, mostly Muslims, were killed across the state. Trials of those accused in the train case continued for many years, with convictions in 2011 later partly upheld and partly commuted on appeal.

Geography and economy

The surrounding Panchmahal region is partly tribal and forested; agriculture (maize, paddy, pulses), dairy farming and small industry anchor the local economy. Godhra is a trading centre for agricultural produce and timber.
