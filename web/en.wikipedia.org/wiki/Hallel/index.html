Hallel

Hallel (Hebrew: הלל, "praise") is a Jewish prayer and scriptural unit consisting of Psalms 113–118, recited as a prayer of praise and thanksgiving on Jewish holidays. The psalms open and close with the exclamation Hallelujah ("Praise the Lord").

Occasions

Full Hallel is recited on the major festivals — Sukkot, Passover (on the first days, and at the Passover seder), Shavuot and Hanukkah, as well as on Rosh Chodesh in a shortened form ("Half Hallel") and, in many communities, on Israeli Independence Day. It is not recited on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, whose solemn character is considered inappropriate for the prayer's joyfulness.

Structure

The six psalms include Psalm 113 ("Praise, O servants of the Lord"), the narrative of the Exodus in Psalm 114, and Psalm 118, whose verses ("This is the day the Lord has made") figure centrally in festival liturgy. At the seder, Hallel is split: part is recited before the meal and the remainder after it.
