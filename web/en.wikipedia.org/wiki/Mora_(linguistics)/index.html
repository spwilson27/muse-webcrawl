Mora (linguistics)

Mora
FieldPhonology
Symbolμ
MeasuresSyllable weight (timing unit)

In linguistics, a mora (plural morae or moras) is a unit of syllable weight that determines stress and timing in some languages. A light syllable contains one mora, while a heavy syllable contains two; some languages also recognise superheavy syllables of three morae. Morae are the basis of "mora-timed" rhythm, famously exemplified by Japanese.

Structure

Within a syllable, the onset consonant is generally non-moraic (with exceptions such as the Japanese geminate and syllabic nasal). The nucleus contributes one mora if it is a short vowel; a long vowel or diphthong contributes two. A coda consonant may add a second mora, making the syllable heavy — phonologists call such languages "quantity-sensitive" in their stress systems.

Examples

Japanese

Japanese is the classic mora language: each kana character corresponds roughly to one mora, so that "Tōkyō" has four morae (to-o-kyo-o) but two syllables. The syllabic nasal /N/ and the first half of a geminate consonant each count as a full mora, which is why they occupy a full beat in Japanese verse — haiku are counted in morae (on), not syllables.

Other languages

Ancient Greek and Latin verse was quantitative, built on patterns of heavy and light syllables counted in morae. Hawaiian, Gilbertese, and several other languages are also analysed as mora-timed, and mora structure plays a role in the phonology of many languages' stress, compensatory lengthening, and minimal-word requirements.
