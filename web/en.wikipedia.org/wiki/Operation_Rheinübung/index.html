Operation Rheinübung

Operation Rheinübung ("Exercise Rhine") was the sortie of the German battleship Bismarck and heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen into the Atlantic Ocean in May 1941, intended to attack Allied merchant shipping supplying Britain.

Intercepted in the Denmark Strait, the German ships fought the Battle of the Denmark Strait on 24 May 1941, in which Bismarck sank the battlecruiser HMS Hood with the loss of all but three of her crew; Prinz Eugen detached to raid independently. Damaged and leaking fuel, Bismarck headed for Brest but was crippled by torpedo bombers from HMS Ark Royal, which jammed her rudder.

On 27 May 1941 Bismarck was brought to action by the battleships King George V and Rodney and sank with heavy loss of life. The operation was the last major surface commerce-raiding sortie attempted by the Kriegsmarine.
