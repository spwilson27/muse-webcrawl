Wolfe Creek Crater
LocationWestern Australia, near Halls Creek

ProtectedWolfe Creek Meteorite Crater National Park

Wolfe Creek Crater is a well-preserved meteorite impact crater in the Kimberley region of Western Australia, on the edge of the Great Sandy Desert. It is the second largest crater in the world from which meteorite fragments have been recovered, after Barringer Crater in Arizona.

Formation

The crater formed about 300,000 years ago when an iron meteorite estimated at some 50,000 tonnes struck the Earth at cosmic velocity. The impact excavated a crater 875 metres across and originally about 120 metres deep; wind-blown sand has since partly filled it, and its central floor now supports a distinctive ring of large trees where water collects.

Discovery and significance

Aboriginal Jaru people knew the crater for millennia as Kandimalal, and it figures in Dreaming stories, including accounts of a giant serpent emerging from the ground. Europeans first reported it after an aerial survey in 1947. The site is protected as Wolfe Creek Meteorite Crater National Park, reached from the Great Northern Highway via a gravel road from Halls Creek, about 150 km away.
