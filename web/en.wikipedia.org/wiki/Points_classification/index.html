Points classification

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The points classification is a secondary competition in some stage races, in which points are awarded for high finishes in stages and for winning intermediate sprints, with the overall leader typically wearing a distinctive jersey. It is most commonly associated with sprinters, whose consistency in stage finishes and intermediate sprints earns them points even if they cannot compete for the general classification in the mountains.

Points classification in the Grand Tours

Tour de France

At the Tour de France, the points classification leader wears the green jersey (maillot vert), introduced in 1953. Points are awarded at stage finishes — with flat stages worth more points than mountain stages — and at a single intermediate sprint on each road stage. Slovak sprinter Peter Sagan holds the record with seven green jersey victories.

Giro d'Italia

The Giro d'Italia awards the maglia ciclamino, a cyclamen (formerly red) jersey. The classification has existed since 1966, with the jersey colour changing over the years; Francesco Moser and Giuseppe Saronni are among its most successful riders.

Vuelta a España

At the Vuelta a España, the points leader also wears a green jersey (since 2009; previously other colours including blue). Sean Kelly, Laurent Jalabert and Alejandro Valverde have all won the classification multiple times.
