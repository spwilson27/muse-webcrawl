Time-of-flight mass spectrometry

Time-of-flight mass spectrometry (TOF-MS) is a method of mass spectrometry in which an ion's mass-to-charge ratio is determined via a time-of-flight measurement: ions are accelerated by an electric field of known strength, and the time each ion takes to travel a known distance to the detector depends on its mass-to-charge ratio — lighter ions arrive first.

Principle

Because all ions receive approximately the same kinetic energy, their velocities vary inversely with the square root of m/z. Flight times are typically measured in microseconds. A reflectron — an ion mirror — can correct for energy spread and improve resolution.

Applications

TOF analysers are commonly coupled with MALDI sources for analysing proteins and polymers, and with electrospray in Q-TOF instruments. They offer high mass range, fast acquisition and good resolution, and are used in proteomics, imaging mass spectrometry and spaceflight instruments.
