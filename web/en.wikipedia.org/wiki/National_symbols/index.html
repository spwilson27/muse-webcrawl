National symbols

National symbols
Common typesFlag, emblem, anthem, animal, bird, flower, tree, dish, sport
PurposeRepresentation of a nation's identity, values and heritage

National symbols are emblems, images, objects or traditions that represent a nation and its people, expressing its identity, history, culture and values. They may be formally adopted by law or constitution, or may arise informally from popular usage.

Official and unofficial symbols

Nearly every country has a national flag, a coat of arms or emblem, and a national anthem. Many also designate a national animal (such as the bald eagle of the United States or the Bengal tiger of India), a national bird, flower, tree, foundation myth, patron saint or personification — figures like Marianne for France or Britannia for Britain.

Functions

National symbols are used on currency, passports, government buildings and sporting uniforms, and are displayed at official ceremonies and national holidays. They can serve as rallying points for unity and patriotism, though they are sometimes contested as societies debate whose heritage they reflect.

Protection

Many states legally protect their central symbols: flag desecration laws, restrictions on commercial use of coats of arms, and protocols for the performance of anthems are common, though their scope and constitutionality vary widely between countries.
