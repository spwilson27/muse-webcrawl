The Democratic-Republican Party was an American political party founded by Thomas Jefferson and James Madison in the 1790s. It championed republicanism, individual liberty, states' rights, and an agrarian vision of America, and opposed the Federalist Party's programme of a strong national government, a national bank and close ties with Britain.

History

The party coalesced in opposition to the policies of Treasury Secretary Alexander Hamilton. Jefferson's victory in the election of 1800 — the "Revolution of 1800" — began a generation of Democratic-Republican dominance: Jefferson, Madison and James Monroe held the presidency from 1801 to 1825, the period Monroe's tenure being dubbed the Era of Good Feelings as the Federalists collapsed.

Fragmentation

In the disputed election of 1824 the party's unity broke down. Its factions evolved into the Democratic Party organised by supporters of Andrew Jackson, and the National Republican Party (later the Whigs) around John Quincy Adams and Henry Clay.
