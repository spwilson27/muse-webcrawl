Bishōjo game
OriginJapan
Literal meaning"Pretty girl game"
Related genresVisual novel, dating sim, eroge

A bishōjo game (Japanese: 美少女ゲーム, "pretty girl game") is a type of Japanese video game centered on interactions with attractive anime-style female characters. The term encompasses dating simulation games, visual novels, and related genres marketed primarily toward a male audience.

Characteristics

Gameplay typically emphasizes narrative and character relationships: players make choices that develop storylines and deepen bonds with heroines, often across branching routes. Visual presentation relies on illustrated character sprites over backgrounds, with voice acting common in modern releases.

History

The genre grew out of Japanese PC games of the early 1980s and expanded greatly with the popularity of adventure games and visual novels in the 1990s, including landmark titles by studios such as Leaf and Key. Although a large share of early bishōjo games were adult-oriented (eroge), all-ages titles became increasingly prominent, and the genre's conventions influenced mobile games and international visual novel development. The corresponding genre aimed at female audiences, featuring handsome male characters, is known as otome games.
