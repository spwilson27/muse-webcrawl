Insignia

An insignia (from Latin insignia, plural of insigne — a distinguishing mark) is a sign or mark distinguishing a group, grade, rank, or function. It can be a symbol of authority, and is particularly used to denote a specific or general authority or office, or membership of an organisation. Insignia include badges, emblems, patches, ribbons, and devices worn on uniforms or regalia.

Military insignia

Armed forces use insignia to show rank (chevrons, stars, pips, stripes, and epaulettes), branch or corps, unit (shoulder sleeve insignia and formation signs), and qualifications such as parachutist or pilot wings. Systems differ by country and often descend from heraldic and naval traditions — for example, the rings on naval officers' sleeves and the eagles, stars, and oak leaves of US rank badges. Laws in many countries restrict the unauthorised wearing of military insignia.

Civil and other uses

Police, fire, and emergency services use badge and rank insignia similar to the military's. Orders and decorations have their own insignia — stars, sashes, collars, and medals — conferred by states and dynasties. Heraldic arms, flags, logos, and trademarks all function as insignia of identity, and the word is also used for the regalia of office, such as the crown jewels' role as insignia of monarchy. Vehicle and aircraft insignia, including national roundels and fin flashes, mark state ownership in the same spirit.
