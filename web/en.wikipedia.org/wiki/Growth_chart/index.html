Growth chart

A growth chart is a tool used by paediatricians and other health care providers to follow the growth of children. Growth charts consist of a series of percentile curves that illustrate the distribution of selected body measurements—such as length, weight, and head circumference—in children of a given age and sex.

Use

A child's measurements are plotted on the chart at successive visits; a child who consistently tracks along a percentile curve is usually growing normally, whereas sharp crossings of percentile lines may prompt investigation. Because children grow at different rates, growth charts describe population distributions rather than a single "ideal" trajectory.

Standards

In the United States, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) published revised growth charts in 2000 based on national survey data. In 2006 the World Health Organization released growth standards based on breastfed children raised in optimal conditions in six countries; the WHO standards are recommended for children under two years in many countries.
