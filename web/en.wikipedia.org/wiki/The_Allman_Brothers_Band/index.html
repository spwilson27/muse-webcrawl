The Allman Brothers Band

The Allman Brothers Band was an American rock band formed in Jacksonville, Florida, in 1969 by brothers Duane Allman (slide and lead guitar) and Gregg Allman (vocals, keyboards), along with Dickey Betts (lead guitar, vocals), Berry Oakley (bass), Butch Trucks (drums) and Jai Johanny "Jaimoe" Johanson (drums). The band pioneered Southern rock, blending blues, jazz, country and rock and roll in extended, improvisational performances built around twin lead guitars and two drummers.

History

The band's first two studio albums met modest commercial success, but the live double album At Fillmore East (1971) was a breakthrough, capturing celebrated performances of "Whipping Post", "Statesboro Blues" and "In Memory of Elizabeth Reed". Duane Allman was killed in a motorcycle accident in Macon, Georgia, in October 1971, aged 24; bassist Berry Oakley died in a similar accident just over a year later, three blocks from the site of Allman's crash.

With Chuck Leavell on piano, the band reached its commercial peak with Brothers and Sisters (1973), featuring Dickey Betts's "Ramblin' Man" and the instrumental "Jessica". Internal conflict, drug problems and Gregg Allman's legal troubles led to a bitter break-up in 1976. The band re-formed in 1978 and again, more durably, in 1989. A later line-up with guitarists Warren Haynes and Derek Trucks sustained the band's reputation as a live institution, with an annual residency at New York's Beacon Theatre. The Allman Brothers Band played its final concert on October 28, 2014, at the Beacon.

Legacy

The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2012. Rolling Stone ranked them among the greatest artists of all time, and their improvisation-driven model helped inspire the jam band movement.
