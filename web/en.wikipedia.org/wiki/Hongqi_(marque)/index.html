Hongqi (marque)

Hongqi
Chinese: 红旗 ("Red Flag")
Founded: 1958
Parent: FAW Group
Headquarters: Changchun, Jilin, China
Products: Luxury automobiles

Hongqi (Chinese: 红旗; pinyin: Hóngqí, "Red Flag") is a Chinese luxury automobile marque owned by the FAW Group. Launched in 1958, it is the oldest Chinese passenger-car brand and historically served as the official vehicle of senior Chinese leaders, including the limousines used in National Day military parades.

History

The first Hongqi, the CA72, debuted in 1958; the long-running CA770 limousine followed in the 1960s and became the marque's signature model. Production was intermittent after 1981, with later models based on joint-venture platforms, including Lincoln Town Car–derived cars in the 1990s. FAW relaunched Hongqi as a standalone premium brand in 2013 with the H7 sedan, and invested heavily from 2018 in new models (H5, H9, the E-HS9 electric SUV, and the L-series limousines such as the L5, one of China's most expensive production cars). The ceremonial N701 carries the Chinese head of state. Under the relaunch, Hongqi sales climbed from a few thousand to several hundred thousand units annually, and the brand expanded into export markets in Europe and the Middle East.
