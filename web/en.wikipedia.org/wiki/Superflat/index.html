Superflat

Superflat is a postmodern art movement founded by the Japanese artist Takashi Murakami, characterized by a flattened, two-dimensional aesthetic that draws on anime, manga, and traditional Japanese painting, and by a critique of consumerism and otaku culture in post-war Japan. Murakami coined the term around 2000 and curated a touring exhibition of the same name that introduced the movement internationally. "Superflat" also refers to the blending of high art and low culture — the flattening of distinctions between fine art, commercial design, and subculture.

Superflat merges anime aesthetics, traditional Japanese art, and consumer culture.

Concept

Murakami argued that Japanese visual culture, from the flattened picture planes of Rinpa painting and ukiyo-e to contemporary anime, exhibits a distinctive "super flat" sensibility. In his manifesto he extended this flatness to Japanese society itself — its consumer culture, its blurred hierarchy of art and merchandise, and what he portrayed as the infantilized condition of post-war Japan under American influence.

Exhibitions and artists

The Superflat exhibition opened in Tokyo in 2000 and toured to Los Angeles, Minneapolis, and Seattle. Artists associated with the movement, many promoted through Murakami's Kaikai Kiki company, include Yoshitomo Nara, Chiho Aoshima, Aya Takano, and Mr. Murakami's own work — smiling flowers, the character DOB, and collaborations with Louis Vuitton — exemplifies the movement's fusion of gallery art and mass-produced goods.

Criticism

Critics have debated whether Superflat is a genuine artistic critique or a branding strategy that commodifies the very consumerism it claims to examine. It nonetheless became one of the most internationally influential Japanese art movements of the early twenty-first century.
