Leach Pottery

The Leach Pottery, founded in 1920 in St Ives, Cornwall, by Bernard Leach and the Japanese potter Shoji Hamada, is regarded as one of the most influential potteries of the 20th century and the birthplace of the modern British studio pottery movement.

The original climbing kiln at the Leach Pottery.

History

Leach and Hamada built a traditional Japanese-style three-chambered climbing kiln (noborigama), the first of its kind in the West, and produced stoneware and slipware combining Eastern and English country traditions. Generations of potters trained there, including Michael Cardew, Katherine Pleydell-Bouverie, and Leach's sons David and Michael Leach, and its "standard ware" tableware embodied Leach's philosophy of honest, functional craft set out in his A Potter's Book (1940).

Today

After Bernard Leach's death in 1979 the pottery declined, but it was restored and reopened in 2008 with a museum, gallery, and working studio, and continues to produce studio pottery and train apprentices.
