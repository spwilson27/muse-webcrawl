World record progression 100 metres men

Usain Bolt set the current record of 9.58 seconds in Berlin in 2009.

The first world record in the men's 100 metres sprint was recognised by the IAAF (now World Athletics) in 1912. The record has been lowered from 10.6 seconds, run by Donald Lippincott of the United States in Stockholm in 1912, to the current mark of 9.58 seconds, set by Usain Bolt of Jamaica at the 2009 World Championships in Berlin. Records were hand-timed to the tenth of a second until 1975; fully automatic electronic timing to the hundredth has been required since 1977.

History

The 10-second barrier was first broken electronically by Jim Hines at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, where altitude assisted sprinting. Carl Lewis and Leroy Burrell traded the record in the early 1990s, and Donovan Bailey's victory at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics restored it to Canada. Between 2005 and 2009 the record fell five times in a Jamaican duel between Asafa Powell and Usain Bolt, culminating in Bolt's 9.58, a mark that improved the record by the largest margin of the electronic era.
