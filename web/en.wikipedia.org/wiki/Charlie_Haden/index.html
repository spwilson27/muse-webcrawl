Charles Edward Haden (6 August 1937 – 11 July 2014) was an American jazz double bassist, bandleader, composer and educator. He came to prominence as a member of the pioneering Ornette Coleman Quartet that launched the free jazz movement with The Shape of Jazz to Come (1959), and later led the Liberation Music Orchestra and Quartet West.

Early life

Haden was born in Shenandoah, Iowa, into a musical family that performed country music on radio as the Haden Family; as a small child he sang and yodelled on their broadcasts until polio damaged his vocal cords. He took up the double bass as a teenager, was drawn to jazz, and moved to Los Angeles in 1957.

Career

With Coleman, Don Cherry and Billy Higgins, Haden helped redefine jazz improvisation, abandoning fixed chord changes; his resonant, lyrical bass lines grounded the group's explorations. He later recorded with Keith Jarrett's American Quartet, with Alice Coltrane, and in many duo settings. His Liberation Music Orchestra, formed in 1969 with composer Carla Bley, fused jazz with protest music of the Spanish Civil War and later Latin American and anti-apartheid themes; its debut album was widely acclaimed. Quartet West (1987 onwards) evoked noir-era Los Angeles.

His later work included Grammy-winning albums of Latin ballads (Nocturne, 2001), duets with Pat Metheny (Beyond the Missouri Sky, 1997), and a return to his country roots in Rambling Boy (2008), recorded with his family, including his triplet daughters Petra, Rachel and Tanya Haden and his son Josh Haden. He founded the jazz studies programme at the California Institute of the Arts in 1982.

Death and honours

Haden died in Los Angeles on 11 July 2014 after a long illness. He received the NEA Jazz Masters Award in 2012 and a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award posthumously in 2015.
