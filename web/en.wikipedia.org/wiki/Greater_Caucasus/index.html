Greater Caucasus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greater Caucasus is the major mountain range of the Caucasus Mountains, lying between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea in Eurasia. The range runs roughly west-northwest to east-southeast, from near Taman on the Black Sea to the Absheron Peninsula on the Caspian, a distance of more than 1,100 kilometres (680 miles).

Together with the Lesser Caucasus to the south, it forms the Caucasus mountain system, a natural barrier between the North Caucasus (Ciscaucasia) and the South Caucasus (Transcaucasia). The Greater Caucasus watershed also forms, in large part, the conventional boundary between Europe and Asia.

Geography

The range is usually divided into three sections: the Western Caucasus, from the Black Sea to Mount Elbrus; the Central Caucasus, from Elbrus to Mount Kazbek; and the Eastern Caucasus, from Kazbek to the Caspian Sea. The highest peaks include Mount Elbrus (5,642 m), Dykh-Tau (5,205 m), Shkhara (5,193 m), and Mount Kazbek (5,047 m). The main ridge is heavily glaciated, with several hundred glaciers.

Climate and rivers

The Greater Caucasus strongly influences regional climate, shielding the South Caucasus from cold northern air masses. Major rivers draining the range include the Kuban and Terek to the north, and the Rioni, Enguri, and Kura tributaries to the south. Mountain passes such as the Darial Gorge and the Cross Pass have been strategically important since antiquity.

Countries

The range lies within Russia to the north and Georgia and Azerbaijan to the south. Its foothills are home to a remarkable diversity of peoples and languages, often described as a "mountain of languages".
