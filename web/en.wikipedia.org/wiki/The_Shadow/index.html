The Shadow is a fictional character created by writer Walter B. Gibson for the American publisher Street & Smith. One of the most famous figures of the pulp magazine era, the Shadow began as the mysterious narrator of the Detective Story Hour radio program in 1930 and became the hero of The Shadow Magazine (1931–1949), in which Gibson wrote 282 of the 325 novels.

The character

In the pulps, the Shadow is secretly Kent Allard, a famed aviator and former spy, who frequently impersonates millionaire Lamont Cranston; in the radio series, Cranston himself is the Shadow, "a wealthy young man about town" who learned in the Orient "the hypnotic power to cloud men's minds so they cannot see him." The radio version, voiced most famously by a young Orson Welles (1937–1938), opened with the signature lines: "Who knows what evil lurks in the hearts of men? The Shadow knows!" The character wears a black cloak and slouch hat, is aided by a network of agents — including companion Margo Lane, created for the radio show — and wages war on crime with twin .45 automatics.

Legacy

The Shadow was a major influence on later costumed crimefighters, most notably Batman, whose creators drew on the Shadow's appearance, methods, and double identity. The character has appeared in comics, serials, and the 1994 feature film The Shadow starring Alec Baldwin.
