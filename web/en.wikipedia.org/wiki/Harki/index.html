Harki

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Harkis served as auxiliaries of the French Army during the Algerian War (1954–1962).

Harki (from Arabic ḥarka, "movement"/mobile unit) is the generic term for Muslim Algerians who served as auxiliaries (supplétifs) in the French Army during the Algerian War (1954–1962), fighting against the National Liberation Front (FLN). At their height in 1961 the various categories of auxiliaries — harkis proper, moghaznis, groupes mobiles de sécurité and self-defence groups — numbered well over 200,000 men, exceeding the number of FLN fighters.

Background

Algerian soldiers had served France since the conquest of 1830, in both world wars. During the war of independence the French recruited rural auxiliaries for local knowledge, language and intelligence, often for economic motives or village protection rather than ideology; some were former FLN members who had changed sides. The harkas were mobile units of up to a few dozen men attached to French regular units.

After the Évian Accords

The Évian Accords of March 1962 ended the war and guaranteed the safety of those who had served France; in practice the French government refused mass repatriation and disarmed the harkis before withdrawing. Abandoned to reprisals, tens of thousands — estimates range from 30,000 to 150,000 — were killed in massacres in the summer of 1962. Around 90,000 harkis and family members reached France, many only through the efforts of individual officers acting against orders.

In France

In France the refugees were held for years in camps such as Rivesaltes and Saint-Maurice-l'Ardoise, then in segregated housing estates and forestry hamlets, officially treated not as repatriates but as refugees of a special kind. Their children led protest movements in 1975 and 1991 demanding recognition. France progressively acknowledged its debt: a 2001 day of national homage, a 2005 law recognising the nation's obligation, President Macron's 2021 request for forgiveness in the name of France, and a 2022 law of recognition and reparations.
