Galois/Counter Mode and the Advanced Encryption Standard

This article discusses the use of Galois field arithmetic in the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES). AES, standardised by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology in 2001 (FIPS 197), is built almost entirely on operations in the finite field GF(2⁸), also written as GF(2)[x]/(x⁸ + x⁴ + x³ + x + 1).

Finite field arithmetic in AES

Each byte of the AES state is interpreted as an element of GF(2⁸): a polynomial of degree at most 7 over GF(2), with arithmetic performed modulo the irreducible polynomial m(x) = x⁸ + x⁴ + x³ + x + 1 (0x11B). Addition in the field is bitwise XOR. Multiplication is polynomial multiplication followed by reduction modulo m(x); in software this is often accelerated with tables or with the "xtime" operation for multiplication by x.

Where the field is used

  • SubBytes: each byte is replaced by its multiplicative inverse in GF(2⁸) (with zero mapped to zero), followed by a fixed affine transformation over GF(2).
  • MixColumns: each column of the state is multiplied by a fixed 4×4 matrix over GF(2⁸) with entries {01}, {02} and {03}, providing diffusion.
  • Key expansion: the RotWord/SubWord steps use the same S-box, and round constants are generated as powers of x in the field.

The use of finite-field operations gives AES strong algebraic structure that supports its security analysis, while remaining implementable efficiently in hardware and software; the field arithmetic can be computed with small lookup tables or, in modern processors, with dedicated instructions such as AES-NI.
