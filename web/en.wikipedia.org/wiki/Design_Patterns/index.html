Design Patterns

Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software is a software engineering book describing software design patterns, published in 1994 by Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides — known collectively as the "Gang of Four" (GoF). The book, originating in Gamma's doctoral work and the authors' experience with object-oriented frameworks, catalogues 23 recurring solutions to common design problems in object-oriented programming and remains one of the most influential texts in software engineering.

Contents

The 23 patterns are organised into three families:

  • Creational patterns — Abstract Factory, Builder, Factory Method, Prototype, Singleton.
  • Structural patterns — Adapter, Bridge, Composite, Decorator, Facade, Flyweight, Proxy.
  • Behavioural patterns — Chain of Responsibility, Command, Interpreter, Iterator, Mediator, Memento, Observer, State, Strategy, Template Method, Visitor.

Each pattern is documented with a standard template: intent, motivation, applicability, structure (in UML/O MT diagrams), participants, collaborations, consequences, implementation, sample code (in C++ and Smalltalk), known uses, and related patterns.

Reception and influence

The book introduced a shared vocabulary that changed how programmers discuss design, and it received the ACM SIGSOFT Outstanding Research Award's recognition via the authors' later honours, including the 2010s programming-language achievement recognition for the Gang of Four. It has also drawn criticism: some argue the patterns compensate for missing language features, and that they are over-applied. Nevertheless, its patterns underpin countless frameworks and remain standard teaching material.
