Workers' Party (Brazil)

The Workers' Party (Portuguese: Partido dos Trabalhadores, PT) is a centre-left to left-wing political party in Brazil, one of the country's largest and most important parties. Its symbol is a red flag with a five-pointed star bearing the initials PT.

History

The PT was founded in São Paulo on 10 February 1980 by trade unionists, intellectuals, rural workers and Catholic activists opposed to Brazil's military dictatorship; among its founders was metalworker Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Lula won the presidency in 2002 and 2006; his successor Dilma Rousseff won in 2010 and 2014 but was removed by impeachment in 2016. Lula returned to the presidency after winning the 2022 election.
