Robert Moog


Born: May 23, 1934, New York City, U.S.
Died: August 21, 2005, Asheville, North Carolina
Occupation: Engineer, inventor
Known for: Moog synthesizer

Robert Arthur Moog (May 23, 1934 – August 21, 2005) was an American engineer and pioneer of electronic music, inventor of the Moog synthesizer, the first commercially successful analogue synthesizer with a keyboard.

Work

Moog built theremins as a teenager and founded a company selling theremin kits in 1954. In the mid-1960s, working with composer Herbert Deutsch, he developed voltage-controlled oscillator and amplifier modules that became the Moog modular synthesizer. Wendy Carlos's album Switched-On Bach (1968), recorded on a Moog, brought the instrument to wide public attention.

In 1970 Moog introduced the Minimoog, a compact, pre-wired portable synthesizer that became a standard instrument in rock, funk, jazz and electronic music. He lost control of his company in the 1970s, later regained the rights to the Moog name, and founded Moog Music in Asheville, North Carolina, releasing instruments such as the Minimoog Voyager. Moog received the Polar Music Prize in 2001 and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
