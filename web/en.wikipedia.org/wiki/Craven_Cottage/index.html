Craven Cottage

Craven Cottage
Location: Fulham, London, England
Home of: Fulham F.C.
Opened: 1896
Capacity: approx. 29,600 (after redevelopment)

Craven Cottage is a football stadium in Fulham, west London, and the home ground of Fulham Football Club since 1896. It stands on the banks of the River Thames, on the site of a royal hunting lodge built in 1780 by William Craven, 6th Baron Craven — the "cottage" from which the ground takes its name. The stadium is noted for its intimate atmosphere, its listed cottage pavilion and Johnny Haynes Stand, and its riverside setting.

History

Fulham moved to the site in 1896 after the original cottage had burned down, and the ground was developed under the guidance of the Scottish stadium architect Archibald Leitch, whose Johnny Haynes Stand (originally the Stevenage Road Stand) of 1905 is now a Grade II listed building. The Cottage pavilion in the corner of the ground survives as one of the most distinctive features of any English stadium. In 2002 Fulham left temporarily to share Loftus Road with Queens Park Rangers while redevelopment options were considered, returning to a modernised Craven Cottage in 2004.

Development

The Riverside Stand, backing onto the Thames, was demolished in 2019 and rebuilt as a larger stand incorporating hospitality and public riverside facilities; the project raised the stadium's capacity towards 29,600 and was completed in stages during the early 2020s. Craven Cottage has also hosted occasional matches for other teams and international fixtures, and it remains one of the oldest grounds in English professional football still in use by its original club.
