Star hopping

Star hopping is a technique used by amateur astronomers to locate celestial objects by moving a telescope in a series of short steps from a bright, easily found star or asterism to progressively fainter guide stars near the target.

Technique

The observer first centres a bright reference star in a low-power eyepiece or finder scope, then follows a chain of star patterns shown on a star atlas or chart until the desired object enters the field of view. The method is especially useful with Dobsonian telescopes lacking setting circles or GoTo drives, and for finding faint deep-sky objects under dark skies.

Equipment

A wide-field finder, a red torch to preserve dark adaptation, and detailed charts are the essential tools. Unit-power finders such as reflex sights simplify the first step of the hop.
