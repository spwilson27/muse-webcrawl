Sagebrush steppe

Region: Intermountain West, North America

Dominant plant: Big sagebrush (Artemisia tridentata)

Climate: Arid to semi-arid, cold winters

Sagebrush steppe is a type of shrub-steppe, a plant community characterised by the presence of shrubs and grasses, found in the arid intermountain region of western North America. It is dominated by big sagebrush (Artemisia tridentata) growing with bunchgrasses such as bluebunch wheatgrass and Idaho fescue, and historically covered a vast area across the Great Basin, Columbia Plateau, and Wyoming Basin — sometimes called the "sagebrush sea".

Ecology

The ecosystem supports sagebrush-obligate wildlife including the greater sage-grouse, pronghorn, pygmy rabbit, sage thrasher and Brewer's sparrow, as well as mule deer winter range. Sagebrush plants are adapted to cold winters, low precipitation and periodic fire, though the shrubs themselves are killed by fire and regenerate slowly from seed.

Threats and conservation

More than half of the historical sagebrush steppe has been lost or degraded through conversion to agriculture, energy development, altered fire regimes and the invasion of cheatgrass, which increases fire frequency beyond what sagebrush can tolerate. The decline of the greater sage-grouse prompted major conservation plans across eleven western states in the 2010s, one of the largest landscape conservation efforts in North America.
