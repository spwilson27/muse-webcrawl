Logistic function

Logistic function
Standard formf(x) = 1 / (1 + e−x)
Range(0, 1)

A logistic function or logistic curve is a common S-shaped curve (a sigmoid function) with the equation

f(x) = L / (1 + e−k(x − x0))

where L is the curve's maximum value (the carrying capacity), k is the logistic growth rate or steepness, and x0 is the x-value of the curve's midpoint. In the standard form (L = 1, k = 1, x0 = 0) the function is f(x) = 1/(1 + e−x), mapping any real number to a value between 0 and 1 — which makes it natural for modelling probabilities.

History

The function was introduced by the Belgian mathematician Pierre François Verhulst in papers of 1838 and 1845 on population growth, as a correction to Malthusian exponential growth: populations grow nearly exponentially at first, but growth slows as resources become scarce and the population approaches a ceiling. Verhulst coined the name "logistique" for the curve. The model was rediscovered in 1920 by Raymond Pearl and Lowell Reed, who promoted it widely in demography and biology.

Applications

  • Ecology — the logistic differential equation dP/dt = rP(1 − P/K) models population growth with carrying capacity K.
  • Statistics — logistic regression models the log-odds (logit) of a binary outcome as a linear function of predictors.
  • Machine learning — the sigmoid activation function in neural networks and the output function of classifiers.
  • Medicine and epidemiology — growth of tumours and the spread of epidemics often follow logistic-like curves.
  • Economics and technology — diffusion of innovations and market saturation follow S-curves; the Elo rating system and the Rasch model also build on the logistic form.
  • Chemistry — autocatalytic reactions produce logistic product growth.

Related concepts

Discretising the logistic differential equation gives the famous logistic map, a canonical example of how simple nonlinear dynamics generate chaos. The inverse of the logistic function is the logit function, log(p/(1 − p)), central to generalised linear models.
