Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest in the Solar System, after Jupiter. It is a gas giant composed predominantly of hydrogen and helium, most famously adorned with a spectacular ring system made mainly of ice particles, with smaller amounts of rocky debris and dust, ranging in size from grains of sand to mountains.

Rings and moons

Galileo first observed Saturn's rings in 1610 but, with a low-resolution telescope, thought them "handles" or companion bodies; Christiaan Huygens correctly described a thin flat ring in 1659 and discovered the largest moon, Titan. Titan is larger than Mercury and has a thick nitrogen atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane and ethane. Icy Enceladus sprays water plumes from a subsurface ocean into space, feeding Saturn's E ring — both moons are prime astrobiology targets discussed by NASA.

Exploration

Pioneer 11 flew by in 1979, then Voyagers 1 and 2 in 1980–81. The Cassini–Huygens mission — a NASA/ESA/ASI collaboration — orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017, dropped the Huygens probe onto Titan (the most distant landing ever made), and ended with a deliberate plunge into Saturn's atmosphere, the "Grand Finale".
