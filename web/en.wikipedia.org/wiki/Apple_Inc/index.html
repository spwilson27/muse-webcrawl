Apple Inc. is an American multinational technology company headquartered in Cupertino, California. It designs, develops, and sells consumer electronics, software, and services, and is the world's largest technology company by revenue and among the most valuable publicly traded companies. Apple is known for the iPhone smartphone, the Mac line of computers, the iPad tablet, the Apple Watch, AirPods, and the Apple Vision Pro, together with services such as the App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple Pay, and Apple TV+.

Apple Park, opened in 2017, is the company's corporate headquarters in Cupertino, California.

History

Apple Computer Company was founded on April 1, 1976, by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne to sell Wozniak's Apple I personal computer kit. The Apple II (1977) became one of the first mass-produced microcomputers. After visiting Xerox PARC, Apple developed graphical user interface machines — the unsuccessful Lisa (1983) and the landmark Macintosh (1984), introduced by the Ridley Scott-directed "1984" Super Bowl advertisement. Jobs left in 1985 after a boardroom conflict, founding NeXT; Apple struggled through the early 1990s against Microsoft Windows PCs and neared bankruptcy before acquiring NeXT in 1997, which brought Jobs back.

Under Jobs, Apple launched the iMac (1998), the iPod portable music player (2001), the iTunes Store (2003), the iPhone (2007), and the iPad (2010), redefining several industries. Jobs resigned for health reasons in August 2011, naming Tim Cook as CEO, and died that October. Under Cook, Apple introduced the Apple Watch (2015), AirPods (2016), its own Apple silicon chips for the Mac (beginning with the M-series in 2020), and the Vision Pro mixed-reality headset (2024). Apple became the first publicly traded U.S. company valued at $1 trillion in 2018, $2 trillion in 2020, and briefly $3 trillion in 2022.

Products and services

The iPhone accounts for roughly half of Apple's revenue. The Mac and iPad lines use Apple's own ARM-based silicon. Services — the fastest-growing segment — include the App Store, Apple Music, iCloud+, Apple TV+, Apple Arcade, Apple Fitness+, Apple News+, and Apple Pay. The company operates more than 500 retail stores worldwide and sells heavily through its online store and third-party carriers and resellers.

Criticism and controversies

Apple has faced sustained scrutiny over App Store commissions and rules, labour conditions in its supply chain (notably at Foxconn plants in China), tax practices (including a long-running European Commission state-aid case over Irish tax arrangements), environmental impact, product repairability, and its market power as a designated "gatekeeper" under the EU Digital Markets Act. Apple emphasises user privacy as a product differentiator and has committed to carbon neutrality across its supply chain by 2030.
