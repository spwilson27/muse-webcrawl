Kernville is a census-designated place (CDP) in Kern County, California, United States, in the Sierra Nevada foothills at the southern end of the Kern River Valley, near Lake Isabella. The population was 1,549 at the 2020 census.

The original town, founded in 1860 as a gold-mining camp called Whiskey Flat, lay where Lake Isabella now is. When Isabella Dam was built in the 1950s, the town was relocated uphill to its present site, and some buildings were preserved; the old cemetery and foundations can still emerge when the lake is low. The modern town is a centre for outdoor recreation, including whitewater rafting and kayaking on the Kern River, fishing, mountain biking, and the annual Whiskey Flat Days festival celebrating its frontier heritage.

Geography

Kernville sits at an elevation of about 2,660 feet (811 m) where the North Fork of the Kern River enters Lake Isabella. The surrounding area is within and adjacent to the Sequoia National Forest, with the Kern River designated in part as a Wild and Scenic River upstream of the town.
