An-Nas (Arabic: الناس, "Mankind") is the 114th and final chapter (surah) of the Quran. It is a short Meccan surah consisting of six verses (ayat). Together with the preceding chapter, Al-Falaq, it forms the pair known as al-Mu'awwidhatayn, "the two [chapters] of seeking refuge", because both begin with the Prophet being instructed to say "I seek refuge in the Lord…".

Text and meaning

The chapter opens: "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the King of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer — who whispers into the breasts of mankind — from among jinn and mankind." The "whisperer" (al-waswas) is traditionally understood as Satan or satanic temptation that withdraws when God is remembered.

Use

An-Nas and Al-Falaq are among the most frequently recited chapters in daily Muslim practice. They are recited for protection, including in the morning and evening remembrances (adhkar), and, according to hadith reports, the Prophet would recite them and blow over himself when unwell. They also conclude the Quran's canonical ordering, closing the book with an appeal for refuge in God.
