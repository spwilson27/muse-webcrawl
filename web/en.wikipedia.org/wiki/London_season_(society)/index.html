London season (society)

The London season, or London social season, was historically the part of the year, traditionally from after Easter to mid-August, when British high society was in residence in London for a round of balls, concerts, sporting events and charitable occasions. It coincided with the sitting of Parliament, when members of the aristocracy and landed gentry came to the capital.

History

The season reached its height in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The calendar included events that continue today: the Chelsea Flower Show, Royal Ascot, the Henley Royal Regatta, Wimbledon, the Royal Academy Summer Exhibition, and the cricket at Lord's. A central ritual was the presentation of debutantes at court, in which young women of aristocratic families were formally introduced to society — and to the marriage market. Court presentations were abolished in 1958.

Modern season

Today the term survives as a marketing and sponsorship umbrella for a series of summer cultural and sporting fixtures, coordinated informally since the 2000s. While no longer centred on debutantes, events such as Royal Ascot, Wimbledon and the Proms retain dress codes and social rituals inherited from the traditional season. Queen Charlotte's Ball and a small number of charity debutante balls continue the presentation tradition in modified form.
