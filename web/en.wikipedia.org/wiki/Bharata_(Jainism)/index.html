Bharata (Jainism)

Bharata, elder brother of Bahubali, depicted in Jain tradition

Bharata was the first chakravartin (universal emperor) of the present half-cycle of time (avasarpiṇī) in Jain cosmology. He was the eldest of the hundred sons of Rishabhanatha, the first Jain tirthankara, and is regarded in Jain tradition as the figure after whom India is named Bhāratavarṣa, or Bharat. His story is told in texts such as the Ādipurāṇa of Jinasena.

Life in Jain tradition

When Rishabhanatha renounced the world, Bharata succeeded him at Ayodhya and set out to conquer the six continents of the world with the aid of the divine chakra-ratna, the treasure-wheel of a chakravartin. All rulers submitted except his younger brother Bahubali, lord of Takshashila (in some accounts Podanapura). To avoid a devastating war, the brothers fought three contests — staring, water-splashing, and wrestling — in all of which Bahubali prevailed. Ashamed at having raised a weapon against his elder brother in anger, Bahubali renounced the kingdom and attained omniscience through meditation, standing motionless for a year while creepers grew over him.

Bharata ruled as emperor, instituted many social customs, and is credited in Jain accounts with ordering the construction of temples and with entering Bahubali's audience only after recognising his brother's spiritual supremacy. Eventually Bharata too renounced the world and attained liberation (moksha). A colossal monolithic statue of Bahubali at Shravanabelagola, and a large statue of Bharata facing it, commemorate the brothers; Bharata is also honoured as one of the figures of Jain universal history.
