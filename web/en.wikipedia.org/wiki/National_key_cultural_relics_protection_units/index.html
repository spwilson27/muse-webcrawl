National key cultural relics protection units

The national key cultural relics protection units (全国重点文物保护单位, Quánguó zhòngdiǎn wénwù bǎohù dānwèi) are the highest-level designation for officially protected historical and cultural sites in China. The list is designated by the State Council on the recommendation of the National Cultural Heritage Administration, under the Law on the Protection of Cultural Relics.

History and scale

The first batch of 180 sites was announced in 1961 and included the Forbidden City, the Mogao Grottoes, and Zhoukoudian. Further batches followed in 1982, 1988, 1996, 2001, 2006, 2013, and 2019, expanding the list to over 5,000 sites. Sites include ancient architecture, grotto temples, archaeological sites, revolutionary memorials, and examples of modern and industrial heritage.

Protection

Designation requires that a protection plaque be erected, a defined protection zone and construction-control zone established, and archives maintained. Alteration or demolition of a listed site requires approval at the highest level; provincial- and county-level lists form lower tiers of the same system. The corresponding intangible and natural counterparts include China's UNESCO World Heritage Sites, many of which are also on this list.
