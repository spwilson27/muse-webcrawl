The Tempest is a play by William Shakespeare, probably written in 1610–1611 and thought to be the last play Shakespeare wrote alone. It is set on a remote island, where the magician Prospero, the exiled Duke of Milan, conjures a storm to wreck the ship carrying his usurping brother Antonio and Alonso, King of Naples.

Plot

Prospero, deposed twelve years earlier and set adrift with his infant daughter Miranda, rules the island with the aid of the spirit Ariel and the enslaved Caliban, the island's native inhabitant. When his enemies sail near, Prospero raises the tempest of the title and scatters them across the island. Alonso's son Ferdinand, presumed drowned, meets and falls in love with Miranda. Plots by Antonio and Sebastian to kill Alonso, and by Caliban, Stephano, and Trinculo to kill Prospero, are thwarted by Ariel. In the final act Prospero forgives his enemies, renounces his magic, frees Ariel, and prepares to return to Milan: "I'll break my staff... I'll drown my book." His epilogue asks the audience to set him free with their applause.

Interpretation

The play is often read as Shakespeare's farewell to the stage, with Prospero's renunciation of magic as a metaphor for the playwright's retirement. In the 20th century it also became a central text of postcolonial criticism, with readings focusing on Prospero's domination of Caliban and Ariel as an allegory of European colonization of the New World.
