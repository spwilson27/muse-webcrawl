Jota (music)

The jota is a traditional music and dance genre of Spain, most closely associated with Aragon but performed, with regional variants, across much of the country, including Navarre, La Rioja, Valencia, Castile and the Canary Islands. It is characterised by lively triple meter, castanets, and sung verses (coplas) that often improvise on everyday themes.

Characteristics

The jota is typically performed by a rondalla of guitars, bandurrias, laúds and sometimes wind instruments, accompanying solo or duet singers. Dancers wear regional costume and mark the rhythm with castanets while executing animated steps, raised arms and small jumps. Sung coplas are quatrains of octosyllabic verse, frequently witty, satirical or amorous.

Regional variants and influence

The Aragonese jota is the best known and is a symbol of regional identity; competitions and festivals are held throughout Aragon, and famous singers such as José Oto became emblematic performers. The genre influenced classical composers including Mikhail Glinka, Franz Liszt, Camille Saint-Saëns, Georges Bizet and Isaac Albéniz, and appears in zarzuelas and ballets.
