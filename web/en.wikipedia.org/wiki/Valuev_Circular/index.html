Valuev Circular

The Valuev Circular (Russian: Валуевский циркуляр) was a decree issued on 18 July 1863 by Pyotr Valuev, Minister of Internal Affairs of the Russian Empire, which severely restricted the publication of religious, educational and popular literature in the Ukrainian language. Issued in the aftermath of the Polish January Uprising, it embodied the imperial doctrine that a separate Ukrainian ("Little Russian") language "never has existed, does not exist and cannot exist".

Contents and rationale

The circular instructed censors to permit Ukrainian-language publication only of works of belles-lettres, while banning religious texts, textbooks and books intended for popular education. The government justified the measure by claiming that peasants were being drawn into separatism through Ukrainian publications instigated by Polish intrigue. In practice it crippled the growing Ukrainian cultural revival, halting works such as the Ukrainian translation of the Gospels and closing down Sunday schools that taught in Ukrainian.

Aftermath

The Circular was a confidential instruction rather than a law, yet it remained in force for decades and was followed by the still harsher Ems Ukaz of 1876, which banned Ukrainian publications and performances almost entirely. Ukrainian publishing consequently shifted westward to Austrian-ruled Galicia, centred on Lviv. The restrictions were effectively lifted only after the 1905 revolution, when the Imperial Academy of Sciences recognised Ukrainian as a distinct language. The Valuev Circular remains a central symbol in Ukrainian historiography of imperial Russification policy.
