Vladimir Levenshtein

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Vladimir Iosifovich Levenshtein (1935–2017).

Vladimir Iosifovich Levenshtein (20 May 1935 – 6 September 2017) was a Russian and Soviet scientist who did research in information theory, error-correcting codes and combinatorial design. Among other contributions, he is known for the Levenshtein distance and the Levenshtein algorithm, which he developed in 1965.

Biography

Levenshtein graduated from the Department of Mathematics and Mechanics of Moscow State University in 1958 and worked at the Keldysh Institute of Applied Mathematics in Moscow for most of his career. He received the IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal in 2006, "for contributions to the theory of error-correcting codes and information theory, including the Levenshtein distance".

Levenshtein distance

The Levenshtein distance, also called edit distance, is a string metric for measuring the difference between two sequences. It is defined as the minimum number of single-character edits — insertions, deletions or substitutions — required to change one string into the other. It is widely used in spell checking, approximate string matching, computational linguistics and bioinformatics.

Levenshtein also obtained influential bounds on the size of error-correcting codes and designs, known as the Levenshtein bounds, and worked on insertion- and deletion-correcting codes.
