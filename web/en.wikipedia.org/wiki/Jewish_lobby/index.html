Jewish lobby

The term "Jewish lobby" is used to describe organised advocacy by Jewish individuals and organisations on issues of concern to Jewish communities, most often in the United States and particularly in relation to US support for Israel. The phrase is controversial: supporters of the term describe ordinary interest-group politics, while critics argue it can echo antisemitic tropes about Jewish power and dual loyalty when used loosely.

Usage and debate

Advocacy organisations such as AIPAC (the American Israel Public Affairs Committee) describe their own work as pro-Israel advocacy and reject the label "Jewish lobby", noting that their supporters include many non-Jews and that Jewish Americans hold diverse views on Israel. The related term "Israel lobby" was popularised by John Mearsheimer and Stephen Walt's 2007 book The Israel Lobby and U.S. Foreign Policy, which argued that a loose coalition of organisations and individuals significantly shapes US Middle East policy; the book drew both support and sharp criticism. Historians and Jewish communal bodies debate whether either term fairly describes a pluralistic landscape that also includes dovish groups such as J Street.

References

Mearsheimer and Walt, The Israel Lobby and U.S. Foreign Policy (2007), and published responses and reviews.
