History of McDonald's

Founded15 April 1955 (San Bernardino / Des Plaines)
FoundersRichard and Maurice McDonald; Ray Kroc
HeadquartersChicago, Illinois
Key systemSpeedee Service System (1948)

The history of McDonald's traces the growth of a single California drive-in into the world's largest fast-food restaurant chain, built on standardisation, franchising and the "Speedee Service System".

The McDonald brothers

Richard and Maurice McDonald opened their first restaurant in San Bernardino, California, in 1940. In 1948 they relaunched it with the Speedee Service System: a limited menu of hamburgers, fries and shakes, prepared assembly-line style with no carhops or indoor seating, cutting prices and waiting times.

Ray Kroc and expansion

Multimixer salesman Ray Kroc visited in 1954, became the brothers' franchising agent, and opened his own restaurant in Des Plaines, Illinois, on 15 April 1955. In 1961 Kroc bought out the brothers for $2.7 million and built McDonald's into a franchise empire, aided by real-estate deals arranged by Harry Sonneborn.

Global growth

The Big Mac (1968), Egg McMuffin (1972) and Happy Meal (1979) broadened the menu. The first international restaurants opened in Canada and Costa Rica in the late 1960s; by the 1990s McDonald's operated in more than 100 countries, becoming a symbol of globalisation — and a frequent target of criticism over nutrition, labour and the environment.
