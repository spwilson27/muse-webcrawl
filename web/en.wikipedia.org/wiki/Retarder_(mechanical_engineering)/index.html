Retarder (mechanical engineering)

A retarder is a device used to augment or replace some of the functions of primary friction-based braking systems, usually on heavy vehicles. Retarders serve to slow vehicles, or maintain a steady speed while travelling down gradients, and help prevent the main brakes from overheating ("brake fade") and wearing out. Unlike the service brakes, most retarders cannot bring a vehicle to a complete stop.

Types

Hydraulic (fluid) retarders, such as those made by Voith and integrated into automatic gearboxes, use viscous drag on oil circulated between rotor and stator vanes, converting kinetic energy into heat dissipated by the vehicle cooling system. Electromagnetic retarders (for example those of Telma) use eddy currents induced in a rotor by stator coils to produce a contactless braking torque. Engine brakes — exhaust brakes and compression-release brakes (the "Jake Brake") — make the engine itself absorb energy. Electric traction motors in hybrid and electric vehicles can act as retarders through regenerative braking, returning energy to the battery.

Use

Retarders are standard on coaches, heavy trucks and many diesel rail vehicles, controlled by the driver through a stalk or pedal with several intensity levels, and increasingly integrated with cruise control and automatic transmissions.
