Jōmon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan (北海道・北東北の縄文遺跡群) is a serial UNESCO World Heritage Site consisting of seventeen Jōmon-period archaeological sites in Hokkaidō and northern Tōhoku (Aomori, Iwate, and Akita Prefectures). The sites were inscribed in July 2021 as testimony to the sedentary hunter-fisher-gatherer society that developed in northeast Asia from around 13,000 BCE and endured for some ten thousand years.

Description

The component sites span the full arc of the Jōmon period, from the emergence of pottery and settled life to the flowering and decline of the culture. They include settlements, shell middens, stone circles, burial grounds, and ritual sites. Notable components include the Sannai-Maruyama Site in Aomori, one of the largest Jōmon settlements; the Ōdai Yamamoto I Site, where some of Japan's oldest pottery fragments were found; the Kakinoshima and Kitakogane shell middens in Hakodate; the Ōyu Stone Circles in Akita; and the Kiusu Earthwork Burial Circles in Hokkaidō.

Significance

UNESCO inscribed the property under criteria (iii) and (v), recognising it as outstanding evidence of a pre-agricultural yet sedentary society that developed complex spiritual culture — expressed in lacquerware, clay figurines (dogū), and monumental stone circles — without transition to farming. The sites demonstrate how the Jōmon people adapted to environmental changes, including post-glacial warming and subsequent cooling, over a span of more than ten millennia.

Component sites

The seventeen components are distributed across four prefectures: six in Hokkaidō (including Ōfune, Kakinoshima, Kitakogane, Irie, Takasago, and Kiusu), eight in Aomori Prefecture (including Sannai-Maruyama, Ōdai Yamamoto I, Futatsumori, and Korekawa), and the remainder in Iwate (Goshono) and Akita (Ōyu Stone Circles and Isedōtai). Many are protected as Special Historic Sites of Japan and are interpreted through local museums.
