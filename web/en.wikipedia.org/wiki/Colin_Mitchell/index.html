Lieutenant Colonel Colin Campbell Mitchell (17 November 1925 – 13 January 1996), universally known as "Mad Mitch", was a British Army officer famous for leading the reoccupation of the Crater district of Aden in July 1967 during the Aden Emergency — the last occasion on which British troops fought a set-piece action of that kind in a colonial withdrawal. His aggressive methods made him a hero to much of the British press and public, while drawing official criticism.

Aden, 1967

Mitchell commanded the 1st Battalion, Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders. After Arab police mutinied and ambushed a British patrol in Crater on 20 June 1967, killing members of the Argylls and other regiments, the district was abandoned to insurgents. On 3 July Mitchell led his battalion back into Crater in a meticulously planned night operation, heralded by regimental pipers, and retook the district without loss of life among his own men. He then governed Crater firmly until the final British withdrawal from Aden in November 1967.

Later life

Passed over for further promotion, Mitchell left the Army in 1968. He was elected Conservative Member of Parliament for West Aberdeenshire in 1970 and served until 1974. Thereafter he worked in land conservation in Scotland, founding the Halo Trust's predecessor efforts in mine clearance charity work in later years being carried on by others; he died in 1996.
