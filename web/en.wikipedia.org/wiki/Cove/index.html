Cove

A cove is a small type of bay or coastal inlet. Coves usually have narrow, restricted entrances and are often circular or oval in shape. They form where bands of rock of differing resistance run parallel to the coast: waves erode a weak band (such as clay) behind a resistant band (such as limestone or chalk), producing a sheltered, enclosed bay.

Formation

On a concordant coastline, where alternating hard and soft rock layers lie parallel to the shore, the sea can breach the hard outer band at a weak point and then erode the softer rock behind it more rapidly, hollowing out a rounded cove. Lulworth Cove in Dorset, England, formed in this way through a band of Portland limestone, with softer clays and sands eroded behind it; nearby Stair Hole shows an earlier stage of the process.

Usage of the term

In some regions, particularly around Sydney, Australia, "cove" is used loosely for small bays and inlets, such as Watsons Bay's coves on Sydney Harbour. Coves, being sheltered, were historically favoured anchorages and were also associated with smuggling.
