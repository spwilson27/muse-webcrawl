Flashforward

A flashforward (also spelled flash-forward; in narratology, prolepsis) is a scene that temporarily takes the narrative forward in time from the current point of the story in literature, film, television, video games and other media. Flashforwards are often used to represent events expected, projected, or imagined to occur in the future.

Contrast with flashback

The flashforward is the counterpart of the flashback, which shifts the narrative backward in time. Because audiences are generally less accustomed to jumps forward, flashforwards are used less frequently than flashbacks and are often framed as dreams, prophecies, visions or plans — devices that allow the story to show a possible future without committing to it.

Examples

Charles Dickens used proleptic narration in A Christmas Carol (1843), in which the Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come shows Ebenezer Scrooge scenes of a future that will come to pass unless he changes his ways. Television series such as Lost introduced celebrated "flash-forwards" in their later seasons, and the 2009 series FlashForward (based on Robert J. Sawyer's 1999 novel) built its premise around humanity glimpsing its collective future during a global blackout.

Function

Flashforwards can create irony when the audience knows more than the characters, build suspense about how a shown outcome will come about, or establish a frame story, as when an older narrator shows the endpoint of a journey the narrative then works toward.
