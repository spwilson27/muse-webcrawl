Battle of Kursk

The Battle of Kursk (5 July – 23 August 1943) was a major battle of the Eastern Front of World War II. Germany's Operation Citadel sought to pinch off the Kursk salient; the Soviet Union, forewarned, built deep defences, stopped the offensive, and counterattacked in Operations Kutuzov and Rumyantsev. It was one of the largest armoured battles in history and gave the strategic initiative permanently to the Red Army.

Background

After Stalingrad, spring fighting left a Soviet-held bulge around Kursk. Hitler delayed Citadel to await new Panther and Tiger tanks and Ferdinand tank destroyers, giving the Soviets months to lay minefields, anti-tank strongpoints and successive defensive belts, backed by the Steppe Front reserve.

Battle

The German northern pincer under Model stalled within days. In the south, II SS Panzer Corps advanced toward Prokhorovka, where on 12 July a huge armoured clash occurred with the 5th Guards Tank Army. News of the Allied landing in Sicily and Soviet offensives at Orel led Hitler to halt Citadel. Soviet counteroffensives liberated Orel, Belgorod and, on 23 August, Kharkov.

Significance

Germany lost irreplaceable armour and never regained the ability to mount a major Eastern offensive. Soviet losses were heavier in men and tanks, but replaceable. Kursk, with Stalingrad, is regarded as a turning point of the war.
