San Antonio Missions National Historical Park


Location: San Antonio, Texas, United States
Established: April 1, 1983
Governing body: National Park Service
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 2015

San Antonio Missions National Historical Park is a National Historical Park and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site preserving four of the five Spanish frontier missions in San Antonio, Texas, United States. These outposts were established by Catholic religious orders to spread Christianity among the local natives in the early eighteenth century. The park was established on April 1, 1983, and covers roughly 819 acres (331 ha).

The missions

The four missions in the park, running from north to south along the San Antonio River, are:

  • Mission Concepción (Nuestra Señora de la Purísima Concepción de Acuña), founded 1716 in East Texas and moved to San Antonio in 1731; its stone church, dedicated in 1755, is one of the oldest unrestored stone churches in the United States.
  • Mission San José (San José y San Miguel de Aguayo), founded in 1720, famed for its ornate church facade and the celebrated "Rose Window."
  • Mission San Juan (San Juan Capistrano), founded in 1716 and moved in 1731, with productive farmlands irrigated by acequias.
  • Mission Espada (San Francisco de la Espada), founded in 1690, the oldest of the Texas missions, noted for its well-preserved aqueduct.

World Heritage status

The fifth mission, Mission San Antonio de Valero (the Alamo), is not part of the park but was included, along with the park's four missions, in the UNESCO World Heritage listing of 2015 — the first World Heritage Site in Texas. The missions remain active Catholic parishes, administered in partnership with the Archdiocese of San Antonio.
