Dan Bricklin

Daniel Bricklin (born July 16, 1951) is an American businessman and engineer who is the co-creator, with Bob Frankston, of the VisiCalc spreadsheet program. He also founded Software Garden, Inc., of which he is currently president, and Trellix. Bricklin is often referred to as "the father of the spreadsheet".

Early life and education

Bricklin was born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, and attended Akiba Hebrew Academy. He received a B.S. in electrical engineering and computer science from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1973, and an MBA from Harvard Business School in 1979.

VisiCalc

While at Harvard Business School, Bricklin conceived the idea of an interactive electronic spreadsheet. In 1979 he and Bob Frankston created VisiCalc for the Apple II, the first killer application for personal computers and a key driver of their adoption in business. VisiCalc was marketed through their company Software Arts. Bricklin won the Grace Murray Hopper Award in 1981 for his work on VisiCalc.

Later work

After Software Arts, Bricklin founded Software Garden in 1985, producing programs such as Dan Bricklin's Demo Program and PageGarden. He later co-founded Trellix, a web site builder company acquired by Interland (now Web.com) in 2003. Bricklin has continued to develop software, including Note Taker HD for the iPad, and has been an advocate for open data formats and tablet computing. He is the author of Bricklin on Technology (2009).
