Brit Award for British Female Solo Artist

The Brit Award for British Female Solo Artist was an award given by the British Phonographic Industry (BPI), the organisation representing record companies and artists in the United Kingdom. It was presented annually at the Brit Awards ceremony from 1977 until 2021, after which the gendered solo-artist categories were replaced by the gender-neutral Artist of the Year award.

History

Shirley Bassey won the inaugural award at the first Brit Awards in 1977. Annie Lennox holds the record with six wins, including four as a solo artist during the 1990s. Other multiple winners include Dua Lipa (the final recipient of the category in its original form, in 2018 and 2021), Adele, Emeli Sandé, Lisa Stansfield, Duffy, and Bonnie Tyler. Winners such as Amy Winehouse (2007) and Adele (2012, 2016) reflected defining moments in British pop across the decades.

Abolition of gendered categories

In November 2021 the BPI announced that the separate male and female solo categories would be removed for the 2022 ceremony and replaced by a single British Artist of the Year award, alongside a new International Artist of the Year award, a change intended to recognize artists solely on their music and work rather than gender.
