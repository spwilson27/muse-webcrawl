Bank of Greece

The Bank of Greece (Greek: Τράπεζα της Ελλάδος) is the central bank of Greece. Founded in 1928 under a League of Nations-backed reform, it began operations on 14 May 1928 and took over note issuance from the National Bank of Greece. Its head office is on Panepistimiou Street in Athens.

Functions

Since Greece adopted the euro on 1 January 2001, the Bank of Greece has formed part of the Eurosystem, implementing the monetary policy of the European Central Bank, managing foreign-exchange and gold reserves, supervising credit institutions and insurers, and operating payment and settlement systems. It also compiles statistics and acts as treasurer and fiscal agent of the Greek state. The governor sits on the Governing Council of the ECB; notable governors include Xenophon Zolotas, Lucas Papademos — later prime minister — and Yannis Stournaras, in office since 2014.

Greek debt crisis

During the Greek government-debt crisis the Bank of Greece played a central role in administering Emergency Liquidity Assistance (ELA) to Greek banks, whose cap was set by the ECB Governing Council. The imposition of capital controls in June 2015, following the freezing of ELA, underscored the bank's position at the junction of the Greek financial system and the Eurosystem. Before the euro, the bank was responsible for the drachma, Greece's currency for much of its modern history.
