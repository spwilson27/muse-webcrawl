Thames Estuary
Location: South-east England, into the North Sea
Begins: Teddington / Thames Tideway
Features: Mudflats, saltmarsh, sea forts, London Gateway port

The Thames Estuary is the tidal estuary of the River Thames in south-east England, where the river meets the North Sea. It stretches from London eastwards past Southend and Sheerness, characterised by broad mudflats, sandbanks and shifting shipping channels.

Geography and hazards

Its treacherous shoals — the reason for extensive pilotage and the wartime Maunsell sea forts — guard approaches to the Port of London, once the world's largest port.

Ecology and development

Intertidal habitats support internationally important wintering birds. The Thames Gateway regeneration, London Gateway deep-sea port, and the wreck of the SS Richard Montgomery (loaded with wartime explosives) are notable features.
