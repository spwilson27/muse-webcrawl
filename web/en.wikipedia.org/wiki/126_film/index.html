126 film is a cartridge-based film format for still photography, introduced by Kodak in 1963 for its Instamatic cameras. The plastic cartridge, containing 35 mm-wide film on paper backing, could be loaded in daylight without threading, making photography accessible to casual users.

Format

Each frame is nominally 28 × 28 mm, producing square prints. A single perforation per frame let the camera advance and stop the film without a frame counter mechanism tracking sprockets. Cartridges held 12, 20 or 24 exposures.

History

The Instamatic line sold in the hundreds of millions, and 126 became one of the most popular amateur formats of the 1960s and 1970s. It was gradually displaced by the smaller 110 format (introduced in 1972), disc film, and finally digital cameras. Kodak ended production in 1999; other manufacturers continued until about 2008, and specialist firms have since offered hand-loaded cartridges.
