The Dictionnaire de l'Académie française is the official dictionary of the French language published by the Académie française, the body charged since 1635 with regulating French usage. It is a prescriptive dictionary, recording the usage the Academy considers correct.

History

The first edition appeared in 1694 after decades of work by the Academy's members, the "Immortals". Subsequent editions followed in 1718, 1740, 1762, 1798, 1835, 1878 and 1932–1935. Work on the ninth edition began in the 1980s and proceeded volume by volume into the twenty-first century.

Content

The dictionary defines words, gives examples of usage, and rules on disputed points of spelling and grammar. Although it carries great prestige, it has often been criticised for conservatism and for the slow pace of its revisions; descriptive dictionaries such as the Petit Robert and Larousse are more widely used day to day.
