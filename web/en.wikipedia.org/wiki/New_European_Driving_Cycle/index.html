The New European Driving Cycle (NEDC) is a driving cycle designed to assess the emission levels of car engines and fuel economy in passenger cars. It was used for type approval of vehicles in the European Union from the 1990s until it was replaced by the Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicles Test Procedure (WLTP).

Design

The NEDC consists of four repeated urban driving cycles (ECE-15) followed by one extra-urban driving cycle (EUDC). The test is performed on a chassis dynamometer with prescribed speed traces, gear changes and ambient conditions.

Its average speed is about 34 km/h with a maximum of 120 km/h in the extra-urban segment.

Criticism

The NEDC was widely criticised for producing fuel consumption and CO₂ figures far lower than those achieved in real driving, because of its gentle accelerations, long idling periods and numerous test flexibilities exploited by manufacturers.

Concerns about the gap between laboratory and real-world results, amplified by the Dieselgate scandal of 2015, led the EU to adopt the more demanding WLTP and supplementary Real Driving Emissions (RDE) tests using portable emissions measurement systems.
