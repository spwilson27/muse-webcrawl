Lidocaine, also known as lignocaine, is a local anesthetic of the amino amide type and a class Ib antiarrhythmic drug. It is used to numb tissue in a specific area (infiltration anesthesia), to block nerves, and intravenously in the treatment of ventricular arrhythmias. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Lidocaine works by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in nerve cell membranes, preventing the initiation and conduction of action potentials. It has a rapid onset of action (within minutes when injected) and a medium duration of effect, typically 30 minutes to three hours depending on the site and whether a vasoconstrictor such as epinephrine is added.

Medical uses

Lidocaine is widely used in dentistry, minor surgery, and emergency medicine. Topical preparations are used for painful mouth ulcers, sore throat, and procedures such as endoscopy. Transdermal patches are used for postherpetic neuralgia. Intravenous lidocaine is used for ventricular tachyarrhythmias, particularly after myocardial infarction, although other agents are now often preferred. Solutions with epinephrine prolong the effect and reduce bleeding but must not be used in end-arterial areas such as fingers in some traditional teaching, a rule that has been increasingly questioned for healthy patients.

History

Lidocaine was synthesized in 1943 by the Swedish chemist Nils Löfgren (who named it xylocaine) and his colleague Bengt Lundqvist. It was the first amino amide-type local anesthetic to be marketed, in 1949, and it proved safer and less allergenic than the earlier ester-type agents such as procaine.

Adverse effects

Adverse effects are uncommon at normal doses but can occur with overdose or accidental intravascular injection, causing central nervous system effects (dizziness, tinnitus, seizures) and cardiovascular depression — a condition known as local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST), treated with intravenous lipid emulsion. Lidocaine should be used cautiously in people with certain heart block or liver disease, since it is metabolized in the liver by cytochrome P450 enzymes.
