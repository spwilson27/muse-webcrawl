Ministry of culture

A ministry of culture is a type of government ministry responsible for cultural policy: the arts, heritage, museums, libraries, archives, language policy, and often media, sport, tourism, or religious affairs. Such ministries exist in many countries, though their exact portfolios vary widely, and in some states cultural responsibilities are combined with education or handled at regional level.

Functions

Typical functions include funding national museums, theatres, orchestras, and film industries; protecting monuments and archaeological sites; administering copyright and cultural property law; promoting the national language and literature; and representing the country in international cultural cooperation, for example through UNESCO.

Examples

France's Ministry of Culture, founded in 1959 with André Malraux as its first minister, is often considered the archetype, championing state support for the arts and the concept of the maisons de la culture. Other examples include India's Ministry of Culture, China's Ministry of Culture and Tourism, Brazil's Ministry of Culture, and the United Kingdom's Department for Culture, Media and Sport. The United States, by contrast, has no ministry of culture; federal support flows mainly through independent agencies such as the National Endowment for the Arts.
