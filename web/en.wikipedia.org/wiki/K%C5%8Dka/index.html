Kōka

Country: Japan
Prefecture: Shiga
Area: 481.62 km²
Population: approximately 88,000

Kōka (甲賀市, Kōka-shi) is a city in Shiga Prefecture, Japan. It is best known historically as the home of the Kōka ninja, one of the two great schools of ninjutsu alongside Iga. The city was formed on 1 October 2004 from the merger of the towns of Kōka, Konan, Minakuchi, Shigaraki and Tsuchiyama in Kōka District.

History

During the Sengoku period, the region's autonomous warrior families — the Kōka-ikki — served as mercenaries and intelligence agents for various warlords, and Kōka became famous for its ninja (shinobi) clans. The region was also an important post station area, with Tsuchiyama and Minakuchi serving travellers on the Tōkaidō road between Kyoto and Edo.

Minakuchi Castle was built in 1634 as a way station for the shōgun Tokugawa Iemitsu. Shigaraki, in the south of the city, is one of the Six Ancient Kilns of Japan and remains a major centre of pottery production, famous for its tanuki (raccoon dog) statues.

Geography

Kōka lies in the southern part of Shiga Prefecture, bordering Mie Prefecture to the east and Kyoto Prefecture to the west. The Yasu River flows through the northern part of the city. Much of the terrain is mountainous and forested, and Kōka is the second-largest municipality in the prefecture by area.

Attractions

  • Kōka Ninja Village (Kōka no Sato Ninjutsumura) — a museum village with a ninja house full of traps and demonstrations of ninjutsu.
  • Shigaraki Ceramic Cultural Park — exhibitions and workshops celebrating Shigaraki ware.
  • Minakuchi Castle ruins and the Ōmi Railway lines serving the city.
