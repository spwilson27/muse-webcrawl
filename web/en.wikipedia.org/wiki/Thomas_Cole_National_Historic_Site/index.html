Cedar Grove, home of painter Thomas Cole from 1836 to 1848

The Thomas Cole National Historic Site, also known as Cedar Grove, is a National Historic Site in Catskill, New York, preserving the home and studios of Thomas Cole (1801–1848), the founder of the Hudson River School of American landscape painting. Cole lived and worked on the property — a Federal-style main house built about 1812 overlooking the Hudson River and the Catskill Mountains — from 1836 until his death.

Preservation

After decades in private hands, the house was rescued from threatened demolition, designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965, and opened to the public; Congress authorised it as a National Historic Site in 1999, affiliated with the National Park Service. The restored Old Studio, where Cole painted works including The Voyage of Life, and the reconstructed New Studio (built on the footprint of Cole's 1846 Italianate studio, demolished in the 1970s) host exhibitions and the artist's paintings, brushes, and papers.
