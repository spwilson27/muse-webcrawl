The Royal Ballet

The Royal Ballet is the largest of the United Kingdom's five major ballet companies and is based at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden, London. Founded in 1931 by Ninette de Valois as the Vic-Wells Ballet, it received its Royal Charter in 1956 and is internationally regarded as one of the world's leading classical ballet companies.

History

De Valois's company grew at Sadler's Wells Theatre and, after the Second World War, became resident at Covent Garden, reopening the house in 1946 with The Sleeping Beauty. Frederick Ashton was its founding choreographer, and Margot Fonteyn its defining prima ballerina; her partnership with Rudolf Nureyev from 1962 brought worldwide fame. Kenneth MacMillan later served as artistic director and created ballets such as Romeo and Juliet, Manon and Mayerling.

Repertoire and school

The repertoire combines 19th-century classics by Marius Petipa with works by Ashton and MacMillan and new commissions. Its associate school, The Royal Ballet School, trains dancers at White Lodge, Richmond Park, and at Covent Garden; a sister company, Birmingham Royal Ballet, descends from the touring arm.
