Regional Transportation District

Headquarters: Denver, Colorado
Founded: 1969
Services: Bus, light rail, commuter rail
Area: Denver metropolitan area

The Regional Transportation District, commonly called RTD, is the public transit authority for the Denver metropolitan area in Colorado, United States. It operates bus, light rail and commuter rail services across an eight-county district of about 6,160 km² (2,380 square miles).

History

RTD was created by the Colorado legislature in 1969 and began operating bus services in the 1970s after taking over private and municipal operators. Light rail service began in 1994 with the opening of the Central Corridor line in downtown Denver.

FasTracks

In 2004 voters approved FasTracks, a multibillion-dollar expansion programme funded by a sales tax increase. It built new light rail lines, the commuter rail lines to Denver International Airport (the A Line, opened 2016) and Arvada/Wheat Ridge (the G Line), and the Flatiron Flyer bus rapid transit service along US 36. Cost overruns and delays left some planned lines, notably to Boulder and Longmont, unbuilt for many years.

Operations

RTD runs more than 170 bus routes, a light rail network centred on downtown Denver, and diesel and electric commuter rail. Its principal hub is Denver Union Station, reopened in 2014 after redevelopment as a multimodal terminal.
