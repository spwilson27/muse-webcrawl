Gloria E. Anzaldúa

Gloria Evangelina Anzaldúa (September 26, 1942 – May 15, 2004) was an American scholar of Chicana feminism, cultural theory, and queer theory, based in the Rio Grande Valley of South Texas and later California.

Work

Her best-known book, Borderlands/La Frontera: The New Mestiza (1987), interweaves memoir, poetry, history, and theory across English and Spanish to explore the US–Mexico border as physical, cultural, sexual, and psychological boundary, and develops her concept of mestiza consciousness. She also co-edited the landmark anthology This Bridge Called My Back: Writings by Radical Women of Color (1981) with Cherríe Moraga, and edited Making Face, Making Soul/Haciendo Caras (1990).

Legacy

Anzaldúa's concepts, including nepantla and the borderlands, have profoundly influenced feminist, queer, and decolonial scholarship. She died in 2004 of complications from diabetes.
