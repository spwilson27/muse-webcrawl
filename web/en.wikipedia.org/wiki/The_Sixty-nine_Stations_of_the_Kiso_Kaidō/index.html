The Sixty-nine Stations of the Kiso Kaidō (Japanese: 木曽街道六十九次, Kisokaidō Rokujūkyū-tsugi) is a series of ukiyo-e woodblock prints depicting the post stations of the Nakasendō — also called the Kisokaidō — the inland highway that connected Edo (modern Tokyo) with Kyoto during Japan's Edo period. The series was created between about 1834 and 1842.

The series

The Nakasendō had 69 post stations (shukuba) between its endpoints at Nihonbashi in Edo and Sanjō Ōhashi in Kyoto. The print series comprises 71 designs in total, including the starting and ending points. Keisai Eisen produced the first 23 station prints (plus the Nihonbashi starting print); Utagawa Hiroshige, whose celebrated Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō had made highway series fashionable, completed the remaining designs. The prints show travellers, porters, and local people in landscapes ranging from mountain passes and river crossings to the streets of the post towns, rendered in all seasons and weathers.

Context

The series was published by Takenouchi Magohachi (Hoeidō) and later by Iseya Rihei. Though less famous than Hiroshige's Tōkaidō series, the Kiso Kaidō prints are admired for their atmospheric depictions of Japan's mountainous interior. The two series together are the subject of a number of major museum collections and exhibitions.
