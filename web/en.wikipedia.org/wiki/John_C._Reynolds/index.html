John Charles Reynolds (1 June 1935 – 28 April 2013) was an American computer scientist whose work on the design and semantics of programming languages was among the most influential of the twentieth century. He is best known for independently inventing the polymorphic lambda calculus (System F), for his theory of definitional interpreters, and for originating separation logic, a widely used method for reasoning about programs that manipulate pointers.

Education and career

Reynolds earned his bachelor's degree from Purdue University and his Ph.D. in theoretical physics from Harvard University in 1961. After work at Argonne National Laboratory, he joined Syracuse University in 1970 and then Carnegie Mellon University in 1986, where he remained a professor of computer science until his death. He was a visiting researcher at institutions including Queen Mary College London, the University of Edinburgh, Oxford, and Microsoft Research Cambridge.

Research contributions

Polymorphism and System F

In 1974 Reynolds introduced the polymorphic lambda calculus in his paper "Towards a Theory of Type Structure," independently of Jean-Yves Girard, who had developed the same system (System F) in 1972. The calculus underlies generic programming in languages such as ML, Haskell and Java generics. His subsequent work on parametricity — the idea that polymorphic programs behave uniformly at all types — became a cornerstone of type theory.

Definitional interpreters and continuations

Reynolds's 1972 paper "Definitional Interpreters for Higher-Order Programming Languages" clarified the relationship between interpreters, compilers and continuations, introducing techniques including defunctionalization and the systematic use of continuation-passing style.

Separation logic

Around 2000 Reynolds developed separation logic, an extension of Hoare logic with a "separating conjunction" that supports local reasoning about heap-manipulating programs. Developed alongside work by Peter O'Hearn and others, it transformed program verification and underlies tools used in industry.

Honours

Reynolds was a Fellow of the ACM and received the ACM SIGPLAN Programming Language Achievement Award in 2003. He was a highly regarded teacher; his book The Craft of Programming (1981) and his lectures influenced generations of programming-language researchers.
