The Massachusetts State House, located in the Beacon Hill neighbourhood of Boston, is the state capitol and seat of government of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts. Designed by Charles Bulfinch and completed in 1798, it is famed for its gilded dome.

Architecture

Bulfinch's Federal-style building was constructed on land once owned by John Hancock. Its dome, originally of wooden shingles, was later covered in copper and gilded with 23-karat gold leaf in 1874. The grounds contain statues of figures including Daniel Webster, Horace Mann, and John F. Kennedy.

Government

The building houses the Massachusetts General Court (the state legislature) and the offices of the governor. Its chambers include the Senate chamber and the Hall of Flags, and the 'Sacred Cod' carving hangs in the House chamber as a symbol of the state's maritime heritage.
