Maqdala manuscripts

The Maqdala manuscripts are a collection of Ethiopian manuscripts taken by British forces from the fortress of Maqdala (Magdala) after the Battle of Magdala in April 1868, at the end of the British Expedition to Abyssinia against Emperor Tewodros II.

Removal

After the emperor's defeat and death, British troops looted the fortress and church treasuries. Hundreds of illuminated manuscripts in Ge'ez, including royal chronicles and religious texts, were carried off. A sale at auction distributed many volumes, and several hundred entered the British Museum (now the British Library).

Returns and restitution claims

Ethiopia has long called for the return of the manuscripts and associated treasures, including a tabot and the crown of Tewodros. In 2007 Ethiopia made a formal request for their return. Some items looted in 1868 have been returned by private donors and institutions in recent years, but the bulk of the manuscript collection remains in the British Library.

Significance

The manuscripts are among the finest surviving examples of Ethiopian book art and Christian literature, and their removal is a prominent case in debates over cultural property and museum restitution.
