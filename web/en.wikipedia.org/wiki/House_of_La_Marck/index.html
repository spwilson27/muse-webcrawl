House of La Marck

House of La Marck
CountryGermany, France, Netherlands

TitlesCount/Duke of Mark, Cleves, Nevers, Bouillon; Prince of Sedan

The House of La Marck (German: von der Mark) was a German noble family originating in the County of Mark in Westphalia. Rising from counts of Mark in the 12th century, its branches acquired Cleves, Berg, and Ravensberg in the Rhineland and, through a cadet line established in France, the counties of Nevers and the sovereign principality of Sedan and duchy of Bouillon in the Ardennes.

Branches

The senior line ruled the United Duchies of Jülich-Cleves-Berg until its extinction in 1609 triggered the War of the Jülich Succession. The French cadet branch began with Erard de La Marck, prince-bishop of Liège, and his brother Robert, lord of Sedan. Their descendants included Robert IV de La Marck, marshal of France, and Charlotte de La Marck, whose marriage carried Sedan and Bouillon to the La Tour d'Auvergne family in the 1590s. William de La Marck, the "Wild Boar of the Ardennes", notorious for the murder of the bishop of Liège in 1482, belonged to a younger branch.

Decline

The direct line of La Marck counts of Mark ended in the 17th century, though the name survived in collateral and adopted lines, notably the La Marck counts who held Lummen in the Austrian Netherlands. The family gave France marshals, cardinals, and soldiers of fortune across three centuries.
