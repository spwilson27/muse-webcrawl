The Vendée Globe is a single-handed (solo), non-stop, unassisted round-the-world yacht race. The race was founded by Philippe Jeantot in 1989, and since 1992 has taken place every four years. It is named after the Vendée department in France, where the race starts and finishes, in the port of Les Sables-d'Olonne. The race is contested in IMOCA 60 class monohull yachts and is widely considered the toughest solo sailing race in the world; it is nicknamed the "Everest of the seas".

Competitors must round the Cape of Good Hope, Cape Leeuwin and Cape Horn, leaving the Antarctic continent to starboard, without stopping and without outside assistance. They may not come ashore, and outside routing advice is prohibited. The course covers roughly 24,300 nautical miles (45,000 km), and the fastest sailors complete it in under 70 days.

History

The first edition started on 26 November 1989 with thirteen competitors, of whom seven finished. It was won by French sailor Titouan Lamazou aboard Écureuil d'Aquitaine II in 109 days. The race quickly grew in popularity, particularly in France, where the start and finish at Les Sables-d'Olonne draw hundreds of thousands of spectators.

The 1992–93 edition was won by Alain Gautier. The 1996–97 race was overshadowed by severe Southern Ocean storms; Canadian sailor Gerry Roufs was lost at sea, and several competitors were rescued after capsizes, prompting tighter safety rules. Michel Desjoyeaux won the 2000–01 edition and repeated the feat in 2008–09, becoming the first sailor to win the race twice. Ellen MacArthur finished second in 2000–01, just a day behind Desjoyeaux.

François Gabart set a new record of 78 days in 2012–13, and Armel Le Cléac'h lowered it to 74 days in 2016–17. The 2020–21 edition was won by Yannick Bestaven after time compensation was applied for his role in the rescue of Kevin Escoffier, whose yacht sank in the Indian Ocean. In the 2024–25 edition, Charlie Dalin won and set a new course record of 64 days, 20 hours, breaking the 70-day barrier for the first time.

The race

The race starts and finishes at Les Sables-d'Olonne in November, so that competitors cross the Southern Ocean during the austral summer. Sailors must pass an "Ice Exclusion Zone" around Antarctica, defined by gates they must stay north of, designed to keep the fleet away from icebergs. Competitors face solitude, sleep deprivation, storms, freezing temperatures in the south and calms in the doldrums near the equator.
