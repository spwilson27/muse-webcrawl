Marsha M. Linehan (born 5 May 1943) is an American psychologist and author. She is the creator of dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), a form of psychotherapy originally developed for chronically suicidal individuals with borderline personality disorder and now used for a wide range of mental health conditions. She is professor emerita of psychology at the University of Washington, where she founded the Behavioral Research and Therapy Clinics.

Life and work

Linehan grew up in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and experienced severe mental illness as a teenager, spending time in an inpatient psychiatric unit. She later studied at Loyola University Chicago and completed her PhD at Loyola in 1971. Drawing on her own recovery, her training in behaviour therapy, and Zen meditation practice, she developed DBT in the late 1970s and 1980s.

DBT combines standard cognitive-behavioural techniques with mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness skills, delivered through individual therapy, group skills training, telephone coaching and therapist consultation teams. Randomised trials showed DBT significantly reduced suicidal behaviour and hospitalisation compared with treatment as usual, establishing it as an evidence-based treatment.

Later career

Linehan publicly disclosed her own mental illness history in 2011. Her memoir Building a Life Worth Living (2020) recounts both her illness and the development of DBT. She has received numerous awards, including the Gold Medal Award for Life Achievement in the Application of Psychology from the American Psychological Foundation.
