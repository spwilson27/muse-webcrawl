Teresa of Ávila

Born: 28 March 1515, Ávila, Spain
Died: 4 October 1582, Alba de Tormes
Honours: Saint; Doctor of the Church (1970)

Teresa of Ávila, also called Saint Teresa of Jesus, was a Spanish Carmelite nun, mystic, and religious reformer. Together with John of the Cross she founded the Discalced Carmelites, restoring the order's contemplative austerity.

Writings

Her spiritual classics include her autobiography The Life of Teresa of Jesus, The Interior Castle, and The Way of Perfection, works that combine vivid practicality with profound accounts of mystical prayer. Bernini's sculpture The Ecstasy of Saint Teresa in Rome depicts her vision of an angel piercing her heart.

Legacy

Canonized in 1622, she was proclaimed a Doctor of the Church in 1970 by Pope Paul VI — the first woman to receive the title.
