Potiphar

Potiphar (Hebrew: פּוֹטִיפַר) is a figure in the Book of Genesis, an Egyptian official — captain of Pharaoh's guard — who purchased Joseph, son of Jacob, as a slave and later imprisoned him after a false accusation by Potiphar's wife.

In Genesis

According to Genesis 37:36, the Midianites sold Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard. Joseph prospered in his master's house and was made overseer of the household. When Joseph refused the advances of Potiphar's wife, she accused him of attempting to assault her, displaying his garment as evidence, and Potiphar had Joseph thrown into prison — where Joseph's gift for interpreting dreams eventually brought him to Pharaoh's attention.

Name and historicity

The name Potiphar is generally understood as a shortened form of the Egyptian name Potiphera, meaning "he whom Ra gave", also borne by the priest of On who became Joseph's father-in-law. Scholars debate the historical background of the Joseph story and the period of Egyptian history it best fits.

In later tradition

The story of Potiphar's wife was elaborated in Jewish, Christian and Islamic tradition. In the Quran (Surah Yusuf), the wife of the 'Aziz — identified in later tradition as Zuleikha — is a central figure, and the tale of Yusuf and Zuleikha became a beloved subject of Persian and Turkish poetry and art.
