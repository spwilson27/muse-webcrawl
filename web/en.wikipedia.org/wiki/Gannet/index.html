Gannets are seabirds comprising the genus Morus in the family Sulidae, closely related to boobies. They are large white birds with yellowish heads, black-tipped wings, and long bills, famous for their spectacular plunge-diving for fish from heights of up to 30 metres, entering the water at speeds approaching 100 km/h.

Species

The three species are the northern gannet (Morus bassanus) of the North Atlantic — the largest seabird in that region — the Cape gannet (M. capensis) of southern Africa, and the Australasian gannet (M. serrator) of Australia and New Zealand.

Behaviour

Gannets breed in dense colonies on steep cliffs and offshore islands, such as Bass Rock in Scotland, Bonaventure Island in Quebec, and Hermaness in Shetland. They are adapted to plunge-diving with air sacs under the skin to cushion impact, forward-facing binocular vision, and nostrils that open inside the mouth. They hunt fish and squid, often in association with dolphins and predatory fish that drive prey to the surface. Gannets are mostly silent at sea but produce harsh calls at the colony; pairs mate for life and perform elaborate greeting displays.

Conservation

The northern gannet is of least concern overall, though colonies were severely affected by the 2022 avian influenza outbreak. The Cape gannet is classified as endangered owing to declines linked to sardine and anchovy stocks off Namibia and South Africa.
