Himalayan salt

Himalayan salt is rock salt (halite) mined from the Punjab region of Pakistan, principally at the Khewra Salt Mine in the Salt Range, some 190 miles (300 km) from the Himalayas proper. The salt is known for its pink colouration, caused by trace minerals, and is sold as a gourmet food, as a decorative material for lamps and cooking slabs, and in spa products.

Geology and composition

The deposits formed from the evaporation of an ancient sea and were folded into the Salt Range during the collision of the Indian plate with Eurasia. Chemically the salt is about 96–99 per cent sodium chloride, comparable to ordinary table salt, with the remainder consisting of trace minerals — notably iron oxide, which produces the pink-to-red hues, along with calcium, potassium, magnesium and others in small amounts.

Uses

Coarse crystals are marketed as a finishing salt and for salt grinders; thick slabs are used as cooking and serving surfaces that can be heated or chilled. Blocks are carved into salt lamps, which are hollowed out to hold a bulb or candle and are claimed by sellers to purify air by releasing negative ions. The salt is also sold for bathing and as salt caves in halotherapy spas.

Health claims

Promoters frequently claim the salt contains 84 essential trace elements and confers health benefits beyond those of ordinary salt. Scientific assessments note that the trace minerals are present in quantities too small to have meaningful nutritional effects and that claims for salt lamps' air-purifying effects lack robust supporting evidence; sodium intake from Himalayan salt carries the same health considerations as any other salt.
