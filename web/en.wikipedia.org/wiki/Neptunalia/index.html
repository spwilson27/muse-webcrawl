Neptunalia

The Neptunalia was an archaic two-day festival in honour of Neptune, the Roman god of waters and the sea, celebrated at Rome on July 23 at the height of summer, during a period of drought. It is one of the oldest festivals in the Roman calendar, although little is known of its detailed rituals.

Celebration

During the festival, the Romans built huts of branches and foliage called umbrae, in which they feasted, drank and made merry in the shade. The celebrations likely included games and were associated with the conservation and drawing of water at a time of year when streams ran low.

Context

The Neptunalia formed part of a cluster of July festivals connected with water and agriculture, including the Lucaria (a festival of grove-clearing) and the Furrinalia, honouring Furrina, another water deity. Some traditions also associate the festival with Neptune's consort Salacia.
