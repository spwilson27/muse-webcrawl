Muthuswami Dikshitar

Muthuswami Dikshitar (24 March 1775 – 21 October 1835) was a South Indian poet, singer and veena player, and a composer of Indian classical music. He is considered one of the musical trinity of Carnatic music, along with Tyagaraja and Syama Sastri. His compositions, about 500 of which are commonly known, are noted for their elaborate and poetic descriptions of Hindu gods and temples and for their detailed treatment of raga.

Life

Dikshitar was born in Tiruvarur, in present-day Tamil Nadu, to Ramaswami Dikshitar, himself a musician and composer credited with creating the raga Hamsadhvani. As a young man he accompanied the monk Chidambaranatha Swamigal to Varanasi, where he spent several years studying music — including exposure to Hindustani music — philosophy and scripture. Returning south, he composed at Tiruttani, Kanchipuram and finally Tiruvarur, weaving the iconography and legends of each temple into his songs.

Compositions

Most of his kritis are in Sanskrit, with some in Telugu and Manipravalam, and bear his signature (mudra) "Guruguha," a reference to Kartikeya. His works include group compositions such as the Kamalamba Navavarna kritis and the Navagraha kritis on the nine planets, the celebrated "Vatapi Ganapatim" in Hamsadhvani, and the Nottusvara Sahityas — Sanskrit texts set to melodies adapted from British band tunes he heard at Fort St. George, Madras, reflecting his knowledge of Western music. His younger brother Baluswami Dikshitar is credited with introducing the violin into Carnatic music.

Legacy

Dikshitar's slow, majestic compositions are regarded as some of the most scholarly in the Carnatic repertoire, and his family's descendants and disciples preserved them in collections such as the Sangita Sampradaya Pradarshini.
