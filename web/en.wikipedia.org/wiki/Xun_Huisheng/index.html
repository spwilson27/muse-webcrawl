Xun Huisheng

Xun Huisheng (Chinese: 荀慧生; pinyin: Xún Huìshēng; 5 January 1900 – 26 December 1968) was a Chinese Peking opera artist specializing in dan roles — female characters played traditionally by male performers. He is counted among the "Four Great Dan" of the golden age of Peking opera, alongside Mei Lanfang, Shang Xiaoyun, and Cheng Yanqiu.

Career

Born into poverty in Dongguang, Hebei, Xun was sold to a traveling opera troupe as a child and trained in Hebei opera (bangzi) before turning to Peking opera in Beijing. His art, known as the Xun school (Xun pai), blended bangzi vocal techniques with Peking opera, favoring lively, naturalistic, and emotionally vivid portrayals, particularly of vivacious young women (huadan roles). Signature plays included The Unofficial History of the Red Chamber adaptations, Dujuan Shan, and Hua Tian Cuo.

Later life

After 1949 Xun performed under the new state troupe system and taught younger artists. He was persecuted during the Cultural Revolution and died in Beijing in 1968. His school continues through students and recordings, and he is remembered for making dan performance more grounded, playful, and lifelike.
