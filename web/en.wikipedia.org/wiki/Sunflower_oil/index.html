Sunflower oil

SourceSeeds of the sunflower (Helianthus annuus)
Smoke pointAbout 232 °C (450 °F) refined
Major producersUkraine, Russia, Argentina, EU

Sunflower oil is the non-volatile oil pressed from the seeds of the sunflower (Helianthus annuus). It is pale amber in colour with a mild flavour and is used worldwide in cooking, frying, salad dressings, margarine and processed foods, as well as in cosmetics and as a base for biodiesel. Sunflower oil is one of the most widely produced vegetable oils in the world.

Composition and types

Traditional sunflower oil is rich in the polyunsaturated fatty acid linoleic acid (roughly 60–70 per cent) and relatively low in saturated fat. Breeding programmes have produced varieties with different fatty-acid profiles: high-oleic sunflower oil contains 75 per cent or more monounsaturated oleic acid and is valued for frying stability; mid-oleic types fall between the two. The oil is naturally a good source of vitamin E (alpha-tocopherol). Refined oil has a high smoke point of around 232 °C, making it suitable for deep frying; cold-pressed unrefined oil retains more flavour and is used for dressings.

Production

The sunflower was domesticated in North America and brought to Europe in the sixteenth century; large-scale oil production developed in the Russian Empire in the nineteenth century, aided by the Orthodox Church's Lenten rules, which permitted sunflower oil when other fats were restricted. Seeds are cleaned, dehulled, crushed and pressed, and the remaining oil is extracted with solvent; the residual meal is used as animal feed. Before the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, Ukraine was the world's leading exporter, supplying roughly half of world trade; the disruption of Black Sea exports caused sharp global price increases and reformulation in the food industry.

Uses

Beyond food, sunflower oil is used in soaps, paints, lubricants, and cosmetics, and hydrogenated sunflower oil in margarines and shortenings. It is also being investigated and used as a feedstock for biodiesel in Europe.
