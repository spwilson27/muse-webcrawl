Winston Mawdsley Graham OBE (30 June 1908 – 10 July 2003) was an English novelist best known for the Poldark series of historical novels set in Cornwall. He wrote more than forty books, including thrillers, historical fiction, and the psychological suspense novel Marnie (1961), which Alfred Hitchcock adapted into the film Marnie (1964).

Life

Graham was born in Manchester and moved with his family to Perranporth, Cornwall, at the age of 17, after his father's early death; Cornwall became his home and the setting of his most famous work. Unable to afford university, he began writing full time, supported by his mother, and published his first novel, The House with the Stained Glass Windows, in 1934. During the Second World War he served in the Auxiliary Fire Service and with the coastguard. He lived for many years in Sussex and the south of France while continuing to write.

The Poldark novels

The first Poldark novel, Ross Poldark, appeared in 1945. The series follows Ross Poldark, a British Army officer who returns from the American War of Independence to find his estate ruined, and charts the fortunes of his family through twelve novels ending with Bella Poldark (2002), published when Graham was in his nineties. The books were dramatized by the BBC in 1975–77 (starring Robin Ellis) to great popularity and again in 2015–2019 (starring Aidan Turner), the second adaptation sparking a major revival of interest in Graham's work and in Cornwall itself. Graham also wrote Poldark's Cornwall, a companion volume.

Other works and honours

His thrillers include The Walking Stick (1967), filmed in 1970, and Stephanie (1992). He was a fellow of the Royal Society of Literature, was appointed OBE in 1983, and served as chairman of the Society of Authors. He died in 2003, aged 95.
