The 2014 Sony Pictures hack was a cyberattack conducted against Sony Pictures Entertainment on 24 November 2014 by a hacker group identifying itself as the "Guardians of Peace" (GOP). The attackers stole a large quantity of confidential data — unreleased films, employees' personal information including Social Security numbers and salaries, and embarrassing internal emails — and destroyed data on the studio's computers with wiper malware. The United States government attributed the attack to North Korea.

The attack and leaks

On 24 November, Sony employees logging in were greeted by an image of a red skeleton and the message "Hacked by #GOP." The attackers then released stolen material in stages to journalists and file-sharing sites, including five Sony films (four unreleased, among them Annie and Fury), the script of the forthcoming James Bond film Spectre, and extensive internal correspondence. Emails between co-chair Amy Pascal and producer Scott Rudin containing racially insensitive jokes about President Barack Obama drew wide criticism and contributed to Pascal's resignation in February 2015.

The Interview and the theatre threats

The hackers demanded that Sony cancel The Interview, a Seth Rogen–James Franco comedy about a plot to assassinate North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, and threatened attacks on cinemas showing it, invoking "the 9/11." Major theatre chains dropped the film, and Sony initially cancelled its release — drawing criticism from President Obama — before releasing it online and in limited theatres on 25 December 2014.

Attribution and response

On 19 December 2014 the FBI formally attributed the attack to North Korea, citing similarities in malware and infrastructure to earlier attacks (such as the 2013 DarkSeoul attacks on South Korea). Some independent security researchers initially expressed scepticism, proposing a disgruntled insider, but subsequent reporting and a 2018 US criminal complaint against North Korean operative Park Jin Hyok supported the official attribution. In January 2015 President Obama imposed additional sanctions on North Korea. The incident is regarded as a landmark in state-sponsored cyber operations against a private company and in debates over free expression and corporate capitulation to threats.
