Cumulene

Butatriene, H2C=C=C=CH2, a [3]cumulene.

A cumulene is a hydrocarbon composed of a chain of carbon atoms joined by cumulative (consecutive) double bonds — three or more carbons linked as =C=C=C=. The simplest example is butatriene. Cumulenes with three double bonds are called [3]cumulenes, those with four [4]cumulenes, and so on; cyclic cumulenes are known as cyclocumulenes.

Structure and stereochemistry

Each internal carbon of a cumulene is sp-hybridized, so the double bonds alternate their orientation in perpendicular planes. As a result, cumulenes with an even number of double bonds have their terminal substituents in the same plane and can show cis–trans isomerism, while those with an odd number have perpendicular end groups and can exhibit axial chirality, like allenes. Long cumulene chains are the finite analogues of the hypothetical carbon allotrope carbyne.

Synthesis and occurrence

Cumulenes are typically prepared by reductive elimination of geminal dihalides or by rearrangement reactions of alkynes. Short cumulenes are reactive and often unstable, but sterically protected derivatives with bulky end groups have been isolated with up to nine consecutive double bonds. Cumulene-type species have also been detected in interstellar space and are studied as molecular wires for nanoelectronics.

Related compounds

Heterocumulenes replace one or more carbons with heteroatoms; familiar examples include carbon dioxide (O=C=O), carbon disulfide, isocyanates and ketenes.
