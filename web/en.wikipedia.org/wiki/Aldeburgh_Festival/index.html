Aldeburgh Festival

The Aldeburgh Festival of Music and the Arts is an English arts festival devoted mainly to classical music. It takes place each June in the town of Aldeburgh, Suffolk, and since 1967 its principal venue has been the concert hall at Snape Maltings. It is one of Britain's best-known music festivals.

History

The festival was founded in 1948 by the composer Benjamin Britten, the singer Peter Pears and the librettist and producer Eric Crozier. Britten and Pears had made Aldeburgh their home, and the first festival used venues around the town, including the parish church and the Jubilee Hall. Britten's works, and new British music generally, were central to its programming from the start; his church parable Curlew River and the opera A Midsummer Night's Dream are among the works closely associated with the festival.

Snape Maltings

In 1967 the festival moved much of its activity to a converted Victorian maltings at Snape, a few miles inland. The Snape Maltings Concert Hall was opened by Queen Elizabeth II. It was destroyed by fire on the opening night of the 1969 festival but rebuilt in time for the following year. The Britten–Pears Foundation and the organisation now known as Britten Pears Arts run the festival and year-round residencies for young musicians.
