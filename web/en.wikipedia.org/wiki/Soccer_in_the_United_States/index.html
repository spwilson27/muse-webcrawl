Soccer in the United States

Soccer is one of the most widely played sports in the United States, particularly among youth.

Soccer in the United States (known as football in most of the world) is governed by the United States Soccer Federation (U.S. Soccer), founded in 1913. The sport is one of the most popular participation sports in the country, especially among children and young adults, and has grown steadily as a spectator sport since the 1990s.

History

Soccer was played in the United States in the 19th century, notably by the Oneida Football Club of Boston in the 1860s, and the United States fielded a team at the third FIFA World Cup in 1930, reaching the semi-finals. The North American Soccer League (1968–1984), featuring stars such as Pelé with the New York Cosmos, brought a first wave of popularity but collapsed under financial strain. The United States hosted the 1994 FIFA World Cup, which remains among the best-attended in history and led directly to the founding of Major League Soccer (MLS), launched in 1996.

Professional leagues

Major League Soccer is the top-tier men's league, operating as a single-entity structure with franchised clubs across the United States and Canada. The USL Championship forms the second tier. In women's soccer, the National Women's Soccer League (NWSL), founded in 2012, is a leading top-flight league. Domestic cup competition is headed by the U.S. Open Cup, first held in 1914.

National teams

The United States women's national team is one of the most successful in the world, winning the FIFA Women's World Cup in 1991, 1999, 2015, and 2019, as well as multiple Olympic gold medals. The men's national team has qualified for most World Cups since 1990 and reached the quarter-finals in 2002; the United States will co-host the 2026 FIFA World Cup with Canada and Mexico.

Popularity and culture

Youth soccer is a fixture of suburban American life, administered largely through organizations such as the American Youth Soccer Organization (AYSO) and U.S. Youth Soccer. Television audiences for international tournaments and European leagues have grown markedly, and soccer has a strong following among immigrant and Hispanic communities. Despite its growth, professional soccer still trails American football, basketball, and baseball in overall domestic television revenue and media attention.
