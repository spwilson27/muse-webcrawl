FieldTurf

FieldTurf is a brand of artificial turf playing surface used primarily for sports stadiums and arenas. Known for mimicking the look, feel and performance of natural grass more closely than earlier carpet-style turfs, it became the dominant synthetic surface in North American professional and collegiate sports in the early 2000s. The company is headquartered in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, and is a division of the French flooring group Tarkett.

Construction

A FieldTurf surface consists of long polyethylene monofilament fibres tufted into a backing, designed to imitate grass blades. The fibres are held upright by an infill of sand and cryogenically ground rubber pellets (often called "crumb rubber") spread between them. The infill provides cushioning, traction and ball-roll characteristics intended to approximate a natural grass pitch.

History

The technology originated with inventor John Gilman and was developed through the 1980s and 1990s. FieldTurf gained prominence after installations at the University of Nebraska's Memorial Stadium (1999) and numerous NFL venues; by the mid-2000s a majority of NFL stadiums with artificial surfaces used the product. The company was acquired by Tarkett in 2005.

Use

FieldTurf is used for American football, association football (soccer), baseball, lacrosse, field hockey and multi-purpose school fields. FIFA has certified certain FieldTurf systems under its quality programme for football turf.

Criticism and safety debate

Synthetic surfaces have been debated regarding player injuries — some studies have linked artificial turf to higher rates of certain knee and ankle injuries, while others find comparable rates — as well as heat retention on sunny days and the environmental and health questions surrounding crumb-rubber infill made from recycled tyres. The debate intensified around the 2015 FIFA Women's World Cup in Canada, played entirely on artificial turf.
