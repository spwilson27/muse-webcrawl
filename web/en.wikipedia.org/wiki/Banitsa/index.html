Banitsa

Banitsa (Bulgarian: баница) is a traditional Bulgarian pastry dish, made by layering a mixture of whisked eggs, natural yoghurt, and pieces of white brined cheese (sirene) between filo pastry sheets, and then baking it in an oven. It is a close relative of dishes found across the Balkans and the eastern Mediterranean, such as Serbian gibanica and Greek spanakopita-style pies.

Preparation and varieties

The pastry may be rolled into a spiral, laid in flat layers, or folded into individual parcels (banichki). Fillings vary: besides the classic cheese filling, banitsa is made with spinach, leeks, pumpkin and sugar (a sweet version called tikvenik), cabbage, onions, or minced meat. A breakfast staple in Bulgaria, it is commonly eaten with ayran, boza, or plain yoghurt.

Traditions

On New Year's Eve, banitsa is traditionally baked with lucky charms (kŭsmeti) hidden inside — small folded paper fortunes, cornel twigs, or a coin — which are believed to predict the finder's fortune for the coming year. The head of the household spins the dish and cuts slices for each family member.
