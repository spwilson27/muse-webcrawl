South magnetic pole

The south magnetic pole is the point on Earth's surface in the Southern Hemisphere where the planet's magnetic field points vertically upward — the dip pole at which a freely suspended magnetic needle stands vertical. Because Earth's field is generated by the geodynamo in the liquid outer core, the magnetic poles wander over time; the south magnetic pole has moved steadily from inland Antarctica toward the coast and out to sea off the Adélie Coast.

Location and movement

The pole was first reached in 1909 by members of Ernest Shackleton's Nimrod Expedition, who located it inland near the coast of George V Land. Successive expeditions and models have tracked its movement northwestward; by the 21st century it lay offshore in the Southern Ocean, moving at a rate of roughly 10–15 km per year. It is distinct from the south geomagnetic pole, the pole of the best-fitting dipole model, which lies near Vostok Station.

Expeditions

Edgeworth David, Douglas Mawson, and Alistair Mackay reached the pole's 1909 position after a gruelling man-hauling journey during Shackleton's expedition, claiming the area for Britain. Australian expeditions repeatedly re-located the pole through the 20th century.
