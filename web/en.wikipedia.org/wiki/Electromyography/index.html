Electromyography

Electromyography (EMG) is a technique for evaluating and recording the electrical activity produced by skeletal muscles. It is performed with an instrument called an electromyograph, which produces a record called an electromyogram. EMG detects the electric potential generated by muscle cells when they are neurologically activated, and is used clinically to diagnose disorders of muscle and of the nerves that control them, usually together with nerve conduction studies.

Techniques

Needle EMG inserts a fine needle electrode directly into a muscle to record the activity of individual motor units at rest and during contraction. Healthy resting muscle is electrically silent; abnormal spontaneous activity such as fibrillation potentials and positive sharp waves indicates denervation, while the size and shape of motor unit potentials help distinguish muscle disease (myopathy, with small short potentials) from nerve disease (neuropathy, with large polyphasic potentials after reinnervation).

Surface EMG places electrodes on the skin over a muscle. It cannot resolve individual motor units but is useful in biomechanics, ergonomics, sports science, rehabilitation and biofeedback, and for controlling prosthetic devices.

Clinical uses

EMG and nerve conduction studies help localise lesions and diagnose conditions such as carpal tunnel syndrome, radiculopathy, peripheral neuropathy, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), myasthenia gravis (with specialised repetitive stimulation and single-fibre techniques), and primary muscle diseases. The examination is mildly uncomfortable because of the needle insertions and electrical stimuli used in the conduction studies, but serious complications are rare.

History

The electrical activity of muscle was explored as early as the experiments of Luigi Galvani in the 18th century. Practical clinical EMG developed in the 1940s and 1950s with improved amplifiers and oscilloscopes, and concentric needle electrodes; later computerisation allowed quantitative analysis of motor unit potentials and interference patterns.
