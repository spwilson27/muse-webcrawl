Plasma etching

Plasma etching is a form of plasma processing used in microfabrication to etch material from substrates such as semiconductor wafers. It is commonly employed to remove material selectively using chemically reactive plasma species, ions, and free radicals generated from a suitable source gas or mixture of gases, and is a critical step in the manufacture of integrated circuits.

Process

In a plasma etcher, a vacuum chamber is filled with a low-pressure process gas (often fluorine- or chlorine-based) and excited, typically by radio-frequency power, to form a plasma. Reactive species etch exposed regions of the wafer, while a patterned mask of photoresist or hard mask protects areas to remain. Volatile reaction products are pumped away.

Types

Reactive-ion etching (RIE) adds directional ion bombardment to chemical etching, yielding anisotropic profiles with nearly vertical sidewalls. Deep reactive-ion etching (DRIE), using alternating etch and passivation cycles (the Bosch process), produces very deep, high-aspect-ratio structures used in MEMS devices.

Applications

Beyond semiconductor manufacturing, plasma etching is used for cleaning and surface preparation, micromachining, and patterning thin films of dielectrics, metals, and silicon.
