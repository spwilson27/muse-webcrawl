The Toyota Camry (Japanese: トヨタ・カムリ) is an automobile sold internationally by Toyota since 1982, spanning multiple generations. Originally compact in size, the Camry grew into a mid-size sedan and became Toyota's flagship family car in many markets. The name derives from the Japanese word kanmuri (冠), meaning "crown", continuing Toyota's tradition of crown-related names for its sedans.

History

The Camry name first appeared on the Celica Camry of 1980–1982, a sedan version of the Celica sold in Japan. The standalone Camry line began in 1982 with the V10 series, offered as a sedan and hatchback with front-wheel drive. Successive generations — V20 (1986), V30 (1990), and the wide-body XV10 (1991) — moved the car upmarket. The XV10 and its successors proved enormously successful in the United States, where the Camry was the best-selling passenger car for most years from 1997 onward.

Later generations include the XV20 (1996), XV30 (2001), XV40 (2006), XV50 (2011) and XV70 (2017), the latter built on Toyota's TNGA platform with a hybrid variant prominent in the range. The ninth-generation XV80, introduced for 2025, is sold exclusively as a hybrid in North America.

Production and markets

Camrys have been built in Japan, the United States (Georgetown, Kentucky), Australia (until the Altona plant closed in 2017), China, Thailand, Russia and elsewhere. The model has also been sold under other badges, including as the Daihatsu Altis in Japan and formerly as the Holden Apollo in Australia. A luxury-oriented sibling, the Toyota Avalon, was derived from the Camry platform for North America.

Motorsport

The Camry nameplate has been used in NASCAR since Toyota entered the Cup Series in 2007, with Camry-bodied cars winning multiple manufacturers' and drivers' championships.
