Independence Day (Israel)

Independence Day (Hebrew: Yom Ha'atzmaut) is Israel's national day, commemorating the declaration of the establishment of the State of Israel by David Ben-Gurion on 14 May 1948, corresponding to 5 Iyar 5708 in the Hebrew calendar. Because it follows the Hebrew calendar, the holiday falls on or near 5 Iyar each year — in late April or May — and the date is shifted when necessary to avoid conflict with the Sabbath.

An Israel Air Force flypast is a traditional highlight of the celebrations.

Observance

Independence Day begins at sundown, immediately following Memorial Day (Yom HaZikaron) for Israel's fallen soldiers and victims of terrorism; the abrupt transition from mourning to celebration is marked by the official torch-lighting ceremony on Mount Herzl in Jerusalem, where twelve torches — for the twelve tribes of Israel — are lit by honoured citizens. The day itself features the International Bible Contest, the awarding of the Israel Prize, military flyovers, open bases, and widespread picnics and barbecues in parks and beaches. Many families display the national flag from homes and cars.

Context

The holiday marks the end of the British Mandate and the establishment of the first Jewish state in some two thousand years, an event that Palestinians commemorate separately as the Nakba ("catastrophe"), recalling the displacement of Palestinians during the 1948 war. The juxtaposition gives the period a dual character in Israeli and Palestinian public life.
