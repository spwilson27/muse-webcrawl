Book of Ruth

The Book of Ruth is a short book of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament, telling the story of Ruth, a Moabite woman who remains loyal to her Israelite mother-in-law Naomi and is eventually married to Boaz, becoming an ancestor of King David.

Summary

During a famine, Naomi's family migrates from Bethlehem to Moab, where her sons marry Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. After the deaths of Naomi's husband and sons, Ruth insists on accompanying her back to Bethlehem—"where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay"—while Orpah returns home. In Bethlehem, Ruth gleans in the fields of Boaz, a wealthy relative of Naomi's late husband, who shows her kindness and ultimately marries her as kinsman-redeemer. Their son Obed becomes the grandfather of David, and the book ends with a genealogy leading to the king.

Themes

The book is noted for its themes of loyalty (hesed), redemption, and the inclusion of a foreigner in Israel's story. In Jewish tradition it is read during the festival of Shavuot. Its setting is "in the days when the judges ruled," though scholars debate the date of its composition, with proposals ranging from the monarchic period to the Persian era.
