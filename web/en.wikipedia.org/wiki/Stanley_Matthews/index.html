Stanley Matthews

Born: 1 February 1915, Hanley, Stoke-on-Trent
Died: 23 February 2000 (aged 85), Stoke-on-Trent
Position: Outside forward (right winger)
Clubs: Stoke City, Blackpool
England caps: 54

Sir Stanley Matthews (1 February 1915 – 23 February 2000) was an English footballer who played as an outside right. Regarded as one of the greatest dribblers in the history of the game, he was the first winner of the European Footballer of the Year award (Ballon d'Or) in 1956 and the first footballer to be knighted while still playing.

Career

Matthews joined Stoke City as a teenager and made his debut in 1932. After the Second World War — during which he served in the RAF — he moved to Blackpool in 1947 for £11,500. At Blackpool he lost two FA Cup finals before the famous 1953 final, in which Blackpool came back from 3–1 down to beat Bolton Wanderers 4–3; although Stan Mortensen scored a hat-trick, the game is remembered as "the Matthews Final" for his decisive wing play at the age of 38. He returned to Stoke in 1961, helped them win the Second Division title in 1963, and played his final top-flight match in February 1965, just after his 50th birthday — a top-level career spanning 33 years, kept going by a rigorous fitness regime and near-vegetarian diet remarkable for his era.

Style and honours

Matthews was famed for his body swerve, acceleration over short distances, crossing accuracy and exemplary sportsmanship: he was never booked or sent off in his career. He won 54 caps for England between 1934 and 1957, played at the 1950 and 1954 World Cups, and was part of the 1947 "Great Britain" side against a Rest of Europe XI. He was appointed CBE and knighted in 1965, and a statue of him stands at Stoke City's stadium, where his ashes are buried beneath the centre circle.
