Drumlanrig Castle

Drumlanrig Castle is a great country house near Thornhill in Dumfries and Galloway, south-west Scotland. Built of distinctive pink sandstone between 1679 and 1689 for the 1st Duke of Queensberry, it is one of the finest examples of late 17th-century Renaissance architecture in Scotland, and remains a seat of the Duke of Buccleuch and Queensberry.

History

The Douglas family had held lands at Drumlanrig since the 15th century. William Douglas, 1st Duke of Queensberry, commissioned the present palace on the site of earlier Douglas strongholds; the architect is traditionally identified as James Smith, then Scotland's leading architect. The castle's four ranges around a central courtyard, with tall corner towers, marked a shift from fortified tower house to classical palace.

Collections

Drumlanrig houses part of the Buccleuch art collection, one of the largest private collections in Britain. Works have included Rembrandt's An Old Woman Reading (1655) and Leonardo da Vinci's Madonna of the Yarnwinder, the latter stolen from the castle in 2003 and recovered in 2007. The house also displays fine French furniture, tapestries, and silver.

Gardens and estate

The extensive gardens and the surrounding estate, with its avenues and the 18th-century formal gardens, are open to the public in season. Drumlanrig was also the site of an early Douglas sponsorship of cycling: the first bicycle with pedals, built by Kirkpatrick Macmillan in 1839, is associated with the nearby village of Keir Mill rather than the castle itself.
