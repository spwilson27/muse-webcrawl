Cross of Honour of the German Mother

The Cross of Honour of the German Mother (German: Ehrenkreuz der Deutschen Mutter), often called the Mother's Cross (Mutterkreuz), was a state decoration instituted by Nazi Germany on 16 December 1938. It was awarded to German mothers who had raised large families and fulfilled criteria of racial, political and moral "worthiness" under the regime's pronatalist policies.

Classes

The cross was awarded in three classes: bronze for mothers of four or five children, silver for mothers of six or seven children, and gold for mothers of eight or more children. Recipients were expected to be of German blood, politically reliable, and to have raised their children in accordance with National Socialist ideology. Members of the League of German Girls and the Hitler Youth were instructed to salute wearers of the decoration.

Design

The decoration took the form of a blue enamelled cross pattée with white enamel edges, bearing a central medallion with a swastika and the inscription "Der Deutschen Mutter" ("To the German Mother"). It was suspended from a blue ribbon with white stripes. The first awards were presented on Mother's Day, 21 May 1939, and millions of crosses had been awarded by the end of the war.

History

The award formed part of a broader Nazi demographic policy that glorified motherhood, alongside measures such as marriage loans and restrictions on abortion for "Aryan" women. After 1945 the decoration, like other Nazi awards, was prohibited from being worn in its original form in the Federal Republic of Germany.
