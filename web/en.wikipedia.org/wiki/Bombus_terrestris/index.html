Bombus terrestris

Buff-tailed bumblebee
Scientific name: Bombus terrestris (Linnaeus, 1758)
Family: Apidae
Range: Europe, North Africa, western and central Asia; introduced elsewhere
Common name: Buff-tailed bumblebee; large earth bumblebee

Bombus terrestris, the buff-tailed bumblebee or large earth bumblebee, is one of the most numerous bumblebee species in Europe and one of the main species used in commercial pollination. It is a social insect living in underground colonies founded by a single queen, with worker numbers that can reach several hundred.

Description and life cycle

Queens are 20–22 mm long, with two yellow bands and a buff-coloured tail; workers are smaller and have whiter tails. Colonies are annual in temperate regions: the queen emerges in spring, founds a nest (often in an abandoned rodent burrow), rears workers through the summer, and produces new queens and males in late summer and autumn, after which the colony dies out and mated queens hibernate.

Commercial use and invasiveness

Since the late 1980s, B. terrestris has been reared commercially on a large scale for pollination of greenhouse crops such as tomatoes. Shipped worldwide, it has become established as an invasive species in places including New Zealand, Tasmania, Japan, and South America, where it competes with native bees and can spread parasites and pathogens to them.
