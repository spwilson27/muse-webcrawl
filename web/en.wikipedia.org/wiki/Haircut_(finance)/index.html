Haircut (finance)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A haircut reduces the recognised value of collateral below its market price.

In finance, a haircut is the difference between the market value of an asset used as collateral and the value ascribed to it by a lender — i.e., the percentage by which the asset's value is reduced for the purpose of calculating the amount that can be lent against it, margin requirements, or regulatory capital. For example, collateral worth $100 subject to a 15% haircut supports a loan of only $85. Haircuts protect the lender against declines in the collateral's value during the time it would take to liquidate it after a default.

Where haircuts are used

  • Repurchase agreements (repos) — the initial margin on a repo is expressed as a haircut on the securities sold; US Treasury collateral typically carries haircuts of about 2%, while equities and lower-grade bonds carry far more.
  • Central bank lending — central banks such as the ECB and the Federal Reserve apply schedules of haircuts to collateral pledged at their lending facilities, graded by credit quality, maturity, and liquidity.
  • Margin accounts and securities lending — brokers impose haircuts (margin requirements) on positions financed with borrowed money.
  • Sovereign debt restructuring — by extension, "haircut" is the standard term for the loss imposed on creditors: in the 2012 Greek debt restructuring, private bondholders took a nominal haircut of 53.5% (about 75% in net-present-value terms).

Role in crises

Haircuts are procyclical and central to modern accounts of financial crises. In the run-up to 2008, haircuts on structured-credit collateral in the repo market were near zero; when they rose abruptly to 20–45%, borrowers faced margin calls equivalent to a bank run, forcing fire sales — the "run on repo" analysed by Gary Gorton. Regulators (Basel III, the FSB framework) now prescribe minimum haircut floors for non-centrally cleared securities financing transactions precisely to limit this build-up of leverage.
