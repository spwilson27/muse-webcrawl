Tankōbon

Tankōbon volumes collecting manga chapters previously serialised in magazines

A tankōbon (Japanese: 単行本, "independent/standalone book") is a standard Japanese book format for a complete work — most commonly a volume collecting individual chapters of a manga series that were originally published in a magazine. Outside Japan, the term is widely used for collected manga volumes, equivalent in concept to a trade paperback or graphic novel in English-language comics publishing.

Format

Manga tankōbon are typically printed in a small paperback size (about 17.6 × 11.2 cm, the B6/JIS shinsho format, though sizes vary by imprint), with a paper cover and often a dust jacket. A volume usually contains between 150 and 250 pages, comprising several chapters plus author notes, bonus comics or illustrations. The low price and standardised dimensions make volumes easy to collect and shelve in uniform series.

Publishing process

Most commercial manga first appear chapter by chapter in weekly, monthly or online magazines. Once enough material has accumulated, the publisher issues the chapters in tankōbon form, sometimes with corrected art and added extras. Successful series may later be reissued in deluxe formats such as wide-ban (larger editions), kanzenban (complete editions with colour pages restored) or bunkoban (small, thick paperback reprints).

Other uses

The word tankōbon in Japanese publishing is not limited to manga: it generally denotes a standalone book published independently, as opposed to one volume of a multi-volume set (zenshū) or a magazine issue. Light novels and prose works may also be released in tankōbon form.
