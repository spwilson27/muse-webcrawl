Marcel Duchamp

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Fountain (1917): a white porcelain urinal signed 'R. Mutt', photographed against a plain backdrop]

Henri-Robert-Marcel Duchamp (28 July 1887 – 2 October 1968) was a French painter, sculptor, chess player and writer whose work is associated with Cubism, Dada and conceptual art. He is commonly regarded as one of the three artists (with Picasso and Matisse) who defined the revolutionary developments in the plastic arts in the opening decades of the 20th century.

Duchamp scandalised the art world with his 'readymades' — ordinary manufactured objects presented as art — most famously Fountain (1917), a porcelain urinal signed 'R. Mutt 1917', rejected by the Society of Independent Artists' exhibition. His complex glass work The Bride Stripped Bare by Her Bachelors, Even (1915–23) and his defacement of a Mona Lisa reproduction (L.H.O.O.Q., 1919) anticipated conceptual art by half a century. He later publicly 'retired' to chess while secretly building his final installation, Étant donnés.
