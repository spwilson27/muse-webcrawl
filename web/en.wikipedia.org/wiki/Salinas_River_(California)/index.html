Salinas River (California)

Salinas River
Length~175 mi (282 km)
SourceSanta Lucia Range, San Luis Obispo County
MouthMonterey Bay, near Marina

The Salinas River is the longest river of the Central Coast region of California, flowing about 175 miles (282 km) north-west through the Salinas Valley before emptying into Monterey Bay near Marina. Unusual among California rivers for its northward course, it drains a largely agricultural valley famed as the "Salad Bowl of the World".

Course

The river rises in the Santa Lucia Range near Santa Margarita in San Luis Obispo County and flows north through Paso Robles, King City, Greenfield, Soledad, Gonzales, Chualar and Salinas, before reaching the Pacific at Monterey Bay. Much of its flow is seasonal and, in dry years, large stretches run underground through the valley's gravel aquifer — prompting the nickname "the upside-down river". Principal tributaries include the Estrella, San Antonio and Nacimiento rivers, the latter two dammed for water supply and flood control.

History and ecology

The valley was home to Salinan and Esselen peoples before Spanish explorers of the Portolá expedition named the river in 1769. John Steinbeck, born in Salinas, set many of his works — including East of Eden — in the valley and along the river. The river and its lagoons support steelhead trout and form the Salinas River National Wildlife Refuge near the mouth, important for migratory birds and the endangered Smith's blue butterfly.
