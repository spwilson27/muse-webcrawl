World language

World language
ExamplesEnglish (present); historically Latin, French, Arabic, Persian
Related termsLingua franca; international language; global language

A world language is a language that is spoken internationally and is learned and used by many people as a second language in addition to their native tongue. A world language need not have the most native speakers; its defining feature is its geographical spread and its use in international communication, diplomacy, trade, science and media.

Historical world languages

Successive languages have played this role in their regions or worldwide: Latin in Europe, Arabic across the Islamic world, Persian in Central and South Asia, and French in European diplomacy from the 17th to the 20th century. Since the mid-20th century, English has become the pre-eminent global language, used as the working language of the United Nations (one of six official languages), international aviation and maritime communication, and much scientific publishing.

Criteria

Sociolinguists point to factors such as the economic and military power of speakers, the number of countries in which the language has official status, its role in international organisations, and the volume of literature and media produced in it. Other widely learned languages, including Spanish, French, Russian, Arabic, Mandarin Chinese and Hindi, function as regional or secondary world languages.

Constructed world languages

Several international auxiliary languages, most notably Esperanto, have been proposed as neutral, easily learned world languages, and organisations such as the United Nations have occasionally considered them.
