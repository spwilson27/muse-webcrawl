Ifa

This article is written without the diacritic used in the Yoruba spelling "Ifá". For the divination system itself as centred on the Yoruba corpus, see also Ifá.

Ifa is the name commonly used in English-language and diaspora sources for the Yoruba system of divination and the religious tradition built around it. The system is centred on the worship of Orunmila, the orisha of wisdom, knowledge, and divination, and on a vast oral corpus of 256 odu chapters memorised by trained priests known as babalawo.

Practice

Consultations are performed for individuals at turning points in life — birth, marriage, illness, or major undertakings. The diviner manipulates sixteen palm nuts or casts the opele chain eight times to determine the sign under which the client falls, then recites the verses attached to that sign. Interpretations are drawn from the stories within the verses, and a prescribed offering, or ebo, may be required.

In the diaspora

Through the Atlantic slave trade, Ifa travelled to Cuba, Brazil, Trinidad, and elsewhere in the Americas, where it merged with Catholic and other influences to form traditions including Santería and Candomblé. Modern practitioners exist worldwide, and Ifa festivals in Nigeria draw international visitors.

Recognition

The Ifa divination system was proclaimed by UNESCO in 2005 as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.
