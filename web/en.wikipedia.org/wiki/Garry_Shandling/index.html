Garry Emmanuel Shandling (November 29, 1949 – March 24, 2016) was an American stand-up comedian, actor, director, writer, and producer. He was best known for his work in It's Garry Shandling's Show (1986–1990) and The Larry Sanders Show (1992–1998), which were among the most influential and critically acclaimed comedy series of their eras.

Early life

Shandling was born in Chicago, Illinois, to Muriel Estelle, a pet store proprietor, and Irving Shandling, a print shop owner. He grew up in Tucson, Arizona, and attended the University of Arizona, initially studying electrical engineering before switching to marketing. After graduating, he moved to Los Angeles and worked at an advertising agency while writing scripts for sitcoms such as Sanford and Son and Welcome Back, Kotter.

Career

Shandling began performing stand-up comedy in 1978. In 1981 he made his first appearance on The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson and soon became one of Carson's most frequent and favored guest hosts, widely considered a leading candidate to succeed Carson before Jay Leno was chosen.

From 1986 to 1990, Shandling starred in It's Garry Shandling's Show, a sitcom on Showtime and later Fox famous for breaking the fourth wall: Shandling played a version of himself who knew he was the star of a television show. The series is regarded as a landmark of meta-television comedy.

His greatest success was The Larry Sanders Show (1992–1998) on HBO, in which he played the neurotic host of a late-night talk show, supported by Jeffrey Tambor as sidekick Hank Kingsley and Rip Torn as producer Artie. The show won three Primetime Emmy Awards and is frequently ranked among the greatest television series of all time; it influenced a generation of comedies about show business and cringe humor.

Shandling also wrote and starred in the film What Planet Are You From? (2000), appeared in movies including Iron Man 2 (2010) and Captain America: The Winter Soldier (2014) as Senator Stern, and voiced Verne the turtle in the animated film Over the Hedge (2006). He hosted the Grammy Awards and the Primetime Emmy Awards several times.

Death and legacy

Shandling died suddenly on March 24, 2016, in Los Angeles, at the age of 66, of a pulmonary embolism caused by a blood clot. The 2018 documentary The Zen Diaries of Garry Shandling, directed by Judd Apatow, explored his life, career, and spiritual practice through his private journals.
