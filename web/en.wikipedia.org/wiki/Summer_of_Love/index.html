The Summer of Love was a social phenomenon that occurred during the summer of 1967, when as many as 100,000 people, mostly young and identifying as part of the hippie counterculture, converged on the Haight-Ashbury neighbourhood of San Francisco. It became a defining moment of the 1960s counterculture, associated with anti-war protest, psychedelic music and art, communal living, and experimentation with drugs.

Background and events

The gathering was preceded by the Human Be-In in Golden Gate Park in January 1967 and popularised by media coverage and Scott McKenzie's hit song "San Francisco (Be Sure to Wear Flowers in Your Hair)". The Monterey International Pop Festival in June 1967 launched stars such as Jimi Hendrix and Janis Joplin. Local groups like the Diggers ran free stores and clinics to support the influx.

Legacy

By autumn, overcrowding, crime, and drug problems had overwhelmed the neighbourhood, and participants staged a mock "Death of the Hippie" funeral in October 1967. The Summer of Love nonetheless left a lasting legacy in popular music, fashion, and attitudes toward the Vietnam War and social norms, and parallel gatherings took place in cities across the United States and Europe.
