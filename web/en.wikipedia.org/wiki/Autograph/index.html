An autograph is a person's own signature, typically that of a celebrity, given to an admirer or collector. Collecting autographs is a popular hobby, pursued in fields from sport and film to politics and space exploration; collectors of signatures are sometimes called philographers. The word also has a scholarly meaning: an "autograph manuscript" is a document written in the author's own hand, as opposed to a copy made by a scribe.

Collecting

Autographs are sought in person at events, stage doors and sports venues, through the post, at conventions, and through dealers and auctions. Signed items range from paper and photographs to baseballs, guitars and film props. The value of an autograph depends on the signer's fame, scarcity, the desirability of the item signed, condition and authenticity. Signatures of historical figures such as William Shakespeare are exceptionally rare — only a handful of examples are known — while those of figures such as Neil Armstrong, who stopped signing in the 1990s, command high prices.

Authentication and forgery

Because of their value, autographs are commonly forged, and a professional authentication industry has developed using forensic handwriting comparison, ink and paper analysis and documented provenance. Certificates of authenticity accompany dealer sales, though their reliability varies. Some celebrity signatures are produced by "autopen" machines, secretaries or printed facsimiles, and collectors learn to distinguish these from genuine hand-signed examples. In music, an "autograph" score in a composer's hand is a primary source for scholars preparing critical editions.

History

The collecting of signatures became fashionable in Europe in the 16th century with the album amicorum (friendship book), in which students collected inscriptions from professors and notables. Modern celebrity autograph culture grew with 19th-century photography, cinema and professional sport, and dealer organisations and price guides appeared in the twentieth century.
