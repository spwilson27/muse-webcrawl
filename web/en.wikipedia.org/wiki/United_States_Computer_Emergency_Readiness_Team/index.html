United States Computer Emergency Readiness Team

The United States Computer Emergency Readiness Team (US-CERT) was a United States government cybersecurity organization within the Department of Homeland Security. Created in 2003 in partnership with the CERT Coordination Center at Carnegie Mellon University, it served as a national point for detecting, analysing and responding to cyber threats and vulnerabilities.

Functions

US-CERT received incident reports from federal agencies, businesses and the public; issued alerts and advisories through the National Cyber Awareness System; analysed malware and vulnerabilities; and coordinated responses between government and critical-infrastructure operators. It contributed to federal network defence activities, including systems for monitoring civilian executive-branch networks. Its publications included technical alerts, vulnerability notes and guidance for home users and system administrators.

Transition to CISA

US-CERT was part of the National Cybersecurity and Communications Integration Center and later the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), established in 2018. Its functions were progressively presented under the CISA name, and the US-CERT brand was retired as CISA consolidated its public reporting channels. The historical US-CERT mission continues in CISA's incident reporting, binding operational directives, alerts and the national risk management functions for federal and critical infrastructure networks.
