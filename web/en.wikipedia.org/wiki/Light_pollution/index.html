Light pollution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A city skyline at night under an orange, washed-out sky with very few visible stars]
Light pollution washes out the night sky over cities.

Light pollution is the presence of excessive or misdirected artificial light in the night environment. It includes sky glow over cities, light trespass into homes, glare, and clutter from bright signage. Light pollution wastes energy, disrupts ecosystems and human circadian rhythms, and hides the stars: much of the world's population now lives under skies too bright to see the Milky Way.

Contents1. Effects
2. Mitigation

Effects

Nocturnal wildlife — insects, birds, bats and nesting sea turtles — is harmed by artificial light at night. Astronomers are especially affected as observatories must be sited in remote dark locations. Studies associate excessive night-time light exposure with sleep disruption in humans.

Mitigation

Shielded, downward-directed fixtures, warmer colour temperatures, dimming and switching off unused lights reduce light pollution. Dark-sky reserves and lighting ordinances have been established in many regions.
