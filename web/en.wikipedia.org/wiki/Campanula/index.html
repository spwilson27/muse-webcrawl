Campanula

Campanula is the type genus of the Campanulaceae family of flowering plants. Campanula are commonly known as bellflowers and are often found in both gardens and the wild. They are characterised by their bell-shaped, usually blue, flowers. The genus includes over 500 species and several subspecies, distributed across the temperate and subtropical regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with the highest diversity in the Mediterranean region east to the Caucasus.

Description

The species include annual, biennial and perennial plants, varying in habit from dwarf arctic and alpine species under 5 cm high to large temperate grassland and woodland species growing to 2 metres tall. The leaves are alternate, often varying in shape on a single plant. The flowers are produced in panicles or singly, with a five-lobed corolla, typically large and blue to purple, sometimes white or pink.

Cultivation and uses

Many species are popular garden plants, including Campanula persicifolia (peach-leaved bellflower), C. medium (Canterbury bells), and C. rotundifolia (harebell). Some species, such as Campanula rapunculus (rampion), were historically grown as vegetables for their roots and leaves. Bellflowers are also associated with folklore and symbolise gratitude and constancy in the language of flowers.
