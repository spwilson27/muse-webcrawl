Matorral

Matorral is a Spanish word, along with tomillares, for shrubland, thicket or bushes. It is used in naming and describing a Mediterranean climate ecosystem in Southern Europe and the Americas. Matorral originally referred to the Mediterranean scrub of central Spain, but the term is now used more broadly for similar shrubland communities elsewhere, most notably the Chilean matorral of central Chile.


Sclerophyllous matorral shrubland in central Chile.

Mediterranean matorral

In the Iberian Peninsula, matorral is a plant community dominated by low, often aromatic shrubs such as rockroses (Cistus), lavender, rosemary, thyme, and broom, typically growing on poor, rocky, or limestone soils. It is often a degradation stage of former evergreen oak forest, maintained by grazing, burning, and clearing, and corresponds broadly to the French garrigue and maquis and the Italian macchia.

Chilean matorral

The Chilean matorral is an ecoregion of central Chile, lying between about 32° and 37° south latitude, between the Pacific Ocean and the Andes. It is one of the world's five Mediterranean-climate regions and a recognised biodiversity hotspot. Its vegetation is an evergreen sclerophyllous shrubland and open woodland of species such as litre (Lithraea caustica), quillay (Quillaja saponaria), peumo (Cryptocarya alba), boldo (Peumus boldus), and the Chilean wine palm (Jubaea chilensis) in sheltered valleys. The ecoregion is heavily modified by agriculture, grazing, and urban expansion around Santiago, and only a small fraction is formally protected.

Other uses

The term is also applied to shrublands in Mexico and other parts of Spanish-speaking America, where it may denote dry thorn scrub, including the tamaulipan matorral of north-eastern Mexico and southern Texas.
