Pramoedya Ananta Toer

Pramoedya Ananta Toer (6 February 1925 – 30 April 2006) was an Indonesian novelist, essayist and historian, regarded as Indonesia's foremost writer. He authored more than 50 works, most famously the Buru Quartet — This Earth of Mankind, Child of All Nations, Footsteps and House of Glass — which chronicles the birth of Indonesian nationalism under Dutch colonial rule.

Imprisonment

Pramoedya was imprisoned by the Dutch during the independence struggle (1947–1949), and again under Suharto's New Order regime: arrested in 1965, he spent 14 years as a political prisoner, most of it in the penal colony on Buru Island, without trial. There, denied writing materials at first, he composed the Buru Quartet orally to fellow prisoners before finally being allowed to write it down. His books were banned in Indonesia until after 1998.

Later life and recognition

Released in 1979 but kept under city arrest and surveillance until the fall of Suharto, Pramoedya received numerous international honours, including the PEN/Barbara Goldsmith Freedom to Write Award, and was repeatedly mentioned as a candidate for the Nobel Prize in Literature.
