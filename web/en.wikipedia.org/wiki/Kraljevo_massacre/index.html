Kraljevo massacre

The Kraljevo massacre was the mass murder of some 2,000 residents of the city of Kraljevo in German-occupied Serbia by the Wehrmacht in October 1941, during the Second World War. The killings were carried out in reprisal for a joint Partisan–Chetnik attack on the German garrison during the Siege of Kraljevo, and were conducted under the order that 100 hostages be shot for every German soldier killed and 50 for every one wounded.


The October 14 Memorial Park in Kraljevo commemorates the victims.

Background

Kraljevo, an important railway junction and industrial town in central Serbia, was occupied by German forces in April 1941. In the autumn of 1941 the Yugoslav Partisans and Chetniks jointly invested the town, attacking the garrison in early October. German forces suffered casualties, including killed and wounded soldiers.

The massacre

Between 15 and 20 October 1941, German units — principally of the 717th Infantry Division — rounded up male inhabitants of the city, including workers from the railway workshops and aircraft factory, schoolboys, refugees and others, and shot them in groups at the railway workshop grounds and other sites. Estimates of the dead generally range from about 1,700 to 2,300; Serbian commemorations commonly cite a figure of over 2,000. The massacres at Kraljevo occurred in the same period as the better-known Kragujevac massacre.

Aftermath and commemoration

The site of the shootings was later turned into the October 14 Memorial Park (Spomen-park 14. oktobar), designed with symbolic stone markers. The massacre is commemorated annually in Kraljevo on 14 October, and the events form part of the broader record of Wehrmacht reprisal killings in occupied Serbia in 1941.
