Lists of integrals

Integration is the basic operation in integral calculus. While a complete table of integrals can be compiled, many functions have antiderivatives that cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions; such integrals are evaluated by series, special functions, or numerical methods. Wikipedia maintains lists of integrals by function type.

Elementary examples

  • ∫ xn dx = xn+1/(n+1) + C (n ≠ −1)
  • ∫ 1/x dx = ln |x| + C
  • ∫ ex dx = ex + C
  • ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C; ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C
  • ∫ dx/(1+x²) = arctan x + C

Historical tables

The standard reference is the table of Gradshteyn and Ryzhik, first published in the Soviet Union in 1943 and repeatedly expanded. Earlier tables date to the 19th century; Joseph Liouville proved in the 1830s that many familiar functions, such as ex², have no elementary antiderivative, a result systematised by the Risch algorithm in the 20th century.
