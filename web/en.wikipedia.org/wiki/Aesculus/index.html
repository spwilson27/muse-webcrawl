The genus Aesculus comprises 13–19 species of deciduous trees and shrubs native to the temperate Northern Hemisphere, with most species in North America and several in Europe and Asia. The best known is the European horse chestnut, Aesculus hippocastanum; North American species are called buckeyes.

Description

Aesculus species have opposite, palmately compound leaves with 5–9 leaflets, and showy upright panicles of white, pink, red or yellow flowers in spring. The fruit is a spiny or smooth capsule splitting to release one or two large glossy brown seeds — "conkers" in Britain. The seeds superficially resemble sweet chestnuts but are unrelated and are poisonous to most animals, containing the saponin aesculin and the glycoside mixture aescin.

Uses and culture

Horse chestnuts are widely planted as ornamental street and park trees, though the European species suffers from the leaf-mining moth Cameraria ohridella and bleeding canker. Extracts of the seed (aescin) are used in herbal medicine for chronic venous insufficiency. In Britain and Ireland children play the traditional game of conkers with the seeds. Ohio is nicknamed the "Buckeye State" after A. glabra.

Selected species

Species include A. hippocastanum (horse chestnut), A. × carnea (red horse chestnut), A. pavia (red buckeye), A. flava (yellow buckeye), A. glabra (Ohio buckeye), A. parviflora (bottlebrush buckeye), A. indica (Indian horse chestnut) and A. chinensis (Chinese horse chestnut).
