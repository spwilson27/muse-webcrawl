Codon usage bias

Codon usage bias refers to differences in the frequency of occurrence of synonymous codons in coding DNA. A codon is a series of three nucleotides (a triplet) that encodes a specific amino acid residue in a polypeptide chain or terminates the translation process. Because most amino acids can be specified by several synonymous codons, genomes differ in how often each is used.

Causes

Bias arises from a combination of mutation pressure (notably GC content), genetic drift, and natural selection for translational efficiency and accuracy. Highly expressed genes typically show the strongest bias, favouring codons that match abundant tRNAs.

Applications

Codon usage analysis is used to predict gene expression levels, identify horizontally transferred genes, and optimise transgenes for expression in heterologous hosts, a process called codon optimisation.
