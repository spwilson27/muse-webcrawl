Squares in London

The squares of London are a distinctive feature of the city's urban landscape: residential garden squares laid out mainly between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries as London expanded westward and northward. Typically, terraced houses enclose a central railed garden, originally for the private use of residents.

History

The earliest London squares, such as Covent Garden (1630s) and Bloomsbury Square, followed continental models. During the Georgian era, great estates like the Bedford and Grosvenor developed squares as the centrepiece of fashionable new districts — Berkeley Square, Grosvenor Square, and Russell Square among them. The London Squares Preservation Act 1931 protected hundreds of squares from development.

Notable squares

Well-known examples include Trafalgar Square, a public square rather than a garden square, Leicester Square, Parliament Square, and the surviving garden squares of Kensington and Chelsea. Many garden squares are now open to the public, and an annual Open Garden Squares Weekend invites visitors into those that remain private.
