Lawfare

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lawfare is the use of the legal system and its principles to achieve a political or military objective, or to damage, delegitimise, or incapacitate an opponent. The term is a portmanteau of law and warfare. It is used both descriptively, for strategic litigation and the exploitation of legal constraints in armed conflict, and pejoratively, for legal actions perceived as frivolous, abusive, or intended to burden an adversary rather than to obtain justice.

History of the term

The word appeared in the 1970s in discussions of law as an alternative to armed struggle. It gained prominence after a 2001 essay by U.S. Air Force officer Charles J. Dunlap Jr., who defined lawfare as "the strategy of using — or misusing — law as a substitute for traditional military means to achieve an operational objective". Dunlap later clarified that lawfare itself is neutral: it can be used by any side, and its evaluation depends on the ends to which it is put.

Usage

In international relations, lawfare describes such practices as bringing cases before international tribunals, using domestic universal-jurisdiction statutes against foreign officials, and framing military operations to exploit an adversary's legal constraints. In domestic politics, the term is applied to strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs), serial complaints intended to exhaust an opponent's resources, and the alleged instrumentalisation of prosecutions against political figures. Because the label is contested, commentators often disagree over whether a given case is lawfare or legitimate law enforcement.
