SPOT (French: Satellite Pour l'Observation de la Terre, "Satellite for observation of Earth") is a series of French Earth observation satellites operated commercially by Spot Image and later Airbus Defence and Space. Initiated by the French space agency CNES in the late 1970s, the programme was developed with participation from Belgium and Sweden. SPOT 1 was launched on 22 February 1986 aboard an Ariane rocket, becoming one of the first systems to sell satellite imagery commercially on a large scale.

Design

The early SPOT satellites carried two identical HRV (High Resolution Visible) push-broom scanners that could be pointed off-nadir, allowing the satellite to revisit a site much more frequently than its 26-day orbital repeat cycle and to acquire stereoscopic image pairs for terrain modelling. SPOT 1–3 offered 10 m panchromatic and 20 m multispectral resolution; SPOT 4 added a shortwave infrared band and the VEGETATION instrument for global monitoring; SPOT 5 improved resolution to 2.5–5 m panchromatic.

SPOT 6 and 7

SPOT 6 (2012) and SPOT 7 (2014) were built by Airbus Defence and Space on a new platform, providing 1.5 m panchromatic imagery over wide 60 km swaths. Together with the very-high-resolution Pléiades satellites they form a constellation able to image points on Earth daily. SPOT imagery is used in cartography, agriculture, forestry, land-use planning, defence and disaster monitoring, including through the International Charter on Space and Major Disasters.
