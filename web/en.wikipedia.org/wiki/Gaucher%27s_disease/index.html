Gaucher's disease

SpecialtyMedical genetics
InheritanceAutosomal recessive
GeneGBA (glucocerebrosidase), chromosome 1
TypesType 1 (non-neuronopathic), Types 2 and 3 (neuronopathic)

Gaucher's disease is the most common of the lysosomal storage diseases. It is an autosomal-recessive inherited disorder in which the fatty substance glucocerebroside accumulates in cells of the liver, spleen, bone marrow, and (in some forms) the brain, because of deficiency of the enzyme glucocerebrosidase. It was first described by the French physician Philippe Gaucher in 1882.

Signs and symptoms

Type 1, the most common form, causes enlargement of the spleen and liver, anaemia and low platelets (with easy bruising), bone pain and fractures, and fatigue; it does not primarily affect the brain. Types 2 and 3 involve the nervous system: type 2 is rapidly fatal in infancy, while type 3 progresses more slowly with abnormal eye movements and seizures. The disease is more prevalent among Ashkenazi Jews, in whom type 1 occurs in roughly 1 in 850 births.

Treatment

Since 1991, enzyme replacement therapy with recombinant glucocerebrosidase (such as imiglucerase) has effectively treated visceral and blood manifestations; oral substrate reduction therapies such as eliglustat are also available. Gaucher's disease is notable in medical economics as one of the earliest and most expensive enzyme replacement treatments.
