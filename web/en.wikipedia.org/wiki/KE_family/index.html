KE family

The KE family is a large British family, anonymised in the scientific literature by the initials "KE", whose members are affected by a hereditary speech and language disorder. About half of the family's members across several generations have a severe developmental verbal dyspraxia (childhood apraxia of speech) together with broader language impairments.

Investigation of the family by geneticists at the University of Oxford in the 1990s led in 2001 to the identification of a mutation in the FOXP2 gene on chromosome 7, inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion. FOXP2 became widely known as the first gene implicated in speech and language, and the KE family remains a landmark case in the genetics of language.

Affected members have particular difficulty with sequences of orofacial movements and with grammatical production and comprehension, while unaffected members develop normally.
