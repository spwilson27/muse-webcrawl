Superoxide dismutase

EC number1.15.1.1
Reaction2 O2•− + 2 H+ → H2O2 + O2
IsozymesCuZnSOD (SOD1, SOD3), MnSOD (SOD2), FeSOD, NiSOD

Superoxide dismutase (SOD) is an enzyme that alternately catalyzes the dismutation (or partitioning) of the superoxide (O2•−) radical into either ordinary molecular oxygen (O2) or hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). Superoxide is produced as a by-product of oxygen metabolism and, if not regulated, causes oxidative damage to cells. Thus, SOD is an important antioxidant defense in nearly all living cells exposed to oxygen. It was discovered in 1968 by Joe M. McCord and Irwin Fridovich, who recognised that the copper protein previously called erythrocuprein had enzymatic activity.

Reaction and mechanism

The metal ion at the active site cycles between oxidation states: the oxidised enzyme takes an electron from one superoxide, releasing O2, and the reduced enzyme donates an electron to a second superoxide, which with two protons forms hydrogen peroxide. The reaction proceeds at close to the diffusion limit (around 2 × 109 M−1s−1 for CuZnSOD), making SOD among the fastest enzymes known. The hydrogen peroxide produced is subsequently removed by catalase or glutathione peroxidase.

Types

  • Copper–zinc SOD — SOD1 in the cytosol of eukaryotes (and the chloroplasts of plants); SOD3 (EC-SOD) is a tetrameric extracellular form.
  • Manganese SOD — SOD2, found in mitochondria and many bacteria.
  • Iron SOD — found in bacteria and plant chloroplasts.
  • Nickel SOD — found in some bacteria, notably Streptomyces.

Physiology and disease

SOD works with catalase and peroxidases as the cell's antioxidant defence system. Mutations in the human SOD1 gene cause a subset (roughly 15–20%) of familial amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), apparently through a toxic gain of function involving protein misfolding. SOD2 knockout mice die early with severe oxidative damage, underscoring the enzyme's essential role, and SOD activity is studied in relation to ageing, inflammation, ischaemia–reperfusion injury and cancer. Orgotein, a pharmaceutical preparation of bovine CuZnSOD, was formerly used as an anti-inflammatory drug.
