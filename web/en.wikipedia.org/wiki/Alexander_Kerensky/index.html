Alexander Kerensky

Alexander Fyodorovich Kerensky (4 May 1881 – 11 June 1970) was a Russian lawyer and revolutionary who led the Russian Provisional Government in 1917. A member of the Socialist Revolutionary Party and the most prominent moderate socialist of the Russian Revolution, he served as Minister of Justice, then Minister of War, and finally Minister-Chairman (prime minister) from July 1917 until the Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution.

Career in 1917

Kerensky rose to fame as a defence lawyer in political trials and as a Duma deputy investigating the Lena goldfields massacre of 1912. After the February Revolution he became the only socialist in the first Provisional Government and, simultaneously, vice-chairman of the Petrograd Soviet — the bridge between the "dual power" institutions. As Minister of War he launched the June 1917 (Kerensky) Offensive, which collapsed disastrously. Becoming head of government in July, he proclaimed Russia a republic, survived the Kornilov affair — in which he armed the very Red Guards he would soon face — and was overthrown by Lenin's Bolsheviks on 7 November 1917 (25 October, old style), escaping Petrograd as the Winter Palace fell.

Exile

After a failed attempt to rally troops with General Krasnov, Kerensky lived in exile in Paris, London, Australia and finally the United States, writing memoirs and lecturing — including at Stanford's Hoover Institution — while remaining a vocal critic of the Soviet regime. He died in New York in 1970, the last surviving leader of the Provisional Government era.
