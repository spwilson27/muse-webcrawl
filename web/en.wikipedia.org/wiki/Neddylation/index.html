Neddylation (also spelled neddylation and sometimes called RUBylation in plants) is a post-translational modification in which the ubiquitin-like protein NEDD8 is conjugated to target proteins. The process is analogous to ubiquitination, but uses its own set of enzymes. NEDD8 is the ubiquitin-like protein most closely related to ubiquitin itself, sharing about 60% amino-acid identity with it in humans.

Mechanism

NEDD8 is synthesised as a precursor and processed to expose a C-terminal glycine. It is then activated in an ATP-dependent reaction by the NEDD8-activating enzyme (NAE), a heterodimer of NAE1 (APPBP1) and UBA3, transferred to one of two E2 conjugating enzymes, UBE2M (UBC12) or UBE2F, and finally ligated to a lysine residue of the substrate, often with the help of E3 ligases such as RBX1/RBX2 and DCN1-family co-E3s. The modification is reversible: the COP9 signalosome (through its CSN5/JAB1 subunit) and the cysteine protease NEDP1 (also called DEN1 or SENP8) remove NEDD8 from substrates.

Substrates and function

The best-characterised substrates are the cullin scaffold proteins of the cullin-RING ubiquitin ligases (CRLs), the largest family of E3 ubiquitin ligases. Neddylation of a conserved lysine near the cullin C-terminus induces a conformational change that activates the ligase, greatly enhancing ubiquitination of CRL substrates and hence their proteasomal degradation. Through CRLs, neddylation regulates the cell cycle, signal transduction, transcription and DNA repair. A growing list of non-cullin substrates, including p53, MDM2, ribosomal proteins and EGFR, has also been reported.

Clinical significance

Because neddylation is hyperactivated in many cancers, the pathway is a drug target. The NAE inhibitor pevonedistat (MLN4924) blocks the entire cascade and has been investigated in clinical trials for myelodysplastic syndromes, acute myeloid leukaemia and solid tumours. Disruption of neddylation in model organisms is lethal in most animals, reflecting its essential role, and mutations affecting the pathway are associated with developmental defects.
