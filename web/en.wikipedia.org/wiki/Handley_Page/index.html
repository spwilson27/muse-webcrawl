Handley Page Limited was a British aircraft manufacturer founded in 1909 by Frederick Handley Page. Based at Cricklewood in London and later at Radlett aerodrome in Hertfordshire, it was one of the pioneering companies of British aviation, noted for large bombers and transport aircraft, and for the invention of the leading-edge slot.

History

Frederick Handley Page established the company at Barking in 1909 and soon moved to Cricklewood. During the First World War its O/100 and O/400 heavy bombers were the Royal Flying Corps' principal strategic bombing types, and the related V/1500 was designed to bomb Berlin. Between the wars the company's big biplane airliners, such as the H.P.42 Hannibal class, became emblems of Imperial Airways' empire routes.

Second World War and after

In the Second World War Handley Page built the Hampden medium bomber and, above all, the Halifax, one of the RAF's three four-engined heavy bombers, of which more than 6,000 were produced. Post-war products included the Hermes and Hastings transports, the Victor — the most futuristic of the three V-bombers, later converted to a tanker — the Herald airliner, and the Jetstream turboprop.

Decline

Refusing to merge during the government-driven consolidation of the British industry around 1960, Handley Page was starved of official orders. Sir Frederick died in 1962, and despite American interest in the Jetstream, the company went into voluntary liquidation in 1970. The Jetstream design survived under other manufacturers, eventually becoming the British Aerospace Jetstream family.
