The German Empire (Deutsches Kaiserreich) was the German nation state that existed from the unification of Germany in 1871 until the abdication of Emperor Wilhelm II in November 1918. It consisted of 26 states, dominated by the Kingdom of Prussia, whose king served as German Emperor. Proclaimed at Versailles during the Franco-Prussian War, the Empire rapidly became continental Europe's leading industrial and military power.

The Bismarck era

Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, architect of unification through the wars of 1864–1871, dominated the Empire's first two decades. Domestically he fought the Catholic Church in the Kulturkampf and repressed the Social Democrats under the Anti-Socialist Laws, while simultaneously creating Europe's first modern welfare state with health, accident and old-age insurance. Abroad, his alliance system — centred on the League of the Three Emperors and the Dual Alliance with Austria-Hungary — aimed to isolate France and preserve the new order. Bismarck was dismissed by the young Wilhelm II in 1890.

Wilhelmine Germany and collapse

Under Wilhelm II, Germany pursued Weltpolitik: a colonial empire in Africa and the Pacific, a battle fleet rivalling Britain's, and a more assertive foreign policy that contributed to the tensions leading to the First World War. Defeat in 1918, following the failure of the Spring Offensive and the naval mutiny at Kiel, triggered the German Revolution; Wilhelm II abdicated on 9 November 1918 and the Weimar Republic was proclaimed. The Empire at its peak had a population of about 65 million (1910) and was a world leader in science, chemistry and engineering. See also Otto von Bismarck, World War I and Weimar Republic.
