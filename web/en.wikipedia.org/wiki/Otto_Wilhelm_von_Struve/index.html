Otto Wilhelm von Struve

Otto Wilhelm von Struve
Born: 7 May 1819, Dorpat (Tartu), Russian Empire
Died: 14 April 1905, Karlsruhe, German Empire
Field: Astronomy
Known for: Double star observations; director of Pulkovo Observatory

Otto Wilhelm von Struve (May 7, 1819 – April 14, 1905) was a Russian astronomer of Baltic German origin. In Russian his name is normally given as Otto Vasil'evich Struve (Отто Васильевич Струве). Together with his father, Friedrich Georg Wilhelm von Struve, he is considered the founder of a dynasty of astronomers that spanned four generations.

Life and career

Struve was born at Dorpat (now Tartu, Estonia), where his father directed the university observatory. He studied at the University of Dorpat and began assisting his father in the great double-star survey that made the family name famous. After graduating he joined the staff of the new Pulkovo Observatory near St Petersburg, then the world's leading astronomical institution, and in 1862 succeeded his father as its director, a post he held until 1889.

Scientific work

Struve's lifelong speciality was the measurement of double stars, following in the field his father pioneered. Using Pulkovo's 15-inch refractor, he discovered more than 500 new double and multiple systems and published definitive catalogues of star positions and binary measurements in the Observations de Poulkova. He also worked on the satellites of the outer planets, observed the solar system bodies and the ring of Saturn, and determined the constant of precession with great precision.

As director he maintained Pulkovo's position at the forefront of astrometry, acquiring the 30-inch refractor — for a time the largest telescope in the world — and overseeing the observatory's role as the centre of the Russian geodetic and time services.

Family and honours

His sons Hermann Struve and Ludwig Struve continued the family tradition in astronomy. Struve received the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1850. The lunar crater Struve is named jointly for him and other members of the family. He died in Karlsruhe in 1905.
