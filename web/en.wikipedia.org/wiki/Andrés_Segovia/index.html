Andrés Segovia

Andrés Segovia Torres
Born: 21 February 1893, Linares, Spain
Died: 2 June 1987, Madrid, Spain
Occupation: Classical guitarist
Known for: Establishing the guitar as a concert instrument
Segovia, the foremost classical guitarist of the 20th century.

Andrés Segovia Torres, 1st Marquis of Salobreña (21 February 1893 – 2 June 1987) was a Spanish virtuoso classical guitarist. Many professional classical guitarists today were either students of Segovia or of his students. His career is credited with elevating the guitar from an instrument of popular and folk music to a respected vehicle for serious concert repertoire.

Career

Largely self-taught, Segovia made his debut in Granada at sixteen. His Paris debut in 1924 and his New York debut at Town Hall in 1928 won over audiences and critics who had doubted the guitar's concert potential. To enlarge the repertoire he transcribed works by Bach and other composers and commissioned new pieces from Heitor Villa-Lobos, Joaquín Rodrigo (whose famous Concierto de Aranjuez exemplifies the era, though written for Regino Sainz de la Maza), Mario Castelnuovo-Tedesco, Manuel Ponce, and Federico Moreno Torroba.

Teaching and legacy

Segovia's master classes, notably at the Accademia Chigiana in Siena, shaped generations of guitarists including John Williams, Julian Bream, Christopher Parkening, and Alirio Díaz. He advocateded nylon strings, which replaced gut after the Second World War, and a fingernail-and-flesh technique producing his distinctive warm tone. He continued performing into his nineties and died in Madrid in 1987.
