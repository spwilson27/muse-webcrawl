American Cocker Spaniel

The American Cocker Spaniel is a breed of sporting dog. It is a spaniel type dog that is closely related to the English Cocker Spaniel; the two breeds diverged in the 20th century due to differing breed standards in the United States and the United Kingdom. In the United States, the breed is usually called simply the Cocker Spaniel.

Appearance

The American Cocker is the smallest of the sporting spaniels, typically standing about 34–39 cm (13.5–15.5 in) at the withers and weighing roughly 9–14 kg (20–30 lb). It has a rounded head, a short muzzle, long drooping ears, and a profuse, silky coat that comes in black, buff, red, brown, and parti-colour patterns. The coat requires regular grooming.

History

Spaniels arrived in North America in the 19th century, and by the 1920s–1940s American breeders had developed a smaller, more domed-headed dog than the English type. The American Kennel Club recognised the breeds as separate in 1946. Cockers became enormously popular in the United States, ranking as the AKC's most registered breed for many years, helped by famous examples such as the cartoon and film dog Lady in Disney's Lady and the Tramp (1955).

Temperament and health

The breed standard describes a merry, equable temperament and a dog that is eager to please, though popularity-driven breeding produced some lines with behavioural and health problems. Common health issues include ear infections, eye conditions such as cataracts and glaucoma, hip dysplasia, and autoimmune disorders. Average lifespan is about 10–14 years.
