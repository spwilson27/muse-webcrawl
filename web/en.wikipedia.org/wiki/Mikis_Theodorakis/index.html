Mikis Theodorakis

Mikis Theodorakis
Born: 29 July 1925, Chios, Greece
Died: 2 September 2021, Athens
Occupations: Composer, politician
Known for: Zorba the Greek, Canto General

Michail "Mikis" Theodorakis (29 July 1925 – 2 September 2021) was a Greek composer and lyricist who became an international symbol of Greek culture and resistance. His vast output spans symphonies, operas, ballets, song cycles and more than a thousand songs.

Music

Theodorakis brought Greek folk instruments, especially the bouzouki, into art music and popular song. His score for the film Zorba the Greek (1964) — including the famous "Zorba's Dance", the sirtaki — made him world-famous. Other major works include the oratorio Canto General on poems by Pablo Neruda, the ballad cycles on poetry by George Seferis and Odysseas Elytis, and scores for Serpico and State of Siege.

Politics

A leftist activist, he was imprisoned and tortured during the Greek Civil War era and again exiled by the military junta of 1967–1974, which banned his music. After democracy returned he served as a member of parliament and minister, and remained a vocal public figure into old age.
