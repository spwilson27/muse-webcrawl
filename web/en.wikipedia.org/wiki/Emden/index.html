Emden

Country: Germany
State: Lower Saxony
Region: East Frisia
Population: approx. 50,000

Emden is a town and seaport in the northwestern part of the German state of Lower Saxony, in the region of East Frisia, on the river Ems. It is the main port city of the Ems estuary region near the Dutch border and the North Sea.

History

The exact founding date of Emden is unknown, but it has existed at least since the 8th century. The town grew as a trading port and received city rights in the 15th century; its coat of arms and motto were granted by Emperor Maximilian I in 1495. In the 16th century Emden became a center of the Protestant Reformation and a refuge for Dutch religious refugees, and for a time it rivalled larger ports in shipping volume.

Economy

Modern Emden is a major port for automobile exports, hosting a large Volkswagen plant, and is a significant site for shipbuilding (historically the Nordseewerke yard) and offshore wind energy servicing.

Sights

Landmarks include the reconstructed Renaissance town hall, which houses the East Frisian State Museum and the Emden Armoury (Rüstkammer), the Kunsthalle Emden art museum, and the lightship and museum ships in the harbor.
