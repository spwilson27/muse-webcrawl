Scalping (trading)

Scalping, in the arbitrage sense, is a trading strategy in which a trader attempts to profit from small price changes, opening and closing positions within seconds or minutes. Scalpers aim to make many small gains that, accumulated over hundreds or thousands of trades, outweigh occasional losses. It is one of the most short-term styles of day trading.

Method

Scalping depends on liquidity and tight bid–ask spreads: the scalper buys at the bid and sells at the ask (or vice versa), capturing the spread, or trades breakouts of very short-term momentum on one-minute or tick charts. Success requires low commissions, fast execution, real-time level-2 market data and strict risk discipline — a single large adverse move can erase many small wins. Many scalpers set a maximum holding time and exit positions mechanically regardless of profit or loss.

Markets and automation

Scalping is common in foreign exchange, index futures (such as the E-mini S&P 500) and large-cap equities. Because the style requires speed beyond human capacity, modern scalping is heavily automated: high-frequency trading firms run algorithms that perform similar spread-capture and microstructure strategies at microsecond timescales. Some brokers and jurisdictions restrict or discourage scalping because of the heavy message traffic it generates.
