Pipe band

A pipe band on parade, pipers in front and drummers behind.

A pipe band is a musical ensemble consisting of bagpipers and drummers. The standard Scottish pipe band combines a pipe corps playing the Great Highland bagpipe with a drum corps of snare (side) drummers, tenor drummers and a bass drummer. The band is led by the pipe major, and on parade by a drum major wielding a mace.

History

Pipe bands grew out of the military: during the nineteenth century regiments of the British Army stationed pipers and drummers together, and by the time of the First World War the pipe band was established as the characteristic musical unit of Scottish regiments. After the war, civilian bands spread through Scotland and the Scottish diaspora — Canada, Australia, New Zealand and beyond.

Music and competition

The band repertoire includes marches, strathspeys and reels — including the formal March, Strathspey and Reel (MSR) set — and medley selections. Because all chanters are alike, pipers play in unison, the harmony coming from the drones and the intricate drumming, which is written to complement the pipe music. Bands are graded (Grade 1 to Grade 4/5 and juvenile grades) and compete at Highland games and championships, culminating in the World Pipe Band Championships, held in Glasgow each August and dominated in recent decades by bands such as Field Marshal Montgomery, Shotts and Dykehead, Simon Fraser University and Inveraray & District.

Irish pipe bands use the same instrumentation; other traditions, such as Breton bagads, use related but distinct line-ups.
