Agnelli family

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gianni Agnelli (1921–2003), longtime head of Fiat

The Agnelli family is an Italian multi-industry business dynasty, founders and principal shareholders of Fiat, Italy's largest automobile manufacturer. Sometimes described as Italy's "royal family" of industry, the Agnellis have exercised an influence on Italian economic, political, and cultural life comparable to that of a reigning house, controlling interests that have included automobiles, media, finance, and the football club Juventus.

Origins

The family's industrial fortune begins with Giovanni Agnelli (1866–1945), who in 1899 was among the founders of Fabbrica Italiana Automobili Torino (Fiat) and became its dominant figure. His son Edoardo (1892–1935) died young in an air accident, leaving the succession to his son Gianni Agnelli (1921–2003), known as l'Avvocato ("the Lawyer"), who led Fiat from 1966 and became the emblematic figure of postwar Italian capitalism.

Holdings

Through the family holding companies — historically the Istituto Finanziario Industriale (IFI) and IFIL, today merged in Exor — the family controls stakes in Stellantis (the successor to Fiat Chrysler), Ferrari, CNH Industrial, Juventus, and the media group GEDI, among others. After Gianni Agnelli's death, leadership passed to his grandson John Elkann, chairman of Stellantis and Exor.

Influence and culture

Members of the family have served as senators for life, mayors of Turin, and patrons of the arts. The family's tragedies — including the early deaths of Edoardo Agnelli (1935), of Gianni's son Edoardo (2000), and of his nephew and designated heir Giovanni Alberto Agnelli (1997) — have been extensively chronicled in Italian public life.
