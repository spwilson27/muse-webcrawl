Omalo (Georgian: ომალო) is a principal village in the mountainous historical region of Tusheti in northeastern Georgia. Situated between the Greater Caucasus and the Pirikita ranges at about 1,880 metres above sea level, it serves as the administrative and visitor centre of the Tusheti National Park and the gateway for travel within the region.

Geography and access

Omalo is divided into an upper old village, crowned by the Keselo fortress towers, and a lower, newer part with guesthouses and the park visitor centre. It is reached from the Kakheti lowlands by a single mountain road over the Abano Pass (2,850 m), built in the 1980s and open only from roughly June to October; the road is regularly described as one of the most dramatic and dangerous in Georgia. In winter the village is cut off and most residents move to villages in Kakheti, leaving only a handful of families and border guards year-round.

History and culture

The Tush people, Georgian highlanders traditionally engaged in transhumant sheep herding, fortified Omalo against raids from Dagestan and Chechnya; the Keselo towers, built in the Middle Ages, sheltered families during attacks. Tusheti's isolation preserved distinctive customs, dialect, and a syncretic folk Christianity with surviving pagan elements — women are still traditionally barred from certain shrines. Tusheti was emptied by Soviet resettlements in the 1950s but repopulated seasonally thereafter. The 1975 film Mimino, beloved across the former Soviet Union, popularized the region's landscapes. Today Omalo is the trailhead for trekking routes to Dartlo, Shenako, and Diklo and the hub of a growing guesthouse tourism.
