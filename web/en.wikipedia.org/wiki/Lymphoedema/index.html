Lymphoedema

Lymphoedema (American spelling lymphedema) is a chronic condition of localised swelling caused by a compromised lymphatic system, in which lymph fluid accumulates in the tissues, most commonly of an arm or leg. The swelling may range from mild enlargement to severe disfigurement.

Causes

Primary lymphoedema results from developmental abnormalities of the lymph vessels and may be present at birth (as in Milroy's disease) or appear later. Secondary lymphoedema is far more common and follows damage to or obstruction of the lymphatics: worldwide the leading cause is filariasis, a parasitic infection; in developed countries it most often follows cancer treatment involving lymph node removal or radiotherapy, as with breast-cancer-related arm swelling.

Management

There is no cure, but the condition can be managed with complete decongestive therapy: manual lymphatic drainage, multilayer compression bandaging and garments, meticulous skin care, and exercise. Prompt treatment of bacterial cellulitis, a frequent complication, is important. Surgical options, including lymphaticovenular anastomosis and vascularised lymph node transfer, are used in selected cases. Long-standing untreated lymphoedema can, rarely, be complicated by a lymphangiosarcoma (Stewart–Treves syndrome).
