Luca Bartolomeo de Pacioli (c. 1447 – 19 June 1517) was an Italian mathematician, Franciscan friar, and a collaborator of Leonardo da Vinci. He is known as the "father of accounting" for publishing the first printed description of the double-entry bookkeeping system used by Venetian merchants.

Life

Pacioli was born in Sansepolcro in Tuscany, where he likely studied under the painter-mathematician Piero della Francesca. He worked as a mathematics teacher in Venice, Rome, and other Italian cities, joined the Franciscan order, and held lectureships at several universities. At the court of Ludovico Sforza in Milan he befriended Leonardo da Vinci, who illustrated his later work.

Works

His Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita (1494) was the first printed book on algebra and included his famous account of double-entry bookkeeping — journals, ledgers, and the closing of accounts — which spread the Venetian method across Europe. His De divina proportione (published 1509, with illustrations by Leonardo) explored the golden ratio in mathematics, art, and architecture. He also wrote on chess; a rediscovered manuscript of his chess problems is among the oldest known works on the game.

Legacy

Pacioli's codification of double-entry accounting underpins modern accounting practice, and his works mark an early union of practical commerce and Renaissance mathematics.
