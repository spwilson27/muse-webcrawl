Four Sacred Mountains (Buddhism)

The Four Sacred Mountains (Chinese: 四大佛教名山) of Buddhism in China are four mountains sacred to Chinese Buddhism, each associated with a particular bodhisattva. They are among the most important pilgrimage sites in Chinese Buddhism.

The four mountains

  • Mount Wutai in Shanxi, associated with Mañjuśrī (Wenshu), bodhisattva of wisdom.
  • Mount Putuo in Zhejiang, an island mountain associated with Avalokiteśvara (Guanyin), bodhisattva of compassion.
  • Mount Emei in Sichuan, associated with Samantabhadra (Puxian), bodhisattva of practice and meditation.
  • Mount Jiuhua in Anhui, associated with Kṣitigarbha (Dizang), bodhisattva of the underworld and of vows.

Significance

Each mountain hosts major temple complexes at its summit and slopes and has been a pilgrimage destination for well over a thousand years. Together with the Five Sacred Mountains of Daoism-influenced Chinese tradition, they form the core of China's sacred geography. Mount Wutai and Mount Emei are also UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
