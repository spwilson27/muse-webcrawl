Galatea (moon)

Galatea (Neptune VI)
Discovery: 28 July 1989, by Stephen P. Synnott and the Voyager Imaging Team
Orbital period: 0.429 days
Dimensions: approx. 204 × 184 × 144 km
Mean radius: ~88 km

Galatea /ɡæləˈtiːə/, also known as Neptune VI, is the fourth-closest inner moon of Neptune. It is named after Galatea, one of the fifty Nereids of Greek mythology, with whom the cyclops Polyphemus was vainly in love.

Discovery and orbit

Galatea was discovered in late July 1989 from images taken by the Voyager 2 probe; it received the temporary designation S/1989 N 4 and was formally named on 16 September 1991. It orbits Neptune in a nearly circular, equatorial orbit with a semi-major axis of about 61,950 km, just inside the orbit of Larissa, with an orbital period of about 0.43 days. Galatea lies below Neptune's synchronous orbit radius, so its orbit is slowly decaying due to tidal forces and it may eventually break up into a planetary ring or impact the planet.

Physical characteristics

Galatea is irregularly shaped and shows no sign of geological modification. Like the other inner Neptunian moons discovered by Voyager 2, it is very dark, with a low albedo, and is likely a re-accreted rubble pile formed from fragments of Neptune's original satellites that were disrupted when Triton was captured.

Ring shepherding

Galatea orbits just inside Neptune's brightest ring, the Adams ring. Its 42:43 corotation inclination resonance is considered the most likely mechanism confining the Adams ring's distinctive arcs.
