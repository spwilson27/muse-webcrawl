Exclusive Brethren

The Exclusive Brethren are a group of Christian evangelical churches that arose, along with the Open Brethren, from the Plymouth Brethren movement founded in Dublin in the late 1820s. The exclusives are distinguished by a doctrine of separation from evil, which led them to practise closed communion and, in the stricter branches, to limit social contact — including eating — with those outside the fellowship, and even with members under discipline.

History

The split between Open and Exclusive Brethren followed the Bethesda controversy of 1848, in which John Nelson Darby accused the Bethesda chapel in Bristol, led by George Müller and Henry Craik, of tolerating doctrinal error. Darby's followers formed exclusive assemblies that required separation from any fellowship deemed to be in error. Under the leadership of James Taylor and later James Taylor Jr. in the twentieth century, the largest exclusive group tightened its rules considerably; in 1970 an "Aberdeen incident" involving Taylor Jr. caused a major schism.

The largest body now uses the name Plymouth Brethren Christian Church. Smaller exclusive groups include the Raven–Taylor–Hales stream and various independent assemblies. Estimates of membership worldwide are in the tens of thousands, with significant communities in the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, North America, and Europe.

Beliefs and practices

Exclusive Brethren hold to the authority of Scripture, salvation by faith, and the weekly observance of the Lord's Supper (the "breaking of bread"). Their separation doctrine, drawn from passages such as 2 Timothy 2:19–21 and 2 Corinthians 6:14–18, historically extended to refusing membership of trade unions and professional associations, limits on dining with non-members, and in some eras restrictions on television, radio, and university education. Practices vary between branches, and several have moderated in recent decades; members run many businesses and schools, including the OneSchool Global network associated with the Plymouth Brethren Christian Church.
