Neutron capture

Neutron capture is a nuclear reaction in which an atomic nucleus collides with one or more neutrons and merges with them to form a heavier nucleus. It plays an important role in the nucleosynthesis of elements heavier than iron and in the operation of nuclear reactors.

Process

After capturing a neutron, the compound nucleus is often left in an excited state and releases the excess energy as gamma radiation. The probability of capture is described by the neutron cross section and is usually much higher for slow (thermal) neutrons than for fast ones.

Astrophysical nucleosynthesis

Two capture processes build heavy elements in stars: the slow s-process, in which beta decay keeps pace with capture, and the rapid r-process, which occurs in extreme neutron-rich environments such as neutron star mergers and some supernovae.

Applications

Neutron capture is exploited in nuclear reactors (where neutron-absorbing control materials regulate the reaction), in neutron activation analysis, and in boron neutron capture therapy for certain cancers.
