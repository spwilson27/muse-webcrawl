Christopher Sean Lowe (born 4 October 1959) is an English musician, songwriter and co-founder of the synth-pop duo Pet Shop Boys, which he formed with Neil Tennant in 1981. Lowe provides keyboards, programming and backing vocals, and is known for his deadpan stage presence, rarely speaking or smiling in public performances.

[Hero image: Chris Lowe performing with Pet Shop Boys — image placeholder]

Early life

Lowe was born in Blackpool, Lancashire, and attended Arnold School. He studied architecture at the University of Liverpool and was working on an architecture placement in London when he met Neil Tennant by chance in an electronics shop on the King's Road in August 1981. Sharing an interest in electronic dance music, the two began writing together.

Pet Shop Boys

With Tennant, Lowe has written and produced a string of international hits including "West End Girls", "It's a Sin", "Always on My Mind" and "Go West". The duo have sold more than 100 million records worldwide and are recognised as the most successful duo in UK music history. Lowe studied architecture and reportedly designed the staircase in one early band rehearsal space; his interest in design is reflected in the group's visual presentation.

Solo work

Outside the group, Lowe wrote the music for the football anthem used by Arsenal — "London" changed to a chant — and composed the song "Streets of Berlin" for the 1990s revival of Bent. He has also made a cameo appearance in the Australian soap Neighbours.
