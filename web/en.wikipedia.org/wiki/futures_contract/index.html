Futures contract

In finance, a futures contract is a standardized legal contract to buy or sell something at a predetermined price for delivery at a specified time in the future, between parties not yet known to each other. The asset transacted is usually a commodity or financial instrument.

TypeDerivative
Traded onFutures exchanges (e.g. CME, ICE)
SettlementDaily mark-to-market; cash or physical delivery

Standardization and margin

Futures contracts specify the quality and quantity of the underlying asset and are traded on exchanges, which act as counterparties through a clearing house. Traders post an initial margin and positions are marked to market daily, with variation margin exchanged to cover gains and losses.

Uses

Futures are used by hedgers, such as farmers or airlines, to lock in prices, and by speculators seeking leveraged exposure. Most positions are closed out before delivery.

History

Organized futures trading developed in the nineteenth century, notably at the Chicago Board of Trade (founded 1848), initially for agricultural commodities.
