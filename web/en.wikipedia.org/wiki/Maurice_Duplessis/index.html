Maurice Le Noblet Duplessis (20 April 1890 – 7 September 1959) was a Canadian lawyer and politician who served as the 16th premier of Quebec. Leader of the conservative Union Nationale party, he governed from 1936 to 1939 and again from 1944 until his death in 1959, dominating Quebec politics for nearly two decades. Known as "le Chef" ("the Boss"), he remains one of the most controversial figures in Quebec history.

Premiership

Duplessis first came to power in 1936 on promises of reform, but his first term was marked by the 1937 Padlock Law, which allowed the closure of premises used for "communist propaganda" and was widely used against labour unions and dissenters. After losing the 1939 election, he returned to power in 1944 and held it through four more elections.

His governments promoted rural electrification, road building, and economic development by foreign (especially American) capital, while resisting federal centralisation and defending provincial autonomy. Duplessis maintained a close alliance with the Roman Catholic Church, opposed organised labour — breaking strikes such as the 1949 Asbestos strike by force — and used patronage extensively. His regime persecuted Jehovah's Witnesses, leading to landmark civil-liberties cases such as Roncarelli v. Duplessis (1959), in which the Supreme Court of Canada ruled against him personally.

Legacy

Critics dubbed his era the Grande Noirceur ("Great Darkness"), a term popularised by his opponents after his death during the Quiet Revolution. More recent historians have offered a more nuanced assessment, noting his nationalism, defence of provincial jurisdiction, and infrastructure achievements alongside his authoritarianism and corruption.
