Scenic design

A scenic designer's model box showing a stage set design

Scenic design (also known as scenography, set design, or production design in film) is the creation of theatrical, film, or television scenery. Scenic designers come from a variety of artistic backgrounds, but in recent decades are mostly trained professionals, often holding MFA degrees. The scenic designer works with the director and other designers — lighting, costume, sound — to establish the visual world of the production.

Responsibilities

The scenic designer is responsible for developing the physical environment in which the action takes place: scenery, set dressing, and often properties. The design must support the production's concept, define period and place, enable the actors' movement and the mechanics of scene changes, and remain within budget and the venue's technical capacity.

Process

  • Research and concept — script analysis and visual research with the director
  • Thumbnails and sketches — rough drawings exploring composition
  • Model — a white-card or fully painted scale model (commonly 1:25 or 1:24) presented to the production team
  • Drafting — ground plans, elevations, and sections from which the set is built
  • Paint elevations and properties lists — colour and finish specifications
  • Build, load-in, and technical rehearsals — supervision of realisation on stage

History

Scenery in the Western theatre descends from Renaissance perspective staging — Sebastiano Serlio's codified comic, tragic, and satyric scenes (1545) — through the wing-and-drop system of Baroque opera, the box set realism of the 19th century, and the reforms of Adolphe Appia and Edward Gordon Craig, who championed light, space, and abstraction. In the 20th century, designers such as Josef Svoboda expanded scenography with projection and kinetic scenery, and the field has continued into digital and virtual production techniques.

Notable scenic designers

Prominent figures include Inigo Jones, Boris Aronson, Ming Cho Lee, Es Devlin, and in film, production designers such as Ken Adam and Stuart Craig. The Tony Awards, Olivier Awards, and Academy Awards all recognise excellence in scenic and production design.
