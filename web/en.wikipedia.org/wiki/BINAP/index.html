BINAP

BINAP (2,2′-bis(diphenylphosphino)-1,1′-binaphthyl) is a widely used chiral phosphine ligand in asymmetric catalysis. It is an axially chiral (atropisomeric) compound existing as (R)- and (S)-enantiomers, and its transition-metal complexes catalyse highly enantioselective hydrogenations and other reactions.

History

BINAP was introduced by Ryoji Noyori and co-workers around 1980. Ruthenium–BINAP complexes enabled practical asymmetric hydrogenation of functionalised alkenes and ketones; the Rh–BINAP-catalysed isomerisation of allylamines is used industrially by Takasago to manufacture (−)-menthol. Noyori shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in Chemistry partly for this work.

Structure and use

The C2-symmetric ligand chelates metals through its two phosphorus atoms, creating a well-defined chiral environment. Variants such as Tol-BINAP, H8-BINAP and SEGPHOS tune steric and electronic properties, and BINAP derivatives are prepared from enantiopure BINOL.

Industrially significant applications include the hydrogenation steps in the manufacture of the antibiotic levofloxacin and the herbicide (S)-metolachlor (using a related ferrocene ligand).
