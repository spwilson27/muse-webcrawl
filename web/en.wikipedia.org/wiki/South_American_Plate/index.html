South American Plate

Type: Major tectonic plate

Area: approx. 43,600,000 km²

Movement: Westward, away from the Mid-Atlantic Ridge

Features: South America continent, western South Atlantic oceanic lithosphere

The South American Plate is a major tectonic plate which includes the continent of South America as well as a sizeable region of the Atlantic Ocean seabed extending eastward to the African Plate, with which it forms the southern part of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

Boundaries

The eastern border is a divergent boundary with the African Plate, part of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge system where new oceanic crust is created. The northern border meets the Caribbean Plate, and the northwestern edge interacts with the Nazca Plate's northern segment near the Galápagos region. The western border is a convergent boundary with the subducting Nazca Plate: the Nazca Plate dives beneath South America along the Peru–Chile Trench, driving the uplift of the Andes, the longest continental mountain range on Earth, and producing intense seismicity and volcanism. The southern border, with the Scotia and Antarctic plates, is a complex system of transform faults and small subduction zones in the far south.

Movement and history

The plate moves westward at roughly 3–4 cm per year relative to the African Plate, a motion driven by ridge push and slab pull that began with the breakup of Gondwana and the opening of the South Atlantic during the Cretaceous. Its interaction with the Nazca Plate generated some of the largest earthquakes ever recorded, including the 1960 Valdivia earthquake in Chile (magnitude ~9.5) and the 2010 Maule earthquake (magnitude 8.8).
