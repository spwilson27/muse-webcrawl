Chinese paddlefish

Chinese paddlefish
Psephurus gladius
Family: Polyodontidae
Status: Extinct (declared 2022)
Former range: Yangtze and Yellow River basins, China

The Chinese paddlefish (Psephurus gladius; Chinese: 白鱘, báixún), also known as the Chinese swordfish, was one of the largest freshwater fish in the world, endemic to the Yangtze and Yellow River basins of China. It was declared extinct in 2022, with the extinction estimated to have occurred by 2010; the last confirmed live specimen was seen in 2003.

Description

The Chinese paddlefish had a silvery-grey body and a greatly elongated, blade-like rostrum (paddle) that could account for up to a third of its length. Adults commonly reached 3 metres, and historical accounts describe fish of up to 7 metres and 500 kilograms, though such sizes are unverified. Unlike its relative the American paddlefish, which filter-feeds on plankton, the Chinese paddlefish was a piscivore, feeding mainly on other fish and crustaceans and using electroreceptors on its rostrum to detect prey.

Decline and extinction

The species declined drastically from the 1970s due to overfishing and habitat fragmentation. The construction of the Gezhouba Dam in 1981 blocked its migration between feeding grounds in the lower Yangtze and spawning sites upstream without providing a fish passage, and the later Three Gorges Dam compounded the isolation. A study published in 2020 concluded the species was extinct, with a functional extinction (too few individuals to reproduce) around 1993; the IUCN formally listed it as extinct in 2022.
