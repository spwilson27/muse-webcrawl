The Mariel boatlift was a mass emigration of Cubans to the United States in 1980, in which approximately 125,000 Cubans departed from Cuba's Mariel Harbour for Florida between April and October 1980.

Background

The crisis began after a bus crashed into the Peruvian embassy in Havana and thousands of Cubans sought asylum in the embassy grounds. Fidel Castro responded by opening the port of Mariel, declaring that anyone who wished to leave could do so.

The exodus

Cuban exiles in Florida sailed to Mariel to collect relatives; Cuban authorities also forced boat owners to carry released prisoners and psychiatric patients, creating lasting controversy over the emigrants' composition. President Jimmy Carter's administration struggled to respond amid the 1980 election campaign.

Legacy

The Marielitos reshaped Miami's demographics, labour market and culture — a time later studied for its effects on low-skilled wages. The episode influenced US immigration politics and was dramatised in the 1983 film Scarface, whose protagonist is a Mariel emigrant.
