Zugdidi (Georgian: ზუგდიდი; Mingrelian: ზუგდიდი, literally "big hill") is a city in the western Georgian region of Samegrelo (Mingrelia), near the border with Abkhazia. It is the capital of the Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti region and the historical centre of the Mingrelian people, who speak their own Kartvelian language, Mingrelian.

History

Zugdidi was the capital of the Principality of Mingrelia (Odishi) from the seventeenth century until the principality was absorbed into the Russian Empire in 1867. Its principal landmark is the Dadiani Palace, the residence of the Dadiani dynasty of princes, which now houses a museum established in 1849 — one of the oldest in the Caucasus. Its treasures include a death mask of Napoleon and relics associated with the Virgin Mary, notably the robe said to have been brought to Georgia; the robe is carried in procession during the annual Vlakernoba festival.

Following the war in Abkhazia in the early 1990s, Zugdidi received large numbers of ethnic Georgian internally displaced persons from Abkhazia, and it remains an important administrative and aid centre near the ceasefire line.

Economy and transport

The city lies on the main railway and highway between Tbilisi and the Abkhaz boundary at the Enguri River, about 30 kilometres from the Black Sea coast. The surrounding district is known for tea growing — a Soviet-era tea factory operated in the city — and for hazelnut production, one of Georgia's major agricultural exports. The Botanical Garden of Zugdidi, laid out in the nineteenth century, adjoins the palace complex.
