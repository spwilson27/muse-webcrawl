Chatelherault Country Park

Chatelherault Country Park is a country park in Hamilton, South Lanarkshire, Scotland. It is centred on the 18th-century hunting lodge of the Dukes of Hamilton, designed by William Adam and completed in 1734, which served as the kennels and a banqueting pavilion for the now-demolished Hamilton Palace.

Features

The park extends over some 500 acres of woodland and parkland along the Avon Water gorge, with trails through ancient oak woodlands in the Avon Gorge, a designated Site of Special Scientific Interest. The lodge building, named after the Dukes' French title of Duc de Châtellerault, now houses a visitor centre with exhibitions on the Dukes of Hamilton and the local landscape.

Attractions include the restored lodge and its formal gardens, woodland walks, the ruins of Cadzow Castle, and the surviving Cadzow White Park cattle herd grazing in the park.
