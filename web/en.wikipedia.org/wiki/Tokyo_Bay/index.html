Tokyo Bay

Tokyo Bay (Japanese: 東京湾, Tōkyō-wan) is a bay located in the southern Kantō region of Japan, bounded by the Bōsō Peninsula of Chiba Prefecture to the east and the Miura Peninsula of Kanagawa Prefecture to the west. It covers about 1,500 square kilometres and connects to the Pacific Ocean through the Uraga Channel.

Geography

The bay is generally shallow, with a mean depth of about 40 metres. Rivers flowing into it include the Tama, Tsurumi, Edo, and Arakawa. Extensive land reclamation since the Edo period has created artificial islands such as Odaiba and much of the port area of modern Tokyo.

History

Historically known as Edo Bay, it was the site of Commodore Matthew Perry's arrival in 1853–54, which led to the opening of Japan to foreign trade. In 1945, the Japanese Instrument of Surrender was signed aboard the USS Missouri anchored in the bay.

Infrastructure

The Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line, a bridge–tunnel combination opened in 1997, crosses the bay between Kawasaki and Kisarazu. Major ports on the bay include Tokyo, Yokohama, Chiba, Kawasaki, and Yokosuka, forming one of the largest port complexes in the world. The bay's shores host heavy industry, power plants, and the Keihin and Keiyō industrial zones.
