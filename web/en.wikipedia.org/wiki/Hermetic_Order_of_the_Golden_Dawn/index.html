Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, more commonly the Golden Dawn, was a secret society devoted to the study and practice of occult Hermeticism and metaphysics during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It became one of the most influential organisations in the history of Western esotericism, shaping modern occultism, Wicca, and Thelema.

History

The order was founded in 1888 by three Freemasons — William Wynn Westcott, Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers, and William Robert Woodman — who claimed authority from a set of "Cipher Manuscripts" and correspondence with a German adept named Anna Sprengel. Its first temple, Isis-Urania in London, attracted artists and intellectuals, including the poet W. B. Yeats, the actress Florence Farr, and, briefly and turbulently, Aleister Crowley.

Internal disputes — including Mathers's autocratic leadership, a schism in 1900, and scandal surrounding fraudulent "Horos" initiations — fragmented the original order into successor organisations such as the Stella Matutina and the Alpha et Omega.

Teachings and structure

Members progressed through a graded curriculum modelled on the Kabbalistic Tree of Life, studying tarot, astrology, geomancy, alchemy, Enochian magic, and the rituals codified by Mathers, including the Lesser Ritual of the Pentagram. The system synthesised Hermetic Qabalah, Rosicrucianism, Egyptian religion, and Christian mysticism.

Legacy

The order's rituals and teachings, published piecemeal by Crowley and comprehensively by Israel Regardie in the 1930s, form the backbone of much contemporary ceremonial magic.
