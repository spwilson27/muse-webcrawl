Syrah

Syrah, also known as Shiraz, is a dark-skinned grape variety grown throughout the world and used primarily to produce red wine. DNA profiling in 1999 showed that Syrah originated in southeastern France, as the offspring of the Dureza and Mondeuse Blanche grapes, in the northern Rhône region.

Styles

In the northern Rhône appellations such as Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie, Syrah produces full-bodied wines with notes of black fruit, black pepper, smoke, and cured meat. In Australia, where it is called Shiraz, it makes richer, riper styles — famously from the Barossa Valley — as well as cool-climate peppery examples. The grape is a key component of the classic southern Rhône blend with Grenache and Mourvèdre ("GSM").

Viticulture

Syrah vines produce small, thick-skinned berries of deep colour and high tannin. The variety is planted widely in France, Australia, South Africa, Chile, Argentina, and the United States, particularly California and Washington.
