Goldenvoice

Goldenvoice is an American concert promotion company based in Los Angeles, California. Founded in 1981 by Gary Tovar, it began as a promoter of punk rock shows in Southern California and grew into one of the largest live-music promoters in the United States. It is best known as the creator of the Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival and the Stagecoach Festival. Since 2001 it has been a subsidiary of the Anschutz Entertainment Group (AEG).

History

Gary Tovar founded Goldenvoice in Huntington Beach in 1981, promoting early Los Angeles-area punk concerts by bands such as Black Flag, the Circle Jerks, Bad Religion, Social Distortion and Jane's Addiction. The company's name derived from a strain of marijuana Tovar smuggled — an enterprise that led to his arrest and imprisonment on federal drug charges in the early 1990s.

During Tovar's absence, Paul Tollett and Rick Van Santen took over and rebuilt the company. In 1999 they launched Coachella at the Empire Polo Club in Indio, California; despite losing money in its first year, the festival became one of the world's most successful music events. AEG acquired Goldenvoice in 2001. Van Santen died in 2003; Tollett remained president and CEO.

Festivals and venues

Goldenvoice produces Coachella, the country-music Stagecoach Festival, and other events including the punk and metal festival No Values and the Power Trip festival. It operates or books venues in California including the Shrine Auditorium and Expo Hall, the El Rey Theatre, the Fonda Theatre, the Warfield in San Francisco, the Regency Ballroom and the Fox Theater in Pomona, and it promotes thousands of concerts annually across North America.
