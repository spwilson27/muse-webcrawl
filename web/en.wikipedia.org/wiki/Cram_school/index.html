Cram school

A cram school is a specialised school that trains its students to achieve specific goals, most commonly passing entrance examinations for high schools and universities. Cram schools are prevalent in East and Southeast Asia — known as juku in Japan, hagwon in South Korea, buxiban in Taiwan and buxiban/buke in China — as well as in South Asia and elsewhere where examinations are highly competitive.

Operation

Students typically attend after regular school hours, at weekends and during holidays, for intensive drilling in examination subjects, practice tests and memorisation techniques. Some cram schools prepare students for a single examination such as Japan's university entrance exams, China's gaokao, South Korea's suneung or India's JEE and NEET; others provide general supplementary tuition, languages, music or civil-service examination coaching.

Criticism and regulation

Critics argue cram schools increase inequality between wealthy and poor families, add to student stress and sleep deprivation, and encourage rote learning over understanding. Governments have repeatedly tried to curb the sector: South Korea banned private tutoring outright between 1980 and 2000 and still enforces curfews on hagwon hours; China in 2021 imposed a sweeping "double reduction" policy that forced academic tutoring companies to become non-profit and banned classes on weekends and holidays, devastating the industry almost overnight.
