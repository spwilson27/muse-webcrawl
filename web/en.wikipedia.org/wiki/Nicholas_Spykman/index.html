Nicholas Spykman

Nicholas John Spykman (1893–1943) was a Dutch-American journalist, political scientist, and a leading theorist of geopolitics. A professor at Yale University, he founded the Yale Institute of International Studies and is regarded as one of the founders of the realist tradition in American foreign policy thought.

Career

Born in Amsterdam, Spykman worked as a journalist in the Dutch East Indies, Egypt, and India before emigrating to the United States, where he earned a doctorate from the University of California, Berkeley. He joined the Yale faculty in 1925 and became Sterling Professor of International Relations. His major works include America's Strategy in World Politics (1942) and the posthumously published The Geography of the Peace (1944).

Rimland thesis

Spykman is best known for his "Rimland" thesis, a counterpoint to Halford Mackinder's Heartland theory. Where Mackinder emphasized the Eurasian interior, Spykman argued that the coastal fringes of Eurasia — the Rimland, encompassing Western Europe, the Middle East, South Asia, and East Asia — were the key to world power, famously writing: "Who controls the Rimland rules Eurasia; who rules Eurasia controls the destinies of the world." His analysis influenced the United States' Cold War strategy of containment along Eurasia's periphery.
