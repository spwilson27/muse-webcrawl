Lugol's iodine
Composition5% iodine, 10% potassium iodide in water
Formulated1829, by Jean Lugol
UsesAntiseptic; starch test; thyroid medicine

Lugol's iodine, also known as aqueous iodine or strong iodine solution, is a solution of potassium iodide and elemental iodine in water, first prepared in 1829 by the French physician Jean Lugol. The classic formula contains 5% iodine and 10% potassium iodide; the iodide dissolves the otherwise poorly soluble iodine by forming triiodide ions.

Uses

Lugol's solution is used as an antiseptic and disinfectant, though it has largely been replaced by milder iodophors such as povidone-iodine. In medicine it has been employed in the management of thyroid storm and hyperthyroidism — iodine temporarily suppresses thyroid hormone release (the Wolff–Chaikoff effect) — and to protect the thyroid from radioactive iodine in nuclear emergencies. Applied to the cervix in the Schiller test, it stains normal glycogen-rich tissue brown and helps reveal abnormal areas; it is likewise used in endoscopy to highlight dysplasia in the oesophagus.

Laboratory use

In the laboratory, Lugol's iodine is the standard reagent for the starch test: in the presence of starch it produces a deep blue-black colour, because iodine lodges inside the amylose helix. It is also used as a stain in microscopy, for example to visualise cells and nuclei in wet mounts and in Gram staining as a mordant.

Safety

Concentrated Lugol's solution is an irritant and is harmful if swallowed in quantity. Because it can be used as a precursor in illicit drug manufacture, its sale is regulated in some jurisdictions.
