The CW Television Network (commonly referred to as The CW) is an American English-language commercial broadcast television network. It launched on September 18, 2006, as the successor to UPN and The WB, both of which had shut down that month; the network's name is taken from the first letters of its original parent companies, CBS Corporation and Warner Bros. The CW operates as a limited-liability joint venture and is controlled by Nexstar Media Group, which acquired a 75% stake in 2022.

History

UPN and The WB had both launched in January 1995 and spent a decade competing for younger viewers, splitting affiliates and audiences and losing money for their owners. In January 2006 CBS and Warner Bros. announced they would close both networks and merge their strongest programming and affiliate bodies into a single new network, The CW. The new schedule combined hits from both predecessors, such as America's Next Top Model, Gilmore Girls, Smallville, Supernatural, and Everybody Hates Chris, and the network targeted viewers aged 18 to 34, particularly women.

The CW built its identity in the 2010s on distinctive scripted series: the superhero "Arrowverse" franchise beginning with Arrow (2012), The Vampire Diaries and its spin-offs, the comedy-dramas Jane the Virgin and Crazy Ex-Girlfriend, and the long-running Riverdale. Many of these performed far better in streaming and international sales than in live ratings, supported by a landmark Netflix output deal. Children's programming blocks (Kids' WB, later Vortexx) occupied Saturday mornings until 2014.

In 2022 Nexstar Media Group — already the network's largest affiliate owner — acquired a controlling 75% interest, with Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery each retaining 12.5%. Under Nexstar the network broadened its target audience, cancelled most of its scripted slate, and pivoted towards cheaper unscripted programming, acquired series, and sports, including LIV Golf, ACC college sports, NASCAR Xfinity Series races, and WWE NXT.

Programming and affiliates

Unlike the "Big Four" networks, The CW has historically programmed fewer hours: two hours of prime time on weeknights and Sunday, plus limited daytime and Saturday sports/unscripted hours, leaving affiliates to fill the rest of their schedules. Its programming reached roughly 100% of US television households through broadcast affiliates and cable carriage, with stations in most markets also carrying the network on subchannels where no full-power affiliate exists. Shows are also streamed via The CW app and website.
