Douglas Richard Hofstadter (born February 15, 1945) is an American cognitive and computer scientist whose research focuses on consciousness, analogy-making and creativity. He is a professor at Indiana University Bloomington and directs the Fluid Analogies Research Group (FARG).


Douglas Hofstadter, author of Gödel, Escher, Bach.

Gödel, Escher, Bach

His first book, Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid (1979), interweaves mathematics, art and music to explore self-reference and "strange loops", arguing that consciousness emerges from such loops in the brain. It won the Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction and the National Book Award.

Other work

Hofstadter co-authored The Mind's I (1981) with Daniel Dennett, wrote the "Metamagical Themas" column in Scientific American, and explored translation in Le Ton beau de Marot (1997). In I Am a Strange Loop (2007) he developed his theory that the self is a self-referential pattern. His research group built models of analogy-making such as Copycat.
