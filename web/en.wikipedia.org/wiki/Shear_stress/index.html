Shear stress

Shear stress (symbol τ, Greek tau) is the component of stress coplanar with a material cross-section. It arises from a shear force, the component of force vector parallel to the material cross-section, as opposed to normal stress, which arises from forces perpendicular to the cross-section.

General shear stress

The average shear stress is the shear force divided by the area over which it acts: τ = F / A, measured in pascals. In beams, the horizontal shear stress varies parabolically over the depth, reaching a maximum at the neutral axis; it is computed with the formula τ = VQ / (Ib), where V is the shear force, Q the first moment of area, I the second moment of area and b the width of the section.

In fluids

In a Newtonian fluid, shear stress is proportional to the rate of shear strain, the constant of proportionality being the dynamic viscosity. Wall shear stress is important in fluid mechanics, aerodynamics and physiology, including in the study of blood flow.
