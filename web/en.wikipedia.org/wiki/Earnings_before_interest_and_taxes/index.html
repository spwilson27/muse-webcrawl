Earnings before interest and taxes

In accounting and finance, earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT) is a measure of a firm's profit that includes all incomes and expenses (operating and non-operating) except interest expenses and income tax expenses. It is also commonly called operating income or operating profit when a business has no non-operating income or expenses.

EBIT sits between gross profit and net income on the income statement.

Calculation

EBIT = Revenue − Operating expenses (excluding interest and taxes)

Equivalently, EBIT can be derived from net income by adding back interest and taxes:

EBIT = Net income + Interest + Taxes

Use

Because EBIT strips out the effects of different capital structures (interest) and tax regimes, analysts use it to compare the underlying operating performance of companies in the same industry. It is the numerator in the interest coverage ratio and a key input to enterprise-value multiples such as EV/EBIT. A related measure, EBITDA, adds back depreciation and amortisation as well.

Limitations

EBIT is not defined by generally accepted accounting principles and is not a substitute for cash flow: it ignores capital expenditure, changes in working capital, and the actual cash cost of debt and taxes. Critics note that companies may present "adjusted EBIT" figures that exclude recurring costs, flattering performance.
