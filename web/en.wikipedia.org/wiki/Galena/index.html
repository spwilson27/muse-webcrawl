Galena, also called lead glance, is the natural mineral form of lead(II) sulfide (PbS), the most important ore of lead and an important source of silver. It is one of the most abundant and widely distributed sulfide minerals, recognised by its bright metallic lustre, cubic cleavage and unusually high density.

Properties and occurrence

Galena crystallises in the cubic system, commonly forming cubes and octahedra, and cleaves perfectly in three directions at right angles, so specimens break into small cubes. Fresh surfaces are bright silver-grey but tarnish dull on exposure. It occurs in hydrothermal veins and replacement deposits, often with sphalerite, chalcopyrite, fluorite, barite, quartz and carbonates, and in sedimentary lead-zinc districts such as the Mississippi Valley-type deposits of the American Midwest. Famous localities include the mines of Missouri, the English Pennines, Broken Hill in Australia and Freiberg in Germany.

Uses

Galena has been mined since antiquity: the ancient Egyptians ground it for kohl eye paint, and it has been the world's principal lead ore ever since, its associated silver content (argentiferous galena may carry up to about one percent silver) historically a major prize. In the twentieth century galena crystals found a second career as the semiconductor in crystal radio sets, where a fine wire "cat's whisker" touching the crystal formed a point-contact rectifier. Galena is also the state mineral of Missouri and Wisconsin.
