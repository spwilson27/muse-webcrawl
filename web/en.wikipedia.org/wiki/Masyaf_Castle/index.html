Masyaf Castle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Masyaf Castle (Arabic: قلعة مصياف) is a medieval fortress in the town of Masyaf in Hama Governorate, western Syria, overlooking the Orontes valley from a rocky outcrop in the foothills of the coastal mountains. It is famous as the stronghold of the Nizari Ismailis — the order known in Crusader sources as the Assassins — during the 12th and 13th centuries.

History

The site was fortified in the Byzantine and early Islamic periods and passed through the hands of local dynasties before being acquired by the Nizari Ismailis in the 1140s. Under their most celebrated chief, Rashid al-Din Sinan — the "Old Man of the Mountain" of Crusader legend — Masyaf became the centre of the Nizari state in Syria from the 1160s until Sinan's death in 1193. From its network of mountain castles the small state survived between the Crusader states and the Muslim powers of Aleppo and Damascus, balancing diplomacy, tribute and the threat of assassination.

Saladin besieged Masyaf in 1176 but withdrew, an episode around which legends gathered of Assassin infiltration of his camp. The fortress resisted the Mongols longer than most but submitted to Hulagu in 1260; it was later taken by the Mamluk sultan Baibars in 1270, ending independent Nizari rule in Syria, though the Ismaili community remained in the town. The castle continued in use under the Mamluks and Ottomans and was restored in modern times, including work supported by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture.

Architecture

The castle is built of local limestone on an elongated rocky spur, with massive curtain walls, towers and machicolations largely dating from the Nizari and Mamluk periods. A single entrance leads up through passages to the inner citadel containing cisterns, storage rooms and residential quarters.

In popular culture

Masyaf achieved new fame as the headquarters of the Assassin Brotherhood in the video game Assassin's Creed (2007) and its sequels, which depict the fortress in the time of the Third Crusade.
