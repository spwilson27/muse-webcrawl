William Edward Forster (11 July 1818 – 5 April 1886) was an English industrialist and Liberal politician best remembered for the Elementary Education Act 1870, often called Forster's Education Act, which established a framework for state elementary schooling in England and Wales.

Early life and career

Forster was born into a Quaker family and became a partner in a Bradford woollen firm, where he developed an interest in workers' education and social reform. He entered Parliament in 1861 as MP for Bradford and rose through junior offices in Gladstone's first government.

Education Act 1870

As Vice-President of the Committee of Council on Education, Forster piloted the Elementary Education Act through Parliament in 1870. The Act created elected school boards empowered to build and run schools in districts where church provision was inadequate, laying the foundation for universal elementary education in England and Wales.

Ireland and later life

Forster served as Chief Secretary for Ireland from 1880 to 1882 during the Land War, a tenure marked by coercion legislation and his eventual resignation over the Kilmainham Treaty. He died in 1886.
