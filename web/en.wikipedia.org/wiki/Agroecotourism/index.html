Agroecotourism

Agroecotourism is a form of tourism that combines agritourism with ecotourism. Visitors stay on or tour working farms that follow ecologically sound practices, learning about sustainable agriculture, local ecosystems, and rural culture while contributing income that supports environmentally friendly land management.

Principles

Whereas conventional agritourism may focus mainly on recreation — farm stays, harvest festivals, pick-your-own operations — agroecotourism adds explicit conservation goals. Host farms typically practise organic farming, agroforestry, crop diversification, and water and soil conservation, and visits are designed to have low environmental impact. Educational components often include guided walks, participation in planting or harvesting, wildlife observation, and explanations of how farming can maintain biodiversity.

Benefits and challenges

For farmers, agroecotourism diversifies income, which can make sustainable practices economically viable where they might otherwise lose out to intensive methods. For visitors it offers direct contact with food production and rural landscapes. Critics note challenges: visitor traffic can disturb ecosystems, income may bypass the smallest farmers, and "green" marketing claims are not always verified. Certification schemes and community-managed projects in countries such as Costa Rica and Nepal are frequently cited as models.
