All Souls College (official name: College of All Souls of the Faithful Departed, of Oxford) is a constituent college of the University of Oxford. Founded in 1438, it is unique among Oxford colleges in having no undergraduate students: all of its members are fellows, and its purpose is the advancement of research and scholarship.

History

The college was founded by Archbishop Henry Chichele and King Henry VI in 1438, partly as a memorial to the English dead of the Hundred Years' War — the fellows were charged with praying for the souls of the faithful departed. The front quadrangle and chapel date from the founding period; the North Quadrangle, dominated by Nicholas Hawksmoor's twin towers, was built in the early 18th century. The Codrington Library, housing a major collection, was completed in 1751.

Fellowships

All Souls elects distinguished scholars as fellows by research, and each year holds its celebrated examination fellowship competition, long reputed one of the hardest examinations in the world, through which a small number of recent Oxford graduates are elected to seven-year fellowships. Fellows have included Isaiah Berlin, A. L. Rowse, and many leading figures in history, law, philosophy, economics, and politics.

Customs

The college is known for the Mallard Song and its centenary Mallard ceremony, in which fellows process around the roofs hunting a legendary mallard. It is among the wealthiest Oxbridge colleges per member, with a large endowment derived substantially from estates granted by its founders.
