Rolls-Royce Holdings

Rolls-Royce Holdings plc is a British multinational aerospace and defence company incorporated in February 2011. It is the successor to Rolls-Royce Limited, founded in 1906 by Charles Rolls and Henry Royce. The company is one of the world's largest manufacturers of aircraft engines and also produces power systems for ships and other applications. It is not affiliated with Rolls-Royce Motor Cars, which has been a subsidiary of BMW since 1998.

Business

Rolls-Royce's principal divisions are Civil Aerospace, Defence, and Power Systems. Its Trent family of turbofan engines powers aircraft including the Airbus A380, A350 and Boeing 787 Dreamliner. The company also provides long-term service agreements for the engines it sells, which generate a large share of revenue. Its defence business supplies engines for military aircraft and propulsion for the Royal Navy's nuclear submarines, including the reactors for the Dreadnought-class.

History

The original company was nationalised in 1971 after the costly development of the RB211 engine bankrupted it; it was privatised in 1987. In 2011 the business was transferred to the new holding company. Rolls-Royce is listed on the London Stock Exchange and is a constituent of the FTSE 100 Index.
