Convolvulaceae, commonly called the bindweed or morning glory family, is a family of about 60 genera and more than 1,650 species of mostly herbaceous vines, but also trees, shrubs and herbs. The family is recognised by its funnel-shaped, radially symmetrical flowers, which typically have five fused petals and produce the characteristic trumpet shape associated with morning glories.

Description

Most members of Convolvulaceae are twining or prostrate vines with simple, alternate leaves that lack stipules. The flowers are bisexual, regular and hypogynous, with a corolla of five fused petals that is often pleated or folded in bud. The fruit is usually a capsule, berry or nut. Many species contain ergoline alkaloids, which make some of them important in traditional medicine and others notable for their psychoactive properties.

Distribution and habitat

The family has a nearly cosmopolitan distribution, occurring on every continent except Antarctica, though it is most diverse in tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas and Asia. Species occupy habitats ranging from coastal dunes and roadsides to rainforest margins, and several bindweeds have become aggressive agricultural weeds.

Genera and notable species

The largest genus is Ipomoea, with more than 600 species, including the sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas), one of the world's most important food crops, and numerous ornamental morning glories. Other significant genera include Convolvulus (bindweeds), Cuscuta (dodders, a group of parasitic plants sometimes placed in their own family), Calystegia and Jacquemontia.

Uses

Besides the sweet potato, members of the family are widely grown as ornamentals for their showy flowers. Seeds of some Ipomoea species, such as Ipomoea tricolor and Ipomoea violacea, contain lysergic acid amide (LSA) and have a long history of ritual use in Mexico. Jalap resin, derived from Ipomoea purga, was historically used as a powerful purgative.
