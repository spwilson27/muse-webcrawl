Black Friday (shopping)

Black Friday is the day after Thanksgiving in the United States, traditionally the beginning of the Christmas shopping season. Many retailers offer heavily discounted "doorbuster" sales, often opening very early or, in recent decades, on Thanksgiving evening itself. It is routinely one of the busiest shopping days of the year.

Origin of the term

The phrase was used by Philadelphia police in the 1950s and 1960s to describe the chaotic crowds and traffic that descended on the city the day after Thanksgiving. Retailers later promoted a more positive folk etymology: that the day was when retailers' accounts moved "into the black" (profit) from "the red" (loss). The term spread nationally in the 1980s.

Spread and evolution

Black Friday customs have spread to many other countries, often detached from Thanksgiving. The growth of e-commerce has shifted much of the spending online, with sales beginning days or weeks earlier and continuing into Cyber Monday. The event has attracted criticism for consumerism, crowd-crush incidents at doorbuster sales, and pressure on retail workers, prompting alternatives such as Buy Nothing Day.
