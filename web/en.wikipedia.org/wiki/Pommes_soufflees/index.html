Pommes soufflées

Pommes soufflées are a French preparation of fried potatoes in which thin slices of potato, fried twice at two different temperatures, puff up into hollow pillows. They are considered a classic, technically demanding item of French haute cuisine.

History

According to tradition, pommes soufflées were discovered by accident on 26 August 1837 at the Pavillon Henri IV in Saint-Germain-en-Laye, during the inauguration of the railway line from Paris. The chef Collinet, told that the train carrying King Louis-Philippe was delayed, withdrew the frying potatoes from the oil and, on the king's arrival, plunged them into hotter oil, whereupon they inflated.

Preparation

Potatoes are peeled and cut into uniform slices about 3 millimetres thick, dried, and fried first in oil at about 150 °C (300 °F) until lightly cooked, then drained and fried a second time in hotter oil at about 190 °C (375 °F), where the moisture inside turns to steam and the slices balloon. They are salted and served immediately.

Service

Pommes soufflées are traditionally served with grilled meats and béarnaise sauce, famously alongside chateaubriand steak.
