Third Ward is a historic African-American neighbourhood of Houston, Texas, United States. It is one of the city's six original wards, created in 1840, and lies southeast of downtown, roughly bounded by Interstate 69 to the north, State Highway 288 to the west, and Old Spanish Trail to the south.

History

After emancipation, freed African Americans settled in the Third Ward, building one of Houston's most significant Black communities. In 1870 the ward's residents and members of Antioch Missionary Baptist Church and Trinity Methodist Episcopal Church purchased land that became Emancipation Park, the oldest park in Houston and a site of Juneteenth celebrations. The area became a centre of Black business, jazz, and civil rights organising, and is home to Texas Southern University, founded in 1927 as the Houston Colored Junior College.

Culture

The ward is noted for its blues and jazz heritage, its churches, and community institutions such as Project Row Houses, an art and social-service project founded in 1993 in a block of historic shotgun houses. Rapper Beyoncé, raised partly in the area, has frequently referenced the Third Ward in her work.

Gentrification

Since the 1990s, proximity to downtown, the Museum District and the Texas Medical Center has brought redevelopment pressure, rising property taxes and displacement of long-time residents, prompting community land trusts and preservation campaigns.
