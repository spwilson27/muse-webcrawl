Theropoda ("beast feet") is a dinosaur clade whose members are characterized by hollow bones and three-toed limbs. Theropods first appeared during the Late Triassic period, about 231 million years ago, and include all the large terrestrial carnivores of the Jurassic and Cretaceous, from Allosaurus and Spinosaurus to Tyrannosaurus. They are the only group of dinosaurs to survive to the present day: modern birds, numbering more than 11,000 living species, are theropods.

Characteristics

Theropods were generally bipedal and carnivorous, although several lineages — including the therizinosaurs and ornithomimosaurs — became herbivorous or omnivorous. Their arms ranged from the massive two-fingered hands of tyrannosaurids to the winged forelimbs of birds. Many theropods bore feathers, a feature first confirmed in fossils from the Liaoning deposits of China in the 1990s and now known to have been widespread among coelurosaurs and possibly more basal groups.

Evolution of birds

The hypothesis that birds are descended from small feathered theropods, proposed in the nineteenth century on the basis of Archaeopteryx, is now supported by an extensive fossil record of transitional forms. Birds are nested within Maniraptora, the group that also includes dromaeosaurids ("raptors") and troodontids, and share with them features such as a furcula (wishbone), brooding behaviour, and pneumatic bones.

Classification

Major theropod groups include the Ceratosauria, the Megalosauroidea, the Allosauroidea, the Tyrannosauroidea, the Ornithomimosauria, the Therizinosauria, the Oviraptorosauria, the Dromaeosauridae, the Troodontidae, and the Avialae (birds and their closest extinct relatives). Non-avian theropods all perished in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago.
