Anaphora (liturgy)

The anaphora is the central Eucharistic prayer of the Divine Liturgy in Eastern Christianity, corresponding broadly to the Eucharistic Prayer of the Roman Rite. The name derives from the Greek anaphora, "offering" or "carrying up," referring to the offering of the gifts of bread and wine. In the West Syriac tradition, the many prayers of this type are called anaphoras individually; in the Byzantine tradition the term usually denotes the prayers of the Liturgies of St John Chrysostom and St Basil the Great.

Structure

A classical anaphora typically includes an opening dialogue (including the Sursum corda, "Lift up your hearts"), a preface of thanksgiving for creation and salvation, the Sanctus, a recital of the history of salvation leading to the Words of Institution, the anamnesis (memorial of the Passion and Resurrection), the epiclesis invoking the Holy Spirit upon the gifts, and intercessions for the living and the dead, concluding with a doxology.

Major anaphoras

The Byzantine rite chiefly uses the Anaphora of St John Chrysostom and, on certain days, the longer Anaphora of St Basil. The West Syriac tradition preserves more than seventy anaphoras, attributed to various saints, among them the Anaphora of St James. The East Syriac tradition uses the ancient Anaphora of Addai and Mari, notable for lacking an explicit recitation of the Words of Institution. The Alexandrian tradition developed the Anaphora of St Mark (St Cyril).
