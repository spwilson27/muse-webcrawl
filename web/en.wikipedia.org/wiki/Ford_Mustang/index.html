The Ford Mustang is a series of American automobiles manufactured by Ford. In continuous production since 1964, the Mustang is currently the longest-produced Ford car nameplate. Currently in its seventh generation, it is the fifth-best selling Ford car nameplate. The namesake of the "pony car" automobile segment, the Mustang was developed as a highly styled line of sporty coupes and convertibles derived from existing model lines, initially distinguished by "long hood, short deck" proportions.

History

Originally predicted to sell 100,000 vehicles yearly, the 1965 Mustang became the most successful vehicle launch since the 1927 Model A. Introduced as a 1964½ model on 17 April 1964 (informally dubbed "1964½" by enthusiasts), the Mustang was unveiled at the New York World's Fair and sold more than 400,000 units in its first year; within two years more than one million had been sold. Executive stylist John Najjar is credited with suggesting the name, after the Second World War P-51 Mustang fighter plane.

Generations and legacy

The Mustang has passed through seven generations, from the compact Falcon-based original, through the enlarged 1971–73 models, the fuel-efficient Mustang II (1974–78), the long-lived Fox-body (1979–93), the SN-95 (1994–2004), the retro-styled S197 (2005–14), and the globally marketed S550 (2015–24), to the current S650. The nameplate has also been applied to the battery-electric Mustang Mach-E crossover. The Mustang is one of the most recognisable American cars and a cultural icon, famously appearing in films such as Bullitt (1968).
