The Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge (Chinese: 武汉长江大桥), sometimes called the First Wuhan Yangtze Bridge, is a double-deck road and railway truss bridge across the Yangtze River in Wuhan, Hubei province, China. Completed in 1957, it was the first bridge ever built across the Yangtze, ending the reliance on train ferries between the Beijing–Guangzhou railway's two halves and uniting the cities of Wuchang and Hanyang on opposite banks.

Construction

Plans for a bridge at Wuhan had been drawn up repeatedly since the early 20th century, but construction only began in 1955 with technical assistance from Soviet engineers. The main bridge is 1,670 metres long, with a steel truss main span structure; together with approach viaducts the full crossing extends about 4.7 km. The upper deck carries a two-way road and pedestrian walkways, while the lower deck carries a double-track railway. Nine-storey Chinese-style towers stand at each end of the bridge.

History and significance

The bridge opened to road traffic in October 1957 and became a symbol of the new China's industrial achievement; Mao Zedong, who had celebrated the project in his 1956 poem "Swimming" with the line about "a bridge will fly across" the river, crossed it and swam the Yangtze at Wuhan several times. It remains in heavy use, though it has suffered damage from collisions by river vessels on several occasions, and it has been joined by many later crossings, including the Second Wuhan Yangtze Bridge (1995) and the Yingwuzhou and Yangsigang bridges. In 2013 the bridge was listed as a Major Historical and Cultural Site Protected at the National Level.
