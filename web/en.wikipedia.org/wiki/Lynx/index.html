Lynx

A lynx is any of the four species in the genus Lynx, medium-sized wild cats of the family Felidae native to Europe, Asia, and North America. Lynxes are characterised by their short ("bobbed") tails, tufts of black hair on the tips of their ears, long whiskers, and large padded paws adapted for walking on snow.

Species

The Eurasian lynx (Lynx lynx) is the largest, ranging across European and Siberian forests and Central Asia. The Canada lynx (Lynx canadensis) inhabits boreal forests of North America and preys especially on snowshoe hares, whose population cycles the lynx follows. The bobcat (Lynx rufus) ranges across North America into Mexico. The Iberian lynx (Lynx pardinus) of southwestern Spain, once the world's most endangered felid, has been brought back from near-extinction by intensive conservation and now numbers over a thousand individuals, though it remains vulnerable; it depends heavily on rabbits as prey.

Behaviour

Lynxes are solitary, primarily nocturnal hunters of small to medium-sized mammals and birds. They have keen eyesight — the name may derive from a Greek word for brightness of the eyes — and excellent hearing aided by their ear tufts.
