Dansgaard–Oeschger event

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Dansgaard–Oeschger event (often abbreviated D–O event) is a rapid climate fluctuation that occurred repeatedly during the last glacial period. Twenty-five such events have been identified in Greenland ice cores between about 115,000 and 11,500 years ago. They are named after ice-core scientists Willi Dansgaard and Hans Oeschger, who first documented them.

Characteristics

Each event consists of an abrupt warming of Greenland temperatures — by 8 to 16 °C — within a few decades, followed by a gradual cooling over centuries before a rapid return to cold stadial conditions. The events show a characteristic sawtooth pattern in the δ18O records of the GRIP, GISP2 and NGRIP ice cores. Their Southern Hemisphere counterparts are muted and opposite in phase, consistent with the "bipolar seesaw" of ocean heat transport.

Causes

D–O events are generally attributed to changes in the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, sea-ice extent and atmospheric circulation, with freshwater input from ice sheets — connected in some cases with Heinrich events — capable of triggering shifts between circulation modes. They recur with a rough periodicity near 1,470 years, though the regularity and its cause remain debated. See also Ice core, Younger Dryas, and Abrupt climate change.
