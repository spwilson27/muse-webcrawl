Mgahinga Gorilla National Park
Location: Kisoro District, southwestern Uganda
Area: 33.9 km² (13.1 sq mi)
Established: 1991
Governing body: Uganda Wildlife Authority

Mgahinga Gorilla National Park is a national park in southwestern Uganda, in the Virunga Mountains along the borders with Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. At just 33.9 square kilometres it is Uganda's smallest national park, but it protects the Ugandan portion of a transboundary ecosystem shared with Rwanda's Volcanoes National Park and the DRC's Virunga National Park. The park was gazetted in 1991.

Geography

The park's altitude ranges from about 2,227 to 4,127 metres and includes three of the eight Virunga volcanoes: Mount Muhavura (4,127 m), Mount Mgahinga (3,474 m), and Mount Sabinyo (3,645 m), whose summit marks the point where the three countries meet. Vegetation ranges from bamboo and montane forest to afro-alpine moorland near the peaks.

Wildlife

The park is best known for its mountain gorillas. The habituated Nyakagezi group — notable for having several silverbacks — can be tracked by permit, although historically the group ranged across the three countries. Mgahinga is also one of only two places in the world where endangered golden monkeys are habituated for tourism, and it supports forest elephants, buffalo, bushbuck, and around 79 recorded bird species.

People and tourism

The Batwa (Twa) hunter-gatherers, evicted from the forest when the parks were created, share their culture with visitors on the Batwa Trail, which includes the Garama Cave, once a residence of Batwa kings. Tourism activities include gorilla and golden-monkey tracking, volcano climbing, and birding; the park is reached from the town of Kisoro, about 540 km from Kampala.
