Meir Shapiro

Yehuda Meir Shapiro (3 March 1887 – 27 October 1933), commonly known as the Lubliner Rav, was a prominent Polish Hasidic rabbi, community leader, and founder of the Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin. He is best known for proposing the Daf Yomi programme of daily Talmud study, in which all participants worldwide study the same page (daf) of the Babylonian Talmud each day, completing the entire Talmud in about seven and a half years.

Life

Shapiro was born in Suceava (Shotz), Bukovina, then part of Austria-Hungary, a descendant of distinguished rabbinic families. Ordained as a teenager, he served as rabbi of several communities, including Galina, Piotrków Trybunalski, and Sanok, before becoming rabbi of Lublin in 1931. He was active in Agudath Israel, served as a deputy in the Polish Sejm (parliament) from 1922 to 1927, and campaigned for Jewish education, including support of the Bais Yaakov school movement for girls.

Daf Yomi

Shapiro announced the Daf Yomi idea at the first World Congress (Knessia Gedolah) of Agudath Israel in Vienna in 1923. The first cycle began on Rosh Hashanah 1923, and the practice spread across the Jewish world; completion celebrations (Siyum HaShas) now draw tens of thousands of participants.

Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin

In 1930 Shapiro founded the Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin, an ambitious, modern yeshiva building in Lublin with dormitories, a large study hall, and a famous library, intended to restore Lublin's status as a centre of Torah scholarship. He died suddenly in 1933, aged 46. The yeshiva was closed under Nazi occupation; its building survived, was later used by a medical academy, and was restored and returned to Jewish communal use in the 2000s.
