East of Eden is a novel by American author and Nobel Prize winner John Steinbeck, published in September 1952. Often described as Steinbeck's "first novel" in ambition — the book he had been preparing all his life to write — it is an epic retelling of the biblical stories of Adam and Eve and of Cain and Abel, set in California's Salinas Valley.

Plot summary

The narrative interweaves the histories of two families, the Trasks and the Hamiltons (the latter based on Steinbeck's own maternal family, with the author appearing as narrator). Adam Trask, a Connecticut-born wanderer, settles on a ranch near Salinas with his wife Cathy, who abandons him and their twin sons immediately after childbirth to run a brothel in town. Raised believing their mother dead, the twins Cal and Aron grow into a new Cain and Abel: Aron the favoured, golden son, Cal the brooding outsider who discovers the truth about their mother and struggles over whether evil is inherited.

Themes

The novel's philosophical centre is the Hebrew word timshel — "thou mayest" — from God's promise to Cain in Genesis, which Steinbeck reads not as a command or a guarantee but as a choice: human beings are not doomed to repeat sin but may overcome it. The family sage Lee, Adam's Chinese-American servant, and the neighbouring Samuel Hamilton carry much of the book's reflection on free will, guilt and redemption.

Reception and adaptation

Contemporary reviews were mixed, but the novel became a bestseller and is now regarded as one of Steinbeck's finest works. Elia Kazan's 1955 film adaptation, covering only the final part of the book, introduced James Dean in his first major role. A television miniseries followed in 1981, and the novel's selection for Oprah's Book Club in 2003 returned it to the top of bestseller lists.
