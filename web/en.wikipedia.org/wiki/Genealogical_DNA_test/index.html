Genealogical DNA test

[Image placeholder: DNA test kit]

A genealogical DNA test is a DNA-based genetic test used in genetic genealogy to determine a person's ancestry and find genetic relatives. Commercial testing became widely available in the 2000s through companies such as Family Tree DNA, AncestryDNA, 23andMe, and MyHeritage DNA, and tens of millions of people have been tested.

Types of tests

Autosomal DNA tests examine DNA inherited from both parents and are used to find relatives within roughly the last five to seven generations and to estimate ethnic ancestry proportions. Y-DNA tests trace the direct paternal line via the Y chromosome and are used in surname studies. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) tests trace the direct maternal line. Results are compared against company databases to identify shared segments indicating common ancestors.

Uses and controversies

Tests are used by adoptees seeking biological relatives, genealogists breaking through documentary "brick walls", and researchers studying population history. They have also been used by law enforcement through investigative genetic genealogy — famously in the 2018 identification of the Golden State Killer — raising privacy debates. Informed-consent, data-sharing, and unexpected paternity discoveries are recurring ethical issues, and accuracy of ethnicity estimates depends heavily on reference populations.
