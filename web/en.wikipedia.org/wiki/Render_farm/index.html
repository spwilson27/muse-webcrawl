Render farm

A render farm is a high-performance computer system, such as a computer cluster, built to render computer-generated imagery (CGI), typically for film and television visual effects. Rendering is an embarrassingly parallel task at the level of frames or tiles, so a farm can divide a shot among many machines and complete in hours a job that would take a single workstation months.

Operation

Jobs are submitted to a queue manager, which distributes frames to render nodes, monitors progress and restarts failed tasks. Large animation studios operate farms of thousands of cores; public cloud rendering services also let smaller productions rent capacity on demand. As GPUs have become central to modern rendering, farms increasingly consist of GPU-dense servers.

Scale

Feature films may require hundreds of thousands of processor-hours: complex shots with global illumination, volumetrics and simulation data can each take tens of hours per frame. Studios such as Pixar, Weta FX and Industrial Light & Magic run some of the largest private render farms.
