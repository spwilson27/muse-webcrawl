Asturian language

Asturian (endonym: asturianu), also called Bable, is a West Iberian Romance language spoken in the Principality of Asturias, Spain. Together with Leonese and Mirandese it forms the Asturleonese language group. Estimates of speakers vary, generally in the range of 100,000 to 450,000 people who can speak or understand it, though intergenerational transmission has declined.

History and status

Asturian descends from the Latin spoken in the medieval Kingdom of Asturias and León; legal documents in the vernacular appear from the 13th century. Under Franco, its public use was discouraged, and it remains without full official status in Asturias, though the Statute of Autonomy grants it protection and it is taught optionally in schools. The Academia de la Llingua Asturiana, founded in 1980, publishes a grammar and dictionary and promotes a standard written form.

Features

Characteristic features include the preservation of initial Latin F- in some dialects (elsewhere shifting to h-), palatalisation of initial L-, and the "neuter of matter" (neutru de materia) — a mass-noun gender distinction for uncountable substances. Dialects are conventionally grouped as Western, Central, and Eastern.
