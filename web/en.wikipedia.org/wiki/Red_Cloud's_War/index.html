Red Cloud's War

Red Cloud's War
Date: 1866–1868
Location: Powder River Country, present-day Wyoming and Montana
Combatants: United States vs. Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Northern Arapaho
Result: Native American victory; Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868)

Red Cloud's War (also called the Bozeman War or the Powder River War) was an armed conflict between the Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Northern Arapaho peoples and the United States, fought in the Wyoming and Montana territories from 1866 to 1868. It was named for Red Cloud, the Oglala Lakota leader who organised the resistance.

Background

The discovery of gold in Montana led emigrants along the Bozeman Trail, which cut through prime Lakota hunting grounds guaranteed by treaty. In 1866 the U.S. Army began building forts along the trail — Fort Reno, Fort Philip Kearny, and Fort C. F. Smith — even while peace negotiations were under way at Fort Laramie. Red Cloud walked out of the talks when he learned the forts were already being built.

The fighting

Native forces besieged the forts and attacked wagon trains and work parties along the trail. On 21 December 1866, Captain William Fetterman and all 80 men under his command were killed in the Fetterman Fight, the Army's worst defeat on the northern Plains to that date. In August 1867, defenders armed with new breech-loading rifles held off much larger attacking forces in the Hayfield Fight and the Wagon Box Fight.

Outcome

Unable to secure the trail at acceptable cost, the United States agreed to the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868), abandoning the Bozeman Trail forts — the only war in which the United States conceded essentially all of the Native demands. Red Cloud refused to sign until the soldiers had left and his warriors had burned the forts. The peace held until the Black Hills gold rush led to the Great Sioux War of 1876.
