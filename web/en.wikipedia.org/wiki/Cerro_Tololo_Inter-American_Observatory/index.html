Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory

CTIO
Location: Cerro Tololo, Coquimbo Region, Chile
Altitude: 2,207 m (7,241 ft)
Established: 1962
Key telescopes: Víctor M. Blanco 4 m; SOAR 4.1 m

The Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory (CTIO) is an astronomical observatory complex in the Coquimbo Region of northern Chile, about 80 km east of La Serena in the foothills of the Andes. It is operated by the US NOIRLab (formerly AURA) with funding from the National Science Foundation.

Telescopes

The main instrument is the 4-metre Víctor M. Blanco Telescope, completed in 1976 and a southern twin of the Mayall telescope at Kitt Peak. It hosts the Dark Energy Camera (DECam), a 570-megapixel camera used for the Dark Energy Survey. The site also hosts the 4.1 m SOAR telescope and a number of smaller telescopes, including SMARTS consortium instruments.

Research

CTIO's dry, clear southern skies give access to the galactic centre and the Magellanic Clouds. Observations here contributed to the 1998 discovery of the accelerating expansion of the universe through studies of distant Type Ia supernovae, work recognised by the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics.
