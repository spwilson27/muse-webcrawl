The national symbols of Pakistan are the official emblems representing the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, adopted at and after its independence from British India on 14 August 1947. They express the country's founding ideals — summarised in Muhammad Ali Jinnah's motto "Faith, Unity, Discipline" — as well as its history, culture, and natural heritage.

Principal symbols

The national flag, designed by Amiruddin Kidwai and adopted days before independence, is a dark green field with a white vertical stripe at the hoist, bearing a white crescent and five-pointed star; green represents the Muslim majority, the white stripe the religious minorities, and the crescent and star progress and light. The state emblem (adopted 1954) shows a shield with cotton, wheat, tea, and jute — the main crops at independence — beneath a crescent and star, flanked by a wreath of jasmine, the national flower, and a scroll bearing the national motto in Urdu. The national anthem, with lyrics by Hafeez Jullundhri set to music by Ahmad G. Chagla, was officially adopted in 1954.

Other symbols

Pakistan's national animal is the markhor (a wild goat), its national bird the chukar partridge, its national tree the Himalayan cedar (deodar), and its national fruit the mango. The national language is Urdu, and the national poet is Muhammad Iqbal. Official monuments include Minar-e-Pakistan in Lahore, marking the site of the 1940 Lahore Resolution, and the Pakistan Monument and Faisal Mosque in Islamabad.
