Systems integrator

A systems integrator (or system integrator) is a person or company that specialises in bringing together component sub-systems into a whole and ensuring that those subsystems function together, a practice known as system integration. They also solve problems of automation. Systems integrators may work in many fields but the term is generally used in the information technology (IT) field — such as computer networks and enterprise software — the defence industry, the telecommunications industry, and in industrial automation and process control.

Roles and activities

A systems integrator typically undertakes the design, integration, and deployment or commissioning of a system assembled from products supplied by multiple vendors. Activities include requirements analysis, architecture and interface definition, procurement support, configuration and customisation, interface development, testing, training, and ongoing support. The integrator takes overall responsibility for the delivered system meeting the client's requirements, even though it may manufacture few or none of the components itself.

In information technology

In IT, systems integrators — from global firms such as Accenture, Deloitte, IBM Consulting and Capgemini to regional specialists — implement enterprise resource planning (ERP), customer relationship management (CRM), cloud migration, and data platform projects, combining packaged software, infrastructure and custom code. Related business models include value-added resellers (VARs) and managed service providers.

In industrial automation

In process industries and manufacturing, systems integrators design and commission control systems built from programmable logic controllers (PLCs), distributed control systems (DCS), SCADA and MES software. The Control System Integrators Association (CSIA) certifies firms in this sector and maintains directories of qualified integrators.
