The 2015 United Nations Climate Change Conference, known as COP 21, was held in Paris, France, from 30 November to 12 December 2015. It was the 21st yearly session of the Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change and produced the landmark Paris Agreement, the first universal, legally binding global climate accord.

Negotiations

Unlike the top-down Kyoto Protocol, COP 21 was built on nationally determined contributions (NDCs) submitted in advance by over 180 countries. French foreign minister Laurent Fabius presided over two weeks of negotiations, extended by a day, that resolved disputes over finance, loss and damage, and the temperature goal. The conference took place under tight security just weeks after the November 2015 Paris terrorist attacks, with some 150 heads of state and government attending the opening day — the largest such gathering of world leaders.

The Paris Agreement

Adopted by consensus on 12 December 2015, the Agreement commits parties to hold global warming "well below 2 °C" above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C; to reach a global peak of emissions as soon as possible and net-zero in the second half of the century; and to review and ratchet up national pledges every five years. Developed countries reaffirmed a goal of mobilising $100 billion a year in climate finance. The Agreement entered into force on 4 November 2016.

Significance

COP 21 is regarded as a turning point in international climate policy, succeeding where the 2009 Copenhagen conference had failed, and it remains the framework within which subsequent COPs operate.
