Ulrich of Augsburg (German: Ulrich von Augsburg; c. 890 – 4 July 973) was Bishop of Augsburg for fifty years and a leading churchman of the Ottonian Empire. He is famous for his defence of Augsburg during the Magyar invasions and was the first saint to be formally canonized by a pope.

Saint Ulrich, patron saint of Augsburg.

Life

Ulrich was born into a noble Alemannic family and educated at the Abbey of Saint Gall. After serving as chamberlain to Bishop Adalbero of Augsburg, he was appointed bishop in 923 by King Henry I. He rebuilt the cathedral, fortified the city, founded churches and schools, and was noted for rigorous pastoral visitation of his diocese and care for the poor.

Battle of Lechfeld

In 955, when the Magyars besieged Augsburg, Ulrich organised and encouraged the defence of the city, appearing unarmed among the defenders, until King Otto I arrived and destroyed the Magyar army at the Battle of Lechfeld on 10 August 955 — a turning point that ended the Magyar raids into Western Europe. Ulrich's Vita, written by the cathedral provost Gerhard of Augsburg shortly after his death, is a key source for the battle.

Canonization

Ulrich died on 4 July 973 and was buried in the Church of St Afra in Augsburg. On 4 July 993, exactly twenty years later, Pope John XV issued a bull canonizing him — the first documented formal canonization of a saint by a pope, a landmark in the development of canonization procedure. He remains the patron saint of Augsburg, and his feast day, 4 July, is still celebrated in the city, where his relics and crozier are preserved.
