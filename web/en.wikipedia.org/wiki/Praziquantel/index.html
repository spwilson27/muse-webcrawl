Praziquantel

Praziquantel, sold under the brand name Biltricide among others, is a medication used to treat a number of types of parasitic worm infections, including schistosomiasis, clonorchiasis, opisthorchiasis, tapeworm infections, cysticercosis, and hydatid disease. Developed by Bayer and Merck in the 1970s, it is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is the mainstay of large-scale mass drug administration programmes against schistosomiasis, which affects more than 200 million people.

Medical uses

Praziquantel is the drug of choice for all species of Schistosoma infecting humans, typically given as a single-day course dosed by body weight. It is also effective against liver and lung flukes (except Fasciola, which requires triclabendazole) and adult intestinal tapeworms. Annual mass treatment of school-age children in endemic areas of sub-Saharan Africa has substantially reduced heavy infections and their complications.

Mechanism of action

Praziquantel rapidly increases the permeability of the worm's tegument to calcium ions, causing spastic paralysis of the musculature and vacuolisation and damage of the outer surface. The damaged parasite is then attacked by the host immune system and dislodged or destroyed. A schistosome transient receptor potential channel has been identified as a molecular target. The drug is effective mainly against adult worms, which is why treatment may be repeated after juvenile stages mature.

Adverse effects

Side effects are usually mild and transient — dizziness, headache, abdominal pain, nausea, and fever — and are partly caused by the host reaction to dying parasites. Praziquantel is extensively metabolised by CYP enzymes, so interactions with drugs such as rifampicin and some anticonvulsants can reduce its levels. Concerns about possible emerging reduced susceptibility in schistosomes motivate ongoing surveillance, but clinically significant resistance has not become established.
