Generalized phrase structure grammar

Generalized phrase structure grammar (GPSG) is a framework for describing the syntax of natural languages, developed in the late 1970s and 1980s by Gerald Gazdar and collaborators (Ewan Klein, Geoffrey Pullum and Ivan Sag). Its definitive statement appeared in Gazdar et al., Generalized Phrase Structure Grammar (1985).

Features

GPSG is a constraint-based, monostratal theory: it rejected transformational derivations in favour of enriched phrase structure rules using features, metarules, and immediate-dominance/linear-precedence (ID/LP) rules separating constituency from word order. It aimed to show that natural languages could be described with context-free power for many phenomena once thought to require transformations. GPSG strongly influenced head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG), which grew directly out of it.
