Micropayment

A micropayment is a financial transaction involving a very small sum of money, typically a few cents or less, usually made online. The concept became prominent in the 1990s as a proposed way to pay for web content piece by piece, but the fixed costs of conventional card processing made true sub-dollar payments uneconomic for many years.

History and challenges

Early systems such as DigiCash, Millicent, NetBill and FirstVirtual explored cryptographic tokens and aggregated billing, but most failed commercially. Critics argued that besides transaction fees, the "mental transaction cost" — the effort of deciding whether each item is worth its price — doomed per-article micropayments, and subscriptions and advertising became the dominant models for online content. Micropayments nevertheless flourished inside closed platforms where costs could be pooled: premium-rate SMS content, in-game purchases and microtransactions in mobile apps, and stored-value wallets.

Modern forms

Aggregation is the usual remedy: platforms accumulate many small charges and settle them in batches. Digital currencies and payment networks designed for very low fees, including second-layer systems such as the Bitcoin Lightning Network and streaming-money protocols, have revived interest in machine-to-machine and per-use payments. Social platforms have added tipping and small creator payments, and services such as in-app purchase systems routinely process transactions of under a dollar by bundling them through a platform account.
