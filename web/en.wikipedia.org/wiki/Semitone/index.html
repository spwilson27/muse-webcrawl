Semitone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A semitone, also called a half step or a half tone, is the smallest musical interval commonly used in Western tonal music, and it is considered the most dissonant when sounded harmonically. It is defined as the interval between two adjacent notes in a 12-tone scale. For example, C is adjacent to C♯; the interval between them is a semitone.

In a 12-note approximately equally divided scale, any interval can be defined in terms of an appropriate number of semitones (e.g. a whole tone or major second is 2 semitones wide, a major third 4 semitones, and a perfect fifth 7 semitones).

Minor second

The semitone is also the interval of a minor second. In music theory, a minor second is the interval between two adjacent scale degrees in a major or minor scale, such as between C and D♭. Minor seconds are considered especially dissonant in Western harmony and are often used for expressive effects.

Size in different tunings

In twelve-tone equal temperament, a semitone is equal to 100 cents. In just intonation, semitones vary in size depending on the tuning system: the 5-limit just semitone of ratio 16:15 measures about 111.7 cents, while the Pythagorean semitone (limma) of ratio 256:243 measures about 90.2 cents. The difference between these two sizes is the subject of much historical discussion in tuning theory.
