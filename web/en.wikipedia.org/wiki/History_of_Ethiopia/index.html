History of Ethiopia

The history of Ethiopia stretches from some of the earliest evidence of anatomically modern humans through the Aksumite Empire, medieval dynasties, and the modern state.

Ancient and medieval periods

The Kingdom of Aksum, converted to Christianity in the 4th century, was a major trading power. The Zagwe dynasty built the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, and the Solomonic dynasty, from 1270, claimed descent from Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.

Modern period

Ethiopia defeated Italy at Adwa in 1896, preserving its independence during the Scramble for Africa. Occupied by Italy from 1936 to 1941, it was restored under Haile Selassie, who was overthrown by the Derg military regime in 1974. After the Derg's fall in 1991, Ethiopia became a federal republic.
