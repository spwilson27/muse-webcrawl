Krujë is a town in north central Albania, located on the slopes of Mount Krujë at the edge of the Ishëm River plain, about 20 kilometres northeast of the capital Tirana. It is best known as the stronghold of the Albanian national hero Skanderbeg, who made its fortress the centre of his quarter-century resistance to the Ottoman Empire.

History

The site was inhabited in antiquity by the Illyrian tribe of the Albanoi, and a castle stood here by the early medieval period. In 1190 Krujë became the centre of the Principality of Arbër, generally regarded as the first Albanian state. The town fell to the Ottomans in 1415, but in November 1443 Skanderbeg seized the castle and raised the double-headed eagle flag, beginning his revolt. The fortress withstood three major Ottoman sieges (1450, 1466 and 1467); Sultan Mehmed II himself led the first. Krujë finally surrendered to the Ottomans in 1478, a decade after Skanderbeg's death, following a fourth siege.

Sights

Within and below the restored castle walls stand the Skanderbeg Museum (opened 1982), designed in the form of a medieval fortress, and the National Ethnographic Museum in an Ottoman-era house. The old bazaar, one of Albania's oldest, selling carpets, filigree and antiques, runs along the cobbled lane leading to the castle gate. The nearby Sari Saltik tekke on the mountain summit is a Bektashi pilgrimage site.
