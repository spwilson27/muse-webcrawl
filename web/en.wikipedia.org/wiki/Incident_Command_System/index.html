Incident Command System

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Incident Command System (ICS) is a standardized, hierarchical management system used by emergency responders in the United States and many other countries to command, control, and coordinate the response to incidents ranging from structure fires to natural disasters and terrorist attacks. It provides a common structure, terminology, and set of procedures that allow personnel from many agencies to work together under a single management organization.

History

ICS was developed in California in the 1970s by the FIRESCOPE programme, after a series of catastrophic wildfires exposed failures of coordination among responding agencies — unclear chains of command, incompatible terminology, and unmanageable spans of control. The system was adopted across the US fire service and, following the September 11 attacks, was incorporated into the National Incident Management System (NIMS) in 2004, making it mandatory for federal agencies and for organizations receiving federal preparedness funding.

Organization

An ICS organization is headed by an Incident Commander (or a Unified Command when several jurisdictions share authority), supported by Command Staff (Public Information, Safety, and Liaison Officers) and four General Staff sections: Operations, Planning, Logistics, and Finance/Administration. The structure is modular and expands or contracts with the size of the incident; key principles include management by objectives, a manageable span of control (ideally three to seven subordinates per supervisor), common terminology, and unified command.
