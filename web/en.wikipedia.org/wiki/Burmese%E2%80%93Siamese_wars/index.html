Burmese–Siamese wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Burmese–Siamese wars were a series of wars fought between Burma and Siam (Thailand) from the 16th to the 19th century. The conflicts arose from rival ambitions for dominance over mainland Southeast Asia, control of the Tenasserim coast and its trade, and disputes over tributary states such as Lan Na.

Major conflicts

The wars began in earnest in the 1540s when King Tabinshwehti of Burma invaded the Ayutthaya Kingdom. Siam fell under Burmese suzerainty after the war of 1568–1569, but Prince Naresuan declared independence in 1584 and defeated Burmese invasions, culminating in the famous elephant duel of 1593. Burma and Siam fought again repeatedly in the 17th and 18th centuries; the Burmese invasion of 1765–1767 destroyed Ayutthaya and ended the kingdom, though Siam was reconstituted under Taksin and the Chakri dynasty.

Later wars

Wars continued into the early 19th century, including the Nine Armies' War of 1785–1786, which Siam repelled. The rivalry gradually subsided as both kingdoms came under pressure from Western colonial powers, and the last major clashes took place in the 1840s and 1850s over the Shan and Lao states.

Legacy

The wars are deeply embedded in the national histories and popular culture of both Myanmar and Thailand, where kings such as Naresuan, Bayinnaung, and Alaungpaya are remembered as national heroes.
