Tateyama, Chiba

Tateyama at the southern tip of the Bōsō Peninsula, Chiba Prefecture

Tateyama (Japanese: 館山市, Tateyama-shi) is a city located in Chiba Prefecture, Japan, at the southern end of the Bōsō Peninsula. Facing Tateyama Bay and the entrance to Tokyo Bay to the west and the Pacific Ocean to the south, the city is a popular resort area known for its mild climate, beaches and flowers.

Geography

Tateyama lies about 100 kilometres south of Tokyo. Its coastline includes Hojo Beach and other swimming beaches along the bay, noted for calm waters and views of Mount Fuji on clear days. The surrounding hills form part of the Minami Bōsō Quasi-National Park. The city's population is around 45,000, with an area of roughly 110 square kilometres.

History

The area was the centre of the small Tateyama Domain under the Tokugawa shogunate, ruled from Tateyama Castle by the Inaba clan in the later Edo period. The modern town was established in 1889, and Tateyama was elevated to city status in 1939 through the merger of several towns and villages. During the Second World War the area hosted Imperial Japanese Navy air facilities around Tateyama Bay.

Economy and attractions

Tourism, commercial fishing and agriculture — especially flowers, strawberries and dairy products — form the local economy. Tateyama Castle, reconstructed atop Shiroyama Park, houses a museum devoted to the Nansō Satomi Hakkenden, the epic 19th-century novel set in the region. Other attractions include the Daifukuji temple carved into a cliff, the Tateyama Family Park, and seasonal swimming, surfing and diving. The city is served by Tateyama Station on the JR Uchibō Line.
