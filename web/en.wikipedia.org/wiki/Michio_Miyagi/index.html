Michio Miyagi

Born: 7 April 1894, Kobe, Japan
Died: 25 June 1956 (aged 62)
Instrument: Koto
Known for: Haru no Umi ("The Sea in Spring"); inventing the 17-string bass koto

Michio Miyagi (宮城 道雄, Miyagi Michio; 7 April 1894 – 25 June 1956) was a Japanese musician and composer, the most famous koto player of the twentieth century. Blind from childhood, he transformed the koto from an instrument of a closed traditional repertoire into a vehicle for new composition, blending Japanese and Western musical ideas.

Life and career

Born in Kobe, Miyagi lost his sight at about the age of eight and was apprenticed to a koto master of the Ikuta school, himself becoming a master (iemoto) while still young. He spent part of his youth in Korea, where his father worked, before returning to Japan and settling in Tokyo. From the 1910s he began composing works that departed from classical koto forms, and he became a central figure of the "New Japanese Music" (Shin Nihon Ongaku) movement, collaborating with Western-trained musicians.

Works and innovations

His best-known composition is Haru no Umi ("The Sea in Spring", 1929), a duet for koto and shakuhachi inspired by the sea at Tomonoura, which is broadcast widely in Japan at New Year. To enrich ensemble textures Miyagi invented the jūshichigen, a 17-string bass koto, as well as smaller and larger variants of the instrument, and he wrote hundreds of works, including concertos combining koto with Western orchestra. He performed internationally and recorded extensively, and his playing was admired by musicians such as the French flautist René Le Roy, with whom he performed Haru no Umi.

Death and legacy

Miyagi died in 1956 after falling from a moving train while on tour. The Miyagi Michio Memorial Hall in Tokyo preserves his instruments and manuscripts, and Haru no Umi remains one of the most recognised pieces in the Japanese repertoire.
