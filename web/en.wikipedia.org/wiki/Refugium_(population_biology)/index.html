Refugium (population biology)

Refugium
FieldPopulation biology, biogeography

In biology, a refugium (plural: refugia) is a location which supports an isolated or relict population of a once more widespread species. This isolation (allopatry) can be due to climatic changes, geography, or human activities such as deforestation and overhunting.

Present examples of refugial animal species are the mountain gorilla, isolated to specific mountains in central Africa, and the southern elephant seal, which is restricted to a few islands in the far south of the Indian Ocean. This isolation, in many cases, can be seen as a temporary state; however, some refugia may be longstanding, thereby having many endemic species not found elsewhere.

Glacial refugia

During the last glacial maximum, areas such as the Iberian, Italian and Balkan peninsulas in Europe acted as refugia where species survived cold conditions before re-expanding northwards as the climate warmed. Genetic studies of present-day populations often reveal signatures of these refugia in patterns of genetic diversity, with refugial populations harbouring greater diversity than those in recolonised areas.

Refugia and conservation

Identifying and protecting refugia is important in conservation biology, since such areas may allow species to persist through periods of adverse climate, including ongoing climate change. In agriculture, "refuges" of non-Bt crops are also mandated to slow the evolution of resistance in insect pests.
