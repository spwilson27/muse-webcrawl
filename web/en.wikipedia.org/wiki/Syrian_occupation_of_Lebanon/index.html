Syrian occupation of Lebanon

The Syrian occupation of Lebanon refers to the period from 1976 to 2005 during which Syrian military forces were stationed in Lebanon and Syria exercised dominant influence over Lebanese politics. What began as an intervention in the Lebanese Civil War ended three decades later with a withdrawal forced by mass protests and international pressure after the assassination of Rafic Hariri.

Intervention and civil war

Syria intervened militarily in 1976, initially against Palestinian and leftist forces, and was legitimized as the core of an Arab Deterrent Force. After Israel's 1982 invasion and subsequent withdrawals, Syria consolidated control over most of Lebanon except the Israeli-occupied south. The 1989 Taif Agreement, which ended the civil war, called for a redeployment of Syrian troops, but they remained, numbering roughly 30,000–40,000 at their peak.

Tutelage era

During the 1990s and early 2000s, Syria vetted Lebanese political appointments, influenced elections, and its intelligence services were widely resented; thousands of Lebanese were detained in Syrian prisons. UN Security Council Resolution 1559 (2004) demanded the withdrawal of all foreign forces from Lebanon.

Withdrawal

The February 14, 2005 bombing that killed former premier Rafic Hariri sparked the Cedar Revolution; huge demonstrations in Beirut and diplomatic pressure compelled Syria to complete its withdrawal by April 26, 2005.
