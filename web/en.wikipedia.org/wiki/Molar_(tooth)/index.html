Molar (tooth)

The molars or molar teeth are large, flat teeth at the back of the mouth. They are more developed in mammals. They are used primarily to grind food during chewing. The name molar derives from Latin, molaris dens, meaning "millstone tooth", from mola, millstone, and dens, tooth. Molars show a great deal of diversity in size and shape across mammal groups.

A human molar, showing crown, cusps and roots.

In humans

Adult humans have twelve molars, three in each quadrant of the mouth. They erupt at roughly ages 6, 12 and 17–25 respectively; the third molars are the "wisdom teeth", which are vestigial in many people and commonly extracted. Human molars have four or five cusps and typically two to four roots. The first molars are the largest teeth and bear much of the force of mastication; their pits and fissures make them susceptible to dental caries, so they are often sealed in childhood.

Across mammals

Molar form reflects diet: carnivores have blade-like molars reduced in number, while herbivores have high-crowned (hypsodont), ridged molars for grinding vegetation, and the tribosphenic molar of early mammals — with cusps arranged in a triangle — is a key innovation in mammalian evolution. Molar morphology is central to palaeontology, since teeth fossilize well and reveal both diet and phylogeny.
