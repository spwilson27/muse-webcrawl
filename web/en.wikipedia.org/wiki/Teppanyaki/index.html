Teppanyaki (Japanese: 鉄板焼き, from teppan, "iron plate", and yaki, "grilled") is a style of Japanese cuisine in which ingredients are cooked on a large flat iron griddle, often directly in front of the diners. Typical ingredients include beef (notably wagyu and Kobe beef), chicken, shrimp, scallops, lobster and vegetables, seasoned with soy sauce, garlic and oil.

History

The style is generally credited to Shigeji Fujioka of the Misono restaurant chain, which opened in Kobe in 1945 and introduced griddle-cooked steak aimed at Western tastes. Teppanyaki spread in Japan and then abroad: in the United States the Benihana chain, founded in New York in 1964 by Hiroaki "Rocky" Aoki, popularised a theatrical version in which chefs perform knife tricks, juggle utensils and build flaming "onion volcanoes" at a communal table.

In Japan

Within Japan, teppanyaki ranges from high-end steak houses serving premium wagyu to casual restaurants where the griddle is used for dishes such as okonomiyaki, monjayaki and yakisoba. Western-style performance teppanyaki is regarded domestically as a largely export creation, though it has been re-imported for tourists.

Technique

The chef works with two metal spatulas and a knife, searing ingredients quickly at high heat, cutting them into bite-sized pieces on the griddle and serving them directly onto diners' plates, accompanied by dipping sauces and often garlic fried rice.
