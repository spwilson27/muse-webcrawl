The Crabbet Arabian Stud was a famous stud farm at Crabbet Park, Sussex, England, founded in 1878 by the poet and explorer Wilfrid Scawen Blunt and his wife Lady Anne Blunt (a granddaughter of Lord Byron). It was the most influential Arabian horse breeding programme in the English-speaking world: the Blunts imported desert-bred horses directly from Arabia and Egypt, and a large proportion of Arabian horses worldwide today carry Crabbet bloodlines.

Desert-bred Arabians imported by the Blunts founded the Crabbet lines.

Origins

On journeys through the Middle East in 1877–78 the Blunts visited the stud of Ali Pasha Sherif in Egypt and the Bedouin tribes of the Euphrates and northern Arabia, including the Anazah. Lady Anne's journals of these expeditions are classic sources on the desert Arabian. They bought foundation stock — including the mare Rodania and horses of the Kehilan and Seglawi strains — and established breeding at Crabbet Park in 1878, also maintaining the Sheykh Obeyd stud near Cairo, from which Crabbet drew Egyptian-bred stock.

The estate dispute and dispersal sales

After the Blunts separated, a bitter legal and family dispute over the estate culminated in the 1920 "partition sale" and a period in which many horses were sold, including the celebrated stallion Skowronek (sold to Musketeer's stud and later influential in Poland and Russia). Lady Anne's daughter, Judith Blunt-Lytton (Lady Wentworth), eventually regained control and ran Crabbet for decades, breeding notable horses such as the stallion Naseem and building the stud to renewed prominence.

Later history and legacy

After Lady Wentworth's death in 1957 the stud continued under her daughter's family until the building of the M23 motorway across the park forced the final dispersal in 1972. Crabbet horses were exported worldwide; the stud strongly influenced Arabian breeding in the United States, Australia, Poland, Russia and South Africa. Today the Crabbet Organisation and breeders of "Crabbet", "Old English" and high-percentage-Crabbet Arabians preserve the lines, valued for their soundness, athleticism and classic type. Skowronek, Mesaoud, Rodania and the mares of the "Crabbet dam lines" remain foundational names in Arabian pedigrees.
