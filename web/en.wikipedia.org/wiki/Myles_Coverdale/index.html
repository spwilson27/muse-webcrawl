Myles Coverdale, first name also spelled Miles (c. 1488 – 20 January 1569), was an English churchman and Bible translator. He is best known for producing the first complete printed translation of the Bible into English, the Coverdale Bible of 1535.

Life

Coverdale was educated at Cambridge and became an Augustinian friar at Norwich. Influenced by the ideas of the Reformation, he left England and worked on the Continent, assisting William Tyndale. Drawing on Tyndale's translations, the Latin Vulgate, and German versions by Luther and others, he published the complete Bible in English in 1535, probably printed at Antwerp.

He later contributed to the Great Bible of 1539, the first authorised version for use in English churches. Under Edward VI he became Bishop of Exeter in 1551, but was deprived of the see under Mary I and went into exile. After Elizabeth I's accession he returned to England and remained an influential figure among Puritan reformers. The metrical Psalms published in his Goostly Psalmes and Spirituall Songes, and his translations of Psalms, long remained in use; his renderings still appear in the Book of Common Prayer Psalter.
