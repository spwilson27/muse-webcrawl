Medium wave

Medium wave (MW) is the part of the medium frequency (MF) radio band used mainly for AM radio broadcasting. The spectrum provides about 120 channels, with more limited sound quality than FM stations, but greater broadcast range, especially at night.

In most of the world, medium wave stations use amplitude modulation (AM). The band's audio bandwidth is limited, giving a characteristic lower-fidelity sound suited to speech and news.

Propagation

During the day, medium wave signals travel mainly as groundwaves, reliably covering tens to a few hundred kilometres depending on transmitter power and ground conductivity. At night, the disappearance of the absorbing D layer of the ionosphere lets signals reflect from higher layers (skywave propagation), allowing reception hundreds or thousands of kilometres away — which also causes distant stations on the same frequency to interfere. Hobbyists known as DXers exploit this to log faraway stations.

Use and decline

Medium wave was the dominant broadcast band for much of the 20th century, carrying national networks, news, sport, and popular music. Since the late 20th century, audiences have migrated to FM, digital radio (DAB), and streaming, and many countries have closed or reduced medium wave services, though the band remains widely used in the Americas, Asia, and parts of Europe for talk, news, religious, and ethnic programming.
