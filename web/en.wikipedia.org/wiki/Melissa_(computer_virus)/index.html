Melissa (computer virus)

The Melissa virus is a mass-mailing macro virus released on March 26, 1999, that spread rapidly through email, infecting Microsoft Word documents and using Microsoft Outlook to mail itself to the first 50 addresses in a victim's address book. It caused an estimated $80 million in damages, disrupted email services at corporations and government agencies including the FBI and the U.S. Marine Corps, and prompted a major investigation that led to the arrest of its creator, David L. Smith.

Mechanism

Melissa spread in a Word document (commonly named list.doc) purporting to contain passwords for websites. When opened with macros enabled, the virus infected the Normal.dot template, attached itself to subsequently saved documents, and emailed the document to addresses from the user's address book with the subject "Important Message From [name]". The flood of mail overloaded many servers, forcing organisations to shut down email gateways.

Aftermath

Smith, a New Jersey programmer, was arrested within a week and in 2002 was sentenced to 20 months in federal prison and a fine. Melissa was one of the earliest high-profile demonstrations of the danger of mass-mailing malware and foreshadowed worms such as ILOVEYOU the following year.
