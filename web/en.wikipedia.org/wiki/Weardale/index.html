Weardale

Weardale is a dale, or valley, on the east side of the Pennines in County Durham, England. The River Wear flows through it from its source in the hills near Wearhead down to Bishop Auckland. Much of the upper dale lies within the North Pennines Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Geography and settlements

The dale runs for roughly 25 miles. Its principal settlements include Wearhead, Cowshill, Westgate, St John's Chapel, Daddry Shield, Eastgate, Stanhope, Frosterley and Wolsingham. Stanhope is the largest village and a traditional market and administrative centre for the upper dale.

History

Weardale was historically a hunting preserve of the Bishops of Durham and later became an important lead-mining district, worked from the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries by companies including the London Lead Company, whose legacy includes villages such as Nenthead just over the watershed and distinctive mining landscapes, hushes and levels. Limestone quarrying and cement works at Eastgate, and later aggregate quarrying, continued the industrial tradition. The heritage Weardale Railway runs between Stanhope and Bishop Auckland, with restoration efforts extending up the dale.

Notable features

The dale is noted for fluorite specimens from mines such as Rogerley and Heights, prized by collectors worldwide, and for winter sports at the Yad Moss ski tow near the head of the valley.
