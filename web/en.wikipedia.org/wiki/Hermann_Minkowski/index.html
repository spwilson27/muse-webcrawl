Hermann Minkowski

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hermann Minkowski (22 June 1864 – 12 January 1909) was a German mathematician and professor at Königsberg, Zürich, and Göttingen. He created and developed the geometry of numbers and later provided the mathematical framework of four-dimensional Minkowski space for Albert Einstein's theory of special relativity.

Life and work

Born in Aleksotas (now part of Kaunas, Lithuania) to a family of German and Jewish heritage, Minkowski won the Paris Academy's mathematics prize at eighteen for work on quadratic forms. In Zürich he taught at the ETH, where Einstein attended his lectures. In 1907–1908 he reformulated special relativity by fusing space and time into a single four-dimensional continuum, declaring that "space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality." Einstein initially resisted the formalism but soon adopted it — it became essential to general relativity.

Death

Minkowski died suddenly of appendicitis in Göttingen in 1909, aged 44. Concepts named after him include Minkowski space, Minkowski diagrams, the Minkowski inequality, and the Minkowski functional.
