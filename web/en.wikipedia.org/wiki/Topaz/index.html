Topaz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Topaz
Hardness: 8 on the Mohs scale (reference mineral)
Composition: Aluminium fluorosilicate
Varieties: Imperial topaz (golden-orange); blue topaz (usually irradiated)

Topaz is a silicate mineral of aluminium and fluorine, valued as a gemstone since antiquity. It defines hardness 8 on the Mohs scale — hard, though with perfect basal cleavage that makes it brittle to a sharp blow.

Most natural topaz is colourless; prized colours are the golden-orange "imperial" topaz of Ouro Preto, Brazil, and pink and sherry tones. Most blue topaz on the market is colourless material coloured by irradiation and heating — a standard, disclosed treatment. (The name caused historic confusion: the "topaz" of older texts was often peridot from Topazios island in the Red Sea.)

Giant crystals from pegmatites reach tens of kilograms; the Smithsonian holds cut stones of thousands of carats. As November's birthstone and a symbol of clarity, topaz sits in the same family of reference gems as corundum (9) and diamond (10) at the top of the scale taught alongside talc at its foot.
