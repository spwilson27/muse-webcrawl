Morgan Crofton

Born1826, Dublin, Ireland
Died13 May 1915, Brighton, England
FieldsMathematics, probability theory
InstitutionsRoyal Military Academy, Woolwich; Queen's University of Ireland

Morgan William Crofton (1826 – 13 May 1915) was an Irish mathematician who contributed to the field of geometric probability theory. He also worked with James Joseph Sylvester and contributed to the Encyclopædia Britannica article on probability.

Life and career

Crofton was educated at Trinity College Dublin. He held a post at Queen's College, Galway, which he resigned in 1857 because, as a convert to Catholicism working at a nondenominational institution during controversy over religious tests, he felt his position untenable; he later spent time teaching in France before becoming professor of mathematics at the Royal Military Academy, Woolwich, succeeding Sylvester.

Work

Crofton is remembered for work in integral geometry. Crofton's formula expresses the length of a plane curve in terms of the expected number of intersections of the curve with random lines — a foundational result later generalised in the Crofton–Hadwiger theory and used in stereology.
