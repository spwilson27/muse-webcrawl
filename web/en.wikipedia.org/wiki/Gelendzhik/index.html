Gelendzhik (Russian: Геленджик) is a resort town in Krasnodar Krai, Russia, situated on the Gelendzhik Bay of the north-eastern Black Sea coast, about 30 kilometres south-east of Novorossiysk. It is one of the principal seaside resorts of the Russian Caucasus coast, noted for its long embankment, beaches and surrounding pine-clad foothills of the Markotkh range.

History

The area was inhabited in antiquity and known to Greek traders; the bay was later a Genoese and Ottoman trading point, and the name is thought to derive from Turkish gelincik, "little bride". Russia founded a fortification here in 1831 during the Caucasian War, and the settlement grew as a trading and agricultural centre before receiving town status in 1915. During the Second World War Gelendzhik served as a major naval base and hospital town supporting the defence of Novorossiysk.

Resort and landmarks

Modern Gelendzhik markets itself as a family resort with an embankment of roughly 14 kilometres along the bay, three water parks, an oceanarium, dolphinarium, safari park and cable cars. The vicinity includes the Parus ("Sail") rock at Praskoveyevka, ancient dolmens in the Zhane valley, and a large palace complex near the town that attracted international attention in 2021 following an investigative film by Alexei Navalny's team. Gelendzhik Airport serves the town, and the M-4 Don highway links it with Krasnodar and Novorossiysk.
