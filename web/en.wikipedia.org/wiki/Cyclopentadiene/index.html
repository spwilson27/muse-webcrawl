Cyclopentadiene

Cyclopentadiene is an organic compound with the formula C5H6. It is often abbreviated CpH. This colorless liquid has a strong and unpleasant odor. At room temperature, this cyclic diene dimerizes over the course of hours to give dicyclopentadiene via a Diels–Alder reaction. This dimer can be restored by heating to give the monomer.

The compound is mainly used for the production of cyclopentene and its derivatives. It is popularly used as a precursor to the cyclopentadienyl anion (Cp−), an important ligand in cyclopentadienyl complexes in organometallic chemistry.

Production and availability

Cyclopentadiene production is usually not distinguished from dicyclopentadiene because they are interconverted. They are obtained from coal tar (about 10–20 g per tonne) and by steam cracking of naphtha (about 14 kg per tonne). Dicyclopentadiene is therefore the main commercially available form, and it is "cracked" by heating to regenerate the monomer.

Structure and bonding

The molecule consists of a planar ring of five carbon atoms, four of them bearing one hydrogen each and two of them connected by a double bond; the fifth carbon bears two hydrogens. The cyclopentadienyl anion derived from it is aromatic, with six delocalized pi electrons, which accounts for its remarkable acidity for a hydrocarbon (pKa about 16).

Uses

Beyond making cyclopentene and norbornene, cyclopentadiene is used to prepare cyclopentadienyl (Cp) complexes such as ferrocene, as well as substituted cyclopentadienes used in metallocene catalysts for olefin polymerization.
