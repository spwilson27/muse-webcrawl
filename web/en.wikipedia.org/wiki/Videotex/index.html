Videotex

Videotex (or interactive videotex) was one of the earliest implementations of an end-user information system. From the late 1970s to the early 1990s it was used to deliver information, usually in the form of pages of text and crude graphics, to a user via a television set or a simple terminal. A videotex system used a modem to connect the terminal to a central computer over the public telephone network, with data transmitted at very low speeds by modern standards, typically 1200/75 bit/s.

History

Videotex was pioneered in the United Kingdom in the 1970s by the Post Office research centre, leading to the Prestel service, launched in 1979. France developed the Minitel system, distributed free terminals to telephone subscribers and achieved mass adoption in the 1980s, offering directories, banking, shopping, and messaging. Canada developed Telidon, and Germany operated Bildschirmtext. The technology is often considered a precursor of the modern World Wide Web.

Technology

Videotex pages were built from a character set with mosaic graphics, typically displayed at 40 columns by 24 rows. Users navigated by entering page numbers or following menus. Interactive services allowed transactions such as booking travel tickets and home banking, making videotex one of the first online consumer services.

Decline

Most videotex services were discontinued in the 1990s as the graphical, open World Wide Web superseded their proprietary, text-based model. Minitel survived longest, finally closing in 2012.
