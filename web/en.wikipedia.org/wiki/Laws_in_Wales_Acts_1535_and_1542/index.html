Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542

The Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542 were Acts of the Parliament of England under Henry VIII which legally annexed the Principality of Wales to the Kingdom of England, extended English law to Wales, and gave Welsh constituencies representation in the English Parliament. They completed the incorporation of Wales begun with the conquest of Edward I and the Statute of Rhuddlan (1284).

The Welsh shires created by the Acts.

Provisions

The Act of 1535 abolished the Marcher lordships, divided Wales into counties (adding Monmouthshire, Brecknockshire, Radnorshire, Montgomeryshire, and Denbighshire to the existing shires), imposed English common law and administration, and required that legal proceedings and office-holding be in English — a provision with long-lasting consequences for the Welsh language. The Act of 1542 refined the settlement, establishing the Court of Great Sessions and the system of Welsh parliamentary boroughs and counties.

Repeal and legacy

The Acts were widely regarded as the formal union of England and Wales within a single state (long styled "England and Wales"). Most of their provisions were repealed by the Statute Law (Repeals) Act 1993, by which time devolution had begun to re-establish distinct Welsh institutions.
