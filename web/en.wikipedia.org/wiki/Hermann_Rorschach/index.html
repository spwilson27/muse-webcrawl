Hermann Rorschach (8 November 1884 – 1 April 1922) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst, best known for developing the projective psychological test that bears his name, the Rorschach inkblot test, published in his monograph Psychodiagnostics shortly before his early death.

Life

Rorschach was born in Zürich and grew up in Schaffhausen, where his father, an art teacher, encouraged his love of painting; at school his fascination with inkblot pictures — then a popular children's game called Klecksography — earned him the nickname "Klecks" ("inkblot"). Torn between art and science, he chose medicine, studying at Neuchâtel, Zürich, Bern and Berlin. In Zürich he came under Eugen Bleuler at the Burghölzli clinic, who coined the terms schizophrenia and autism, and took his doctorate in 1912 under Bleuler on reflex hallucinations.

In 1910 he married Olga Stempelin, a Russian doctor; they had two children. Rorschach spent a year working in Russia, then held posts at Swiss asylums at Münsterlingen and, from 1915, as associate director of the asylum at Herisau. He was active in the Swiss psychoanalytic movement and served as vice-president of the Swiss Psychoanalytic Society.

The inkblot test

Others — Binet among them — had used inkblots to study imagination, but Rorschach's innovation was to use them to diagnose mental illness: not chiefly by what patients saw, but how they perceived — whether they responded to the whole blot or details, to form, colour or texture, and what kind of movement they imagined. He standardised a set of ten inkblots (five black-and-white, five coloured) and published his method in Psychodiagnostics (1921). The test spread worldwide after his death, becoming one of the most famous — and most debated — instruments in clinical psychology.

Death

Rorschach died in Herisau on 1 April 1922, aged only 37, of peritonitis following a ruptured appendix. His archive is preserved at the University of Bern.
