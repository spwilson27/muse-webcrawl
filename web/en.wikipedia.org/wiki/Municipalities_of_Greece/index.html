Municipalities of Greece

The municipalities of Greece (Greek: dimoi) are the lowest level of government within the administrative structure of the Hellenic Republic. Following the Kallikratis reform, which took effect on 1 January 2011, Greece was divided into 325 municipalities, replacing the previous 1,033 municipalities and communities. A follow-up reform, the Kleisthenis I Programme of 2019, split several municipalities and raised the number to 332.

Structure

Municipalities are subdivided into municipal units, coinciding with the pre-2011 municipalities, and further into municipal and local communities. Each municipality is governed by a mayor and a municipal council, elected for five-year terms. Municipalities sit below the country's 13 regions (peripheries) and 74 regional units. The largest municipality by population is Athens, while many island and mountain municipalities have only a few thousand inhabitants.

Responsibilities

Greek municipalities are responsible for local services including water supply and waste management, urban planning and building permits, local roads, social welfare services, and the operation of municipal cultural and sporting facilities. Their finances combine state transfers with local fees and charges.
