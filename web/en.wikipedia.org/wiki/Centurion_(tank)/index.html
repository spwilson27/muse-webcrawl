Centurion (tank)

Centurion
Type: Main battle tank
Place of origin: United Kingdom
In service: 1945–1990s (variants still in use)
Main armament: 105 mm L7 (later marks); earlier 17-pdr / 20-pdr
Produced: 1945–1962; 4,423 built

The Centurion was the primary British main battle tank of the post–Second World War period. Introduced in 1945, it is widely considered one of the most successful tank designs of the century, serving for decades with many armies and forming the basis of numerous variants.

Development

Designed as a "universal tank" combining the firepower of the 17-pounder gun, protection against the German 8.8 cm gun and the mobility of a cruiser tank, the Centurion arrived in Germany just before the war ended. Successive marks up-gunned it with the 20-pounder and then the famous 105 mm L7 rifled gun, with a Rolls-Royce Meteor (later fuel-injected) engine.

Combat service

Centurions fought in Korea (notably at the Imjin River in 1951), with India in 1965 and 1971, with Israel in 1967 and 1973 (as the up-armoured Sho't), in Vietnam with Australia, and with South Africa in Angola (as the Olifant). Up-gunned and re-engined derivatives remained in service into the 21st century.
