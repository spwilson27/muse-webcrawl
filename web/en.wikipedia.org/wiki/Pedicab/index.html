Pedicab

A modern pedicab in service.

A pedicab is a small local means of transport. It is a type of cycle rickshaw, a tricycle designed to carry passengers on a for-hire basis. Pedicabs are common in many Asian cities, where they are also known by regional names such as becak (Indonesia), cyclo (Vietnam and Cambodia), and trishaw, and they also operate as tourist and leisure transport in many Western cities.

Design

The most common configuration places a bench seat for two or three passengers behind the driver, who pedals the vehicle. In the Vietnamese cyclo, by contrast, the passengers sit in front of the driver. Modern pedicabs may include canopies, lighting, and electric pedal assistance, and are typically built on heavy-duty tricycle frames with multiple gears.

History

Cycle rickshaws developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries from hand-pulled rickshaws. They spread widely across Asia after the Second World War as affordable urban transport. In many cities their numbers later declined under competition from motorized transport and regulatory bans, though they persist as an important livelihood and as short-distance transport in dense neighbourhoods.

Regulation and tourism

In Western cities such as London, New York, and Berlin, pedicabs operate mainly in tourist and entertainment districts and are subject to varying licensing regimes. Critics cite unregulated fares and congestion, while supporters emphasize their zero-emission operation and accessibility.
