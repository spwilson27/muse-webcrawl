Walk-off (baseball)

In baseball, a walk-off is an event (such as a hit or a home run) that results in the home team winning the game in the bottom of the final inning or in extra innings, so that the visiting team must "walk off" the field immediately. A walk-off home run is the most famous variety, but walk-offs can also occur on singles, doubles, errors, bases-loaded walks ("walk-off walk"), hit by pitch, wild pitches, and other plays.

Terminology

The term was popularized in the late 1980s and 1990s, often credited to Oakland Athletics Hall of Fame relief pitcher Dennis Eckersley, who famously surrendered a walk-off home run to Kirk Gibson in Game 1 of the 1988 World Series.

Notable examples

Famous walk-offs include Bill Mazeroski's home run in Game 7 of the 1960 World Series, Carlton Fisk's home run in Game 6 of the 1975 World Series, Joe Carter's home run in Game 6 of the 1993 World Series, and David Freese's home run in Game 6 of the 2011 World Series.
