Heimat

Heimat is a German word translating to "home" or "homeland". The word has connotations specific to German culture, deriving from the root of "home" in the sense of a place where one feels a sense of belonging and identity. It is broader than a mere dwelling place: Heimat can refer to a region, a town, a landscape, a dialect community, or a set of customs and social relationships with which an individual identifies and by which the individual derives a sense of identity.

A traditional Heimat landscape

Meaning

Unlike the English "home", Heimat is not restricted to a house or property and often denotes the larger community and landscape in which a person's life is rooted. It encompasses geography, history, culture, and interpersonal relationships. Because the term is connected with ideas of origin, rootedness and tradition, it has also at times been associated with conservative and nationalist movements; during the Nazi era the concept was drawn into the ideology of "blood and soil". In contemporary German discourse Heimat is widely discussed in relation to regional identity, migration and integration.

Heimatfilm

The Heimatfilm ("homeland film") was a popular genre of German cinema, especially in the 1950s, typically set in idyllic rural settings such as the Alps, the Black Forest or the Lüneburg Heath, with storylines centred on love, family, tradition and the contrast between city and country life. The influential television series Heimat (1984), directed by Edgar Reitz, chronicles life in a fictional village in the Hunsrück across much of the 20th century and is regarded as a landmark treatment of the theme.
