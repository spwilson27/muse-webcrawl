Personally identifiable information

Personally identifiable information (PII) is any information that can be used to identify a specific individual, either on its own or in combination with other information. The concept is central to privacy law and information security. Examples include a person's full name, Social Security number, driver's licence number, passport number, date and place of birth, biometric records, financial account numbers, and contact details such as home address, telephone number, and personal email address.

Definitions and law

The United States National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines PII as information that can distinguish or trace an individual's identity, alone or combined with other linked data. Legal regimes define the concept variously: the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation uses the broader term "personal data", while laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act enumerate categories of "personal information". Guidance documents distinguish direct identifiers (such as passport numbers) from quasi-identifiers (such as ZIP code and date of birth) that can re-identify individuals when combined.

Protection

Because breaches of PII enable identity theft and fraud, organisations are expected to safeguard it through measures such as encryption, access control, data minimisation, and de-identification. Many breach-notification laws require disclosure when PII is compromised.
