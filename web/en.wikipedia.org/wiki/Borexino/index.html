Borexino was a particle physics experiment at the Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso (LNGS) in Italy, designed to measure low-energy solar neutrinos in real time. Its name is a diminutive of BOREX (BORon EXperiment). Using an ultra-pure liquid scintillator target, Borexino achieved the most complete spectroscopy of neutrinos from the proton–proton fusion chain that powers the Sun, and in 2020 reported the first detection of neutrinos from the CNO cycle.

[ Image: The stainless steel sphere of the Borexino detector lined with photomultiplier tubes ]

Detector

The heart of the detector was 278 tonnes of pseudocumene-based liquid scintillator contained in a thin nylon vessel 8.5 metres across. Neutrinos scattering off electrons in the scintillator produced flashes of light recorded by 2,212 photomultiplier tubes mounted on a surrounding stainless steel sphere. Layers of purified water and buffer liquid shielded the target from external radiation, and the 1,400 metres of rock above the Gran Sasso laboratory suppressed cosmic-ray muons. Extreme radiopurity — contamination levels below one part in 1018 — was essential to see the faint signals.

Results

Borexino began data taking in May 2007. It made the first real-time measurement of the monoenergetic 7Be solar neutrino line (2007–2008), then measured the pep and pp neutrinos and placed limits on 8B neutrinos at low energy. Its precision measurements confirmed the MSW-LMA oscillation solution and the expected transition between vacuum and matter-dominated oscillation. In 2020 the collaboration announced the first experimental evidence of neutrinos from the CNO fusion cycle in the Sun. Data taking ended in October 2021.

SOX

A planned short-baseline experiment, SOX, would have placed a radioactive source beneath the detector to search for sterile neutrinos; it was cancelled in 2017 when the source could not be produced to specification.
