Boughton House is a country house in Northamptonshire, England, near Kettering. It is the home of the Duke of Buccleuch and is famous for its French-inspired architecture, fine art collection, and landscape designed in part by the gardener of Versailles. It is often called "the English Versailles."

History

The site began as a medieval monastery converted into a Tudor manor. Ralph Montagu, later 1st Duke of Montagu, who had served as ambassador to the court of Louis XIV, transformed the house from the 1680s onward, giving its north front a distinctly French appearance with a long, symmetrical façade. The house passed by marriage into the Buccleuch family in the 18th century and remained little altered thereafter, preserving its interiors to an unusual degree.

Collections

The house holds an outstanding collection of paintings (including works by Van Dyck and Gainsborough), French furniture, tapestries, porcelain, and one of Britain's finest collections of arms and armour. The library and state rooms retain their painted ceilings by Louis Chéron.

Gardens

The surrounding parkland contains grand avenues and canals laid out in the early 18th century, together with a modern landform artwork, Orpheus, by Kim Wilkie, which inverts the pattern of historic landscape mounds. The house and gardens open to the public seasonally.
