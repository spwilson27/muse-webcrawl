Proscription

Proscription (Latin: proscriptio) is, in current usage, a publicly posted list of persons officially condemned, declaring them outlaws whose property is confiscated and who may be killed with impunity. The term is most associated with ancient Rome, where it was a tool of political purge.

Roman proscriptions

The dictator Sulla introduced large-scale proscriptions in 82 BC, posting lists of his enemies in the Forum; those named could be murdered for a reward and their estates seized and auctioned. The practice was revived by the Second Triumvirate in 43 BC, proscribing thousands of senators and equestrians, including Cicero, whose head and hands were displayed on the Rostra.

Later usage

By extension, "proscription" now also denotes any official prohibition or condemnation, such as the banning of an organization or of a book.
