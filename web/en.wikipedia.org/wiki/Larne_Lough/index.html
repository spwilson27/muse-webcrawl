Larne Lough (Irish: Loch Latharna) is a sea loch or inlet in County Antrim, Northern Ireland. It lies between the Islandmagee peninsula and the mainland, opening into the North Channel through a narrow entrance at the port town of Larne. The lough is an important wetland habitat and is designated as a Ramsar site, a Special Protection Area, and an Area of Special Scientific Interest.

Geography

The lough extends roughly southwest from its mouth at Larne harbour. At the southwestern end lies Swan Island, a small nature reserve noted for its breeding roseate terns, one of the rarest breeding seabirds in Europe. The mudflats and saltmarshes exposed at low tide support internationally important numbers of wintering waterfowl, including light-bellied brent geese that migrate from the Canadian Arctic.

Industry and energy

The shores of the lough are home to major energy infrastructure, including the Ballylumford power station on Islandmagee, which supplies a large share of Northern Ireland's electricity, and Kilroot power station on the northern shore. A long-running proposal would store natural gas in caverns to be created in salt deposits deep beneath the lough bed off Islandmagee, a project that has been the subject of sustained local debate and legal proceedings.

Transport

The port of Larne, at the mouth of the lough, is one of Northern Ireland's principal ferry ports, with services to Scotland. The A2 coast road and the Belfast–Larne railway line run along the western shore.
