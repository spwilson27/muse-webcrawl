The Communist Party of Poland (Polish: Komunistyczna Partia Polski, KPP) was a communist party active in the Second Polish Republic from 1918 until 1938. It was founded on 16 December 1918 as the Communist Workers' Party of Poland by the merger of the Social Democracy of the Kingdom of Poland and Lithuania (SDKPiL) — the party of Rosa Luxemburg — with the left wing of the Polish Socialist Party. It should not be confused with the Polish Workers' Party (PPR) of 1942–1948 or the governing Polish United Workers' Party (PZPR) of 1948–1989.

History

The party opposed the reborn Polish state's borders, supported the Red Army during the Polish–Soviet War of 1920, and was banned and driven underground for most of the interwar period, though it retained influence in trade unions, among some national minorities, and in cultural organisations. Its membership fluctuated in the low tens of thousands. The party suffered heavily from factional conflict and from Moscow's interventions: during the Great Purge, most of its leaders resident in the USSR were arrested and executed.

Dissolution

On 16 August 1938 the Executive Committee of the Comintern, acting on Stalin's direction and on fabricated charges that the party had been infiltrated by Polish intelligence agents, dissolved the KPP. Nearly all of its senior cadres in the Soviet Union perished in the purges. The party was formally rehabilitated in 1956 by the communist parties of Poland, the USSR, Italy, Bulgaria, and Finland, which acknowledged the charges had been baseless.
