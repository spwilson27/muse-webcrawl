Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome

Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome (HBOC) is an inherited cancer predisposition syndrome characterised by a greatly increased risk of breast cancer and ovarian cancer at unusually young ages. It is most commonly caused by germline mutations in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 tumour suppressor genes, and is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern.

Signs and risks

Women who carry a pathogenic BRCA1 variant have a lifetime breast cancer risk often estimated at 55–72% and an ovarian cancer risk of roughly 39–44%; figures for BRCA2 are somewhat lower. The syndrome is also associated with increased risks of prostate, pancreatic and male breast cancer. Certain populations, including people of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, carry founder mutations at unusually high frequency.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is by genetic testing of the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes, offered to people whose personal or family history suggests the syndrome — for example, multiple relatives with early-onset breast cancer or any case of high-grade serous ovarian cancer.

Management

Management includes intensified screening (breast MRI and mammography), risk-reducing surgery such as bilateral mastectomy and salpingo-oophorectomy, and chemoprevention in selected cases. Cancers arising in carriers may respond particularly well to platinum chemotherapy and PARP inhibitors.
