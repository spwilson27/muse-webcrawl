Henry Condell (baptised 5 September 1576 – December 1627) was an English actor in the King's Men, the playing company for which William Shakespeare wrote. He was one of the two editors — with his colleague John Heminges — of the First Folio, the collected edition of Shakespeare's plays published in 1623, seven years after Shakespeare's death. Without the work of Condell and Heminges, eighteen of Shakespeare's plays, including Macbeth, Twelfth Night, Julius Caesar, and The Tempest, might have been lost, since they had never previously been printed.

Life and career

Condell was originally from Norfolk. His will names nine children, of whom three survived to adulthood. He first appears in theatrical records in the cast lists of Ben Jonson's plays: he acted in Every Man in His Humour (1598) and Every Man out of His Humour (1599) for the Lord Chamberlain's Men. Unlike most of his fellow actors, Condell is documented as a sharer in the company by the early 1600s: when the company became the King's Men in 1603 under the patronage of James I, Condell was one of the sharers named in the royal patent.

Condell became financially successful, acquiring shares in both the Globe Theatre and the Blackfriars Theatre, and property in Fulham, where he lived at a house near the river. Shakespeare remembered him in his will with a bequest of 26 shillings and 8 pence to buy a mourning ring, as he did for Heminges and Richard Burbage.

The First Folio

In 1623, Condell and Heminges oversaw the publication of Mr. William Shakespeare's Comedies, Histories, & Tragedies, known as the First Folio. Their dedicatory epistle "To the great Variety of Readers" and the preface are famous documents of theatre history; in them the editors claimed to offer the plays "cured, and perfect of their limbs", collected from Shakespeare's own papers. Scholars continue to debate how literally to take these claims, but the Folio's central importance is undisputed: it preserved 36 plays, half of them previously unpublished, and established the canon of Shakespeare's drama.

Condell died in Fulham in December 1627 and was buried at St Mary Woolchurch Haw in London. His will, proved in 1628, left his shares in the Globe and Blackfriars to his family; his widow Elizabeth later remarried and the shares were eventually sold under complicated circumstances.
