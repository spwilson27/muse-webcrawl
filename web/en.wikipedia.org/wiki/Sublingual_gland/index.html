Sublingual gland

The sublingual gland is one of the three paired major salivary glands. Lying beneath the tongue in the floor of the mouth, above the mylohyoid muscle, it is the smallest of the major glands and produces a predominantly mucous saliva.

SystemDigestive system
LocationFloor of mouth, beneath the tongue
ArterySublingual artery
NerveLingual nerve / chorda tympani (facial nerve)

Anatomy

Each sublingual gland is almond-shaped and weighs about 2–4 grams. Unlike the parotid and submandibular glands, it lacks a true capsule and a single main duct; instead it drains through 8–20 small ducts (the ducts of Rivinus) opening along the sublingual fold, and sometimes a larger Bartholin's duct joining the submandibular duct.

Clinical significance

Blockage or damage to a sublingual duct can produce a ranula, a mucous retention cyst in the floor of the mouth. The gland can also be affected by sialadenitis, sialolithiasis and, rarely, tumours.
