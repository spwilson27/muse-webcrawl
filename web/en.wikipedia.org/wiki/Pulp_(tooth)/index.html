Pulp (tooth)

The dental pulp is the soft living tissue at the centre of a tooth, enclosed by dentine within the pulp chamber of the crown and the root canals. It contains nerves, blood vessels, connective tissue and specialised cells including odontoblasts that form dentine.

Functions

The pulp forms dentine during tooth development, nourishes the tooth, provides sensation to stimuli such as temperature and pressure, and mounts defensive responses to injury and infection by laying down reparative dentine.

Clinical aspects

Inflammation of the pulp (pulpitis), usually from deep caries or trauma, causes toothache and may progress to pulp necrosis and periapical infection. Treatments include pulp capping, pulpotomy and root canal therapy, in which the pulp is removed and the canals are cleaned, shaped and filled.
