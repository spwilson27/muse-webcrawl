A personal digital assistant (PDA) is a multi-purpose mobile device which functions as a personal information manager. PDAs were largely displaced by the widespread adoption of highly capable smartphones, in particular those based on iOS and Android in the late 2000s and early 2010s.

History

The term PDA was first used in 1992 by Apple Computer's CEO John Sculley in reference to the Apple Newton. In 1996, Palm introduced the Pilot series of PDAs, which became an immediate success. Early devices ran software for managing contacts, calendars, to-do lists and notes, and synchronised with a desktop computer via a cradle or infrared link. Later models added colour screens, wireless connectivity, web browsing, media playback and handwriting recognition.

Typical features

A typical PDA included a touchscreen or stylus-operated interface, a calendar, an address book, a task list, a memo pad and a calculator. Business-oriented devices often supported document editing and email. Popular operating systems included Palm OS, Microsoft's Pocket PC / Windows Mobile, Symbian and BlackBerry OS.

Decline

As smartphones combined telephony, internet access and PDA functions in a single device, standalone PDAs declined. By the early 2010s most manufacturers had discontinued them, though specialised PDAs continued in niches such as healthcare, warehousing and field service, where rugged handheld data-collection devices remain common.
