Grubenhaus

A grubenhaus (plural grubenhäuser) is a type of sunken-featured building that was widespread in early medieval northern and western Europe, particularly in Germanic settlements from roughly the 5th to the 12th centuries. In English archaeology they are often called pit-houses or sunken huts.

The structure was built over a shallow rectangular pit, typically around 3–5 metres long, with a pitched roof supported by posts, often two main posts at the gable ends. The excavated hollow may have formed the floor of the building, or a suspended plank floor may have been laid above it, leaving an insulating air gap beneath.

Grubenhäuser are frequently interpreted as workshops rather than dwellings, especially where finds of loom weights and spindle whorls indicate textile production. Excavated examples are known from sites such as West Stow and Mucking in England, and from settlements across Germany, the Netherlands, Denmark and Scandinavia.
