Indo-Surinamese

Indo-Surinamese, also known as Hindustanis (Dutch: Hindoestanen), are Surinamese nationals of Indian ancestry. They are the largest ethnic group in Suriname, descended from indentured labourers brought from British India to the Dutch colony of Surinam in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

History

The first ship, the Lalla Rookh, arrived in 1873, ten years after the Netherlands abolished slavery in its colonies. Around 34,000 Indians migrated under the indenture system before it ended in 1916. Many settled permanently, and after Suriname's independence in 1975 a large number of Indo-Surinamese emigrated to the Netherlands, where they form a significant community.

Culture

Indo-Surinamese culture preserves North Indian traditions adapted to Suriname. Sarnami Hindustani, a Caribbean Hindustani dialect, is widely spoken. Hinduism and Islam are the main religions; baithak gana music, roti, and celebrations of Diwali and Eid are prominent. The group has been influential in Surinamese politics, business and agriculture, especially rice farming in the Nickerie district.
