Weyl group

FieldLie theory, group theory
Named afterHermann Weyl
Related conceptsRoot system, Coxeter group, Lie algebra

In mathematics, in particular the theory of Lie algebras, the Weyl group of a root system Φ is the subgroup of the isometry group of that root system generated by reflections through the hyperplanes perpendicular to the roots. Equivalently, it is the quotient group of the normalizer of a maximal torus in a Lie group by the centralizer of that torus.

Weyl groups are named after Hermann Weyl and are finite reflection groups — indeed, they are exactly the finite Coxeter groups arising from crystallographic root systems, and they play a central role in the classification and representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras and algebraic groups.

Definition

Let Φ be a root system in a Euclidean space V. For each root α ∈ Φ, let sα denote the reflection in the hyperplane perpendicular to α:

sα(λ) = λ − 2(λ, α)/(α, α) · α

The Weyl group W is the group generated by all such reflections sα. Because reflections in the simple roots already generate the group, W is generated by |Δ| simple reflections subject to Coxeter-type relations, and is therefore a finite Coxeter group whose Coxeter diagram coincides with the Dynkin diagram of the root system (with multiple bonds collapsed appropriately).

Examples

  • For the root system An−1 (the Lie algebra sln), the Weyl group is the symmetric group Sn, permuting the coordinates.
  • For Bn and Cn, the Weyl group is the group of signed permutations, of order 2n n! — the hyperoctahedral group.
  • For Dn, it is the subgroup of signed permutations with an even number of sign changes, of order 2n−1 n!.
  • For the exceptional root systems G2, F4, E6, E7 and E8, the Weyl groups are finite reflection groups of orders 12, 1152, 51840, 2903040 and 696729600 respectively.

Relation to Lie groups

If G is a compact connected Lie group with maximal torus T, the Weyl group can be defined as

W = NG(T) / ZG(T)

the quotient of the normalizer of T by its centralizer. This group acts on the character lattice of T (identified with the root lattice's ambient space) and agrees with the reflection-group definition. It acts simply transitively on the set of Weyl chambers — the connected components into which the root hyperplanes divide the space — and its action governs such structural facts as the Bruhat decomposition and the flag variety G/B.

Role in representation theory

The Weyl group controls the representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras. In the Weyl character formula, the character of an irreducible representation is expressed as an alternating sum over the Weyl group; the Weyl dimension formula likewise sums over W. Highest weights of irreducible representations are permuted, up to a shift by the half-sum of positive roots ρ, by the "dot action" of the Weyl group, and each orbit contains a unique dominant weight.
