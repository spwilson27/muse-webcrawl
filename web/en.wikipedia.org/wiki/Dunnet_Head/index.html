Dunnet Head

Dunnet Head (Scottish Gaelic: Ceann Dùnaid) is a peninsula in Caithness, on the north coast of Scotland. Its headland, also called Easter Head, is the most northerly point of the British mainland — further north than John o' Groats, which lies to the southeast. The point stands at approximately 58.67° N latitude, and from its cliffs, which rise about 105 metres above the Pentland Firth, the Orkney Islands are visible on clear days.

Features

Dunnet Head lighthouse, standing on the cliff edge, was built in 1831 by Robert Stevenson, grandfather of the author Robert Louis Stevenson. The headland is composed of Old Red Sandstone and supports important seabird colonies, including puffins, guillemots, razorbills, and fulmars; much of it is protected as a nature reserve managed by the RSPB. During the Second World War the headland hosted a radar station, part of the Chain Home network, and other military installations guarding the approaches to Scapa Flow.

The peninsula also includes Dunnet Bay, a long sandy beach backed by dunes, and the village of Dunnet. Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother, who owned the nearby Castle of Mey, took an interest in the headland and its viewpoint.
