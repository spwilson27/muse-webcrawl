Middle Park Stakes

The Middle Park Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to two-year-old colts. It is run at Newmarket Racecourse over a distance of 6 furlongs (1,207 metres), and it is scheduled to take place each year in late September or early October, during the Cambridgeshire Meeting.

History

The event was established in 1866 and named after Middle Park, a stud at Eltham in south-east London owned by William Blenkiron, who founded the race. It quickly became one of the most prestigious races for juveniles in the British calendar and is traditionally a pointer to the following season's 2000 Guineas, though its emphasis has increasingly shifted toward sprinting pedigrees.

The Middle Park Stakes is one of Britain's few Group 1 races restricted to two-year-old colts; its fillies' counterpart is the Cheveley Park Stakes, run at the same meeting.

Notable winners

Famous winners include Ormonde (1885), Bayardo (1908), Tetraema-era champions, Brigadier Gerard (1970), Grundy-era contemporaries, Rodrigo de Triano, Royal Academy, Oasis Dream, Dutch Art, Dark Angel, Dream Ahead, Astaire, and later sprint stars such as Ten Sovereigns and Vandeek. Many winners subsequently became influential sires, particularly as sources of speed.

Records

The race is contested over the straight six furlongs of the Rowley Mile course. Leading jockey honours in the modern era include several multiple winners, and the race forms part of a celebrated autumn programme for juveniles alongside the Dewhurst Stakes over seven furlongs.
