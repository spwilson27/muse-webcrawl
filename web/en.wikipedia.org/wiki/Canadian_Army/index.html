Canadian Army

The Canadian Army (French: Armée canadienne) is the command responsible for the operational readiness of the conventional ground forces of the Canadian Armed Forces. As of the early 2020s, the army has roughly 23,000 regular soldiers, 19,000 reserve soldiers (including about 3,000 Canadian Rangers), and about 3,300 civilian employees. It maintains regular forces units at bases across Canada and is also responsible for the Army Reserve, the largest component of the Primary Reserve.

History

Prior to the creation of the Permanent Active Militia in 1883, Canada's defence rested on volunteer militia units and British garrisons. Canadian forces served in the North-West Rebellion of 1885, the Second Boer War, and both World Wars. In the First World War the Canadian Corps fought at the Somme, at Vimy Ridge (1917), and in the Hundred Days Offensive. In the Second World War, the First Canadian Army was formed in 1942 and fought in Italy, in Normandy, and in the liberation of the Netherlands.

During the Cold War, Canada maintained a brigade group in West Germany. Canadian soldiers served in the Korean War, in numerous United Nations peacekeeping missions, and in the War in Afghanistan (2001–2014), Canada's longest war. In 1968 the army was merged into the unified Canadian Forces as Mobile Command; the historic designation "Canadian Army" was restored in 2011.

Structure

The army is organized into four divisions (the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 5th Canadian Divisions), aligned geographically, a Canadian Army Doctrine and Training Centre, and a combat support brigade. Regular force field units include three mechanized brigade groups, each composed of infantry battalions, an armoured regiment, artillery, engineers, and support units. Principal equipment includes the Leopard 2 main battle tank, the LAV 6.0 infantry fighting vehicle, and the M777 howitzer.
