Non-breaking space

Non-breaking space
Unicode: U+00A0
HTML entity: &nbsp;
UTF-8: 0xC2 0xA0
Other names: Hard space, fixed space

In word processing and digital typesetting, a non-breaking space (also called an unbreakable space, hard space, or fixed space) is a space character that prevents an automatic line break at its position. In most formats it is also not collapsed when adjacent to other whitespace, unlike a regular space.

Uses

Non-breaking spaces are used where a line break between two elements would be awkward or misleading: between a number and its unit ("100 km"), between initials and a surname, in expressions such as "Section 5," and in French typography before certain punctuation marks such as the colon and exclamation mark in some conventions.

Encodings

The character is encoded in Unicode at U+00A0, inherited from ISO/IEC 8859-1 (Latin-1). In HTML it can be written as the named entity &nbsp; or as numeric references &#160; and &#xA0;. Word processors typically insert it with Ctrl+Shift+Space (Microsoft Word) or similar shortcuts. Unicode also defines related characters such as the narrow no-break space (U+202F) and the no-break space of zero width (U+FEFF).

Issues

Because the non-breaking space is a different character from the ordinary space, copying text containing it can cause unexpected behaviour in search, sorting, and programming contexts where a plain space was expected.
