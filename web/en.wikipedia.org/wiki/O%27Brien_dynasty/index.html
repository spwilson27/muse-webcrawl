The O'Brien dynasty (Irish: Ó Briain) is a royal and noble house founded in the Kingdom of Munster in early medieval Ireland, descended from Brian Boru (died 1014), the High King of Ireland who broke the power of the Vikings at the Battle of Clontarf. The O'Briens ruled Munster and later the Kingdom of Thomond in the northwest of the province for centuries.

Origins

The family belonged to the Dál gCais, a rising dynasty of the Shannon region, whose propagandists crafted genealogies linking them to ancient kings of Munster. Brian Boru's seizure of the high kingship made his descendants — taking the surname Ó Briain, "grandson/descendant of Brian" — one of the great dynasties of Gaelic Ireland.

Medieval Thomond

After the Anglo-Norman invasion of the twelfth century, the O'Briens, now centered on Thomond (modern County Clare and adjoining districts), alternately resisted and accommodated the newcomers. Kings such as Domnall Mór Ó Briain and his successors maintained a largely independent Gaelic lordship into the sixteenth century, even as Norman families settled the south of Munster.

Tudor surrender and regrant

In 1543 Murrough O'Brien submitted to Henry VIII under the policy of surrender and regrant, receiving the title Earl of Thomond, while his nephew Donough was made Baron Inchiquin — a dual succession that split the family's inheritance. The Inchiquin branch later became prominent in Irish and British military and political life, and the O'Briens remained significant landowners through the upheavals of the seventeenth century and beyond.
