The Imitation of Christ (Latin: De imitatione Christi) is a Christian devotional book written in Latin, generally attributed to the German-Dutch canon regular Thomas à Kempis (c. 1380–1471). Composed between about 1418 and 1427 within the Devotio Moderna movement, it is one of the most widely read devotional works in history and, after the Bible, among the most translated books ever published.

Structure and content

The work is divided into four books: (1) "Helpful Counsels of the Spiritual Life"; (2) "Directives for the Interior Life"; (3) "On Interior Consolation", written as a dialogue between Christ and the disciple; and (4) "On the Blessed Sacrament", a dialogue on the Eucharist. Its spirituality emphasises humility, renunciation of worldly ambition, interior prayer, and the imitation of Christ's life and suffering — summed up in its opening quotation from John's Gospel: "He who follows Me, walks not in darkness."

Influence

The Imitation shaped Catholic and, after translation (including John Wesley's edition), Protestant devotion alike; it influenced figures from Ignatius of Loyola to Thérèse of Lisieux. At least 800 early manuscripts survive, a record for a medieval work, and thousands of printed editions have appeared in dozens of languages.
