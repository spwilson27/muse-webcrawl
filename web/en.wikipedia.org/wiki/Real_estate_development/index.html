Real estate development

Real estate development, or property development, is the business process of creating value from land and buildings: acquiring sites, obtaining planning permission, arranging finance, building or refurbishing property, and selling or leasing the finished product. Developers range from small firms building a handful of houses to large companies delivering office districts, shopping centres and new towns.

The development process

A typical project proceeds through site identification and feasibility appraisal; land acquisition; design and planning consent; financing, usually combining equity with construction loans; procurement and construction; and finally marketing, sale or letting. The developer's profit depends on the margin between the completed value and total costs including land, construction, finance and professional fees — appraised in advance by the residual method of valuation.

Risks

Development is inherently risky: property markets are cyclical, construction is slow relative to market movements, and projects are highly leveraged, so a downturn can leave completed schemes worth less than their cost. Planning risk, construction cost overruns and interest-rate changes add further uncertainty.

Types

Development may be residential, commercial, industrial or mixed-use; speculative (built without a pre-committed occupier) or pre-let/pre-sold. Land development or subdivision prepares raw land with roads and utilities for sale as serviced plots. Public-sector and community development corporations undertake projects intended to regenerate deprived areas rather than maximise profit.
