Arkham is a fictional city in Massachusetts created by American author H. P. Lovecraft and used in many of his stories and those of other writers in the Cthulhu Mythos. Situated on the Miskatonic River in Essex County, it is home to Miskatonic University, whose library holds one of the few copies of the Necronomicon, and to the Arkham Sanitarium.

In Lovecraft's fiction

Arkham first appeared in the short story "The Picture in the House" (written 1920). It is the principal setting of "The Dunwich Horror" (in the surrounding hill country), "The Dreams in the Witch House", "The Thing on the Doorstep", and "The Shadow Over Innsmouth" (nearby Innsmouth), among others. Lovecraft modelled its gambrel-roofed, witch-haunted old town on real New England settlements such as Salem and Newburyport. August Derleth and later writers expanded the setting, and the region became known collectively as "Lovecraft Country".

Legacy

The name was borrowed by DC Comics for Arkham Asylum, the Gotham City psychiatric hospital for the criminally insane in the Batman universe, whose founders named it after the Lovecraft city. "Arkham" also names the board game Arkham Horror and the publishing imprint Arkham House, founded by Derleth to preserve Lovecraft's work.
