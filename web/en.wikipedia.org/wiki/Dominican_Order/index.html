Dominican Order

The Order of Preachers (Latin: Ordo Praedicatorum, OP), commonly known as the Dominican Order, is a Catholic mendicant religious order founded by the Spanish priest Saint Dominic de Guzmán in France and approved by Pope Honorius III on 22 December 1216.

Order of Preachers
Founded: 1216, Toulouse
Founder: Saint Dominic
Abbreviation: OP
Motto: Laudare, Benedicere, Praedicare
Habit: White tunic, black cloak

History

Dominic founded the order to preach the Gospel and combat heresy, notably Albigensianism in southern France, combining monastic prayer and study with itinerant preaching and voluntary poverty. The order spread rapidly across Europe and staffed the medieval universities; its greatest theologian was Thomas Aquinas. Dominicans played a central role in the Inquisition, in missions to the Americas and Asia — Bartolomé de las Casas defended Indigenous peoples — and in the development of the Rosary tradition.

Structure and life

The order comprises friars (priests and brothers), cloistered nuns, apostolic sisters, and lay Dominicans, governed democratically by elected chapters and a Master of the Order based at Santa Sabina in Rome. Study, common prayer, community life and preaching are its four pillars.

Notable Dominicans

Albertus Magnus, Catherine of Siena, Meister Eckhart, Giordano Bruno (former), Savonarola, and Fra Angelico.
