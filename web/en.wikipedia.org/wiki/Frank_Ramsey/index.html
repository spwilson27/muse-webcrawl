Frank Ramsey

Frank Plumpton Ramsey (22 February 1903 – 19 January 1930) was a British philosopher, mathematician, and economist who made fundamental contributions to each field before his death at age 26.

Work

In mathematics he founded Ramsey theory with his theorem on combinatorics ("Ramsey's theorem"). In philosophy, his 1926 paper "Truth and Probability" laid foundations for subjective probability and decision theory, and he proposed what became known as the Ramsey sentence. In economics, his papers on optimal taxation (the Ramsey rule) and optimal saving (the Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans model) remain influential.

Life

Ramsey was a fellow of King's College, Cambridge, translated Wittgenstein's Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus into English, and died following abdominal surgery in 1930.
