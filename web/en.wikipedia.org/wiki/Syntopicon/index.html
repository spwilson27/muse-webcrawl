Syntopicon

A Syntopicon: An Index to The Great Ideas is a two-volume reference work published in 1952 as volumes 2 and 3 of the Great Books of the Western World, issued by Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc. Compiled under the direction of philosopher Mortimer J. Adler and his Institute for Philosophical Research, the Syntopicon indexes the set's works by idea rather than by author or subject keyword.

The 102 Great Ideas

The Syntopicon organizes the Western intellectual tradition around 102 "Great Ideas" — from Angel, Animal, and Aristocracy through Truth, Virtue, War and Peace, Wealth, Wisdom, and World. Each idea is treated in a chapter containing an introductory essay (written by Adler and his colleagues), an outline of topics, and an inventory of passages across the Great Books — some 3,000 topics in all, backed by roughly 163,000 page references — supplemented by cross-references and bibliographies of additional readings.

Adler's team spent nearly a decade and about a million dollars on the indexing. The method, called "syntopical reading", asks the reader to compare how different authors across centuries treat the same question, rather than reading each work in isolation. Adler popularized the approach in later works such as How to Read a Book (revised 1972). A revised edition of the Great Books, published in 1990, retained the Syntopicon in updated form.
