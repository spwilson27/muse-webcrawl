Phar Lap (4 October 1926 – 5 April 1932) was a champion New Zealand-bred Thoroughbred racehorse who became an Australian national icon during the Great Depression. In a career of 51 starts he won 37 races, including the 1930 Melbourne Cup, two Cox Plates and, in his final race, the Agua Caliente Handicap in Mexico — then the richest race in North America.

Career

Bought cheaply at yearling sales for trainer Harry Telford, Phar Lap was initially unimpressive but developed into a dominant stayer, famous for powerful finishing runs. His 1930 Melbourne Cup victory, carrying a heavy weight after surviving an attempt to shoot him days before the race, made him a folk hero to a public suffering through the Depression. His name derives from a Thai expression for "lightning".

Death and legacy

Phar Lap died suddenly in California in April 1932, shortly after his Agua Caliente triumph. The cause was disputed — arsenic poisoning, whether accidental or deliberate, has been the leading explanation supported by later forensic analysis. His remains became famous relics: his hide is displayed at Melbourne Museum, his skeleton in New Zealand, and his unusually large heart at the National Museum of Australia. Books, a 1983 film and songs have kept him among the most celebrated racehorses in history.
