Politics of Iceland

The Alþingishúsið in Reykjavík, seat of the Althing

The politics of Iceland take place in the framework of a parliamentary representative democratic republic, in which the president is head of state and the prime minister is head of government. Legislative power is vested in the Althing, founded in 930 and often described as one of the world's oldest parliaments. Iceland has a multi-party system, and governments are almost always coalitions.

Constitution

The present constitution was adopted in 1944, when Iceland became a republic, ending the union with Denmark. It is based on the earlier 1920 constitution of the Kingdom of Iceland. A process to draft a new constitution followed the 2008 financial crisis and an advisory referendum in 2012, but the resulting bill was never ratified by the Althing.

Institutions

The Althing has 63 members elected by proportional representation in six multi-member constituencies for four-year terms. The president, elected by popular vote for a four-year term, has limited powers but may refuse to sign legislation, sending it to referendum. Halla Tómasdóttir became president in August 2024.

Parties

The historically dominant party is the conservative Independence Party. Other major parties include the Progressive Party, the Social Democratic Alliance, the Left-Green Movement, the People's Party, the Pirate Party, the Reform Party, and the Centre Party. The 2008–2011 Icelandic financial crisis reshaped the party system, bringing down the government in the "Kitchenware Revolution" protests and leading to the prosecution of bankers and a former prime minister. Kristrún Frostadóttir of the Social Democratic Alliance became prime minister in December 2024, leading a coalition with the Reform Party and the People's Party.
