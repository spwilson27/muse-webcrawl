St James's Park

LocationCity of Westminster, London
Area23 hectares (57 acres)
Created1603 (as a royal park)

St James's Park is one of the Royal Parks of London, in the City of Westminster, east of Buckingham Palace and west of Downing Street. It is the oldest royal park in London and covers 23 hectares, centred on a small lake famous for its waterfowl, including a resident colony of pelicans first introduced in 1664.

History

The land was acquired by Henry VIII in 1532 and landscaped as a deer park for St James's Palace. James I kept exotic animals there, including camels and an elephant. Charles II redesigned the park in the French style with a long canal and opened it to the public. In the 1820s John Nash remodelled it into its present romantic form for George IV.

Surroundings

The park is bounded by The Mall to the north, Horse Guards to the east, and Birdcage Walk to the south. The Blue Bridge over the lake offers a classic view between Buckingham Palace and the government buildings of Whitehall.
