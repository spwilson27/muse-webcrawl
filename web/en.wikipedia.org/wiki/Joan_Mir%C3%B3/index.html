Joan Miró

Joan Miró i Ferrà (1893–1983) was a Catalan painter, sculptor and ceramicist born in Barcelona. A leading figure of Surrealism, he developed a distinctive visual language of biomorphic shapes, stars, birds and bold primary colours.

Career

Miró moved to Paris in 1920 and associated with André Breton and the Surrealists, though he kept an independent course. Works such as The Farm (1921–22), Harlequin's Carnival (1924–25) and his "dream paintings" established his reputation. During the Spanish Civil War he painted The Reaper for the Spanish Republic's pavilion at the 1937 Paris Exhibition.

Later work and legacy

After the Second World War Miró divided his time between Barcelona, Palma de Mallorca and Mont-roig del Camp. He produced large public works, ceramics with Josep Llorens Artigas, and monumental sculptures. The Fundació Joan Miró in Barcelona opened in 1975; he died in Palma in 1983.
