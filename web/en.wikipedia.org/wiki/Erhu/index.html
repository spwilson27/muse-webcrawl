Erhu

Erhu 二胡
ClassificationBowed string instrument
Related instrumentsGaohu, zhonghu, jinghu, huqin family
RangeAbout three octaves (D4–A7 in practice)

The erhu (Chinese: 二胡, pinyin: èrhú, "two-stringed [instrument]") is a Chinese two-stringed bowed musical instrument, more specifically a spike fiddle, which may also be called a "Southern Fiddle" and is sometimes known in the Western world as the "Chinese violin" or a "Chinese two-stringed fiddle". It is used as a solo instrument as well as in small ensembles and large orchestras, and is the most popular of the huqin family of traditional bowed string instruments.

Construction

The erhu consists of a long vertical neck with two large tuning pegs, two strings (usually tuned to D and A, a fifth apart), and a small sound box covered on the front with python skin. Unusually, the bow passes between the two strings and is never separated from the instrument; there is no fingerboard, the player stopping the strings by pressing them without touching a neck.

History

The instrument descends from Central Asian prototypes introduced to China, becoming established by the Song dynasty as the xiqin and later tiqin. For centuries it served mainly in folk and opera accompaniment; in the early 20th century the virtuoso and composer Liu Tianhua raised it to a concert solo instrument, expanding its technique and composing foundational études. The blind street musician Abing (Hua Yanjun) is famed for the piece "The Moon Reflected on the Second Spring" (Erquan Yingyue), now a staple of the repertoire.
