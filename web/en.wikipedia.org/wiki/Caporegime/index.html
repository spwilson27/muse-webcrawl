Caporegime

Also known asCapo, capodecina, captain
ContextSicilian Mafia, American Mafia
Reports toUnderboss / boss
CommandsA regime (crew) of soldati

A caporegime or capodecina, usually shortened to simply a capo, is a rank in the Mafia (both the Sicilian Mafia and Italian-American Mafia) for a "made member" of an Italian crime family who heads a "regime" or "crew" of soldiers and has major status and influence in the organization. Caporegime is an Italian word, which is used to signify the head of a family in Sicily, but has now come to mean the rank of a member.

Role

The capo supervises the criminal activities of his crew — typically around ten soldiers (soldati), though the number varies — and collects a share of their earnings, part of which he passes upward to the family's administration. Capos are appointed by the boss, usually from among trusted made members, and serve as the essential link between the leadership and the street-level operations of the family. A capo with a particularly large or lucrative crew may hold considerable independent power.

In the family structure

The classic structure of an American crime family descends from boss, to underboss and consigliere, then to caporegimes, then soldiers, and finally associates who are not made members. This pyramid mirrors the Sicilian model, in which each decina (group of ten) was led by a capodecina answering to the capofamiglia.

In popular culture

The rank was popularised by films and television series about the Mafia, including The Godfather and The Sopranos, whose fictional captains — such as Clemenza and Tessio in the Corleone family — illustrate the capo's mixture of delegated authority and personal following.
