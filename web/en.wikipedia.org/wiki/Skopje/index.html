Skopje

Skopje is the capital and largest city of North Macedonia, with about 530,000 residents — more than a quarter of the country's population. It lies in the north of the country on the Vardar River, near the border with Kosovo.

Skopje is over two thousand years old — Roman Scupi stood nearby — and was an Ottoman city for five centuries. A catastrophic earthquake in 1963 destroyed 80 percent of the city and killed over 1,000 people; it was rebuilt with international help to plans by Japanese architect Kenzō Tange. The controversial 'Skopje 2014' project covered the centre with neoclassical facades, triumphal arches and giant statues, most famously of Alexander the Great ('Warrior on a Horse'). The Old Bazaar, the Stone Bridge (15th century), Kale Fortress and Mother Teresa's birthplace (she was born here in 1910) are landmarks.
