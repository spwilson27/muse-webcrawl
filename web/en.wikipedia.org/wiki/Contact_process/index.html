Contact process

The contact process is the current dominant method of producing sulfuric acid in high concentration. It involves the catalytic oxidation of sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide, patented by Peregrine Phillips in 1831.

Steps

  1. Burning: sulfur (or pyrite/sulfide ores) is burned in dry air: S + O2 → SO2.
  2. Catalytic oxidation: 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3, over a vanadium(V) oxide (V2O5) catalyst at about 450 °C and 1–2 atm.
  3. Absorption: SO3 is absorbed into concentrated sulfuric acid (not water, to avoid mist) giving oleum, H2S2O7, which is diluted to 98% acid.

Catalysis and conditions

The oxidation is exothermic and reversible, so a moderate temperature balances rate against yield per Le Chatelier's principle; gas passes over multiple catalyst beds with cooling between them (the double-contact double-absorption variant exceeds 99.5% conversion and reduces SO2 emissions).
