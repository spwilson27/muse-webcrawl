Brig-sloop

A brig-sloop was a type of sloop-of-war used by the Royal Navy during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. The term combined the vessel's rig — a two-masted brig rig, with square sails on both masts — with its naval rating function as a sloop, a small warship below the rating system used for frigates and ships of the line.

Characteristics

Brig-sloops were typically armed with 10 to 18 guns, mounted on a single deck. They were commanded by an officer with the rank of commander, rather than a post-captain, which distinguished sloops from rated ships. Classes such as the Cruizer class (18 guns) and the Cherokee class (10 guns) were built in large numbers during the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars.

Role

Brig-sloops were used for convoy escort, dispatch carrying, reconnaissance, commerce raiding and anti-privateer patrols. They were cheaper to build and man than frigates, but their low freeboard made them wet in heavy weather and their brig rig required a relatively large crew for their size compared with fore-and-aft rigged vessels.

The Cherokee-class brig-sloop HMS Beagle, later famous for carrying Charles Darwin on his voyage of discovery, was originally built as a 10-gun brig-sloop of this type, though she was refitted with a third (mizzen) mast for her survey voyages, making her a bark.
