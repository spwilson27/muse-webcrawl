General Motors Building (Manhattan)

General Motors Building
Location: 767 Fifth Avenue, Manhattan, New York City
Completed: 1968
Height: 705 ft (215 m), 50 stories
Architect: Edward Durell Stone, Emery Roth & Sons
Style: International Style

The General Motors Building is a 50-story, 705-foot (215 m) office tower at 767 Fifth Avenue in Manhattan, New York City. The building, in the International Style, was designed by Edward Durell Stone, with the architectural firm of Emery Roth & Sons acting as associate architects. It occupies the full block between Fifth and Madison Avenues and 58th and 59th Streets, facing Grand Army Plaza and the southeast corner of Central Park.

History

The building was erected in 1964–1968 on the site of the former Savoy-Plaza Hotel. Developed by Cecilia Benattar for General Motors, which occupied much of the building in its early years, it was completed in 1968. Its white marble cladding and tall, narrow vertical bays attracted both praise and criticism; the architect Ada Louise Huxtable was among those who criticised the demolition of the Savoy-Plaza and the building's design.

The tower has passed through several ownerships and at times ranked among the most valuable office buildings in the United States. In 2013, a stake in the building was sold in a transaction valuing it at approximately $3.4 billion. Boston Properties has held a major interest in the property.

Tenants and plaza

The tower's sunken plaza on Fifth Avenue became famous as the site of the flagship FAO Schwarz toy store, which operated at the base of the building from 1986 to 2015, and of Apple's Fifth Avenue store, opened in 2006 and marked by its distinctive 32-foot glass cube entrance. The building's corporate tenants have included Estée Lauder and Weil, Gotshal & Manges.
