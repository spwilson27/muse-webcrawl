Tirich Mir

Tirich Mir is the highest mountain of the Hindu Kush range, and the highest mountain in the world outside of the Himalaya–Karakoram range, at 7,708 metres (25,289 ft) above sea level. It lies in the Chitral District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, near the border with Afghanistan, and is visible from the town of Chitral.

Climbing history

The mountain was first climbed on 21 July 1950 by a Norwegian expedition: Arne Næss, P. Kvernberg, H. Berg and Tony Streather reached the summit via the South Barum Glacier. Tirich Mir has several subsidiary peaks, including Tirich Mir East (7,692 m), first climbed in 1964.

Local tradition

In Chitrali folklore the mountain is the home of Kisu, a giant, and of the goddess of the mountain; its glaciers are associated with the spirits of traditional Kho religion and folklore. The name is sometimes explained as deriving from "Mir" (king) of Tirich village in the Mulkow valley.
