Trinidad and Tobago Carnival

The Trinidad and Tobago Carnival is an annual festival held on the Monday and Tuesday before Ash Wednesday in Trinidad and Tobago. It is one of the largest and best-known Caribbean carnivals, centred on the capital, Port of Spain, and celebrated with music, costume bands ("mas"), and street parades.

History

The festival grew from pre-Lenten masquerade balls introduced by French Catholic settlers in the late 18th century. After emancipation in 1834–1838, formerly enslaved Afro-Trinidadians transformed the celebration, merging it with the Canboulay (from French cannes brûlées, "burnt canes") processions commemorating emancipation, accompanied by drumming, stick-fighting (kalinda), and satirical song — the roots of calypso.

Events

The season opens weeks before Carnival Monday with calypso tents, soca parties ("fetes"), and competitions including the Panorama steelpan championship, Calypso Monarch, and the International Soca Monarch. J'ouvert, a pre-dawn street party with mud, paint, and oil, begins at 4 a.m. on Carnival Monday, followed by the Parade of the Bands in elaborate feathered and sequined costumes, judged at the Queen's Park Savannah.

Music and characters

Traditional characters include the Dame Lorraine, Midnight Robber, Moko Jumbie (stilt walker), and Jab Jab. The steelpan, invented in Trinidad in the 20th century, is the festival's signature instrument.
