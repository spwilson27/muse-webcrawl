The Saab 35 Draken (English: "The Dragon" or "The Kite") is a Swedish fighter aircraft designed and manufactured by Saab AB. It is distinguished by its distinctive double-delta wing — a sharply swept inner section and a less-swept outer section — developed to combine supersonic performance with the short take-off and landing runs needed for Sweden's dispersed wartime road-base system.

Development

In the early 1950s the Swedish Air Force required an interceptor able to reach Mach 2 and engage high-altitude bombers, replacing the Saab 29 Tunnan and Saab 32 Lansen. Saab's design team under Erik Bratt explored the double-delta configuration, validating it first with the small Saab 210 test aircraft, nicknamed Lilldraken ("Little Dragon"). The full-size prototype flew on 25 October 1955, powered by a licence-built Rolls-Royce Avon engine (Svenska Flygmotor RM6). The Draken entered service as the J 35A in 1960. Successive versions added improved radar and infrared search-and-track, air-to-air missiles (licence-built AIM-9 Sidewinders and later AIM-4 Falcons), a reconnaissance variant (S 35E), a trainer (Sk 35C) and the definitive Swedish interceptor, the J 35J.

Service

Drakens served with the air forces of Sweden, Denmark, Finland and Austria, and licence production took place in Finland. Danish RF-35 and TF-35 aircraft were heavily modified for strike and reconnaissance. Austria, the last operator, retired its Draken fleet in 2005, some having been bought second-hand from Sweden. The Draken was notable for being the first Western European aircraft in service to achieve Mach 2 in level flight, and its pilots could perform the dramatic high-angle-of-attack "Cobra" manoeuvre long before it was popularised by later fighters. Despite its age, the type never fired a shot in combat, Sweden and its other operators remaining at peace throughout its career.

Survivors

Numerous Drakens are preserved in museums in Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Austria and elsewhere, and a small number remain airworthy with civilian operators used for adversary support and research flying in the United States.
