Digit (anatomy)

A digit is one of several most distal parts of a limb, such as fingers or toes, present in many vertebrates.

Human digits

Humans normally have five digits on each hand and foot: the thumb, index finger, middle finger, ring finger and little finger on the hand, and the corresponding toes on the foot, the largest being the big toe (hallux). Each digit consists of several bones called phalanges, surrounded by soft tissue; the thumb and big toe have two phalanges, the others three.

Evolution and variation

The five-digit (pentadactyl) pattern is inherited from early tetrapods, though many lineages have reduced or fused digits — horses have a single functional toe, and birds fuse digits in the wing. Some animals have evolved extra digits or digit-like structures, such as the panda's "thumb", a modified wrist bone. In humans, congenital variations include polydactyly (extra digits) and syndactyly (fused digits).

Function

Digits provide fine manipulation in primates (especially with an opposable thumb) and tactile sensation through dense populations of mechanoreceptors in the fingertips; toes contribute to balance and propulsion in walking.
