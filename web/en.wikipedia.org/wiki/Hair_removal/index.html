Hair removal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shaving is the most common form of depilation.

Hair removal is the deliberate removal of body hair, practised for cultural, aesthetic, hygienic, medical, religious, and sporting reasons. Removal above the skin only is called depilation; removal of the whole hair, including the part below the skin, is epilation. Hair removal is ancient — flint and bronze razors survive from prehistory — and today supports a large global industry.

Methods

Depilation

  • Shaving with manual or electric razors cuts hair at skin level; regrowth appears within days. Includes wet shaving, dry shaving, and head shaving.
  • Depilatory creams use chemicals (typically thioglycolates) to dissolve the hair shaft.
  • Abrasion and threading — threading removes hair by rolling twisted thread over it, common for eyebrows and facial hair.

Epilation

  • Waxing and sugaring pull hair out by the root with a sticky substance; results last weeks.
  • Tweezing and rotary epilators pluck hairs individually or mechanically.
  • Electrolysis destroys each follicle with an electric probe — the only method the FDA allows to be marketed as permanent removal.
  • Laser and intense pulsed light (IPL) use light absorbed by melanin to damage follicles, giving long-term "permanent hair reduction", best on dark hair and light skin.

Reasons and norms

Motives vary widely by culture and era: ancient Egyptian priests removed all body hair for ritual purity; many Muslim and Sikh traditions regulate hair differently (fitra practices include underarm and pubic removal, while uncut hair is sacred in Sikhism); competitive swimmers and cyclists remove hair for performance or convenience; soldiers and hospital patients may be required to shave; and in much of the Western world, fashion since the early twentieth century has normalised women's leg and underarm shaving, while male grooming norms have shifted repeatedly. Removal is also medical: before surgery, for treating hirsutism and hypertrichosis, or as part of gender transition care.
