Laddu

Laddu or laddoo is a spherical sweet originating in the Indian subcontinent. Laddus are made from combinations of flour, sugar, and ghee or oil, and are commonly served at festivals, religious offerings, and celebrations such as weddings.

Varieties

Boondi laddu is made from tiny fried droplets of chickpea flour batter soaked in sugar syrup and pressed into balls. Motichoor laddu uses finer boondi; besan laddu is made from roasted chickpea flour, sugar, and ghee; coconut laddu and rava (semolina) laddu are also popular. The Tirupati laddu, distributed at the Venkateswara Temple in Tirumala, holds a Geographical Indication tag.

Cultural significance

Laddus are offered to deities — they are the favourite sweet of the god Ganesha — and distributed as prasad to devotees. The largest laddu ever made, prepared for festivals in Andhra Pradesh, weighed several tonnes.
