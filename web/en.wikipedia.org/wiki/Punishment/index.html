Punishment

Punishment, in the context of criminal law and social control, is the imposition of an undesirable or unpleasant outcome upon a person or group in response to an offence or rule violation. It is authorised by an authority such as a state, a court, a school, or a parent.

Justifications

Theories of punishment include retribution (deserved suffering proportionate to the offence), deterrence (discouraging future offences), incapacitation (removing offenders from society, e.g. imprisonment), rehabilitation (reforming the offender), and restoration (repairing harm to victims and communities). Philosophers from Kant and Bentham to modern writers debate which aims justify punishment.

Types

Forms include fines, imprisonment, community service, probation, corporal punishment, and capital punishment. Severity historically ranged from public execution and mutilation to modern emphasis on imprisonment and fines, a transformation described by Michel Foucault in Discipline and Punish.
