Martin Luther (10 November 1483 – 18 February 1546) was a German priest, theologian and Augustinian friar who was the central figure of the Protestant Reformation. His Ninety-five Theses (1517) challenged the sale of indulgences and, in the ensuing controversy, papal authority itself; excommunicated in 1521 and outlawed at the Diet of Worms ('Here I stand'), he was sheltered by Frederick the Wise in the Wartburg, where he translated the New Testament into German.

Luther's doctrines of justification by faith alone (sola fide) and scripture alone (sola scriptura), his German Bible, hymns, catechisms and vast polemical output — amplified by printing — reshaped European religion, education and language. Late in life he published violently anti-Jewish writings that remain a source of condemnation. Lutheran churches worldwide and the Evangelical tradition trace directly to him.
