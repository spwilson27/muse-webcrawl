War in the Vendée
Date: March 1793 – 1796 (main phase 1793)
Location: Western France
Result: French Republican victory

The War in the Vendée (1793–1796) was a counter-revolutionary insurrection in the Vendée region of western France during the French Revolution, in which royalist and Catholic rebels rose against the revolutionary Republic.

Causes and course

Resentment at conscription (the levée en masse of 1793), the Civil Constitution of the Clergy and the execution of Louis XVI ignited revolt in March 1793. The rebels formed the Catholic and Royal Army, led by men such as Jacques Cathelineau, Maurice d'Elbée, the young Henri de La Rochejaquelein and Jean-Nicolas Stofflet, winning early victories before defeat at Cholet and the disastrous Virée de Galerne. Republican reprisals, including the "infernal columns" of General Turreau and mass drownings at Nantes, caused enormous loss of life and remain historically controversial.

Legacy

Sporadic fighting continued until 1796 and flared again in later years. The war is commemorated in the Vendée and its death toll and character continue to be debated by historians. See also French Revolution.
