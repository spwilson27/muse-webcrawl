Abraham Darby II (12 May 1711 – 31 March 1763) was an English ironmaster, the son of Abraham Darby I, the pioneer of coke smelting. He developed the Coalbrookdale works in Shropshire and is credited with expanding the use of coke-smelted pig iron for the production of wrought iron, a key development of the early Industrial Revolution.

Life and career

Darby was born at Coalbrookdale into a Quaker family. He took over management of the family business while still young, following the death of his father in 1717 and periods of management by others, and became a partner in the Coalbrookdale Company. In 1735, while abroad in Holland with his brother-in-law, he experimented with methods of smelting and refining iron.

Darby II is associated with the development, around the 1750s, of converting coke pig iron into bar iron in finery forges using coal-fired reverberatory furnaces, freeing iron production from dependence on charcoal. He also expanded the company's works, built furnaces at Horsehay and Ketley, promoted the use of steam engines for pumping water back over waterwheels, and laid early iron railways (wagonways) to serve the works.

Legacy

His son, Abraham Darby III, continued the business and built the famous Iron Bridge over the River Severn, opened in 1781. The Darbys' Coalbrookdale enterprise became a centre of the Industrial Revolution and is now commemorated by the Ironbridge Gorge Museums.
