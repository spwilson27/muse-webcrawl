Restenosis

Restenosis is the recurrence of stenosis, a narrowing of a blood vessel, leading to restricted blood flow. Restenosis usually pertains to an artery or other large blood vessel that has previously been treated by angioplasty or stenting and has narrowed again, typically because of the proliferation of smooth muscle cells and neointimal hyperplasia in response to vessel injury.

Incidence

Restenosis was a major limitation of balloon angioplasty, occurring in a substantial fraction of patients within six months of the procedure. The introduction of bare-metal stents reduced rates, and drug-eluting stents, which release antiproliferative agents such as sirolimus or paclitaxel, reduced them further. In-stent restenosis remains a clinically important problem, particularly in diabetes, small vessels, and long lesions.

Treatment

Treatment options include repeat angioplasty, drug-coated balloon angioplasty, placement of another stent, atherectomy, and coronary artery bypass grafting in appropriate cases.
