Number theory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A spiral of numbers with the primes highlighted, illustrating the distribution of prime numbers]

Number theory is a branch of pure mathematics devoted primarily to the study of the integers and arithmetic functions. Once called 'arithmetic' or 'higher arithmetic', it was for centuries studied for its own sake — Gauss called mathematics the queen of the sciences and number theory the queen of mathematics.

Central topics include prime numbers, divisibility, congruences and Diophantine equations. Fermat's Last Theorem, proposed in 1637 and proved by Andrew Wiles in 1994-95, is its most famous problem.

Applications

Despite its 'pure' reputation, number theory underpins modern cryptography: the RSA public-key cryptosystem rests on the difficulty of factoring large integers, and elliptic curves are used in key exchange and signatures.

Subfields

Analytic number theory uses calculus and complex analysis (as in the Riemann hypothesis, an unsolved problem about the distribution of primes); algebraic number theory studies number fields and their rings of integers.
