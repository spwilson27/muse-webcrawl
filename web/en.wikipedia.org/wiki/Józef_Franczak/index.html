Józef Franczak (17 March 1918 – 21 October 1963), known by the nom de guerre "Lalek", was a Polish soldier and anti-communist resistance fighter. He is remembered as the last of the "cursed soldiers" — the Polish partisans who continued armed resistance against the communist government of Poland after the Second World War — dying in a gun battle with communist security forces in 1963, eighteen years after the war ended.

Early life and wartime service

Franczak was born in the village of Kozice Górne near Lublin, into a peasant family. Before the war he volunteered for the Polish Army and served as a military policeman (żandarm). During the September 1939 campaign he was captured by the Soviets but escaped. Under the German occupation he joined the underground Home Army (Armia Krajowa) in the Lublin region, taking part in sabotage and partisan operations.

Anti-communist resistance

After the Red Army entered Poland in 1944, Franczak refused to accept the communist order. Captured by the Soviet NKVD, he escaped from a transport and returned to the forests. From 1946 he served in the ranks of the Freedom and Independence Association (Wolność i Niezawisłość, WiN), one of the main organisations of the anti-communist underground. Following the amnesty of 1947 and the progressive destruction of the organised resistance, Franczak remained in hiding, supported by a network of trusted villagers in the Lublin countryside.

For years he was hunted by the Security Office (UB) and later the SB, which ran repeated operations and surveillance against his family and helpers in an effort to locate him. He survived in hiding longer than any other member of the armed underground.

Death

On 21 October 1963, acting on information from a secret collaborator, a joint force of the Citizens' Militia (MO) and Security Service operatives surrounded the farmstead in Majdan Kozic Górnych where Franczak was hiding. Franczak attempted to break out and was killed in the ensuing firefight. His body was taken by the security services; his remains were reportedly mistreated and buried without a marker, and his family was not informed of the burial place at the time.

Legacy

After 1989 Franczak became an emblem of uncompromising resistance to communism in Poland. Monuments and commemorative events in the Lublin region honour his memory, and his story is frequently cited in public discussion of the cursed soldiers. In 2008 he was posthumously awarded the Commander's Cross with Star of the Order of Polonia Restituta by President Lech Kaczyński.
