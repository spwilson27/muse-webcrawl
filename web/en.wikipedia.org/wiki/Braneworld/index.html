Braneworld

In string theory and cosmology, braneworld scenarios propose that the visible, three-dimensional universe is restricted to a brane — a higher-dimensional object — embedded within a larger higher-dimensional space, often called the "bulk". In such models, the fundamental forces of the Standard Model are confined to the brane, while gravity can propagate through the bulk, which may explain why gravity appears so much weaker than the other forces.

Background

The idea descends from the domain-wall solutions of supergravity and from the observation, in the 1990s, that open strings — whose excitations describe gauge theories — must end on D-branes, while closed strings describing gravity can move freely through the full spacetime. Physicists such as Joseph Polchinski helped establish D-branes as fundamental objects. In 1998, Nima Arkani-Hamed, Savas Dimopoulos, and Gia Dvali proposed the ADD model with large extra dimensions, and in 1999 Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum introduced warped-geometry scenarios known as the Randall–Sundrum models RS1 and RS2.

Randall–Sundrum models

In the RS1 model, there are two branes bounding a slice of five-dimensional anti-de Sitter space; the warping of the geometry exponentially suppresses scales on one brane relative to the other, providing an alternative solution to the hierarchy problem. In RS2, a single brane is embedded in an infinite extra dimension, yet four-dimensional gravity is recovered at large distances because gravity is localized near the brane. These models predict deviations from Newtonian gravity at short distances and possible signatures, such as Kaluza–Klein excitations of the graviton, in collider experiments.

Braneworld cosmology

Cosmological versions, including the ekpyrotic and cyclic models proposed by Paul Steinhardt and collaborators, describe the Big Bang as a collision between branes. Braneworld cosmology modifies the Friedmann equations on the brane and has been studied for its implications for inflation, dark energy, and the cosmic microwave background. Experimental searches at the Large Hadron Collider and in precision gravity experiments have placed strong constraints on the simplest scenarios but have not excluded the general framework.
