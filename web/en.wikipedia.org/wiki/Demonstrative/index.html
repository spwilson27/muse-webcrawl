Demonstrative

In linguistics, demonstratives (abbreviated DEM) are words, such as this and that, used to indicate which entities are being referred to and to distinguish them from others. They are typically deictic, varying with the speaker's spatial or discourse perspective. Demonstratives can be used as pronouns (this is good), as determiners (this book), or as adverbs (this tall).

Distal and proximal demonstratives

Most languages distinguish at least two degrees of distance: proximal demonstratives refer to things near the speaker (this, these in English) and distal demonstratives to things farther away (that, those). Many languages have a three-way system — Spanish este / ese / aquel, for example — and some distinguish still more degrees, or add distinctions of elevation, visibility, or position relative to the hearer. Latin had a three-term system: hic (near me), iste (near you), ille (over there), the last of which developed into the definite articles and third-person pronouns of the Romance languages.

English demonstratives

Modern English has four demonstratives: this and these (proximal, singular and plural) and that and those (distal). Older English also had yon and yonder for a third, remote degree, now dialectal or archaic.
