The Tiger II was a German heavy tank of the Second World War. Its official designation was Panzerkampfwagen Tiger Ausf. B, and it is often known by the informal name Königstiger ("Bengal tiger"), which Allied troops translated as "King Tiger" or "Royal Tiger". The design combined the thick, sloped armour of the Panther medium tank with the powerful 8.8 cm KwK 43 gun, the longest and most powerful German tank gun of the war.

Development and design

Development began in 1941, and production ran from January 1944 until the end of the war, with 492 built. Two turret designs were used: an early curved-front "Porsche" turret and the later, simpler "Henschel" production turret. At nearly 70 tonnes, the Tiger II was one of the heaviest tanks to enter service, and it was powered by the same Maybach V12 petrol engine as much lighter vehicles, contributing to poor fuel economy and mechanical unreliability.

Combat history

The Tiger II first saw combat in Normandy in July 1944 and later fought on both the Western and Eastern fronts, including the Ardennes offensive, where several were abandoned or captured. Its armour was virtually impervious frontally to most Allied guns at normal combat ranges, but fuel shortages, breakdowns, air attack and its sheer weight — too heavy for many bridges — limited its impact. A number survive in museums around the world.
