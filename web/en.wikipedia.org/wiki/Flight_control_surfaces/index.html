Flight control surfaces

Primary flight control surfaces of a fixed-wing aircraft.

Flight control surfaces are aerodynamic devices that allow a pilot to adjust and control an aircraft's flight attitude. They are the fundamental means by which the pilot controls the forces of flight and the aircraft's direction and attitude.

Primary control surfaces

  • Ailerons — mounted on the trailing edges of the wings, they move in opposite directions to control roll about the longitudinal axis.
  • Elevator — mounted on the horizontal stabilizer, it controls pitch about the lateral axis.
  • Rudder — mounted on the vertical stabilizer, it controls yaw about the vertical axis.

Some aircraft combine functions: elevons combine elevator and aileron functions on delta-wing aircraft, ruddervators combine rudder and elevator on V-tail designs, and flaperons combine flaps and ailerons.

Secondary control surfaces

  • Flaps — increase lift and drag for takeoff and landing.
  • Slats — leading-edge devices that delay stall at high angles of attack.
  • Spoilers — disrupt airflow over the wing to reduce lift and increase drag, used for roll control, speed braking and lift dumping on landing.
  • Air brakes — increase drag without affecting lift.
  • Trim tabs — small adjustable surfaces that relieve control forces.

Actuation

On light aircraft the surfaces are moved directly by cables, pushrods and pulleys connected to the stick and pedals. Large and high-performance aircraft use hydraulically powered actuators, and modern airliners and military jets employ fly-by-wire systems in which electronic signals command the actuators, often with envelope protection provided by flight-control computers.
