Westward Ho!

Westward Ho! is a seaside village near Bideford in Devon, England. The village, which takes its name from Charles Kingsley's 1855 novel Westward Ho!, is unusual in having an exclamation mark in its name. It is noted for its long sandy beach backed by a pebble ridge, its surf, and the adjacent Northam Burrows Country Park.

History

The settlement was developed as a Victorian resort in the 1860s after the popularity of Kingsley's Elizabethan adventure novel, whose hero Amyas Leigh came from nearby Northam. The Westward Ho! and Bideford Railway served the village from 1901 to 1917. Rudyard Kipling attended the United Services College here in the 1870s–80s, an experience reflected in Stalky & Co.

The village today

The beach is popular for surfing and kite-surfing, and the South West Coast Path passes through the village. Behind the pebble ridge lies the Royal North Devon Golf Club, founded in 1864 and the oldest golf course in England still on its original site.
