Kanun (Albania)

The Kanun (also Kanû, Gheg Albanian: Kanuni i Lekë Dukagjinit) is a set of traditional Albanian oral customary laws. Transmitted orally for centuries among the clans of northern Albania, it was codified in written form in the early 20th century by the Franciscan priest Shtjefën Gjeçovi, who collected and published the version attributed to the 15th-century prince Lekë Dukagjini.

Contents

The Kanun comprises twelve books governing nearly every aspect of social life: family, marriage, the house and property, work, the church, honour, hospitality, and the settlement of disputes. Central concepts include besa (the pledged word of honour), the sanctity of the guest, and gjakmarrja (blood feud), a regulated obligation of revenge for murder that historically confined families to fortified tower houses.

History and influence

The Kanun governed life in the northern Albanian highlands throughout the Ottoman period, when state authority was weak. It was suppressed under Enver Hoxha's communist regime but revived in parts of northern Albania and Kosovo after 1991 amid weak state institutions, contributing to renewed blood feuds that reconciliation movements have worked to end. The Kanun also influences Albanian literature, notably Ismail Kadare's novel Broken April.
