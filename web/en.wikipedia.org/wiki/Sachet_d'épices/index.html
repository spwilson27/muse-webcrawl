Sachet d'épices

A sachet d'épices (French for "spice sachet", plural sachets d'épices) is a small bag of spices used in French cuisine to flavour stocks, soups, stews, and sauces. The spices are enclosed in a piece of cheesecloth or muslin tied with kitchen string, or in a ready-made muslin pouch, so that they can perfume a dish during cooking and be easily removed before serving, leaving no fragments in the finished preparation.

Composition

Unlike the bouquet garni, which is principally a bundle of fresh or dried herbs, a sachet d'épices is typically made with whole dried spices. A classic combination includes:

  • Black peppercorns, often lightly cracked
  • Whole cloves
  • Bay leaves
  • Dried thyme
  • Parsley stems

Depending on the dish, cooks may add coriander seed, allspice berries, dried orange peel, a piece of cinnamon stick, or a blade of mace. Because the sachet restrains the spices, strongly flavoured ingredients can be used without overwhelming the dish or clouding a clear stock or consommé.

Use

The sachet is added to the cooking liquid near the beginning of cooking and removed at the end. It is a standard element of classical French technique, appearing in white stocks, pot-au-feu, blanquettes, court-bouillon, and poaching liquids. Modern cooks sometimes substitute a metal tea infuser or a disposable paper spice bag for the traditional cloth sachet, achieving the same effect.
