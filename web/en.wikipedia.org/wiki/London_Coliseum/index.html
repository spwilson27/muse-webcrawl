The London Coliseum is a theatre on St Martin's Lane in the City of Westminster, London. It is one of London's largest and best-equipped theatres and has been the home of English National Opera since 1968. The theatre is also used by English National Ballet for performances when not in use by the opera company.

History

The Coliseum was built by impresario Oswald Stoll to the designs of the prolific theatre architect Frank Matcham, and opened on 24 December 1904 as the London Coliseum Theatre of Varieties. It was one of the first theatres in England to have a revolving stage and was crowned by a distinctive revolving globe. After decades as a variety, music hall and cinema venue, it became the home of the Sadler's Wells Opera Company in 1968; the company was renamed English National Opera in 1974.

Architecture

The building is noted for its Baroque-style terracotta façade and lavish Edwardian interior, with a wide proscenium arch and three tiers of seating. A major restoration completed in 2004 returned much of the auditorium and public spaces to their original appearance.

Productions

English National Opera performs all its productions in English at the Coliseum, with seating for 2,359 making it the largest lyric theatre in London.
