Open skies

An open skies agreement is an international air transport treaty that minimises government interference in commercial airline decisions about routes, capacity and pricing, allowing airlines of the signatory countries to fly between and often beyond each other's territories. Such agreements are intended to promote competition, lower fares and expand service.

History

The United States pioneered the concept after its domestic airline deregulation in 1978, signing its first open skies deal with the Netherlands in 1992; it has since concluded more than 100 such agreements. The European Union negotiated collectively, culminating in the EU–US Open Skies Agreement of 2007 (in force 2008), which opened transatlantic routes including London Heathrow to all EU and US carriers.

Related usage

"Open Skies" also names the Treaty on Open Skies (1992, in force 2002), a separate arms-control agreement permitting unarmed reconnaissance flights over signatories' territory; the United States withdrew from it in 2020 and Russia in 2021. In aviation policy, open skies agreements relate to the "freedoms of the air" and stand in contrast to restrictive bilateral air service agreements.
