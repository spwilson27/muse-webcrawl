Spanish Italy refers to the Italian territories ruled by the Habsburg Spanish Monarchy from the mid-16th century until the early 18th century. Following the Italian Wars and the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis (1559), Spain held the kingdoms of Naples, Sicily, and Sardinia, the Duchy of Milan, and the coastal State of the Presidi in Tuscany, making Spain the dominant power in the peninsula.

Administration

The territories were governed from Madrid through the Council of Italy, with viceroys in Naples, Palermo, and Cagliari and a governor in Milan. Spanish rule blended with local institutions: taxation and defence demands grew over time, provoking resistance, most famously the revolt of Masaniello in Naples in 1647 and the Messina revolt in Sicily (1674–78).

Strategic role

Italy was a pillar of the Spanish system: Milan was the hinge of the "Spanish Road" carrying troops to the Netherlands, Naples provided soldiers, grain, and galleys for Mediterranean warfare against the Ottoman Empire and the Barbary states, and Italian bankers and commanders served throughout the monarchy.

Decline and end

During the War of the Spanish Succession, Austria conquered Milan and Naples; the treaties of Utrecht (1713) and Rastatt (1714) transferred Milan, Naples, and Sardinia to Austria and Sicily to Savoy, ending a century and a half of Spanish dominion — although a Spanish Bourbon cadet branch regained Naples and Sicily in 1734–35.
