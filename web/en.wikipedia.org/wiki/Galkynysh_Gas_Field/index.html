Galkynysh Gas Field

Galkynysh Gas Field
Country: Turkmenistan
Region: Mary Province (Iolotan area)
Discovery: 2006 (South Iolotan–Osman)
Estimated reserves: up to ~21 trillion cubic metres (audited estimates vary)

The Galkynysh Gas Field, formerly known as Iolotan or South Iolotan–Osman, is a natural gas field near Ýolöten in Mary Province, Turkmenistan. Independent audits by Gaffney, Cline & Associates assessed it as one of the largest natural gas fields in the world, with estimated reserves ranging up to about 21 trillion cubic metres — placing it second only to the South Pars/North Dome field shared by Iran and Qatar.

Geology and development

The field lies in the Amu Darya basin and comprises the South Iolotan, Osman, Minara and Yashlar structures, which the Turkmen government unified under the name Galkynysh ("revival") in 2011. Gas production began in September 2013 following a major development programme involving gas-processing trains built with Chinese, South Korean and UAE contractors, financed in part by loans from the China Development Bank.

Export routes

Galkynysh gas supplies the Central Asia–China gas pipeline, which began delivering Turkmen gas to China in 2009 and became the backbone of Turkmenistan's export strategy. The field is also intended to supply the proposed TAPI pipeline (Turkmenistan–Afghanistan–Pakistan–India) and to underpin Turkmenistan's claimed reserves, which rank among the largest national gas reserves in the world.

Darvaza crater

The region is also associated with the Darvaza gas crater, a burning natural-gas crater in the Karakum Desert that has burned for decades, though it lies in a different part of the gas-bearing basin.
