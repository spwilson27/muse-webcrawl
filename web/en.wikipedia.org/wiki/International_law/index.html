International law

International law is the set of rules, norms and standards generally recognised as binding between states and other international actors. Its sources, per the International Court of Justice statute, are treaties, customary international law, general principles of law, and (as subsidiary means) judicial decisions and scholarship.

Fields include the law of treaties, diplomatic law, the law of the sea (UNCLOS), trade law (WTO), human-rights law, international humanitarian law, international criminal law (ICC) and environmental law. Enforcement is decentralised — no world police — relying on reciprocity, reputation, sanctions, collective security through the UN Security Council, and courts whose jurisdiction states accept. The modern system traces to the Peace of Westphalia (1648) and Hugo Grotius's On the Law of War and Peace (1625).
