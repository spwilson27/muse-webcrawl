iNaturalist is an online social network of naturalists, citizen scientists, and biologists built on the concept of mapping and sharing observations of biodiversity across the globe. Users record observations — photographs or sound recordings of wild organisms, with time and location — and the community, assisted by computer-vision suggestions, identifies them. iNaturalist is one of the largest citizen science platforms in the world, with hundreds of millions of observations contributed by millions of users.

History

The platform began in 2008 as a master's project by Nate Agrin, Jessica Kline, and Ken-ichi Ueda at the University of California, Berkeley. It became an initiative of the California Academy of Sciences in 2014, and a joint initiative with the National Geographic Society in 2017. In 2023 it spun out as an independent non-profit organisation supported by grants and donations.

How it works

An observation becomes "research grade" when it has a date, location, and media evidence, and when the community's identification reaches a two-thirds consensus at species level or finer. Research-grade observations flow into scientific aggregators such as the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), where they are used in research on species distributions, phenology, invasive species, and conservation. The platform's computer vision model, trained on its own observation corpus, suggests identifications from photographs. Features such as the Seek app (aimed at families and using the same identification model without collecting location data) extend the concept to a wider audience.

Events and use

iNaturalist underpins the annual City Nature Challenge, a friendly competition among cities worldwide to record the most observations, and thousands of local "projects" and bioblitzes. Its data have contributed to the documentation of range shifts, rediscoveries of species thought lost, and the description of species new to science.
