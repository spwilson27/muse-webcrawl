Icelandic Coast Guard

The Icelandic Coast Guard (Icelandic: Landhelgisgæsla Íslands, literally "Iceland's Territorial Defence") is the service responsible for Iceland's maritime law enforcement, search and rescue, fisheries protection, and the defence of Iceland's territorial waters and exclusive economic zone. Founded in 1926, it also operates Iceland's air defence radar system and the NATO base infrastructure at Keflavík, since Iceland maintains no standing army.

History

The Coast Guard traces its origins to the 1920s, when Iceland began patrolling its fisheries limits with Danish assistance and then with its own vessels; its formal founding date is 1 July 1926. Its most famous chapter came during the "Cod Wars" (1958–1976), a series of confrontations with the United Kingdom over Iceland's unilateral extensions of its fisheries zone. Coast Guard patrol vessels used net cutters to sever the trawl wires of British fishing boats, while Royal Navy frigates escorted British trawlers; the disputes ended with British recognition of Iceland's 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone, a settlement that influenced the international Law of the Sea.

Duties

The Coast Guard enforces Icelandic law at sea, inspects fishing vessels, conducts search and rescue in Iceland's vast maritime response region of about 1.9 million square kilometres, monitors vessel traffic, performs hydrographic surveying and charting, and disposes of naval mines and unexploded ordnance. It operates the Icelandic Air Defense System, a network of radar sites funded by NATO, and oversees explosive ordnance and diving units.

Fleet and aircraft

The fleet has centred on offshore patrol vessels such as ICGV Þór, commissioned in 2011, and ICGV Týr and ICGV Ægir of the Ægir class, which served for decades from the 1960s. The Coast Guard also operates survey vessels, and its Aeronautical Division flies helicopters — notably the Airbus H225 Super Puma — and a Bombardier Dash 8 maritime patrol aircraft from Reykjavík Airport.
