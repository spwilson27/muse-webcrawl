World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts

The World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS) is a global association supporting the female-oriented and female-only Guiding and Scouting organisations in 153 countries. It was founded in 1928 and has its headquarters in London, United Kingdom. It is the counterpart of the World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM), and is the largest voluntary organisation for girls and young women in the world, with a membership of around 10 million.

History

Girl Guiding began in 1909 when girls appeared at the first Scout rally at Crystal Palace in London asking to join; Robert Baden-Powell asked his sister Agnes Baden-Powell to organize a movement for girls, and later his wife Olave Baden-Powell became World Chief Guide. An International Council was formed in 1919, and at the fifth international conference in 1928 the World Association was formally founded.

Activities

WAGGGS is organised into five regions (Africa, Arab, Asia Pacific, Europe, and Western Hemisphere) and runs five World Centres: Our Chalet in Switzerland (opened 1932), Our Cabaña in Mexico, Sangam in India, Pax Lodge in London, and Kusafiri in Africa. It coordinates global campaigns such as the Free Being Me body-confidence programme and promotes girls' education and leadership, and it coordinates World Thinking Day, celebrated each year on 22 February, the joint birthday of Robert and Olave Baden-Powell.
