Singin' in the Rain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Don Lockwood swinging from a lamppost, umbrella aloft, soaked and euphoric, in the title number's immortal image]

Singin' in the Rain is a 1952 American musical romantic comedy directed by Gene Kelly and Stanley Donen, starring Kelly, Donald O'Connor and Debbie Reynolds. Set in Hollywood during the transition from silent films to talkies, it was only a modest hit on release and is now regularly voted the greatest film musical ever made.

Kelly plays silent star Don Lockwood, whose studio pairs him with the screech-voiced Lina Lamont (Jean Hagen) just as sound arrives; with his partner Cosmo Brown (O'Connor) and the singer Kathy Selden (Reynolds), he converts their disaster picture into a musical — dubbing Lina's voice — while falling in love. The numbers, mostly pre-existing Arthur Freed/Nacio Herb Brown songs, include the title song (Kelly dancing in rain, shot while he was feverish), O'Connor's 'Make 'Em Laugh' acrobatics, and the 'Broadway Melody' ballet with Cyd Charisse. Song titles cited only.

Production

Screenwriters Betty Comden and Adolph Green built the story backwards from the Freed songbook; producer Freed himself had written the title song in the twenties. Kelly's perfectionism drove the shoot (Reynolds, 19, danced until her feet bled; her own singing was partly dubbed in the dubbing plot's richest irony — by Betty Noyes, and Hagen's own speaking voice was used where Reynolds 'dubs' her, a hall of mirrors the film never advertises).

Canonisation

Ignored by the Oscars beyond a supporting nomination for Hagen, the film grew by revival and television into the musical against which all others are measured — second in the AFI's musicals list to none, first in most hearts. Its portrait of the talkies transition is also, historians note, surprisingly accurate in spirit.
