Alpine skiing

Alpine skiing, or downhill skiing, is the pastime of sliding down snow-covered slopes on skis with fixed-heel bindings, unlike other types of skiing (cross-country, Telemark, or ski jumping), which use skis with free-heel bindings. Whether for recreation or for sport, it is typically practised at ski resorts, which provide services such as ski lifts, artificial snow making, snow grooming, restaurants, and ski patrol.

History

Alpine skiing evolved from cross-country skiing when ski lift infrastructure was developed at mountain resorts, chiefly in the Alps. Modern alpine skiing is often traced to the 1850s and 1860s, when Sondre Norheim of Telemark, Norway, developed techniques for turning on skis with bindings that held the boot firmly at the heel. In the early 20th century the Austrian Hannes Schneider developed the Arlberg technique, and Mathias Zdarsky pioneered slalom racing. Alpine events were first included in the Winter Olympic Games at Garmisch-Partenkirchen in 1936, initially as a combined event.

Technique

Unlike cross-country skiing, in which the skier provides propulsion, alpine skiing relies on gravity. Turns are made by edging the skis and shifting weight; the classic carved turn uses the ski's sidecut to arc through the snow with minimal skidding. Beginners typically start with the snowplough (wedge) turn before progressing to parallel turns.

Competitive disciplines

FIS Alpine Ski World Cup competition comprises five disciplines: downhill, super-G, giant slalom, slalom, and alpine combined. Speed events (downhill and super-G) feature longer courses and higher speeds; technical events (giant slalom and slalom) emphasise turning through gates.

Equipment

Modern alpine skis are shorter and shaped with a pronounced sidecut compared with historical long, straight skis. Boots are rigid plastic shells fixed to the ski by release bindings designed to let go in a fall to reduce injury. Helmets have become standard for most recreational skiers.

Safety

Common injuries include knee ligament damage, particularly to the anterior cruciate ligament. The International Ski Federation publishes rules of conduct for pistes, covering speed, overtaking, and stopping.
