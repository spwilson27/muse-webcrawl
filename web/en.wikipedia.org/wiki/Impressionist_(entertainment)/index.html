Impressionist (entertainment)

An impressionist or impersonator is an entertainer who imitates the voice, mannerisms and appearance of famous people, typically for comic effect. Impressionism has been a staple of variety theatre, radio, television sketch comedy and stand-up, and notable practitioners have included Rich Little, Frank Gorshin, Dana Carvey, Mike Yarwood, Rory Bremner and the casts of programmes such as Spitting Image and Saturday Night Live.

Technique

Successful impressions depend on capturing distinctive features of a subject's speech — pitch, rhythm, accent, characteristic phrases — together with body language and facial expressions. Impressionists often exaggerate a small set of recognisable traits rather than reproducing a voice exactly, and they build repertoires of political figures, actors and singers.

History

Celebrity mimicry flourished in music hall and vaudeville, moved to radio in the twentieth century, and reached mass audiences with television. Political satire has often relied on impressionists to lampoon presidents and prime ministers, and voice impressionists also provide sound-alike work in animation and advertising.
