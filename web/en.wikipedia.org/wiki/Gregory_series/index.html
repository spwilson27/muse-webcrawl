Gregory series

Partial sums of the Gregory series converging to arctan x

The Gregory series is an infinite series expansion for the inverse tangent function, discovered by the Scottish mathematician James Gregory in 1671 and independently known earlier in India to the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics. It states that for |x| ≤ 1, x ≠ ±i:

arctan x = x − x³/3 + x⁵/5 − x⁷/7 + ⋯

History

The series was derived by Gregory around 1670–1671 and communicated in letters to John Collins. A few years later Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz independently obtained the special case x = 1, giving the celebrated Leibniz formula for π:

π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + ⋯

Unbeknownst to the Europeans, the same series had been discovered in the fourteenth or fifteenth century by Madhava of Sangamagrama and his successors in Kerala, who used it to compute π to high accuracy.

Properties and use

The Gregory series is the Taylor (Maclaurin) series of arctan x. It converges only slowly at x = 1, requiring hundreds of thousands of terms for a handful of digits of π, but for small values of x it converges rapidly. By combining the series at cleverly chosen small arguments — using identities such as those of John Machin — mathematicians and later computers calculated π to millions of digits; such arctangent relations are known as Machin-like formulas. Gregory also discovered the corresponding series for arcsecant and was among the first to grasp the general idea of a Taylor series.
