Min'yō

Min'yō (民謡) is a genre of traditional Japanese folk music, originally the songs of ordinary working people — farmers, fishermen, labourers — sung while working or at festivals. The term, a calque of the German Volkslied, was coined in the Meiji period.

Characteristics

Min'yō melodies typically use pentatonic scales and are accompanied by shamisen, taiko drums, and the shinobue flute, with singers using strong vibrato and melismatic ornamentation (kobushi). Well-known examples include "Sōran Bushi" from Hokkaido's herring fishermen and "Tsugaru Jongara Bushi" from Aomori. Many songs accompany bon odori dances at summer festivals.
