Critical mineral

Critical minerals
ExamplesLithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earth elements, graphite, gallium, germanium
Key usesBatteries, renewable energy, semiconductors, defence

A critical mineral (or critical raw material) is a mineral resource considered essential to a country's economy, technology, or national security whose supply is at risk of disruption. There is no single universal definition: governments maintain their own lists, which change as technology and geopolitics evolve.

The United States Geological Survey publishes a list of critical minerals — 50 entries in its 2022 list — including lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earth elements, platinum group metals, graphite, manganese, gallium, and germanium. The European Union maintains a similar list of critical raw materials, and countries such as Canada, Australia, Japan, and India have adopted their own.

Supply concerns

Concern centres on the geographic concentration of production and, especially, processing: China dominates the refining of many critical minerals, including most rare earths, graphite, and much lithium and cobalt processing. The energy transition has sharply increased demand for battery and magnet minerals, prompting policies such as the US Inflation Reduction Act incentives, the EU Critical Raw Materials Act, strategic stockpiling, recycling initiatives, and efforts to develop alternative supply chains among allied countries.
