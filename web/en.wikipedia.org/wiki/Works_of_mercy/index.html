Works of mercy

The works of mercy are practices central to Christian ethics, drawn especially from the parable of the sheep and the goats in Matthew 25. Tradition distinguishes corporal works, concerning bodily needs, from spiritual works, concerning the soul.

Corporal works

The seven corporal works are: feed the hungry, give drink to the thirsty, clothe the naked, shelter the homeless, visit the sick, visit the imprisoned (ransom the captive), and bury the dead.

Spiritual works

The seven spiritual works are: instruct the ignorant, counsel the doubtful, admonish sinners, bear wrongs patiently, forgive offences, comfort the afflicted, and pray for the living and the dead.

In art

The theme inspired famous art, including Caravaggio's The Seven Works of Mercy (1607) in Naples, and the Master of Alkmaar's polyptych (1504).
