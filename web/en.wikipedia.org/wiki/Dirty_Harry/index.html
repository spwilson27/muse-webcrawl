Dirty Harry

Dirty Harry is a 1971 American neo-noir action thriller film directed by Don Siegel and starring Clint Eastwood as San Francisco police inspector Harry Callahan, nicknamed "Dirty Harry" for his willingness to take on the jobs others avoid and his unorthodox, violent methods. The film was a critical and commercial success and launched a franchise of four sequels.

Plot

Callahan pursues a psychopathic serial killer, "Scorpio" (modelled in part on the real-life Zodiac Killer), who murders a woman in a rooftop swimming pool, sends taunting ransom letters to the city, and kidnaps a teenage girl. Callahan's pursuit is frustrated by legal constraints — evidence obtained after he tortures Scorpio about the girl's location is ruled inadmissible — and Scorpio is released, hijacks a school bus, and is finally confronted by Callahan at a quarry, in the scene containing the famous "Do I feel lucky?" speech about his .44 Magnum revolver.

Reception and legacy

The film provoked debate over police brutality and criminal rights in the law-and-order politics of the 1970s, with critic Pauline Kael famously attacking it, while others praised Siegel's direction and Eastwood's performance. It was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry in 2012. Sequels followed: Magnum Force (1973), The Enforcer (1976), Sudden Impact (1983) — source of the line "Go ahead, make my day" — and The Dead Pool (1988).
