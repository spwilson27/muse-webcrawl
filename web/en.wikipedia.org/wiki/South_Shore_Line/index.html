South Shore Line

The South Shore Line is an electrically powered interurban commuter rail line between Chicago, Illinois, and South Bend, Indiana, operated by the Northern Indiana Commuter Transportation District (NICTD). It is the last surviving interurban railway in the United States still operating as such, famous for street running through Michigan City.

History

The line developed from the Chicago, Lake Shore and South Bend Railway, completed in 1908, and was reorganised under Samuel Insull in the 1920s as the Chicago South Shore and South Bend Railroad. Freight service continues under a separate company, while passenger service passed to NICTD in the late 1980s, saving the line from abandonment.

Route

Trains run about 90 miles from Millennium Station in Chicago, through Hammond, Gary and Michigan City to South Bend International Airport. Much of the route parallels the southern shore of Lake Michigan, serving the Indiana Dunes area. A major double-tracking project between Gary and Michigan City, completed in 2024, substantially improved capacity and journey times, and a new West Lake Corridor branch toward Dyer has been under construction.
