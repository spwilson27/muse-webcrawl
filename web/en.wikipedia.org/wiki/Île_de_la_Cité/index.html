Île de la Cité

The Île de la Cité is a natural island in the River Seine in the centre of Paris. It is the historic heart of the city, where the medieval settlement of Lutetia grew into the French capital, and it remains home to some of Paris's most famous landmarks.

Landmarks

At the island's eastern end stands Notre-Dame Cathedral, begun in 1163; nearby are the Sainte-Chapelle, Louis IX's jewel-box of stained glass, and the Conciergerie, part of the old royal palace where Marie Antoinette was imprisoned during the Revolution. The western tip holds the Square du Vert-Galant and the Pont Neuf, the oldest surviving bridge in Paris.

History

The Parisii tribe settled the island before Roman conquest, and it was fortified as the seat of kings, bishops, and law courts. Baron Haussmann's 19th-century renovations cleared most of its dense medieval housing for administrative buildings, but its silhouette of towers and spires remains the emblematic centre of Paris.
