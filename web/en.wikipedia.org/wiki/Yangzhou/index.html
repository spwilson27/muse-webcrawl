Yangzhou

Yangzhou (揚州, Yángzhōu) is a prefecture-level city in central Jiangsu Province, China, on the north bank of the Yangtze River near its junction with the Grand Canal. One of China's most historically celebrated cities, it was a great commercial and cultural metropolis under the Sui, Tang, Song and Qing dynasties, famed for its salt merchants, gardens, and cuisine.

History

A settlement on the site dates to the Spring and Autumn period (as Han and Guangling). The Sui emperor Yang, who completed the Grand Canal linking north and south China, made Jiangdu (Yangzhou) his southern capital and died there in 618. Under the Tang, Yangzhou was among the greatest trading ports of the empire — a hub for salt, silk, tea and foreign merchants from Persia and Arabia — and a byword for luxury celebrated by poets such as Li Bai and Du Mu.

Under the Song the city was a major garrison and commercial centre, surviving a Mongol siege in the 1270s. Its Qing-dynasty zenith came from the Lianghuai salt monopoly: immensely wealthy salt merchants built the celebrated gardens around Slender West Lake (Shouxi Hu) — the Ge Garden and He Garden among them — and patronised the Eight Eccentrics of Yangzhou painters. The city suffered terribly during the Taiping Rebellion and declined in the late 19th century as the Grand Canal silted and railways shifted trade elsewhere.

Culture and cuisine

Yangzhou is a cradle of Huaiyang cuisine, one of China's "four great traditions," known for delicate knife-work and dishes such as lion's head meatballs, Wensi tofu, and Yangzhou fried rice, which takes its name from the city. Other famed crafts include jade carving, lacquerware, paper-cutting, and the morning tea-house culture of teahouses such as Fuchun. Marco Polo claimed in his book to have served as an official in Yangzhou under Kublai Khan, a statement historians regard as uncertain.

Modern city

Today Yangzhou is a mid-sized industrial and tourist city; the Grand Canal at Yangzhou forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the canal (inscribed 2014), and the city has been recognised by UN-Habitat for its old-city regeneration.
