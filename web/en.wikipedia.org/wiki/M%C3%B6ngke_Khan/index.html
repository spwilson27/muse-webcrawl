Möngke Khan

Möngke Khan
Born: 1209
Died: 11 August 1259, Diaoyu Fortress
Reign: 1251–1259 (Great Khan)
Father: Tolui; Grandfather: Genghis Khan
Brothers: Kublai, Hulagu, Ariq Böke

Möngke Khan (1209–1259) was the fourth Great Khan of the Mongol Empire, reigning from 1251 until his death. The eldest son of Tolui and grandson of Genghis Khan, he came to power with the support of Batu Khan, shifting the khanate from the Ögedeid to the Toluid line.

Reforms

Möngke conducted a census across the empire, reformed taxation by fixing levies and limiting the demands of officials and merchants, and sought to stabilise the currency and restrain the abuses of princely appanages. He maintained religious tolerance, hosting debates among Christians, Muslims, Daoists, and Buddhists at his court in Karakorum, where the Flemish friar William of Rubruck visited in 1253–54.

Conquests

He directed two great campaigns: his brother Hulagu's conquest of Persia and the Abbasid Caliphate (culminating in the sack of Baghdad in 1258), and the conquest of the Song dynasty in China, where Möngke personally took the field. He died in 1259 during the siege of Diaoyu Fortress in Sichuan, reportedly of wounds or disease.

Succession

His death triggered a succession struggle between his brothers Kublai and Ariq Böke, which effectively ended the unified Mongol Empire.
