Operation Homecoming

Released POWs arriving at Clark Air Base, Philippines, in 1973.

Operation Homecoming was the return of 591 American prisoners of war held by North Vietnam and the Viet Cong following the Paris Peace Accords, carried out between February and April 1973.

Repatriation

Under the accords, releases were staged over 60 days. C-141 Starlifter aircraft flew the former POWs from Hanoi's Gia Lam Airport to Clark Air Base in the Philippines, where they received medical care before returning to the United States. Among them were future Senator John McCain and the longest-held POWs, some imprisoned for over eight years.

Legacy

The homecomings were widely televised and became an emblematic closing chapter of American involvement in the Vietnam War, while controversy continued over Americans believed still unaccounted for.
