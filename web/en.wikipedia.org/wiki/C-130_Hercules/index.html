The Lockheed C-130 Hercules is an American four-engine turboprop military transport aircraft designed and built by Lockheed (now Lockheed Martin). Capable of using unprepared runways for takeoffs and landings, it was originally designed as a troop, medevac, and cargo transport aircraft.

Design and development

The Hercules first flew in 1954 and entered service in 1956. Its high wing, rear loading ramp and four Rolls-Royce/Allison T56 turboprops made it exceptionally versatile. It has the longest continuous military aircraft production run in history, with the improved C-130J Super Hercules still in production.

Variants and roles

The airframe has been adapted to dozens of roles, including the AC-130 gunship, HC-130 search and rescue, KC-130 tanker, LC-130 ski-equipped polar transport, and weather reconnaissance "Hurricane Hunters". It has served with more than 60 nations.
