Fame

Fame is the state of being known or talked about by many people, especially on account of notable achievements or public prominence. A person who achieves fame is called a celebrity. The concept has been studied in sociology, psychology, and media studies, and its nature has changed substantially with the rise of mass media and social media.

History

In ancient societies, fame (Greek kleos, Latin fama) was bound to heroic deeds, poetry, and public monuments. Print culture and the theatre created new celebrity figures in the 18th and 19th centuries, while film, radio, and television in the 20th century produced fame on an industrial scale, analyzed by theorists such as Daniel Boorstin, who defined a celebrity as "a person who is known for his well-knownness".

Modern fame

Reality television and online platforms have lowered the barriers to visibility, producing short-lived "micro-celebrities" and the phenomenon of being, in Andy Warhol's phrase anticipating the shift, "famous for fifteen minutes". Research links the pursuit of fame to narcissism in some populations and documents psychological costs of intense public scrutiny.
