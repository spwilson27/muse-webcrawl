Turkish music refers to the music traditions of Turkey and of Turkic peoples more broadly, encompassing the refined classical music of the Ottoman court, the richly varied folk music of Anatolia, and a large modern popular music industry. Its theoretical foundation is the makam system of melodic modes, shared with Arabic and Persian traditions.

Classical music

The Ottoman court tradition — today called Turkish classical or art music — developed over centuries in Istanbul and other imperial centres, performed by ensembles of ud, tanbur, ney, kanun, and kemençe, and organised into the fasıl suite. See Turkish classical music.

Folk music

Anatolian folk music (Türk halk müziği) is inseparable from the bağlama, the long-necked lute family that accompanies regional dances and the sung poetry of travelling minstrels (âşık). Rhythms frequently use asymmetric aksak cycles such as 9/8. Folk songs (türkü) vary strongly by region, from the Black Sea coast to the southeast.

Popular music

From the 1960s, Anadolu pop and Anatolian rock fused folk melodies with Western instruments; arabesque, a heavily ornamented urban style sung by artists such as Orhan Gencebay and Müslüm Gürses, dominated the 1970s–80s. Turkish pop flourished in the 1990s with stars like Tarkan and Sezen Aksu, and Turkey gained international attention by winning the Eurovision Song Contest in 2003 with Sertab Erener's "Everyway That I Can". Hip hop, electronic music, and indie scenes thrive in Istanbul today.
