Kingdom of Cyprus

The Kingdom of Cyprus was a Crusader state established on the island of Cyprus by the House of Lusignan, which ruled from 1192 until 1489. It was one of the longest-lived of the crusader states in the eastern Mediterranean.

Foundation

Richard I of England conquered Cyprus from the Byzantine governor Isaac Komnenos in 1191 during the Third Crusade, and sold the island first to the Knights Templar and then to Guy of Lusignan, the dispossessed king of Jerusalem. Guy's brother Aimery secured recognition of the royal title, and the Lusignan dynasty ruled Cyprus for nearly three centuries, imposing a Latin feudal system and a Frankish aristocracy on the Greek population.

Height and decline

The kingdom prospered as a centre of trade between Europe and the Levant, and Famagusta grew rich after the fall of Acre in 1291. King Peter I launched the Alexandrian Crusade of 1365. Genoese and Venetian rivalry, the Genoese occupation of Famagusta (1373–1464), and Mamluk suzerainty after 1426 eroded the kingdom's independence. James II seized the throne in 1460 with Egyptian support.

End

The last monarch, Catherine Cornaro, widow of James II, was pressured by Venice to abdicate in 1489, and Cyprus passed to the Republic of Venice — ending the kingdom and Latin rule on the island until the Ottoman conquest of 1571.
