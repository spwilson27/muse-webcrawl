The International Olympic Committee (IOC) is a non-governmental sports organisation based in Lausanne, Switzerland. Founded in June 1894 by Pierre de Coubertin and Demetrios Vikelas, it is the supreme authority of the worldwide modern Olympic Movement, organising the Summer, Winter and Youth Olympic Games and owning the Olympic symbols — the five rings, flag, motto and anthem — as trademarks.

The IOC comprises around 100 individual members, who elect its president for an eight-year term (renewable once for four years). It recognises 206 National Olympic Committees and the international federations governing each Olympic sport. Thomas Bach of Germany served as president from 2013 to 2025; in March 2025 the IOC elected Kirsty Coventry of Zimbabwe — a former Olympic swimming champion — as its first female and first African president, taking office in June 2025. The IOC selects host cities roughly seven to eleven years ahead; upcoming Summer hosts are Los Angeles (2028) and Brisbane (2032).
