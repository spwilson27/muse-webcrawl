Kingdom of Munster

The Kingdom of Munster (Irish: Ríocht Mhumhan) was a kingdom of Gaelic Ireland that existed in the southwest of the island from at least the first century BC until 1118. Comprising roughly the territory of the modern province of Munster, it was one of the five traditional provinces (cúigí) of Ireland, ruled from Cashel by the King of Munster.

Early history

Munster emerges in early Irish tradition as the realm of peoples such as the Érainn; its mythic history is told in cycles of tales concerning kings like Conaire Mór and Cú Roí. From the fifth century the Eóganachta dynasty, claiming descent from Eógan Mór, dominated the kingdom and founded its royal and ecclesiastical seat at Cashel. Munster kings occasionally asserted claims to the high kingship of Ireland but were rarely able to enforce them against the Uí Néill.

Viking age and the Dál gCais

The Vikings founded ports at Limerick, Cork and Waterford on Munster's coasts. In the tenth century the Dál gCais dynasty rose from Thomond: Brian Boru became king of Munster in 978 and high king of Ireland in 1002, breaking the Uí Néill monopoly before his death at Clontarf in 1014.

Division

In 1118, at the Synod of Ráth Breasail's settlement and by the decision of Tairrdelbach Ua Conchobair, the kingdom was divided between the Uí Briain (Thomond, in the north) and the Mac Cárthaigh (Desmond, in the south), ending Munster as a single kingdom; the two successor kingdoms endured in various forms until the sixteenth century.
