Keck Telescopes

The Keck Telescopes are a pair of astronomical observatories located near the summit of Mauna Kea in Hawaii. Each telescope has a primary mirror 10 metres (33 ft) in diameter composed of 36 hexagonal segments, making them the second-largest optical and infrared reflecting telescopes in the world for much of their history since first light in the 1990s.

LocationMauna Kea, Hawaii, United States
Altitude4,145 m (13,599 ft)

Diameter10 m (33 ft) each

Design

The mirrors are made of 36 hexagonal segments that act together as a single piece of reflective glass. A sophisticated active optics system adjusts the position of each segment twice per second to maintain the mirror's figure. Both telescopes are equipped with adaptive optics systems that compensate for atmospheric turbulence.

Observations

Together the two telescopes can be used as the Keck Interferometer, with the light-collecting power of an 85-metre mirror across their baseline. Instruments on the Keck telescopes have contributed to exoplanet discoveries, studies of the supermassive black hole at the centre of the Milky Way, and observations of distant galaxies.
