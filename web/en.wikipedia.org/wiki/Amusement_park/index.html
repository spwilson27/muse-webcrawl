Amusement park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An amusement park is a park that features various attractions, such as rides and games, as well as other events for entertainment purposes. A theme park is a type of amusement park that bases its structures and attractions around a central theme, often featuring multiple areas with different themes.

History

Amusement parks evolved from European pleasure gardens and fairs. Bakken in Denmark, opened in 1583, and the Prater in Vienna are among the oldest still operating. In the United States, trolley companies built "trolley parks" at the ends of their lines to encourage weekend ridership; Coney Island in New York became the archetype of the American park with its roller coasters and midway. The opening of Disneyland in California in 1955 introduced the modern theme park, organised into immersive themed lands.

Attractions

Typical attractions include roller coasters, Ferris wheels, carousels, water rides, dark rides, drop towers, and flat rides, alongside arcades, shows, parades, and food concessions. Admission models range from pay-per-ride to a single gate price with unlimited rides.

Industry

The largest operators include Disney Experiences, Universal Destinations & Experiences, Merlin Entertainments, and Six Flags. Parks are seasonal in colder climates and compete through record-setting rides and licensed intellectual property.
