Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf

LocationWeddell Sea, Antarctica
AreaApprox. 430,000 km²
Named forWilhelm Filchner; Edith Ronne

The Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf is an Antarctic ice shelf bordering the southern Weddell Sea. Together, the Filchner and Ronne portions form the second largest ice shelf in the world by area after the Ross Ice Shelf, covering roughly 430,000 square kilometres.

Geography

The ice shelf occupies the southern end of the Weddell Sea embayment. It is conventionally divided by Berkner Island into the eastern Filchner Ice Shelf and the larger western Ronne Ice Shelf. The shelf is fed by several large ice streams and outlet glaciers — including the Foundation Ice Stream, Recovery Glacier, and Slessor Glacier — draining the East and West Antarctic ice sheets.

Exploration and research

The Filchner portion was discovered by Wilhelm Filchner's German Antarctic Expedition of 1911–12, while the Ronne shelf was mapped and named by the Ronne Antarctic Research Expedition of 1947–48, led by Finn Ronne, after his wife Edith. Several research stations have operated on or near the shelf, including the former Filchner Station and the Argentine Belgrano stations. Scientists monitor the shelf for large iceberg calving events and for warm-water intrusion that could affect its stability and, in turn, global sea level.
