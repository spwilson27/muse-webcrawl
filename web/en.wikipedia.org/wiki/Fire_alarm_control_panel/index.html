Fire alarm control panel

A fire alarm control panel (FACP), also called a fire alarm control unit (FACU), is the central hub of a fire alarm system. It receives information from initiating devices such as smoke detectors, heat detectors, and manual pull stations, and in response activates notification appliances such as horns, strobes, and speakers, and may notify a monitoring service or the fire department.

Functions

The panel monitors the state of its circuits, supplies power to connected devices, displays alarms, supervisory signals, and trouble conditions, and allows operators to acknowledge, silence, and reset the system. Panels may also control building systems in an emergency, such as recalling elevators, releasing magnetic door holders, shutting down HVAC equipment, and unlocking egress doors.

Types

Conventional panels divide detectors into zones wired in parallel; an alarm indicates only the zone, not the specific device. Addressable panels assign a unique address to each device on a signalling line circuit, allowing the panel to identify the exact device in alarm and to monitor each device's condition. Addressable systems dominate in larger buildings.

Codes and standards

In the United States, fire alarm control panels are installed and maintained under NFPA 72, the National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, and equipment is typically listed by UL. In the United Kingdom, systems follow BS 5839.
