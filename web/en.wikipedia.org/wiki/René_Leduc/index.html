René Leduc

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Leduc 0.22 interceptor

René Leduc (1898 – 1968) was a French engineer who is much acclaimed for his work on ramjets. In 1930 he applied for the first patent of the pulsating jet engine, in fact the basis of the German V-1 flying bomb's engine. His groundbreaking work on ramjet-powered aircraft led to a series of experimental aircraft built in France after the Second World War.

Ramjet aircraft

Leduc's design used the airframe itself as the ramjet: the aircraft's fuselage formed the duct, with the pilot seated inside the intake cone. The Leduc 0.10, first flown in 1949 (carried aloft on a mother aircraft), was the first aircraft to fly under ramjet power alone. The Leduc 0.16 and the larger Leduc 0.21 followed, and the Leduc 0.22 was designed as a supersonic interceptor, reaching speeds around Mach 0.9 in testing before the program was cancelled in 1958 as the government favored conventional jet fighters such as the Mirage III.

Legacy

Although none of Leduc's aircraft entered production — ramjets cannot produce thrust at rest and require a carrier aircraft or booster, and they consume fuel heavily — his work demonstrated the practicality of ramjet propulsion and influenced the later development of ramjet-powered missiles. The Leduc 0.10 is preserved at the Musée de l'Air et de l'Espace at Le Bourget.
