Shakespeare in the Park

Shakespeare in the Park is an annual, free theatrical program that stages productions of William Shakespeare's plays — and occasionally other works — at the Delacorte Theater, an open-air amphitheater in New York City's Central Park. It is produced by The Public Theater and is one of the best-known free theatre programs in the world. Since its founding, more than five million people have attended, and the summer queue for free tickets has become a New York ritual.

History

The program grew out of the work of producer Joseph Papp, who began staging Shakespeare in public spaces in 1954 through his New York Shakespeare Festival. When parks commissioner Robert Moses demanded rental fees and tried to block performances on the Central Park site, Papp fought him through the courts and the press, winning public sympathy and permanent free access; the controversy also helped raise money to build the Delacorte Theater, which opened in 1962 with The Merchant of Venice starring George C. Scott. The theater is named for publisher and philanthropist George T. Delacorte Jr. Papp's founding principle — that Shakespeare belongs to everyone — shaped the multicultural casting and popular accessibility that remain hallmarks of the productions.

The Delacorte and the season

The Delacorte seats about 1,800 and uses Belvedere Castle and Turtle Pond as a natural backdrop. Typically two productions run each summer, lasting from late May or June into August. In 2024–25 the theater underwent a major revitalization — the largest renovation in its history — with improved accessibility, backstage facilities, and a new facade. Tickets are distributed free on the day of performance at the theater, at The Public's downtown box office, through a digital lottery, and in borough distribution events; lines famously form before dawn.

Notable productions

Over six decades the Park has hosted major actors early and late in their careers, including Meryl Streep, Al Pacino, Denzel Washington, Philip Seymour Hoffman, Anne Hathaway, Lupita Nyong'o, and Oscar Isaac. Celebrated productions include the 2012 Into the Woods, the 2019 all-Black Much Ado About Nothing directed by Kenny Leon, and numerous starry Twelfth Nights. Many Public Theater productions that began at the Delacorte or The Public's Astor Place home, such as Hamilton and A Chorus Line, transferred to Broadway.

Worldwide counterparts

The "Shakespeare in the Park" idea has been widely imitated: free outdoor Shakespeare festivals operate in cities across the United States and internationally, including programs in Louisville (the oldest free Shakespeare festival in the US), San Francisco, and numerous "Shakespeare in the Parks" touring companies.
