The Montreal International Jazz Festival (French: Festival International de Jazz de Montréal) is an annual jazz festival held in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. The Guinness World Records listed it in 2004 as the world's largest jazz festival.

History

The festival was founded in 1980 by Alain Simard with André Ménard, initially as a modest event on Île Sainte-Hélène. It grew rapidly through the 1980s and settled downtown in the Quartier des Spectacles, where streets are closed to traffic and hundreds of free outdoor concerts are staged alongside ticketed indoor shows.

Programming

Each edition presents roughly 3,000 artists from dozens of countries, performing some 650 concerts, about two-thirds of them free. Although centred on jazz, the festival has embraced blues, world music, rock, and hip hop. Legends such as Miles Davis, Ella Fitzgerald, Oscar Peterson, and Ray Charles performed there; the festival's awards include the Miles Davis Award and the Oscar Peterson Award.
