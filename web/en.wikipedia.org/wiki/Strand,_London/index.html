The Strand (commonly known as just Strand) is a major thoroughfare in the City of Westminster, central London. It runs just over three-quarters of a mile from Trafalgar Square eastwards to Temple Bar, where the road becomes Fleet Street in the City of London. The name derives from the Old English strond, meaning the edge of a river — the street historically ran along the north bank of the River Thames.

History

In the Middle Ages the Strand linked the commercial City with the royal centre at Westminster and was lined with the mansions of bishops and nobles, including the Savoy Palace. From the 17th century it became a fashionable shopping and entertainment district, home to theatres, coffee houses, and publishers. Two island churches stand in the roadway: St Mary le Strand and St Clement Danes, the latter celebrated in the nursery rhyme "Oranges and Lemons."

Notable institutions

Somerset House, a grand neoclassical complex, houses the Courtauld Gallery and hosts open-air concerts and an ice rink. King's College London's Strand Campus, the Royal Courts of Justice, the Savoy Hotel, and the Aldwych theatres line the street. Australia House and India House mark its eastern end, and the street is served by Temple and Charing Cross stations.
