Dingane kaSenzangakhona Zulu (c. 1795 – 29 January 1840) was the king of the Zulu Kingdom from 1828 to 1840. He was the half-brother of Shaka, the founder of the kingdom, whom he had assassinated in 1828 together with another half-brother, Mhlangana, and Shaka's bodyguard Mbopha.

Reign

Dingane established his royal capital at uMgungundlovu, a great homestead whose name means "the secret place of the elephant". Early in his reign he attempted to consolidate the kingdom Shaka had built, but his rule was marked by growing conflict with the Voortrekkers — Dutch-speaking settlers moving into Natal during the Great Trek.

Conflict with the Voortrekkers

In February 1838, after negotiations over land, Dingane's warriors killed the Voortrekker leader Piet Retief and his party at uMgungundlovu, and attacked Voortrekker encampments in the Weenen massacre. The Voortrekkers under Andries Pretorius retaliated, defeating Dingane's army decisively at the Battle of Blood River on 16 December 1838.

Downfall and death

Weakened by defeat, Dingane faced a rebellion by his half-brother Mpande, who allied with the Voortrekkers. Dingane's forces were defeated at the Battle of Maqongqo in January 1840; he fled north and was killed shortly afterwards, traditionally by Swazi or Nyawo forces near the Lebombo mountains. Mpande succeeded him as king.
