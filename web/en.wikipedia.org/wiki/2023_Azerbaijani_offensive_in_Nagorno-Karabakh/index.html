2023 Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh

On 19–20 September 2023, Azerbaijan launched a large-scale military offensive against the self-declared Republic of Artsakh in Nagorno-Karabakh, an ethnic Armenian enclave internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan. After roughly 24 hours of fighting, the Artsakh authorities agreed to a Russian-brokered ceasefire, disbanded their armed forces, and days later announced the republic would dissolve. Nearly the entire ethnic Armenian population of the region — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia within a week.

2023 offensive

Date: 19–20 September 2023
Location: Nagorno-Karabakh
Result: Azerbaijani victory; dissolution of the Republic of Artsakh
Consequence: Flight of over 100,000 ethnic Armenians

Background

Since the 2020 war, the reduced Artsakh entity had depended on the Lachin corridor, guarded by Russian peacekeepers, for its link to Armenia. From December 2022 Azerbaijanis blockaded the corridor, causing severe shortages of food, medicine and fuel in the enclave, a situation international observers warned amounted to a siege.

Offensive and capitulation

Azerbaijan described the operation as "local anti-terrorist measures", demanding the withdrawal of Armenian forces. Air and artillery strikes hit Stepanakert and other settlements. On 20 September, Artsakh's leadership accepted a ceasefire under which its forces would disarm and disband. On 28 September, Artsakh president Samvel Shahramanyan issued a decree dissolving all state institutions by 1 January 2024.

Exodus

Fearing persecution, almost the whole Armenian population left for Armenia via the Lachin corridor; by early October, Azerbaijani officials reported that only a handful of Armenians remained. Armenia and many international commentators described the exodus as ethnic cleansing, a characterisation Azerbaijan rejected.
