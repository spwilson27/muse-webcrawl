Dolmabahçe Palace

Dolmabahçe Palace
Location: Beşiktaş, Istanbul, Turkey
Built: 1843–1856
Commissioned by: Sultan Abdülmecid I
Architects: Garabet and Nigoğayos Balyan

Dolmabahçe Palace is a palace in Istanbul, Turkey, on the European coast of the Bosphorus, which served as the main administrative centre of the Ottoman Empire from 1856 to 1922, replacing Topkapı Palace.

Construction

Ordered by Sultan Abdülmecid I and built by the Armenian Balyan court architects, its name means "filled garden", from land reclaimed from the Bosphorus. In Baroque, Rococo and Neoclassical styles, it has 285 rooms, 46 halls and some 600 metres of façade, decorated with 14 tons of gold leaf, Baccarat crystal — including a 4.5-ton crystal chandelier gifted by Queen Victoria — and Hereke carpets.

History

Six sultans resided here; the empire's decline and 1924 abolition of the caliphate followed. Mustafa Kemal Atatürk used it as his Istanbul residence and died there on 10 November 1938; all palace clocks are traditionally said to be stopped at 9:05, the time of his death. It is now a museum.
