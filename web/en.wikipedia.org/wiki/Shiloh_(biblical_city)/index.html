Shiloh (biblical city)

Shiloh (Hebrew: שִׁלֹה) was an ancient city and sanctuary in Samaria, in the hill country allotted to the tribe of Ephraim. In the Hebrew Bible it was the first major religious centre of ancient Israel after the conquest of Canaan: the Tabernacle and the Ark of the Covenant were kept there during the period of the Judges, before the Temple in Jerusalem was built. The site is identified with modern Khirbet Seilun, near the modern Israeli settlement of Shilo in the West Bank.

Biblical account

According to the Book of Joshua, the Israelites assembled at Shiloh to cast lots for the division of the land among the tribes, and the Tabernacle was set up there. The Book of Judges describes an annual festival at Shiloh, during which the men of Benjamin seized wives from the dancing daughters of Shiloh. In the Book of Samuel, the priest Eli served at the shrine, the prophet Samuel was raised there, and the Ark was kept at Shiloh until it was captured by the Philistines at the battle of Aphek. The prophet Jeremiah later invoked the destruction of Shiloh as a warning to Jerusalem.

Archaeology

Danish excavations in the 1920s and 1930s and later Israeli excavations revealed settlement from the Middle Bronze Age, an Iron Age town corresponding to the period of the shrine, and evidence of destruction in the 11th century BCE, roughly matching the era after the Philistine victory. The site continued to be occupied, with Byzantine churches, in later periods.
