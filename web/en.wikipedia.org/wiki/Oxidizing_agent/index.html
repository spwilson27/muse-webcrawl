Oxidizing agent

An oxidizing agent (also called an oxidant, oxidizer, or electron acceptor) is a substance in a redox chemical reaction that gains or receives electrons from a reducing agent. In doing so, the oxidizing agent is reduced while the other reactant is oxidized.

Mechanism

Common oxidizing agents include oxygen, hydrogen peroxide, potassium permanganate, nitric acid, halogens such as chlorine and fluorine, ozone, and potassium dichromate. Fluorine is often considered the strongest elemental oxidizing agent.

Applications and hazards

Oxidizers are used in combustion, bleaching, disinfection, rocket propellants, and many industrial syntheses. Many are hazardous because they can intensify fires and react violently with fuels and reducing agents; transport regulations classify them separately from flammable materials.

Electron acceptors

In biochemistry, electron acceptors such as NAD+ and FAD play central roles in cellular respiration and metabolism.
