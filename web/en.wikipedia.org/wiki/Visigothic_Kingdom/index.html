The Visigothic Kingdom (Latin: Regnum Visigothorum) was a kingdom that occupied what is now southwestern France and the Iberian Peninsula from the 5th century until the early 8th century. Founded by the Visigoths — a Germanic people who had entered the Roman Empire fleeing the Huns and had sacked Rome under Alaric I in 410 — it was one of the successor states of the Western Roman Empire.

Kingdom of Toulouse

In 418 the Visigoths were settled as foederati in Aquitaine by the Romans, with their capital at Toulouse. Under kings such as Theodoric I — who died fighting Attila at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains in 451 — and Euric (466–484), who broke the treaty with Rome and codified Visigothic law in the Code of Euric, the kingdom expanded across Gaul and into Hispania. In 507 the Franks under Clovis I defeated the Visigoths at the Battle of Vouillé, killing King Alaric II and driving the kingdom out of most of Gaul.

Kingdom of Toledo

Thereafter the kingdom centred on Hispania, with its capital eventually at Toledo. Leovigild (568–586) subdued much of the peninsula, conquering the Suevic Kingdom in the northwest, and his son Reccared I renounced Arianism for Nicene Christianity at the Third Council of Toledo in 589, uniting Goths and Hispano-Romans in one faith. Later kings such as Recceswinth promulgated the comprehensive Liber Iudiciorum (654), a territorial law code influential for centuries in Iberia. Isidore of Seville, whose Etymologiae became a standard medieval reference work, flourished in this period.

Fall

The kingdom was weakened by succession disputes inherent in its elective monarchy and by a contested succession after 710. In 711 a Muslim army under Tariq ibn Ziyad crossed from North Africa and destroyed the Visigothic army at the Battle of Guadalete, where King Roderic was killed. Within a few years almost the entire peninsula was under Muslim rule; only in the far north did resistance coalesce, traditionally dated from the Battle of Covadonga (c. 718/722) under Pelagius, seeding the Christian kingdoms of the Reconquista.
