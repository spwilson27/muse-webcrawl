CAD/CAM

CAD/CAM refers to the integration of computer-aided design (CAD) and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) technologies, in which a digital design model created in CAD software is used directly to drive manufacturing processes such as CNC machining, turning, milling, and 3D printing.

Overview

In a traditional design-to-manufacturing workflow, engineers produced drawings that machinists interpreted to program machine tools manually. CAD/CAM closes this gap: the geometry defined in the CAD model is used by CAM software to generate toolpaths, which are post-processed into numerical control (NC) code (G-code) for machine tools. This reduces transcription errors, shortens lead times, and makes it practical to manufacture complex free-form surfaces that would be extremely difficult to program by hand.

CAD/CAM is used across the automotive, aerospace, medical, and consumer product industries. It is particularly prominent in dentistry and prosthetics, where CAD/CAM systems scan a patient's teeth or limbs, design restorations or devices digitally, and mill them from ceramic or other materials, sometimes in a single appointment. Major commercial CAD/CAM platforms include integrated suites such as CATIA, NX, and Fusion 360.
