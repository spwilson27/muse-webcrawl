CAD/CAM dentistry
Field: Prosthodontics / restorative dentistry
Components: Intraoral scanner, design software, milling machine / 3D printer
Pioneering system: CEREC (1980s)

CAD/CAM dentistry is a field of dentistry using computer-aided design (CAD) and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) to improve the design and creation of dental restorations, including crowns, veneers, inlays, onlays, bridges and implant-supported prostheses.

Workflow

The tooth preparation is captured with an intraoral scanner, replacing conventional impressions. Software designs the restoration, and a milling machine carves it from a block of ceramic, zirconia or composite — or it is produced by 3D printing. Chairside systems such as CEREC allow a permanent crown to be completed in a single appointment.

Advantages

Benefits include digital accuracy, elimination of impression materials and temporary restorations, and fast turnaround. Drawbacks include equipment cost and a learning curve for practitioners.
