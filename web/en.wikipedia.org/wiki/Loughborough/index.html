Loughborough (pronounced /ˈlʌfbərə/) is a market town in the Charnwood borough of Leicestershire, England, situated between Leicester and Nottingham on the River Soar. It is the second largest settlement in Leicestershire after Leicester and the seat of Charnwood Borough Council. The town is best known internationally for Loughborough University, a leading centre for sport and sports science, and historically for bell founding, hosiery and the Ladybird Books publishing house.

History

Loughborough is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Lucteburne. It received a market charter in 1221 and grew as a centre for wool and, later, hosiery and lace. The town was connected to the national waterways by the Loughborough Navigation in 1778 and by railway in 1840. In 1841 Thomas Cook organised his first excursion, a temperance trip from Leicester to a rally in Loughborough, an event often taken as the beginning of modern organised tourism. In 1889 the town became home to John Taylor & Co, whose Bellfoundry — the largest in the world — has cast famous bells including Great Paul for St Paul's Cathedral and, in 2012, the Olympic Bell for the London Games. The Brush Electrical Engineering Company (Brush Traction) made Loughborough an engineering centre from the late nineteenth century.

Education and sport

Loughborough University, founded in 1909 as a technical institute and granted university status in 1966, dominates the town's west side. It has topped national rankings for sports-related subjects and was the preparation base for the British Olympic team; its alumni and resident athletes include Sebastian Coe, Paula Radcliffe and Adam Peaty. Sporting facilities on campus include the National Cricket Performance Centre and the Paula Radcliffe Stadium. Loughborough also hosts Loughborough Grammar School, founded in 1495, and the endowed Loughborough Schools Foundation.

Landmarks and culture

Landmarks include the parish church of All Saints with St Michael, the Old Rectory Museum, Queen's Park with its Carillon Tower war memorial — whose bells were cast locally — and the Great Central Railway, a preserved heritage line running from Loughborough Central station to Leicester North. The town's market, held since the thirteenth century, remains one of the largest in the East Midlands, and the annual Loughborough Fair each November is among England's oldest street fairs. Ladybird Books, founded in the town in 1915, published its famous children's series from its Loughborough print works for decades.
