Tsinghua University (THU; Chinese: 清华大学) is a public research university in Beijing, China. It is widely regarded, together with Peking University, as one of the two most prestigious universities in China, and is consistently ranked among the leading universities in Asia and the world, particularly in engineering and computer science.

History

Tsinghua was founded in 1911 as Tsinghua Xuetang, a preparatory school for students sent to study in the United States under the Boxer Indemnity Scholarship Program, funded by the remission of part of China's Boxer Indemnity payments. It became a full university in 1925 and adopted the name National Tsing Hua University in 1928. During the Second Sino-Japanese War it moved to Changsha and then Kunming as part of Changsha Temporary University and the National Southwestern Associated University (Lianda). After 1949 the university was remodelled on the Soviet pattern as a polytechnic institution; from the 1980s it rebuilt its humanities, law, medicine and other faculties.

Academics and campus

The university has over twenty schools and dozens of departments, with strengths in engineering, computer science, architecture, economics and management. Its campus, in the Haidian District of northwest Beijing, occupies the site of a former Qing dynasty imperial garden and is noted for its mixture of Chinese-style landscaping and early American-influenced architecture, including the Grand Auditorium and the Old Gate. Alumni include many senior Chinese political leaders, scientists and business figures, among them former president Hu Jintao and current leader Xi Jinping, both of whom studied engineering at Tsinghua.
