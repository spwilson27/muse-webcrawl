Calluna

Calluna vulgaris, known as common heather, ling, or simply heather, is the sole species in the genus Calluna in the flowering plant family Ericaceae. It is a low-growing evergreen shrub growing to 20 to 50 centimetres tall, or rarely to 1 metre and taller, and is found widely in Europe and Asia Minor on acidic soils in open sunny situations and in moderate shade.

Description

Heather has small scale-like leaves arranged in opposite pairs and bears masses of small urn-shaped flowers, usually mauve or purple, in late summer. It differs from the closely related genus Erica in having smaller leaves less than 2–3 mm long and a corolla divided into separate petals.

Distribution and habitat

It is the dominant plant in most heathland and moorland in Europe, and in some bog and acidic pine and oak woodland. It is tolerant of grazing and regenerates following occasional burning, and is often managed in nature reserves and grouse moors by regular burning. Heather is an important food source for sheep and deer, and for the larvae of some Lepidoptera. Numerous cultivars have been selected for garden use with flowers ranging from white to deep purple and foliage in gold and silver tones.

Cultural references

White heather is regarded as lucky in Scotland, and heather honey and heather ale are traditional products. The plant is the clan badge of several Scottish clans.
