Powerball is an American lottery game offered by 45 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the US Virgin Islands, and coordinated by the Multi-State Lottery Association (MUSL). Players choose five numbers from 1 to 69 (white balls) plus one number from 1 to 26 (the red Powerball). Drawings are held three times a week — Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday nights — at the Florida Lottery studio in Tallahassee. A ticket costs $2, with an optional $1 "Power Play" feature that multiplies non-jackpot prizes.

History and record jackpots

Powerball began in 1992 as the successor to MUSL's Lotto America. Rule changes over the years have deliberately lengthened the odds to build larger jackpots, fuelling ticket sales. The game produced the largest lottery prize in history on 7 November 2022, when a single ticket sold in Altadena, California, won a jackpot advertised at $2.04 billion. Other landmark prizes include the $1.586 billion jackpot of January 2016, split three ways, at the time a world record. Winners may choose between an annuity paid over 30 years and a reduced lump-sum cash option, before taxes.

Prizes

The jackpot requires matching all five white balls and the Powerball; eight lower tiers pay fixed amounts for partial matches, with a $1 million prize for matching the five white balls alone (doubled when Power Play is active in most circumstances). The overall odds of winning any prize are about 1 in 25. Powerball's main rival, Mega Millions, cross-sells tickets in nearly all the same jurisdictions following a 2010 agreement between the two games.
