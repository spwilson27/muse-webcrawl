Duke of Buccleuch

Duke of Buccleuch is a title in the Peerage of Scotland created in 1663 for Anne Scott, 4th Countess of Buccleuch. The dukedom is currently held jointly with the Dukedom of Queensberry. The holder is traditionally one of the largest private landowners in the United Kingdom, and the family seats include Bowhill House, Drumlanrig Castle, Dalkeith Palace and Boughton House.

History

The title was created for Anne Scott on her marriage to James Scott, Duke of Monmouth, the illegitimate son of Charles II; the couple took the surname Scott. When Monmouth was executed in 1685 after his rebellion, Anne retained the dukedom. In 1810 the 3rd Duke inherited the Dukedom of Queensberry, and the two titles have descended together since. Walter Montagu Douglas Scott, 5th Duke, was a major Tory politician and Privy Councillor.

Estates and art collection

The Buccleuch Estates encompass roughly 200,000 acres across Scotland and England. The family's art collections at Bowhill, Drumlanrig and Boughton include works by Rembrandt, Leonardo da Vinci and Gainsborough; the Drumlanrig collection's Madonna of the Yarnwinder, attributed to Leonardo, was stolen in 2003 and recovered in 2007.
