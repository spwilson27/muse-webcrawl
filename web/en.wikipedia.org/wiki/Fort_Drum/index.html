Fort Drum

Fort Drum is a U.S. Army military reservation and census-designated place (CDP) located in Jefferson County, near the western edge of northern New York, United States. The population of the CDP portion of the post was 12,955 at the 2010 census. This 107,265-acre (434 km²) reservation is used for military training of regional Army National Guard and Reserve units.

History

The site was first used as a military training area in 1908 under the name Pine Camp. It was renamed Camp Drum in 1951, in honor of Lieutenant General Hugh A. Drum, and redesignated Fort Drum in 1974. In 1984 the post was selected as the home of the reactivated 10th Mountain Division (Light Infantry), which has since become one of the most frequently deployed divisions in the U.S. Army.

Geography and climate

Fort Drum lies in the North Country region of New York, east of Watertown and south of the Canadian border. Its winters are among the coldest and snowiest in the eastern United States, making it a valuable cold-weather training environment.
