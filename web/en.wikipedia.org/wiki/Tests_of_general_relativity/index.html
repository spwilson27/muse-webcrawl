Tests of general relativity
Theory: Einstein, 1915
Classic tests: Mercury perihelion, light deflection, gravitational redshift
Modern: Binary pulsars, gravitational waves, black hole imaging

Tests of general relativity verify Albert Einstein's 1915 theory of gravitation. The three classic tests are the anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury (43 arcseconds per century), the deflection of starlight by the Sun (confirmed by Eddington in the 1919 eclipse), and gravitational redshift.

Modern tests

Later confirmations include the Shapiro time delay, frame-dragging (Gravity Probe B), orbital decay of the Hulse–Taylor binary pulsar, GPS clock corrections, LIGO's direct detection of gravitational waves (2015), and the Event Horizon Telescope image of a black hole shadow.
