William Rowley (c. 1585 – February 1626) was an English Jacobean dramatist and actor, known for his comic subplots and for collaborations with Thomas Middleton, Thomas Dekker and John Ford. He seems to have specialised in playing fat clown roles, writing parts for himself to perform.

Career

Rowley performed with several companies, including Prince Charles's Men and the King's Men, and helped manage the Red Bull and Curtain theatres. Some seventeen plays wholly or partly attributed to him survive, many of them collaborations typical of the commercial theatre of his time.

Works

His most famous work is The Changeling (1622), written with Thomas Middleton and generally considered one of the greatest tragedies of the English Renaissance; Rowley is usually credited with the comic subplot and the opening and closing scenes. With Dekker and Ford he wrote The Witch of Edmonton (1621), a domestic tragedy based on the real-life witchcraft case of Elizabeth Sawyer. Solo plays attributed to him include the city comedy A New Wonder, a Woman Never Vexed and A Shoemaker a Gentleman; he also collaborated on The Birth of Merlin and, reputedly, contributed to the lost Cardenio.

Rowley died in London in February 1626, leaving a widow and son.
