Worcester College, Oxford

Worcester College is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in England. It was founded in 1714 by the benefaction of Sir Thomas Cookes, a Worcestershire baronet, on the site of Gloucester College, a medieval home for Benedictine monks which had been dissolved in 1539. The college occupies extensive grounds with gardens and a lake, unusual among central Oxford colleges.

History

Gloucester College was founded in 1283 as a place of study for Benedictine monks. After the Dissolution of the Monasteries the site passed through several hands before Cookes's endowment created Worcester College in 1714. Its 18th-century buildings, including the chapel and hall, reflect designs associated with Nicholas Hawksmoor and were decorated in a striking style by William Burges in the 19th century.

Grounds and buildings

The college's 26 acres include gardens designed in the early 19th century, a lake, and a playing field within the site. Surviving medieval cottages from Gloucester College form one side of the main quadrangle, contrasting with the classical north side.

People

Notable alumni include the writer and director Richard Curtis, actress Emma Watson, media proprietor Rupert Murdoch, and novelist Elena Ferrante (honorary fellow associations aside), along with many academics and public figures.
