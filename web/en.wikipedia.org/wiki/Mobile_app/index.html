Mobile app

A mobile application, or mobile app, is a computer program or software application designed to run on a mobile device such as a phone, tablet, or watch. Mobile applications often stand in contrast to desktop applications, which are run on desktop computers, and web applications, which run in mobile web browsers rather than directly on the device.

The term "app" entered popular usage after the launch of Apple's App Store in 2008, alongside the iPhone. Apps are generally small, individual software units with limited function, and are distributed through platform-controlled stores such as the App Store (iOS) and Google Play (Android), which provide discovery, payment handling, and updates.

Types

Mobile apps are commonly grouped into native apps (built for a specific platform using its SDKs and languages), web-based apps (delivered through a browser), and hybrid apps (web content wrapped in a native shell). Progressive web apps (PWAs) blur these categories further.

History

Early mobile software, such as the games and utilities bundled on 1990s feature phones and PDAs, preceded the modern app economy. The rapid growth of app stores after 2008 created a major software industry segment, with millions of apps for communication, commerce, entertainment, productivity, and health.
