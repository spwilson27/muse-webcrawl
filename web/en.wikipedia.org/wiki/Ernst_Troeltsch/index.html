Ernst Troeltsch

Ernst Peter Wilhelm Troeltsch (17 February 1865 – 1 February 1923) was a German liberal Protestant theologian and a founder of the sociology of religion.

Ernst Peter Wilhelm Troeltsch (17 February 1865 – 1 February 1923) was a German liberal Protestant theologian, philosopher of history, and politician. A central member of the history of religions school (Religionsgeschichtliche Schule) and a close friend of Max Weber, he developed an influential sociology of Christianity and wrestled throughout his work with the problem of historicism — how absolute religious claims can survive historical relativism.

Career

Troeltsch was born in Haunstetten, near Augsburg, and studied theology at Erlangen, Göttingen and Berlin. He taught at Göttingen and Bonn before holding the chair of systematic theology at Heidelberg (1894–1915), where his association with Weber and the Heidelberg intellectual circle shaped his turn toward sociology and cultural history. In 1915 he moved to Berlin as professor of philosophy. After the First World War he entered politics with the German Democratic Party, serving in the Prussian ministry of education and as a member of the Prussian state assembly, advocating a democratic republic.

Major ideas

His monumental The Social Teaching of the Christian Churches (1912) traced how Christian ethics interacted with social and economic life across two millennia. In it he elaborated the famous threefold typology of religious organisation: church (inclusive, institutional, accommodating to the world), sect (voluntary, exclusive, demanding) and mysticism (individual, loosely organised) — a scheme taken up by later sociology through H. Richard Niebuhr and others. Troeltsch argued that early Christianity's social ethic was shaped by eschatological expectation, and that each age must construct its own cultural synthesis from the historically conditioned materials available to it. His posthumously published Der Historismus und seine Probleme (1922) confronted the "crisis of historicism" directly.

Legacy

Troeltsch's work helped establish the sociology of religion as a discipline and remains a reference point for debates about church–sect dynamics, the social history of doctrine, and the relationship between Christianity and modernity. He died in Berlin in 1923, shortly before he was due to lecture in England.
