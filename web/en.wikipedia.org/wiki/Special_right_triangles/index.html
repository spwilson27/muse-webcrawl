Special right triangle

A special right triangle is a right triangle with some regular feature that makes calculations on the triangle easier, or for which simple formulas exist. Examples include right triangles whose angles or side lengths follow simple patterns, so that trigonometric values can be given exactly rather than approximated.

Angle-based

The 45°–45°–90° triangle is isosceles, with sides in the ratio 1 : 1 : √2. The 30°–60°–90° triangle, half of an equilateral triangle, has sides in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2. These two triangles provide the exact values of sine and cosine for 30°, 45° and 60° used throughout trigonometry teaching.

Side-based

Right triangles with integer side lengths are called Pythagorean triples; the simplest is 3 : 4 : 5. Others with simple ratios include the Kepler triangle, whose sides are in geometric progression involving the golden ratio, and triangles based on the silver ratio.
