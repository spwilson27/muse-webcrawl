Linwood, Renfrewshire

Linwood is a small town in Renfrewshire in the west central Lowlands of Scotland, about 14 miles (23 km) west of Glasgow and immediately west of Paisley. Its population was around 8,000–9,000 in recent censuses.

History

Linwood transformed in the 1960s when the Rootes Group opened a car factory there to build the Hillman Imp, the first mass-produced British car with an all-aluminium rear engine. The plant, later operated by Chrysler and Peugeot Talbot, closed in 1981 with major local job losses — an enduring episode in the history of Scottish industrial policy and deindustrialization.

Today

The town is now largely residential, with retail and leisure developments on and around the former factory site, and it gained attention in the 2010s when it was ironically voted Scotland's most dismal town (the "Carbuncle Award") before community-led regeneration efforts won praise.
