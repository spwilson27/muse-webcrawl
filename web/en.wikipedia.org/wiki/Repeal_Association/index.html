Repeal Association

The Repeal Association was an Irish political movement founded by Daniel O'Connell in 1840 with the aim of repealing the Acts of Union 1800 between Great Britain and Ireland, and restoring an Irish parliament under the British Crown. It succeeded O'Connell's earlier campaigns and became one of the first mass-membership political organisations in Europe.

Background

Following the achievement of Catholic emancipation in 1829, O'Connell turned his attention to the repeal of the Union. He had previously founded the Loyal National Repeal Association in 1840. The Association demanded the restoration of the Parliament of Ireland, abolished in 1801, while retaining the link to the British monarch — a position often described as seeking a form of home rule rather than full independence.

Monster meetings

The Association is best known for its "monster meetings" of 1843, enormous peaceful rallies held across Ireland that drew crowds estimated in the hundreds of thousands. Meetings at Tara, Mullaghmast and elsewhere demonstrated the scale of popular support for repeal. The British government of Sir Robert Peel responded by banning the planned meeting at Clontarf in October 1843; O'Connell cancelled the meeting to avoid bloodshed, a decision that weakened the movement's momentum.

Decline

O'Connell was prosecuted and imprisoned for sedition in 1844, though the conviction was overturned on appeal. Younger members, organised as Young Ireland, grew impatient with O'Connell's moral-force approach and with his alliance with the Whigs, and seceded to form the Irish Confederation in 1847. O'Connell died in Genoa in May 1847 while travelling to Rome, and the Association faded during the catastrophe of the Great Famine.

Legacy

The Repeal Association pioneered techniques of mass peaceful mobilisation — membership cards, penny-a-month subscriptions (the "Repeal rent") and organised local branches — that later Irish nationalist movements, including the Land League and the Irish Parliamentary Party, would emulate.
