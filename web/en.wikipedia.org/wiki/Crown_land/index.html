Crown land

Crown land (sometimes spelled crownland), also known as royal domain, is a territorial area belonging to a monarch, who personifies the Crown. It is the equivalent of an entailed estate and passes with the monarchy, being inseparable from it. In Commonwealth realms, the term is commonly used to describe publicly held land administered by the Crown.

Canada

In Canada, Crown land is land owned by the federal or provincial governments. Much of the country's land area is Crown land: in provinces such as British Columbia, Ontario and Newfoundland and Labrador, the great majority of the land base is provincial Crown land, used for forestry, mining, recreation and conservation, and subject in many places to Indigenous rights and title claims.

Australia

In Australia, Crown land is land held in the name of the respective state or territory government, amounting to roughly a quarter of the continent. It is administered under state Crown lands legislation and includes reserves, national parks, travelling stock routes and land under pastoral lease.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, the Crown Estate is a collection of lands and holdings managed by an independent board, with revenues paid to the Treasury. Historically, the monarch's "crown lands" financed the expenses of government until revenue was surrendered in exchange for the Civil List beginning in 1760.
