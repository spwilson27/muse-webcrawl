Ixion

In Greek mythology, Ixion was king of the Lapiths of Thessaly and one of the most notorious sinners of the underworld, condemned to be bound eternally to a burning wheel revolving in Tartarus.

Myth

Ixion treacherously murdered his father-in-law Deioneus, becoming the first man guilty of kin-slaying; Zeus purified him and admitted him to Olympus, but Ixion repaid the favour by attempting to seduce Hera. Zeus fashioned a cloud in Hera's shape—Nephele—with whom Ixion fathered Centaurus, ancestor of the centaurs. Some versions also make Ixion the father of Pirithous.

Punishment

For his ingratitude and lust, Zeus hurled Ixion into Tartarus and bound him to a winged, fiery wheel that turns forever. His story appears in Pindar's Pythian Ode 2 and was a favourite subject of ancient and later art, including a famous painting by Titian.

Namesake

The Kuiper belt object 28978 Ixion, a plutino discovered in 2001, is named after him.
