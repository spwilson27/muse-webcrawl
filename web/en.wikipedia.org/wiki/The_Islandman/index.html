The Islandman (Irish: An tOileánach) is the autobiography of Tomás Ó Criomhthain (1856–1937), a fisherman and native Irish speaker of the Great Blasket Island off the Dingle Peninsula, County Kerry. Published in Irish in 1929 and translated into English by Robin Flower in 1934, it is one of the foundational works of twentieth-century Irish-language literature.

Content

Written at the encouragement of scholars who visited the island, the memoir recounts Ó Criomhthain's whole life: childhood in a subsistence community, fishing and hunting expeditions, storms and shipwrecks, weddings, wakes, emigration of his children to America, and the slow decline of the island community. Its plain, aphoristic style preserves the oral storytelling voice of Blasket Gaelic.

Significance

An tOileánach is the most famous of the "Blasket autobiographies," alongside Peig Sayers' Peig and Muiris Ó Súilleabháin's Twenty Years a-Growing. For decades it was a set text in Irish schools. The Great Blasket was evacuated in 1953; Ó Criomhthain's house has been restored as part of the Ionad an Bhlascaoid heritage centre. His epitaph-like closing reflects his stated aim: "the like of us will never be again."
