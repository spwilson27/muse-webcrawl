Old Yishuv

The Old Yishuv (Hebrew: היישוב הישן, ha-Yishuv ha-Yashan) were the Jewish communities of the region of Palestine in the Ottoman Empire before the beginning of Zionist aliyah and the establishment of the New Yishuv in the 1880s. The term distinguishes these long-established, largely religious communities from the later, mostly secular Zionist settlers.

Communities

The Old Yishuv was concentrated in the Four Holy Cities of Judaism: Jerusalem, Hebron, Safed and Tiberias, with smaller communities in Jaffa, Haifa, Peki'in, Acre, Nablus and Gaza. Its population included Sephardic Jews, whose families had lived in the region for centuries after the expulsion from Spain in 1492, and Ashkenazi Jews who immigrated mainly in the 18th and 19th centuries, including Hasidim and the Perushim, followers of the Vilna Gaon.

Economy and society

Many members devoted themselves to Torah study and prayer on behalf of world Jewry, supported by the halukka system of charitable funds raised in the diaspora. The community also included merchants, artisans and craftsmen. Numbers grew from a few thousand around 1800 to roughly 25,000 by 1880. Relations between the Old Yishuv and the Zionist New Yishuv were often strained over religion, funding and political aims, though the older community's institutions — synagogues, hospitals and schools — formed part of the foundation of the later Jewish state.
