Kokin Wakashū

The Kokin Wakashū (古今和歌集, "Collection of Japanese Poems of Ancient and Modern Times"), often shortened to Kokinshū, is an early Heian-period imperial anthology of waka poetry, compiled around 905 on the orders of Emperor Daigo. It is the first of the twenty-one imperial anthologies of Japanese poetry and one of the most influential works in Japanese literature.

Compilation and contents

The anthology contains about 1,111 poems arranged in twenty books by theme — the seasons, love, travel, mourning and so on. Its chief compiler, Ki no Tsurayuki, also wrote its famous Japanese preface (kanajo), the first major work of poetry criticism in Japanese, in addition to a Chinese preface by Ki no Yoshimochi.

Influence

The Kokinshū established the aesthetics and diction of court poetry for centuries; its poems became standard allusive references in later literature, and it helped elevate waka — and vernacular writing itself — to canonical status.
