Carloman (c. 830 – 22 September 880) was a Frankish king of the Carolingian dynasty, the eldest son of Louis the German. He ruled as King of Bavaria from 876 and also as King of Italy from 877 until he was incapacitated by a stroke in 879.

Life

Carloman rebelled against his father in 861 and again in 863, but was reconciled and entrusted with the marches of Pannonia and Carinthia. On Louis's death in 876 the East Frankish kingdom was divided among his sons: Carloman received Bavaria, Louis the Younger received Saxony and Franconia, and Charles the Fat received Swabia. In 877 Carloman crossed the Alps and was crowned King of Italy after the death of Charles the Bald. Struck by illness in 879, he yielded Italy to his brother Charles the Fat and Bavaria to his young son Arnulf of Carinthia, an illegitimate son who would later become king and emperor. Carloman died at Altötting in 880.
