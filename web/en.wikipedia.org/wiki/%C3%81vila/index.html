Ávila

Ávila is a city and municipality in Spain, the capital of the province of Ávila in the autonomous community of Castile and León. Situated more than 1,100 metres above sea level on the banks of the Adaja River, it is among the highest provincial capitals in Spain. Ávila is renowned for its complete and exceptionally well-preserved medieval city walls, and the Old Town and its extra-muros churches were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.

History

The area of Ávila was inhabited in pre-Roman times by the Vettones, and later formed part of Roman Hispania. After the Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula, the city changed hands several times before being definitively repopulated under Alfonso VI of León and Castile in the late 11th century. Construction of the famous walls began around 1090. Ávila is closely associated with Saint Teresa of Ávila, the mystic and reformer of the Carmelite Order, who was born there in 1515.

Walls of Ávila

The Walls of Ávila are a complete circuit of Romanesque defensive walls some 2.5 kilometres long, with 87 or 88 semicircular towers and nine gates. They are considered one of the finest examples of medieval fortification in Europe and are the city's most famous landmark.

Landmarks

  • Ávila Cathedral, a fortified cathedral built in Romanesque and Gothic styles
  • Basilica of San Vicente, a Romanesque church outside the walls
  • Convent of Saint Teresa, built on the site of her birthplace
  • Church of San Pedro, facing the Alcázar gate

Climate

Ávila has a temperate climate with continental influences; its altitude gives it cold winters with frequent frost and snow, and warm but mild summers compared with the Meseta to the south.
