The Apollo Theater is a legendary music hall at 253 West 125th Street in the Harlem neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City. It is one of the most famous venues in the history of American popular music and a central institution of African-American culture, celebrated for launching the careers of countless performers.

History

The building opened in 1914 as a burlesque theatre that excluded Black audiences. In 1934 it reopened as the Apollo, presenting Black performers, and introduced its famous Amateur Night — the notoriously tough talent contest, judged by the audience, whose winners included Ella Fitzgerald (its first winner), Sarah Vaughan, Billie Holiday, James Brown, the Jackson 5, Lauryn Hill and many others. Performers traditionally rub the "Tree of Hope" stump on stage for luck.

Legacy

The Apollo was a showcase of jazz, swing, bebop, R&B, gospel, funk and hip hop, and its live albums — notably James Brown's Live at the Apollo (1963) — are landmarks of recorded music. The theatre was designated a New York City landmark and added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1983. Since 1991 it has been operated by the non-profit Apollo Theater Foundation, and the televised Showtime at the Apollo carried its Amateur Night tradition to a national audience.
