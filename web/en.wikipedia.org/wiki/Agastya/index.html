Agastya was a revered Vedic sage (rishi) in Hindu tradition, counted among the seven great sages, the Saptarishi. Hymns in the Rigveda are attributed to him, and he figures prominently in the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Puranas. He is especially venerated in South India, where tradition credits him with bringing Vedic culture across the Vindhya mountains, composing the first Tamil grammar, and founding lineages of Siddha medicine and the martial art of varma kalai.

Legends

Famous legends tell how Agastya humbled the Vindhya mountain range, which had grown so tall it blocked the sun: the mountain bowed to the sage and promised to stay low until he returned from the south — which he never did. In another tale he drank up the ocean to expose demons hiding in its depths, and received the sage's wife Lopamudra, a princess who composed hymns alongside him. In the Ramayana, Rama visits Agastya's hermitage and receives divine weapons from him, including the bow of Vishnu.

Attributions and worship

Numerous texts bear his name, including the Agastya Samhita and works on medicine, astrology and alchemy; the Nadi astrology palm-leaf manuscripts of Tamil Nadu are attributed to him. Temples dedicated to Agastya stand across South India and in Southeast Asia — statues of the pot-bellied sage are found as far as Java and Cambodia. The bright star Canopus, visible from southern latitudes, is named Agastya in Indian astronomy.
