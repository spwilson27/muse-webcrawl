Hull classification symbol

The United States Navy, United States Coast Guard and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) use a hull classification symbol (sometimes called hull code or hull number) to identify their ships by type and mission. The system assigns a letter code, typically two or three letters, followed by a number unique to each ship of that type.

History

The system evolved from the Naval Registry of 1895 and was standardised in 1920 with the adoption of the modern two-letter codes. Under the scheme, the first letter indicates the broad ship type and the second refines it: for example, CV denotes an aircraft carrier, BB a battleship, DD a destroyer, and SS a submarine.

Examples

  • CVN — aircraft carrier, nuclear powered
  • DDG — guided-missile destroyer
  • SSBN — ballistic-missile submarine, nuclear powered
  • LHD — amphibious assault ship
  • CG — guided-missile cruiser

A suffix "N" conventionally denotes nuclear propulsion, while "G" denotes a ship armed with guided missiles.
