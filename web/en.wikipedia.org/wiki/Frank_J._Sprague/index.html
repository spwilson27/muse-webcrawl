Frank J. Sprague

Frank Julian Sprague (July 25, 1857 – October 25, 1934) was an American inventor and a naval officer, known as the "Father of Electric Traction". He made important contributions to the development of the electric motor, electric railways, and electric elevators. His contributions were especially important in promoting urban development by increasing the size cities could reasonably attain and by allowing greater density of business in commercial sections.

Early life and career

Sprague graduated from the United States Naval Academy in 1878 and served as a naval officer while pursuing experiments with electricity. He resigned his commission in 1883 and briefly worked for Thomas Edison before founding the Sprague Electric Railway and Motor Company in 1884.

Electric street railways

In 1887–88, Sprague installed the first successful large electric street railway system in Richmond, Virginia — the Richmond Union Passenger Railway. The system demonstrated reliable electric traction with trolley poles, under-running current collection, and regenerative braking, and it became the model for electric streetcar systems adopted in hundreds of cities around the world.

Multiple-unit control and elevators

Sprague developed the multiple-unit system, patented in 1897, which allowed all the motors of an electric train to be controlled from a single driving position; it was first applied on Chicago's South Side Elevated Railroad and became standard for subway and elevated trains. He also founded the Sprague Electric Elevator Company and made innovations in high-speed electric elevator control and safety systems.

Later life and legacy

Sprague received the Franklin Institute's Elliott Cresson Medal and the Edison Medal, among other honours. His work laid the foundations for modern urban transit, and he is remembered as one of the principal figures in the electrification of transport.
