Clog

A clog is a type of footwear made in part or completely from wood. Traditional clogs are shoes carved from a single block of wood, worn for centuries by workers across northern and western Europe; modern clogs may combine a wooden sole with leather or synthetic uppers.

Types and history

The best-known form is the Dutch klomp, carved whole from willow or poplar and still made in the Netherlands as working footwear and as a souvenir symbol of the country. In France the equivalent is the sabot — from which the word sabotage is popularly (though uncertainly) derived, from tales of workers damaging machinery with their wooden shoes. In Britain, clogs with wooden soles and leather uppers were standard footwear for mill and factory workers in Lancashire and Yorkshire through the Industrial Revolution, and gave rise to clog dancing, performed in mills and music halls. Swedish träskor, often with painted leather uppers, became an international fashion item in the 1970s.

Modern use

Wooden-soled shoes protect the foot, keep it raised above wet or dirty floors, and are easy to slip on and off. Today clogs are worn in gardens, kitchens, hospitals, and food processing, usually in modified forms: brands such as Dansko and Crocs sell clog-style shoes (the latter entirely in moulded resin) aimed at workers who stand all day. The clog also remains central to traditional dance in the Netherlands, Britain, and Appalachia, where "clogging" is a form of folk dance performed in hard-soled shoes.
