Quetzalcoatlus

Quetzalcoatlus northropi
PeriodLate Cretaceous (Maastrichtian), ~68–66 Ma
RangeWestern North America (Texas)
Wingspan10–11 m
Height (ground)~3 m at the shoulder
Mass~200–250 kg (estimates vary)
FamilyAzhdarchidae

Quetzalcoatlus is a genus of giant azhdarchid pterosaur from the Late Cretaceous of North America, and one of the largest flying animals ever known. It was named in 1975 by Douglas Lawson after Quetzalcoatl, the feathered-serpent god of Mesoamerica. Two species are recognised: the huge Q. northropi and the smaller Q. lawsoni, though some workers treat them as distinct genera.

Discovery

The first fossils — a partial left wing from a single individual — were found in 1971 in the Javelina Formation of Big Bend National Park, Texas, by Douglas Lawson; John Klaus Northrop, founder of the Northrop Corporation, funded the research and is honoured in the species name. Later work by Wann Langston Jr. and others recovered more material, including a second, smaller species originally treated as a juvenile.

Biology

With a wingspan comparable to a small aeroplane and a height of about 3 m at the shoulder when grounded, Quetzalcoatlus was a giant among pterosaurs. Like other azhdarchids it was probably an adept quadrupedal walker and flier, feeding on the ground — possibly scavenging or hunting small prey on floodplains. Whether it could launch itself into powered flight or only soar remains debated; biomechanical studies generally conclude that it could take off from the ground with a running leap.
