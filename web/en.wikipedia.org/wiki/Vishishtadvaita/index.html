Vishishtadvaita

Vishishtadvaita (Sanskrit: विशिष्टाद्वैत, "qualified non-dualism") is a school of Vedānta philosophy and the principal theological tradition of Sri Vaishnavism. It teaches that Brahman — identified with the god Vishnu (Narayana) — is the supreme reality, of which individual souls (chit) and the material world (achit) are real attributes or modes, inseparably related to Brahman as body to soul. Its foremost systematiser was the 11th–12th century philosopher Ramanuja.

Philosophy

Vishishtadvaita differs from Shankara's Advaita by affirming the reality of the world and the plurality of souls; unity is "qualified" by these real entities, which constitute the body (śarīra) of Brahman, who is their inner self (antarātman). God is thus both the material and efficient cause of the universe, and creation is the expansion of the subtle body of Brahman into gross form.

Knowledge, following the doctrine of dharmi-jñāna and dharma-bhūta-jñāna, is always of a qualified object. Bondage arises from karma and ignorance of the soul's dependence on God; liberation (moksha) is the soul's release from samsara to eternal, blissful service (kainkarya) of God in Vaikuntha, retaining its individuality rather than dissolving into undifferentiated Brahman.

Devotion and surrender

The path of devotion (bhakti) and self-surrender (prapatti or śaraṇāgati) lie at the heart of Vishishtadvaita practice. Grace (prasāda) is essential to liberation. Ramanuja's works, including the Sri Bhashya commentary on the Brahma Sutras, the Gita Bhashya and the prose Gadyatraya, drew on the hymns of the Tamil Alvars as well as Sanskrit scripture, treating both as authoritative.

Later tradition

After Ramanuja, the tradition developed through teachers such as Vedanta Desika and Pillai Lokacharya. In the 14th–15th centuries Sri Vaishnavism split into the northern Vadakalai and southern Tenkalai schools, differing over the relative roles of grace and human effort and the status of the goddess Lakshmi. Vishishtadvaita remains the living philosophy of Sri Vaishnava temples such as Srirangam and Tirupati.
