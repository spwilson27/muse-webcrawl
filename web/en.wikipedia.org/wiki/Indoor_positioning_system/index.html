Indoor positioning system

An indoor positioning system (IPS) is a network of devices used to locate people or objects inside a building, where satellite navigation systems such as GPS are unreliable or unavailable due to signal attenuation and multipath effects caused by walls and structures.

IPS applications include navigation in airports, hospitals and malls.

Technologies

IPS implementations draw on a wide range of signals and sensors. Radio-based methods use Wi-Fi access points, Bluetooth Low Energy beacons, ultra-wideband (UWB) radio, and RFID tags. Other approaches exploit magnetic-field anomalies inside steel-framed buildings, inertial sensors in smartphones (pedestrian dead reckoning), visible-light communication, acoustic signals, and computer-vision systems. UWB systems can reach decimetre or centimetre accuracy, while Wi-Fi fingerprinting typically delivers accuracy of a few metres.

Methods

Common estimation techniques include trilateration from measured ranges (derived from time of arrival or signal strength), fingerprinting — matching a live signal measurement against a pre-recorded radio map of the building — proximity detection, and sensor fusion combining several of these with inertial data and map constraints.

Applications

Uses include wayfinding in large public buildings, asset and staff tracking in hospitals and warehouses, location-based services in retail, assistive navigation for visually impaired people, and positioning for indoor robots and drones. Because indoor tracking can be highly intrusive, IPS deployments also raise notable privacy questions.
