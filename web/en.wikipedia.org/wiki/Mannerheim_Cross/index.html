Mannerheim Cross

The Mannerheim Cross (Finnish: Mannerheim-risti; Swedish: Mannerheimkorset) is the most distinguished Finnish military decoration. It was introduced during the Continuation War in 1940 by Marshal Carl Gustaf Emil Mannerheim, Commander-in-Chief of the Finnish Defence Forces, as part of the Order of the Cross of Liberty.

Criteria

The Cross was awarded for exceptional bravery, decisive achievement in combat, or especially successful conduct of operations. Uniquely for a Finnish decoration, it could be granted to any soldier regardless of rank; its recipients, who received the title Knight of the Mannerheim Cross (Mannerheim-ristin ritari), included privates as well as generals.

Classes and awards

The decoration exists in two classes. The 1st class was awarded only twice, to Mannerheim himself and to General Erik Heinrichs. The 2nd class was awarded 191 times during the Second World War; four men received it twice. The cross is a white enamelled swastika (the traditional Finnish hakaristi) in gold on a black cross, suspended from a red ribbon; awardees received a monetary grant as well as the decoration.

Legacy

No award has been made since 1945, but the decoration remains formally second in Finland's order of precedence, after the Grand Cross of the Order of the White Rose. The last surviving Knight, Tuomas Gerdt, died in 2020.
