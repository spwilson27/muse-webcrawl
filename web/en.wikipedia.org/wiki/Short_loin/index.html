Short loin

The short loin is a primal cut of beef located along the back of the animal, between the rib primal in front and the sirloin and round behind. It is cut from the lumbar region, roughly from the 13th rib back to the hip bone, and contains part of the spine and the highly prized tenderloin (psoas major) muscle along with the top loin (longissimus) muscle.

Because the muscles of the short loin do relatively little work, the meat is among the most tender on the carcass, and the short loin yields many of the most expensive steakhouse cuts.

Cuts from the short loin

  • T-bone steak — a cross-section containing both top loin and a portion of tenderloin separated by a T-shaped bone.
  • Porterhouse steak — a larger T-bone cut from the rear of the short loin with a bigger tenderloin portion; in the United States the tenderloin side must be at least 1.25 inches wide to be labelled porterhouse.
  • Strip steak (New York strip, Kansas City strip) — cut from the top loin muscle, usually boneless.
  • Tenderloin / filet mignon — the tenderloin may be sold whole or cut into filets; the forward part lies in the short loin.
  • Club steak — from the front of the short loin, adjacent to the rib.

Terminology

In British and Commonwealth butchery the corresponding region is divided differently; the strip steak portion is generally part of the sirloin, while the T-bone spans what those traditions treat as the boundary between sirloin and the rump side. The Italian bistecca alla fiorentina is traditionally a thick T-bone/porterhouse-style cut from this region, grilled rare over coals.
