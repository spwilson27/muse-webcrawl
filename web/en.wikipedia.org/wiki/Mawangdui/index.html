Mawangdui

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The T-shaped silk banner from Mawangdui depicts the Chinese cosmos.

Mawangdui (simplified Chinese: 马王堆; traditional Chinese: 馬王堆; pinyin: Mǎwángduī) is an archaeological site located in Changsha, China. The site consists of two saddle-shaped hills and was originally the burial site of Li Cang (died 186 BC), the first Chancellor of the Changsha Kingdom, and his wife Xin Zhui and a son. Excavated between 1972 and 1974, the site is famous for the remarkably well-preserved tomb of Xin Zhui and the Mawangdui Silk Texts.

The tombs

Three tombs were excavated: Tomb 1 contained the mummified body of Xin Zhui, wife of the Chancellor, which was astonishingly well preserved, with skin still supple and blood vessels intact. Tomb 2 was Li Cang's tomb, and Tomb 3, dated 168 BC, belonged to their son. The tombs contained over a thousand artefacts, including lacquerware, silk garments, musical instruments and a T-shaped silk painting depicting the underworld, earth and heavens, now a treasure of the Hunan Museum.

The silk texts

The Mawangdui Silk Texts include early versions of the Dao De Jing attributed to Laozi, along with previously unknown Taoist texts, astronomical and medical writings — including diagrams of daoyin gymnastic exercises and the earliest known Chinese star charts and medical treatises — and silk maps that are the oldest surviving maps from China. The texts are of enormous importance for understanding early Chinese philosophy, medicine and science.
