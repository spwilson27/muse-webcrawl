BM-21 Grad

The BM-21 "Grad" (Russian: БМ-21 «Град», lit. "hailstorm") is a Soviet-designed 122 mm multiple rocket launcher. Developed in the early 1960s and entering service in 1963, it is one of the most widely used artillery rocket systems in the world, exported to dozens of countries and copied in numerous local variants. The name Grad also refers to the wider family of systems built around the 122 mm rocket.

Design

The launcher consists of a bank of 40 steel tubes arranged in four rows of ten, mounted on the back of a 6×6 truck. A full salvo of 40 rockets can be fired in about 20 seconds, delivering roughly two tonnes of high-explosive warheads onto a target area. The rockets are spin- and fin-stabilised and are reloaded by hand, a process taking around ten minutes with a trained crew.

Employment

The Grad was designed for area saturation fire against troop concentrations, artillery positions and soft targets rather than precision strikes. It has seen combat in numerous conflicts, including the Vietnam War era border conflicts, the Iran–Iraq War, wars in Afghanistan, the Chechen wars, and, on a large scale, the Russo-Ukrainian War. Successor and derivative systems include the Russian BM-21 variants, the Tornado-G, and licensed or reverse-engineered versions produced in China, Egypt, Romania and many other states.
