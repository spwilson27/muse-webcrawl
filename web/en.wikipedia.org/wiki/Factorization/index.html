Factorization

In mathematics, factorization (also factorisation) is the decomposition of an object — such as a number, polynomial, or matrix — into a product of other objects, called factors, which when multiplied together give the original. For example, the integer 15 factors as 3 × 5, and the polynomial x2 − 4 factors as (x − 2)(x + 2).

Integers

By the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, every positive integer greater than 1 has a unique factorisation into prime numbers. Finding this factorisation for very large integers — the integer factorization problem — is computationally hard, and its difficulty underpins the RSA cryptosystem. The best general-purpose classical algorithm is the general number field sieve; Shor's quantum algorithm factorises in polynomial time.

Polynomials

Polynomials over a field can be factored into irreducible factors; over the complex numbers the fundamental theorem of algebra guarantees a complete factorisation into linear factors. Factoring polynomials is used to solve equations, simplify rational expressions, and analyse roots.

Other objects

Matrix factorisations (decompositions) such as LU, QR and singular value decomposition are central to numerical linear algebra, though they are not products of like objects in the strict sense.
