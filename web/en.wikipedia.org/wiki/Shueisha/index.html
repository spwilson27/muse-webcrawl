Shueisha Inc. (Japanese: 株式会社集英社, Kabushiki-gaisha Shūeisha) is a Japanese publishing company headquartered in Chiyoda, Tokyo. Founded in 1925 as the entertainment division of Shogakukan, it became an independent company in 1949 and is part of the Hitotsubashi Group alongside Shogakukan and Hakusensha. Shueisha is Japan's largest manga publisher, best known for the magazine Weekly Shōnen Jump and the manga it has serialised, including Dragon Ball, One Piece, Naruto, Slam Dunk and Demon Slayer.

History

Shueisha was founded in 1925 by Takeo Ōga of Shogakukan; its name derives from shūeisha ("gathering of intellects"). It split from Shogakukan in 1949 and expanded rapidly in the postwar manga boom, launching Ribon (1955) for girls and, in 1968, Weekly Shōnen Jump, which reached a record circulation of over 6.5 million copies a week in the mid-1990s. The company co-founded the North American publisher Viz Media (originally with Shogakukan) to license its manga in English.

Magazines and imprints

Shueisha publishes manga magazines for every demographic: Weekly Shōnen Jump, Jump Square and V Jump for boys; Ultra Jump and Weekly Young Jump for young men; Ribon, Margaret, Bessatsu Margaret and Cookie for girls; and You and Cocohana for women. Collected volumes appear under the Jump Comics imprint; light novels under Jump J-Books and Dash X Bunko; and general magazines include the Japanese edition of Playboy (until its closure) and non-no and More for women. Its digital platform Shōnen Jump+ (2014) and the international service Manga Plus (2019) have become major channels as print circulation has declined.

Influence

Through its reader-survey-driven editorial system and the global success of Jump franchises, Shueisha has shaped the modern manga and anime industries; Jump series anchor many of the best-selling manga of all time, with One Piece the best-selling manga series in history.
