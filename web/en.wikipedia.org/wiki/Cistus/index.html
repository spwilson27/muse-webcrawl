Cistus

Cistus (from the Greek kistos) is a genus of flowering shrubs in the rockrose family Cistaceae. The plants, commonly called rockroses, are native to the Mediterranean region and the Canary Islands, where they grow on dry, rocky soils.

Description

Rockroses are evergreen or semi-evergreen shrubs with opposite leaves, often sticky or aromatic. Their showy five-petalled flowers — white, pink or purple, frequently with a crimson blotch at the base of each petal — open in the morning and drop their petals by the afternoon. Many species produce the fragrant resin labdanum, long used in perfumery and traditional medicine.

Ecology and cultivation

Cistus species are classic plants of Mediterranean scrubland such as maquis and garrigue. They are adapted to summer drought and to fire: their hard seeds germinate readily after burning. In gardens they are popular drought-tolerant shrubs for poor soils and coastal sites, though many are short-lived. Well-known species include Cistus ladanifer (gum rockrose), C. salviifolius, C. monspeliensis and the hybrid garden plant C. × purpureus.
