Wayback Machine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Wayback Machine
Launched2001 (public access)
OperatorInternet Archive
PurposePreserving snapshots of websites
[Image: Screenshot-style image of the Wayback Machine calendar interface showing capture dates for a website]

The Wayback Machine is a digital archive of the World Wide Web built by the Internet Archive. Launched publicly in 2001 (its crawls reach back to 1996), it allows users to go 'back in time' to see how websites looked in the past.

Anyone can enter a URL and browse a calendar of captures: snapshots of pages — HTML, images and stylesheets — recorded by the Archive's crawlers or submitted by users through 'Save Page Now'. The archive holds hundreds of billions of pages, and researchers use it to recover deleted pages, verify what a site said on a given date, and study the web's own history; lawyers use it as evidence, and journalists to hold officials to their earlier statements.

The Wayback Machine honours some exclusions (historically robots.txt, and removal requests), and its completeness is patchy — deep pages, paywalled content and interactive applications survive poorly. But as link rot steadily erases the early web, the Wayback Machine has become the default time machine of the Internet age, and a working argument that someone must deliberately keep the copies.
