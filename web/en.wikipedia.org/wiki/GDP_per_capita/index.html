GDP per capita

GDP per capita is the gross domestic product (GDP) of a country divided by its total population. It is a commonly used indicator of a country's average economic output per person and is often cited as a rough measure of average living standards or economic well-being, although it does not measure personal income or the distribution of wealth.

Calculation

GDP per capita = GDP ÷ midyear population. GDP figures may be expressed in nominal terms (converted at market exchange rates) or in purchasing power parity (PPP) terms, which adjust for differences in the cost of living between countries. PPP-based figures are generally preferred for comparing living standards, while nominal figures better reflect a country's purchasing power on international markets.

Interpretation and limits

GDP per capita correlates with indicators such as life expectancy, literacy, and the Human Development Index, but it has well-known limitations: it says nothing about income inequality within a country (the median income may be far lower), it omits non-market activity and household production, and it does not account for environmental costs or the sustainability of growth. Small financial centres and oil-rich states often top per-capita rankings for structural reasons.

Rankings

The International Monetary Fund, the World Bank, and the CIA World Factbook each publish annual lists of countries by GDP per capita, both nominal and PPP. Microstates such as Liechtenstein and Monaco, and countries such as Luxembourg, Ireland, Singapore, Qatar, and Switzerland, typically rank highest, while the poorest countries, concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, record figures below one thousand international dollars on a PPP basis.
