Papaver

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Papaver is a genus of 70–100 species of frost-tolerant annuals, biennials, and perennials native to temperate and cold regions of Eurasia, Africa, and North America. It is the type genus of the poppy family, Papaveraceae. The flowers have two sepals that fall off as the bud opens, and four (or up to six) petals in reds, pinks, oranges, yellows, or lilac.

Description

The stems of most species exude a milky latex when cut. Leaves are lobed and often hairy or bristly. The fruit is a capsule crowned by a disc of stigmas; when ripe, pores beneath the disc open and the tiny dark seeds are shaken out by the wind. Historically, the unripe seed capsules of Papaver somniferum have been the source of opium and its alkaloids, including morphine and codeine, while the ripe seeds are widely used as a food and for oil.

Notable species

  • Papaver somniferum — opium poppy; source of opium, poppy seeds and poppyseed oil
  • Papaver rhoeas — common poppy, corn poppy, Flanders poppy; symbol of remembrance for fallen soldiers
  • Papaver orientale — Oriental poppy, a popular garden perennial
  • Papaver nudicaule — Iceland poppy (sometimes placed in Oreomecon)
  • Papaver radicatum — Arctic poppy
  • Papaver bracteatum — Iranian poppy
  • Papaver dubium — long-headed poppy
  • Papaver argemone — prickly poppy

Cultivation and uses

Several species are grown as ornamental garden plants for their large, brightly coloured flowers; cultivars of the Oriental poppy and Shirley poppy (derived from P. rhoeas) are especially popular. The seeds of the opium poppy are used worldwide in baking and as a source of a drying oil. Cultivation of P. somniferum is regulated in many countries because of its narcotic alkaloids, though it is legally grown for pharmaceutical production and culinary seed in others.

Taxonomy

The genus was described by Carl Linnaeus in Species Plantarum (1753). Recent phylogenetic studies have led to some species being moved to other genera, such as Oreomecon for the Arctic and alpine poppies.
