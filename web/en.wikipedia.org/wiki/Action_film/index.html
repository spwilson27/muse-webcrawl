Action film

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The action film is a film genre in which the protagonist is thrust into a series of events involving violence, physical stunts, chases, fights and extended action sequences, typically concluding with the hero's victory over the antagonist. Action films emphasise spectacle, pacing and set pieces at least as much as character development, and they form one of the most commercially successful genres in world cinema.

History

Elements of the genre trace to early adventure serials and swashbucklers, but the modern action film crystallised in the 1970s with crime thrillers such as Bullitt (1968) and its famous car chase, The French Connection (1971) and the Hong Kong martial arts films of Bruce Lee. The 1980s made the genre dominant in Hollywood through stars such as Sylvester Stallone, Arnold Schwarzenegger and Bruce Willis, with films including Raiders of the Lost Ark, Lethal Weapon and Die Hard (1988) setting templates still imitated today. From the 1990s, digital effects and accelerated editing transformed staging, while Hong Kong choreography and later franchises such as The Matrix, Mad Max: Fury Road and the John Wick series renewed emphasis on practical stunt work.

Subgenres and conventions

Subgenres include action-adventure, martial arts films, action comedy, disaster films, spy films, war films and superhero films. Recurring conventions include the climactic one-on-one confrontation, ticking-clock deadlines, large-scale destruction, and the lone hero or mismatched team pitted against overwhelming odds.
