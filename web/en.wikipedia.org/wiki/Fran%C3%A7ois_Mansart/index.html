François Mansart

Born23 January 1598, Paris, France
Died23 September 1666, Paris, France
OccupationArchitect
MovementFrench Baroque / classicism

François Mansart (23 January 1598 – 23 September 1666) was a French architect credited with introducing classicism into Baroque architecture of France. He is best known as the inventor of the mansard roof, named after him, which allowed usable attic space in buildings.

Works

Among his major works are the Château de Blois (Gascon wing), the Château de Maisons, the church of the Val-de-Grâce in Paris (begun to his designs), and the Hôtel de Toulouse. His perfectionism and repeated revisions of his designs strained relations with patrons, and he never received the Louvre commission he hoped for.

Legacy

Mansart's disciplined use of classical orders and his interplay of volumes deeply influenced later French architecture, including the work of his great-nephew Jules Hardouin-Mansart at Versailles.
