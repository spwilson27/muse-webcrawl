Volscian language

Volscian was a Sabellic (Osco-Umbrian) Italic language formerly spoken by the Volsci, an Italic people of Latium in central Italy, in the region south-east of Rome around Velitrae (modern Velletri), Antium and the Pontine plain.

Attestation

Volscian is known from only a handful of inscriptions. The most important is a bronze tablet from Velitrae, dating to the early 3rd century BC, written in the Latin alphabet and dealing with religious regulations concerning a grove sacred to the goddess Declona, including fines for violations. A few shorter inscriptions, including one from Antinum in Marsian territory referring to a priest, are also attributed to Volscian or closely related dialects.

Classification

Linguistically Volscian is most closely related to Umbrian: it shares Umbrian features such as the treatment of labiovelars (for example Volscian pis "who" corresponding to Umbrian usage) and the retention of diphthongs, while also showing some Oscan influence. Because of its Umbrian-like character it is sometimes grouped within the Umbrian complex of Sabellic languages rather than with Oscan.

Decline

The Volsci were conquered by Rome during the Samnite Wars, and their territory was Romanised; Volscian disappeared in favour of Latin by the late Republic.
