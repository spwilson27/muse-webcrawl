Arthur Leigh Allen (18 December 1933 – 26 August 1992) was an American convicted child molester and former elementary school teacher who became the best-known suspect in the unsolved Zodiac Killer murders of the late 1960s in Northern California. He was never charged in connection with the Zodiac crimes, and forensic comparisons conducted during his lifetime and after his death did not conclusively link him to them; he remains one of many persons of interest, though the most extensively investigated.

Background

Allen was born in Burbank and raised in Vallejo. He served in the US Navy in the 1950s and taught at elementary schools in Atascadero and elsewhere before being fired over allegations of molesting students. In 1975 he pleaded guilty to child molestation and served a prison term at Atascadero State Hospital until 1977 — a period sometimes noted because the confirmed Zodiac letters ceased during his incarceration.

Zodiac investigation

Allen first came to police attention in 1969–71: he was interviewed after reportedly making statements to acquaintances and because he wore a Zodiac-brand wristwatch, owned typewriters and handwriting characteristics of interest, and lived near early crime scenes. The case against him was built most fully by Robert Graysmith in his books Zodiac (1986) and Zodiac Unmasked, which argued circumstantial evidence pointed to Allen; he is the basis for the character pursued in David Fincher's 2007 film Zodiac.

Counter-evidence is substantial: Allen's fingerprints did not match latent prints attributed to the killer, his handwriting was judged not to match the Zodiac letters by document examiners, and DNA recovered from a Zodiac letter envelope in 2002 did not match Allen. Vallejo police searched his home under warrant in 1991, seizing weapons and a pipe bomb for which he faced separate charges, but he died of a heart attack and kidney failure at home on 26 August 1992 while awaiting further investigation. No one has ever been charged with the Zodiac murders.
