Harrow School

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Harrow School is a public school (English private boarding school for boys) in Harrow on the Hill, Greater London, England. It was founded in 1572 under a royal charter granted by Queen Elizabeth I to local landowner John Lyon. The school educates roughly 800 boys aged 13 to 18 in a dozen boarding houses and is a member of the Eton Group.

History and traditions

John Lyon's endowment of 1572 also funded the maintenance of the Harrow road and the education of local scholars. Harrow is famous for its traditions: the straw boater uniform, the school songs (above all "Forty Years On"), and Harrow football, an ancestor of association football played only at the school. Its annual cricket match against Eton at Lord's, played since 1805, is one of the oldest fixtures in sport.

Old Harrovians

Alumni include seven British prime ministers, among them Winston Churchill and Robert Peel; the poet Lord Byron; India's first prime minister, Jawaharlal Nehru; and King Hussein of Jordan.
