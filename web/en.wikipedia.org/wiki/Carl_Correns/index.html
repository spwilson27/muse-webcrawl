Carl Correns

Carl Erich Correns (19 September 1864 – 14 February 1933) was a German botanist and geneticist notable primarily for his independent discovery of the principles of heredity, which he achieved simultaneously with, but independently of, the botanist Hugo de Vries, and for his endorsement and recognition of Gregor Mendel's earlier paper on that subject.

Early life and education

Correns was born in Munich. Orphaned at an early age, he was raised by an aunt and attended schools in St. Gallen, Switzerland. He entered the University of Munich in 1885 to study botany, where he was influenced by Karl Wilhelm von Nägeli, with whom Mendel had corresponded about his pea experiments. After completing his doctorate in 1889, Correns became a tutor at the University of Tübingen.

Rediscovery of Mendel

In 1900, Correns, Hugo de Vries and Erich von Tschermak independently rediscovered Mendel's laws of heredity; Correns and Tschermak both acknowledged Mendel's priority. Correns' paper, "G. Mendel's law concerning the behavior of progeny of varietal hybrids", was published in 1900. Correns also provided the first convincing evidence for cytoplasmic (extrachromosomal) inheritance, in a 1909 study of variegation in the four o'clock plant Mirabilis jalapa, showing that leaf colour was transmitted maternally through chloroplasts.

Later career

From 1914 Correns directed the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Biology in Berlin-Dahlem, one of the first institutes devoted to the new science of genetics. He died in Berlin in 1933. Much of his unpublished work and laboratory notes were destroyed in the bombing of Berlin in 1945.
