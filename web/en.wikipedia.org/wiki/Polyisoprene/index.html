Polyisoprene is a polymer of isoprene (C5H8) that occurs naturally and is also produced synthetically. Depending on the geometry of the double bonds in its chains, polyisoprene exists as several stereoisomers with very different properties. The cis-1,4 isomer is natural rubber, the elastic material obtained from the latex of the rubber tree Hevea brasiliensis, while the trans-1,4 isomer is gutta-percha, a hard, inelastic material once widely used for golf-ball covers and undersea cable insulation.

Natural polyisoprene

In the rubber tree, polyisoprene is biosynthesised as long chains with a molecular weight often exceeding one million, nearly all in the cis configuration. Coagulated and dried latex yields natural rubber, whose elasticity arises from the coiling of the chains and their ability to crystallise under strain. Gutta-percha and the related balatá, obtained from other tropical trees, contain the trans isomer, which packs into a crystalline, rigid solid.

Synthetic polyisoprene

Synthetic polyisoprene ("IR") is manufactured by polymerising isoprene with Ziegler–Natta or lithium catalysts that favour the cis-1,4 structure, giving a product similar—though not identical—to natural rubber, with slightly inferior green strength and tack because it lacks the proteins and lipids of the natural material. Synthetic polyisoprene is used in tyres, footwear, adhesives, and medical goods such as gloves and catheters, where its purity and freedom from latex proteins are advantageous.
