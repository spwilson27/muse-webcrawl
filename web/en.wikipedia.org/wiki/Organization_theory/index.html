Organization theory

A classic organisational chart, one of the objects studied by organization theory.

Organization theory (also spelled organisation theory) is an interdisciplinary field of study of organisations, examining how they are structured, how they function, and how they interact with their environments. It draws on sociology, economics, political science, psychology and management studies, and is closely related to organisational behaviour and organisational studies.

Classical perspectives

Early organisation theory grew out of the work of Max Weber, who analysed bureaucracy as a rational-legal form of domination, and of management writers such as Frederick Winslow Taylor (scientific management) and Henri Fayol (administrative theory). These classical perspectives emphasised hierarchy, formal rules, division of labour and efficiency.

Modern and contemporary perspectives

The human relations movement, sparked by the Hawthorne studies of the 1920s and 1930s, shifted attention to informal groups, motivation and social needs. Later approaches include contingency theory, which holds that the best structure depends on the organisation's environment and technology; systems theory, which models organisations as open systems; institutional theory, which stresses legitimacy and imitation; and critical approaches examining power and inequality within organisations.

Key topics

Major topics include organisational structure, organisational culture, leadership, decision-making, change, networks, and the boundaries of the firm. Organisation theory informs both academic research and the practical design of companies, public agencies and non-profit organisations.
