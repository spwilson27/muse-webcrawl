Somerville College, Oxford

Somerville College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. It was founded in 1879 as Somerville Hall, one of the first women's colleges at Oxford, and was named after the Scottish scientist and writer Mary Somerville. Unlike the denominational Lady Margaret Hall, Somerville was founded as non-denominational. The college has admitted men since 1994.

History

Somerville was established by the Association for the Higher Education of Women to give women access to an Oxford education at a time when they could sit examinations but not receive degrees (women were first awarded Oxford degrees in 1920). The college moved to its present site on Woodstock Road, and its library, opened in 1904, was among the largest undergraduate libraries in the university — notable at a time when women were excluded from the Bodleian.

Notable alumnae and fellows

Somerville's alumnae include two prime ministers — Margaret Thatcher (chemistry) and Indira Gandhi (history, though she left without a degree) — as well as Nobel laureate Dorothy Hodgkin, who studied and later taught at the college, and writers such as Dorothy L. Sayers and Iris Murdoch, and the novelist and scholar Helen Gardner among its fellows.

College life

The college has around 400 undergraduates and 200 graduates. Its motto derives from Philippians: Donec rursus impleat orbem. Somerville is known for its liberal ethos, its gardens, and traditions such as its choir and the quad's plantings.
