Kate Carmack

Kate Carmack
Born: c. 1857, near Bennett Lake, Yukon
Died: 29 March 1920, Carcross, Yukon
Known for: role in the discovery of gold that started the Klondike Gold Rush

Kate Carmack (c. 1857 – 29 March 1920), born Shaaw Tlàa, was a Tagish First Nation woman whose family is credited with the discovery of gold in the Klondike in 1896, an event that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush. She was the wife (later common-law wife) of George Carmack, and travelled with him, her brother Skookum Jim (Keish), and her nephew Dawson Charlie (Tagish Charlie).

Life

Shaaw Tlàa was born near Bennett Lake into the Dakl'aweidí clan of the Tagish people. After her first husband died, she entered a relationship with prospector George Carmack in the late 1880s. In August 1896 the party was fishing at the mouth of the Klondike River when gold was found on Rabbit Creek (later renamed Bonanza Creek); family tradition holds that Kate and her brother made or shared in the discovery, though the claim was registered in George Carmack's name.

The Carmacks became wealthy, moved to California, and Kate was displayed to curious audiences as part of the gold-rush story. George later left her and bigamously married another woman. Kate returned to the Yukon and spent her later years in Carcross, where she died in the influenza epidemic of 1920. She was inducted into the Canadian Mining Hall of Fame in 2019.
