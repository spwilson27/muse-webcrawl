Scottish–Norwegian War
Date: 1262–1266
Location: Western Scotland, the Hebrides
Result: Treaty of Perth (1266)
Belligerents: Kingdom of Scotland; Kingdom of Norway

The Scottish–Norwegian War was a conflict fought between 1262 and 1266 between the kingdoms of Scotland and Norway over sovereignty of the Hebrides and the Isle of Man, which Norway claimed as part of its island realm (the Sudreyjar).

Background

Alexander III of Scotland sought to purchase or recover the western islands from Haakon IV of Norway. When negotiations failed, Scottish forces raided Skye in 1262, provoking a major Norwegian expedition the following year.

The campaign of 1263

Haakon sailed with a large fleet to the Clyde in the autumn of 1263. After inconclusive fighting, including the skirmish later mythologised as the Battle of Largs (2 October 1263), storms damaged the Norwegian fleet and Haakon withdrew to Orkney, where he died in December 1263.

Treaty of Perth

The war ended with the Treaty of Perth in 1266, by which Norway ceded the Hebrides and Man to Scotland in return for a payment of 4,000 marks and an annual rent. Orkney and Shetland remained Norwegian until the fifteenth century.
