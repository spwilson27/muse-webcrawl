Santa Maria Maggiore, Bergamo

LocationPiazza del Duomo, Bergamo, Italy
DenominationRoman Catholic
Founded1137
StyleRomanesque (exterior); Baroque (interior)

The Basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore is a major church in Bergamo, northern Italy, located in the Città Alta (upper town) on the Piazza del Duomo. According to tradition, it was founded in 1137 by the citizens of Bergamo in fulfilment of a vow made to the Virgin Mary to spare the city from a drought and plague then afflicting northern Italy.

The Protiro porch of Santa Maria Maggiore

Architecture

The basilica preserves its Romanesque Lombard exterior, though it has no conventional west facade — its entrances are on the north and south sides, flanked by porches (protiro) supported by marble lions, the northern porch built by Giovanni da Campione in 1353. The adjacent Colleoni Chapel, a Renaissance mausoleum for the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni, was added in the 1470s. Inside, by contrast, the church was transformed in the 16th and 17th centuries into a rich Baroque interior of stucco and gold.

Interior and treasures

The basilica is renowned for its cycle of Florentine and Flemish tapestries depicting the life of the Virgin, and above all for the wooden intarsia (inlaid) choir panels designed by Lorenzo Lotto in the 1520s, celebrated masterpieces of Renaissance marquetry with intricate biblical and allegorical imagery. The church also contains the tomb of the composer Gaetano Donizetti, a native of Bergamo, who died in the city in 1848.
