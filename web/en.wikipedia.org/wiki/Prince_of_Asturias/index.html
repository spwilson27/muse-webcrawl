Prince of Asturias

Arms of the heir to the Spanish throne as Prince of Asturias.

Prince of Asturias (Spanish: Príncipe de Asturias; feminine: Princesa de Asturias) is the principal substantive title held by the heir apparent to the Spanish throne. Under the Spanish Constitution of 1978, the title belongs to the Crown Prince or Crown Princess from birth or from the moment they become heir. The current holder is Leonor, Princess of Asturias, elder daughter of King Felipe VI.

History

The title was created in 1388 by John I of Castile for his son, the future Henry III, and his wife Catherine of Lancaster, in imitation of the English Prince of Wales and the French Dauphin, as part of the Treaty of Bayonne that ended a dynastic war. The principality of Asturias, the region from which the Reconquista began, gave the heir an income and jurisdiction of his own. Historically the title was sworn in the Cortes and accompanied by an oath by the Asturian representatives.

The Princess of Asturias Awards

The title lends its name to the Princess of Asturias Awards (formerly Prince of Asturias Awards, established 1981), among the most prestigious prizes in the Spanish-speaking world, presented each October in Oviedo in categories including the arts, sciences, letters, international cooperation, and sports.
