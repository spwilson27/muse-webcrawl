The First Battle of the Marne was a battle of the First World War fought from 6 to 12 September 1914. A counteroffensive by French and British forces checked the German advance toward Paris that had begun in August, ending Germany's hopes of a swift victory on the Western Front and setting the stage for four years of trench warfare.

Background

Germany's war plan, the Schlieffen Plan, called for a vast wheeling movement through Belgium and northern France to envelop Paris and destroy the French armies quickly. After the Battle of the Frontiers, Allied forces retreated toward the capital. As the German First Army under General Alexander von Kluck swung southeast of Paris in pursuit of the French Fifth Army, it exposed its flank to forces gathering around the capital.

Battle

The French commander-in-chief, General Joseph Joffre, ordered a general counteroffensive on 6 September. Around Paris, the newly formed Sixth Army under General Maunoury struck Kluck's flank along the Ourcq River; famously, some 6,000 reservists were rushed to the front in Parisian taxis and requisitioned vehicles — the "Taxis of the Marne." Kluck turned to meet this threat, opening a gap of some 30 miles (50 km) between his First Army and General Karl von Bülow's Second Army. The British Expeditionary Force and the French Fifth Army advanced into the gap, threatening the German armies with envelopment.

With his armies out of communication and facing the possibility of disaster, the German chief of staff Helmuth von Moltke ordered a general retreat on 9 September. German forces withdrew northward to the Aisne River, where they dug in — the beginning of the trench systems that would define the Western Front.

Aftermath

The battle cost roughly a quarter of a million casualties on each side. Its strategic significance was enormous: Germany's bid for a short war had failed, and the ensuing "Race to the Sea" produced a continuous line of trenches from the Swiss border to the North Sea. The Marne also saved Paris and is remembered in France as the "Miracle of the Marne."
