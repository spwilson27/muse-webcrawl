Naval War College

Location: Newport, Rhode Island, United States
Established: 1884
Type: Staff college / graduate institution
First president: Stephen B. Luce

The Naval War College (NWC) is the staff college and postgraduate research institution of the United States Navy, located at Naval Station Newport in Newport, Rhode Island. Founded in 1884, it is the oldest institution of its kind in continuous existence in the world and educates senior officers in strategy, warfighting, and national security decision-making.

History

The college was founded by Rear Admiral Stephen B. Luce, who became its first president and recruited Captain Alfred Thayer Mahan as a lecturer. Mahan's lectures at Newport became the basis for his epochal book The Influence of Sea Power upon History, 1660–1783 (1890). The college pioneered the use of war gaming to study future conflicts; its interwar games at Newport explored a possible war with Japan and are credited with shaping U.S. naval planning in the Pacific — Admiral Chester W. Nimitz later remarked that in the games nothing in the Pacific war surprised him except the kamikaze tactics.

Academics

The college comprises the College of Naval Warfare (for senior officers), the College of Naval Command and Staff (for mid-career officers), the College of Distance Education, and the Naval War College's graduate programme awards a Master of Arts in national security and strategic studies or in defense and strategic studies. Its Naval War College Press publishes the Naval War College Review. Students include officers of all U.S. services and civilians from government agencies, and the Naval Command College educates international officers from allied and partner navies.
