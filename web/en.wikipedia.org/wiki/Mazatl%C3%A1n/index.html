Mazatlán

Mazatlán is a city in the Mexican state of Sinaloa, on the Pacific coast near the southern tip of the Baja California peninsula. Nicknamed the "Pearl of the Pacific", it is Sinaloa's second-largest city and a major port, beach resort and centre of commercial shrimp fishing.

Geography

Mazatlán lies just south of the Tropic of Cancer and has a tropical savanna climate with hot, humid summers. Its shoreline is backed by hills known as the Cerro de la Nevería and Cerro del Vigía, and three islands — Isla de la Piedra, among them — sit just offshore.

History

The city's name derives from the Nahuatl word Mazatl-tlan, "place of the deer". Founded in the 16th century, it grew as a port for mining districts of the Sierra Madre and served as Sinaloa's capital from 1859 to 1873.

Economy and culture

Mazatlán hosts one of the world's largest Carnival celebrations, held each year before Lent. Tourism, along with the port, fishing fleet and brewing industry, drives the local economy. The historic centre, the Zona Dorada hotel strip and the long malecón (seawall promenade) are major attractions.
