Fusion power

Fusion power is a proposed form of energy generation that would produce electricity by using heat from nuclear fusion reactions — light atomic nuclei, typically deuterium and tritium, combining to form helium and releasing energy, as in the core of the Sun and stars.


Main approaches: Magnetic confinement (tokamak, stellarator), inertial confinement
Key projects: ITER, JET, NIF

Principles

Fusion requires temperatures of tens of millions of degrees, at which fuel becomes plasma that must be confined long enough, at sufficient density, for net energy gain — the Lawson criterion. Deuterium–tritium fusion is the easiest reaction but produces energetic neutrons that activate reactor materials.

Research history

Building on stellar nucleosynthesis work by Fred Hoyle and others, fusion research began in the 1950s. The tokamak design became dominant; the international ITER reactor in France aims to produce 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of heating. In December 2022 the US National Ignition Facility achieved scientific breakeven in an inertial-confinement shot. Commercial fusion remains an open engineering challenge pursued by public programmes and private startups.
