Diablo wind

The Diablo wind is a name for a hot, dry wind that blows from the northeast over the San Francisco Bay Area of California during the spring and autumn. Similar in nature to the Santa Ana winds of Southern California, Diablo winds arise when high pressure over the Great Basin drives air westward toward the coast; as the air descends from the interior valleys and passes through gaps in the coastal ranges, it compresses, warms and dries.

Fire risk

Diablo wind events are strongly associated with California's most destructive wildfires in the Bay Area. Strong gusts combined with very low humidity can rapidly spread fires; the 1991 Oakland firestorm, which killed 25 people and destroyed thousands of homes, occurred during a Diablo wind event. Utilities such as Pacific Gas and Electric have implemented public safety power shutoffs during forecast Diablo wind conditions to reduce ignition risk from power lines, as occurred before the 2019 Kincade Fire.

Name

The name is thought to derive from the wind's direction, blowing toward the coast from the direction of Mount Diablo and the Diablo Valley in the East Bay. Its use became widespread in media coverage during the 2017 North Bay fires and subsequent fire seasons.
