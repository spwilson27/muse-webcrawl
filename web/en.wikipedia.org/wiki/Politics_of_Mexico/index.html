Politics of Mexico

The politics of Mexico take place in the framework of a federal presidential representative democratic republic whose government is structured according to the Constitution of 1917. The President of Mexico is both head of state and head of government, and the system is formally multi-party. Mexico is divided into 31 states and Mexico City, each with its own constitution and elected governor.

History

For most of the twentieth century Mexico was dominated by a single party: the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) and its predecessors held the presidency continuously from 1929 to 2000 — a system the writer Mario Vargas Llosa called "the perfect dictatorship". Electoral reforms in the 1990s created an autonomous electoral authority (IFE, now INE). In 2000 Vicente Fox of the National Action Party (PAN) won the presidency, ending 71 years of PRI rule. The PRI returned to power in 2012 under Enrique Peña Nieto.

Contemporary era

In 2018 Andrés Manuel López Obrador, founder of the National Regeneration Movement (MORENA), won a landslide victory on an anti-corruption platform he called the "Fourth Transformation". In 2024 his chosen successor, Claudia Sheinbaum, was elected Mexico's first female president, and MORENA and its allies won large congressional majorities, which they used in 2024 to pass a constitutional reform providing for the popular election of judges — a measure debated intensely at home and abroad.

Institutions

The Congress of the Union consists of a 128-member Senate and a 500-member Chamber of Deputies. The judiciary is headed by the Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation. A strict principle of no re-election (sufragio efectivo, no reelección), a legacy of the Mexican Revolution, originally applied to all offices; since 2014 legislators and mayors may serve consecutive terms, but the president remains limited to a single six-year term.

Issues

Corruption, organised-crime violence and the militarisation of public security, inequality, and relations with the United States (trade, migration and drug trafficking) dominate the political agenda.
