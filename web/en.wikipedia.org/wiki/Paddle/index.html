Paddle

A paddle is a handheld tool with an elongated shaft and a flat blade, used to propel a watercraft by pushing against water. Unlike an oar, a paddle is held with two hands and is not supported by the craft; the paddler's hands provide both the fulcrum and the force. Paddles commonly consist of a shaft with a broad, flat blade at one or both ends.

Types

Single-bladed paddles are used for canoeing, standup paddleboarding, dragon boat racing, and outrigger canoeing, with the paddler typically stroking on one side or alternating sides. Double-bladed paddles, with a blade at each end of the shaft, are used for kayaking; the paddler alternates strokes on each side without switching hands.

Materials and construction

Traditional paddles were carved from a single piece of wood, such as maple, ash, or cherry. Modern paddles are made from aluminium and plastic for inexpensive models, and from fibreglass or carbon fibre composites for lightweight, high-performance designs. Blades may be feathered—rotated at an angle relative to one another on double-bladed paddles—to reduce wind resistance during the recovery phase of the stroke.

Use

The paddling stroke consists of a catch (blade entering the water), a power phase (pulling the blade through the water to move the craft forward), and a recovery. Specialized strokes, such as the J-stroke in canoeing, correct the tendency of a single-bladed paddle to turn the boat.

The word "paddle" also denotes similar implements in other contexts, such as the flat bats used in paddle-ball sports (pickleball, padel, table tennis in some usages), kitchen mixing attachments, and the flat boards historically used for corporal punishment in schools.
