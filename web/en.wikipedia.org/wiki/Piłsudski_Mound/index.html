Piłsudski's Mound (Polish: Kopiec Piłsudskiego), also called the Independence Mound or Freedom Mound, is an artificial mound in the Wolski Forest in Kraków, Poland. At 36 metres high, with its summit about 383 metres above sea level, it is the largest of the several historic mounds surrounding the city.

History

Construction began in 1934, on the twentieth anniversary of the march of the Polish Legions, to honour Marshal Józef Piłsudski, leader of Poland's independence movement and head of state. Tens of thousands of volunteers, including veterans and schoolchildren, carried earth to the site; soil was brought from battlefields and places significant to Polish history. After Piłsudski's death in 1935 the mound was named for him, and it was completed in 1937.

During the German occupation and later under the communist government, the mound was neglected and its commemoration suppressed; an attempt was even made to level it, but its size defeated the effort. It was restored after 1980 and again in the 1990s. In 1997 torrential rain caused severe slumping, and a long restoration followed, incorporating earth from still more historic sites.

The Kraków mounds

Kraków's mound tradition includes the prehistoric Krakus Mound and Wanda Mound, the Kościuszko Mound (1820–1823), Piłsudski's Mound, and the John Paul II Mound of 1997. From Piłsudski's summit, on clear days, the Tatra Mountains are visible.
