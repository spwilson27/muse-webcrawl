Red panda

The red panda (Ailurus fulgens), also known as the lesser panda, is a small mammal native to the eastern Himalayas and southwestern China. It has dense reddish-brown fur with a black underside, a white face with tear markings, and a long, bushy, ringed tail. Slightly larger than a domestic cat, it is the only living species of the family Ailuridae.

Diet and behaviour

Red pandas are arboreal and primarily herbivorous, feeding mainly on bamboo leaves and shoots, supplemented with berries, blossoms, eggs and small animals. A bony extension of the wrist acts as a false thumb for grasping bamboo. They are solitary and mainly active at dawn and dusk.

Conservation

The red panda is listed as endangered on the IUCN Red List; the wild population is estimated at fewer than 10,000 mature individuals and continues to decline owing to habitat loss and fragmentation, poaching and inbreeding. It is protected throughout its range, which includes Nepal, India, Bhutan, Myanmar and China.
