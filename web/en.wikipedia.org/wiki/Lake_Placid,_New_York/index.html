Lake Placid, New York

Lake Placid is a village in the Adirondack Mountains in Essex County, New York, United States. As of the 2020 census its population was 2,205. The village, named after the nearby lake of the same name, is famous as the host of the 1932 and 1980 Winter Olympic Games, and for the 1980 "Miracle on Ice" hockey game. It lies within the town of North Elba.

History

The area was settled in the early 19th century, initially in connection with iron mining. In the late 19th century it became a fashionable resort for wealthy families, and Melvil Dewey founded the Lake Placid Club in 1895, which popularised winter sports in the United States. The village was incorporated in 1900.

Winter Olympics

Lake Placid hosted the 1932 Winter Olympics and the 1980 Winter Olympics, and is one of only a handful of places to have hosted the Winter Games twice. The 1980 Games are remembered for the United States men's ice hockey team's upset victory over the Soviet Union — the "Miracle on Ice" — at the Olympic Center arena, and for Eric Heiden's five gold medals in speed skating. Olympic venues, including the ski jumps, the bobsleigh and luge track at Mount Van Hoevenberg, and Whiteface Mountain ski area, remain in use for training and World Cup competition.

Geography and tourism

The village sits beside Mirror Lake; the larger Lake Placid lies just to the north. Surrounded by the High Peaks of the Adirondacks, the village is a year-round resort centre for skiing, hiking, boating and Ironman triathlon competition, and hosts the annual Ironman Lake Placid, one of the longest-running Ironman events in North America.
