Castro District, San Francisco

The Castro District, commonly called the Castro, is a neighbourhood in Eureka Valley, San Francisco, California, centred on Castro Street between Market Street and 19th Street. It is one of the first gay neighbourhoods in the United States and remains a symbol of LGBTQ activism.

History

Formerly a working-class Irish and Scandinavian district, the Castro transformed in the 1960s–70s as gay men moved in, opening businesses around the Castro Theatre (1922). Harvey Milk, whose camera shop stood at 575 Castro, became the first openly gay man elected to public office in California in 1977. The district was a centre of the response to the AIDS crisis and hosts the giant Rainbow Flag at Harvey Milk Plaza.
