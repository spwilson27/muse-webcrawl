Wat Phra Kaew (Thai: วัดพระแก้ว), commonly known in English as the Temple of the Emerald Buddha and officially as Wat Phra Si Rattana Satsadaram, is regarded as the most sacred Buddhist temple in Thailand. The complex consists of a number of buildings within the precincts of the Grand Palace in the historical centre of Bangkok.

The temple's most important feature is the Emerald Buddha, a highly revered Buddha image carved from a single block of jade. It is protected from public eyes by elaborate vestments, which are changed three times a year by the King of Thailand in accordance with the seasons.

History

Construction of the temple began in 1783, when Rama I, founder of the Chakri dynasty, moved the capital of Siam from Thonburi to Bangkok. Unlike most previous royal temples, Wat Phra Kaew was built with a strictly religious purpose: it contains no living quarters for monks, but is richly decorated with buildings, holy images and statues representing scenes from the Ramayana and Hindu mythology.

The Emerald Buddha had a long and legendary history before arriving in Bangkok. According to tradition it was first revealed at Wat Pa Yia in Chiang Rai in 1434, and it was later kept at temples in Lampang, Chiang Mai, Vientiane and Thonburi before being enshrined in its present home.

Architecture

The temple complex is surrounded by a long gallery wall whose inner surface is painted with murals depicting the entire Ramakien, the Thai version of the Ramayana. Guardian figures, including the giant yaksha statues, watch over the entrances. The principal buildings include the ubosot (ordination hall) housing the Emerald Buddha, the Phra Mondop library, and the Royal Pantheon.
