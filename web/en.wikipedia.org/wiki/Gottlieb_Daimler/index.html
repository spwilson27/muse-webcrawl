Gottlieb Daimler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Daimler motorised carriage of 1886, an early four-wheeled automobile, museum photograph]
Daimler's 1886 motor carriage.

Gottlieb Wilhelm Daimler (1834–1900) was a German engineer and industrial designer who, with Wilhelm Maybach, developed the high-speed petrol engine and one of the first practical automobiles. Their 1885 \'riding car\' (Reitwagen) was the first motorcycle, and the 1886 motorised carriage the first four-wheeled automobile powered by an internal combustion engine. The company he founded, Daimler-Motoren-Gesellschaft, merged with Benz & Cie. in 1926 to form Daimler-Benz, maker of Mercedes-Benz cars.

Early life and engines

Trained as a gunsmith and engineer, Daimler worked across Europe (including at the Karlsruhe Maschinenbau-Gesellschaft) and managed Nikolaus Otto\'s Deutz engine works, where he and Maybach quarrelled with Otto and left in 1882 to develop a small, fast petrol engine in a Cannstatt greenhouse workshop.

The first vehicles

Their 1885 Grandfather-Clock engine powered the Reitwagen motorcycle and, in 1886, a converted stagecoach — in the same year Karl Benz patented his three-wheeler across the Rhine (the two pioneers never met). Daimler engines powered boats, trams and France\'s early Panhard and Peugeot cars, igniting the French car industry.

Legacy

Daimler died in 1900; the Mercedes name (from Emil Jellinek\'s daughter, applied to the landmark 1901 Mercedes 35 hp) defined the modern car layout. His company\'s motto is traditionally rendered \'the best or nothing\'. Licensing spread his name to Britain\'s separate Daimler Company — an enduring source of confusion.
