Lydia was an Iron Age kingdom of western Asia Minor, located in the modern Turkish provinces of Uşak, Manisa and İzmir. Its capital was Sardis. The Lydian language was an Indo-European language of the Anatolian family.

History

Lydia emerged after the fall of the Hittite Empire, initially as the kingdom of the Maeonians. Under the Mermnad dynasty, founded by Gyges in the 7th century BC, Lydia expanded to dominate most of western Anatolia. The Lydians are credited by ancient sources such as Herodotus with the invention of coined money: the first coins, made of electrum, a natural alloy of gold and silver from the River Pactolus, were struck in Lydia in the 7th century BC.

The kingdom reached its greatest wealth and power under Croesus (reigned c. 560–546 BC), whose riches became proverbial ("as rich as Croesus"). Croesus was defeated by Cyrus the Great of Persia at the Battle of Thymbra in 546 BC, and Lydia was incorporated into the Achaemenid Empire as a satrapy. It later passed to the successors of Alexander the Great, the Roman Empire and Byzantium.
