The M110 Semi-Automatic Sniper System (M110 SASS) is an American semi-automatic precision rifle chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge. It is manufactured by Knight's Armament Company and was developed to give United States Army snipers a semi-automatic rifle with greater rapid-fire capability than bolt-action systems.

History

The M110 was selected following the U.S. Army's Semi-Automatic Sniper System competition. It is derived from the Knight's Armament SR-25, itself based on the AR-10 design of Eugene Stoner. The system was fielded beginning in 2008 and saw service in the Iraq and Afghanistan wars.

The complete system includes the rifle, a day optic sight, a hard carrying case, cleaning and maintenance tools, bipod, and a quick-detachable sound suppressor.

Design

The M110 uses a direct impingement gas system with a rotating bolt. Its free-floating barrel and match-grade trigger are intended to deliver accuracy comparable to bolt-action rifles while allowing faster follow-up shots. The rifle accepts the M118LR long-range cartridge.

Replacement

The United States Army selected the Heckler & Koch G28 variant, designated the M110A1 Compact Semi-Automatic Sniper System, as a successor for many roles, though the original M110 remains in service.
