Prefix

A prefix is an affix which is placed before the stem of a word. Adding it to the beginning of one word changes it into another word. For example, when the prefix un- is added to the word happy, it creates the word unhappy. Particularly in the study of languages, a prefix is also called a preformative, because it alters the form of the words to which it is affixed.

Prefixes, like other affixes, can be either inflectional, creating a new form of a word with the same basic meaning, or derivational, creating a new word with a new semantic meaning and sometimes a different lexical category. Prefixes in English are all derivational.

Other languages

Prefixes are common across Indo-European languages and many others. In German, several separable prefixes change their position depending on sentence structure. In Bantu languages such as Swahili, prefixes play a grammatical role in noun classes and verb agreement.

Number prefixes

English also uses number prefixes derived from Greek and Latin, such as mono- (one), bi- (two), tri- (three) and poly- (many), which form the basis of the SI prefix system used in science.
