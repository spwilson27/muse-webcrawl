Port Hueneme (pronounced "wy-NEE-mee") is a small beach city in Ventura County, on the south coast of California. The name derives from the Spanish spelling of the Ventureño Chumash name Wene Me, meaning "Resting Place". The population was about 22,000 at the 2020 census.

The city is home to the Port of Hueneme, the only deep-water port between Los Angeles and the San Francisco Bay Area, a major importer of automobiles and exporter of agricultural produce, especially citrus and other goods from the Oxnard Plain. Adjacent to the port is Naval Base Ventura County, Port Hueneme division, headquarters of the Naval Construction Battalions — the Seabees — whose museum is located in the city.

History

The area was inhabited by the Chumash people for centuries before Spanish colonisation. The town site was laid out in the 1870s after Thomas Bard built a wharf to ship grain from the surrounding farms; the wharf and later the deep-water port, constructed in the late 1930s and taken over by the U.S. Navy during the Second World War, shaped the city's development. Port Hueneme was incorporated in 1948.

Geography

Port Hueneme lies on the Oxnard Plain at the edge of the Santa Barbara Channel, bordered by Oxnard to the east and the Pacific Ocean to the south and west. Its beach, Hueneme Beach Park, has a historic pier used for fishing and recreation.
