Laplacian matrix

In the mathematical field of graph theory, the Laplacian matrix, also called the graph Laplacian, admittance matrix, Kirchhoff matrix or discrete Laplacian, is a matrix representation of a graph. For a graph with n vertices it is defined as L = D − A, where D is the degree matrix (a diagonal matrix of vertex degrees) and A is the adjacency matrix. Thus each diagonal entry equals the degree of its vertex and each off-diagonal entry is −1 if the two vertices are adjacent and 0 otherwise (with weights replacing the 1s for weighted graphs).

Properties and applications

The Laplacian is symmetric and positive semidefinite; its smallest eigenvalue is 0, and the multiplicity of the eigenvalue 0 equals the number of connected components of the graph. The second-smallest eigenvalue — the algebraic connectivity or Fiedler value — measures how well connected the graph is. Kirchhoff's Matrix Tree theorem states that any cofactor of the Laplacian equals the number of spanning trees of the graph.

Graph Laplacians underpin spectral graph theory and have wide applications: random walks and diffusion on graphs, spectral clustering and graph partitioning, electrical network analysis, mesh processing, and graph signal processing and graph neural networks. Variants include the symmetric normalised Laplacian and the random-walk normalised Laplacian, widely used in machine learning.
