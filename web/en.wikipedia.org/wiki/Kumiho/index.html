A kumiho (Korean: 구미호; Hanja: 九尾狐; lit. "nine-tailed fox") is a creature that appears in the folktales and legends of Korea. It is a fox that has lived for a thousand years and thereby gained magical powers, including the ability to transform into a beautiful woman. Unlike the largely benevolent or ambiguous fox spirits of China (huli jing) and Japan (kitsune), the Korean kumiho is almost invariably portrayed as evil.

Characteristics

According to legend, a fox that lives a thousand years becomes a kumiho. The creature can freely transform among its true fox form, an intermediate form, and a human — most often a beautiful young woman who seduces men in order to eat their hearts or livers, the organ believed to hold human energy. A recurring detail is the yeou guseul (여우구슬, "fox bead" or "fox marble"), a magical orb said to contain the kumiho's knowledge and power, which it may pass mouth-to-mouth to a human, granting wisdom or supernatural abilities.

Becoming human

Many tales revolve around a kumiho's wish to become fully human. The conditions vary by story: it may have to abstain from eating people for a hundred or a thousand days, or keep its true nature secret from a husband for a set period. In one famous tale, a kumiho who married a man was discovered when he broke his promise and followed her at night; in another, a kumiho is revealed by a hunting dog, since animals can sense its true form. If the conditions are fulfilled, the kumiho loses its fox nature and becomes a real human; if it fails at the last moment, it remains a fox forever.

In popular culture

The kumiho is a staple of Korean television and film, including the horror anthology series Hometown of Legends, the romantic comedy My Girlfriend Is a Gumiho (2010), and the fantasy drama Tale of the Nine Tailed (2020), which recast the creature as a male protagonist. It also appears in games and webtoons, and the nine-tailed fox form is a recurring motif in K-pop imagery.
