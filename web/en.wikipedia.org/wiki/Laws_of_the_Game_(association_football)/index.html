The Laws of the Game are the codified rules of association football. The laws mention the number of players, the length of the match, the size of the field and ball, the type and nature of fouls, and many other rules that define the sport. The laws are maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB), which meets annually to debate possible changes.

History

The first Laws of the Game were drawn up in 1863 by Ebenezer Cobb Morrell and the newly formed Football Association in London, heavily influenced by earlier public-school and Cambridge rules. The IFAB was founded in 1886 by the four British associations, and FIFA joined in 1913. The laws were comprehensively rewritten in 1938 by Stanley Rous, and again reorganised in 1997.

Amendments and application

Changes to the laws require a three-quarters majority of the IFAB, in which FIFA holds four of the eight votes and each British association one. National associations may modify certain laws (such as pitch dimensions and match duration) for youth, veterans', disability and grassroots football. Notable recent changes include the introduction of video assistant referees (VAR) in 2018 and the temporary, later permanent, expansion of permitted substitutions to five.
