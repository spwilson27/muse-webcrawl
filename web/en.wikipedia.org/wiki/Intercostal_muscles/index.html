Intercostal muscles

Intercostal muscles
Origin: Ribs
Insertion: Ribs below
Nerve: Intercostal nerves
Action: Elevate or depress the ribs in breathing

The intercostal muscles are several groups of muscles that run between the ribs and help form and move the chest wall. They are mainly involved in the mechanical aspect of breathing.

The external intercostal muscles, on the outer surface of the rib cage, elevate the ribs during inhalation, while the internal intercostal muscles assist forced exhalation by depressing the ribs. A third, innermost layer is also recognized. The intercostal spaces also contain the intercostal nerves and blood vessels.
