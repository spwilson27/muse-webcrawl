Philosophy of action

The philosophy of action is the branch of philosophy that studies action: what distinguishes intentional actions from mere bodily movements, how actions are explained by reasons, beliefs, and desires, and what it is to act freely. It overlaps with philosophy of mind and ethics, including debates over free will.

Central questions

Key problems include the nature of intention, weakness of will (akrasia), and whether reasons can be causes of action. Elizabeth Anscombe's Intention (1957) revived the field; Donald Davidson argued that rationalizing explanations are causal explanations, against Wittgensteinian accounts.

Agency

Theories of agency examine how an agent — rather than events happening in them — brings about action, with applications to moral responsibility, law, and artificial intelligence.
