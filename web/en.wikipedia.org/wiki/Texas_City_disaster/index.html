The Texas City disaster was an industrial accident that occurred on April 16, 1947, in the Port of Texas City, Texas, United States. It was the deadliest industrial accident in US history and one of history's largest non-nuclear explosions. The explosion was triggered by a fire aboard the French-registered vessel SS Grandcamp, which detonated her cargo of about 2,300 tons of ammonium nitrate.

The burning port after the initial explosion

Events

The fire aboard the Grandcamp was noticed shortly after 8 a.m. Attempts to smother it by closing the hatches and injecting steam failed, and at approximately 9:12 a.m. the ship exploded. The blast levelled nearly 1,000 buildings, ignited oil storage tanks and refineries along the waterfront, and hurled the ship's anchor some two miles inland. A second ship, the SS High Flyer, also loaded with ammonium nitrate, caught fire and exploded the following day.

Casualties and damage

At least 581 people were killed, including all but one member of the Texas City volunteer fire department, and more than 5,000 were injured. Hundreds of homes were destroyed or damaged, and property losses were estimated at more than $100 million — over a billion dollars in modern terms.

Aftermath

The disaster led to the first-ever class action lawsuit against the United States government, on behalf of 848 plaintiffs, under the newly enacted Federal Tort Claims Act. Although an initial judgment for the plaintiffs was overturned on appeal, the case prompted changes in the handling, labelling and transport of ammonium nitrate and other hazardous cargoes.
