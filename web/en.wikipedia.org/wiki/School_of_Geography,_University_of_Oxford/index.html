School of Geography and the Environment
Location: Oxford, England
Parent institution: University of Oxford
Founded: 1899 (geography at Oxford)

The School of Geography and the Environment at the University of Oxford is one of the leading centres for geographical and environmental research and teaching in the United Kingdom. Geography has been taught at Oxford since 1887, when Halford Mackinder was appointed Reader in Geography; the School traces its origins to the establishment of a dedicated geography programme and diploma in 1899, with Mackinder as its first director.

Research

The School hosts several research centres and units, including the Environmental Change Institute (founded 1991), the Transport Studies Unit, and the Smith School of Enterprise and the Environment. Research spans climate science, biodiversity, water, food systems, urban geography, and political ecology. The School was ranked first in the world for geography in several editions of the QS World University Rankings.

Teaching

The School offers an undergraduate BA in Geography and a range of master's programmes, including the MSc in Environmental Change and Management and the MSc in Nature, Society and Environmental Governance, together with a large doctoral programme.
