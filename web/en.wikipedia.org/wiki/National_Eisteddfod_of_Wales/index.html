The National Eisteddfod of Wales (Welsh: Eisteddfod Genedlaethol Cymru) is the largest of several eisteddfodau held annually in Wales, and is regarded as the largest cultural festival of its kind in Europe. Held during the first week of August, it alternates between locations in north and south Wales, attracting around 150,000 visitors and over 6,000 competitors. It is a celebration of Welsh language and culture, with competitions in music, dance, literature, drama, and visual arts.

History

The eisteddfod tradition is traced to a gathering of bards held by Lord Rhys at Cardigan Castle in 1176. Competitive gatherings were revived in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, and the first modern National Eisteddfod was held at Aberdare in 1861. The Gorsedd of the Bards, the ceremonial order founded by Iolo Morganwg in 1792, became closely associated with the festival, conducting druidic-style ceremonies in a circle of standing stones erected on the festival field.

Principal ceremonies and prizes

  • The Chairing of the Bard — the Chair (Y Gadair) is awarded for a poem in strict metre (awdl); the winning poet is chaired in an elaborate ceremony on the Friday of the festival.
  • The Crowning of the Bard — the Crown (Y Goron) is awarded for a poem in free metre (pryddest).
  • The Prose Medal (Y Fedal Ryddiaith) — awarded for a volume of prose.
  • Welsh Learner of the Year (Dysgwr y Flwyddyn) — a prominent competition celebrating adult learners of Welsh.

The festival's famous "Welsh-only rule" requires all competitions and main-stage proceedings to be conducted in Welsh, a policy that has made the Eisteddfod a cornerstone institution of the Welsh-speaking community.

The Maes

The festival site, known as the Maes, includes a large central pavilion for competitions and ceremonies, hundreds of stalls run by Welsh organisations and traders, and satellite venues such as the Maes D for learners, the literary pavilion, and the science and technology pavilion. Caravan and camping fields accommodate thousands of visitors who spend the full week at the festival.
