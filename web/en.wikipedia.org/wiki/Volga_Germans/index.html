Volga Germans

The Volga Germans (German: Wolgadeutsche) are an ethnic German group that settled along the lower Volga River in Russia. Responding to manifestos issued by Catherine the Great in 1762–1763 offering land, tax exemptions, and religious freedom, tens of thousands of Germans emigrated and founded over a hundred colonies near Saratov.

History

The colonies prospered as farming communities while retaining German language, churches, and customs. From the 1870s, as privileges were withdrawn and military conscription imposed, many emigrated to North and South America, settling the Great Plains and the Argentine pampas. After the Russian Revolution, the Volga German Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic was established in 1924 with its capital at Engels.

Deportation and aftermath

Following the German invasion of the USSR in 1941, Stalin ordered the deportation of the Volga Germans to Siberia and Kazakhstan; the ASSR was abolished and hundreds of thousands were confined to labour camps. Survivors were barred from returning home until decades later. After the collapse of the Soviet Union, large numbers emigrated to Germany as Aussiedler. Today descendant communities and cultural organizations in Russia, Germany, the United States, Canada, and Argentina preserve the heritage.
