America West Airlines

America West Airlines was a major United States airline headquartered in Tempe, Arizona. Founded in 1981 in the wake of airline deregulation, it began flight operations in 1983 from hubs at Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport and later Las Vegas McCarran International Airport. In 2005 the airline's holding company acquired US Airways out of bankruptcy; the combined carrier adopted the US Airways name, and the America West brand was retired in 2007.

History

America West was founded by Edward R. Beauvais and partners as a low-fare carrier on the model of Southwest and People Express. It expanded rapidly during the 1980s, becoming the first post-deregulation start-up to grow into a major airline, but overexpansion and the 1990–91 industry downturn forced it into Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 1991. Under chief executive W. Douglas Parker, who joined in 1995, the airline returned to profitability and became known for low costs and a cross-utilised workforce.

Merger with US Airways

In September 2005 America West Holdings completed its acquisition of US Airways Group, which was in its second bankruptcy. Because US Airways had the stronger international brand, the merged airline kept the US Airways name, while the America West operating certificate survived within it — even the "Cactus" call sign was retained. The combined US Airways later merged with American Airlines in 2013.

Fleet and network

At its peak the airline operated Boeing 737s, 757s and Airbus A319/A320/A321 narrow-bodies on a network concentrated in the western United States, with transcontinental and Hawaii services.
