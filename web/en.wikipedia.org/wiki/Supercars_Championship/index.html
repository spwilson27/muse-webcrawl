Supercars Championship

The Supercars Championship (known commercially as the Repco Supercars Championship) is a touring car racing category based in Australia and run as an International Series under FIA Super Touring rules. It is the country's premier motorsport series, famous for the Ford versus Holden (later General Motors) rivalry and for marquee events including the Bathurst 1000 at Mount Panorama.

History

The series descends from the Australian Touring Car Championship, first held in 1960. In 1993 the championship adopted the Group 3A formula for five-litre V8 Ford Falcons and Holden Commodores — the V8 Supercars era — which built a mass audience around the two locally built sedans. The category rebranded as the Supercars Championship in 2016 and admitted new body shapes under "Gen2" rules in 2018, when the turbocharged four- and six-cylinder engines were permitted alongside V8s.

Gen3 era

From 2023 the "Gen3" regulations introduced the Chevrolet Camaro ZL1 and Ford Mustang GT as the rival models, with less downforce, more road-car relevance, and control components to cut costs following Holden's closure in 2020. The calendar includes street circuits such as Adelaide and Surfers Paradise, permanent tracks like Phillip Island, and a round in New Zealand. Triple Eight Race Engineering has been the dominant team of the modern era; champion drivers include Jamie Whincup (seven titles), Scott McLaughlin, Shane van Gisbergen, and Broc Feeney. The Bathurst 1000, held each October, remains the series' flagship race and one of Australia's largest sporting events.

Support categories

The Dunlop Super2 Series serves as the development ladder, alongside SuperUtes and other support classes. Supercars also sanctions the Bathurst 12 Hour through affiliated event management.
