Eni S.p.A. is an Italian multinational energy company headquartered in Rome. Founded in 1953 as the state-owned Ente Nazionale Idrocarburi under Enrico Mattei, it is one of the world's largest integrated oil and gas companies, active in exploration, production, refining, chemicals, and increasingly renewable energy.

History

Eni was created to give Italy an independent energy champion, consolidating the earlier Agip (founded 1926). Under Mattei it challenged the dominance of the "Seven Sisters" oil majors by offering producer countries more favourable partnership terms, notably in Iran and Libya. Mattei died in a plane crash in 1962 in circumstances that were later investigated as sabotage. Eni was partially privatised in the 1990s, though the Italian state retains a significant stake.

Operations

Eni operates in dozens of countries, with major upstream positions in North and West Africa, Kazakhstan, and the Eastern Mediterranean (including the giant Zohr gas field offshore Egypt, discovered in 2015). Through its Plenitude subsidiary it is expanding in renewables and retail energy services.
