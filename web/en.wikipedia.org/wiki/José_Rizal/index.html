José Rizal

José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda (June 19, 1861 – December 30, 1896) was a Filipino nationalist, writer and polymath active at the end of the Spanish colonial period of the Philippines. He is considered one of the national heroes (pambansang bayani) of the Philippines. An ophthalmologist by profession, Rizal became a writer and a key member of the Filipino Propaganda Movement, which advocateded political reforms for the colony under Spain.

Early life and education

Rizal was born in Calamba, Laguna, the seventh of eleven children of Francisco Rizal Mercado and Teodora Alonso Realonda. He studied at the Ateneo Municipal de Manila and the University of Santo Tomas before travelling to Spain, where he completed a medical degree at the Universidad Central de Madrid and further specialised in ophthalmology in Paris and Heidelberg.

Writings

His two novels, Noli Me Tangere (1887) and El Filibusterismo (1891), exposed the abuses of Spanish friars and colonial officials and awakened Filipino national consciousness. He also wrote essays for the newspaper La Solidaridad and annotated Antonio de Morga's Sucesos de las Islas Filipinas.

Exile and execution

After founding the civic league La Liga Filipina in 1892, Rizal was exiled to Dapitan in Mindanao. Arrested following the outbreak of the Philippine Revolution, he was convicted of rebellion, sedition and conspiracy, and executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park) in Manila on December 30, 1896. His poem "Mi último adiós" ("My Last Farewell"), written on the eve of his death, became a classic of Philippine literature.

Legacy

Rizal is commemorated by Rizal Day (December 30), monuments throughout the Philippines — most famously the Rizal Monument in Luneta — and courses on his life and works mandated in Philippine schools.
