Ethnoreligious group

An ethnoreligious group is an ethnic group whose members are also unified by a common religious background or identity. In such groups, ethnic and religious identity are closely intertwined, often through a shared history, endogamy, and traditions in which religious practice functions as a marker of ethnic belonging.

Characteristics

Ethnoreligious communities typically descend from a population in which a particular religion became embedded over centuries, so that joining or leaving the religion is often treated socially as joining or leaving the people. Conversion may be possible but is frequently rare or regulated, and secular members may still identify with the group culturally.

Examples

Commonly cited examples include Jews, Sikhs, Druze, Copts, Assyrians, Armenians, Mennonites and other Anabaptist communities, Parsis, and the Yazidis. In some legal systems, ethnoreligious identity affects personal-status law, such as marriage and inheritance rules administered by religious courts in parts of the Middle East.
