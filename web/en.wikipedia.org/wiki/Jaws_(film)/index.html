Jaws (film)

The iconic theatrical poster art for Jaws

Jaws is a 1975 American thriller film directed by Steven Spielberg, based on the 1974 novel by Peter Benchley. It tells the story of a great white shark that terrorizes a New England beach town, and the police chief, marine biologist, and professional shark hunter who set out to kill it. Starring Roy Scheider, Robert Shaw, and Richard Dreyfuss, the film is widely credited with inventing the summer blockbuster.

Plot

After a young swimmer is killed by a shark in the resort town of Amity Island, police chief Martin Brody urges the mayor to close the beaches; he refuses for fear of losing tourist revenue. When more attacks occur, Brody joins oceanographer Matt Hooper and shark hunter Quint aboard Quint's boat, the Orca, in a climactic hunt in which Quint is killed before Brody destroys the shark.

Production

The mechanical sharks — nicknamed "Bruce" — malfunctioned constantly in salt water, forcing Spielberg to suggest the shark's presence through point-of-view shots, floating barrels, and John Williams' ominous two-note score, which won an Academy Award. Shooting on Martha's Vineyard ran far over schedule and budget, but the difficulties are credited with shaping the film's suspenseful style.

Reception and legacy

Jaws was the highest-grossing film of its time, earning over $470 million worldwide, and won three Academy Awards. Its wide saturation release backed by television advertising established the modern blockbuster model. It spawned three sequels and a lasting cultural effect on public perceptions of sharks; the American Film Institute ranks it among the greatest American films, and it was selected for the National Film Registry in 2001.
