Gaston Louis Alfred Leroux (1868–1927) was a French novelist and journalist best known for The Phantom of the Opera (1910). Trained as a lawyer, he became a travelling reporter for Paris newspapers, covering trials, revolutions and wars, before turning to fiction.

Works

His detective novel The Mystery of the Yellow Room (1907), introducing the young reporter-detective Joseph Rouletabille, is a classic of the locked-room mystery genre, praised by John Dickson Carr as among the finest ever written. Other works include The Perfume of the Lady in Black, Balaoo, and Chéri-Bibi, about a convict-hero. The Phantom of the Opera, serialized in 1909–1910, was only a modest success in Leroux's lifetime but achieved worldwide fame through the 1925 film with Lon Chaney and Andrew Lloyd Webber's 1986 stage musical, the longest-running show in Broadway history.
