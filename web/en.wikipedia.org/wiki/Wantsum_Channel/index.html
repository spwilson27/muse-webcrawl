Wantsum Channel

The Wantsum Channel was a strait separating the Isle of Thanet from the mainland of Kent in south-eastern England. In Roman and early medieval times it was a major, navigable shipping route; it gradually silted up during the Middle Ages and was fully closed by reclamation in the 16th century. The Wantsum name survives in the River Wantsum, which follows part of its course.

History

In Roman times the channel was about two to three miles wide and guarded at either end by the Saxon Shore forts of Regulbium (Reculver) in the north and Rutupiae (Richborough) in the south — Richborough was the principal port of entry to Roman Britain, where the emperor Claudius's invasion force landed in AD 43. The channel provided a sheltered route from the English Channel to the Thames estuary, avoiding the dangerous waters around the North Foreland.

Decline

From about the 10th century, silting and land reclamation narrowed the channel. Canterbury's port of Fordwich declined as ships could no longer reach it, and by the 15th century the channel was passable only by small boats at high water. The final closure came in the early 16th century, conventionally dated to around 1510s, when the last gap near Sarre was dammed, joining Thanet permanently to the mainland.

Geography today

The former channel is now a low-lying marshy valley drained by the River Wantsum and the Stour, crossed by roads at Sarre. Settlements such as St Nicholas-at-Wade and Sarre preserve the memory of the old shoreline, and the Reculver Towers remain a landmark at the channel's northern entrance.
