Kai Tak Development

The Kai Tak Development (Chinese: 啟德發展計劃) is a large-scale urban development project in Hong Kong on and around the site of the former Kai Tak Airport in Kowloon. Covering more than 320 hectares of land — the old airport site plus adjoining districts in Kowloon City, Wong Tai Sin, and Kwun Tong — it is one of the biggest urban-regeneration schemes ever undertaken in the territory.

Background

Kai Tak Airport, Hong Kong's international airport from 1925, was famous for its challenging approach over densely built Kowloon and its single runway (13/31) jutting into Victoria Harbour. It closed on 6 July 1998, when operations moved to the new Hong Kong International Airport at Chek Lap Kok. The vast brownfield site in the heart of urban Kowloon then became the subject of years of planning studies, delayed in part by the Protection of the Harbour Ordinance and public opposition to further reclamation.

The plan

The statutory outline zoning plan for Kai Tak was approved in 2007, adopting the concepts of "a distinguished, vibrant, green and people-oriented Kai Tak". The development mixes housing, offices, retail, sports, tourism, and government/institutional uses, organised into areas including the former runway, the south apron, and the "Kai Tak City Centre". Key elements include:

  • Kai Tak Sports Park — Hong Kong's largest sports complex, built on the north apron, with a main stadium of about 50,000 seats (with a retractable roof), an indoor sports centre, and a public sports ground; it is the centrepiece venue of the 2025 National Games events in Hong Kong.
  • Kai Tak Cruise Terminal — designed by Foster + Partners (Norman Foster's practice) on the southern tip of the old runway, opened in 2013, with two berths for the world's largest cruise ships and a large rooftop park.
  • Kai Tak Runway Park and waterfront promenades along Victoria Harbour.
  • Large public and private housing developments (such as Kai Ching Estate and Tak Long Estate), the twin-tower Trade and Industry Tower and other government offices, the Hong Kong Children's Hospital (opened 2018), and Kai Tak station on the Tuen Ma line.

Implementation and criticism

Works began in 2009 and have proceeded in stages, with completion of the whole development anticipated in phases into the 2020s. A proposed environmentally friendly linkage system (a monorail) for the area was shelved in 2020 in favour of buses, cycling, and pedestrian networks. The development has been praised for returning a landmark site to public use, but criticised for slow delivery of housing and sports facilities, for the cruise terminal's early under-use, and for planning that some commentators say under-realised the site's harbourfront potential.
