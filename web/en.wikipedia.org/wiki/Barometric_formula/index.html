Barometric formula

The barometric formula describes how the pressure (or density) of the air changes with altitude in Earth's atmosphere. In its simplest form, assuming constant temperature, pressure decreases exponentially with height:

p(h) = p0 · exp(−Mgh / RT)

where p0 is the reference pressure at sea level, M the molar mass of air, g gravitational acceleration, R the universal gas constant, and T absolute temperature.

Derivation and assumptions

The formula follows from hydrostatic equilibrium, in which the pressure gradient balances the weight of the air above, combined with the ideal gas law. For an isothermal atmosphere, pressure falls by a factor of e over each scale height, about 8.4 km for Earth's atmosphere. More realistic models, such as the U.S. Standard Atmosphere, divide the atmosphere into layers with constant lapse rates and apply the formula piecewise in each layer.

Applications

The barometric formula is used in altimetry, aviation, meteorology, and the calibration of pressure sensors. Aircraft altimeters infer altitude from measured static pressure using a standard atmosphere model. Variants of the formula also govern the density distribution used in flight simulation and in estimating atmospheric drag on satellites.
