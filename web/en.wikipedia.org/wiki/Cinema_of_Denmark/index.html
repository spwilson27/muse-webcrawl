Cinema of Denmark

The cinema of Denmark has a long history, from pioneering silent films and Nordisk Film (founded 1906) to the internationally acclaimed Dogme 95 movement and directors such as Carl Theodor Dreyer, Lars von Trier, Susanne Bier and Thomas Vinterberg.

Cinema of Denmark
Key studio: Nordisk Film (1906)
Key movement: Dogme 95
Major directors: Dreyer, von Trier, Bier, Vinterberg, Refn

History

Denmark was a major silent-film producer before the First World War, exporting melodramas worldwide. Dreyer's The Passion of Joan of Arc (1928) and Ordet (1955) are canonical works.

Modern era

State support through the Danish Film Institute sustained production. Babette's Feast (1987) and Pelle the Conqueror (1988) won Academy Awards. Dogme 95, launched by Lars von Trier and Thomas Vinterberg with a manifesto of austere rules, produced Festen (1998) and The Idiots (1998).

International success

Susanne Bier's In a Better World (2010) and Vinterberg's Another Round (2020) won Oscars for Best International Feature; The Hunt (2012) and television series such as Borgen further raised Denmark's profile.
