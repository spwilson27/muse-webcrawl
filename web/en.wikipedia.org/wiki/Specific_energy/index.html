Specific energy

Specific energy
SI unit: joule per kilogram (J/kg)
Other units: MJ/kg, Wh/kg, cal/g

Specific energy or massic energy is energy per unit mass. It is used to quantify, for example, stored heat and other thermodynamic properties of substances, the energy carried by fuels, and the capacity of batteries. The SI unit is the joule per kilogram (J/kg); megajoules per kilogram (MJ/kg) and watt-hours per kilogram (Wh/kg) are also common.

Energy density of fuels

Specific energy is a key figure of merit for fuels. Hydrogen has one of the highest specific energies by mass (about 120 MJ/kg lower heating value) but is bulky, while gasoline and diesel are around 44–46 MJ/kg. In food science, the energy content of food is likewise expressed per unit mass, conventionally in kilocalories per gram.

Batteries

For electrochemical storage, specific energy distinguishes the energy a battery can deliver per kilogram from its power capability. Lithium-ion cells typically achieve roughly 100–265 Wh/kg, far more than lead–acid batteries at about 30–50 Wh/kg, which is a major reason for their use in portable electronics and electric vehicles.

Related concepts

Specific energy is distinct from energy density, which is energy per unit volume; the two are related through the material's density. In orbital mechanics, "specific energy" also refers to the specific orbital energy, the sum of kinetic and potential energy per unit mass of an orbiting body.
