Acacia pycnantha, most commonly known as the golden wattle, is a tree of the family Fabaceae native to southeastern Australia. It grows to a height of 3–8 m (10–26 ft) and has smooth, dark brown to grey bark. It is the floral emblem of Australia and appears on the nation's coat of arms.

Description

Rather than true leaves, the plant has glossy green phyllodes 9–15 cm long. In late winter and spring it produces masses of fragrant, bright golden-yellow spherical flower heads, followed by long seed pods. The species name pycnantha derives from Greek words meaning "dense flower", referring to the crowded flower clusters.

Distribution and cultivation

The golden wattle occurs naturally in southeastern Australia from southern New South Wales through Victoria into South Australia. It is widely cultivated in temperate regions for ornament, tannin bark, and timber, and has become naturalised — and in places invasive — in South Africa, New Zealand, and parts of Europe and North America.

Symbolism

Golden wattle was proclaimed Australia's national floral emblem in 1988, and 1 September is celebrated as National Wattle Day.
