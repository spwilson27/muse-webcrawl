Vikramaditya

Vikramaditya (Sanskrit: "sun of valour") is a legendary emperor of ancient India, famous in Sanskrit and regional folklore as the ideal king — wise, brave, just, and generous. Tradition places his capital at Ujjain in present-day Madhya Pradesh and credits him with founding the Vikram Samvat calendar era beginning in 57 BC, after a victory over the Shakas.

Vikramaditya is the hero of two celebrated cycles of tales: the Vetala Panchavimshati (the "Twenty-five Tales of the Vetala"), in which he repeatedly carries a riddle-telling spirit to a sorcerer, and the Simhasana Dvatrimsika ("Thirty-two Tales of the Throne"), in which statues on his throne recount his virtues to King Bhoja. His court was said to include the "nine gems" (navaratna), among them the poet Kalidasa, the astronomer Varahamihira, and the lexicographer Amarasimha.

Historicity

No contemporary inscriptions attest a first-century BC emperor of this name; historians regard the legendary Vikramaditya as a composite figure. The title was borne by several historical kings, most notably the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II (r. c. 375–415 CE), who defeated the Western Kshatrapa Shakas and assumed the title Vikramaditya, and the Chalukya king Vikramaditya VI (r. 1076–1126). Many legendary exploits probably migrated from the Gupta king to the folk hero. The Vikram Samvat remains the official calendar of Nepal and is widely used in India.

The name endures in modern India: the aircraft carrier INS Vikramaditya, numerous schools and institutions, and the popular comic and film hero derived from the Vetala tales all carry it.
