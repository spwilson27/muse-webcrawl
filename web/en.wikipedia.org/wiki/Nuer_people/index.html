Nuer people

The Nuer people are a Nilotic ethnic group native to the Sudd wetlands and the upper Nile basin of South Sudan and western Ethiopia. They call themselves Naath, meaning "human beings." Numbering in the millions, they are one of the largest ethnic groups in South Sudan, where they form the second-largest group after the closely related Dinka.

Society and culture

Traditionally the Nuer are semi-nomadic pastoralists whose lives revolve around cattle, which provide milk, meat, and blood, and which serve as bridewealth in marriage and as compensation in the settlement of feuds. Their society, famously described by the British anthropologist E. E. Evans-Pritchard in The Nuer (1940), is organised through a segmentary lineage system without centralised political authority — an "ordered anarchy" in which groups unite or divide according to context.

Language and religion

The Nuer language (Thok Naath) belongs to the Western Nilotic branch of the Nilo-Saharan family and is closely related to Dinka. Traditional Nuer religion centres on a high god, Kwoth, and a world of spirits; prophets have played important political and religious roles. Since the twentieth century, many Nuer have become Christians.

Recent history

The Nuer were deeply affected by the Sudanese civil wars and by the South Sudanese Civil War that began in 2013, in which Nuer communities were both victims of atrocities, such as the 2013 killings in Juba, and participants in armed movements, including the SPLM-IO led by Riek Machar. Large numbers of Nuer live as refugees and in the diaspora.
