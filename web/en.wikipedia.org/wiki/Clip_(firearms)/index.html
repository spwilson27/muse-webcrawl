Clip (firearms)

A clip is a device used to store multiple rounds of ammunition together as a unit in order to speed up the loading of a firearm. A clip differs from a magazine: a magazine feeds ammunition into the chamber and contains a spring and follower, whereas a clip merely holds cartridges together, typically when loading them into an internal magazine. Despite the distinction, "clip" is often used colloquially — and incorrectly — to refer to detachable box magazines.

Types

Stripper clips (charger clips) hold cartridges in a row; the shooter presses the rounds down into the magazine and removes the empty clip, as with the Mauser and many bolt-action military rifles and the SKS. En-bloc clips are inserted into the magazine together with the cartridges and form part of the feeding mechanism, as in the French Berthier, the Italian Carcano, and most famously the American M1 Garand, whose clip is ejected with a distinctive "ping" when empty. Moon clips are circular clips that hold rimless cartridges for use in revolvers, with half-moon clips holding three rounds and full-moon clips six.

History

Clips became standard with the adoption of magazine rifles in the late nineteenth century, beginning with designs such as the Mannlicher en-bloc system of the 1880s. They allowed soldiers to reload far faster than with loose cartridges, and stripper-clip loading remained standard for military rifles until detachable magazines became universal after the Second World War.
