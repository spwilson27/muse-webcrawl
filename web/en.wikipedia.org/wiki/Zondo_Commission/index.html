SubjectState capture and corruption in South Africa

The Zondo Commission, formally the Judicial Commission of Inquiry into Allegations of State Capture, Corruption and Fraud in the Public Sector including Organs of State, was a public inquiry established in January 2018 by South African president Jacob Zuma, in line with a court order arising from the Public Protector's 2016 "State of Capture" report. It was chaired by Deputy Chief Justice (later Chief Justice) Raymond Zondo. The commission investigated how the Gupta family and associated officials had allegedly "captured" state institutions during Zuma's presidency.

Proceedings

Public hearings began on 20 August 2018. Over more than 400 sitting days, the commission heard testimony from nearly 300 witnesses — including ministers, officials, whistleblowers and business figures — about corruption at state-owned enterprises such as Eskom, Transnet, Denel and South African Airways, and about the influence exerted by the Gupta brothers over cabinet appointments and state contracts. Evidence leaders included Paul Pretorius SC. In July 2021, former president Zuma was imprisoned for contempt of court after refusing to appear; his arrest preceded widespread unrest in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.

Findings and report

The commission cost about R1 billion and produced a final report of some 5,500 pages, handed to President Cyril Ramaphosa in parts between January and June 2022. It found that state capture had been real and systematic, recommended prosecutions of numerous individuals, the recovery of misappropriated funds, and reforms including an independent anti-corruption body and changes to the appointment of state-owned enterprise boards.

Aftermath

The National Prosecuting Authority has pursued a number of cases arising from the commission's findings, though prosecutions have proceeded slowly. The commission is widely regarded as a landmark exercise in public accountability in South Africa's democratic era, even as critics question the pace of consequences for those implicated.
