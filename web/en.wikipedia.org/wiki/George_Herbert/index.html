George Herbert (3 April 1593 – 1 March 1633) was a Welsh poet, orator, and priest of the Church of England. He is regarded as one of the foremost British devotional lyricists and a leading figure among the metaphysical poets.

Life

Herbert was born into an eminent Welsh family — his brother was the philosopher Edward Herbert, 1st Baron Herbert of Cherbury — and educated at Westminster School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he became a fellow and, in 1620, the university's Public Orator. Turning from hopes of a court career to the priesthood, he was ordained and in 1630 became rector of the small parish of Bemerton near Salisbury, where he served devotedly until his death from tuberculosis three years later.

Poetry

On his deathbed Herbert sent his manuscript to Nicholas Ferrar, asking him to publish it only if he thought it might help "any dejected poor soul". Ferrar published it in 1633 as The Temple: Sacred Poems and Private Ejaculations. Its roughly 160 poems, including "The Altar", "Easter Wings", "The Collar", "Love (III)" and "The Pulley", are celebrated for their formal ingenuity (including pattern poems), intimate conversational voice, and spiritual wrestling. Herbert's prose manual A Priest to the Temple (published 1652) describes the ideal country parson. He is commemorated as a saint in the Anglican Communion, with feast day 27 February.
