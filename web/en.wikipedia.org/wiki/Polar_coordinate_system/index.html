Overview

The polar coordinate system is a two-dimensional coordinate system in which each point is fixed by its distance from a central reference point (the pole) and its angle from a reference direction. It is the natural language for anything involving rotation or radial symmetry: spirals, orbits, waves spreading from a source.

Polar coordinates are converted to Cartesian coordinates by x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ. Their three-dimensional extension is the spherical coordinate system. In map-making, polar azimuthal projections — the view from directly above the North or South Pole — are built on the same idea, and the flag of the United Nations uses such a projection.
