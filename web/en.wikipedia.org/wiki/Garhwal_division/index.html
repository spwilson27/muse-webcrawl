Garhwal division is one of the two administrative divisions of the Indian state of Uttarakhand, the other being the Kumaon division. It occupies the western part of the state, corresponding broadly to the historic Garhwal region, and its divisional headquarters are at Pauri.

Districts

The division comprises seven districts:

  • Chamoli
  • Dehradun
  • Haridwar
  • Pauri Garhwal
  • Rudraprayag
  • Tehri Garhwal
  • Uttarkashi

Geography

Garhwal is a mountainous Himalayan region, rising from the plains at Haridwar and Rishikesh to peaks such as Nanda Devi (in the neighbouring Kumaon boundary area), Chaukhamba, Kamet and Trishul. The Alaknanda and Bhagirathi rivers, whose confluence at Devprayag forms the Ganges, flow through the division. Dehradun, the state capital and largest city of Uttarakhand, lies in the division's south-west.

Culture and religion

The people of the region are known as Garhwalis and predominantly speak the Garhwali language, alongside Hindi. Garhwal is one of Hinduism's most sacred landscapes: it contains the Char Dham pilgrimage shrines of Badrinath, Kedarnath, Gangotri and Yamunotri, and the confluence towns (Panch Prayag) of the Alaknanda. Historically the region was ruled by the Garhwal Kingdom, whose capital was at Srinagar, before becoming part of British India and then of Uttar Pradesh, until Uttarakhand was created in 2000.
