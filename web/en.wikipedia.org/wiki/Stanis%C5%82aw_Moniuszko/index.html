Stanisław Moniuszko (May 5, 1819 – June 4, 1872) was a Polish composer, conductor and teacher. He is regarded as the father of Polish national opera, writing many popular art songs and operas that combine Polish folk melodies and rhythms with the conventions of Italian, French and German opera.

Life

Moniuszko was born in Ubiel, near Minsk, into a Polish-Lithuanian noble family. He studied music in Warsaw and later in Berlin, where he was a pupil of Carl Friedrich Rungenhagen at the Singakademie. After returning to the Russian Empire, he worked as an organist and teacher in Vilnius for nearly two decades, composing and organising musical life there.

In 1858 he moved to Warsaw, where he became conductor at the Grand Theatre (Teatr Wielki). The Warsaw premiere of the four-act version of his opera Halka on January 1, 1858, established his reputation. He also taught at the Warsaw Conservatory, where his students included Zygmunt Noskowski and Henryk Jarecki; for a time the young Ignacy Jan Paderewski also studied in Warsaw during Moniuszko's era of influence.

Works

Moniuszko's best-known operas are:

  • Halka (1848, concert version; staged 1854 in Vilnius; expanded 1858) – the story of a peasant girl seduced and abandoned by a nobleman.
  • The Haunted Manor (Straszny dwór, 1865) – a patriotic, comic-romantic opera whose overture and arias, including the famous Mazurka and Stefan's aria with chimes, remain concert favourites.
  • The Raftsman (Flis, 1858) and The Countess (Hrabina, 1860).
  • Paria (1869), set in India.

He also composed operettas, ballets, cantatas such as the "Crimean Sonnets" to words by Adam Mickiewicz, sacred music including the Litanie Ostrobramskie (Ostrobamska Litanies), and roughly 300 songs published in his Home Songbooks (Śpiewniki domowe), which became central to Polish domestic music-making in the 19th century.

Legacy

Moniuszko died of a heart attack in Warsaw in 1872 and was buried at Powązki Cemetery. His funeral drew huge crowds and was a national manifestation. The Grand Theatre in Warsaw and numerous streets, schools and competitions in Poland bear his name, as does the International Stanisław Moniuszko Vocal Competition, held in Warsaw since 1992.
