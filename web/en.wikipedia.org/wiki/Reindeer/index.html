Reindeer

The reindeer or caribou (Rangifer tarandus) is a species of deer with circumpolar distribution, native to Arctic, subarctic, tundra, boreal, and mountainous regions of Northern Europe, Siberia, and North America. It is the only representative of the genus Rangifer. Unique among deer, both males and females grow antlers, although those of males are typically larger.

Ecology and herding

Reindeer are adapted to cold climates with a thick insulated coat and broad hooves for walking on snow. Many populations undertake long seasonal migrations. Semi-domesticated reindeer have been herded for centuries by Indigenous peoples of Eurasia, including the Sámi of Fennoscandia and the Nenets and Chukchi of Siberia, for meat, hides, antlers, milk, and transport. In North America, wild populations are called caribou and remain fully wild, though reindeer herding was introduced to Alaska in the late 19th century.

In culture

In Western Christmas tradition, flying reindeer pull Santa Claus's sleigh; the famous named reindeer were popularised by the 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" and the 1939 story of Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer.
