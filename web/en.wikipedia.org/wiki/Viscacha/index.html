Viscacha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A southern viscacha (Lagidium viscacia) in the Andes.

Viscachas or vizcachas are rodents of two genera (Lagidium and Lagostomus) in the family Chinchillidae, native to South America. They are close relatives of chinchillas and resemble rabbits with long tails, although they are not closely related to either.

Species

The plains viscacha (Lagostomus maximus), of the Pampas of Argentina, is the largest species, living in communal burrow systems in lowland grasslands. The mountain viscachas of the genus Lagidium — including the southern viscacha (Lagidium viscacia), northern viscacha (Lagidium peruanum) and Ecuadorian viscacha (Lagidium ahuacaense) — inhabit rocky terrain in the Andes at elevations up to about 5,000 metres.

Characteristics

Viscachas have thick, soft fur, long ears and long, bushy, curled tails. Mountain viscachas are agile climbers among rocks and feed on grasses, mosses and lichens. The plains viscacha constructs extensive communal burrow systems called vizcacheras, and is considered an agricultural pest in parts of its range because its burrowing damages pasture.

Behaviour

Plains viscachas are nocturnal and social, living in groups that can number several dozen animals in a shared burrow system. They communicate with a variety of vocalisations, and males are considerably larger than females. Mountain viscachas are diurnal, sunning themselves on rocks during the day.
