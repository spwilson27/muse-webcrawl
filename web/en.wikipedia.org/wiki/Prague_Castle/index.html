Prague Castle (Czech: Pražský hrad) is a castle complex in Prague, Czech Republic, founded around 870 and serving continuously as a seat of power for Bohemian kings, Holy Roman emperors, and presidents of Czechoslovakia and the Czech Republic. According to the Guinness Book of Records it is the largest ancient castle in the world, covering almost 70,000 square metres. It is one of the most visited sights in Prague.

Prague Castle, dominated by St Vitus Cathedral, seen from across the Vltava river.

History

The first fortified settlement on the site dates to about 870, when the Church of the Virgin Mary was built. Prince Bořivoj I of the Přemyslid dynasty made it his seat. The Romanesque palace was rebuilt in Gothic style under Charles IV in the fourteenth century, and the Vladislav Hall — a vast late-Gothic ceremonial space — was added under Vladislav II. The castle was damaged in the Hussite Wars and by fire in 1541, and rebuilt under the Habsburgs; Rudolf II made it a centre of art and science. The Defenestration of Prague in 1618, an opening act of the Thirty Years' War, took place here. After 1918 it became the seat of the president of Czechoslovakia, renovated by the Slovenian architect Jože Plečnik for Tomáš Masaryk; Václav Havel and his successors have continued this tradition.

Principal buildings

  • St Vitus Cathedral — the great Gothic cathedral, founded 1344, housing the tombs of Bohemian kings and the Crown Jewels.
  • Old Royal Palace — with the Vladislav Hall and the Bohemian Chancellery.
  • St George's Basilica and Convent — Romanesque church containing the National Gallery's old Bohemian art collection.
  • Golden Lane — a street of small colourful houses once occupied by castle marksmen and goldsmiths; Franz Kafka lived briefly at No. 22.
  • New Royal Palace — the presidential residence and state rooms.
