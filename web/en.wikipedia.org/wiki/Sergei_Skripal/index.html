Sergei Skripal

Sergei Skripal

Born: 23 June 1951, Kaliningrad, Russian SFSR, Soviet Union
Occupation: Former military intelligence officer
Known for: Espionage conviction; 2018 poisoning in Salisbury

Sergei Viktorovich Skripal (born 23 June 1951) is a former Russian military intelligence officer who acted as a double agent for the United Kingdom's intelligence services during the 1990s and early 2000s. In 2006 he was convicted of high treason in Russia and sentenced to 13 years in prison; he was released in 2010 as part of a spy swap and settled in Salisbury, England. In March 2018, Skripal and his daughter Yulia were poisoned in Salisbury with a Novichok nerve agent, an attack the British government attributed to Russian military intelligence, sparking a major diplomatic crisis.

Career and espionage case

Skripal served in the Soviet and Russian armed forces, reaching the rank of colonel in the GRU, Russia's military intelligence agency, and worked in human resources and overseas postings, including in Malta and Spain. According to the case against him, he was recruited by British intelligence (MI6) in the mid-1990s and passed information about Russian agents and operations for roughly a decade. He retired from the GRU in 1999 but continued to travel abroad and, Russian prosecutors alleged, to supply information.

He was arrested in Moscow in December 2004 and charged with high treason in the form of espionage. In August 2006 a military court sentenced him to 13 years in a penal colony, stripped him of his rank and awards, and he served part of the sentence before being pardoned by President Dmitry Medvedev in July 2010. He was one of four prisoners exchanged for ten Russian sleeper agents arrested in the United States in the "Illegals Program" case — the largest spy swap since the Cold War. Skripal settled in Salisbury, Wiltshire, and became a British citizen, living a largely quiet life; his wife Lyudmila died in 2012 and his son Alexander in 2017.

2018 poisoning

On 4 March 2018, Skripal and his daughter Yulia, visiting from Moscow, were found unconscious on a bench in Salisbury city centre and hospitalized in critical condition. Investigators identified the poison as a Novichok nerve agent, and a police officer, Detective Sergeant Nick Bailey, was also seriously exposed. The British government concluded it was "highly likely" that Russia was responsible; Prime Minister Theresa May expelled 23 Russian diplomats, and more than two dozen countries and NATO followed with coordinated expulsions of over 150 Russian officials — the largest such collective action on record. Russia denied involvement.

British prosecutors later charged two men, identified as Alexander Petrov and Ruslan Boshirov, with conspiracy to murder and related offences; British officials said the names were aliases for officers of the GRU, Anatoliy Chepiga and Alexander Mishkin. A third suspect was charged in 2021. In June–July 2018, two British citizens in nearby Amesbury were accidentally exposed to the same agent, apparently from a discarded container; one of them, Dawn Sturgess, died, prompting a public inquiry. Both Skripals survived and were discharged from hospital; they have since lived under protection and have not appeared in public, though Yulia Skripal issued a written statement during her recovery. In 2024, a public inquiry into Sturgess's death heard extensive evidence about the attack.
