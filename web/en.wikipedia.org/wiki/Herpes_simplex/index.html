Herpes simplex

Herpes simplex is a viral infection caused by the herpes simplex viruses HSV-1 and HSV-2. Infection is categorised by the site affected: oral herpes causes cold sores around the mouth, while genital herpes affects the genitals or buttocks. Most infections are asymptomatic, and the virus remains latent in nerve ganglia for life, periodically reactivating.

Signs and symptoms

Symptomatic episodes produce small fluid-filled blisters on a red base that ulcerate and crust over within days. First episodes may be accompanied by fever and swollen lymph nodes. Other presentations include herpetic whitlow (fingers), herpes keratitis (eye), herpes encephalitis (a medical emergency) and severe disease in newborns and the immunocompromised.

Transmission and life cycle

The virus spreads by direct contact with lesions or with mucosal surfaces during asymptomatic shedding. After primary infection it travels along sensory nerves to ganglia — the trigeminal ganglion for oral infection, the sacral ganglia for genital infection — where it persists latently, reactivating in response to triggers such as illness, stress or ultraviolet light.

Treatment

There is no cure and no vaccine, but nucleoside analogue antivirals — aciclovir, valaciclovir and famciclovir — shorten episodes and, taken daily, suppress recurrences and reduce transmission. Most of the world's population carries HSV-1, which the WHO estimates infects about two-thirds of people under 50.
