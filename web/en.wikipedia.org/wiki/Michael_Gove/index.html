Michael Gove, British politician and former journalist.

Michael Andrew Gove, Baron Gove (born Graeme Andrew Logan, 26 August 1967) is a British politician and journalist who served in senior cabinet posts under David Cameron, Theresa May, Boris Johnson, and Rishi Sunak. A leading figure in the Conservative Party, he was a prominent campaigner for Brexit as co-convenor of Vote Leave in the 2016 referendum.

Early life and journalism

Gove was born in Edinburgh and adopted at four months old, growing up in Aberdeen, where his adoptive father ran a fish-processing business. He was educated at the independent Robert Gordon's College and read English at Lady Margaret Hall, Oxford. Before politics he worked as a journalist, including as a columnist and news editor at The Times, and wrote biographies and political works.

Political career

Elected MP for Surrey Heath in 2005, Gove became Shadow Secretary of State for Children, Schools and Families and, after the 2010 election, Secretary of State for Education, where he drove the expansion of academies and free schools and reformed GCSEs and the national curriculum — reforms that made him a divisive figure among teachers.

In 2016 Gove was a leading Leave campaigner. After the referendum he unexpectedly withdrew his support for Boris Johnson's leadership bid and stood himself, finishing third. He served as Environment Secretary under May, leading work on animal welfare and the 25 Year Environment Plan, then as Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster under Johnson, and from 2021 as Secretary of State for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities, overseeing the Renters (Reform) Bill and the government's response on building safety after the Grenfell Tower fire. He stood down as an MP at the 2024 general election and was later elevated to the House of Lords, becoming editor of The Spectator.
