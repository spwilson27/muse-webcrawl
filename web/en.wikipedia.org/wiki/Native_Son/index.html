Native Son

Native Son

Author: Richard Wright

Published: 1 March 1940 (Harper & Brothers)

Genre: Novel, social protest fiction

Setting: Chicago, 1930s

Protagonist: Bigger Thomas

Native Son (1940) is a novel by American author Richard Wright. It tells the story of Bigger Thomas, a 20-year-old Black man living in poverty on Chicago's South Side, who is hired as a chauffeur by a wealthy white family and, in fear and panic, accidentally kills their daughter, setting off a manhunt, trial, and his condemnation to death.

Plot

The novel is divided into three books — "Fear", "Flight", and "Fate". Bigger smothers Mary Dalton while trying to avoid detection in her bedroom, burns her body in the furnace, and later murders his girlfriend Bessie Mears. Captured after a massive manhunt, he is defended by communist lawyer Boris Max, whose courtroom speech argues that American racism and oppression created Bigger's violence.

Reception and legacy

A Book of the Month Club selection, Native Son sold 215,000 copies in its first weeks and made Wright the most famous Black writer in America. It is considered a landmark of African American literature and naturalism, though later writers such as James Baldwin critiqued its protest-novel framing. It has been adapted for stage (1941, with Orson Welles directing) and film several times.
