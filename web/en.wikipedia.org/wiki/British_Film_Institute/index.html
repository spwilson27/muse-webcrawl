The British Film Institute (BFI) is a film and television charitable organisation which promotes and preserves filmmaking and television in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1933, it maintains the BFI National Archive, runs BFI Southbank and the BFI IMAX cinema in London, publishes Sight and Sound magazine, organises the BFI London Film Festival, and distributes Lottery funding for film production, education and audience development.

History

The BFI was established by the Incorporated Association of Kinematograph Manufacturers and others following the 1932 report The Film in National Life. Its National Film Library, founded in 1935, grew into one of the largest film archives in the world, holding hundreds of thousands of film and television titles. In 2011 the BFI took over the responsibilities of the abolished UK Film Council as the lead agency for film.

Activities

The BFI National Archive curates Britain's moving-image heritage at its conservation centre in Berkhamsted. BFI Southbank (formerly the National Film Theatre) shows over 2,000 screenings a year; the BFI London Film Festival, founded in 1957, is the UK's largest film festival. Every ten years since 1952, Sight and Sound's critics' poll has named the "greatest films of all time" — topped by Vertigo in 2012 and Jeanne Dielman in 2022.

Lists

The Institute is known for its canonical lists, including the BFI Top 100 British films (1999) and the BFI Flipside label for neglected British cinema.
