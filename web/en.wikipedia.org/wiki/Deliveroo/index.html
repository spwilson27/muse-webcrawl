Deliveroo

Deliveroo is a British online food delivery company founded in London in 2013 by Will Shu and Greg Orlowski. Operating through a website and mobile app, it connects customers with local restaurants and grocers and delivers orders using a network of self-employed couriers, recognisable by their teal kangaroo logo (the name combines "deliver" and "kangaroo"). The company operates in markets across Europe, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific.

Business model

Deliveroo charges restaurants commission on orders and customers delivery and service fees. In addition to restaurant meals it has expanded into grocery delivery, partnering with supermarket chains, and operates "dark kitchens" (under the Editions brand) — kitchen spaces rented to restaurants to serve delivery-only areas — and delivery-only grocery stores (Hop). Its logistics platform uses machine-learning-based dispatch, nicknamed "Frank", to assign orders to riders.

History

Deliveroo expanded rapidly from London across the UK and internationally, raising large venture funding rounds, including investment from Amazon in 2019 that was investigated by the UK Competition and Markets Authority before being cleared. It listed on the London Stock Exchange in March 2021 in one of the exchange's largest technology IPOs, though the shares fell sharply on debut. The company has exited some markets, including Australia (2022), Spain and the Netherlands, while concentrating on its strongest markets. In 2025, US rival DoorDash agreed to acquire Deliveroo.

Labour issues

Like other gig-economy platforms, Deliveroo has faced sustained controversy and litigation over the employment status of its riders, who are engaged as independent contractors. In the UK, the Supreme Court ruled in 2023 that Deliveroo riders were not in an employment relationship for the purposes of collective bargaining rights, while courts and legislatures in other countries have reached differing conclusions, and the company has separately recognised the GMB union for its UK riders.
