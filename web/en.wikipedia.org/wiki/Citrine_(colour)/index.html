Citrine (colour)

Citrine is a colour, the most common reference for which is certain coloured varieties of quartz — specifically the yellow to orange-brown gemstone variety known as citrine. The colour is a vivid yellow, sometimes with greenish or orange tints, named after the citron fruit and the French word citron, meaning "lemon".

History of use

The first recorded use of citrine as a colour name in English was in 1386. The word derives from the Latin citrus and was applied to the fruit, the wood of the citron tree, and yellow colourings derived from them. In heraldry and older colour terminology, citrine was sometimes listed among the tertiary colours.

In nature

Yellow-green and golden-yellow plumage in some birds, and the yellowish colour of certain insects and fungi, are sometimes described as citrine. The citrine wagtail (Motacilla citreola) takes its name from its yellow plumage.
