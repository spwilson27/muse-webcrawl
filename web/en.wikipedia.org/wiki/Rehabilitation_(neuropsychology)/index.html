Rehabilitation (neuropsychology)

Neuropsychological rehabilitation is a set of therapeutic activities, based on brain–behaviour relationships, intended to reduce the functional deficits caused by damage to the brain, whether from stroke, traumatic brain injury, tumour, infection, or neurodegenerative disease. It aims to help patients achieve their optimum level of physical, psychological, and social functioning.

Approaches

Interventions combine restorative exercises intended to retrain impaired functions — such as attention process training and memory rehearsal strategies — with compensatory strategies, including external aids (diaries, alarms, smartphones), environmental modification, and metacognitive strategy training. Rehabilitation is usually delivered by multidisciplinary teams including neuropsychologists, occupational therapists, speech and language therapists, and rehabilitation physicians, and increasingly draws on evidence-based practice reviews such as the Cicerone standards.
