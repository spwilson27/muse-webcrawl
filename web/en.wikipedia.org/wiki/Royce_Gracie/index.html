Royce Gracie (born December 12, 1966) is a Brazilian retired professional mixed martial artist and Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioner, a member of the Gracie family. He is best known for winning the first, second and fourth Ultimate Fighting Championship tournaments in 1993–94, defeating larger opponents with grappling and submission techniques and thereby revolutionising martial arts and launching modern MMA.

Early UFC career

Chosen by his brother Rorion as the Gracie representative in UFC 1 (November 1993) partly for his unassuming build, Gracie submitted Art Jimmerson, Ken Shamrock and Gerard Gordeau in one night. At UFC 2 he won four fights in a night, and at UFC 4 he famously defeated wrestler Dan Severn with a triangle choke after nearly sixteen minutes. His 90-minute draw with Kazushi Sakuraba under special rules at Pride Grand Prix 2000 — ended when his corner threw in the towel, his first family defeat — is considered a landmark bout; Sakuraba was nicknamed the "Gracie Hunter".

Later career and legacy

Gracie drew with Hideo Tokoro in K-1 (2005), lost a rematch with Matt Hughes at UFC 60 (2006), and defeated Sakuraba in a 2007 rematch, after which he tested positive for anabolic steroids. He closed his career by beating Ken Shamrock by TKO at Bellator 149 in 2016. Gracie was the first inductee into the UFC Hall of Fame (2003, with Shamrock) and is credited with demonstrating worldwide that technique and ground fighting — embodied by Brazilian jiu-jitsu — could defeat size and striking power.
