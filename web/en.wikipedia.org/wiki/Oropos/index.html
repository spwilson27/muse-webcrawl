Oropos (Greek: Ωρωπός) is a small town and municipality in East Attica, Greece, on the southern shore of the South Euboean Gulf opposite Eretria. The modern municipality, formed in the 2011 local-government reform, includes the seaside settlements of Skala Oropou and Nea Palatia, where ferries cross to Euboea. In antiquity Oropos was a contested border town between Attica and Boeotia and the site of the celebrated healing sanctuary and oracle of Amphiaraos, the Amphiareion.

History

Oropos owed its history to its position: it commanded the shortest crossing to Euboea and the coastal road between Athens and Thebes. Throughout the classical period it passed repeatedly between Athenian and Boeotian (Theban) control, its status a recurring point of dispute mentioned by Herodotus, Thucydides and later writers. It finally became durably Athenian in the Hellenistic period. The town was a modest trading and ferry port rather than a major city.

The Amphiareion

About 6 km southeast of the town lay the Amphiareion, a sanctuary of the seer-hero Amphiaraos, who was said to have been swallowed by the earth near Thebes and to have become an oracular, healing deity. Founded in the late 5th century BC, the sanctuary flourished under Athenian administration; it featured a temple, a long stoa where pilgrims slept to receive dream cures (incubation), a sacred spring, baths, a theatre seating about 300, and a stadium that hosted the Amphiareia games. The site was excavated from 1884 onwards and remains the principal archaeological attraction of the area.

Modern town

Today Oropos is a resort and commuter area for Athens, with beaches along the gulf and summer residences. Skala Oropou hosts the ferry to Eretria, and the area also contains the historic former political prison site of the Greek civil-war era at nearby Nea Palatia.
