Lamia (Greek: Λαμία) is a city in central Greece and the capital of the regional unit of Phthiotis and of the region of Central Greece. It stands at the foot of Mount Othrys overlooking the plain of the Spercheios river, near the Maliac Gulf, and is a transport hub on the main road and rail corridor between Athens and Thessaloniki.

History

The city's site has been inhabited since the Bronze Age. Its name is traditionally connected either to Lamia, a daughter of Poseidon in Greek mythology, or to the surrounding peoples. In antiquity Lamia was the principal city of the Malians and became important because it commanded the routes between Thessaly and southern Greece; the nearby pass of Thermopylae lies to the southeast. The Lamian War (323–322 BC), in which a coalition of Greek cities fought Macedon after the death of Alexander the Great and besieged Antipater in the city, takes its name from Lamia. The city prospered in the Hellenistic and Roman periods.

In the Middle Ages the town was known as Zetounion and was fortified; the castle that still crowns its acropolis was rebuilt under the Catalans and later the Ottomans, who called the town İzzeddin. During the Greek War of Independence the surrounding region was the theatre of operations of the hero Athanasios Diakos, who was captured and executed by the Ottomans in Lamia in 1821 after the Battle of Alamana. Lamia joined the modern Greek state in 1832 and grew as an agricultural and commercial centre for the Spercheios valley.

Landmarks

Sights include the medieval castle and the Archaeological Museum housed within it, the cenotaph of Athanasios Diakos, the church of the Panagia Archontiki (18th century), and the municipal theatre. The hot springs of Thermopylae and the Gorgopotamos bridge — blown up in a celebrated act of resistance sabotage in 1942 — lie within the municipality's wider area.

Economy and transport

Lamia's economy rests on services, trade and processing of the region's agricultural produce (cotton, olives, livestock). The city sits on the A1 motorway and the Athens–Thessaloniki railway, whose main station lies a few kilometres west of the centre.
