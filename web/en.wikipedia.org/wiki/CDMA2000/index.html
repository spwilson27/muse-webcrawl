CDMA2000

A CDMA2000 handset

CDMA2000 (also known as C2K or IMT Multi-Carrier, IMT-MC) is a family of 3G mobile technology standards for sending voice, data, and signaling data between mobile phones and cell sites. It is developed by the 3rd Generation Partnership Project 2 (3GPP2) as a backwards-compatible successor to second-generation cdmaOne (IS-95) technology, and competes with the GSM-based path through UMTS.

Standards and evolution

The first CDMA2000 standard, 1x (IS-2000), doubled the voice capacity of cdmaOne and added packet data at up to 153 kbit/s in a single 1.25 MHz carrier. CDMA2000 1xEV-DO (Evolution-Data Optimized), standardised as IS-856, added high-speed packet data: Revision A offered up to 3.1 Mbit/s downlink, and Revision B increased rates further using multiple carriers. A voice-optimised variant, 1x Advanced, provided additional capacity for operators that continued to run CDMA voice services.

Deployment

CDMA2000 networks were deployed widely in North America, East Asia, and parts of Latin America and Africa, with major operators including Verizon Wireless and Sprint in the United States, KDDI in Japan, and SK Telecom in South Korea. At its peak the technology served hundreds of millions of subscribers. From the 2010s, operators migrated subscribers to LTE and 5G, and most CDMA2000 networks worldwide were shut down in the early 2020s, ending the CDMA family of standards.

Technical features

Like cdmaOne, CDMA2000 uses code-division multiple access with a chip rate of 1.2288 Mchip/s in the 1x mode, forward-link pilot channels, fast power control at 800 Hz, and soft handoff between sectors. Multi-carrier modes (3x) were specified but rarely deployed commercially.
