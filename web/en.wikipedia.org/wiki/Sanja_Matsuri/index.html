Sanja Matsuri

Sanja Matsuri (三社祭)
Location: Asakusa, Tokyo, Japan
Venue: Sensō-ji and Asakusa Shrine
When: Third Friday–Sunday of May
Attendance: up to ~2 million

Sanja Matsuri (三社祭, "Three Shrine Festival") is one of the three great Shinto festivals of Tokyo, held annually in the Asakusa district in honour of the three men who founded the Sensō-ji temple. It takes place over three days on the third weekend of May and draws crowds estimated at up to two million people.

Origins

The festival honours Hinokuma Hamanari, Hinokuma Takenari, and Hajino Nakatomo. According to legend, the two Hinokuma brothers fished a statue of Kannon, the goddess of mercy, from the Sumida River in 628, and the wealthy landowner Hajino Nakatomo converted his house into the temple that became Sensō-ji. The three men are enshrined as kami at Asakusa Shrine, built in 1649.

Festivities

The celebration begins on Friday with the Daigyōretsu, a grand parade of musicians, dancers, and geisha through Asakusa. Over the weekend, around one hundred neighbourhood mikoshi (portable shrines) are carried through the streets by teams in matching happi coats, accompanied by chanting and drumming. The climax comes on Sunday, when the three great mikoshi of Asakusa Shrine are carried from the shrine at dawn, paraded through the district's neighbourhoods, and returned at nightfall amid intense jostling crowds.
