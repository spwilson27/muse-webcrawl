Traditional pop (also called classic pop and pop standards) is a category of popular music that pre-dates and largely ran parallel to the rise of rock and roll. It centres on the songs of the Great American Songbook — the standards written by composers such as Irving Berlin, Cole Porter, George and Ira Gershwin, and Richard Rodgers and Lorenz Hart — performed by singers backed by big bands or orchestras.

Characteristics

Traditional pop emphasises the song and the interpretation: smooth "crooning" vocals, lush orchestral arrangements, and melodies and lyrics of Tin Pan Alley craftsmanship. Singers such as Bing Crosby, Frank Sinatra, Nat King Cole, Ella Fitzgerald, Billie Holiday, Dean Martin, Perry Como, Doris Day and later Tony Bennett and Barbra Streisand defined the genre's sound.

History

The style dominated American popular music from the 1920s through the mid-1950s, when rock and roll displaced it among younger audiences. It persisted on easy-listening radio and in "standards" formats, and has enjoyed repeated revivals — notably through Linda Ronstadt's albums with Nelson Riddle in the 1980s, Harry Connick Jr., Michael Bublé, and Tony Bennett's late-career duet albums, including his 2021 collaboration with Lady Gaga.
