The politics of Ethiopia take place in a federal parliamentary republic. The President is head of state, while the Prime Minister is head of government and chief executive. Legislative power is vested in the bicameral Federal Parliamentary Assembly, consisting of the House of Federation and the House of Peoples' Representatives.

The Parliament building in Addis Ababa.

Ethnic federalism

The 1995 constitution organised Ethiopia as a federation of ethnically based regional states — including Oromia, Amhara, Tigray, and the Somali Region — with a constitutional right of secession for "nations, nationalities and peoples." This system of ethnic federalism, introduced by the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF) after it overthrew the Derg military regime in 1991, remains a central and contested feature of Ethiopian politics.

Recent history

Prime Minister Meles Zenawi dominated politics from 1991 until his death in 2012. His successor Hailemariam Desalegn resigned amid protests in 2018, and Abiy Ahmed became prime minister, launching reforms and dissolving the EPRDF into the new Prosperity Party (2019). Relations with the Tigray People's Liberation Front deteriorated into the Tigray War (2020–2022), ended by a peace agreement signed in Pretoria in November 2022. Conflicts in Amhara and Oromia regions have continued to shape the political landscape.
