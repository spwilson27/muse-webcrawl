List of monastic houses in Scotland

This is a list of monastic houses in Scotland, from early Celtic foundations such as Iona to the great Border abbeys endowed by David I in the 12th century. Most were suppressed or decayed after the Scottish Reformation of 1560.

Notable houses

  • Iona Abbey — founded by St Columba in 563; centre of early Scottish Christianity.
  • Melrose Abbey — Cistercian, founded 1136; heart of Robert the Bruce is buried here.
  • Dryburgh, Jedburgh, Kelso — the other great Border abbeys.
  • Dunfermline Abbey — Benedictine; royal mausoleum of Scotland.
  • Arbroath Abbey — Tironensian, founded 1178; associated with the Declaration of Arbroath (1320).
  • Sweetheart Abbey — Cistercian, founded by Devorgilla for John Balliol.
  • Pluscarden Abbey — Valliscaulian, later Benedictine; uniquely still occupied by monks.
  • Holyrood Abbey — Augustinian, beside the palace in Edinburgh.

Legacy

Many Scottish abbeys survive as romantic ruins in state care; a few, such as Pluscarden and Nunraw, remain living monastic communities.
