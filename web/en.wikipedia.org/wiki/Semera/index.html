Semera (also Samara) is the capital of the Afar Region in northeastern Ethiopia. A planned town on the Awash River area near the border with Djibouti, it replaced the older town of Asaita as the regional capital.

Geography and development

Semera lies on the main road from Awash to Djibouti's port, a vital trade corridor for landlocked Ethiopia, in one of the hottest inhabited regions on Earth near the Danakil Depression. Construction began in the 1990s with wide planned avenues, government buildings, and later Samara University (founded 2007) and Semera Airport. Its administrative centre includes the regional council buildings; nearby Logiya is a companion commercial town.

Context

The Afar are a pastoralist Cushitic people; the region is known for salt extraction at Lake Karum, potash development, and Afar pastoral livelihoods. Semera's growth reflects Ethiopia's federal restructuring after 1991 and investment along the Djibouti corridor.
