Gerard of Cremona

Bornc. 1114, Cremona, Lombardy
Died1187, Toledo, Spain
OccupationTranslator
Known forLatin translations of Arabic scientific works

Gerard of Cremona (Italian: Gerardo da Cremona; c. 1114 – 1187) was an Italian translator of scientific books from Arabic into Latin. One of the most prolific translators of the Middle Ages, he worked in Toledo, Spain, and rendered more than seventy works — including Ptolemy's Almagest and works of Aristotle, Euclid, Avicenna and al-Khwarizmi — into Latin, making them available to Western European scholars.

Life

Dissatisfied with the limited philosophical works available in Italy, Gerard travelled to Toledo, then recently reconquered from Muslim rule (1085) and home to great Arabic libraries. Intending only to find Ptolemy's Almagest, he stayed for the rest of his life, learning Arabic and translating with the help of Mozarabic assistants such as Galippus.

Translations

Gerard's translations included Euclid's Elements, Archimedes' works, Aristotle's Physics and Posterior Analytics (from Arabic versions), Avicenna's Canon of Medicine, al-Razi's medical writings, al-Khwarizmi on algebra and astronomy, and the Toledan Tables. His Latin Almagest (1175) remained the standard astronomical text in Europe until the Renaissance.

Legacy

Gerard is regarded as the leader of the Toledo School of Translators. By transmitting Greek science preserved and developed in the Islamic world, his work laid foundations for the 12th-century Renaissance and the rise of scholasticism in European universities.
