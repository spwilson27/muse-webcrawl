Leukocoria

Leukocoria (from Greek leukos, white, and kore, pupil) is an abnormal white reflection from the retina of the eye, visible through the pupil. Instead of the normal red reflex seen with an ophthalmoscope or in flash photographs, the pupil appears white or yellowish-white. Leukocoria in a child is a medical warning sign that requires prompt ophthalmological evaluation.

Causes

The most serious cause is retinoblastoma, a malignant tumour of the retina in young children, for which leukocoria is the most common presenting sign. Other causes include congenital cataract, Coats disease, persistent fetal vasculature (persistent hyperplastic primary vitreous), retinopathy of prematurity, toxocariasis, retinal detachment, and coloboma.

Detection and diagnosis

Leukocoria is often first noticed by parents in photographs, where one pupil shows a white glow instead of "red eye." Screening with the red reflex test is part of routine newborn and infant examinations. Diagnosis involves dilated fundus examination, ocular ultrasound, and imaging; suspected retinoblastoma is managed by ocular oncology specialists, and biopsy is generally avoided because of the risk of tumour spread.

Significance

Because retinoblastoma can be life-threatening and vision-threatening, any child with leukocoria needs urgent referral. Early detection dramatically improves survival and the chance of preserving the eye and vision.
