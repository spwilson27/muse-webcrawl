Roman Catholic Diocese of Limburg

The Roman Catholic Diocese of Limburg (Latin: Dioecesis Limburgensis) is a diocese of the Catholic Church in Germany, covering parts of the states of Hesse and Rhineland-Palatinate. Founded in 1827 as part of the reorganisation of the Church after the Congress of Vienna, it is a suffragan of the Archdiocese of Cologne. Its seat is Limburg Cathedral, dedicated to St George, overlooking the river Lahn.

History

The diocese was erected by Pope Pius VII's bull Provida solersque of 1821 and formally established in 1827, with territory drawn largely from the former archdioceses of Trier and Mainz. It includes the city of Frankfurt am Main, one of Germany's largest Catholic urban communities.

Recent history

The diocese drew international attention in 2013–2014 over the cost of Bishop Franz-Peter Tebartz-van Elst's episcopal residence, reported at some €31 million; he resigned in March 2014. Georg Bätzing became bishop in 2016 and was elected chairman of the German Bishops' Conference in 2020, playing a prominent role in the Synodal Way reform process.
