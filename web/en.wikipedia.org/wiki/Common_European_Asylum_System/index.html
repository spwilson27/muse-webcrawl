Common European Asylum System

The CEAS seeks common standards for asylum across the European Union.

The Common European Asylum System (CEAS) is a set of European Union laws and arrangements intended to harmonise asylum procedures, reception conditions, and qualification standards for international protection across EU member states, so that applicants receive similar treatment wherever they apply and "asylum shopping" is reduced.

Legal framework

The system rests on several instruments. The Dublin Regulation determines which member state is responsible for examining an application — generally the state of first entry — supported by the Eurodac fingerprint database. The Asylum Procedures Directive sets common procedures for granting and withdrawing refugee status; the Qualification Directive defines who qualifies as a refugee or for subsidiary protection; and the Reception Conditions Directive lays down minimum standards for housing, healthcare, and access to work for applicants. The European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA), formerly EASO, provides operational and technical support.

History

Cooperation began with the Dublin Convention of 1990. The Treaty of Amsterdam (1999) brought asylum into the EU framework, and the system's first phase (1999–2005) set minimum standards. A second phase, launched under the Hague and Stockholm programmes, recast the directives with the goal of a genuinely common system by 2012.

Crisis and reform

The arrival of more than a million asylum seekers in 2015 exposed deep weaknesses: frontline states such as Greece and Italy were overwhelmed, Dublin transfers largely stalled, and member states disagreed sharply over relocation quotas. After years of negotiation, the EU adopted the New Pact on Migration and Asylum in 2024, introducing mandatory solidarity mechanisms, faster border procedures, and a new Asylum and Migration Management Regulation to replace Dublin, with application from 2026.
