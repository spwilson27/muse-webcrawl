Frances Arnold

Frances Hamilton Arnold (born 25 July 1956) is an American chemical engineer and Nobel laureate. She is the Linus Pauling Professor of Chemical Engineering, Bioengineering and Biochemistry at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech). In 2018 she shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for pioneering the use of directed evolution to engineer enzymes.

Education and career

Arnold studied mechanical and aerospace engineering at Princeton University (1979) and earned a PhD in chemical engineering from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1985. After postdoctoral work she joined Caltech in 1986.

Directed evolution

Her method applies iterative rounds of mutation and screening to evolve proteins with useful properties — enzymes that work in organic solvents, catalyse novel reactions such as carbon–silicon bond formation, and support greener chemical manufacturing and renewable fuels. She co-founded companies including Gevo and Provivi.

Honours

Arnold received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation, the Millennium Technology Prize (2016), and election to all three US National Academies. In 2021 she became co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology.
