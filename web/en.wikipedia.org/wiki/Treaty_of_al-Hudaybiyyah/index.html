Treaty of al-Hudaybiyyah

DateMarch 628 CE (Dhu al-Qadah, 6 AH)
LocationAl-Hudaybiyyah, near Mecca
PartiesMuhammad / the Muslims of Medina; the Quraysh of Mecca

The Treaty of al-Hudaybiyyah (Arabic: صلح الحديبية) was an event that took place during the time of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. It was a pivotal treaty between Muhammad, representing the state of Medina, and the tribe of the Quraysh in Mecca in March 628 (corresponding to Dhu al-Qadah, AH 6).

Background

In 628, Muhammad set out from Medina with about 1,400 followers, in pilgrim garb and largely unarmed, intending to perform the Umrah (lesser pilgrimage) at Mecca. The Quraysh blocked his approach, and he encamped at al-Hudaybiyyah, on the edge of the sacred territory. After negotiations — and the Pledge of the Tree (Bay'at al-Ridwan), in which his followers swore loyalty when his envoy Uthman was rumoured killed — the parties concluded a truce.

Terms

  • A ten-year truce between the Muslims and the Quraysh;
  • The Muslims would return to Medina that year without performing the pilgrimage, but would be allowed to perform it the following year and remain in Mecca for three days;
  • Any Meccan who went over to Muhammad without his guardian's permission would be returned, while any Muslim going over to Mecca would not be returned;
  • Arab tribes were free to ally themselves with either side.

Significance

Although many of Muhammad's companions, notably Umar, initially saw the terms as humiliating, the Qur'an describes the event as a "clear victory" (fath). The treaty recognized the Muslim polity as an equal negotiating party, and the ensuing peace allowed Islam to spread widely. In 630, after the Quraysh's allies attacked a tribe allied to Muhammad in violation of the truce, Muhammad marched on Mecca and the city surrendered largely without bloodshed.
