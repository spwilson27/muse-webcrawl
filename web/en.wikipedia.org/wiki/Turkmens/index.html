The Turkmens (Turkmen: Türkmenler) are a Turkic ethnic group native to Central Asia, living mainly in Turkmenistan and in neighbouring parts of Iran and Afghanistan, with related communities — Iraqi Turkmens and Syrian Turkmens — in the Middle East. They speak Turkmen, an Oghuz Turkic language, and are traditionally known for carpet weaving and the Akhal-Teke horse.

History

The Turkmens descend largely from the Oghuz Turks who migrated westward from the Altai region from about the 10th century. Turkmen tribal groups such as the Teke, Yomut, Ersari, and Salor formed the backbone of the population of the Karakum region. Turkmen dynasties of Oghuz origin, including the Seljuks, shaped the history of Persia and Anatolia.

In the 19th century the Russian Empire conquered the Turkmen lands, culminating in the Battle of Geok Tepe in 1881. Under Soviet rule the Turkmen Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1924; Turkmenistan became independent in 1991.

Culture

Turkmen carpets, with their tribal göl medallions, are world-famous; five traditional carpet motifs appear on the national flag of Turkmenistan. Traditional society was organized by tribe, and despite collectivization and urbanization, tribal identity remains significant. The dutar (two-stringed lute) and the epic tradition of the dastan are central to Turkmen music and oral literature.
