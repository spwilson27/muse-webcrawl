The Battle of Nijmegen was fought from 17 to 20 September 1944 during Operation Market Garden in the Second World War. American paratroopers of the 82nd Airborne Division, later joined by British ground forces of XXX Corps, fought to capture the Dutch city of Nijmegen and its vital bridges over the Waal river — objectives needed for the Allied drive toward Arnhem.

Background

Operation Market Garden aimed to outflank the Siegfried Line by seizing a corridor of bridges in the Netherlands. The 82nd Airborne landed near Nijmegen on 17 September, taking the Groesbeek heights and the Grave bridge, but the Waal road and rail bridges in Nijmegen itself remained in German hands.

The Waal crossing

On 20 September, after Guards Armoured tanks and artillery arrived, paratroopers of the 3rd Battalion, 504th Parachute Infantry Regiment crossed the Waal in canvas assault boats under heavy fire — an action remembered as one of the boldest of the war — and attacked the bridges from the north bank while British tanks and infantry fought through the city from the south. Both bridges were captured intact shortly before the Germans could demolish them.

Aftermath

Nijmegen was liberated, but the delay meant the corridor to Arnhem could not be opened in time to save the British 1st Airborne Division. The Nijmegen salient became a static front through the winter of 1944–45.
