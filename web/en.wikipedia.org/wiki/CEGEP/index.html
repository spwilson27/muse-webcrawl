A CEGEP (French: Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel, pronounced [seʒɛp]) is a publicly funded post-secondary level of education unique to the province of Quebec, Canada. CEGEPs form the first stage of higher education in Quebec, bridging secondary school and university.

A CEGEP campus in Quebec.

History

The CEGEP system was created in 1967 following the recommendations of the Parent Commission (1963–1966), which undertook a sweeping reform of education in Quebec during the Quiet Revolution. The first CEGEPs opened their doors in September 1967, replacing a patchwork of classical colleges, technical institutes, and teacher-training schools.

The reform aimed to democratize access to higher education by making the first two years of post-secondary study free of tuition for Quebec residents and universally accessible across the province's regions.

Programs of study

CEGEPs offer two broad types of programs. Pre-university programs last two years and prepare students for university studies; they include programs in natural sciences, social science, arts and letters, and computer science and mathematics. Technical programs last three years and lead directly to the labour market in fields such as nursing, engineering technology, business administration, and information technology.

Upon completion, students receive a Diploma of College Studies (DEC; French: diplôme d'études collégiales), which is required for admission to Quebec universities. Because Quebec students complete secondary school after Grade 11, the CEGEP years mean Quebec bachelor's degrees are typically three years rather than four.

Structure and notable institutions

There are 48 public CEGEPs in Quebec, most teaching in French, with five English-language institutions, including Dawson College, Vanier College, and John Abbott College in the Montreal area. Private colleges and a small number of public institutions also deliver college-level programs outside the CEGEP network.
