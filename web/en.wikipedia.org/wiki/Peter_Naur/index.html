Peter Naur (1928–2016) was a Danish computer scientist, a pioneer of the discipline, who won the Turing Award in 2005 for fundamental contributions to programming language design and the definition of ALGOL 60, to compiler design, and to the art and practice of computer programming.

Work

Trained as an astronomer — he completed a doctorate in astronomy in 1957 and worked on minor-planet computations — Naur moved into computing at Regnecentralen, Denmark's first computer company. As editor of the Report on the Algorithmic Language ALGOL 60, he introduced the notation, developed with John Backus, that became known as Backus–Naur form (BNF) for specifying programming-language syntax — one of the foundational tools of computer science.

Naur coined the term datalogy (Danish: datalogi) for the science of data and data processes, and in 1969 became the first professor of datalogy at the University of Copenhagen. He was a co-author of influential work on structured programming and software engineering (the 1968 NATO conference era), though he later became a critic of formalist and reductionist views of programming, arguing in essays such as "Programming as Theory Building" (1985) that programming is fundamentally an activity of human understanding. His later writings explored psychology and the philosophy of science.
