Cintamani

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cintāmaṇi (Sanskrit: चिन्तामणि, "wish-fulfilling jewel") is a magical jewel in Hindu and Buddhist traditions said to grant whatever its possessor wishes. It is often identified with the maṇi (jewel) held by deities and bodhisattvas and is compared in the West to the philosopher's stone of alchemy.

In Hinduism

The cintāmaṇi is associated with the gods Vishnu and Ganesha and with the serpent king Nāga Mani; in some tellings it fell to Earth or is kept in the underwater palace of the nāgas. In the Yoga Vasistha and other texts it figures in parables about desire and contentment — including the story of its identification by the sage, and meditations in which the true wish-fulfilling jewel is the contented mind itself.

In Buddhism

In Mahayana Buddhism the jewel (nyoi-hōju in Japanese) symbolises the Buddha's teachings and the awakened mind that fulfils all wholesome wishes. It is an attribute of the bodhisattvas Kṣitigarbha and Avalokiteśvara (often held by the wish-granting form Cintāmaṇicakra) and appears atop banners, stupas and flaming triple-jewel emblems in East and Central Asian art.

Cultural echoes

The motif survives widely: as a decorative pattern on Ottoman textiles and tiles (the çintamani motif of circles), as the "Tama" of Indonesian legend, and in modern fantasy games and fiction as a wish-granting stone.
