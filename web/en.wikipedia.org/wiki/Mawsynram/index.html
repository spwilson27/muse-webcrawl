Mawsynram

Mawsynram is a village in the East Khasi Hills district of Meghalaya state in north-eastern India, about 65 kilometres from Shillong. It is widely reported to be the wettest place on Earth, with an average annual rainfall of about 11,872 millimetres (467 inches), a figure recognised by Guinness World Records.

Climate

Mawsynram's extreme rainfall results from its position on the windward slopes of the Khasi Hills. During the summer monsoon, moisture-laden winds from the Bay of Bengal are funnelled up the Brahmaputra and Meghna valleys and forced to rise over the hills, where the air cools and its moisture condenses and falls as prolonged heavy rain. The village's rival for the "wettest place" title is nearby Cherrapunji (Sohra), about 15 kilometres to the east, which holds records for the most rain in a single year and calendar month.

Life in the rain

Residents adapt with thick bamboo-and-grass rain covers called knups, soundproofing of roofs against the deafening downpours, and careful stockpiling of supplies before the monsoon. Paradoxically, winters are dry and drinking water can be scarce. The surrounding landscape of waterfalls, living root bridges and limestone caves — including Krem Puri, among the longest sandstone caves in the world — draws visitors in the drier months.
