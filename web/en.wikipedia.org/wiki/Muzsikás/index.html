Muzsikás

Muzsikás is a Hungarian folk music ensemble formed in 1973, internationally the best-known representative of Hungarian folk music. The group emerged from the táncház ("dance house") movement, which revived the traditional village dance music of Hungary and Transylvania by learning directly from elderly village musicians. Muzsikás performs with traditional instruments — violin, viola (kontra), double bass, cimbalom, and various flutes and pipes — and for many years collaborated with the singer Márta Sebestyén, whose voice brought the band to worldwide attention.

Career

The band's members, including Mihály Sipos, Sándor Csoóri Jr., Péter Éri and Dániel Hamar, researched and collected music in Hungarian-speaking villages, especially in Transylvania (Romania), where the traditions had survived most strongly. Muzsikás has released numerous albums, including The Bartók Album (1999), which connects Béla Bartók's field recordings with the living tradition, and appeared on the soundtrack of the film The English Patient (1996) with Sebestyén. The group has toured worldwide, performed with classical orchestras and string quartets, and received Hungary's Kossuth Prize in 1999, the highest Hungarian state award for the arts.
