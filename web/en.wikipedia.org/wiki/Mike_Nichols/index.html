Mike Nichols

Mike Nichols (born Mikhail Igor Peschkowsky; November 6, 1931 – November 19, 2014) was a German-born American film and theatre director, producer, actor, and comedian. He is one of the few people to have won all four major American entertainment awards — an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony (an "EGOT") — and he directed landmark works including Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf? (1966), The Graduate (1967), and the Broadway premiere of Barefoot in the Park (1963).

Early life and comedy career

Born in Berlin to a Russian-Jewish family, Nichols emigrated to the United States as a child in 1939, fleeing Nazi Germany. At the University of Chicago he met Elaine May, and the two formed the improvisational comedy duo Nichols and May, whose albums and Broadway show An Evening with Mike Nichols and Elaine May (1960) made them stars and helped define modern sketch comedy.

Director

Nichols became Broadway's most sought-after director with a string of hits including Barefoot in the Park, The Odd Couple, and later Annie and Spamalot. His film debut, Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf? (1966), earned 13 Oscar nominations, and The Graduate (1967) won him the Academy Award for Best Director and became a defining film of its generation. Later films include Carnal Knowledge (1971), Silkwood (1983), Working Girl (1988), The Birdcage (1996), Primary Colors (1998), Closer (2004), and Charlie Wilson's War (2007). For television he directed the acclaimed adaptations Wit (2001) and Angels in America (2003).

Legacy

Nichols won nine Tony Awards, three Emmys, and a Grammy, and received the AFI Life Achievement Award in 2010. He was married to journalist Diane Sawyer from 1988 until his death from a heart attack in 2014.
