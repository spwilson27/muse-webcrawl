Magic constant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The magic constant or magic sum of a magic square is the sum of numbers in any row, column, or diagonal of the magic square. For example, the magic square shown to the right has a magic constant of 15. For a normal magic square of order n — that is, a square containing the numbers 1, 2, ..., n2 — the magic constant is given by the formula:

M = n(n2 + 1)/2.

For normal magic squares of orders 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, ... the magic constants are, respectively: 1, 5, 15, 34, 65, 111, 175, 260, ... There is no normal magic square of order 2 using distinct entries 1..4 in the usual sense, so the value 5 is the formal value of the formula at n = 2.

Magic cubes and hypercubes

The concept extends to other shapes. For a normal magic cube of order n, containing the numbers 1 through n3, the magic constant is n(n3 + 1)/2. More generally, for a magic hypercube of dimension d and order n, the constant is n(nd + 1)/2, provided all lines through the cells in the standard directions sum to the same value.
