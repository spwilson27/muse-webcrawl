Chudnovsky algorithm

David and Gregory Chudnovsky

The Chudnovsky algorithm is a fast method for calculating the digits of π. It was published by the Chudnovsky brothers in 1988. The algorithm is based on a rapidly converging hypergeometric series similar to some of Srinivasa Ramanujan's formulae for π.

Each term of the series adds roughly fourteen decimal digits, making the algorithm dramatically faster per term than earlier series. Combined with binary splitting techniques, it has been used in most record-breaking computations of π, including computations reaching tens of trillions of digits in the twenty-first century.

The formula

The algorithm rests on the identity involving the constant 426880√10005 divided by an infinite sum whose terms involve factorials and powers of 640320 — a number related to the Heegner number 163 and the near-integer value of eπ√163. The huge denominator constant gives the series its rapid convergence.

Applications

The Chudnovsky algorithm is implemented in software such as y-cruncher, which has set multiple world records for digits of π. The brothers themselves used a home-built supercomputer, nicknamed "m zero", in their New York apartment to compute π to over two billion digits in the early 1990s.
