Inari Ōkami

Inari Ōkami (Japanese: 稲荷大神), also called Ō-Inari-san, is the Japanese kami (deity) of foxes, fertility, rice, tea and sake, agriculture and industry, general prosperity and worldly success. Inari is among the most popular and widely worshipped kami in Shinto, with roughly 32,000 shrines — about a third of all Shinto shrines in Japan — dedicated to the deity, the head shrine being Fushimi Inari-taisha in Kyoto.

Depiction

Inari is depicted variously as male, female, or androgynous, sometimes as an old man, a beautiful woman, or a bodhisattva. Foxes (kitsune) serve as Inari's messengers and are honoured with offerings of fried tofu (aburaage), said to be their favourite food, which gave its name to the dish inarizushi.

Worship

Worship of Inari dates to at least the eighth century, when the Fushimi shrine was founded in 711. Red torii gates and vermilion shrine buildings are characteristic of Inari shrines. Over time the kami's remit expanded from rice and agriculture to commerce, manufacturing, and general good fortune.
