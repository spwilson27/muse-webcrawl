Moselle Franconian (German: Moselfränkisch, Moselle Franconian: Muselfränkesch) is a group of West Central German dialects spoken along the Moselle River in western Germany (southern Rhineland-Palatinate and the Saarland), in Luxembourg, in the French department of Moselle in Lorraine, and in a small adjacent area of Belgium. It belongs to the Central Franconian dialect continuum and forms a dialect continuum with Ripuarian to the north and Rhine Franconian to the south.

Classification and varieties

Moselle Franconian is distinguished from neighbouring Rhine Franconian by features of the High German consonant shift, in particular the pronunciation of words such as dat/das ("that") and Dorf ("village"): Moselle Franconian lies west of the Dorf–Dorp isogloss in parts of its area and is traditionally divided from Ripuarian by the Bad Honnef line. Local varieties are associated with the cities and regions of Trier, the Eifel, the Hunsrück, the Saarland, and Wittlich. The variety spoken in the Siegerland and parts of the Westerwald is transitional.

Luxembourgish

The most prominent Moselle Franconian variety is Luxembourgish (Lëtzebuergesch), spoken in the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and the Arelerland region of Belgium. Unusually for a dialect of the group, Luxembourgish was standardized and, in 1984, declared the national language of Luxembourg, where it is used in administration, media, and daily life alongside French and German. Varieties spoken just across the German and French borders — for example in the area around Thionville in France — are closely related but have no comparable official status.

Status and use

In Germany and France, Moselle Franconian varieties are regional dialects used mainly in informal and rural settings, and like many German dialects they have been receding under pressure from Standard German (and, in France, from French), though they remain vigorous in some communities and in local literature, theatre, and carnival traditions. The dialects are not normally written except in local contexts, where spellings follow German or, in Luxembourg, the official Luxembourgish orthography.
