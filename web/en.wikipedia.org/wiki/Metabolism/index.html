Metabolism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Metabolism is the set of chemical reactions in living organisms that sustains life. It has two complementary halves: catabolism, breaking down molecules to release energy, and anabolism, using energy to build the cell's components — proteins, nucleic acids, carbohydrates and lipids.

Energy currency

Nearly all cellular work is powered by ATP (adenosine triphosphate), produced mainly by cellular respiration in the cytoplasm and mitochondria, and in plants additionally by photosynthesis in chloroplasts. Enzymes — mostly proteins — catalyse each step of metabolic pathways such as glycolysis, the citric-acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

Regulation and disorder

Metabolism is tightly regulated by hormones such as insulin and by feedback inhibition within pathways, maintaining homeostasis. Inherited enzyme defects cause inborn errors of metabolism (the term was coined by Archibald Garrod in 1908); acquired metabolic dysfunction underlies obesity, type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome. The sum of an organism's energy expenditure is its metabolic rate.
