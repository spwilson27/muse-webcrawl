Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions

FieldMathematical optimisation
Named afterWilliam Karush; Harold Kuhn; Albert Tucker
IntroducedKarush 1939; Kuhn & Tucker 1951
GeneralisesLagrange multipliers

In mathematical optimisation, the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions (KKT conditions) are first-order necessary conditions for a solution in nonlinear programming to be optimal, provided certain regularity (constraint qualification) conditions hold. They generalise the method of Lagrange multipliers, which handles only equality constraints, to problems with inequality constraints.

Statement

For a problem of minimising f(x) subject to inequality constraints gi(x) ≤ 0 and equality constraints hj(x) = 0, the KKT conditions require: stationarity of the Lagrangian (the objective gradient is balanced by constraint gradients weighted by multipliers), primal feasibility, dual feasibility (inequality multipliers are non-negative), and complementary slackness (each inequality multiplier is zero unless its constraint is active at the optimum). Under convexity the conditions are also sufficient for global optimality.

History and use

The conditions appeared in William Karush's 1939 master's thesis and were published by Harold Kuhn and Albert Tucker in 1951. They are foundational in operations research, economics (where the multipliers are shadow prices), control theory and machine learning — notably in the derivation of support vector machines.
