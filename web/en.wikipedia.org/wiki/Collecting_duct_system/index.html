Collecting duct system

Collecting duct system
Part of: Kidney (nephron outflow)
Segments: Connecting tubule, cortical and medullary collecting ducts, papillary duct
Key hormones: Aldosterone, vasopressin (ADH)

The collecting duct system of the kidney is the series of tubules and ducts that carry urine from the nephrons to the renal pelvis. Although embryologically distinct from the nephron proper, it performs the final adjustments to urine volume and composition under hormonal control.

Segments

The system begins with the connecting tubules, which join several distal convoluted tubules to cortical collecting ducts. These descend through the medulla as medullary collecting ducts, merging into the larger papillary ducts (ducts of Bellini), which open at the renal papilla into a minor calyx.

Cell types and function

Principal cells reabsorb sodium through epithelial sodium channels and secrete potassium, processes regulated by aldosterone; they also reabsorb water through aquaporin-2 channels inserted in response to vasopressin. Intercalated cells regulate acid–base balance by secreting hydrogen ions or bicarbonate. Through these mechanisms the collecting duct fine-tunes salt, water, potassium, and pH balance.

Clinical significance

Disorders of the collecting duct include nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, in which the duct fails to respond to vasopressin; renal tubular acidosis; and Liddle syndrome, caused by overactive sodium channels. Diuretics such as amiloride act on this segment.
