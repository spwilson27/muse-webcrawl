Santonio Holmes Jr. (born 3 March 1984) is an American former professional football wide receiver who played in the National Football League (NFL) for nine seasons. He is best remembered for his game-winning touchdown catch for the Pittsburgh Steelers in Super Bowl XLIII, for which he was named Super Bowl MVP.

College career

Holmes grew up in Belle Glade, Florida, and played college football at Ohio State. With the Buckeyes he became a leading receiver and return man, catching 140 passes for over 2,300 yards in his career, and was part of the team that won the 2002 national championship as a freshman (redshirted during the title season).

Professional career

The Pittsburgh Steelers selected Holmes in the first round (25th overall) of the 2006 NFL draft. He developed into the Steelers' primary deep threat. In Super Bowl XLIII against the Arizona Cardinals on 1 February 2009, Holmes caught nine passes for 131 yards, including the championship-winning six-yard touchdown from Ben Roethlisberger with 35 seconds remaining — a toe-tapping catch in the back corner of the end zone widely considered one of the greatest plays in Super Bowl history. He was voted the game's Most Valuable Player.

In 2010 Holmes was traded to the New York Jets, where he signed a large contract and made further notable game-winning plays, but injuries (including a Lisfranc foot injury in 2012) and reported dressing-room friction curtailed his effectiveness. He finished his career with brief stints with the Oakland Raiders and Chicago Bears in 2014. He formally retired as a Steeler in 2017.

Career highlights

Holmes finished his career with more than 6,000 receiving yards and 36 touchdowns, a Super Bowl championship and a Super Bowl MVP award.
