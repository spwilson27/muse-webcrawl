Tricolon

A tricolon is a rhetorical device consisting of a series of three parallel words, phrases or clauses. The three elements are often similar in length and grammatical structure, and the device lends balance, rhythm and memorability to a sentence. In classical rhetoric a tricolon whose members grow in length or intensity is called a tricolon crescens.

Examples

Famous tricolons include Julius Caesar's "Veni, vidi, vici" ("I came, I saw, I conquered"), the French revolutionary motto "Liberté, égalité, fraternité", and Abraham Lincoln's "government of the people, by the people, for the people" in the Gettysburg Address. The "rule of three" in storytelling and comedy — three wishes, three brothers, three attempts — draws on the same cognitive appeal of triplets.

Related figures

A two-member counterpart is the dicolon; a longer series is a tetracolon. When the three members are of identical structure and roughly equal length the figure overlaps with isocolon; when arranged in ascending climax it is a form of auxesis. Hendiatris — expressing one idea in three words, as in "wine, women and song" — is closely related.
