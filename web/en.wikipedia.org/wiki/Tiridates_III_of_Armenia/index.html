Tiridates III (Armenian: Տրդատ Գ, Trdat III; c. 250s – c. 330), also known as Tiridates the Great, was king of Arsacid Armenia from about 298 to 330 AD. He is celebrated as the king who proclaimed Christianity the state religion of Armenia — conventionally dated to 301 AD — making Armenia the first nation in history to adopt Christianity officially, a decade before Constantine's toleration of the faith in the Roman Empire.

Early life and reign

Tiridates was the son of King Khosrov II, who was assassinated, after which the Sassanid Persians occupied Armenia. The young prince was taken to Rome and raised there. With Roman support, and under arrangements confirmed after Diocletian's victory over Persia and the Peace of Nisibis (298/299), Tiridates was restored to the Armenian throne and reigned as a Roman client king. His reign was a constant balancing act between Rome and Sassanid Persia, the two superpowers contending for Armenia.

Conversion

According to the Armenian historian Agathangelos, Tiridates initially persecuted Christians and imprisoned the preacher Gregory the Illuminator in the pit of Khor Virap for some thirteen or fourteen years. Stricken with an affliction (tradition says he was turned into the likeness of a boar), the king was healed and converted after Gregory was released and prayed for him. Tiridates then decreed Christianity the religion of the state; Gregory was consecrated as the first Catholicos, and the cathedral of Etchmiadzin was built at Vagharshapat, traditionally on the site where Gregory saw Christ descend in a vision. The date of the conversion is debated (301 and 314 are the leading candidates), but Armenia's claim to be the first Christian state is traditional and widely repeated.

Legacy

Tiridates is venerated as a saint in the Armenian Apostolic Church together with Gregory the Illuminator and the virgin martyrs Hripsime and Gayane, whose churches he was said to have founded. His conversion permanently oriented Armenian civilization toward Christianity and created one of the oldest national churches in the world.
