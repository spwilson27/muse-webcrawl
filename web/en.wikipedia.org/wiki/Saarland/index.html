Saarland (German: das Saarland; French: Sarre) is a state (Land) of Germany in the country's southwest, bordering France and Rhineland-Palatinate. With an area of 2,570 km² and about one million inhabitants, it is the smallest German state by area apart from the city-states of Berlin, Hamburg and Bremen, and the smallest by population. Its capital and largest city is Saarbrücken. The state is named after the Saar river, a tributary of the Moselle.

Geography

Saarland is largely hilly and forested, forming part of the Saar-Nahe hills and the northern foothills of the Vosges. Its best-known natural landmark is the Saarschleife, a dramatic incised meander of the Saar near Mettlach. Administrative districts are Merzig-Wadern, Neunkirchen, Regionalverband Saarbrücken, Saarlouis, Saarpfalz-Kreis and St. Wendel.

History

The region's history has been shaped by its coal and steel industries and by its position on the Franco-German border. After the First World War, the Territory of the Saar Basin was administered by the League of Nations for 15 years; a 1935 plebiscite returned it to Germany. After the Second World War, Saarland became a French protectorate (the Saar Protectorate, 1947–1956), economically linked to France, issuing its own stamps and fielding its own football team and Olympic team in 1952. In a referendum on 23 October 1955, voters rejected a proposed "European Statute" for the Saar, and the territory acceded to the Federal Republic of Germany on 1 January 1957 — an event sometimes called the "little reunification". Monetary union with West Germany followed on 6 July 1959 ("Tag X").

Economy

Coal mining, once the backbone of the Saar economy, ended in 2012 with the closure of the last mine at Ensdorf. Steel (Saarstahl, Dillinger Hütte) remains important, alongside automotive suppliers, ceramics (Villeroy & Boch, founded in Mettlach), and a growing services and research sector centred on Saarland University, known for computer science.

Culture

The Saarland dialect belongs to the Moselle Franconian and Rhine Franconian groups. Regional specialities include Dibbelabbes and Schwenker (marinated grilled pork). The Völklingen Ironworks, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserves a complete 19th–20th-century pig-iron plant. Cross-border ties with French Lorraine and Luxembourg remain close, and French is widely taught.
