The Lourdes apparitions are a series of eighteen reported visions of the Virgin Mary experienced in 1858 by fourteen-year-old Bernadette Soubirous at the grotto of Massabielle near Lourdes in southwestern France. The apparitions made Lourdes one of the most important sites of Catholic pilgrimage in the world.

The apparitions

On 11 February 1858, Bernadette reported seeing a beautiful lady in a hollow of the rock at Massabielle. Over the following months she returned regularly; crowds grew to thousands. During the ninth apparition, Bernadette dug in the earth and uncovered a spring, whose water became central to the Lourdes devotion. At the sixteenth apparition, on 25 March, the lady identified herself in the local dialect: "Que soy era Immaculada Councepciou" — "I am the Immaculate Conception" — a dogma proclaimed only four years earlier, which Bernadette could not have been expected to know.

Recognition and pilgrimage

After a canonical investigation, the Bishop of Tarbes declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1862. The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes was built over the grotto, and millions of pilgrims, many of them sick people seeking healing, visit each year. The Catholic Church has officially recognised dozens of miraculous cures at Lourdes after medical scrutiny.
