Mitte

Mitte (German for "middle" or "centre") is the central borough of Berlin, created in 2001 by merging the former boroughs of Mitte, Tiergarten, and Wedding. It is also the name of the locality (Ortsteil) of Mitte within the borough, corresponding to Berlin's historic centre.

Sights

The locality contains many of Berlin's best-known landmarks: the Brandenburg Gate and Unter den Linden, Museum Island with the Pergamon Museum and Berlin Cathedral, Alexanderplatz with the Fernsehturm (TV tower), Potsdamer Platz, the Reichstag and government quarter, and Hackescher Markt.

History

The old borough of Mitte lay in East Berlin during the division of the city and contained the historic core around the medieval twin towns of Berlin and Cölln.
