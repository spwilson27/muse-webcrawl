CONCACAF W Championship

The CONCACAF W Championship is an association football competition organized by CONCACAF that is contested by the senior women's national teams of North America, Central America and the Caribbean. It serves as the confederation's championship and as the qualifying tournament for the FIFA Women's World Cup and the Olympic football tournament.

History

The tournament began in 1991 as the CONCACAF Women's Championship, held in Haiti ahead of the first FIFA Women's World Cup. It was later known as the CONCACAF Women's Gold Cup during the 2000s. The United States has been the dominant team, winning the great majority of editions, with Canada the only other champion for many years.

The 2022 edition, held in Monterrey, Mexico, was the first under the CONCACAF W Championship name as part of a revamped women's national team calendar that also introduced the CONCACAF W Gold Cup (first played in 2024). The United States won the 2022 final 1–0 over Canada; both finalists, along with semifinalists Costa Rica and Jamaica, qualified directly for the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup, and the champion also qualified for the 2024 Summer Olympics.

Results

Champions by edition include the United States (1991, 1993, 1994, 2000, 2002, 2006, 2014, 2018, 2022) and Canada (1998, 2010).
