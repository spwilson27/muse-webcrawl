OSCAR protocol

OSCAR (Open System for CommunicAtion in Realtime) is a proprietary instant messaging protocol developed by AOL. It powered AOL Instant Messenger (AIM) and ICQ, for many years two of the most popular instant messaging services in the world.

Overview

OSCAR is a binary protocol organised around frames called FLAPs, within which commands are sent as SNACs (Simple Network Atomic Communication) grouped by "foodgroups" covering functions such as sign-on, buddy list management, presence, and message delivery. Clients typically connected to a login server for authentication before being directed to a main "BOS" (Basic OSCAR Service) server, with auxiliary services such as chat rooms handled by separate servers.

Reverse engineering and decline

Although AOL kept the protocol proprietary, developers reverse-engineered it to build third-party and multi-protocol clients such as Pidgin and Miranda IM, prompting a long-running contest between AOL and independent client authors. AOL published partial specifications in 2008. Usage collapsed with the decline of AIM, which was shut down in December 2017; ICQ, by then owned by another company, closed in 2024.
