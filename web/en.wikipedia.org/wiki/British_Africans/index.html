British Africans

British Africans are citizens or residents of the United Kingdom whose ancestry lies in Africa, particularly sub-Saharan Africa. In census and official statistics they form the core of the "Black African" category within the wider Black British population. The 2021 census recorded roughly 1.5 million Black African residents in England and Wales (about 2.5 per cent of the population), making Black Africans one of the fastest-growing ethnic groups in the UK, with large communities of Nigerian, Ghanaian, Somali, Zimbabwean, Kenyan and South African heritage.

History

Africans have lived in Britain since Roman times, and a Black presence grew in the port cities of the 18th and 19th centuries, including freed slaves, sailors, and students. Numbers expanded substantially after the Second World War and especially from the 1980s–2000s through migration from Nigeria and Ghana, asylum flows from Somalia and Zimbabwe, and professional and student migration under post-2000 immigration policy.

Community and culture

British Africans are prominent in the NHS, higher education, business, sport, and the arts; notable figures include footballers, musicians, and writers such as Booker Prize winner Bernardine Evaristo. London boroughs such as Peckham ("Little Lagos") and Woolwich host major West African commercial and cultural scenes, while Somali communities are significant in London, Birmingham, Cardiff and Sheffield. The umbrella category sits alongside Black Caribbean and "Black British" identities within UK diversity statistics.
