Mollen Commission

The Mollen Commission was a commission established in 1992 by New York City mayor David Dinkins to investigate corruption in the New York City Police Department. It was chaired by Milton Mollen, a former judge and deputy mayor. The commission's full name was the Commission to Investigate Allegations of Police Corruption and the Anti-Corruption Procedures of the Police Department.

Background

The commission was created in the wake of the arrest of officer Michael Dowd on Long Island in 1992 on drug-related charges, which exposed extensive corruption involving officers in Brooklyn and elsewhere who stole money and drugs from dealers, dealt narcotics, and brutalized suspects. The scandal suggested that the department's internal anti-corruption mechanisms had failed.

Findings

The commission held public hearings in 1993, hearing testimony from corrupt officers, including Dowd, who described crews of officers engaging in theft, drug dealing, and brutality, often under the cover of drug enforcement. Its final report, issued in 1994, concluded that corruption in the department was characterized not so much by individual officers accepting bribes as by groups of officers committing crimes, and that the department's internal affairs apparatus had been deliberately weakened and discouraged from pursuing corruption. The report recommended creating a permanent external monitoring body — leading to the establishment of the Commission to Combat Police Corruption in 1995 — and extensive reforms of internal investigations, recruitment, and supervision.
