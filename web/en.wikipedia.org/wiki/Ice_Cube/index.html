Ice Cube

O'Shea Jackson (born June 15, 1969), known professionally as Ice Cube, is an American rapper, songwriter, actor, and film producer. He first gained recognition as the primary lyricist of the pioneering gangsta rap group N.W.A, whose 1988 album Straight Outta Compton is widely credited with popularizing gangsta rap. He left the group in 1989 and launched a highly successful solo career alongside an expanding career in film.

Career

Jackson was born in Los Angeles, California, and began writing rhymes as a teenager. With Dr. Dre and Eazy-E he formed N.W.A, writing much of the group's most famous material, including "Straight Outta Compton" and "Fuck tha Police," as well as much of Eazy-E's debut album. Departing over a royalty dispute, he recorded the solo albums AmeriKKKa's Most Wanted (1990) and Death Certificate (1991), both commercial successes and enduring, controversial classics of political rap.

Film

Ice Cube made his acting debut in John Singleton's Boyz n the Hood (1991). He later co-wrote and starred in the comedy Friday (1995), which spawned sequels, and headlined the Barbershop and Are We There Yet? franchises, the action film xXx: State of the Union, and the comedies 21 Jump Street and Ride Along. Through his company Cube Vision he has produced many of his own films, and he co-produced the N.W.A biopic Straight Outta Compton (2015), in which he was portrayed by his son, O'Shea Jackson Jr.

Legacy

Ice Cube is consistently ranked among the greatest rappers of all time, noted for his incisive, confrontational lyricism. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of N.W.A in 2016 and received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2017. He also co-founded the BIG3 three-on-three basketball league in 2017.
