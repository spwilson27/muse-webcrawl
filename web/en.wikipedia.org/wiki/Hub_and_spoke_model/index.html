Hub and spoke model

The hub and spoke arrangement.

The hub and spoke model is a network design in which connections run between peripheral nodes (spokes) and one or more central nodes (hubs), rather than directly between every pair of nodes. It takes its name from the bicycle or cart wheel, in which spokes link the rim to the central hub. The design is widely used in transportation, logistics, computing and organisational theory.

Principle

For n locations, a fully connected point-to-point network requires on the order of n(n−1)/2 links, while a hub and spoke network needs only n−1 links to connect every location through the hub. Consolidating flows at the hub permits larger vehicles, fuller loads and more frequent departures on each spoke, at the price of an intermediate transfer and dependence on the hub's reliability.

Uses

  • Air transport: airlines route passengers through hub airports; parcel carriers sort freight overnight at central hubs.
  • Computing: the star topology and many client–server and cloud architectures are hub and spoke designs.
  • Public services: hospital networks, university systems and retail chains concentrate specialist functions at hub sites serving local spokes.

Criticism

Critics note that hub and spoke systems can waste distance (routing via a hub may be far longer than the direct path), concentrate congestion and risk at the hub, and reduce service quality for spoke-to-spoke journeys. Pure hub and spoke networks are therefore often blended with some direct point-to-point links.
