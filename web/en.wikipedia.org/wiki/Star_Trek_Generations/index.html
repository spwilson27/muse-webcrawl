Star Trek Generations

Star Trek Generations is a 1994 American science fiction film directed by David Carson, the seventh feature film in the Star Trek franchise and the first to feature the cast of Star Trek: The Next Generation. It serves as a bridge between the original series films and the Next Generation era, and is notable for the death of Captain James T. Kirk.

Plot

In 2293, the newly commissioned USS Enterprise-B rescues El-Aurian refugees from an energy ribbon called the Nexus; Captain Kirk (William Shatner) is apparently killed during the rescue. Seventy-eight years later, Captain Jean-Luc Picard (Patrick Stewart) and the Enterprise-D confront Dr. Tolian Soran (Malcolm McDowell), an El-Aurian obsessed with returning to the timeless paradise of the Nexus — even if it means destroying inhabited star systems to alter the ribbon's course.

Picard, drawn into the Nexus, finds Kirk alive there and persuades him to return and help stop Soran on Veridian III. The two captains defeat Soran, but Kirk is fatally injured, dying with the words "It was fun." The Enterprise-D is also destroyed after a battle with a Klingon bird-of-prey, its saucer section crash-landing on the planet.

Production and reception

The screenplay was written by Ronald D. Moore and Brannon Braga. Production designer Herman Zimmerman and cinematographer John A. Alonzo gave the film a warmer, more cinematic look than the television series. The film earned roughly $118 million worldwide and received mixed reviews, though the meeting of the two iconic captains was widely seen as its centerpiece.
