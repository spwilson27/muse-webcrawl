Cornus

Cornus is a genus of about 30–60 species of woody plants in the family Cornaceae, commonly known as dogwoods, which can generally be distinguished by their blossoms, berries, and distinctive bark. Most are deciduous trees or shrubs, but a few are nearly herbaceous perennial subshrubs, and some are evergreen.

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Plantae

Order: Cornales

Family: Cornaceae

Genus: Cornus L.

Description

Dogwoods typically have opposite, simple leaves and small flowers often surrounded by conspicuous petal-like bracts in the flowering species, such as the eastern North American Cornus florida and the Pacific dogwood Cornus nuttallii. Their fruits are drupes eaten by many birds and mammals. Several species, including the red-osier dogwood and Cornelian cherry (Cornus mas), are widely planted as ornamentals; Cornus mas is also grown for its edible fruit.

Distribution

The genus is widely distributed in temperate and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with diversity centres in eastern Asia and North America.
