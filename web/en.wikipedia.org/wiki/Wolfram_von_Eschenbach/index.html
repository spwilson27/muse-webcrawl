Wolfram von Eschenbach

Wolfram von Eschenbach (c. 1160/80 – c. 1220) was a German knight and poet, regarded as one of the greatest epic poets of medieval German literature. He is the author of Parzival, the most famous German Arthurian romance, based loosely on Chrétien de Troyes's unfinished Perceval, the Story of the Grail.

Life

Little is known of Wolfram's life beyond what his works reveal. He came from Eschenbach in Franconia (now Wolframs-Eschenbach, renamed for him in 1917), was a ministerialis in the service of local lords, and spent time at the court of Hermann I, Landgrave of Thuringia, the great literary patron of the Wartburg. He claimed — perhaps as a literary pose — that he could neither read nor write, having his works read to him and dictating his poems.

Works

Parzival, written in the first decade of the 13th century, runs to nearly 25,000 lines and follows the hero Parzival from naive folly through failure at the Grail castle to a hard-won maturity, interwoven with the adventures of Gawain. Wolfram's Holy Grail is famously a stone rather than a cup. His other works are the unfinished crusading epic Willehalm, about William of Orange's wars against the Saracens, the fragmentary Titurel, and a handful of lyric songs (Tagelieder). Richard Wagner's opera Parsifal drew on Wolfram's poem.
