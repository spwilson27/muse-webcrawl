Mount Tabor (Hebrew: הר תבור, Har Tavor; Arabic: جبل الطور) is a hill rising about 588 metres above sea level at the eastern end of the Jezreel Valley in Lower Galilee, Israel, some 18 km west of the Sea of Galilee. In Christian tradition it has long been identified as the site of the Transfiguration of Jesus.

History

Mount Tabor formed a boundary point between the territories of the Israelite tribes of Zebulun, Issachar and Naphtali. In the Book of Judges, the prophetess Deborah summoned Barak to gather his forces at Tabor before the battle against Sisera's army in the valley below. Its commanding position over the Via Maris trade route made the summit a natural fortress; Hellenistic and Roman-period fortifications stood there, and Josephus fortified it during the First Jewish–Roman War before it fell to Vespasian's forces in 67 CE.

In June 1799, during the French campaign in Egypt and Syria, Napoleon's general Kléber fought the Battle of Mount Tabor against a much larger Ottoman army in the plain beneath the mountain, an engagement relieved by Napoleon himself with a small division.

Religious significance

Although the Gospels do not name the "high mountain" of the Transfiguration, an early Christian tradition — firmly established by the 4th century and endorsed by figures such as Cyril of Jerusalem and Jerome — identified it with Mount Tabor. Pilgrims were ascending it within a few centuries of the event, and Byzantine churches were built on the summit.

Church of the Transfiguration

The summit plateau today holds two monasteries. The Franciscan Church of the Transfiguration, completed in 1924 to designs by the Italian architect Antonio Barluzzi, is built over the remains of Byzantine and Crusader churches; its twin towers are said to evoke the "three tabernacles" Peter proposed to build. Nearby stands a Greek Orthodox monastery also dedicated to the Transfiguration. The summit is reached by a steep, winding road with numerous hairpin bends.

Geography and ecology

The mountain is an isolated, almost perfectly domed hill of limestone rising abruptly from the surrounding plain. Much of it is a nature reserve, covered in places with oak and pine woodland, and it is a notable landmark visible from across the Galilee. Villages at its foot include Shibli-Umm al-Ghanam, Daburiyya and Iksal; the Afula–Tiberias road passes nearby.
