Helioseismology

Helioseismology is the study of the structure and dynamics of the Sun through its oscillations — pressure waves (p-modes) trapped inside the Sun, visible as rhythmic movements of the solar surface. Analogous to seismology on Earth, it allows scientists to probe the solar interior, which cannot be observed directly.

Method

Instruments measure Doppler shifts of the Sun's surface, revealing millions of oscillation modes, most prominently the five-minute oscillations. The frequencies of the modes depend on the sound speed, density and rotation along their paths, so inversions of the observed frequencies yield maps of temperature, composition and internal rotation.

Discoveries

Helioseismology confirmed the depth of the convection zone, mapped differential rotation through the interior, revealed the tachocline at the base of the convection zone, and provided the sound-speed profile that helped resolve the solar neutrino problem in favour of neutrino oscillation. Local helioseismology images sunspots and flows beneath the surface and is used in space-weather forecasting of far-side active regions. Key observatories include the space missions SOHO (GOLF and MDI instruments) and the Solar Dynamics Observatory, and the ground-based GONG network.
