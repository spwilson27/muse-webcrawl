The Argentina national football team (Spanish: Selección de fútbol de Argentina), nicknamed La Albiceleste, represents Argentina in men's international football and is administered by the Argentine Football Association. Argentina is one of the most successful national teams in the world, having won the FIFA World Cup three times: in 1978, 1986, and 2022.

History

Argentina played its first international match in 1902 and contested the first World Cup final in 1930, losing to Uruguay. The team won its first World Cup on home soil in 1978 under César Luis Menotti, led by Mario Kempes. In 1986 in Mexico, Diego Maradona produced one of the greatest individual tournament performances in history, including the "Hand of God" goal and the "Goal of the Century" in the quarter-final against England.

After losing the finals of 1990 and 2014, Argentina entered a long trophy drought at senior level despite the brilliance of Lionel Messi, who debuted in 2005 and became the team's record appearance-maker and scorer. The drought ended under coach Lionel Scaloni with the 2021 Copa América in Brazil, followed by the 2022 Finalissima, and then the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, where Argentina beat France in a final widely considered among the greatest ever, decided on penalties after a 3–3 draw. Argentina defended its continental title at the 2024 Copa América in the United States.

Honours

  • FIFA World Cup: champions 1978, 1986, 2022; runners-up 1930, 1990, 2014
  • Copa América: 16 titles (a record), most recently 2024
  • FIFA Confederations Cup: 1992
  • Olympic gold: 2004, 2008 (under-23 tournament)
  • CONMEBOL–UEFA Cup of Champions (Finalissima): 1993, 2022

Rivalries and identity

Argentina's fiercest rivalries are with Brazil (the Superclásico), Uruguay, and England, the latter intensified by the 1982 Falklands War and the 1986 quarter-final. The team plays in sky-blue and white vertical stripes, drawn from the national flag, and its principal home ground is the Estadio Monumental in Buenos Aires.
