Badminton is a racket sport played using rackets to hit a shuttlecock across a net. Although it may be played with larger teams, the most common forms are "singles" and "doubles". Matches are best of three games to 21 points under rally scoring; the shuttlecock — a feathered or synthetic projectile with uniquely high drag — is the fastest projectile in sport, with smashes recorded above 400 km/h.

The game developed in British India from battledore and shuttlecock and was named after Badminton House in Gloucestershire, England; the first rules were codified in the 1870s and the All England Championships, the sport's oldest tournament, began in 1899. Badminton is dominated internationally by Asian nations — China above all, plus Indonesia, Malaysia, South Korea, Japan and India — and by Denmark in Europe. China's Lin Dan, a two-time Olympic and five-time world champion, is widely considered the greatest player ever; the Thomas Cup (men) and Uber Cup (women) are the premier team events.
