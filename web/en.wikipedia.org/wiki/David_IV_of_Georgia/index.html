David IV of Georgia

David IV (Georgian: დავით IV, romanized: Davit IV), also known as David the Builder (დავით აღმაშენებელი, Davit Aghmashenebeli), (1073 – 24 January 1125) was the fifth king (mepe) of the Bagrationi dynasty of the Kingdom of Georgia from 1089 until his death in 1125. He is widely considered the greatest and most successful Georgian ruler in history.

Reign

Ascending the throne at sixteen during the Seljuk invasions, David reorganized the Georgian army and administration, expelled the Seljuks, and in 1122 captured Tbilisi, ending centuries of Arab rule and making it his capital. His victory at the Battle of Didgori in 1121 is celebrated as a decisive turning point that inaugurated the Georgian Golden Age.

Reforms and culture

David promoted education and religious tolerance, founded the Gelati Monastery and Academy, and strengthened Georgia's position across the Caucasus. He was canonized by the Georgian Orthodox Church as Saint David the King.
