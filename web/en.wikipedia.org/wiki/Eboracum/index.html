Eboracum

Eboracum was a fortress and a city in Roman Britain, on the site of the modern city of York in North Yorkshire, England. Founded around AD 71 by the Ninth Legion (Legio IX Hispana), it became one of the most important military and administrative centres of Roman Britain, serving as the capital of the province of Britannia Inferior and later of Britannia Secunda.

History

The fortress was established where the River Foss joins the River Ouse, during the campaigns of governor Quintus Petillius Cerialis against the Brigantes. The Sixth Legion (Legio VI Victrix) replaced the Ninth around AD 119 and remained Eboracum's garrison for the rest of the Roman period. A civilian settlement (colonia) grew up across the river, receiving the status of a self-governing colony by the early third century.

Eboracum hosted emperors: Septimius Severus made it his base for campaigns in Scotland and died there in AD 211, and Constantius Chlorus died in the city in AD 306, whereupon his son Constantine was proclaimed emperor by the troops — one of the most consequential moments in late Roman history.

Archaeology

Substantial remains survive, including stretches of the fortress walls incorporated into York's medieval city walls, the Multangular Tower, an underground strongroom visible beneath the Yorkshire Museum, and bathhouses and inscriptions preserved in local museums. Excavations at sites such as Hungate and the former railway station have revealed barracks, workshops, cemeteries and evidence of a flourishing port and trading centre.
