Data cleansing

Data cleansing or data cleaning is the process of detecting and correcting (or removing) corrupt or inaccurate records from a record set, table, or database, and refers to identifying incomplete, incorrect, inaccurate or irrelevant parts of the data and then replacing, modifying, or deleting the dirty or coarse data. Data cleansing may be performed interactively with data wrangling tools, or as batch processing through scripting or a data quality firewall.

Process

After cleansing, a data set should be consistent with other similar data sets in the system. The inconsistencies detected or removed may have been originally caused by user entry errors, by corruption in transmission or storage, or by different data dictionary definitions of similar entities in different stores. Typical cleansing activities include parsing, standardization (for example, converting values to a common format or unit), matching and deduplication of records, and validation against known constraints and reference data.

Data quality criteria

High-quality data is commonly judged against criteria such as validity (conformance to defined rules and ranges), accuracy (agreement with the real-world values), completeness (no unexpected missing values), consistency (the same fact recorded the same way across sources), and uniformity (values expressed in the same unit and format). Poor-quality data is costly: surveys have attributed substantial losses in business to bad data, and cleansing is often estimated to consume a large share of effort in data warehousing and data science projects.

Methods and tools

Approaches range from manual inspection and simple scripts to dedicated ETL (extract, transform, load) tools and machine-learning-based anomaly detection. Common techniques include range and format checks, checksums, record linkage, fuzzy matching for near-duplicate text, and statistical methods for flagging outliers. Because cleansing decisions can change analytical results, they are ideally documented so that transformations are reproducible.
