Liquefied petroleum gas

Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG, LP gas, or autogas when used as vehicle fuel) is a fuel gas consisting of a flammable mixture of hydrocarbons, principally propane and butane, which can be stored as a liquid under moderate pressure. It is produced during natural gas processing and petroleum refining.

Uses

LPG is widely used for heating, cooking and hot water in homes beyond the reach of piped gas, as a motor fuel, in agriculture, and as a petrochemical feedstock. It is stored and transported in pressurised steel cylinders and tanks; because the liquid expands about 250-fold on vaporising, small vessels hold large amounts of energy. LPG is also used as the propellant and refrigerant in some applications, and blends marketed as winter or summer grades vary the propane–butane ratio.

Safety

LPG vapour is heavier than air and can pool in low places; an odorant (usually ethanethiol) is added so leaks can be smelled. It burns cleanly, producing less carbon dioxide per unit energy than petrol or diesel, and very little soot or sulphur.
