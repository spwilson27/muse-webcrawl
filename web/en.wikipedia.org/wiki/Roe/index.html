Roe

Roe (/roʊ/) is the fully ripe internal egg masses in the ovaries, or the released external egg masses, of fish and certain marine animals such as shrimp, scallops, sea urchins and squid. As a seafood, roe is used both as a cooked ingredient in many dishes, and as a raw ingredient for delicacies such as caviar.

Types

The roe of sturgeon species is processed into caviar, among the most expensive foods in the world. Salmon roe (ikura in Japanese) is used in sushi, while flying fish roe (tobiko) and capelin roe (masago) garnish rolls. Cod roe is eaten boiled and fried in Scandinavia and Britain; sea urchin roe, known as uni, is prized in Japanese cuisine.

Culinary use

Roe may be cured, smoked, salted, or cooked fresh. In Greece, taramasalata is made from cured carp or cod roe; in Italy, bottarga is salted and dried mullet or tuna roe. Hard roe comes from female fish, while the milt of male fish is known as soft roe.
