Caldo gallego ("Galician broth") is a traditional soup of Galicia, in northwestern Spain, and one of the emblematic dishes of Galician cuisine. It is a rustic peasant soup traditionally made with seasonal greens, potatoes, dried white beans and a piece of pork fat or cured meat, simmered together in a pot.

Ingredients

The characteristic green component varies through the year: turnip tops (grelos) in season, or cabbage, collard greens or chard at other times. Potatoes are essential, as are white beans (fabas); a small piece of unto (cured pork fat) flavours the broth, and some versions add ham hock, bacon, chorizo or beef. The soup is traditionally served in deep clay bowls (cuncas).

Variations

In the diaspora the soup travelled with Galician emigrants to Cuba, Puerto Rico and elsewhere in the Caribbean and Latin America, where local greens and tubers replaced the originals. The dish is related to the Portuguese caldo verde, which uses finely shredded kale and potato but no beans.

Cultural role

Caldo gallego was a food of subsistence farmers, eaten daily in winter in rural Galicia, often with corn or rye bread. It remains a fixture of Galician restaurants and winter festivals, including the carnival season when turnip tops are at their best.
