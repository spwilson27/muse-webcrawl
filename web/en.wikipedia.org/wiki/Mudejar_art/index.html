Mudejar art

Mudejar art (more commonly spelled Mudéjar art) is the artistic style that flourished in the Christian kingdoms of medieval Spain from the 12th to the 17th century, created largely by Mudéjar Muslim craftsmen and characterised by the fusion of Islamic decorative techniques with Romanesque, Gothic, and Renaissance forms.

Characteristics

The style is notable for its inventive use of humble materials — brick, glazed ceramic tile, carved plaster, and wood — worked into elaborate geometric patterns, Arabic calligraphy and pseudo-calligraphy, interlaced arches, muqarnas vaulting, and richly coffered artesonado ceilings. Monumental brick bell towers in Aragon, such as those of Teruel, imitate the form of Almohad minarets.

Principal monuments

Outstanding examples include the Alcázar of Seville, the Aljafería of Zaragoza, the synagogues of Santa María la Blanca and El Tránsito in Toledo, and the Mudéjar towers of Teruel. The "Mudéjar Architecture of Aragon" is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
