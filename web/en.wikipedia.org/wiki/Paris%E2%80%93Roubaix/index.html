Paris–Roubaix is a one-day professional men's road cycling race in northern France, held each spring, usually in early April. Beginning near Paris (since 1977 in Compiègne) and finishing at the Roubaix Velodrome, it is one of cycling's five Monuments and is famed for its brutal stretches of cobblestones (pavé).

The course

The race covers roughly 250–260 km, of which more than 50 km run over rough cobbled farm roads such as the Trouée d'Arenberg forest sector, Mons-en-Pévèle, and the Carrefour de l'Arbre. Feared sectors are graded by difficulty, and the winner receives a cobblestone as a trophy. Weather can turn the pavé into mud or dust, producing chaotic, attritional racing.

History

First run in 1896, the race was interrupted by both World Wars; the devastation of the First World War in the region gave it the nickname "The Hell of the North" (l'Enfer du Nord). A women's edition, Paris–Roubaix Femmes, was inaugurated in 2021. Legends of the race include Roger De Vlaeminck and Tom Boonen, each with four victories.
