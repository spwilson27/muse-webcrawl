Radif

Placeholder image: poetry manuscript with a repeated refrain word.

Radif (Persian: ردیف, "row, series") is a term used in two related arts of the Perso-Arabic world:

  • In Persian classical music, the radif is the canonical repertoire of melodies (gushehs) organised within the modal system of dastgahs, memorised by students as the foundation for performance and improvisation.
  • In Persian, Urdu and Arabic prosody, the radif is the refrain of one or more words that is repeated identically at the end of every line (after the rhyme, or qafia) in forms such as the ghazal.

In poetry

In the ghazal, each couplet (sher) ends with the same rhyming syllable (the qafia) followed by the repeated word or phrase (the radif); both lines of the opening couplet (matla) carry the rhyme and radif. A ghazal may also be written without a radif, in which case it is called ghair-muraddaf. The radif gives the poem a musical refrain-like quality and constrains the poet, who must make the repeated phrase fit grammatically and semantically into every couplet. Famous examples occur in the ghazals of poets such as Mir Taqi Mir and Mirza Ghalib in Urdu, and Hafez in Persian.
