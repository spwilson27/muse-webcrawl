Southbank Centre

The Southbank Centre is a complex of artistic venues on the South Bank of the River Thames in London, between Hungerford Bridge and Westminster Bridge. It is one of the largest arts centres in the world, presenting music, dance, literature and visual art, and grew out of the 1951 Festival of Britain.

Venues

The centre comprises the Royal Festival Hall (opened 1951), the Queen Elizabeth Hall and Purcell Room (1967), and the Hayward Gallery (1968), all notable examples of post-war modernist and brutalist architecture. Resident ensembles include the London Philharmonic Orchestra and the Orchestra of the Age of Enlightenment.

Activities

Southbank Centre programmes festivals such as the London Literature Festival, Meltdown and WOW – Women of the World, and operates the National Poetry Library. The undercroft beneath the Queen Elizabeth Hall became a famous skateboarding spot from the 1970s; a campaign by Long Live Southbank preserved it after redevelopment plans threatened its removal, and parts were restored in the 2010s.
