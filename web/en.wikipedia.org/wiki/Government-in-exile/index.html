Government-in-exile

[Image: Government-in-exile]

A government-in-exile is a political group which claims to be the legitimate government of a country or territory, but is unable to exercise legal power and instead resides in a foreign country. Governments-in-exile usually plan to return to their native country and regain formal power.

History

During the Second World War, numerous governments-in-exile operated from London, including those of Poland, Norway, the Netherlands, Belgium, Czechoslovakia, and Yugoslavia, alongside Charles de Gaulle's Free France. Earlier examples include the Jacobite pretenders and the Spanish Republican government after the Spanish Civil War.

Recognition

The effectiveness of a government-in-exile depends on international recognition and support from its host and allies. Some, such as the Central Tibetan Administration, function for decades without returning. Others, like the Kuwaiti government during the 1990–91 Gulf War, were restored after liberation.
