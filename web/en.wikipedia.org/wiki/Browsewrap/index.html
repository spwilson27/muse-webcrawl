Browsewrap

[Image: a website footer containing a Terms of Use hyperlink]
Browsewrap agreement
Area of lawContract law; internet law
Related conceptsClickwrap, shrinkwrap, sign-in wrap

Browsewrap is a term used in internet law to describe a website's terms and conditions of use that are presented merely as a hyperlink, often in the footer of a page, under which a user is said to agree to the terms simply by visiting or continuing to use the website, without any affirmative action such as clicking an "I agree" button.

Enforceability

Courts, particularly in the United States, have frequently declined to enforce pure browsewrap agreements against ordinary users on the ground that the user had no actual or constructive notice of the terms and therefore never assented to them. In Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. (2002), the Second Circuit refused to bind users who downloaded software without being required to view licence terms. In Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble (2014), the Ninth Circuit held that the mere availability of terms via a hyperlink, without more, did not put a consumer on inquiry notice.

Comparison with other online contracts

Browsewrap contrasts with clickwrap agreements, in which a user must actively click to accept terms and which are routinely enforced, and with shrinkwrap licences in software. Intermediate designs ("sign-in wrap") present terms alongside a registration or purchase button. The enforceability of any form generally turns on whether the design provided reasonably conspicuous notice and unambiguous assent.
