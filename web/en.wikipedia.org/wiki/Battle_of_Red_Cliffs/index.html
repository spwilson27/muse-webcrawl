The Battle of Red Cliffs, also known as the Battle of Chibi, was a decisive naval battle fought in the winter of AD 208–209 between the warlord Cao Cao and the allied forces of Sun Quan and Liu Bei. It was the climactic battle at the end of the Han dynasty, immediately preceding the Three Kingdoms period, and is one of the most famous battles in Chinese history.

Background

By 208 Cao Cao had unified northern China and, having secured the surrender of Jing Province, moved south down the Yangtze with a huge army to destroy Sun Quan in Jiangdong. Against the advice of counsellors who favoured surrender, Sun Quan — persuaded by Zhou Yu and Lu Su — allied with the fugitive warlord Liu Bei, whose strategist Zhuge Liang helped broker the alliance. Zhou Yu took command of the allied fleet of about 50,000 men on the river at Red Cliffs.

The battle

Cao Cao's northern troops, weakened by seasickness on unfamiliar waters, had chained their ships together to steady them. The allied commander Huang Gai feigned a surrender and sailed a squadron of fire ships — loaded with kindling, reeds and oil and covered to suggest grain transports — toward the northern fleet. As they approached, the crews set them alight and escaped in small boats; driven by a timely southeast wind, the burning ships crashed into Cao Cao's chained fleet, which went up in flames. Amid smoke and confusion the allies attacked, and Cao Cao retreated north in disorder along the marshy Huarong road, his army further ravaged by disease and hunger.

Significance

Red Cliffs checked Cao Cao at the Yangtze, permanently dividing China into northern and southern spheres. The surviving warlords consolidated into the three states of Wei, Shu Han and Wu, inaugurating the Three Kingdoms era. The battle was immortalised in the fourteenth-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, which embellished it with episodes such as Zhuge Liang "borrowing arrows with straw boats", and it remains a byword in Chinese culture for the triumph of the weaker side through strategy. The exact site of the battlefield is still debated among several locations on the middle Yangtze.
