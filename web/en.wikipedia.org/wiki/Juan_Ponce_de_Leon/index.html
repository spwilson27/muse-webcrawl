Juan Ponce de León (c. 1474 – July 1521) was a Spanish explorer and conquistador. He led the first known European expedition to Florida in 1513, naming it La Florida, and served as the first governor of Puerto Rico. In popular legend he is associated with the search for the Fountain of Youth.

Early career in the Caribbean

Ponce de León likely sailed with Christopher Columbus on his second voyage in 1493. He settled on Hispaniola, helped subdue the eastern province of Higüey, and in 1508–1509 led the colonization of Puerto Rico (then called Borinquen), founding the settlement of Caparra and becoming the island's first governor. Rich from gold and encomiendas, he was displaced as governor in a political struggle with Diego Colón.

Florida voyages

Seeking new lands, Ponce de León sailed northwest in 1513, landing on Florida's east coast near Easter (Pascua Florida, the "feast of flowers", gave the land its name). His expedition charted the Florida Keys, rounded into the Gulf of Mexico, and noted the powerful Gulf Stream current. In 1521 he returned with two ships and about 200 colonists to settle Florida's southwest coast, but the expedition was attacked by Calusa warriors; Ponce de León was wounded, reportedly by a poisoned arrow, and died after being carried back to Havana.

Fountain of Youth legend

Posthumous accounts linked Ponce de León's voyages to a quest for a fountain whose waters restored youth, a legend that became attached to Florida in popular culture; historians note the story was not recorded in his lifetime and may have been a satirical invention by later chroniclers.
