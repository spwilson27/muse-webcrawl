Modal voice

Modal voice is the register used in everyday speech and most singing

Modal voice is the vocal register used most frequently in speech and singing in most languages. It is the usual phonation type in which the vocal folds vibrate fully, with neither the breathiness of breathy voice nor the constriction of creaky voice. The term "modal" refers to its being the most common, or statistically modal, mode of vibration of the vocal folds.

Physiology

In modal phonation the vocal folds are moderately adducted and vibrate along their full length with a mucosal wave, opening and closing completely once per cycle. Pitch is controlled mainly by tension in the folds, and within the modal register speakers produce the great majority of their normal pitch range. Above modal voice lies the falsetto (or head) register, and below it vocal fry.

In linguistics and singing

Phoneticians use modal voice as the baseline against which other phonation types — breathy, creaky, harsh, falsetto and whisper — are described; some languages contrast modal vowels with breathy or creaky vowels phonemically. Singing teachers likewise treat the modal (chest) register as the foundation of classical and popular technique.
