Icelandic calendar

The Icelandic calendar (Icelandic: íslenskt tímatal) is a system of time reckoning formerly used in Iceland, consisting of twelve months of 30 days each, plus four epagomenal days in midsummer, making a year of 364 days, with a leap week added every seventh year or so.

Structure

The year was divided into two seasons only: winter (vetur) and summer (sumar). The winter months were Gormánuður, Ýlir, Mörsugur (or Jólmánuður), Þorri, Góa and Einmánuður; the summer months were Harpa (or Gaukmánuður), Skerpla, Sólmánuður, Heyannir, Tvímánuður and Haustmánuður.

History

The calendar is traditionally attributed to the 10th-century settler Þorsteinn surtur, who proposed the addition of a leap week (sumarauki) to keep the calendar aligned with the seasons. Remarkably, the old month names remain in use in modern Icelandic, and the first day of summer is still a public holiday.
