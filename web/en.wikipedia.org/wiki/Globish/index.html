Created byJean-Paul Nerrière
Date2004
TypeControlled language, subset of English

Globish is a simplified version of English proposed by French businessman Jean-Paul Nerrière, a former vice-president of IBM Europe, as a tool for international communication among non-native speakers. The word is a portmanteau of "global" and "English". Globish uses a restricted vocabulary of about 1,500 words, short sentences and basic grammar, avoiding idioms, slang and complex constructions that can impede understanding between speakers of different native languages.

History

Nerrière developed the concept from his experience of international business meetings at IBM, where he observed that non-native speakers of English often communicated with each other more effectively than they did with native speakers, whose use of idiom and cultural reference created barriers. He set out his proposals in the books Parlez Globish (2004) and Don't Speak English, Parlez Globish (2006), and elaborated them in Globish the World Over (2009), written with David Hon.

Features

Globish is not intended as an artificial language but as a codified subset of standard English. Its rules include using only the 1,500 most common English words (supplemented by technical terms where necessary), keeping sentences to a maximum of roughly 15 words, avoiding the passive voice where possible, repeating important points, and supplementing speech with gestures and visual aids. Pronunciation is broadly tolerant: intelligibility, not native-like accuracy, is the goal.

Reception

The concept attracted widespread media attention after being popularised by journalist Robert McCrum in his book Globish: How the English Language Became the World's Language (2010), which used the term more broadly for the global spread of simplified English. Linguists have noted similarities between Globish and earlier controlled languages such as Basic English, and have debated whether such codification is necessary or merely describes how international English is already used.
