Bombardier Q400

The Bombardier Q400, marketed since 2019 as the De Havilland Canada Dash 8-400, is a twin-engined, medium-range turboprop airliner developed by de Havilland Canada and produced successively by Bombardier Aerospace and De Havilland Canada. It is the largest member of the Dash 8 family, seating up to 90 passengers, and is notable for a cruise speed approaching that of regional jets while retaining turboprop fuel efficiency on short sectors.

Development

The Q400 was launched as a stretched, faster derivative of the Dash 8-300, incorporating a longer fuselage, more powerful PW150A engines driving six-bladed Dowty propellers, and a computerised noise and vibration suppression (NVS) system — the source of the "Q" (quiet) branding shared with the Q200 and Q300. It entered service in 2000. In 2019 Bombardier sold the Dash 8 programme to Longview Aviation Capital, which revived the De Havilland Canada name; production at Downsview, Toronto ended in 2021 with a planned restart at a new facility in Alberta.

Operations

Major operators have included Flybe (once the largest), Horizon Air/Alaska Airlines, Air Canada Express, SpiceJet, and WestJet Encore. The type is prized for short-field performance and for serving airports where jets are uneconomical. It has also been adapted for special missions, including aerial firefighting (the Q400AT and MR tanker conversions) and maritime patrol.

Accidents

The type's most prominent accident was Colgan Air Flight 3407 in February 2009 near Buffalo, New York, which killed 50 people and prompted major changes to United States regional airline pilot training and fatigue rules. A series of landing-gear incidents in 2007 led SAS to retire its fleet and seek compensation from Bombardier.
