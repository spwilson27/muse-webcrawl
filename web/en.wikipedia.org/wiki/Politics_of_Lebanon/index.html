Politics of Lebanon

The politics of Lebanon take place in a framework of a parliamentary democratic republic based on confessionalism, in which the prime minister is the head of government, and of a pluriform multi-party system. The President of Lebanon is the head of state. Executive power is exercised by the government, while legislative power is vested in both the government and parliament.

Confessional system

Lebanon's political system allocates power among its recognised religious communities. Under the unwritten National Pact of 1943, the president must be a Maronite Christian, the prime minister a Sunni Muslim, and the speaker of parliament a Shia Muslim. Seats in the 128-member Chamber of Deputies (parliament) are divided equally between Christians and Muslims, and further subdivided among the recognised sects. The Taif Agreement of 1989, which ended the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), adjusted this arrangement by strengthening the Council of Ministers and the Sunni premiership at the expense of the presidency, and established parity between Christian and Muslim deputies.

Institutions

The president is elected by parliament for a six-year term and appoints the prime minister after binding consultations with deputies. The prime minister forms a cabinet, which must win a vote of confidence. Governments in Lebanon are typically broad national-unity coalitions reflecting the confessional balance, and the system frequently produces gridlock, including long vacancies in the presidency and extended caretaker governments.

Political parties and blocs

Lebanese parties are largely organised around sectarian communities and prominent political families. Major forces have included Hezbollah and the Amal Movement (Shia), the Future Movement (Sunni), the Free Patriotic Movement and the Lebanese Forces (Christian), and the Progressive Socialist Party (Druze). Since the Syrian withdrawal in 2005, parliamentary politics was long structured by the rival March 8 and March 14 alliances. A protest movement beginning in October 2019, amid financial collapse, demanded an end to the sectarian system.
