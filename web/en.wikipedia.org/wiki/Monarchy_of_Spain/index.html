The monarchy of Spain, constitutionally referred to as The Crown (la Corona), is a constitutional, but hereditary, monarchy. The monarch is the head of state of Spain and the highest-ranking officer of the Spanish Armed Forces. The current king is Felipe VI, who ascended the throne on 19 June 2014 following the abdication of his father, Juan Carlos I.

History

The Spanish monarchy traces its roots to the Visigothic kingdom and the Christian kingdoms of the Reconquista — Asturias, León, Castile, Aragon, and Navarre. The dynastic union of the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile (1479), laid the foundations of Spain, which became a leading world empire under the Habsburgs (Charles I and Philip II) and continued under the Bourbons from 1700. Spain was a republic twice (1873–74 and 1931–39); after the Franco dictatorship, the monarchy was restored with Juan Carlos I in 1975, and he played a central role in the transition to democracy, including opposing the 23-F coup attempt of 1981.

Constitutional role

Under the Constitution of 1978, the King is head of state, symbol of the unity and permanence of Spain, and arbitrator and moderator of the institutions. His acts — sanctioning and promulgating laws, summoning and dissolving the Cortes Generales, proposing the candidate for President of the Government, and serving as supreme commander of the armed forces — require ministerial countersignature (the refrendo) and are not personally attributable to him; the King's person is inviolable and not subject to responsibility. Succession follows male-preference cognatic primogeniture among the descendants of Juan Carlos I, a rule whose reform to absolute primogeniture has been debated.

Recent decades

The reign of Juan Carlos was marked by democratic consolidation but later by scandal, including the Nóos corruption case involving his son-in-law and a 2012 elephant-hunting trip to Botswana during the economic crisis; he abdicated in 2014 and left Spain in 2020 amid investigations into his finances. Felipe VI has sought to restore the institution's standing with transparency reforms, while republican sentiment and the Catalan question continue to fuel debate about the monarchy's future.
