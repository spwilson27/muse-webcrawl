The Cucurbitales are an order of flowering plants in the rosid group of dicotyledons, best known for the gourd family Cucurbitaceae, which includes economically important crops such as cucumbers, melons, squashes, pumpkins and watermelons.

Families

Under the APG IV classification, the order contains eight families: Anisophylleaceae, Apodanthaceae, Begoniaceae (the begonia family), Coriariaceae, Corynocarpaceae, Cucurbitaceae, Datiscaceae and Tetramelaceae. The order includes roughly 2,600 species; Begoniaceae alone accounts for some 1,800 of them, almost all in the genus Begonia.

Characteristics

Members of the order are mostly herbs, vines and shrubs, with some trees. Many Cucurbitaceae are fast-growing climbers with tendrils, palmately veined leaves and unisexual flowers; their fruit is often a berry modified into a hard-rinded pepo. Begonias are popular ornamentals with characteristically asymmetric leaves.

Distribution and uses

The order is distributed worldwide, with its greatest diversity in the tropics. Beyond the gourd crops, the order yields ornamental begonias and a handful of regional fruits and timber species.
