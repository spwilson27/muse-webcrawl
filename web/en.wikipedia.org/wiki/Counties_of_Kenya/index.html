The counties of Kenya are geographical units created by the 2010 Constitution of Kenya as the second level of government below the national government. There are 47 counties, each with an elected governor and county assembly. The system of devolved county government took effect after the general election of March 2013, replacing the former provinces and districts as the main units of regional administration.

Devolution

County governments are responsible for functions including county health services, agriculture, pre-primary education, county roads, trade development and water services. They receive a constitutionally guaranteed share of national revenue — not less than 15 per cent — distributed by the Commission on Revenue Allocation, supplemented by an Equalisation Fund for marginalised areas. Each county is also represented in the Senate, which protects county interests at the national level.

The counties

The counties vary enormously. Nairobi, the capital, is a city-county of over four million people; Mombasa and Kisumu are major urban counties; Turkana in the arid northwest is the largest by land area, while tiny Mombasa County is the smallest. Others include Kiambu, Nakuru, Kakamega, Kilifi, Narok, Meru, Uasin Gishu (Eldoret), Garissa, Wajir, Marsabit and Lamu. Each county is subdivided into sub-counties and wards, wards being the electoral units for members of county assemblies (MCAs).

Background

The 47 counties largely follow the boundaries of the districts that existed in 1992. Devolution was a central promise of the 2010 constitution, intended to spread resources and political power beyond Nairobi after the 2007–08 post-election violence exposed the dangers of a winner-takes-all centralised state.
