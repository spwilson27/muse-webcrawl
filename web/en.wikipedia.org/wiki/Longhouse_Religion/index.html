Longhouse Religion

FounderHandsome Lake (Sganyodaiyo)
Founded1799–1800, Seneca country, New York
AdherentsMembers of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) nations
Core textThe Code of Handsome Lake (Gaihwiio, "the Good Message")

The Longhouse Religion is the popular name for the religious movement also known as the Code of Handsome Lake or the Gaihwiio ("Good Message"), a new religious movement that swept through the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) communities of New York and Ontario around 1800. It was founded by the Seneca prophet Handsome Lake, who received a series of visions beginning in June 1799.

Teachings

Handsome Lake's visions, in which messengers from the Creator conveyed a moral code, combined traditional Iroquois ceremonialism — the cycle of thanksgiving festivals tied to the agricultural year — with elements influenced by Quaker and Christian teaching: temperance, condemnation of alcohol and witchcraft, stable family life, land retention, and a vision of heaven and hell. The code is still recited over several days at longhouses each year.

Practice today

The Longhouse Religion remains a living faith among the Seneca, Onondaga, Cayuga, Tonawanda and other Haudenosaunee communities, centred on the longhouse as a ceremonial building. It preserves the traditional festival cycle — Midwinter, Maple, Planting, Strawberry, Green Corn and Harvest ceremonies — alongside the recitation of the Gaihwiio.
