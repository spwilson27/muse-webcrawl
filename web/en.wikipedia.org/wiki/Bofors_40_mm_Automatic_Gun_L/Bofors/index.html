AB Bofors (UK: /ˈbɒfərz/ BOF-ərz, US: /ˈboʊfərz/ BOH-fərz, Swedish: [buːˈfɔʂː]) is a former Swedish arms manufacturer that originated as an ironworks founded in 1649 near Karlskoga. The name has been associated with the iron industry and with the design and production of artillery since the late 19th century; from 1894 to 1896 the company was owned by Alfred Nobel, who turned it from an iron producer into a modern cannon manufacturer and chemical company.

Bofors' most famous product is the 40 mm anti-aircraft gun, designed in the 1930s and used by nearly every major belligerent in the Second World War; versions remain in service worldwide. Other major products include the 57 mm naval gun, the 155 mm FH77 and Archer howitzers, the Carl Gustaf recoilless rifle, the AT4 anti-tank weapon, the RBS 70 air-defence system and the BILL anti-tank missile.

History and the Bofors scandal

In 1986 the company was implicated in the Bofors scandal in India, in which allegations of bribes paid to secure a contract for 410 FH-77B howitzers rocked Indian politics and contributed to the defeat of Rajiv Gandhi's government in the 1989 elections. The scandal also engulfed Swedish politics, and investigations and litigation continued in India for decades.

During the 1990s and 2000s Bofors' businesses were divided: the heavy weapons units passed to Celsius, then to United Defense Industries (2000), and in 2005 to BAE Systems, which operates them as BAE Systems Bofors; the anti-tank and shoulder-launched weapons business became part of Saab as Saab Bofors Dynamics.
