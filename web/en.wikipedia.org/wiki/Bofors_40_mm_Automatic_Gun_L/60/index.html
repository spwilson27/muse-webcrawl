Bofors 40 mm Automatic Gun L/60

The Bofors 40 mm Automatic Gun L/60 (often simply the "Bofors gun") is a Swedish anti-aircraft autocannon designed in the 1930s by the arms manufacturer AB Bofors. It was one of the most popular medium-weight anti-aircraft systems of the Second World War, used by most Western Allies as well as by the Axis powers, and in naval and land versions. Famously described as the gun that helped win the war at sea, it remained in service around the world for the rest of the 20th century.

Development

The Swedish Navy asked Bofors in 1928 for an improved anti-aircraft gun; the resulting design entered service in 1934 and was rapidly exported and licence-produced. Poland, Britain, France, the Netherlands and others adopted it before 1939. The United States manufactured enormous numbers after acquiring the design in 1940–41, with Chrysler and York Safe & Lock among the producers; American-built guns filled Army (M1) and Navy mountings, the Navy's Mark 1/2 guns in single, twin and quadruple mounts becoming standard light anti-aircraft armament on nearly every major US warship.

Design

The L/60 fires 40×311mmR high-explosive shells from four-round clips loaded into a hopper above the breech, achieving about 120 rounds per minute. Its recoil-operated automatic action, simple sights (later supplemented by gyro and director sights with proximity-fuzed ammunition) and rugged construction suited it to ships, field carriages and self-propelled mounts such as the M19 and M42 Duster.

Service

Bofors guns served in every theatre of the Second World War, in Korea, Vietnam, the Falklands and many other conflicts; captured examples were used by Germany and Japan. The longer-barrelled L/70 successor, introduced in the late 1940s, extended the family into the 21st century.
