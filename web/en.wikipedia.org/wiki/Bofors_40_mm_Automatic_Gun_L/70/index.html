Bofors 40 mm Automatic Gun L/70

The Bofors 40 mm Automatic Gun L/70 is a multi-purpose autocannon developed by the Swedish arms manufacturer AB Bofors (today BAE Systems Bofors) after the Second World War, as a successor to the famous wartime 40 mm L/60. The designation "L/70" refers to the barrel length of 70 calibers. Introduced from 1951, the L/70 fires the longer and more powerful 40×364mmR cartridge, offers roughly double the effective range of the L/60, and remains in service worldwide in land-based anti-aircraft and vehicle mountings and in naval guns such as the Bofors 40 Mk3 and Mk4.

Development

Post-war jets made the wartime L/60 marginal, so Bofors designed a new gun around a longer cartridge (40×364mmR, versus 40×311mmR), with a longer barrel, powered operation, and higher rate of fire (240 rounds/min, later raised). The land version was adopted by Sweden in 1951 and bought or license-built by many NATO and other countries; the Dutch-built guns equipped air-defense units across Western Europe. Italy's Breda (now Leonardo) developed widely used twin naval mountings with rates of fire up to 300–330 rounds/min combined.

Ammunition

The L/70 fires a family of ammunition including high-explosive incendiary, armour-piercing, and programmable proximity-fuzed rounds. Modern Bofors 3P (pre-fragmented, programmable, proximity) ammunition can be set in six modes — proximity, time, impact and armour-piercing combinations — allowing one round type to engage air, surface, and shore targets; it is a hallmark of the 40 Mk4 naval gun.

Applications

Land and vehicle uses include the Swedish Combat Vehicle 90 (CV9040), the Korean K21 infantry fighting vehicle (as the K40), anti-aircraft systems such as the Swedish Lvkv 90, and towed guns still in service in many states. Naval mountings include the Bofors 40 Mk3 (Trinity) and the lightweight 40 Mk4, fitted to corvettes, patrol vessels, and fast attack craft; the United States Coast Guard and others use Mk4 variants. The gun saw combat in the Falklands War (Argentine and British ships carried Bofors-type guns), the Gulf Wars, and numerous other conflicts.
