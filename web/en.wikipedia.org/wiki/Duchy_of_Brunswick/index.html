Duchy of Brunswick

Duchy of Brunswick (Herzogtum Braunschweig)
Status: State of the German Confederation, later German Empire
Existed: 1815–1918
Capital: Brunswick (Braunschweig)

The Duchy of Brunswick was a historical German state, re-established at the Congress of Vienna in 1815 from the territories of the former Principality of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel, and ruled by the House of Welf. It existed until the German Revolution of 1918, when it became the Free State of Brunswick.

History

The first duke, Frederick William — the "Black Duke" — was killed at the Battle of Quatre Bras in 1815, days after taking the throne. His son Charles II ruled controversially and was deposed in the revolution of 1830, fleeing as the ducal palace burned. Under William VIII the duchy modernised, built one of Germany's first state railways (1838), and joined the German Zollverein.

When William died childless in 1884, the Prussian-dominated regency council governed until 1913, when reconciliation between the Welfs and Hohenzollerns brought Ernest Augustus, husband of the Kaiser's daughter Viktoria Luise, to the throne. He abdicated on 8 November 1918.

Geography

The duchy was an enclave-rich state within Prussian territory in what is now Lower Saxony, with its capital at Brunswick and other towns including Wolfenbüttel, Helmstedt and Blankenburg in the Harz.
