The World Memory Championships is an annual competition in memory sport, founded in 1991 by inventor Tony Buzan and chess grandmaster Raymond Keene. Competitors, known as mental athletes, memorize vast quantities of information — including decks of playing cards, long strings of random numbers, names and faces, and binary digits — under strict time limits.

Format

The championship traditionally consists of ten disciplines contested over several days: one-hour numbers, five-minute numbers, spoken numbers, binary digits, one-hour cards, random lists of words, names and faces, five-minute images, speed cards (memorizing a shuffled deck as fast as possible), and dates and historical events. Points are awarded in each discipline and the competitor with the highest total score is declared World Memory Champion.

History

The first championship was held at the Athenaeum Club in London in 1991. Dominic O'Brien, who went on to win the title eight times, became the public face of the event in its early years. Other multiple champions include Andi Bell, Ben Pridmore, and German competitor Simon Reinhard. From the 2010s onward, Mongolian and Chinese competitors rose to prominence, and the event has frequently been hosted in China.

Techniques

Competitors rely on mnemonic systems such as the method of loci (memory palace), the major system for encoding numbers, and person–action–object (PAO) schemes for cards. Elite athletes can memorize a shuffled deck of cards in under 20 seconds and thousands of random digits in an hour.
