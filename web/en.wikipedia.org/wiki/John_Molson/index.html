John Molson

John Molson (28 December 1763 – 11 January 1836) was an English-born Canadian brewer, businessman and politician. He founded the Molson Brewery in Montreal in 1786, which grew into one of Canada's largest brewing companies, and became a leading figure in the commercial development of Lower Canada.

Early life

Molson was born in Moulton, Lincolnshire, England. Orphaned at a young age, he inherited property and emigrated to Canada in 1782, settling in Montreal. In 1786, at the age of 22, he purchased a small brewery on the banks of the St. Lawrence River, laying the foundations of the Molson brewing enterprise.

Business ventures

Beyond brewing, Molson was an energetic entrepreneur. He financed and built the Accommodation, the first steamboat launched in British North America, in 1809, and established a steamboat service between Montreal and Quebec City. He invested in hotels, banking and railways, helping to found the Montreal Bank (later the Bank of Montreal) in 1817 and promoting the Champlain and St. Lawrence Railroad.

Politics and legacy

Molson served in the Legislative Assembly of Lower Canada and was later appointed to the Legislative Council. The Molson family remained prominent in Canadian business and philanthropy, endowing institutions including McGill University's medical school.
