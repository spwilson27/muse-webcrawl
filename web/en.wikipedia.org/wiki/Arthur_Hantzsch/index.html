Arthur Rudolf Hantzsch (1857–1935)

Arthur Rudolf Hantzsch (7 March 1857 – 14 March 1935) was a German chemist who made foundational contributions to stereochemistry and heterocyclic chemistry. He held chairs at the universities of Zurich, Würzburg, and Leipzig.

Scientific work

Hantzsch is remembered for the Hantzsch pyridine synthesis (1881–82), a multicomponent reaction of an aldehyde, two equivalents of a β-keto ester, and ammonia to form dihydropyridines — a scaffold later important in calcium-channel-blocker drugs such as nifedipine. With Alfred Werner he developed the stereochemistry of nitrogen, explaining the isomerism of oximes through syn/anti configurations around the C=N bond (1890).

Other contributions

He also worked on thiazoles, diazo compounds, indicators and acid–base theory, and co-developed the Hantzsch–Widman nomenclature system for heterocyclic rings, still used in systematic organic nomenclature.
