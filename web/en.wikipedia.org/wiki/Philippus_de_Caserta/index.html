Philippus de Caserta

Philippus de Caserta, also known as Filippotto da Caserta (fl. late 14th century), was an Italian composer and music theorist of the late medieval era, associated with the style known as the ars subtilior. His name indicates that he came from Caserta, near Naples.

Life

Little is known of Philippus's biography. He appears to have been active in the 1370s and 1380s, possibly in connection with the papal court at Avignon, where the ars subtilior style flourished under the patronage of the Avignon Papacy and aristocratic courts of southern France and northern Italy. His music survives principally in the Chantilly Codex, the most important source for the ars subtilior repertory.

Music

Philippus's surviving works are all ballades in the French courtly tradition, notable for their extraordinary rhythmic complexity — a hallmark of the ars subtilior, in which composers explored intricate mensural notation, syncopation, and proportional relationships. His ballades include De ma dolour, Il n'est nulz homs, and Par le grant senstri. His music also shows an awareness of Italian trecento practice, and some of his works appear to be modelled on pieces by the French composer Jehan Vaillant.

Theoretical writings

Philippus is credited with a treatise, the Tractatus de diversis figuris, which discusses the notation of altered note values and proportions, including the interpretation of coloured notation (red notes indicating rhythmic alteration). The treatise is an important source for understanding the notational system of the ars subtilior.
