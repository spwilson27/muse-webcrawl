Bachata (music)

Bachata is a genre of Latin American music and dance that originated in the Dominican Republic in the early twentieth century. It blends Spanish guitar music with Indigenous Taíno and African elements, and its lyrics typically dwell on romance, heartbreak and rural life.

History

Bachata developed in the poor rural neighbourhoods and bars of the Dominican Republic and was long stigmatised as vulgar "bitter music" (música de amargue) associated with poverty and brothels, receiving little radio play during the Trujillo era and beyond. Early pioneers included José Manuel Calderón, who recorded the first recognised bachata single, "Qué será de mí", in 1961.

In the 1980s and 1990s artists such as Blas Durán introduced the electric guitar, while Juan Luis Guerra's Grammy-winning album Bachata Rosa (1990) brought the genre mainstream respectability. The New York group Aventura, fronted by Romeo Santos, modernised bachata with R&B and hip-hop influences in the 2000s, and Romeo Santos and Prince Royce carried it to global stadium audiences.

Characteristics and dance

Bachata music is played in 4/4 time, led by a requinto (lead guitar) with rhythm guitar, bass, bongos and güira. The partner dance is danced in a close embrace with a basic side-to-side step pattern and a characteristic hip movement on the fourth beat. In 2019, UNESCO inscribed Dominican bachata music on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, with the dance inscribed separately.
