Cîteaux Abbey

Cîteaux Abbey (French: Abbaye de Cîteaux) is a Catholic abbey located in Saint-Nicolas-lès-Cîteaux, south of Dijon, France. It is the mother house of the Cistercian Order, founded there in 1098 by a group of Benedictine monks led by Robert of Molesme who sought a stricter observance of the Rule of Saint Benedict. Today it is home to a community of Trappist monks (Cistercians of the Strict Observance).

History

The founders settled in a marshy wilderness named Cistercium (from cisteaux, reeds, or from Latin cis tertium lapidem, "on this side of the third milestone"). Under abbots Alberic and Stephen Harding the community adopted distinctive practices — an undyed white habit, manual labour, and a refusal of feudal revenues — codified in the Carta Caritatis (Charter of Charity). The arrival of Bernard of Clairvaux with thirty companions in 1112/1113 transformed the struggling house, and daughter abbeys — La Ferté, Pontigny, Clairvaux and Morimond — spread the order across Europe; by 1200 there were more than 500 Cistercian abbeys.

As mother house, Cîteaux hosted the order's annual General Chapter. The abbey suffered during the Hundred Years' War and the Wars of Religion, and was suppressed in the French Revolution (1791), its lands sold and many buildings demolished. Trappist monks repurchased the site and refounded the abbey in 1898. The present community is known for prayer, manual work and cheese-making (Cîteaux cheese).
