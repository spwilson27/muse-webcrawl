Hydroxyurea

Hydroxyurea (also known as hydroxycarbamide) is a medication used in the treatment of sickle cell disease, essential thrombocythaemia, chronic myeloid leukaemia, polycythaemia vera and cervical cancer. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

In sickle cell disease, hydroxyurea increases the production of fetal haemoglobin (haemoglobin F), which does not polymerise like sickle haemoglobin, thereby reducing the frequency of painful vaso-occlusive crises and the need for blood transfusions. It was approved for this use in adults in 1998 and later for children. In myeloproliferative disorders it acts as a cytoreductive agent, lowering dangerously high blood cell counts.

Mechanism of action

Hydroxyurea inhibits the enzyme ribonucleotide reductase, which is required for the synthesis of deoxyribonucleotides and hence of DNA. By arresting cells in the S phase of the cell cycle it suppresses rapidly dividing cells, including malignant cells and bone marrow precursors.

Side effects

Common side effects include bone marrow suppression (myelosuppression), gastrointestinal symptoms, skin changes and leg ulcers. Because it interferes with DNA synthesis it is potentially teratogenic and is generally avoided in pregnancy. Long-term monitoring of blood counts is required during therapy.
