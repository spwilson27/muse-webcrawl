GMHC (formerly Gay Men's Health Crisis) is a New York City–based nonprofit organisation that provides HIV/AIDS prevention, care and advocacy services. Founded in 1981–82, it was the first community-based AIDS service organisation in the world.

History

The organisation grew out of a 1981 meeting of gay men in the apartment of writer Larry Kramer, concerned by reports of a rare cancer (Kaposi's sarcoma) affecting gay men. Early volunteers ran an information hotline from a desk at the community centre and raised funds for research. GMHC developed the "buddy" programme pairing volunteers with people with AIDS, and became a model for AIDS service organisations worldwide.

Services

GMHC provides HIV and STI testing, mental-health and substance-use services, meals and nutrition programmes, housing support, legal assistance and policy advocacy. In 2010 it moved to a large facility in Manhattan's Chelsea/Far West Side area. The organisation has served tens of thousands of clients regardless of sexual orientation, and in 2018 shortened its name officially to GMHC.

Legacy

Frustration with what some founders saw as governmental inaction and the organisation's caution led Kramer and others to found ACT UP in 1987. GMHC remains a major provider and advocate in the response to HIV/AIDS.
