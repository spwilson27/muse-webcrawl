United Nations Mission in Ethiopia and Eritrea

The United Nations Mission in Ethiopia and Eritrea (UNMEE) was a United Nations peacekeeping mission established by Security Council Resolution 1320 of 15 September 2000 to monitor the ceasefire between Ethiopia and Eritrea following the Eritrean–Ethiopian War (1998–2000), and to help ensure observance of the security commitments agreed in the Algiers Agreement of December 2000.

Mandate

UNMEE monitored a 25-kilometre-wide Temporary Security Zone (TSZ) inside Eritrean territory along the disputed border, chaired the Military Coordination Commission, coordinated demining and humanitarian assistance, and supported the work of the Eritrea–Ethiopia Boundary Commission, established to delimit and demarcate the border.

Operations and difficulties

From 2002 the Boundary Commission issued its delimitation decision, but demarcation on the ground stalled because Ethiopia rejected the award of the town of Badme to Eritrea. Eritrea progressively restricted UNMEE's operations — banning helicopter flights in 2005 and expelling Western staff — and in early 2008 Eritrea cut off fuel supplies to the mission, forcing UNMEE to evacuate most of its personnel from the TSZ. The Security Council terminated the mission effective 31 July 2008 by Resolution 1827.

Aftermath

The border dispute remained unresolved until 2018, when Ethiopia's new prime minister Abiy Ahmed accepted the Boundary Commission's decision, leading to a peace declaration between the two countries.
