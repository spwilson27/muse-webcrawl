Energy quality

Energy quality is a measure of how readily a form of energy can be converted into useful work, or, in the terminology of thermodynamics, of the exergy content of an energy source relative to its total energy. High-quality energy — such as electricity, mechanical work and chemical fuels — is concentrated and flexible, and can be converted almost completely into other forms. Low-quality energy, such as low-temperature heat, is dispersed and much of it cannot be recovered as work.

Forms of energy ranked by quality: electricity and mechanical work rank highest; low-temperature heat lowest.

Concept

The concept follows from the second law of thermodynamics: while energy is conserved in quantity, every real conversion degrades its quality, increasing entropy. Burning high-quality chemical fuel merely to produce lukewarm water wastes exergy, whereas a heat pump or cogeneration system matches the quality of the supply to the quality demanded by the task.

Applications

Energy quality considerations underlie exergy analysis in engineering, the cascading use of industrial waste heat, and debates in ecological economics about the role of energy in production.
