Stockbridge is a town in Berkshire County, Massachusetts, United States. It is part of the Pittsfield metropolitan area. The population was 2,018 at the 2020 census. A year-round resident population, alongside a substantial seasonal population, make the town a destination in the summer months, in part because it is home to the Tanglewood Music Festival, in the village of Lenox nearby, as well as the Norman Rockwell Museum.

History

The area was inhabited by the Mahican people, who called it Wachketoohuk ("by the winding river"). In 1734 the General Court of Massachusetts established a mission and "praying town" among the Mahicans, led by missionary John Sergeant. The town was incorporated in 1739 and named after Stockbridge in Hampshire, England. The mission's Indian community sided with the colonists during the American Revolutionary War, but postwar land losses led most Mahicans to remove westward, eventually to Wisconsin, where their descendants form the Stockbridge-Munsee Community.

Geography

Stockbridge lies in the Housatonic River valley in the Berkshire Hills of western Massachusetts. According to the United States Census Bureau, the town has a total area of about 23.7 square miles (61 km²), of which roughly 22.9 square miles is land. Villages within the town include Stockbridge, Interlaken, Glendale, and Stockbridge Bowl (Lake Mahkeenac).

Culture

The town is closely associated with illustrator Norman Rockwell, who lived there from 1953 until his death in 1978; the Norman Rockwell Museum, founded in 1969, preserves his studio and works. Main Street was depicted in Rockwell's famous painting Stockbridge Main Street at Christmas. The town is also home to Chesterwood, the summer estate of sculptor Daniel Chester French, and Naumkeag, a Gilded Age estate designed by McKim, Mead & White. The folksinger Arlo Guthrie's song "Alice's Restaurant" is set partly in Stockbridge, where the former church that housed the restaurant still stands.

Berkshire County towns nearby include Great Barrington, Lee, and Lenox.
