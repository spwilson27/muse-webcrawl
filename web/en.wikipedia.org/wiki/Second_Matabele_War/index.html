Second Matabele War
DateMarch 1896 – October 1897
LocationMatabeleland and Mashonaland, Rhodesia (Zimbabwe)
Also known asFirst Chimurenga

The Second Matabele War, also known in Zimbabwe as the First Chimurenga, was fought in 1896–1897 by the British South Africa Company against the Ndebele (Matabele) and Shona peoples in what is now Zimbabwe.

Causes and course

Resentment at Company rule, hut taxes, forced labour, and a devastating rinderpest epidemic and drought — interpreted by spiritual leaders as punishment — sparked revolt in March 1896, encouraged by the religious authority Mlimo. Settlers were killed and besieged in Bulawayo and other towns. Company forces weakened after the disastrous Jameson Raid (1895–96) had drawn troops away; the Shangani Patrol context belongs to the earlier First Matabele War (1893).

End

Cecil Rhodes negotiated peace with Ndebele izinduna in the Matobo Hills in 1896, while Shona resistance, associated with spirit mediums including Nehanda and Kaguvi, continued until 1897, when its leaders were captured and executed. The war entrenched Company rule but became a founding symbol of Zimbabwean resistance.
