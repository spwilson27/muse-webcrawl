Apollonian gasket

An Apollonian gasket, also called an Apollonian net, is a fractal generated from three mutually tangent circles. The gaps between the circles are filled with further circles tangent to the surrounding ones, and the process is repeated indefinitely in every remaining curvilinear triangular gap.

Construction

The construction rests on Descartes' theorem, which relates the curvatures of four mutually tangent circles. Given three tangent circles, there are generally two circles tangent to all three; repeatedly choosing these solutions produces an Apollonian circle packing. If the starting curvatures are integers, all generated curvatures remain integers, a property that has made Apollonian packings a rich topic in number theory.

History

The problem of circles tangent to three given circles goes back to Apollonius of Perga. The gasket's modern study connects geometry, group theory and number theory; its symmetry group is related to the Apollonian group and to reflections in the generating circles. Leibniz had described a similar iterative circle-packing idea in the seventeenth century.

Properties

The residual set left after removing all the open discs has Hausdorff dimension about 1.3057 and Lebesgue measure zero. Apollonian gaskets appear in art and design because of their intricate, self-similar structure.
