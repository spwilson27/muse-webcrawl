We have met the enemy and he is us

"We have met the enemy and he is us" is a phrase popularized by cartoonist Walt Kelly in his comic strip Pogo. It is a parody of the message sent by Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry after the Battle of Lake Erie in 1813: "We have met the enemy and they are ours."

Origin

Kelly first used variations of the phrase in the early 1950s, including in The Pogo Papers (1953), where it referred to McCarthyism and political suspicion. Its most famous appearance came in 1970–1971, when Kelly drew Pogo and Porkypine looking out over a forest floor strewn with garbage. The poster version, produced for the first Earth Day in 1970 and widely reproduced for Earth Day 1971, carried the caption beneath the two characters surveying the trash left behind.

Meaning and use

The phrase became a slogan of the environmental movement, expressing the idea that pollution and ecological damage are not inflicted by an external enemy but by humanity itself. It has since been quoted broadly in contexts of self-inflicted harm, from public health to economics, and is frequently reprinted in discussions of the original Perry dispatch and of Kelly's work.
