Jena Romanticism

Jena Romanticism (German: Jenaer Romantik), also called Early Romanticism (Frühromantik), was the first phase of the Romantic movement in German literature, centred on the university town of Jena from roughly 1798 to 1804.

The circle

Its core members included the brothers August Wilhelm Schlegel and Friedrich Schlegel, Friedrich's wife Dorothea Schlegel, August Wilhelm's wife Caroline Schlegel (later Schelling), the poet Novalis (Friedrich von Hardenberg), Ludwig Tieck, and the philosopher Friedrich Wilhelm Joseph Schelling. Their journal, the Athenäum (1798–1800), published the famous fragments in which the movement defined itself.

Ideas

The Jena Romantics developed the concepts of Romantic poetry as "progressive universal poetry", Romantic irony, and the fragment as a literary form. Responding to Kant and Fichte, they treated art and philosophy as intertwined and celebrated the creative imagination. Friedrich Schlegel's Athenäum Fragment 116 described Romantic poetry as still in the process of becoming and never perfected. The circle dispersed after about 1804, and the movement's centre shifted to Heidelberg Romanticism.
