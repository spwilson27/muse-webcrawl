Sayat-Nova

BornHarutyun Sayatyan, 14 June 1712, Tiflis (Tbilisi)
Died22 November 1795, Haghpat Monastery
OccupationPoet, musician, ashugh (court minstrel)
LanguagesArmenian, Georgian, Azerbaijani, Persian

Sayat-Nova (Armenian: Սայաթ-Նովա; born Harutyun Sayatyan; 1712–1795) was an Armenian poet, musician and ashugh (court troubadour) who composed in Armenian, Georgian, Azerbaijani Turkic and Persian. His name Sayat-Nova, bestowed by contemporaries, means "Master of Song" or "King of Songs" (from Persian sayyid and a word for melody). He is regarded as the greatest of the Caucasian ashughs and a shared cultural figure for Armenians, Georgians and Azerbaijanis alike.

Life

Born in Tiflis (Tbilisi) into a family of Armenian weavers, Sayat-Nova learned the kamancheh and the craft of the ashugh. His talent brought him to the court of Heraclius II of Georgia, where he served as court musician and diplomat. Legend holds that he fell in love with the king's sister, Princess Anna; whatever the truth, he was dismissed from court around 1759 and became a monk with the name Stepanos at Haghpat Monastery in Armenia. He was killed there in 1795 during the invasion of Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar.

Work

More than 220 of his songs survive in his own songbook, the Daftar, written in a personal phonetic notation. His lyrics combine courtly love poetry, Sufi-inflected imagery and sharp observation of everyday life, and his melodies remain standards of Armenian and Georgian folk performance. Sergei Parajanov's 1969 film The Color of Pomegranates is a celebrated cinematic portrait of his life.
