Catfishing

Catfishing refers to the creation of a fictitious online persona, or fake identity (typically on social networking platforms), with the intent of deception, usually to mislead a victim into an online romantic relationship or to commit financial fraud. Perpetrators, usually referred to as catfish, generally use fake photos and lie about their personal lives to present themselves as more attractive for financial gain, personal satisfaction, evasion of legal consequences, or to troll.

Terminology

The term was popularised by the 2010 American documentary film Catfish, in which a young man builds a romantic relationship with a woman on Facebook who turns out not to be who she claimed. The film's title derives from a story about how live cod were shipped with catfish in the same tanks to keep the cod active. The term subsequently gave its name to the MTV reality series Catfish: The TV Show.

Motives and harms

Catfishing may be motivated by loneliness, fantasy, financial scams, or revenge. Victims can suffer emotional distress and financial loss, and the practice can overlap with romance scams in which perpetrators extract money from victims under false pretences. A well-known public case was the 2012–2013 hoax involving American football player Manti Te'o, whose supposed girlfriend did not exist.

Legal responses

Catfishing itself is not always a distinct criminal offence, but conduct associated with it — fraud, extortion, identity theft and harassment — is punishable in many jurisdictions. Some US states have enacted laws addressing online impersonation.
