Wenceslaus I (Czech: Václav; c. 907 – 28 September 935), known as Saint Wenceslaus, was Duke of Bohemia from 921 until his murder in 935. Celebrated for his piety and charity, he became the patron saint of Bohemia and of the Czech state, and is immortalised in the Christmas carol "Good King Wenceslas".

Life

Wenceslaus was the son of Duke Vratislaus I and grandson of Saint Ludmila, who raised him as a Christian. Succeeding his father in 921 while still young, he ruled under the influence of his Christian grandmother against his pagan mother Drahomíra, who had Ludmila murdered. As duke he promoted Christianity in Bohemia, founded the church of St Vitus at Prague Castle (the nucleus of today's St Vitus Cathedral), and sought peace with Germany, accepting a tributary relationship with King Henry the Fowler.

Death and cult

His younger brother Boleslav, backed by nobles resentful of Wenceslaus's piety and pro-German policy, invited him to Stará Boleslav for a feast and had him murdered at the door of the church on 28 September 935. Wenceslaus was immediately venerated as a martyr; Boleslav, repenting, translated his remains to St Vitus in Prague. His feast, 28 September, is a Czech national holiday, and his equestrian statue dominates Wenceslaus Square in Prague.
