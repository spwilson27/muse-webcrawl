RS-485

RS-485, officially TIA-485-A, is a standard defining the electrical characteristics of drivers and receivers for serial communications over balanced twisted-pair lines. It supports multipoint networks of up to 32 unit loads (more with modern transceivers) over distances up to about 1,200 metres.

Characteristics

RS-485 uses differential signalling, giving strong noise immunity. It specifies only the physical layer; protocols such as Modbus RTU, Profibus, and DMX512 run over it. Networks are typically half-duplex on a single pair, terminated at both ends with resistors matching the cable impedance (typically 120 Ω).

Applications

Common in industrial automation, building control, stage lighting, and aircraft systems, RS-485 remains widely used for robust long-distance low-speed communication.
