Kibble balance

Kibble balance
InventorBryan Kibble (1975), National Physical Laboratory, UK
PurposeMeasuring mass by relating it to the Planck constant
Former nameWatt balance
RoleKey experiment in the 2019 redefinition of the kilogram

A Kibble balance (formerly called a watt balance) is an electromechanical measuring instrument that measures the weight of a test object very precisely by the strength of an electric current and a voltage. It relates mechanical power to electrical power so that mass can be expressed in terms of the Planck constant. Kibble balances at national metrology institutes provided the measurements that enabled the 2019 redefinition of the kilogram in terms of fundamental constants, replacing the physical prototype kept in Paris.

Principle

The instrument was proposed in 1975 by Bryan Kibble of the UK National Physical Laboratory. It operates in two modes. In weighing mode, a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences a force that balances the weight of a test mass (mg = BLI). In moving mode, the same coil is moved through the field at a measured velocity, inducing a voltage (U = BLv). Eliminating the hard-to-measure geometric factor BL between the two equations gives mgv = UI — an equality of mechanical and electrical power, hence the former name "watt balance." Because voltage and resistance can be measured via the Josephson and quantum Hall effects, the product UI can be tied exactly to the Planck constant, allowing mass to be determined from h.

Role in the SI redefinition

Building a Kibble balance capable of parts-per-hundred-million accuracy is a project of years. Instruments at NIST (United States), NRC (Canada), METAS (Switzerland), LNE (France), and the BIPM achieved the concordant measurements of the Planck constant that allowed the 26th General Conference on Weights and Measures to fix h exactly and redefine the kilogram effective 20 May 2019. The device was renamed in Kibble's honor in 2016, shortly after his death.
