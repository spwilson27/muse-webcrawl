Hoba meteorite

Type: Iron (ataxite, IVB)
Mass: ~60 tonnes
Location: Hoba West farm, near Grootfontein, Namibia
Discovered: 1920

The Hoba meteorite is the largest known intact meteorite and the largest naturally occurring piece of iron on Earth. It lies on the farm Hoba West in the Otjozondjupa Region of Namibia, near the town of Grootfontein, where it has remained since its discovery because of its enormous mass.

Description

The meteorite is a tabular slab measuring about 2.95 m × 2.84 m (9 ft 8 in × 9 ft 4 in), with a thickness of roughly 0.75–1.22 m. Its mass was originally estimated at around 66 tonnes and is now about 60 tonnes, weathering having reduced it slightly. It is composed of about 84% iron and 16% nickel, with traces of cobalt, and is classified as an ataxite iron meteorite of chemical group IVB.

Impact and discovery

The meteorite is thought to have fallen less than 80,000 years ago. Unusually, it left no crater; scientists suggest its flat shape and the Earth's atmosphere slowed it to terminal velocity so that it landed relatively gently. It was discovered in 1920 by the farm's owner, Jacobus Hermanus Brits, whose plough struck the buried metal object. The meteorite was excavated and described, and in 1955 the Namibian authorities declared it a national monument. It was opened to the public in 1987, with an amphitheatre-like viewing area built around it.
