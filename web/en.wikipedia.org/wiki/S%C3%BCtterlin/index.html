Sütterlin

Sütterlin is a historical form of German handwriting developed by the graphic artist Ludwig Sütterlin. Commissioned by the Prussian ministry of culture in 1911, it was taught in schools from 1915 and became the standard German cursive in the interwar period.

Characteristics

Sütterlin was designed in two variants, for Latin and for German (Kurrent-derived) script. Its letters are upright and rounded, written with a uniform slant-free ductus, intended to be easier for children than the older Kurrent cursive.

History

The script was adopted across Germany in the 1920s and 1930s. In 1941 the Nazi regime banned blackletter typefaces and deprecated Sütterlin in schools in favour of a Latin-based "Normalschrift". After the Second World War, Sütterlin fell out of teaching, though a simplified Deutsche Kurrentschrift was still taught for a time in West Germany.

Today

Because most German documents of the first half of the 20th century use Sütterlin or Kurrent, the script is still studied by historians, genealogists and archivists.
