The National Forest System (NFS) is a system of federally owned land in the United States comprising national forests and national grasslands. Administered by the United States Forest Service, an agency of the Department of Agriculture, the system covers about 193 million acres (roughly 780,000 km²) of land.

Components

The system includes 154 national forests and 20 national grasslands, organized into ranger districts and grouped under forest supervisors' offices across nine Forest Service regions. The largest concentrations of NFS land are in the western states, and the single largest unit is the Tongass National Forest in Alaska, the largest national forest in the country.

Management

Unlike national parks, which are primarily preservation-oriented, national forests are managed for multiple uses: timber production, livestock grazing, water, wildlife habitat and outdoor recreation. The system also contains congressionally designated wilderness areas, wild and scenic rivers, and national recreation areas. Management is guided by land and resource management plans prepared under the National Forest Management Act of 1976.
