Tallapaka is a village in the Annamayya district (formerly part of Kadapa district) of Andhra Pradesh, India, located near Rajampet. It is renowned across the Telugu-speaking world as the birthplace and home of Annamacharya (1408–1503), the saint-composer regarded as the grandfather of Telugu song literature (padakavita pitamaha), whose devotional hymns to Lord Venkateswara of Tirumala remain central to Carnatic music and temple worship.

Annamacharya heritage

Annamacharya is said to have composed as many as 32,000 sankirtanas (devotional songs) in Telugu and Sanskrit; roughly 13,000 survive, preserved on copper plates discovered in a sealed chamber of the Tirumala Venkateswara Temple in the 20th century. His family, the Tallapaka poets, produced several generations of composers, including his son Pedda Tirumalacharya and grandson Tallapaka Chinnayya. The village's temple, dedicated to Chennakesava (a form of Vishnu), preserves associated sculptures and inscriptions, and the 108-foot statue and meditation temple (Dhyana Mandiram) erected at Tallapaka by the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD) have made the village a pilgrimage destination in its own right.

Geography and life

Tallapaka lies in the Rayalaseema region of southern Andhra Pradesh, in a landscape of red soils, farms, and scrub hills at the edge of the Eastern Ghats, not far from the temple town of Vontimitta with its famous Kodandarama temple. Agriculture — groundnut, paddy, and horticulture — is the mainstay of the local economy, and the village is connected by road to Rajampet and the Kadapa–Tirupati corridor.
