Dhiban (Arabic: ذيبان) is a town in Madaba Governorate, Jordan, about 70 kilometres south of Amman and east of the Dead Sea. It stands on and around the site of ancient Dibon, a capital of the kingdom of Moab in the Iron Age.

Ancient Dibon

Dibon is well known from the Hebrew Bible, where it appears as a Moabite town later allotted to the tribes of Gad and Reuben. Excavations at the site have revealed Iron Age fortifications, a reservoir, temples and Nabataean, Roman, Byzantine and early Islamic remains. In 1850–51, Bedouin of the area uncovered at Dhiban the famous Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone), a basalt inscription of King Mesha of Moab from about 840 BC recording his wars against Israel; it is now in the Louvre.

Excavations and community archaeology

American excavations in the 1950s and 1960s were followed from the 2000s by the Dhiban Excavation and Development Project, a collaboration between universities and the local community that combines archaeology with heritage training and economic development for the town's residents.
