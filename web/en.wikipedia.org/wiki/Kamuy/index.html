Kamuy

A kamuy (Ainu: カムイ, kamuy) is a divine being or spiritual entity in the religion of the Ainu people, the indigenous people of Hokkaido, northern Honshu, Sakhalin and the Kurils. The concept is often compared to the kami of Shinto, though kamuy are embedded in a distinctively Ainu worldview in which humans and gods depend reciprocally on one another.

Beliefs

In Ainu belief, kamuy inhabit the natural and human worlds: animals, plants, fire, water, weather, tools and even diseases can be kamuy. The spirit world (kamuy mosir) mirrors the human world (ainu mosir); kamuy visit the human world wearing the disguise of animals or objects, bringing gifts — a hunted bear, for example, offers its meat and fur while its spirit is entertained and then ceremonially sent back to the spirit world in the iomante ritual.

Notable kamuy

  • Kotankor kamuy — Blakiston's fish owl, guardian of the village.
  • Kim-un kamuy — the brown bear, "god of the mountains", the most honoured kamuy.
  • Rep-un kamuy — the orca, god of the sea.
  • Ape huci kamuy — the fire goddess, central to the household and to prayer.
  • Kotakar kar kamuy — a creator deity who fashioned the world.
  • Wakka-us kamuy — goddess of fresh water.

Oral epics (yukar) often narrate events from the kamuy's own perspective, and are a major source for Ainu mythology.
