The Cincinnati Bengals are a professional American football team based in Cincinnati, Ohio, competing in the National Football League as a member of the AFC North division. The franchise was founded in 1968 by legendary coach Paul Brown as a member of the American Football League and joined the NFL in the 1970 merger.

History

Paul Brown named the team after an earlier Cincinnati Bengals club and dressed it in orange and black. The Bengals reached the playoffs in their third season and won AFC championships in 1981 and 1988, losing both Super Bowls (XVI and XXIII) to the San Francisco 49ers. After Paul Brown's death in 1991, his son Mike Brown took control; a long playoff drought followed, broken by the 2021 season, when quarterback Joe Burrow and the "Who Dey" Bengals won the AFC and reached Super Bowl LVI, losing narrowly to the Los Angeles Rams.

Identity

The team's tiger-striped helmets, introduced in 1981, are among the most recognisable in the NFL. Its chant "Who Dey!" dates to the 1981 season. Home games are played at Paycor Stadium (opened 2000 as Paul Brown Stadium) on the Cincinnati riverfront.
