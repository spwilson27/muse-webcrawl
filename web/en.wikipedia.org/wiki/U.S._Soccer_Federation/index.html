The United States Soccer Federation (USSF), commonly referred to as U.S. Soccer, is the official governing body of the sport of soccer in the United States. Headquartered in Chicago, the federation is a nonprofit organization and a member of FIFA and CONCACAF. It is responsible for the United States men's and women's national teams, youth national teams, the U.S. Open Cup, and the sanctioning of professional and amateur leagues.

History

The federation was founded on 5 April 1913 in New York as the United States of America Football Association, making it one of the world's first organizations to be affiliated with FIFA. It adopted the name United States Soccer Football Association in 1945 and its current name in 1974.

National teams

U.S. Soccer administers the men's national team, which first competed at the 1930 FIFA World Cup and finished third, and the women's national team, one of the most successful in the world with four FIFA Women's World Cup titles (1991, 1999, 2015, 2019). The federation also oversees futsal, beach soccer, and para national teams.

Competitions and leagues

The federation sanctions the professional leagues in the United States, including Major League Soccer, and runs the Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup, the oldest ongoing national soccer competition in the country, first held in 1914. In 2023 the federation announced plans for a new National Training Center near Atlanta, Georgia.
