Spindletop

Spindletop is an oil field located in the southern portion of Beaumont, Texas, in the United States. On January 10, 1901, a well at Spindletop struck oil — a gusher known as the Lucas Gusher — which blew oil more than 150 feet (46 m) in the air and flowed at an initial rate of nearly 100,000 barrels per day.

Spindletop
Location: Beaumont, Texas
Discovery: 10 January 1901
Discoverer: Anthony F. Lucas et al.
Significance: Start of Texas oil boom

Discovery

Patillo Higgins had long suspected oil beneath the salt dome known as Spindletop Hill. Mining engineer Anthony Francis Lucas, drilling with the Hamill brothers, struck the reservoir on 10 January 1901 at about 1,139 feet. The well was uncontrolled for nine days, wasting hundreds of thousands of barrels.

Boom

The discovery touched off the Texas oil boom, transformed Beaumont into a boomtown, and helped found companies that became Texaco, Gulf Oil and Humble Oil (later part of Exxon). Oil prices fell sharply as production flooded the market, and hundreds of wells were drilled on tiny plots around the dome.

Later production

Production peaked early and declined, with later revivals from deeper drilling and sulphur mining by Frasch process. The field has produced well over 150 million barrels in total. The Lucas Gusher is commemorated at the Spindletop-Gladys City Boomtown Museum.
