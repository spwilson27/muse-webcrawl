East African Community

HeadquartersArusha, Tanzania
TypeIntergovernmental organisation
Membership8 partner states
Established7 July 2000 (revived)
Working languagesEnglish, Swahili, French

The East African Community (EAC) is an intergovernmental organisation composed of eight partner states in East Africa: Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Kenya, Rwanda, Somalia, South Sudan, Uganda and Tanzania. Its headquarters are in Arusha, Tanzania. The organisation aims at ever-deeper regional integration, progressing through a customs union, a common market, a monetary union and ultimately a political federation.

History

Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda have a long history of cooperation dating to the early 20th century. A first East African Community, founded in 1967, collapsed in 1977 amid political and economic differences. The treaty reviving the Community was signed on 30 November 1999 and entered into force on 7 July 2000. Rwanda and Burundi joined in 2007, South Sudan in 2016, the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 2022, and Somalia's accession treaty was signed in 2023, with full membership following in 2024.

Integration milestones

A Customs Union protocol took effect in 2005, followed by a Common Market protocol in 2010 guaranteeing the free movement of goods, persons, labour, services and capital. A Monetary Union protocol was signed in 2013, envisaging a single currency. The Community is also founded on work toward a Political Federation as its ultimate goal.

Institutions

The EAC's organs include the Summit of Heads of State, the Council of Ministers, the East African Legislative Assembly (EALA), the East African Court of Justice (EACJ) in Arusha, and the Secretariat headed by a Secretary General. Partner states contribute to a combined market of roughly 300 million people.
