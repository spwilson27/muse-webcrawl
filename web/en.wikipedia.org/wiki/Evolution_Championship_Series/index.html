The Evolution Championship Series, commonly known as Evo, is an annual esports event that focuses exclusively on fighting games. Held in Las Vegas, Nevada, it is the largest and longest-running fighting game tournament in the world, drawing thousands of competitors in an open-bracket format in which any entrant can compete against professional players. The tournament is notable for its double-elimination format and for iconic moments in competitive gaming history, such as "Evo Moment 37" in 2004, in which Daigo Umehara parried a full sequence of attacks in Street Fighter III.

History

Evo traces its origins to the Battle by the Bay tournament held in Northern California in 1996, organised by Tom Cannon and others associated with the Shoryuken website. The event was renamed the Evolution Championship Series in 2002. It grew steadily through the 2000s, moving to Las Vegas and expanding its game lineup beyond the Street Fighter series. In 2016 the Sunday finals moved into the Mandalay Bay arena, a landmark for the genre. In 2021, Sony Interactive Entertainment and Endeavor's RTS jointly acquired the event. An international edition, Evo Japan, has also been held since 2018.

Format and games

Evo's main lineup typically includes recent entries in series such as Street Fighter, Tekken, Guilty Gear, Mortal Kombat, and Super Smash Bros., alongside extensive side tournaments run by the community for hundreds of other titles. Entrant numbers for headline games have reached many thousands, making Evo's open brackets among the largest in esports.

Legacy

Evo is widely regarded as the de facto world championship for fighting games and the centrepiece of the fighting game community (FGC). Champions such as Daigo Umehara, Justin Wong, and SonicFox built much of their reputations on Evo victories.
