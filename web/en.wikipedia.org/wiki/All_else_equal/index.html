All else equal

All else equal — more commonly expressed by the Latin phrase ceteris paribus ("other things being equal") — is a methodological clause used in economics and other sciences to isolate the effect of a single variable by assuming that all other relevant factors remain unchanged.

Usage in economics

Statements of economic laws are typically qualified ceteris paribus: for example, the law of demand states that, all else equal, a rise in the price of a good reduces the quantity demanded. The clause permits analysis of one causal relationship at a time in systems where many variables move simultaneously. John Stuart Mill and Alfred Marshall were influential in codifying the method in economics.

Criticism

Critics note that in real economies other things rarely stay equal, and that heavy reliance on the assumption can make models misleading when the "held constant" factors in fact interact with the variable of interest. The related but distinct Latin phrase mutatis mutandis ("with the necessary changes having been made") is used when comparing cases that differ in detail.
