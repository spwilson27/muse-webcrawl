Jenji Kohan

Jenji Leslie Kohan (born July 5, 1969) is an American television writer and producer. She is best known as the creator of the Showtime comedy-drama series Weeds and the Netflix comedy-drama series Orange Is the New Black.

Early life and education

Kohan was born into a Jewish family in Los Angeles, California. Her father, Alan W. "Buz" Kohan, is an Emmy Award-winning television writer and music composer; her mother, Rhea Kohan, is a television writer, novelist, and occasional actress. Her older brothers are David Kohan, co-creator of Will & Grace, and Jono Kohan. She graduated from Beverly Hills High School in 1987 and attended Brandeis University before transferring to Columbia University, where she graduated in 1991 with a degree in English language and literature.

Career

Kohan began her career writing for shows such as The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, Mad About You, Tracey Takes On..., and Sex and the City. She created Weeds (2005–2012), about a widowed suburban mother who becomes a marijuana dealer, and Orange Is the New Black (2013–2019), adapted from Piper Kerman's memoir about her year in a women's federal prison. The latter became one of Netflix's most successful original series. Kohan also executive-produced GLOW through her production company, Tilted Productions.

Personal life

Kohan lives in the Los Feliz neighborhood of Los Angeles. She has three children, the eldest of whom, Charlie Noxon, died in a skiing accident in Park City, Utah, in December 2019.
