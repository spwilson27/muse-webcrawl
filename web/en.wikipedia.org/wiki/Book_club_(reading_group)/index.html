Book club (reading group)

A book club, also called a reading group, is a group of people who meet regularly to discuss books they have all read. Typically, members select a book in advance, read it independently, and then gather—in person or online—to share impressions, interpretations, and criticism. Book clubs may focus on a particular genre, author, or theme, or may read a varied selection chosen by rotation among members.

History

Reading groups have roots in the literary salons and societies of the 17th and 18th centuries, and in the 19th century they became a popular means of self-education, particularly for women excluded from formal education. In the 20th century, commercial book clubs that mailed selected volumes to subscribers also flourished. Television and celebrity-endorsed clubs, notably Oprah's Book Club, launched in 1996, dramatically increased the commercial impact of club selections. Online and virtual book clubs have since expanded the format worldwide.

Formats

Clubs vary widely: some are informal social gatherings among friends, while others are organized by libraries, schools, workplaces, or publishers. Meetings commonly last one to two hours and may include prepared discussion questions provided by publishers.
