Hector Pieterson

Hector Pieterson (19 August 1963 – 16 June 1976) was a South African schoolboy who was shot and killed by police during the Soweto uprising of 1976, when police opened fire on black students protesting the imposition of Afrikaans as a medium of instruction in local schools. He became an icon of the uprising after photographer Sam Nzima captured an image of the dying boy being carried by fellow student Mbuyisa Makhubo, with Pieterson's sister Antoinette Sithole running beside them.

The uprising

On 16 June 1976, thousands of students in Soweto marched to protest the Afrikaans Medium Decree of 1974, which required black schools to teach certain subjects in Afrikaans rather than English. Police confronted the march and fired on the crowd. Pieterson, aged twelve, was among the first students killed; estimates of the total death toll over the ensuing unrest range from 176 to several hundred. The killings sparked nationwide protests and intensified international opposition to apartheid.

Legacy

The Sam Nzima photograph was published around the world and became one of the most famous images of the apartheid era. In democratic South Africa, 16 June is commemorated as Youth Day, a public holiday. The Hector Pieterson Museum, opened in Orlando West, Soweto, in 2002 near the site of the shooting, documents the events of the uprising and Pieterson's story.
