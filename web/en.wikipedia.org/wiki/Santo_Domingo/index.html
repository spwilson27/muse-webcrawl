Santo Domingo

Santo Domingo, once known as Santo Domingo de Guzmán, is the capital and largest city of the Dominican Republic and the most populous metropolitan area in the Caribbean, with about 3.6 million residents. It is situated on the southeast coast of Hispaniola, at the mouth of the Ozama River.

Founded in 1496/1498 by Bartholomew Columbus, Santo Domingo is the oldest continuously inhabited European settlement in the Americas and was the first seat of Spanish colonial rule in the New World: its Colonial Zone (UNESCO) holds the Americas' first cathedral (Santa María la Menor), first hospital, first customs house and first university. The city was renamed Ciudad Trujillo under the dictator Rafael Trujillo (1930-1961). It is the Dominican Republic's economic and cultural centre.
