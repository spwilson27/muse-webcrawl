The Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch and All the East is an autocephalous Eastern Orthodox church, one of the ancient patriarchates of early Christianity. It traces its foundation to the apostles Peter and Paul, and its faithful were first called "Christians" in Antioch according to the Acts of the Apostles. Although named for the ancient city of Antioch (modern Antakya in Turkey), the patriarchate's seat has been in Damascus, Syria, since the fourteenth century.

History

Antioch was one of the great centres of the early church and home to a major theological school. The patriarchate was divided by the aftermath of the Council of Chalcedon (451), from which a parallel Syriac Orthodox tradition also descends. Under Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman rule, the church's fortunes varied; from 1724 a separate Melkite Greek Catholic Church emerged in communion with Rome. Until the late nineteenth century the patriarchs were usually ethnic Greeks, but in 1899 the Arab Meletius II was elected, beginning the modern Arab character of the patriarchate's hierarchy.

Organisation and diaspora

The patriarchate is governed by the Patriarch together with the Holy Synod of metropolitans. Its heartland is Syria and Lebanon, with additional dioceses in Iraq, Kuwait and the Arabian Peninsula. Emigration has produced large archdioceses abroad, including in North America, South America, Australia and Western Europe; the Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese of North America is a prominent self-governing part of the patriarchate. The church maintains the University of Balamand in Lebanon as a centre of theological education.

Relations with other churches

The patriarchate is in full communion with the other Eastern Orthodox churches. It has a particularly close relationship with the Syriac Orthodox Church, with which agreements on pastoral cooperation have been reached, while full communion remains unrealised. Its primates have played a notable role in the modern ecumenical movement and in Middle Eastern Christian affairs.
