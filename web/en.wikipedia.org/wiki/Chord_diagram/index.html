Chord diagram

A chord diagram is a graphical method of displaying the inter-relationships between data in a matrix. The data are arranged radially around a circle with the relationships between the data points typically drawn as arcs connecting the data, showing that they share a common resource or connection.

Description

Each entity is represented by a fragment on the outer part of the circle. Arcs (chords) between fragments represent flows or shared quantities; the size of an arc at its endpoints is proportional to the importance of the flow. Asymmetric flows are often shown by tapering or by offsetting the arc from one end.

Uses

Chord diagrams are popular in genomics, where they display similarities between chromosomes, and in studies of migration, trade, and information flows. They were popularized in data visualization by tools such as Circos and D3.js, though critics note they can become cluttered with many categories.

History

The form descends from flow maps and from chord diagrams in knot theory, a separate mathematical usage in which points on a circle are joined by chords to represent pairings, used in the study of Vassiliev invariants and string theory.
