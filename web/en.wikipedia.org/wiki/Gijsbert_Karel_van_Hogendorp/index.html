Gijsbert Karel van Hogendorp

Born: October 27, 1762, Rotterdam, Dutch Republic

Died: August 5, 1834 (aged 71), The Hague, Netherlands

Occupation: Statesman

Known for: Dutch Constitution of 1814

Gijsbert Karel van Hogendorp (October 27, 1762 – August 5, 1834) was a Dutch statesman who played a leading role in the restoration of Dutch independence in 1813 and in drafting the first constitution of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. He is regarded as one of the founding fathers of the modern Dutch state.

Early life and career

Van Hogendorp was born in Rotterdam into a prominent regent family; his mother was a granddaughter of the admiral Michiel de Ruyter's line of public servants, and his father held colonial office in the East Indies. Educated at home and in Berlin, where he spent time at the Prussian court, he became a pensionary of Rotterdam in 1788 and an Orangist supporter of Stadholder William V. When French forces invaded and the Batavian Republic was proclaimed in 1795, he withdrew from public life.

Restoration of 1813

During the French annexation (1810–1813) Van Hogendorp quietly prepared for a restoration, drafting a sketch for a new constitution in advance. When French authority collapsed in November 1813, he formed, with Frans Adam van der Duyn van Maasdam and Leopold van Limburg Stirum, the famous Triumvirate (Driemanschap) that proclaimed a provisional government in The Hague and invited William Frederick, Prince of Orange, to return as sovereign prince.

Constitution and later career

Van Hogendorp chaired the commission that wrote the Constitution of 1814, which established a constitutional monarchy with a relatively progressive framework for its time, and he revised it in 1815 after the union with Belgium created the United Kingdom of the Netherlands. He served as Minister of Foreign Affairs (1813–1814) and was made a Count in 1815 and Minister of State in 1816. Ill health and growing disagreements over economic policy and the Belgian question led him to withdraw to his estate, where he wrote extensively on trade, taxation, and the colonies, advocating free trade and colonial reform.
