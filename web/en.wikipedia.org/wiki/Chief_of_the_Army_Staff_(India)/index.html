Overview

The Chief of the Army Staff (COAS) is the professional head and highest-ranking officer of the Indian Army, and a member of the Chiefs of Staff Committee. The COAS is a four-star general appointed by the President of India on the advice of the government, normally for a term until the age of 62.

History

The office descends from the Commander-in-Chief, India, of British India. After independence in 1947, General Sir Rob Lockhart became the first Commander-in-Chief of the Indian Army; General K. M. Cariappa, who took over in January 1949, was the first Indian to hold the post, an event commemorated as Army Day. The title was changed to Chief of the Army Staff in 1955. Field Marshal Sam Manekshaw, chief during the 1971 war, is among the most celebrated holders of the office.

Role

The COAS commands the world's second-largest standing army from Army Headquarters in New Delhi, oversees its seven operational commands, and advises the government on land warfare. Since 2020 the COAS has served alongside the Chief of Defence Staff, who is the senior-most officer of the Indian Armed Forces.
