Ogden Mills (December 18, 1856 – January 29, 1929) was an American financier and Thoroughbred racehorse owner. He was the son of Darius Ogden Mills, a wealthy California banker and investor who founded the Bank of California, and inherited a large share of the family fortune derived from banking, mining, and railroads.

Life

Mills was educated at Harvard University. Active in business in New York, he was a partner in his family's financial interests and served on the boards of numerous companies. He was also a notable figure in American society, maintaining homes in New York City and at Staatsburg, New York, where his mansion — now the Staatsburgh State Historic Site — overlooks the Hudson River.

Thoroughbred racing

A prominent owner and breeder of Thoroughbred racehorses, Mills operated Wheatley Stable in partnership with his sister Gladys Mills Phipps. His son, Ogden Livingston Mills, served as United States Secretary of the Treasury under President Herbert Hoover from 1932 to 1933.
