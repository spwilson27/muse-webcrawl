The Arctic convoys of World War II were oceangoing convoys that sailed from the United Kingdom, Iceland, and North America to the northern ports of the Soviet Union — primarily Murmansk and Arkhangelsk — between 1941 and 1945. They carried Lend-Lease supplies of tanks, aircraft, fuel, and food to support the Soviet war effort against Nazi Germany after the German invasion of the USSR in June 1941.

Route and conditions

Convoys assembled at Loch Ewe in Scotland or at Hvalfjörður in Iceland, passed north of occupied Norway, and ran the gauntlet of German U-boats, aircraft, and surface ships based in Norway. The route was among the most dangerous of the war: in winter the convoys faced polar darkness, storms, and pack ice that forced them close to enemy-held coasts; in summer, near-constant daylight exposed them to air attack.

Convoy series

Outbound convoys were designated PQ until late 1942, then JW; homeward convoys were QP, then RA. The first convoy, Operation Dervish, arrived at Arkhangelsk in August 1941. The most disastrous sailing was Convoy PQ 17 in July 1942: fearing an attack by the German battleship Tirpitz, the Admiralty ordered the convoy to scatter, and 24 of its 35 merchant ships were sunk. Convoys were suspended at intervals during the summer months.

Outcome

In total about 1,400 merchant ships made the passage, delivering more than four million tons of cargo. Some 85 merchant ships and 16 Royal Navy warships were lost, and roughly 3,000 seamen died. Veterans of the convoys were later recognised by the United Kingdom with the Arctic Star medal, instituted in 2012, and by Russia with the Medal of Ushakov.
