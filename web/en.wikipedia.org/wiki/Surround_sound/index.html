Surround sound is a technique for enriching the fidelity and depth of sound reproduction by using multiple audio channels from speakers that surround the listener (surround channels). At its most basic it adds one or more channels behind the listener to stereo's front left and right channels, so that sound can be heard from directions other than the screen or stage. Surround sound is characterised by a listener location or sweet spot where the audio effects perform best, and by a fixed or dynamic spread of sound that envelops the audience.

History

The first documented use of surround sound was for Walt Disney's Fantasia (1940), whose "Fantasound" system used multiple channels played through speakers around specially equipped theatres. Multichannel experiments continued through the 1950s with Cinerama and CinemaScope releases. A decisive step came in 1976–77 with Dolby Stereo, a matrixed optical format that carried left, centre, right, and a mono surround channel on standard 35mm film; its success with films such as Star Wars (1977) made surround a cinema standard. The digital era began with Batman Returns (1992) in Dolby Digital 5.1, followed by DTS and SDDS. For the home, DVD carried 5.1 sound into living rooms in the late 1990s, and object-based formats — Dolby Atmos (2012) and DTS:X — later added height channels and position-based audio objects.

Channel configurations

Configurations are written as n.m, where n is the number of full-range channels and m the number of low-frequency effects (LFE) channels feeding a subwoofer. The classic home layout, 5.1, comprises front left, centre, front right, two surround speakers at the sides or rear, and one subwoofer; the centre channel anchors dialogue to the screen. 7.1 adds two rear surrounds for smoother panning behind the listener. Object-based systems extend the notation (for example 5.1.4) with ceiling or upward-firing height speakers.

Technology

Surround mixes are created in the studio by positioning sounds in a multichannel field during the mix. Distribution formats either carry each channel discretely (modern digital formats) or matrix-encode extra channels into a stereo pair (legacy Dolby Pro Logic). Some systems, such as Ambisonics and wave field synthesis, aim to reconstruct a complete sound field rather than feed discrete speakers, and virtual surround uses psychoacoustic processing (HRTFs) to simulate surround through headphones or a soundbar.

Applications

Beyond cinema and home theatre, surround sound is used in video games for positional awareness, in music production (notably SACD and DVD-Audio releases, and modern spatial-audio streaming), in theme-park attractions, and in broadcast sports, where crowd ambience is placed in the surrounds while commentary stays in the centre channel.
