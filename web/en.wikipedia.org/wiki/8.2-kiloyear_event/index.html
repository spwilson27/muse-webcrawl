8.2-kiloyear event

The 8.2-kiloyear event was an abrupt global climatic cooling around 8,200 years before present (c. 6,200 BCE), lasting roughly 150 years. It is the largest abrupt climate change of the Holocene and is recorded in Greenland ice cores and worldwide proxies.


Date: c. 6200 BCE
Duration: ~150 years
Cause: Lake Agassiz–Ojibway outburst flood into North Atlantic

Cause

The final drainage of proglacial Lakes Agassiz and Ojibway in Canada released a huge pulse of fresh water into the Labrador Sea, weakening Atlantic meridional overturning circulation and cooling the Northern Hemisphere. North Atlantic temperatures fell several degrees; conditions became drier in parts of Africa and Asia.

Effects

Effects are linked to shifts in Neolithic settlement and agriculture in the Near East and Europe, and it forms a formal marker: the event defines the boundary between the Northgrippian and Meghalayan stages in the geological time scale subdivisions of the Holocene.
