Arcade game

An arcade game is a coin-operated entertainment machine, typically installed in public venues such as restaurants, bars, and amusement arcades. Most arcade games are video games, pinball machines, electro-mechanical games, or redemption games such as claw machines and skee-ball.

History

Coin-operated amusements date to the late 19th century, and pinball dominated arcades through the mid-20th century. The arcade video game era began with Computer Space (1971) and Atari's Pong (1972). A golden age followed from the late 1970s to the early 1980s, driven by Space Invaders (1978), Asteroids, Pac-Man (1980), and Donkey Kong (1981). After the North American video game crash of 1983, arcades revived in the late 1980s and peaked again with fighting games such as Street Fighter II (1991) and Mortal Kombat, and with 3D racers and light-gun games in the 1990s.

Decline and culture

As home consoles matched and then surpassed arcade hardware in the late 1990s, arcades declined sharply in the West, though they remained strong in Japan, where game centres continue to feature rhythm games, prize games, and networked competitive titles. Arcade design — short sessions, escalating difficulty, high-score tables, and payment per play — shaped video game conventions generally, and classic cabinets and scores remain a focus of collector and retro-gaming culture.
