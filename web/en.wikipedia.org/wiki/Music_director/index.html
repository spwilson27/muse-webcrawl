Music director

A music director, musical director or director of music is the person responsible for the musical aspects of a performance, production, or organization. This term applies to many contexts in music, including orchestras, theatre, film, television, and churches. In a theatre production, the music director may also be called the musical director and is responsible for overseeing the musical elements of the production, including rehearsing the orchestra and the singers, arranging music, and conducting performances.

Orchestras

In an orchestra, the music director is the artistic leader and principal conductor of the ensemble. The music director is responsible for selecting repertoire, shaping the ensemble's sound and artistic identity, engaging guest soloists and conductors, and often serves as the public face of the organization. Famous music directors have included Herbert von Karajan of the Berlin Philharmonic and Leonard Bernstein of the New York Philharmonic.

Theatre

In musical theatre, the music director (abbreviated MD) teaches the music to the cast, rehearses the ensemble, may arrange or orchestrate material, and frequently conducts the performances from the orchestra pit, often playing keyboard as a conductor-pianist. In Broadway and West End productions the music director is distinct from the director, who is responsible for staging, and the choreographer, who creates the dances.

Film and television

In film, a music director may supervise the composer's work, coordinate recording sessions, and work with music editors and orchestrators to fit the score to the picture. The role overlaps with that of music supervisor, though music supervisors typically focus on licensing existing recordings rather than original score production.

Church music

In churches, the director of music oversees the choir, organist, and other musicians, plans music for services, and may conduct the choir. The title "Minister of Music" is also common in some denominations.
