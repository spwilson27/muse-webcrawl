Pre-main-sequence star

A pre-main-sequence star (also known as a PMS star or PMS object) is a star in the stage of stellar evolution between being a protostar and reaching the main sequence. By this phase the object has become visible as an optical star, having blown away much of its surrounding envelope of gas and dust, but it has not yet begun sustained hydrogen fusion in its core; its energy comes instead from gravitational contraction.

Evolution

A pre-main-sequence star descends the Hayashi track on the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, remaining fully convective while it contracts at roughly constant surface temperature. Low-mass stars then move onto the Henyey track, developing a radiative core as they approach the main sequence. The duration of the phase depends strongly on mass: a star of one solar mass spends tens of millions of years contracting, while a ten-solar-mass star reaches the main sequence in well under a million years.

Types

Low- and intermediate-mass pre-main-sequence stars are observed as T Tauri stars and Herbig Ae/Be stars, respectively. They are characterised by strong emission lines, infrared excess from circumstellar disks, irregular variability, and powerful stellar winds and jets. These disks are the birthplaces of planets, and many pre-main-sequence systems show direct evidence of ongoing planet formation. See also stellar evolution.
