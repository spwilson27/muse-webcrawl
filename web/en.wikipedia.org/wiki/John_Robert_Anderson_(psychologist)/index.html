John Robert Anderson (born 27 August 1947) is a Canadian-born American cognitive psychologist. He is a professor of psychology and computer science at Carnegie Mellon University and is best known as the creator of the ACT-R cognitive architecture, one of the most influential computational theories of human cognition.

Research

Anderson studied at the University of British Columbia and completed his doctorate at Stanford University in 1972, working on human memory. Early in his career, with Gordon Bower, he developed the HAM theory of memory. At Carnegie Mellon he developed the ACT (Adaptive Control of Thought) family of theories, which evolved through ACT* into ACT-R, a production-system architecture intended to model the full range of human cognition, from perception and memory to problem solving and language.

Applying ACT-R to education, Anderson and colleagues developed cognitive tutors — computer-based instructional systems grounded in models of student knowledge — which were widely deployed in American schools for mathematics instruction through the company Carnegie Learning. His textbook Cognitive Psychology and Its Implications has gone through many editions.

Honours

Anderson has received the American Psychological Association's Distinguished Scientific Contribution Award and the Rumelhart Prize for contributions to the theoretical foundations of human cognition, and is a member of the National Academy of Sciences.
