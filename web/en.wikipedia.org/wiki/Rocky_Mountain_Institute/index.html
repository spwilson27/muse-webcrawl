Rocky Mountain Institute

Rocky Mountain Institute (RMI) is an American non-profit organization founded in 1982 by physicist Amory Lovins and Hunter Lovins. Based in Basalt, Colorado, with offices worldwide, RMI works to transform global energy systems toward clean, efficient, renewable energy.

Originally a small think tank known for promoting 'soft energy paths' and the concept of negawatts—energy saved being cheaper than energy generated—RMI has grown into an organisation of several hundred staff working on electricity, buildings, mobility, industry and finance in the United States, China, India, Africa and island economies.

Work

RMI is known for books such as Winning the Oil Endgame (2004) and Reinventing Fire (2011), and for its net-zero Innovation Center headquarters (2015). In 2019 Carbon War Room merged into RMI. The institute describes itself as non-partisan and market-focused, collaborating with companies and governments on decarbonisation.
