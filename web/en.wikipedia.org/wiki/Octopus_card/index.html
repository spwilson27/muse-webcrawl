Octopus card

The Octopus card (Chinese: 八達通) is a rechargeable contactless stored value smart card for making electronic payments in Hong Kong. Launched in September 1997 to collect fares for the territory's mass transit system, the Octopus system became the world's first major contactless smart card payment system and has since grown into a widely used payment system for public transport and many other purposes.

The card is issued by Octopus Cards Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary of Octopus Holdings Limited, which is jointly owned by major transport operators in Hong Kong. The system initially accepted payment only on transport systems, but the card soon expanded for use in convenience stores, supermarkets, fast-food restaurants, on-street parking meters, car parks, and service stations.

History

The Octopus system was introduced in 1997 after three years of development. It was launched on 1 September 1997 initially covering the MTR, KCR East Rail, KCR Light Rail, and franchised buses. By 2000, the card had been accepted on almost all public transport in Hong Kong.

Types of cards

Octopus cards are available in several forms, including standard cards, mini Octopus, and versions embedded in key fobs, watches and mobile phone cases. Anonymous cards can be purchased without registration, while personalised cards are registered to an individual and can be used for access control and concessions.
