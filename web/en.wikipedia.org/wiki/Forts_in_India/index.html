Forts in India

India possesses one of the largest numbers of forts of any country, built over more than two millennia by Hindu kingdoms, sultanates, the Mughal Empire, the Marathas, regional powers, and European trading companies. Ancient texts such as the Arthashastra classically divided forts into types by terrain: water forts (jal-durga), desert forts, hill forts (giri-durga), forest forts, and mud forts.

Notable forts

Famous examples include the Red Fort in Delhi and Agra Fort, both Mughal imperial residences and UNESCO World Heritage Sites; the hill forts of Rajasthan — Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh, Ranthambore, Gagron, Amber, and Jaisalmer — inscribed collectively by UNESCO in 2013; the Deccan forts of Daulatabad, Golconda, and Bidar; the Maratha hill and sea forts of Maharashtra, such as Raigad, Rajgad, Sindhudurg, and Vijaydurg; and southern strongholds like Gingee and Vellore. Colonial powers built coastal works including Fort St. George in Chennai and Fort William in Kolkata.

Architecture and preservation

Indian forts typically combine massive curtain walls, bastions, moats, layered gateways, palaces, temples, mosques, granaries, and water-harvesting systems designed to withstand long sieges. Many are now protected monuments under the Archaeological Survey of India or state archaeology departments and are major tourist attractions, though encroachment and decay remain persistent problems.
