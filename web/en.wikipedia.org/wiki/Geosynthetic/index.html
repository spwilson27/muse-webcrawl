Geosynthetic

Geosynthetics are synthetic products, generally made from petrochemical-based polymers such as polypropylene, polyester, and polyethylene, used to stabilise terrain and solve civil engineering problems. They are employed in roads, embankments, reservoirs, canals, landfills, coastal works, and environmental containment, performing one or more of five basic functions: separation, reinforcement, filtration, drainage, and containment.

Types

Geotextiles are permeable fabrics, woven or nonwoven, used for separation, filtration, and reinforcement. Geomembranes are essentially impermeable sheets used as liners for landfills, ponds, and tunnels. Geogrids are open mesh structures that interlock with soil or aggregate to reinforce slopes and retaining walls. Geonets and geocomposites provide in-plane drainage; geocells are honeycomb structures that confine infill; and geosynthetic clay liners combine bentonite clay with geotextiles as hydraulic barriers.

History and use

The modern geosynthetics industry dates from the late 1950s and 1960s, with woven fabrics first used beneath riprap in coastal engineering in the Netherlands and the United States; the term "geotextile" was coined in the 1970s. Geosynthetics are attractive because they are factory-controlled, quick to install, and can replace scarce natural materials such as graded stone filters or thick clay liners, though their long-term durability, UV degradation, and microplastic release are studied concerns.
