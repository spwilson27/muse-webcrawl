Japan Meteorological Agency

Abbreviation: JMA
Founded: 1875 (as Tokyo Meteorological Observatory)
Headquarters: Tokyo, Japan

The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA; Japanese: 気象庁, Kishō-chō) is the Japanese government agency responsible for meteorological, seismological, and related geophysical observation and forecasting. An agency of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, it operates national weather services, issues warnings for typhoons, heavy rain and snow, earthquakes and tsunamis, and monitors volcanoes and the climate.

History

The agency traces its origins to the Tokyo Meteorological Observatory founded in 1875; it later became the Central Meteorological Observatory and adopted its present name and agency status in 1956. JMA has long been a leader in numerical weather prediction and operates the Himawari series of geostationary weather satellites, launched and monitored in cooperation with Japan's space programmes.

Services

JMA issues public weather forecasts, typhoon warnings for the western North Pacific (in its role as the WMO Regional Specialized Meteorological Center for typhoons, it assigns names from the regional list), and real-time earthquake information under the Japan Meteorological Agency seismic intensity scale (shindo). Its Earthquake Early Warning system broadcasts alerts seconds before strong shaking. JMA also serves as one of the world's Volcanic Ash Advisory Centers and issues tsunami warnings for Japan's coasts.
