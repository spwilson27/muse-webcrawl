Soft rock

Soft rock
Stylistic origins: Rock, pop, folk rock, singer-songwriter
Cultural origins: Late 1960s–early 1970s, US and UK
Typical instruments: Acoustic guitar, piano, vocals, bass, soft drums

Soft rock (also light rock) is a derivative form of pop rock that emphasizes slower tempos, smooth productions, melodic songs, and harmonious vocals, with lyrics often focused on romantic love and everyday life. It emerged in the late 1960s and early 1970s as a mellower counterpart to hard rock, and it fed directly into the adult contemporary radio format. It is unrelated to soft drinks and to soft science fiction.

History

Precursors include the lush pop of the late Beatles, the harmonies of the Beach Boys, and the singer-songwriter movement of James Taylor, Carole King, and Cat Stevens. Through the 1970s, acts such as Bread, America, the Carpenters, Chicago, Fleetwood Mac, and the Eagles defined the sound on AM and FM radio. Billy Joel, Elton John, and Paul McCartney (with Wings) brought a piano-led variant to enormous commercial success.

By the late 1970s, soft rock blended into yacht rock — the polished, jazz- and R&B-inflected West Coast sound of Steely Dan, Toto, and Christopher Cross. In the 1980s, adult contemporary radio kept soft rock commercially central with artists such as Phil Collins, Lionel Richie, and Air Supply, even as the term itself acquired a faintly pejorative sense of blandness among rock critics. Periodic revivals and critical reappraisals have since highlighted the craftsmanship of the genre's songwriting and studio production.

Characteristics

Soft rock songs typically feature acoustic guitar or piano as the lead instrument, restrained electric guitar, smooth vocal harmonies, and an unobtrusive rhythm section. Production emphasizes clarity and warmth, and song structures follow verse–chorus pop conventions with middle-of-the-road themes.
