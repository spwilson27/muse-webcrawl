DefinitionContent purporting to represent truth and facts about reality

ContrastFiction

Nonfiction (also spelled non-fiction) is any content that, in good faith, purports to represent truth and accuracy regarding information, events or people. It stands in contrast to fiction, whose narratives are imagined. Nonfiction spans an enormous range, from reference works and textbooks to personal essays and narrative journalism.

Forms

  • Life writing: biography, autobiography and memoir.
  • History and chronicle.
  • Essays and criticism, from Montaigne to the modern personal essay.
  • Journalism and reportage; "New Journalism" and literary journalism blur toward narrative art while asserting factual accuracy.
  • Reference: encyclopaedias, dictionaries, atlases, manuals and how-to guides.
  • Science and nature writing, philosophy, religious texts, and academic monographs.

Distinctions and debates

The fiction/nonfiction boundary is a convention of publishing and shelving rather than an absolute: nonfiction writers use narrative techniques, dialogue and scene construction, and must still represent events truthfully. Scandals over fabricated memoirs — such as James Frey's A Million Little Pieces — highlight the implicit "nonfiction contract" with readers. "Creative nonfiction" is a widely taught genre label for factually accurate, literary prose.

Statistics and awards

Nonfiction accounts for a large share of book sales, especially in areas like self-help, business and popular science. Major honours include the Pulitzer Prizes for General Nonfiction and Biography, the Baillie Gifford Prize (UK), and the National Book Award for Nonfiction.
