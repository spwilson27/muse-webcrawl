Bluefin tuna

Bluefin tuna
SpeciesAtlantic bluefin (Thunnus thynnus), Pacific bluefin (T. orientalis), Southern bluefin (T. maccoyii)
SizeUp to ~3 m and over 600 kg (Atlantic)
Notable traitWarm-bodied (regional endothermy)

Bluefin tuna are the three largest species of tuna in the genus Thunnus: the Atlantic bluefin, the Pacific bluefin, and the southern bluefin. They are powerful, streamlined predators that can swim at high speed, dive to great depths, and — unusually for fish — maintain body temperatures above that of the surrounding water through counter-current heat exchange in their muscles.

Biology

Atlantic bluefin can exceed 3 metres in length and 600 kilograms, and live for up to 40 years. All bluefins migrate over whole ocean basins between feeding grounds in cold waters and spawning grounds in warm waters such as the Gulf of Mexico and the Mediterranean Sea. Tagging studies have revealed trans-Atlantic crossings and remarkable navigational fidelity to spawning sites.

Fisheries and conservation

Bluefin, especially the fatty belly meat (toro), command the highest prices of any fish in the sushi and sashimi market; a single Pacific bluefin famously sold for over US$3 million at Tokyo's New Year auction in 2019. Intense demand drove severe overfishing: Atlantic and southern bluefin stocks collapsed in the late 20th century, prompting strict international quotas under bodies such as ICCAT. Management measures have since allowed partial recoveries, and the Atlantic bluefin's conservation status improved, though the fisheries remain controversial. "Ranching" of wild-caught juveniles fattened in pens supplies much of the market.
