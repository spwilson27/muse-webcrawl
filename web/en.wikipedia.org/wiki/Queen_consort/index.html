Queen consort

A queen consort is the wife of a reigning king, or an empress consort in the case of an emperor. A queen consort usually shares her spouse's social rank and status and holds the feminine equivalent of his monarchical titles, but she does not normally share his political or sovereign powers — in contrast to a queen regnant, who reigns in her own right.

Role

Historically, the chief dynastic role of a queen consort was to produce heirs, and consorts have often exercised informal influence at court or served as regents for minor children. Some consorts, such as Eleanor of Aquitaine or Catherine de' Medici, were major political figures in their own right. Consorts of female monarchs hold analogous titles: the husband of a queen regnant is typically a king consort or, in the British practice, a prince consort — the title famously held by Prince Albert, husband of Queen Victoria.

Titles and styles

A queen consort is ordinarily styled "Her Majesty" and is crowned alongside her husband in countries retaining coronation rites. On the death of the king, a consort becomes a queen dowager; if she is the mother of the new monarch she is often styled queen mother. When a consort outlives a reign into that of a grandchild, further compound styles have occasionally been used.

Examples

Notable queen consorts include Marie Antoinette of France, Alexandra of Denmark (consort of Edward VII), and, in Britain, Queen Camilla, consort of Charles III, crowned with him in May 2023. In Japan, the consort of the emperor is the empress consort, currently Empress Masako; in Spain, Queen Letizia is consort of Felipe VI.
