Remdesivir

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Structure of remdesivir

Remdesivir, sold under the brand name Veklury, is a broad-spectrum antiviral medication developed by the biopharmaceutical company Gilead Sciences. It is administered via injection into a vein. During the COVID-19 pandemic, remdesivir was approved or authorized for emergency use to treat COVID-19 in numerous countries.

Remdesivir is a prodrug (specifically a phosphoramidate prodrug of an adenosine nucleotide analogue) that, after intracellular metabolism, interferes with the action of viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase and evades proofreading by viral exonuclease, causing a decrease in viral RNA production and, in some viruses, producing mutations in the viral RNA.

Medical uses

In October 2020, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved remdesivir for use in adults and adolescents hospitalized with COVID-19; it was later extended to non-hospitalized patients at high risk of severe disease when given early. In November 2020, the World Health Organization issued a conditional recommendation against the use of remdesivir in hospitalized patients, based on the Solidarity trial; later analyses yielded a more nuanced picture, with subsequent guidance supporting use in certain patient groups.

Development

Remdesivir was originally developed beginning in 2009 as part of a program on RNA viruses. It was investigated for hepatitis C and respiratory syncytial virus before showing activity against the Ebola virus during the 2013–2016 West African epidemic, though in a randomized trial it was less effective than monoclonal antibody treatments. It also showed promise in animal models against MERS-CoV and SARS-CoV, which led to its rapid testing against SARS-CoV-2 in 2020.

Mechanism of action

As an adenosine nucleoside triphosphate analog, remdesivir interferes with the action of viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. It causes delayed chain termination of the nascent viral RNA, inhibiting viral replication.
