STS-95

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

STS-95
OrbiterDiscovery
Launch29 October 1998
Duration8 days, 21 hours
Notable crewJohn Glenn (aged 77); Pedro Duque; Chiaki Mukai
Glenn returns to space, 1998.

STS-95 was a Space Shuttle mission flown by Discovery in October–November 1998, forever associated with one passenger: Senator John Glenn, who at 77 became the oldest person to fly in space, returning 36 years after his pioneering Mercury orbital flight. The mission drew enormous public attention — its launch crowd rivalled Apollo-era turnouts.

Officially, Glenn flew as a payload specialist for geriatric research, NASA comparing the effects of spaceflight on his ageing body with data from young astronauts; critics called it a publicity flight for a sitting senator, a charge Glenn deflected by pointing to the science programme of over 80 experiments, including solar and astronomy payloads and the deployment of a free-flying satellite.

Context

STS-95 flew as ISS assembly was beginning, and its crew included future ESA and JAXA astronauts Pedro Duque and Chiaki Mukai, plus commander Curtis Brown and pilot Steven Lindsey. Glenn's smooth flight and return were widely read as a full-circle moment for American spaceflight — the Mercury era blessing the station era.
