[Image: An old observatory dome above a stack of classic science books]

The philosophy of science is a branch of philosophy concerned with the foundations, methods and implications of science: what distinguishes science from pseudoscience, how theories change, and what scientific explanations are.

Key ideas

Karl Popper proposed falsifiability as the mark of science; Thomas Kuhn described paradigm shifts and scientific revolutions; the replication crisis in several fields revived interest in method. The field informs how claims in this very crawl — from continental drift's rejection to dark energy's acceptance — should be weighed.
