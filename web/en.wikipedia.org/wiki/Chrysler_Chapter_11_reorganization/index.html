Chrysler Chapter 11 reorganization

Chrysler headquarters, Auburn Hills, Michigan

The Chrysler Chapter 11 reorganization was a process in which the American automobile manufacturer Chrysler underwent bankruptcy proceedings under Chapter 11 of the United States Bankruptcy Code in 2009. Chrysler filed for protection on April 30, 2009, in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, after failing to reach an agreement with its lenders.

The bankruptcy was notable for its speed and for the involvement of the United States government, which provided financing through the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) and brokered an alliance with the Italian automaker Fiat.

The sale and emergence

Under the plan, substantially all of Chrysler's operating assets were sold to a new company, Chrysler Group LLC, owned by the United Auto Workers' retiree health care trust (VEBA), Fiat, and the United States and Canadian governments. Fiat initially received a 20 percent stake, with the opportunity to increase it as performance targets were met. The sale was approved by the bankruptcy court and upheld on appeal; the United States Supreme Court declined to block it.

Chrysler emerged from bankruptcy on June 10, 2009, only 41 days after filing. Sergio Marchionne, the chief executive of Fiat, became CEO of the new Chrysler. Fiat later acquired full ownership, and in 2014 the combined company Fiat Chrysler Automobiles was formed.

Controversy

The treatment of secured creditors, some of whom received less than unsecured claimants such as the union trust, drew criticism and legal challenges, including from Indiana pension funds. Defenders argued the government's intervention prevented liquidation and saved tens of thousands of jobs during the Great Recession.
