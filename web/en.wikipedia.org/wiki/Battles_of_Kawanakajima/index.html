The Battles of Kawanakajima were a series of five engagements fought during Japan's Sengoku period between the rival daimyō Takeda Shingen of Kai and Uesugi Kenshin of Echigo, on the plain of Kawanakajima in northern Shinano Province, where the Saigawa and Chikumagawa rivers meet. The battles are among the most famous episodes of the samurai era.

Background

Shingen's expansion into Shinano brought him into conflict with Kenshin, who intervened to defend the northern Shinano lords and to check Takeda power. Kawanakajima, controlling the routes between their domains, became the repeated battleground.

The five battles

The first (1553), second (1555) and third (1557) battles were essentially manoeuvres and standoffs around the Zenko-ji temple and the castles guarding the plain, with neither commander willing to risk a full engagement. The fourth battle, in September 1561, was by far the largest and bloodiest. Shingen's chancellor Yamamoto Kansuke devised the "woodpecker" plan: a detachment would drive Kenshin's army off Mount Saijo down onto the plain, where the main Takeda force waited. Kenshin anticipated the move, descended silently at night and attacked the Takeda main body at dawn. Legend holds that Kenshin rode into Shingen's command post and struck at him with his sword, Shingen parrying the blows with his iron war fan. Both armies suffered enormous casualties — by some accounts over half their strength — before disengaging. A fifth, final confrontation in 1564 was again indecisive.

Legacy

The rivalry of Shingen and Kenshin at Kawanakajima has been celebrated for centuries in Japanese literature, theatre, film and television, standing as the archetype of chivalrous warfare between evenly matched warlords. Control of Shinano remained divided, and neither clan destroyed the other.
