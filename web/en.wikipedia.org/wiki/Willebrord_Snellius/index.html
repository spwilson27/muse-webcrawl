Willebrord Snellius

Born: June 13, 1580, Leiden

Died: October 30, 1626, Leiden

Known for: Snell's law of refraction

Willebrord Snellius (born Willebrord Snel van Royen; June 13, 1580 – October 30, 1626) was a Dutch astronomer and mathematician, commonly known by his Latinised name Snellius. He is remembered chiefly for the law of refraction of light that bears his name in English-speaking countries — Snell's law.

Snellius studied law and mathematics at Leiden University, where his father Rudolf was professor of mathematics. He travelled widely in Europe, meeting astronomers including Tycho Brahe and Johannes Kepler, before succeeding his father at Leiden in 1613.

Work

In 1615–1617 Snellius carried out one of the first great geodetic triangulations, measuring the distance between Dutch cities through a network of triangles to determine the size of the Earth — a pioneering application of triangulation to surveying. Around 1621 he formulated the law of refraction, stating that the ratio of the sines of the angles of incidence and refraction is constant for a given pair of media; although he did not publish it, the law was later published by Descartes and Huygens.

His name also survives in the loxodrome studies, in methods of resecting a position from three known points (the Snellius–Pothenot problem), and in the lunar crater Snellius.
