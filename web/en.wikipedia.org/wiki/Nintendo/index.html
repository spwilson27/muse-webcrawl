Nintendo Co., Ltd. is a Japanese multinational video game company headquartered in Kyoto, Japan. It develops, publishes, and manufactures both video games and video game consoles. Nintendo was founded in 1889 as Nintendo Koppai by craftsman Fusajiro Yamauchi to produce and distribute hanafuda playing cards.

During the mid-20th century, Nintendo experimented with ventures including taxis, hotels, and toys before settling on electronic entertainment. Its arcade games, such as Donkey Kong (1981), and the Nintendo Entertainment System (1985), helped revive the North American video game industry after the crash of 1983.

History

1889–1979: playing cards and toys

Nintendo began by hand-printing hanafuda cards and became Japan's largest playing-card company. Under third president Hiroshi Yamauchi, the company diversified into instant rice, a taxi service, "love hotels", and toys — including the Ultra Hand. Game designer Gunpei Yokoi's electronic toys and the Game & Watch line of handheld games (1980) pointed the company toward electronics.

1980s–2000s: home consoles

The Family Computer (Famicom), released as the NES in the West, dominated the 8-bit era, followed by the Game Boy (1989), Super Nintendo, Nintendo 64, and GameCube. Franchise series such as Mario, The Legend of Zelda, Pokémon, Metroid, and Donkey Kong became among the best-selling in the medium.

Wii, Wii U, and Switch

The motion-controlled Wii (2006) and the Nintendo DS line broadened the gaming audience enormously. The Wii U (2012) underperformed commercially, but the hybrid Nintendo Switch (2017) became one of the best-selling consoles of all time, passing 140 million units. Satoru Iwata served as president from 2002 until his death in 2015; Shuntaro Furukawa has led the company since 2018.

Products

Nintendo's major hardware lines include the NES, Game Boy family, SNES, Nintendo 64, GameCube, Nintendo DS, Wii, Nintendo 3DS, Wii U, and Switch. Its software franchises have sold hundreds of millions of copies; the company also licenses its characters for film, theme parks (Super Nintendo World), and merchandise.

Corporate structure

Nintendo's principal subsidiaries include Nintendo of America (Redmond, Washington), Nintendo of Europe (Frankfurt), development studios such as Nintendo EPD, Monolith Soft, Retro Studios, and Next Level Games, and The Pokémon Company, a joint venture with Game Freak and Creatures Inc.
