Projection (mathematics)

In mathematics, a projection is a mapping of a set (or other mathematical structure) into a subset (or sub-structure), which is equal to its square for mapping composition—the basic property of being idempotent. That is, applying the projection twice gives the same result as applying it once. Everyday examples include dropping a perpendicular from a point to a plane in geometry, and the shadow cast by an object.

Linear algebra

In linear algebra, a projection is a linear transformation P from a vector space to itself such that P2 = P. When the transformation is represented by a matrix, the projection matrix is idempotent and (for orthogonal projections) symmetric. Orthogonal projections onto a subspace underpin least-squares approximation and the projection theorem in Hilbert spaces.

Other uses

In set theory and relational algebra, projection restricts a relation to a subset of its attributes. In category theory, projection maps pick out components of a product. Map projections in cartography transform the surface of a sphere onto a plane. See also linear map and idempotence.
