Trident

A trident is a three-pronged spear. It has been used both as a weapon and as a fishing tool for spearing fish since antiquity. The word derives from the Latin tridens, "three teeth".

Mythology and symbolism

The trident is most famously the weapon of Poseidon (Neptune), the Greek and Roman god of the sea, who could stir up storms and earthquakes by striking the ground or waves with it. It is also the trishula of the Hindu god Shiva, and appears in the hands of sea deities in many cultures. As a heraldic and national symbol, the trident appears on the flag of Barbados and in the emblem of Ukraine, whose national symbol, the tryzub, is a stylised trident.

Uses

Fishermen used tridents (also called fishgigs) for spearfishing and frog gigging. In the Roman arena, the retiarius gladiator fought with a trident and a net. Trident shapes persist in pitchfork-like agricultural tools, in military unit insignia, and in the name of the mascot and missiles of various armed forces; the US Navy SEAL insignia is known as the "Trident".
