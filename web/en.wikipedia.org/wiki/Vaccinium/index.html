Vaccinium

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Ericales
Family:Ericaceae
Genus:Vaccinium L.
Type species:Vaccinium uliginosum

Vaccinium is a common and widespread genus of shrubs or dwarf shrubs in the heath family (Ericaceae). The fruits of many species are eaten by humans, and some — including the cranberry, blueberry, bilberry (whortleberry), lingonberry (cowberry), and huckleberry — are of commercial importance.

The genus contains about 450 species, which are found predominantly in the cooler regions of the Northern Hemisphere, although there are also tropical species from areas such as Madagascar and Hawaii. The plants tend to thrive in acidic soils and in heaths, bogs, and acidic woodlands.

Description

The plant structure varies considerably between species: some trail along the ground, some are dwarf shrubs, and some are larger shrubs growing to 1–3 m (3–10 ft) tall. The flowers are most often urn-shaped (urceolate), ranging in colour from white through pink to red, and are sometimes greenish. The fruit is a berry, typically blue, purple, or red, and often edible and sweet or tart.

Taxonomy

The taxonomy of the genus is complex and still under investigation. It has traditionally been divided into subgenera and sections, including sections for cranberries (Oxycoccus), lingonberries (Vitis-idaea), blueberries (Cyanococcus and Myrtillus), and huckleberries. Molecular phylogenetic studies have shown that some groups traditionally separated are nested within Vaccinium, and the classification continues to be revised.

Cultivation and uses

Several species are grown commercially. Blueberries and cranberries are major fruit crops in North America, cultivated in acidic, well-drained soils. Lingonberries are widely harvested from the wild in Scandinavia. The fruits are eaten fresh and used in jams, juices, baked goods, and dried products, and they are valued for their content of anthocyanin pigments and other antioxidants.
