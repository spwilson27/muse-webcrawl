The Modern Language Association of America, often referred to as the Modern Language Association (MLA), is the principal professional association in the United States for scholars of language and literature. Founded in 1883, it has around 25,000 members in some 100 countries, mostly teachers and scholars of English, foreign languages and comparative literature.

Activities

The MLA publishes the journal PMLA and the MLA International Bibliography, the standard index of scholarship in the field. Its annual convention, held each January, is among the largest academic conferences in the humanities. The association also issues statements on academic freedom, working conditions and the state of language study.

MLA style

The MLA is widely known outside its membership for MLA style, the citation and formatting system set out in the MLA Handbook. MLA style is the standard for much school and undergraduate writing in English and the humanities, particularly in North America, specifying conventions for in-text citations and works-cited lists.
