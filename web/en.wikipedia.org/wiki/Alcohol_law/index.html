Alcohol law

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alcohol laws are laws relating to the manufacture, use, and sale of alcoholic beverages. They vary widely around the world, reflecting religious, cultural, and public-health differences, and commonly regulate the minimum drinking age, licensing of producers and sellers, hours and places of sale, taxation, advertising, drunk driving, and public drinking.

Drinking age

Most countries set a minimum legal drinking (or purchase) age between 16 and 21. The United States maintains 21 nationwide — established through the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984, which tied highway funding to state adoption — while most of Europe sets 18 (16 for beer and wine in some countries). Some countries, including several Muslim-majority states, prohibit alcohol entirely for Muslims or for all citizens.

Prohibition

Several countries have experimented with total or partial prohibition. The most famous case is the United States (1920–1933), where the Eighteenth Amendment and the Volstead Act banned the manufacture and sale of alcohol nationwide, encouraging organised-crime bootlegging before repeal by the Twenty-first Amendment. Iceland, Finland, Norway, and Russia also had prohibition periods in the early 20th century, and several Indian states maintain prohibition today.

Sale and distribution controls

Control models include government monopolies — such as Systembolaget in Sweden, the Vinmonopolet in Norway, and provincial liquor boards and control states in Canada and the US — and licensing systems restricting off-premises and on-premises sales. "Dry counties" and local-option jurisdictions persist in parts of the United States. Many jurisdictions restrict Sunday sales, sales hours, and open containers in public places and vehicles.

Drunk driving

Virtually all countries penalise driving under the influence, with legal blood-alcohol limits ranging from zero tolerance to the common 0.05% and 0.08% thresholds, enforced by breath testing, fines, licence suspension, and imprisonment.
