Economy of Poland

The economy of Poland is an industrialised, mixed economy and the sixth largest economy in the European Union by nominal GDP, and the fifth largest by GDP (PPP). Since the transition from a planned economy after 1989, Poland has been one of Europe's fastest-growing economies and famously avoided recession during the 2008 financial crisis.

Overview

Poland is classified as a high-income economy by the World Bank and a developed market. Its currency is the złoty (PLN). Major sectors include services, industry (machinery, vehicles, food processing, chemicals, coal), and agriculture. Poland joined the European Union in 2004 and is a major recipient of EU structural funds.

History

The "shock therapy" Balcerowicz Plan of 1990 liberalised prices, privatised state assets, and stabilised the currency. Subsequent decades saw sustained growth, rising foreign investment, and integration into German-centred manufacturing supply chains.
