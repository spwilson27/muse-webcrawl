University of Louisville

The University of Louisville (UofL) is a public research university in Louisville, Kentucky. Chartered in 1798 as one of the first city-owned universities in the United States, it became a state-supported institution in 1970. It enrolls more than 22,000 students across three campuses in the Louisville area.

History and academics

The university grew from early institutions including the Louisville Medical Institute (1837) and the Louisville Collegiate Institute. It operates twelve colleges and schools, including schools of medicine, dentistry, law, business, and engineering. The university achieved international attention in 1954 when its researchers developed early work connected with the human papillomavirus vaccine, and in 1999 it performed the first self-contained artificial heart transplant.

Campus landmarks

Grawemeyer Hall, fronting the Belknap Campus oval, is modeled on Thomas Jefferson's Rotunda at the University of Virginia. A bronze cast of Rodin's The Thinker sits in front of the hall. The campus borders Churchill Downs, home of the Kentucky Derby.

Athletics

The Louisville Cardinals compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference, which the university joined in 2014. The men's basketball program has won national championships and plays at the KFC Yum! Center in downtown Louisville.
