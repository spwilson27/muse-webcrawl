Castillo San Cristóbal

Castillo San Cristóbal
LocationSan Juan, Puerto Rico
Built1634–1790
Areaabout 27 acres (11 ha)
DesignationUNESCO World Heritage Site

Castillo San Cristóbal is a Spanish fortification in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and the largest fortress built by Spain in the Americas. Together with Castillo San Felipe del Morro it formed the defensive system of the walled city of San Juan, guarding the landward approach while El Morro guarded the harbour entrance.

History

Construction began in 1634 and continued, with major expansion in the 18th century, until about 1790. The fort is built on the principle of defence in depth, with concentric works — ravelins, a hornwork, moats, tunnels, and the great outwork El Abanico — designed to slow an attacker advancing from the east. Its guns fired during Sir Ralph Abercromby's British attack in 1797 and during the American bombardment of 1898, when the first shot of the Spanish–American War in Puerto Rico was fired from its batteries. The famous sentry box known as the Garita del Diablo is wrapped in local legend.

Preservation

The fort became part of the San Juan National Historic Site in 1949, administered by the US National Park Service, and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983 as part of La Fortaleza and San Juan National Historic Site.
