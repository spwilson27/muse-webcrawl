Sorley MacLean

Sorley MacLean
Gaelic: Somhairle MacGill-Eain
Born: 26 October 1911, Osgaig, Raasay, Scotland
Died: 24 November 1996, Inverness
Occupation: Poet, teacher
Language: Scottish Gaelic (and English translation)
Major work: Dàin do Eimhir (1943)

Sorley MacLean (Scottish Gaelic: Somhairle MacGill-Eain; 26 October 1911 – 24 November 1996) was a Scottish poet of the Isle of Raasay, widely regarded as the most important Scottish Gaelic poet of the 20th century. His work brought Gaelic poetry into dialogue with modern European literature and politics.

Life

Raised in a Gaelic-speaking family steeped in oral tradition, MacLean studied at the University of Edinburgh. He served with the Royal Signals in North Africa during the Second World War and was wounded three times, including at El Alamein. Afterwards he taught in schools across Scotland, becoming headmaster of Plockton High School.

Poetry

His masterpiece, Dàin do Eimhir agus Dàin Eile ("Poems to Eimhir", 1943), intertwines a love sequence with the Spanish Civil War, Highland history and socialist conviction. Poems such as "Hallaig" — an elegy for a cleared village on Raasay — brought him international attention, amplified by his own English translations and by admirers including Seamus Heaney. MacLean was central to the 20th-century Scottish Gaelic renaissance.
