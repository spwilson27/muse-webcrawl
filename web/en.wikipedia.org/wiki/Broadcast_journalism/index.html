Broadcast journalism

[Image placeholder: Broadcast journalism]

Broadcast journalism is the field of news and journals produced for broadcast on radio, television and, increasingly, online streaming platforms. It differs from print journalism in its immediacy, use of audio and video, and (traditionally) strict scheduling in bulletins and rolling-news formats.

History and practice

Radio news grew in the 1920s–30s; television news followed after the Second World War, with figures such as Edward R. Murrow shaping the field. The launch of CNN in 1980 inaugurated 24-hour television news, and later digital platforms blurred the line between broadcast and online media. Broadcast journalists include anchors, correspondents and producers, and the field has developed conventions of impartiality, source verification and, in many countries, public-service broadcasting mandates.
