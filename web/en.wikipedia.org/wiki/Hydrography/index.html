Hydrography

Hydrography is the branch of applied sciences which deals with the measurement and description of the physical features of oceans, seas, coastal areas, lakes and rivers, as well as with the prediction of their change over time, primarily for the purpose of safety of navigation and in support of all other marine activities, including economic development, security and defence, scientific research, and environmental protection.

Techniques

Hydrographic surveyors measure water depth (bathymetry), position dangers to navigation such as shoals and wrecks, locate aids to navigation, and record tides and currents. Historically, depths were measured with lead lines; modern surveys use single-beam and multibeam echo sounders, side-scan sonar, airborne lidar bathymetry, and satellite-derived bathymetry, with positions fixed by GNSS. Data are compiled into nautical charts — increasingly electronic navigational charts (ENCs) — maintained by national hydrographic offices.

International coordination

The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO), founded in 1921 and headquartered in Monaco, coordinates the work of national hydrographic offices and sets standards for surveys and charts, including the S-57 and the newer S-100 framework for digital hydrographic data. World Hydrography Day is observed on 21 June.

Applications

  • Production and updating of nautical charts for safe navigation.
  • Port and harbour surveys, dredging control, and coastal engineering.
  • Offshore energy development, cable and pipeline route surveys.
  • Marine environmental monitoring and habitat mapping.
  • Maritime boundary delimitation and support of the law of the sea.

The term is sometimes used in a looser sense, synonymous with maritime cartography, or in physical geography for the description of waters of the Earth — rivers, lakes, and drainage basins.
