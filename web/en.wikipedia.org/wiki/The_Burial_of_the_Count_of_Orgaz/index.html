The Burial of the Count of Orgaz


El Greco, The Burial of the Count of Orgaz, 1586–1588

The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (Spanish: El Entierro del Conde de Orgaz) is a painting by El Greco, the Greek-born master of the Spanish Renaissance. Painted between 1586 and 1588, it is widely considered his masterpiece. The work remains in the church of Santo Tomé in Toledo, Spain, for which it was commissioned, in a chapel above the count's tomb.

Subject

The painting depicts a local legend of 1323: when Don Gonzalo Ruiz de Toledo, Count of Orgaz, a famously pious benefactor of the church, was buried, Saints Stephen and Augustine miraculously descended from heaven to lay his body in the tomb with their own hands. El Greco divides the composition into two zones: below, the earthly burial, attended by contemporary Toledan nobles in black armour and dress, pointedly including portraits of the artist himself and his young son Jorge Manuel; above, heaven opens in glory, where the soul of the Count — a small naked figure carried by an angel — is received by Christ, the Virgin and John the Baptist.

Style

The work is a supreme example of Mannerism: elongated figures, cold supernatural light, and a crowded horizontal frieze of individualized portraits below contrasting with the swirling, visionary heaven above. The parish priest Andrés Núñez, who commissioned the painting, is shown reading the funeral rite; a dispute over the parish's payment to El Greco produced legal documents that illuminate the commission.

Reception

Long overshadowed after El Greco's death, the painting's reputation rose with the artist's rediscovery in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. It is now routinely ranked among the greatest paintings of Spanish art, and the town of Orgaz and the church of Santo Tomé draw large numbers of visitors to see it in situ.
