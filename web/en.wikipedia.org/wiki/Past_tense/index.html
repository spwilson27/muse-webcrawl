Past tense

The past tense is a grammatical tense whose function is to place an action or situation in the past. Examples of verbs in the past tense are the English words jumped, has jumped, and had jumped. Not all languages have a past tense; in some, the temporal location is indicated by context, aspect, or auxiliary words rather than by inflection of the verb.

English past tenses

English verbs take several forms to refer to past time. The simple past (I walked) describes a completed action at a definite or indefinite past time. The present perfect (I have walked) connects a past event to the present. The past perfect or pluperfect (I had walked) places an event before another past event. The past progressive (I was walking) describes an ongoing past action, often interrupted by another event.

Regular English verbs form the simple past by adding -ed or -d to the base form. Irregular verbs change their vowel or take other forms, as in go/went, sing/sang, and be/was/were.

Other languages

Many Indo-European languages distinguish imperfective and perfective pasts: the imperfect for ongoing or habitual past action and the aorist or preterite for completed action. Slavic languages rely heavily on aspect. Some languages, such as Mandarin Chinese, lack grammatical tense altogether and use adverbs and aspect markers such as le for completed action.
