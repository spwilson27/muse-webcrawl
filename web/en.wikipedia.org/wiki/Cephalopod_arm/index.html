Cephalopod arm

A cephalopod arm is a muscular hydrostat appendage of cephalopod molluscs such as octopuses, squid, and cuttlefish. Arms are distinguished from tentacles: arms are shorter, bear suckers along their entire length, and are used for walking, grasping, and manipulating prey, while tentacles are longer, retractable, and have suckers only at a club-shaped tip.

Structure and number

Octopuses have eight arms; squid and cuttlefish have eight arms and two tentacles. The arms surround the mouth and are connected at their bases by a web of tissue in some species. Suckers may be lined with chitinous rings or hooks in some squid. In male cephalopods, one arm is modified into a hectocotylus used to transfer spermatophores to the female during mating.

Cephalopod arms contain a large proportion of the animal's neurons and are capable of complex, partly autonomous movements, including bending, elongation, and grasping.
