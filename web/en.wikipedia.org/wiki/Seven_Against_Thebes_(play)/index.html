Seven Against Thebes (play)

Seven Against Thebes (Ancient Greek: Ἑπτὰ ἐπὶ Θήβας, Hepta epi Thēbas) is the third play in an Oedipus-themed trilogy produced by Aeschylus in 467 BC. The trilogy is sometimes called the Oedipodea; its first two plays, Laius and Oedipus, and the satyr play Sphinx are lost. The play won first prize at the Dionysia.

Plot

The play dramatises the war of the Seven against Thebes: Polynices, exiled son of Oedipus, returns with an Argive army to claim the throne from his brother Eteocles. Most of the drama consists of Eteocles assigning a Theban defender to each of the city's seven gates as a scout describes the seven attacking champions, until at the seventh gate Eteocles learns that his brother stands there and insists on facing him himself, fulfilling their father's curse: the brothers kill each other. The closing scene, in which a herald forbids the burial of Polynices and Antigone defies the decree, is widely considered a later addition modelled on Sophocles' Antigone.
