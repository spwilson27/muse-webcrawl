Ashford International station, Kent

Ashford International station is a railway station in Ashford, Kent, England. It is a major interchange on the South Eastern Main Line and on High Speed 1, the high-speed line linking London with the Channel Tunnel. The station serves domestic services operated by Southeastern, including high-speed trains to London St Pancras, and was built to serve international Eurostar trains between London, Paris and Brussels.

History

The original Ashford station opened on 1 December 1842 on the South Eastern Railway's line from London to Dover. Ashford grew into an important railway town, home to a large locomotive works. When the Channel Tunnel opened in 1994, a new international station was constructed alongside the existing one; it opened on 28 February 1996, with Eurostar services calling between London Waterloo and the continent.

Domestic high-speed services over High Speed 1 began in December 2009, cutting journey times to London St Pancras to around 38 minutes. Eurostar services were suspended during the COVID-19 pandemic and did not initially resume, the company citing post-Brexit border capacity constraints.

Services

Southeastern operates frequent services to London St Pancras, London Charing Cross and London Victoria, as well as to Dover Priory, Folkestone Central, Canterbury West, Ramsgate and Margate. Southern also runs services towards Brighton via Hastings. The station has six platforms, three of which are used by international trains and are equipped with the facilities required for border controls.
