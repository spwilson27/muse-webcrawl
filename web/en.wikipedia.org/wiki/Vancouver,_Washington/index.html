Vancouver, Washington

Vancouver is a city on the north bank of the Columbia River in the U.S. state of Washington, and the county seat of Clark County. With a population of about 190,000 at the 2020 census, it is the fourth-largest city in Washington and forms part of the Portland–Vancouver metropolitan area. Founded in 1825 as Fort Vancouver, a Hudson's Bay Company fur-trading post, it is the oldest permanent European settlement in the Pacific Northwest and should not be confused with the larger city of Vancouver, British Columbia, about 300 miles (480 km) to the north.

History

The Hudson's Bay Company established Fort Vancouver in 1825 under Chief Factor John McLoughlin as the headquarters of its Columbia District fur trade. American settlers arrived in increasing numbers along the Oregon Trail; the post passed to the United States under the Oregon Treaty of 1846, and the U.S. Army established Vancouver Barracks nearby in 1849. The city grew with railroads, shipyards (notably the Kaiser shipyards of the Second World War, which produced Liberty ships and escort carriers), and later as a suburb of Portland, Oregon, connected by the Interstate Bridge and the Glenn L. Jackson Memorial Bridge.

Geography and economy

Vancouver lies at the western end of the Columbia River Gorge lowlands, with views of Mount Hood and Mount St. Helens. Its economy combines port and industrial activity along the river, healthcare, technology and the absence of a state income tax, which — paired with Oregon's lack of sales tax across the river — shapes the retail geography of the region. Sites of interest include the Fort Vancouver National Historic Site, Officers Row, and Esther Short Park, one of the oldest public squares in the western United States.
