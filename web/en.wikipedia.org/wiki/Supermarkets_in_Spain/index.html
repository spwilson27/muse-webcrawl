Supermarkets in Spain

Spain's grocery market is led by Mercadona, with roughly a quarter of national market share.

Spain has a large and competitive supermarket sector that supplies the great majority of the country's food retailing, alongside traditional municipal markets, small neighbourhood shops, and hypermarkets. Spanish supermarket chains are notable for their strong private-label (own-brand) penetration and for the dominance of a Valencia-based market leader, Mercadona.

Major chains

  • Mercadona — Spain's largest supermarket chain, founded in 1977 and led by Juan Roig. Its own brands (Hacendado, Bosque Verde, Deliplus, Compy) account for an unusually high share of its sales, and it operates more than 1,600 stores in Spain plus a growing network in Portugal under the Mercadona name.
  • Carrefour — the French group's Spanish arm, operating hypermarkets (the format's pioneers in Spain, originally as Pryca), supermarkets, and convenience stores.
  • Lidl and Aldi — German discount chains with substantial and growing Spanish networks.
  • Grupo Eroski — a Basque cooperative group operating Eroski and Caprabo stores.
  • Día — a discount chain founded in Madrid, historically strong in small urban stores.
  • Alcampo — the Spanish banner of France's Auchan group, mainly hypermarkets.
  • Consum and Bon Preu — strong regional cooperative chains in the Valencian/Catalan east.
  • El Corte Inglés — operates the premium Supercor supermarkets and supermarket departments within its department stores.

Characteristics

Spanish supermarkets differ from northern European norms in their extensive fresh fish counters, cured-ham (jamón) sections, and the survival of staffed deli, butchery, and bakery counters within large stores. Shopping hours were historically restricted on Sundays in many autonomous communities, though liberalisation has proceeded unevenly. The sector has faced public debate over pricing margins on olive oil and other staples during the inflation of the early 2020s.
