Lloyds Bank plc is a British retail and commercial bank and one of the traditional "Big Four" clearing banks in the United Kingdom. It is the principal banking brand of Lloyds Banking Group. Founded in Birmingham in 1765 as Taylors and Lloyds, it is one of the oldest banks in Britain, and its emblem — the black horse — dates from the 17th century, when it was the sign of a London goldsmith's shop.

History

The bank was founded by Quaker families: button-maker John Taylor and iron producer Sampson Lloyd. It expanded by absorbing dozens of local banks during the 19th and 20th centuries, and in 1995 merged with the Trustee Savings Bank to form Lloyds TSB. Following the 2008 financial crisis, the group acquired HBOS in 2009 with government support, and the TSB business was later divested. The Lloyds Bank name returned to the high street as the group's main retail brand in England and Wales.

Operations

Lloyds Bank provides current accounts, savings, mortgages, loans, credit cards and business banking through a large branch network, telephone and digital channels, serving tens of millions of customers.
