The Institute of Physics (IOP) is a UK-based not-for-profit learned society and professional body for physicists. It works to advance physics education, research, and application, and represents physicists in the United Kingdom and Ireland. Its membership is on the order of 20,000–23,000. Members may use designations including MInstP and, for fellows, FInstP, and the institute awards Chartered Physicist (CPhys) status.

History

The institute descends from the Physical Society of London, founded in 1874. In 1920 the society's professional arm was reorganized as the Institute of Physics, and the two bodies merged in 1960 to form the Institute of Physics and the Physical Society, taking the present name in 1970. A Royal Charter was granted in 1970. The IOP moved in 2018 to a new headquarters at 37 Caledonian Road, King's Cross, London.

Publishing

Through its subsidiary IOP Publishing, the institute publishes more than 100 scientific journals, including the Journal of Physics series, Reports on Progress in Physics, New Journal of Physics, and Environmental Research Letters, as well as the magazines Physics World and books through IOP ebooks. Publishing surplus supports the institute's charitable activities.

Activities

The IOP accredits university physics degrees, campaigns on physics education and diversity in STEM, runs conferences and subject groups, and awards medals and prizes — including the Isaac Newton Medal and Lecture, the institute's highest honor, and awards named for Dirac, Maxwell, and others.
