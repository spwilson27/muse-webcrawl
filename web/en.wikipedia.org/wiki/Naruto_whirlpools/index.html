Naruto whirlpools

Naruto whirlpools
Location: Naruto Strait, between Awaji Island and Shikoku, Japan
Max. diameter: about 20 m (66 ft)
Max. speed: about 20 km/h (12 mph)
Occurrence: Twice daily at high and low tide

The Naruto whirlpools (Japanese: 鳴門の渦潮, Naruto no uzushio) are tidal whirlpools in the Naruto Strait, a channel between Awaji Island and the city of Naruto in Tokushima Prefecture on Shikoku, Japan. They are among the largest tidal whirlpools in the world and a famous tourist attraction.

Formation

The strait between Awaji Island and Shikoku is only about 1.3 km wide and connects the Seto Inland Sea with the Kii Channel of the Pacific Ocean. Because the tidal range differs by up to about 1.5–1.7 m between the two bodies of water, powerful currents of up to roughly 13–20 km/h rush through the narrow channel when the tide changes. The rapid water flowing over an uneven seabed—with a sharp undersea ridge and depressions on either side—generates rotating eddies that can reach 20 m in diameter during spring tides.

Viewing

The whirlpools occur roughly every six hours and can be seen for about an hour around high and low tide. Sightseeing boats from both the Tokushima and Awaji sides take visitors close to the eddies, and the whirlpools can also be viewed from the Eddy Hall (Uzu no Michi), a glass-floored walkway suspended beneath the Ōnaruto Bridge, 45 m above the strait.

Cultural references

The whirlpools are a celebrated subject in Japanese art and literature; they appear in ukiyo-e prints by Utagawa Hiroshige and in popular culture. The word uzumaki (spiral/whirlpool) associated with the Naruto phenomenon is referenced in manga and anime, and the swirling fishcake narutomaki is named after the whirlpools.
