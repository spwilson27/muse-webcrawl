Ramzi Ahmed Yousef (born 20 May 1968) is a Kuwaiti-born convicted terrorist who was the mastermind of the 1993 World Trade Center bombing and the Bojinka plot, a plan to bomb eleven U.S.-bound airliners over the Pacific. He is the nephew of Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, the principal architect of the September 11 attacks.

Early life and radicalization

Yousef was born in Kuwait to Pakistani parents from Balochistan. He studied electrical engineering at the West Glamorgan Institute of Higher Education in Swansea, Wales, graduating in 1989, and improved his English there. He later trained in bomb-making at camps in Afghanistan and Pakistan before travelling to the United States in 1992.

Attacks and capture

On 26 February 1993, a truck bomb assembled under Yousef's direction exploded in the parking garage beneath the North Tower of the World Trade Center in New York, killing six people and injuring more than a thousand. Yousef fled to Pakistan the same day. In Manila, Philippines, he developed the Bojinka plot and tested a small bomb on Philippine Airlines Flight 434 in December 1994, killing one passenger. After an accidental fire in his Manila apartment in January 1995 exposed the plot, he fled; acting on a tip from an associate, U.S. and Pakistani agents arrested him in Islamabad on 7 February 1995.

Trials and imprisonment

Yousef was convicted in 1996 for the Bojinka plot and in 1997 for the World Trade Center bombing. He was sentenced to life imprisonment plus 240 years and has been held in solitary confinement at federal supermax facilities in Colorado.
