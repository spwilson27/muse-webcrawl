Stockwell Burt Day Jr. PC (born 16 August 1950) is a Canadian former politician. He served as leader of the Canadian Alliance from 2000 to 2001, leading the Official Opposition in the House of Commons. Following the merger of the Alliance with the Progressive Conservative Party, he served in the cabinet of Prime Minister Stephen Harper as Minister of Public Safety and later as President of the Treasury Board and Minister for the Asia-Pacific Gateway.

Early career in Alberta

Before entering federal politics, Day was a member of the Legislative Assembly of Alberta for Red Deer North and held several portfolios in Premier Ralph Klein's government, including Minister of Social Services, Minister of Labour, and Provincial Treasurer. He gained a reputation as a social and fiscal conservative.

Canadian Alliance leadership

In 2000, Day won the leadership of the newly formed Canadian Alliance, defeating Preston Manning, the party's founding leader. In the 2000 federal election the Alliance increased its seat count but failed to make the breakthrough in Ontario that Day had promised. Internal party turmoil followed, including defections by dissident MPs, and Day was forced to call a new leadership race; he lost to Stephen Harper in 2002.

In Harper's cabinet and later life

Day served as foreign affairs critic before becoming Minister of Public Safety in 2006, where he oversaw the Canada Border Services Agency, the RCMP, and CSIS. He moved to the Treasury Board in 2010 and did not seek re-election in 2011. After politics, he worked as a commentator and advisor.
