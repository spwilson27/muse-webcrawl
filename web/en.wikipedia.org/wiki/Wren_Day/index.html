Wren Day

Wren Day, also known as Wren's Day, Hunt the Wren Day or St Stephen's Day (Irish: Lá an Dreoilín), is celebrated on 26 December in Ireland and on the Isle of Man. Groups of people, traditionally boys and young men called wrenboys or strawboys, dress in costumes — often of straw — and parade through towns and villages with music, singing, and a model or (historically) a dead wren carried on a decorated pole or in a holly bush, soliciting money from householders, nowadays usually for charity.

Origins and folklore

The custom is associated with numerous folk explanations. The wren, "king of all birds" in Irish tradition (dreoilín), was said in one legend to have betrayed St Stephen by chirping and revealing his hiding place to his pursuers, and in another to have alerted Danish (Viking) invaders to an Irish ambush by pecking on a drum. The hunt may derive from older midwinter ritual and was once widespread across Ireland, Wales (where a similar custom occurred around Twelfth Night), the Isle of Man, and parts of France.

The traditional rhyme sung by wrenboys begins: "The wren, the wren, the king of all birds, / St Stephen's Day was caught in the furze..."

Modern celebration

Although the actual killing of a wren has largely died out (and the bird is now legally protected), the processions continue, especially in County Kerry — Dingle's Wren's Day is a major street festival — and in parts of counties Clare, Galway, and Wexford. Costumes range from straw suits to fancy dress, and collecting is typically for local charities. On the Isle of Man, the Hunt the Wren custom involves dancing around a decorated wren pole in town squares.
