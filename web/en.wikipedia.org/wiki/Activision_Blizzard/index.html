Activision Blizzard, Inc. is an American video game holding company based in Santa Monica, California. It was formed in 2008 by the merger of Activision and Vivendi Games (the parent of Blizzard Entertainment), creating one of the world's largest video game companies. Its operating units include Activision Publishing, Blizzard Entertainment, and the mobile developer King, acquired in 2016.

Activision Blizzard's headquarters campus in Santa Monica, California.

History

The 2008 merger combined Activision's console publishing strength — led by the Call of Duty franchise — with Blizzard's PC franchises Warcraft, StarCraft and Diablo. The company bought itself out of Vivendi in 2013 and joined the S&P 500 in 2015. In 2021 the company faced a California state lawsuit and widespread reporting over workplace culture, leading to internal reforms and executive changes.

Acquisition by Microsoft

Microsoft announced its intention to acquire Activision Blizzard for $68.7 billion in January 2022. After extended regulatory review — including litigation with the U.S. Federal Trade Commission and conditions imposed by the UK Competition and Markets Authority involving cloud-gaming rights — the deal closed on 13 October 2023, making Activision Blizzard part of Microsoft Gaming and the largest acquisition in Microsoft's history.
