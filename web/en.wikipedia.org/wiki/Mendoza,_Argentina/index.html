Mendoza is the capital city of Mendoza Province in Argentina. It is located in the northern-central part of the province, in a region of foothills and high plains on the eastern side of the Andes, at an elevation of about 746 metres above sea level. The metropolitan area, Greater Mendoza, has a population of around one million, making it one of Argentina's largest urban areas.

History

The city was founded in 1561 by Pedro del Castillo, who named it after the governor of Chile, Don García Hurtado de Mendoza. It served as a staging point for crossings of the Andes; in 1817 José de San Martín's Army of the Andes departed from Mendoza to liberate Chile and Peru. An earthquake in 1861 destroyed much of the old city, which was rebuilt nearby with wide avenues and open plazas designed to reduce damage from future quakes.

Economy and wine

Mendoza is the centre of Argentina's wine industry: the surrounding irrigated oases produce roughly two-thirds of the country's wine, with Malbec the signature grape. Olive oil production, agriculture, and tourism are also important. The city is a base for excursions to Aconcagua, the highest peak in the Americas, and for visits to the bodegas (wineries) of Maipú and Luján de Cuyo.

Climate and geography

Mendoza has an arid climate with hot summers and mild, dry winters. Because rainfall is low, vegetation in the city depends on irrigation from Andean rivers; its streets are lined with trees watered by a network of open channels called acequias, a legacy of the indigenous Huarpe people and Spanish colonial engineering.
