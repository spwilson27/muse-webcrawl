Years of Lead (Italy)

The Years of Lead (Italian: Anni di piombo) is a term for the period of political violence and social upheaval in Italy from the late 1960s into the 1980s (roughly 1968–1988), marked by bombings, assassinations, and kidnappings by far-left and far-right armed groups, and by a perceived "strategy of tension" aimed at destabilizing the democratic state. The name, drawn from lead bullets, is also used of similar periods in other countries.

Course of events

The period is usually dated from the protest movements of 1968 and the "Hot Autumn" strikes of 1969. The Piazza Fontana bombing in Milan on 12 December 1969, which killed 17 people, opened the era of mass-casualty neo-fascist attacks, followed by bombings such as Piazza della Loggia in Brescia (1974), the Italicus train (1974), and the Bologna railway station bombing of 2 August 1980, the deadliest, which killed 85. Far-left organizations, above all the Red Brigades (Brigate Rosse), carried out a campaign of sabotage, kneecappings, and murder, culminating in the 1978 kidnapping and murder of former prime minister Aldo Moro during the proposed "historic compromise" between his Christian Democrats and the Communist Party. Other leftist groups included Prima Linea and the Nuclei Armati Proletari.

Resolution and legacy

Determined policing under figures such as General Carlo Alberto Dalla Chiesa, new repentance (pentiti) laws encouraging defectors' testimony, and mass arrests in the early 1980s broke the main armed groups; the release of kidnapped NATO general James Dozier in January 1982 is often taken as a symbolic endpoint, though sporadic attacks continued through the decade. Thousands were killed or injured across the period; hundreds were convicted of political violence. Controversies persist over the role of elements of the Italian state, the secret "Gladio" stay-behind network, and foreign intelligence services, and the Years of Lead remain a central, contested memory in Italian public life, revisited in films such as The Best of Youth and Il Divo.
