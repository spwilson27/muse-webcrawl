Vaslav Nijinsky

Vaslav Nijinsky (12 March 1889 – 8 April 1950) was a Russian ballet dancer and choreographer of Polish ancestry. Regarded as one of the greatest male dancers of the early 20th century, he was celebrated for his virtuosity, extraordinary leaps, and intense characterisations with Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes.

Born in Kyiv to Polish dancer parents, Nijinsky trained at the Imperial Ballet School in Saint Petersburg. With the Ballets Russes in Paris he created iconic roles in The Firebird, Petrushka, and Scheherazade. As a choreographer, his works L'Après-midi d'un faune (1912), Jeux (1913), and The Rite of Spring (1913), with music by Igor Stravinsky, scandalised audiences and reshaped modern dance.

Later life

His marriage to Romola de Pulszky in 1913 led to his dismissal by Diaghilev. Diagnosed with schizophrenia in 1919, Nijinsky spent much of his later life in psychiatric care. He died in London in 1950. His diary, written in early 1919, is a notable document of art and mental illness.
