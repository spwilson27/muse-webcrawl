Ramming

Ramming is a tactic in which a vehicle, vessel, or aircraft deliberately strikes a target with its own body, using the force of the collision itself as a weapon. It has been employed in warfare on sea, on land, and in the air from antiquity to the modern era.

Naval warfare

The naval ram — a reinforced beak at the waterline of a galley — was the principal weapon of Mediterranean war fleets for centuries, famously used by Greek triremes at Salamis and by Roman fleets. Ramming fell out of use as cannon came to dominate, but briefly revived in the ironclad era after the Battle of Lissa (1866), when the Austrian flagship rammed and sank an Italian ironclad, prompting navies to fit ram bows to capital ships for several decades.

Air and land

Aerial ramming was practised in both World Wars, notably by Soviet pilots performing the taran manoeuvre, and occasionally by other air forces as a last resort. On land, tanks and armoured vehicles have rammed opponents and obstacles, and ramming is also a documented police and criminal tactic involving automobiles.
