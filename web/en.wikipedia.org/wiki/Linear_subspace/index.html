Linear subspace

In mathematics, and more specifically in linear algebra, a linear subspace (or vector subspace) of a vector space V is a subset W of V that is itself a vector space under the operations inherited from V. Equivalently, W is a subspace if it contains the zero vector and is closed under addition and scalar multiplication.

Examples

In three-dimensional Euclidean space, the subspaces are the origin alone, all lines through the origin, all planes through the origin, and the whole space. Other standard examples include the kernel (null space) and image (column space) of a linear map, the set of solutions of a homogeneous system of linear equations, and the span of any set of vectors — the smallest subspace containing them.

Operations

The intersection of any family of subspaces is a subspace, and the sum of two subspaces is again a subspace. The collection of all subspaces of a vector space forms a lattice. Subspaces that pass through the origin are distinguished from affine subspaces, which are their translates.
