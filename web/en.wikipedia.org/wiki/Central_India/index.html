Central India

Central India is a loosely defined region of India comprising the states of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, sometimes extended to include northern Maharashtra (Vidarbha) and southern Uttar Pradesh (Bundelkhand). Madhya Pradesh, the largest component, is often called the "heart of India".

Geography

The region is dominated by the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, the Deccan plateau fringe, and the basins of the Narmada, Tapti, Son and Mahanadi rivers. Much of the land is covered by dry deciduous forest, home to tiger reserves such as Kanha, Bandhavgarh and Pench, which inspired Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book.

History

Central India was ruled in succession by the Mauryas, Guptas, Paramara dynasty of Malwa, the Sultanate of Delhi, the Mughals, and the Marathas. Under British rule the area formed the Central Provinces and the Central India Agency of princely states, including Gwalior, Indore and Bhopal. Madhya Pradesh was formed in 1956; Chhattisgarh was carved out of it in 2000.

Economy and culture

Agriculture (soybean, wheat, cotton, rice) dominates, alongside mining of coal, manganese and diamonds (Panna). Major cities include Indore, Bhopal, Jabalpur, Raipur and Nagpur. The region is noted for the temples of Khajuraho, the stupas of Sanchi and the rock shelters of Bhimbetka, all UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
