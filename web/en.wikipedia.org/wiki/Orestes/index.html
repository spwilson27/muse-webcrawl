Orestes

In Greek mythology, Orestes (Greek: Ὀρέστης) was the son of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, and the brother of Electra and Iphigenia. Pursuant to the commands of Apollo, he avenged his father's murder by killing his mother Clytemnestra and her lover Aegisthus, and was thereafter pursued by the Furies (Erinyes) until he was acquitted at Athens in a trial founded, in Aeschylus's telling, by the goddess Athena.

Myth

When Agamemnon returned from Troy, Clytemnestra and Aegisthus murdered him. The child Orestes was smuggled to safety in Phocis, at the court of King Strophius, where he grew up with the king's son Pylades, who became his devoted companion. Grown to manhood, Orestes consulted the Delphic oracle and was ordered by Apollo to avenge his father. Returning secretly to Argos, he met his sister Electra at Agamemnon's tomb, then gained entry to the palace and killed Aegisthus and Clytemnestra.

The Furies and the trial

For the matricide, the Erinyes hounded Orestes into madness. In Aeschylus's Eumenides (458 BCE), the final play of the Oresteia trilogy, Orestes is tried at Athens before a jury established by Athena on the Areopagus; with the votes equal, Athena's casting vote acquits him, and the Furies are transformed into the kindly Eumenides. The myth thus dramatizes the replacement of blood vengeance by civic justice.

Later adventures

In Euripides' Iphigenia in Tauris, Orestes is sent to steal the image of Artemis from the Taurians to end his torment, and discovers his sister Iphigenia serving there as priestess; they escape together. Orestes married Hermione, daughter of Menelaus and Helen, and in later tradition ruled Mycenae and Argos, dying of a snakebite at Oresteium in Arcadia.
