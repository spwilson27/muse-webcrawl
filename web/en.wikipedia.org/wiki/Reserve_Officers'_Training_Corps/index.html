Reserve Officers' Training Corps

The Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC) is a group of college- and university-based officer training programs for the training of commissioned officers of the United States Armed Forces. It is one of the largest sources of commissioned officers, alongside the service academies and Officer Candidate Schools.

History

ROTC traces its origins to the Morrill Act of 1862, which required land-grant colleges to offer military instruction, and was formalized by the National Defense Act of 1916. Army ROTC, Naval ROTC (including the Marine Corps option), and Air Force ROTC programs now operate at more than a thousand colleges and universities.

Programs

Participants, called cadets or midshipmen, take military science courses alongside their degrees and attend training such as summer field exercises. Many receive scholarships in exchange for a service obligation after graduation — typically four years of active duty and further reserve service. A related program, Junior ROTC (JROTC), is offered in high schools but confers no commission.
