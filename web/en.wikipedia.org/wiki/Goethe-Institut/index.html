Goethe-Institut

Founded1951
HeadquartersMunich, Germany
TypeCultural institute
PurposePromotion of the German language and culture worldwide

The Goethe-Institut is a non-profit German cultural association operating worldwide, with 159 institutes in 98 countries. It promotes the study of the German language abroad, fosters international cultural cooperation, and conveys a comprehensive image of Germany by providing information on cultural, social, and political life. It is named after the German poet and statesman Johann Wolfgang von Goethe.

The institute was founded in 1951 as the successor to the Deutsche Akademie. It is financed primarily by the German Federal Foreign Office. Its activities include German language courses and examinations — its certificates, such as the Goethe-Zertifikat, are internationally recognised — as well as libraries, film screenings, concerts, exhibitions, and residency programmes for artists and translators.

Examinations

The Goethe-Institut's language examinations correspond to the levels of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages, from A1 for beginners to C2 for the highest level of proficiency. Its exams are administered at its institutes and at licensed examination centres around the world.
