Eunice Kennedy Shriver

Eunice Mary Kennedy Shriver (July 10, 1921 – August 11, 2009) was an American philanthropist and a member of the Kennedy family. The fifth of the nine children of Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. and Rose Kennedy, and a sister of President John F. Kennedy, she founded the Special Olympics in 1968 and is widely credited with transforming public attitudes toward people with intellectual disabilities.

Early life and career

Shriver graduated from Stanford University in 1943 and worked at the U.S. State Department and the Justice Department, where she worked with juvenile delinquents. Her interest in intellectual disability was personal: her elder sister Rosemary had an intellectual disability and had been institutionalised after a lobotomy in 1941. As executive vice-president of the Joseph P. Kennedy Jr. Foundation, Eunice directed funding toward research and model programmes, and she influenced her brother's administration to establish the President's Panel on Mental Retardation and pass landmark legislation in the field.

Special Olympics

In 1962 she opened Camp Shriver at her Maryland home, Timberlawn, inviting children with intellectual disabilities to a free summer sports camp staffed partly by local teenagers. The camp's success led to the first International Special Olympics Games, held at Soldier Field in Chicago on July 20, 1968, with about 1,000 athletes from the United States and Canada. The movement grew into a worldwide organisation reaching millions of athletes in more than 170 countries, and its "Eunice Kennedy Shriver Day" is observed annually. She argued throughout her life that through sport people with intellectual disabilities could demonstrate competence and claim their place in society.

Shriver married Sargent Shriver, the first director of the Peace Corps, in 1953; their children include Maria Shriver, journalist and former First Lady of California, and Timothy Shriver, longtime chairman of Special Olympics. In 1984 she received the Presidential Medal of Freedom. She died in Hyannis, Massachusetts, in 2009, aged 88.
