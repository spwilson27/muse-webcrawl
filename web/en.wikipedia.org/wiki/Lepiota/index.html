Lepiota

Lepiota is a genus of gilled mushrooms in the family Agaricaceae. Its members are typically small, white-spored agarics with a ring on the stem and free gills. The genus is found worldwide, though many species are difficult to identify and some are dangerously poisonous.

KingdomFungi
OrderAgaricales
FamilyAgaricaceae
Type speciesLepiota clypeolaria

Description

Lepiota species produce small to medium fruit bodies with scaly caps, white to cream spore prints, and a partial veil that leaves a ring on the stipe. They are saprotrophic, growing on soil, leaf litter and decaying wood in forests and gardens.

Toxicity

Several small Lepiota species, including Lepiota brunneoincarnata and Lepiota helveola, contain amatoxins — the same class of poisons found in the death cap — and have caused fatal poisonings. Because of their resemblance to edible parasol mushrooms, Lepiota species are a recognised hazard for foragers.
