Wilcoxon signed-rank test

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is a non-parametric statistical hypothesis test used either to test the location of a population based on a sample of data, or to compare the locations of two populations using two matched samples. It is a paired-difference test and serves as a non-parametric alternative to the paired Student's t-test when the distribution of the differences cannot be assumed to be normally distributed. It was introduced by Frank Wilcoxon in 1945.

Paired differences are ranked by absolute value, then ranks are signed by the direction of each difference.

Procedure

For paired observations, the differences between pairs are computed; pairs with zero difference are typically discarded. The remaining differences are ranked by their absolute values, and the ranks are then given the sign of the original difference. The test statistic is the sum of the positive ranks (or the smaller of the positive and negative sums), which under the null hypothesis — that the distribution of differences is symmetric about zero — has a known distribution used to obtain a p-value. Ties in absolute differences are assigned average ranks.

Assumptions and use

The test assumes the data come from matched pairs, that the pairs are independent, and that the differences are drawn from a symmetric distribution on at least an ordinal scale. It is widely used in medicine, psychology, and other fields when sample sizes are small or normality is doubtful. For two independent samples, the corresponding test is the Mann–Whitney U test, also known as the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.
