Tallarín saltado

Tallarín saltado is a Peruvian stir-fried noodle dish that forms part of the country's chifa tradition — the fusion of Chinese and Peruvian cuisines created by Chinese immigrants who arrived in Peru beginning in the mid-19th century. Its name literally means "jumped noodles" (saltado, "jumped", referring to the tossing action of the stir-fry in the wok).

Description

The dish combines thick noodles — typically spaghetti-like pasta rather than Chinese wheat noodles — with strips of beef, red onion, tomatoes, scallions and ají pepper, all stir-fried at high heat in a wok with soy sauce, vinegar and Peruvian spices such as cumin. It is a close relative of lomo saltado, which uses the same stir-fried beef-and-vegetable base but is served with rice and French fries instead of noodles.

Chifa cuisine

Chifa restaurants are ubiquitous in Peru, and tallarín saltado is one of their staple dishes alongside arroz chaufa (fried rice) and sopa wantán. The fusion reflects the adaptation of Cantonese cooking techniques to ingredients available in Peru, including local potatoes, peppers and pasta, which became entrenched after waves of Chinese migration in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
