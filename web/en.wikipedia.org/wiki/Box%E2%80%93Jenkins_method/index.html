Box–Jenkins method

In time series analysis, the Box–Jenkins method, named after statisticians George Box and Gwilym Jenkins, applies autoregressive moving average (ARMA) or autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) models to find the best fit of a time-series model to past values of a time series, so as to make forecasts.

Approach

The method proceeds iteratively in three stages: model identification, parameter estimation, and diagnostic checking. In the identification stage the analyst examines the series and its autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation functions, applying differencing if needed to achieve stationarity and tentatively choosing the orders of the autoregressive and moving-average components, including seasonal terms where appropriate. Parameters are then estimated, usually by maximum likelihood, and the residuals are checked — they should resemble white noise; if not, the model is revised and the cycle repeats.

Models

An ARIMA(p,d,q) model combines p autoregressive lags, d differences to remove nonstationarity, and q moving-average terms. Seasonal ARIMA models add corresponding seasonal orders, handling monthly or quarterly data with seasonal patterns. Although newer machine-learning and state-space methods compete with it, the Box–Jenkins approach remains a standard, interpretable framework for univariate forecasting.
