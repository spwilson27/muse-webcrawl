The British Fashion Council (BFC) is a non-profit organisation, founded in 1983, that promotes British fashion design internationally and supports the development of the fashion industry in the United Kingdom. It organises London Fashion Week and The Fashion Awards, and runs talent-support schemes for emerging designers.

Activities

The BFC's best-known initiative is NEWGEN, launched in 1993 with Topshop as sponsor, which provides financial support and show space to new designers; alumni include Alexander McQueen, Christopher Kane and Erdem. Other programmes include the BFC/Vogue Designer Fashion Fund, Fashion Forward, the Colleges Council linking education with industry, and the Institute of Positive Fashion, which promotes sustainability and circularity in the industry.

London Fashion Week and The Fashion Awards

London Fashion Week, held each February and September, is one of the "big four" fashion weeks alongside Paris, Milan and New York. The Fashion Awards (previously the British Fashion Awards), held annually — for many years at the Royal Albert Hall — honour designers, models and creative figures, and raise funds for the BFC Foundation's education and talent programmes.

Leadership

The Council has been chaired by figures including Harold Tillman, Natalie Massenet (2013–2017) and Stephanie Phair; its long-serving chief executive from 2009 was Caroline Rush, who announced her departure in 2024–25 after some 16 years leading the organisation.
