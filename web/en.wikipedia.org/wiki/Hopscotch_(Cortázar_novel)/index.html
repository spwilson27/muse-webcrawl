Hopscotch (Spanish: Rayuela) is a novel by the Argentine writer Julio Cortázar published in 1963. Considered a landmark of the Latin American Boom and of experimental fiction, it follows the Argentine intellectual Horacio Oliveira through Paris and Buenos Aires and can be read either in ordinary chapter order or by "hopscotching" through the chapters according to a table of instructions supplied by the author, which incorporates otherwise "expendable" chapters of fragments, quotations, and reflections.

Plot and structure

The novel is divided into three parts: "From the Other Side", set in Paris, where Oliveira drifts among expatriate friends and his lover La Maga; "From This Side", set in Buenos Aires after his return; and "From Diverse Sides", the expendable chapters, which include the notebooks of the eccentric writer Morelli, whose meditations on the novel mirror Cortázar's own experiment. The reader who follows the hopscotch sequence interleaves the main narrative with Morelli's theoretical fragments, producing a different book from the linear reading.

Reception and influence

Hopscotch established Cortázar's international reputation and won the first Prix Médicis étranger for its French translation. Gregory Rabassa's English translation (1966) won the National Book Award for translation. The novel's open structure anticipated hypertext fiction and influenced generations of experimental writers; it remains Cortázar's most famous work.
