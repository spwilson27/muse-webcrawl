Rescue

Rescue comprises responsive operations that usually involve the saving of life, removal from danger, liberation from restraint, or the urgent extraction of people (or animals) from harm. Rescue operations are conducted by military forces, emergency services and specialist volunteer organisations, and range from individual first aid to large-scale responses to natural disasters.

Types of rescue

  • Search and rescue (SAR): locating and aiding people in distress, at sea, in mountains, in deserts or after disasters.
  • Water rescue: lifeguarding, swift-water and flood rescue, and dive rescue.
  • Technical rescue: rope rescue, confined-space rescue, trench and structural-collapse rescue, and vehicle extrication using tools such as hydraulic cutters ("jaws of life").
  • Combat search and rescue (CSAR): military recovery of downed aircrew and isolated personnel.
  • Animal rescue: sheltering and rehoming of abandoned or mistreated animals by rescue organisations.
  • Cave and mine rescue: highly specialised operations, exemplified by the 2010 Copiapó mining rescue in Chile and the 2018 Tham Luang cave rescue in Thailand.

Organisation

Rescue is typically organised under incident command systems. Responders include firefighters, paramedics, coast guards, mountain rescue teams and organisations such as the International Red Cross and national civil defence services. Training emphasises rescuer safety, triage and coordination, since poorly planned rescues can endanger both rescuers and casualties.
