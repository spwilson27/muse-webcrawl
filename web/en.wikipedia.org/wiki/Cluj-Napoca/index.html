Cluj-Napoca (German: Klausenburg; Hungarian: Kolozsvár) is the second-most populous city in Romania and the unofficial capital of the historical region of Transylvania. It lies in the valley of the Someșul Mic river in northwestern Romania and serves as the seat of Cluj County.

History

The site has been inhabited since prehistory; the Romans established the municipium of Napoca, from which the modern name derives. Cluj flourished as a medieval Saxon and Hungarian trading town, and was the birthplace of King Matthias Corvinus, whose equestrian statue dominates Union Square. After the First World War the city became part of Romania.

Culture and education

Cluj is a major university centre: Babeș-Bolyai University is the largest university in Romania, teaching in Romanian, Hungarian and German. The city hosts the Transilvania International Film Festival (TIFF) and the Untold electronic music festival, and has a growing technology sector sometimes nicknamed the "Silicon Valley of Transylvania". Landmarks include the Gothic St. Michael's Church, the Banffy Palace (now an art museum), and the botanical garden.
