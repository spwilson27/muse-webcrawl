Science in Brazil

Science in Brazil developed substantially over the twentieth century and accounts for roughly half of Latin America's scientific output. Research is concentrated in public universities and federal institutes, funded principally through the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation, CNPq, CAPES, and state agencies such as FAPESP in São Paulo.

History

Early milestones include the Oswaldo Cruz Institute (1900) for tropical medicine and the founding of the University of São Paulo (1934), which recruited European scholars. Postwar institutions such as CNPq (1951) and the Brazilian Center for Research in Physics built modern research capacity.

Strengths

Brazil is known for deep-water oil exploration (Petrobras), agricultural research (Embrapa), aircraft manufacture (Embraer), biofuels, tropical medicine, and the synchrotron light source Sirius at LNLS in Campinas. Notable scientists include Oswaldo Cruz, Carlos Chagas, César Lattes, and Marcelo Gleiser.

Challenges

Funding has been volatile, with severe budget cuts in the 2010s and 2020s prompting protests and emigration of researchers; spending remains around 1.2% of GDP.
