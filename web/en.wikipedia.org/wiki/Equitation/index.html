Equitation

Equitation is the art or practice of horse riding or horsemanship. More specifically, equitation may refer to a rider's position while mounted, and encompasses a rider's ability to ride correctly and with effective aids. In horse show competition, the rider's performance, rather than the animal's, is judged in equitation classes.

History

The systematic study of riding dates to Xenophon's On Horsemanship (c. 355 BC). Renaissance riding masters such as Federigo Grisone and later François Robichon de La Guérinière codified classical dressage, and military riding schools — including the Spanish Riding School in Vienna and the Cadre Noir at Saumur — preserved and developed the classical tradition.

Styles and competition

Equitation is judged in hunt seat, dressage seat, saddle seat, and Western disciplines, with riders assessed on position, balance, and the subtlety and correctness of their aids. In dressage, the rider's seat and influence are evaluated in tests such as the FEI's rider tests, while working equitation combines dressage with obstacle and speed phases rooted in field work with horses.
