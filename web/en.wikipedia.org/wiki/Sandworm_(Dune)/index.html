Sandworm (Dune)

Sandworms are a fictional species of colossal worm-like creatures that live in the deserts of the planet Arrakis in Frank Herbert's Dune universe. The Fremen call them Shai-Hulud and revere them as manifestations of the divine. Sandworms are central to the economy of the universe because their life cycle produces the spice melange, the most valuable substance known.

Description

Sandworms are enormous segmented creatures, armoured with crystalline teeth and tough orange-brown hides, reaching lengths of hundreds of metres. They roam the deep desert of Arrakis, attracted by rhythmic vibrations on the sand, which they attack as they would prey or intruding rivals. Their interiors are furnace-hot chemical factories; they are effectively immortal unless killed by water — which is poisonous to them — by electrocution, or by atomic weapons.

Life cycle

The sandworm is the adult stage of a cycle that begins with sand plankton, which feed on spice scattered in the sand. These grow into sandtrout, leathery pseudo-larvae that sequester water in the desert and, when they die in great cysts, undergo a chemical explosion that produces a "pre-spice mass". Surviving sandtrout metamorphose into small sandworms, which grow into the giants of the deep desert. In God Emperor of Dune, Leto II merges with sandtrout to become a human–sandworm hybrid who rules for 3,500 years.

Riding and cultural role

The Fremen summon worms with thumpers and mount them using maker hooks to pry open a segment ring, steering the creature across the desert as transport. Riding a worm is a rite of passage; Paul Atreides' first ride marks his acceptance among the Fremen in Dune. The drowning of a small worm produces the poisonous Water of Life, used by the Bene Gesserit in their rituals.
