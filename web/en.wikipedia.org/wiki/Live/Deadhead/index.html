Live performance culture: Deadhead

Deadheads built a travelling live-music culture around the Grateful Dead's concerts.

A Deadhead is a fan of the American rock band the Grateful Dead, renowned for devotion to the band's live performances. Because the Dead improvised extensively and never played the same setlist twice, Deadheads followed the band from city to city, attending dozens or hundreds of shows, trading concert tapes, and building a self-sustaining travelling community around the tour.

Live music culture

The Grateful Dead encouraged fans to record their concerts, setting aside "taper sections" behind the soundboard — an unusual policy that made live recordings the currency of the subculture and spread the band's music far beyond its studio albums. The parking-lot scene outside venues, nicknamed "Shakedown Street", featured vending, tie-dyed clothing, and communal living. Deadheads developed their own lore: ranking shows, tracking setlists, and debating legendary performances such as Cornell 5/8/77.

After the Dead

Following Jerry Garcia's death in 1995, the culture persisted through successor acts — Furthur, Dead & Company (with John Mayer), Phil Lesh & Friends, and a wider "jam band" scene including Phish, whose fans inherited many Deadhead customs. The Internet Archive hosts thousands of live Grateful Dead recordings, and the band's vault releases (the Dick's Picks and Dave's Picks series) continue the live-centred legacy.
