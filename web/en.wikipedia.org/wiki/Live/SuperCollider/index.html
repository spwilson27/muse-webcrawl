SuperCollider in live performance

SuperCollider is a mainstay of live coding and experimental live electronic performance.

SuperCollider is an environment and programming language for real-time audio synthesis and algorithmic composition, originally developed by James McCartney and released in 1996. Because it allows code to be evaluated and modified while sound is running, it became one of the foundational tools of live electronic music and the live coding movement: performers write, patch, and improvise with synthesis processes directly on stage.

Use in live performance

In concert use, SuperCollider's client–server architecture (the sclang interpreter controlling the scsynth synthesis server) lets a performer start, stop, and reshape sound processes with very low latency. Live coders perform at algoraves and experimental music events using SuperCollider directly or through higher-level systems built on it, such as the pattern library JITLib (just-in-time programming), which is designed specifically for improvising code in front of an audience. Its demands on the performer — fluency in both syntax and synthesis — have made it a benchmark of technical live coding practice.

Live-oriented features

  • Code can be executed at any time without restarting the audio engine.
  • Synth definitions (SynthDefs) can be compiled and loaded during a performance.
  • Proxy spaces (NodeProxy, ProxySpace) support gradual, glitch-free substitution of running processes — ideal for live improvisation.
  • Open Sound Control messaging allows collaboration between performers and integration with visuals, sensors, and other instruments.

Community

SuperCollider is free and open-source software, maintained by an international community of composers, researchers, and performers. It is taught in computer-music courses worldwide and used in installations, dance, theatre, and film sound as well as the concert hall and club.
