Live/Dead (1969) was the Grateful Dead's first live album.

Live/Dead is the first official live album by the American rock band the Grateful Dead, released on November 10, 1969, by Warner Bros. Records. Recorded during a run of early-1969 concerts at the Fillmore West and Avalon Ballroom in San Francisco, the double album is widely regarded as one of the finest live rock albums ever made, capturing the band's improvisational psychedelic sound at its height.

Recording and content

The album was recorded on 16-track tape — among the earliest live albums made that way — by engineers Bob Matthews and Betty Cantor. Its first side is a continuous performance of "Dark Star", flowing into "St. Stephen" and "The Eleven", a sequence prized by fans as the definitive document of the band's early improvisational style. Further tracks include "Turn On Your Love Light" (sung by Ron "Pigpen" McKernan), "Death Don't Have No Mercy", and the closing a cappella "And We Bid You Goodnight".

Reception and legacy

Although the album sold modestly on release, its reputation grew steadily; it is frequently placed on critics' lists of the greatest live albums. The title is a play on words — the band recorded "live" performing music about death — and its success helped establish the Grateful Dead's identity as a live act first and a studio band second. The full concerts drawn on for the album were later released in expanded archival editions.
