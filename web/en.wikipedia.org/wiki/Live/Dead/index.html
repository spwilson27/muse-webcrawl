Live/Dead is the first official live album by the American rock band the Grateful Dead, released on November 10, 1969, by Warner Bros. Records. Recorded over a series of concerts in San Francisco in early 1969 — including dates at the Avalon Ballroom and the Fillmore West — it was the first live rock album recorded in 16-track, and it captured the band's improvisational concert style far better than their studio records had done.

Content

The double album opens with a celebrated 23-minute version of "Dark Star", the band's signature improvisation vehicle, which flows through "St. Stephen" and "The Eleven" before the rhythm-and-blues of "Turn On Your Love Light", sung by Ron "Pigpen" McKernan. The second disc pairs the feedback experiments of "Feedback" with the acoustic "And We Bid You Goodnight". Live/Dead reached number 64 on the Billboard chart — modest at the time — but is now widely regarded as one of the greatest live albums ever made and the record that defined the Grateful Dead's reputation as a live act; it has been selected for the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry. The cover art by Alton Kelley and Stanley Mouse, with lettering by Rick Griffin, is a classic of psychedelic poster design.
