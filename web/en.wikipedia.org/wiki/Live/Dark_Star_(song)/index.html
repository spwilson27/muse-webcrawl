"Dark Star" is a song released as a single by the Grateful Dead on Warner Bros. Records in 1968. It was written by lyricist Robert Hunter and composed by guitarist Jerry Garcia with contributions from the whole band; the lyrics were inspired in part by T. S. Eliot's poem The Waste Land. Although the single sold poorly, the song became the best-known vehicle for the band's extended improvisation in concert, and is regarded as the quintessential Grateful Dead jam piece.

Composition

Hunter wrote the lyrics in 1967, shortly after joining forces with Garcia; the music grew out of a modal, two-chord vamp over which Garcia's guitar lines could drift freely. The sparse lyric — "Dark star crashes, pouring its light into ashes" — serves as a poetic frame rather than a narrative.

Live performances

In concert, "Dark Star" was stretched far beyond its studio length: the version on the 1969 album Live/Dead runs over 23 minutes, and performances in 1972–1974 frequently exceeded half an hour, passing through free-form improvisation, feedback, and themes that segued into other songs. The song was played more than 230 times between 1968 and 1994, though it was retired for long stretches (1975–1978 and after 1984 it became rare and eagerly anticipated). Famous versions include 27 August 1972 at Veneta, Oregon ("Sunshine Daydream"), and the 1972 European tour documented on Europe '72 releases and the steal your face era recordings.

Legacy

"Dark Star" is widely considered the centrepiece of the Grateful Dead's improvisational approach and one of the founding documents of the jam-band genre. In 2011, Rolling Stone readers and critics ranked its live versions among the greatest live recordings of all time. The song's title named the band's side project releases and an asteroid, 2410 Garcia notwithstanding, and its opening riff remains a touchstone for improvising musicians.
