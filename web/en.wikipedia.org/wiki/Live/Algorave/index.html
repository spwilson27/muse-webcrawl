Live performance: Algorave

Algorave events pair dance music with live-coded algorithms, projected for the audience to see.

An algorave (from "algorithm" and "rave") is an event at which music and visuals are generated live from algorithms, usually through live coding: performers write and modify code on stage, with their screens projected so the audience can follow the process. The term was coined in the United Kingdom around 2011–2012 by live coders Alex McLean and Nick Collins, and algoraves have since been held on every continent.

Practice

Algorave music is typically dancefloor-oriented — techno, breakbeat, house, footwork, and experimental club music — produced in real time using live coding environments such as TidalCycles, SuperCollider, Sonic Pi, FoxDot, and Orca, or with modular and algorithmic hardware setups. Visual artists similarly code graphics with tools like Hydra. The scene's informal motto, "show us your screens", expresses a commitment to transparency: the audience sees the code that makes the music.

Community and culture

Emerging from the TOPLAP live coding community, the algorave movement positions itself between academic computer music and club culture, emphasizing open-source tools, inclusivity, and DIY organization. Events range from warehouse parties to festival stages and streamed sessions; during the COVID-19 pandemic, online "live code" streams sustained the community. Algorave has been covered by music press as a democratizing force in electronic music, since performers build tracks from nothing in front of the crowd.
