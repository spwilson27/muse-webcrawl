Nikon

Nikon Corporation is a Japanese optics and imaging company founded in Tokyo in 1917 as Nippon Kōgaku Kōgyō Kabushikigaisha ("Japan Optical Industries"), formed by merging three optical firms with support from the Mitsubishi group. The Nikon name, adopted for its cameras in the 1940s and as the company name in 1988, derives from "Nippon Kōgaku".

History

Nikon began by making microscopes, binoculars, surveying instruments and military optics. Its first camera, the Nikon I rangefinder, appeared in 1948; the Nikon SP (1957) and above all the Nikon F (1959) established its Nikkor-lensed 35mm system as the choice of professional photojournalists — famously in the Korean and Vietnam wars. Later landmarks include the F3 (1980), the F4 and F5 autofocus flagships, the first practical digital SLR for professionals, the D1 (1999), and the Z-mount mirrorless system launched with the Z6 and Z7 in 2018, followed by the flagship Z9 (2021).

Business

Beyond cameras and Nikkor interchangeable lenses, Nikon is a major maker of microscopes, ophthalmic instruments, measuring equipment and — notably — precision semiconductor lithography steppers and scanners, in which it competes with ASML and Canon. It is a member of the Mitsubishi group of companies and is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.
