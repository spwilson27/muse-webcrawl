Poisson bracket

Formula for the Poisson bracket in canonical coordinates

In Hamiltonian mechanics, the Poisson bracket is an important binary operation, named after Siméon Denis Poisson, that plays a central role in the time evolution of a dynamical system and in the formulation of canonical transformations.

Definition

For two functions f and g of the canonical coordinates qi and momenta pi, the Poisson bracket is defined as the sum over all degrees of freedom of (∂f/∂qi)(∂g/∂pi) − (∂f/∂pi)(∂g/∂qi). The time evolution of any observable f is then given by df/dt = {f, H} + ∂f/∂t, where H is the Hamiltonian. Fundamental brackets include {qi, qj} = 0, {pi, pj} = 0, and {qi, pj} = δij.

Properties

The bracket is antisymmetric, bilinear, satisfies the Leibniz rule, and obeys the Jacobi identity, making the smooth functions on phase space into a Lie algebra and the phase space into a Poisson manifold. In quantum mechanics, Poisson brackets correspond to commutators divided by iħ, a correspondence central to canonical quantisation.
