The River Nore (Irish: An Fheoir) is a 140-kilometre (87 mi) long river in south-east Ireland and one of The Three Sisters, along with the River Barrow and the River Suir.

Course

The Nore rises on the eastern slopes of the Devil's Bit Mountain in County Tipperary, near Borris-in-Ossory, and flows south-east through Durrow in County Laois, then through County Kilkenny, passing Ballyragget, the city of Kilkenny — where it flows beneath Kilkenny Castle — and the towns of Thomastown and Inistioge. It becomes tidal below Inistioge and joins the Barrow a few kilometres north of New Ross, whence the combined waters reach the sea at Waterford Harbour.

Features

The Nore is a noted salmon and trout river, and its valley is rich in heritage: Jerpoint Abbey, Woodstock Gardens at Inistioge, and the medieval city of Kilkenny all stand along its banks. The river is the only habitat in Ireland of the endangered Nore freshwater pearl mussel (Margaritifera durrovensis), a subspecies of the freshwater pearl mussel found nowhere else. The river also flooded Kilkenny repeatedly in the past, leading to a major flood relief scheme completed in the early 2000s.
