The Nature Conservancy (TNC) is a global environmental organization headquartered in Arlington County, Virginia. Founded in the United States in 1951, it is one of the largest environmental nonprofits in the world by assets and revenue, working — as of 2021 — in 79 countries and territories and all 50 U.S. states, with more than a million members.

History

TNC grew out of the Ecological Society of America's efforts in the 1910s–1940s to protect natural areas; after professional scientists decided the society should stay a scholarly body, the activists formed the Ecologists' Union in 1946, which was incorporated as The Nature Conservancy on October 11, 1951. Its first land acquisition, in 1955, protected the Mianus River Gorge along the New York–Connecticut border.

Approach

The organization is known for a pragmatic, science-based, non-confrontational style: buying land and conservation easements from willing sellers, managing preserves, and partnering with governments, corporations, and indigenous communities. It has protected tens of millions of acres, operates hundreds of preserves open to the public, and works on rivers, oceans, regenerative agriculture, and climate policy; notable projects include debt-for-nature swaps in the Seychelles and Belize. It manages a rotating conservation lands base and publishes the magazine Nature Conservancy.

Criticism

TNC has faced scrutiny over executive compensation, selling land to trustees, allowing oil drilling on a Texas preserve that harms the Attwater's prairie chicken, and the integrity of carbon-offset projects, as well as criticism of corporate partnerships. It has also responded to scientific disputes over "novel ecosystems" and the resignations prompted by a 2019 internal investigation into workplace culture.
