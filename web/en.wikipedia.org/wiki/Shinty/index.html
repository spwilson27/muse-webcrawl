Shinty

Shinty (Scottish Gaelic: camanachd or iomain) is a traditional Scottish Gaelic field sport played with sticks and a ball. It is played mainly in the Scottish Highlands, where it has been organized competitively since the late nineteenth century under the Camanachd Association.

The game

Teams of twelve players use a curved stick called a caman to strike a small leather ball, attempting to score by driving the ball into the opposing team's goal (a "hail"). Unlike field hockey, players may strike the ball in the air, use both sides of the stick, and tackle with the body subject to the rules. Matches are played over two halves of 45 minutes.

History

Stick-and-ball games have been played in Scotland for many centuries and share roots with Irish hurling; mass matches between neighboring parishes were once a feature of winter festivals. The modern game was codified when the Camanachd Association was founded in Kingussie in 1893 and the first Camanachd Cup was contested in 1896.

Competitions and related games

The premier competition is the Camanachd Cup, with league competition headed by the Premiership; notable clubs include Kingussie, Newtonmore, Lovat, and Kyles Athletic. A composite-rules game, shinty-hurling, is played between Scottish shinty players and Irish hurlers in an annual international series. The women's game is governed by the Women's Camanachd Association.
