'Obby 'Oss festival

TypeFolk festival
LocationPadstow, Cornwall, England
DateMay Day (1 May)
FeaturesHobby horse processions, May song, dancing

The 'Obby 'Oss festival is a folk custom that takes place each May Day (1 May) in Padstow, Cornwall, England, and is believed to be one of the oldest fertility rites in the United Kingdom. The festival centres on two stylised hobby horses — the "Red" (Old) 'Oss and the "Blue Ribbon" 'Oss — which parade through the town accompanied by musicians, singers and dancers.

The 'osses are large circular frames worn on the shoulders, draped in black oilskin skirts painted with coloured discs, with a small horse's head on a long neck mounted at the front. Each 'oss is capered by a dancer supported by a "teazer", who leads the performance with a painted club, while the crowd sings the traditional Padstow May Song.

The day

Festivities begin at midnight on 30 April with the singing of the "Morning Song" outside the Golden Lion Inn. In the morning the town is decorated with greenery, flags and flowers. The two 'osses emerge from their stables during the day, dancing through the streets, visiting houses and the Maypole, and attempting to catch young women beneath their skirts — a supposed charm for luck and fertility. At the end of the day the 'osses meet and dance before returning to rest until the next May Day.

Origins

The origins of the custom are obscure; it was documented by the early 19th century and local tradition claims much greater antiquity, possibly as a rite to mark the arrival of summer or to frighten away winter and evil spirits. The festival attracts thousands of visitors to the small town each year. A children's 'oss parade and colt 'osses also participate.
