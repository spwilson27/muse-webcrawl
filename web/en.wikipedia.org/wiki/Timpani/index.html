Timpani

Timpani
Classification: Membranophone, percussion
Other names: Kettledrums
Inventor: developed from military kettledrums

Timpani (/ˈtɪmpəni/), or kettledrums, are musical instruments in the percussion family. They consist of a membrane called a head, stretched over a large bowl traditionally made of copper or fibreglass. Unlike most drums, timpani are tuned to specific pitches, usually by means of a pedal mechanism, and are a standard part of the orchestral percussion section.

Construction

Modern pedal timpani can be tuned quickly and accurately during performance. The drums come in standard sizes ranging roughly from 20 to 32 inches in diameter, and an orchestra typically uses a set of four or five. The player, a timpanist, strikes the head with padded mallets of varying hardness.

History and repertoire

Derived from military and ceremonial kettledrums of the Middle East and Europe, timpani entered the orchestra in the Baroque era. Composers such as Beethoven greatly expanded their expressive role, and they remain central to orchestral, concert band and ensemble repertoire.
