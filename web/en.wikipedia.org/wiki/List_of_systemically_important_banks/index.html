List of systemically important banks

This article lists banks that have been designated systemically important by international or national regulators. The Financial Stability Board (FSB), in consultation with the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, publishes an annual list of global systemically important banks (G-SIBs), which are required to hold additional capital buffers according to the bucket to which they are allocated. Many jurisdictions also maintain lists of domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs). The framework was created after the 2007–2008 financial crisis to reduce the risk posed by institutions considered "too big to fail".

The exact composition changes from year to year: Chinese banks have climbed the buckets as their global footprints grew, and UBS absorbed Credit Suisse in 2023, removing it from the list after the takeover.

Assessment methodology

Banks are scored on five categories of systemic importance:

  • Size
  • Cross-jurisdictional activity
  • Interconnectedness
  • Substitutability / financial institution infrastructure
  • Complexity

Additional consequences of G-SIB status include higher loss absorbency requirements (including total loss-absorbing capacity, TLAC), intensified supervision, and mandatory recovery and resolution planning ("living wills").

Domestic systemically important banks

National regulators apply parallel frameworks to their largest domestic institutions; examples include the United States' G-SIB surcharge for its eight largest banks, the United Kingdom's ring-fencing regime, and D-SIB designations across the euro area under the ECB's Single Supervisory Mechanism.
