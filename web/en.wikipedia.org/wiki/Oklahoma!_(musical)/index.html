Oklahoma! is the first musical written by the duo of Rodgers and Hammerstein. The musical is based on Lynn Riggs' 1931 play, Green Grow the Lilacs, and is set in farm country outside the town of Claremore, Indian Territory, in 1906, in the years before Oklahoma statehood.

Plot and songs

The story centres on the romance between cowboy Curly McLain and farm girl Laurey Williams, who is also courted by the sinister farmhand Jud Fry; a secondary plot follows Ado Annie, Will Parker and the peddler Ali Hakim. Well-known songs include "Oh, What a Beautiful Mornin'", "The Surrey with the Fringe on Top", "People Will Say We're in Love" and the title song "Oklahoma". Agnes de Mille's choreography, including the "dream ballet" ending the first act, was groundbreaking.

Reception and legacy

The original Broadway production opened on March 31, 1943, and ran for 2,212 performances — an unprecedented success. Oklahoma! is widely credited with launching the "Golden Age" of the American musical by fully integrating songs, story and dance. A special Pulitzer Prize was awarded to Rodgers and Hammerstein in 1944. The 1955 film adaptation starred Gordon MacRae and Shirley Jones, and the title song became the state song of Oklahoma in 1953.
