TeamLab

teamLab
Founded: 2001, Tokyo, Japan
Founder: Toshiyuki Inoko
Type: Art collective / interdisciplinary creative group
Known for: Immersive digital art installations

teamLab (チームラボ) is an international art collective founded in Tokyo in 2001 by Toshiyuki Inoko and collaborators. Describing its members as "ultra-technologists", the group brings together artists, programmers, engineers, CG animators, mathematicians and architects to create large-scale interactive digital installations that respond to the presence and movement of visitors.

Work

teamLab's installations typically project computer-generated imagery — flowers that bloom and scatter, waterfalls, schools of fish, flocks of birds — across walls, floors and, in some works, water surfaces, with the imagery continuously computed in real time and influenced by viewers' behaviour. The group emphasises dissolving the boundary between the artwork and the audience, and between individual works within an exhibition space.

Museums and exhibitions

In 2018 the collective opened the MORI Building DIGITAL ART MUSEUM: teamLab Borderless in Tokyo, which Guinness World Records recognised as the most visited single-artist museum in the world, and teamLab Planets, a second Tokyo venue, also drew record audiences. Permanent museums and long-running exhibitions followed in Shanghai, Singapore, Macau, Abu Dhabi and elsewhere, and teamLab's works have appeared in exhibitions and public spaces around the world.

Reception

The collective's popularity helped define the genre of immersive digital art museums, though critics have debated whether its highly photogenic spectacles represent a new art form or theme-park-style entertainment.
