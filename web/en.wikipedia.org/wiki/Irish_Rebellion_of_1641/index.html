Irish Rebellion of 1641

Date23 October 1641 – May 1642
LocationIreland
ResultFounding of Confederate Ireland; start of the Irish Confederate Wars

The Irish Rebellion of 1641 was an uprising by Irish Catholics in the Kingdom of Ireland, directed against English and Scottish Protestant settlers and the administration in Dublin. Beginning on 23 October 1641, the rebellion was initially led by Sir Phelim O'Neill and other Gaelic Irish landowners who had lost land in the plantations of Ireland and feared further dispossession and religious persecution.

Causes

The rebellion grew out of decades of colonisation: the Plantation of Ulster and other settlements had transferred land from Catholic Irish and Old English owners to Protestant settlers from England and Scotland. Catholics faced discrimination, insecurity of tenure, and heavy debts, while the policies of Thomas Wentworth, Earl of Strafford, and fears that the English Parliament intended to impose further anti-Catholic measures sharpened tensions.

Course

The rebels quickly seized much of Ulster, but a plot to capture Dublin Castle was betrayed and failed. In the chaos that followed, thousands of Protestant settlers were killed or expelled; massacres such as that at Portadown in November 1641, and exaggerated reports of atrocities in England, inflamed opinion and hardened the English response. The rebellion spread across the island, and in 1642 the insurgents organised themselves as the Irish Catholic Confederation, based at Kilkenny, transforming the uprising into the Irish Confederate Wars, part of the wider Wars of the Three Kingdoms.

Legacy

The rebellion and the depositions collected about it shaped Protestant memory in Ireland for centuries. Its suppression during the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland (1649–1653) brought confiscations that drastically reduced Catholic landholding.
