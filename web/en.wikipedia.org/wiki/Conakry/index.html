Conakry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Conakry, capital and largest city of Guinea

Conakry is the capital and largest city of Guinea. A port city, it serves as the economic, financial and cultural centre of Guinea. Its population was 1,660,973 according to the 2014 census, and it is estimated that it contains almost two million inhabitants today, making up about one sixth of the entire population of the country.

History

Conakry developed on Tombo Island, at the tip of the Kaloum Peninsula, and later spread onto the mainland. The French established a trading post and administrative capital there in the late 1880s after acquiring the site from local Baga and Soussou inhabitants; by 1904 it had become the capital of French Guinea. After Guinea's independence in 1958 under Ahmed Sékou Touré, the city grew rapidly as the seat of government and the country's principal port.

Geography

The city occupies the Kaloum Peninsula on the Atlantic coast, with the original settlement on Tombo Island now connected to the mainland by a causeway. The port handles exports of alumina, bauxite, bananas, and iron ore. Conakry is administratively divided into five communes: Kaloum, Dixinn, Ratoma, Matam, and Matoto.

Landmarks and culture

Notable sites include the Conakry Grand Mosque (one of the largest mosques in West Africa), the Guinea National Museum, the Palais du Peuple, and the botanical gardens. Conakry International Airport (now Ahmed Sékou Touré International Airport) serves the city. The city is also known for its markets and for Camp Boiro, the notorious detention camp of the Touré era, a memorial site nearby.
