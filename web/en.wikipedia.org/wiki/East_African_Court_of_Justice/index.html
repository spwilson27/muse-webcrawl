East African Court of Justice

The East African Court of Justice (EACJ) is a treaty-based judicial body of the East African Community (EAC) charged with ensuring adherence to the law in the interpretation and application of, and compliance with, the Treaty for the Establishment of the East African Community. The Court was established under Article 9 of the Treaty and became operational in 2001. Its temporary seat is in Arusha, Tanzania, alongside other EAC organs.

Structure

The Court has two divisions: a First Instance Division and an Appellate Division, the latter introduced by a 2007 amendment to the Treaty. Judges are appointed by the Summit of EAC Heads of State from among persons of proven integrity, impartiality and independence, recommended by the Partner States, and serve terms of up to seven years.

Jurisdiction

The Court hears references by Partner States and by the Secretary General alleging infringement of the Treaty, applications by employees of the Community, and — most significantly in practice — references by legal and natural persons resident in the Partner States challenging the legality of acts of Partner States or Community institutions under Article 30 of the Treaty. Although the Treaty withholds an express general human rights jurisdiction, the Court has entertained rule-of-law and good-governance claims framed as Treaty violations, building a notable body of jurisprudence on elections, media freedom and arbitrary detention. Decisions of the First Instance Division may be appealed to the Appellate Division on points of law.
