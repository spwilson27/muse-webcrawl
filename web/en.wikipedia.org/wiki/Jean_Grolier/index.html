Jean Grolier

Born: 1479, Lyon, France
Died: 22 October 1565, Paris

Jean Grolier de Servières, vicomte d'Aguisy (1479 – 22 October 1565) was a French bibliophile and royal official, treasurer of France, and one of the most celebrated book collectors of the Renaissance. His name is synonymous with fine bookbinding.

Life and library

Grolier served the French crown in Italy, where as treasurer of the Duchy of Milan he came into contact with the humanist printers of Venice, including the Aldine Press of Aldus Manutius, with whom he corresponded. Over his long life he assembled a library of some 3,000 volumes, bound in Paris in distinctive designs — gilt interlaced geometrical patterns (plaquette bindings) in coloured morocco leathers — frequently stamped with the motto Io. Grolierii et amicorum ("[the property] of Jean Grolier and of his friends"), expressing his belief that books should be shared.

Legacy

Only about 400–500 Grolier bindings survive, held by major libraries including the Bibliothèque nationale de France, the British Library, and the Morgan Library. They are considered the archetype of French Renaissance binding, and Grolier's name is honoured by the Grolier Club of New York, the leading American society of bibliophiles, founded in 1884.
