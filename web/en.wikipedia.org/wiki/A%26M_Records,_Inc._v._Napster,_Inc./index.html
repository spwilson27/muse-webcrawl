A&M Records v. Napster
CourtU.S. Court of Appeals, Ninth Circuit
DecidedFebruary 12, 2001
Citation239 F.3d 1004 (9th Cir. 2001)

A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc., 239 F.3d 1004 (9th Cir. 2001), was a landmark copyright case in which the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit upheld a preliminary injunction against the peer-to-peer file sharing service Napster, finding that it was liable for contributory and vicarious copyright infringement.

Background

Napster, launched in 1999, let users share MP3 music files through a central index maintained on its servers. A&M Records and other record labels sued in December 1999, and the district court issued an injunction ordering Napster to block access to infringing works.

Holding

The Ninth Circuit held that Napster's users directly infringed the labels' reproduction and distribution rights, that Napster's fair use defenses — including sampling and space-shifting arguments — were unlikely to succeed, and that Napster, having actual knowledge of infringement and the ability to police its system while profiting from it, could be held secondarily liable. The court modified the injunction to place the burden on the labels to identify infringing files.

Impact

Unable to filter effectively, Napster shut down its service in 2001 and later filed for bankruptcy. The decision helped shape the legal framework for online intermediaries and preceded the Supreme Court's inducement ruling in MGM Studios v. Grokster (2005).
