Dorothy Wordsworth

Dorothy Wordsworth
Born: 25 December 1771, Cockermouth, Cumberland
Died: 25 January 1855, Rydal Mount
Occupation: Diarist, poet
Relatives: William Wordsworth (brother)

Dorothy Mae Ann Wordsworth (1771–1855) was an English author, diarist, and poet, sister of the Romantic poet William Wordsworth, with whom she shared a close lifelong household.

Journals

Living at Dove Cottage, Grasmere, Dorothy kept the Grasmere Journal (1800–1803), whose vivid nature observations directly inspired William's poems—her description of daffodils underlies 'I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud'. She wrote, she said, to give William pleasure when he returned.

Other writings and life

Her Recollections of a Tour Made in Scotland (1803) is regarded as a classic of travel writing, though her journals were only published posthumously, from 1897 onward. Ill from the 1830s, she spent her last decades at Rydal Mount. Modern scholarship recognises her as a significant Romantic writer in her own right.
