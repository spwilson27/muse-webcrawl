Scheelite


Formula: CaWO4
Crystal system: Tetragonal
Mohs hardness: 4.5–5
Named after: Carl Wilhelm Scheele

Scheelite is a calcium tungstate mineral with the chemical formula CaWO4. It is an important ore of tungsten (wolfram). Well-formed crystals are sought by collectors, and specimens faceted for gem use exist, though the mineral is somewhat soft for jewelry. The mineral was named in 1821 by Karl Caesar von Leonhard for the Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele (1742–1786).

Properties

Scheelite occurs in tetragonal bipyramidal crystals and granular masses, ranging in color from colorless and white to yellow, orange, brown, and greenish. Its crystals may be transparent to translucent with an adamantine to vitreous luster. A distinctive identification aid is its bright blue-white fluorescence under short-wave ultraviolet light, a property exploited in prospecting. Its specific gravity is high, about 5.9–6.1.

Occurrence

Scheelite occurs in contact metamorphic skarns, high-temperature hydrothermal veins, and greisen, often associated with cassiterite, wolframite, fluorite, and quartz. Major deposits have been worked in China (the largest producer), and in places such as the King Island mine in Tasmania, Australia, and the Flat River area of Canada. Together with wolframite it supplies most of the world's tungsten, used in hard metals, alloys, and filaments.
