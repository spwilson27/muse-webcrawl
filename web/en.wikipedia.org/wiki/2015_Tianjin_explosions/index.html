2015 Tianjin explosions

Date: 12 August 2015

Time: 23:34 CST

Location: Binhai New Area, Tianjin, China

Deaths: 173 (including 99 firefighters)

Injuries: 797

Cause: Detonation of improperly stored hazardous chemicals, including ammonium nitrate

On 12 August 2015, a series of explosions at the Port of Tianjin in the Binhai New Area of Tianjin, China, killed 173 people, including 99 firefighters, and injured 797 others. The blasts originated at a warehouse operated by Ruihai Logistics that stored hazardous chemicals. The second, larger explosion, involving the detonation of about 800 tonnes of ammonium nitrate, was equivalent to roughly 256 tonnes of TNT and was visible from space.

Background

The Ruihai Logistics warehouse stored large quantities of hazardous materials, including sodium cyanide, calcium carbide, and ammonium nitrate, in quantities far exceeding what it was licensed to hold, and far closer to residential areas than safety regulations allowed. The company had obtained its approvals through connections with local officials.

The explosions

Firefighters were first called to a fire at the warehouse late on the evening of 12 August. At 23:34, a first explosion equivalent to about 3 tonnes of TNT occurred, followed some 30 seconds later by a much larger blast. The explosions levelled the warehouse site, destroyed thousands of new automobiles in nearby lots, damaged apartment buildings, and left a large crater. Dozens of firefighters sent in before the explosions were killed, making it the deadliest incident for Chinese firefighters in decades.

Aftermath

The disaster prompted a nationwide review of chemical storage safety. Dozens of people, including Ruihai executives and government officials, were prosecuted; Ruihai chairman Yu Xuewei was sentenced to death with a two-year reprieve. Authorities undertook an enormous environmental cleanup of cyanide contamination at the site.
