Load management

Load management, also known as demand-side management (DSM), is the process of balancing the supply of electricity on a network with the electrical load by adjusting or controlling the load rather than the power station output. This can be achieved by direct intervention of the utility in real time, by the use of frequency-sensitive relays triggering circuit breakers (ripple control), by time clocks, or by using special tariffs to influence consumer behaviour.

Load management flattens peaks in demand

Purpose

Electricity demand varies strongly over the day and with the weather, while generation and network capacity must be sized for the peak. Load management reduces maximum demand, allowing utilities to serve load with less peaking plant, defer network investment, lower costs and emissions, and improve reliability. Techniques include direct load control of water heaters and air conditioners, interruptible tariffs for large industrial users, time-of-use and real-time pricing, energy efficiency programmes, and — increasingly — coordinated charging of electric vehicles and dispatch of battery storage and smart appliances.

Relation to demand response

Load management is closely related to demand response: historically, load management referred to utility-controlled, one-way programmes (such as ripple control of off-peak water heating in Australia, New Zealand, and parts of Europe), while demand response implies price-responsive or market-participating consumers with two-way communication through smart meters and the smart grid. In practice the terms are now often used interchangeably.

In sports

The term is also used in professional sports — notably the NBA — for the practice of resting healthy players during the regular season to manage injury risk, a usage that prompted league rules on star-player rest in 2023.
