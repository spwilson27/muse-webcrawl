Sapporo Breweries

Sapporo Breweries Ltd. (サッポロビール株式会社, Sapporo Bīru Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese brewery company founded in 1876 in Sapporo, Hokkaido. It is the oldest beer brand in Japan and one of the country's four major brewers, alongside Asahi, Kirin and Suntory. The company is part of Sapporo Holdings, a holding company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

History

Beer brewing in Japan began in earnest when the Hokkaido Development Commission (Kaitakushi) established a brewery in Sapporo in 1876, drawing on German brewing expertise and the island's cold climate, which suited lager production. Seibei Nakagawa, who had trained in Germany, directed the first brews. The brewery's product, sold from 1877, eventually took the name Sapporo Beer, marked by the five-pointed star that had been the symbol of the Hokkaido pioneers.

Through the twentieth century the company expanded, merged and re-emerged as an independent brewer after the Second World War. Sapporo's flagship products include Sapporo Draft (Nama), Yebisu Beer — a premium brand revived in 1971 — and Sapporo Classic, sold only in Hokkaido. The former Sapporo brewery site is now the Sapporo Beer Museum and Sapporo Beer Garden, a popular tourist attraction where visitors can sample beer and jingisukan (Genghis Khan) grilled lamb.

International operations

Sapporo expanded overseas, notably acquiring Canada's Sleeman Breweries in 2006 and the American Anchor Brewing Company in 2017 (Anchor later closed in 2023). Sapporo beer is exported widely and brewed under licence in several countries.
