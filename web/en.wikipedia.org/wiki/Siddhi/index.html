Siddhi

Siddhi (Sanskrit: सिद्धि, siddhi; Pali: iddhi) is a Sanskrit term meaning "perfection," "accomplishment," or "attainment." In Indian religions it most commonly denotes special, seemingly supernatural powers and abilities acquired through spiritual practice (sadhana), such as meditation and yoga. In Buddhism the related Pali term iddhi refers to psychic powers that may arise on the path but are not the ultimate goal; in Hinduism, siddhis are classically enumerated in the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali and in later tantric and hatha yoga literature.

The eight classical siddhis

The Bhagavata Purana and other texts list eight primary siddhis (aṣṭa-siddhi):

SiddhiMeaning
AṇimāBecoming infinitesimally small
MahimāBecoming infinitely large
GarimāBecoming infinitely heavy
LaghimāBecoming weightless; levitation
PrāptiReaching anywhere; obtaining anything
PrākāmyaRealising whatever one desires
ĪśiṭvaLordship; power to create
VaśiṭvaPower to control all beings and elements

In the Yoga Sutras

The third book of Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, the Vibhuti Pada, catalogues numerous powers arising from samyama (combined concentration, meditation, and absorption) applied to different objects — knowledge of past lives, of others' minds, invisibility, strength of an elephant, and so on. The text itself warns that these powers are accomplishments in the worldly state but obstacles to final liberation (samadhi), and the fourth book distinguishes siddhis born of practice from those arising from birth, herbs, mantras, or austerities.

In Buddhism

In the Pali Canon, iddhi includes powers such as walking on water, passing through walls, flying, and multiplying the body. The Buddha is portrayed as possessing iddhi but as discouraging its display for show; the Kevatta Sutta ranks the "miracle of instruction" above psychic feats. In Mahayana and Vajrayana sources, siddhis are divided into mundane powers and the supreme siddhi of Buddhahood itself; tantric adepts who mastered them were called mahasiddhas, of whom a famous list counts eighty-four.
