Portus

LocationFiumicino, Lazio, Italy
RegionTyrrhenian coast, near the Tiber mouth
TypeArtificial harbour
BuiltBegun AD 42 (Claudius); hexagonal basin added AD 112 (Trajan)

Portus was a large artificial harbour of ancient Rome. Located on the Tyrrhenian coast about 25 kilometres (16 mi) southwest of the city, near the mouth of the Tiber and the earlier port of Ostia, it was built to relieve Ostia's silted and storm-exposed anchorage and to secure the grain supply of the capital. Fiumicino Airport now occupies much of the site, though Trajan's hexagonal basin survives as a lake.

History

The emperor Claudius began construction in AD 42. His harbour — the Portus Claudius — consisted of two curving moles projecting into the sea, an artificial island with a great lighthouse modelled on the Pharos of Alexandria, and a channel connecting the basins to the Tiber. Part of the works reused the hull of Caligula's giant obelisk ship, filled with concrete and sunk as a foundation. The harbour opened in AD 64 under Nero, but storms still endangered shipping; in AD 62 a gale sank some 200 grain ships inside the harbour.

Between AD 100 and 112 Trajan added a second, landward basin: a hexagon of some 32 hectares (79 acres), each side about 358 metres (1,175 ft) long, dug inland and ringed with warehouses (horrea), docks and canals. This Trajanic basin made Portus an all-weather port and the commercial heart of Rome's annona — the grain imports from Egypt, Africa and Sicily that fed the city. A town grew beside the harbours, and Portus eventually became an independent city and an important early Christian see.

Portus flourished while the empire's grain fleets sailed. It declined with Rome itself in the 5th and 6th centuries AD, when shifting coastlines, silting and the loss of the African grain supply reduced it to a minor anchorage. The coastline has since advanced several kilometres seaward, leaving the basins inland.

Archaeology

The hexagonal basin of Trajan is still clearly visible from the air, and remains of the Claudian moles, warehouses, temples (including one to Neptune and one to Portunus), baths, and the late-antique defensive walls have been excavated. The site lies in a protected archaeological area adjacent to Rome Fiumicino Airport; a museum displays finds from the port, and the surrounding necropolis of Isola Sacra contains many tombs of the port's merchants and workers.
