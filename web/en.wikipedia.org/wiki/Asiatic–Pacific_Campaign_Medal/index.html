The Asiatic–Pacific Campaign Medal is a United States military award of the Second World War, established by Executive Order 8808 on 6 November 1942. It was awarded to personnel who served in the Asiatic-Pacific Theater of Operations between 7 December 1941 and 2 March 1946.

Design

The bronze medal, designed by Thomas Hudson Jones, depicts on the obverse a tropical landing scene with troops, ships and aircraft beneath a palm tree, with the words "Asiatic Pacific Campaign" around the upper rim; the reverse bears the American bald eagle and the years 1941–1945. The ribbon's yellow field represents the Pacific, with narrow red, white and blue stripes echoing both the United States and Japan. Bronze service stars denoted participation in designated campaigns — 43 for the Army, 48 for the Navy and Marine Corps — and an arrowhead device marked combat parachute jumps and amphibious assault landings.

Companion awards

It was one of three Second World War campaign medals, alongside the American Campaign Medal and the European–African–Middle Eastern Campaign Medal.
