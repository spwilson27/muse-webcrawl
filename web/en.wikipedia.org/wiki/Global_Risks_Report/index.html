Global Risks Report

The Global Risks Report is an annual study published by the World Economic Forum ahead of its annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland. First published in 2006, the report assesses the most severe global risks facing the world over short-term (two-year) and long-term (ten-year) horizons, drawing on the perceptions of experts and decision-makers together with quantitative data.

Methodology

The report's core input is the Global Risks Perception Survey, completed by roughly 900–1,500 respondents from academia, business, government, international organisations and civil society. Risks — numbering around 33–37 in recent editions — are grouped into five categories: economic, environmental, geopolitical, societal and technological. Respondents rank risks by severity over two- and ten-year periods, and the report analyses their interconnections and potential cascading effects.

Recent findings

Recent editions have placed misinformation and disinformation at the top of short-term risks, while environmental risks — extreme weather events, critical change to Earth systems, biodiversity loss and natural resource shortages — dominate the ten-year horizon. Earlier editions highlighted risks such as infectious diseases, livelihood crises, interstate conflict and cyberattacks. The 2020 edition, published shortly before the COVID-19 pandemic, listed infectious diseases among the top risks by impact, a ranking widely noted afterwards.
