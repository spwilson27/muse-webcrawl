Family policy

Family policy refers to the set of government policies that aim to influence the structure, functioning, and well-being of families. Common instruments include parental leave, child benefits and tax credits, subsidized childcare, housing support, and legal frameworks governing marriage, divorce, and child custody.

Approaches

Policy regimes differ widely: Nordic countries emphasize universal childcare and generous paid leave to support dual-earner households; continental European systems have traditionally supported caregiving through cash transfers; liberal welfare states rely more on means-tested benefits and market-provided care. Comparative researchers such as Gøsta Esping-Andersen have classified these arrangements into welfare regime types.

Objectives and debates

Family policies variously seek to reduce child poverty, raise fertility rates, promote gender equality, and support children's development. Debates center on cost, the balance between cash and services, and the extent to which the state should shape private family decisions.
