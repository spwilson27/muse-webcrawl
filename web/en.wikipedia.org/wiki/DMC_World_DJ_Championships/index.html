The DMC World DJ Championships is an annual DJ competition founded in 1985 by Tony Prince's Disco Mix Club (DMC). It is one of the most prestigious events in turntablism, credited with bringing scratching and battle DJing to a worldwide audience and launching the careers of many leading DJs.

Format

Competitors perform short routines — traditionally six minutes in the classic singles category — using two turntables and a mixer, judged on technical skill, originality, composition and crowd response. National champions advance to the world final, historically held in London. Over time the event added categories such as the Battle for World Supremacy (a head-to-head elimination format) and team championships.

History

The first championship in 1985 was won by London DJ Roger Johnson. DJ Cheese (1986) introduced scratching into winning routines, and successive champions — Cash Money, Roc Raida, the X-Ecutioners (as the X-Men), A-Trak (who won in 1997 aged 15), DJ Craze (the only DJ to win three consecutive solo titles, 1998–2000), DJ Kentaro and DJ Netik — pushed turntablism technique forward. The championships also popularised techniques such as beat juggling.

Influence

The DMC championships, together with events such as the ITF and later Red Bull 3Style, formed the competitive circuit of battle DJ culture, and videos of winning sets circulated widely as instructional material for aspiring turntablists.
