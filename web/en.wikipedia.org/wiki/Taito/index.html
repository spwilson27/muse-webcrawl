Taito Corporation is a Japanese company specialising in video games, toys, arcade cabinets and game centres, based in Tokyo. Founded in 1953 by Russian-Jewish businessman Michael Kogan, Taito began by importing and distributing vending machines and jukeboxes before becoming a pioneering arcade game manufacturer. Its most famous creation, Space Invaders (1978), designed by Tomohiro Nishikado, became a worldwide phenomenon credited with launching the golden age of arcade video games.

History

Taito started as the Taito Trading Company, importing goods into Japan, and was the first company to distill and sell vodka in Japan. It moved into manufacturing amusement machines, including Japan's first domestically produced jukeboxes and pinball-like pachinko machines, then electromechanical games. With Space Invaders the company became one of the dominant forces in the arcade industry; the game's popularity in Japan was famously said to have caused a shortage of 100-yen coins. Subsequent Taito successes include Qix (1981), Elevator Action (1983), Arkanoid (1986), Bubble Bobble (1986), Chase H.Q. (1988), Puzzle Bobble (Bust-a-Move, 1994) and the Darius shooting-game series.

Square Enix ownership

In 2005, Square Enix acquired Taito, making it a wholly owned subsidiary in 2006. Taito continues to operate its "Taito Station" amusement arcades in Japan, develops games for consoles and mobile platforms, and licenses its classic catalogue. Its in-house sound team, Zuntata, is noted for its game music.
