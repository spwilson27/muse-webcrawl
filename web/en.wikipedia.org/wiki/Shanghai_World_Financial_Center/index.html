Shanghai World Financial Center

Height: 492 m (1,614 ft)
Floors: 101
Construction: 1997–2008
Architect: Kohn Pedersen Fox
Developer: Mori Building Co.

The Shanghai World Financial Center (SWFC; Chinese: 上海环球金融中心) is a supertall skyscraper in the Lujiazui district of Pudong, Shanghai, China. Designed by the American firm Kohn Pedersen Fox and developed by Japan's Mori Building Company, it stands 492 metres (1,614 ft) tall with 101 floors and was the tallest building in mainland China when it opened on 28 August 2008; it was later surpassed by the neighbouring Shanghai Tower. Its distinctive trapezoidal aperture near the top has earned it the nickname "the bottle opener."

Design

The tower's form derives from a square prism — representing Earth in Chinese cosmology — intersected by two arcs representing the heavens, narrowing as it rises. The original design crowned the building with a circular opening, but objections that it resembled the Japanese rising-sun flag led the developer to redesign the aperture as a trapezoid, which also proved cheaper and structurally simpler. The opening houses an observation bridge on the 100th floor, long marketed as the world's highest observatory, with glass floors at 474 metres. The building mixes offices, the Park Hyatt Shanghai hotel, conference facilities, and shops.

Construction

Ground was broken in 1997, but the Asian financial crisis halted work in 1998 with only the foundations complete. Construction resumed in 2003 with an enlarged design that increased the height from 460 to 492 metres. The building was completed in 2008 essentially on the eve of the global financial crisis — an irony noted at the time for a tower named after world finance. Its diagonal-braced megaframe with outrigger trusses was engineered to withstand Shanghai's typhoon winds and seismic demands on soft delta soil.

Records and awards

On completion the SWFC was the second-tallest building in the world after Taipei 101, and its observatory won it repeated "world's highest" claims. It received the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat's Best Tall Building Worldwide award for 2008.
