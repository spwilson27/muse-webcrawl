Saigyō


Born: 1118, Kyoto, Japan
Died: March 23, 1190 (aged 71–72), Kawachi Province
Occupation: Poet, Buddhist monk
Pen name: Saigyō Hōshi

Saigyō Hōshi (西行法師, 1118 – March 23, 1190) was a famous Japanese poet of the late Heian and early Kamakura periods. Born Satō Norikiyo (佐藤 義清), he came from a noble military family serving the retired Emperor Toba. At the age of 22 or 23, at the height of a promising career as a guard to the retired emperor, he abruptly renounced the world and became a Buddhist monk, taking the religious name En'i and later the pen name Saigyō, meaning "Western Journey," a reference to Amida Buddha and the Western Paradise.

Life

Saigyō lived during a time of great transition in Japan, when political power was passing from the imperial court in Kyoto to the military government of the shogunate. He spent much of his life as a wandering monk, travelling extensively through the northern provinces, to Shikoku, and to the island of Sado, composing poetry wherever he went. In his later years he undertook two great journeys to northern Honshū, experiences that inspired many of his most celebrated poems.

Around 1186 he visited Kamakura and conversed with Minamoto no Yoritomo, the founder of the Kamakura shogunate. He spent his final years at Hirokawa-dera temple in Kawachi Province, where he died in 1190 — on the anniversary of the Buddha's death, as tradition holds he had wished.

Poetry

Saigyō's poetry is characterised by themes of impermanence (mujō), loneliness, nature, and the pathos of things (mono no aware). His collection, the Sankashū ("Mountain Hut Collection"), contains roughly 1,500 poems. He is one of the most represented poets in the Shin Kokin Wakashū, the great imperial anthology compiled around 1205, and 94 of his poems appear in the Hyakunin Isshu selections compiled by Fujiwara no Teika.

His most famous poem invokes the moon and cherry blossoms, expressing his wish to die beneath the blossoms in spring, around the time of the full moon — a wish traditionally said to have been granted.
