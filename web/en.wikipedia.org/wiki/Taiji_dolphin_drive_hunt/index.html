Taiji dolphin drive hunt

LocationTaiji, Wakayama, Japan
SeasonSeptember to March (approx.)
SpeciesBottlenose dolphins, Risso's dolphins, pilot whales, others
PurposesMeat; live capture for aquariums

The Taiji dolphin drive hunt is a drive hunt of dolphins and small whales that takes place annually in Taiji, a coastal town in Wakayama Prefecture, Japan. During the hunting season, roughly from September to March, fishermen herd migrating pods of small cetaceans into a shallow cove, where some animals are selected for live capture and sale to aquariums and marine parks, and the remainder are killed for meat.

Method

Fishermen in boats locate a pod offshore and, by striking metal poles fixed to their vessels to create a wall of sound, drive the animals toward the shore and into the cove, which is then closed off with nets. Trainers and buyers inspect the captured animals the following day; young, unblemished dolphins — especially bottlenose dolphins — command high prices from dolphinariums in Japan and abroad. The other animals are slaughtered in the cove.

History and controversy

Drive hunting has a long history in Japan, and Taiji promotes it as a centuries-old local tradition. The hunt drew global attention after the 2009 Academy Award-winning documentary The Cove, filmed partly in secret, depicted the slaughter and raised concerns about animal welfare and the mercury content of dolphin meat. Animal welfare organisations and several governments have condemned the hunt, while Japanese officials and Taiji representatives defend it as a legal, regulated continuation of coastal whaling culture, conducted under prefectural quotas.

Quotas and species

The hunt is authorised by the Wakayama prefectural government under quotas set each year, covering species such as bottlenose dolphins, striped dolphins, pantropical spotted dolphins, Risso's dolphins, short-finned pilot whales and false killer whales. Annual catches have declined from earlier decades, partly in response to reduced demand from aquariums.
