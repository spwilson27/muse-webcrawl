Dementia with Lewy bodies

Lewy bodies — abnormal deposits of alpha-synuclein protein — in cortical neurons

Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) is a type of dementia characterized by changes in thinking, behavior, movement and mood. It is associated with abnormal deposits of the protein alpha-synuclein, called Lewy bodies, in the brain. Along with Parkinson's disease dementia, it makes up the Lewy body dementias, among the most common causes of dementia after Alzheimer's disease.

Signs and symptoms

Core clinical features include fluctuating cognition with pronounced variations in attention and alertness, recurrent complex visual hallucinations, rapid eye movement (REM) sleep behavior disorder, and spontaneous features of parkinsonism. Patients are often highly sensitive to antipsychotic (neuroleptic) medications, which can cause severe reactions.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is clinical, supported by biomarkers such as dopamine transporter imaging and polysomnography. There is no cure; treatment focuses on managing symptoms, with cholinesterase inhibitors such as rivastigmine often used for cognitive and psychiatric symptoms, and careful avoidance of typical antipsychotics. Prognosis after diagnosis is typically a survival of several years, comparable to or shorter than Alzheimer's disease.
