Yom Kippur War

The Yom Kippur War, also known as the Ramadan War, the October War, or the 1973 Arab–Israeli War, was fought from 6 to 25 October 1973 between Israel and a coalition of Arab states led by Egypt and Syria.

Yom Kippur War
Date: 6–25 October 1973
Belligerents: Israel vs Egypt, Syria and allies
Result: UN ceasefire; Israeli military gains, Egyptian political gains
Aftermath: 1973 oil crisis, Camp David Accords

Course of the war

Egypt and Syria launched a surprise attack on Yom Kippur, the holiest day in Judaism, which coincided with Ramadan. Egyptian forces crossed the Suez Canal and breached the Bar Lev Line, while Syrian forces attacked the Golan Heights. After initial Israeli setbacks, Israel repelled the Syrians, crossed the Suez Canal under Ariel Sharon, and encircled the Egyptian Third Army.

Superpower involvement

The United States airlifted supplies to Israel (Operation Nickel Grass) and the Soviet Union resupplied its Arab partners; a tense superpower confrontation led to a US DEFCON 3 alert before a UN ceasefire took hold.

Consequences

The war led to the 1973 oil embargo and energy crisis, disengagement agreements, and ultimately the 1978 Camp David Accords and the Egypt–Israel peace treaty of 1979.
