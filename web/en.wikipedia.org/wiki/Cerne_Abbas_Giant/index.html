The Cerne Abbas Giant is a hill figure near the village of Cerne Abbas in Dorset, England. It is a giant outline of a nude man, 55 metres (180 ft) high, cut into the chalk hillside, wielding a large knotted club, famously with prominent genitalia. Listed as a scheduled monument, the figure is one of Britain's best known hill figures and has long been associated with fertility folklore.

History and dating

Unlike the ancient Uffington White Horse, the Giant is not mentioned in surviving records until 1694, when Cerne Abbas churchwardens' accounts record payment for its repair. Theories of its origin have ranged from an Iron Age or Roman depiction of Hercules, to a creation of the Saxon period, to a 17th-century political satire connected with the Lord of the Manor, Denzil Holles, or a lampoon of Oliver Cromwell. Scientific dating by optically stimulated luminescence of soil samples, reported by the National Trust in 2021, placed the figure's origin in the late Saxon period, between about 700 and 1100 AD, possibly associated with the nearby Cerne Abbey.

Maintenance and folklore

The figure is maintained by periodic re-chalking, most recently involving volunteers packing fresh chalk into its trenches. Local tradition held that a woman sleeping on the figure would be blessed with fertility, and couples seeking to conceive have long visited the site. The Giant is protected from direct public access; a viewing area is provided across the valley. Over the years the figure has been the target of publicity stunts and temporary additions, and its image is trademarked to prevent commercial misuse.
