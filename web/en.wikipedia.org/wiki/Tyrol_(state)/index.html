Tyrol (German: Tirol) is an Austrian federal state (Land) in western Austria, comprising the northern and eastern parts of the historic County of Tyrol. It consists of two separated areas — North Tyrol and East Tyrol — divided by the Italian province of South Tyrol. Its capital and largest city is Innsbruck.

Geography

Tyrol is the third-largest Austrian state by area and is almost entirely mountainous, lying within the Central Eastern Alps. The Inn valley forms its main east–west axis, with side valleys such as the Ötztal, Zillertal and Stubaital. The state's highest peak is the Großglockner (3,798 m), on the border with Carinthia in East Tyrol, and the Wildspitze (3,768 m) is the highest mountain wholly in North Tyrol. The Brenner Pass, on the border with Italy, is one of the principal Alpine crossings between northern and southern Europe.

History

The region was united under the Counts of Tyrol in the Middle Ages and passed to the Habsburgs in 1363. Tyroleans famously resisted Bavarian and French occupation during the Tyrolean Rebellion of 1809 under Andreas Hofer. After the First World War, the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919) split the historic crown land: South Tyrol and Trentino went to Italy, while North and East Tyrol remained with Austria, a division that persists today; cooperation continues through the cross-border Euroregion Tyrol–South Tyrol–Trentino.

Economy and culture

Tourism dominates the economy: Tyrol is one of Europe's leading winter-sports destinations, with resorts such as Kitzbühel, St. Anton am Arlberg, Sölden and Ischgl, and Innsbruck twice hosted the Winter Olympics (1964 and 1976). Industry includes glass (Swarovski, headquartered at Wattens), machinery and hydropower. Tyrolean culture is marked by Alpine traditions, brass-band music, and the German dialect spoken throughout the state.
