Sweepstakes

A sweepstakes is a type of contest in which a prize or prizes are awarded to one or more winners chosen at random from all entries. Unlike a lottery, a legitimate sweepstakes in the United States may not require entrants to pay or purchase anything to enter — the legal distinction turns on the three elements of prize, chance and consideration; a promotion that has all three is an illegal private lottery.

Forms and regulation

Sweepstakes are widely used as marketing promotions: entrants mail a postcard, submit an online form, or enter automatically with a purchase, with a free alternative method of entry always offered. They are regulated at federal level through postal and deceptive-mail laws and at state level, with some states requiring registration and bonding for large prize pools. The term originally described a horse race in which the owners of the entrants each contributed to the winner's purse — stakes that were "swept" by the victor.

Famous promotions and abuses

The Publishers Clearing House sweepstakes, with its Prize Patrol and televised surprise cheque presentations, made the format famous from the 1980s onward. The format was also exploited by fraudulent operators who sent misleading "you have won" mailings targeted at the elderly, leading to stricter disclosure rules requiring clear statements of odds and the fact that no purchase is necessary.
