World Sailing (WS) is the world governing body for the sport of sailing, recognised by the International Olympic Committee and the International Paralympic Committee. It was founded in Paris in 1907 as the International Yacht Racing Union (IYRU), changed its name to the International Sailing Federation (ISAF) in 1996, and adopted its present name in 2015.

Role

World Sailing is responsible for the Racing Rules of Sailing, which are revised every four years, for the classification and measurement rules of the classes of boats raced internationally, and for the appointment of international race officials. It organises or sanctions the Sailing World Championships, the Sailing World Cup series and youth and para sailing world championships, and it decides which classes are used at the Olympic Games.

History

The IYRU was formed after a 1906 conference in London agreed uniform measurement rules for racing yachts; its founding members included many European nations. Sailing had first appeared at the 1900 Paris Olympics, and the federation has overseen the Olympic regatta ever since. In the 2010s the organisation moved its headquarters from Southampton to London and back, and introduced new events such as kiteboarding and foiling classes.

Classes

World Sailing recognises more than a hundred classes of boat, from single-handed dinghies such as the ILCA (Laser) to keelboats, multihulls, boards and radio-controlled boats. For the Paris 2024 Olympic Games the equipment included the ILCA 6 and 7, 470, 49er, 49erFX, Nacra 17, iQFoil windsurfers and Formula Kite kiteboards.
