Tōkaidō (road)

The Tōkaidō was the most important of the Five Routes of the Edo period in Japan, connecting the shogunal capital of Edo (modern Tokyo) with Kyoto, the imperial capital. Running along the Pacific coast of Honshū for roughly 500 kilometres, it had 53 post stations (shukuba), immortalized in Utagawa Hiroshige's woodblock print series The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō.

Legacy

The route is perpetuated today by the Tōkaidō Main Line railway and the Tōkaidō Shinkansen (opened 1964, the world's first high-speed railway), as well as the Tōmei and Meishin expressways. Travelers in the Edo period included daimyō processions under the sankin-kōtai system.
