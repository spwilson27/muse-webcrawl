Indica (Arrian)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indica (Greek: ἰνδική, Indikē) is a work by the Greek historian Arrian, written in the 2nd century CE, describing India and the voyage of Nearchus from the Indus delta to the Persian Gulf. Together with his Anabasis of Alexander, it survives as one of the principal Greek accounts of India and of Alexander the Great's campaigns in the East.

Contents

The work falls into two parts. The first, drawing heavily on the lost Indica of Megasthenes (the Seleucid ambassador to the Mauryan court around 300 BCE) and on Eratosthenes, describes the geography of India, its rivers and peoples, customs such as sati and the caste system, and its fauna, including elephants and gold-digging ants inherited from Herodotus. The second part narrates the voyage of Nearchus, the admiral whom Alexander appointed in 325 BCE to sail from the mouth of the Indus along the Makran coast to Mesopotamia, based on Nearchus's own lost account.

Style and sources

Unusually, Arrian wrote the Indica in the Ionic dialect of Herodotus rather than the Attic Greek of the Anabasis, an archaizing choice modelled on his sources. Modern historians treat the Megasthenes-derived material as valuable but uneven evidence for Mauryan India, while the account of Nearchus's voyage is regarded as broadly reliable in its geography.

Transmission

The text survives through the medieval manuscript tradition of Arrian's works and is printed in standard editions of Arrian alongside the Anabasis. English translations include E. Iliff Robson's Loeb Classical Library version.
