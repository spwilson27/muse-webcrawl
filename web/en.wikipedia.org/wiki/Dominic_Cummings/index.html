Dominic Mckenzie Cummings (born 25 November 1971) is a British political strategist. He directed the successful Vote Leave campaign in the 2016 Brexit referendum and served as chief special adviser to Prime Minister Boris Johnson from July 2019 until his departure from Downing Street in November 2020.

Career

After Oxford, Cummings worked in Russia and then in British political campaigns, including the campaign against UK membership of the euro and the North East referendum campaign of 2004. He was a special adviser to Education Secretary Michael Gove from 2011 to 2014, driving contentious school reforms. As campaign director of Vote Leave he is associated with the "£350 million a week" claim and the slogan "Take Back Control".

In Downing Street

Cummings recruited advisers outside normal civil-service channels and pushed a programme of Whitehall reform. In May 2020 he became the centre of a major controversy after it emerged he had travelled from London to County Durham during the COVID-19 lockdown; he defended the trip, including a drive to Barnard Castle, at a press conference in the Downing Street rose garden, and remained in post with Johnson's backing. He left government in November 2020 after an internal power struggle, and later became a prominent critic of Johnson's handling of the pandemic, giving lengthy testimony to a parliamentary committee in 2021.
