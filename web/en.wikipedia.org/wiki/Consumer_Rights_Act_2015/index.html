Consumer Rights Act 2015

The Consumer Rights Act 2015 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that consolidates and reforms consumer protection law, covering contracts for goods, digital content, and services, and unfair contract terms. It came into force on 1 October 2015.

Overview

The Act replaced the Sale of Goods Act regime for consumer contracts and implemented recommendations of the Law Commissions, clarifying remedies in plain structure. See consumer protection.

Goods

Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and as described. Consumers have a 30-day short-term right to reject faulty goods for a full refund, after which repair or replacement applies, with a final right to reject or a price reduction if repair fails.

Digital content and services

For the first time, the Act gives statutory rights for paid digital content (satisfactory quality, fitness, description) with repair/replacement or price-reduction remedies, and requires services to be performed with reasonable care and skill, for a reasonable price and within a reasonable time where not agreed.

Unfair terms

Part 2 reforms unfair terms law (absorbing the Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts Regulations), extending fairness review and listing "grey list" terms, with enforcement by the Competition and Markets Authority and Trading Standards.
