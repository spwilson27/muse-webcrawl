Bariatric surgery

Bariatric surgery (from Greek baros, "weight") is a group of surgical procedures performed on the stomach and intestines to treat obesity and related metabolic diseases. By restricting food intake, altering nutrient absorption, and changing gut hormone signalling, these operations produce substantial and usually durable weight loss and frequently induce remission of type 2 diabetes.

Procedures

  • Sleeve gastrectomy — removal of about 80% of the stomach, leaving a narrow tube; now the most commonly performed procedure worldwide.
  • Roux-en-Y gastric bypass — creation of a small stomach pouch connected directly to the small intestine, bypassing most of the stomach and duodenum.
  • Adjustable gastric band — an inflatable silicone band around the upper stomach; once popular, it has declined because of complications and inferior results.
  • Biliopancreatic diversion with duodenal switch — the most powerful but most complex and nutritionally risky operation.

Eligibility and outcomes

Guidelines generally recommend metabolic and bariatric surgery for adults with a body mass index of 40 or above, or 35 or above with serious comorbidities; the 2022 ASMBS/IFSO guidelines lowered thresholds further. Long-term studies show large reductions in mortality, cardiovascular events, and diabetes complications. Risks include leaks, strictures, dumping syndrome, gallstones, and micronutrient deficiencies requiring lifelong supplementation and follow-up.
