Bourbon whiskey (or simply bourbon) is a type of barrel-aged American whiskey made primarily from corn (maize). The name derives from the French Bourbon dynasty, although the precise inspiration is uncertain; contenders include Bourbon County, Kentucky, and Bourbon Street in New Orleans, both of which are named after the dynasty. Bourbon has been distilled since the 18th century, and the use of the term "bourbon" for the whiskey has been traced to the 1820s, becoming consistent in the 1870s.

Although bourbon may be made anywhere in the United States, it is strongly associated with the American South in general, and with Kentucky in particular. As of 2014, the distillers' wholesale market revenue for bourbon sold within the U.S. was about $2.7 billion, and Kentucky distilleries produced roughly 95 percent of the world's bourbon.

Legal requirements

Under United States law and trade agreements, bourbon must satisfy strict requirements. It must be made in the United States, produced from a grain mixture that is at least 51% corn, aged in new charred oak containers, distilled to no more than 80% alcohol by volume (160 proof), entered into the barrel at no more than 62.5% ABV (125 proof), and bottled at 40% ABV (80 proof) or more. Whiskey labelled "straight bourbon" must additionally be aged for at least two years and contain no added colouring, flavouring, or other spirits.

Production

Most bourbons use a mash bill of corn supplemented with rye or wheat (a "wheated" bourbon) and malted barley. Fermentation is often conducted with a portion of the previous mash — the "sour mash" process. After distillation, the clear spirit, sometimes called "white dog", is placed in new charred oak barrels, where it gains colour and flavour from the wood during ageing in rackhouses subject to seasonal temperature swings.

History

Settlers in western Virginia and the District of Kentucky — from which the Commonwealth of Kentucky was formed in 1792 — distilled surplus corn into whiskey. Distillers including Jacob Spears and Elijah Craig are frequently credited in (partly legendary) accounts of bourbon's origins. A 1964 act of Congress declared bourbon "a distinctive product of the United States."
