Ming–Qing transition

The Ming–Qing transition, also known as the Manchu conquest of China, was a decades-long period of conflict between the Ming dynasty and the rising Jurchen-led Later Jin and Qing dynasty, together with rebel forces led by Li Zicheng and Zhang Xianzhong, lasting roughly from 1618 to 1683. It ended with the Qing established as rulers of China proper, though at enormous human cost.

Background

By the early 17th century the Ming state was weakened by fiscal crisis, court factionalism, famine, epidemic disease and the effects of the Little Ice Age. In Manchuria, the Jurchen chieftain Nurhaci unified the Jurchen tribes, organized the Eight Banners, and in 1618 issued the Seven Grievances against the Ming. His successors, Huang Taiji and the regent Dorgon, built a multi-ethnic state that adopted the name Qing in 1636.

Fall of Beijing

In April 1644 the rebel army of Li Zicheng captured Beijing, and the Chongzhen Emperor hanged himself on Coal Hill. The Ming general Wu Sangui, guarding Shanhai Pass on the Great Wall, allied with the Qing, and their combined forces defeated Li at the Battle of Shanhai Pass. Dorgon entered Beijing and installed the child Shunzhi Emperor, claiming the Mandate of Heaven.

Conquest of the south

Southern Ming loyalist regimes resisted from Nanjing, Fujian and Guangdong, while massacres accompanied the conquest of cities such as Yangzhou and Jiading in 1645. The imposition of the queue hairstyle on Han men provoked fierce resistance. Qing forces gradually subdued the south, and the last Southern Ming claimant, the Yongli Emperor, was executed in 1662 after fleeing to Burma. The final major loyalist challenge, the Revolt of the Three Feudatories (1673–1681), was defeated by the Kangxi Emperor, and in 1683 Qing forces conquered the Ming-loyalist Kingdom of Tungning on Taiwan, completing the transition.

Demographic impact

War, famine and disease during the transition caused catastrophic population loss; estimates of excess deaths range into the tens of millions, with some regions such as Sichuan devastated and later repopulated by migration.
