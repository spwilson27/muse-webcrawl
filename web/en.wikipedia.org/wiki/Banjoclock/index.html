Banjoclock

A banjoclock is a novelty style of clock in which a clock face or movement is combined with a banjo-shaped case or a banjo-like display, typically produced as kitsch Americana décor. Banjoclocks are usually wall-mounted, with the round "drum" of the banjo form housing the dial and the "neck" extending upward or downward. They were mass-marketed in the United States during the 20th century by novelty and gift manufacturers, and examples range from simple quartz souvenir clocks to ornate wooden mantel pieces in which a real miniature banjo body frames the movement.

Description

The typical banjoclock takes the silhouette of a five-string banjo: a circular body — standing in for the banjo's pot — serves as the clock case, while a fretted neck rises from it, sometimes carrying a decorative peghead. Dial markings occupy the vellum-like face, and some models include pendulums or chime movements. Because the design is a visual pun rather than a horological category, construction quality varies widely, from stamped plastic souvenir items sold at tourist attractions to handcrafted wooden pieces.

Collecting

Banjoclocks are collected as examples of Americana and novelty horology rather than for technical clockmaking merit. They are occasionally exhibited alongside other figural clocks — such as banjo clocks (a distinct, earlier form: the Willard patent timepiece, whose case merely resembles a banjo in outline) — with which they should not be confused.
