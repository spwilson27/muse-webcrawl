Upper Austria (German: Oberösterreich) is one of the nine federal states (Bundesländer) of Austria. Its capital is Linz, the third-largest city in the country. Upper Austria is the fourth-largest state by area and the third-largest by population, with around 1.5 million inhabitants. It borders Germany (Bavaria) and the Czech Republic, as well as the Austrian states of Lower Austria, Styria, and Salzburg.

Geography

The state is traditionally divided into four quarters: the Mühlviertel north of the Danube, a granite and gneiss highland forming part of the Bohemian Massif; the Hausruckviertel in the west; the Traunviertel in the south, which includes the lakes and mountains of the Salzkammergut and reaches the Dachstein massif on the Styrian border; and the Innviertel in the northwest, acquired from Bavaria in 1779. The Danube flows through the state from west to east, joined by the Traun, Enns, and Inn rivers.

Economy

Upper Austria is Austria's leading industrial region, with Linz a major centre for steel (voestalpine), chemicals, and machinery. The state accounts for a large share of Austrian exports, while the Salzkammergut lakes and Alpine foothills support significant tourism.

History

The region formed the core of the medieval "Austria above the Enns" (Österreich ob der Enns). The Innviertel was ceded by Bavaria under the Treaty of Teschen in 1779. After the First World War the area became the federal state of Upper Austria, and from 1938 to 1945 it was administered as part of the Nazi Reichsgau Oberdonau. The Mauthausen concentration camp was located near Linz during this period.
