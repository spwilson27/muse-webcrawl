Marching band

InstrumentsBrass, woodwinds, percussion, color guard equipment
SettingsSchools, universities, military units, parades

A marching band is a group of instrumental musicians who perform while marching, often for entertainment or competition. Marching bands generally wear matching uniforms, incorporate visual elements such as a color guard, and are led by one or more drum majors. Instrumentation typically includes brass, woodwind, and percussion instruments, with a front ensemble ("pit") of stationary percussion at scholastic competitions.

History

The tradition descends from military field music — drummers, fifers, and buglers who regulated camp life and battlefield movement. After the American Civil War, surplus instruments and demobilised bandsmen seeded town and school bands, and marching became central to American college football culture in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Modern show bands combine military precision drill with themed field shows, while historically Black college and university (HBCU) bands developed a distinct "show style" emphasising high-stepping, dance, and popular music.

Activities

Marching bands perform at parades, sporting events, ceremonies, and judged competitions run by circuits such as Bands of America in the United States. Related performance ensembles include drum and bugle corps, indoor drumlines, and winter guard. Repertoire ranges from classical transcriptions and jazz to arrangements of contemporary popular songs.
