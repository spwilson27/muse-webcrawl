Joanne Rowling (born 1965), known by her pen name J. K. Rowling, is a British author best known as the creator of the Harry Potter fantasy series, the best-selling book series in history. The seven novels (1997–2007) spawned a global entertainment franchise of films, stage productions and theme parks and are credited with revitalising children's publishing. Rowling also writes the Cormoran Strike detective novels under the name Robert Galbraith.

Early life and Harry Potter

Rowling studied French and classics at the University of Exeter and worked for Amnesty International and as an English teacher in Portugal. The idea for Harry Potter came during a delayed train journey in 1990; she completed the first novel, Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone, while living on state benefits in Edinburgh after her mother's death and the breakdown of her first marriage. Rejected by numerous publishers, the book was taken by Bloomsbury in 1996 and published in June 1997. The series' successive volumes — Chamber of Secrets (1998), Prisoner of Azkaban (1999), Goblet of Fire (2000), Order of the Phoenix (2003), Half-Blood Prince (2005) and Deathly Hallows (2007) — were publishing phenomena, and Warner Bros.' eight-film adaptation (2001–11) became one of the highest-grossing film series ever. Rowling later expanded the franchise as screenwriter of the Fantastic Beasts films and co-writer of the stage play Harry Potter and the Cursed Child (2016).

Later work and philanthropy

Her adult novel The Casual Vacancy appeared in 2012; the Galbraith crime series began with The Cuckoo's Calling (2013) and was adapted for BBC television as Strike. Rowling's fortune — she is among the world's wealthiest authors — has funded extensive philanthropy through the Volant Charitable Trust and Lumos, the charity she founded to end the institutionalisation of children; she has said charitable giving returned her to, then took her from, billionaire status at various points.

Public controversies

Since 2019 Rowling's statements on sex and gender identity have drawn sustained criticism from transgender-rights advocates, some former fans and several Harry Potter film actors, as well as support from others, including feminist campaigners; the dispute has been a recurring feature of her public life and of discussion of the franchise.
