Ian Maddieson

Ian Maddieson (born 1942) is a British-American phonetician and linguist known for his work on the cross-linguistic typology of sound systems. He is particularly noted for compiling and analyzing the UCLA Phonological Segment Inventory Database (UPSID), a database of the phoneme inventories of several hundred of the world's languages.

Career

Maddieson worked for many years at the University of California, Los Angeles, and later at the University of New Mexico and the University of California, Berkeley. His research combines laboratory phonetics with large-scale typological surveys, examining how the sounds of the world's languages are distributed and what universal tendencies govern the structure of phonological inventories.

Work

His book Patterns of Sounds (1984), part of the Cambridge Studies in Speech Science and Communication series, presented statistical analyses of consonant and vowel inventories across languages, documenting tendencies such as the prevalence of certain consonants (e.g., /t/, /k/, /m/, /n/) and the relative rarity of clicks and ejectives. The UPSID database, containing segment inventories for over 450 languages, became a foundational resource for phonological typology and was later incorporated into larger databases such as PHOIBLE.

Maddieson also co-authored, with Peter Ladefoged, The Sounds of the World's Languages (1996), a widely used reference on phonetic description. He served as editor of the Journal of the International Phonetic Association.
