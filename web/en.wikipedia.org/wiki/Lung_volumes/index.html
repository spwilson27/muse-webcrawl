Lung volumes

Lung volumes and lung capacities refer to the volume of air in the lungs at different phases of the respiratory cycle. They are measured by spirometry and body plethysmography and are fundamental to assessing respiratory function. The average total lung capacity of an adult human is about 6 litres.

The four volumes

VolumeTypical valueDescription
Tidal volume (VT)~500 mLAir inhaled or exhaled in a normal breath
Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)~3,100 mLExtra air that can be inhaled after a normal inspiration
Expiratory reserve volume (ERV)~1,200 mLExtra air that can be exhaled after a normal expiration
Residual volume (RV)~1,200 mLAir remaining after a maximal exhalation; cannot be measured by simple spirometry

Capacities

Capacities are sums of two or more volumes: inspiratory capacity (VT + IRV), functional residual capacity (ERV + RV — the resting lung volume at end-expiration), vital capacity (IRV + VT + ERV — the maximum air exhaled after a maximal inhalation), and total lung capacity (all four volumes).

Clinical significance

Obstructive diseases such as asthma and COPD typically show air trapping with increased residual volume and total lung capacity, whereas restrictive diseases such as pulmonary fibrosis reduce total lung capacity. Reference values depend on age, sex, height, and ethnicity.
