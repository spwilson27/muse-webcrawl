Master Humphrey's Clock was a weekly periodical written and "conducted" by Charles Dickens, published in London from 4 April 1840 to December 1841. Instead of issuing his next novel in monthly parts, Dickens devised a miscellany magazine built around a framing device, intending to mix short pieces with longer fiction.

The framing device

The periodical is narrated by Master Humphrey, an elderly, reclusive Londoner who keeps his manuscripts in the case of an old clock. He forms a small club with friends — including the deaf gentleman, the retired schoolmaster Jack Redburn, and Mr Pickwick, borrowed from Dickens's earlier Pickwick Papers — who meet to read one another the stories stored in the clock.

Contents

Early numbers contained sketches, essays and short tales such as "Mr Pickwick's Tale" and "Mr Weller's Watch". Sales of the miscellany format disappointed, so Dickens introduced a continuous story. The tale of Little Nell, The Old Curiosity Shop, began in the fourth number and swelled until it absorbed the whole magazine, with the framing characters gradually fading away. When it concluded in February 1841, Dickens followed it with his historical novel Barnaby Rudge, set during the Gordon Riots of 1780, which ran until November 1841 and closed the venture.

Illustration and legacy

Numbers were illustrated with wood engravings by George Cattermole and Hablot Knight Browne ("Phiz"). Although the miscellany idea was abandoned, the experiment established Dickens's model of author-edited weekly journals, later realised in Household Words and All the Year Round. The framing device was dropped from later book editions of the two novels, but Master Humphrey's Clock is still reprinted, sometimes including the interpolated sketches and the full frame.
