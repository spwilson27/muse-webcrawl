Al-Muwatta

A manuscript page of the Muwatta of Imam Malik

Al-Muwaṭṭaʾ (Arabic: الموطأ, "the well-trodden path" or "the approved one") is an early collection of Islamic law and hadith compiled by the Imam Malik ibn Anas (711–795) in Medina. It is regarded as one of the earliest surviving works of Islamic jurisprudence and, by many accounts, the first structured book of hadith, combining reports from the Prophet Muhammad, verdicts of his Companions, and the settled practice ('amal) of the people of Medina.

Contents and approach

The Muwatta is arranged by legal topic — prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, marriage, commerce, penalties and inheritance — rather than by narrator. In the most widely transmitted recension, that of Yahya ibn Yahya al-Laythi, it contains roughly 1,700–1,900 reports, including prophetic hadith with chains of transmission, sayings of the Companions and Successors, and Malik's own legal opinions. Malik privileged the consensus practice of Medina, the Prophet's city, as strong evidence of the sunna.

Recensions and influence

Malik taught the work over decades and revised it, so numerous recensions circulated through his students, including those of Muhammad al-Shaybani and Ibn Wahb. The Muwatta became the foundational text of the Maliki school of law, dominant in North and West Africa and historically in al-Andalus. Al-Shafi'i is reported to have called it the most sound book on earth after the Qur'an, and it was memorised and commented upon for centuries; major commentaries include the Tamhīd and Istidhkār of Ibn Abd al-Barr and the Muntaqā of al-Baji.
