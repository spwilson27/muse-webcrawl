Base (chemistry)

A base is a substance that neutralises an acid. In the Arrhenius sense it produces hydroxide ions in water; in the Brønsted–Lowry sense it accepts protons; in the Lewis sense it donates an electron pair. Alkalis are bases that dissolve in water.

Bases feel slippery, turn litmus blue, and react with acids to give a salt and water only. Common bases include sodium hydroxide, ammonia solution, calcium hydroxide (limewater) and sodium hydrogencarbonate, used in cleaners, antacids, agriculture and industry.

[Image: Bottles of common bases: sodium hydroxide pellets and ammonia solution]

Strength

Strong bases (NaOH, KOH) dissociate completely; weak bases (ammonia, ethanoate ions) only partially accept protons, setting up equilibria. pH measures the result in solution, running to 14 and beyond for concentrated strong alkalis.

Bases in analysis

Titration of an acid against a standard alkali with an indicator is the bread-and-butter method of volumetric analysis; back-titrations measure insoluble bases such as antacid tablets.
