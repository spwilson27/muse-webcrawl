Hessian matrix

Hessian matrix
Named afterLudwig Otto Hesse (1811–1874)
FieldMathematical analysis, optimisation
DefinitionSquare matrix of second-order partial derivatives of a scalar-valued function

In mathematics, the Hessian matrix, or simply the Hessian, is the square matrix of second-order partial derivatives of a scalar-valued function of several variables. For a function f(x1, …, xn) whose second derivatives exist, the entry in row i and column j is ∂²f/∂xi∂xj. The matrix is named after the German mathematician Ludwig Otto Hesse, who studied such determinants in the 19th century. If the function is twice continuously differentiable the Hessian is symmetric, because mixed partial derivatives are equal.

Critical points

The Hessian's main application is the second derivative test. At a critical point of f (where the gradient vanishes), the eigenvalues of the Hessian determine the point's character: if the Hessian is positive definite (all eigenvalues positive), the point is a local minimum; if negative definite, a local maximum; if indefinite, a saddle point; if semidefinite, the test is inconclusive. In two variables this reduces to examining the determinant of the Hessian together with one diagonal entry.

Applications

The Hessian describes the local curvature of a function and appears wherever second-order information matters: in Newton's method for optimisation, quadratic approximations (the second-order Taylor expansion uses the Hessian as its quadratic form), the bordered Hessian test for constrained optimisation in economics, discriminant analysis in geometry, and in physics and machine learning, where Hessian-based methods and approximations (such as the Laplace approximation and Gauss–Newton) are used in fitting and uncertainty estimation. The differential-geometric counterpart on manifolds is the Hessian operator defined with a connection.
