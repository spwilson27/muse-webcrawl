The Shishunaga dynasty was the third ruling dynasty of Magadha, an empire of ancient India. According to the Puranas, it succeeded the Haryanka dynasty and ruled from about 413 BCE to 345 BCE, when it was replaced by the Nanda Empire.

History

The dynasty was founded by Shishunaga, a minister (amatya) of the last Haryanka ruler Nagadasaka, who was placed on the throne by the people. Shishunaga temporarily moved the capital to Vaishali and conquered the kingdom of Avanti, ending its long rivalry with Magadha, and possibly Kosala and Vatsa as well.

His successor Kakavarna Kalashoka is associated with the Second Buddhist Council held at Vaishali. Kalashoka's ten sons are said to have ruled jointly afterwards. The last Shishunaga ruler, often identified as Mahanandin, was overthrown by Mahapadma Nanda, founder of the Nanda dynasty.

Rulers

  • Shishunaga (c. 413–395 BCE)
  • Kakavarna Kalashoka (c. 395–367 BCE)
  • The ten sons of Kalashoka (c. 367–355 BCE)
  • Nandivardhana / Mahanandin (to c. 345 BCE)

Chronology and regnal years vary between Puranic and Buddhist sources.
