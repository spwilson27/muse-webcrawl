Captain (armed forces)

Image placeholder: Typical captain's insignia.

Captain is a commissioned officer rank used in many armies and other armed forces around the world. In NATO terms it corresponds to the grade OF-2. The army rank of captain typically sits above lieutenant and below major, and should not be confused with the naval rank of captain, which is a much more senior grade (OF-5) equivalent to an army colonel.

Role

An army captain normally commands a company-sized unit of roughly 100–200 soldiers, or serves as a staff officer at battalion or brigade level. It is usually the most senior rank held by officers in direct day-to-day command of troops, and in many armies it is the first rank at which officers are considered for significant staff appointments. Captains are typically officers with several years of commissioned service.

History

The word derives from the Late Latin capitaneus, "chief", from caput, "head". Historically a captain was the leader of a company of soldiers raised by feudal levy or contract; captains originally raised and equipped their own troops. In the British Army the rank is denoted by three pips ("stars") on the shoulder; in the United States Army by two vertical silver bars.

Other uses

Air forces following army traditions also use captain, while many air forces use "flight lieutenant" for the equivalent grade. Some police, fire, and merchant marine services also use the title captain for supervisory ranks.
