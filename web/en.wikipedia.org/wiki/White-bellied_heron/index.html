White-bellied heron

White-bellied heron (Ardea insignis)
Conservation status: Critically Endangered (IUCN)
Family: Ardeidae
Also known as: Imperial heron

The white-bellied heron (Ardea insignis), also known as the imperial heron, is a large species of heron found in the foothills of the eastern Himalayas in India, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Myanmar. It is one of the rarest herons in the world and is listed as Critically Endangered, with a global population estimated at only a few hundred individuals — possibly fewer than 250 mature birds.

Description

This large heron stands about 127 cm (50 in) tall, making it the second-largest heron after the goliath heron. It is mostly dark grey with a white belly, white throat, and whitish plumes; the long bill is dark with a yellowish-green lower mandible. Breeding birds develop two long white occipital plumes.

Habitat and behaviour

The species inhabits undisturbed rivers and wetlands with sand or gravel bars and shallow water in subtropical and temperate forests, generally below 1,500 m. It feeds mainly on fish, foraging alone or in small groups in riverine habitats. It nests in tall trees, often in mixed heronries, during late winter and spring.

Threats

Habitat loss and degradation from hydroelectric development, sand and gravel mining, poaching, and disturbance are the main threats. Bhutan is a major stronghold, and the bird is a flagship species for river conservation in the region. In 2021, a captive-rearing and conservation breeding centre was established in Bhutan.
