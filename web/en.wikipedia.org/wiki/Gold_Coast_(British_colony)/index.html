Gold Coast (British colony)

StatusCrown colony of the United Kingdom
CapitalAccra
Established1821
Independence6 March 1957 (as Ghana)

The Gold Coast was a British Crown colony on the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa from 1821 until its independence in 1957 as Ghana. The term Gold Coast is also used historically for the region, named by European traders for its abundant gold resources; the Portuguese, the first Europeans to arrive in the 15th century, called it Costa do Ouro and built the castle of São Jorge da Mina (Elmina Castle) in 1482.

The colony was formed from coastal forts administered first by British merchant companies and, from 1821, by the Crown. Its territory gradually expanded inland through treaties and wars with the Ashanti Empire, and it eventually united four territories: the Gold Coast proper, Ashanti, the Northern Territories, and British Togoland.

Independence

After the Second World War, nationalist movements led by Kwame Nkrumah and his Convention People's Party pressed for self-government. Following elections in the 1950s and a United Nations-supervised plebiscite in British Togoland, the Gold Coast became independent on 6 March 1957 — the first sub-Saharan African colony to achieve independence from European colonial rule — taking the name Ghana, with Nkrumah as its first prime minister.
