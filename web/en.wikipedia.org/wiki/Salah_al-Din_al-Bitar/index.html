Salah al-Din al-Bitar (Arabic: صلاح الدين البيطار‎; 5 May 1912 – 21 July 1980) was a Syrian politician and, with Michel Aflaq, co-founder of the Arab Ba'ath Party. A Sunni Muslim from Damascus, he served several times as Prime Minister of Syria after the Ba'athist coup of 1963 before being purged, exiled, and ultimately assassinated in Paris.

Early life and the Ba'ath

Al-Bitar was born into a prominent Sunni landowning family of Damascus. Studying in Paris in the early 1930s alongside Aflaq, he helped form the Arab Ba'ath movement, formally established in 1947. He edited the party newspaper al-Ba'th and, after the party merged with Akram al-Hawrani's Arab Socialist Party in 1952, became one of the Ba'ath's leading figures. As foreign minister in the late 1950s he played a prominent role in the union of Syria and Egypt as the United Arab Republic (1958–1961).

In power

After Ba'athist officers seized power in March 1963, al-Bitar became prime minister, heading four governments between 1963 and 1966. His cabinets pursued socialist measures — land reform and nationalisations — while struggling against radical rivals within the party and army. The February 1966 coup by Salah Jadid's faction drove Aflaq and al-Bitar from power; al-Bitar was imprisoned and then exiled.

Exile and assassination

Living in exile, al-Bitar broke openly with the regime of Hafez al-Assad and in 1978 founded a Paris-based journal, al-Ihya al-Arabi, attacking the government. On 21 July 1980 he was shot dead in Paris as he arrived at his office, an assassination widely attributed to Syrian intelligence.
