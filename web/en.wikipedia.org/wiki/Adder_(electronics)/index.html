Adder (electronics)

An adder, or summer, is a digital logic circuit that performs addition of numbers. Adders are a fundamental building block of the arithmetic logic unit (ALU) of processors, and are also used in address calculation, multipliers, and many other parts of digital systems.

Types

A half adder adds two single bits, producing a sum and a carry. A full adder adds three bits — two operands and a carry-in — producing a sum and a carry-out; full adders are chained to add multi-bit numbers. Simple ripple-carry adders propagate the carry bit by bit and are slow for wide words, so practical designs use faster schemes such as carry-lookahead, carry-save, carry-select and parallel-prefix (Kogge–Stone, Brent–Kung) adders. A circuit that performs subtraction using two's complement addition is called an adder–subtractor.
