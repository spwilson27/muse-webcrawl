Matignon most commonly refers to the Hôtel de Matignon in Paris, the official residence and office of the Prime Minister of France since 1935; by metonymy, "Matignon" is used in French political journalism to mean the office of the Prime Minister itself, much as "the Élysée" refers to the Presidency.

The Hôtel de Matignon

The mansion at 57 Rue de Varenne in the 7th arrondissement was built in the 1720s for the Prince de Tingry and passed through several aristocratic owners, including the Matignon family from whom it takes its name. It was the Paris residence of Talleyrand in the early 19th century and hosted celebrated salons. The French state acquired the property in 1922, and in 1935 it became the seat of the head of government. Behind the building lies one of the largest private gardens in Paris, covering about three hectares, where each departing prime minister traditionally plants a tree.

Other uses

Matignon is also the name of a commune in the Côtes-d'Armor department of Brittany in northwestern France, near the town of Dinard, with a population of roughly 1,700.
