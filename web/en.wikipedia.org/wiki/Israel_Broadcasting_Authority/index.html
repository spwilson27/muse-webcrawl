The Israel Broadcasting Authority (IBA; Hebrew: רשות השידור, Rashut HaShidur) was Israel's public broadcaster from 1965 until its closure in 2017. It operated television and radio services in Hebrew, Arabic and other languages under the Broadcasting Authority Law of 1965.

History

Radio broadcasting in Israel began with Kol Israel ("Voice of Israel"), founded at independence in 1948 as part of the Prime Minister's Office. The IBA was established as an independent statutory authority in 1965, taking over Kol Israel radio and launching regular television broadcasts in 1968. Its services included Channel 1 television, the Arabic-language Channel 33, and radio networks such as Reshet Bet and the classical music station Kol Hamusika.

Closure

Following years of criticism over inefficiency and a 2014 reform law, the IBA was dissolved and replaced by the Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation, known as Kan. The IBA's final television news broadcast aired on 9 May 2017, and its last transmission was the Eurovision Song Contest final on 13 May 2017, after which Kan took over.
