Magic Leap, Inc. is an American technology company that released a head-mounted augmented reality display, the Magic Leap One, which superimposes 3D computer-generated imagery over real-world objects by projecting a digital light field into the user's eye. Founded in 2010 by Rony Abovitz, the company raised more than $2.6 billion from investors including Google and Alibaba Group before releasing its first product in 2018.

History

Magic Leap operated in secrecy for years, attracting enormous investment on the strength of demonstrations of its "photonic lightfield chip" technology. In December 2016, reporting revealed that some of the company's promotional videos had been produced by special-effects studios rather than captured through its hardware, raising questions about the gap between its claims and its progress. The Magic Leap One Creator Edition was released in August 2018 at a price of $2,295.

Products

The Magic Leap One consists of a pair of goggles tethered to a small belt-worn computer (the "Lightpack") and a handheld controller. Sales were reportedly far below the company's internal targets. In April 2020 the company laid off roughly half its workforce and announced a pivot from consumer to enterprise applications. The Magic Leap 2, aimed at business and professional users, was released in 2022. In 2020, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund became a major stakeholder, taking a controlling interest in the company.

Technology

The company described its approach as a "digital light field" display, using waveguide optics to project images at varying focal depths into the eye. Its headsets perform spatial mapping of the user's environment so that virtual objects can be placed persistently in real space.
