Chuvash people

The Chuvash people (Chuvash: чӑвашсем) are a Turkic ethnic group native to Chuvashia in Russia, in the Volga region. They speak the Chuvash language, the sole surviving member of the Oghur branch of Turkic languages.

Chuvash
Total population: c. 1.5 million
Regions: Chuvashia, Russia
Language: Chuvash, Russian
Religion: Orthodox Christianity, traditional beliefs

History

The Chuvash are often linked to the Volga Bulgars and earlier Oghur-speaking groups. After the Mongol invasions and the fall of Volga Bulgaria, they came under the Khanate of Kazan and then the Russian Empire from the 16th century, with gradual Christianisation.

Culture

Traditional culture is noted for intricate geometric embroidery in red and black, folk music, wooden architecture, and cuisine including shartan sausage and chuvash beer. Most Chuvash today are Russian Orthodox, while some preserve indigenous religious practices.

Demographics

Most live in the Chuvash Republic, whose capital is Cheboksary, with communities in Tatarstan, Bashkortostan, Ulyanovsk and Samara oblasts, and a diaspora elsewhere in Russia.
