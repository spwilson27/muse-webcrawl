Nymphalidae

The Nymphalidae are the largest family of butterflies, with more than 6,000 species distributed worldwide. They are commonly called brush-footed butterflies because in adults of most species the forelegs are reduced to brush-like, non-functional structures, so the insects appear to stand on four legs. The family includes many familiar species, such as the monarch, the painted lady, admirals, emperors, fritillaries, and tortoiseshells.

Subfamilies

The family is divided into about a dozen subfamilies, including the Danainae (milkweed butterflies, including the monarch), Satyrinae (browns and ringlets), Heliconiinae (longwings and fritillaries), Limenitidinae (admirals), and Nymphalinae (true brush-foots, including peacocks and tortoiseshells). Several groups formerly treated as separate families — such as the Danaidae and Satyridae — are now classified within Nymphalidae based on morphological and molecular evidence.

Ecology

Nymphalid caterpillars feed on a wide range of host plants, often with striking specialisations: monarch larvae feed on milkweeds and sequester toxic cardiac glycosides, advertising their distastefulness with bright warning colours. Many adults feed on rotting fruit, sap, or dung rather than flower nectar. The painted lady is among the most widespread butterflies on Earth, undertaking long-distance migrations across continents.
