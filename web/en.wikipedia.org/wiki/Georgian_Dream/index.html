Georgian Dream

Georgian Dream – Democratic Georgia (Georgian: ქართული ოცნება – დემოკრატიული საქართველო) is a political party in Georgia. Founded in 2012 by billionaire businessman Bidzina Ivanishvili, it has been the ruling party of Georgia since winning the 2012 parliamentary election as the centrepiece of the Georgian Dream coalition, ending the nine-year rule of the United National Movement.

History

Ivanishvili announced his entry into politics in October 2011 and registered the party in April 2012. The Georgian Dream coalition won the October 2012 parliamentary elections — the first democratic transfer of power in Georgia's post-Soviet history — and Ivanishvili became prime minister. He resigned in 2013, succeeded by Irakli Garibashvili, but has remained the party's dominant figure, serving as its honorary chairman.

The party won re-election in 2016, 2020, and 2024, each time amid opposition allegations of electoral manipulation, which the government denied. The 2024 election result was disputed by the pro-Western opposition and President Salome Zourabichvili, who refused to recognise the new parliament.

Policies and controversies

Georgian Dream describes itself as centre-left and socially conservative, and officially supports Georgia's European integration; the country received EU candidate status in December 2023. However, relations with the EU and United States deteriorated sharply in 2024 over the adoption of a "foreign influence" law modelled on Russian legislation, which triggered mass protests, and the government's decision in November 2024 to suspend EU accession talks until 2028, sparking further nationwide demonstrations. Critics accuse the party of democratic backsliding and alignment with Russia; the party denies this and says it seeks to keep Georgia out of the Russo-Ukrainian war.
