Five Ks

The Five Ks (Punjabi: Pañj Kakār) are five articles of faith worn by initiated Sikhs of the Khalsa order. They were established by Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, at the founding of the Khalsa at Anandpur Sahib on Vaisakhi in 1699. Each article's name begins with the letter k in Punjabi.

The five articles

Kesh

Kesh is uncut hair, signifying acceptance of God's will and respect for the natural form. It is usually covered by a turban (dastar) or, for some women, a scarf.

Kangha

The kangha is a small wooden comb worn in the hair. It symbolises cleanliness and order, and is used to keep the kesh neat — a reminder that spiritual discipline includes care of the body.

Kara

The kara is a plain steel or iron bangle worn on the wrist of the dominant hand. Its circular form symbolises the eternity of God and the wearer's bond to the community; its steel stands for strength and for restraint — the hand it encircles should do no wrong.

Kachera

The kachera is a pair of cotton undergarments or breeches, originally practical for a mounted warrior. It symbolises modesty, self-control and readiness.

Kirpan

The kirpan is a curved dagger or small sword worn in a strap across the body. It symbolises a Sikh's duty to protect the weak and defend against injustice, and the qualities of honour and courage. Because it is an article of faith, the kirpan has been the subject of legal exemptions and controversies in schools, courts and airports in several countries.

Significance

Together the Five Ks are an outward, visible identity marking the initiated Sikh's commitment to the Khalsa's code of conduct (rahit). They are generally worn by Amritdhari (initiated) Sikhs, though some non-initiated Sikhs keep some of them, particularly kesh and the kara.
