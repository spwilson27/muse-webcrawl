Puquio

A puquio is an underground aqueduct system built by the Nazca culture of coastal Peru to tap groundwater and carry it to settlements and fields in one of the driest regions on Earth. The term also refers to the spiral-shaped access openings of these systems.

Structure

Puquios consist of horizontal underground galleries that intercept an aquifer and slope gently to surface outlets, with funnel-shaped spiral openings (ojos) that allow access for cleaning and maintenance and may help ventilate and pressurise the flow. Many, such as the Cantalloc aqueducts near Nazca, still supply water today.

Dating debate

The systems are traditionally attributed to the Nazca culture (c. 100 BCE–800 CE), though researchers have debated whether parts were built or modified in later pre-Columbian or colonial times; satellite imagery studies have argued for a pre-Hispanic origin of the network.
