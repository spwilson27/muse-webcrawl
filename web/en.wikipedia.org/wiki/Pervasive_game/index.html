Pervasive game

A pervasive game is a game in which the game world is blended with the physical world, so that gameplay extends out into the real world in time, space, or social relations. The concept draws on the "magic circle" of play described by Johan Huizinga: in pervasive games that boundary is deliberately expanded or blurred.

Characteristics and examples

Researchers such as Markus Montola and the EU-funded IPerG project distinguished spatial expansion (games played in streets and public places), temporal expansion (games interleaved with everyday life), and social expansion (bystanders drawn into play). Early examples include the SMS-based game BotFighters (2001), the alternate reality game The Beast (2001), live-action games such as Killer, and mixed-reality performances by groups like Blast Theory. The genre reached mass audiences with location-based augmented-reality mobile games, above all Ingress (2012) and Pokémon Go (2016).

Issues

Pervasive games raise questions about safety, trespass, and the involvement of non-players who have not consented to be part of a game; several incidents associated with location-based play have prompted legal and design responses.
