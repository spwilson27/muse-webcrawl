Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum

The Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum (Polish: Państwowe Muzeum Auschwitz-Birkenau) is a museum and memorial on the site of the former Nazi German concentration and extermination camps Auschwitz I and Auschwitz II-Birkenau in Oświęcim, Poland.

History

The museum was established by an act of the Polish parliament on 2 July 1947, following efforts by former prisoners to preserve the site. It covers about 191 hectares of the former camp complex. In 1979 the site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Collections and exhibitions

The permanent exhibition, opened in the former prisoner blocks of Auschwitz I in 1955, displays artefacts including victims' belongings, photographs and documents. At Birkenau, the ruins of the gas chambers and crematoria, the railway ramp and preserved barracks form the memorial landscape. National exhibitions are maintained by several countries.

Preservation and visitation

The museum conducts large-scale conservation of the fragile brick and wooden structures and archival research. Visitor numbers reached a record of about 2.3 million in 2019, making it one of the most visited memorial museums in the world.
