NVDA (NonVisual Desktop Access) is a free and open-source screen reader for Microsoft Windows. It allows blind and vision-impaired people to use computers by reading the text on the screen with synthesised speech and by supporting refreshable braille displays. NVDA is developed and maintained by NV Access, a nonprofit organisation based in Australia.

History

Development of NVDA began in 2006, when Michael Curran, himself blind, began writing a screen reader after finding commercial alternatives prohibitively expensive. Together with James Teh he founded NV Access in 2007 to support the project. NVDA was an early proof that a fully featured screen reader could be produced and distributed without charge, and it is funded through grants, donations and contracts.

Features

NVDA is written primarily in Python. It ships with the eSpeak NG speech synthesiser, supports Microsoft's Speech API 5 voices and many third-party synthesisers, and works with a wide range of braille displays. It provides support for popular applications including web browsers, email clients, office suites and Windows system tools, includes a built-in add-on system for extending functionality, and can be run from a portable copy without installation. The interface has been translated into dozens of languages.

Reception

Usage surveys of screen reader users have consistently placed NVDA among the most widely used screen readers worldwide, alongside commercial products such as JAWS. It is particularly common in education and in developing countries because of its zero cost.
