Renfrew

Renfrew (Scottish Gaelic: Rinn Friù) is a town 6 miles (10 km) west of Glasgow in the west central Lowlands of Scotland. It is the historic county town of Renfrewshire and gives its name to the modern council area, although the administrative centre is now nearby Paisley. The town lies on the south bank of the River Clyde, close to its confluence with the River Cart.

History

Renfrew's origins date to the 12th century, when Walter fitz Alan, the first High Steward of Scotland, built a castle there; the settlement became a royal burgh in 1396. The motto of the town is associated with its status as the "Cradle of the Royal Stewarts", since the Stewart (later Stuart) dynasty held lands in the area. Renfrew grew in the 19th century with shipbuilding, engineering and the arrival of the railway. The Renfrew Ferry across the Clyde to Yoker has operated in various forms for centuries and continues as a passenger service.

Present day

The town has a population of roughly 22,000. Modern landmarks include the Braehead shopping centre nearby, the Blythswood retail and business parks, and the Renfrew Bridge, a cable-stayed opening road bridge across the Clyde opened in 2025, linking Renfrew with Clydebank and Yoker. Glasgow Airport lies a short distance to the west at Abbotsinch.
