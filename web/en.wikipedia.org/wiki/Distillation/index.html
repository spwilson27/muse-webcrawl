Distillation

Distillation

Distillation is a method of separating mixtures by their different boiling points: the mixture is boiled, the vapour (richer in the more volatile component) is condensed and collected, leaving the less volatile material behind.

Simple distillation purifies water and separates a solvent from solutes; fractional distillation — with a column providing many successive condensations — separates crude oil into fractions and ethanol from fermented wash. Distillation is among humanity's oldest chemical techniques, used for perfumes, medicines and spirits since antiquity.

[Image: Laboratory distillation apparatus with a Liebig condenser]

Principles

For ideal mixtures, Raoult's law relates vapour composition to liquid composition; close-boiling or azeotropic mixtures (like ethanol–water at about 95.6%) resist complete separation by ordinary distillation, requiring special techniques.

Industrial scale

Oil refineries are forests of fractionating towers; air is liquefied and fractionally distilled for nitrogen, oxygen and argon; desalination plants distil seawater in water-scarce regions.
