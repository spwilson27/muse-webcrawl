LW Theatres

LW Theatres is a British theatre ownership and production company founded by composer Andrew Lloyd Webber. Formerly known as the Really Useful Theatres group, it owns and operates a portfolio of major West End theatres in London, including the London Palladium, the Theatre Royal, Drury Lane, His Majesty's Theatre, the Cambridge Theatre, the Gillian Lynne Theatre, and the Adelphi Theatre (the last in co-ownership).

Venues

The company's flagship venues include the London Palladium, famed for variety and the Royal Variety Performance; the Theatre Royal, Drury Lane, extensively refurbished in a £60 million restoration completed in 2021; His Majesty's Theatre, long-time home of The Phantom of the Opera; the Cambridge Theatre, home of Matilda the Musical; and the Gillian Lynne Theatre, renamed in 2018 for the choreographer Gillian Lynne — the first West End theatre named after a woman who was not royalty.

History

The theatres were originally part of Lloyd Webber's Really Useful Group. In 2005 the venues were spun into Really Useful Theatres, and in 2018 the company rebranded as LW Theatres. In 2022 Lloyd Webber bought out the remaining partners to take full ownership of the group.
