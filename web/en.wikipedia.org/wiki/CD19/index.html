CD19

Cluster of differentiation 19 (CD19), or B-lymphocyte antigen CD19, is a transmembrane protein found on the surface of B cells. In humans it is encoded by the CD19 gene located on chromosome 16. CD19 is a critical co-receptor that modulates B cell activation and signaling through the B cell receptor (BCR).

CD19 is expressed from the early pre-B cell stage through mature B cells, and it is lost as B cells differentiate into plasma cells. Because of this expression pattern, CD19 has become one of the most important biomarkers for identifying B lymphocytes and one of the most valuable targets in immunotherapy for B cell malignancies.

Structure

The mature human CD19 protein consists of 556 amino acids. Its extracellular portion contains two C2-type immunoglobulin-like domains and a number of potential N-linked glycosylation sites. A single transmembrane helix connects the extracellular region to a cytoplasmic tail of about 242 amino acids, which is unusually long for a surface receptor and contains several conserved tyrosine residues that become phosphorylated upon B cell activation.

Function

CD19 forms a complex with CD21 (complement receptor 2), CD81, and CD225 (Leu-13) on the B cell surface. When complement component C3d-tagged antigen binds CD21, the CD19 complex is brought into proximity with the BCR, lowering the threshold for B cell activation by as much as a hundred- to a thousand-fold. The cytoplasmic tail of CD19 recruits signaling molecules including Lyn, PI3-kinase, Vav, and phospholipase C, amplifying signals that drive B cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the CD19 gene cause a form of common variable immunodeficiency (CVID3), in which patients have impaired antibody responses despite normal numbers of B cells. In oncology, CD19 is the target of several approved therapies:

  • CAR T cell therapies such as tisagenlecleucel (Kymriah) and axicabtagene ciloleucel (Yescarta), used in relapsed or refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia and large B-cell lymphoma.
  • Blinatumomab, a bispecific T-cell engager binding CD19 and CD3.
  • Loncastuximab tesirine, an antibody–drug conjugate.
  • Tafasitamab, a monoclonal antibody used with lenalidomide.

Because nearly all B-cell malignancies express CD19, and because it is absent from hematopoietic stem cells and most non-hematopoietic tissues, it remains a near-ideal immunotherapeutic target; B cell aplasia is the main on-target toxicity and is managed with immunoglobulin replacement.
