The Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (French: Société Radio-Canada), branded as CBC/Radio-Canada, is a Canadian federal Crown corporation that serves as the national public broadcaster for both radio and television. It is Canada's oldest existing broadcasting network and its largest news organization. The CBC operates services in English and French, as well as programming in Indigenous languages and services for international audiences.

History

The CBC was created on November 2, 1936, under the Canadian Broadcasting Act, succeeding the Canadian Radio Broadcasting Commission (CRBC, 1932–1936), itself established following the Aird Commission's 1929 recommendation of a publicly funded national broadcasting system. The new corporation was intended to strengthen Canadian culture and national unity and to counteract the growing influence of American radio networks spilling across the border.

CBC television began broadcasting on September 6, 1952, in Montreal (CBFT) and two days later in Toronto (CBLT), making the CBC among the earlier national television services in the world. The corporation built a coast-to-coast network, carried programming by microwave relay and later satellite, and provided service to remote northern communities through the Northern Service. Colour television began in 1966, and the CBC's distinctive exploding-pizza "gem" logo was introduced in 1974.

Services

In English, the CBC operates the CBC Television network, the CBC News Network cable channel, the documentary channel, and radio networks CBC Radio One (news and talk, largely commercial-free) and CBC Music (formerly Radio 2). In French, services include Ici Radio-Canada Télé, Ici RDI, Ici Première and Ici Musique. Radio Canada International, founded in 1945, broadcasts to international audiences. The corporation also runs the streaming service CBC Gem, the French-language platform Ici Tou.tv, and extensive digital news services. Regional stations produce local news and programming across the country.

Funding and governance

The CBC is funded principally by an annual appropriation from Parliament, supplemented by advertising revenue on television and other commercial income; its radio services have largely been advertising-free since the 1970s. The corporation is governed by a board of directors appointed by the federal government, and its president is appointed by the Governor in Council. It operates under the Broadcasting Act, which charges it with providing programming that is predominantly and distinctively Canadian, reflects the country and its regions, and serves both official-language communities. Its funding and mandate are recurrent subjects of political debate in Canada.

News and programming

CBC News is the largest news organization in Canada, with flagship programs including The National on television and The World at Six on radio. Notable CBC programming over the decades includes Hockey Night in Canada (long associated with the network, though production passed to Rogers Media in 2014 while the CBC continues to air games), The Beachcombers, Degrassi, This Hour Has 22 Minutes, Rick Mercer Report, Schitt's Creek, and Kim's Convenience. The CBC is also the Canadian broadcaster most associated with coverage of Olympic Games and national events.
