Ulmus parvifolia

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Ulmaceae
Genus: Ulmus
Species: U. parvifolia Jacq.
Common names: Chinese elm, lacebark elm

Ulmus parvifolia, commonly known as the Chinese elm or lacebark elm, is a species of elm native to eastern Asia, including China, Japan, Korea and Vietnam. It is widely planted worldwide as an ornamental and street tree.

Description

The Chinese elm is a medium-sized, semi-deciduous tree growing to 10–18 metres, with a slender trunk and a rounded, finely branched crown. Its small, glossy, toothed leaves are 2–5 cm long. Distinctively, it flowers and sets seed in autumn rather than spring. The bark of mature trees exfoliates in mottled patches of grey, green, orange and brown, giving rise to the name "lacebark".

Disease resistance and uses

U. parvifolia has a high natural resistance to Dutch elm disease, which made it a popular replacement for lost American and European elms in cities. It tolerates drought, poor soils and air pollution and is also a favoured species for bonsai. Numerous cultivars, including 'Drake' and 'True Green', are grown in North America, Australia and Europe.
