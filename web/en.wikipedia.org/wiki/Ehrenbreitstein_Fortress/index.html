Ehrenbreitstein Fortress

The fortress overlooking the Deutsches Eck at Koblenz

Ehrenbreitstein Fortress (German: Festung Ehrenbreitstein) is a fortress on the eastern bank of the Rhine at Koblenz, Germany, opposite the confluence of the Rhine and the Moselle. Built by Prussia between 1817 and 1828, it is the largest preserved fortress in Europe and forms part of the UNESCO Upper Middle Rhine Valley World Heritage Site.

History

The hilltop was fortified from around 1000 BC and held a castle of the Archbishops of Trier from about the 11th century. French revolutionary troops besieged and destroyed the earlier fortress in 1799–1801. After the Congress of Vienna awarded the Rhineland to Prussia, Ehrenbreitstein was rebuilt as part of the Fortress Koblenz system, one of the largest fortifications in Europe, designed to hold a garrison of some 1,500 soldiers. It never came under attack; after the First World War its works survived because of their historic value, and American and later French forces occupied it after both world wars.

Today

The fortress now houses the Koblenz State Museum (Landesmuseum Koblenz) with exhibitions on regional history, archaeology and wine, plus a youth hostel and restaurants. A cable car built for the 2011 Federal Horticultural Show crosses the Rhine from Koblenz to the fortress plateau, one of the largest aerial tramways in Germany.
