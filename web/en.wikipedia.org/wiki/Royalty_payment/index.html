Royalty payment

Royalty payments flow from the user of an asset to its owner

A royalty payment is a payment made by one party to another that owns a particular asset, for the right to ongoing use of that asset. Royalties are payments for the exploitation of intellectual property such as patents, copyrighted works, trademarks and franchises, or for the extraction of natural resources.

Basis of calculation

Royalty payments are most often calculated as a percentage of the revenue generated by the asset — the "royalty rate" applied to gross or net sales. Alternatives include a fixed fee per unit produced or sold, a share of profits, or lump-sum payments. "Running royalties" scale with output, while minimum annual payments may guarantee the licensor a floor income.

Terms

The licence or franchise agreement defines the property covered, the geographic scope, the term, whether the licence is exclusive, and reporting obligations. Licensors commonly reserve audit rights to verify the sales figures on which payments depend. Advances against royalties are common in publishing and music.

Economic role

Royalty payments let owners monetize assets without selling them and let users access technology, content or brands without buying them outright. They are a major income source for authors, composers, inventors, universities licensing research, and resource-owning governments.
