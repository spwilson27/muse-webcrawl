South Australia

South Australia (abbreviated SA) is a state in the southern central part of Australia. It covers some of the most arid parts of the country, with a total land area of 984,321 square kilometres, making it the fourth-largest state. It shares borders with all other mainland states and the Northern Territory, and is bounded to the south by the Great Australian Bight. The capital and largest city is Adelaide, home to around 78% of the state's population of about 1.8 million people.

History

Aboriginal peoples have inhabited South Australia for tens of thousands of years. The coastline was charted by Matthew Flinders and Nicolas Baudin in 1802. Unlike other Australian colonies, South Australia was founded as a planned free settlement under the "systematic colonisation" theory of Edward Gibbon Wakefield, and was proclaimed a British colony on 28 December 1836 at Glenelg. It became a founding state of the Commonwealth of Australia at Federation in 1901.

Geography and economy

The state's landscape ranges from the arid deserts of the north — including much of the Great Victoria Desert — to the fertile agricultural districts of the Mount Lofty Ranges, the Barossa Valley and the Limestone Coast. The River Murray flows through the state's southeast. South Australia is famed for its wine regions (Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale, Coonawarra), which produce more than half of Australia's wine. Mining (including the giant Olympic Dam copper and uranium mine), defence industries, manufacturing and education are also major sectors, and the state has been a leader in renewable energy generation.
