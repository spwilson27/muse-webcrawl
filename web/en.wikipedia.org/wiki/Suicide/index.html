Suicide

Suicide is the act of intentionally causing one's own death. It is a major global public health issue, with more than 700,000 deaths worldwide each year according to the World Health Organization. Risk factors include mental disorders such as depression, substance use, chronic pain, previous attempts and stressful life events; protective factors include social support, access to care and restrictions on lethal means.

Prevention

Evidence-based prevention includes treating underlying mental illness, crisis intervention and helplines, reducing access to means such as firearms and pesticides, responsible media reporting, and school and community programmes. Many suicides occur impulsively during crises, and most people who survive an attempt do not go on to die by suicide.

Methods and epidemiology

Common methods globally include pesticide ingestion, hanging and firearms, varying by region. Rates differ by country, age and sex, with men dying by suicide more often in most countries. Suicide is among the leading causes of death in young people.

History and views

Attitudes have varied across cultures and religions, from condemnation and criminalisation — largely repealed in modern law — to ritualised forms in some historical contexts. Modern approaches emphasise prevention, compassion and public health. People in distress are encouraged to seek help from local crisis services or healthcare professionals.
