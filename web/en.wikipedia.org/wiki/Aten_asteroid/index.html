Aten asteroid

The Aten asteroids are a dynamical group of asteroids whose orbits bring them into proximity with Earth. By definition, Atens are Earth-crossing asteroids with a semimajor axis smaller than one astronomical unit (AU) — the distance from the Earth to the Sun. The group is named after 2062 Aten, the first of its kind discovered on 7 January 1976 by American astronomer Eleanor Helin.

Definition

An asteroid is classed as an Aten if its semimajor axis is less than 1.0 AU while its aphelion distance exceeds Earth's perihelion distance of 0.983 AU, so that its orbit crosses Earth's. Because their orbits lie mostly inside Earth's, Atens are hard to observe: they are best seen near the Sun in twilight, and many were discovered only by space-based or dedicated twilight surveys.

Notable members

Notable Atens include 2062 Aten, 99942 Apophis (briefly classified an Aten before being reclassified after its orbit was refined), 3753 Cruithne (Earth's quasi-satellite), and 2020 AV2, the first asteroid found entirely within Venus's orbit. As of the early 2020s, more than 2,000 Atens were known, a fraction classified as potentially hazardous asteroids.
