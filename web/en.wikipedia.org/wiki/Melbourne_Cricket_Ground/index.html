Melbourne Cricket Ground

The Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG), known locally as "the 'G", is Australia's largest sports stadium, located in Yarra Park, Melbourne, Victoria. Founded in 1853, it is the home of the Melbourne Cricket Club and the principal venue for Australian rules football and Test cricket in Australia. Its current capacity is 100,024, making it one of the largest stadiums in the world.

History

The MCG hosted the first-ever Test match in 1877, between Australia and England, and the first One Day International in 1971. It was the main stadium of the 1956 Summer Olympics and the 2006 Commonwealth Games. The annual Boxing Day Test, beginning on 26 December, and the AFL Grand Final are its signature events. The Australian Sports Museum is housed within the ground.

Records

The record crowd at the MCG is 121,696, for the 1970 VFL Grand Final between Collingwood and Carlton. The 1956 Olympic opening ceremony drew around 107,700. The stadium's Great Southern Stand (opened 1992) and Northern Stand redevelopment (completed 2006) brought the ground to its modern form, with four light towers added in 1984 enabling day-night matches.
