Penal system in China

The penal system in China comprises the laws, courts, prisons, and forms of punishment administered by the People's Republic of China. Punishments under the Criminal Law include fines, confiscation of property, fixed-term and life imprisonment, suspended sentences, and the death penalty, as well as administrative sanctions imposed without trial.

Structure

Criminal cases are investigated by public security organs and people's procuratorates and tried by people's courts. Principal penalties are supplemented by community correction for minor offenders. China has one of the world's largest prison populations, and prisoners are generally required to work; prison enterprises historically formed part of the system known as laogai ("reform through labor").

Capital punishment and reform

China is widely believed to execute more people than the rest of the world combined, though figures are a state secret. Reforms since 2007 restored Supreme People's Court review of all death sentences, and the number of capital crimes was reduced from 68 to 46. Re-education through labor (laojiao), an administrative detention system, was abolished in 2013.
