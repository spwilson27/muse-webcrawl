Star Trek (franchise)

Created by: Gene Roddenberry
Original work: Star Trek: The Original Series (1966–1969)
Owner: Paramount (CBS Studios / Paramount Television)
Media: Television series, films, novels, comics, video games
Setting: Primarily the 22nd–32nd centuries; the starship Enterprise and the United Federation of Planets

Star Trek is an American science fiction media franchise created by Gene Roddenberry, beginning with the television series Star Trek (now called The Original Series), which debuted on NBC on 8 September 1966. The franchise depicts the adventures of starship crews of Starfleet, the space service of the United Federation of Planets, exploring the galaxy in the centuries to come. It has grown to encompass numerous television series, more than a dozen feature films, and an extensive body of books, comics and games, and is one of the most culturally influential franchises in television history.

Television series

The Original Series followed Captain James T. Kirk, Spock and Dr. Leonard McCoy aboard the USS Enterprise; cancelled after three seasons, it became a phenomenon in syndication. Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987–1994), set a century later under Captain Jean-Luc Picard, was a major syndication success and revived the franchise. Successors included Deep Space Nine (1993–1999), Voyager (1995–2001) and the prequel Enterprise (2001–2005). A streaming era began with Discovery (2017), followed by Picard (2020–2023), the animated Lower Decks and Prodigy, and Strange New Worlds (2022), alongside the earlier animated series of 1973–74.

Films

The film series began with Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979) and continued through The Wrath of Khan (1982) and four further films with the original cast, four with the Next Generation cast (including First Contact, 1996), and three "Kelvin timeline" reboots beginning with Star Trek (2009), directed by J. J. Abrams.

Themes and influence

Roddenberry envisioned a future in which humanity had overcome war, poverty and prejudice; the series used alien encounters as allegory for contemporary issues — civil rights, the Cold War, religion and technology — and featured one of American television's first interracial kisses. Star Trek popularised concepts such as the communicator (an inspiration for the mobile phone), built a devoted fan culture ("Trekkies"), influenced real astronauts and scientists, and was honoured when NASA named its first Space Shuttle Enterprise. Its optimistic, exploratory ethos remains the franchise's signature.
