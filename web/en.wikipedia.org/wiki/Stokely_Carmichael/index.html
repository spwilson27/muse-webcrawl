Stokely Carmichael (29 June 1941 – 15 November 1998), later known as Kwame Ture, was a Trinidadian-American activist who played a major role in the civil rights movement in the United States and the pan-African movement. He was a key leader of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) and later the "Honorary Prime Minister" of the Black Panther Party.

Early life

Born in Port of Spain, Trinidad, Carmichael moved to Harlem, New York, in 1952 to join his parents. He attended the elite Bronx High School of Science and then Howard University, where he became involved in the Freedom Rides of 1961 and was repeatedly arrested and jailed for his activism in the segregated South.

Black Power

Elected chairman of SNCC in 1966, Carmichael popularised the slogan "Black Power" during the Meredith March Against Fear in Mississippi, arguing that Black Americans needed to build independent political and economic power rather than rely solely on integration into existing institutions. His 1967 book Black Power: The Politics of Liberation, co-written with Charles V. Hamilton, laid out this philosophy and introduced the concept of institutional racism to a wide audience.

Later life

Growing increasingly committed to pan-Africanism, Carmichael moved to Guinea in 1969, took the name Kwame Ture in honour of Kwame Nkrumah and Ahmed Sékou Touré, and worked with the All-African People's Revolutionary Party for the rest of his life. He died of prostate cancer in Conakry in 1998.
