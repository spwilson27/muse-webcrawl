Inti


Religion: Inca mythology
Domain: The Sun
Consort: Mama Killa (moon)

Inti is the ancient Inca god of the Sun. He is revered as the national patron of the Inca Empire, and the ruling Sapa Inca was regarded as his living descendant and representative on earth. His wife and sister is Mama Killa, the moon goddess. Inti was worshipped at temples throughout the empire, most famously at the Coricancha in Cusco, whose walls were once covered in gold sheet.

Worship

As the god of the sun — the source of warmth, light, and agricultural fertility — Inti stood near the top of the Inca pantheon, below only the creator Viracocha. Offerings of food, chicha, animals, and precious objects were made to him, and priests predicted agricultural cycles from solar observations made at pillars and towers around Cusco.

Festivals

The most important festival honouring Inti was Inti Raymi, the Festival of the Sun, held at the winter solstice (around 24 June) in Cusco. Suppressed after the Spanish conquest, it was revived in the 20th century and is now celebrated annually at Sacsayhuamán as a major cultural and tourist event.

Legacy

Inti's image appears in modern South American symbolism: the Sun of May derived from Inti features on the flags of Argentina and Uruguay, and Peru's former currency, the inti (1985–1991), was named after him.
