Waze (pronounced "ways") is a GPS navigation software application owned by Google. It works on smartphones and other devices with GPS support and provides turn-by-turn navigation informed by user-submitted travel times and route details. Unlike conventional navigation apps, Waze is built around crowdsourcing: users ("Wazers") report traffic congestion, accidents, hazards, road closures, and police presence in real time, and volunteer map editors maintain the road network.

History

Waze began in Israel in 2006 as FreeMap Israel, a community project founded by Ehud Shabtai to create a free digital map of Israel. The commercial company, founded with Amir Shinar and Uri Levine, relaunched the app as Waze in 2009 and expanded internationally. In June 2013, Google acquired Waze for a reported sum of approximately $1 billion, committing to keep the development team in Israel. The acquisition was reviewed by competition regulators in the US, UK, and Israel.

Features

Waze calculates routes using real-time speeds from its user base and reroutes drivers around congestion. Its gamified points system rewards reporting and map editing. Municipal partners in the Waze for Cities programme (formerly Connected Citizens) exchange closure and incident data with the company. The app has also been used for carpooling (Waze Carpool, discontinued in 2022) and for local advertising.

Criticism

The app has drawn criticism from residents of quiet streets diverted into rat runs, from police organisations objecting to officer-location reporting, and over safety when drivers interact with the app while driving. Waze's routing through neighbourhoods during events and wildfires has occasionally prompted public controversy.
