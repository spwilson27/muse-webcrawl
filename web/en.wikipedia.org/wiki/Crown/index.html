Crown

A crown is a traditional form of head adornment, or hat, worn by monarchs as a symbol of their power and dignity. A crown is often, by extension, a symbol of the monarch's government or items endorsed by it.

Crown

Worn by: Monarchs
Symbol of: Sovereignty, legitimacy

History

Crowns developed from wreaths and diadems of antiquity. Persian, Egyptian and Roman rulers wore distinctive headgear; medieval European crowns became closed, jewelled gold circlets, often with arches and a cross, symbolising divine authority. Famous examples include St Edward's Crown and the Imperial State Crown in Britain, and the Crown of St Stephen in Hungary.

Types

Heraldic crowns show rank; coronets are worn by nobles; mural, naval and astral crowns derive from Roman military awards. 'The Crown' also denotes the state in Commonwealth realms.
