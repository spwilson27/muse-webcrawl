Francis of Assisi (born Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone; 1181/1182 – 3 October 1226) was an Italian Catholic friar, deacon, mystic, and preacher. He founded the men's Order of Friars Minor (the Franciscans), the women's Order of St. Clare, and the Third Order of St. Francis for lay men and women. He is among the most venerated religious figures in Christianity and is the patron saint of animals and the environment.

Early life

Francis was born in Assisi to a prosperous cloth merchant, Pietro di Bernardone, and his French wife, Pica. He lived a carefree youth, served as a soldier in the war against Perugia, and was captured and held prisoner for a year. A series of experiences — including a vision in the ruined chapel of San Damiano, where he heard Christ tell him to "repair my church" — led him to renounce his inheritance, embrace absolute poverty, and devote himself to prayer, preaching, and care of the poor and lepers.

Founding of the orders

Disciples gathered around Francis, and in 1209 Pope Innocent III orally approved their simple rule of life. Clare of Assisi founded the companion women's order in 1212. In 1219 Francis travelled to Egypt during the Fifth Crusade and met Sultan al-Kamil, an encounter later celebrated as an early example of interfaith dialogue. In 1223 he arranged the first live Nativity scene (crèche) at Greccio, and in 1224, while praying on Mount La Verna, he reportedly received the stigmata — the wounds of Christ's Passion.

Death and legacy

Francis died on 3 October 1226 and was canonised less than two years later. His "Canticle of the Sun" is one of the earliest works of Italian literature, and his reverence for nature led Pope John Paul II to proclaim him patron saint of ecology in 1979. Pope Francis took his papal name in honour of the saint in 2013.
