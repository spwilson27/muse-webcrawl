Peter John Joseph Murphy (born 11 July 1957) is an English singer and songwriter, best known as the frontman of the post-punk band Bauhaus, whose 1979 debut single "Bela Lugosi's Dead" is regarded as a founding record of gothic rock, earning Murphy the title "Godfather of Goth".

Bauhaus

Murphy joined Bauhaus in 1978, bringing a gaunt, theatrical stage presence and a deep baritone voice. The band released four studio albums — including In the Flat Field (1980) and Mask (1981) — before splitting in 1983; their cover of David Bowie's "Ziggy Stardust" reached the UK top 20. Bauhaus reunited for tours in 1998 and 2005–2008 and again from 2019, releasing the album Go Away White in 2008.

Solo career

After the short-lived project Dalis Car with Mick Karn, Murphy began a solo career with Should the World Fail to Fall Apart (1986). Deep (1989) produced his biggest US hit, "Cuts You Up", a number one on the Billboard Modern Rock chart. Later albums include Holy Smoke (1992), Dust (2002, influenced by Turkish music — Murphy lived for many years in Turkey), Ninth (2011), and Lion (2014), produced by Youth. His collaboration with Trent Reznor and his championing of younger acts sustained his cult stature; a major Bauhaus reunion tour was cut short in 2022 when Murphy entered rehabilitation.
