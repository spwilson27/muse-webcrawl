Integration by parts

In calculus, and more generally in mathematical analysis, integration by parts or partial integration is a process that finds the integral of a product of functions in terms of the integral of the product of their derivative and antiderivative. It is the integral counterpart of the product rule of differentiation.

Formula

If u = u(x) and v = v(x) are differentiable functions, then ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du. For definite integrals, ∫ab u dv = [uv]ab − ∫ab v du. The mnemonic LIATE suggests an order for choosing u: Logarithmic, Inverse trigonometric, Algebraic, Trigonometric, Exponential.

Applications

The technique is used to integrate products such as x·ex and x·sin x, to prove reduction formulae (for example for powers of trigonometric functions), in deriving the gamma function's recurrence, and in functional analysis, where its discrete analogue is summation by parts and its generalisation appears in the theory of distributions.

History

The method derives from the product rule known to Leibniz and Newton; Brook Taylor stated a version in 1715.
