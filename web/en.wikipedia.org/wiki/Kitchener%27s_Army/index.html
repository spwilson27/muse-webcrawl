Kitchener's Army

Kitchener's Army, also called the New Army, was the force of volunteers raised in the United Kingdom during the First World War at the appeal of Lord Kitchener, the Secretary of State for War. Britain entered the war in August 1914 with a small professional army — the British Expeditionary Force — and Kitchener, anticipating a long conflict, called for mass enlistment instead of relying on the Territorial Force.

The appeal

Kitchener's first call, issued on 7 August 1914, asked for 100,000 men; it was filled within weeks. A famous recruiting poster showing Kitchener pointing with the words "Lord Kitchener Wants You" became an enduring image of the war. Successive "armies" (K1, K2, K3 and beyond) were raised, and by the end of 1915 some two and a half million men had volunteered — the largest volunteer army in British history.

Pals battalions

To encourage recruitment, men were allowed to enlist alongside friends, neighbours and workmates in so-called Pals battalions, such as the Accrington Pals and the Grimsby Chums. Whole communities thus trained and eventually fought together, with devastating consequences for those communities when the battalions suffered heavy casualties.

Training and deployment

The New Army divisions required more than a year of training, hampered by shortages of uniforms, rifles and instructors. They first fought in strength at the Battle of Loos in 1915, and formed the bulk of the attacking force on the first day of the Battle of the Somme, 1 July 1916 — the worst day in British military history, with some 57,000 casualties. Volunteering was replaced by conscription under the Military Service Act of 1916.
