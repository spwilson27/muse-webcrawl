Romance film

[Image: A classic romance film still — a couple embracing in the rain outside a grand railway station, in soft focus.]

Romance films involve romantic love stories recorded in visual media for broadcast in theatres or on television that focus on passion, emotion and the affectionate romantic involvement of the main characters. Typically their journey through dating, courtship or marriage is the central plot, often tested by obstacles such as separation, misunderstanding, class difference or rival suitors.

Subgenres and history

The romantic drama has been a staple since the silent era — from The Sheik (1921) and the weepies of the 1930s and 1940s (Casablanca, Brief Encounter, Now, Voyager) to the glossy melodramas of Douglas Sirk. The romantic comedy, by contrast, plays the same material for laughter: the screwball comedies of the 1930s (It Happened One Night), the Doris Day–Rock Hudson films, and the modern wave launched by When Harry Met Sally... (1989) and Pretty Woman (1990). Other subgenres include the romantic thriller, the historical epic romance (Gone with the Wind, Titanic) and the teen romance.

Conventions and reception

Common devices include the "meet cute", the misunderstanding that drives the couple apart in the second act, the grand gesture and the climactic reunion. Critics have sometimes dismissed romance as formulaic "chick flicks", but the genre contains some of cinema's most honoured and profitable films — Titanic (1997) was for years the highest-grossing film ever made — and its structures underpin love stories in every other genre.
