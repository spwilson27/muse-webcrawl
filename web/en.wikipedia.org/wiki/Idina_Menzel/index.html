Idina Menzel

Idina Kim Menzel (born May 30, 1971) is an American actress and singer. Known for her powerful mezzo-soprano voice and "belt" technique, she rose to prominence originating the role of Maureen Johnson in the Broadway musical Rent (1996) and won the Tony Award for Best Actress in a Musical for originating Elphaba in Wicked (2003). She achieved global fame as the singing voice of Elsa in Disney's Frozen (2013), whose song "Let It Go" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Early life and career

Menzel was born in New York City and raised in Syosset, Long Island. She studied drama at New York University's Tisch School of the Arts and worked as a wedding and bar mitzvah singer before being cast in Jonathan Larson's Rent, which transferred from off-Broadway to Broadway in 1996 and earned her a Tony nomination. She released her first solo album, Still I Can't Be Still, in 1998, and appeared in productions including The Wild Party and Aida.

Wicked and Frozen

In 2003 Menzel originated the role of Elphaba, the green-skinned Wicked Witch of the West, in Stephen Schwartz's Wicked, winning the 2004 Tony Award; she reprised the role in the original London production in 2006. In 2013 she voiced Queen Elsa in Disney's animated film Frozen, performing "Let It Go," which became a worldwide hit; she returned for Frozen II (2019), whose song "Into the Unknown" was Oscar-nominated. At the 2014 Academy Awards, presenter John Travolta famously mispronounced her name as "Adele Dazeem," an error widely covered in the press.

Other work

Menzel's screen roles include Nancy Tremaine in Disney's Enchanted (2007) and its sequel Disenchanted (2022), Shelby Corcoran on Glee, and Dinah Ratner in Uncut Gems (2019). She starred in the Broadway musical If/Then (2014), earning another Tony nomination, and has released several further albums, including Holiday Wishes (2014) and Christmas: A Season of Love (2019). She co-founded the A BroaderWay Foundation, which brings arts programs to young girls from underserved communities.
