Supreme Court of India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Supreme Court of India is the supreme judicial authority of India and the highest court of the Republic, established in 1950 under the Constitution. With a sanctioned strength of 34 judges it sits atop one of the world's largest judiciaries, exercising original jurisdiction (including fundamental-rights petitions under Article 32, which Ambedkar called the Constitution's "heart and soul"), appellate jurisdiction over the High Courts, and advisory jurisdiction on presidential references.

Power and controversies

The Court articulated the basic structure doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973): Parliament may amend the Constitution but not destroy its basic structure — the strongest judicial-review doctrine in any democracy, born of the Parliament–Court struggle that peaked in the Emergency (1975–77). Its public interest litigation (from the late 1970s) relaxed standing and turned the Court into a continuing administrator of prisons, pollution, elections and policy — celebrated as rights-expanding, criticised as governance by judiciary. Appointments run through the judges' own "collegium" system (created by the Court in 1993; Parliament's replacement NJAC was struck down in 2015 as violating judicial independence), the system's most contested feature. Pendency — tens of thousands of cases before the Court, tens of millions across the system — is its chronic crisis.
