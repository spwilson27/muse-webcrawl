Robert Torrens FRS (1780 – 27 May 1864) was a British army officer, political economist and politician. A Royal Marines officer who was wounded defending Anholt island in the Napoleonic Wars, he became a prolific writer on economics and a Member of Parliament.

Economics

In his Essay on the External Corn Trade (1815) Torrens set out, independently of and around the same time as David Ricardo, the principle of comparative advantage in international trade. He was a leading figure of the Currency School, arguing that banknote issue should be tied to gold reserves — ideas embodied in the Bank Charter Act 1844 — and he debated Ricardo on value theory and the "profits of trade" doctrine.

Colonisation

Torrens was a prime mover of systematic colonisation in South Australia: as chairman of the colonisation commissioners he oversaw the founding of the colony in the 1830s, and the city of Adelaide's River Torrens is named for him. His son Robert Richard Torrens became Premier of South Australia and devised the Torrens title system of land registration.
