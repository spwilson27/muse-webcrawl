Lyrical Ballads, with a Few Other Poems is a collection of poems by William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge, first published in 1798. It is generally considered to mark the beginning of the English Romantic movement in literature.

Contents

The 1798 edition opened with Coleridge's "The Rime of the Ancient Mariner" and closed with Wordsworth's "Lines written a few miles above Tintern Abbey". Wordsworth contributed most of the remaining poems, many treating rural and common life — "The Idiot Boy", "Simon Lee", "We Are Seven", and "Lines written in early spring" among them — while Coleridge added a handful of poems including "The Nightingale". The poets had planned the volume during their walks in the Quantock Hills in Somerset: Coleridge was to treat supernatural subjects so as to procure willing suspension of disbelief, Wordsworth to give the charm of novelty to everyday things.

The Preface

For the expanded two-volume edition of 1800, Wordsworth added the famous Preface (further enlarged in 1802), a manifesto defining poetry as "the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings" recollected in tranquillity, and advocating the "real language of men" over the artificial poetic diction of the eighteenth century. The Preface became a foundational document of Romantic literary theory, although Coleridge later publicly questioned parts of its argument in his Biographia Literaria (1817).
