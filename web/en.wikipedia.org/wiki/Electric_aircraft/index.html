Electric aircraft

An electric aircraft is an aircraft powered by one or more electric motors, usually via rechargeable batteries, solar cells, or fuel cells. Electric propulsion offers low operating costs, reduced noise and zero in-flight emissions, but the low energy density of batteries compared with aviation fuel severely limits range and payload.

History

Electric model aircraft have existed since the 1970s. Manned electric flight began with tethered and battery-powered experiments, and the solar-powered Solar Challenger crossed the English Channel in 1981. The Solar Impulse 2 completed a solar-powered circumnavigation in 2015–2016. In 2020 the Pipistrel Velis Electro became the first fully electric aircraft to receive a type certificate from EASA, for pilot training.

Design challenges

Lithium-ion batteries store roughly 250 Wh/kg, against about 12,000 Wh/kg for jet fuel, though electric motors are far more efficient. Designers compensate with efficient airframes, distributed propulsion, and short-haul roles such as training, air taxi services using eVTOL designs, and regional flight. Hybrid-electric designs combine electric motors with combustion or turbine generators.

Applications

Current and proposed applications include flight training, unmanned aerial vehicles, urban air mobility, and short regional routes. Harbour Air has flown a retrofitted de Havilland Beaver on battery power, and several startups and major manufacturers are developing electric regional aircraft for entry into service in the late 2020s.
