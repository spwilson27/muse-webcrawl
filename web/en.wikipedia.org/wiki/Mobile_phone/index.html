Mobile phone

A mobile phone (also cellular phone or cell phone) is a portable telephone that can make and receive calls over a radio frequency link while the user is moving within a telephone service area, as opposed to a fixed-location phone. The radio link connects to the switching systems of a mobile phone operator, providing access to the public switched telephone network (PSTN). Modern mobile telephony relies on a cellular network architecture, in which the service area is divided into cells, each served by a base station.

History

Mobile radio telephony existed in limited forms from the mid-20th century, including car phones. The modern era began on 3 April 1973, when Motorola researcher Martin Cooper made the first handheld mobile phone call to his rival at Bell Labs. The first commercial handheld phone, the Motorola DynaTAC 8000X, went on sale in 1983 for nearly $4,000. First-generation (1G) analogue networks gave way in the 1990s to digital 2G systems — GSM in Europe and much of the world, CDMA in parts of North America and Asia — which added text messaging (SMS) and greater capacity. The Nokia-led feature-phone era made mobile phones mass consumer objects.

Third-generation (3G) networks added useful mobile data. The decisive change came in 2007 with Apple's iPhone and the subsequent spread of Android smartphones, which turned the phone into a general-purpose touchscreen computer with app stores, cameras, GPS and mobile broadband. 4G LTE and then 5G networks progressively raised data speeds, and by the 2010s there were more mobile subscriptions than people on Earth.

Features

Beyond voice, mobile phones support SMS and internet-based messaging, email, web browsing, photography and video, navigation, mobile payments and a vast ecosystem of applications. Modern smartphones integrate multiple radios (cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, UWB), sensors (accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers, barometers, fingerprint and face recognition) and satellite positioning.

Impact

Mobile phones have transformed communication, commerce and media, especially in developing countries where they leapfrogged fixed-line infrastructure and enabled mobile money services such as M-Pesa. They are also associated with debates about attention and mental health, distracted driving, privacy and electronic waste.
