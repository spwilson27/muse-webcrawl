Principality of Achaea

Existed: 1205–1432
Capital: Andravida, later Mystras region
Common language: French, Greek
First prince: William of Champlitte
Last prince: Centurione II Zaccaria

The Principality of Achaea was one of the three vassal states of the Latin Empire created after the Fourth Crusade's conquest of the Byzantine Empire in 1204. Founded in 1205 by William of Champlitte, it covered most of the Peloponnese (the Morea) in southern Greece and became the strongest of the Frankish states in Greece.

History

Under Geoffrey I and Geoffrey II of Villehardouin, and especially Prince William II (r. 1246–1278), Achaea reached its height, with a brilliant French-speaking feudal court described in the Chronicle of the Morea. William's defeat and capture by the Byzantines at the Battle of Pelagonia (1259) forced him to cede the southeastern Peloponnese, enabling the Byzantine recovery centred on Mystras.

Decline

From the late 13th century the principality passed by inheritance and marriage through Angevin, Savoyard and other claimants, often absentee rulers, while the Catalan Company, Navarrese mercenaries and the advancing Despotate of the Morea ate away at its territory. The last prince, Centurione II Zaccaria, lost his remaining lands to the Byzantine despot Thomas Palaiologos in 1430–1432, ending the principality; Centurione died in 1432.
