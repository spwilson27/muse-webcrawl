Frank Hornby

Frank Hornby (15 May 1863 – 21 September 1936) was an English inventor, businessman and politician. He is best known for inventing Meccano, the construction toy system, and for founding the company that produced Hornby Trains and Dinky Toys.

Early life

Hornby was born in Liverpool, the son of a provisions merchant. He left school at 16 and worked as a clerk and later as a bookkeeper in his father's business and then for a meat importing firm. In his spare time he experimented in a small workshop at home.

Meccano

In 1901 Hornby patented "improvements in toy or educational devices for children" — a system of perforated metal strips, plates, wheels, axles and nuts and bolts that could be assembled and taken apart to build models of machines. Marketed first as "Mechanics Made Easy" and from 1907 as Meccano, the system became a worldwide success and made Hornby a wealthy man.

Meccano Ltd and other products

Hornby founded Meccano Ltd in Liverpool, with a factory on Binns Road that became one of the largest toy factories in Britain. In 1920 the company launched clockwork O gauge model trains under the Hornby name, beginning the Hornby Trains range. The Meccano Magazine, launched in 1916, was edited by Hornby for many years. In 1934 the company introduced die-cast model vehicles as Dinky Toys.

Politics and later life

Hornby served as a Liverpool city councillor and, from 1931 to 1935, as Conservative Member of Parliament for the Everton division of Liverpool. He died in Maghull, Lancashire, in 1936, leaving an estate valued at over £300,000.

Legacy

Hornby's toys shaped the childhoods of generations of engineers and hobbyists. The Hornby and Dinky brands survive today, and Meccano remains in production. Hornby Hobbies, the successor company, continues to use his name.
