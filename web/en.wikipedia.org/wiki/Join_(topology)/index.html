Join (topology)

The join of two points is an interval; iterated joins give spheres
FieldTopology, algebraic topology
NotationA * B

In topology, a branch of mathematics, the join of two topological spaces A and B, often denoted A * B, is the space formed by taking the disjoint union of the two spaces and attaching line segments joining every point in A to every point in B.

Definition

Formally, the join A * B is the quotient of A × B × [0,1] in which the subspace A × B × {0} is collapsed onto A and the subspace A × B × {1} is collapsed onto B. A point of the join can be written as a formal convex combination t·a + (1 − t)·b for a in A, b in B, and t in [0,1].

Examples

The join of two points is an interval. The join of a space with a point is its cone, and the join of a space with two points is its suspension. Notably, the join of two spheres S^m and S^n is the sphere S^(m+n+1). Joins play a role in the theory of homotopy groups and in configuration spaces.
