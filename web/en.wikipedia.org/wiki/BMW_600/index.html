The BMW 600 is a four-seater microcar produced by the German manufacturer BMW from mid-1957 to November 1959. It was developed from the BMW Isetta bubble car, which BMW built under licence from the Italian firm Iso, but was stretched and substantially re-engineered to carry four people.

Design

Like the Isetta, the 600 is entered through a single front-opening door to which the steering column is attached, swinging out with it. Uniquely, it added a conventional side door on the right for access to the rear bench seat. The car was powered by the 582 cc air-cooled flat-twin motorcycle engine from the BMW R67, mounted at the rear and driving the rear wheels through a four-speed gearbox; the rear track is notably narrower than the front. Top speed was about 100 km/h (62 mph).

Sales history

The 600 arrived as West German buyers were graduating from microcars to full-size economy cars such as the Volkswagen Beetle, and at its price — close to that of a Beetle — it sold poorly. Only 34,813 were built before production ended in 1959. The failure contributed to BMW's severe financial crisis of that year, although its successor, the conventionally styled BMW 700, sold well enough to help rescue the company.
