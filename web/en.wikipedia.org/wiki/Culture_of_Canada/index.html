Culture of Canada

The culture of Canada embodies the artistic, culinary, literary, humour, musical, political and social elements that are representative of Canada and Canadians. Throughout Canada's history, its culture has been influenced firstly by its Indigenous peoples, and later by European — particularly British and French — traditions, and by its proximity to and migration from the United States. Over time, elements of the cultures of its immigrant populations have combined to form a Canadian cultural mosaic.

Foundations

Canada's culture is often characterized as a "mosaic" — in contrast to the American "melting pot" — reflecting official policies of bilingualism and multiculturalism adopted since the 1970s. Regional identities remain strong, including the distinct Francophone society of Quebec, the Atlantic provinces' maritime traditions, the Prairie provinces, British Columbia, and the Arctic territories where Inuit and First Nations cultures are prominent.

Symbols and identity

The maple leaf has served as a national symbol since the 18th century and appears on the national flag adopted in 1965. Other emblems include the beaver, the Canadian horse, and the national anthem "O Canada". Canadian identity is frequently discussed in terms of distinguishing features from the United States — universal public healthcare, official multiculturalism, and parliamentary government — as well as shared North American popular culture.

Arts, media and sport

Canada has produced internationally known authors such as Margaret Atwood, Alice Munro and Lucy Maud Montgomery, musicians including Leonard Cohen, Joni Mitchell, Neil Young and Drake, and a strong film and television industry centred in Toronto and Vancouver. Public broadcasting is provided by the CBC/Radio-Canada. Ice hockey is the dominant spectator sport and a central part of the national culture; lacrosse, of Indigenous origin, is the official summer sport, and Canadian football and basketball also draw large audiences.

Cuisine and holidays

Canadian cuisine varies by region and draws on Indigenous, French, British and immigrant traditions; widely recognized dishes include poutine, tourtière, butter tarts, Nanaimo bars and maple syrup products. Major public holidays include Canada Day (July 1), Thanksgiving (second Monday in October), Remembrance Day, and Victoria Day.
