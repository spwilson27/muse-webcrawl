University of Science and Technology of China

The University of Science and Technology of China (USTC; Chinese: 中国科学技术大学) is a public research university in Hefei, Anhui, China. Founded in 1958 in Beijing by the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), it is administered by the Academy and is a member of the elite C9 League of Chinese universities. USTC is regarded as one of China's most selective and research-intensive institutions, especially in the natural sciences and engineering.

History

USTC was founded in Beijing in 1958 to train scientists for China's strategic programs, with institute directors of the Chinese Academy of Sciences serving as department chairs — a model dubbed "the academy runs the university." During the Cultural Revolution the university was relocated to Hefei, Anhui, in 1970, losing much of its faculty and equipment, yet it rebuilt to become a national leader. In 1978 it created the first "Class for the Gifted Young" in China, educating exceptionally talented teenagers; its graduates include prominent scientists and entrepreneurs.

Research

The university hosts major national facilities and laboratories, including the National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory (home to the Hefei Light Source) and research groups central to China's quantum information program. USTC scientists led the development of the Micius satellite, which in 2016 achieved the first satellite-to-ground quantum key distribution, and of the Jiuzhang photonic quantum computer. The university consistently places at or near the top of the Nature Index among Chinese institutions relative to its size.

Academics

USTC deliberately maintains a small undergraduate intake of about 1,800 students per year, emphasizing fundamentals in mathematics, physics, chemistry, life sciences, and engineering. It is a member of the C9 League and of the Double First-Class initiative.
