Ravi Shankar (1920–2012) was an Indian sitar virtuoso and composer who became the best-known ambassador of Hindustani classical music in the West. A disciple of Allauddin Khan, he combined deep traditional training with an extraordinary gift for cross-cultural collaboration.

Career

Shankar toured Europe and America from the 1950s, performed at the Monterey Pop Festival and Woodstock, and famously taught Beatles guitarist George Harrison, whose use of the sitar in "Norwegian Wood" sparked a Western raga-rock vogue. He collaborated with violinist Yehudi Menuhin and composer Philip Glass, scored films including Satyajit Ray's Apu Trilogy and Gandhi, and organised the 1971 Concert for Bangladesh. He served in India's upper house of parliament and received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour, in 1999.

Family

His daughters are the singer Norah Jones and the sitarist Anoushka Shankar, who continues to perform his compositions.
