Iðunn

Iðunn, keeper of the apples that keep the gods young.

In Norse mythology, Iðunn is a goddess associated with apples and youth. She is attested in the Poetic Edda, compiled in the 13th century from earlier traditional sources, and in the Prose Edda, written in the 13th century by Snorri Sturluson. Iðunn is the wife of the skaldic god Bragi and the keeper of the golden apples that grant the gods eternal youth.

The abduction of Iðunn

The best-known myth about Iðunn is told in the Skáldskaparmál section of the Prose Edda and in the poem Haustlöng. The jötunn Þjazi, in eagle form, compels the god Loki to lure Iðunn out of Asgard with the promise of finer apples, and Þjazi carries her off to his home, Þrymheimr. Without her apples the gods grow old and grey, and they threaten Loki, who borrows Freyja's falcon-feather cloak, flies to Þrymheimr, turns Iðunn into a nut, and carries her home in his claws. Þjazi pursues in eagle form but is killed when the gods set fire to his feathers at Asgard's walls.

Other attestations

In the eddic poem Lokasenna, Loki insults Iðunn during the gods' flyting, accusing her of embracing her brother's slayer. Iðunn's name is often interpreted as meaning "ever young" or "rejuvenating one," and she has been compared to other Indo-European figures associated with life-giving fruit. Her apples are sometimes linked with the apples of the Hesperides in Greek myth.
