Lop Nur or Lop Nor (from Mongolian: ᠯᠣᠪ ᠨᠠᠭᠤᠷ, "Lop Lake") is a former salt lake in the eastern Tarim Basin, in Ruoqiang County, Xinjiang, China. Once one of the largest lakes in China, it dried up during the 20th century as the waters of the Tarim River and its tributaries were diverted for irrigation. The dried lake bed, with its distinctive yardang formations visible from space as a giant "ear", is now an important source of potash and the site of China's nuclear weapons test base.

History

Lop Nur was the terminus of the Tarim and Konqi rivers and supported the ancient kingdom of Loulan (Kroraina) on its northwestern shore, an important stopping point on the northern Silk Road. The lake was called a "wandering lake" because changes in the course of the Tarim River shifted its position over the centuries — a puzzle investigated by the Swedish explorer Sven Hedin, who argued the lake migrated between northern and southern basins over a roughly 1,600-year cycle.

Explorers including Nikolai Przhevalsky, Sven Hedin, and Aurel Stein visited the region; Stein excavated ruins and documents at Loulan in the early 20th century. The lake shrank rapidly after the 1950s as dams and irrigation schemes upstream consumed the Tarim's flow, and it had largely dried by the 1970s, contributing to the death of the surrounding poplar forests and the abandonment of settlements.

Nuclear testing

China established the Lop Nur nuclear test base in 1959–1960. China's first atomic bomb (Project 596) was detonated there on 16 October 1964, and its first hydrogen bomb test followed on 17 June 1967. A total of 45 nuclear tests were conducted at the site — 23 atmospheric and 22 underground — before China signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty in 1996.

Present day

The lake bed is rich in potash, and large-scale mining now operates there. The surrounding Lop Nur Wild Camel National Nature Reserve protects one of the last populations of wild Bactrian camels. In 1980 the Chinese scientist Peng Jiamu disappeared in the desert while searching for water for his expedition, one of China's most famous unsolved mysteries.
