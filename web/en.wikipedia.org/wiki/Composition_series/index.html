Composition series

In abstract algebra, a composition series provides a way to break up an algebraic structure, such as a group or a module, into simple pieces. The need for considering composition series in the context of modules arises from the fact that many naturally occurring modules are not semisimple, hence cannot be decomposed into a direct sum of simple modules. A composition series of a module M is a finite increasing filtration of M by submodules such that the successive quotients are simple modules. The Jordan–Hölder theorem states that two composition series of the same module have the same length and the same list of composition factors, up to permutation and isomorphism.

For groups

If G is a group, a composition series of G is a finite sequence of subgroups

{1} = H0 ⊂ H1 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ Hn = G

such that each Hi is a proper normal subgroup of Hi+1 and each quotient group Hi+1/Hi is simple. These quotients are called the composition factors of the series. By the Jordan–Hölder theorem for groups, any two composition series of a finite group have the same length and the same composition factors up to order. A group has a composition series if and only if it is finite; the simple composition factors are fundamental invariants of the group.

Examples

  • The cyclic group C12 has composition series with factors C2, C2, C3, reflecting the prime factorization 12 = 2 · 2 · 3.
  • An abelian group has a composition series if and only if it is finite, and then each composition factor is cyclic of prime order.
  • The symmetric group S4 has composition factors C2, C3, C2, C2 (including the alternating group A4 and the Klein four-group in its chain).

For modules

A composition series for a module M is a finite chain of submodules 0 = J0 ⊂ J1 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ Jn = M with each quotient Jk+1/Jk simple. A module has a composition series if and only if it is both Noetherian and Artinian; the common length of all its composition series is called the length of the module. Composition series exist in particular for modules of finite length over Artinian rings, and over any ring for finite-dimensional vector spaces (where every composition factor is one-dimensional).
