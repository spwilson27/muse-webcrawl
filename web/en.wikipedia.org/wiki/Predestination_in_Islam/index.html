Predestination in Islam

Predestination in Islam, known as al-qada wa'l-qadar (divine decree and predestination), is the doctrine that Allah has ordained all things — that nothing happens outside God's knowledge and will. Belief in divine decree is traditionally counted among the six articles of faith in Sunni Islam, based on the Hadith of Gabriel, while the relationship between God's decree and human free will has been one of the most debated questions in Islamic theology (kalam).

Scriptural basis

The Qur'an contains verses affirming God's complete sovereignty — "We created all things in due measure (qadar)" (54:49) — alongside verses emphasising human responsibility and accountability for deeds. Hadith literature reports the Prophet Muhammad teaching that the Pen has written all that will occur, and that a person's ultimate fate is recorded before birth, while also instructing believers to act and strive rather than resign themselves to fate.

Theological schools

  • The Jabriyya held a deterministic position: humans are compelled in their acts, which are created entirely by God.
  • The Qadariyya and later the Mu'tazila affirmed human free will and moral responsibility, arguing that God does not will evil or unbelief.
  • Ash'ari theology developed the doctrine of kasb ("acquisition"): God creates human acts, but humans "acquire" them and so bear responsibility.
  • Maturidi theology, dominant among Hanafis, took a similar mediating position, granting human choice a real role within God's creative will.
  • Shia theology classically describes the matter as "a position between the two positions" (amr bayn al-amrayn) — neither compulsion nor absolute delegation.

History

Debate over qadar began in the Umayyad period and carried political weight: critics charged the caliphs with using predestination to excuse tyranny, while figures such as Ma'bad al-Juhani and Ghaylan al-Dimashqi were executed partly over the issue. The question shaped the great kalam controversies of the ninth and tenth centuries and remains a live topic in Muslim preaching and philosophy.
