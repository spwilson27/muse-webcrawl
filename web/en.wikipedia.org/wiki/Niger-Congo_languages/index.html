Niger-Congo (also written Niger–Congo) is the dominant language family of sub-Saharan Africa, encompassing around 1,500 languages — among them Yoruba, Igbo, Fula, Akan and the Bantu languages such as Swahili and Zulu.

Niger-Congo languages

The Niger-Congo languages form the largest language family of Africa, whether measured by the number of languages (roughly 1,500), the number of speakers (over 700 million) or the geographic area covered, from Senegal in the west to Kenya in the east and South Africa in the south.

Classification

The classification derives from the work of Diedrich Westermann and, above all, Joseph Greenberg, whose The Languages of Africa (1963) grouped the family into branches including West Atlantic, Mande, Gur, Kwa, Benue-Congo and Adamawa-Eastern. Later scholarship (for instance by John Bendor-Samuel and the contributors to The Niger-Congo Languages, 1989) refined this scheme, and the large "Atlantic-Congo" core — excluding the more divergent Mande, Dogon, Ijoid and Kordofanian groups — is the part of the family whose unity is most securely established by comparative evidence, especially the shared noun-class system.

Features and major languages

Typical features include noun classes with full concord, verb extensions (causative, applicative, reciprocal suffixes), and lexical and grammatical tone. The Bantu subgroup alone accounts for about 500 languages and hundreds of millions of speakers, the result of a prehistoric expansion associated with farming and ironworking. In West Africa, the family's biggest languages — Yoruba, Igbo, Fula, Akan, Wolof, Ewe and Bambara — are spoken across Nigeria, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Mali and neighbouring states.

Research issues

Because so many of its languages are under-documented and the time depth since the proto-language is great, the reconstruction of Proto-Niger-Congo remains incomplete, and specialists continue to debate the family's outer limits — some proposing an even larger "Niger-Kordofanian" phylum, others treating Niger-Congo as a linkage of several families.
