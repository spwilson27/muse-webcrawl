Willy Russell

Born: 23 August 1947, Liverpool, England
Occupation: Playwright, novelist, composer
Known for: Blood Brothers; Educating Rita; Shirley Valentine

William "Willy" Russell (born 23 August 1947) is an English dramatist, lyricist, and composer from Liverpool, best known for the plays Educating Rita and Shirley Valentine and the musical Blood Brothers, one of the longest-running productions in British theatre history.

Early life

Russell grew up in a working-class family in Whiston and Kirkby near Liverpool, left school at 15, and worked as a hairdresser before training as a teacher at St Katharine's College. His experiences in education inform much of his writing.

Career

His first play, Keep Your Eyes Down (1971), was staged while he was a student. He wrote the Beatles-themed musical John, Paul, George, Ringo... and Bert (1974), then achieved major success with Educating Rita (1980), about a hairdresser pursuing an Open University education, which he adapted into the acclaimed 1983 film starring Julie Walters and Michael Caine. Blood Brothers, first produced in 1982 and revised for the West End in 1988, tells of twins separated at birth; Shirley Valentine (1986) also became a successful film.

Other work

Russell wrote the television series Our Day Out and the novel The Wrong Boy (2000). His work is marked by humour, strong female characters, and an engagement with class and education in Liverpool life.
