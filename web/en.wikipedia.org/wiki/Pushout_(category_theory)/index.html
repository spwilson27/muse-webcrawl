Pushout (category theory)

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, a pushout (also called a fibered coproduct) is the colimit of a diagram consisting of two morphisms f : Z → X and g : Z → Y with a common domain. The pushout consists of an object P and two morphisms X → P and Y → P that complete the diagram, and it is universal among such completions: any other object completing the square factors uniquely through P.

Definition

Formally, the pushout of the diagram X ← Z → Y is an object P together with morphisms i1 : X → P and i2 : Y → P such that i1 ∘ f = i2 ∘ g, and for any other such pair (Q, j1, j2) there exists a unique morphism u : P → Q making the resulting diagrams commute.

Examples

In the category of sets, the pushout is the disjoint union of X and Y with the images of elements of Z identified — the "gluing" of two sets along a common subset. In topology, pushouts describe attaching spaces along subspaces; the adjunction space and the van Kampen theorem's analysis of fundamental groups are built on pushouts. In the category of groups, the pushout is the free product with amalgamation.

Duality

The pushout is the categorical dual of the pullback: reversing all arrows in the definition converts one notion into the other.
