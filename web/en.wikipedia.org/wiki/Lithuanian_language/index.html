Lithuanian language

Lithuanian (lietuvių kalba) is an Eastern Baltic language belonging to the Indo-European family. It is the official language of Lithuania and one of the official languages of the European Union, spoken by roughly 3 million people. Together with Latvian it is one of only two surviving Baltic languages.

History

Lithuanian is noted among linguists for its conservatism: it retains many features of Proto-Indo-European lost in other languages, including a rich system of pitch accent and archaic forms comparable to Sanskrit and ancient Greek. The oldest surviving Lithuanian text is a handwritten prayer from about 1503–1525; the first printed book, Martynas Mažvydas's Catechism, appeared in 1547 in Königsberg. From 1864 to 1904 the Russian Empire banned Lithuanian publications in the Latin alphabet (the Lithuanian press ban); books printed abroad were smuggled into the country by knygnešiai (book smugglers).

Dialects and standard language

Lithuanian has two main dialect groups: Aukštaitian (Highland) and Samogitian (Lowland, Žemaitian), each with sub-dialects. The modern standard language is based mainly on the West Aukštaitian dialect and was codified in the early 20th century, notably through the work of Jonas Jablonskis.

Grammar

Lithuanian is a highly inflected language. Nouns decline in seven cases (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, locative and vocative) and two numbers, with relics of the dual. There are three noun genders in pronouns (masculine, feminine, neuter) and two in nouns. Verbs conjugate for person, number, tense (present, past, past iterative, future), mood (indicative, conditional, imperative) and numerous participles. Word order is relatively free. Stress is free and mobile, with two intonations (acute and circumflex) in the standard language.

Writing system

Lithuanian uses a 32-letter Latin-based alphabet with diacritics: ą, č, ę, ė, į, š, ų, ū, ž. Letters q, w and x appear only in foreign names.
