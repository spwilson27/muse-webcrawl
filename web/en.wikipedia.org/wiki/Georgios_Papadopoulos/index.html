Georgios Papadopoulos, leader of the Greek military junta.

Georgios Papadopoulos
Born: 5 May 1919, Elaiochori, Greece
Died: 27 June 1999, Athens, Greece
President of Greece: 1973 (under the junta)

Georgios Papadopoulos (5 May 1919 – 27 June 1999) was a Greek army officer and dictator, the leader of the military coup d'état of 21 April 1967 that established the "Regime of the Colonels", the military junta that ruled Greece until 1974. As prime minister (1967–1973) and briefly as President of the Republic in 1973, he presided over an authoritarian regime marked by censorship, political imprisonment, and torture of opponents, before being overthrown by his hardline colleague Dimitrios Ioannidis.

Early life and career

Papadopoulos was born in the village of Elaiochori in Achaea. He graduated from the Hellenic Military Academy in 1940, fought in the Greco-Italian War and against the Axis occupation, and later served in anti-communist operations during the Greek Civil War. He rose through the artillery and intelligence services, becoming an officer of the KYP intelligence agency and a key conspirator in the IDEA officers' network that plotted against parliamentary government.

Rule

After the 1967 coup, Papadopoulos became the dominant figure of the regime, taking the premiership in December 1967 after King Constantine II's failed counter-coup. He combined repression—through courts-martial, exile to island camps, and the military police (ESA)—with promises of national "rebirth" and gradual liberalisation. In 1973, amid the Athens Polytechnic uprising and moves to civilianise the regime (the Markezinis experiment), he abolished the monarchy and declared himself President. The Polytechnic uprising of November 1973 was crushed by tanks, and days later Ioannidis ousted him in a second coup.

Trial and death

After the junta collapsed in 1974 following the Cyprus crisis, Papadopoulos was tried in 1975 in the Greek Junta Trials, convicted of high treason and insurrection, and sentenced to death; the sentence was commuted to life imprisonment by the Karamanlis government. He remained in Korydallos Prison, refusing to seek amnesty, and died of cancer in Athens in 1999.
