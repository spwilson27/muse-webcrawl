Moulding (also spelled molding in American English) is a strip of material with a shaped profile, used to cover transitions between surfaces or for decoration in architecture and interior design. Mouldings are traditionally made of wood or plaster, but modern versions are also produced in plastic, foam, and medium-density fibreboard (MDF).

Decorative mouldings serve both a practical purpose — concealing joints and gaps between walls, ceilings, floors, and openings — and an aesthetic one, articulating surfaces with patterns of light and shadow.

Common profiles

Classical architecture codified a repertoire of moulding profiles, including the cyma recta and cyma reversa (S-curves), the ovolo (convex quarter-round), the cavetto (concave quarter-round), the torus (half-round), the scotia (concave curve), and the flat fillet. Interior trim mouldings include crown moulding at the ceiling line, baseboards (skirting boards) at the floor, chair rails, picture rails, and architraves around doors and windows.

History

Mouldings were a central element of Greek and Roman classical architecture, where stone profiles structured temples and public buildings. Renaissance architects revived and systematised the classical profiles, and they remained standard in European building through the neoclassical period. Machine production in the nineteenth century made elaborate wood mouldings cheap and ubiquitous in domestic interiors.
