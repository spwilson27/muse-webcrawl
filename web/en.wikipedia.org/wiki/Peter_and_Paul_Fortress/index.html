Peter and Paul Fortress

The Peter and Paul Fortress (Russian: Петропавловская крепость, Petropavlovskaya krepost) is the original citadel of Saint Petersburg, Russia, founded by Peter the Great in 1703 and built to Domenico Trezzini's designs between 1706 and 1740. It stands on Zayachy (Hare) Island in the Neva River and contains the Peter and Paul Cathedral, where almost all Russian emperors and empresses from Peter the Great to Alexander III are buried.

History

Peter the Great laid out the fortress on 27 May 1703 (16 May in the Old Style), a date taken as the founding of Saint Petersburg, during the Great Northern War against Sweden. Built first of earth and timber and rebuilt in stone under Trezzini, the star-shaped fortress was designed to defend the Neva delta but never saw battle in that role. From the 1720s it served as a garrison and as a notorious political prison: its inmates included Peter's own son Tsarevich Alexei, the Decembrists, Fyodor Dostoevsky, Maxim Gorky, and Leon Trotsky, as well as the anarchist Peter Kropotkin, who escaped in 1876.[1]

Peter and Paul Cathedral

The cathedral, built 1712–1733, was the first stone church in the city. Its gilded baroque spire rises 122.5 metres (402 ft), topped by an angel weather vane, and remained the tallest building in Saint Petersburg for centuries. Inside are the tombs of the Romanov emperors; in 1998 the remains of Nicholas II and his family, murdered at Yekaterinburg in 1918, were reinterred here.

Museum and traditions

Since 1924 the fortress has been a museum, now the State Museum of the History of Saint Petersburg. A cannon fired daily at noon from the Naryshkin Bastion continues a tradition begun in the 18th century. The fortress also houses a branch of the Saint Petersburg Mint and the Trubetskoy Bastion prison museum.
