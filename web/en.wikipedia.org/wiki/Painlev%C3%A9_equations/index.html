Painlevé equations

In mathematics, the Painlevé equations are six nonlinear second-order ordinary differential equations discovered by Paul Painlevé and his school. Their defining property, the Painlevé property, is that their solutions have no movable critical singularities — movable poles are allowed, but not movable branch points or essential singularities.

The six equations

Labelled PI through PVI, the equations generalise classical special functions; their solutions are called Painlevé transcendents. PVI is the most general, with four parameters, and others arise from it by coalescence limits.

Applications

Painlevé equations appear in statistical mechanics (including the Ising model), random matrix theory, quantum gravity, nonlinear optics, and the theory of integrable systems, often via isomonodromic deformation of linear systems.
