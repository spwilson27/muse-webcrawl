1953 Iranian coup d'état

The 1953 Iranian coup d'état, known in Iran as the 28 Mordad coup, overthrew the democratically elected prime minister Mohammad Mosaddegh on 19 August 1953. It was orchestrated by the United States (Operation Ajax) and the United Kingdom (Operation Boot) and restored the power of Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi.

Date15–19 August 1953
LocationTehran, Iran
OrganisersCIA (Kermit Roosevelt Jr.); MI6; royalist Iranian officers under Fazlollah Zahedi
OutcomeMosaddegh overthrown and imprisoned; Shah's authoritarian rule consolidated

Background

In 1951 Iran nationalised the British-controlled Anglo-Iranian Oil Company, a hugely popular move led by Mosaddegh. Britain responded with an embargo and legal pressure; by 1953 the Eisenhower administration, fearing Iran might drift toward the Soviet sphere, agreed to joint covert action. A propaganda campaign and bribed crowds and officers supported the plot.

The coup

A first attempt on 15 August failed and the Shah fled to Rome. On 19 August, pro-Shah crowds and military units, mobilised with CIA support, attacked Mosaddegh's residence; after fighting in Tehran he surrendered. General Fazlollah Zahedi became prime minister and the Shah returned in triumph.

Aftermath

Mosaddegh was imprisoned and then held under house arrest until his death in 1967. The coup ended Iran's experiment in parliamentary democracy, deepened the Shah's dependence on the United States, and left a lasting legacy of Iranian distrust of foreign intervention that fed into the 1979 revolution. The CIA formally acknowledged its role in 2013.
