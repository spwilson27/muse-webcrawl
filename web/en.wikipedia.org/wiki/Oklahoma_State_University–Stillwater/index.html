Oklahoma State University–Stillwater (often referred to as Oklahoma State, OSU, or OK State) is a public land-grant research university in Stillwater, Oklahoma. Founded in 1890 under the Morrill Act as Oklahoma Agricultural and Mechanical College, it is the flagship institution of the Oklahoma State University System and one of the largest universities in the state.

The university is known for its programs in agriculture, engineering, business, veterinary medicine, and fire protection. Its athletic teams, the Cowboys and Cowgirls, compete in NCAA Division I and the Big 12 Conference, and the school has won dozens of national championships, most notably in wrestling and golf.

History

Oklahoma A&M College was established on December 25, 1890, when the first Oklahoma Territorial Legislature accepted the provisions of the federal Morrill Act. Classes began in 1891 in local churches before the first campus building, Old Central, was completed in 1894. The institution was renamed Oklahoma State University in 1957 to reflect its expanded mission.

Under long-serving president Henry G. Bennett (1928–1951), the campus grew dramatically into a master-planned Georgian-style university. In 1951, OSU also became notable as the home of one of the first large-scale college programs for fire protection and safety technology.

Academics

Oklahoma State offers more than 200 undergraduate degree options through colleges including the College of Agricultural Sciences and Natural Resources, the Spears School of Business, the College of Engineering, Architecture and Technology, and the Center for Veterinary Health Sciences. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" by the Carnegie Classification.

Campus

The Stillwater campus is distinguished by its unified Georgian Revival architecture, landscaped formal gardens, and Theta Pond. Landmarks include Old Central, the Edmon Low Library, the Student Union (one of the largest in the United States), and Boone Pickens Stadium. The university also operates the Botanic Garden at Oklahoma State University and numerous research farms and stations statewide.

Athletics

The Cowboys wrestling program is among the most successful in NCAA history, with more than thirty team national championships. OSU football rose to national prominence in the 2010s under coach Mike Gundy, winning the 2011 Big 12 championship and the Fiesta Bowl. Alumnus Barry Sanders won the 1988 Heisman Trophy while at OSU. The annual football rivalry with the University of Oklahoma is known as Bedlam.

Notable alumni

Alumni include country singer Garth Brooks, businessman T. Boone Pickens, wrestler and coach John Smith, golfer Rickie Fowler, and former U.S. Senator Tom Coburn.
