The battles of Quảng Trị refer to a series of battles during the Vietnam War that occurred in Quảng Trị Province in 1972. The city of Quảng Trị became the only provincial capital captured by North Vietnamese forces during the Easter Offensive, and its recapture by South Vietnamese troops in September 1972 became a major symbol of the war.

The shattered citadel of Quảng Trị, defended by North Vietnamese troops through the summer of 1972.

First battle (March–May 1972)

On 30 March 1972 North Vietnam launched its Easter Offensive. People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) divisions crossed the Demilitarized Zone and shattered South Vietnamese (ARVN) defences in Quảng Trị Province. After heavy fighting and confusion in the ARVN command, the city and its old citadel fell on 1–2 May 1972, and ARVN forces fell back to the Mỹ Chánh line north of Huế.

Second battle (June–September 1972)

In Operation Lam Sơn 72, launched on 28 June 1972, ARVN airborne and marine divisions, supported by massive American air and naval firepower including B-52 strikes, fought north to retake the province. The decisive struggle was for the Quảng Trị citadel, a walled fortress some 500 metres square, which ARVN marines finally stormed on 16 September 1972 after 81 days of fighting. Both sides suffered thousands of casualties, and the city was almost completely destroyed.

Significance

The recapture of Quảng Trị was a badly needed success for South Vietnam and influenced the Paris peace negotiations; under the January 1973 accords the province remained divided roughly along the Thạch Hãn River. Quảng Trị fell again to North Vietnam in the final offensive of 1975.
