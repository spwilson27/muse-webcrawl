Spyridon Louis (Greek: Σπυρίδων Λούης; 12 January 1873 – 26 March 1940), often called Spyros Louis, was a Greek water carrier who won the first modern Olympic marathon at the 1896 Summer Olympics in Athens, becoming a national hero. His victory in the most eagerly anticipated event of the revived Games — the race from Marathon to Athens commemorating the legendary run of Pheidippides — made him the most famous athlete of the first modern Olympics.

Life and the 1896 marathon

Louis was born in Marousi, north of Athens, into a poor family; he worked hauling water for his father's business in Athens, work that kept him walking and running long distances daily. He was persuaded to enter the marathon after serving in the army under Colonel Papadiamantopoulos, the race starter, who knew his endurance. On 10 April 1896, seventeen runners set out from Marathon. Louis ran steadily, took the lead near the end after the early leaders faltered or dropped out, and entered the Panathenaic Stadium first, to roars from some 60,000–80,000 spectators, completing the roughly 40 km course in 2 hours, 58 minutes, and 50 seconds. The Greek crown princes Constantine and George reportedly ran the final lap beside him.

Offers of gifts — free meals, shaves, and land — poured in, but legend says Louis asked only for a donkey and cart to help with his water business. He retired from athletics after the Games and worked as a farmer and local police officer.

Later life and legacy

Louis appeared as an honoured guest at the 1936 Berlin Olympics, presenting Adolf Hitler with an olive branch from Olympia. He died in Marousi in 1940. The main stadium of the Athens Olympic Sports Complex, home of the 2004 Summer Olympics, is named the Spyridon Louis Stadium in his honour, and his name in Greek ("like Louis") became synonymous with running fast.
