Packet loss

Packet loss occurs when one or more packets of data travelling across a computer network fail to reach their destination. It is caused by errors in transmission, network congestion, faulty hardware, or packets being deliberately dropped by routers whose buffers are full.

Causes

Common causes include congested links where router queues overflow, corrupted packets discarded after failing checksum verification, weak wireless signals, duplex mismatches, and failing network equipment. Software bugs and denial-of-service attacks can also produce loss.

Effects and measurement

For reliable protocols such as TCP, lost packets are retransmitted, which reduces effective throughput and increases latency. For real-time applications such as voice over IP and online games, loss appears as dropouts, jitter, and lag, since retransmission arrives too late to be useful. Packet loss is commonly measured with tools such as ping, expressed as a percentage of packets sent that receive no reply. Loss above a few percent noticeably degrades most applications.
