Mount Moreh (Hebrew: Givat HaMoreh, "Hill of the Teacher") is a mountain in northern Israel, on the northeastern edge of the Jezreel Valley, opposite Mount Gilboa and south of Mount Tabor. It rises to about 517 metres above sea level and overlooks the city of Afula and the Harod Valley.

Biblical associations

The hill takes its name from the "hill of Moreh" mentioned in the Book of Judges, near which the Midianites camped before Gideon's night attack with his three hundred men. At the foot of the hill lay Shunem, identified with the modern village of Sulam, where the wealthy Shunammite woman hosted the prophet Elisha, who later raised her son from the dead. Nearby Endor, where King Saul consulted the medium on the eve of his last battle, is also located in the area. The name "Moreh" is sometimes connected with the oak or terebinth of Moreh near Shechem mentioned in Genesis, though the two places are distinct.

Modern geography

Several Arab villages stand on and around the hill, including Nein (biblical Nain, where according to the Gospel of Luke Jesus raised a widow's son), Dahi — whose name the mountain carries in Arabic, Jebel ed-Dahi — Tamra and Sulam. Parts of the slopes are forested, and a nature reserve protects the hill's Mediterranean vegetation and its spring wildflowers.
