Grosse Isle (French: Grosse Île) is an island in the St. Lawrence River in Quebec, Canada, about 46 kilometres downstream of Quebec City. From 1832 to 1937 it operated as Canada's principal quarantine station for immigrants arriving by ship, and it is remembered above all for the catastrophe of 1847, when thousands of Irish emigrants fleeing the Great Famine died there of typhus. It is now the Grosse Île and the Irish Memorial National Historic Site, administered by Parks Canada.

Quarantine station

The station was established in 1832 in response to the cholera epidemic then spreading from Europe. All ships bound for Quebec City were required to stop at Grosse Isle for inspection; the sick were landed in its hospitals while the healthy were detained under observation. Facilities grew to include disinfection buildings, hospitals, chapels, staff housing and cemeteries, arranged in sectors at the island's east and west ends.

The summer of 1847

In 1847, over 100,000 emigrants, most of them Irish fleeing famine, sailed for Canada in overcrowded "coffin ships". Typhus broke out on board and overwhelmed the station: at the peak, dozens of ships carrying thousands of sick and dying passengers waited at anchor. More than 5,000 people died at sea, and over 5,000 more were buried on Grosse Isle in mass trenches — the largest famine-era burial ground in North America. Doctors, nurses, priests and the station's own staff died alongside the emigrants; the mayor of Montreal, John Easton Mills, died of typhus contracted while nursing the sick in the city's fever sheds.

A towering Celtic cross erected in 1909 by the Ancient Order of Hibernians commemorates the dead, and the names of those buried on the island are recorded in the station's memorial.

Later use and heritage

The station closed in 1937 as steamship disinfection and medical inspection improved. The island was later used for bacteriological warfare research during the Second World War and as an animal quarantine station before passing to Parks Canada. Today visitors reach it by boat from Berthier-sur-Mer and can tour the disinfection building, hospital sector, village and cemeteries.
