Bitemporal hemianopsia

FieldNeuro-ophthalmology
Site of lesionOptic chiasm
Common causePituitary adenoma

Bitemporal hemianopsia (also spelled hemianopia) is a visual field defect in which vision is lost in the outer (temporal) half of the visual field in both eyes. It is the classic field defect produced by a lesion at the optic chiasm.

Cause

At the optic chiasm, nerve fibers from the nasal half of each retina cross to the opposite side. These crossing fibers carry information from the temporal visual fields. A mass compressing the chiasm from below — most commonly a pituitary adenoma growing upward out of the sella turcica — damages the crossing fibers first, producing loss of both temporal fields while sparing the nasal fields.

Other causes include craniopharyngioma, meningioma of the tuberculum sellae, aneurysms, and inflammatory conditions.

Clinical features and treatment

Patients may notice difficulty with peripheral vision, bumping into objects on either side, or problems with depth perception. Diagnosis is by formal visual field testing (perimetry) and imaging of the pituitary region. Treatment addresses the underlying cause, often surgical removal of the tumor, and early treatment may restore some vision.
