Global distribution system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A travel agent terminal connected to a global distribution system.

A global distribution system (GDS) is a computerised network system owned or operated by a company that enables transactions between travel industry service providers, mainly airlines, hotels, car rental companies, and travel agencies. The GDS mainly uses real-time inventory (e.g. number of hotel rooms available, seats available, or cars available) from the service providers. Travel agencies traditionally rely on GDS for services, products and rates in order to provide travel-related services to end consumers.

History

The GDS grew out of airline computer reservation systems. In the 1960s, American Airlines and IBM developed Sabre, one of the first centralised systems allowing travel agents to book flights electronically. Other airlines followed with systems such as Apollo (United Airlines) and Galileo, and the systems expanded beyond air travel into hotels, rail, cruises, and car hire.

Major systems

The three dominant global distribution systems are Amadeus, Sabre, and Travelport (which operates the Galileo, Apollo, and Worldspan brands). Amadeus, founded in 1987 by a consortium of European airlines, is the largest by market share. Historically, GDS companies charged airlines booking fees per segment while paying incentives to subscribing travel agencies.

Function

A GDS connects buyers and sellers of travel in real time. When a travel agent searches for a flight, the GDS queries airline inventory, displays fares and availability, records the booking in a passenger name record (PNR), and issues ticketing information. It also handles schedule changes, cancellations, and ancillary services such as seat selection and baggage.

Developments

The rise of online travel agencies, airline direct sales, and the IATA New Distribution Capability (NDC) standard has challenged the traditional GDS model, but the systems remain central to corporate travel management and complex itinerary booking.
