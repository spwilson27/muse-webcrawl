Su Shi (Chinese: 蘇軾; 8 January 1037 – 24 August 1101), courtesy name Zizhan, better known by his art name Su Dongpo (蘇東坡, "Su of the Eastern Slope"), was a Chinese poet, essayist, calligrapher, painter, and statesman of the Song dynasty. He is widely regarded as one of the most accomplished figures in classical Chinese literature, excelling in poetry, prose, calligraphy, and painting.

Life

Su Shi was born in Meishan, in present-day Sichuan province, into a family of scholars. His father, Su Xun, and his younger brother, Su Zhe, were also celebrated writers; together the three are known as the "Three Sus" and are all counted among the Eight Great Prose Masters of the Tang and Song. In 1057, Su Shi and his brother both passed the imperial examinations at the highest level (jinshi), an extraordinary achievement that brought the family fame in the capital, Kaifeng.

Official career and exiles

Su's official career was repeatedly disrupted by factional politics. Opposing the New Policies of Chancellor Wang Anshi, he was arrested in 1079 in the Crow Terrace Poetry Trial, imprisoned for over a hundred days for poems deemed slanderous toward the court, and exiled to Huangzhou in Hubei. There, farming a plot of land on an eastern slope, he took the name Dongpo ("Eastern Slope"). It was during this exile that he wrote some of his most famous works, including the two Red Cliff Rhapsodies and the ci poem "Prelude to Water Melody."

After returning to favor under the regency of Empress Dowager Gao, he was exiled again when the reformist faction regained power, first to Huizhou in Guangdong and then, in 1097, to Danzhou on the remote island of Hainan. He was pardoned in 1100 but died the following year at Changzhou while travelling north.

Works

Some 2,700 of Su's poems survive, along with celebrated prose essays, letters, and treatises on subjects ranging from hydraulic engineering to medicine and tea. His ci poetry helped liberate the form from its origins in the entertainment quarters, expanding its thematic range to philosophy, history, and rural life. His calligraphy is ranked with the finest of the Song dynasty, and he was a formative theorist of literati painting (wenrenhua).

Legacy

Su Dongpo remains one of the most beloved cultural figures in China. The dish Dongpo pork, a braised pork belly preparation, is named after him, as is the causeway he built across West Lake in Hangzhou. His life and work have inspired countless later writers, artists, and cooks.
