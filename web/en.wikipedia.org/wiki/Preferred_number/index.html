Preferred number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Preferred numbers (also called preferred values or preferred series) are standard guidelines for choosing exact product dimensions within a given set of constraints. The system was developed in the late 19th century by French army engineer Charles Renard and is based on geometric series, so that each step is a roughly constant percentage increase and values repeat in every decade.

Renard series

The Renard series R5, R10, R20, R40 and R80 divide each decade into 5, 10, 20, 40 or 80 steps, with common ratio 101/n. The basic series are standardized in ISO 3.

Applications

Preferred numbers underlie standard screw lengths, paper and photographic formats, coin denominations in many currencies, and the E series of preferred values used for electronic components: the E6, E12, E24 and finer series specify the nominal resistances and capacitances manufactured, so that component tolerances overlap and any required value can be approximated. Audio frequencies in ISO 266 follow a similar scheme.
