Ardbeg distillery (Scottish Gaelic: Taigh-staile Àrd Beaga) is a Scotch whisky distillery on the south coast of the island of Islay, Argyll and Bute, Scotland, near Port Ellen. Established commercially in 1815 by John MacDougall — though illicit distillation had taken place on the site since 1794 — it produces some of the most heavily peated single malt whisky in Scotland, with its spirit typically peated to about 55 parts per million phenols.

Character

Ardbeg's whisky is famed for an intense, oily, smoky character balanced by citrus and sweet notes, arising from its peated malt, a distinctive purifier on the spirit still that returns heavier vapours for reflux, and maturation mainly in ex-bourbon casks. Core expressions include Ardbeg 10 Years Old, Uigeadail (named for the loch supplying the distillery's water, and matured partly in sherry casks), Corryvreckan, and An Oa.

History

The distillery flourished in the 19th century, supporting a small village whose school and cottages stood around it. It suffered through the whisky downturns of the 20th century: mothballed in 1981, it ran only intermittently under Allied Distillers in the late 1980s and 1990s before closing again in 1996. The Glenmorangie Company bought and restored it in 1997, relaunching Ardbeg to a passionate cult following; ownership passed to LVMH in 2004. The Ardbeg Committee, founded in 2000, counts well over 100,000 members worldwide.

Ardbeg Day and space experiment

Each June, Ardbeg Day is celebrated during Islay's Fèis Ìle festival with a limited-edition bottling. In 2011 the distillery sent vials of its spirit to the International Space Station to study maturation in microgravity — the distillery claims it was the first whisky in space; the samples returned in 2014 and showed measurable differences from Earth-aged controls.
