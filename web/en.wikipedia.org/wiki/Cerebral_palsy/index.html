Cerebral palsy

SpecialtyPaediatrics, neurology
OnsetPrenatal to early childhood
CausesAbnormal brain development or damage to the developing brain
PrevalenceAbout 2–3 per 1,000 live births
TypesSpastic, dyskinetic, ataxic, mixed

Cerebral palsy (CP) is a group of permanent movement disorders that appear in early childhood. Signs and symptoms vary among people and over time, but commonly include poor coordination, stiff muscles (spasticity), weak muscles, abnormal gait and involuntary movements. Cerebral palsy is caused by abnormal development or damage to the parts of the brain that control movement, balance and posture, most often occurring before or during birth. It is the most common cause of physical disability in childhood.

Classification

CP is classified by the predominant movement disorder: spastic (about 80% of cases, with stiff, jerky movements, including hemiplegic, diplegic and quadriplegic patterns), dyskinetic (involuntary writhing or dystonic movements), ataxic (poor balance and coordination), and mixed types. Function is often described with the Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS), levels I–V.

Causes and risk factors

Causes include congenital brain malformations, intrauterine infection or stroke, genetic factors, complications of prematurity such as periventricular leukomalacia and intraventricular haemorrhage, and — less commonly than once believed — oxygen deprivation during birth. Many cases have no single identifiable cause. Associated conditions may include intellectual disability, epilepsy, speech and visual impairments, and chronic pain.

Management

There is no cure, but early intervention improves outcomes. Management is multidisciplinary: physical, occupational and speech therapy; orthotics; medications such as botulinum toxin and baclofen for spasticity; orthopaedic surgery and selective dorsal rhizotomy in selected cases; and assistive technology for mobility and communication. Life expectancy is often near normal for people with milder forms. Prevention includes antenatal magnesium sulfate for preterm labour and therapeutic hypothermia for neonatal encephalopathy.
