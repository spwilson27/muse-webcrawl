Fifteen-inch gauge railway

Track gauge15 in (381 mm)
CategoryMinimum gauge railway
PioneerSir Arthur Percival Heywood

Fifteen-inch gauge railways are a type of minimum gauge railway with a track gauge of 15 inches (381 mm). The gauge was developed in the 1870s by Sir Arthur Percival Heywood for estate railways, and became the most widely used minimum gauge for passenger-carrying miniature railways, particularly in Britain.

Heywood and minimum gauge

Heywood built the Duffield Bank Railway on his estate in Derbyshire to demonstrate that a very narrow gauge line could haul substantial loads for agriculture and industry, while remaining cheap to construct. His locomotives and rolling stock were designed to the smallest dimensions consistent with carrying useful loads.

Notable lines

Well-known fifteen-inch gauge lines include the Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Railway in Kent, opened in 1927 as a public railway with main-line style steam locomotives built to one-third scale; the Ravenglass and Eskdale Railway in Cumbria, converted to the gauge in 1915; and the Bure Valley Railway in Norfolk. Similar lines operate in parks and estates in other countries, including the United States and Japan.
