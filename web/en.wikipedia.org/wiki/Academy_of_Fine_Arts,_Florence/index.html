Academy of Fine Arts, Florence

The Academy of Fine Arts, Florence (Italian: Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze) is an art academy in Florence, Tuscany, founded in 1563. It was the first academy of drawing in Europe, established under the patronage of Cosimo I de' Medici at the instigation of the painter and historian Giorgio Vasari.

History

The Accademia e Compagnia delle Arti del Disegno was founded on 13 January 1563, with Michelangelo and Cosimo I as its honorary heads. It brought together Florence's leading painters, sculptors and architects, separating fine artists from the artisan guilds. In 1784 Pietro Leopoldo, Grand Duke of Tuscany, reformed it as an academy of fine arts and concentrated the teaching of painting, sculpture and architecture in premises in Via Ricasoli.

Galleria dell'Accademia

The associated gallery, the Galleria dell'Accademia, was founded in 1784 as a collection of works for students to study. It is now a major public museum, most famous as the home of Michelangelo's David (1501–1504), which was moved there in 1873.

The academy today

The academy remains a public institution of higher artistic education, offering courses in painting, sculpture, scenography, graphics and decoration, and forms part of Italy's AFAM system of art and music higher education.
