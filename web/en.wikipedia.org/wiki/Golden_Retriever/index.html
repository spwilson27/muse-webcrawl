The Golden Retriever is a Scottish breed of retriever dog characterised by a gentle, affectionate nature and a striking golden coat. Developed in the Scottish Highlands during the 19th century as a gundog to retrieve shot waterfowl and game, it is now one of the most popular dog breeds in many Western countries and is widely used as a guide dog, assistance dog, therapy dog and search-and-rescue dog.

History

The breed was developed at Guisachan House near Glen Affric by Dudley Marjoribanks, later Lord Tweedmouth, who from the 1860s crossed the now-extinct yellow Tweed Water Spaniel with Flat-Coated Retrievers, Bloodhounds, Irish Setters and later Labrador Retrievers, keeping meticulous studbook records. The Kennel Club recognised the breed in 1913.

Characteristics

Golden Retrievers are medium-to-large dogs with broad heads, dark eyes, a dense double coat in shades from pale cream to deep gold, and a characteristically wagging, feathered tail. They are intelligent, eager to please and easy to train, consistently ranking among the most obedient breeds. They are active and require substantial exercise, and they retain strong retrieving instincts, including a fondness for water.

Health

The breed is predisposed to certain health problems, notably cancers — studies have found unusually high rates of hemangiosarcoma and lymphoma, with cancer a leading cause of death — as well as hip and elbow dysplasia, heart conditions such as subvalvular aortic stenosis, and eye disorders. Typical lifespan is 10 to 12 years.
