Franz Ferdinand are a Scottish rock band formed in Glasgow in 2002. The band's original line-up was composed of Alex Kapranos (lead vocals, lead guitar), Nick McCarthy (rhythm guitar, keyboards, vocals), Bob Hardy (bass guitar), and Paul Thomson (drums). They take their name from Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria, whose assassination in 1914 precipitated the First World War; the name was partly inspired by a racehorse called Archduke.

History

The band formed after its members met through Glasgow's art and music scene, rehearsing in a disused warehouse they nicknamed "the Chateau," where they also staged art exhibitions and gigs. Their first EP, Darts of Pleasure (2003), brought them a deal with Domino Records.

Their self-titled debut album Franz Ferdinand (2004) was a critical and commercial success, driven by the single "Take Me Out," whose abrupt tempo change became a signature of the era's indie rock. The album won the 2004 Mercury Prize. The follow-up, You Could Have It So Much Better (2005), produced the hits "Do You Want To" and "Walk Away." Later albums include Tonight: Franz Ferdinand (2009), Right Thoughts, Right Words, Right Action (2013), Always Ascending (2018), and The Human Fear (2025). In 2015 the band also released the collaborative album FFS with the American band Sparks.

Style and influence

Franz Ferdinand's music combines angular post-punk guitars with danceable rhythms, aiming, in Kapranos's phrase, to make music "for girls to dance to." They were leading figures of the 2000s post-punk revival alongside bands such as the Strokes and Arctic Monkeys, and their sharp-suited art-school aesthetic became influential in British indie music.
