Giant Steps is a studio album by the American jazz saxophonist John Coltrane, released in January 1960 by Atlantic Records. It was Coltrane's first album for Atlantic and is regarded as one of the most influential recordings in jazz history.

Music

The album showcases Coltrane's ground-breaking harmonic approach, the "Coltrane changes", in which chord progressions cycle rapidly through keys a major third apart. The title track's swift, demanding changes became a rite of passage for jazz musicians; pianist Tommy Flanagan's hesitant solo on the original take is famous in jazz lore. Other tracks include "Cousin Mary", "Countdown", "Spiral", "Syeeda's Song Flute", "Naima" — a tender ballad for Coltrane's wife that became one of his most loved compositions — and "Mr. P.C.", named for bassist Paul Chambers.

Legacy

Giant Steps has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for the US National Recording Registry. Its title track is a standard of the jazz repertoire and a benchmark of improvisational virtuosity, studied by generations of saxophonists.
