Wolffia

Wolffia is a genus of aquatic flowering plants in the family Araceae (subfamily Lemnoideae, the duckweeds). The genus includes the smallest flowering plants on Earth: species such as Wolffia globosa and Wolffia angusta produce plant bodies (fronds) only about a millimetre long and flowers so tiny they are barely visible to the naked eye. The genus was named in honour of the German botanist Johann Friedrich Wolff.

Description

Wolffia plants are rootless, free-floating aquatics found on the surface of still or slow-moving fresh water worldwide. Each plant consists of a small, oval, green thallus-like frond without distinct stem or leaves. Reproduction is mainly vegetative, by budding, though the plants do flower — each flower, emerging from a cavity in the frond, consists of a single pistil and a single stamen.

Uses

In parts of Southeast Asia, particularly Thailand, Wolffia globosa (known locally as khai-nam, "water eggs") is eaten as a protein-rich vegetable. Because of its fast growth and high protein content, Wolffia has attracted interest as a potential sustainable food source.
