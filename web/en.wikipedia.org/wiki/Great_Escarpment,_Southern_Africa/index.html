Great Escarpment, Southern Africa

The Great Escarpment is a major geological formation in southern Africa, forming the rim of the elevated central plateau of the subcontinent. Formed largely as the Gondwana supercontinent broke up around 150–120 million years ago, the escarpment rises steeply from the narrow coastal plains to the interior Highveld and related plateaus, in places exceeding 3,000 m in elevation.

Sections

The most dramatic section is the eastern Drakensberg in South Africa and Lesotho, whose basalt-capped cliffs — including the Amphitheatre and peaks such as Thabana Ntlenyana — are the highest ground in southern Africa. The escarpment continues through the Eastern Highlands of Zimbabwe, the west coast of Namibia and into Angola. Its uplift strongly influences regional climate and rainfall, forces orographic rain on its eastern slopes, and the rivers descending it have cut deep gorges, such as that of the Blyde River.
