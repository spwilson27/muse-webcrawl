Glazing (window)

Glazing, in the context of windows, is the glass component of a window, and the act of installing glass into a frame. A person who installs glazing is a glazier. Glazing may be single, double or triple layered, and modern units are usually supplied as sealed insulated glazing units (IGUs).

Types

Single glazing consists of one pane of glass; it offers poor thermal insulation. Double glazing uses two panes separated by a sealed cavity filled with dry air or an inert gas such as argon, greatly reducing heat loss and condensation. Triple glazing adds a third pane and is common in cold climates and in passive house construction. Low-emissivity (low-E) coatings, warm-edge spacers and laminated or toughened safety glass are widely used.

Glazing compounds and methods

Traditional glazing fixes panes with glazier's points and linseed-oil putty; modern systems use rubber or silicone gaskets, glazing beads and structural silicone in curtain walling.
