Charlton Heston

Charlton Heston (October 4, 1923 – April 5, 2008) was an American actor. Known for his commanding presence in epic films, he played Moses in The Ten Commandments (1956), won the Academy Award for Best Actor for the title role in Ben-Hur (1959), and starred in El Cid (1961), Planet of the Apes (1968), and Soylent Green (1973). He later became a prominent political activist and served as president of the National Rifle Association from 1998 to 2003.

Career

Born John Charles Carter in Illinois, Heston won a drama scholarship to Northwestern University and served in the U.S. Army Air Forces during the Second World War. After Broadway and early television work, Cecil B. DeMille cast him in The Greatest Show on Earth (1952) and then as Moses in The Ten Commandments, which made him the face of the Hollywood biblical epic. William Wyler's Ben-Hur, with its famous chariot race, earned him the Oscar. Through the 1960s and 1970s he headlined historical epics and science-fiction films including Planet of the Apes and The Omega Man.

Activism

Heston marched for civil rights in the 1960s and supported Martin Luther King Jr.; in later decades he became a conservative activist, campaigning for Republican candidates and serving five terms as president of the NRA, famously pledging that his gun would be taken only "from my cold, dead hands." In 2002 he announced that he had symptoms consistent with Alzheimer's disease, and in 2003 he received the Presidential Medal of Freedom. He died at his Beverly Hills home in 2008.
