The Ocean Race

The Ocean Race is a yacht race around the world, held every three or four years since 1973. It was originally known as the Whitbread Round the World Race after its initiating sponsor Whitbread, and from 2001 to 2019 as the Volvo Ocean Race after Volvo took over sponsorship. It is widely considered one of the most demanding team sporting events, testing crews through the Southern Ocean and around the great capes.

Format

The race is run in legs between host ports, with in-port races contributing to the scoring. Early editions were sailed in a mixed fleet on handicap; later editions used one-design classes, most recently the IMOCA 60 and VO65 classes. Crews race day and night for weeks at a time, with points awarded per leg. The 2022–23 edition started in Alicante, Spain, and finished in Genoa, Italy, visiting Cape Town, Itajaí, Newport, Aarhus, Kiel, and The Hague among other ports, and was won by 11th Hour Racing Team — the first American-flagged winner.

History

The first race, in 1973–74, was won by the Mexican entry Sayula II. The race built a reputation for extreme conditions and has been marked by tragedies, including the loss of Dutch skipper Hans Horrevoets in 2006 and British sailor John Fisher, lost overboard from Sun Hung Kai/Scallywag in 2018.
