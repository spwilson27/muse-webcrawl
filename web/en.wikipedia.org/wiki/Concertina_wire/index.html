Concertina wire

Concertina wire or Dannert wire is a type of barbed wire or razor wire that is formed in large coils which can be expanded like a concertina. In conjunction with plain barbed wire (or razor wire/tape) and steel pickets, it is most often used as a military wire obstacle.

History

During the First World War, German soldiers made concertina wire from ordinary barbed wire, using no tools, by winding it around pegs in the ground. The resulting coils could be quickly deployed and stretched out to form entanglements. The name "Dannert wire" comes from a German officer associated with its development.

Construction and use

Modern concertina wire typically consists of razor wire formed into large-diameter coils, often around 1 metre across, which can be compressed for transport and expanded to many metres in length. Triple concertina fences stack coils in a pyramid for added depth. It is widely used at military positions, prisons, borders, and other secure facilities as a perimeter barrier.
