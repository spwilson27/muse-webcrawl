Exim

Exim is a mail transfer agent (MTA) used on Unix-like operating systems. It was first developed in 1995 by Philip Hazel at the University of Cambridge as a successor to Smail, and its name was originally short for "EXperimental Internet Mailer". Exim is free software released under the GNU General Public License.

Exim is highly configurable through a single configuration file and supports extensive access control lists, content scanning, encryption, and flexible routing of email. It is the default MTA on Debian GNU/Linux systems and is widely used by internet service providers; surveys have frequently ranked it among the most used mail servers on the public internet.

The software follows the general model of Sendmail but with a different philosophy emphasising a single binary and centralised configuration.
