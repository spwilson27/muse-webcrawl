The Roman Martyrology (Martyrologium Romanum) is the official martyrology of the Catholic Church, a catalogue of martyrs and saints arranged by the day of the year on which each is commemorated, with a brief account of their lives and deaths. It is read in religious communities and forms the basis for the Church's calendar of feast days, though it lists several thousand saints beyond those on the General Roman Calendar.

History

The Martyrology descends from early medieval lists, notably the Martyrologium Hieronymianum (falsely attributed to St Jerome) and the martyrologies of Bede, Florus of Lyon, Ado of Vienne and Usuard. As part of the post-Tridentine reform of liturgical books, Pope Gregory XIII promulgated an official edition in 1584, prepared under the direction of Cardinal Cesare Baronius, whose scholarly revisions appeared in 1586 and 1589.

Modern editions

Subsequent editions added newly canonized saints and corrected historical errors. The 2001 editio typica, corrected in 2004, reorganized entries critically and remains the current standard. An English translation of the complete text was long lacking; partial and full translations have appeared in modern times.
