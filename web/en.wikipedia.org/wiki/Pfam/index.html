Pfam is a database of protein families, represented by multiple sequence alignments and hidden Markov models (HMMs). It enables researchers to identify the domain composition of proteins and infer their function: a large fraction of known protein sequences match at least one Pfam family. Each family in the database is curated with a seed alignment, a full alignment of detected members, and an HMM that can be used to search new sequences.

Features

Pfam families are organised into clans of related families descended from a common ancestor. Entries are annotated with descriptions, literature references and links to other resources such as UniProt and Wikipedia. The database was widely used through the HMMER web server and software to annotate newly sequenced genomes and metagenomes.

History

Pfam was created in the 1990s by researchers including Erik Sonnhammer, Richard Durbin, Sean Eddy and Alex Bateman, and was maintained successively at the Wellcome Trust Sanger Institute and the European Bioinformatics Institute, with mirrors elsewhere. In 2021 its final standalone release, Pfam 35.0, contained nearly 20,000 families; the resource was then fully integrated into the InterPro database, which continues to distribute Pfam models.
