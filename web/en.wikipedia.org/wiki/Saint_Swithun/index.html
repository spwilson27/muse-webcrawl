Swithun (or Swithin; Old English: Swīþhūn; c. 800 – 2 July 863) was an Anglo-Saxon bishop of Winchester and subsequently a patron saint of Winchester Cathedral. Remembered in life as a humble and charitable bishop, he became famous after death for miracles at his shrine and for the weather proverb attached to his feast day.

Life

Almost nothing is known of Swithun's early life. He was a priest during the reign of Egbert of Wessex, tutored the young Æthelwulf, and was consecrated Bishop of Winchester in 852 or 853. He is credited with building churches and bridges, and with restoring the bridge at Winchester's east gate — the setting for the legend in which he miraculously restored a basket of eggs broken by workmen.

Cult and weather lore

Swithun asked to be buried humbly outside the cathedral, where rain would fall on his grave. In 971 his remains were translated inside the Old Minster, reportedly during heavy rain, which popular tradition linked to his displeasure. From this arose the famous proverb:

"St Swithun's day if thou dost rain / For forty days it will remain; / St Swithun's day if thou be fair / For forty days 'twill rain nae mare."

His shrine in Winchester Cathedral was destroyed in the Reformation, but a modern memorial marks the site, and his cult spread as far as Norway (Stavanger Cathedral is dedicated to him).
