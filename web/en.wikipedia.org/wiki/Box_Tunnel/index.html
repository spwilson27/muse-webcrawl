Box Tunnel passes through Box Hill on the Great Western Main Line between Bath and Chippenham in Wiltshire, England. The tunnel was designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel and, at 1.83 miles (2.95 km) in length, was the world's longest railway tunnel when it opened in 1841. It carries a notable gradient of 1 in 100 and is reputed — though the story is debated — to be aligned so that the rising sun shines through it on Brunel's birthday, 9 April.

Construction

Construction began in 1836 and required the excavation of the tunnel through oolitic limestone and Fuller's earth. Around 4,000 men worked on the project, consuming a ton of gunpowder and a ton of candles each week; at least 100 workers were killed during construction. The tunnel's completion in 1841 removed the final obstacle to opening the Great Western Railway between London Paddington and Bristol Temple Meads.

Air shafts and portals

Six vertical ventilation shafts connect the tunnel to the surface of Box Hill. The western portal, near Corsham, is built in an elaborate classical style with rusticated masonry, while the eastern portal at Box is comparatively plain. During the Second World War, parts of the adjacent quarries and a portion of the tunnel complex were adapted as a protected underground facility associated with the nearby RAF and ammunition storage sites at Corsham.
