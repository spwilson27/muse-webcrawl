Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty is a 2012 book by economist Daron Acemoglu and political scientist James A. Robinson. It argues that a nation's prosperity is determined primarily by its institutions: countries grow when they develop inclusive economic and political institutions that distribute power broadly, protect property rights, and reward innovation, and stagnate under extractive institutions that concentrate power and wealth in a narrow elite.

Argument

The book contrasts famous paired cases — Nogales, Arizona versus Nogales, Sonora; North versus South Korea — to argue that geography, climate, and culture (rival theories associated with Jared Diamond, and with ignorance of good policies) cannot explain divergent outcomes. Growth under extractive institutions, as in the Soviet Union or contemporary China, is characterised as inherently limited because elites fear creative destruction. "Critical junctures" such as the Black Death or the Glorious Revolution can shift societies onto new institutional paths.

Reception

The book was a bestseller and widely debated. Reviewers including Bill Gates and Diamond praised its sweep, while critics — including economists such as Jeffrey Sachs and David Landes — challenged its dismissal of geography and culture, and historians questioned some of its case studies. In 2024 Acemoglu and Robinson shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (with Simon Johnson) for their work on institutions and prosperity.
