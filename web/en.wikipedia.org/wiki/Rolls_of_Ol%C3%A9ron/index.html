Rolls of Oléron

Rolls of Oléron
TypeMaritime law code
OriginÎle d'Oléron, France
Datec. 1160–1286

The Rolls of Oléron (Jugements de la mer) are a collection of regulations governing commerce and navigation by sea, originating on the island of Oléron off the Atlantic coast of France around the 12th century. They form one of the foundational codes of European maritime law.

Content

The Rolls set out the duties of shipmasters and crews, rules for jettison and general average, pilotage, collisions, and the division of losses. They are traditionally associated with Eleanor of Aquitaine, said to have introduced them after her travels, and were later promoted in England by Richard I.

Influence

The code was widely adopted along the Atlantic coasts of France, England, Flanders, and beyond, influencing later compilations such as the Laws of Wisby and the ordinances incorporated into the English Black Book of the Admiralty.
