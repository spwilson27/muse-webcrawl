Hutsuls

The Hutsuls are an ethnic group of Ukrainian highlanders who inhabit the Carpathian Mountains in western Ukraine and northern Romania, in the region known as Hutsulshchyna. They speak a variety of Ukrainian dialects with influences from neighbouring languages.

Culture

Hutsul culture is noted for pastoral sheep herding, logging, woodcarving, embroidery, elaborate Easter eggs (pysanky), brass work, and distinctive music and dance, including the trembita, a long alpine horn. Traditional wooden churches and houses dot their mountain villages. Their origins are debated, with theories involving Slavic, Romanian, and earlier Carpathian populations.

History

Historically part of various states including Moldavia, the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Austrian Empire and later Ukraine and Romania, the Hutsuls maintained a semi-independent highland way of life for centuries.
