The American Statistician


Cover of The American Statistician

The American Statistician is a quarterly peer-reviewed scientific journal covering statistics published by Taylor & Francis on behalf of the American Statistical Association (ASA). It was established in 1947 as the American Statistician bulletin of the association and has become one of the most widely read general-interest journals in the discipline.

Scope

Unlike the ASA's more technical Journal of the American Statistical Association, The American Statistician publishes articles of general interest to statisticians: expository and review papers, discussions of statistical practice and teaching, commentary on statistical philosophy, and short notes on methods, computing and data visualization. Articles are expected to be accessible to a broad audience of practicing statisticians.

Notable content

The journal has been central in debates over statistical inference. It published the ASA's 2016 statement on p-values, and in 2019 a special issue on "Statistical Inference in the 21st Century: A World Beyond p < 0.05", which recommended abandoning the term "statistically significant" in many contexts and attracted worldwide attention.

Editors and indexing

The journal is abstracted and indexed in major bibliographic databases including Scopus, MathSciNet, and the Science Citation Index Expanded. Its articles are frequently cited in discussions of statistical education and practice.
