Protest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A protest (also called a demonstration, remonstration or remonstrance) is a public expression of objection, disapproval or dissent toward an idea or action, typically a political one — by gatherings, marches, occupations, strikes, boycotts, petitions, performances and digital campaigns. Protest is the repertoire of the comparatively powerless: where votes, money and access are lacking, numbers, disruption and publicity are the currencies.

Study and law

Social-movement scholarship (Tilly's "repertoires of contention"; resource-mobilisation and political-process theories; framing research) studies protest as organised, learned and consequential — agenda-setting, elite division and policy change being its documented effects, alongside backlash. Empirically, nonviolent campaigns have succeeded at roughly double the rate of violent ones in the major 20th–21st-century dataset of regime-change campaigns (Chenoweth's NAVCO research — the "3.5 per cent" participation rule of thumb drawn from it is widely quoted and debated). Law protects protest unevenly: assembly and expression rights (First Amendment doctrine on permits, time-place-manner, and the heckler's veto; ECHR Articles 10–11 with margin for regulation) versus public-order offences, anti-mask and anti-encampment laws, and the expanding category of infrastructure and "aggravated trespass" offences aimed at climate and other direct action — the boundary of tolerated dissent being re-litigated in most democracies this decade.
