William MacAskill (born 1987; born William Crouch) is a Scottish philosopher at the University of Oxford and one of the founders of the effective altruism movement. As a student he co-founded Giving What We Can (2009), whose members pledge at least 10% of their income to the most effective charities, and 80,000 Hours, which researches how graduates can do the most good with their careers. His books Doing Good Better (2015) and What We Owe the Future (2022) popularised effective altruism and longtermism respectively.

Work

MacAskill's academic philosophy focuses on normative ethics, decision theory under moral uncertainty (the subject of his co-authored Moral Uncertainty, 2020), and global priorities research — the discipline he helped institutionalise in Oxford's Global Priorities Institute. What We Owe the Future argues that the long-term future could contain enormous value, that our century may be a "hinge" at which it can still be steered, and that preventing existential catastrophe and avoiding the lock-in of bad values are therefore moral priorities. The book became a bestseller and the public manifesto of longtermism.

Reception and the FTX crisis

MacAskill's organising made him a central, and sometimes controversial, figure: profiled widely in the press, he served as an adviser to the philanthropic efforts associated with Sam Bankman-Fried, whose fraud and the 2022 collapse of FTX — a major source of funding for EA-aligned projects — damaged the movement's reputation and prompted public reckonings, including MacAskill's own statements that he had raised concerns about Bankman-Fried in 2018 and had been deceived. The episode is now a standard part of accounts of the movement's history and of debates about the risks of its closeness to wealthy donors.
