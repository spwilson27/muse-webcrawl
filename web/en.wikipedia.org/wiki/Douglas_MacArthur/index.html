Douglas MacArthur

Douglas MacArthur (26 January 1880 – 5 April 1964) was an American general who served as a top commander during World War II and the Korean War. He achieved the rank of General of the Army and was one of only five men to do so, and he also held the rank of Field Marshal in the Philippine Army.

Early career

The son of General Arthur MacArthur Jr., MacArthur graduated first in his class from West Point in 1903, served with distinction in the First World War as commander of the 42nd "Rainbow" Division, became the Army's youngest major general, and served as Chief of Staff of the Army from 1930 to 1935 — including the controversial dispersal of the Bonus Army in 1932.

World War II and Japan

After defending and then losing the Philippines in 1942 — vowing "I shall return," which he did at Leyte in 1944 — MacArthur commanded Allied forces in the Southwest Pacific, accepted Japan's surrender aboard USS Missouri, and as Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers directed the occupation and democratisation of Japan.

Korean War and dismissal

MacArthur commanded UN forces in Korea from 1950, landing brilliantly at Inchon but then clashing with President Harry S. Truman over expanding the war to China. Truman relieved him on 11 April 1951. MacArthur addressed Congress with the line "old soldiers never die; they just fade away," and died in 1964.
