Swaledale (sheep)

TypeUpland / hill sheep
UseMeat, wool, crossbreeding
Face colourBlack with white muzzle
HornsBoth sexes horned

The Swaledale is a breed of domestic sheep named after the Yorkshire valley of Swaledale in England, where it originated. It is one of the classic British upland or hill breeds, prized for its hardiness on exposed moorland and high fells.

Characteristics

Swaledales are medium-sized sheep with off-white, coarse wool. Both ewes and rams are horned, the rams carrying heavy, curled horns. The face is black with a distinctive white muzzle, and the legs are mottled black and white. The breed is exceptionally hardy, able to thrive on poor hill grazing in harsh weather, and ewes are noted as protective, long-lived mothers.

Breeding and use

Purebred Swaledales are kept on the highest ground. Draft ewes are commonly crossed with a Bluefaced Leicester ram to produce the North of England Mule, one of Britain's most important commercial crossbred ewes, which is in turn crossed with terminal sires for prime lamb production. The breed society, the Swaledale Sheep Breeders Association, was founded in 1919, and pedigree sales at centres such as Kirkby Stephen draw large prices for top rams. The wool, though coarse, is durable and used in carpets and tweeds; the meat is also valued.
