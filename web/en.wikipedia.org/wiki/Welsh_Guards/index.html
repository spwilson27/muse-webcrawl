The Welsh Guards (WG) is an infantry regiment of the British Army and one of the five regiments of Foot Guards in the Household Division. It was raised in 1915, during the First World War, by royal warrant of King George V, making it the most recently formed of the Foot Guards regiments. Its cap badge is the leek, the national emblem of Wales, and its soldiers are recruited principally from Wales.

History

The regiment first saw action at the Battle of Loos in September 1915 and fought on the Western Front for the remainder of the war. In the Second World War its battalions served in France in 1940, North Africa, Tunisia, Italy and north-west Europe, including the liberation of Brussels in 1944. Post-war service included tours in Northern Ireland, the Falklands War of 1982 — where the regiment suffered heavy losses when the landing ship Sir Galahad was attacked at Bluff Cove — and deployments to Iraq and Afghanistan.

Role

The Welsh Guards combine operational infantry duties with state ceremonial and public duties in London and Windsor, including mounting the King's Guard and participating in Trooping the Colour. The regiment consists of a single regular battalion, based in recent years at Combermere Barracks, Windsor, and Elizabeth Barracks, Pirbright.
