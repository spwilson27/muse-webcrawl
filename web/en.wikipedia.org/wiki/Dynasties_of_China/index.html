Dynasties of China

For most of its history, China was organised into a series of dynasties — hereditary monarchies whose rulers belonged to a single family or clan. A dynasty typically began when its founder seized power, often claiming the Mandate of Heaven, and ended when a successor regime displaced it. Between the legendary Xia and the abdication of the last Qing emperor in 1912, dynastic rule spanned some four thousand years.

Patterns

Traditional historiography described a "dynastic cycle" in which vigorous founders gave way to corrupt later rulers, natural disasters were read as omens of lost legitimacy, and rebellion founded the next dynasty. Periods of division — such as the Three Kingdoms, the Sixteen Kingdoms, and the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms — alternated with reunifications under the Sui, Tang and Song. Conquest dynasties (Liao, Jin, Yuan, Qing) were founded by non-Han peoples who ruled all or part of China.
