Homo Ludens

Homo Ludens: Versuch einer Bestimmung des Spielelementes der Kultur (1938) is a book by Dutch historian Johan Huizinga arguing that play is a primary, necessary condition of culture. Huizinga proposes Homo ludens — "man the player" — alongside Homo sapiens and Homo faber.

Thesis

Huizinga defines play as a free activity standing outside ordinary life, absorbing the player, bounded in time and space, ordered by rules, and creating social groupings. He traces the play element in law, war, poetry, philosophy and art, arguing culture arises in and as play.

Influence

The book founded modern play studies and influenced game studies, anthropology and the Situationists; Roger Caillois's Man, Play and Games (1958) responded to and extended it.

References: Huizinga (1938); Caillois (1958).
