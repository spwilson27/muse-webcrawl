Pedestrian zone

Related conceptsCar-free movement, traffic calming, low traffic neighbourhood
Typical locationCity centres, shopping districts, historic old towns

A pedestrian zone (in German, Fußgängerzone) is an area of a city or town, usually in the centre, reserved for pedestrian-only use and from which most motor vehicles are excluded. Pedestrian zones are often created in areas with heavy retail activity and narrow historic streets.

Development

Pedestrian zones spread widely in Europe from the 1960s and 1970s, beginning with German and Dutch cities and the pioneering schemes in Copenhagen and Norwich. Many historic old towns in Europe are now substantially pedestrianised. Similar schemes exist worldwide, from car-free resort towns to the largely vehicle-free centre of Venice, where wheeled motor traffic has never been possible.

Rules and exceptions

Within a pedestrian zone, deliveries are typically allowed in early morning windows, and residents, public transport, cyclists and people with disabilities may have special access. Zones may be enforced by bollards, cameras or time restrictions, and are sometimes combined with park-and-ride and public transport improvements outside the zone.
