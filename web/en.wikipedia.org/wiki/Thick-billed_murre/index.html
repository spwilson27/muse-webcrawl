Thick-billed murre

Thick-billed murre
Scientific name: Uria lomvia
Family: Alcidae
Conservation status: Least Concern

The thick-billed murre or Brünnich's guillemot (Uria lomvia) is a bird in the auk family (Alcidae). This bird is named after the Danish zoologist Morten Thrane Brünnich. The very closely related common murre (Uria aalge), also known as the common guillemot, occupies similar habitats. Thick-billed murres are among the deepest underwater divers of all birds, regularly descending to depths of more than 100 metres, and occasionally below 200 metres.

Description

Adults measure 40–48 cm in length with a wingspan of 64–81 cm. They are black on the head, neck, back, and wings, with white underparts. The bill is thick, black, and pointed, with a distinctive white stripe along the gape; the legs and feet are black. In winter plumage the throat and lower face become white. The thick-billed murre can be distinguished from the common murre by its thicker, shorter bill with a white gape stripe, and by its darker head.

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds in the polar and sub-polar regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with colonies in the North Atlantic and North Pacific, including Canada, Greenland, Iceland, Norway, Russia, Alaska, and northern Japan. It nests in dense colonies on steep cliff ledges, laying a single egg directly on the rock. Birds winter at sea in ice-free waters south of the breeding range.

Behaviour

Thick-billed murres feed mainly on fish such as Arctic cod, capelin, and sand lance, as well as crustaceans and squid. They swim underwater using their wings to pursue prey. Chicks leave the colony at about three weeks old, jumping from the cliff ledge to the sea accompanied by the male parent, which cares for them at sea for several weeks.
