Frankfurt am Main (German pronunciation: [ˈfʁaŋkfʊʁt ʔam ˈmaɪn]; lit. "Frank ford on the Main") is the most populous city in the German state of Hesse and the fifth-most populous city in Germany. It is a global hub for commerce, culture, education and tourism, and one of the world's leading financial centres.


The Frankfurt skyline, sometimes nicknamed "Mainhattan"

History

Frankfurt, at a ford on the River Main, was first mentioned in 794 in a document of Charlemagne. As a free imperial city it became one of the most important cities of the Holy Roman Empire: from 1562 the coronations of emperors took place in its cathedral. In 1848 the first freely elected German parliament, the Frankfurt Parliament, met in St. Paul's Church. The city was annexed by Prussia in 1866. Heavily bombed in the Second World War, Frankfurt rebuilt itself as West Germany's financial capital.

Economy

Frankfurt is home to the European Central Bank, the Deutsche Bundesbank, the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (operated by Deutsche Börse) and hundreds of banks, earning it the nickname "Bankfurt." Frankfurt Airport is one of the world's busiest international airports, and the city hosts the Frankfurt Book Fair, the world's largest trade fair for books.

Culture

The city's most famous son is Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, whose birthplace is now a museum. The Museumsufer (Museum Embankment) along the Main contains institutions such as the Städel Museum. Regional specialties include Apfelwein (apple wine), green sauce (Grüne Soße) and the Frankfurter sausage.
