Chinese astrology

Chinese astrology is based on traditional Chinese astronomy and calendrical cycles, most famously the twelve-year cycle of the Chinese zodiac, in which each year is represented by an animal and its reputed attributes. Developed during the Han dynasty, Chinese astrology is intertwined with the theory of the five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal and water), yin and yang, and the sexagenary cycle formed by the ten Heavenly Stems and twelve Earthly Branches. It was historically used to cast horoscopes, choose auspicious dates and advise emperors, and remains popular across East and Southeast Asia.

The zodiac cycle

Each year in the twelve-year cycle corresponds to one of the Earthly Branches and is represented by an animal: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig. A person's zodiac animal is determined by their birth year, with the year beginning at Chinese New Year rather than January 1. The animals also cycle through the five elements in successive years, creating a 60-year sexagenary cycle used for calendrical reckoning since antiquity.

Four Pillars of Destiny

The most developed form of Chinese astrological fortune-telling is Bazi, the "Four Pillars of Destiny", which records the Heavenly Stem and Earthly Branch for a person's year, month, day and hour of birth. Practitioners analyse the balance of the five elements across the eight resulting characters to interpret personality, fortune and compatibility, including the tradition of matching zodiac animals for marriage.

History and influence

Astral omens were recorded on oracle bones in the Shang dynasty, and systematic star lore developed alongside the imperial calendar. Court astrologers interpreted celestial phenomena as portents for the state under the Mandate of Heaven doctrine. Chinese astrology spread to Korea, Japan, Vietnam and Tibet, where it blended with local traditions.
