The Storm on the Sea of Galilee

ArtistRembrandt van Rijn
Year1633
MediumOil on canvas
StatusStolen 1990; missing

Christ in the Storm on the Sea of Galilee, commonly known as The Storm on the Sea of Galilee, is a 1633 oil-on-canvas painting by the Dutch Golden Age painter Rembrandt van Rijn. It depicts the biblical episode of Jesus calming the storm, with Christ amid his terrified disciples in a boat battered by enormous waves.

Description

Rembrandt places fourteen figures in the boat rather than thirteen: the artist included a self-portrait, at the centre of the canvas, wearing a dark beret and holding his hat, looking directly out at the viewer — an early and famous example of the painter inserting himself into a biblical scene. Dramatic chiaroscuro heightens the contrast between the dark, spray-lashed sea and a shaft of light breaking through the clouds.

Theft

The painting hung in the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston until the early hours of 18 March 1990, when two thieves disguised as police officers stole it along with twelve other works, including Vermeer's The Concert — the largest art theft in United States history, valued at hundreds of millions of dollars. The empty frame still hangs in the museum's Dutch Room, in accordance with Isabella Stewart Gardner's will that the collection's arrangement never change. Despite a substantial reward, the painting has never been recovered.
