Half-transitive graph

In the mathematical field of graph theory, a half-transitive graph is a graph that is both vertex-transitive and edge-transitive, but not arc-transitive. That is, its automorphism group acts transitively on its vertices and on its edges, but not on its ordered pairs of adjacent vertices (arcs): no automorphism maps one orientation of an edge to the other orientation of the same edge. A half-transitive graph must have even degree, and its edges can be given an orientation that is preserved by all automorphisms, making it an oriented graph with no symmetry reversing any edge.

History and examples

The existence of such graphs was established in the 1960s–70s. The smallest half-transitive graph is the Holt graph (also called the Doyle graph), found by Peter G. Doyle in 1976 and independently by Derek Holt in 1981. It has 27 vertices, is 4-regular, and has girth 5. Although the Holt graph is the smallest example by vertices, infinite families of half-transitive graphs are now known, all of degree divisible by 4...

Half-transitive graphs were shown to exist for degree 4k for every positive integer k, through constructions by Marušič and others; the degree-4 case is the most studied. Every half-transitive graph has a cycle of length related to its properties, and all known examples have even girth or girth five. A theorem of Marušič and Nedela relates half-transitive graphs to certain covering graphs and group actions, classifying them by means of the structure of their automorphism groups and the quotient graphs obtained from group orbits.

Properties

A half-transitive graph is necessarily regular of even degree. Because the automorphism group preserves an orientation of every edge while acting transitively on edges, the number of arcs is twice the number of edges and the group fails to be transitive on arcs exactly because the two directions of an edge lie in different orbits. Half-transitive graphs cannot be bipartite with the obvious symmetries reversed; in particular their study connects to the theory of Cayley graphs, maps on surfaces, and the classification of symmetric graphs of small degree, notably the cubic and quartic cases in work by Marušič, Praeger and others.
