Cosmopolitan (cocktail)

IBA official cocktail
Type: Cocktail
Primary alcohol: Vodka
Served: Straight up; without ice
Garnish: Lemon twist
Drinkware: Cocktail glass

A cosmopolitan, or informally a cosmo, is a cocktail made with vodka, Cointreau (or triple sec), cranberry juice, and freshly squeezed or sweetened lime juice. It is typically served in a large cocktail glass (martini glass) and garnished with a twist of lemon or lime. The drink gained popularity in the 1990s, helped by its frequent appearance in the television series Sex and the City, in which it was the favourite drink of the lead character Carrie Bradshaw.

Preparation

The International Bartenders Association (IBA) recipe calls for 40 ml vodka citron, 15 ml Cointreau, 15 ml fresh lime juice and 30 ml cranberry juice, shaken with ice, strained into a chilled cocktail glass and garnished with a lemon twist. The cranberry juice gives the drink its characteristic blush-pink colour; the drink belongs to the family of vodka-based "martinis" popular in the 1980s and 1990s.

History

The origin of the cosmopolitan is disputed. Similar drinks appear in print as early as the 1930s, and a recipe resembling the modern drink circulated in gay bars in Provincetown, Massachusetts, in the 1970s, where cranberry juice (a local product) was added to a kamikaze-style cocktail. Bartender Cheryl Cook in Miami is often credited with a 1985–86 version using the newly marketed Absolut Citron vodka, while Toby Cecchini, at the Odeon in New York, developed an influential 1987–88 version based on a description of Cook's drink. Dale DeGroff's version at the Rainbow Room in the mid-1990s is credited with standardising the recipe and bringing it to wider attention, after which celebrity association — first with Madonna and then with Sex and the City — made it one of the defining cocktails of the decade.

Variations

Variations include the white cosmopolitan (white cranberry juice instead of red), the cosmocello (with limoncello), the cosmopolitan with raspberry (using raspberry liqueur or raspberry vodka), and drinks in the same family such as the metropolitan and the kamikaze. A related earlier drink, the harpoon, combined vodka, cranberry and lime and was promoted by Ocean Spray in the 1960s.
