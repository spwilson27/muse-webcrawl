Vladimir Borisovich Kramnik (born 25 June 1975) is a Russian chess grandmaster. He was the Classical World Chess Champion from 2000 to 2006 and the undisputed World Chess Champion from 2006 to 2007. He is renowned for his positional style, his mastery of endgames, and for ending Garry Kasparov's fifteen-year reign as world champion.

Early career

Kramnik, a graduate of the Botvinnik–Kasparov chess school, made a sensation at the 1992 Manila Olympiad, scoring 8½/9 as an untitled reserve for the Russian team at Kasparov's insistence. He became a grandmaster the same year and soon entered the world top five. In 1995 he served as Kasparov's second in the world championship match against Viswanathan Anand.

World championship

In 2000, in London, Kramnik defeated Kasparov 8½–6½ without losing a game, neutralising his opponent with the Berlin Defence to the Ruy Lopez — a line he thereby returned to elite popularity. He defended the Classical title against Peter Leko in 2004 (drawn match, champion retained) and won the 2006 reunification match against FIDE champion Veselin Topalov in Elista, despite the "toiletgate" controversy, thereby becoming the first undisputed champion since 1993. He lost the title to Anand in the 2007 Mexico City tournament, and failed to regain it in their 2008 match in Bonn.

Later career and retirement

Kramnik won the Chess World Cup in 2013, three Olympiad gold medals with Russia, and elite tournaments including Dortmund (a record ten times), Linares and the Tal Memorial. He came close to another title challenge at the 2013 Candidates Tournament, finishing level with Magnus Carlsen but behind on tiebreak. In January 2019 he announced his retirement from professional chess, citing health and motivation, and has since worked on chess in education and on research into online cheating, becoming a prominent public critic of cheating in internet chess.
