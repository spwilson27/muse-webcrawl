Digital Domain

Type: Subsidiary
Industry: Visual effects, animation
Founded: 1993
Founders: James Cameron, Stan Winston, Scott Ross
Headquarters: Playa Vista, Los Angeles, California

Digital Domain is an American visual effects and digital production company based in Playa Vista, Los Angeles, California. Founded in 1993 by director James Cameron, special-effects artist Stan Winston, and former Industrial Light & Magic general manager Scott Ross, the company became one of the leading visual-effects houses in Hollywood, known for photorealistic digital characters and environments.

History

The company began its first work in 1993 with films such as True Lies (1994), Interview with the Vampire (1994), and Apollo 13 (1995). Its work on Titanic (1997) and What Dreams May Come (1998) won Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects, as did The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (2008), for which it pioneered a fully computer-generated head performance for Brad Pitt.

Ownership changes

Digital Domain went public in 2011 as Digital Domain Media Group, but filed for bankruptcy in September 2012. Its core visual-effects business was sold to a joint venture between China's Galloping Horse and India's Reliance MediaWorks. In 2016, Hong Kong-listed Digital Domain Holdings acquired the remaining stake in the company. The studio has since expanded into virtual reality, virtual production, and digital humans, with facilities in Los Angeles, Vancouver, Montreal, Hyderabad, and elsewhere.
