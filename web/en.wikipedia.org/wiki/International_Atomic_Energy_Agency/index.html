International Atomic Energy Agency

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) is an intergovernmental organisation that seeks to promote the peaceful use of nuclear energy and inhibit its use for military purposes, including nuclear weapons. Established in 1957 following US President Eisenhower's "Atoms for Peace" speech (1953), headquartered in Vienna, it forms part of the UN system (reporting to the General Assembly and, on non-compliance, the Security Council) while remaining an autonomous organisation by treaty — its dual mandate, promoter and policeman of the atom, is written into its structure.

Safeguards and safety

Under the Non-Proliferation Treaty, non-nuclear-weapon states accept IAEA safeguards: declared nuclear material is accounted for and inspected (cameras, seals, environmental sampling, and — since the 1990s Iraq and North Korea revelations — Additional Protocol access intended to detect undeclared activities). The agency's findings fed the defining proliferation dossiers of the era (Iraq pre-2003, Iran's programme and the JCPOA verification regime, North Korea's expulsion of inspectors). After Chernobyl (1986) and Fukushima (2011) its safety standards and peer-review missions expanded; the Zaporizhzhia occupation (2022–) produced an unprecedented IAEA presence at an active-war nuclear plant. The agency and its Director General Mohamed ElBaradei shared the 2005 Nobel Peace Prize.
