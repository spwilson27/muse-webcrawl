Seguidilla

The seguidilla is a traditional Spanish song and dance form — and the poetic metre in which its lyrics are written — originating in La Mancha and Castile and known since the 16th century. It became one of Spain's most popular folk dances and an important ingredient of the zarzuela and of classical music drawing on Spanish themes.

The dance and music

The seguidilla is danced by couples in triple time (usually 3/4 or 3/8), at a lively tempo, accompanied by guitar, bandurrias, and castanets, with singing interspersed with instrumental interludes during which the dancers perform intricate steps and pauses. Regional variants include the seguidilla manchega of La Mancha, the seguidillas sevillanas of Seville (a staple of ferias), and forms in Murcia, Valencia, and the Canary Islands. Its brisk, graceful character also led it into ballet and opera — a seguidilla appears in Bizet's Carmen, and the form underlies the famous dance in de Falla's The Three-Cornered Hat.

The verse form

As poetry, the seguidilla is a stanza of seven lines alternating heptasyllabic (seven-syllable) and pentasyllabic (five-syllable) lines — a long-short pattern (7-5-7-5-5-7-5) with assonant rhyme in the even lines — giving the sung coplas their characteristic lilt. The metre is one of the oldest and most widespread in Spanish folk song, and survives in flamenco's siguiriya only in a much-transformed, more solemn guise (despite the similar name, the two are distinct).
