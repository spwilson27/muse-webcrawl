The Prefontaine Classic is an annual track and field meeting held in Eugene, Oregon, United States. Named after the American distance runner Steve Prefontaine, it is one of the premier athletics meets in North America and a stop on the Diamond League circuit.

The meeting originated in 1973 as the Hayward Field Restoration Meet, organized to raise funds for renovating the west grandstand at Hayward Field, the track at the University of Oregon. After Prefontaine's death in a car accident in 1975, the meet was renamed in his honor; Prefontaine had competed in the early editions of the event and was a beloved figure in Eugene, where he had starred for the Oregon Ducks.

History

Over the decades, the Prefontaine Classic has hosted many of the sport's biggest stars and produced numerous world-leading performances. The meet is particularly famous for its distance races, above all the Bowerman Mile, named for legendary Oregon coach Bill Bowerman, which has repeatedly drawn elite milers from around the world. The meet also traditionally features the 10,000 metres and other distance events, reflecting Prefontaine's own specialty.

In 2019 the meet was temporarily held at Stanford University in California while Hayward Field was rebuilt. The reconstructed stadium reopened and the meet returned to Eugene in 2021. In 2023 the Prefontaine Classic hosted the Diamond League final, the first time the season-ending event was held outside Europe.

Notable performances

  • The Bowerman Mile has seen multiple sub-3:50 performances and has at times featured fields with several Olympic and World Championship medalists.
  • The two miles and 10,000 metres races at the meet have produced American records and world-leading times.
  • The meet regularly attracts reigning Olympic champions across sprint, distance, and field events.
