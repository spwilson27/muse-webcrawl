Simplified Technical English

ASD Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100) is a controlled language developed originally for the aerospace industry's maintenance documentation. It is a carefully limited and standardised subset of English, defined by a dictionary of approved words and a set of writing rules, intended to make technical texts unambiguous and easier to understand for non-native speakers and to reduce translation cost and error.

History

Work began in the late 1970s when the European Association of Aerospace Industries (AECMA) sought a standard for aircraft maintenance manuals: much maintenance was performed by mechanics for whom English was a second language, and ambiguous instructions carried safety risks. The first guide appeared in the 1980s; after AECMA merged into the AeroSpace and Defence Industries Association of Europe (ASD) in 2004, the specification was renamed ASD Simplified Technical English, issue reference ASD-STE100. New issues of the specification are released periodically by the ASD STEMG (Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group).

Rules and dictionary

STE consists of two parts:

  • Writing rules — around fifty rules governing grammar and style: use short sentences (generally no more than 20 words in instructions), one instruction per sentence, the active voice, the present tense where possible, no more than three nouns in a noun cluster, and explicit articles and punctuation.
  • Dictionary — a controlled vocabulary of roughly 900 general approved words, each with a single approved meaning and part of speech (for example, "follow" means "come after" and may not be used to mean "obey"). Technical names and technical verbs specific to a product may be used in addition when defined by the user's terminology.

Use

Although created for aerospace, STE has been adopted in defence, land and sea vehicles, medical devices, and other industries producing complex maintenance and operating documentation. Software "checkers" assist authors by flagging non-approved words and rule violations.
