Lloyd's Register (LR) is a global professional services company specialising in engineering and technology for the maritime industry. It is the world's first marine classification society, created more than 260 years ago to improve the safety of ships, and provides compliance, risk management and technical consultancy services across the marine, offshore and energy sectors. The company is wholly owned by the Lloyd's Register Foundation, a UK charity dedicated to engineering-related research and education.

History

The organisation takes its name from Lloyd's Coffee House in London, a meeting place for merchants, ship owners and underwriters from the late 1680s. In 1760, the customers of the coffee house formed a committee and printed a register of ships — recording their condition, hulls, equipment and masters — so that underwriters and merchants could judge the risks of vessels they insured or chartered. The Register of Ships was first published in 1764, and its classification grades (including the famous "A1") entered everyday language as a byword for first-class quality.

During the nineteenth century the society developed rules for the construction of iron and later steel ships, expanded its surveyor network to ports worldwide, and in 1834 was reconstituted as Lloyd's Register of British and Foreign Shipping. In the twentieth century its work extended to offshore structures, land-based industry, rail, and quality management systems certification.

Activities

  • Classification: setting and verifying rules for the design, construction and through-life maintenance of ships and offshore assets
  • Compliance services: statutory certification on behalf of flag states, including safety and environmental conventions
  • Consultancy and software: risk assessment, asset performance management and digital tools for fleet operators
  • Training and inspection services across energy and industrial supply chains

Relationship to other "Lloyd's" bodies

Lloyd's Register is entirely separate from Lloyd's of London, the insurance market, and from Lloyds Bank; the three share only the historical association with Edward Lloyd's coffee house.
