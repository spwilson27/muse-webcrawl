Buk missile system
Type: Medium-range surface-to-air missile system
Origin: Soviet Union / Russia
NATO reporting name: SA-11 Gadfly / SA-17 Grizzly
In service: 1980–present

The Buk missile system (Russian: "Бук", meaning "beech") is a family of self-propelled, medium-range surface-to-air missile systems developed by the Soviet Union and the Russian Federation. Successor to the Kub (SA-6) system, it is designed to engage aircraft, cruise missiles, helicopters, and unmanned aerial vehicles.

Design

A Buk battery typically comprises a command vehicle, a target acquisition radar, several transporter erector launcher and radar (TELAR) vehicles — each carrying four radar-guided missiles and able to operate semi-independently — and loader-launcher vehicles. Successive versions (Buk-M1, M2, M3) have increased range, altitude coverage, and the number of simultaneously engaged targets; engagement ranges reach roughly 45–70 km in later variants.

Service history

The system has been exported widely and used in several conflicts, including the Russo-Georgian War, the war in Donbas, and the Russian invasion of Ukraine. A Buk TELAR of the Russian 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade was determined by the Dutch-led Joint Investigation Team to have fired the missile that destroyed Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 over eastern Ukraine on 17 July 2014, killing all 298 people aboard.
