Textile manufacturing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Rows of power looms in a nineteenth-century cotton mill]

Textile manufacturing or textile engineering is a major industry based on the conversion of fibres into yarn, then fabric, and then textiles which are dyed, printed, or fabricated into clothing and other goods. Different types of fibres are used to produce yarns, principally cotton, flax, wool, and silk, alongside a large range of synthetic fibres.

Processes

Manufacturing proceeds through several stages. Fibres are cleaned and carded to align them, then spun into yarn. Yarn is converted into fabric by weaving, knitting, crocheting, felting, or bonding. Wet processing — scouring, bleaching, dyeing, and printing — gives fabric its colour and finish, after which it is cut and sewn into finished products.

History

Textile production was among the first industries to be mechanised during the Industrial Revolution. Inventions such as the flying shuttle, spinning jenny, water frame, spinning mule, and power loom transformed production in Britain's cotton mills in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, and the factory system spread worldwide. Today the largest manufacturers are in Asia, including China, India, Bangladesh, and Vietnam.

Environmental issues

The industry is a significant source of water pollution from dyeing and finishing, of microplastic fibres from synthetic garments, and of waste associated with fast fashion.
