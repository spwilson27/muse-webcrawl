Patrick Stewart, 2nd Earl of Orkney


Born: c. 1566/67
Died: 6 February 1615, Edinburgh
Title: Earl of Orkney (1593–1614)
Father: Robert Stewart, 1st Earl of Orkney

Patrick Stewart, 2nd Earl of Orkney (c. 1566/67 – 6 February 1615), known as "Black Patie," was a Scottish nobleman who ruled Orkney and Shetland with notorious harshness in the early seventeenth century. He succeeded his father, Robert Stewart, an illegitimate son of James V, as Earl of Orkney in 1593.

Rule

Patrick consolidated power over the Northern Isles, feuding with local lairds and with the Lawman and leading families of Shetland. He is remembered for oppressive taxation, forced labour, and manipulation of the islands' Norse-derived udal law to dispossess landholders. His great building projects included the Earl's Palace at Birsay's successor — the Earl's Palace in Kirkwall, completed around 1607 — and Scalloway Castle in Shetland (1600), both raised with coerced labour and now prominent ruins.

Downfall

Complaints against his tyranny led to his arrest in 1609 and imprisonment in Edinburgh. While he was confined, his illegitimate son Robert Stewart raised a rebellion in Orkney in 1614 and seized Kirkwall Castle; the rising was suppressed by the Earl of Caithness, and Robert was executed. Patrick himself was tried for high treason and beheaded in Edinburgh on 6 February 1615. The earldom was forfeited and Orkney and Shetland were annexed to the Scottish crown's direct administration.

Legacy

Black Patie remains a central figure of Orcadian and Shetland folklore, remembered as a archetypal tyrant; his palaces survive as monuments of his ambition and oppression.
