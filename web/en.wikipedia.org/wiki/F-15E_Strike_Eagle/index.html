The McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) F-15E Strike Eagle is an American all-weather multirole strike fighter derived from the McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle, itself designed in the 1970s as a dedicated air superiority fighter. The F-15E was designed for long-range, high-speed interdiction of enemy ground targets deep behind front lines, without needing escort or electronic-warfare aircraft from other aircraft. It carries a crew of two — a pilot and a weapon systems officer — and can carry over 10,400 kg (23,000 lb) of weapons on conformal and fuselage stations while retaining the F-15's full air-to-air capability.

Design and development

The Strike Eagle began as a company-funded demonstrator, the first production F-15E flying on 11 December 1986. Structural strengthening, conformal fuel tanks, the APG-70 attack radar, and LANTIRN navigation and targeting pods distinguished it from the single-seat F-15C. Deliveries to the United States Air Force began in 1988, and the type achieved initial operational capability in 1989.

Operational history

The F-15E saw its combat debut in the 1991 Gulf War, flying night deep-strike missions, Scud-hunting patrols and close air support, and it has served in every major US air campaign since, including over the Balkans, Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria. Export variants include the F-15I Ra'am for Israel, the F-15S and F-15SA for Saudi Arabia, the F-15K Slam Eagle for South Korea and the F-15SG for Singapore. The single-seat F-15EX Eagle II, ordered by the USAF from 2020, draws heavily on the Strike Eagle's airframe and the advanced F-15QA developed for Qatar.

Specifications (approximate)

  • Crew: 2
  • Length: 19.43 m (63 ft 9 in)
  • Wingspan: 13.05 m (42 ft 10 in)
  • Powerplant: 2 × Pratt & Whitney F100-PW-220 or -229 turbofans
  • Maximum speed: Mach 2.5+ at altitude
  • Combat range: about 1,270 km (790 mi) on interdiction missions
  • Armament: 1 × 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon; AIM-9 and AIM-120 air-to-air missiles; a wide range of precision-guided and unguided bombs including JDAM, Paveway and the GBU-39 Small Diameter Bomb
