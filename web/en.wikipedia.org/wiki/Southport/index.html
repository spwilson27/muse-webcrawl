Southport

Southport is a seaside town in the Metropolitan Borough of Sefton, Merseyside, England. It lies on the West Lancashire coastal plain on the Irish Sea coast, about 27 kilometres (17 miles) north of Liverpool. At the 2021 census the town's population was around 94,000.Historically part of Lancashire, Southport developed as a Victorian holiday resort and remains known for its shopping street Lord Street, its pier, and events such as the Southport Flower Show and the Southport Air Show.

History

The area was settled from the Domesday era, with villages such as Churchtown and Birkdale recorded in 1086. The modern town began in 1792 when William Sutton built a bathing house and hotel at South Hawes, promoting sea-bathing for wealthy visitors from Manchester and Liverpool. A canal link (and later, from 1848, a railway) brought mass tourism; Southport grew rapidly, laying out wide tree-lined streets, gardens, and a promenade. Lord Street's cast-iron canopies and arcades are said to have inspired Napoleon III's rebuilding of Paris after his exile stay in the town.

Southport Pier, opened in 1860, was the first pleasure pier built with iron in Britain and, at over 1,000 metres, remains one of the longest. The town hosted the lifeboat disaster of 1886, when 27 lifeboatmen from Southport and St Annes died rescuing the crew of the barque Mexico — commemorated by memorials and by the charity legacy that helped found what became the "lifeboat" fundraising tradition of street collections.

Attractions and culture

Modern attractions include Pleasureland, the Lakeside Miniature Railway, the Model Railway Village, and the British Lawnmower Museum. Royal Birkdale Golf Club, in the sand dunes south of the town, is one of the venues of The Open Championship and is often ranked among the world's finest links courses. The town is also associated with comedian Ken Dodd, who had a home nearby, and with the Red Rum stables in the wider Sefton area. Southport FC, nicknamed the Sandgrounders, play at Haig Avenue.

2024 stabbings

On 29 July 2024, a mass stabbing at a children's dance class in Southport killed three young girls and injured ten others. The attack was followed by violent disorder in Southport and other English towns, fuelled by false online claims about the attacker. The perpetrator was later convicted of murder and sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term exceeding 50 years.
