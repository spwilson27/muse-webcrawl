Edward VII

Edward VII (Albert Edward; 9 November 1841 – 6 May 1910) was King of the United Kingdom and the British Dominions, and Emperor of India, from 22 January 1901 until his death in 1910. The eldest son of Queen Victoria and Prince Albert, he was Prince of Wales for nearly sixty years, longer than any predecessor at the time.

Reign

As king, Edward fostered good relations between Britain and other European countries, especially France, for which he was popularly called "the Peacemaker" — he was closely associated with the Entente Cordiale of 1904. The Edwardian era, which bears his name, coincided with the start of the twentieth century and heralded significant changes in technology and society, including the rise of socialism and the Labour Party, the suffragette movement and Irish nationalism. Edward involved himself in the modernisation of the British armed forces and the reform of the army after the Second Boer War.

Personal life

In 1863 Edward married Alexandra of Denmark, with whom he had six children. As Prince of Wales he enjoyed a pleasure-loving social life and was involved in society scandals, including the Mordaunt divorce case and the Tranby Croft baccarat affair. He was succeeded by his second son, George V.
