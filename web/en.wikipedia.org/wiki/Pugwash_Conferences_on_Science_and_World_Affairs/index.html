The Pugwash Conferences on Science and World Affairs is an international organisation that brings together scholars and public figures to work toward reducing the danger of armed conflict and to seek cooperative solutions to global security problems. Taking as its starting point the Russell–Einstein Manifesto of 1955 — which called on scientists to evaluate the perils of weapons of mass destruction — the first conference was held in July 1957 at the summer home of philanthropist Cyrus Eaton in Pugwash, Nova Scotia, Canada.

History

The Pugwash meetings created one of the few channels of communication between Western and Soviet scientists during the Cold War. Participants attend as individuals rather than as representatives of governments, allowing frank private discussion of sensitive issues including nuclear arms control, verification, and later chemical and biological weapons, missile proliferation, and regional conflicts. Pugwash discussions contributed background to the Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963), the Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968), the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty (1972), and the Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions.

Structure and recognition

Conferences are held at least annually, with workshops on specific topics and national Pugwash groups in many countries. In 1995 the Nobel Peace Prize was awarded jointly to the Pugwash Conferences and to Joseph Rotblat, a physicist who had been one of the movement's founders and its leading figure for decades.
