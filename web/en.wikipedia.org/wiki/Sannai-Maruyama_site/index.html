The Sannai-Maruyama site (三内丸山遺跡, Sannai-Maruyama iseki) is an archaeological site in the city of Aomori, northern Japan, containing the ruins of one of the largest and best-preserved settlements of the Jōmon period. Occupied for roughly 1,700 years, from about 3900 to 2200 BCE, the village overturned older assumptions that Jōmon hunter-gatherers lived only in small, transient bands, and revealed a large, organised, sedentary community.

Discovery

The site had been known since the Edo period — Jōmon pottery and clay figurines from the area were recorded by visitors in the 17th century — but its scale became clear only in 1992, when excavations preceding construction of a prefectural baseball stadium uncovered extensive settlement remains. Public and scholarly outcry led Aomori Prefecture to cancel the stadium in 1994 and preserve the site; it was designated a Special Historic Site of Japan in 2000 and inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2021 as part of the "Jōmon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan".

Findings

Excavations uncovered the post-hole foundations of hundreds of pit dwellings, raised-floor storehouses, burials (including roadside graves and clay-jar burials for children), middens, and a large six-pillared structure — reconstructed on site — thought to have supported a tower or ceremonial building. More than 1,600 clay figurines (dogū), mostly fragmentary, were found, along with jade ornaments, lacquered objects, obsidian, and one of Japan's largest assemblages of Jōmon artefacts.

Evidence of diet and environment includes huge quantities of chestnut remains, suggesting the inhabitants managed chestnut groves, along with walnut, fish bones and deer remains. A remarkable find was a waterlogged wooden artefact and lacquerware preserved in a marshy area, together with what appear to be flattened boards or planks.

Site museum

The site is now an open-air park with reconstructed dwellings and the great pillared structure, served by the Jōmon Jiyūkan museum, which displays excavated artefacts and explains Jōmon life. Nearby stands the Aomori Museum of Art, whose design and siting were influenced by the excavation trenches of Sannai-Maruyama.
