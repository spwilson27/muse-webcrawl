Vocal trance

Vocal trance is a subgenre of trance music characterised by melodic, emotive tracks built around sung vocals — typically a featured female or male vocalist performing complete verses and choruses, rather than the sampled vocal fragments of much electronic dance music. It combines the long breakdowns, arpeggiated synth lines, and euphoric builds of uplifting trance with the songwriting structure of pop, and it has been the most commercially accessible form of trance since the late 1990s.

Characteristics

Vocal trance tracks are usually produced at 128–142 BPM in a major key, with lush pads, piano, and a lead vocal mixed prominently over the instrumental. Lyrics commonly treat love, longing, and transcendence. The genre's classic format places the full vocal in an extended breakdown, followed by a climax in which voice and synths combine. British and Dutch producers and labels (including Anjunabeats and Armada) drove the style; acts such as Above & Beyond (notably with their OceanLab project) made the female-vocal-led emotional track a signature.

History and artists

Early landmarks include tracks by Chicane ("Saltwater", 1999, with Máire Brennan) and the wave of commercial hits around 2000–2004 such as Ian Van Dahl's "Castles in the Sky" and Lasgo. Vocalists such as Audrey Gallagher, Emma Hewitt, Sarah Howells, and Christina Novelli became stars in their own right, and singers like Jes and Nadia Ali (with iiO's "Rapture") crossed into the pop charts. Though the wider trance scene moved through tech- and psy-influenced phases, vocal trance remained a festival mainstay and the dominant style on trance radio shows such as Armin van Buuren's A State of Trance.
