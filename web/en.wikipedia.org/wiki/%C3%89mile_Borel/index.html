Émile Borel

Félix Édouard Justin Émile Borel (7 January 1871 – 3 February 1956) was a French mathematician and politician. A founder of measure theory and its applications to probability, he made fundamental contributions to analysis, set theory, and game theory. Borel sets, the Borel measure, and the Heine–Borel theorem bear his name. He also served as a deputy and as Minister of the Navy in 1925.

Mathematical career

Borel studied at the École Normale Supérieure and defended his doctorate in 1893, aged 22. His 1898 work Leçons sur la théorie des fonctions introduced the measurable sets now called Borel sets, a cornerstone of modern analysis and probability. He also worked on divergent series (Borel summation), function theory, and, in a series of papers from 1921 to 1927, gave the first modern treatment of strategic games, anticipating parts of von Neumann's minimax theorem.

Public life

Borel founded the Institut Henri Poincaré in Paris and the journal Revue du Mois. Entering politics with the Radical Party, he sat in the Chamber of Deputies from 1924 to 1936 and served briefly as Minister of the Navy in 1925. During the Second World War he worked with the French Resistance; he was arrested by the Gestapo in 1941 and later released.

Legacy

Besides the concepts bearing his name, Borel is commemorated by the Centre Émile Borel in Paris and a lunar crater. His popular writings on probability, including discussions of the infinite monkey theorem (the "Borel's law" notion that sufficiently improbable events never occur), reached a wide audience.
