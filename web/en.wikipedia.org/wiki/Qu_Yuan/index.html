Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BCE) was a Chinese poet and aristocrat in the State of Chu during the Warring States period. He is known for his contributions to classical poetry and verses, especially through the poems in the anthology Chu Ci (Songs of Chu). He is also remembered as the first known person to be associated with the origin of the Dragon Boat Festival.

Biography

Qu Yuan was a member of the Chu royal clan and served as a minister under King Huai of Chu. He advocated political reforms, including an alliance with the State of Qi against the expansionist State of Qin. Slandered by jealous courtiers, he fell from favour and was exiled. In 278 BCE, when Qin forces captured the Chu capital of Ying, Qu Yuan is said to have composed his final lament and drowned himself in the Miluo River. According to legend, local people raced out in boats to save him or to scare fish away from his body — the origin of dragon boat races — and threw wrapped rice into the water, the origin of zongzi.

Works

The Chu Ci anthology preserves works attributed to Qu Yuan, most famously the long autobiographical poem Li Sao ("Encountering Sorrow"), in which the poet, personified as a wronged and fragrant hero, wanders heaven and earth seeking a worthy lord. Other attributed works include the Nine Songs (Jiu Ge), adapted from shamanic hymns, and the cosmic question-poem Heavenly Questions (Tian Wen).

Legacy

Qu Yuan has been venerated for two millennia as a paragon of patriotic loyalty and poetic integrity. The Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu), held on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month, is popularly celebrated in his memory. In 1953 he was recognised by the World Peace Council as one of four world cultural celebrities.
