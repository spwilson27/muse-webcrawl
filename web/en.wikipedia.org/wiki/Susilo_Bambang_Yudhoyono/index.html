Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono (born 9 September 1949), commonly known by his initials SBY, is an Indonesian politician and retired army general who served as the sixth president of Indonesia from 2004 to 2014. He was the first Indonesian president elected directly by the people, winning the country's first direct presidential election in 2004 and re-election in 2009.

Early life and military career

Born in Pacitan, East Java, Yudhoyono graduated from the Indonesian Military Academy in 1973 and rose through the army's ranks, serving in East Timor and on peacekeeping duty in Bosnia. He earned a master's degree and later a doctorate in agricultural economics, and retired from the military in 2000 to enter government.

Presidency

His administration is credited with steady economic growth, democratic consolidation, the 2005 Aceh peace agreement ending the long insurgency in that province, and counter-terrorism efforts following the Bali bombings. He was succeeded by Joko Widodo in 2014. Yudhoyono later founded and led the Democratic Party and remains an elder statesman; his son Agus Harimurti Yudhoyono also entered politics.
