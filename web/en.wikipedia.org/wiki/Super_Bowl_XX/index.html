Super Bowl XX was an American football game between the National Football Conference (NFC) champion Chicago Bears and the American Football Conference (AFC) champion New England Patriots to decide the National Football League (NFL) champion for the 1985 season. The Bears defeated the Patriots by the score of 46–10, capturing their first NFL championship since 1963. The game was played on January 26, 1986, at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans.

The Bears entered the game with a 17–1 record (including playoffs), powered by defensive coordinator Buddy Ryan's aggressive "46 defense," which had allowed the fewest points in the league. The Patriots, a wild-card team at 11–5, had won three road playoff games to reach their first Super Bowl.

Game summary

New England scored first on a field goal after a Walter Payton fumble, but it was the Patriots' only lead. Chicago scored 44 unanswered points, including rushing touchdowns by fullback Matt Suhey and quarterback Jim McMahon (two), and a memorable one-yard touchdown plunge by 300-pound defensive tackle William "The Refrigerator" Perry. The Bears defense sacked Patriots quarterbacks Tony Eason and Steve Grogan seven times and held New England to a then-record-low seven rushing yards. Defensive end Richard Dent, with 1.5 sacks and two forced fumbles, was named Most Valuable Player.

At the time, the 36-point margin was the largest in Super Bowl history, and the Bears' 46 points set a Super Bowl record. The 1985 Bears are widely ranked among the greatest single-season teams in NFL history, and the team also became a cultural phenomenon through the novelty song and music video "The Super Bowl Shuffle," recorded during the season.
