Erik Hilding Carlsson (5 March 1929 – 27 May 2015) was a Swedish rally driver for Saab. He won the RAC Rally three times in a row (1960–1962) and the Monte Carlo Rally twice (1962 and 1963), achievements that brought great publicity to the small Swedish manufacturer and to the front-wheel-drive Saab 96 with its two-stroke engine. He was nicknamed "Carlsson på taket" ("Carlsson on the roof") for his habit of rolling his car in a controlled way to get through difficult corners, and "Mr. Saab" for his lifelong association with the company.

Career

Carlsson worked as a test driver for Saab and began rallying in the early 1950s. Despite his Saab's considerable power disadvantage against larger-engined rivals, his mastery of left-foot braking and of the front-wheel-drive car's handling made him a dominant force in winter and gravel events. His major victories included the RAC Rally of 1960, 1961 and 1962, the Monte Carlo Rally of 1962 and 1963, the Acropolis Rally, and the Liège–Sofia–Liège marathon.

In 1964 he married Pat Moss, one of the most successful female rally drivers of the era and the younger sister of Stirling Moss. After retiring from competition Carlsson worked as an ambassador and public-relations representative for Saab for many years.
