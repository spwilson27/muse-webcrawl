The Battle of Shusha (Armenian: Շուշիի ազատագրում; Azerbaijani: Şuşanın işğalı) was the capture on 8–9 May 1992 of the town of Shusha during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War by Armenian forces of the self-proclaimed Nagorno-Karabakh Republic. The operation, codenamed "Wedding in the Mountains", removed the Azerbaijani artillery position from which the nearby city of Stepanakert had been bombarded for months.

Shusha occupies a commanding plateau overlooking Stepanakert.

Background

Shusha, a historic fortress town on a plateau, was the last Azerbaijani-held stronghold inside the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast. From late 1991 Azerbaijani forces used it to shell Stepanakert, the Karabakh Armenian capital a few kilometres below, including with Grad multiple rocket launchers, causing heavy civilian losses and hardship during the blockade winter of 1991–92.

The battle

Under the overall command of Arkady Ter-Tadevosyan ("Komandos"), Armenian forces attacked on the night of 8 May 1992 from several directions, scaling the cliffs and entering the town in fighting that lasted into 9 May. The defenders, outnumbered and cut off, abandoned Shusha and retreated toward Lachin. Armenian accounts put their own dead at several dozen, with Azerbaijani losses higher.

Aftermath

The capture of Shusha was a turning point in the war: within days Armenian forces opened the Lachin corridor, linking Nagorno-Karabakh to Armenia. In Azerbaijan the loss of the town — a cultural centre revered by Azerbaijanis — was a national trauma. Shusha remained under Armenian control until it was retaken by Azerbaijan in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War in November 2020.
