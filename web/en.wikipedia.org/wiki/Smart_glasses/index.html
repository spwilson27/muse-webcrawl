Smart glasses

Smart glasses are wearable computing devices in the form of eyeglasses that add information alongside or over what the wearer sees. Many models superimpose a display in the user's field of vision — effectively a head-up display — while others provide audio output, cameras, or sensors without a visual display. Smart glasses can be controlled by voice commands, touchpads on the frame, or paired devices.

History

Early head-worn displays were developed for military and research use from the 1960s onward. The category entered public attention with Google Glass, released to "Explorers" in 2013; its camera provoked privacy controversy and the consumer version was withdrawn in 2015, though an enterprise edition continued in industrial and medical settings until 2023. Subsequent products include Microsoft's HoloLens mixed-reality headsets, Snap's Spectacles camera glasses, and camera-and-audio glasses such as the Ray-Ban Meta line, which omit a display but add photography, speakers and AI assistants.

Applications

Smart glasses are used in warehousing and manufacturing for hands-free instructions and barcode scanning, in surgery and telemedicine, for navigation and translation overlays, and as accessibility aids that describe scenes or read text aloud for visually impaired users. Optical see-through displays compete with camera-based "video passthrough" approaches used by larger mixed-reality headsets.

Privacy and criticism

The ability to record video discreetly has raised privacy concerns, leading some venues to ban camera glasses; later models added recording indicator lights. Battery life, weight, social acceptability and limited fields of view remain persistent engineering and design challenges.
