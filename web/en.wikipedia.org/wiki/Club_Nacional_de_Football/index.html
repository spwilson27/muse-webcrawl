Club Nacional de Football

Full name: Club Nacional de Football

Founded: 14 May 1899

Ground: Estadio Gran Parque Central, Montevideo

Capacity: 34,000

League: Uruguayan Primera División

Nicknames: El Bolso, Los Albos, El Decano, Tricolores

Club Nacional de Football (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈkluβ nasjoˈnal ðe ˈfuðβol], "National Football Club") is a Uruguayan professional sports club based in Montevideo, Uruguay. Founded on 14 May 1899 as the merger of Uruguay Athletic Club and Montevideo Football Club, it is the oldest football club in Uruguay and was created by local Creole players in reaction to the foreign-dominated clubs of the era. Nacional is one of the most successful clubs in South American football, and its rivalry with Peñarol is the Uruguayan Clásico.

History

Nacional won its first Uruguayan championship in 1902 and has since accumulated dozens of Primera División titles. The club formed the backbone of the Uruguay national team that won the first FIFA World Cup in 1930, which was hosted at venues including Nacional's own Gran Parque Central, and contributed players to the 1924 and 1928 Olympic gold-medal teams and the 1950 World Cup winners.

International honours

Nacional has won the Copa Libertadores three times (1971, 1980 and 1988) and the Intercontinental Cup in each of those same years, defeating European champions including Panathinaikos, Nottingham Forest and PSV Eindhoven. The club has also won the Copa Interamericana and the Recopa Sudamericana. In total, Nacional holds a large collection of international titles, rivalling its domestic dominance.

Stadium

The Estadio Gran Parque Central, opened in 1900 and repeatedly rebuilt, hosted matches of the 1930 World Cup, including the first match in World Cup history. Nacional also uses the Estadio Centenario for major fixtures.
