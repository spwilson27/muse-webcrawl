Wherries

A wherry is a type of boat traditionally used for carrying cargo or passengers on rivers and shallow waterways. The term covers several distinct regional types in England, most famously the Norfolk wherry — a large sailing barge of the Norfolk Broads — and the lighter Thames passenger wherries rowed by watermen on the River Thames in London.

Norfolk wherry

The Norfolk trading wherry was a clinker-built, single-masted sailing vessel, typically 12–18 m (40–60 ft) long, with a huge gaff sail of black canvas and a mast that could be lowered to pass under bridges. At its peak in the 19th century hundreds carried grain, timber, coal and other goods between Norwich, Great Yarmouth and Lowestoft. Almost all disappeared with the coming of railways and motor transport; a handful survive in preservation, including the pleasure wherry Hathor (1905) and the wherry yacht White Moth.

Thames wherry

On the Thames, wherries were the water taxis of pre-industrial London, rowed by licensed watermen organised in the Company of Watermen and Lightermen; the craft inspired the light racing "wager wherries" from which modern sculling shells evolved.
