Symbolic order

In psychoanalytic theory, particularly in the work of Jacques Lacan, the Symbolic order (also simply "the Symbolic") is one of the three orders of the psyche, alongside the Imaginary and the Real. It is the order of language, law, culture and social structures through which the subject is constituted.

Concept

Lacan adapted the term from structural anthropology and linguistics. Entry into the Symbolic occurs when the child acquires language and submits to the "Name-of-the-Father", the symbolic function representing law and prohibition. The subject is thereafter spoken by the signifier as much as it speaks: the unconscious, in Lacan's formula, is "structured like a language".

The big Other

The Symbolic is closely tied to the "big Other", the locus of language, law and social convention to which speech is addressed and by which the subject is recognised. Desire and identity are mediated through this register rather than belonging to the individual alone.
