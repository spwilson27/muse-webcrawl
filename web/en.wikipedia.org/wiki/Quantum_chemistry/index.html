Quantum chemistry

Quantum chemistry, also called molecular quantum mechanics, is the branch of physical chemistry that applies quantum mechanics to chemical systems — above all to calculate the electronic structure of atoms and molecules and thereby predict their properties, reactivity and spectra. Its calculations range from highly accurate ab initio treatments of small molecules to approximate models used in drug design and materials science.

History

The field is usually dated to 1927, when Walter Heitler and Fritz London explained the covalent bond in the hydrogen molecule — the same year brought the Burrau treatment of H₂⁺ and the Born–Oppenheimer approximation, which separates electronic from nuclear motion. Valence bond theory (associated with Linus Pauling) and molecular orbital theory (Friedrich Hund, Robert Mulliken) developed as complementary pictures. From the 1960s, digital computers transformed the field, and John Pople's Gaussian programs made routine calculations feasible; the 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Pople and to Walter Kohn for density-functional theory.

Methods

The central problem is solving the electronic Schrödinger equation under the Born–Oppenheimer approximation. Hartree–Fock theory gives a mean-field starting point; post-Hartree–Fock methods such as Møller–Plesset perturbation theory, configuration interaction and coupled cluster capture electron correlation at increasing cost. Density functional theory (DFT), based on the Hohenberg–Kohn theorems and the Kohn–Sham equations, is the most widely used approach because of its favourable balance of accuracy and cost. Semi-empirical methods and molecular mechanics serve for very large systems such as proteins.

Applications

Quantum chemistry predicts molecular geometries, bond energies, reaction barriers and transition states, vibrational and electronic spectra, and properties such as dipole moments and NMR shifts. It is integral to computational drug discovery, catalysis research, photochemistry and the design of battery and photovoltaic materials.
