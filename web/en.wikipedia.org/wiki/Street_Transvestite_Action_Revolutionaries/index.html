Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries

Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries (STAR) was an organization founded in New York City in 1970 by Sylvia Rivera and Marsha P. Johnson. It was one of the first organizations in the United States run by and for homeless transgender and gender-nonconforming youth, particularly street queens and trans people of color who had been marginalized within the gay liberation movement.

History

Rivera and Johnson, both veterans of the 1969 Stonewall uprising, founded STAR in the autumn of 1970, in part after Rivera's activism within the Gay Activists Alliance highlighted the exclusion of trans people and drag queens from mainstream gay organizations. STAR initially operated as a caucus and protest group, advocating for homeless queer youth, sex workers, and prisoners.

In November 1970, STAR opened STAR House in the East Village, often described as the first LGBTQ+ youth shelter in the United States. Rivera and Johnson funded the house through sex work, and provided food, clothing, and shelter to homeless queer and trans youth. The house operated for a period of months before the residents were evicted in 1971.

Legacy

Though short-lived, STAR is credited with pioneering approaches to queer youth homelessness and mutual aid, and with articulating an intersectional politics linking trans liberation with anti-poverty, anti-prison, and anti-racist struggles. Rivera revived the group's spirit in 2001 with Street Transgender Action Revolutionaries. STAR House is commemorated as a landmark in LGBTQ history.
