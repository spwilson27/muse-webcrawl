Urban ecology

Cities are ecosystems shaped by both natural and human processes

Urban ecology is the scientific study of the relation of living organisms with each other and their surroundings in urban environments. It examines how plants, animals, and ecological processes persist and function in cities — in parks, vacant lots, waterways, green roofs, and built structures — and how urbanisation alters temperature, hydrology, soil, and species composition.

Key topics

Characteristic phenomena of urban ecosystems include the urban heat island effect, habitat fragmentation, altered nutrient cycles, and communities shaped by introduced species. Some species, such as peregrine falcons, coyotes, and many weeds, thrive in cities, while specialists decline. Urban ecology also has a social-science branch studying how humans interact with urban nature, informing policies on green infrastructure, biodiversity conservation, and environmental justice.

History

The field grew from early studies of city flora and fauna and from the Chicago school of sociology, and it matured as a distinct ecological discipline in the late 20th century with long-term urban ecological research sites such as those in Baltimore and Phoenix.
