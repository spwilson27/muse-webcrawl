Rail transport in Israel comprises a standard-gauge intercity network operated by the state-owned Israel Railways, plus the light rail/tram systems of Jerusalem and Tel Aviv and the Carmelit underground funicular in Haifa. The railway has its origins in the Ottoman-era Hejaz Railway branches and the British Mandate's Palestine Railways; after 1948 the network was rebuilt and repeatedly extended by the young state.

Israel Railways

The core of the network is the coastal main line from Nahariya through Haifa and Tel Aviv to Ashkelon and Beersheba. Major modern extensions include the high-speed electrified Tel Aviv–Jerusalem line via Ben Gurion Airport (opened 2018), the Jezreel Valley line to Beit She'an (2016), and the Ashkelon–Beersheba line. A system-wide electrification programme converted most of the network from diesel to 25 kV electric operation by the early 2020s, allowing new double-decker and multiple-unit rolling stock to raise speeds and frequency.

Urban rail

Jerusalem's light rail Red Line opened in 2011; Tel Aviv's Red Line, Israel's first metro-scale light rail line with an underground section, opened in 2023, with further lines under construction. Haifa's Carmelit, opened in 1959 and rebuilt several times, is a short underground funicular serving the Carmel ridge — Israel's oldest urban rail transit. Cross-border connections to Jordan and the Palestinian territories have long been proposed but not built.
