Relational grammar

Relational grammar (RG) is a theory of syntax and grammar developed by David Perlmutter and Paul Postal in the 1970s. It focuses on the grammatical relations — subject, direct object and indirect object — as primitive notions of linguistic theory, rather than defining them in terms of phrase structure configurations.

Basic ideas

In relational grammar, clauses are analysed as networks of relations arranged in strata. Nominals bear grammatical relations numbered 1 (subject), 2 (direct object), 3 (indirect object), as well as oblique and other relations. Constructions such as passivisation are described as changes in the relations a nominal bears between strata (e.g. a 2 advancing to 1).

Universals and laws

The theory proposed universal constraints such as the Stratal Uniqueness Law and the Relational Succession Law, and generated a large body of cross-linguistic work in the 1970s and 1980s. Although later largely displaced by other frameworks, it directly influenced the development of arc pair grammar and, indirectly, aspects of lexical functional grammar.
