Imola Circuit

The layout of the Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari

The Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari, better known as Imola Circuit, is a 4.909 km (3.050 mi) motor racing circuit in the town of Imola, in the Emilia-Romagna region of Italy, 40 km (25 mi) east of Bologna. It is one of the few major international circuits to run in an anti-clockwise direction. The circuit is named after Enzo Ferrari and his son Dino.

History

Racing at Imola began on roads around the town in the early 1950s; the permanent circuit was inaugurated in 1953. Formula One first visited for a non-championship race in 1979, and the circuit hosted the Italian Grand Prix in 1980 before taking over the San Marino Grand Prix from 1981 to 2006.

1994 San Marino Grand Prix

The 1994 race weekend became one of the darkest in Formula One history: Roland Ratzenberger was killed in qualifying on 30 April, and three-time world champion Ayrton Senna died after crashing at the Tamburello corner during the race on 1 May. The tragedies prompted sweeping safety changes in Formula One and led to chicanes being added at Tamburello and Villeneuve, fundamentally altering the circuit's character.

Return to Formula One

Imola returned to the Formula One calendar in 2020 as the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix, initially as a one-off during the COVID-19-disrupted season, and remained on the calendar in subsequent years. The circuit also hosts rounds of the Superbike World Championship, touring car racing, and the 6 Hours of Imola endurance race.
