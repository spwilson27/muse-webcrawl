Connachta

[Image placeholder: map of Connacht, Ireland]

The Connachta were a group of dynasties claiming descent from the semi-legendary Conn Cétchathach ("of the Hundred Battles"), whose lines came to dominate the western and northern parts of Ireland, above all the province of Connacht, from roughly the 5th century AD onward. The Connachta are conventionally divided into the Uí Briúin, Uí Fiachrach and Uí Ailello.

History

The Uí Briúin produced the kings of Connacht and, through the Sil Muiredaig line, the O'Connors (Ó Conchobhair), who were among the most powerful Irish dynasties of the later medieval period and supplied the last High Kings of Ireland, including Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair (died 1198). The Uí Fiachrach divided into branches centred on Aidne and Muaidhe. The northern Connachta, including the Cenél Conaill and Cenél nEógain (the Northern and Southern Uí Néill), conquered and settled much of the north of Ireland. As a tribal label, "Connachta" gave its name to the modern province of Connacht (Connaught).
