The Constitution of Afghanistan (Dari: قانون اساسی افغانستان) was the supreme law of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan. Adopted by the Constitutional Loya Jirga in Kabul on 4 January 2004 and signed by President Hamid Karzai on 26 January 2004, it provided the legal framework of the Afghan state until the collapse of the republic in August 2021, after which the Taliban stated that they would govern by Islamic law and parts of earlier constitutional arrangements pending a new settlement.

The 2004 Constitution

The constitution established a strongly centralised presidential system: the president was both head of state and head of government, elected with two vice presidents for a five-year term, and served as commander-in-chief. The National Assembly consisted of the Wolesi Jirga (lower house, 249 members) and the Meshrano Jirga (upper house). At least 27% of Wolesi Jirga seats were reserved for women. The document declared Afghanistan an Islamic republic, provided that no law could contradict the beliefs and provisions of Islam, guaranteed equality of citizens and a catalogue of rights, and recognised Pashto and Dari as official languages. A Supreme Court headed the judiciary, and an Independent Election Commission administered elections.

Historical constitutions

Afghanistan has had several constitutions. King Amanullah Khan promulgated the first in 1923, part of his modernising programme; successors followed in 1931 under Nadir Shah and in 1964 under Zahir Shah — the latter widely regarded as a liberal-democratic high point, creating a constitutional monarchy with an elected parliament and independent judiciary. The republican constitution of 1977, the communist-era documents of 1980 and 1987, and the Taliban's draft of the 1990s followed, before the 2001 Bonn Agreement set the process leading to the 2004 constitution in motion.

Status after 2021

After taking Kabul in August 2021, Taliban officials announced that the 2004 constitution was suspended, while suggesting that provisions not contrary to Sharia from the 1964 constitution might be applied temporarily. Governance has since proceeded by decrees of the supreme leader and the movement's interpretation of Islamic law, without a formally promulgated constitution.
