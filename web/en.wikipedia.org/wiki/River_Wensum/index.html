The River Wensum is a chalk river in Norfolk, England. It rises near the villages of Colkirk and Whissonsett in north Norfolk and flows east and south-east through Fakenham, Dereham's outskirts and the city of Norwich, where it joins the River Yare.

Course

Unlike many Norfolk rivers, the Wensum is relatively long and its valley cuts through chalk, giving rise to a string of watermills including those at Fakenham, Pensthorpe and Swanton Morley. In Norwich the river passes the cathedral, Cow Tower and the medieval bridges of Bishop Bridge, Fye Bridge and Whitefriars Bridge before meeting the Yare at Whitlingham.

Ecology

The Wensum is designated a Special Area of Conservation and Site of Special Scientific Interest for its chalk-stream plant communities, including water crowfoot, and for species such as the white-clawed crayfish, brook lamprey and bullhead. It is a noted barbel and chub fishery. Abstraction, siltation and phosphate pollution have damaged the river, prompting major restoration projects.
