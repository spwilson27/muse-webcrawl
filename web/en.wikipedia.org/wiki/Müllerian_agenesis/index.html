Müllerian agenesis

SpecialtyGynaecology, medical genetics
Also known asMayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser (MRKH) syndrome
FrequencyAbout 1 in 4,500 female births

Müllerian agenesis, also known as Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser syndrome (MRKH syndrome), is a congenital condition in which the Müllerian ducts fail to develop, resulting in a missing uterus and upper part of the vagina in individuals with a normal 46,XX karyotype and normal ovaries. It affects approximately 1 in 4,500 newborn girls.

Signs and symptoms

Because the ovaries function normally, affected individuals develop typical secondary sexual characteristics at puberty. The condition usually comes to attention in adolescence when menstruation fails to begin (primary amenorrhoea). The external genitalia are normal, while the vagina may be shortened or absent.

Types and associated features

Type I involves isolated absence of the uterus and upper vagina. Type II is associated with other malformations, including renal anomalies (such as unilateral renal agenesis), skeletal abnormalities (especially of the spine), hearing defects, and, more rarely, heart and limb anomalies. Type II overlaps with the MURCS association.

Treatment

Treatment focuses on creating a functional vagina, most often by non-surgical dilation therapy or, if that fails, surgical vaginoplasty. Fertility with one's own genetic offspring is possible through in vitro fertilisation with a gestational carrier, and uterine transplantation has resulted in live births in some cases since 2014.
