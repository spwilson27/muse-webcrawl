Kalemegdan Park

Kalemegdan Park (Serbian: Калемегдански парк, Kalemegdanski park) is the largest park and the most important historical monument in Belgrade, Serbia. It occupies the area surrounding the Belgrade Fortress on a cliff at the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers, in the municipality of Stari Grad.

History

The name Kalemegdan derives from the Turkish words kale (fortress) and meydan (field or square). The open ground in front of the fortress was kept clear for military reasons during the Ottoman period; after Serbia gained control of the fortress in 1867, Prince Mihailo Obrenović ordered the area to be landscaped as a public park. Planting began in the 1870s and continued into the early twentieth century.

Features

The park is divided into the Great Kalemegdan and the Little Kalemegdan. It contains numerous monuments and statues of prominent Serbs, the Victor monument (Pobednik) erected in 1928 to commemorate Serbia's victories in the Balkan Wars and First World War, the Military Museum, the Belgrade Zoo, sports courts, and promenades overlooking the rivers. It is the most visited tourist attraction in Belgrade.
