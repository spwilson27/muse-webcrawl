Rodgers and Hart

Rodgers and Hart were an American songwriting partnership between composer Richard Rodgers (1902–1979) and the lyricist Lorenz Hart (1895–1943). They worked together on 28 stage musicals and more than 500 songs from 1919 until Hart's death in 1943. Their songs, including "My Funny Valentine", "The Lady Is a Tramp", "Bewitched, Bothered and Bewildered" and "Blue Moon", became standards of the Great American Songbook.

History

Rodgers and Hart met in 1919 through a mutual friend; Rodgers was sixteen, Hart twenty-three and a Columbia graduate. Their first Broadway success came with the revue The Garrick Gaieties (1925), whose hit "Manhattan" launched their career.

Musicals

Their principal shows include A Connecticut Yankee (1927, "Thou Swell"), On Your Toes (1936, featuring the "Slaughter on Tenth Avenue" ballet), Babes in Arms (1937, "My Funny Valentine", "The Lady Is a Tramp", "Johnny One Note"), The Boys from Syracuse (1938, "Falling in Love with Love"), and Pal Joey (1940, "I Could Write a Book", "Bewitched"). In Hollywood they wrote Love Me Tonight (1932, "Isn't It Romantic?").

End of the partnership

Hart's alcoholism and depression made him increasingly unreliable. After By Jupiter (1942), Rodgers began working with Oscar Hammerstein II. Hart died of pneumonia in November 1943, days after the opening of a revival of A Connecticut Yankee for which he had written new lyrics.
