The 1983 Code of Canon Law (Latin: Codex Iuris Canonici) is the comprehensive codification of the canon law of the Latin Church of the Catholic Church. Promulgated by Pope John Paul II on January 25, 1983, with the apostolic constitution Sacrae Disciplinae Leges, it took effect on November 27, 1983, replacing the 1917 Code promulgated by Benedict XV.

History

Pope John XXIII announced a revision of canon law alongside his convocation of the Second Vatican Council in 1959. Because the Council's teaching was to shape the new code, the commission of experts worked throughout and after the Council (1962–1965), guided by principles approved by the Synod of Bishops in 1967, including greater subsidiarity, protection of rights, and a reduced emphasis on penalties. Successive schemata were circulated to bishops and universities worldwide before the final text was completed under John Paul II.

Structure

The Code contains 1,752 canons in seven books: (I) General Norms; (II) The People of God; (III) The Teaching Function of the Church; (IV) The Sanctifying Function of the Church; (V) The Temporal Goods of the Church; (VI) Sanctions in the Church; and (VII) Processes. Among its notable changes were the articulation of the rights and obligations of all the Christian faithful, a streamlined marriage-nullity process, permission in defined cases for lay ministry, and the reduction of latae sententiae penalties.

Related law

Eastern Catholic Churches are governed by the separate Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches (1990). The 1983 Code has since been amended by several motu proprios, including Omnium in Mentem (2009), Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus (2015), and Pascite Gregem Dei (2021), which extensively revised Book VI on penal law.
