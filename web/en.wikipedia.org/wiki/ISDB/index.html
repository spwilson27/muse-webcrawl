ISDB

Integrated Services Digital Broadcasting (ISDB; Japanese: 統合デジタル放送サービス) is a Japanese broadcasting standard for digital television and digital radio. It was developed by NHK and standardized by the Association of Radio Industries and Businesses (ARIB). ISDB took over from the analog MUSE Hi-Vision system in Japan and was later adopted, in modified form, by most countries in South America.

Variants

The ISDB family includes ISDB-T for terrestrial television, ISDB-S for satellite, ISDB-C for cable, and the 1seg system for mobile reception, which uses one segment of the ISDB-T signal. A defining feature of ISDB-T is band-segmented transmission using orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing: the channel is divided into 13 segments, which can be allocated flexibly between high-definition, standard-definition, and mobile services within a single broadcast.

Adoption

Digital terrestrial broadcasts using ISDB-T began in Japan in December 2003, and analog broadcasting ended in most of the country in July 2011. Brazil adopted a Japanese-Brazilian variant, ISDB-Tb (marketed domestically as SBTVD), in 2006, adding MPEG-4 video and interactivity; this variant was subsequently chosen by most other Latin American countries and several countries elsewhere, including the Philippines and Botswana.
