Oersted

The oersted (symbol Oe) is the unit of the magnetising field (H-field) in the centimetre–gram–second (CGS) system of units. It is named after the Danish physicist Hans Christian Ørsted, who in 1820 discovered that an electric current deflects a compass needle, the first demonstration of the connection between electricity and magnetism known as electromagnetism.

Definition

One oersted is defined as one dyne per unit magnetic pole, or equivalently in terms of current through a long straight conductor. In SI units, 1 Oe = 1000/(4π) amperes per metre, approximately 79.577 A/m. The corresponding CGS unit of magnetic flux density, the gauss, is equal in magnitude to the oersted in vacuum, which is why older literature often treats the two interchangeably in free space.

Usage

Although the SI unit (ampere per metre) is official in most contexts, the oersted remains in everyday use in magnetics engineering and in specifications for permanent magnets and magnetic recording media. The coercivity of magnetic materials, for example, is still commonly quoted in oersteds in commercial datasheets, and Earth's magnetic field strength is roughly half an oersted.
