An album is a collection of audio recordings (or, in earlier usage, of photographs or other items) issued as a single release, typically of music. In its modern sense the album became the dominant artistic unit of popular music in the mid-20th century: a body of songs conceived and sequenced by an artist, presented with cover art and liner notes, and marketed as a unified work.

History

The word comes from the Latin album ("white", a blank tablet) and was applied in the 19th century to bound books for collecting autographs, stamps and photographs. In the early 1900s, record companies sold collections of 78 rpm discs bound in book-like folders resembling photo albums — hence "record albums". A single album might contain a symphony or an operetta across several discs.

The decisive change came in 1948, when Columbia Records introduced the 12-inch long-playing (LP) record spinning at 33⅓ rpm, holding about 22 minutes of music per side. RCA answered with the 7-inch 45 rpm single. The LP's capacity allowed artists to think in terms of a coherent forty-minute programme, and by the 1960s the album had eclipsed the single as the prestige format in rock and jazz.

The album era

From the mid-1960s — landmark works by the Beatles, Bob Dylan, the Beach Boys and others — albums increasingly were created as integrated artistic statements, sometimes as explicit concept albums. Album cover art became a major graphic medium, and the album sequence of tracks a standard listening experience. Formats evolved from vinyl to cassette, compact disc (from 1982), and digital download and streaming, with "album" persisting as the industry unit for charts, awards such as the Grammy Award for Album of the Year, and royalty accounting.

Types

  • Studio album — newly recorded material by the artist
  • Live album — recordings of concert performances
  • Compilation album — previously released tracks, e.g. greatest hits
  • Tribute / cover album — versions of another artist's songs
  • Soundtrack album — music from a film, show or game
  • Remix album — re-worked versions of existing recordings
