Radio-controlled clock

A radio-controlled clock, often called an "atomic clock" in consumer marketing, is a clock that periodically synchronises itself to a time signal broadcast by radio from a reference station linked to an atomic clock standard. Such clocks keep time to within a fraction of a second of the legal time scale.

Time signals

Major long-wave time signal stations include WWVB in Fort Collins, Colorado (United States, 60 kHz), DCF77 in Mainflingen, Germany (77.5 kHz), MSF in Anthorn, United Kingdom (60 kHz), and JJY in Japan (40 and 60 kHz). China, Russia and other countries operate similar services. Shortwave stations such as WWV and CHU broadcast time signals as well.

Operation

A typical radio-controlled clock receives and decodes the signal once a day, usually at night when long-wave propagation is best, and corrects any drift of its quartz movement. Multi-band receivers can decode several stations and are sold as travel clocks and wristwatches.
