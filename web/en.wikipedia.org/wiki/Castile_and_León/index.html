Castile and León

Castile and León (Spanish: Castilla y León) is an autonomous community in northwestern Spain, the largest in the country and one of the largest first-level regions in the European Union. It was formed in 1983 from the historic regions of Old Castile and León and comprises nine provinces: Ávila, Burgos, León, Palencia, Salamanca, Segovia, Soria, Valladolid and Zamora.

Geography

The community occupies the northern Meseta, a high plateau drained by the Duero (Douro) river and ringed by mountain ranges including the Cantabrian Mountains, the Sistema Central and the Montes de León. Its continental climate brings cold winters and hot, dry summers.

Heritage

Castile and León is exceptionally rich in historic monuments: the cathedrals of Burgos, León and Salamanca, the Roman aqueduct of Segovia, the walls of Ávila and the Romanesque churches along the Camino de Santiago, which crosses the region. Several of its sites hold UNESCO World Heritage status, and the region is a major producer of Ribera del Duero and Rueda wines. See Castile.
