The 1985 Chicago Bears season was the franchise's 66th season in the National Football League. Coached by Mike Ditka, the Bears finished the regular season 15–1 and capped the year by defeating the New England Patriots 46–10 in Super Bowl XX. The 1985 Bears are widely considered one of the greatest teams in NFL history, chiefly because of their dominant "46" defence coordinated by Buddy Ryan.

The Bears' 46 defence, led by Mike Singletary, allowed the fewest points in the league.

Season summary

Chicago won its first twelve games before losing to the Miami Dolphins on Monday Night Football — the Dolphins preserving their 1972 team's status as the only undefeated champions. Quarterback Jim McMahon directed an offence built around running back Walter Payton, who rushed for 1,551 yards, while rookie defensive tackle William "The Refrigerator" Perry became a cultural phenomenon, scoring a touchdown in the Super Bowl. The team also recorded the novelty hit "The Super Bowl Shuffle", released before the playoffs.

Playoffs

The Bears shut out both the New York Giants (21–0) and the Los Angeles Rams (24–0) in the NFC playoffs, then overwhelmed New England in Super Bowl XX on January 26, 1986, at the Louisiana Superdome. Defensive end Richard Dent was named the game's most valuable player. Across the season the Bears outscored their opponents by a combined margin rarely matched in league history, and their defence — featuring Dent, Dan Hampton, Steve McMichael, Mike Singletary, Otis Wilson and Gary Fencik — allowed just 198 points in the regular season.
