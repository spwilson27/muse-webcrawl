If This Is a Man (Italian: Se questo è un uomo) is a memoir by Italian-Jewish writer Primo Levi, first published in 1947. It describes his arrest by Fascist forces in 1943, his imprisonment at Auschwitz concentration camp, and his survival until the camp's liberation in January 1945. In the United States it was published under the title Survival in Auschwitz.

Background

Levi, a chemist from Turin, was deported to Auschwitz in February 1944 and assigned to the Monowitz subcamp, where his chemistry training helped him obtain work that improved his chances of survival. After liberation he returned to Italy and wrote the book in the immediate postwar period; it was initially issued by a small publisher and only reached a wide audience after republication by Einaudi in 1958.

Content and style

The memoir is noted for its restrained, analytical prose. Levi examines how the camp system dehumanised prisoners, describes the struggle for survival, and reflects on moral questions such as the "grey zone" between victims and collaborators. Its title comes from a poem by Levi, also known as the Shema, which prefaces the book.

Reception

The book is regarded as one of the most important works of Holocaust literature and of twentieth-century Italian writing, and is widely taught in schools.
