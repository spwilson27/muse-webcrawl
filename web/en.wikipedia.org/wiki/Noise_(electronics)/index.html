Noise (electronics)

In electronics, noise is an unwanted disturbance in an electrical signal — a sum of unwanted or disturbing energy introduced into a system from natural and man-made sources. Noise limits the minimum signal a circuit can usefully amplify and is characterised together with the signal by the signal-to-noise ratio.

Types

  • Thermal (Johnson–Nyquist) noise — random voltage fluctuations caused by the thermal agitation of charge carriers in a conductor; present in every resistor.
  • Shot noise — fluctuations arising because electric current consists of discrete charges crossing a barrier, as in diodes and transistors.
  • Flicker (1/f) noise — noise whose power density falls with frequency, dominant at low frequencies in many devices.
  • Burst noise — sudden step-like transitions between levels, seen in some semiconductors.
  • Interference — external coupling from power lines, radio transmitters and other equipment, reduced by shielding and layout.

Measurement and reduction

Noise performance of amplifiers and receivers is expressed by quantities such as noise figure and equivalent noise temperature. Design techniques to minimise noise include careful grounding, shielding, twisted-pair wiring, filtering and choosing low-noise components for the first amplification stage, whose noise dominates the system total.
