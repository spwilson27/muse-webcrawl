Topography

FieldGeography, geodesy, cartography
FocusSurface features and relief of terrain

Topography is the study of the forms and features of land surfaces. It concerns the relief of an area — its hills, valleys and slopes — together with natural and artificial features such as rivers, roads and settlements, and the methods used to measure and depict them.

Methods

Traditionally, topography was recorded through ground surveying with instruments such as the theodolite, level and chain, and represented on topographic maps using contour lines joining points of equal elevation, along with spot heights and shading. Modern topographic data are gathered using total stations, satellite navigation (GNSS), aerial photography, photogrammetry, radar and lidar, and are stored as digital elevation models (DEMs) within geographic information systems.

Applications

Topographic information is used in civil engineering, construction, land-use planning, military operations, geology, hydrology, environmental management and recreation such as hiking and orienteering. The word derives from the Greek topos ("place") and graphein ("to write").
