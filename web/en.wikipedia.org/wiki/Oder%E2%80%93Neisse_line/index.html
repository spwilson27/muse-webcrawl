Oder–Neisse line

The Oder–Neisse line is the border between Germany and Poland, running along the Oder and Lusatian Neisse rivers. Established in the aftermath of the Second World War, it placed the former German territories east of the rivers under Polish administration.

Origins

The line was agreed in principle at the Potsdam Conference of 1945, compensating Poland in the west for territory annexed by the Soviet Union in the east. Millions of German inhabitants of Silesia, Pomerania, and East Prussia fled or were expelled westward, while Poles — many themselves displaced from the eastern territories — resettled the region.

Recognition

East Germany recognized the border in the Treaty of Zgorzelec (1950), but West Germany long treated it as provisional pending a final peace settlement, a stance that generated lasting political controversy. The Treaty of Warsaw (1970) saw West Germany accept the line's inviolability, and after reunification the German–Polish Border Treaty of 1990 confirmed it definitively as Germany's eastern frontier.

Today

The Oder–Neisse line now forms part of the eastern boundary of the European Union and the Schengen Area. Divided cities such as Görlitz/Zgorzelec and Frankfurt (Oder)/Słubice have become symbols of cross-border cooperation.
