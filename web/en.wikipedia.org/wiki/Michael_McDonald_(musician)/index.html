Michael McDonald (musician)

Born: 12 February 1952, Ferguson, Missouri, U.S.

Genres: Blue-eyed soul, soft rock, pop

Instruments: Vocals, keyboards

Associated acts: The Doobie Brothers, Steely Dan

Michael H. McDonald (born February 12, 1952) is an American singer, songwriter, and keyboardist, known for his soulful baritone voice and for his work as a member of the Doobie Brothers (1975–1982, 2019–present) and as a background vocalist for Steely Dan. He has won five Grammy Awards.

Early career

McDonald grew up in Ferguson, Missouri, and played in local bands before moving to Los Angeles in 1970. He joined Steely Dan's touring band in 1973 and sang background vocals on albums including Katy Lied, The Royal Scam, and Aja, his voice prominent on tracks such as "Peg". In 1975 he joined the Doobie Brothers after Tom Johnston's illness, reshaping the band's sound toward soul- and jazz-inflected soft rock.

Doobie Brothers and solo success

With the Doobies, McDonald wrote and sang "Takin' It to the Streets", "It Keeps You Runnin'", "Minute by Minute", and "What a Fool Believes" — the latter, co-written with Kenny Loggins, won the Grammy Award for Song of the Year in 1980. His solo debut, If That's What It Takes (1982), produced the hit "I Keep Forgettin' (Every Time You're Near)". His duets "Yah Mo B There" with James Ingram (1983) and "On My Own" with Patti LaBelle (1986) both reached number one or won Grammy Awards. In the 2000s he released the best-selling Motown tribute albums. McDonald rejoined the Doobie Brothers for their 50th-anniversary tour and the album Liberté (2021), and he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the band in 2020.

Voice and legacy

McDonald's husky baritone and keyboard-driven songwriting made him a defining voice of the "yacht rock" era, a label later revived by the web series of the same name, in which he was a recurring character. His 2023 memoir, What a Fool Believes, co-written with Paul Reiser, recounts his career and struggles with substance abuse.
