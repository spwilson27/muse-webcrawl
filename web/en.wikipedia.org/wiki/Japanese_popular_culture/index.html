Japanese popular culture

ConceptsKawaii, otaku, Cool Japan

Japanese popular culture includes the popular forms of entertainment produced in Japan and recognized globally, most prominently manga (comics), anime (animation), video games, popular music (J-pop), television drama and variety, fashion, and cuisine. Since the late twentieth century these exports have become one of Japan's most visible cultural influences worldwide.

Manga and anime

Post-war manga, reshaped by Osamu Tezuka, grew into a huge domestic publishing industry spanning every demographic, and anime series and films — from Astro Boy (1963) and Mobile Suit Gundam (1979) to the films of Studio Ghibli and the global franchises of Pokémon, Dragon Ball, and One Piece — achieved worldwide audiences. Fan conventions, cosplay, and scanlation culture spread the forms long before official licensing caught up.

Video games

Japanese companies Nintendo, Sega, Sony (PlayStation), Capcom, Konami, Bandai Namco, and Square Enix shaped the modern video game industry, creating iconic characters such as Mario, Sonic the Hedgehog, Link, and Pikachu, and genres from the JRPG to survival horror.

Music, idols, and fashion

J-pop is dominated by the idol system — groups such as AKB48 and Johnny's groups — alongside rock, hip-hop, and the Vocaloid phenomenon of Hatsune Miku. Japanese fashion, from Harajuku street styles (Japanese street fashion) to avant-garde designers, has exerted international influence, while the aesthetic of kawaii ("cute"), epitomized by Sanrio's Hello Kitty, pervades consumer culture.

Soft power

The Japanese government has promoted these exports under the "Cool Japan" banner since the 2000s, following journalist Douglas McGray's 2002 essay "Japan's Gross National Cool," treating pop culture as an instrument of soft power and tourism (including "contents tourism" to real locations featured in anime).
