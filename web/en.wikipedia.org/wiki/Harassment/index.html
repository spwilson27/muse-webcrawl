Harassment

Harassment
TypesVerbal, psychological, sexual, online
Related conceptsBullying, stalking, intimidation

Harassment covers a wide range of behaviours of an offensive nature. It is commonly understood as behaviour that demeans, humiliates or embarrasses a person, and it is characteristically identified by its unreasonableness in terms of social and moral reasonableness. In the legal sense, it is behaviour that appears to be disturbing or threatening and that is unwanted or unwelcome.

Types

Forms of harassment include verbal harassment (derogatory remarks, insults, epithets), psychological harassment such as mobbing in the workplace, sexual harassment — which in many jurisdictions is a form of unlawful discrimination when it creates a hostile environment or is tied to employment decisions — and online harassment or cyberharassment, including doxing and coordinated abuse on social media.

Legal treatment

Many countries criminalise repeated harassing conduct causing alarm or distress, such as the United Kingdom's Protection from Harassment Act 1997, and provide civil remedies and restraining orders. Employment and anti-discrimination law in the United States, the European Union and elsewhere prohibits harassment based on protected characteristics including sex, race, religion, disability and age. Schools and employers are commonly required to maintain policies to prevent and address harassment.
