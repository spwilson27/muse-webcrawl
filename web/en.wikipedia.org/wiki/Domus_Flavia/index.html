Domus Flavia

The Domus Flavia is the modern name given to the public wing of the vast imperial palace built by the emperor Domitian on the Palatine Hill in Rome. Completed in 92 AD, it formed the official, ceremonial part of the Palace of Domitian, adjoining the more private residential wing known as the Domus Augustana. The name "Flavia" derives from the Flavian dynasty, to which Domitian belonged.

History

Domitian commissioned his architect Rabirius to replace earlier republican and early imperial houses on the Palatine with a monumental palace complex. The Domus Flavia was inaugurated in 92 AD and served as the setting for imperial audiences, state banquets, and court ceremony for centuries; later emperors continued to reside in and modify the palace.

Description

The wing was organised around a large peristyle garden. Its principal rooms included:

  • The Basilica, a large hall probably used for audiences and judicial proceedings, with an apse at one end.
  • The Aula Regia, a vast throne room where the emperor received embassies and held court, flanked by the basilica and the Lararium.
  • The Lararium, a smaller room possibly used for a select audience chamber or shrine.
  • The Cenatio, the great state dining room (triclinium), opening onto the peristyle and an oval fountain.

The rooms were richly decorated with coloured marbles and towering columns, designed to impress visitors with the power and divinity of the emperor.

Excavations

The ruins visible today are largely the brick-faced concrete cores of the walls, stripped of their marble revetment in later centuries. Excavations have revealed the plan of the palace and fragments of its decoration, some of which are displayed in the Palatine Museum.
