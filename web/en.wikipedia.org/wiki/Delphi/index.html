Delphi

Delphi (Greek: Δελφοί) is an ancient sanctuary and archaeological site in central Greece, on the slopes of Mount Parnassus above the Gulf of Corinth. It was home to the famous Oracle of Delphi and the Pythian Games, and was regarded by the Greeks as the centre (omphalos, "navel") of the world.

The Oracle

Consulted for over a thousand years, the oracle spoke through the Pythia, a priestess of Apollo who delivered often enigmatic prophecies in the Temple of Apollo, interpreted by priests. States and individuals consulted Delphi before founding colonies, waging wars and passing laws; famous responses include those to Croesus of Lydia and to Athens before Salamis (the "wooden walls"). The sanctuary was also said to stand where Apollo slew the serpent Python.

Site and games

The Sacred Way winds past treasuries of city-states (notably the Athenian Treasury), the Stoa of the Athenians, theatre and stadium. Every four years the Pythian Games—second only to the Olympics—combined athletic and musical contests. The Delphic Amphictyony, a league of tribes, administered the sanctuary.

Decline and excavation

Delphi declined with Christianity; the oracle is traditionally said to have been silenced under Theodosius I in the late 4th century CE. French excavations from 1892 (the "Great Excavation") relocated the village of Kastri and uncovered the site; the Charioteer of Delphi bronze is in its museum. Delphi is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
