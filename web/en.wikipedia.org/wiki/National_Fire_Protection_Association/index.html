The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) is a U.S.-based international nonprofit organization founded in 1896 devoted to eliminating death, injury, property, and economic loss due to fire, electrical, and related hazards. The NFPA publishes more than 300 consensus codes and standards intended to minimise the possibility and effects of fire and other risks, which are administered by more than 250 technical committees composed of approximately 9,000 volunteers.

History

The NFPA grew out of efforts in the 1890s to standardise automatic fire sprinkler installations, whose inconsistent designs hampered fire fighting in New England factories. A committee of representatives of insurers, manufacturers, and fire services agreed on a uniform standard in 1896, and the association was formally established that year in Boston. It soon broadened its scope to building construction, electrical installations, and fire prevention generally.

Codes and standards

Well-known NFPA documents include:

  • NFPA 1, Fire Code
  • NFPA 13, Standard for the Installation of Sprinkler Systems
  • NFPA 70, National Electrical Code
  • NFPA 101, Life Safety Code
  • NFPA 704, the "fire diamond" hazard identification system for emergency responders
  • NFPA 921, Guide for Fire and Explosion Investigations

NFPA standards are developed through an ANSI-accredited consensus process with public input and comment, and are typically revised on three-to-five-year cycles. Many are adopted into law by state and local jurisdictions in the United States and referenced internationally.

NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code)

First published in 1897, the National Electrical Code is the benchmark for safe electrical design, installation, and inspection in the United States. It is adopted in all 50 states and forms the basis of electrical regulation across much of the Americas; its requirements are enforced by local authorities having jurisdiction.

Other activities

The NFPA conducts fire research through its Fire Protection Research Foundation, collects incident statistics such as the annual firefighter fatality and fire loss reports, and sponsors Fire Prevention Week each October, commemorating the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. Its mascot, Sparky the Fire Dog, was created in 1951. The association is headquartered in Quincy, Massachusetts, and maintains offices and affiliates internationally.
