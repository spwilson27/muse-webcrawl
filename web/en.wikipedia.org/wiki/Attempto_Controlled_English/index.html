Attempto Controlled English (ACE) is a controlled natural language: a precisely defined subset of English with a restricted grammar and a fixed interpretation, designed so that texts written in it can be unambiguously translated into first-order logic. ACE was developed at the University of Zurich, beginning in the mid-1990s, by Norbert Fuchs and collaborators.

Features

ACE looks like ordinary English — sentences such as "Every customer who buys a book gets a discount" — but follows a set of construction rules (governing how sentences may be combined, how references and quantifiers are used) and interpretation rules (which fix how ambiguous-looking constructions are read). The Attempto Parsing Engine (APE) translates ACE texts into Discourse Representation Structures, a variant of first-order logic, which can then be used for reasoning, querying and verification.

Applications

ACE has been used for software specifications, knowledge representation, ontology authoring (including tools that let domain experts write OWL ontologies in controlled English), legal and technical documents, and question answering over knowledge bases. It served as an input language in European research projects on the Semantic Web and automated reasoning.
