Overview

Nord Pool is a European power exchange that operates electricity markets across the Nordic and Baltic countries, the United Kingdom, and parts of central and western Europe. It is one of the world's largest marketplaces for electrical energy, offering day-ahead and intraday trading.

History

Nord Pool traces its origins to 1993, when Norway's grid operator Statnett established Statnett Marked, the first national power exchange in the deregulating Norwegian market. As the other Nordic countries liberalised their electricity sectors, the exchange expanded into a common Nordic market, adopting the name Nord Pool in 1996. Finland, Sweden, and Denmark joined over the following years, and the Baltic states were integrated after 2010.

In 2020, Euronext acquired a majority stake in the company, with the Nordic transmission system operators retaining a minority share.

Markets

Nord Pool's principal market is the day-ahead auction (Elspot), in which electricity for delivery the following day is traded and system prices are calculated for each bidding zone. Its intraday market (Elbas) allows continuous trading close to real time, helping participants balance production and consumption.
