The Trubetskoy family (Russian: Трубецкие) is a Russian princely family of Gediminid origin, descended from Dmitry Olgerdovich (died 1399 at the Battle of the Vorskla River), a son of Algirdas, Grand Duke of Lithuania. Their name derives from the town of Trubchevsk (Trubetsk), which Dmitry received as an appanage.

History

The family entered Muscovite service in the 16th century and became one of the great aristocratic houses of the Russian Empire, producing generals, statesmen, and courtiers. In the 17th century members of the family were prominent boyars; Prince Yuri Petrovich Trubetskoy served under Peter the Great and became governor of Belgorod and a senator. Prince Nikita Yurievich Trubetskoy was a field marshal and Procurator General of the Senate under Elizabeth of Russia.

Decembrists and later members

Prince Sergei Petrovich Trubetskoy (1790–1860) was a leading figure of the Decembrist revolt of 1825 and was exiled to Siberia; his wife Ekaterina famously followed him into exile. In the late imperial period, the brothers Sergei Nikolaevich Trubetskoy and Evgenii Nikolaevich Trubetskoy were noted religious philosophers. Prince Nikolai Trubetskoy (1890–1938), the linguist and a founder of the Prague school of structural linguistics and of the Eurasianist movement, was arguably the family's most influential 20th-century member. After the Russian Revolution of 1917, most members emigrated.
