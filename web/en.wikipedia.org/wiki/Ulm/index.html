Ulm (German pronunciation: [ʊlm]) is a city in the south German state of Baden-Württemberg, situated on the Danube River on the border with Bavaria, opposite its twin city of Neu-Ulm. The city, whose population is estimated at around 129,000, forms an urban district of its own and is the administrative seat of the Alb-Donau district.

Landmarks

Ulm is famous for Ulm Minster (Ulmer Münster), a Lutheran church whose 161.5-metre (530 ft) steeple, completed in 1890, is the tallest church tower in the world. Other sights include the leaning Metzgerturm (Butchers' Tower), the fifteenth-century crooked house (Schiefes Haus) in the fishermen's quarter (Fischerviertel) on the river Blau, the historic town hall with its astronomical clock, and the remains of the city walls along the Danube promenade.

History

The area was settled from the Neolithic era, and the oldest known work of figurative art, the Lion-man of Hohlenstein-Stadel carved from mammoth ivory some 40,000 years ago, was found in caves near Ulm; it is displayed in the city's museum. First mentioned in 854, Ulm became a free imperial city in the Holy Roman Empire and a wealthy centre of trade in linen and fustian. It joined the Reformation and remained a leading city of the Swabian League. The city was largely destroyed by Allied bombing in December 1944, though the Minster survived.

Notable people

Ulm is the birthplace of physicist Albert Einstein (born 1879), commemorated by a memorial at the site of his birth house. Other natives include the filmmaker and mountaineer, the painter, and — in the broader cultural memory — the "Tailor of Ulm", Albrecht Berblinger, whose failed flight attempt over the Danube in 1811 made him a byword for doomed ambition. The University of Ulm, founded in 1967, and a cluster of technology companies characterise the modern city.
