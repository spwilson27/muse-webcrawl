Italian Eritrea (Colonia Eritrea) was Italy's first colony, formally established on 1 January 1890 on the western shore of the Red Sea, in what is now Eritrea. It was named after the classical Mare Erythraeum (Red Sea).

Establishment

Italy acquired Assab Bay (1882) and Massawa (1885), expanded inland, and signed the Treaty of Wuchale with Emperor Menelik II of Ethiopia (1889) — whose disputed text Italy used to claim a protectorate. Defeat by Ethiopia at the Battle of Adwa (1896), the worst defeat of a European colonial power in Africa, fixed Eritrea's borders roughly as they remain, and ended Crispi's expansion.

Colonial rule

Governors such as Ferdinando Martini developed Asmara into a planned capital; Italians built railways (Massawa–Asmara), roads, ports and modernist architecture that later made Asmara a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2017). Eritrean ascari soldiers served in Italy's wars in Libya, Somalia and Ethiopia. Land expropriation and racial laws under Fascism deepened discrimination. Eritrea became the industrial and logistical base for the 1935–36 invasion of Ethiopia and part of Italian East Africa (1936–1941).

End

British forces defeated Italy in 1941 (Battle of Keren) and administered Eritrea until 1952, when the UN federated it with Ethiopia; annexation by Ethiopia in 1962 sparked Eritrea's independence struggle.
