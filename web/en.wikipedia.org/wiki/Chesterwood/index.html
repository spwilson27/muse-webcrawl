Chesterwood is the former summer home, studio and gardens of sculptor Daniel Chester French (1850–1931), located in the Glendale section of Stockbridge, Massachusetts. French purchased the 150-acre farm in 1896 and summered there for the last three decades of his life. It is now a museum and site of the National Trust for Historic Preservation, designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965.

The studio

The centrepiece of the estate is French's studio, designed in 1897–98 by architect Henry Bacon (who later designed the Lincoln Memorial) in consultation with the sculptor. The large, north-lit workspace contains a 30-foot-high doorway and a section of railway track in the floor, used to roll large sculptures outdoors so they could be judged in natural light at a distance. In the studio French modelled his most famous commission, the seated Abraham Lincoln for the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, D.C.; a six-foot plaster study of the statue is displayed there today.

House and gardens

The residence, a stuccoed farmhouse enlarged by French, is furnished with the family's belongings. The grounds include formal garden rooms designed by French himself — a perennial garden, a fountain terrace and woodland paths — reflecting his belief that sculpture should be considered in dialogue with landscape.

Museum

Chesterwood preserves more than 500 pieces of French's sculpture, models and drawings, and mounts an annual exhibition of contemporary outdoor sculpture, Contemporary Sculpture at Chesterwood, continuing the site's association with living artists. The property is open to the public seasonally.
