James Joseph Sylvester

James Joseph Sylvester FRS (3 September 1814 – 15 March 1897) was an English mathematician. He made fundamental contributions to matrix theory, invariant theory, number theory, partition theory, and combinatorics. He coined many of the terms used in modern mathematics, including "matrix", "discriminant" and "graph" (in the mathematical sense).

Early life

Sylvester was born in London to Jewish parents. He studied at Cambridge but, as a Jew, was unable to receive a degree or hold a fellowship under the rules then in force; he received his degrees from Trinity College Dublin instead. He briefly practised as an actuary and taught mathematics at military academies.

Career

Sylvester was professor at the Royal Military Academy, Woolwich (1855–1869), where with Arthur Cayley he developed the theory of algebraic invariants. After a period without a post, in 1877 he moved to the United States to become professor at the newly founded Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, where he founded the American Journal of Mathematics in 1878 and helped establish graduate mathematical research in America. In 1883 he returned to England as Savilian Professor of Geometry at Oxford.

Legacy

Sylvester's work underlies much of modern algebra. Sylvester's law of inertia, Sylvester matrices, Sylvester sequences and the Sylvester–Gallai theorem bear his name, as does the Sylvester Medal of the Royal Society, awarded for mathematical research.
