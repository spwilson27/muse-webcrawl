Tragic Week (Argentina)

The Tragic Week (Spanish: Semana Trágica) was a period of violence in Buenos Aires, Argentina, in January 1919, during which a metalworkers' strike was violently repressed by police, military forces, and right-wing vigilante groups. Hundreds of people were killed, and the events included the only pogrom against Jews recorded in the Americas. The repression took place under the government of President Hipólito Yrigoyen.

Background

In December 1918, workers at the Vasena metalworks in Buenos Aires went on strike demanding an eight-hour day, wage increases, and better conditions. The anarcho-syndicalist federation FORA supported the strike. Tensions in the city were heightened by fears of Bolshevism following the Russian Revolution, and employers and conservative groups accused the strikers of plotting a revolution.

Events

On 7 January 1919, police fired on picketing workers near the factory, killing several. At the funerals on 9 January, police again opened fire on the procession, killing mourners and bystanders. A general strike paralyzed the city, and the government deployed the army. The Liga Patriótica Argentina, a nationalist vigilante organization, attacked workers' organizations and carried out raids in the Jewish neighborhood of Once, beating and murdering residents and burning synagogues and libraries, amid claims of a supposed Jewish-Bolshevik conspiracy. Estimates of the dead range from several hundred to around a thousand, with many more injured and arrested.

Aftermath

The strike was eventually settled with some concessions, but the repression marked a turning point in Argentine labor history and in the politics of the Yrigoyen era. The Tragic Week is frequently cited as an early example of state and paramilitary violence against the labor movement in Argentina.
