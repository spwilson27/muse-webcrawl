Chandrasekhar–Fermi method

The Chandrasekhar–Fermi method, proposed by S. Chandrasekhar and Enrico Fermi in 1953, estimates the strength of the plane-of-sky magnetic field in an interstellar cloud from the dispersion of polarization angles of dust emission or starlight.

Principle

Turbulent gas motions distort magnetic field lines; the spread in polarization position angles, combined with the gas density and velocity dispersion, gives the field strength: B ≈ Q √(4πρ) σv / σθ, with correction factor Q ≈ 0.5 from simulations. The Davis–Chandrasekhar–Fermi (DCF) method is widely used in studies of star formation.
