Poly [ADP-ribose] polymerase 1 (PARP-1) is an enzyme encoded by the PARP1 gene in humans. It is the best-known member of the PARP family of enzymes and uses NAD+ to build chains of poly(ADP-ribose) (PAR) on target proteins, a post-translational modification called PARylation.

Function

PARP1 detects DNA single-strand breaks through its zinc-finger DNA-binding domains; upon binding it becomes catalytically activated and PARylates itself and chromatin proteins such as histones, recruiting repair factors including XRCC1. PARP1 also participates in chromatin organisation, transcription and programmed cell death; excessive activation can deplete NAD+ and trigger parthanatos.

Clinical significance

PARP inhibitors (such as olaparib) exploit synthetic lethality in cancers with defective homologous recombination, notably BRCA1/2-mutant breast and ovarian cancers, and are an established class of anticancer drugs.
