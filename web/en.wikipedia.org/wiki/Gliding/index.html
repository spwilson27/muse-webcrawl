Gliding

Gliding is a recreational activity and competitive air sport in which pilots fly unpowered aircraft known as gliders or sailplanes using naturally occurring currents of rising air in the atmosphere to remain airborne. The word soaring is also used for the sport.

Gliding as a sport began in the 1920s. Initially the objective was to increase the duration of flights, but pilots soon attempted cross-country flights away from the place of launch. Improvements in aerodynamics and in the understanding of weather phenomena have allowed greater distances at higher average speeds. Long distances are now flown in good conditions using rising air from thermals, ridge lift, and lee waves.

Launching

The most common launching methods are by towing with a light aeroplane (aerotow) or by a ground-based winch. Gliders can also be launched by bungee, by auto-tow, or, in the case of motor gliders, under their own power.

Soaring flight

To stay airborne, glider pilots seek lift: thermals (columns of rising warm air), ridge lift where wind is deflected upward by terrain, and mountain waves. Modern racing gliders have glide ratios of 40:1 or better; flights of over 1,000 km and altitudes above 14,000 m have been achieved. The world governing body is the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI).
