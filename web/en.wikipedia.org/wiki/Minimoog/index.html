Minimoog

Minimoog
Manufacturer: Moog Music
Produced: 1970–1981 (original run)
Type: Analog monophonic synthesizer

The Minimoog is a monophonic analog synthesizer introduced by Moog Music in 1970. Designed by Robert Moog and engineer Bill Hemsath, it distilled the large modular Moog systems into a portable, self-contained instrument with a fixed signal path, making the synthesizer practical for live performance.

Design

The instrument features three voltage-controlled oscillators, a noise source, a mixer, the famous Moog ladder filter — a 24 dB-per-octave low-pass filter patented by Moog — a voltage-controlled amplifier, and two envelope generators. A 44-note keyboard, pitch wheel, and modulation wheel give the performer expressive control. Its fat, warm bass and lead sounds derive in part from the oscillators being driven slightly into saturation in the mixer.

Impact

The Minimoog became one of the most influential synthesizers ever made, used by artists including Wendy Carlos, Keith Emerson, Rick Wakeman, Jan Hammer, Gary Numan, and countless progressive rock, jazz fusion, funk, and electronic musicians. Around 13,000 original units were produced. Moog reissued the instrument in 2016 and again in 2022, faithful to the original design.

Legacy

The Minimoog's layout — oscillators into mixer, filter, and amplifier, modulated by envelopes — became the template for virtually every subsequent analog synthesizer, and software emulations are ubiquitous.
