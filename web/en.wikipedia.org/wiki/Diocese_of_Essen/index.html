The Diocese of Essen (Latin: Dioecesis Essendiensis; German: Bistum Essen) is a Latin Church diocese of the Catholic Church in Germany, covering the Ruhr area of North Rhine-Westphalia. It is the smallest German diocese by area but one of the most populous, serving (in round figures) the cities of Essen, Bochum, Duisburg, Oberhausen, Gelsenkirchen, Mülheim, Bottrop, Gladbeck and parts of the Ennepe-Ruhr and Märkischer districts.

History

The diocese was erected by Pope Pius XII on 23 February 1957, taking effect on 1 January 1958, from territory of the archdioceses of Cologne and Paderborn and the diocese of Münster, to serve the rapidly growing industrial population of the Ruhr. Its first bishop was Franz Hengsbach (1958–1991), who was made a cardinal in 1988. The cathedral is Essen Minster, the former church of Essen Abbey, founded around 845 by Bishop Altfrid of Hildesheim; its treasury preserves masterpieces of Ottonian goldsmith work, including the Golden Madonna of Essen (c. 980), the oldest known fully sculptural representation of the Virgin and Child north of the Alps.

Recent developments

Like other German dioceses, Essen has faced sharp declines in membership and practice; in the 2000s it carried out an extensive programme of parish mergers and church closures and conversions, attracting national attention for its redevelopment concepts. Hubert Luthe (1991–2002) and Felix Genn (2003–2008) succeeded Hengsbach; Franz-Josef Overbeck became bishop in 2009 and also serves as military bishop of Germany. The diocese is a suffragan of the Archdiocese of Cologne.
