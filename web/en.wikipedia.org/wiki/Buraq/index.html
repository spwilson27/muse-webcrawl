Buraq

The Buraq (Arabic: الْبُرَاق, "the lightning") is a winged creature in Islamic tradition that carried the prophet Muhammad on the Isra and Mi'raj — the night journey from Mecca to Jerusalem and the ascension through the heavens — as described in hadith literature. Tradition places the Buraq's tethering at the Western Wall in Jerusalem, known in Arabic as the Buraq Wall.

Description

Hadith describe the Buraq as a white animal, smaller than a mule but larger than a donkey, whose stride reached as far as the eye could see. Later art, especially Persian and Mughal miniatures, often depicts it with a human or peacock-like face, a horse's body, and wings — imagery that spread across Islamic and South Asian folk art.

Cultural legacy

The Buraq lends its name to airlines (Buraq Air of Libya, Al Boraq high-speed rail in Morocco), businesses, and works of art throughout the Muslim world.
