The Red and the Black (French: Le Rouge et le Noir) is a psychological novel in two volumes by Stendhal, published in 1830. It chronicles the attempts of Julien Sorel, a poor but brilliant young provincial, to rise above his station through talent, hypocrisy and seduction in the rigidly hierarchical France of the Bourbon Restoration.

Plot

Julien, a carpenter's son who idolises Napoleon, becomes tutor to the children of the mayor of Verrières, M. de Rênal, and has an affair with Madame de Rênal. Sent to a seminary in Besançon, he then becomes secretary in Paris to the Marquis de la Mole, whose daughter Mathilde he seduces and becomes engaged to marry after being granted a title and fortune. A letter from Madame de Rênal, dictated by her confessor, destroys his prospects; Julien shoots her in church (she survives) and is tried and guillotined, refusing to appeal. The title is usually read as contrasting the red of the army uniform with the black of clerical dress — the two paths open to an ambitious young man of his class.

Significance

Little appreciated on publication, the novel is now considered a founding work of psychological realism and one of the greatest French novels, noted for its ironic narration, free indirect style and analysis of ambition, class and desire.
