Scooby-Doo is an American animated media franchise based on the animated television series launched in 1969 and continued through many derivative media. Writers Joe Ruby and Ken Spears created the original series, Scooby-Doo, Where Are You!, for Hanna-Barbera Productions. The series features a talking Great Dane named Scooby-Doo and four teenage friends — Fred Jones, Daphne Blake, Velma Dinkley and Norville "Shaggy" Rogers — who travel in a van called the Mystery Machine solving mysteries involving supposedly supernatural creatures, invariably unmasked at the end as ordinary criminals in disguise.

Premise and characters

In each episode, the gang ("Mystery Incorporated") stumbles upon a mystery in a spooky location — a haunted mansion, abandoned amusement park or ghost town — where a monster is frightening people away. Splitting up to look for clues (Fred and Daphne one way, Velma with Shaggy and Scooby the other), they gather evidence, set a trap that usually goes comically wrong, and capture the villain, who is unmasked and explains the scheme, often lamenting that they "would have gotten away with it too, if it weren't for you meddling kids." Scooby and Shaggy, both notoriously cowardly and perpetually hungry, can be bribed with "Scooby Snacks" to act as live bait.

History

The original series ran on CBS from 1969 to 1970 for two seasons; it was created partly in response to protests against violence in Saturday-morning cartoons, offering mystery and comedy instead. Numerous spin-offs and successors followed, including The New Scooby-Doo Movies (1972–73), The Scooby-Doo Show (1976–78), Scooby-Doo and Scrappy-Doo (1979, introducing Scooby's feisty nephew), A Pup Named Scooby-Doo (1988–91), What's New, Scooby-Doo? (2002–06), Scooby-Doo! Mystery Incorporated (2010–13) and Be Cool, Scooby-Doo! (2015–18), along with dozens of direct-to-video films since 1998 and live-action theatrical films in 2002 and 2004. The franchise held the record for the most episodes of an animated television series for many years.

Cultural impact

Scooby-Doo is one of the most recognisable characters in animation history, and the show's formula — rational explanations for apparent supernatural events — has been credited with teaching generations of children sceptical thinking. The franchise has generated extensive merchandise, comic books, video games and stage shows, and remains in production under Warner Bros. Animation.
