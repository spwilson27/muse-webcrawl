A brougham (pronounced "broo-am") is a light, four-wheeled horse-drawn carriage built in the 19th century. It was named after Henry Brougham, 1st Baron Brougham and Vaux, who had such a carriage built to his specification by the London coachbuilder Robinson & Cook in 1838 or 1839.

Description

The brougham has an enclosed body seating two passengers, with a driver's box seat at the front, outside the cabin. Unlike a coach, it has no rear boot for luggage and is drawn by a single horse or a pair, making it light and well suited to everyday town use. The body features a sharply squared rear end and a curving lower line, often with doors giving access to the low-stepped interior.

Legacy

The brougham became one of the most popular carriage types of the Victorian era. When early automobiles adopted enclosed bodies of similar form, the term "brougham" was transferred to motor cars, and it survived as a styling designation for various luxury automobiles throughout the 20th century.
