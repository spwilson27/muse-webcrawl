A carrier strike group (CSG) is a naval formation centred on an aircraft carrier, operated principally by the United States Navy. It is the successor to the carrier battle group, the term "strike group" being adopted in 2004 to emphasise the formation's offensive, power-projection role. A CSG is one of the largest and most powerful naval formations in the world and a central instrument of American foreign policy.

Composition

A typical American CSG consists of a nuclear-powered supercarrier with an embarked carrier air wing of roughly 70 aircraft; at least one guided-missile cruiser and a destroyer squadron of two or three guided-missile destroyers providing air defence (including Aegis ballistic-missile defence), anti-submarine and strike capability with Tomahawk missiles; one or two nuclear attack submarines; and a fast combat support ship for fuel, ammunition and stores. The group is commanded by a rear admiral embarked in the carrier.

Role and deployment

The United States maintains eleven carrier strike groups, homeported on both coasts and in Japan, rotating through deployments to the Mediterranean, the Middle East and the western Pacific. They provide sustained air power without host-nation bases, sea control, deterrence and humanitarian assistance. Other navies — including those of the United Kingdom, France, China and India — operate comparable carrier groups, usually smaller.
