Mummy

A mummy is a dead human or animal whose soft tissues have been preserved, whether through intentional embalming or through accidental exposure to chemicals, extreme cold, very low humidity, or a lack of air, so that the recovered body does not decay further if kept in cool and dry conditions. The best-known mummies are those of ancient Egypt, where artificial mummification was practised for some three thousand years.

Egyptian mummies were wrapped in linen bandages and placed in coffins inscribed with funerary texts.

Egyptian mummification

In the classic Egyptian process, embalmers removed the internal organs — storing the lungs, liver, stomach, and intestines in canopic jars, while the heart was left in place as the seat of thought — and desiccated the body with natron salt for about 40 days. The body was then anointed with oils and resins, wrapped in hundreds of metres of linen with amulets placed between the layers, and laid in a coffin. The brain, considered unimportant, was often extracted through the nose and discarded. The practice reflected the belief that the soul required a preserved body in the afterlife.

Mummies around the world

The Chinchorro people of northern Chile produced the world's oldest known artificial mummies, around 5000 BC, older than Egypt's. Naturally preserved mummies include Ötzi the Iceman (c. 3300 BC), found in the Alps; the bog bodies of northern Europe; the frozen tombs of the Pazyryk culture in Siberia; and the mummies of the Tarim Basin in Xinjiang. In the Andes, the Inca practised capacocha sacrifices whose child victims were freeze-dried on mountain summits. Buddhist "self-mummified" monks are known from Japan.

Modern study

CT scanning, DNA analysis, and stable-isotope studies now allow mummies to be investigated non-destructively, revealing diet, disease, and ancestry. Mummies held in museums have also become the focus of ethical debate about the display of human remains.

In popular culture

The mummy became a stock monster of horror fiction and film, from The Mummy (1932) starring Boris Karloff to later adventure franchises — a tradition rooted in Victorian fascination with Egypt following the discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922.
