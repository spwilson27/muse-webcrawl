André Robert Breton (19 February 1896 – 28 September 1966) was a French writer and poet, best known as the co-founder, leader, and principal theorist of surrealism.

Early life

Breton studied medicine and worked in psychiatric wards during the First World War, where he encountered the ideas of Sigmund Freud. After the war he participated in the Paris Dada movement before breaking with it to found surrealism.

Surrealism

In 1924 Breton published the Manifesto of Surrealism, defining surrealism as "pure psychic automatism". His novel Nadja (1928) and volumes of poetry explored dreams, chance encounters, and the unconscious. He led the surrealist group for decades, famously excommunicating members who departed from his line.

Later life

Breton spent the Second World War in exile in the United States, returning to Paris in 1946. He continued to write and organise surrealist exhibitions until his death in 1966.
