Parachute Regiment (United Kingdom)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Parachute Regiment, colloquially known as the Paras, is the airborne infantry regiment of the British Army. It was formed in the Second World War by order of Winston Churchill in 1940, who called for a corps of parachute troops after the German airborne successes of that year. The regiment's motto is Utrinque Paratus — "Ready for Anything" — and its soldiers are distinguished by the maroon beret and the cap badge of wings around an open parachute.

Second World War

The first unit raised was No. 2 Commando, later 11 Special Air Service Battalion and then 1st Battalion, The Parachute Regiment. British airborne forces expanded into the 1st and 6th Airborne Divisions. Parachute battalions fought in North Africa, Sicily, Italy and Normandy. The regiment's most famous action was at Arnhem in September 1944 during Operation Market Garden, where the 2nd Battalion under Lt-Col John Frost held the northern end of the bridge against overwhelming odds; the battle honours earned there and elsewhere fill the regimental colours.

Post-war service

Since 1945 the regiment has served in Palestine, Malaya, Cyprus, Aden, Borneo, Northern Ireland, the Falklands War (where 2 and 3 PARA fought at Goose Green and Mount Longdon), the Balkans, Sierra Leone, Iraq and Afghanistan. Its battalions have won numerous gallantry awards, including Victoria Crosses for Corporal Bryan Budd in Afghanistan (2006).

Organisation

The regiment today consists of three regular battalions: 1 PARA, which forms the core of the Special Forces Support Group; 2 PARA and 3 PARA, which form part of 16 Air Assault Brigade Combat Team; and 4 PARA, a reserve battalion. Selection begins with the arduous P Company course at Catterick, testing endurance and determination before volunteers proceed to parachute training at RAF Brize Norton.
