Province of Sondrio

The Province of Sondrio (Italian: Provincia di Sondrio) is a province in the Lombardy region of northern Italy. Its capital is the city of Sondrio. Largely mountainous, it corresponds closely to the Valtellina valley, together with Valchiavenna and the Livigno area, and borders Switzerland (the canton of Graubünden) to the north.

Geography

The province covers about 3,200 km² of the Rhaetian and Bergamo Alps, drained by the Adda and Mera rivers. It includes the ski resorts of Bormio, Livigno, Aprica and Madesimo, part of Stelvio National Park, and the Bernina massif on the Swiss border. The population is around 180,000.

History

The Valtellina was ruled by the Three Leagues (Graubünden) from 1512 until 1797, a period marked by the Sacred Butcher (Sacro Macello) massacre of Protestants in 1620 during the Thirty Years' War. It passed to the Cisalpine Republic, then to Lombardy–Venetia, and joined Italy in 1859.

Economy

The economy rests on tourism, hydroelectric power from Alpine reservoirs, agriculture — notably Nebbiolo wines (Valtellina Superiore and Sforzato) and apples — and banking; the Banca Popolare di Sondrio was founded here in 1871.
