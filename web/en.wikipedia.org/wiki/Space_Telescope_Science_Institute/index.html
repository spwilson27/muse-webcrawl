The Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI) is the science operations center for the Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope, and the planned science operations center for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope. It was established in 1981 as a community-based science center operated for NASA by the Association of Universities for Research in Astronomy (AURA), and it is located on the Homewood campus of Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, Maryland.

Activities

STScI carries out the science programs of its observatories: it solicits and peer-reviews observing proposals from astronomers worldwide, schedules observations, operates the telescopes' science instruments, processes and calibrates the data, and distributes them to the community. Its Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) preserves data from more than twenty missions and makes them freely available.

The institute also conducts its own research program, employing several hundred scientists and engineers, and runs the public outreach program — through the Office of Public Outreach — that produces Hubble's famous imagery and news releases.

History

STScI's founding reflected a deliberate decision to place science operations under community governance rather than inside a NASA center. Its first director was Riccardo Giacconi, who later received the Nobel Prize in Physics for his work in X-ray astronomy. The institute directed Hubble science from before its 1990 launch, through the 1993 servicing mission that corrected the telescope's spherical aberration, and assumed science operations for the Webb telescope well before its 2021 launch and 2022 commissioning.
