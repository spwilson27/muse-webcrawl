Bridge Day

Bridge Day is an annual one-day festival held on the third Saturday of October at the New River Gorge Bridge in Fayetteville, West Virginia, United States. It is the largest single-day festival in West Virginia and one of the few occasions in the world where BASE jumping from a major bridge is legally permitted en masse, drawing hundreds of jumpers and tens of thousands of spectators.

History

The New River Gorge Bridge, a steel arch bridge carrying U.S. Route 19, was completed in 1977 and was for a time the world's longest single-span arch bridge; its deck stands 876 feet above the New River. The first Bridge Day was held on November 8, 1980, when the bridge was closed to traffic and opened to pedestrians; two people parachuted from it — one from a plane and one from the bridge itself. BASE jumping was formally incorporated thereafter.

The event

On Bridge Day the bridge is closed to vehicles and filled with vendors, food stalls, and spectators. Registered jumpers — typically several hundred making around 800–1,000 jumps in about six hours — leap from the deck, freefall briefly, deploy parachutes, and land in the gorge below, where boats recover those landing in the river. Rappelling, a high line, and zip-line activities are also offered. The event underpins the local tourism economy of the New River Gorge, designated a U.S. national park in 2020.
