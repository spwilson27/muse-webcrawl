Dollart

The Dollart (German name) or Dollard (Dutch name) is a bay in the Wadden Sea between the northern Netherlands and Germany, on the west side of the estuary of the Ems river. Most of it dries at low tide, and large parts of it have been reclaimed as polders.

History

The Dollart was formed by catastrophic sea floods in the Middle Ages, traditionally dated to 1277, which drowned dozens of parishes and villages in the historical Reiderland. Further storm surges, including the St. Lucia's flood of 1287, enlarged the bay. From the 16th century onward, systematic dike building and reclamation by both Dutch and German landowners gradually reduced its area to roughly half its medieval extent.

The border between the Netherlands and Germany through the Dollart and the Ems estuary was long disputed; a 2014 treaty between the two countries established a practical arrangement for the area without finally settling the boundary question. Today the Dollart is an important nature reserve for wading birds and seals, protected under national and European conservation designations.
