Sykes–Picot Agreement

The Sykes–Picot Agreement was a 1916 secret treaty between the United Kingdom and France, with assent from the Russian Empire and the Kingdom of Italy, defining their mutually agreed spheres of influence and control in the Middle East should the Ottoman Empire be defeated in the First World War.

Sykes–Picot Agreement
Signed16 May 1916
NegotiatorsMark Sykes, François Georges-Picot
PartiesUnited Kingdom, France (assent: Russia, Italy)

Terms

Britain was to control the areas roughly corresponding to modern Jordan, southern Iraq and the Haifa area, while France was to control south-eastern Turkey, northern Iraq, Syria and Lebanon. Palestine was to be placed under international administration. Zones of indirect influence surrounded each sphere.

Exposure and aftermath

The agreement conflicted with the promises made to Sharif Hussein of Mecca in the Hussein–McMahon Correspondence. After the Bolsheviks published the secret treaties in 1917, the agreement embarrassed Britain and France; the postwar mandates system and the San Remo conference of 1920 implemented a revised version of the partition, shaping the modern Middle Eastern state system.
