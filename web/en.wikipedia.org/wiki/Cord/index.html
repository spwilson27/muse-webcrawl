Cord

Cord and cording generally refer to rope or string, but may also refer to other meanings, including various technical and scientific terms. This article describes cord as a material: a long, thin, flexible strand composed of fibres or filaments twisted or braided together, intermediate in thickness between string and rope.

Uses

Cords are used for tying, suspending, pulling and connecting. Common examples include electrical cords (flexible insulated cables supplying power to appliances), drawcords in clothing, blind and curtain cords, starter cords on small engines, and cords used in upholstery such as piping. Paracord, originally used in parachute suspension lines, is widely used as a general-purpose utility cord.

Materials and construction

Cordage is made from natural fibres such as cotton, hemp, manila and sisal, and from synthetics such as nylon, polyester and polypropylene. Construction may be twisted, braided, or kernmantle, with a braided sheath surrounding a core of fibres for strength.

Other meanings

"Cord" is also a unit of volume for firewood, equal to 128 cubic feet (about 3.62 m³) in a stacked pile measuring 4 by 4 by 8 feet. In anatomy, the term appears in structures such as the spinal cord, vocal cords, umbilical cord and spermatic cord.
