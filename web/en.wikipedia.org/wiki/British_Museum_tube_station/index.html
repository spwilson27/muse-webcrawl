British Museum is a disused station on the London Underground that served the British Museum in Bloomsbury. It was opened in 1900 as a station on the Central London Railway (now the Central line) and closed in 1933 after a new station at Holborn provided a more convenient interchange with the Piccadilly line just 100 metres away.

History

The station was authorised in 1892 and opened on 30 July 1900, with two lift shafts and platform tiling in the Central London Railway's house style. Because the station was only 100 metres from Holborn and the Piccadilly line's platforms, British Museum was little used from 1907 onwards, when the Piccadilly line opened at Holborn and provided an alternative.

Closure and partial demolition

When Holborn station was modernised in 1933, the distance between the two stations was shortened further by the opening of a direct escalator link. British Museum closed on 24 September 1933. The surface building was demolished in the late 1980s when a new office block was built on the site; the platforms and tiles were removed in 2003 when the buildings above were sold, and the vaults now form part of the loading bay of a large office building.
