The Landwasser Viaduct (German: Landwasserviadukt) is a single-track limestone railway viaduct near Filisur in the canton of Graubünden, Switzerland. It crosses the Landwasser river and passes straight into a tunnel through a vertical cliff face, and it is one of the most famous railway bridges in the world and a symbol of the Rhaetian Railway.

Description

The viaduct is 136 metres (446 ft) long and 65 metres (213 ft) high. It consists of six arch spans of 20 metres each, supported on five tall piers, and it carries the track in a curve (radius 100 metres) — an unusual feature for a masonry viaduct. At its southern end, trains emerge from or enter the Landwasser Tunnel, whose portal is set directly into the near-vertical rock wall of the Schmitten gorge.

History

The bridge was built between 1901 and 1902 as part of the Albula Railway, designed by Alexander Acatos. Remarkably, the piers were constructed without scaffolding, using cranes and an auxiliary cableway to handle the limestone blocks; horses and a small construction railway moved materials. The line opened in 1903. Since 2008 the Albula Railway, including the viaduct, has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Rhaetian Railway in the Albula/Bernina Landscapes" site. Trains of the Glacier Express and Bernina Express routes cross it, and it is one of the most photographed structures on the Swiss railway network.
