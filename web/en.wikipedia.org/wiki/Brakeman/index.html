Brakeman

A brakeman is a rail transport worker whose original job was to assist the braking of a train by applying brakes on individual railcars. The role has changed over time with the adoption of continuous automatic air brakes and modern operating practices. In modern North American practice, the brakeman works as part of the train crew, assisting the conductor and performing tasks such as switching cars.

History

Before automatic air brakes, brakemen rode in the cabooses and car tops, turning hand brake wheels in response to whistle signals from the engineer. The job was notoriously dangerous, involving walking on car roofs in all weather, and brakemen suffered high rates of injury. The introduction of the Westinghouse air brake and automatic couplers in the late 19th century gradually transformed the role.

Modern role

Today the brakeman is often the junior member of a freight train crew, responsible for duties such as connecting air hoses, setting and releasing hand brakes in yards, and throwing switches during switching operations.
