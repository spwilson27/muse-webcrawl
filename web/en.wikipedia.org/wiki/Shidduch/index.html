Shidduch

The shidduch (Hebrew: שִׁדּוּךְ, pl. shidduchim) is a system of matchmaking in Orthodox Jewish communities in which singles are introduced for the purpose of marriage. Matches are typically arranged by a professional or volunteer matchmaker, the shadchan, often at the suggestion of family members, rabbis, friends, or teachers.

Process

A match usually begins with a shidduch résumé — a brief profile of the single, their family, education, religious observance, and what they are looking for in a spouse. Families or their representatives make enquiries about a proposed match, after which the couple meets on a series of dates, frequently in public places such as hotel lobbies, in keeping with community norms of modesty (tznius) that limit physical contact and being alone together before marriage. If the couple agrees to marry, a celebration called a vort (engagement party) may be held, at which a document of conditions (tenaim) is sometimes signed. The shadchan traditionally receives a fee (shadchanus) for a successful match.

Cultural context

The practice rests on the traditional belief that marriages are decreed in heaven — the Talmud teaches that forty days before a child is formed, a heavenly voice announces their destined spouse (bashert). In contemporary Orthodox life the shidduch system coexists with websites and singles events. Commentators within the community discuss an alleged "shidduch crisis", concerning imbalances in the numbers of marriageable men and women and the pressures the system places on singles and families.
