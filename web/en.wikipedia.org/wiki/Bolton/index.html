Bolton
Country: England
Region: North West England
County: Greater Manchester
Population: about 185,000 (town)
Historic county: Lancashire

Bolton is a large town in Greater Manchester, England, historically part of Lancashire. It lies about 10 miles (16 km) northwest of Manchester city centre, at the edge of the West Pennine Moors. Bolton was once one of the world's major centres of textile manufacture and became a classic Lancashire mill town.

History

Bolton grew from a small settlement recorded in the medieval period into an important market town for woollen cloth. During the Industrial Revolution it became a boomtown of cotton spinning: Samuel Crompton, inventor of the spinning mule, was born in Bolton and developed his machine at nearby Hall i' th' Wood. By the late 19th century dozens of mills employed tens of thousands of workers. The town was also an early centre of association football; Bolton Wanderers F.C. was founded in 1874.

Modern Bolton

The decline of the textile industry in the 20th century led to economic restructuring toward services, logistics, and education; the University of Bolton (formerly Bolton Institute) was granted university status in 2005. Landmarks include Bolton Town Hall on Victoria Square, the parish church of St Peter, Bolton Museum and Aquarium, and Smithills Hall. Bolton is administratively the centre of the Metropolitan Borough of Bolton.
