Siberia

[Image: Endless taiga forest and a winding river in Siberia]
Endless taiga forest and a winding river in Siberia

Siberia is a vast region spanning Northern Asia from the Ural Mountains in the west to the Pacific Ocean in the east, and from the Arctic Ocean south to the hills of north-central Kazakhstan and the borders of Mongolia and China. It constitutes almost all of Russia east of the Urals — about 13.1 million square kilometres, over three quarters of Russia's territory — but holds only about a quarter of its population.

Siberia is known for extreme continental winters (the village of Oymyakon is among the coldest inhabited places on Earth, at about minus 68 degrees Celsius), the taiga coniferous forest — the world's largest forest biome — the Trans-Siberian Railway, and immense reserves of oil, gas, diamonds, gold and timber. Indigenous peoples have inhabited it for millennia; Russia conquered it between the late 16th and 18th centuries, and it later became notorious for exile and the Gulag prison camps.
