Type: private liberal arts university
Established: 1876
Location: Grand Rapids, Michigan
Students: about 3,300
Affiliation: Christian Reformed Church

Calvin University is a private Christian liberal arts university in Grand Rapids, Michigan. Founded in 1876 by the Christian Reformed Church in North America as a school for training ministers, it is named for the 16th-century Protestant reformer John Calvin. Until 2019 it was known as Calvin College.

Academics

The university offers more than 100 majors and programs across the liberal arts and professions, including notable programs in engineering, nursing, business, and the sciences, within a Reformed Christian worldview. Its January interim term, the Calvin Institute of Christian Worship, and the H. Henry Meeter Center for Calvin Studies are distinctive features. Philosophers Alvin Plantinga and Nicholas Wolterstorff are among its best-known former faculty.

Campus and athletics

The 390-acre campus in southeast Grand Rapids includes a nature preserve. Calvin's Knights compete in NCAA Division III, with a historic basketball rivalry against nearby Hope College frequently cited among the best in small-college sports.
