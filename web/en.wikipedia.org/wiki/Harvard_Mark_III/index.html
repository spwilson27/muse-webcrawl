The Harvard Mark III, also known as the ADEC (Aiken Dahlgren Electronic Calculator), was an early computer built at Harvard University under the direction of Howard H. Aiken and delivered to the United States Navy in 1950.

Design

The Mark III was built for the Naval Proving Ground at Dahlgren, Virginia, largely for computing ballistic firing tables. Unlike the purely electromechanical Mark I and Mark II, it incorporated electronic components, including thousands of vacuum tubes for some functions, alongside relays. Its principal memory was a large rotating magnetic drum, an early form of drum memory, storing thousands of words and giving the machine much faster access than its predecessors' counters and paper tape.

Performance and legacy

The Mark III could perform roughly thousands of additions per second and was the fastest electromechanical-electronic hybrid of its generation at Harvard. It was succeeded by the fully electronic Harvard Mark IV, completed in 1952, which Aiken's team built for the US Air Force.
