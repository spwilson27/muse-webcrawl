Robot series

The Robot series is a series of science fiction short stories and novels by American writer Isaac Asimov, centred on positronic robots governed by the Three Laws of Robotics. Together with the Galactic Empire and Foundation series, it forms part of Asimov's greater future-history saga.

Content

The series grew from short stories written from 1940 onward, many narrated by robopsychologist Dr Susan Calvin and collected in I, Robot (1950) and The Complete Robot (1982). The novels — The Caves of Steel (1954), The Naked Sun (1957), The Robots of Dawn (1983) and Robots and Empire (1985) — are detective stories pairing human investigator Elijah Baley with the humaniform robot R. Daneel Olivaw, exploring tensions between Earth and the robot-dependent Spacer worlds.

The Three Laws

Asimov's Laws — a robot may not injure a human or allow a human to come to harm; a robot must obey human orders unless they conflict with the First Law; and a robot must protect its own existence unless that conflicts with the first two Laws — were designed as a framework for stories probing their loopholes and interactions. In later novels Asimov linked the series to the Foundation universe, with Daneel secretly guiding humanity across millennia, guided by a "Zeroth Law" protecting humanity as a whole.
