James P. Starlin (born 9 October 1949) is an American comics artist and writer known for space-opera and metaphysical storylines, especially at Marvel Comics, where he created or revamped characters including Thanos, Drax the Destroyer and Gamora, and wrote and drew the bestselling 1991 miniseries The Infinity Gauntlet.

Career

Starlin rose to prominence in the 1970s on Captain Marvel and Warlock, whose psychedelic, death-obsessed storylines became classics; he scripted the death of Captain Marvel in Marvel's first large-format graphic novel, The Death of Captain Marvel (1982). For Epic Comics he created Dreadstar, a long-running creator-owned space opera. At DC Comics he wrote Batman (including "A Death in the Family", in which the Joker killed Robin, Jason Todd) and the cosmic series The Weird.

Legacy

His Infinity trilogy — The Infinity Gauntlet, The Infinity War and The Infinity Crusade — made Thanos one of Marvel's defining villains, and Starlin's creations became central to the Marvel Cinematic Universe films Guardians of the Galaxy, Avengers: Infinity War and Avengers: Endgame, in which he made a cameo appearance. He was inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 2017.
