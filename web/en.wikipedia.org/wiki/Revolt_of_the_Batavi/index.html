Revolt of the Batavi

The Revolt of the Batavi took place in the Roman province of Germania Inferior between AD 69 and 70. It was an uprising against the Roman Empire led by Gaius Julius Civilis, a prince of the Batavi, a Germanic tribe long famed for supplying elite auxiliary troops to Rome. The revolt broke out amid the civil wars of the Year of the Four Emperors.

Course of the revolt

Exploiting Rome's internal conflict, Civilis raised the Batavi and allied tribes, defeated Roman forces along the Rhine, and captured the legionary fortress of Vetera (near modern Xanten) after a long siege. Several legions were destroyed or humiliated, and the revolt drew in Gallic tribes under leaders such as Julius Classicus, who briefly proclaimed a Gallic empire. Once Vespasian secured power in Rome, a large army under Quintus Petillius Cerialis campaigned up the Rhine, defeated the rebels, and negotiated a settlement in late AD 70.

Legacy

The revolt is narrated in Tacitus's Histories. In later centuries the Batavian uprising became a founding myth of Dutch national identity, commemorated in art by Rembrandt and others.
