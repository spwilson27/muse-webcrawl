In mathematics, the genus is a property of surfaces and curves that, informally, counts the number of "holes" or "handles" they possess. A sphere has genus 0, a torus (doughnut shape) has genus 1, and a double torus has genus 2. Genus is a topological invariant: it does not change under continuous deformation.

Genus of a surface

For a compact orientable surface, the genus g is related to the Euler characteristic χ by the formula χ = 2 − 2g. It can also be defined as the maximum number of non-intersecting simple closed curves that can be drawn on the surface without disconnecting it, or as half the rank of the first homology group. The classification theorem for surfaces states that every compact orientable surface is a sphere with g handles attached, so the genus completely classifies such surfaces up to homeomorphism. For non-orientable surfaces an analogous genus (the number of cross-caps, or demigenus) is used.

Genus of a curve

For a smooth algebraic curve, the genus is a central invariant appearing in the Riemann–Roch theorem. A smooth plane curve of degree d has genus (d−1)(d−2)/2. Elliptic curves are precisely the curves of genus 1; the genus of the Riemann surface of a curve also equals the dimension of its space of holomorphic differentials. Arithmetic genus generalises the notion to higher-dimensional varieties in algebraic geometry.
