Roman Polanski (born 18 August 1933) is a Polish and French film director, producer, screenwriter, and actor. A Holocaust survivor of the Kraków Ghetto, he is regarded as one of the most important filmmakers of the post-war era.

Career

His Polish debut Knife in the Water (1962) earned an Oscar nomination. In Britain and Hollywood he made Repulsion (1965), Rosemary's Baby (1968), and Chinatown (1974). Later acclaimed works include Tess (1979), The Pianist (2002) — winner of the Palme d'Or and Academy Award for Best Director — The Ghost Writer (2010), and An Officer and a Spy (2019).

Personal life and legal case

His pregnant wife Sharon Tate was murdered by Manson Family members in 1969. In 1977 Polanski was arrested in Los Angeles for unlawful sex with a 13-year-old; he fled the U.S. in 1978 before sentencing and has lived in Europe since, a fugitive from U.S. justice, while continuing to work and facing further allegations and controversy.
