The Aragonese Crusade (1284–1285) was a crusade declared by Pope Martin IV against King Peter III of Aragon, following Peter's intervention in Sicily after the Sicilian Vespers of 1282. The pope offered Aragon to Charles of Valois, son of Philip III of France, and a French army invaded Catalonia in 1285.

Campaign

French forces under Philip III took Girona after a siege, but their fleet was destroyed by Roger of Lauria at the Battle of Les Formigues, and disease devastated the French camp. Philip died at Perpignan during the retreat, and the invasion collapsed; the crusade is remembered as one of the most disastrous undertakings of the Capetian monarchy.

Aftermath

The war continued at sea and in Sicily until the Peace of Anagni (1295) and the Treaty of Caltabellotta (1302) confirmed Aragonese influence in the western Mediterranean.
