High Plains (United States)

The High Plains are a subregion of the Great Plains of North America, covering parts of eastern Colorado and New Mexico, western Kansas and Nebraska, eastern Wyoming, the Oklahoma and Texas panhandles, and southwestern South Dakota. They are characterized by a comparatively high elevation—rising from about 600 m in the east to over 2,400 m near the Rocky Mountains—and a semi-arid climate.

Geography and ecology

The region is largely shortgrass prairie (blue grama and buffalo grass), historically grazed by bison and pronghorn. Its eastern boundary lies near the 100th meridian, a traditional dividing line between the humid East and arid West.

Economy

Agriculture depends heavily on irrigation from the Ogallala Aquifer, one of the world's largest aquifers, which underlies much of the High Plains and is being depleted by centre-pivot irrigation of corn, wheat, and cattle feedlots. The 1930s Dust Bowl devastated the southern High Plains; dryland farming, ranching, and oil and gas extraction remain important.
