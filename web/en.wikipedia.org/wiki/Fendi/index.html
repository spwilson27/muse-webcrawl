Fendi is an Italian luxury fashion house founded in Rome in 1925 by Adele and Edoardo Fendi. Renowned for its leather goods — especially the Baguette and Peekaboo handbags — and for its fur craftsmanship, the house is part of the LVMH group.

History

The Fendis opened a fur and leather boutique on Via del Plebiscito in Rome in 1925. Their five daughters — Paola, Anna, Franca, Carla and Alda — joined the business after the Second World War and transformed it into an international brand. In 1965 the sisters recruited a young Karl Lagerfeld, whose five-decade creative partnership with the house produced the inverted "FF" Zucca logo (1965) and revolutionised fur design. Silvia Venturini Fendi, of the third generation, created the Baguette bag in 1997, a cultural phenomenon credited with launching the "It bag" era, and the Peekaboo in 2008.

LVMH acquired control of Fendi around 1999–2001. After Lagerfeld's death in 2019, Kim Jones became artistic director of womenswear in 2020, while Silvia Venturini Fendi remained responsible for accessories and menswear.

Headquarters and stores

Since 2015 Fendi's headquarters have been in the Palazzo della Civiltà Italiana in Rome's EUR district, a landmark of Italian rationalist architecture, which the house restored and leases. The flagship Palazzo Fendi stands on Largo Carlo Goldoni in central Rome. Fendi operates boutiques in major cities worldwide.
