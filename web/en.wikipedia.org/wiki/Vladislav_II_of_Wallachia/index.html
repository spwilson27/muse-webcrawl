Vladislav II of Wallachia

Reign: 1447–1456 (with an interruption in 1448)
House: Drăculești (Basarab)
Predecessor: Vlad II Dracul
Successor: Vlad III the Impaler
Died: 1456

Vladislav II (died 1456) was Voivode (Prince) of Wallachia from 1447 to 1456, a member of the House of Drăculești. Placed on the throne with the support of John Hunyadi of Hungary after the murder of his predecessor Vlad II Dracul, he steered Wallachia between the competing pressures of Hungary and the Ottoman Empire until he was overthrown and killed by his famous contemporary and rival, Vlad III the Impaler.

Accession

In late 1447, Vlad II Dracul and his eldest son Mircea were killed by boyars at Târgoviște with Hungarian connivance. John Hunyadi, regent of Hungary, invaded Wallachia and installed Vladislav, a kinsman from the Drăculești line, as voivode. Vladislav's position remained precarious: in autumn 1448 the Ottomans briefly installed Vlad III, son of Dracul, while Vladislav was campaigning with Hunyadi at the Second Battle of Kosovo; Vladislav recovered his throne within months when Vlad withdrew.

Reign

Vladislav sought good relations with Hungary while paying tribute to the Ottoman sultan to preserve Wallachia's autonomy. He transferred the princely mint to Bucharest — the first documentary mention of Bucharest (1459) falls just after his reign, but his charters are associated with the town's rise — and issued charters favouring trade with the Saxon towns of Transylvania, particularly Brașov. Relations with Hunyadi cooled after 1450, and Vladislav refused to join the anti-Ottoman campaigns, even confiscating Hunyadi's estates at Amlaș and Făgăraș in disputes over border districts.

Fall and death

In 1456, Vlad III, backed by Hungarian support this time, invaded Wallachia. Vladislav was defeated and, according to tradition, killed by Vlad himself in hand-to-hand combat near Târgșor. He was buried at the church of Dealu Monastery near Târgoviște. His death cleared the way for the second, famous reign of Vlad the Impaler.
