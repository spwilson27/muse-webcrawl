Nuclear safety culture

Safety culture is the assembly of characteristics and attitudes in organizations and individuals which establishes that, as an overriding priority, nuclear plant safety issues receive the attention warranted by their significance. The concept was first introduced in the International Atomic Energy Agency's (IAEA) post-accident review of the Chernobyl disaster in 1986, which identified deficiencies in the safety culture of the operating organization and the regulatory environment as root causes of the accident.

Characteristics

According to the IAEA, a strong safety culture is characterized by leadership commitment to safety, a questioning attitude among staff, rigorous and prudent decision-making, open communication in which workers can raise concerns without fear of reprisal, and continuous learning from operating experience. Regulatory bodies such as the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission have adopted formal safety culture policy statements applying these traits to licensees.

Application

Safety culture assessments are now a routine part of nuclear oversight, including IAEA OSART and SCART review missions. The concept has since spread beyond the nuclear industry to aviation, healthcare, chemical processing and other high-hazard sectors.
