Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC)
Founded: 25 September 1969
Headquarters: Jeddah, Saudi Arabia
Members: 57 member states
Official languages: Arabic, English, French

The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), formerly the Organisation of the Islamic Conference, is an intergovernmental organisation founded in 1969, following an arson attack on the Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem. With 57 member states spread over four continents, it describes itself as "the collective voice of the Muslim world" and is the second-largest intergovernmental organisation after the United Nations.

Structure

The OIC's highest body is the Islamic Summit of heads of state, held every three years; the Council of Foreign Ministers meets annually. Its permanent secretariat is in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, and it maintains specialised bodies including the Islamic Development Bank.

Activities

The organisation coordinates member positions on political issues, promotes economic and scientific cooperation, and has adopted a human rights declaration and a programme of action on countering extremism. It holds observer status at the United Nations.
