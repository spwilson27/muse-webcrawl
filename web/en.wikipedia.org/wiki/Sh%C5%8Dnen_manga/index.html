Shōnen manga

Target audienceTeenage boys (roughly 12-18)
Major magazineWeekly Shōnen Jump

Shōnen manga (少年漫画) is a category of Japanese comics marketed primarily toward adolescent boys. It is the most popular demographic category of manga, typically featuring action-filled stories with male protagonists, themes of friendship, effort and victory, and frequent martial arts, sports or science-fiction settings.

Characteristics

Shōnen series are usually serialised in weekly or monthly anthology magazines aimed at school-age readers. Stories tend to emphasize character growth through training and competition, strong camaraderie among the cast, and humour. The demographic label describes the target readership rather than a strict genre, so shōnen magazines also run romance, comedy and slice-of-life titles.

History

Boys' magazines existed in Japan before the Second World War, but the modern shōnen market took shape in the postwar decades with artists such as Osamu Tezuka. Shueisha's Weekly Shōnen Jump, founded in 1968, became the best-selling manga magazine, launching series including Dragon Ball, Naruto and One Piece, the best-selling manga series of all time.
