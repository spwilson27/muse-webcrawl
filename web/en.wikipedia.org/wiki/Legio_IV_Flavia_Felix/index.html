Legio IV Flavia Felix ("Flavian Lucky Fourth Legion"), was a legion of the Imperial Roman army founded in AD 70 by the emperor Vespasian (r. 69–79) from the cadre of the disbanded Legio IV Macedonica. The Fourth Macedonica had fought on the losing side in the civil war of AD 69, and Vespasian refounded it under the name of his own gens, Flavia, with the cognomen Felix ("lucky" or "fortunate"). The legion's symbol was the lion.

History

The legion was first stationed at Burnum in Dalmatia. In AD 86 the Dacians under Decebalus invaded Moesia, and after the disaster at the First Battle of Tapae the legion was transferred to the Danube frontier, eventually settling at Singidunum (modern Belgrade). It fought in Domitian's Dacian Wars and in Trajan's conquest of Dacia (AD 101–106), and detachments (vexillations) took part in Trajan's Parthian campaign.

During the Marcomannic Wars of Marcus Aurelius the legion defended the middle Danube, and its troops are attested across Moesia Superior for centuries. The legion supported Septimius Severus in the civil war of AD 193. It is still listed in the Notitia Dignitatum of the early fifth century, guarding the Danube from its base at Singidunum region under the Dux Moesiae Primae.

Legacy

Coins of Vespasian and later emperors honoured the legion, and inscriptions of its veterans are found throughout the Danubian provinces.
