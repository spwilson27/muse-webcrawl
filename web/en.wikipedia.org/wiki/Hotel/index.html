A hotel is an establishment that provides paid lodging on a short-term basis, typically with staffed reception, furnished rooms with private or shared bathrooms, and services such as housekeeping, meals and security. Facilities range from modest roadside inns to luxury palaces with restaurants, conference centres and spas. The word derives from the French hôtel, originally a grand house or town mansion.

History

Lodging for travellers is ancient: Greek and Roman inns, caravanserais on Silk Road trade routes, and monastic guest houses all served travellers. Coaching inns flourished with the stagecoach networks of early modern Europe. The modern hotel emerged in the late 18th and 19th centuries with urbanisation and the railway: grand hotels such as London's Savoy (1889) and the establishments of César Ritz set standards of private bathrooms, electric light and lifts. In the United States, the roadside motel arose with the automobile, and after 1945 chains such as Holiday Inn standardised the mid-market hotel, while Conrad Hilton and others built international brands.

Types and classification

Hotels are classified by size, location, clientele and service level: airport and resort hotels, business and conference hotels, boutique hotels, extended-stay properties, capsule hotels in Japan, and luxury resorts. Star ratings (one to five) are awarded by national bodies or automobile associations under varying criteria and broadly signal the range of facilities and service. Alternative lodging—hostels, bed and breakfasts, guest houses, aparthotels and short-term holiday rentals—competes with and complements the hotel sector.

Operations

Most large hotels are run under one of three models: owner-operated, franchised (the owner pays to use a brand and its reservation system), or managed by a hotel company under contract. Key performance measures include occupancy rate, average daily rate (ADR) and revenue per available room (RevPAR). The industry is a major employer and a significant part of tourism economies; it is also cyclical and sensitive to economic conditions, fuel prices and events such as pandemics, which caused record declines in 2020 followed by recovery driven by leisure demand.

Records

Among the world's largest hotels by room count are the First World Hotel in Malaysia's Genting Highlands and several Las Vegas properties such as the MGM Grand. The oldest continuously operating hotels include Japan's Nishiyama Onsen Keiunkan, founded in 705.
