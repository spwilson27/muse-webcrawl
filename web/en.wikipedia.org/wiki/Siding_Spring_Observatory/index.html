Siding Spring Observatory

Siding Spring Observatory (SSO) is an astronomical observatory near Coonabarabran, New South Wales, Australia, on the edge of Warrumbungle National Park. Situated at an altitude of about 1,165 metres on Mount Woorat (Siding Spring Mountain), it is Australia's premier optical and infrared observatory and is operated by the Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics of the Australian National University (ANU). The site hosts the 3.9-metre Anglo-Australian Telescope, among many other instruments.

History

The site was selected in the early 1960s after site testing by ANU astronomers seeking dark skies away from city light pollution; the first telescope, a 40-inch reflector, began operating in 1964. The Anglo-Australian Telescope (AAT), a joint UK–Australian project, was inaugurated in 1974 and was among the largest optical telescopes in the world at the time. Siding Spring also became home to telescopes of the University of New South Wales, the UK Schmidt Telescope (famous for its photographic sky surveys of the southern hemisphere), and numerous international facilities.

Telescopes and surveys

Current instruments include the AAT with spectrographs such as 2dF/AAOmega and HERMES (used for the GALAH galactic archaeology survey); the SkyMapper 1.35 m telescope, which produced a digital survey of the southern sky; the ANU 2.3 m telescope; the Faulkes Telescope South (part of the Las Cumbres Observatory network); and the Uppsala Southern Schmidt, used by the Siding Spring Survey to search for near-Earth asteroids and comets. The Siding Spring Survey discovered Comet C/2013 A1, which passed extremely close to Mars in October 2014.

2013 bushfire

On 13 January 2013 the Wambelong bushfire swept over the observatory, destroying the lodge and several buildings and damaging others; all staff were safely evacuated and the main telescopes survived, though the site was closed for several months for repairs. The observatory subsequently reopened and continues research and public outreach from Coonabarabran, promoted as the "Astronomy Capital of Australia."
