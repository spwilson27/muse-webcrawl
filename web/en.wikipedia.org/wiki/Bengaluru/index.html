Bengaluru, formerly called Bangalore, is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Karnataka. Situated on the Deccan Plateau at about 900 metres above sea level, it enjoys a moderate climate year-round. It is India's leading information technology centre and one of the country's fastest-growing metropolitan areas.

History

The modern city was founded in 1537 by Kempe Gowda I, a chieftain under the Vijayanagara Empire, who built a mud fort and established the pete (market town). The city passed through Bijapur, Mughal and Mysore rule — Haider Ali and Tipu Sultan fortified it — and after the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War the British established a cantonment in 1809, which grew alongside the older city. After Indian independence in 1947 Bengaluru became capital of Mysore state (later Karnataka). The official name was changed from Bangalore to Bengaluru in 2014, reflecting the Kannada name.

Economy

Bengaluru hosts the headquarters of major IT firms including Infosys and Wipro, the software campuses of multinational companies in areas such as Whitefield and Electronic City, and a large start-up ecosystem, earning it the title "Silicon Valley of India". It is also a hub for aerospace and defence (Hindustan Aeronautics Limited, ISRO headquarters), biotechnology and research institutions such as the Indian Institute of Science (founded 1909). Rapid growth has strained infrastructure, with severe traffic congestion and pressure on the city's lakes and water supply.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Vidhana Soudha, Bangalore Palace, Tipu Sultan's Summer Palace, the Lalbagh Botanical Garden with its 1840 glasshouse and ancient Kempe Gowda watchtower rock, and Cubbon Park. The city is known for its pubs, its masala dosa and filter coffee, the Karaga festival, and a large cosmopolitan population speaking Kannada, English, Tamil, Telugu, Hindi and other languages.
