Hurdling (horse race)

A hurdle race in Great Britain and Ireland is a National Hunt horse race in which the horses jump over obstacles called hurdles. Hurdles are smaller and more flexible than the fences used in steeplechases, typically about 3 feet 6 inches (1.07 m) high, and can be knocked down without necessarily unseating the horse.

Overview

Hurdle races are run over distances of between two and three and a half miles. Horses usually begin their jumping careers over hurdles before progressing to steeplechase fences, which are bigger and more demanding. Hurdle races are divided into classes, with the most prestigious designated Grade 1.

Major races

The most famous hurdle race is the Champion Hurdle, run over two miles at the Cheltenham Festival, first held in 1927. Other major races include the Stayers' Hurdle (World Hurdle) at Cheltenham and the Christmas Hurdle at Kempton. In France, the Grande Course de Haies d'Auteuil is the leading hurdle race, where hurdles are larger and the style of obstacle differs.

Novice hurdlers

"Novice" hurdlers are horses in their first season over hurdles; notable novice championships include the Supreme Novices' Hurdle and the Triumph Hurdle for four-year-olds at Cheltenham.
