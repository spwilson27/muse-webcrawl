Science festival

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A science festival is a festival that showcases science and technology through talks, demonstrations, workshops, performances and hands-on activities aimed at the general public. Like music or film festivals, science festivals typically run for a few days to two weeks in a city or campus, mixing ticketed headline events with free programmes in museums, universities, theatres and public spaces. They are a major vehicle of public engagement with science.

History

The modern science festival is often traced to the annual meetings of the British Association for the Advancement of Science, whose public science meetings date to the nineteenth century, and to the Edinburgh International Science Festival, founded in 1989 and generally regarded as the first dedicated festival of its kind. Through the 1990s and 2000s the format spread widely: the Cheltenham Science Festival (2002), the World Science Festival in New York (2008), and hundreds of regional and campus festivals followed. In the United States, the Science Festival Alliance, founded in 2009, coordinates a network of independent festivals, and large events such as the USA Science and Engineering Festival in Washington, D.C. draw hundreds of thousands of attendees.

Format

Typical festival programming includes:

  • Lectures and panel discussions by research scientists and science communicators;
  • Interactive exhibits, maker activities and laboratory tours;
  • Science comedy, theatre, film screenings and art–science collaborations;
  • Events for school groups and families, often free;
  • "Late" events for adults combining demonstrations with social formats.

Many festivals also run fringe and community programmes, and some — such as science busking and street-performance circuits — take activities into shopping centres, pubs and train stations.

Notable festivals

  • Edinburgh Science Festival (Edinburgh, United Kingdom)
  • Cheltenham Science Festival (Cheltenham, United Kingdom)
  • World Science Festival (New York City, United States; also Brisbane, Australia)
  • British Science Festival (rotating UK host cities)
  • Cambridge Science Festival (Cambridge, United Kingdom, and the MIT-led festival in Massachusetts)
  • National Science and Engineering Festival events in various countries

Purpose and impact

Science festivals aim to raise public interest in science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM), to give scientists experience in public communication, and to provide informal learning outside classrooms. Evaluations commonly find increased interest in science careers among young attendees, though festivals have also been criticised for preaching to already-interested audiences, prompting efforts to reach underserved communities through neighbourhood-based programming.
