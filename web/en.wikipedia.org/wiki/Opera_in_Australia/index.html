Opera has been performed in Australia since the early years of the British colony, and the country supports a national company, Opera Australia, state companies, and a major international profile through the Sydney Opera House. Singers such as Nellie Melba and Joan Sutherland are among the most celebrated in operatic history.

History

Excerpts of ballad opera were staged in Sydney in the 1790s, and touring companies brought Italian and English opera to gold-rush cities in the 19th century. Dame Nellie Melba, the first Australian to achieve international fame as a classical musician, toured opera seasons that entrenched the art form. In 1956 the Australian Elizabethan Theatre Trust founded the Australian Opera, which became Opera Australia, the principal company, resident at the Sydney Opera House since its opening in 1973.

Companies and festivals

State companies include Opera Queensland, State Opera South Australia, West Australian Opera, and Victorian Opera. Handa Opera on Sydney Harbour, an outdoor spectacular staged since 2012, is among the country's best-known operatic events. Australian composers — from Alfred Hill to Brett Dean and Elena Kats-Chernin — have contributed a growing repertoire of new works, some drawing on Indigenous themes and performers.
