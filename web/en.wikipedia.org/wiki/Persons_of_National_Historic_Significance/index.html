Persons of National Historic Significance

Persons of National Historic Significance (National Historic People) are individuals designated by the Government of Canada as having made an outstanding and lasting contribution to Canadian history. The designation is one of three classes of national historic significance administered by Parks Canada, alongside National Historic Sites and National Historic Events.

Designation process

Nominations are reviewed by the Historic Sites and Monuments Board of Canada, established in 1919, which recommends designations to the Minister of the Environment. Generally a person must have been deceased for at least 25 years to be eligible, although prime ministers are exempt from this waiting period. Designations are often marked with a federal plaque erected at a place associated with the person's life.

Scope

More than 700 people have been designated, ranging from explorers, Indigenous leaders, and Fathers of Confederation to artists, scientists, athletes, and activists. The list is periodically reviewed for balance and inclusiveness, and in some cases plaques have been revised as historical interpretations change. Designation is commemorative only and carries no legal protection in itself.
