Borehole

Borehole
Type: Narrow drilled shaft in the ground
Purposes: Water wells, oil and gas, geotechnical investigation, geothermal, science
Deepest: Kola Superdeep Borehole, 12,262 m
Methods: Rotary, percussion, auger drilling

A borehole is a narrow shaft drilled vertically (or at an angle) into the ground, typically with a diameter much smaller than its depth. Boreholes are drilled for many purposes, including extracting water, oil, or natural gas, investigating subsurface geology and soil conditions, installing geothermal heat pumps, monitoring groundwater, and scientific research into the Earth's crust.

Drilling and construction

Boreholes are excavated by techniques such as rotary drilling with circulating drilling fluid, cable-tool percussion drilling, and augering. Depending on purpose, a borehole may be lined with casing to prevent collapse and contamination, screened at aquifer level for water abstraction, or instrumented with sensors for geotechnical and environmental monitoring. Water-supply boreholes are common in rural and arid regions, where a hand pump or submersible electric pump lifts groundwater to the surface.

Notable boreholes

The deepest borehole ever drilled is the Kola Superdeep Borehole in Russia, which reached 12,262 metres (40,230 ft) in pursuit of purely scientific objectives. Deep oil and gas wells, and scientific ocean-drilling boreholes, also extend several kilometres below the surface.
