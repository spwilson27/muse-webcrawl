Speed limits by country

Road speed limits are used in most countries to set the legal maximum speed at which vehicles may travel on a given stretch of road. Limits are generally indicated on a traffic sign reflecting the maximum permitted speed, expressed in kilometres per hour (km/h) or miles per hour (mph). The limits vary substantially between countries and often between road classes — built-up areas, roads outside built-up areas, and motorways or expressways.

Unusual cases

Germany's Autobahn network is famous for stretches with no mandatory limit for cars, although a substantial portion of the network carries permanent or temporary limits and an advisory speed applies nationwide. Historically, a few jurisdictions — such as Montana in 1995–1999 and the Isle of Man's unrestricted rural roads — also allowed driving without a fixed numerical maximum, subject to a "reasonable and prudent" standard.

Enforcement

Enforcement methods include police patrols, radar and lidar guns, speed cameras, and average-speed systems. Penalties range from fines and penalty points to licence suspension, and in several European countries fines are scaled to income.
