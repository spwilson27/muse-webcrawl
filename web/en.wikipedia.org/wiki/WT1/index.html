WT1

WT1 transcription factor
Symbol: WT1
Chromosome: 11p13
Function: transcription factor, zinc finger protein

WT1 (Wilms tumor 1) is a protein in humans encoded by the WT1 gene on chromosome 11p13. It is a transcription factor containing four zinc finger motifs at the C-terminus and functions in the development of the kidneys and gonads.

Clinical significance

Germline mutations in WT1 cause Wilms tumor (a childhood kidney cancer) predisposition, as well as Denys–Drash syndrome, Frasier syndrome, and WAGR syndrome (Wilms tumor, aniridia, genitourinary anomalies, and intellectual disability). WT1 is overexpressed in many leukemias and solid tumors and has been studied as a target for cancer immunotherapy vaccines.

Function

WT1 regulates genes involved in urogenital development, including during formation of the kidneys, gonads, spleen and mesothelium. Alternative splicing produces isoforms with differing DNA-binding properties, including the ±KTS variants. It can act as both a transcriptional activator and repressor and is described as both a tumor suppressor and, in some contexts, an oncogene.
