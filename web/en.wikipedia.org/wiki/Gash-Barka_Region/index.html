Gash-Barka Region


The Gash River lowlands of western Eritrea.

The Gash-Barka Region (Tigrinya: ጋሽ-ባርካ) is one of the six administrative regions of Eritrea. It lies in the southwestern part of the country, bordering Sudan to the west and Ethiopia to the south, and the Anseba, Maekel, Debub and Southern Red Sea regions internally. Its capital is Barentu. With an area of about 33,200 km², it is Eritrea's largest region, and it takes its name from the Gash and Barka rivers that drain it.

Geography and economy

Gash-Barka is considered the "breadbasket" of Eritrea because of its fertile lowland plains along the Gash and Barka rivers, producing sorghum, millet, sesame and cotton. The region is inhabited by several ethnic groups, including the Tigre, Tigrinya, Kunama and Nara peoples. The towns of Barentu, Tesseney and Agordat are its main settlements, and the region hosts the Aligider agricultural estate and significant gold mining at Zara.

History

The region was heavily affected by the Eritrean War of Independence and by the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000, when fighting around Barentu displaced many residents. It was formed in 1996 when Eritrea's provinces were reorganised into six regions.
