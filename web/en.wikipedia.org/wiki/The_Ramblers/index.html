The Ramblers, formerly the Ramblers' Association, is the largest walking rights organisation in Great Britain, representing walkers and campaigning for public access to the countryside. Founded in 1935, it operates in England, Scotland and Wales through hundreds of local groups and thousands of volunteers.

History and campaigns

The Ramblers grew out of regional rambling federations and campaigns for access to moorland, notably the mass trespass of Kinder Scout in 1932. It played a central role in securing the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949, the mapping of rights of way, and the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000, which created a statutory right to roam in England and Wales. It continues to campaign for the protection and maintenance of the footpath network and for projects such as the England Coast Path.

Activities

Local groups organise led walks, working holidays and path-maintenance work; the charity also promotes walking for health and publishes route information.
