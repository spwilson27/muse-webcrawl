Al-Hafiz

Al-Hafiz li-Din Allah (Arabic: الحافظ لدين الله‎; 1075/76 – 8 October 1149), regnal name Abu'l-Maymun Abd al-Majid ibn Muhammad, was the eleventh Fatimid caliph, reigning in Egypt from 1132 until his death in 1149. His accession, after a period of regency following the murder of his cousin al-Amir, provoked the schism between the Hafizi and Tayyibi branches of Isma'ili Shi'ism.

Accession and schism

Al-Hafiz was the eldest surviving male of the Fatimid line, a cousin of Caliph al-Amir. After al-Amir's assassination in 1130, al-Hafiz served as regent for al-Amir's infant son al-Tayyib, who disappeared in obscure circumstances; in 1132 al-Hafiz had himself proclaimed caliph and imam. Most Isma'ilis in Egypt and parts of Syria and Yemen refused to accept the succession of a cousin over a son, splitting into the Hafizi branch, which recognised al-Hafiz, and the Tayyibi branch, which upheld the hidden imamate of al-Tayyib and survives today in the Dawoodi Bohra and related communities.

Reign

His reign was marked by chronic instability: powerful viziers such as the Armenian Bahram and the military strongman Ridwan ibn Walakhshi dominated the government, revolts and plots shook Cairo, and Fatimid authority contracted in the face of the Crusader states, losing Ascalon soon after his death (1153). Al-Hafiz nevertheless maintained court culture and diplomacy with Norman Sicily and the Crusader kingdoms. He was succeeded by his son al-Zafir.
