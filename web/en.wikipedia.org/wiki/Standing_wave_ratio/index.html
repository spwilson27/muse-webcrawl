Standing wave ratio

In radio engineering and telecommunications, the standing wave ratio (SWR) is a measure of impedance matching between a load — usually an antenna — and the transmission line or source feeding it. SWR is defined as the ratio of the amplitude of the partial standing wave at its maximum to its amplitude at a minimum along the line. A related measure, the voltage standing wave ratio (VSWR), is the most commonly used form:

VSWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 − |Γ|)

where Γ is the (complex) reflection coefficient at the load.

Interpretation

An SWR of 1:1 means a perfect match: all power delivered to the line reaches the load and none is reflected. As the mismatch grows, SWR rises toward infinity (total reflection, as at an open or short circuit). A VSWR of 2:1 corresponds to a reflection coefficient of 1/3, about 11% of power reflected (a return loss of about 9.5 dB) — generally acceptable in many practical systems, while values above 3:1 are commonly considered poor. Mismatch losses and, in high-power transmitters, the reflected power can damage the output stage, so antenna systems are routinely checked with an SWR meter or a vector network analyser.

Measurement and matching

SWR is traditionally measured with a slotted line or with directional couplers that sample forward and reflected waves. To reduce SWR, engineers insert matching networks — stub tuners, quarter-wave transformers, or L-networks of inductors and capacitors — between line and load; in amateur radio, an "antenna tuner" at the transmitter matches the system as seen by the radio, though it does not change the standing waves (and associated losses) on the feedline itself. The Smith chart is the classical graphical tool for these calculations.

Related quantities

SWR is directly related to the reflection coefficient, return loss, and mismatch loss; in acoustics and optics, analogous standing-wave ratios describe mismatched impedances and interfaces.
