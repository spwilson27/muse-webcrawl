Leaving Certificate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Leaving Certificate Examination (Irish: Scrúdú na hArdteistiméireachta), commonly referred to as the Leaving Cert, is the university matriculation examination in Ireland and the final examination of the Irish secondary school system. It takes a minimum of two years of study to prepare for, and is taken by students aged 16–19, at the end of their final year of secondary school, usually in June.

Format and subjects

Students typically study seven or eight subjects from a list of over 30, including Irish, English, mathematics, modern languages, sciences, history, geography, business, and art. Most subjects can be taken at Higher or Ordinary level, with mathematics also offered at Foundation level. Examinations are set and marked externally by the State Examinations Commission.

Grades in a student's best six subjects are converted into points under the Central Applications Office (CAO) system—up to 625 points in total, including a bonus for higher-level mathematics—which determine admission to universities and institutes of technology. A separate Leaving Certificate Vocational Programme (LCVP) and Leaving Certificate Applied (LCA) cater to more vocational pathways.

History and assessment debate

The Leaving Certificate was introduced in 1924, shortly after the foundation of the Irish Free State. The examinations were cancelled in 2020 for the first time because of the COVID-19 pandemic and replaced by calculated and accredited grades; a hybrid model operated in 2021 and 2022. The high-stakes, single-terminal-exam format has long been debated, and reforms toward greater continuous assessment have been announced under the Senior Cycle redevelopment programme.
