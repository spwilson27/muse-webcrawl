General Hospital

General Hospital (often abbreviated as GH) is an American daytime television soap opera. It is listed in Guinness World Records as the longest-running American soap opera in production, having premiered on the ABC network on April 1, 1963. Created by husband-and-wife soap writers Frank and Doris Hursley, the show is set in the fictional city of Port Charles, New York, and originally centred on the staff of the city's General Hospital.

History and highlights

The series was a modest success in its early years but became a cultural phenomenon in the late 1970s and early 1980s under producer Gloria Monty, who introduced faster-paced, youth-oriented storylines involving action, adventure, and romance. The 1981 wedding of supercouple Luke Spencer and Laura Webber drew about 30 million viewers — the largest audience in American daytime television history. Iconic storylines include the Ice Princess saga, the Quartermaine and Spencer families' feuds, and acclaimed social-issue plots such as Stone Cates' HIV/AIDS storyline in the 1990s.

Legacy

General Hospital has won numerous Daytime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Drama Series and launched or boosted the careers of performers such as Anthony Geary, Genie Francis, Demi Moore, John Stamos, and Ricky Martin. Spin-offs include Port Charles and the primetime series General Hospital: Night Shift. It is the last remaining soap opera on ABC and one of only a few daytime dramas still in production in the United States.
