The Convair B-36 "Peacemaker" is a strategic bomber that was built by Convair and operated by the United States Air Force (USAF) from 1949 to 1959. The B-36 is the largest mass-produced piston-engined aircraft ever built, with the longest wingspan of any combat aircraft to enter service. It was the first bomber capable of delivering the first thermonuclear weapons in the US stockpile from within its four bomb bays without major modification, and with an unrefuelled range of 10,000 miles it was the first true intercontinental bomber.

Development

Work began in 1941, when it appeared Britain might fall to Germany and the United States would need a bomber able to strike Europe from North American bases. The requirement evolved into a "10,000-mile bomber" carrying 10,000 lb of bombs. The huge design that emerged from Convair used six 3,800 hp Pratt & Whitney R-4360 Wasp Major radial engines in a pusher configuration, driving 19-foot propellers. The prototype XB-36 flew on 8 August 1946.

Design and service

The wing, 230 ft (70 m) in span, was so thick at the root — over 7 ft — that crew members could crawl through it to service the engines in flight. Defensive armament initially comprised remotely operated turrets with 20 mm cannon. To improve performance over the target, the B-36D and later models added four General Electric J47 jet engines in two underwing pods, giving rise to the phrase "six turning and four burning." The RB-36 reconnaissance variant served for long-range photoreconnaissance, and experimental versions included the NB-36H, which flew with an operating nuclear reactor aboard to test shielding for a nuclear-powered bomber programme, and the GRB-36 "FICON" parasite-fighter carrier.

The B-36 never dropped a bomb in combat. It served as the backbone of Strategic Air Command's nuclear deterrent through the early Cold War until the all-jet B-52 Stratofortress replaced it; the last B-36 retired on 12 February 1959. Its great size, cost and political controversy also made it the centrepiece of the 1949 "Revolt of the Admirals" dispute between the Air Force and the Navy.
