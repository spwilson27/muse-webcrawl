Nick LaRocca

Born April 11, 1889, New Orleans, Louisiana, U.S.

Died February 22, 1961 (aged 71)

Occupation Cornetist, bandleader, composer

Dominic James "Nick" LaRocca (April 11, 1889 – February 22, 1961) was an American jazz cornetist and composer who was the leader of the Original Dixieland Jazz Band (ODJB), the band that made the first jazz recordings in 1917. He composed several jazz standards, most famously "Tiger Rag."

Career

Born in New Orleans to Sicilian immigrant parents, LaRocca was drawn to the city's brass band tradition and taught himself cornet. In 1916 he moved to Chicago and then New York with the band that became the Original Dixieland Jazz Band. In February 1917 the band recorded "Livery Stable Blues" and "Dixie Jass Band One-Step" for the Victor Talking Machine Company; the record was a sensation and helped launch the jazz age.

LaRocca and the ODJB toured England in 1919–1920 to great acclaim. The band's rapid tempos, novelty effects, and energetic ensemble playing shaped early jazz, and LaRocca's compositions — including "Tiger Rag," "Fidgety Feet," "Clarinet Marmalade," and "At the Jazz Band Ball" — became staples of the Dixieland repertoire.

Controversy

LaRocca was notorious for extravagant claims that he had personally invented jazz, and for denying the contributions of African American musicians, whose innovations were central to the music's creation. These claims were rejected by critics and historians even in his lifetime and have permanently colored his reputation. The band broke up in the mid-1920s; LaRocca left music for a career in construction and real estate, briefly reuniting the ODJB in the 1930s.
