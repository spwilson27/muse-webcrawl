Third-party logistics (3PL)
DefinitionOutsourced logistics services: transport, warehousing, fulfilment and related functions
Service typesTransportation, warehousing, freight forwarding, cross-docking, pick and pack, reverse logistics
Related models4PL (lead logistics provider), 5PL (supply-chain integrator)
Major providersDHL Supply Chain, Kuehne+Nagel, DB Schenker, XPO, C.H. Robinson

Third-party logistics (abbreviated 3PL) refers to a company's use of third-party businesses to outsource elements of its distribution, warehousing and fulfilment services. Third-party logistics providers typically specialise in integrated operations — transportation, warehousing, cross-docking, inventory management, packaging and freight forwarding — allowing client firms to focus on their core business while gaining scale, network reach and logistics expertise.

Types and services

Providers range from standard 3PLs offering basic transport and storage, to service developers adding value-added functions such as tracking, cross-docking and specific packaging, to customer adapters that take over a client's complete logistics organisation. Fourth-party logistics (4PL) providers manage a client's whole supply chain, often coordinating multiple 3PLs, and the term 5PL is applied to integrators managing networks through e-business platforms. E-commerce fulfilment — pick, pack, ship and returns for online retailers — has become one of the fastest-growing 3PL segments.

Advantages and disadvantages

Outsourcing converts fixed logistics costs into variable ones, gives access to established distribution networks and technology (warehouse and transport management systems), and supports rapid geographic expansion. Against this, clients surrender a degree of operational control, depend on the provider's quality, and may face integration and communication problems; contracts and service-level agreements are used to manage these risks. The global 3PL market is measured in the trillions of dollars and grows with world trade and e-commerce.
