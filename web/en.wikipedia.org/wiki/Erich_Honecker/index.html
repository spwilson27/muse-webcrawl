Erich Honecker (1912–1994) was a German communist politician who led the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) from 1971 until the weeks before the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. As General Secretary of the Socialist Unity Party (SED) and head of state, he presided over a period of international recognition for the GDR combined with deepening economic stagnation.

Early life

A roofer's son from the Saar, Honecker joined the Communist Party of Germany, worked underground against the Nazis and spent 1937–1945 in Brandenburg-Görden prison. After liberation he rose through the Free German Youth (FDJ), which he chaired.

Leadership

With Soviet backing he replaced Walter Ulbricht in 1971, pursuing the "unity of economic and social policy" — expanded housing and consumer provision financed by Western loans. The GDR gained UN membership and signed the Helsinki Accords, yet dissent was suppressed by the Stasi and the border regime remained lethal.

Fall and exile

Refusing Gorbachev-style reforms, Honecker was ousted by his own Politburo in October 1989. After reunification he faced prosecution for the border shootings, but proceedings were halted on health grounds and he emigrated to Chile, where he died in 1994.
