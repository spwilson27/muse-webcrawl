Fridtjof Wedel-Jarlsberg Nansen (10 October 1861 – 13 May 1930) was a Norwegian polymath and Nobel Peace Prize laureate. He gained fame for leading the team that made the first crossing of the Greenland interior in 1888, traversing the island on cross-country skis. He subsequently achieved international fame as an explorer during his attempt to reach the geographical North Pole aboard the purpose-built ship Fram in 1893–1896, during which he and Hjalmar Johansen reached a record northern latitude of 86°14′.

Nansen also made significant contributions to zoology and oceanography, and later in life turned to diplomacy and humanitarian work. As the League of Nations' High Commissioner for Refugees, he devised the Nansen passport, a travel document for stateless refugees, and supervised relief efforts during the Russian famine of 1921. He was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1922 for his work on behalf of displaced victims of the First World War and related conflicts.

Early life

Nansen was born at Store Frøen, near Christiania (now Oslo), into a family of Danish descent. A champion skier and ice skater in his youth, he studied zoology at the Royal Frederick University, worked at the Bergen Museum, and completed doctoral research on the central nervous system of lower marine creatures.

Greenland crossing

In 1888 Nansen led the first crossing of the Greenland ice cap. Landing on the uninhabited east coast, his party of six crossed westward to Godthaab on skis, proving that the interior was covered by a continuous ice sheet — a journey that captured international attention and made him a national hero.

Fram expedition

Nansen theorised that a transpolar current carried ice from Siberia across the Arctic Ocean toward Greenland. He had the vessel Fram built with a rounded hull designed to be lifted, rather than crushed, by freezing ice, and deliberately froze her into the ice north of Siberia in 1893. When the drift proved slower and less direct than hoped, Nansen and Johansen left the ship in March 1895 and made a dash for the pole by dog sled, reaching 86°14′ N before retreating to Franz Josef Land, where they wintered and were rescued in 1896 — shortly before Fram herself emerged from the ice near Spitsbergen.

Humanitarian work

After Norway's independence in 1905, Nansen served as its first minister in London. Following the First World War he worked for the League of Nations on the repatriation of prisoners of war and, from 1921, as High Commissioner for Refugees. His Nansen passport was eventually recognised by more than fifty governments and allowed hundreds of thousands of refugees to travel and resettle. The Nansen International Office for Refugees received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1938, continuing his legacy after his death.
