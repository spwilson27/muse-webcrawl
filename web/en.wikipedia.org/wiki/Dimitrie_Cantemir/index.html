Dimitrie Cantemir

Dimitrie Cantemir (26 October 1673 – 21 August 1723) was a Moldavian prince, statesman, scholar, historian, philosopher, and composer. He ruled Moldavia twice (1693 and 1710–1711) and became one of the most learned men of his age, a member of the Berlin Academy of Sciences and a figure equally at home in Ottoman Constantinople and in the Russia of Peter the Great.

Cantemir spent much of his youth in Constantinople, first as a hostage and later as Moldavia's envoy to the Sublime Porte, where he mastered Turkish, Arabic, and Persian alongside Greek, Latin, and several European languages. His great historical work, written in Latin, was the History of the Growth and Decay of the Ottoman Empire, long a standard European source on the Ottomans. He also wrote the Descriptio Moldaviae, a description of his homeland's geography, customs, and institutions, commissioned by the Berlin Academy.

As prince, Cantemir allied Moldavia with Russia against the Ottomans in 1711, but the campaign ended in defeat at the Battle of Stănilești on the river Prut. Cantemir fled to Russia, where Peter granted him estates and the title of prince of the Russian Empire; he spent his remaining years writing. He also devised a notation system for Ottoman Turkish music and composed pieces that survive in the Turkish classical repertoire. His son Antioch Cantemir became a noted Russian poet and diplomat.
