Nohkalikai Falls is a waterfall in India, located in the East Khasi Hills district of Meghalaya, near Cherrapunji (Sohra), one of the wettest places on Earth. With a height of about 340 metres (1,120 feet), it is the tallest plunge waterfall in India. The water falls from a plateau above into a deep gorge, landing in a striking turquoise pool.

Name and legend

The name Nohkalikai comes from the Khasi words noh ("jump"), ka (a feminine prefix), and Likai — "the jump of Likai". According to local legend, a woman named Likai lived in the village of Rangjyrteh upstream of the falls. After her husband died, she remarried, but her second husband grew jealous of the love she gave her infant daughter. While Likai was away at work, he killed the child and cooked her flesh in a meal. Returning home, Likai ate the food, then discovered her daughter's fingers in a basket of betel nuts and, driven mad with grief, ran to the edge of the plateau and threw herself over. The falls were thereafter named for her.

Geography

The waterfall is fed almost entirely by rainwater collected on a small plateau and loses much of its flow in the dry season, when it reduces to a trickle. Cherrapunji's extreme monsoon rainfall makes the falls most spectacular between June and September. Nohsngithiang Falls (also called the Seven Sisters Falls) and Nohkalikai are among the best-known waterfalls of the region, and a viewpoint near the village of Laitkynsew offers views of the gorge.

Tourism

Nohkalikai is one of Meghalaya's most visited natural attractions. A viewing platform maintained near the falls allows visitors to see the full drop. During the monsoon the pool below glows a vivid blue-green colour that has become a signature of photographs of the falls.
