Longest flights

The longest flights are the non-stop scheduled passenger routes with the greatest distance and flight time. Since the late 2010s, ultra-long-haul services using Airbus A350-900ULR and Boeing 777-200LR aircraft have linked cities more than 15,000 km apart, with scheduled times approaching 19 hours.

Singapore Airlines' New York–Singapore service, operated with the A350-900ULR in a two-class configuration, is generally regarded as the world's longest scheduled passenger flight by distance and duration. Qantas has pursued "Project Sunrise" — A350-1000 services from Sydney to London and New York exceeding 19 hours — for launch in the mid-2020s.

History

The title of longest scheduled flight has moved progressively: Singapore Airlines' Singapore–Newark route (2004–2013, A340-500) was the first modern ultra-long-haul service; it was surpassed by Emirates' Dubai–Auckland service in 2016 and then by Qatar Airways' Doha–Auckland flight in 2017. Earlier endurance records belong to military and test flights, and the longest commercial operations were curtailed at various times by fuel prices and aircraft economics.

Cargo and records

By great-circle distance, the longest non-stop cargo and record flights exceed passenger services; the absolute flight endurance record remains the 1958–59 Cessna 172 flight of 64 days 22 hours, sustained by aerial refuelling.
