The Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress, commonly called the Poet Laureate of the United States, is appointed by the Librarian of Congress and earns a stipend. The laureate's office is administered by the Library's Poetry and Literature Center. Unlike the British Poet Laureate, the American office carries few formal duties; each laureate undertakes a signature project to broaden the audience for poetry.

History

The position grew out of the Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress, created in 1937 with funds donated by Archer M. Huntington. Consultants included Robert Frost, William Carlos Williams, Conrad Aiken, Elizabeth Bishop, and Robert Lowell. In 1985, an act of Congress redesignated the consultant as Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry, and Robert Penn Warren — who had been a consultant decades earlier — became the first to hold the new title in 1986.

Duties and projects

The laureate gives an annual lecture and reading at the Library of Congress and is expected to promote poetry nationally. Signature projects have included Robert Pinsky's Favorite Poem Project, Billy Collins's Poetry 180 (a poem a day for high schools), Ted Kooser's syndicated newspaper column "American Life in Poetry", and Natasha Trethewey's "Where Poetry Lives" features. Laureates receive a stipend (long $35,000 per year, later supplemented by private funding) and a travel grant.
