Maldon Sea Salt is a sea salt produced by the Maldon Salt Company in the town of Maldon, Essex, England. It is known for its distinctive flaky pyramid-shaped crystals, soft crunchy texture, and pale appearance, and is widely used as a finishing salt by chefs and home cooks around the world.

History

Salt has been harvested around Maldon since at least the medieval period, when the area's salt pans appeared in records relating to the Domesday Book era. The modern Maldon Salt Company was founded in 1882 and continues to operate from Maldon on the Blackwater Estuary.

Production

Seawater from the River Blackwater estuary is filtered and concentrated by evaporation. Craftsmen known as "saltmakers" harvest the flakes by hand using traditional methods, drawing the brine at the point where the delicate pyramid-shaped crystals form on the surface of the pans. The flakes dissolve readily and are prized for their clean, intensely saline taste with minimal bitterness.

Recognition

In 2012 the company received a Royal Warrant from Queen Elizabeth II as purveyor of sea salt. Maldon salt is sold in the company's characteristic green packaging and is used as a finishing salt on dishes ranging from roast vegetables to chocolate desserts.
