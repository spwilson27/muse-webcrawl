The Ottoman–Venetian War of 1570–1573, known as the Fourth Ottoman–Venetian War or the War of Cyprus, was fought between the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Venice for control of Cyprus, a Venetian possession since 1489.

Course of the war

In 1570 Ottoman forces landed on Cyprus, capturing Nicosia and, after a brutal eleven-month siege, Famagusta in August 1571; its Venetian commander Marcantonio Bragadin was flayed alive after surrendering. Pope Pius V assembled a Holy League of Venice, Spain, and other Christian states whose fleet under Don John of Austria annihilated the Ottoman navy at the Battle of Lepanto on 7 October 1571 — one of the largest naval battles in history.

Outcome

Despite the triumph at Lepanto, the League failed to recover Cyprus and soon fractured. Venice made a separate peace in March 1573, formally ceding Cyprus to the Ottomans and paying an indemnity, though it retained trade privileges. The war confirmed Ottoman dominion in the eastern Mediterranean even as Lepanto shattered the aura of Ottoman invincibility at sea.
