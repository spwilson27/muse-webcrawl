Casa da Índia

The Casa da Índia (Portuguese for "House of India") was the Portuguese Crown's central administrative body for overseas trade, shipping and colonial affairs, founded in Lisbon around 1500 under King Manuel I. It managed Portugal's trade with India, Africa and later Brazil and the Far East.

History

The Casa da Índia grew out of earlier institutions such as the Casa da Guiné and the Casa de Ceuta, which had administered Portuguese exploration along the West African coast. As the India run (Carreira da Índia) became the centrepiece of the Portuguese trading empire after Vasco da Gama's voyage of 1497–1499, the Casa was reorganised to handle the enormous flow of spices, especially black pepper, entering Lisbon.

From its headquarters in the Ribeira Palace on the Tagus waterfront, the Casa supervised the outfitting of fleets, the licensing of private traders, the collection of customs duties and the sale of imported goods. It also maintained secret charts and navigational information through its cartographic workshop.

Decline

Portuguese trade declined in the later sixteenth century under pressure from Dutch and English competition, and the Casa's importance waned. The building and its archives were destroyed in the Lisbon earthquake of 1755, a major loss for historians of the Portuguese empire.
