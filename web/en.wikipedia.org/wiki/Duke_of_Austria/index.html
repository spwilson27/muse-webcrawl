Duke of Austria

Duke of Austria
First holder: Henry II Jasomirgott (1156)
Residence: Hofburg, Vienna
Superseded by: Archduke of Austria

Duke of Austria was the title of the ruler of the Duchy of Austria within the Holy Roman Empire. The duchy was created in 1156 when the Privilegium Minus of Emperor Frederick Barbarossa raised the Margraviate of Austria to a duchy and granted it to the Babenberg margrave Henry II "Jasomirgott".

Babenberg and Habsburg dukes

The Babenberg dukes ruled until the male line failed in 1246, after which Austria became a contested prize, eventually won by Ottokar II of Bohemia. In 1278 Rudolf of Habsburg defeated Ottokar at the Battle on the Marchfeld, and in 1282 he enfeoffed his sons with Austria — beginning more than six centuries of Habsburg rule.

From dukes to archdukes

In 1358/59 Duke Rudolf IV forged the Privilegium Maius, claiming the elevated title of Archduke; it was recognised only in 1453 by Emperor Frederick III, himself a Habsburg. Thereafter the Habsburg rulers of Austria styled themselves archdukes, and "Duke of Austria" survived as a subsidiary title within the monarch's grand title. Notable dukes include Leopold III the Holy, patron saint of Austria, and Leopold V, who imprisoned Richard the Lionheart at Dürnstein in 1192.
