United States Department of Transportation

The United States Department of Transportation (USDOT or DOT) is a cabinet department of the U.S. federal government concerned with transportation. Its mission is to ensure a fast, safe, efficient, accessible, and convenient transportation system.

Department of Transportation
Formed: October 15, 1966
Headquarters: 1200 New Jersey Ave SE, Washington, D.C.
Head: Secretary of Transportation

History

The department was created by an act of Congress signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson on October 15, 1966, and began operating April 1, 1967, consolidating transportation functions scattered across government. The Coast Guard belonged to it until moving to Homeland Security in 2003.

Agencies

Operating administrations include the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), Federal Highway Administration, Federal Railroad Administration, Federal Transit Administration, Maritime Administration, and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), which sets vehicle safety standards and oversees recalls.

Functions

DOT funds and regulates highways, aviation, rail, transit, pipelines, and maritime transport, and develops national transportation policy.
