Cerro de la Silla

Cerro de la Silla
Elevation: 1,820 m (5,970 ft)
Location: Monterrey, Nuevo León, Mexico
Designation: Natural Monument (1991)
Cerro de la Silla, the symbol of Monterrey.

The Cerro de la Silla ("Saddle Hill") is a mountain and natural monument located within the metropolitan area of Monterrey, in the northeastern Mexican state of Nuevo León. It is named for its distinctive saddle-like profile and is the best-known symbol of the city of Monterrey.

Geography

The mountain covers an area of about 6,045 hectares and spans the municipalities of Monterrey, Guadalupe and Juárez. It has four principal peaks: Pico Antena, Pico Norte, Pico la Virgen and the Loma de la Antena. The highest, Pico Norte, rises to about 1,820 metres (5,970 ft) above sea level. Telecommunication antennae crown one summit, and a cable car project of the 1960s was abandoned after a fatal accident in 1964.

History and protection

On 26 April 1991 the Cerro de la Silla was declared a Natural Monument by the federal government of Mexico, the first area in the country to receive that category of protection. The mountain appears on the coat of arms and flag of Monterrey and is reproduced in the logos of local institutions, including the football club C.F. Monterrey and the Cervecería Cuauhtémoc Moctezuma brewery's Carta Blanca branding historically.

Ecology and recreation

The slopes support scrubland and oak forest that shelter species such as armadillos, coyotes and numerous birds. Hiking trails lead to the summits, and the mountain is a popular destination for climbers and runners in the Monterrey area.
