Giant trevally

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Animalia

Phylum: Chordata

Class: Actinopterygii

Order: Carangiformes

Family: Carangidae

Genus: Caranx

Species: C. ignobilis

Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The giant trevally (Caranx ignobilis), also known as the lowly trevally, barrier trevally, ronin jack, giant kingfish or ulua in Hawaii, is a species of large marine fish in the jack family, Carangidae. The giant trevally is distributed throughout the tropical waters of the Indo-Pacific region, ranging from South Africa to Hawaii and from Japan to Australia.

Description

The species is the largest member of the genus Caranx, growing to a maximum recorded length of 170 cm and a weight of 80 kg. It is a powerful, heavily built fish with a steep, blunt head profile. Colouration varies from silvery in juveniles and smaller adults to dark grey or near-black in large males. The lateral line bears a series of hard scutes toward the tail.

Distribution and habitat

Giant trevally occupy a wide range of habitats. Juveniles frequent estuaries, lagoons, and shallow bays, while adults range over deep reefs, offshore atolls, and open water, sometimes descending to depths beyond 100 m. Solitary large adults patrol reef drop-offs, while juveniles often form schools.

Biology

An apex predator in many reef systems, the giant trevally feeds mainly on other fish, as well as crustaceans and cephalopods. It hunts both alone and in groups, and is noted for striking at birds resting on the water surface or in flight low over lagoons — behaviour popularised by natural history filming at Farquhar Atoll. Spawning occurs in aggregations during warmer months, often around the new moon.

Relationship to humans

The species is highly prized by sport fishers, particularly in catch-and-release "GT popping" fisheries with topwater lures, and it is an important commercial fish in parts of its range. In Hawaii the ulua holds cultural significance and features in traditional fishing practices. Large specimens from some regions carry a risk of ciguatera poisoning.
