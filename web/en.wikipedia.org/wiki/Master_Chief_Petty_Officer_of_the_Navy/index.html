The Master Chief Petty Officer of the Navy (MCPON, pronounced "mik-pon") is a unique non-commissioned rank and the senior enlisted adviser in the United States Navy. The holder of the post is the senior enlisted member of the Navy and serves as the principal adviser to the Chief of Naval Operations and the Chief of Naval Personnel on all matters affecting enlisted personnel, their families, morale, training and quality of life.

History

The billet was created in 1966–1967 in response to recommendations that the Navy's leadership needed a direct channel to its enlisted force, following the example of the Sergeant Major of the Army. The first MCPON, Master Chief Gunner's Mate Delbert D. Black, took office on 13 January 1967; the position was originally titled the Senior Enlisted Adviser of the Navy before being renamed. Black's widow, Ima Black, remained a revered figure in the Navy community for decades afterward.

Role

The MCPON travels extensively throughout the fleet, meeting sailors and their families, testifies before Congress on enlisted quality-of-life issues, and serves on boards dealing with pay, housing, uniforms and retention. The MCPON is appointed by the Chief of Naval Operations and typically serves a term aligned with that officer, generally around four years. In protocol terms the MCPON is accorded precedence comparable to a three-star admiral and is the senior enlisted leader of the Navy, senior in precedence to all other enlisted members.

Insignia

The MCPON wears a unique rating badge: in place of the specialty mark between the eagle ("crow") and the top chevron, the MCPON badge bears a gold inverted five-pointed star, and the cap device and collar insignia bear three gold stars. Fleet and force master chiefs wear related but distinct insignia.
