Absalon (c. 1128 – 21 March 1201) was a Danish statesman, prelate and warrior who served as Bishop of Roskilde and Archbishop of Lund. A close friend and chief adviser of King Valdemar I the Great, he is traditionally regarded as the founder of Copenhagen.

Early life

Absalon was born into the powerful Hvide clan, son of Asser Rig, and was foster-brother to the future king Valdemar. He studied theology in Paris and became Bishop of Roskilde in 1158.

Crusades against the Wends

With Valdemar, Absalon led Danish crusades against the pagan Wends of the southern Baltic coast. In 1168–69 he helped capture the temple fortress of Arkona on Rügen, destroyed the idol of Svetovid, and founded churches in the conquered territory. He fortified the fishing settlement of Havn with a castle around 1167, a foundation later celebrated — though debated by historians — as the origin of Copenhagen.

Churchman and statesman

Elected Archbishop of Lund in 1177, Absalon reformed the Danish church, promoted monastic orders, and patronised the historian Saxo Grammaticus, whose Gesta Danorum was written at his urging. He died at Sorø Abbey in 1201 and was buried there.

Legacy

An equestrian statue of Absalon stands at Højbro Plads in Copenhagen, and the Danish Navy has named ships after him, including the Absalon-class support ships.
