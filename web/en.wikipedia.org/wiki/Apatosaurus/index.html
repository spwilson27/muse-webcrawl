Apatosaurus (meaning "deceptive lizard") is a genus of herbivorous sauropod dinosaur that lived in North America during the Late Jurassic period, about 152 to 151 million years ago. Its fossils come from the Morrison Formation of Colorado, Wyoming, Utah and Oklahoma.

Description

Apatosaurus was a huge quadruped with a long, whip-like tail, a long neck and a small head, reaching 21–23 metres in length and perhaps 16–23 tonnes in mass. It browsed on vegetation with peg-like teeth and, unlike its relative Diplodocus, had a heavier, more robust build and a deeper chest.

Taxonomy and the Brontosaurus question

Othniel Charles Marsh named Apatosaurus ajax in 1877 and Brontosaurus excelsus in 1879 during the Bone Wars. In 1903 the latter was reclassified as a species of Apatosaurus, and for most of the 20th century "Brontosaurus" was treated as a junior synonym, though the name persisted in popular culture. A large 2015 specimen-level study concluded that Brontosaurus differs enough to be restored as a separate genus, a view many, but not all, palaeontologists accept.
