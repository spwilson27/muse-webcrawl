Fast-moving consumer goods

Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG), also known as consumer packaged goods (CPG), are products that are sold quickly and at a relatively low cost. Examples include non-durable household goods such as packaged foods, beverages, toiletries, candies, cosmetics, over-the-counter drugs, dry goods, and other consumables. The category is characterised by high sales volumes, low profit margins per unit, rapid shelf turnover, and frequent purchase.

Characteristics

From the consumer's perspective, FMCG items are bought frequently, involve low expenditure and little deliberation, and are consumed or used up quickly. From the retailer's perspective, they generate high stock turnover and require intensive distribution: successful brands must be available in very large numbers of outlets. Because margins per unit are thin, producers rely on scale, brand recognition, and advertising to sustain profitability. Shelf space competition in supermarkets is intense, and manufacturers often pay for prominent placement or run continuous promotional cycles.

Industry

The global FMCG sector is dominated by large multinational corporations such as Nestlé, Procter & Gamble, Unilever, PepsiCo, Coca-Cola, and L'Oréal, each owning portfolios of brands across food, drink, household, and personal-care categories. Major market segments include food and beverages; personal care and cosmetics; household care products such as detergents and cleaning agents; and tobacco. In developing markets, single-serve packaging such as shampoo sachets has been an important adaptation to low consumer purchasing power.

Trends

Recent decades have seen growing retailer own-label (private label) competition, consolidation among manufacturers, and a shift of advertising and sales toward e-commerce and direct-to-consumer channels. Consumer pressure over health, plastic packaging, and sustainability has prompted reformulation of products and investment in recyclable or refillable packaging.
