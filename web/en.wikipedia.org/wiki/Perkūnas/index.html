Perkūnas (Latvian: Pērkons; Old Prussian: Perkūns) was the common Baltic god of thunder, one of the most important deities in the Baltic pantheon. In both Lithuanian and Latvian mythology he is the god of the sky, thunder, lightning, storms, rain, fire and oaks, and is invoked as a guardian of law and order who strikes down evil beings, especially the devil (Lithuanian velnias).

Etymology and origins

The name is related to the Slavic thunder god Perun and is usually traced to the Proto-Indo-European root *perkʷu-, "oak", possibly via a form meaning "the striker" or "lord of oaks". Perkūnas is often compared with Thor, Zeus, Jupiter and Indra as reflexes of the Indo-European thunder god.

Mythology and cult

Perkūnas rides through the sky in a chariot, hurling lightning bolts and his stone axe, which returns to his hand. He is associated with sacred oak groves, perpetual fires tended by priests, and hilltop shrines. Chronicles of the Teutonic Order and later writers such as Jan Łasicki describe his cult among the Lithuanians, Samogitians and Old Prussians, including an idol at the Prussian sanctuary of Romuva and a sacred oak at which an eternal flame burned. His day was Thursday, and in folklore thunder is still called by his name in Lithuanian (perkūnija).

Legacy

Perkūnas survived Christianisation in folklore, proverbs and place names, and became an emblem of Lithuanian and Latvian national identity; his name is borne by sports clubs, ships, and the Lithuanian male choir tradition, and his image features in the neo-pagan Romuva revival.
