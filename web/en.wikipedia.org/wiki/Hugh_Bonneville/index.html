Hugh Bonneville (born Hugh Richard Bonniwell Williams; 10 November 1963) is an English actor. He is best known for portraying Robert Crawley, Earl of Grantham, in the ITV historical drama series Downton Abbey from 2010 to 2015, a role for which he received nominations including a Golden Globe Award and two consecutive Primetime Emmy Awards, as well as several Screen Actors Guild Awards as part of the ensemble cast.

Early life and education

Bonneville was born in Paddington, London, the son of a urological surgeon and a nurse. He was educated at Sherborne School in Dorset and read theology at Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, where he acted with the Cambridge Footlights. He then trained at the Central School of Speech and Drama in London.

Career

Bonneville began on the stage, joining the National Theatre and the Royal Shakespeare Company, where his roles included Laertes opposite Kenneth Branagh's Hamlet. On screen he became a familiar face in British film and television, with roles in Notting Hill (1999), Iris (2001) — playing the young John Bayley — The Monocled Mutineer, and the BBC series Twenty Twelve (2011–2012), in which he played Ian Fletcher, a role he reprised in the sitcom W1A (2014–2017).

His portrayal of the Earl of Grantham in Downton Abbey brought him international recognition, and he returned for the feature-film sequels Downton Abbey (2019) and Downton Abbey: A New Era (2022). He also played Mr Brown in the family films Paddington (2014), Paddington 2 (2017), and Paddington in Peru (2024), and appeared in The Monuments Men (2014) and Viceroy's House (2017), in which he played Lord Mountbatten.

Personal life and charity work

Bonneville lives in West Sussex. He is a patron of several charities, including the children's medical research charity Sparks and the homelessness charity Scene & Heard, which he co-founded, and he has narrated documentaries and audiobooks and presented television programmes on classical music.
