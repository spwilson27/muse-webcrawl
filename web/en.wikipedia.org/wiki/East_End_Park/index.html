East End Park

East End Park, currently known as KDM Group East End Park for sponsorship purposes, is a football stadium situated in Dunfermline, Fife, Scotland, with a seating capacity of 11,480. It is the home ground of Dunfermline Athletic Football Club, who have played there since the club's formation in 1885.

History

The ground lies on Halbeath Road in the east end of Dunfermline, from which it takes its name. The stadium was substantially redeveloped during the 1990s under chairman Roy Woodrow, when the main stand and two end stands were rebuilt as modern all-seater structures as part of an ambitious era that saw the club — nicknamed the Pars — compete in the Scottish top flight and European competition.

East End Park's record attendance, in excess of 27,000, was set for a Scottish Cup tie against Celtic in 1968, an era when terracing allowed far larger crowds than the modern seated capacity. The stadium was also used for the club's famous European nights of the 1960s, when Dunfermline, managed by Jock Stein and later George Farm, reached the semi-finals of the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1969.

Facilities

The stadium comprises four stands: the Main Stand, the Norrie McCathie Stand (named after the long-serving club captain), the East Stand and the North Stand. Its pitch was converted to an artificial surface for a period in the 2000s before reverting to grass. The ground also hosts community and youth football activities.
