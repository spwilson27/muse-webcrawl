Antonin Gregory Scalia (1936–2016) was an American jurist who served as an associate justice of the Supreme Court of the United States from 1986 until his death. Nominated by President Ronald Reagan and confirmed 98–0 by the Senate, he was the Court's leading advocate of originalism in constitutional interpretation and textualism in statutory interpretation.

Jurisprudence

Scalia argued that the Constitution should be read according to its original public meaning and opposed the notion of a "living Constitution". Celebrated and criticised for his combative, literary dissents, he wrote majority opinions in cases such as District of Columbia v. Heller (2008), recognising an individual right to keep and bear arms, and Crawford v. Washington (2004) on the Confrontation Clause. He dissented in major cases on abortion, affirmative action and same-sex marriage.

Life and legacy

A devout Catholic and father of nine, Scalia taught law at the University of Virginia and the University of Chicago and served in the Nixon and Ford administrations before Reagan appointed him to the D.C. Circuit in 1982. He died in February 2016 at a Texas ranch; after a year-long vacancy fight, his seat was filled by Neil Gorsuch. Scalia's influence on American legal method — especially the mainstreaming of originalism — is widely regarded as transformative.
