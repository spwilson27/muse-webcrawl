Haystacks (Lake District)

Haystacks is a hill in the Lake District, Cumbria, England. Elevation: 597 m (1,959 ft). Prominence: about 154 m. Listing: Wainwright, Hewitt, Nuttall.

Haystacks is a fell in the English Lake District, situated at the head of the Buttermere valley. Although modest in height at 597 metres (1,959 ft), it is one of the best-loved of the Lakeland fells, largely because of its association with the fell-walking writer Alfred Wainwright, who declared it his favourite mountain.

Geography

The fell rises between Buttermere and the Ennerdale valley, north of the Honister Pass. Its summit plateau is a complex, rocky terrain of small tarns, outcrops and peat hags that Wainwright described as having, for a modest height, "a variety of features seldom matched". Innominate Tarn, on the summit plateau, is among its best-known features, with views to Great Gable and Pillar.

Wainwright

Wainwright wrote of Haystacks: "One should always have a definite objective in a walk, as in life — it is so much more satisfying to reach a target by personal effort than to wander aimlessly... Haystacks it is." Following his wishes, his ashes were scattered beside Innominate Tarn after his death in 1991. The fell appears in Book Seven of his Pictorial Guide to the Lakeland Fells, The Western Fells.

Ascent

The usual ascent is from Gatesgarth at the head of Buttermere via Scarth Gap, or from the Honister Slate Mine at the top of Honister Pass. A popular round combines Haystacks with Fleetwith Pike above Buttermere.
