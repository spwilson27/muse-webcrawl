Levens Hall is a manor house in Cumbria, England, about five miles south of Kendal. Built around a 14th-century pele tower and enlarged in the Elizabethan period, it is a Grade I listed building and the home of the Bagot family. It is internationally renowned for its topiary gardens, among the oldest surviving in the world.

History

The original pele tower dates to about 1350, built by the de Redman family as a defence against Scottish raiders. In the late 16th century the Bellingham family transformed it into a gentleman's residence, adding the fine panelled interiors, plasterwork, and stained glass for which the house is known. After passing through several hands — including Colonel James Grahme, a former keeper of the privy purse to Charles II — the estate came by marriage to the Bagot family, in whose ownership it remains.

The gardens

The topiary garden was laid out from 1694 by Guillaume Beaumont, a French gardener who had trained under André Le Nôtre at Versailles. Its yew and box shapes — umbrellas, peacocks, geometric cones and whimsical forms — have been continuously maintained for over 300 years, making it the oldest topiary garden in the world still in its original design. The grounds also include a nuttery, herb garden, and one of the earliest ha-has in England.

Today

Levens Hall and its gardens are open to the public during the season. The house contains fine furniture, paintings, and reputed Napoleonic relics, and the estate operates a brewery producing beer under the Levens brand.
