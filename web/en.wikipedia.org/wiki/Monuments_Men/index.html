Monuments Men

Monuments, Fine Arts, and Archives program (MFAA)
Active1943–1946
CountriesUnited States, United Kingdom, and other Allied nations
PersonnelApproximately 345 men and women from 13 countries
RoleProtection, recovery, and restitution of cultural property during and after World War II
Nickname"Monuments Men"

The Monuments Men were the members of the Monuments, Fine Arts, and Archives (MFAA) program, established in 1943 under the Civil Affairs and Military Government sections of the Allied armies. Some 345 men and women from thirteen countries — museum directors, curators, art historians, conservators, architects, and artists — served in uniform to protect cultural property during the Second World War and to find and return the millions of artworks and artifacts looted by the Nazis.

Origins

American museum leaders, including Paul J. Sachs of Harvard's Fogg Museum and Francis Henry Taylor of the Metropolitan Museum, pressed the U.S. government to safeguard Europe's monuments. The American Commission for the Protection and Salvage of Artistic and Historic Monuments in War Areas (the Roberts Commission) was created in 1943, and MFAA officers were attached to advancing armies with lists and maps of sites to protect.

Recoveries

As the Reich collapsed, Monuments officers uncovered vast Nazi repositories in castles, churches, and salt mines. At Altaussee in Austria they found some 6,500 paintings, including Michelangelo's Bruges Madonna and the Ghent Altarpiece of the van Eyck brothers; at Merkers they shared in uncovering gold and art stores; and at Neuschwanstein they processed the archives of the ERR, the Nazi looting agency. Two MFAA officers were killed in action. Recovered works were gathered at central collecting points in Munich, Wiesbaden, and Offenbach, and millions of objects were restituted to their countries of origin, though provenance work continues to this day.

Legacy

The program's members included future luminaries of the art world — among them James Rorimer, later director of the Met; Lincoln Kirstein, co-founder of the New York City Ballet; and Charles Parkhurst. Robert M. Edsel's 2009 book The Monuments Men and George Clooney's 2014 film of the same name popularized their story. The Monuments Men and Women Foundation continues the mission of locating and returning looted art.
