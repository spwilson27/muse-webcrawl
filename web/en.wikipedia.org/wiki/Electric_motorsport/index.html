Electric motorsport

Electric motorsport is a category of motor racing that uses electric vehicles powered by batteries rather than internal combustion engines. The field grew rapidly in the 2010s as manufacturers and governing bodies sought sustainable racing formats and a development platform for road-car electric technology.

Major series

The best-known championship is Formula E, the FIA's single-seater electric series launched in 2014, racing mainly on temporary street circuits in world cities; teams have included manufacturers such as Porsche, Jaguar, Nissan and Maserati. Extreme E, launched in 2021, raced electric SUVs in remote locations to highlight climate issues. The FIM MotoE World Cup runs electric motorcycle races supporting MotoGP, and the Electric GT and ETCR series cover touring cars. The World Rallycross Championship switched its top class to electric power in 2022.

Characteristics

Electric race cars deliver instant torque but are constrained by battery capacity and thermal limits, so race formats emphasise energy management, regenerative braking and attack-mode power boosts. Their low noise allows racing in city centres closed to conventional motorsport.

Records and hill climbs

Electric cars have excelled in the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb, where the Volkswagen I.D. R set the outright record in 2018. Electric vehicles also dominate drag-racing acceleration records for production cars.
