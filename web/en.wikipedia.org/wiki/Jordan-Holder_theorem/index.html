Jordan-Holder theorem

FieldAbstract algebra (group theory, module theory)
Named afterCamille Jordan; Otto Hölder
StatementAny two composition series of a given group have the same length and the same composition factors, up to permutation and isomorphism

In group theory, the Jordan-Holder theorem (also written Jordan–Hölder theorem) states that any two composition series of a given group are equivalent: they have the same length, and their composition factors are the same up to permutation and isomorphism. It is a far-reaching generalisation of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.

Background

A composition series of a group G is a finite chain of subgroups in which each term is a maximal proper normal subgroup of the next, so that every quotient (composition factor) is a simple group. Not every group has a composition series, but every finite group does. Camille Jordan proved in 1869 that the composition factors of a group have invariant orders; Otto Hölder strengthened the result in 1889 to full uniqueness up to isomorphism and permutation.

Statement and proof ideas

Formally, if G admits a composition series, then any two composition series have the same length, and there is a bijection between their factors such that corresponding factors are isomorphic. The standard proof proceeds by the Schreier refinement theorem, which says any two subnormal series have equivalent refinements, combined with the Zassenhaus (butterfly) lemma used to compare successive quotients.

Generalisation

The theorem extends verbatim to modules: a module of finite length has composition series whose factors are unique up to order and isomorphism, and its length is well defined. Analogues hold in other algebraic structures admitting a good theory of normal subobjects. The result underlies the classification approach to finite groups through their simple composition factors.
