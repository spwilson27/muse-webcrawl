elBulli

elBulli
Location: Cala Montjoi, Roses, Girona, Spain
Chef: Ferran Adrià
Operated: 1961–2011 (as a restaurant)
Rating: Three Michelin stars

elBulli was a restaurant near the town of Roses, Catalonia, Spain, run by chef Ferran Adrià. Widely described as the most influential restaurant of its era, it was voted the world's best restaurant a record five times by Restaurant magazine's World's 50 Best Restaurants list, and held three Michelin stars.

History

Founded in 1961 as a beach bar by German owners Hans and Marketta Schilling (named after their French bulldogs, the "bullis"), the restaurant evolved into a fine-dining establishment. Ferran Adrià joined the kitchen in 1984 and, with Juli Soler and later his brother Albert Adrià, transformed it into a laboratory of avant-garde "molecular gastronomy" — a term Adrià disliked, preferring "deconstructivist" cooking.

Cuisine

The restaurant served a single tasting menu of some 30–40 courses, open only about six months a year, and received around two million reservation requests for roughly 8,000 seats per season. Signature creations included spherified "olives", hot gelatins, foams made with a siphon, and dishes deconstructed into unexpected forms. Each winter the kitchen closed and the team developed the next season's dishes at the elBulli workshop in Barcelona, publishing the recipes in elaborate catalogues.

Closure and legacy

elBulli served its final meal on 30 July 2011 and was later reconceived as elBulli1846, a museum and creativity centre on the same site. Its alumni — including René Redzepi, Grant Achatz, and the Roca brothers — carried its techniques and ethos across world gastronomy.
