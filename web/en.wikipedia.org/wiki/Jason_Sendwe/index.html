Jason Sendwe (1917 – 19 June 1964) was a Congolese politician and a leader of the Luba people of Katanga Province. He was a close associate of Patrice Lumumba and a rival of Moise Tshombe, the leader of the secessionist Katanga movement. Sendwe led the BALUBAKAT party (Association of the Baluba of Katanga), which represented northern Katanga's Luba population against Tshombe's CONAKAT.

Political career

In the elections preceding Congolese independence in 1960, BALUBAKAT won a significant share of seats in the Katanga provincial assembly. When Tshombe declared Katanga's secession in July 1960, Sendwe's party opposed it, and northern Katanga became a theatre of conflict between Baluba communities and the Katangese gendarmerie. Sendwe served as Deputy Prime Minister in the restored central government of Cyrille Adoula from 1961.

North Katanga

In 1962, northern Katanga was separated from the rest of Katanga as the province of North Katanga (later Shaba and now Tanganyika Province), with Sendwe as its president. After the end of the Katangese secession in January 1963, Sendwe attempted to maintain his position amid continuing instability.

Death

Sendwe was killed on 19 June 1964 near Albertville (Kalemie) during the Simba rebellion, which swept through eastern Congo in 1964. The circumstances of his death, at the hands of Simba rebels, remain a subject of historical debate.
