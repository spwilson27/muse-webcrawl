Swimfins

[Image placeholder: swimfins]

Swimfins, swim fins, diving fins or flippers are fin-like accessories worn on the feet to aid movement through water in water sports activities such as swimming, bodyboarding, bodysurfing, finswimming, and scuba diving. They work by displacing more water than the bare foot, increasing propulsion for the same leg effort.

History

Early concepts were sketched by Leonardo da Vinci and Benjamin Franklin, who as a boy made hand paddles and later oval foot fins. Practical rubber swimfins were developed in the 1930s: Louis de Corlieu patented his propulseurs in France in 1933, and Owen Churchill's design, influenced by fins he saw in Tahiti, was adopted by the United States Navy and popularised during the Second World War.

Types

Full-foot fins enclose the heel and are worn barefoot or with neoprene socks, while open-heel fins use adjustable straps and are worn over diving boots. Blades vary from short, stiff fins used by bodysurfers to long freediving fins and split fins, which have a lengthwise slit claimed to reduce drag. Competitive finswimmers may use a monofin.

Use

In scuba diving, fins enable divers to move while carrying heavy equipment and reduce air consumption. Lifeguards, military divers, and surfers all use specialised variants. Improperly used, powerful fin kicks can damage coral and disturb marine life.
