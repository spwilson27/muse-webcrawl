Alternative country (sometimes called alt-country, and closely related to Americana) is a loosely defined subgenre of country music that draws on traditional country, folk and roots music while incorporating the attitude and DIY ethos of punk and alternative rock. Artists in the genre typically reject the polished production of mainstream Nashville country.

History

The roots of the genre lie in the country-rock of Gram Parsons and The Flying Burrito Brothers, the outlaw country of Waylon Jennings and Hank Williams's honky-tonk tradition. In the 1980s, punk and indie bands such as Jason and the Scorchers, the Long Ryders and Rank and File began fusing country with rock energy, a style sometimes called cowpunk.

The genre took shape in the early 1990s around Uncle Tupelo, whose 1990 album No Depression — named after a Carter Family song — gave the movement its journalistic label through No Depression magazine. Uncle Tupelo's split produced two of the genre's flagship bands: Wilco (Jeff Tweedy) and Son Volt (Jay Farrar).

Key artists

Notable alternative country artists include Ryan Adams and Whiskeytown, Gillian Welch, Lucinda Williams, Steve Earle, Drive-By Truckers, Jason Isbell, Neko Case, Old 97's, and the Jayhawks. Chicago's Bloodshot Records, founded in 1994, became the genre's defining label, dubbing its sound "insurgent country".

Relationship with Americana

From the 2000s the broader term Americana increasingly overlapped with alternative country, encompassing folk, bluegrass, blues and roots rock influences; the Americana Music Association was founded in 1999 and the Grammy Award for Best Americana Album was introduced in 2010.
