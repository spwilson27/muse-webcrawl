Vector space automorphism

In mathematics, a vector space automorphism is an isomorphism of a vector space with itself: a bijective linear map T : V → V from a vector space V over a field F to itself. Because it is both linear and bijective, an automorphism has a linear inverse, and it preserves all the linear structure of the space: addition, scalar multiplication, linear independence, subspaces, dimension and bases.

The general linear group

The set of all automorphisms of V forms a group under composition, called the general linear group of V and denoted GL(V). When V is finite-dimensional of dimension n, choosing a basis identifies GL(V) with the group GL(n, F) of invertible n × n matrices over F): an automorphism is represented by a matrix with non-zero determinant, and composition corresponds to matrix multiplication. Change of basis is itself given by conjugation by such an automorphism.

Properties

In finite dimensions, a linear endomorphism is an automorphism if and only if it is injective, if and only if it is surjective, if and only if its determinant is non-zero. Automorphisms map bases to bases and preserve the rank and nullity of other operators by conjugation. When the vector space carries extra structure — such as an inner product, a bilinear form, or a preferred volume form — one studies the subgroup of automorphisms preserving that structure, giving the orthogonal group, symplectic group, and special linear group respectively.

Infinite dimensions

For infinite-dimensional spaces the situation is subtler: injective and surjective endomorphisms need no longer coincide, and in functional analysis one usually restricts attention to continuous (bounded) automorphisms with continuous inverses, which form the general linear group of a Banach space.
