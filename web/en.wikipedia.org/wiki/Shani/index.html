Shani

Shani (Sanskrit: शनि, Śani) is the Hindu god of karma and justice, and the personification of the planet Saturn. One of the nine Navagraha, he is among the most feared and revered of Hindu deities: Shani delivers the results of a person's deeds — good and bad — during their lifetime, a dispensation called Shani's sade sati when Saturn transits certain positions.

Mythology

Shani is described as the son of Surya, the sun god, and his shadow-wife Chhaya, which accounts for both his dark complexion and his strained relations with his father. He is depicted as dark-skinned, sometimes lame (from a curse or injury in various legends), carrying a sword, bow, arrows and axe, and riding a crow or vulture. His gaze is considered so powerful that even gods fear it: stories tell of him afflicting Shiva, Ravana and the infant Ganesha.

Worship

Devotees propitiate Shani on Saturdays (Shanivar), offering sesame oil, black cloth, mustard oil lamps and black gram at his temples. The period of Saturn's transit across the moon sign and its neighbours — sade sati, lasting seven and a half years — is traditionally regarded as a testing time, mitigated by charity, humility and devotion to Hanuman, who is said to have rescued Shani from Ravana. Major Shani temples include Shingnapur in Maharashtra, where the deity is worshipped as an open-air black stone, and temples at Thirunallar and Kuchanur in Tamil Nadu.
