The Designated Player Rule, nicknamed the Beckham Rule, is a rule in Major League Soccer (MLS) that allows clubs to acquire up to three players whose total compensation and acquisition costs exceed the maximum salary budget charge, with the club bearing the excess cost. The rule was adopted ahead of the 2007 season and is named after David Beckham, the first player signed under it when he joined the LA Galaxy from Real Madrid.

Background

MLS operates as a single-entity league with a strict salary cap (salary budget) to control costs and maintain competitive balance. By the mid-2000s, however, club owners wanted a mechanism to sign internationally famous players whose wages far exceeded the cap. The Designated Player Rule was the compromise: a DP counts only a fixed amount (the "salary budget charge") against the cap, regardless of actual pay, while the club pays the rest.

Evolution

Initially each club received one DP slot, tradable up to a maximum of two; from 2010 clubs could have two, with a third available on payment of a luxury tax, and later three slots became standard. The league subsequently added mechanisms such as Targeted Allocation Money and the U22 Initiative to buy down charges and sign young talent. High-profile Designated Players have included Thierry Henry, David Villa, Zlatan Ibrahimović, and Lionel Messi, whose 2023 move to Inter Miami CF became the rule's most famous use.

Criticism

Critics argue the rule complicates roster building and favours wealthy clubs, while supporters credit it with raising the league's profile, attendance and broadcast value.
