Demetrius I Poliorcetes (Greek: Δημήτριος ὁ Πολιορκητής, "the Besieger of Cities"; 337–283 BC) was a Macedonian nobleman, military leader, and finally king of Macedon (294–288 BC). A member of the Antigonid dynasty, he was the son of Antigonus I Monophthalmus and Stratonice. Demetrius was one of the most colourful of the Diadochi, the successors who fought over the empire of Alexander the Great.

Early career

Demetrius first distinguished himself fighting alongside his father against Ptolemy and Seleucus. Defeated at the Battle of Gaza in 312 BC, he redeemed himself by defeating Ptolemy's forces and, in 307 BC, liberated Athens from the rule of Cassander, for which the Athenians honoured him and his father as gods and kings. In 306 BC he won a great naval victory over Ptolemy at Salamis in Cyprus, capturing the island; Antigonus and Demetrius then assumed the royal title.

Siege of Rhodes

Demetrius earned his surname Poliorcetes ("Besieger of Cities") during the great siege of Rhodes in 305–304 BC, where he deployed enormous siege engines, including the famous Helepolis, a huge armoured tower. Despite the scale of the assault, the Rhodians held out, and Demetrius was forced to abandon the siege under a negotiated settlement — leaving behind siege equipment whose sale funded the construction of the Colossus of Rhodes.

Ipsus and after

In 301 BC Demetrius and his father faced a coalition of the other successors at the Battle of Ipsus. Antigonus was killed and his Asian empire divided among the victors, but Demetrius escaped with his fleet and retained strongholds in Greece and the Aegean. Through a mixture of war, diplomacy, and marriage alliances, he rebuilt his position and in 294 BC seized the throne of Macedon, murdering Cassander's son Alexander V.

King of Macedon and downfall

As king, Demetrius planned a grand expedition to reconquer Asia, assembling a vast army and fleet. His ambitions and haughty manner alienated his subjects and rivals alike. In 288 BC a coalition of Pyrrhus of Epirus and Lysimachus invaded Macedon; Demetrius's army deserted to Pyrrhus, and he fled. After a final campaign in Asia, he was captured by Seleucus in 285 BC and spent his last two years in comfortable captivity at Apamea, dying in 283 BC. His son, Antigonus II Gonatas, eventually secured the Macedonian throne for the Antigonid dynasty.
