Fort Charlotte in Lerwick, Shetland, Scotland, is a five-sided artillery fort with bastions, built to guard the anchorage of Bressay Sound and the town of Lerwick. It is one of the best-preserved examples of its type in Scotland and is in the care of Historic Environment Scotland, open to visitors as a museum and home to Shetland's Territorial Army units.

History

A first fort was erected on the site in 1652–53 during the First Anglo-Dutch War, on the orders of the Commonwealth government, and was strengthened during the Second Anglo-Dutch War in 1665; Dutch forces burned it in 1673 during the Third Anglo-Dutch War, after which it fell into ruin. The present fort was rebuilt from 1781, during the American War of Independence, when fears of privateering renewed interest in defending Lerwick, and named after Queen Charlotte, wife of George III. It was garrisoned during the Napoleonic Wars and served in various military and civic roles thereafter, including as a base for the Royal Naval Reserve and a fishery protection headquarters.

Design

The fort's high curtain walls form an irregular pentagon with bastions mounting cannon over the harbour approach; inside are barrack and store buildings arranged around a parade ground. Although it saw garrison service in two world wars in auxiliary roles, the fort never fired a shot in anger.
