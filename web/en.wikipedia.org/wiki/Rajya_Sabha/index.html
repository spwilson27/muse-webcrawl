Rajya Sabha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rajya Sabha (Council of States) is the upper house of the Parliament of India, representing the states and union territories in the federal structure. Its maximum strength is 250; at present 245 members sit — 233 elected by the state and territory legislatures through proportional representation by single transferable vote, and 12 nominated by the President for distinction in art, literature, science and social service. Members serve six-year terms, one-third retiring every two years; the House is a continuing body, never dissolved.

Role and tensions

The Rajya Sabha co-legislates on ordinary bills (deadlocks resolved by rare joint sittings, where Lok Sabha numbers dominate) and has special powers over state-list subjects in the national interest (Article 249) and all-India services. Money bills reduce it to a recommending chamber — a restriction governments have exploited by routing contentious measures as money bills (the Aadhaar Act litigation), prompting charges that the federal chamber is being designed away. Its indirectly elected, state-based composition makes it the federalism chamber in form; party discipline and national politics make it a second partisan chamber in practice — a duality familiar from other upper houses, sharpened by India's centre–state contests.
