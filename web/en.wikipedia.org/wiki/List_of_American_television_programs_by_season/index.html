List of American television programs by season

This is a list of American television programs organised by the season (television year) in which they debuted or primarily aired, from the beginnings of regular network broadcasting in the late 1940s to the present day. In the United States, the traditional television season runs from September of one year to May of the next, and new series typically premiere in the autumn "fall schedule".

Seasons (selected)

  • 1948–49: early network staples including Texaco Star Theater and The Ed Sullivan Show (as Toast of the Town) define the variety era.
  • 1951–52: I Love Lucy premieres on CBS and establishes the filmed situation comedy format.
  • 1960s: The Andy Griffith Show (1960), Star Trek (1966), and Rowan & Martin's Laugh-In (1968).
  • 1970s: All in the Family (1971), M*A*S*H (1972), Saturday Night Live (1975), Roots (1977 miniseries).
  • 1980s: Dallas continues, Cheers (1982), The Cosby Show (1984), The Simpsons (1989).
  • 1990s: Seinfeld (1989–98), Friends (1994), ER (1994), The Sopranos (1999, HBO).
  • 2000s: Survivor and reality programming expand; The Wire (2002), Lost (2004), Mad Men (2007), Breaking Bad (2008).
  • 2010s: streaming originals grow alongside broadcast: Game of Thrones (2011), House of Cards (2013, Netflix), This Is Us (2016).
  • 2020s: streaming-first releases dominate new premieres, with broadcast seasons shortened and staggered by strikes in 2023.

Each season's full listing catalogues debuts on the broadcast networks (ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox, The WB/UPN and later The CW), cable channels, and, from the 2010s, streaming services.
