Andrology

Andrology (from Ancient Greek ἀνήρ, anēr, genitive ἀνδρός, andros — "man"; and -λογία, -logia) is a name for the medical specialty that deals with male health, particularly relating to the problems of the male reproductive system and urological problems unique to men. It is the counterpart of gynaecology, which deals with medical issues specific to women.

Scope

Andrology covers conditions including male infertility, erectile dysfunction, ejaculatory disorders, hypogonadism (testosterone deficiency), diseases of the prostate, male contraception, and the effects of ageing on male reproductive and sexual health. Andrologists may come from a background in urology, endocrinology, or reproductive medicine, and the specialty overlaps substantially with each of these fields.

History

Although the study of male reproduction has older roots, andrology emerged as a distinct discipline in the mid-20th century. The term gained currency in the 1950s and 1960s, and dedicated societies and journals followed, including the founding of the German society for andrology in 1951 and the launch of the journal Andrologia in 1969. The International Society of Andrology was established in 1981, and the European Academy of Andrology in 1992.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment commonly includes history and physical examination, semen analysis, hormone measurement (such as testosterone, LH, and FSH), genetic testing, and imaging. Treatments range from lifestyle measures and hormone therapy to microsurgical procedures such as varicocele repair and vasectomy reversal, and assisted reproductive techniques including intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).
