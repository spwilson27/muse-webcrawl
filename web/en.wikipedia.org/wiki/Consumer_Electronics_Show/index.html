CES, formerly the Consumer Electronics Show, is an annual trade show organised by the Consumer Technology Association (CTA) and held each January in Las Vegas, Nevada. It is one of the largest technology trade fairs in the world, at which companies present new products and technologies in the consumer electronics industry. Attendance is restricted to those affiliated with the industry and media; the show is not open to the general public.

History

The first Consumer Electronics Show was held in June 1967 in New York City, drawing about 17,500 attendees and 200 exhibitors. It later became a twice-yearly event, with a winter show in Las Vegas and a summer show in Chicago, before consolidating to the single annual Las Vegas show in 1998. From 1978 the winter show was held at the Las Vegas Convention Center. In 2016 the name was officially shortened to "CES." The 2021 show was held entirely online because of the COVID-19 pandemic, returning to a hybrid in-person event in 2022.

Notable product launches

Many landmark products debuted at CES, including the videocassette recorder (1970), the laserdisc player (1974), the Commodore VIC-20 (1981), the Nintendo Entertainment System (1985), the DVD (1996, jointly announced), the Xbox (2001), and numerous generations of televisions, from plasma and OLED displays to 8K and rollable screens. In recent decades the show has expanded beyond consumer electronics into automotive technology, digital health, artificial intelligence, and robotics, attracting more than 130,000 attendees and thousands of exhibitors in strong years.
