West Pier

The West Pier is a ruined pier in Brighton, England. Designed by Eugenius Birch and opened in 1866, it was one of the great pleasure piers of Victorian Britain and the first pier to be Grade I listed. It closed in 1975 and subsequently suffered partial collapse in storms and two fires in 2003, leaving only its skeletal iron remains standing in the sea.

History

The pier originally consisted of an open deck with small oriental-style pavilions. A pier head with a landing stage was added, and in 1893 a concert hall was built; a theatre followed in 1916. In its heyday the pier hosted concerts, theatre and seaside entertainments, and it was used as a location in films including Oh! What a Lovely War (1969).

Decline

After closure in 1975 the pier deteriorated. Part of the concert hall fell into the sea during a storm in December 2002, and fires in March and May 2003 destroyed much of what remained. The West Pier Trust has since promoted the nearby i360 observation tower, opened in 2016, while the pier's evocative ruins have become an iconic part of Brighton's seafront, famous for starling murmurations.
