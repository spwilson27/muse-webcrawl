Cleveland Browns

The Cleveland Browns are a professional American football team based in Cleveland, Ohio. Named after original coach and co-founder Paul Brown, they compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member club of the American Football Conference (AFC) North division. The team plays its home games at Huntington Bank Field.

History

Founded in 1944 as a charter member of the All-America Football Conference (AAFC), the Browns dominated that league, winning all four of its championships before joining the NFL in 1950, where they won the championship in their first season. Under Paul Brown, with stars such as Otto Graham and later Jim Brown, Cleveland won NFL championships in 1950, 1954, 1955, and 1964, but has never appeared in a Super Bowl.

Relocation controversy

Owner Art Modell moved the franchise to Baltimore in 1996, where it became the Baltimore Ravens. In an unusual settlement, the Browns' name, colors, and history remained in Cleveland, and an expansion team revived the Browns in 1999. The team's passionate fan base is known as the "Dawg Pound".
