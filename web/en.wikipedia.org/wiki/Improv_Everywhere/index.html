Improv Everywhere

Improv Everywhere is a New York City-based comedy collective that stages large-scale public pranks, which it calls "missions", in everyday locations. Founded in 2001 by Charlie Todd, the group recruits participants — "agents" — who perform coordinated, surreal actions while unsuspecting bystanders react. The group's stated aim is to create scenes of "chaos and joy" and to give ordinary people an unexpected, memorable experience.

Notable missions

Improv Everywhere's best-known events include the annual No Pants Subway Ride, begun in 2002, in which participants ride the subway without trousers while acting as though nothing is unusual; the event spread to cities around the world. In Frozen Grand Central (2008), more than 200 agents froze in place for five minutes in New York's Grand Central Terminal. Other missions include a fake U2 rooftop concert, a spontaneous musical in a food court in which shoppers burst into song, and the "Mp3 Experiments", in which thousands of participants follow identical audio instructions through headphones in public parks.

Organisation and media

Missions are documented with hidden cameras and posted as videos online, where several have been viewed tens of millions of times. Todd described the group's work in the book Causing a Scene (2009), and the collective was the subject of the documentary We Cause Scenes (2014). Improv Everywhere is distinct from improvisational theatre: its performances are planned in advance, and the "improv" is in the reactions of the crowd.
