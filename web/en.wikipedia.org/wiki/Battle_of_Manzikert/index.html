Battle of Manzikert

The Battle of Manzikert was fought on 19 August 1071 between the Byzantine Empire, led by Emperor Romanos IV Diogenes, and the Seljuk Turks under Sultan Alp Arslan, near Manzikert (modern Malazgirt, Turkey), north of Lake Van. The Byzantine army was routed and Romanos was captured — one of the few times a Byzantine emperor was taken prisoner in battle. The defeat is traditionally seen as a turning point that opened Anatolia to Turkish settlement.

Background

Seljuk raids into Byzantine Armenia and Anatolia intensified through the 1060s. Romanos IV, who took the throne in 1068, campaigned repeatedly to push the Turks back and in 1071 marched east with a large, heterogeneous army including mercenaries and provincial troops of uneven loyalty.

The battle

The Byzantines advanced against the Seljuk crescent formation. When Romanos ordered a withdrawal toward the camp at dusk, confusion spread; the reserve under Andronikos Doukas — a political rival — abandoned the field, and much of the army disintegrated. Romanos was captured and brought before Alp Arslan, who treated him honourably and released him in exchange for a ransom and treaty.

Aftermath

Romanos was deposed and blinded on his return, dying of his injuries. Civil war followed, and Byzantine authority in Anatolia collapsed over the next decade as Turkish tribes and Seljuk forces moved in, leading to the establishment of the Sultanate of Rum. Modern historians note the battle itself was not an annihilating defeat in casualties, but its political consequences were catastrophic for Byzantium.
