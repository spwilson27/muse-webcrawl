Bernoulli polynomials

[Image placeholder: Bernoulli polynomial curves]

In mathematics, the Bernoulli polynomials, named after Jacob Bernoulli, combine the Bernoulli numbers with the binomial coefficients. They are used for series expansion of functions, and with the Euler–Maclaurin formula.

These polynomials occur in the study of many special functions and, in particular, the Riemann zeta function and the Hurwitz zeta function. They are an Appell sequence, i.e. a Sheffer sequence. The first few Bernoulli polynomials are B0(x) = 1, B1(x) = x − 1/2, B2(x) = x² − x + 1/6, and B3(x) = x³ − 3x²/2 + x/2.

Definition

The Bernoulli polynomials are uniquely defined by the generating function t·ext/(et − 1) = Σ Bn(x) tn/n!. Evaluating at x = 0 gives the Bernoulli numbers. They satisfy the identities Bn(x + 1) − Bn(x) = n·xn−1 and d/dx Bn(x) = n·Bn−1(x).

Uses

Bernoulli polynomials appear in the Euler–Maclaurin formula for approximating sums by integrals, in Faulhaber's formula for sums of powers, and in the theory of the zeta function: ζ(−n, x) is expressible in terms of Bn+1(x).
