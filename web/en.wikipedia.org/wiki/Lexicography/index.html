Lexicography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lexicography is the practice and study of compiling dictionaries. It is traditionally divided into practical lexicography — the craft of writing and editing dictionaries — and theoretical lexicography (metalexicography), which studies dictionary types, structures and uses. A person who compiles dictionaries is a lexicographer.

History

Word lists date to ancient Mesopotamia and glossaries were compiled in Greece, India and China, but lexicography as a recognisable discipline grew with printed monolingual dictionaries in early modern Europe: Samuel Johnson's A Dictionary of the English Language (1755) set new standards of definition and illustrative quotation, and the great historical dictionaries of the nineteenth century, above all the Oxford English Dictionary (begun 1884), grounded entries in dated evidence from texts.

Modern practice

Contemporary lexicographers work primarily from electronic corpora — large collections of real-world text — which provide evidence of frequency, collocation and usage. Dictionary entries typically record spelling, pronunciation, part of speech, inflections, senses ordered by frequency or history, example sentences, etymology, synonyms and usage labels. Lexicographic products now include learners' dictionaries, bilingual and specialised dictionaries, thesauri, terminology databases and born-digital resources that are updated continuously rather than in discrete editions.
