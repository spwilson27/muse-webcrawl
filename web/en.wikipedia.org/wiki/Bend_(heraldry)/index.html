Bend (heraldry)

In heraldry, a bend is a diagonal band or stripe running across a shield from the dexter chief (the viewer's upper left, the bearer's upper right) to the sinister base. It is one of the ordinaries, the basic geometric charges of heraldic design.

Description

A bend normally occupies about one third of the shield's width. A narrower version is the bendlet, and a still narrower one is the cotise, often used in pairs flanking a bend (described as "cotised"). A bend running the opposite way — from sinister chief to dexter base — is a bend sinister, historically (though largely mythically in popular lore) associated with illegitimacy.

Variations

Bends may have patterned edges — engrailed, indented, raguly, wavy and others — and may themselves be charged with other devices. In the Scottish system, the bend is a frequent ordinary, and in flag design its analogue is the diagonal stripe seen in such flags as those of Tanzania and the Republic of the Congo.

Blazon

A shield with a gold bend on a blue field is blazoned "Azure, a bend or". The phrase "in bend" describes charges arranged along the diagonal direction of a bend.
