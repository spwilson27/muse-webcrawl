J. Fraser Stoddart

Sir James Fraser Stoddart (24 May 1942 – 30 December 2024) was a Scottish chemist best known for his pioneering work in supramolecular chemistry and the development of mechanically interlocked molecules. He shared the 2016 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Jean-Pierre Sauvage and Ben Feringa "for the design and synthesis of molecular machines".

Early life and education

Stoddart was born in Edinburgh, Scotland, and educated at the University of Edinburgh, where he earned his BSc in 1964 and his PhD in 1966. He carried out postdoctoral work at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, Canada.

Career

Stoddart held academic posts at the University of Sheffield and the University of Birmingham in England before moving to the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) in 1997 as the Saul Winstein Professor of Chemistry. In 2008 he moved to Northwestern University in Illinois, where he was Board of Trustees Professor of Chemistry. At Northwestern he founded the Stoddart Mechanostereochemistry Group.

Research

Stoddart's research centred on molecules whose components are linked not by covalent bonds but by mechanical interlocking. He developed syntheses of rotaxanes and catenanes based on molecular recognition between electron-rich and electron-poor aromatic units. His group's work produced molecular switches, shuttles, and "molecular elevators", and the mechanically interlocked molecules known as olympiadane and Borromean rings. These systems are regarded as early steps toward functioning molecular machines, with applications envisaged in nanoelectronics, drug delivery, and materials science.

Honours

Stoddart was knighted in 2006 for services to chemistry and molecular nanotechnology. He was a Fellow of the Royal Society and a member of the US National Academy of Sciences, among many other distinctions. He died on 30 December 2024, aged 82.
