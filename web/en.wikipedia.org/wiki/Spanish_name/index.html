Spanish name

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Placeholder image — Spanish naming customs give most people two surnames, one from each parent.

A Spanish name typically consists of a given name (simple or composite) followed by two surnames. Historically, the first surname was the father's first surname and the second the mother's first surname. In recent years, the order of the surnames in a family has been decided at the parents' discretion; since the 2011 reform of the Spanish civil registration law, parents may choose the order of their children's surnames, and an adult may also change the order of their own surnames.

Structure

The Spanish naming system is formally called nombre y apellidos. A person is usually known by their given name and first surname, although both surnames are used in legal and formal contexts. Spanish names do not include a middle name in the Anglo-American sense, though a second given name may be added, forming a composite given name such as José María or María José.

Compound surnames

Either surname may itself be compound, sometimes joined by the conjunction y ("and"), a hyphen, or the preposition de, reflecting noble ancestry or a desire to preserve a less common surname. Well-known examples include the writer Camilo José Cela and the philosopher José Ortega y Gasset.

Women and marriage

Unlike in some other cultures, Spanish women do not change their surnames upon marriage. Children receive one surname from each parent, so siblings share the same pair of surnames in the same order under the traditional system.
