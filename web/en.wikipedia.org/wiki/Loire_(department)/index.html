Loire (department)

Loire (French pronunciation: [lwaʁ]; Arpitan: Lêre; Occitan: Léger or Leir) is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of east-central France, named after the river Loire, which traverses it from south to north. Its prefecture is Saint-Étienne. Its INSEE and postcode number is 42.

History

Loire was created on 12 August 1793, when the former department of Rhône-et-Loire was split into two — Rhône and Loire — following Lyon's revolt against the National Convention during the French Revolution. Historically it corresponds largely to the former province of Forez.

Geography

The department lies between the Rhône valley to the east and the volcanic uplands of the Massif Central to the west; the Loire river crosses its fertile Forez plain. It borders Rhône, Isère, Ardèche, Haute-Loire, Puy-de-Dôme, Allier, and Saône-et-Loire.

Economy and culture

Saint-Étienne was a historic centre of coal mining, arms manufacture (the Manufacture d'armes de Saint-Étienne), bicycles, and textiles, and is now noted for design — it is a UNESCO City of Design. The local football club, AS Saint-Étienne, is one of France's most successful. Roanne is famed for gastronomy, associated with the Troisgros family of chefs.
