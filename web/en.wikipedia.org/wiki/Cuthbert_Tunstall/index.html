Cuthbert Tunstall (1474 – 18 November 1559) was an English humanist, bishop, diplomat, and administrator who served under four Tudor monarchs. A friend of Erasmus and Thomas More, he combined a career in the Church with scholarly work in mathematics and law.

Life and career

Educated at Oxford, Cambridge, and Padua, where he took degrees in civil and canon law, Tunstall entered the service of Henry VIII as a diplomat, negotiating in the Low Countries and at the Diet of Worms. He became Bishop of London in 1522 and was translated to the wealthy prince-bishopric of Durham in 1530. A religious conservative, he nonetheless served Edward VI, was deprived and imprisoned under him, restored under Mary I, and finally deprived again by Elizabeth I in 1559 for refusing the Oath of Supremacy; he died shortly afterwards in custody at Lambeth Palace.

Works

Tunstall's De arte supputandi libri quattuor (1522), dedicated to Thomas More, was the first book on arithmetic printed in England. He also wrote on the Eucharist and published an edition of his sermons. While Bishop of London he famously bought up and burned copies of Tyndale's New Testament — prompting Tyndale to correct and reprint it with the proceeds, a story retold ever since.
