Italy of Values (Italian: Italia dei Valori, IdV) is a political party in Italy founded on 21 March 1998 by Antonio Di Pietro, the former Milan prosecutor famous for leading the Mani pulite ("Clean Hands") investigations that exposed the Tangentopoli corruption scandals in the early 1990s.

History

The party campaigned on legality, anti-corruption and opposition to Silvio Berlusconi, and generally allied with the centre-left. Di Pietro served as Minister of Infrastructure in Romano Prodi's second government (2006–2008). At the 2008 general election, standing with the Democratic Party-led coalition, IdV won about 4.4% of the vote; in the 2009 European elections it rose to around 8%, its best national result. The party was a member of the European Liberal Democrat and Reform Party (later ALDE).

Decline

Internal disputes and defections, a break with the Democratic Party in 2012, and competition from the Five Star Movement eroded the party's support; at the 2013 general election its list (Civil Revolution, with other left groups) fell below the threshold and it left Parliament. Di Pietro stepped down as leader in 2013, and the party became a minor force, at times participating in centre-left coalitions and presenting candidates under allied lists, while retaining representation in some local councils and, later, a small national presence through alliances.

Ideology

IdV is commonly described as a populist, anti-corruption and "legalitarian" party, defending the independence of the judiciary, public ethics and constitutional values — its name referring to the values of the Italian Constitution. Its social and economic positions have ranged from centrist liberalism to centre-left positions on welfare and labour.
