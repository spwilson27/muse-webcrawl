Carabinier

A carabinier (also spelled carabineer or carbineer) is a soldier armed with a carbine — a shorter, lighter version of a musket or rifle. Originally a type of cavalry, carabiniers appeared in European armies from the 16th century and later often served as elite mounted or dismounted troops.

Carabinier
Role: Cavalry / mounted infantry
Weapon: Carbine
Era: 16th century onward

History

The term derives from the French carabine. In Napoleonic armies, carabinier regiments such as France's Carabiniers-à-Cheval served as heavy cavalry alongside cuirassiers, distinguished by brass helmets and armour. In Italy, the Carabinieri, founded in 1814, evolved into a national gendarmerie and a branch of the armed forces. In Chile, the Carabineros form the national police.

Modern uses

Today the name survives mainly in gendarmerie and ceremonial units, including Italy's Carabinieri, Chile's Carabineros de Chile, and the Prince of Monaco's Compagnie des Carabiniers du Prince.
