International school

An international school is a school that promotes international education, typically by adopting a curriculum such as the International Baccalaureate, Edexcel or Cambridge International Examinations, or by following a national curriculum different from that of the school's host country. International schools mainly serve the children of expatriate families — diplomats, staff of international organisations and multinational companies — though many also enrol local students.

History

The first international schools were founded in the nineteenth century for missionary and expatriate communities; landmark foundations include the International School of Geneva and Yokohama International School, both established in 1924. The sector expanded rapidly with globalisation: there are now well over ten thousand international schools worldwide, with particularly rapid growth in Asia and the Middle East.

Curricula and accreditation

The International Baccalaureate, founded in Geneva in 1968, is the most widely shared framework. Schools are typically accredited by bodies such as the Council of International Schools, and instruction is most often in English, though French, German and other national systems maintain their own school networks abroad.
