Lansford Hastings

Lansford Warren Hastings
Born 1819, Ohio, U.S.
Died 1870 (aged about 51), St. Thomas, Danish West Indies
Occupation: lawyer, emigrant guide author, soldier
Known for: The Emigrants' Guide to Oregon and California; the Hastings Cutoff

Lansford Warren Hastings (1819–1870) was an American lawyer and adventurer best remembered as the author of The Emigrants' Guide to Oregon and California (1845) and as the promoter of the Hastings Cutoff, a supposed shortcut on the California Trail whose use in 1846 contributed to the tragedy of the Donner Party.

Life and career

Hastings was born in Ohio and trained as a lawyer. In 1842 he travelled overland to Oregon and then to California, where he became involved in the politics of Mexican Alta California and harboured ambitions—reported by contemporaries—of helping detach California from Mexico, possibly as the head of an independent republic. He returned east in 1844 and published his Emigrants' Guide the following year.

The Emigrants' Guide and the cutoff

Hastings's guidebook described a new, supposedly shorter route from Fort Bridger, in present-day Wyoming, across the Great Salt Lake Desert and through the Wasatch Range to the Humboldt River. Hastings had not himself taken wagons over the full route when he promoted it. In 1846 he guided one party (the Harlan–Young party) through the cutoff, which required cutting a road through Weber Canyon. He left notes at Fort Bridger warning a following party, the Donner–Reed party, that the canyon was impassable and marking out an alternative route over the mountains.

The Donner Party attempted to follow Hastings's directions, losing precious weeks building a road through the Wasatch and crossing the salt desert, where they lost cattle and wagons. The delays contributed directly to their being trapped by early snow in the Sierra Nevada, where many died and some resorted to cannibalism. Hastings was widely blamed: survivor accounts and the press portrayed him as having recklessly promoted an untested route to attract emigrants and advance his own ambitions in California.

Later life

Hastings served in California during the Mexican–American War and practised law in Arizona and elsewhere. During the American Civil War he proposed to the Confederacy a scheme to seize Arizona and southern California. After the war he published a new emigrant guide, this time promoting settlement in Brazil, and led a colony of ex-Confederates (Confederados) to Santarém in the Amazon. He died of yellow fever in 1870 while serving as a guide for prospective emigrants, reportedly at St. Thomas in the Danish West Indies.
