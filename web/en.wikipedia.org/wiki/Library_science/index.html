Library science

Library science (often termed library studies or library and information science) is an interdisciplinary field concerned with the organisation, access, collection, and protection of information resources, and with the services and technologies through which people interact with them.

Scope

The field covers cataloguing and classification (systems such as Dewey Decimal and Library of Congress Classification), collection development, reference services, preservation and conservation, and information retrieval. In recent decades it has increasingly overlapped with information science, embracing digital libraries, metadata standards, databases, and user experience in information systems.

Education and history

Melvil Dewey founded the first library school, the School of Library Economy at Columbia University, in 1887. Indian librarian S. R. Ranganathan formulated the influential Five Laws of Library Science in 1931. Most professional librarians today hold an accredited master's degree in library and information science (MLIS).
