Accounts payable (AP) is money owed by a business to its suppliers, shown as a liability on the company's balance sheet. It is distinct from notes payable, which are debts created by formal legal instruments. A company's accounts payable department is responsible for processing supplier invoices and making payments for goods and services purchased on credit.

Process

The standard AP workflow involves receiving a supplier invoice, matching it against the purchase order and goods-received note (the "three-way match"), approving it, recording the liability, and paying within the agreed terms. Common payment terms are "Net 30" (full amount due in 30 days) or "2/10 Net 30" (a 2% discount if paid within 10 days).

Management

Days payable outstanding (DPO) measures how long a company takes to pay its suppliers and is a component of the cash conversion cycle. Delaying payment conserves cash but may forfeit discounts and strain supplier relationships; prompt-payment legislation in some jurisdictions protects small suppliers. Automation and electronic invoicing have reduced manual processing and fraud risks such as duplicate payments.
