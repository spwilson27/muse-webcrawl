Pinwheel Galaxy

The Pinwheel Galaxy (also known as Messier 101, M101, or NGC 5457) is a face-on spiral galaxy about 21 million light-years away in the constellation Ursa Major. It was discovered by Pierre Méchain in 1781 and communicated to Charles Messier, who verified its position for his catalogue.

Structure

M101 is one of the largest known spiral galaxies, roughly 170,000 light-years in diameter — nearly twice the size of the Milky Way — and contains an estimated one trillion stars. Its disk is rich in giant H II regions, more than 3,000 of which have been catalogued; the largest, NGC 5461, hosts extremely vigorous star formation. The galaxy's spiral pattern is somewhat asymmetric, distorted by gravitational interaction with its companion galaxies, which also account for its unusually high rate of star formation.

Companions and supernovae

M101 is the brightest member of the M101 Group, which includes NGC 5204, NGC 5474, NGC 5477, and NGC 5585. Several supernovae have been observed in it, most famously the Type Ia supernova SN 2011fe, one of the closest and best-studied of its kind, discovered only hours after explosion.
