Jebtsundamba Khutuktu

The Jebtsundamba Khutuktu (Mongolian: Жавзандамба хутагт) is the highest lama of the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia and the spiritual head of Mongolian Buddhism. The lineage begins with Zanabazar (1635–1723), son of the Khalkha prince Tüsheet Khan, who was recognised as the reincarnation of the Tibetan scholar Taranatha. The eighth Jebtsundamba, the Bogd Khan, was also the last Khan of Mongolia, reigning from the declaration of independence in 1911 until his death in 1924.

The lineage

Zanabazar, a gifted sculptor and the creator of the Soyombo script, established the lineage's seat at Erdene Zuu and later Urga. After the second Jebtsundamba's involvement in anti-Manchu resistance, the Qing emperors decreed that subsequent reincarnations be found in Tibet rather than among the Mongol nobility. The eighth Jebtsundamba, born in Tibet in 1869, was enthroned in Urga as a child and became the focus of Mongolian national aspirations.

The Bogd Khanate

When the Qing dynasty collapsed in 1911, Mongolian princes and lamas declared independence and enthroned the Eighth Jebtsundamba as Bogd Khan, head of a theocratic monarchy. His position was reduced to autonomy under Chinese suzerainty by the Treaty of Kyakhta (1915), restored to full independence in 1921 — first with the backing of the White Russian adventurer Roman von Ungern-Sternberg and then under the new Mongolian People's Party. The Bogd Khan died in May 1924; the communist government forbade the recognition of a successor and proclaimed a republic.

The ninth and tenth holders

A ninth Jebtsundamba, Jampal Namdol Chökyi Gyaltsen, was identified in Tibet as a small child in the 1930s but kept secret for decades; he lived in exile in India after 1959 and was publicly recognised by the 14th Dalai Lama in 1991. He settled in Mongolia in 2010 and died in Ulaanbaatar in 2012. In 2016 the Dalai Lama announced during a visit to Mongolia that the tenth Jebtsundamba had been born in Mongolia; in March 2023 a young boy, one of twins born to a Mongolian family, was presented at a ceremony in Dharamsala, India, as the Tenth Jebtsundamba Khutuktu — a recognition reported to displease the Chinese government, which claims authority over reincarnations of high lamas.
