Arnolfini Portrait

The Arnolfini Portrait (or The Arnolfini Wedding) is a 1434 oil painting on oak panel by the Early Netherlandish painter Jan van Eyck.

Arnolfini Portrait
ArtistJan van Eyck
Year1434
MediumOil on oak panel
LocationNational Gallery, London

Description

It depicts Italian merchant Giovanni di Nicolao Arnolfini and his wife in their home in Bruges. A convex mirror reflects two additional figures, and van Eyck's signature reads "Jan van Eyck was here 1434" in Latin.

Interpretation

Erwin Panofsky famously interpreted the painting as a marriage contract with hidden symbolism: the dog for fidelity, a single candle, removed shoes, fruit and the woman's gesture. Later scholars questioned whether the woman is pregnant (likely fashion) and whether subjects are Giovanni and Jeanne Cenami, married later, prompting memorial interpretations.
