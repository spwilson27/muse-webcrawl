Slint

Slint
Origin: Louisville, Kentucky, US
Genres: Post-rock, math rock, post-hardcore
Years active: 1986–1992 (reunions 2005, 2007, 2013–2014)
Labels: Touch and Go

Slint was an American rock band from Louisville, Kentucky, formed in 1986 by guitarist/vocalist Brian McMahan, drummer Britt Walford, guitarist David Pajo, and bassist Ethan Buckler (later replaced by Todd Brashear). Though little known during their brief career, the band is regarded as a foundational influence on post-rock and math rock.

Their debut album Tweez (1989), recorded by Steve Albini, mixed jagged rhythms with odd time signatures. Their second album, Spiderland (1991), recorded with Brian Paulson, is now widely acclaimed as a landmark: its hushed, narrative spoken vocals ("Good Morning, Captain"), dynamic shifts from whisper to roar, and interlocking, dissonant guitars defined a template later pursued by bands such as Mogwai and Godspeed You! Black Emperor. The band split before Spiderland was even released; an untitled 1994 EP of two instrumental outtakes followed posthumously.

Slint reunited in 2005 to curate and perform at the All Tomorrow's Parties festival and have since played occasional reunion tours, performing Spiderland in full. Members went on to projects including The For Carnation, Tortoise, Papa M, and Zwan. The 2014 documentary Breadcrumb Trail by Lance Bangs chronicled the band's history.
