Héctor Babenco

Héctor Eduardo Babenco
Born: 7 February 1946, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Died: 13 July 2016, São Paulo, Brazil
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, producer
Notable works: Pixote, Kiss of the Spider Woman, Ironweed, Carandiru

Héctor Eduardo Babenco (7 February 1946 – 13 July 2016) was an Argentine-born Brazilian film director, screenwriter, and producer. He is regarded as one of the most important filmmakers of Brazilian and Latin American cinema, known for unflinching films about the marginalized — street children, prisoners, and the poor.

Early life and career

Babenco was born in Buenos Aires to a Ukrainian Jewish immigrant father and a Polish Jewish mother. After living in Europe and working in film there, he settled in Brazil in 1969, becoming a naturalized Brazilian citizen. His early films included O Rei da Noite (1975) and Lúcio Flávio, o Passageiro da Agonia (1977), a hit crime drama based on the life of a notorious bank robber.

Breakthrough

Pixote: A Lei do Mais Fraco (1981), the story of a São Paulo street child played by the non-actor Fernando Ramos da Silva, brought Babenco international acclaim and is frequently ranked among the greatest Brazilian films. He then directed Kiss of the Spider Woman (1985), an English-language adaptation of Manuel Puig's novel starring William Hurt and Raúl Juliá, which earned four Academy Award nominations; Hurt won Best Actor, and Babenco was nominated for Best Director — the first Latin American so honored.

Later work

Babenco directed Ironweed (1987) with Jack Nicholson and Meryl Streep, and At Play in the Fields of the Lord (1991), filmed in the Amazon. After surviving cancer in the 1990s, he returned with Foolish Heart (1998) and the prison epic Carandiru (2003), based on the 1992 Carandiru Penitentiary massacre, which became one of Brazil's biggest box-office successes. His final film was My Hindu Friend (2015), a semi-autobiographical work starring Willem Dafoe. Babenco died of a heart attack in São Paulo in 2016.
