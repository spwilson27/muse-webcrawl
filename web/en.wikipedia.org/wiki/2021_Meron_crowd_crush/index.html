2021 Meron crowd crush

On 30 April 2021, a crowd crush at the Mount Meron pilgrimage site in northern Israel killed 45 people and injured at least 150 during celebrations of the Jewish holiday of Lag BaOmer at the tomb of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. It was the deadliest civil disaster in Israel's history.

Incident details
Date30 April 2021 (shortly after midnight)
LocationMount Meron, Israel
Deaths45
Injuries~150
CauseOvercrowding; people slipped on stepped passageway

Event

Tens of thousands of mostly Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) Jews attended the annual pilgrimage, the largest gathering in Israel since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic. Shortly after midnight, as worshippers exited a bonfire-lighting ceremony via a narrow, sloped, metal-floored passageway and steps, people slipped and fell, and those behind were crushed in the pile-up. Victims included children and teenagers, several of them dual nationals. Emergency services struggled to reach the site through the dense crowds.

Aftermath and investigation

Israel declared a national day of mourning on 2 May 2021. A state commission of inquiry, established by the incoming Bennett–Lapid government, investigated long-standing neglect of safety at the site despite repeated warnings. Its final report in 2024 assigned personal responsibility to senior officials, including Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu (who had also held office in 2021), the police commissioner and the minister responsible, and criticised decades of mismanagement of the compound. Safety reforms and limits on attendance were introduced for subsequent pilgrimages.
