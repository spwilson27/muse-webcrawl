Tongue

The tongue is a muscular organ in the mouth of a typical tetrapod. It manipulates food for chewing and swallowing as part of the digestive process, and is the primary organ of taste.

The human tongue is about 10 centimetres long on average and is anchored to the floor of the mouth by the frenulum. Its upper surface is covered with small bumps called papillae, many of which house taste buds sensitive to sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami tastes. The tongue is made almost entirely of muscle — four pairs of intrinsic and extrinsic muscles — which let it change shape and position with great precision.

Functions

Besides taste, the tongue is essential for speech, helping form consonants and vowels; for moving food around the mouth and pushing it toward the throat; for cleaning the teeth; and for sucking and swallowing in infants. In many animals it serves other roles too — a frog's tongue catches prey, a snake's forked tongue samples scent, and an anteater's long tongue reaches into ant nests.
