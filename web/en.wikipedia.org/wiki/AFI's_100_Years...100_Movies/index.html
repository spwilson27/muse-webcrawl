AFI's 100 Years...100 Movies is a list of the 100 best American films, as determined by the American Film Institute from a ballot of 400 nominated films voted on by more than 1,500 film artists, critics and historians. It was first presented in a CBS television special in 1998, launching the AFI's "100 Years..." series, and a revised 10th Anniversary edition was released in 2007.

The lists

Both editions were topped by Citizen Kane (1941). The 1998 list's top ten were:

6The Wizard of Oz1939
7The Graduate1967
8On the Waterfront1954
9Schindler's List1993

In the 2007 revision, Casablanca and The Godfather swapped places (Godfather second, Casablanca third), Raging Bull and Vertigo moved into the top ten, and 23 films entered the list, including The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring and Saving Private Ryan; the oldest film remained Intolerance (1916) and the newest became The Fellowship of the Ring (2001).

Criteria and criticism

Jurors were asked to consider critical recognition, major awards, popularity, historical significance and cultural impact; eligible films had to be American, feature-length narratives. Critics have faulted the lists for favouring Hollywood studio classics over independent and avant-garde cinema, but the lists remain widely cited reference points and drove significant home-video sales of classic titles.
