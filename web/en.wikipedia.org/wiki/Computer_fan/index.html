Computer fan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A 120 mm case fan, the most common size in desktop PCs

A computer fan is any fan inside, or attached to, a computer case used for active cooling. Fans are used to draw cooler air into the case from the outside, expel warm air from inside, and move air across a heat sink to cool a particular component. Both axial fans and (less often) centrifugal fans are used; together with heat sinks and liquid cooling, fans are a principal element of computer cooling.

Uses

  • Case fans — mounted on the front, rear, top, or side panels to create airflow through the enclosure, typically arranged so intake and exhaust produce positive or balanced air pressure.
  • CPU and GPU fans — attached to processor heat sinks (or graphics-card coolers) to blow air over the fins.
  • Power supply fans — built into the PSU to cool it and often to exhaust case air.
  • Chipset and accessory fans — small fans on motherboards, hard-drive coolers, and add-in cards.

Sizes and connectors

Case and cooler fans come in standard square frame sizes, most commonly 80, 92, 120, and 140 mm, with thicknesses usually 25 mm. Electrical connectors are typically 3-pin (power plus tachometer output reporting rotational speed) or 4-pin, the fourth pin carrying a pulse-width modulation (PWM) signal that lets the motherboard control speed precisely. Fan speed is measured in revolutions per minute (RPM) and airflow in cubic feet per minute (CFM); noise is quoted in decibels. Larger fans can move the same air at lower speed and are generally quieter than small fans.

Control and issues

Modern systems vary fan speed with temperature using firmware fan curves, balancing cooling against noise. Bearings (sleeve, ball, or fluid dynamic) determine lifespan and acoustic character. Fans gradually accumulate dust, which insulates components and unbalances blades, so periodic cleaning is a standard maintenance task. A completely fanless design, using only passive heat sinks, is an alternative for low-power or silent systems.
