Ammonium nitrate disasters

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ammonium nitrate is a chemical compound widely used as a fertiliser and as an ingredient in industrial explosives (such as ANFO). Although it is relatively stable in storage, large quantities that are contaminated, confined or exposed to fire can detonate with catastrophic force. A number of major disasters — many of them among the largest non-nuclear explosions in history — have resulted.

Major incidents

DateLocationDeathsNotes
21 September 1921Oppau, Germany561Explosion of about 4,500 tonnes of ammonium nitrate/sulfate fertiliser at a BASF plant, after blasting was used to break up a caked stockpile.
16 April 1947Texas City, United States581Fire aboard the ship Grandcamp detonated its cargo of about 2,300 tonnes of ammonium nitrate; the worst industrial accident in US history.
12 August 2015Tianjin, China173Explosions at a container storage station involving stored hazardous chemicals including ammonium nitrate.
4 August 2020Beirut, Lebanon218+About 2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate impounded at the port since 2014 ignited and detonated, devastating large parts of the city and injuring more than 7,000 people.

Causes and prevention

Investigations of these disasters repeatedly point to the same factors: storage of very large quantities near population centres, contamination with combustible material, exposure to fire, and lax regulatory oversight. Following the Texas City disaster and later incidents, many jurisdictions imposed strict rules on the quantity, separation and storage conditions of ammonium nitrate, and formulations with desensitising additives were developed.
