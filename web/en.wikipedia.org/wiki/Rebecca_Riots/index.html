Rebecca Riots

The Rebecca Riots were a series of protests that took place between 1839 and 1844 in the rural counties of south-west and mid Wales. The rioters, many of them small tenant farmers, attacked and destroyed tollgates erected by turnpike trusts on public roads, protesting against the tolls and other grievances affecting the rural poor.

Background

Agricultural depression, falling prices, rising rents and tithes had impoverished Welsh farming communities. The proliferation of tollgates — in some areas a gate every few miles, with tolls charged on the movement of lime, an essential fertiliser — was a focus of resentment against the turnpike trusts, whose finances were perceived as corrupt.

The riots

The rioters took their name from a passage in Genesis (24:60): "and they blessed Rebekah, and said unto her, Thou art our sister, be thou the mother of thousands of millions, and let thy seed possess the gate of those which hate them." Male rioters frequently dressed in women's clothing and called themselves "Rebecca and her daughters". The first prominent attack occurred at Efailwen in Carmarthenshire in 1839. Activity peaked in 1842–43, spreading through Carmarthenshire, Cardiganshire and Pembrokeshire.

Outcome

The government responded with troops and arrests, and several rioters were transported to Australia. It also appointed a commission of inquiry, whose recommendations led to the South Wales Turnpike Trusts Act 1844, consolidating the trusts, simplifying tolls and removing many grievances.
