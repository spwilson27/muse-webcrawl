Uniper

Uniper SE is a German energy company headquartered in Düsseldorf. It was formed in 2016 when E.ON spun off its conventional power generation (coal, gas, and hydro), energy trading, and related businesses into a separate company; the name combines "unique" and "performance". Uniper operates power plants in several European countries and is a major global energy trader, historically one of the largest importers of Russian natural gas into Europe.

History

Uniper began operations on 1 January 2016 and was listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange in September 2016. Finnish utility Fortum acquired a majority stake in 2018–2020. Uniper also held a stake in the Nord Stream 2 gas pipeline project and was a major customer of Gazprom under long-term contracts.

2022 energy crisis and nationalisation

Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, cuts and then the halt of Russian pipeline gas deliveries forced Uniper to buy replacement gas at extremely high market prices, causing massive losses — tens of billions of euros. In July 2022 the German government agreed a first rescue package, and in September 2022 Germany moved to nationalise Uniper; by the end of 2022 the Federal Republic held about 99% of the company. Under EU state-aid conditions, Germany committed to reduce its stake to no more than 25% plus one share by 2028.

Operations

Uniper's business comprises power generation in Europe (gas, coal, hydro, and nuclear stakes in Sweden), global energy trading, and gas storage. It has announced plans to transform its portfolio toward greener gases such as hydrogen and to phase out coal generation.
