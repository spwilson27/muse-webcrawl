Tube feet

Tube feet (podia) are small, flexible, fluid-filled appendages of echinoderms — starfish, sea urchins, sea cucumbers, brittle stars and crinoids — operated by the animal's water vascular system. Arranged in rows along the ambulacral grooves, they are used for locomotion, gripping surfaces, handling food, burrowing and, in some species, respiration and sensation.

PhylumEchinodermata
SystemWater vascular system
FunctionsLocomotion, adhesion, feeding

Mechanism

Each tube foot consists of an external podium and an internal muscular bulb, the ampulla. When the ampulla contracts, fluid is forced into the podium, extending it; longitudinal muscles then shorten the foot, pulling the animal forward or gripping the substrate. In many starfish and urchins the foot ends in a sucker-like disc, and adhesion is assisted by secreted chemicals, allowing starfish to exert the sustained force needed to prise open bivalve molluscs.
