Intersection (road)

An intersection or an at-grade junction is a junction where two or more roads converge, diverge, meet, or cross at the same height, as opposed to an interchange, which uses bridges or tunnels. Intersections may be signalised (controlled by traffic lights), controlled by signs such as stop or give-way markings, or uncontrolled.

Types

The most common forms are the three-way T junction (or Y junction) and the four-way crossroads. Larger junctions may have five or more arms. Traffic may be organised with turning lanes, channelised islands, or roundabouts, in which vehicles circulate around a central island and the intersection itself is not crossed directly.

Turns and traffic control

Turning movements are the main source of conflict points at intersections. Many jurisdictions prohibit some turns (for example, left turns across oncoming traffic in left-hand-driving countries) at busy junctions, or separate them into their own signal phases. Protected pedestrian crossings, cycle lanes, and advanced stop lines are often integrated into intersection design.

Safety

A large share of road traffic collisions occur at or near intersections, making their design a central topic in road safety engineering. Measures such as improved sight lines, signal timing, and conversion to roundabouts reduce severe crashes.
