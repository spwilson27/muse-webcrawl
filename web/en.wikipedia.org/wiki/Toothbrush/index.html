Toothbrush

A toothbrush is an oral hygiene tool used to clean the teeth, gums, and tongue. It consists of a head of tightly clustered bristles mounted on a handle, used with toothpaste to scrub the surfaces of the teeth.

History

People have cleaned their teeth for millennia with chew sticks, frayed twigs, cloth, and feathers. The bristle toothbrush was invented in China, traditionally dated to 1498, with hog bristles set into bamboo or bone handles; it spread to Europe in the 17th century. William Addis mass-produced the first toothbrush in England around 1780. Nylon bristles, introduced by DuPont in 1938, replaced animal hair, and the modern plastic-handled brush followed. Electric toothbrushes appeared in the 1950s and are now common.

Use

Dentists generally recommend brushing twice a day for two minutes with a soft-bristled brush and fluoride toothpaste, and replacing the brush every three to four months. Toothbrushes are made in many sizes and shapes, including brushes for children, orthodontic patients, and people with limited dexterity.
