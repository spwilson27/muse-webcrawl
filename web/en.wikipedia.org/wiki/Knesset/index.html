Knesset

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Knesset is the unicameral legislature of Israel. It passes all laws, elects the president and the prime minister (formally endorsing the government), supervises the work of the government, and reserves the power to remove the president and the state comptroller. The Knesset's 120 members are elected by proportional representation in a single nationwide district, with a legal electoral threshold (3.25 per cent since 2014) — a system that has always produced coalition governments and commonly makes mid-sized and sectoral parties the arbiters of government formation.

Constitutional position

Lacking a formal written constitution, Israel is governed under the Knesset's Basic Laws, enacted piecemeal since 1958; the Supreme Court's "constitutional revolution" (from the 1990s) treats the Basic Laws as a constitution and reviews Knesset legislation against them — a judicial role fiercely contested in the 2023 judicial-overhaul crisis, when the governing coalition legislated to curb the Court and the Court struck down one such amendment in January 2024 (as widely reported at the time). The Knesset sits in Jerusalem; its procedures (three readings, strong committees, question hour) follow parliamentary norms under a sovereign-parliament doctrine in tension with the Court's constitutionalism — the central open question of Israeli public law.
