Avram Finkelstein

Avram Finkelstein is an American artist, writer and activist. He is a founding member of the Silence=Death Project, the AIDS activist art collective that created the "Silence = Death" poster in 1986, and of Gran Fury, the art collective associated with ACT UP (AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power) that produced some of the most influential graphics of the AIDS crisis. His work addresses the politics of public space, propaganda and the representation of illness.

Silence=Death

In 1986, as the AIDS epidemic deepened and government silence persisted, Finkelstein and five friends formed a consciousness-raising group that produced the Silence=Death poster: a pink triangle — reclaimed from its use marking homosexual prisoners in Nazi camps — above the words "SILENCE = DEATH". Wheatpasted across New York City in early 1987, the image was adopted by the newly founded ACT UP as its emblem and became one of the most recognisable political graphics of the twentieth century.

Gran Fury and later work

Finkelstein was a central member of Gran Fury (1988–1995), ACT UP's unofficial propaganda arm, whose billboard and bus-shelter works — including "Kissing Doesn't Kill: Greed and Indifference Do" — mimicked advertising to attack pharmaceutical pricing, church opposition and media indifference. His memoir After Silence: A History of AIDS through Its Images (2017) reflects on the artwork of the epidemic and its legacy; he has remained active in HIV/AIDS advocacy and in discussions of how activist imagery circulates in the age of social media.
