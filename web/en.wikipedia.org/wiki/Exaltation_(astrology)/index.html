Exaltation (astrology)

In astrology, an exaltation is one of the five essential dignities of a planet. Each planet is exalted in a particular sign of the zodiac, where its influence is considered especially strong and fortunate, and in many traditions also at a specific degree within that sign. The opposite sign is the planet's fall, where its dignity is weakest.

Traditional exaltations

  • Sun exalted in Aries (at 19°)
  • Moon exalted in Taurus (at 3°)
  • Mercury exalted in Virgo (at 15°)
  • Venus exalted in Pisces (at 27°)
  • Mars exalted in Capricorn (at 28°)
  • Jupiter exalted in Cancer (at 15°)
  • Saturn exalted in Libra (at 21°)

Origins

The exaltation scheme is of great antiquity, with roots in Babylonian astrology transmitted through Hellenistic sources such as Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos. The planet in whose sign a planet is exalted is sometimes called the exaltation ruler or "host". Alongside domicile (rulership), exaltation ranks among the strongest essential dignities in traditional horary and natal astrology.
