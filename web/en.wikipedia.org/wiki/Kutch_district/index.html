Kutch district

Kutch district (also spelled Kachchh) is a district of Gujarat state in western India, with its headquarters at the city of Bhuj. Covering about 45,674 km², it is the largest district in India. Much of it consists of the Rann of Kutch, a vast salt marsh that gives the district its name (kachchh means "that which intermittently becomes wet and dry" in Sanskrit). Kutch is bounded by the Gulf of Kutch and the Arabian Sea to the south and west, and by Pakistan's Sindh province to the north-west. The district's population is about 2.1 million (2011 census).

Geography and culture

The district is divided between the Great Rann in the north, the Little Rann in the south-east, and the Banni grasslands. The region is seismically active: the 1819 earthquake created the Allah Bund ridge, and the devastating 2001 Gujarat earthquake (magnitude 7.7), centred near Bhuj, killed some 20,000 people across the region and largely destroyed Bhuj and several towns. Kutch is culturally distinct, with the Kutchi language and Sindhi widely spoken, and renowned crafts such as Rogan painting, Ajrakh block printing, Kutchi embroidery and copper bells. Dholavira, one of the largest cities of the Indus Valley Civilisation, lies on Khadir island in the Great Rann and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Wild asses roam the Little Rann's sanctuary.

History and economy

Kutch was for centuries a princely state ruled by the Jadeja Rajputs, acceding to India in 1948. Today the district hosts major ports at Kandla (Deendayal Port, one of India's busiest) and Mundra, extensive salt production, lignite and limestone mining, cement plants, and major wind and solar parks. The white desert of the Rann is promoted internationally through the annual Rann Utsav festival.
