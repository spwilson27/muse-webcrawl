Channel state information

In wireless communications, channel state information (CSI) is the known properties of a communication link. It describes how a signal propagates from the transmitter to the receiver and represents the combined effect of, for example, scattering, fading, and power decay with distance. The method of obtaining CSI is called channel estimation.

Types of CSI

Instantaneous CSI

Instantaneous (short-term) CSI describes the current channel impulse response, allowing the transmitter to adapt the signal to the present conditions, for example by precoding in MIMO systems. It requires the channel to be estimated frequently because wireless channels change with mobility.

Statistical CSI

Statistical (long-term) CSI characterises the channel's probability distribution — its fading model, average gain and spatial correlation — rather than the instantaneous realisation. It is easier to obtain and changes slowly.

CSI at the transmitter and receiver

CSI at the receiver (CSIR) is usually obtained by estimating the channel from known pilot symbols and is assumed available in most system designs. CSI at the transmitter (CSIT) is harder to acquire: in time-division duplex systems it can be inferred from uplink measurements by channel reciprocity, while frequency-division duplex systems rely on limited feedback from the receiver.

Applications

CSI underpins adaptive modulation and coding, transmit beamforming, spatial multiplexing and scheduling in standards such as LTE and 5G NR, where receivers report quantised CSI (CQI, PMI and RI) to the base station. Massive MIMO depends especially heavily on accurate CSIT obtained through reciprocity.
