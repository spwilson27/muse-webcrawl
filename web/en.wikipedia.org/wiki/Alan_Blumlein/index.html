Alan Dower Blumlein (29 June 1903 – 7 June 1942) was an English electronics engineer whose inventions transformed sound recording, television and radar. Working for EMI, he was granted 128 patents during his short career and is regarded as one of the most prolific inventors of the twentieth century.

Stereophonic sound

In 1931 Blumlein filed his landmark patent (British Patent No. 394,325) describing a complete system for recording and reproducing two-channel stereophonic — in his word, "binaural" — sound. It included the 45/45 single-groove stereo record, a moving-coil stereo cutter head, and the coincident microphone techniques still known as the Blumlein pair. His ideas were not commercially exploited until the late 1950s, long after the patent had expired.

Television and audio engineering

Blumlein played a central role in developing the 405-line television system adopted by the BBC, and devised the ultra-linear amplifier circuit widely used in valve power amplifiers.

Radar and death

During the Second World War Blumlein led the team developing the H2S ground-scanning radar for RAF Bomber Command. On 7 June 1942 he was killed when the Handley Page Halifax carrying the prototype crashed during a test flight at Welsh Bicknor. His death was kept secret, and his contribution to radar was only publicly recognised decades later.
