DMAIC

DMAIC (pronounced "duh-may-ick") is a data-driven improvement cycle used for improving, optimizing and stabilizing business processes and designs. The DMAIC improvement cycle is the core tool used to drive Six Sigma projects. The acronym stands for the five phases: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and Control.

The five phases

  • Define — Define the problem, project goals, and customer deliverables. Tools include project charters, voice of the customer analysis, and SIPOC diagrams.
  • Measure — Measure the current process performance and collect reliable data. Teams validate the measurement system and establish a baseline of process capability.
  • Analyze — Analyze the data to identify root causes of defects or variation, using tools such as cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagrams, hypothesis testing, and regression analysis.
  • Improve — Improve the process by eliminating root causes, typically by designing and piloting solutions, often with design of experiments.
  • Control — Control the future process performance so gains are sustained, using control charts, standard work, and control plans.

Relationship to other methods

DMAIC is closely related to the PDCA (plan–do–check–act) cycle and descends from the quality-management tradition of W. Edwards Deming and Motorola's Six Sigma program of the 1980s. For creating entirely new processes or products, Six Sigma practitioners instead use DMADV (Define, Measure, Analyze, Design, Verify), part of Design for Six Sigma. In Lean Six Sigma, DMAIC is combined with lean tools for removing waste.
