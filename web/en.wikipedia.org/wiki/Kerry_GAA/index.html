Kerry players and supporters

The Kerry County Board of the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) (Irish: Cumann Lúthchleas Gael Coiste Ciarraí), or Kerry GAA, is one of the 32 county boards of the GAA in Ireland and is responsible for Gaelic games in County Kerry. The county board is also responsible for the Kerry county teams.

The Kerry county football team is the most successful team in the history of Gaelic football, having won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship a record number of times (39 titles as of 2025). Known as "the Kingdom," Kerry produced celebrated teams under managers such as Mick O'Dwyer, whose side won eight All-Ireland titles between 1975 and 1986, and legendary players including Mick O'Connell, Pat Spillane, and Colm Cooper.

Gaelic football

Kerry compete in the Munster Senior Football Championship and the National Football League. Their principal rivals include Dublin and Cork. The county also has a strong tradition of producing successful club sides in the All-Ireland Senior Club Football Championship.

Hurling

Hurling has a smaller but long-standing presence, concentrated in the north of the county. Kerry won one All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship, in 1891, and compete in the Joe McDonagh Cup in the modern era.
