Systematics

Systematics is the study of the diversification of living forms and the relationships among organisms through time. It encompasses Taxonomy (naming and classifying) and Phylogenetics (reconstructing evolutionary relationships), and it produces the classification systems used across biology.

History

Formal systematics began with Carl Linnaeus, whose binomial nomenclature (1735–1758) still names every Species. Darwin's theory gave classification an evolutionary meaning: groups should reflect descent.

In the 20th century Willi Hennig's cladistics insisted that only clades are natural groups; molecular data and DNA sequencing later transformed the field, revealing, for example, the three-domain tree of life.

Modern practice

Systematists combine morphology, fossils (Palaeontology) and genome data, build phylogenetic trees with statistical models, and name new taxa under the zoological and botanical codes. Their classifications underpin conservation, medicine and agriculture.
