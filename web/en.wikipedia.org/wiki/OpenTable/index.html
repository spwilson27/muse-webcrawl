OpenTable

OpenTable is an online restaurant reservation service and software company. Founded in San Francisco in 1998, it allows diners to search for restaurants, view menus and reviews, and book tables in real time through its website and mobile apps, while providing restaurants with reservation and table-management software.

History

OpenTable was founded by Chuck Templeton in 1998, inspired by the difficulty of making restaurant reservations by phone. The company supplied restaurants with an electronic reservation book that replaced paper books and connected them to its consumer booking network. It went public in 2009. In 2014, OpenTable was acquired by the Priceline Group (now Booking Holdings) for about $2.6 billion.

Services

For diners, OpenTable offers reservations at tens of thousands of restaurants in many countries, loyalty points redeemable for dining rewards, user reviews, and private dining booking. For restaurants, it provides guest and table management tools, marketing services, and analytics. Restaurants typically pay subscription fees for the software plus a per-diner fee for reservations seated through the network.

Criticism

The company has faced criticism from some restaurateurs over the cost of its fees and its market dominance in online reservations, which has prompted competition from other platforms and from restaurants' own booking systems.
