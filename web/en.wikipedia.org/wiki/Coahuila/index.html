Coahuila

Coahuila de Zaragoza
Country: Mexico
Capital: Saltillo
Largest city: Torreón
Area: 151,563 km² (3rd largest)
Population: ~3.1 million (2020)

Coahuila (Spanish pronunciation: [koaˈwila]), formally Coahuila de Zaragoza, is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located in the northeastern part of the country. It borders the Mexican states of Nuevo León to the east, Zacatecas and San Luis Potosí to the south, and Durango and Chihuahua to the west, and the U.S. state of Texas to the north across the Rio Grande.

Geography and economy

Much of the state is covered by the Chihuahuan Desert, and the Sierra Madre Oriental runs along its eastern edge. The capital is Saltillo and the largest metropolitan area is Torreón in the Comarca Lagunera. The economy is based on manufacturing (including automotive and steel production around Monclova and Ramos Arizpe), mining — Coahuila produces virtually all of Mexico's coal — and agriculture, including wine production in Parras de la Fuente, one of the oldest wine regions in the Americas.

History

During the colonial period and after independence, Coahuila was joined with Texas as the state of Coahuila y Tejas until Texas seceded in 1836. The state is named after the Coahuiltecan indigenous peoples of the region, and its official name honours Ignacio Zaragoza, the hero of the Battle of Puebla, who was born in what is now Texas.
