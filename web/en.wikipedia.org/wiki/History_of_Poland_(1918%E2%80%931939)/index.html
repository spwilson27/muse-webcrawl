History of Poland (1918–1939)

The history of Poland from 1918 to 1939 is the history of the Second Polish Republic, re-established after the First World War. Poland's borders were settled through wars and plebiscites, including victory over the Bolsheviks in the Polish–Soviet War (1919–1921), sealed by the Treaty of Riga.

Politics

The republic began as a parliamentary democracy, but in May 1926 Józef Piłsudski seized power in a coup and installed the authoritarian Sanation regime, which governed until 1939. Poland faced economic hardship during the Great Depression and tensions with Germany and the Soviet Union.

Society and culture

Interwar Poland was a multi-ethnic state in which roughly a third of citizens were minorities — Ukrainians, Jews, Belarusians, and Germans. Warsaw, Kraków, Lwów, and Wilno were vibrant cultural and academic centres.

End of the republic

On 1 September 1939 Germany invaded Poland, followed by the Soviet invasion on 17 September, beginning the Second World War and ending the interwar republic. See also Second Polish Republic.
