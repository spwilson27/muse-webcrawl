Private label

A private label product (also called a store brand or own brand) is a product manufactured by one company for offer under another company's brand. Private-label goods are available in a wide range of industries, from food to cosmetics. They are often positioned as lower-cost alternatives to regional, national, or international brands, although some private label brands are positioned as "premium" brands to compete with existing "name" brands.


Store brands are a major part of supermarket sales in many countries.

Economics

Retailers commission private label products to gain greater control over pricing, supply, and margins, and to build customer loyalty. Because private label products avoid much of the advertising cost of national brands, they can be sold at lower prices while still returning healthy margins. In parts of Europe, private label products account for a large share — in some countries approaching half — of supermarket sales by volume.

Manufacturing

Private label manufacturers range from large firms that make both their own brands and private label goods, to small specialised producers. Retailers such as Aldi and Lidl built their businesses largely on private label ranges, while supermarkets such as Tesco, Carrefour, and Walmart sell extensive own-brand lines alongside national brands.
