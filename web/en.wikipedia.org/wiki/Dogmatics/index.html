Dogmatics is the systematic study of doctrine

Dogmatics

Dogmatics is the branch of theology dealing with the theoretical truths of faith concerning God and God's works, especially the official theology recognised by an organised church body, such as the Roman Catholic Church, Dutch Reformed Church, etc. At times the term is used broadly for systematic theology as a whole.

Definition and scope

Dogmatics treats the doctrines (dogmas) of a faith in a systematic and ordered manner, arranging them under heads such as theology proper (the doctrine of God), Christology, pneumatology, soteriology, ecclesiology and eschatology. It draws on Scripture, tradition, and the confessions or councils of the church, and seeks to state coherently what the church believes.

History

The discipline developed in the post-Reformation period, when both Protestant and Catholic theologians produced systematic presentations of doctrine. In Protestant usage, Friedrich Schleiermacher's The Christian Faith (1821–22) and Karl Barth's monumental Church Dogmatics (1932–1967) are landmark works. Catholic dogmatics is closely tied to the magisterium's definitions and to manuals produced after the Council of Trent.

Dogmatics in other religions

Analogous systematic treatments of doctrine exist in Judaism, Islam (as ʿaqīda or kalām) and other traditions, though the term dogmatics is used chiefly in Christian theology.
