Artaxias I

Reign: 190/189 – 160/159 BC
Successor: Artavasdes I
Dynasty: Artaxiad

Artaxata, founded by Artaxias I on the advice of Hannibal

Artaxias I (also spelled Artaxes or Artashes) was the founder of the Artaxiad dynasty of Armenia and king of Greater Armenia from about 190/189 BC to 160/159 BC. A former strategos of the Seleucid king Antiochus III, he took advantage of the Seleucid defeat at the Battle of Magnesia in 190 BC to declare independence, alongside Zariadres in Sophene.

Reign

Rome recognised Artaxias and Zariadres as kings after the Treaty of Apamea (188 BC). Artaxias expanded his kingdom at the expense of neighbouring territories, waged campaigns in Iberia and Media Atropatene, and undertook extensive internal reform. According to Strabo and Plutarch, he founded the capital Artaxata on the Araxes river, reputedly on the advice of the exiled Carthaginian general Hannibal.

Artaxias is credited with a major land reform: marking the boundaries between villages and private estates with upright boundary stones (stelae) inscribed in Aramaic, several of which have been discovered in modern Armenia. The medieval historian Movses Khorenatsi remembered him as an energetic builder and lawgiver.

Legacy

The dynasty Artaxias founded ruled Armenia for nearly two centuries, reaching its height under his great-grandson Tigranes the Great.
