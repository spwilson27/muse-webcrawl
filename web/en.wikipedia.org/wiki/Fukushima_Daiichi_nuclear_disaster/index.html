The Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster began on 11 March 2011 at the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant in Fukushima Prefecture, Japan. Triggered by the magnitude 9.0 Tōhoku earthquake and the tsunami that followed, it is considered the most severe nuclear accident since Chernobyl in 1986 and was classified at the maximum level 7 on the International Nuclear Event Scale.

Accident sequence

The earthquake caused the operating reactors (Units 1–3) to shut down automatically, but the tsunami, with waves of roughly 14–15 metres at the plant, overwhelmed the seawall, flooded the basement emergency diesel generators and caused a complete station blackout. Without cooling, the fuel in Units 1, 2 and 3 overheated and melted down over the following days. Hydrogen generated by the damaged fuel caused explosions that destroyed the upper structures of the Unit 1, 3 and 4 reactor buildings, and large quantities of radioactive material were released to the air and sea.

Consequences

More than 150,000 people were evacuated from the surrounding area, and some districts remained exclusion zones for years. No deaths from acute radiation exposure occurred among the public, and scientific assessments have not found discernible increases in cancer rates attributable to the releases, though the evacuation itself caused substantial hardship and deaths among vulnerable evacuees. Decommissioning of the plant, including removal of melted fuel debris, is expected to take decades. The disaster led Japan to shut down its reactor fleet for extended safety reviews, and prompted countries such as Germany to accelerate their phase-out of nuclear power.
