Pérotin

Pérotin (fl. c. 1200) was a European composer, believed to be French, associated with the Notre Dame school of polyphony in Paris. He is credited with expanding early polyphonic music from two voices to three and four, and is one of the earliest composers known by name whose works survive.

Life and works

Almost nothing is known of Pérotin's life; most information comes from the writings of the English music theorist known as Anonymous IV, who described Pérotin as the "best composer of discant" and editor of the Magnus liber organi, the great book of organum associated with his predecessor Léonin. Pérotin's surviving works include the four-voice organa Viderunt omnes and Sederunt principes, composed for Christmas and St Stephen's Day at Notre Dame Cathedral, and three-voice organa and conductus.

Style

His music features rhythmic modes, melismatic chant tenors, and passages of discant in which upper voices move in lively measured rhythms — techniques foundational to the development of the motet and Western polyphony.
