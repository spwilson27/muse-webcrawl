Tierpark Berlin is one of two zoos in Berlin, Germany. Located in Friedrichsfelde, in the borough of Lichtenberg, it covers 160 hectares (400 acres) and is one of the largest zoological gardens in Europe by area. It was founded in 1955 as the zoo of East Berlin, counterpart to the older Berlin Zoological Garden in the west of the city; since 2007 both have been run by the same company.

History

After the division of Berlin, the Soviet sector lacked a zoo — the famous Zoologischer Garten lay in the British sector — so the East German authorities created the Tierpark on the grounds of Friedrichsfelde Palace park, opening it on 2 July 1955. Under its long-serving director Heinrich Dathe, it grew into a large, scientifically respected zoo known for breeding, including elephants and big cats, and for its spacious enclosures.

After German reunification the Tierpark's future was debated, but it was retained and progressively modernised. Major projects have included the renovation of the historic predator house (the Alfred Brehm Building) into a rainforest hall for Southeast Asian species, and a Himalayan-themed mountain landscape.

Grounds and collections

The Tierpark is laid out as a landscape park with broad lawns, lakes and old trees around Friedrichsfelde Palace (Schloss Friedrichsfelde). Facilities include the Alfred Brehm Building (1963), a large pachyderm house for elephants and rhinos, enclosures for polar bears, and a walk-through lemur forest area. The zoo holds roughly nine thousand animals of some twelve hundred species.
