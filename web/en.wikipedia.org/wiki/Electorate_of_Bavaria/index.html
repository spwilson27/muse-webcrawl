Electorate of Bavaria

The Electorate of Bavaria (German: Kurfürstentum Bayern) was a state of the Holy Roman Empire from 1623 to 1806. It succeeded the Duchy of Bavaria, ruled by the House of Wittelsbach, when Duke Maximilian I was granted the electoral dignity during the Thirty Years' War.

History

In 1623, Emperor Ferdinand II transferred the electoral vote of the Palatinate to Maximilian of Bavaria after Frederick V of the Palatinate, the "Winter King", was placed under the imperial ban for accepting the Bohemian crown. The Peace of Westphalia in 1648 confirmed Bavaria's electoral dignity while restoring a separate eighth electorate for the Palatinate.

The electorate's capital was Munich. In the War of the Spanish Succession, Elector Maximilian II Emanuel allied with France and Bavaria was occupied by Austria after the Battle of Blenheim (1704); he was restored in 1714. In 1777, the extinction of the Bavarian Wittelsbach line led to the union with the Electoral Palatinate under Charles Theodore.

In 1806, following the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire, the electorate was elevated to the Kingdom of Bavaria under Maximilian Joseph, who had sided with Napoleon.
