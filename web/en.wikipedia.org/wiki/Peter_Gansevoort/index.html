Peter Gansevoort (17 July 1749 – 2 July 1812) was a brigadier general in the United States Army and a colonel in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War. He is best known for leading the successful defense of Fort Stanwix in New York during the Saratoga campaign of 1777.

Revolutionary War

Gansevoort, from a prominent Albany Dutch family, joined the Continental Army in 1775 and took part in the invasion of Quebec under Richard Montgomery. In 1776 he was promoted to colonel and given command of the 3rd New York Regiment. In the spring of 1777 he took command of Fort Stanwix (rebuilt and renamed Fort Schuyler) on the Mohawk River.

In August 1777, a British and Loyalist force under Barry St. Leger besieged the fort as one prong of Burgoyne's campaign. Gansevoort refused demands to surrender; his garrison's sortie and the famous raising of an improvised American flag — often described as one of the first uses of the Stars and Stripes in battle — held the post until St. Leger's force withdrew following the Battle of Oriskany and news of Benedict Arnold's approaching relief column. The failure of St. Leger's expedition contributed to Burgoyne's surrender at Saratoga.

Later life

Gansevoort later served on the Sullivan Expedition (1779) and in the defense of the Mohawk Valley. After the war he held militia and civil posts, served as sheriff of Albany County and as a regent of the University of the State of New York, and in 1809 was commissioned a brigadier general in the U.S. Army. He was the grandfather of novelist Herman Melville, whose mother was Gansevoort's daughter Maria. He died in Albany in 1812.
