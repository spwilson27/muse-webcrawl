Azilises (Greek: Ἀζιλίσης Azilisēs, Kharoshthi: Ayiliṣa) was an Indo-Scythian king who ruled in the region of Gandhara in the 1st century BCE. He is known primarily from his coins, some struck jointly with Azes I and others in association with Azes II.

Biography

Azilises appears in the numismatic record first as a co-ruler or subordinate king under Azes I, issuing joint coins on which both names appear. He later became the principal ruler in western Gandhara. His reign is conventionally placed around 57–35 BCE, though the chronology of the Indo-Scythian kings remains debated.

Coinage

Azilises issued silver and copper coins bearing the king on horseback, together with Greek and Kharoshthi legends. Reverse types include Zeus, Heracles, and other Hellenistic deities, reflecting the blend of Greek and Central Asian cultures in his kingdom. Certain coins of Azilises were overstruck by later rulers, and his types were in turn overstruck by Azes II, suggesting a close succession.
