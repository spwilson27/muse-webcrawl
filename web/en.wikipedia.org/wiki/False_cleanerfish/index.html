False cleanerfish

The false cleanerfish (Aspidontus taeniatus) is a species of combtooth blenny, a small marine fish in the family Blenniidae. It is native to the Indo-Pacific region, where it inhabits coral reefs from the Red Sea and East Africa to French Polynesia. The species is famous as an aggressive mimic: it closely imitates the appearance and dancing movements of the common cleaner wrasse (Labroides dimidiatus), a fish that provides a valuable cleaning service to other reef fishes.

Description

The false cleanerfish grows to about 11.5 cm (4.5 in) in length. It has an elongated, laterally compressed body with a silvery-blue to white ground colour and a bold black longitudinal stripe running from the snout through the eye to the tail fin. Its markings, body shape and swimming style so closely resemble those of the cleaner wrasse that the two are difficult to tell apart at a glance. Closer inspection reveals a key difference: the mouth of the false cleanerfish is positioned underneath the head (subterminal), whereas the cleaner wrasse has a terminal mouth at the tip of its snout. The blenny also possesses sharp, enlarged canine teeth in the lower jaw, which it uses to bite its victims.

Behaviour and mimicry

Unlike the genuine cleaner wrasse, which picks parasites and dead tissue from the skin of "client" fish, the false cleanerfish exploits the trust that clients place in cleaners. Having been invited to inspect a client fish that poses expectantly at a cleaning station, the blenny instead darts in and bites out a mouthful of scales, mucus and skin tissue, then flees. To gain access to clients, it imitates not only the cleaner's colour pattern but also its characteristic undulating "dance".

This is an example of aggressive mimicry, in which a predator resembles a harmless or beneficial species in order to approach its prey. The success of the strategy depends on the mimic remaining relatively rare compared with the model; if false cleanerfish become too common, client fish learn to avoid cleaners altogether, to the detriment of both species.

Distribution and habitat

Aspidontus taeniatus is widely distributed across the tropical Indo-Pacific. It is associated with coral reefs and is often found near the cleaning stations of the cleaner wrasse it mimics. It may shelter in empty worm tubes or bottles on the reef, darting out to feed.
