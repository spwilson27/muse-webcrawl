Cameo glass
Technique: layered (cased) glass, carved or etched
Origins: ancient Rome
Famous example: the Portland Vase

Cameo glass is glass decorated by carving or etching through fused layers of differently coloured glass to produce a design, usually with white figures on a dark ground, imitating carved gemstone cameos. The technique reached a first peak in ancient Rome, whose masterpiece is the Portland Vase (about 1–25 AD), of white over dark blue glass.

Technique

A blank of cased glass — typically a thin opaque white layer fused over a coloured body — is shaped, then the design is cut back with wheels, drills, and abrasives, or (in the 19th century) etched with hydrofluoric acid and finished by hand. Multi-layer pieces in three or more colours demand exceptional skill.

Revival

Victorian glasshouses in Stourbridge, England — notably Thomas Webb & Sons with carver George Woodall — and French makers such as Émile Gallé and Daum revived cameo glass in the late 19th century, producing celebrated Art Nouveau vases. Chinese Peking glass used a related overlay-carving technique.
