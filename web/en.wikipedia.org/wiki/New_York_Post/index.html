The New York Post is an American daily newspaper in New York City, founded in 1801 by Alexander Hamilton as the New-York Evening Post, and owned since 1976 (with an interruption) by Rupert Murdoch's News Corp. One of America's oldest papers, it is a conservative tabloid known for punchy headlines, the Page Six gossip column and strong sports coverage.

The Evening Post

Hamilton founded the paper in 1801 with Federalist backers; it became a respected broadsheet under editors including William Cullen Bryant, and supported free trade and abolition.

The tabloid era

In the 20th century the paper passed through owners including Dorothy Schiff (1939-1976), who ran it as a liberal tabloid; its headline writers produced classics of the form.

Murdoch and News Corp

Murdoch bought the Post in 1976; forced to sell in 1988 under cross-ownership rules, he repurchased it in 1993. Under editors like Col Allan it sharpened its populist-conservative voice.

Page Six and the modern paper

Page Six, its gossip column, became a power in celebrity and media circles. The Post is among America's largest-circulation papers and a major digital publisher, while its print front pages remain a New York institution. Its front-page headline conventions are studied as a genre, and its digital sports and Page Six verticals drive much of its online reach.
