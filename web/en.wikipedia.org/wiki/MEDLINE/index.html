MEDLINE

MEDLINE (Medical Literature Analysis and Retrieval System Online, or MEDLARS Online) is a bibliographic database of life sciences and biomedical information maintained by the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM). It contains references and abstracts for journal articles in medicine, nursing, dentistry, veterinary medicine, health care and the preclinical sciences, drawn from several thousand scholarly journals published around the world. MEDLINE records are indexed with Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and form the largest component of PubMed.

History

MEDLINE grew out of MEDLARS (Medical Literature Analysis and Retrieval System), a computerised system introduced by the NLM in 1964 to produce the print Index Medicus and to perform batch literature searches for medical libraries. MEDLINE, the online version, became available in 1971, initially offering access to a few hundred journals to a small network of institutions. Coverage expanded steadily; in 1997 the NLM made searching free to the public through PubMed on the World Wide Web, after which usage grew dramatically. The print Index Medicus ceased publication in 2004.

Content and indexing

The database contains tens of millions of citations, with several hundred thousand added each year. Journals are selected for inclusion by a review committee (the Literature Selection Technical Review Committee) on the basis of scientific quality and importance. Each article is indexed by human indexers and automated methods using MeSH descriptors and qualifiers, enabling precise subject searching by concepts such as diseases, drugs and procedures. Most records include English-language abstracts written by the articles' authors.

Access

MEDLINE can be searched free of charge through PubMed, which adds further content such as in-process citations and links to full text in PubMed Central. The database is also distributed under licence to commercial vendors (such as Ovid, EBSCO and ProQuest), which offer it on their own search platforms, and through national and regional portals in many countries.
