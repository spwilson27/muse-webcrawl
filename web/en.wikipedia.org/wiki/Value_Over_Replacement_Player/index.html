Value Over Replacement Player

SportBaseball
Introduced byKeith Woolner, Baseball Prospectus
AbbreviationVORP

Value Over Replacement Player (VORP) is a sabermetric statistic in baseball that measures a player's total offensive (or pitching) contribution in runs above what a "replacement-level" player — a freely available minor-leaguer or bench player — would contribute at the same position.

Concept

VORP was developed by Keith Woolner of Baseball Prospectus in the late 1990s. Rather than comparing a player to the league average, it compares him to replacement level, which is defined lower: roughly the level of performance a team can obtain at minimal cost. For hitters, VORP is calculated from marginal runs — the runs a player creates above replacement, adjusted for league, park, and (in some versions) position. For pitchers, it is based on runs prevented relative to a replacement-level pitcher.

Use and criticism

VORP became one of the signature statistics of the Moneyball era. Critics note that it ignores defensive value (in its original form) and that the exact definition of replacement level is a modelling choice, so VORP figures differ between publishers. Later statistics such as Wins Above Replacement (WAR) extended the same framework into a single wins-based number. Baseball Prospectus eventually retired VORP in favour of newer metrics.
