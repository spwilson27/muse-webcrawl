Breast cancer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Breast cancer is cancer arising from the tissues of the breast, most often the ducts or lobules. It is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in women worldwide and also occurs, much less often, in men. Advances in screening and treatment have steadily reduced death rates in many countries even as incidence rises.

Risk factors and presentation

Risk increases with age, inherited mutations in BRCA1 and BRCA2, early puberty and late menopause, alcohol, obesity after menopause, and hormone replacement therapy; breastfeeding and physical activity are protective. The classic presentation is a new lump, but skin dimpling, nipple change or discharge also occur. Screening by mammography aims to find cancers before they can be felt.

Types and treatment

Treatment is guided by the tumour's receptors — oestrogen receptor, progesterone receptor and HER2 — and stage. Options include surgery (lumpectomy or mastectomy), Radiation therapy, Chemotherapy, hormone-blocking drugs such as tamoxifen, HER2-targeted antibodies such as trastuzumab, and Immunotherapy for some triple-negative cancers. Survival exceeds 90% at five years for localised disease in well-resourced settings but is far worse once spread.
