Aesculus glabra, commonly known as the Ohio buckeye, is a species of tree in the soapberry family (Sapindaceae) native to North America. Its natural range is primarily in the Midwestern and lower Great Plains regions of the United States, extending southeast into the Nashville Basin and west Tennessee. It is also found in the Mississippi River valley and locally in Ontario, Canada.

Description

The Ohio buckeye is a medium-sized deciduous tree growing 15–24 m (50–80 ft) tall. Its palmately compound leaves have five (rarely seven) elliptic leaflets and are among the first to leaf out in spring and the first to drop in autumn. In spring it bears showy upright panicles of pale yellow-green flowers. The fruit is a spiny husk containing one to three large, glossy brown seeds — the "buckeyes", named for their resemblance to the eye of a deer. All parts of the plant, especially the seeds, contain the glycoside aesculin and are poisonous to livestock and humans if eaten.

Cultivation and significance

The Ohio buckeye is the state tree of Ohio, and its seed lends its name to Ohioans ("Buckeyes") and to the athletic teams of Ohio State University, whose mascot is Brutus Buckeye. Carrying a buckeye seed is a traditional American good-luck charm. The tree is planted as an ornamental and shade tree, especially in parks, though its litter of fallen fruit and early leaf drop limit its use in streets.

Varieties

A variety, Aesculus glabra var. arguta (Texas buckeye), occurs in Texas, Oklahoma, and Kansas, and is smaller and more drought-tolerant. The species hybridizes with the red buckeye (A. pavia) to produce Aesculus × bushii.
