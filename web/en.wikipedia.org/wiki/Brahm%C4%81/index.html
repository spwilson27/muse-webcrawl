Brahmā

Brahmā (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मा) is a Hindu god, referred to as "the Creator" within the Trimurti, the trinity of supreme divinity that includes Vishnu and Shiva. He is associated with creation, knowledge, and the Vedas. Brahmā is traditionally depicted with four heads, four arms, and a red complexion, and is identified with the Vedic creator god Prajapati.

Unlike Vishnu and Shiva, Brahmā has few dedicated temples, the most famous being the Brahma Temple at Pushkar in Rajasthan, India.

Iconography

Brahmā is traditionally depicted with four faces, which represent the four Vedas and the four directions. His vahana (mount) is a hamsa (swan or goose), and he carries items such as the Vedas, a mala (rosary), and a kamandalu (water pot). His consort is Sarasvati, goddess of knowledge.

Legends

In various Puranic texts, Brahmā emerges from a golden egg or is born from a lotus growing from Vishnu's navel at the beginning of each cosmic cycle. A day of Brahmā (kalpa) lasts billions of years in the Hindu cosmology of cyclic time.
