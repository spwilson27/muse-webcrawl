Omri (Hebrew: עָמְרִי, ʿOmrī) was the sixth king of the northern Israelite Kingdom of Israel, reigning in the late 10th or 9th century BC (commonly dated c. 885–874 BC). Although the Hebrew Bible devotes only a few verses to him and condemns his religious policies, Omri is portrayed by archaeology and foreign inscriptions as a major ruler: he founded Samaria as Israel's capital and established a dynasty — the House of Omri — that Assyrian records continued to use as the name for Israel ("Bit-Humri", "House of Omri") long after his line ended.

Accession

According to 1 Kings 16, Omri was commander of the army of King Elah. When Zimri murdered Elah and seized the throne, the troops proclaimed Omri king; after a brief civil war with the rival claimant Tibni, Omri reigned alone. He ruled first from Tirzah and then purchased the hill of Samaria from Shemer, building a new capital there, which remained the capital of Israel until its fall to Assyria in 722 BC.

Reign and legacy

Omri strengthened Israel's position in the region. The Mesha Stele of Moab records that Omri conquered and oppressed Moab. He formed an alliance with Phoenicia, sealed by the marriage of his son Ahab to Jezebel, daughter of Ethbaal of Tyre. His dynasty — continued by Ahab, Ahaziah and Jehoram — was ended by the coup of Jehu around 841 BC. The biblical text's terse, hostile treatment (he "did evil in the eyes of the LORD, and did worse than all that were before him") contrasts with his substantial footprint in the extra-biblical record; the Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III even calls Jehu "son of Omri" in the dynastic sense.
