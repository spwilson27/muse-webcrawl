Stan Hugill

Stanley James Hugill (19 November 1906 – 13 May 1992) was a British folk musician and painter, regarded as the last working shantyman of the British merchant service and an authority on sea shanties.

Stan Hugill
Born: 19 November 1906, Hoylake, Cheshire
Died: 13 May 1992, Aberystwyth, Wales
Occupation: Sailor, folk singer, author, painter
Known for: Shanties from the Seven Seas (1961)

Life

Hugill went to sea in sailing ships in the 1920s, serving in the last commercial square-riggers. He was a prisoner of war in Germany during the Second World War. Later he worked as a sailing instructor at Outward Bound in Aberdovey, Wales, and travelled widely performing shanties.

Works

His magnum opus Shanties from the Seven Seas (1961) collected hundreds of shanties with tunes and histories and remains a standard reference. Other books include The Bosun's Locker and Songs of the Sea. He recorded albums and appeared at folk festivals, influencing the folk revival's maritime repertoire.

Legacy

Hugill is remembered as a key source for authentic working shanties — halyard, capstan and forecastle songs — collected from oral tradition before they vanished.
