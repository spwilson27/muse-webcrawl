The National Missing and Unidentified Persons System (NamUs) is a national information clearinghouse and resource center for missing, unidentified, and unclaimed persons in the United States. It is funded by the National Institute of Justice, an agency of the U.S. Department of Justice, and is managed in partnership with the University of North Texas Health Science Center.

History

NamUs grew out of efforts in the 2000s to address what forensic specialists called the "silent mass disaster" of tens of thousands of unidentified human remains held by coroners and medical examiners across the United States, alongside hundreds of thousands of open missing-person cases. The system's unidentified persons database was launched in 2008, followed by the missing persons database in 2009; an unclaimed persons component was added later.

Function

NamUs maintains three linked databases: missing persons, unidentified persons (deceased individuals whose identities are unknown), and unclaimed persons (identified decedents whose next of kin have not been located). Law enforcement agencies, medical examiners, coroners and — for missing persons — members of the public can enter and search case information. Case records can include photographs, dental records, fingerprints, DNA profiles and identifying features such as tattoos and scars.

The system automatically cross-matches missing-person entries against unidentified-remains entries, and NamUs regional specialists assist agencies in resolving cases. NamUs is credited with helping resolve thousands of missing and unidentified person cases.

Relationship to other systems

NamUs complements the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC) missing person files and the Combined DNA Index System (CODIS). Unlike NCIC, NamUs is publicly searchable, allowing families of missing persons to review unidentified-remains cases themselves.
