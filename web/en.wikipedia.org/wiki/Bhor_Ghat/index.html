Bhor Ghat

[Image placeholder: Bhor Ghat railway incline]

Bhor Ghat is a mountain pass located between Palasdari and Khandala for railway lines and between Khopoli and Khandala on the road route in Maharashtra, India, situated on the crest of the Western Ghats. It carries the Mumbai–Pune railway and the Mumbai–Pune Expressway across the Sahyadri range, climbing from the Konkan lowlands to the Deccan plateau.

The railway incline was a formidable 19th-century engineering achievement of the Great Indian Peninsula Railway, opened in 1863 under chief resident engineer James John Berkley. The original alignment included a reversing station to manage the steep gradient of about 1 in 37, with numerous tunnels and viaducts.

Railway

Today trains are hauled up the ghat with the help of banker (helper) locomotives attached at Karjat. The pass rises to an elevation of about 622 metres (2,041 ft). The road through the ghat was historically an important trade route between the port of Mumbai and the Deccan.
