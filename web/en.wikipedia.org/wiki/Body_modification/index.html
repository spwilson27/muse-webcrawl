Body modification (or body alteration) is the deliberate altering of the human anatomy or physical appearance, for non-medical reasons such as aesthetics, sexual enhancement, rites of passage, religious beliefs, group membership, shock value, or self-expression. It encompasses widely accepted practices — tattooing, body piercing, ear stretching — as well as more extreme modifications such as subdermal implants, scarification, tongue splitting, and nullification.

History

Body modification is among the oldest human cultural practices: Ötzi the Iceman (c. 3300 BCE) bore tattoos, and modification traditions — from Māori tā moko to Japanese irezumi, African scarification, and the elongated skulls and bound feet of various cultures — marked status, beauty, and belonging across the world. A modern Western subculture coalesced in the 1970s–1990s around figures and communities that also revived suspension and "modern primitive" rituals, driving tattooing and piercing into the mainstream.

Types

  • Decorative: tattoos, piercings, microdermals, stretched ears
  • Structural: implants, ear pointing, tongue splitting
  • Surface: scarification, branding, subincision
  • Other: eyeball tattooing, corsetry, waist training

Controversy and regulation

Health risks include infection, scarring, allergic reaction, and blood-borne disease transmission, so many jurisdictions license practitioners and set age limits. Extreme modifications occupy a legal grey area in some countries, where practitioners have been prosecuted for causing bodily harm despite client consent.
