Édouard Dujardin

Édouard Dujardin (10 October 1861 – 31 October 1949) was a French novelist, playwright, poet, and editor associated with the Symbolist movement. He is best known for his novella Les Lauriers sont coupés (1887–88; "We'll to the Woods No More"), widely regarded as the first sustained use of stream of consciousness — the interior monologue — in fiction, and acknowledged by James Joyce as an influence on Ulysses.

Career

Dujardin was born near Blois and educated at the Conservatoire de Paris and the Sorbonne. An inheritance allowed him to finance avant-garde ventures, including the Revue wagnérienne (1885–1887), devoted to the work of Richard Wagner, and the Revue indépendante, in which Les Lauriers sont coupés was serialised in 1887 before appearing in book form in 1888.

Les Lauriers sont coupés

The novella recounts a few hours in the life of a young Parisian student, Daniel Prince, rendered almost entirely through his unspoken thoughts. Long neglected, the work was championed in the 1920s after Joyce praised it, and Dujardin later formalised the technique in his essay Le Monologue intérieur (1931), the first theoretical study of stream of consciousness.

Later life

Dujardin wrote plays, verse, and scholarly works on religion and mythology, and taught at the Sorbonne. He died in Paris in 1949.
