Torque wrench

A torque wrench is a tool used to apply a precisely controlled amount of torque to a fastener such as a nut or bolt. It is essential where uniform tightness of threaded fasteners is critical, for example in engines, bicycles and structural assemblies.

Types

  • Beam type — a pointer deflects along a scale as torque is applied; simple and inexpensive.
  • Click type — an internal mechanism produces an audible click when the preset torque is reached.
  • Dial and electronic types — display torque on a dial or digital readout, sometimes recording values.

Use

The operator sets the desired torque (in newton-metres or foot-pounds), fits a socket to the square drive, and tightens the fastener until the indicator shows the target has been reached. Over- or under-torquing can cause joint failure, so torque wrenches require periodic calibration.

History

The first torque wrench patents date to the early twentieth century; the beam type was developed in the 1920s and the click mechanism became widespread after the Second World War.
