Pori

Pori (Swedish: Björneborg) is a city and municipality on the west coast of Finland, in the region of Satakunta. It lies near the mouth of the Kokemäenjoki river on the Gulf of Bothnia and has a population of roughly 83,000, making it one of the ten largest cities in Finland.

History

Pori was founded in 1558 by Duke John of Finland (later King John III of Sweden), replacing the older trading town of Ulvila as the area's port after land uplift made Ulvila's harbour inaccessible. The city burned down nine times in its history, most devastatingly in 1852, after which it was rebuilt on a wide grid plan with broad esplanades that still characterise the centre.

Culture

Pori is best known for the Pori Jazz festival, held every July since 1966, one of Europe's oldest and largest jazz festivals. The city is also home to the Pori Theatre, one of Finland's oldest theatres, and to the Yyteri beach, a long sandy shore popular with holidaymakers.

Economy

Historically an industrial and port city, Pori's economy has included sawmills, metal and engineering works, and a major harbour at Tahkoluoto and Mäntyluoto. The nearby Olkiluoto nuclear power plant lies in the neighbouring municipality of Eurajoki.

Sport

The city's main ice hockey team is Ässät ("Aces"), which plays in the Finnish Liiga and has won national championships, most recently in 2013. Football is represented by FC Jazz, which won Finnish championships in the 1990s.
