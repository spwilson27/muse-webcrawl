Fagales

The Fagales are an order of flowering plants within the rosid group of the eudicots. They include many familiar trees of temperate forests, such as oaks, beeches, birches, alders, walnuts, hickories and chestnuts.

Families

In the APG IV system, Fagales comprises seven families: Fagaceae (beeches, oaks, chestnuts), Betulaceae (birches, alders, hornbeams, hazels), Juglandaceae (walnuts, hickories), Casuarinaceae (she-oaks), Myricaceae (bayberries), Nothofagaceae (southern beeches) and Ticodendraceae. The order contains roughly 1,000–1,100 species.

Characteristics

Most Fagales are woody, wind-pollinated trees and shrubs with simple leaves and unisexual flowers, often borne in catkins. Many form ectomycorrhizal associations, and several families fix nitrogen through Frankia symbionts. Members are economically important for timber, nuts (walnuts, hazelnuts, chestnuts, acorns) and as ornamental trees.

Evolution

The order's fossil record extends to the Late Cretaceous, and it diversified during the Cenozoic to dominate large areas of northern temperate forest.
