George Bentham (22 September 1800 – 10 September 1884) was an English botanist, widely regarded as one of the greatest systematic botanists of the nineteenth century. He was the nephew of the philosopher Jeremy Bentham.

Work

Self-taught in botany, Bentham published floras of the Pyrenees and of Hong Kong, and the seven-volume Flora Australiensis (1863–1878), the first complete flora of a continental region. With Joseph Dalton Hooker at Kew, he produced Genera Plantarum (1862–1883), a classification of the seed plants describing 7,569 genera that remained influential well into the twentieth century.

Career

Bentham served as president of the Linnean Society from 1861 to 1874. In 1859 he presented Charles Darwin's and Alfred Russel Wallace's papers on natural selection at that society. He donated his herbarium and library to the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew. The standard author abbreviation "Benth." is used for species he described.
