Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo (born 24 June 1962) is a Mexican politician, scientist, and academic who has served as the 66th president of Mexico since 1 October 2024. She is the first woman and the first person of Jewish heritage to hold the office. A member of the National Regeneration Movement (Morena), she previously served as Head of Government of Mexico City from 2018 to 2023.

Sheinbaum, a climate scientist by training, became Mexico's first female president in 2024.

Scientific career

Sheinbaum studied physics at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) and earned a doctorate in energy engineering. Her research focused on energy consumption and climate change mitigation, and she was a contributing author to reports of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, which shared the 2007 Nobel Peace Prize. She served as Secretary of the Environment of Mexico City under Andrés Manuel López Obrador (2000–2006) and as mayor of the Tlalpan borough (2015–2017).

Head of Government of Mexico City

Elected in 2018, Sheinbaum's administration invested in public transport (including new Cablebús gondola lines), digital government, and education, and managed the capital's response to the COVID-19 pandemic. Her tenure also faced criticism over the 2021 Mexico City Metro overpass collapse, which killed 26 people.

Presidency

Winning the June 2024 general election with nearly 60% of the vote as the candidate of the Sigamos Haciendo Historia coalition, Sheinbaum succeeded López Obrador and pledged continuity with his "Fourth Transformation" programme, alongside emphases on renewable energy, security reform, and women's rights. Her government pursued a sweeping judicial reform introducing popular election of judges, expanded social programmes, and negotiated trade and migration issues with the United States amid tariff threats.
