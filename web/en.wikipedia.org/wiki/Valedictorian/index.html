Valedictorian

Valedictorian is an academic title given to the highest-performing student in a graduating class of an educational institution, most commonly in the United States, Canada, and the Philippines. The term derives from the Latin vale dicere, "to say farewell": the valedictorian traditionally delivers the valedictory, the farewell address at the graduation ceremony.

Selection

The title is usually awarded on the basis of grade point average, though some schools weigh course difficulty, use committees, or allow multiple valedictorians when students tie. The student ranked second is traditionally the salutatorian, who gives the opening address. Practices vary widely: some schools have abolished the title or class rank entirely, arguing that they encourage unhealthy competition over tiny grade differences.

At some universities outside North America, "valedictorian" refers simply to the student chosen to give the farewell speech, without regard to rank.
