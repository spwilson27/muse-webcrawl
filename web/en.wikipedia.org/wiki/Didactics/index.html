Didactics

Didactics is the theory and practical study of teaching and instruction — the discipline concerned with what should be taught, how, and why. Particularly developed in continental European and Scandinavian educational traditions, it differs from the more general Anglo-American notion of pedagogy by focusing on the content of teaching and the teacher's professional judgement in transforming subject matter into teachable form.

History

The term derives from the Greek didaktikos, "apt at teaching". Its modern foundation is often traced to John Amos Comenius, whose Didactica Magna (1657) proposed a universal art of teaching. In the German tradition, Johann Friedrich Herbart and later scholars built systematic theories of instruction; in the 20th century Wolfgang Klafki's didactic analysis and Bildung-centred theory became influential in Germany and Scandinavia. Subject-specific branches (such as mathematics didactics or language didactics) address how particular disciplines are best taught.

Key concepts

A classic tool is the didactic (or pedagogical) triangle of teacher, student and content. Didactic analysis asks the teacher to consider the exemplary and present/future significance of a topic, its structure, and its accessibility to learners. In contemporary curricula research the field debates the balance between competence-based standards and the teacher's autonomy, and connects with research on instructional design and assessment.
