The Speyer Cathedral, officially the Imperial Cathedral Basilica of the Assumption and St Stephen (German: Kaiserdom St. Maria und St. Stephan), is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Speyer, Germany. It is the largest Romanesque church still standing in the world and the burial place of Salian, Hohenstaufen, and Habsburg rulers of the Holy Roman Empire.

History

Construction began around 1030 under the Salian emperor Conrad II, who intended the cathedral as an imperial burial church. The first building was completed and consecrated in 1061. Between 1082 and 1106, Conrad's grandson Henry IV had the church substantially rebuilt, raising the nave vaults and enlarging the crypt; the result was one of the first fully groin-vaulted basilicas in Europe.

The cathedral survived the destruction of Speyer in 1689 during the Nine Years' War only in part; the western sections were later rebuilt. In the 19th century the westwork was restored in Neo-Romanesque style and the interior decorated with frescoes under Johann Schraudolph, sponsored by King Ludwig I of Bavaria.

Architecture

The cathedral is a three-aisled basilica on a cruciform plan, 134 metres long, with six towers: four slender towers flank the east and west ends, and two octagonal crossing towers rise over the transept and west block. The eastern apse and the alternating system of piers and columns in the nave are hallmarks of early Romanesque design. A monumental dwarf gallery runs around the exterior beneath the roofline.

Crypt and imperial burials

The vast crypt, one of the largest columned halls of the Romanesque period, covers the entire footprint of the eastern church. It contains the tombs of eight German emperors and kings, including Conrad II, Henry III, Henry IV, and Rudolf of Habsburg, along with several queens and bishops.

Significance

In 1981 UNESCO inscribed Speyer Cathedral on the World Heritage List, recognizing it as a major monument of Romanesque art in the German Empire. It remains the seat of the Bishop of Speyer and a functioning parish church.
