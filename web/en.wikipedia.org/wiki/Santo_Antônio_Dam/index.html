The Santo Antônio Dam is a run-of-the-river hydroelectric dam on the Madeira River near Porto Velho in the state of Rondônia, Brazil. With an installed capacity of about 3,568 megawatts from 50 bulb turbines, it is one of the largest hydroelectric power stations in Brazil and in the world by capacity, and forms part of the Madeira River hydroelectric complex together with the Jirau Dam upstream.

Construction

The project was auctioned in 2007 and construction began in 2008 by the Santo Antônio Energia consortium. Its design uses low-head bulb turbines that generate power from the river's flow with a relatively small reservoir for a plant of its size, an approach intended to reduce flooded area in the Amazon rainforest. The first turbines entered service in 2012 and the plant reached full capacity in 2016.

Environmental and social concerns

The dam has been controversial. Critics point to effects on fish migration — the Madeira is a major route for Amazonian catfish — on sediment transport, and on riverside communities displaced by the reservoir. Exceptional flooding of the Madeira in 2014, which inundated parts of Porto Velho and Bolivia downstream, intensified debate over the dams' role, though operators attributed the flood to extreme natural flows.

Energy supply

Power from Santo Antônio is transmitted to Brazil's southeast by one of the longest high-voltage direct-current lines in the world, a roughly 2,400-kilometre link to the São Paulo region.
