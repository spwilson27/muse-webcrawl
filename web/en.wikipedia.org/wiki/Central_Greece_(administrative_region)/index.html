Central Greece (administrative region)

Central Greece (Greek: Στερεά Ελλάδα, Sterea Ellada) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece. It consists of the part of the traditional geographic region of Central Greece that lies outside the region of Attica, forming the eastern portion of the Greek mainland north of the Peloponnese. Its capital is the city of Lamia.

Geography

The region comprises the regional units of Boeotia, Euboea, Evrytania, Phocis, and Phthiotis. It is mountainous in its western parts, where the Pindus range extends into Evrytania, and includes Mount Parnassus and Mount Giona, among the highest peaks in Greece. The island of Euboea, the country's second-largest island, forms the region's eastern portion, and the region's southern part borders the Gulf of Corinth. Significant plains include the Boeotian plain around Thebes and the Spercheios valley near Lamia.

History and economy

The area contains some of the most famous sites of ancient Greece, including Delphi in Phocis and the battlefield of Thermopylae in Phthiotis. The modern region was established in the 1987 administrative reform and modified under the Kallikratis Plan of 2011. Its economy combines agriculture, aluminium production and mining around Boeotia, tourism in Euboea and the mountain areas, and shipping and industry connected with the ports of the Euboean Gulf.
