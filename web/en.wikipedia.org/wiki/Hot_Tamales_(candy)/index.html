Hot Tamales (candy)

Hot Tamales
Product type: Cinnamon-flavored jelly candy
Owner: Just Born
Introduced: 1950
Related brands: Mike and Ike, Peeps, Peanut Chews

Hot Tamales is an American brand of oblong, red, cinnamon-flavored jelly candies introduced in 1950 by the Just Born candy company of Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. They are closely related to Just Born's Mike and Ike fruit-flavored candies, which share the same shape and chewy texture, but are flavored intensely with cinnamon for a "fiery" taste.

History and product

Just Born, founded by Russian immigrant Sam Born, acquired the Rodda Candy Company in 1950, gaining the jelly-bean technology behind its capsule-shaped candies. Hot Tamales were reportedly named after the Mexican tamale, playing on the idea of spicy heat. The candies are made from sugar, corn syrup, modified food starch, and artificial cinnamon flavoring, coated for a glossy finish. Variations have included Hot Tamales 3 Alarm (with hotter intensity levels), Tropical Heat, and "Fire & Ice" (paired with Cool Mint). Marketing has long emphasized their spiciness, including the character "Fire Mouth" in later campaigns.

Popularity

Hot Tamales are perennially among the best-selling cinnamon candies in the United States and a staple of movie-theater concession stands. In 1999 the brand was the subject of a light-hearted office "theft" debate that led Just Born to run tongue-in-cheek advertising about employees misappropriating each other's candies.
