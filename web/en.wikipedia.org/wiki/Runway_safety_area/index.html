Runway safety area

A runway safety area (RSA) or runway end safety area (RESA) is the surface surrounding the runway, prepared or suitable for reducing the risk of damage to airplanes in the event of an undershoot, overshoot, or excursion from the runway.

Dimensions

Under FAA standards in the United States, the RSA extends 1,000 feet (about 300 m) beyond each runway end and is 500 feet wide for runways serving large aircraft, kept clear of obstacles, graded, and capable of supporting an aircraft and firefighting vehicles. ICAO recommends a runway end safety area extending at least 90 m beyond the runway strip, and preferably 240 m for code 3 and 4 runways — the international equivalent concept differs somewhat in measurement from the U.S. RSA.

History

Early runways simply ended at the airport boundary. Accidents in which aircraft overran into roads, railways, and buildings drove the adoption of protected overrun areas. In the United States, a congressional mandate in 1999 required commercial airports to bring RSAs up to standard by 2015, prompting hundreds of projects — land acquisition, runway shifts, declared distances, and arresting systems — at space-constrained airports such as Chicago Midway and Burbank.

Engineered arresting systems

Where a full-length RSA cannot be provided, an engineered materials arresting system (EMAS) — a bed of crushable cellular concrete blocks at the runway end — can decelerate an overrunning aircraft comparably within a shorter distance. EMAS beds have safely stopped multiple overrunning airliners in the United States.
