Sturlunga saga

Sturlunga saga (often called simply Sturlunga) is a collection of Icelandic sagas dealing with the political history of Iceland in the 12th and 13th centuries, during the period known as the Age of the Sturlungs. It is the largest single compilation of contemporary sagas (samtíðarsögur) and a major historical source for the turbulent decades leading to Iceland's submission to the Norwegian crown in 1262–1264.

Contents

The compilation includes several individual sagas, among them Íslendinga saga (the longest component, attributed to Sturla Þórðarson), Þorgils saga og Hafliða, Sturlu saga, Prestssaga Guðmundar góða, Guðmundar saga dýra, Hrafns saga Sveinbjarnarsonar, Þórðar saga kakala, Svínfellinga saga, and Þorgils saga skarða. Together they chronicle feuds, battles, and alliances among Iceland's leading chieftain families, including the Sturlungs, Haukdælir, Ásbirningar, and Oddaverjar.

Manuscripts

The compilation survives principally in two vellum manuscripts from the 14th century, known as Króksfjarðarbók and Reykjarfjarðarbók, both now preserved in part in the Árni Magnússon Institute in Iceland. The compiler of the collection is unknown, though it is thought to have been assembled around 1300.
