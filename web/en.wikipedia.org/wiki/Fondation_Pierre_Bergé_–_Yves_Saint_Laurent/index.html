Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent
Established2002
Location5 Avenue Marceau, 75116 Paris, France
TypeFashion heritage foundation & museum
FounderPierre Bergé

The Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent is a French foundation recognized as being of public utility, created in 2002 by Pierre Bergé to preserve and promote the work of the couturier Yves Saint Laurent and the history of the house of Yves Saint Laurent. Its headquarters occupy the historic mansion at 5 Avenue Marceau in Paris, the site of the couture house from 1974 until the closure of the haute couture activity in 2002. Since October 2017 the building has housed the Musée Yves Saint Laurent Paris.

Collections

The foundation conserves an exceptional heritage assembled from the founding of the house in 1961: approximately 35,000 objects, including some 7,000 haute couture garments and 15,000 accessories, along with sketches, collection boards (the "bibles" recording every garment in a collection), workshop specification sheets, photographs, films, and the house's archives. Among its emblematic pieces are the Mondrian dress (1965), the first women's tuxedo "Le Smoking" (1966), the safari jacket, the Russian collection of 1976, and tributes to artists including Picasso, Van Gogh, and Matisse. The foundation holds works of art from Bergé and Saint Laurent's celebrated private collection — portions of which were sold at the 2009 "sale of the century" at Christie's, proceeds from which benefited the foundation and AIDS research.

Activities

From 2004, before the opening of the museum, the foundation presented thematic exhibitions in its Paris spaces, devoted among other subjects to Saint Laurent's dialogue with art, his theatre and costume designs, and photographers and artists from the couple's circle. It lends pieces to museums worldwide. The foundation also long supported, at Pierre Bergé's direction, cultural and charitable causes, notably the fight against AIDS through Sidaction, and cultural institutions such as the Maison de la Poésie.

Musée Yves Saint Laurent Paris

On 3 October 2017 the foundation opened the Musée Yves Saint Laurent Paris in the Avenue Marceau building, with exhibition areas, Saint Laurent's preserved studio, and displays drawn from the collection. A sister museum, the Musée Yves Saint Laurent Marrakech, designed by Studio KO, opened the same month near the Jardin Majorelle in Morocco — the country where Bergé and Saint Laurent had a home and which inspired many collections.

Governance

The foundation was chaired by Pierre Bergé until his death in September 2017. Madison Cox, landscape designer and Bergé's husband, subsequently became president of the foundation and directed the museums. Yves Saint Laurent himself had died in 2008; the foundation's statutes charged it with perpetuating his work, conserving the collections, and organizing exhibitions of his creations.
