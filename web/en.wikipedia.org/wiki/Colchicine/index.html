Colchicine is a medication used to treat gout and Behçet's disease, and to prevent attacks of familial Mediterranean fever. It is an alkaloid naturally occurring in plants such as the autumn crocus (Colchicum autumnale) and the glory lily (Gloriosa superba). It is taken by mouth and has a narrow therapeutic index, meaning that the toxic dose is not much higher than the therapeutic dose.

Medical uses

Colchicine is used to treat acute gout flares and to prevent recurrent attacks. It is a mainstay of treatment for familial Mediterranean fever, reducing the frequency of attacks and the risk of amyloidosis. It is also used in acute and recurrent pericarditis, often in combination with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and in Behçet's disease. In 2023, low-dose colchicine was approved in the United States to reduce the risk of cardiovascular events in patients with established atherosclerotic disease.

Mechanism of action

Colchicine binds to tubulin and inhibits microtubule polymerisation, thereby disrupting cytoskeletal functions such as neutrophil migration and degranulation. By impairing the movement and activity of inflammatory cells, it reduces the inflammation that drives gout attacks. The same antimitotic action explains its toxicity to rapidly dividing cells.

Adverse effects

Common side effects are gastrointestinal, including nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea. Overdose can cause severe multi-organ failure and death; there is no specific antidote. Colchicine interacts with drugs that inhibit CYP3A4 or P-glycoprotein, such as clarithromycin and ciclosporin, which can raise its blood levels dangerously.

History

Extracts of autumn crocus were described for joint pain in the Ebers Papyrus of ancient Egypt, around 1500 BCE. The alkaloid itself was isolated in 1820 by the French chemists Pierre-Joseph Pelletier and Joseph-Bienaimé Caventou. Its structure was elucidated in the twentieth century, and it became a classic tool in cell biology for arresting cells in metaphase; plant breeders also use it to induce polyploidy.
