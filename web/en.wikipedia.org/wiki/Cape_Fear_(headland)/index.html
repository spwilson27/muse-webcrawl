Cape Fear is a prominent headland on the coast of North Carolina in the United States, formed where the Cape Fear River meets the Atlantic Ocean near Bald Head Island. It is the southernmost point of the state's barrier island chain in the region and gives its name to the surrounding Cape Fear region, including the city of Wilmington.

History

The cape was named after the fear inspired in early explorers by the dangerous shoals offshore, particularly Frying Pan Shoals, which extend far into the Atlantic. Verrazzano's expedition sighted the coast in 1524, and the area later saw pirate activity — Stede Bonnet was captured in the river's mouth in 1718. During the American Civil War the cape guarded the approaches to Wilmington, a vital Confederate port protected by Fort Fisher.

Geography

The cape marks the point where the coastline turns sharply. Shifting sandbars and shoals have made navigation treacherous for centuries, and the area has been served by lighthouses including Old Baldy on Bald Head Island, first lit in 1817.
