Antilibrary
Coined byNassim Nicholas Taleb
ConceptValue of unread books / unknown knowledge

An antilibrary is a collection of books that are owned but unread. The term was popularised by Nassim Nicholas Taleb in his book The Black Swan (2007), drawing on the example of Umberto Eco's large library.

Concept

Taleb argued that a private library should contain as much of what the owner does not know as their finances allow: the unread books — the antilibrary — are a reminder of the limits of one's knowledge and a resource for future learning, and their value increases rather than decreases the more one reads.

Related ideas

The Japanese word tsundoku describes the habit of acquiring books and letting them pile up unread. The antilibrary reframes this accumulation positively, as intellectual humility made visible.
