Debenture

A debenture is a long-term security yielding a fixed rate of interest, issued by a company and secured against the company's assets. In US usage, the term has come to signify a bond that is not secured by specific assets, whereas in British usage it denotes a loan secured by a floating charge (or, less commonly, a fixed charge) over all the assets of the company issuing it.

Legal meaning

In English law a debenture is a document creating or acknowledging a debt owed by a company. Debenture holders are creditors, not shareholders: they receive interest whether or not the company makes a profit, and on a winding-up they rank ahead of shareholders (though their security determines their priority among creditors). In the United States, debentures are unsecured obligations backed only by the creditworthiness and reputation of the issuer, and therefore typically pay higher interest than secured bonds.

Types

  • Convertible debentures — exchangeable for equity shares of the issuer on specified terms.
  • Redeemable debentures — repayable on a fixed date or after notice.
  • Registered and bearer debentures — differing in how ownership is recorded and transferred.
  • Subordinated debentures — ranked below senior debt in liquidation.
