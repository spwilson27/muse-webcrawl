Samanu (Persian: سمنو), also called samanak or sümölük, is a sweet paste made from germinated wheat (young wheatgrass) that is prepared especially for Nowruz, the Persian New Year, across Iran, Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan and neighbouring regions.

Preparation

Wheat grains are soaked and sprouted over several days, ground, and then cooked slowly with flour and water in a large cauldron — traditionally overnight, often by women working in turns while singing and making wishes. No sugar is added; the sweetness comes from the malt sugars of the sprouted grain, producing a thick, dark-brown, mildly sweet pudding.

Cultural role

Samanu is one of the seven items beginning with the letter S on the Haft-Seen table, symbolising affluence, patience and rebirth. The laborious communal preparation is itself a social ritual, and a folk saying holds that wishes made while stirring the pot will be granted.
