Ahmad Shah Durrani

Ahmad Shah Durrani (c. 1722 – 16 October 1772), born Ahmad Khan Abdali, was the founder of the Durrani Empire and is regarded as the founder of the modern state of Afghanistan. In July 1747 he was elected king by a loya jirga of Afghan chiefs at Kandahar following the assassination of the Persian ruler Nader Shah, under whom he had served as a cavalry commander.

Campaigns

Ahmad Shah united the Pashtun tribes and built an empire stretching from eastern Persia to northern India. He invaded Mughal India nine times, occupying Delhi in 1757 and defeating the Maratha Confederacy decisively at the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761, one of the largest battles of the 18th century. He repeatedly fought the Sikhs of Punjab, and campaigned against the Uzbeks of Bukhara and the Persians in Khorasan.

Legacy

Ahmad Shah was also a poet in Pashto and Persian. He took the regnal title Durr-i-Durran, "Pearl of Pearls," from which the name Durrani derives. His mausoleum stands in Kandahar near the Shrine of the Cloak. He is honoured in Afghanistan as a national hero, often styled Ahmad Shah Baba ("Ahmad Shah the Father").
