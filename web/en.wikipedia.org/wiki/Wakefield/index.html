Wakefield

Country: England

Region: Yorkshire and the Humber

County: West Yorkshire

Metropolitan borough: City of Wakefield

Population: Around 110,000 (city); district over 350,000

Wakefield is a cathedral city and metropolitan borough in West Yorkshire, England, situated on the River Calder about 14 kilometres south-east of Leeds. It is the administrative centre of the City of Wakefield district, a large metropolitan borough that also includes the historic "Five Towns" of the surrounding coalfield. The city has city status by virtue of its Anglican cathedral.

History

The area was settled in Roman and Anglo-Saxon times; the name derives from Old English, plausibly "Waca's field" or the field where a wake (festival) was held. Wakefield was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086. In 1460, during the Wars of the Roses, the Battle of Wakefield was fought at Sandal, south of the town, at which Richard, Duke of York was killed — an event remembered in the mnemonic for the colours of the rainbow, "Richard of York gave battle in vain." Wakefield grew as a market town and inland port on the Calder, trading corn, cattle and wool, and in the industrial era became a centre of coal mining and textile manufacture; its last deep coal mine, in the area, closed in the 21st century.

Culture

Landmarks include Wakefield Cathedral, whose spire at 247 feet is the tallest in Yorkshire; Sandal Castle; the Hepworth Wakefield art gallery (opened 2011), named for sculptor Barbara Hepworth, who was born in the city; and the nearby Yorkshire Sculpture Park at West Bretton. The city was also the birthplace of novelist George Gissing. Each year the area celebrates its forced-rhubarb heritage with a Festival of Food, Drink and Rhubarb, Wakefield forming one point of the "Rhubarb Triangle." Wakefield has two railway stations, Westgate (on the East Coast Main Line, with fast services to London) and Kirkgate.
