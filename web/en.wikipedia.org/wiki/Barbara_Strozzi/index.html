Barbara Strozzi

Barbara Strozzi (1619 – 1677) was an Italian Baroque singer and composer, one of the most prolific composers of secular vocal music of her century. Working in Venice, she published eight volumes of her own music — an exceptional achievement for a woman of her time.

Born1619, Venice
Died1677, Padua
OccupationComposer, singer
EraBaroque

Life

Born Barbara Valle, she was the adopted (and probably natural) daughter of the poet and librettist Giulio Strozzi, who promoted her performances in Venetian academies. She studied with Francesco Cavalli, the leading opera composer of the day.

Music

Strozzi specialised in the cantata for solo voice and basso continuo, setting texts — many by her father — on love, desire, and lament. Her first book of madrigals appeared in 1644, and her Cantate, ariette e duetti (1651) and later collections display expressive chromaticism and virtuoso vocal writing. Unusually, she published more music than most male contemporaries, despite lacking a court or church appointment.

Legacy

Long overlooked, her works were revived in the late 20th century and are now widely recorded as masterpieces of the early Baroque.
