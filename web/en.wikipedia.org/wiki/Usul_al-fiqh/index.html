Usul al-fiqh

Uṣūl al-fiqh (Arabic: أصول الفقه, "roots of jurisprudence") is the study of the principles of Islamic jurisprudence. It explores the methodologies used in deriving Islamic law (sharia) from its primary sources, the Quran and the Sunnah, and the secondary sources and methods agreed upon by legal scholars.

Sources and methods

The classical framework recognises four principal sources: the Quran, the Sunnah (the practice of the Prophet Muhammad), ijmāʿ (scholarly consensus), and qiyās (analogical reasoning). Where the primary texts are silent, jurists employ further principles such as istiḥsān (juristic preference), maṣlaḥa (public interest), and ʿurf (custom), which vary between the legal schools (madhhabs).

History

The discipline is traditionally credited to Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (767–820), whose Risāla systematised the theory of legal derivation. Later scholars of all major schools wrote extensive works refining the principles, modes of interpretation, and rules for resolving conflicts between evidences.

Relation to fiqh

Whereas fiqh concerns the substantive rulings of Islamic law, uṣūl al-fiqh is its theoretical foundation: the meta-discipline governing how rulings are derived, interpreted, and weighed. A scholar qualified to exercise independent reasoning in this framework is known as a mujtahid.
