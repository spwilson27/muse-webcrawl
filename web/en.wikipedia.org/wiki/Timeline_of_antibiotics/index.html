Timeline of antibiotics

This article presents a timeline of antibiotics, listing the year of their first use, introduction or discovery. Antibiotics revolutionised medicine in the 20th century, dramatically reducing deaths from bacterial infection.

Early observations

Ancient civilisations including the Egyptians, Chinese and Greeks used moulds and plants to treat infection. In 1871 Joseph Lister noted that urine contaminated with mould grew bacteria poorly. In 1928 Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin, secreted by the fungus Penicillium rubens, though its therapeutic development waited until 1940–41 by Howard Florey, Ernst Chain and colleagues at Oxford.

Antibiotic resistance

Almost as soon as antibiotics were introduced, bacteria began evolving resistance. Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) appeared in 1960, and multidrug-resistant tuberculosis and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales now pose major public-health threats. The World Health Organization maintains a list of priority pathogens for which new antibiotics are urgently needed.
