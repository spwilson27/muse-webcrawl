Badarayana

Badarayana (Sanskrit: बादरायण, Bādarāyaṇa) was an Indian philosopher traditionally credited as the author of the Brahma Sutras (also called the Vedanta Sutras), the foundational text that systematized the teachings of the Upanishads and founded the Vedanta school of Hindu philosophy. Tradition often identifies him with Vyasa, the legendary compiler of the Vedas and the Mahabharata, though scholars treat the figures as distinct or at least distinguishable.

Dating and identity

Scholarly estimates of Badarayana's date vary widely, roughly between 200 BCE and 450 CE, with many placing the composition of the Brahma Sutras in the early centuries CE. The sutras themselves refer to earlier Vedantic teachers such as Ashmarathya, Audulomi, and Jaimini by name, suggesting that Badarayana synthesized an existing tradition rather than founding one from scratch.

The Brahma Sutras

The Brahma Sutras consist of some 555 terse aphorisms in four chapters, reconciling apparently contradictory Upanishadic passages about Brahman, the ultimate reality, and the self (atman). Because of their brevity, the sutras generated an enormous commentarial literature: the rival interpretations of Shankara (Advaita, non-dualism), Ramanuja (Vishishtadvaita, qualified non-dualism), and Madhva (Dvaita, dualism) define the major sub-schools of Vedanta, and each claimed to present Badarayana's true intent.
