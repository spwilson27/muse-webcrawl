The R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company (RJR) is an American tobacco-manufacturing company based in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, and the second-largest tobacco company in the United States. Founded in 1875 by R. J. Reynolds, it is a wholly owned subsidiary of Reynolds American Inc., which in turn is owned by British American Tobacco. Its flagship brands include Camel, Newport, Pall Mall, Winston, Doral, Salem, Kent and Natural American Spirit, as well as the Vuse vapour brand through a sister company.

History

Richard Joshua "R. J." Reynolds began selling chewing tobacco in 1875 in what was then Winston, North Carolina, building a factory with a small workforce. The company grew rapidly on plug and smoking tobacco, and in 1913 it introduced Camel, the first nationally marketed blended cigarette in a packaged 20-cigarette pack, which became one of the best-selling cigarettes in the world. Reynolds was also a pioneer of the cellophane-wrapped pack and, later, of the king-size and menthol-filter categories with the Winston and Salem brands.

Corporate developments

In the 1980s the company diversified as RJR Nabisco after merging with Nabisco Brands, and in 1988 it was taken private by Kohlberg Kravis Roberts in what was then the largest leveraged buyout in history, chronicled in the book Barbarians at the Gate. The tobacco business was spun off again in 1999. In 2004 the U.S. operations of Brown & Williamson were combined with R. J. Reynolds to form Reynolds American Inc. British American Tobacco acquired full control of Reynolds American in 2017 for about $49 billion.

Controversies

Like other major cigarette manufacturers, the company was a defendant in extensive litigation over the health effects of smoking and was a party to the 1998 Master Settlement Agreement with 46 U.S. states. Its Joe Camel mascot, used from 1987 to 1997, drew criticism for appealing to children and was retired amid regulatory pressure.
