Anoxia

Anoxia is a condition in which there is an absence of oxygen supply to an organ or tissue, or to the body as a whole. It is the most extreme form of oxygen deficiency; hypoxia, by contrast, refers to a partial deficiency. Anoxia can result from interrupted blood flow (ischemia), respiratory failure, drowning, strangulation, or exposure to environments lacking breathable oxygen.

Medical aspects

The brain is especially vulnerable to anoxia: anoxic brain injury can begin within minutes of oxygen deprivation and may cause permanent damage or death. Causes include cardiac arrest, severe asthma, carbon monoxide poisoning and complications of anaesthesia. In newborns, perinatal anoxia is a significant cause of cerebral palsy. Treatment focuses on restoring oxygenation and circulation as quickly as possible.

Environmental anoxia

In aquatic science, anoxic waters are areas of sea water, fresh water or groundwater depleted of dissolved oxygen, such as the "dead zones" associated with eutrophication. In Earth history, oceanic anoxic events were episodes in which large parts of the oceans became depleted of oxygen, often coinciding with mass extinctions and the deposition of black shales.
