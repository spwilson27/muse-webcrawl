Urdu


"Urdu" in Nastaliq script

Urdu (اردو) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken chiefly in South Asia. It is the national language and lingua franca of Pakistan, where it is spoken as a first language by a minority but understood by the great majority, and it is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India, with official status in several states and union territories. Worldwide there are well over 200 million speakers when second-language users are counted.

Relationship to Hindi

Urdu and Hindi share a common colloquial base descended from the Khari Boli dialect of the Delhi region, and at the everyday spoken level they are largely mutually intelligible — together often called Hindustani. They diverge in script and in learned vocabulary: Urdu is written in a Perso-Arabic script (usually the flowing Nastaliq style) and draws formal vocabulary from Persian and Arabic, whereas Hindi is written in Devanagari and draws on Sanskrit.

History

Urdu developed in the medieval period through contact between local Indo-Aryan speech and the Persian, Arabic and Turkic of soldiers, administrators and Sufi saints — its very name derives from the Turkic word ordu ("camp, army"), reflected in its older name Rekhta. It flowered as a literary language in the Deccan and at the Mughal court cities of Delhi and Lucknow, producing a celebrated tradition of poetry — the ghazal and marsiya — with poets such as Mir Taqi Mir, Mirza Ghalib and, in the twentieth century, Muhammad Iqbal and Faiz Ahmad Faiz.

Status

Urdu is strongly associated with Muslim identity in South Asia and was a central symbol of the Pakistan Movement. It remains a major language of print and broadcast media, film song, and religious life.
