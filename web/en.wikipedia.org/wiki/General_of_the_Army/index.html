General of the Army
Country: United States
Service branch: United States Army
Rank group: General officer
Formation: July 25, 1866
Next higher rank: General of the Armies
Insignia: Five stars

General of the Army (abbreviated as GA) is a five-star general officer rank in the United States Army. It is equivalent to the rank of fleet admiral in the United States Navy and is generally considered one of the highest possible military ranks in the United States. The rank stands above general (four stars) and below only General of the Armies.

History

The rank was first created on July 25, 1866, for Ulysses S. Grant, who received the title "General of the Army of the United States" after the American Civil War. It was later held by William Tecumseh Sherman and Philip Sheridan. The rank was revived in December 1944 during the Second World War, when five-star rank was established so that American commanders would not be outranked by allied field marshals.

The five officers promoted to General of the Army during and after the Second World War were George C. Marshall, Douglas MacArthur, Dwight D. Eisenhower, Henry H. Arnold (later also the only General of the Air Force), and Omar Bradley. Bradley, promoted in 1950, was the last officer to hold the rank, and no officer has held it since his death in 1981.

Insignia and privileges

Generals of the Army wear five silver stars arranged in a pentagonal pattern. Officers holding the rank are considered to remain on active duty for life, retaining full pay and allowances, a privilege reflecting the rank's status.
