Polishing

Polishing is the process of creating a smooth and shiny surface by rubbing it or by applying a chemical treatment, leaving a surface with a significant specular reflection (still limited by the index of refraction of the material according to the Fresnel equations). In some materials (such as metals, glasses, black or transparent stones), polishing is also able to reduce diffuse reflection to minimal values.

When an unpolished surface is magnified thousands of times, it usually looks like a succession of mountains and valleys. By repeated abrasion, those "mountains" are worn down until they are flat or just small "hills". The process of polishing with abrasives starts with a coarse grain size and gradually proceeds to the finer ones to efficiently flatten the surface imperfections and hence get optimal results.

Properties

Polishing is often used to enhance the appearance of an item, prevent contamination of instruments, remove oxidation, create a reflective surface, or prevent corrosion in pipes. In metallography and metallurgy, polishing is used to create a flat, defect-free surface for examination of the microstructure of a metal under a microscope. Silicon-based polishing pads or diamond solutions can be used in the polishing process.

Types

  • Mechanical polishing — using abrasives of progressively finer grit.
  • Chemical-mechanical polishing — combining chemical etching with mechanical abrasion, common in semiconductor manufacturing.
  • Electropolishing — an electrochemical process that removes material from a metallic workpiece.
  • Buffing — a finishing process using a cloth wheel and cutting compound.
