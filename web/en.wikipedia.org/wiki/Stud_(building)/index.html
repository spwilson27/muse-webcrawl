Stud (building)

A wall stud is a vertical repetitive framing member in a building's wall of smaller cross section than a post. Traditionally of wood, studs are a fundamental element in platform framing and balloon framing, and are also made of steel in modern construction. Studs form walls and carry vertical structural loads or simply partition space.

Description

In North American construction, studs are typically nominal 2×4 (or 2×6) lumber, spaced 16 or 24 inches apart on centre, running from a bottom (sole) plate to a top plate. Openings for doors and windows are framed with king and jack studs and horizontal headers. Interior finishes such as drywall and exterior sheathing are fastened to the studs, and electrical wiring and plumbing run through holes or notches in them. Metal "C-channel" studs are common in commercial interiors and non-load-bearing partitions.
