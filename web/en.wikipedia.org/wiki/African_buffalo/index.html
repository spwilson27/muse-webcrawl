African buffalo

The African buffalo (Syncerus caffer) is a large sub-Saharan African bovine. It is one of the most successful grazers in Africa, found in savannas, swamps, and floodplains across much of the continent south of the Sahara. The African buffalo is not an ancestor of domestic cattle and is only distantly related to other larger bovines. Because of its unpredictable temperament, it has never been domesticated, and it is regarded as one of Africa's most dangerous animals to hunters—the so-called "Big Five".

Description

The African buffalo is heavily built, with stocky legs and large hooves. Adults have dark brown to nearly black coats; calves are reddish. Both sexes carry heavy horns that curve upward from a massive fused base called a "boss," which in males forms a complete shield across the top of the head. Horn spans can reach over one metre. The Cape buffalo (S. c. caffer), the largest subspecies, inhabits southern and eastern savannas, while the smaller forest buffalo (S. c. nanus) has reddish fur and smaller, straighter horns and lives in the rainforests of Central and West Africa.

Behaviour and ecology

Buffalo live in herds ranging from a few dozen to over a thousand animals. Herds maintain a loose social structure and are known for collective defence: members will mob predators such as lions and have been observed rescuing captured herd-mates. Older males, driven from herds, often form small bachelor groups or live alone; these solitary bulls, wallowing in mud to escape biting flies, are nicknamed "dagga boys."

Buffalo are bulk grazers, feeding mainly on tall, coarse grasses. They need regular access to water and drink daily. Their grazing helps maintain savanna grasslands, and they serve as important prey for lions and spotted hyenas.

Relationship with humans

African buffalo can be dangerous when wounded or cornered and are responsible for numerous hunter fatalities. They also carry diseases transmissible to livestock, notably foot-and-mouth disease and bovine tuberculosis, which complicates management where buffalo range near farms. Populations have declined from hunting, habitat loss, and diseases such as rinderpest, which devastated herds in the 1890s. Buffalo are nonetheless numerous in protected areas such as the Serengeti and Kruger National Park.
