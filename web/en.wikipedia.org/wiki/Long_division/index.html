Long division

In arithmetic, long division is a standard division algorithm suitable for dividing multi-digit Hindu-Arabic numerals (Positional notation) that is simple enough to perform by hand. It breaks down a division problem into a series of easier steps.

As in all division problems, one number, called the dividend, is divided by another, called the divisor, producing a result called the quotient. It enables one to perform division of quite large numbers by hand while still keeping the calculation reasonably simple.

Method

In English-speaking countries, long division does not use the division slash ⟨∕⟩ or obelus ⟨÷⟩ signs; instead, the dividend, divisor, and (in a separate space) the quotient are displayed in a tableau. The procedure works digit by digit from the most significant end of the dividend: at each step a digit of the quotient is determined, multiplied by the divisor, subtracted, and the next digit of the dividend is "brought down". The process ends when the dividend is exhausted; any amount left over is the remainder.

Related algorithms

An abbreviated form of long division, in which multiplication and subtraction are done mentally, is called short division. Chunking (also known as the partial quotients method) is a less mechanical form widely taught in schools. The method can be extended to polynomial long division and to division producing a decimal expansion by continuing past the decimal point.

History

Related algorithms have existed since the 12th century; the specific tableau method in common use was introduced by Henry Briggs around 1600 and spread through textbooks in the following centuries.
