ISO week date

The ISO week date system is a leap week calendar system that is part of the ISO 8601 date and time standard issued by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). The system specifies a week year of 52 or 53 full weeks, and a date is given by the week year, the week number within the year, and the weekday number (1 for Monday through 7 for Sunday). For example, 2020-W53-5 means Friday of week 53 of 2020.

Rules

Weeks begin on Monday. Week 1 of a week year is the first week with the majority (four or more) of its days in the new Gregorian year — equivalently, the week containing the first Thursday of the year, or the week containing 4 January. Because of this rule, dates near the turn of the year may belong to the previous or next week year: 1 January 2011, for example, is 2010-W52-6.

Advantages

Every week year begins on a Monday and ends on a Sunday, the weekday number is determined directly by the date, and every week year has a whole number of weeks, which makes the system convenient for business planning and accounting. Week numbers are widely used in Europe for scheduling, and printed calendars there often show them.

Relation to other systems

The ISO week system differs from week-numbering schemes used in the United States, where weeks typically start on Sunday and week 1 is the week containing 1 January.
