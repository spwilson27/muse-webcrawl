Don Coppersmith

Don Coppersmith (born c. 1950) is a cryptographer and mathematician. He was involved in the design of the Data Encryption Standard block cipher at IBM, particularly the design of the S-boxes, strengthening them against differential cryptanalysis — a method which was not publicly known at the time, and only rediscovered and published in 1990 by Biham and Shamir.

Work

Coppersmith obtained his B.S. from MIT in 1972 and his M.S. and Ph.D. in mathematics from Harvard University in 1975 and 1977. He worked at IBM for many years on cryptography and algorithm design, contributed to the MARS cipher submitted to the AES competition, and is known for Coppersmith's method (Coppersmith's attack), which finds small roots of polynomial equations using lattice reduction (the LLL algorithm) and has many applications in cryptanalysis, including attacks on RSA with low exponents or partially known keys.

With Shmuel Winograd he published the Coppersmith–Winograd algorithm (1987) for fast matrix multiplication, which long held the record for the lowest known asymptotic complexity. He later worked at the Institute for Defense Analyses' Center for Communications Research in Princeton.
