The Battle of Hansan Island (Korean: 한산도 대첩) was fought on 14 August 1592 between the Korean fleet of Admiral Yi Sun-sin and a Japanese fleet under Wakizaka Yasuharu, during the Imjin War. Yi lured the Japanese out of the sheltered harbour of Gyonnaeryang into open water off Hansan Island and destroyed them with his famous "crane wing" formation. It is considered one of the greatest naval victories in Korean history and a decisive blow to the Japanese invasion's supply lines.

Background

In the summer of 1592, Toyotomi Hideyoshi's invasion armies had overrun much of Korea, but Yi Sun-sin's navy dominated the southern coast, cutting the maritime supply route on which the Japanese advance depended. Wakizaka's fleet of 73 ships lay anchored at Gyonnaeryang. Yi judged the strait too narrow and rocky for battle and resolved to draw the Japanese into the open sea off Hansan Island.

The battle

Yi sent a small squadron into the strait to provoke Wakizaka; as planned, the Japanese pursued them out to sea, where Yi's main fleet deployed in the hagyŏk or "crane wing" formation — a concave crescent that enveloped the enemy and allowed Korean cannon-armed panokseon and armoured turtle ships to deliver concentrated, overlapping fire while keeping beyond boarding range, the Japanese strength. The Japanese fleet was annihilated: 59 ships were sunk or captured and thousands of men killed, while Korea lost no ships.

Aftermath

Hansan Island crippled Japanese naval power in Korean waters and, combined with the defeats at Okpo, Sacheon and Busanpo, forced the invasion armies onto the defensive, starved of seaborne supply. Yi was promoted and given command of a combined fleet, but political intrigue later saw him stripped of command — with catastrophic results at Chilcheollyang in 1597, before his restoration and final victory at Myeongnyang.
