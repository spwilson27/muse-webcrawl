Ashtanga (eight limbs of yoga)

Ashtanga (Sanskrit: अष्टाङ्ग, aṣṭāṅga, "eight limbs") is the eightfold path of yoga set out in the Yoga Sutras of Patañjali, the foundational text of classical (Raja) yoga. The eight limbs form a progressive discipline moving from ethical conduct and bodily practice inward to meditation and, finally, samadhi — absorption or liberation.

The eight limbs

  1. Yama — ethical restraints: non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness (satya), non-stealing (asteya), continence (brahmacharya) and non-possessiveness (aparigraha).
  2. Niyama — observances: purity, contentment, austerity, self-study and surrender to the divine.
  3. Asana — a steady, comfortable seat or posture, preparing the body for meditation.
  4. Pranayama — regulation of the breath and the life force (prana).
  5. Pratyahara — withdrawal of the senses from external objects.
  6. Dharana — concentration, fixing the mind on a single point.
  7. Dhyana — meditation, an unbroken flow of attention.
  8. Samadhi — absorption, in which meditator and object merge.

The first five limbs are sometimes called the "outer" practices and the last three the "inner" practices; together dharana, dhyana and samadhi form samyama, the combined practice said to yield knowledge and extraordinary powers.

Context and influence

Patañjali's sutras (compiled between the 2nd century BCE and the 4th century CE, by most scholarly estimates) define yoga as "the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind". The eight-limbed framework profoundly influenced later Indian spirituality and, from the 20th century, global understandings of yoga. It should be distinguished from Ashtanga vinyasa yoga, the dynamic modern posture system taught by K. Pattabhi Jois, which takes its name from Patañjali's scheme but centres on flowing sequences of asanas.
