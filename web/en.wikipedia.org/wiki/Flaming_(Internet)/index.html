Flaming (Internet)

Flaming
DefinitionPosting hostile, insulting or inflammatory messages online
EscalationFlame war — a prolonged exchange of flames between users or groups
VenuesForums, Usenet, comment sections, social media, chat
Related conceptsTrolling, cyberbullying, flame bait, Godwin's law

Flaming, also known as roasting, is the act of posting insults, often including profanity or other offensive language, on the internet. These messages, called "flames", may be directed at an individual or a wider group, and are generally the outcome of heated discussion about real-world issues and reflect the divisiveness of the topic. When flames escalate into sustained exchanges, the result is a flame war.


A flame war erupting in a forum thread

History

The term arose in the early online communities of the 1970s and 1980s, notably on Usenet, where certain newsgroups were renowned for ritualised flame wars over topics such as editors, operating systems and science fiction. Early netiquette guides, such as RFC 1855 (1995), warned against flaming and advised readers to wait before replying in anger. Classic flame-war subjects spawned their own lore, including the invocation of comparisons to Nazis — later codified humorously as Godwin's law.

Causes and research

Psychological research attributes flaming to the online disinhibition effect: anonymity, invisibility, asynchronicity and the absence of non-verbal cues reduce empathy and restraint, so people write things they would not say face to face. Studies of computer-mediated communication note that text strips out tone, sarcasm is easily misread, and alcohol, stress and group identity conflict all raise the likelihood of hostile exchanges. Moderation tools, community norms and platform design (such as rate limits and friction before posting) measurably reduce flaming.

Related behaviour

Flaming differs from trolling, which is the deliberate provocation of others for amusement; a flame is usually a sincere expression of anger, though flame bait — a message posted to provoke flames — blurs the line. Sustained targeted flaming can shade into cyberbullying and online harassment, which many jurisdictions and platforms now treat as sanctionable conduct.
