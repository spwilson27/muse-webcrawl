A coat of arms is a heraldic visual design on an escutcheon (shield), surcoat or tabard, unique to an individual person, family, state, organization or corporation. The art and science of designing, displaying and recording arms is called heraldry, and the formal description of arms is a blazon.

History

Heraldry arose in the 12th century, when armoured knights became unrecognizable in battle and tournaments and began painting distinctive designs on their shields and banners. Arms became hereditary in the male line and were regulated by heralds and, in some countries, by authorities such as the College of Arms in England and the Court of the Lord Lyon in Scotland.

Elements

The central element is the shield, bearing charges (lions, crosses, stars and countless others) arranged on a field divided and coloured according to the rules of tincture. A full "achievement" may add a helmet, crest, mantling, crown or coronet, supporters on either side, a motto, and the insignia of orders. National coats of arms serve as state emblems on passports, coins and official buildings.
