Chhattisgarh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chhattisgarh is a landlocked state in east-central India. It was carved out of Madhya Pradesh on 1 November 2000 as India's 26th state. The capital is Raipur, and the planned capital city of Naya Raipur (Atal Nagar) is under development nearby. The name Chhattisgarh is popularly said to mean "thirty-six forts".

Geography

The state covers about 135,000 km², making it one of India's larger states. The Mahanadi River and its tributaries drain the central plain — the state's rice bowl — while the north and south are hilly and heavily forested; around 44 per cent of the state is covered by forest. The population was about 25.5 million at the 2011 census, with a substantial Scheduled Tribe population, including the Gond people.

Economy

Chhattisgarh is rich in minerals, holding major deposits of coal, iron ore, bauxite, limestone and dolomite. It is a significant producer of steel and electricity; the Bhilai Steel Plant is one of India's largest. Agriculture, especially paddy rice, employs much of the workforce.

History and issues

The region has ancient roots, mentioned in inscriptions and ruled successively by Kalchuri, Maratha and British administrations. Since the 1960s parts of the state's forested south have been affected by the Naxalite–Maoist insurgency, a continuing focus of security operations and development policy.
