Defined and undefined

In mathematics, an expression is defined if it has a well-determined meaning under the rules in use, and undefined if it does not. Operations and expressions that are standard examples of undefined values in ordinary arithmetic include division by zero and the value 00 in contexts where competing conventions give different answers.

Undefined operations

  • Division by zero — the expression a/0 has no value in the real numbers, since there is no number which multiplied by 0 yields a (for nonzero a), and every number does so when a = 0.
  • 00 — often left undefined in analysis, though in combinatorics and algebra it is frequently defined as 1.
  • Square roots of negative numbers — undefined within the real numbers, though well-defined in the complex numbers; whether an expression is "undefined" depends on the number system in use.

In computing

In computer science, "undefined" appears in different senses: a programming language specification may leave a result undefined (as in IEEE 754's NaN, "Not a Number", produced by 0/0), or a program's behaviour may be declared undefined, meaning the standard imposes no requirements — a distinct concept from implementation-defined behaviour and unspecified behaviour.

Indeterminate forms

In calculus, an indeterminate form such as 0/0 arising as a limit does not mean the limit is undefined; the limit may exist and be found by other means, for instance with L'Hôpital's rule.
