Charles IV of Spain

Charles IV (Spanish: Carlos IV; 11 November 1748 – 20 January 1819) was King of Spain and the Spanish Empire from 1788 until his abdication in 1808. His reign was dominated by the upheavals of the French Revolution and the Napoleonic Wars, and by the ascendancy of his favourite, Manuel Godoy.

Reign

Charles succeeded his father, Charles III, in 1788. Well-meaning but indolent and devoted to hunting, he left government first to the Count of Floridablanca and the Count of Aranda, and from 1792 to Manuel Godoy, the young guardsman who became the effective ruler of Spain. Spain's wars against revolutionary France ended with the Treaty of Basel (1795); allied with France by the Treaty of San Ildefonso, Spain suffered the destruction of its fleet alongside France at Trafalgar in 1805.

Abdication and exile

The Tumult of Aranjuez in March 1808 forced Godoy's dismissal and Charles's abdication in favour of his son Ferdinand VII. Under pressure from Napoleon at Bayonne, both kings renounced the throne, and Joseph Bonaparte was installed as king, precipitating the Peninsular War. Charles lived out his life in exile, chiefly in Rome and Naples.

Patronage

Like his father, Charles was a patron of the arts; Francisco Goya became court painter under him and produced the famous group portrait Charles IV of Spain and His Family (1800–1801).
