Rondônia (Portuguese pronunciation: [ʁõˈdõniɐ]) is a state in northwestern Brazil, in the North Region, bordering Bolivia to the west and south and the Brazilian states of Amazonas, Mato Grosso, and Acre. Its capital, Porto Velho, stands on the east bank of the Madeira River below the rapids that once blocked navigation into Bolivia. The state is named for Marshal Cândido Rondon, the army engineer and explorer whose telegraph commissions opened the region in the early 20th century and who famously charted, with former U.S. president Theodore Roosevelt, the "River of Doubt" — renamed the Roosevelt River in his expedition's honor.

History

The territory's modern history runs through a sequence of booms. Gold on the Madeira tributaries drew 18th-century bandeirantes; the rubber era brought the construction of the Madeira–Mamoré Railway (1907–1912), built under the Treaty of Petrópolis to carry Bolivian rubber past the rapids — a project notorious for the thousands of workers who died of malaria, yellow fever, and accidents. The federal Territory of Guaporé was created in 1943 from parts of Amazonas and Mato Grosso, renamed Rondônia in 1956, and a mid-century cassiterite (tin ore) boom and the Second World War rubber program (the "rubber soldiers") added population.

Colonization and deforestation

Rondônia's transformation came with the paving of the BR-364 highway from Cuiabá and the World Bank–financed Polonoroeste colonization program of the early 1980s. Hundreds of thousands of migrants from southern Brazil settled government farming plots laid out in a distinctive "fishbone" pattern along the highway and its side roads. The population exploded from about 110,000 in 1970 to over a million by 1991, cattle ranching and logging spread, and Rondônia became a global symbol of Amazon deforestation: by the early 2000s it had lost a larger share of its original forest than almost any other Amazonian state, although large blocks survive in Indigenous territories (such as the Uru-Eu-Wau-Wau) and conservation units. Satellite monitoring and enforcement slowed clearing after 2004, but the state remains on the active agricultural frontier.

Economy and energy

Today Rondônia is a significant producer of beef, coffee (it is one of Brazil's leading robusta producers), cacao, timber, and soy from its southern cone. The Madeira River hosts two of Brazil's largest hydroelectric plants — Santo Antônio (3,568 MW) and Jirau (3,750 MW), built 2008–2016 just upstream of Porto Velho — whose run-of-river dams and the planned binational projects upstream remain controversial for their effects on fisheries, sediments, and the river's famous flood pulse. Porto Velho's river port ships the grain harvest of the northwest down the Madeira–Amazon waterway.
