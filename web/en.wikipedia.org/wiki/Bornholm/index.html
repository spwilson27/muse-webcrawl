Bornholm

Bornholm is a Danish island in the Baltic Sea, east of the rest of Denmark, south of Sweden and north-east of Germany. It covers about 588 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 39,000. Its main town is Rønne, the ferry port on the west coast.

History

Bornholm's round churches, built in the 12th century, are among its best-known landmarks, and the ruined fortress of Hammershus is the largest medieval castle ruin in Northern Europe. The island passed between Danish, Swedish and Lübeck hands over the centuries; at the end of the Second World War it was bombed and occupied by Soviet forces until April 1946, nearly a year after the rest of Denmark was liberated.

Economy

Fishing (notably for herring, celebrated in the island's smokehouses), agriculture, ceramics and tourism are central to the economy, and the island's granite was historically quarried for buildings across Denmark. Bornholm's distinctive dialect, Bornholmsk, is sometimes described as intermediate between Danish and Swedish.
