Phenylalanine

Phenylalanine
Abbreviations: Phe, F
Chemical formula: C9H11NO2
Molar mass: 165.19 g/mol
Classification: essential α-amino acid

Structure of L-phenylalanine.

Phenylalanine (symbol Phe or F) is an essential α-amino acid with the formula C9H11NO2. It is a precursor for the amino acid tyrosine, the monoamine neurotransmitters dopamine and norepinephrine, and the skin pigment melanin. Phenylalanine is a proteinogenic amino acid, encoded by the codons UUU and UUC.

Biological role

Humans cannot synthesise phenylalanine and must obtain it from the diet; protein-rich foods such as meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and soy are good sources. In the body, the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase converts phenylalanine to tyrosine, a reaction requiring the cofactor tetrahydrobiopterin. From tyrosine the body produces the catecholamine neurotransmitters and thyroid hormones.

Phenylketonuria

The genetic disorder phenylketonuria (PKU) results from deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase, causing phenylalanine to accumulate to toxic levels and impair brain development. Newborn screening for PKU is standard in many countries, and affected individuals follow a diet low in phenylalanine. Products containing the sweetener aspartame — which is partly metabolised to phenylalanine — carry warnings for people with PKU.

Sources and synthesis

Plants and microorganisms synthesise phenylalanine via the shikimate pathway from erythrose 4-phosphate and phosphoenolpyruvate. Commercial phenylalanine is produced largely by fermentation or chemical synthesis, and is used in food, feed, and pharmaceutical applications, including production of aspartame.
