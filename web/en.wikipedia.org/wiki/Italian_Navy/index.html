Italian Navy

The Italian Navy (Italian: Marina Militare) is the navy of the Italian Republic. It was formed in 1946 from the surviving ships of the Regia Marina, the royal navy of the Kingdom of Italy established in 1861. It is one of the larger navies of NATO and of the Mediterranean region, with around 30,000 active personnel.

Fleet

The fleet's flagship is the aircraft carrier Cavour, which operates F-35B and Harrier strike aircraft, joined by the landing helicopter dock Trieste. Major units include Horizon-class and FREMM frigates, Sauro- and Type 212A (Todaro-class) submarines, amphibious transport docks of the San Giorgio class, and the sail training ship Amerigo Vespucci, launched in 1931 and still in service. The COMSUBIN special forces group descends from the famous frogman units of the Second World War.

Roles

Headquartered in Rome with main bases at Taranto, La Spezia and Augusta, the navy is responsible for controlling Italy's extensive maritime approaches, contributing to NATO and EU operations, anti-piracy missions off the Horn of Africa, and search-and-rescue and migration-related operations in the central Mediterranean, including the Mare Nostrum operation of 2013–2014.
