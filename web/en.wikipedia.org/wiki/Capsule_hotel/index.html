A capsule hotel (Japanese: カプセルホテル) is a type of hotel developed in Japan that features a large number of very small, enclosed sleeping pods ("capsules") stacked side by side and end to end, offered at low cost for guests who need only a place to sleep. The first capsule hotel was the Capsule Inn Osaka, opened in 1979 and designed by architect Kisho Kurokawa, who had pioneered capsule architecture with the Nakagin Capsule Tower.

Facilities

Each fibreglass capsule provides a mattress, pillow, lighting, ventilation and often a small television, radio and alarm; privacy is by a curtain or shutter rather than a lockable door. Guests store luggage in lockers, change into provided sleepwear, and share communal bathrooms, bathing facilities (sometimes including a sauna or sento-style bath) and a lounge. Many hotels were historically men-only, though most now provide separate floors or sections for women.

Usage and spread

Capsule hotels cater to office workers who have missed the last train, travellers on tight budgets, and, increasingly, tourists seeking a novelty experience. The concept has spread to airports and cities worldwide, inspiring Western "pod hotels" with somewhat larger units.
