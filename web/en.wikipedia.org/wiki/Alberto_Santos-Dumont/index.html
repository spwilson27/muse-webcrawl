Alberto Santos-Dumont

Aviation pioneer
Born20 July 1873, Palmira, Minas Gerais, Brazil
Died23 July 1932 (aged 59), Guarujá, São Paulo, Brazil
OccupationAviator, inventor
Known forDirigible flights; 14-bis and Demoiselle aircraft

Alberto Santos-Dumont (20 July 1873 – 23 July 1932) was a Brazilian aviation pioneer. The heir of a wealthy coffee-farming family, he dedicated his life to aeronautical study and experimentation in Paris, designing, building and flying balloons, dirigibles and heavier-than-air aircraft. In Brazil he is regarded as a national hero and is often credited there as the inventor of the aeroplane.

Early life

Santos-Dumont was born on the Cabangu estate near Palmira (now Santos Dumont) in Minas Gerais, the son of a French-born coffee planter. Fascinated by machinery from childhood, he moved to Paris in 1891, where he became involved in the city's ballooning scene and began building his own balloons and airships.

Dirigibles

Between 1898 and 1905 Santos-Dumont built and flew a series of numbered dirigibles. On 19 October 1901, in his airship No. 6, he won the Deutsch de la Meurthe prize by flying from Saint-Cloud around the Eiffel Tower and back within thirty minutes, becoming an international celebrity. He later kept a small personal airship, the No. 9 Baladeuse, which he flew over Parisian boulevards, sometimes parking it outside his apartment.

Heavier-than-air flight

On 23 October 1906, Santos-Dumont flew his box-kite-like biplane, the 14-bis, at Bagatelle in Paris, making the first public flight of a powered heavier-than-air machine in Europe; on 12 November he flew 220 metres, setting the first aviation world record recognized by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale. In 1907–1909 he developed the Demoiselle (No. 20), an early ultralight monoplane whose plans he freely distributed.

Later life and death

Suffering from multiple sclerosis and depression, and distressed by the military use of aircraft in the First World War and later conflicts, Santos-Dumont returned to Brazil. He took his own life at Guarujá on 23 July 1932. His wristwatch, popularized after he asked Louis Cartier for a watch he could read while flying, is among his lasting cultural legacies, as is his iconic image — captured in his trademark Panama hat and high collar — as the archetypal gentleman aviator.
