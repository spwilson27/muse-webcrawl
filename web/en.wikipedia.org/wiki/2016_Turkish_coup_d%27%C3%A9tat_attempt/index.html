2016 Turkish coup d'état attempt

On 15 July 2016, a faction within the Turkish Armed Forces attempted a coup d'état against the government of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan. Putschist soldiers seized bridges in Istanbul, attacked the parliament and police headquarters in Ankara, and announced a "Peace at Home Council", but the attempt collapsed within hours as loyal forces and civilians mobilised against it.

Date15–16 July 2016
LocationTurkey (mainly Istanbul and Ankara)
DeathsOver 300 killed; more than 2,000 injured
OutcomeCoup fails; mass purges and a two-year state of emergency follow

The night of 15 July

Jets bombed the Grand National Assembly and the presidential complex; soldiers blocked the Bosphorus bridges. Erdoğan, holidaying in Marmaris, narrowly escaped capture and appealed to supporters via a televised FaceTime call on CNN Türk, urging them into the streets. By dawn the coup had disintegrated, with many conscripts surrendering to police and crowds.

Aftermath

The government blamed the Gülen movement of exiled cleric Fethullah Gülen — designated by Ankara as the "FETÖ" terrorist organisation — which Gülen denied. A state of emergency lasted until 2018; more than 100,000 public employees were dismissed and tens of thousands arrested, including soldiers, judges, teachers, and journalists. The failed coup reshaped Turkish politics and civil-military relations, and 15 July is now commemorated as Democracy and National Unity Day.
