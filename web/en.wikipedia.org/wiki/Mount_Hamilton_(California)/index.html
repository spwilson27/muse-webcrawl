Mount Hamilton is a mountain in Santa Clara County, California, in the Diablo Range, east of San Jose. At 4,265 feet (1,300 m), its summit is the highest point in Santa Clara County and overlooks the Santa Clara Valley. The mountain is the site of the Lick Observatory, the first permanently occupied mountain-top observatory in the world.

History

The mountain was named after Laurentine Hamilton, a Presbyterian minister in San Jose. On August 26, 1861, while on a trip with geologist William H. Brewer and Josiah Whitney of the California Geological Survey, Hamilton laughed at his companions' efforts to name the peak after him — and the name stuck. Lick Observatory was completed in 1888 on the summit, funded by the bequest of eccentric San Francisco millionaire James Lick, who is buried beneath the base of the observatory's great 36-inch refractor.

Geography and access

Mount Hamilton Road (State Route 130) climbs from Alum Rock in San Jose in 19 miles of winding road, traditionally said to have been graded gently for horse-drawn wagons carrying telescope parts, with some 365 curves. The summit area also hosts the small observatory community. Snow occasionally covers the peak, making it a popular spectacle for Bay Area residents. The mountain is ringed by Joseph D. Grant County Park and habitats of oak woodland and chaparral. In 2020 the SCU Lightning Complex fires burned large areas around the mountain, briefly threatening the observatory.
