The Nevi'im (נְבִיאִים, Nəḇî'îm, "Prophets") is the second major division of the Hebrew Bible (the Tanakh), between the Torah (Law) and the Ketuvim (Writings). The word is the plural of navi, "prophet" or "spokesperson." The Nevi'im are traditionally divided into the Former Prophets (Nevi'im Rishonim), which are narrative histories, and the Latter Prophets (Nevi'im Aharonim), which contain the oracles of the literary prophets.

Contents

The Former Prophets are the books of Joshua, Judges, Samuel (counted as one book) and Kings (one book), narrating Israel's history from the conquest of Canaan through the Babylonian exile. The Latter Prophets comprise Isaiah, Jeremiah and Ezekiel (the "Major Prophets") and the Twelve Minor Prophets — Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi — counted as a single book, the Trei Asar ("The Twelve"). In total the Nevi'im count as eight books in the traditional Jewish reckoning of 24 books of the Tanakh.

Use in worship

Selections from the Nevi'im, called the haftarah, are read publicly in synagogue after the Torah reading on Sabbaths, festivals and fast days, chosen for thematic links to the Torah portion or the occasion. In the Christian Old Testament the same books are rearranged: the Former Prophets fall among the historical books, and the Latter Prophets form the concluding section, following the Writings.
