Free Cinema

Free Cinema was a British documentary film movement that emerged in the 1950s. Its manifesto, published in 1956 by Lindsay Anderson, Karel Reisz, Tony Richardson, and Lorenza Mazzetti, declared that "no film can be too personal", rejecting commercial formulas in favour of small-scale, poetic films about everyday working-class life.

Programmes and films

Between 1956 and 1959 the group presented six programmes of short films at the National Film Theatre in London. Key works included Anderson's O Dreamland (1953) and Every Day Except Christmas (1957), Reisz and Richardson's Momma Don't Allow (1956), and Reisz's We Are the Lambeth Boys (1958). Many of the films were sponsored by the BFI Experimental Film Fund or industrial patrons such as the Ford Motor Company.

Legacy

Free Cinema laid the groundwork for the British New Wave of the early 1960s, in which Anderson, Reisz, and Richardson directed landmark features such as Saturday Night and Sunday Morning and This Sporting Life. Its observational, location-shot style influenced documentary practice internationally.
