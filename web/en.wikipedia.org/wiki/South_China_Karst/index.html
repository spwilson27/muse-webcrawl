South China Karst

The South China Karst is a UNESCO World Heritage Site spread over the provinces of Guangxi, Guizhou, and Yunnan in southern China, extended in 2014 to include areas in Chongqing. It protects some of the world's most spectacular examples of humid tropical to subtropical karst landscapes, covering more than 97,000 hectares in its component sites.

Description

The site is a serial property. Phase I (2007) comprised the Shilin (Stone Forest) in Yunnan, Libo Karst in Guizhou, and Wulong Karst in Chongqing. Phase II (2014) added Guilin Karst in Guangxi, Shibing Karst in Guizhou, Jinfoshan in Chongqing, and Huanjiang Karst in Guangxi.

Geology

The karsts developed in thick Devonian-to-Triassic carbonate rocks uplifted with the Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau. Their diversity — pinnacle stone forests at Shilin, cone karst at Libo, tower karst at Guilin, and the giant dolines, natural bridges, and gorges of Wulong — illustrates karst evolution under different climates and degrees of uplift. The landscapes support rich biodiversity and striking scenery along rivers such as the Li.
