Digital radiography

Image: Digital chest radiograph

Digital radiography is a form of radiography that uses x-ray–sensitive plates or detectors to capture data during an examination and immediately transfer it to a computer, instead of using photographic film or intermediate cassette systems. The resulting digital images can be enhanced, stored in picture archiving and communication systems (PACS), and transmitted electronically.

Types

Computed radiography (CR) uses photostimulable phosphor plates in cassettes that are scanned by a laser reader after exposure — a transitional technology retaining film-like workflow. Direct digital radiography (DR) uses flat-panel detectors: indirect detectors convert x-rays to light with a scintillator (such as caesium iodide) before photodiodes read the image, while direct detectors use a photoconductor such as amorphous selenium to convert x-rays directly to electrical charge. Portable flat panels allow wireless bedside imaging.

Advantages

Digital systems offer a wider dynamic range than film, immediate image availability, post-processing (windowing, magnification, measurement), lower repeat rates, and potential for reduced patient dose. Disadvantages include high equipment cost and, with CR, intermediate image quality compared with modern DR detectors.
