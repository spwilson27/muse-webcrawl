Marcus Samuel (1853–1927)

Marcus Samuel, 1st Viscount Bearsted (5 November 1853 – 17 January 1927), known as Sir Marcus Samuel between 1898 and 1921, was a British businessman who founded the Shell Transport and Trading Company, a forerunner of Royal Dutch Shell, one of the world's largest oil companies.

Early life and the shell trade

Samuel was born in Whitechapel, London, into a Jewish merchant family. His father ran M. Samuel & Co., importing decorative seashells from the Far East for the Victorian shell-box trade — the origin of the "Shell" name. Marcus expanded the firm's trading interests in Japan and the Far East, dealing in rice, silk, coal, and machinery.

Oil business

Recognising the potential of Russian oil from Baku, Samuel commissioned a fleet of specially designed tankers able to pass through the Suez Canal — the first, the Murex, sailed in 1892 — allowing him to ship kerosene cheaply to Asian markets and break the Standard Oil monopoly. In 1897 he founded the Shell Transport and Trading Company. A price war and rivalry with Royal Dutch Petroleum followed; in 1907 the two merged their interests in a 60:40 alliance, creating the Royal Dutch Shell Group, with Samuel's company as the British partner.

Public life

Samuel served as a Sheriff of the City of London, was knighted in 1898, and was Lord Mayor of London in 1902–03. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Bearsted in 1921 and promoted to Viscount Bearsted in 1925. A benefactor of Jewish causes and of his Kent estate at Maidstone (The Mote), he died in 1927 and was succeeded by his son Walter.
