The Aberfan disaster was the catastrophic collapse of a colliery spoil tip on 21 October 1966 in the Welsh village of Aberfan, near Merthyr Tydfil, Glamorgan. The tip had been created on a mountain slope above the village, and a period of heavy rain caused it to slide downhill as a slurry.

Disaster

At 9:15 a.m., more than 150,000 cubic metres of water-saturated debris flowed downhill at speed. It destroyed a farm and twenty terraced houses before burying Pantglas Junior School and part of the neighbouring secondary school. A total of 144 people were killed, 116 of them children, most aged between seven and ten.

Inquiry and aftermath

A tribunal of inquiry blamed the National Coal Board for the disaster, finding that the tip had been built over a spring whose existence the Board had known about. Despite this, no NCB official was prosecuted or dismissed. The disaster led to new legislation on mine and quarry tips, and the remaining tips above Aberfan were eventually removed.
