Saudi-led intervention in the Yemeni civil war

The Saudi-led intervention in Yemen, codenamed Operation Decisive Storm and later Operation Restoring Hope, began on 26 March 2015, when a coalition of Arab states led by Saudi Arabia launched air strikes and later ground operations in Yemen. The intervention aimed to restore the internationally recognised government of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, which had been driven from the capital Sanaa by the Houthi (Ansar Allah) movement in 2014–15.

Background

Yemen's crisis deepened after the 2011 uprising forced out President Ali Abdullah Saleh. The Houthis, a Zaidi Shia movement from northern Yemen allied initially with Saleh, seized Sanaa in September 2014 and advanced on Aden in March 2015, prompting Hadi to flee to Saudi Arabia and request military help. Saudi Arabia, viewing the Houthis as an Iranian proxy, assembled a coalition including the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar (until 2017), Egypt, Jordan, Morocco and Sudan, with intelligence, logistics and arms support from the United States, United Kingdom and France.

Course of the war

Coalition air power and allied Yemeni forces retook Aden and the south in 2015, but the front lines around Taiz, Marib and the Red Sea port of Hodeidah hardened into stalemate. The UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council complicated the anti-Houthi camp, seizing Aden in 2019. A UN-brokered truce in April 2022 greatly reduced fighting, and Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping from late 2023 drew US and British strikes.

Humanitarian impact and criticism

The war produced what the UN called the world's worst humanitarian crisis, with widespread hunger, cholera and displacement; UN estimates put total deaths, direct and indirect, at around 377,000 by the end of 2021. Coalition air strikes that hit markets, weddings, funerals and a school bus in Dahyan (2018) drew international condemnation and legal scrutiny of Western arms sales, while the Houthis were also accused of serious abuses. A blockade and restrictions on Yemeni ports and airspace were criticised for deepening civilian suffering.
