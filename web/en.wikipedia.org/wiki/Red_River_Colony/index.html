The Red River Colony (or Selkirk Settlement) was a colonisation project set up in 1811 by Thomas Douglas, 5th Earl of Selkirk, on land in Rupert's Land granted to him by the Hudson's Bay Company. The grant, called Assiniboia, covered some 300,000 km² (116,000 sq mi) of the Red River valley in what is now southern Manitoba and northern North Dakota and Minnesota. Selkirk planned to resettle impoverished Scottish crofters displaced by the Highland Clearances; the first settlers arrived in 1812 and founded a community at the forks of the Red and Assiniboine rivers, the site of present-day Winnipeg.

Conflict and hardship

The colony collided with the rival North West Company and with the Métis, for whom the Red River was a homeland and whose pemmican trade supplied the fur brigades. When Governor Miles Macdonell banned pemmican exports in 1814 (the Pemmican Proclamation), tensions escalated into the Pemmican War: Métis under Cuthbert Grant destroyed the colony's Fort Douglas and killed Governor Robert Semple and 20 others at the Battle of Seven Oaks (1816). Selkirk arrived with hired soldiers, restored the settlement, and made treaties with the local Cree and Saulteaux in 1817. Floods (notably 1826), grasshoppers, frosts and isolation repeatedly devastated the colony's agriculture.

Growth and outcome

After the merger of the Hudson's Bay and North West Companies in 1821, the colony settled into a quiet existence around Fort Garry, governed by the Council of Assiniboia. Its population grew to include retired fur-trade families, Swiss and German mercenaries' descendants (the De Meurons), and French-speaking Métis centred on St. Boniface. The colony's grievances against Company rule and its demand for responsible government culminated in the Red River Resistance of 1869–70 under Louis Riel, after which the settlement became the nucleus of the new province of Manitoba in 1870.
