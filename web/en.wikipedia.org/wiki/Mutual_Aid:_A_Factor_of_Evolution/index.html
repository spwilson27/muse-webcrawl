Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution

Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution is a 1902 collection of essays by the Russian anarchist and naturalist Peter Kropotkin. Originally published as a series of articles in the British journal The Nineteenth Century between 1890 and 1896, the work argues that cooperation and mutual aid — rather than competition alone — are major driving factors in the evolution of animal and human societies.

Background

Kropotkin wrote the essays in response to the essay "The Struggle for Existence in Human Society" (1888) by Thomas Henry Huxley, nicknamed "Darwin's bulldog", which portrayed nature as a gladiatorial arena of relentless competition. Drawing on his own zoological observations in Siberia and Manchuria in the 1860s, Kropotkin reported finding remarkably little of the bitter intraspecific struggle that Darwin and especially the "Social Darwinists" had emphasized, and a great deal of cooperation within species.

Contents

The essays proceed from animal societies to human history:

  • Mutual aid among animals — insects, birds, and mammals that hunt, migrate, rear young, and defend themselves cooperatively.
  • Mutual aid among "savages" — clan and tribal organization among indigenous peoples.
  • Mutual aid among the "barbarians" — the village community and its communal institutions.
  • Mutual aid in the medieval city — guilds, communes, and federations of free cities.
  • Mutual aid amongst ourselves — the persistence of cooperative practices (friendly societies, village customs, trade unions) in modern society despite the individualism promoted by the state.

Reception and influence

The book became a foundational text of anarchist communism and a classic of popular evolutionary writing. Later evolutionary biologists have treated its central claim — that cooperation is an important evolutionary force — as prescient, anticipating modern research on kin selection, reciprocal altruism, and multilevel selection, although Kropotkin's broader social conclusions remain contested. The book has never been out of print for long and continues to be reissued, including a notable 1955 edition with a foreword by Ashley Montagu.
