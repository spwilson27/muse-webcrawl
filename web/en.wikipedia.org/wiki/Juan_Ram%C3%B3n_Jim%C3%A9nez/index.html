Juan Ramón Jiménez

Juan Ramón Jiménez Mantecón (23 December 1881 – 29 May 1958) was a Spanish poet who received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1956 "for his lyrical poetry, which in Spanish language constitutes an example of high spirit and artistical purity". A leading figure of modernismo and pure poetry, he is best known for the prose-poem Platero y yo (Platero and I, 1914).

Life

Born in Moguer, Jiménez studied at Seville and was encouraged by Rubén Darío. Periods of depression led to sanatorium stays in France and Madrid, where he became central to literary life at the Residencia de Estudiantes, mentoring Generation of '27 poets. In 1916 he married Zenobia Camprubí, translator of Tagore and his lifelong collaborator.

Exile and Nobel Prize

Leaving Spain at the outbreak of the Civil War in 1936, he lived in Cuba, the United States, Argentina, and Puerto Rico, teaching at universities. His work evolved through phases often described as sensuous, intellectual, and metaphysical/sufficient, seeking "pure poetry" stripped of ornament. He learned of the Nobel Prize days before Zenobia's death in 1956 and died in San Juan in 1958; both are buried in Moguer.
