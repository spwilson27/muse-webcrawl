CIA triad

The CIA triad is a model designed to guide policies for information security within an organization. The model is also sometimes referred to as the AIC triad (availability, integrity and confidentiality) to avoid confusion with the Central Intelligence Agency. The elements of the triad are considered the three most crucial components of information security:

  • Confidentiality — information is accessible only to those authorized to have access; it is roughly equivalent to privacy and involves measures such as authentication, access control, and encryption.
  • Integrity — data is accurate, complete, and trustworthy throughout its lifecycle, and cannot be modified in an unauthorized or undetected manner; it involves file permissions, checksums, hashes, and version control.
  • Availability — information and systems are accessible and usable upon demand by an authorized entity; it involves redundancy, backups, failover, and protection against denial-of-service attacks.

History

The concepts are old — Julius Caesar's cipher protected confidentiality, and the 1976 Diffie–Hellman paper discussed the three properties — but the triad as a named model crystallized in the information security literature of the 1980s and 1990s. A 1977 NIST publication on audit controls and the 1985 Department of Defense "Orange Book" (Trusted Computer System Evaluation Criteria) addressed the three goals, and the triad became a standard teaching model in security certifications such as CISSP.

Criticism and extensions

Commentators have argued the triad is an oversimplification: real security programs also address authenticity, non-repudiation, and accountability (sometimes added to form the Parkerian hexad, proposed by Donn Parker in 1998, which adds possession/control, authenticity, and utility). Others note that the triad says little about how to balance trade-offs between its components — for example, that aggressive confidentiality controls can reduce availability. Nevertheless, the CIA triad remains the most widely used foundational model in information security.
