The 2nd Foreign Parachute Regiment (French: 2e Régiment étranger de parachutistes, 2e REP) is an airborne infantry regiment of the French Foreign Legion and the only airborne regiment of the Legion. It is one of the most decorated units of the French Army and serves as the spearhead parachute regiment of the 11th Parachute Brigade, based at Camp Raffalli in Calvi, Corsica, since 1967.

History

The regiment traces its lineage to the Foreign Parachute Battalions formed in Indochina in 1948. The 2nd Foreign Parachute Battalion fought throughout the First Indochina War, including at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu in 1954, where it was destroyed. The regiment was reconstituted in Algeria on 1 December 1955 and fought through the Algerian War; elements sympathised with the 1961 putsch, after which the sister 1st Foreign Parachute Regiment was disbanded, leaving the 2e REP as the Legion's sole parachute regiment.

Notable operations

In May 1978 the regiment made a combat jump into Kolwezi, Zaire, rescuing European hostages held by Katangese rebels — an operation that became a byword for rapid airborne intervention. Since then the regiment has deployed repeatedly to Chad, Lebanon, the Balkans, Afghanistan, Mali and the Central African Republic, frequently making operational parachute jumps. Its specialist companies are each trained for a particular environment: mountain, desert, amphibious/urban, and sniping and demolitions (the GCP commandos).

Organisation

The regiment numbers roughly 1,300 legionnaires organised into a headquarters company, an administration and support company, four combat companies with distinct terrain specialisations, a reconnaissance and support company, and a reserve company. Recruitment and selection follow the Legion's standard pattern, with airborne training conducted after basic training at Castelnaudary.
