Tamil Eelam

Tamil Eelam (Tamil: தமிழீழம், Tamiḻīḻam) is the proposed independent state that many Sri Lankan Tamils aspired to create in the north and east of Sri Lanka. The name derives from Eelam, an ancient Tamil name for the island. Large parts of the North-East Province were administered de facto by the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE) from 1990 to 2009, during the Sri Lankan Civil War.

Background

Demands for a separate Tamil state grew after Ceylon's independence in 1948, amid measures such as the Sinhala Only Act of 1956 and episodes of anti-Tamil violence including the 1983 Black July riots. The 1976 Vaddukoddai Resolution by the Tamil United Liberation Front formally called for a sovereign Tamil Eelam. Armed conflict began in 1983 and lasted 26 years.

De facto state

At its height in the mid-2000s, the LTTE administered about 15,000 km² with its own police, courts, bank, and civil administration, with Kilinochchi as its administrative centre. The civil war ended in May 2009 with the military defeat of the LTTE and the death of its leader Velupillai Prabhakaran.

Legacy

Since 2009, advocacy for Tamil self-determination has continued politically in Sri Lanka and in the Tamil diaspora; a "Transnational Government of Tamil Eelam" was formed in exile in 2010. No country has recognized Tamil Eelam as an independent state.
