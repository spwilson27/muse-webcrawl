The Rugby Championship is an annual international rugby union competition contested by the national teams of Argentina, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, organised by SANZAAR. It is the Southern Hemisphere's premier international tournament, the counterpart to Europe's Six Nations Championship.

History

The competition began in 1996 as the Tri Nations, following the advent of professional rugby and the formation of the SANZAR partnership between South Africa, New Zealand and Australia. Each team played the others home and away. Argentina's Pumas joined in 2012 after years of strong World Cup performances, and the tournament was renamed The Rugby Championship. New Zealand's All Blacks have dominated the competition, winning the large majority of editions; South Africa and Australia have won several titles each, while Argentina recorded its first wins over all three rivals during the 2010s and 2020s.

Format

In a standard year each team plays the others twice (home and away), giving six matches per team. Points are awarded on the usual rugby system — four for a win, two for a draw, with bonus points for scoring four or more tries or losing by seven points or fewer. The Bledisloe Cup (Australia–New Zealand), Freedom Cup (New Zealand–South Africa) and Mandela Challenge Plate (Australia–South Africa) are contested within the championship. In Rugby World Cup years a shortened format is used.
