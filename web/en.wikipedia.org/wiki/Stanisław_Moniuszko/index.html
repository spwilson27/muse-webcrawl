Stanisław Moniuszko (5 May 1819 – 4 June 1872) was a Polish composer, conductor and teacher. He wrote many popular art songs and operas, and is often regarded as the founder of Polish national opera. His best-known works are the operas Halka and Straszny dwór (The Haunted Manor).

Life

Moniuszko was born near Minsk, in what was then the Russian Empire, into a Polish–Lithuanian noble family. He studied music in Warsaw and then in Berlin, where his teachers included Carl Friedrich Rungenhagen. Returning to the partitioned Polish lands, he worked as an organist and music teacher in Vilnius (Wilno), where he became involved in Polish cultural life. In 1858 he moved to Warsaw, where the successful expanded version of Halka premiered that year, and he became a conductor at the Grand Theatre and a professor at the Warsaw Conservatory.

Works

Moniuszko's output includes some 20 operas and operettas, among them Flis (The Raftsman), Hrabina (The Countess), Verbum nobile, and Paria. His opera Halka (first performed in concert in 1848, staged in 1854, and in its definitive four-act version in 1858) tells of a peasant girl abandoned by a nobleman and is celebrated for dances such as the mazur and polonaise that draw on Polish folk idioms. Straszny dwór (1865) had a troubled history with Russian censorship because of its patriotic themes.

Beyond opera, Moniuszko composed more than 300 songs, collected in his Śpiewniki domowe (Songbooks for Home Use), as well as sacred music including masses and the cantatas Sonety krymskie (Crimean Sonnets).

Legacy

Moniuszko's music, blending Polish folk melody with European operatic forms, became a cornerstone of Poland's nineteenth-century cultural identity under foreign partition. The Grand Theatre in Warsaw and numerous institutions bear his name, and an international vocal competition named after him is held in Warsaw.
