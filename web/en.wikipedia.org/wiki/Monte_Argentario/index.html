Monte Argentario is a municipality and a mountainous peninsula in the province of Grosseto, Tuscany, Italy. The promontory was once an island before silting and two sandbars (tomboli) joined it to the mainland, enclosing the Orbetello lagoon. Its highest point, Monte Telegrafo, rises to 635 metres.

Settlements

The two main towns are Porto Santo Stefano, the municipal seat on the northern shore, and Porto Ercole on the eastern shore, both fishing ports that have become popular resorts and marinas. The painter Caravaggio died at Porto Ercole in 1610 while travelling north from Naples.

History

The Argentario was likely an Etruscan and then Roman possession. From 1557 to 1801 it formed part of the Spanish Stato dei Presidi (State of the Presidi), a cluster of coastal fortresses controlled from Naples, and the headland retains Spanish-era watchtowers and fortresses, including the Forte Filippo and Forte Santa Caterina at Porto Ercole and the Rocca at Porto Santo Stefano.

Tourism

The peninsula is a well-known sailing and holiday area, with coves, beaches, and the ferry connections from Porto Santo Stefano to the islands of Giglio and Giannutri. The wreck of the cruise ship Costa Concordia off Giglio in 2012 brought the area international attention. Inland, the Convent of the Passionists, founded in the 18th century, sits below the summit of Monte Telegrafo.
