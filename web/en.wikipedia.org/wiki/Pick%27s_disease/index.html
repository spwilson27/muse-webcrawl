TypeFrontotemporal dementia (tauopathy)

Pick's disease, or Pick syndrome, is a type of frontotemporal dementia, a neurodegenerative disease characterised by progressive changes in personality, behaviour, and language, together with shrinkage (atrophy) of the frontal and temporal lobes of the brain.

Pathology

The disease is named after Czech psychiatrist Arnold Pick, who described patients with the condition in the 1890s. Its hallmarks are ballooned neurons ("Pick cells") and spherical inclusions of tau protein ("Pick bodies") in affected neurons, classifying it among the tauopathies. Some cases are associated with mutations in the MAPT gene encoding tau.

Course

Unlike Alzheimer's disease, memory may be relatively spared early on, while social conduct, empathy, and speech deteriorate first. There is no cure; treatment is supportive, and survival after diagnosis is typically six to ten years.
