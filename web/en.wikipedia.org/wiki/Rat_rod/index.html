Rat rod

A rat rod is a style of custom car or truck that deliberately imitates — and often exaggerates — the appearance of the early hot rods of the 1940s, 1950s, and 1960s. Rat rods are typically unfinished in appearance: rusty or primer-coated bodywork, mismatched parts, and exposed mechanical components are celebrated rather than concealed.

Characteristics

Common features include chopped and channelled vintage bodies, matte or rusted finishes, exposed engines, improvised interiors made from found objects, and a general "built not bought" ethos assembled from junkyard and swap-meet parts. Unlike traditional show-quality hot rods, rat rods prioritize attitude, creativity, and low cost over polished paint and chrome.

History and culture

The term emerged in the 1990s within the hot rod scene, initially sometimes used pejoratively for rough, unfinished cars, before being embraced as a distinct subculture. Builders associated with the style emphasize driving the cars rather than displaying them, and rat rod meets often welcome vehicles that would be excluded from concours-style events. Critics within the hobby sometimes question the safety of extremely rough builds, while defenders see the style as a return to hot rodding's do-it-yourself roots.
