Gaohu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The gaohu (高胡; pinyin: gāohú, "high hu") is a Chinese bowed string instrument developed in the 1920s by the musician and composer Lu Wencheng. It was created to serve as the alto voice in the modern Chinese orchestra, filling the register between the erhu and the cello-like gehu.

Description

The gaohu resembles a larger erhu: a two-stringed spike fiddle with a snakeskin-covered sound box, traditionally round or octagonal and bigger than that of the erhu, giving a warmer, rounder tone. It is tuned a fourth or fifth higher than the erhu, typically to G–D or A–E, and has a brighter, louder sound suited to Cantonese music, in which it is a leading instrument, as well as to the Chinese orchestral repertoire.

Lu Wencheng developed the instrument while composing for Cantonese ensembles and used steel strings and a larger resonator to increase projection. In the modern Chinese orchestra the gaohu section plays the alto lines, though folk practice varies regionally, and the zhonghu ("middle hu") serves a similar role in some ensembles.
