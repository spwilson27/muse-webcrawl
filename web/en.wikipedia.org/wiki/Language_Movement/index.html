Bengali Language Movement

Date:1948–1956; climax 21 February 1952

The Bengali Language Movement (Bengali: ভাষা আন্দোলন, Bhasha Andolon) was a political movement in former East Bengal (renamed East Pakistan in 1956) advocating the recognition of the Bengali language as a state language of the then-Dominion of Pakistan, in order to allow its use in government affairs, education and media, and to preserve its writing in the Bengali script.

Background and the events of 1952

When Pakistan was created in 1947, the government in Karachi moved to declare Urdu — spoken as a mother tongue by only a small minority, while Bengali was spoken by the majority of Pakistan's population — the sole state language. Protests began immediately among Bengali students and intellectuals. The movement reached its climax on 21 February 1952, when police opened fire on student demonstrators defying a ban on assemblies in Dhaka, killing several, among them Abdus Salam, Rafiq Uddin Ahmed, Abdul Jabbar, Shafiur Rahman and Abul Barkat. The day is commemorated as Language Martyrs' Day (Shaheed Dibosh), marked by the Shaheed Minar monument in Dhaka.

Legacy

Bengali was finally recognised as a state language alongside Urdu in Pakistan's 1956 constitution. The movement is regarded as the seed of Bengali nationalism that led to the creation of Bangladesh in 1971. In honour of the movement, UNESCO proclaimed 21 February as International Mother Language Day in 1999, observed worldwide to promote linguistic and cultural diversity.
