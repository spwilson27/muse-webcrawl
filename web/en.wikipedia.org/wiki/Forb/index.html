Forb

A forb or phorb is a herbaceous flowering plant that is not a graminoid (grass, sedge, or rush). The term is used in plant biology and in vegetation ecology, especially in relation to grasslands, prairies, and rangeland management.

Usage

Forbs represent a guild of plant species with broadly similar growth form, which in ecology is often more important than the specific taxonomic relationships. They are used in ecology to distinguish plant functional types in grassland communities, and in rangeland science to describe the broad-leaved herbaceous component of forage available to livestock and wildlife. Examples of forbs include sunflowers, clovers (though clovers are also legumes), milkweeds, goldenrods, and most wildflowers found in prairies.

Etymology

The term forb derives from the Greek phorbḗ (φορβή), meaning "pasture" or "fodder". Its spelling with an "f" is influenced by the word herb. The term has been in use in American ecology since at least the 1920s, popularised through the work of prairie ecologists studying the tallgrass prairie.
