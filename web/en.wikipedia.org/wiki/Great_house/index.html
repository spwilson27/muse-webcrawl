Great house

TypePlantation house / large country house
RegionsCaribbean, Ireland, North America

A great house is a large and stately house, most commonly the term used for the owner's house on a plantation in the Caribbean and parts of North America, where it served as the residence of the planter family and the administrative centre of the estate. The term is also used for large country houses in Ireland and Jamaica's sugar estates in particular.

Caribbean plantation houses

On sugar plantations in colonies such as Jamaica and Barbados, the great house stood apart from the slave quarters and works (mill, boiling house, curing house). Built of stone or timber with wide verandas, jalousied windows, and raised ground floors to catch breezes and resist hurricanes, notable Jamaican examples include Rose Hall, Greenwood, and Good Hope. In the antebellum United States, the equivalent planter's residence on large slave plantations is generally called a plantation house or "big house".

Ireland

In Ireland, the term "great house" (or "big house") describes the country seats of the Anglo-Irish landed ascendancy, the subject of a substantial literature on their decline during the land wars and revolutionary period.
