Rashtrapati Bhavan

Rashtrapati Bhavan (Hindi: राष्ट्रपति भवन, "President's House") is the official residence of the President of India, located at the western end of Kartavya Path (formerly Rajpath) in New Delhi. Designed by the British architect Edwin Lutyens as the Viceroy's House during the construction of New Delhi, it was completed in 1929 and is one of the largest presidential residences in the world, set in a 320-acre estate.

Architecture

Lutyens designed the palace in a grand classical idiom infused with Indian elements: the great dome is modeled on the Buddhist stupa at Sanchi, while chhatris, jaalis (pierced screens), and chujjas (overhanging eaves) soften the imperial classicism. The building contains some 340 rooms on four floors across about 19,000 m², including the ceremonial Durbar Hall beneath the dome and the Ashoka Hall with its painted ceiling. Herbert Baker designed the flanking Secretariat buildings and the sloping approach, which partially obscured the palace from India Gate — a compromise Lutyens bitterly called his "Bakerloo."

History

The first Viceroy to occupy the house was Lord Irwin in 1929. After independence in 1947 it became Government House of the Governor-General — C. Rajagopalachari, the last holder of that office, chose to live in a smaller guest suite — and in 1950, with the republic, it took its present name under President Rajendra Prasad.

The estate

The estate includes the celebrated Mughal Gardens, renamed Amrit Udyan in 2023, opened to the public each spring, and stables, staff quarters, and offices that make the complex a small city. Ceremonial events include the Changing of the Guard and state banquets for visiting heads of state.
