Book of Ingenious Devices
Authors: Banū Mūsā brothers
Date: c. 850 CE, Baghdad
Subject: Mechanical devices, automata

The Book of Ingenious Devices (Arabic: Kitāb al-Ḥiyal) is a large illustrated work on mechanical devices written in the 9th century by the Banū Mūsā brothers — Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad, Abū al-Qāsim Aḥmad and Al-Ḥasan — Persian scholars working in Baghdad under the Abbasid Caliphate.

Contents

It describes about 100 devices, including trick vessels, automatic fountains, lamps that trim themselves, a gas mask, a clamshell grab, and water dispensers using feedback control and conical valves.

Significance

The work built on Hellenistic sources such as Hero of Alexandria and Philo of Byzantium, while adding original innovations. It is a landmark in the history of engineering and automata.
