Kodak Brownie

MakerEastman Kodak Company
IntroducedFebruary 1900
Original price$1
TypeBox camera

The Brownie was a long-running series of simple, inexpensive cameras made by the Eastman Kodak Company in the United States. The first model, introduced in February 1900, was a simple cardboard box camera with a meniscus lens that cost only one dollar. The Brownie popularised low-cost photography and the concept of the snapshot, putting a camera in the hands of millions of ordinary people, including children.

History

The original Brownie, designed by Frank A. Brownell, took 2¼-inch square pictures on 117 roll film. Its name invoked the popular "Brownie" elf characters of Palmer Cox's stories, and the camera was marketed heavily toward children. Successive models — the No. 2 Brownie (1901), folding Brownies, and later Bakelite and plastic versions such as the Brownie Hawkeye (1949) — sold in enormous numbers for over half a century.

Significance

By separating picture-taking from processing — customers sent the camera or film to Kodak for developing, under the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest" — the Brownie created mass amateur photography. Brownies documented everyday life, family occasions and both World Wars, and remain iconic examples of industrial design.
