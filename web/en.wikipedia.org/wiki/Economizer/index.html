Economizer

An economizer (or economiser) is a mechanical device intended to reduce energy consumption, or to perform useful functions such as preheating a fluid. The term is used for devices in power plants, boilers, heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, and refrigeration.

Boiler economizers

In steam boilers, an economizer is a heat exchanger placed in the flue-gas stream that transfers residual heat from the exhaust gases to the boiler feedwater before it enters the steam drum. Raising the feedwater temperature reduces the fuel needed to produce steam and improves overall boiler efficiency; a commonly cited rule of thumb is that each 22 °C (40 °F) reduction in flue-gas temperature raises efficiency by about 1 percent. Economizers also reduce thermal shock in the boiler by narrowing the temperature difference between feedwater and drum water. Non-condensing economizers keep flue gases above the acid dew point to avoid corrosion, while condensing economizers deliberately extract latent heat from water vapour in the exhaust.

HVAC economizers

In building air-conditioning, an air-side economizer uses cool outdoor air for "free cooling" when outside conditions are suitable, reducing or eliminating mechanical refrigeration. A water-side economizer circulates condenser water through a heat exchanger to cool building water directly. Energy codes such as ASHRAE 90.1 require economizers on many commercial cooling systems.

Other uses

In engines and refrigeration, economizers recover waste heat or use flash gas to improve efficiency; in carburettors, an economizer valve enriches or leans the fuel mixture according to load.
