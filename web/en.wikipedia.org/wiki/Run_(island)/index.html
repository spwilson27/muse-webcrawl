Run (island)

Run (Indonesian: Pulau Run) is one of the smallest islands in the Banda Islands archipelago in the Maluku Islands of Indonesia. Despite its size — roughly 3 km long and less than 1 km wide — it played an outsized role in the seventeenth-century spice trade.

History

In the early 1600s Run was one of the world's only sources of nutmeg and mace, spices then worth more than their weight in gold in Europe. The English East India Company established a presence on the island in 1616, making it effectively England's first overseas colony claim, administered by Captain Nathaniel Courthope, who defended it against Dutch attacks until his death in 1620.

The Dutch East India Company (VOC) eventually seized control of the Banda Islands, committing massacres of the Bandanese population in 1621. Under the Treaty of Breda (1667), which ended the Second Anglo-Dutch War, the English formally relinquished their claim to Run in exchange for the Dutch colony of New Netherland in North America — including New Amsterdam, which became New York. Run is thus often described as having been "traded for Manhattan."

Geography

Run lies in the Banda Sea, west of the main island of Banda Besar. Its economy historically rested on nutmeg cultivation, and nutmeg trees still grow on the island today.
