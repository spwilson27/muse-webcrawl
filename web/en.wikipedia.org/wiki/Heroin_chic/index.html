Heroin chic

Heroin chic is a fashion aesthetic of the mid-1990s characterised by pale skin, dark eye circles, angular features and an emaciated, waif-like look, associated in the media with heroin use and a grunge-influenced reaction against the healthy supermodel look of the 1980s.

Origins

Photographers such as Corinne Day, Davide Sorrenti and Juergen Teller and campaigns for Calvin Klein featuring Kate Moss epitomised the style. It drew on underground and grunge culture.

Criticism and decline

The look was condemned for glamourising drug use and eating disorders; U.S. President Bill Clinton criticised it in 1997, the year Sorrenti died of a heroin overdose. Fashion shifted toward a healthier, more glamorous image by the end of the decade, though the aesthetic periodically resurfaces and remains debated.
