Purkinje images

Purkinje images are reflections of objects from the surface of the cornea and the surfaces of the lens of the eye, first described by Jan Evangelista Purkyně. They are also known as Purkinje–Sanson images after Purkyně and Louis Joseph Sanson. At least four Purkinje images are usually visible, conventionally labelled P1 to P4.

The four images

  • P1 — reflection from the outer (anterior) surface of the cornea; the brightest image, upright. It is the corneal reflection or "glint" used by video-based eye trackers.
  • P2 — reflection from the inner (posterior) surface of the cornea; much smaller and dimmer, nearly coincident with P1.
  • P3 — reflection from the anterior surface of the lens; dim and diffuse, upright.
  • P4 — reflection from the posterior surface of the lens; inverted, since the back of the lens acts as a concave mirror.

Uses

Tracking the positions of Purkinje images allows measurement of eye movements with high precision: dual-Purkinje-image trackers compare P1 and P4, whose relative displacement signals rotation of the eye independent of head translation. Measuring the images' sizes and separations (phakometry) permits in-vivo estimation of corneal curvature, lens surface curvatures and lens position, and changes in P3/P4 during focusing reveal the mechanism of accommodation. Keratometers and corneal topographers rely on the first Purkinje image.

Related phenomena

Purkyně also described entoptic phenomena including the Purkinje tree — the shadow of the retinal blood vessels seen when light is shone through the sclera — and the Purkinje effect on colour sensitivity. Not to be confused with Purkinje cells of the cerebellum or Purkinje fibers of the heart, the same scientist's other namesakes.
