Ellen Stewart

Ellen Stewart (November 7, 1919 – January 13, 2011) was an American theatre director and producer, founder of La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club in New York City, one of the most influential institutions of the Off-Off-Broadway movement. Affectionately known as "Mama", she nurtured generations of playwrights, directors and performers, many of whom became central figures in modern theatre.

Early life

Stewart was born in Alexandria, Louisiana, and moved to New York, where she worked as a fashion designer on Saks Fifth Avenue. In 1961, hoping to help playwright friends stage their work, she opened Café La MaMa in a small East Village basement, funding it with her design salary.

La MaMa

La MaMa grew from its basement origins into a complex on East Fourth Street with multiple theatres and an annex, and became a home for experimental work by artists including Sam Shepard, Lanford Wilson, Harvey Fierstein, Tom Eyen, and the director Tom O'Horgan, whose productions of Hair began at La MaMa before moving to Broadway. Stewart championed international theatre, touring companies abroad and bringing troupes from Poland, Japan, Korea and elsewhere to New York; she also presented works in non-English languages and productions based on Greek classics. She personally introduced performances by ringing a cowbell, a signature ritual.

Recognition

Stewart received a MacArthur Fellowship ("genius grant") in 1985 and numerous other honours, including induction into the Theater Hall of Fame. She died in New York in 2011, aged 91; La MaMa continues under her successors.
