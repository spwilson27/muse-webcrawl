The Kalka–Shimla Railway is a 2 ft 6 in (762 mm) narrow-gauge railway in North India which traverses a mostly mountainous route from Kalka to Shimla in Himachal Pradesh. It is known for its dramatic views of the hills and surrounding villages, and its engineering feats including 102 tunnels and 864 bridges.

History

Built between 1898 and 1903 under the direction of chief engineer H. S. Harrington, the line served Shimla, then the summer capital of British India. The longest tunnel, at Barog, is associated with the engineer Colonel Barog. The railway climbed from 656 m at Kalka to 2,075 m at Shimla.

Heritage status

In 2008 the line was inscribed by UNESCO as part of the Mountain Railways of India World Heritage Site, alongside the Darjeeling Himalayan and Nilgiri Mountain railways. Diesel and railcar services still run the full route, popular with tourists.
