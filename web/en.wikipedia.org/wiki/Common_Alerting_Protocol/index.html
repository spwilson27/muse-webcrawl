The Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) is an XML-based data format for exchanging public warnings and emergencies between alerting technologies. Maintained by the OASIS standards consortium, CAP allows a single warning message to be consistently disseminated simultaneously over many different alerting systems — broadcast media, sirens, mobile phones, and the web — from one consistent input.

Structure

A CAP message consists of an alert segment containing the message identifier, sender, timestamp, status, and scope, plus one or more info segments describing the event's category, severity, certainty, urgency, headline, description, instructions, and the affected geographic area (which may be given as polygons, circles, or geocodes).

Adoption

CAP was adopted as an ITU-T recommendation (X.1303). It underlies the United States' Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS), through which FEMA and the National Weather Service distribute alerts to the Emergency Alert System and Wireless Emergency Alerts. Environment and Climate Change Canada, the Australian emergency services, Google Public Alerts, and the IFRC's global CAP aggregator all use the protocol.

Background

Development began after studies of disasters such as the 2000 Enschede fireworks disaster and post-9/11 reviews highlighted the fragmentation of warning systems; the Partnership for Public Warning pushed for a single standard, leading OASIS to ratify CAP 1.0 in 2004 and version 1.2 in 2010.
