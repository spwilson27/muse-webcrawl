Bird conservation is a field in the science of conservation biology related to threatened birds. Humans have had a profound effect on many bird species. Over a hundred species have gone extinct in historical times, although the most dramatic human-caused avian extinctions occurred in the Pacific Ocean as humans colonised the islands of Melanesia, Polynesia and Micronesia, during which an estimated 750–1,800 species went extinct. According to BirdLife International, around one in eight of the world's bird species is threatened with extinction.

Threats

The principal threats to birds are habitat loss and degradation, especially of tropical forests; hunting and trapping; introduced predators such as rats and cats on islands; collisions with buildings, vehicles and power lines; pesticide and lead poisoning; and climate change, which shifts ranges and disrupts the timing of migration and breeding. Seabirds are additionally threatened by longline fishing bycatch and plastic pollution.

Conservation action

Conservation measures include legal protection, the establishment of protected areas (including the network of Important Bird Areas identified by BirdLife International), habitat restoration, control or eradication of invasive species, captive breeding and reintroduction, and nest-box and supplementary feeding schemes. Celebrated successes include the recovery of the peregrine falcon after the banning of DDT, the Californian condor and the Mauritius kestrel, both brought back from the brink of extinction.

Citizen science

Because birds are conspicuous and popular, birdwatchers contribute enormous volumes of monitoring data through schemes such as the Breeding Bird Survey, the Christmas Bird Count and the eBird platform; these datasets underpin assessments of population trends and conservation priorities.
