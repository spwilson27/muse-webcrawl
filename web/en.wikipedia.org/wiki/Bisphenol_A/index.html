Bisphenol A (BPA) is a chemical compound primarily used in the manufacturing of various plastics. It is a colourless solid which is soluble in most common organic solvents, but has very poor solubility in water. BPA is produced on an industrial scale by the condensation reaction of phenol and acetone. Global production is in the millions of tonnes per year; it is a key monomer in the production of polycarbonate plastic and epoxy resins.

Uses

Polycarbonates made from BPA are used in water bottles, sports equipment, CDs and DVDs, medical devices, and eyeglass lenses. Epoxy resins containing BPA line food and beverage cans, water pipes, and are used in thermal paper for sales receipts, dental sealants, and flame retardants.

History

BPA was first synthesised in 1891 by the Russian chemist Aleksandr Dianin. In the 1930s its estrogenic properties were identified during the search for synthetic estrogens, though it was never used as a drug. Its use in plastics began in the 1950s with polycarbonate and epoxy production.

Health effects and regulation

BPA is an endocrine disruptor that can mimic estrogen. Human exposure is widespread, primarily through diet, as BPA can leach from food packaging. Scientific and regulatory assessments have differed: the U.S. FDA has maintained that current dietary exposures are safe, while the European Food Safety Authority in 2023 drastically lowered the tolerable daily intake based on immune-system effects. Many jurisdictions have banned BPA in baby bottles, and manufacturers have widely adopted "BPA-free" alternatives such as bisphenol S (BPS) and bisphenol F, though these substitutes are under study for similar endocrine activity.
