Hadean

The Hadean is the first and oldest of the four eons of Earth's history, starting with the planet's formation about 4.6 billion years ago and ending 4 billion years ago with the beginning of the Archean eon. Its name, from the Greek god Hades, refers to the hellish conditions then prevailing on Earth: a partially molten surface, frequent collisions with other planetary bodies, and a largely volcanic landscape.

Geology

No terrestrial rocks are known from most of the Hadean; the oldest dated material consists of detrital zircon grains from Jack Hills, Western Australia, up to about 4.4 billion years old, which suggest that a solid crust and liquid water oceans may have existed surprisingly early. The oldest known rock formations, such as the Acasta Gneiss of Canada (~4.03 Ga), date to the very end of the eon. The giant-impact hypothesis holds that the Moon formed from debris after a Mars-sized body, Theia, collided with the early Earth roughly 4.5 billion years ago.

Atmosphere and oceans

The early atmosphere, formed by outgassing and volatile delivery, likely contained abundant carbon dioxide, nitrogen, and water vapour, with little free oxygen. Evidence from zircon oxygen isotopes has been interpreted to suggest a "cool early Earth" episode with surface water as early as 4.4 billion years ago, though the eon also includes the Late Heavy Bombardment of impacts around 4.1–3.8 billion years ago.

Origin of life

Whether life began during the Hadean is unresolved. Carbon isotope signatures in Jack Hills zircons dated to about 4.1 billion years ago have been claimed as possible evidence of biogenic carbon, though the interpretation is contested.
