Garlic chives

KingdomPlantae
FamilyAmaryllidaceae
GenusAllium
SpeciesA. tuberosum

Garlic chives, Allium tuberosum, is a species of flowering plant in the onion genus Allium, native to southwestern Shanxi province in China and widely cultivated elsewhere. It is grown as a culinary herb, with a flavour described as a mild combination of garlic and onion.

Description

Unlike common chives (Allium schoenoprasum), which have hollow, cylindrical leaves, garlic chives have flat, strap-like green leaves. The plant grows in slowly expanding clumps from small rhizomes and produces starry white flowers in late summer, borne in umbels on stalks rising above the leaves. It is a hardy perennial that spreads readily by seed.

Culinary use

Garlic chives are a staple of Chinese, Korean and Japanese cooking. They are known as jiǔcài in Mandarin, buchu in Korean and nira in Japanese. Uses include dumpling fillings (especially jiucai jiaozi), stir-fries such as pad see ew style dishes, savoury pancakes, and the blanched yellow form called "yellow chives", grown in darkness to produce a milder, golden shoot. The flower buds and scapes are also eaten.

Cultivation

The plant prefers full sun and well-drained soil, is drought tolerant once established, and is grown both as a vegetable and as an ornamental border plant. Because it self-seeds vigorously, it can become invasive in some regions.
