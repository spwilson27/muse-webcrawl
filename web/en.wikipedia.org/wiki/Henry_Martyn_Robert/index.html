Henry Martyn Robert

Henry Martyn Robert (May 2, 1837 – May 11, 1923) was a United States Army officer and engineer best known as the author of Robert's Rules of Order, first published in 1876 as Pocket Manual of Rules of Order for Deliberative Assemblies. The book became the most widely used manual of parliamentary procedure in the United States.

Life and career

Robert graduated from West Point in 1857 and served in the Army Corps of Engineers, working on fortifications and harbour defences, including during the Pig War standoff in the Pacific Northwest and the Civil War. He rose to the rank of brigadier general and served as Chief of Engineers in 1901 before retiring.

Robert's Rules of Order

Prompted by an embarrassing experience chairing a chaotic church meeting in 1863, Robert studied British parliamentary practice and the procedures of the U.S. House of Representatives and adapted them for voluntary societies. His manual sets out rules for motions, debate, voting, committees, and officers. Successive revised editions — continued after his death by his son and later editors — remain the default parliamentary authority adopted in the bylaws of countless organisations.

References: Robert, H. M., "Robert's Rules of Order" (1876; revised editions to 2020).
