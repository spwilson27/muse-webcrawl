ISO 639

ISO 639 is a standard by the International Organization for Standardization that defines short codes for language names. The standard exists in several parts, of which the best known are ISO 639-1, defining two-letter codes for 184 languages (such as en for English and de for German), and ISO 639-3, defining three-letter codes covering more than 7,000 individual languages. The codes are widely used in computing, linguistics, libraries, and localization.

Parts of the standard

ISO 639-1 (2002) covers major languages with two-letter codes derived from earlier practice. ISO 639-2 provides three-letter codes for about 460 languages, sometimes with separate bibliographic (B) and terminology (T) variants. ISO 639-3 aims to cover all known living and extinct languages, using the SIL International code set. ISO 639-5 covers language families and groups, and ISO 639-6 (withdrawn in 2014) attempted to cover language variants comprehensively.

Use

ISO 639 codes form the language component of IETF language tags (BCP 47) used throughout the web, for example in HTML lang attributes and HTTP headers. They are also used in bibliographic cataloging and in locale identifiers in software.

Registration authorities

Different parts have different maintenance agencies: the Library of Congress maintains ISO 639-2, while SIL International maintains ISO 639-3. Change requests are reviewed periodically and new languages are added as documentation is verified.
