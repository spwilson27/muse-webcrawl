Lapland

CountriesNorway, Sweden, Finland, Russia
PeopleSámi
FeaturesMidnight sun, polar night, aurora
[Image: Fell landscape and aurora in Lapland]
Fell landscape and aurora in Lapland

Lapland (Sápmi in the Sámi languages) is the northern region of Fennoscandia spanning northern Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia's Kola Peninsula, largely within the Arctic Circle. It is the homeland of the Indigenous Sámi people.

Geography and culture

A land of tundra, taiga, fells and thousands of lakes, Lapland has subarctic winters with polar night and summers of midnight sun. The Sámi — traditionally reindeer herders, fishers and hunters — maintain parliaments in Norway, Sweden and Finland and a cultural identity across the four states. Finnish Lapland markets Rovaniemi as the official home of Santa Claus; tourism, mining, forestry and reindeer herding are the main economies. "Lapland" strictly denotes the Swedish province or the Finnish region in some usages, but in English it names the whole Sámi homeland.
