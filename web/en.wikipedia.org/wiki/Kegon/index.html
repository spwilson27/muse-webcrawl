Kegon

Kegon is the Japanese transmission of the Chinese Huayan school of Mahayana Buddhism, introduced to Japan in the 8th century. Centred on the Avatamsaka Sutra, it teaches the interpenetration of all phenomena — the idea that each thing contains and reflects all others.

Doctrine

Huayan/Kegon philosophy, systematized in China by masters such as Fazang, describes reality through the image of Indra's net, in which each jewel reflects every other. The school classifies Buddhist teachings into a hierarchy culminating in the Avatamsaka.

In Japan

Kegon became one of the six schools of Nara Buddhism, with its headquarters at Tōdai-ji in Nara, home of the Great Buddha (Daibutsu), consecrated in 752. The school survives today as a small independent denomination.
