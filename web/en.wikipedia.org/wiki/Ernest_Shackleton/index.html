Sir Ernest Henry Shackleton (15 February 1874 – 5 January 1922) was an Anglo-Irish Antarctic explorer who led three British expeditions to the Antarctic during the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration. He is celebrated above all for the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition of 1914–1917, in which, after his ship Endurance was crushed by ice, he brought every member of his crew home alive.

Early expeditions

Shackleton sailed with Robert Falcon Scott's Discovery Expedition (1901–1904), reaching a furthest south of 82°S before being invalided home. Leading the Nimrod Expedition (1907–1909), his party reached 88°23′S — within 100 geographical miles of the South Pole — and made the first ascent of Mount Erebus.

The Endurance expedition

Trapped in Weddell Sea pack ice in 1915, Endurance sank that November. The crew camped on drifting ice, then sailed lifeboats to Elephant Island. Shackleton and five companions made an 800-nautical-mile open-boat voyage in the James Caird to South Georgia and crossed its unmapped interior to reach help; after several attempts the entire stranded party was rescued in August 1916 without a single life lost.

Death and legacy

Shackleton died of a heart attack aboard the Quest at Grytviken, South Georgia, at the start of his fourth expedition, and was buried there. Long overshadowed by Scott, he was re-evaluated in the late 20th century as a model of crisis leadership; the wreck of Endurance was located, remarkably preserved, in March 2022.
