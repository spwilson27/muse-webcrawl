Purchase decision


Stages of the consumer decision process

The purchase decision is the stage of the consumer decision-making process at which a consumer chooses one brand or product from the alternatives evaluated and commits to buying it. In classic models of consumer behaviour, purchase decision follows problem recognition, information search and evaluation of alternatives, and precedes post-purchase behaviour such as satisfaction, dissonance or repeat buying.

Factors influencing the decision

Between evaluation and purchase, two broad groups of factors intervene: the attitudes of others (for example, family members or reviewers) and unexpected situational factors such as price changes, stock availability or a competitor's promotion. Consumers also weigh perceived risk — financial, functional, physical, social and psychological — which increases with the cost and complexity of the purchase.

Types of buying behaviour

Marketing models distinguish complex buying behaviour (high involvement, significant brand differences), dissonance-reducing buying, variety-seeking buying and habitual buying behaviour (low involvement, few perceived differences), following categories popularised by Henry Assael.

Digital context

In e-commerce, the purchase decision is shaped by search rankings, reviews and ratings, social proof, delivery terms and friction in the checkout process. Abandoned-cart rates are a common measure of decisions that were evaluated but not completed. Marketing automation and retargeting are designed to move consumers from evaluation to purchase.
