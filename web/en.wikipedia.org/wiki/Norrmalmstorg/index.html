Norrmalmstorg

Norrmalmstorg is a city square in central Stockholm, Sweden, in the district of Norrmalm. Surrounded by elegant late-19th- and early-20th-century buildings, luxury shops and the Berzelii Park, it is one of Stockholm's most prestigious addresses. The square is the starting point — the "GO" square — on the Swedish edition of the board game Monopoly.

Placeholder: Norrmalmstorg, with the Kreditbanken building on the right.

History

The square was laid out in its modern form in the 19th century as the area around Nybroviken was developed. It has long been a hub of banking and high-end retail.

The Norrmalmstorg robbery and Stockholm syndrome

Norrmalmstorg is internationally known as the site of the Norrmalmstorg robbery of 23–28 August 1973, when escaped convict Jan-Erik Olsson took four employees hostage in the Kreditbanken bank at the square, joined later by his former cellmate Clark Olofsson. During the six-day siege, the hostages developed emotional bonds with their captors and became distrustful of the police attempting to free them. Criminologist and psychiatrist Nils Bejerot, who advised the police during the incident, coined the term Stockholm syndrome for this phenomenon, which has since become widely used in psychology and popular culture.
