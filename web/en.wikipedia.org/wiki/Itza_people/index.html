Itza people

RegionPetén, Guatemala; Yucatán
LanguageItza Maya (critically endangered)
Historic capitalNojpetén (Tayasal)

The Itza are a Maya people of the Petén region of northern Guatemala. Their last independent Maya kingdom, centred on the island capital Nojpetén (Tayasal) in Lake Petén Itzá, held out against Spanish conquest until 1697 — the last independent Maya polity.

History

Itza traditions link them to Chichén Itzá in Yucatán, whose name means "mouth of the well of the Itza"; following its decline, groups migrated south to Petén, probably by the 13th–15th centuries, though scholars debate the details. The Itza kingdom under the Kan Ek dynasty traded and warred with neighbouring Maya groups — the Kowoj, Yalain and Mopan — and repelled Spanish expeditions from 1618 onward, including those following Cortés's 1525 march past the lake.

Conquest

After Franciscan missions and military pressure, Martín de Ursúa's forces stormed Nojpetén on 13 March 1697 using a purpose-built galeota. The conquest opened Petén to Spanish settlement and devastated the Itza population through violence and disease.

Today

Modern Itza live mainly in San José on Lake Petén Itzá. The Itza language, a Yucatecan Maya language, is critically endangered with only a few elderly fluent speakers, though revitalisation efforts continue. Itza identity, agroforestry knowledge and ritual traditions persist.
