Neil Gaiman

Neil Richard MacKinnon Gaiman (born 10 November 1960) is an English author of short fiction, novels, comic books, graphic novels, audio theatre and screenplays. His works include the comic book series The Sandman and the novels Good Omens (with Terry Pratchett), Stardust, American Gods, Coraline and The Graveyard Book.

Career

Gaiman began his career as a journalist and wrote the biography Duran Duran: The First Four Years of the Fab Five and, with Kim Newman, Ghastly Beyond Belief. His collaboration with artist Dave McKean and the DC Comics series The Sandman (1989–1996) brought him wide acclaim and helped redefine mainstream comics for adult readers.

American Gods (2001) won the Hugo, Nebula, Locus and Bram Stoker awards. The Graveyard Book (2008) won both the Newbery Medal and the Carnegie Medal, the first book to win both. His other notable works include Neverwhere, The Ocean at the End of the Lane and the children's book Fortunately, the Milk. Several of his works have been adapted for film and television, including Stardust, Coraline, American Gods and Good Omens.
