The Yeşilırmak (Green River) in northern Anatolia.

The Yeşilırmak (Turkish for "Green River"; known in Greek as the Iris, Ἶρις) is a river in northern Turkey. At about 519 kilometres (322 mi) long, it is one of the longest rivers lying entirely within Turkish territory. It rises northeast of Sivas in the highlands of northeastern Anatolia and flows west and then north through the Pontic Mountains before emptying into the Black Sea just east of Samsun.

Course

The river's headwaters lie in Sivas Province, in the Kösedağ range. It flows southwest past the town of Zara, then turns west and north, passing through or near the cities of Tokat, Turhal and Amasya. Near Amasya the river cuts a dramatic gorge through the mountains. Its principal tributary is the Kelkit River, which joins it from the east. Below Amasya the Yeşilırmak turns north, crosses the Canik Mountains, and reaches the coastal plain, where it forms a delta east of the city of Samsun before discharging into the Black Sea.

History

In antiquity the river was known as the Iris. The geographer Strabo, a native of Amaseia (modern Amasya), described its course through the region of Pontus. The fertile lands along its lower course were part of the ancient kingdoms of Pontus and later the Roman and Byzantine worlds. During the Ottoman period and into the modern era, the river's alluvial plain became an important agricultural district, noted especially for tobacco, hazelnuts and other crops.

Use and development

Several dams have been built on the Yeşilırmak and its tributaries for hydroelectric power generation, irrigation and flood control. The river basin covers roughly 36,100 square kilometres and supports extensive agriculture. Environmental studies have monitored the river's water quality and its delta wetlands, which provide habitat for migratory birds on the Black Sea coast.
