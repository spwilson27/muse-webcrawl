Commonwealth Edison, commonly known as ComEd, is the largest electric utility in Illinois, delivering electricity to more than four million customers across northern Illinois, including the city of Chicago. It is a subsidiary of the Exelon Corporation.

History

The company has roots in the Chicago Edison Company, which Samuel Insull consolidated in 1907 with Commonwealth Electric to form Commonwealth Edison. Insull built the utility into the centrepiece of a vast holding-company empire that collapsed in the Great Depression. ComEd later became one of the largest operators of nuclear power stations in the United States, running plants such as Braidwood, Byron, and LaSalle.

Operations

Since Illinois deregulated its electricity market in the late 1990s, ComEd has focused on transmission and distribution, while generation passed to its Exelon affiliate. The company has invested in smart-grid modernisation and, in 2020, entered a deferred prosecution agreement and paid a large fine in connection with a federal corruption investigation involving lobbying in Springfield.
