Shiraz wine

Shiraz wine refers to wine made from the dark-skinned grape known as Syrah in France and much of the world, and as Shiraz principally in Australia and South Africa. The name "Shiraz" is traditionally associated with the city of Shiraz in Persia, although DNA profiling has shown the grape originated in the Rhône valley of France, from a cross of Dureza and Mondeuse Blanche.

Styles

Syrah produces full-bodied red wines with flavours of blackberry, plum, black pepper, smoked meat, and olive. In the northern Rhône (Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie, Cornas) it yields structured, age-worthy wines, often co-fermented with a little Viognier. Australian Shiraz — famously from the Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale, and Heathcote — tends to be riper and more fruit-forward, with chocolate and spice notes, while cooler-climate regions such as the Yarra Valley produce a more peppery, medium-bodied style increasingly labelled "Syrah."

History in Australia

Cuttings were brought to Australia in the 1830s by James Busby, and Shiraz became the country's signature red variety, including treasured old-vine plantings dating to the 19th century. Iconic wines such as Penfolds Grange (first vintage 1951) established its international reputation.

Food pairing

Shiraz pairs well with grilled and roasted red meats, game, hard cheeses, and richly spiced dishes; its peppery character complements barbecued foods.
