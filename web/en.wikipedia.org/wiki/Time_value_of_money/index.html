The time value of money is the widely accepted conjecture that there is greater benefit to receiving a sum of money now rather than an identical sum later. It may be seen as an implication of the later-developed concept of time preference.

The time value of money is among the factors considered when weighing the opportunity costs of investing or spending money. It is one of the core principles of finance: a sum of money in hand has greater value than the same sum to be paid in the future. It is also used in calculating the present value of an annuity and is the underpinning for calculations of net present value and internal rates of return used in capital budgeting.

History

The concept can be traced to early treatments of interest, including Talmudic discussions of usury and the work of the Spanish theologian Martín de Azpilcueta (1491–1586) of the School of Salamanca, who recognised that money received earlier has different value from money received later. The formal mathematical treatment developed alongside the mathematics of compound interest.

Calculations

Problems involving the time value of money can be framed as finding one unknown given the others:

Future value: FV = PV × (1 + i)n

Present value: PV = FV / (1 + i)n

where PV is the present value, FV the future value, i the interest (discount) rate per period, and n the number of periods. Related standard problems include the present and future values of annuities and perpetuities, loan amortisation, and the valuation of bonds.

Reasons for the time value of money

  • Preference: individuals prefer present consumption to future consumption.
  • Inflation: money loses purchasing power over time when prices rise.
  • Risk: money to be received in the future is uncertain.
  • Opportunity cost: money held now can be invested to earn a return.
