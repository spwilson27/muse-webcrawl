Schwarz–Christoffel mapping

In complex analysis, a Schwarz–Christoffel mapping is a conformal transformation of the upper half-plane (or the unit disc) onto the interior of a polygon. The formula was discovered independently by Elwin Bruno Christoffel (1867) and Hermann Amandus Schwarz (1869), and is a classical application of the Riemann mapping theorem giving an explicit construction of a conformal map.

Formula

Let the vertices of the target polygon correspond to points x1 < ... < xn on the real axis, and let the interior angles of the polygon be αkπ. Then the mapping f from the upper half-plane to the polygon is given, up to an affine transformation, by

f(z) = A + C ∫ (z − x1)α1−1 ··· (z − xn)αn−1 dz

where A and C are complex constants determining the size, position and rotation of the polygon. The prevertices xk are not freely chosen: for polygons with more than three vertices they must satisfy accessory parameter conditions so that the side lengths come out correctly, which in general requires solving nonlinear equations numerically.

Special cases

For triangles, the integral reduces to expressions involving the hypergeometric function, and the mapping problem is always solvable in closed parametric form. The case of a rectangle leads to elliptic integrals and the Jacobian elliptic functions; the inverse map is related to the Schwarz triangle function and the Schwarzian derivative.

Applications

Schwarz–Christoffel mappings are used in potential theory and fluid mechanics to solve Laplace's equation on polygonal domains — for example flow around obstacles, electrostatics of polygonal conductors, and Hele-Shaw flow. Numerical conformal mapping packages, notably the SC Toolbox for MATLAB developed by Tobin Driscoll, compute these maps by solving the accessory parameter problem numerically.
