Seven of Nine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Seven of Nine (born Annika Hansen) is a fictional character in the Star Trek franchise, introduced in the television series Star Trek: Voyager. Portrayed by actress Jeri Ryan, she is a former Borg drone who joins the crew of the starship USS Voyager after being severed from the Borg Collective. She later appears in the series Star Trek: Picard.

Background

Annika Hansen was born in 2348 to exobiologists Magnus and Erin Hansen, who were studying the Borg aboard the research vessel USS Raven. The family was assimilated by the Borg in 2356, and Annika was given the designation Seven of Nine, Tertiary Adjunct of Unimatrix Zero One. She remained a drone for eighteen years until 2374, when Voyager's crew captured her during an alliance against Species 8472 and Captain Kathryn Janeway ordered her disconnected from the Collective.

Character arc

Initially hostile and demanding to be returned to the Borg, Seven gradually reclaimed her humanity under Janeway's mentorship. Her Borg-enhanced strength, intellect and nanoprobe technology made her an invaluable member of the crew, serving in Astrometrics. Her storylines frequently explored the tension between individuality and collectivism, and the difficulty of emotional and social reintegration. She developed close relationships with the Doctor, who assisted in her social development, and with Commander Chakotay.

In Star Trek: Picard, set some twenty years later, Seven has become a member of the Fenris Rangers, a vigilante peacekeeping organisation, and eventually joins Starfleet, becoming executive officer and later captain of the USS Titan-A, renamed the USS Enterprise-G.

Reception

The character was introduced in the fourth-season premiere "Scorpion, Part II" (1997), in part to revitalise Voyager's ratings. Ryan's performance was widely praised, winning a Saturn Award, and Seven of Nine became one of the franchise's most popular characters. Her form-fitting costume was controversial among critics, though the character's writing and Ryan's portrayal were generally regarded as adding significant depth to the series.
