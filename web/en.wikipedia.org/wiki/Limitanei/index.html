The limitanei (Latin, "the ones on the frontier", from limes meaning frontier) were the frontier garrison soldiers of the late Roman Empire, stationed in forts along the empire's borders (the Rhine, Danube, Euphrates and desert frontiers) from the reforms of Diocletian and Constantine I in the late 3rd and early 4th centuries AD until the 7th century in the East.

Unlike the mobile field armies of the comitatenses, the limitanei were permanently settled in frontier districts, defending fortified lines, policing crossings, and acting as a tripwire and first line of resistance against raids. In return for military service they received plots of land which they farmed, and their positions tended to become hereditary, with sons following fathers into the units.

Origins

The system emerged from Diocletian's strengthening of the frontier defences and Constantine's division of the army into frontier troops and mobile field forces. Limitanei units were commanded by a dux limitis (frontier duke) in each sector. The Notitia Dignitatum, a late Roman register of offices, lists their dispositions in detail.

Organisation

Limitanei included legions (smaller than their early-imperial predecessors, perhaps around 1,000 men), cohorts, alae of cavalry and specialised units such as river flotillas on the Danube and Rhine. They were paid less, equipped more lightly, and ranked socially below the comitatenses and the elite palatini. Some units (the pseudocomitatenses) were limitanei transferred into the field army as needed.

Effectiveness debate

Historians have debated whether the limitanei degenerated into little more than a peasant militia — the hostile view of the 5th-century historian Zosimus — or remained effective professional soldiers suited to the static defence, policing and delaying role for which they were designed. Archaeological evidence of maintained forts and continued recruiting in the East suggests the latter for many regions.

Decline

In the West the limitanei faded with the collapse of imperial authority in the 5th century. In the East, Justinian rebuilt frontier forces in the 6th century, but the system largely disappeared after the Arab conquests of the 7th century, when the Byzantine army reorganised into the thematic system.
