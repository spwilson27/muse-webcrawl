Unified Thread Standard

The Unified Thread Standard (UTS) defines a standard thread form and series, along with allowances, tolerances, and designations, for screw threads commonly used in the United States and Canada. It is the main standard for bolts, nuts, and other threaded fasteners in those countries.

Characteristics

UTS threads have a 60° profile, like ISO metric threads, but are dimensioned in inches. Thread size is given by major diameter and threads per inch (TPI). Series include UNC (coarse), UNF (fine), UNEF (extra fine), and constant-pitch series such as 8, 12 and 16 TPI. A designation such as 1/4-20 UNC specifies a quarter-inch diameter thread with 20 threads per inch in the Unified National Coarse series.

History

The standard grew out of the Sellers thread form adopted in the United States in the 19th century, and was formalised by agreement between the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom in 1948–49 to promote interchangeability of fasteners.
