Reducción

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reducciones (Spanish: reducciones, Portuguese: reduções; singular reducción) were settlements established by Spanish colonial authorities and Catholic religious orders in Spanish America and the Spanish East Indies in which Indigenous peoples were concentrated. The Spanish word reducción means "reduction" or "concentration": the settlements were intended to "reduce" dispersed Indigenous populations into ordered towns where they could be more easily governed, taxed, converted to Christianity, and mobilized for labor.

Purposes and organization

Reducciones were laid out on a grid pattern around a central plaza, with a church, administrative buildings, and dwellings arranged by Spanish urban norms. Congregating Indigenous people facilitated the collection of tribute, the administration of the encomienda and labor drafts such as the mita, and the work of missionary friars and priests who taught Christianity and Spanish language and customs.

A large-scale program of reducciones was implemented in the Viceroyalty of Peru under Viceroy Francisco de Toledo in the 1570s, which forcibly resettled roughly 1.4 million Indigenous Andeans into hundreds of new towns. In New Spain, similar congregations were known as congregaciones.

Jesuit reductions

The best-known reducciones were those founded by the Society of Jesus among the Guaraní in the region of present-day Paraguay, northeastern Argentina, and southern Brazil, beginning in the early seventeenth century. At their peak in the 1730s, the thirty Guaraní mission towns were home to well over 100,000 people. The missions offered some protection from enslavement by Portuguese bandeirantes and from the encomienda, and developed notable traditions of music, crafts, printing, and communal agriculture.

The Treaty of Madrid (1750) transferred seven missions east of the Uruguay River to Portugal, provoking the Guaraní War of 1754–1756. Following the expulsion of the Jesuits from Spanish territories in 1767, the missions declined rapidly under other administrators.

Effects

Concentration in reducciones exposed Indigenous communities to epidemic disease, which caused catastrophic population losses, and disrupted traditional social and agricultural patterns. At the same time, the towns became centers in which Indigenous languages and identities persisted and were reshaped; many modern towns in Latin America trace their origins to reducciones.
