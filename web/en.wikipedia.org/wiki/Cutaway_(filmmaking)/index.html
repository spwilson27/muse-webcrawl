Cutaway (filmmaking)


A cutaway to a listener during a conversation scene (placeholder image)

In film editing, a cutaway is a shot that interrupts a continuously filmed action with a view of something else. It is usually followed by a cutback to the original shot. Cutaways differ from insert shots, which show part of the same scene in closer detail: a cutaway shows something outside the main action or space.

Uses

Cutaways are used to compress or expand time, conceal edits (for example, joining two takes of a dialogue scene), supply context such as a listener's reaction, or show related action elsewhere — a ticking clock, an approaching car, a crowd watching an event. Reaction shots and "noddies" in interviews are classic cutaways used to bridge edits in the interviewee's speech.
