The Treaty of Lisbon (initially known as the Reform Treaty) is an international agreement which amends the two treaties that form the constitutional basis of the European Union (EU). The Treaty of Lisbon was signed by EU member states on 13 December 2007, and entered into force on 1 December 2009. It amends the Maastricht Treaty (1993), known in updated form as the Treaty on European Union (TEU), and the Treaty of Rome (1957), known in updated form as the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).

Background

The treaty was drawn up after the failure of the proposed Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe, which was rejected in referendums in France and the Netherlands in 2005. Most of the institutional reforms of the Constitutional Treaty were preserved in the Lisbon Treaty, but presented as amendments to the existing treaties rather than as a single constitutional document.

Main reforms

  • Created a permanent President of the European Council, appointed for a two-and-a-half-year term renewable once;
  • Created the post of High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, who also serves as a Vice-President of the European Commission;
  • Gave the EU a single legal personality, enabling it to sign international agreements in its own right;
  • Made the Charter of Fundamental Rights legally binding;
  • Introduced a double-majority system for qualified majority voting in the Council (55% of member states representing 65% of the EU population);
  • Increased the powers of the European Parliament through the extension of the ordinary legislative procedure;
  • Introduced, for the first time, a formal withdrawal clause (Article 50 TEU) allowing a member state to leave the Union.

Ratification

Ratification was complicated when Irish voters rejected the treaty in a referendum in June 2008. After the EU provided guarantees on taxation, neutrality and social issues, a second Irish referendum in October 2009 approved it, clearing the way for entry into force on 1 December 2009.
