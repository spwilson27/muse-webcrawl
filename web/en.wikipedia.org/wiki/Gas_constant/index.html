Gas constant

The molar gas constant (also known as the gas constant, universal gas constant, or ideal gas constant, denoted R) is a fundamental physical constant arising in the formulation of general laws concerning gases.

Gas constant
Symbol: R
Value: 8.314462618… J·mol−1·K−1
Definition: Boltzmann constant × Avogadro constant

Definition

It is the molar equivalent of the Boltzmann constant: R = NAk, where NA is the Avogadro constant. Since the 2019 redefinition of SI base units, both constants have defined exact values, so R is exactly 8.31446261815324 J·mol−1·K−1.

Ideal gas law

R appears in the ideal gas law: PV = nRT, where P is pressure, V volume, n amount of substance and T absolute temperature. It also appears in the Nernst equation, Arrhenius equation, and in defining the Boltzmann factor at molar scale.

Specific gas constant

The specific gas constant Rspecific for a gas or mixture is R divided by its molar mass, used in engineering forms of the gas law such as P = ρRspecificT.

History

The constant was first introduced in the work of Émile Clapeyron (1834) combining Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's laws, and was later named in honour of work following Regnault's measurements.
