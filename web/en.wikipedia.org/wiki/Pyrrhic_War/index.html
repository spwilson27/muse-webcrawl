Pyrrhic War

Date280–275 BC
LocationSouthern Italy and Sicily
ResultRoman victory
BelligerentsRoman Republic vs. Epirus, Tarentum, Greek cities, Carthage (later phase)
CommandersPyrrhus of Epirus; various Roman consuls

The Pyrrhic War (280–275 BC) was a war fought between the Roman Republic and the combined forces of the Greek city of Tarentum, numerous other Greek cities in Italy, and King Pyrrhus of Epirus, who answered Tarentum's appeal for aid against Rome. It was the first time Rome came into direct military conflict with the Hellenistic world, and Pyrrhus's victories at ruinous cost gave rise to the expression "Pyrrhic victory".

Course of the war

Pyrrhus landed in Italy in 280 BC with some 25,000 men and 20 war elephants — the first elephants the Romans faced in battle. He defeated the Romans at the Battle of Heraclea (280 BC) and again at the Battle of Asculum (279 BC), where, after suffering heavy losses among his best troops, he is reported to have said, "If we are victorious in one more battle with the Romans, we shall be utterly ruined."

In 278 BC Pyrrhus crossed to Sicily to fight the Carthaginians on behalf of the Sicilian Greeks, conquering most of the island except the fortress of Lilybaeum. His high-handed rule alienated his allies, and he returned to Italy in 276 BC. In 275 BC the Romans defeated him at the Battle of Beneventum, and Pyrrhus withdrew to Epirus, leaving a garrison at Tarentum, which fell to Rome in 272 BC.

Consequences

The war established Rome as the dominant power in the Italian peninsula, brought it into confrontation with Carthage over Sicily, and demonstrated the resilience of the Roman military system against a first-rate Hellenistic army. Within a generation the rivalry set in motion by the war led to the Punic Wars.
