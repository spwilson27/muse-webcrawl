Fire-and-forget

Fire-and-forget is a term used in several fields, most notably weapons technology and computing, for a system that does not require further input or guidance from the operator after it has been launched or sent.

Weapons

In military usage, a fire-and-forget weapon is an (often guided) missile or other munition that does not require the operator to keep the target illuminated or otherwise guide it after launch. Examples include the FGM-148 Javelin anti-tank missile, which uses an imaging infrared seeker, and modern air-to-air missiles such as the AIM-120 AMRAAM, which homes on its target with its own active radar. The advantage is that the operator can immediately take cover, move, or engage another target.

Computing

In communications and software, "fire-and-forget" describes a message sent without waiting for or expecting a reply — for instance, a UDP packet, a one-way notification, or an asynchronous event published without an acknowledgement. The sender does not verify delivery, trading reliability for simplicity and low latency.
