A Man for All Seasons is a play by Robert Bolt based on the life of Sir Thomas More, first performed in London in 1960. It portrays More, the Lord Chancellor of England in the 1530s, as a principled man of conscience held in deep regard by all — including King Henry VIII — who nevertheless refuses to endorse the King's wish to divorce Catherine of Aragon and marry Anne Boleyn, or to swear the oath acknowledging Henry as head of the Church in England. More's silence is eventually construed as treason, and he is tried, convicted, and executed.

Title

The title is drawn from a description of More by his contemporary Robert Whittington, who wrote in 1520 that More was "a man of an angel's wit and singular learning... a man for all seasons". Bolt borrowed the phrase to celebrate More as a man who remained true to himself in every circumstance.

Structure and themes

The play makes conspicuous use of Brechtian devices. The character of the Common Man — played by one actor who also takes the roles of steward, boatman, innkeeper, jailer, foreman of the jury, and executioner — addresses the audience directly and comments on the action, representing the ordinary person's instinct for self-preservation against which More's integrity is set. Bolt's preface explains that he was drawn to More as a man with "an adamantine sense of his own self", and the play explores the conflict between conscience and political expediency, and the meaning of silence in law.

"When a man takes an oath, Meg, he's holding his own self in his own hands. Like water. And if he opens his fingers then — he needn't hope to find himself again."

Productions and adaptations

The original London production, starring Paul Scofield as More, opened at the Globe Theatre on 1 July 1960 and transferred to Broadway in 1961, where it won the Tony Award for Best Play; Scofield also won the Tony for his performance. Bolt adapted the play for the acclaimed 1966 film, directed by Fred Zinnemann, which won six Academy Awards including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Actor for Scofield. A 1988 television film directed by and starring Charlton Heston followed, and the play was revived on Broadway in 2008 with Frank Langella as More.
