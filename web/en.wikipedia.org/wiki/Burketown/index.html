Burketown, on the Albert River near the Gulf of Carpentaria.

Burketown is an isolated outback town and locality in the Shire of Burke, in north-western Queensland, Australia. Situated on the Albert River about 30 kilometres from the shores of the Gulf of Carpentaria, it serves a vast cattle-raising hinterland in the Gulf Country. The town is named after the explorer Robert O'Hara Burke, leader of the ill-fated Burke and Wills expedition.

History

The area is the traditional land of Indigenous peoples including the Ganggalida and Garawa. Burketown was founded in 1865 as a port and supply depot for the Gulf Country pastoral stations, but an outbreak of fever devastated the settlement within its first year, and the town was relocated to higher ground after early flooding. It grew as a meatworks and shipping point for live cattle exported through the Gulf.

Geography and climate

Burketown lies on flat coastal plains crossed by tidal rivers. The climate is tropical savanna, with a hot wet season from roughly November to April, during which the town can be cut off by floodwaters for weeks, and a mild dry season. The surrounding wetlands attract large numbers of birds and are noted for barramundi and saltwater crocodiles.

Morning glory clouds

Burketown is famous among meteorologists and glider pilots for the Morning Glory cloud, a rare roll cloud formation that sweeps in from the Gulf of Carpentaria in the early morning during the late dry season. Pilots travel to the town to "surf" the atmospheric wave generated by these spectacular cloud lines.
