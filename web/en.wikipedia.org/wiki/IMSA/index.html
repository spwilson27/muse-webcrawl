IMSA

International Motor Sports Association
Founded: 1969
Founders: John Bishop, Bill France Sr.
Headquarters: Daytona Beach, Florida, United States
Main series: IMSA SportsCar Championship

The International Motor Sports Association, generally known as IMSA, is a North American sports car racing sanctioning body based in Daytona Beach, Florida. It was founded in 1969 by former SCCA executive director John Bishop and NASCAR founder Bill France Sr. IMSA is owned by NASCAR Holdings and serves as the North American member body of the Automobile Competition Committee for the United States (ACCUS), affiliated with the FIA.

History

Bishop left the SCCA in 1969 and, with France's backing, built IMSA around professionally run, sponsor-friendly racing. Its GT Championship began in 1971 and grew through the 1970s and 1980s with the Camel GT series, featuring production-based GT cars and later the exotic GTP prototypes — machines from Porsche, Nissan, Jaguar, and Toyota that rivaled or exceeded contemporary Formula One technology in some respects. The GTP era collapsed in the early 1990s amid rising costs, and IMSA passed through changes of ownership and a split with the rival United States Road Racing Championship, before re-emerging in the 2000s as sanctioning body for the American Le Mans Series.

Modern era

In 2012 Don Panoz sold IMSA and the ALMS to the France family's Grand-Am operation, and for 2014 the two series merged into the Tudor United SportsCar Championship — today the IMSA SportsCar Championship (WeatherTech Championship), headlined by the 24 Hours of Daytona (Rolex 24), the 12 Hours of Sebring, and Petit Le Mans. Classes include GTP (hybrid LMDh prototypes, from 2023), LMP2, GTD Pro, and GTD. IMSA also sanctions support series including the Michelin Pilot Challenge, MX-5 Cup, and Lamborghini Super Trofeo North America.
