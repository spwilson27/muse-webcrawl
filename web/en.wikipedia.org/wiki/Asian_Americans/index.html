Asian Americans are Americans of Asian ancestry, including naturalised immigrants and their descendants. The United States Census Bureau defines the category broadly to include people with origins in East Asia, Southeast Asia and the Indian subcontinent. The largest ethnic groups are Chinese Americans, Indian Americans, Filipino Americans, Vietnamese Americans, Korean Americans and Japanese Americans.

History

Significant Asian immigration to the United States began with Chinese labourers during the California Gold Rush of the 1850s and railroad construction. Restrictive laws followed, including the Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882 and the Immigration Act of 1924, and Japanese Americans were incarcerated in camps during the Second World War. The Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 abolished national-origins quotas and transformed the population, which grew rapidly and diversified. The term "Asian American" was coined by activist Yuji Ichioka in 1968.

Demographics

Asian Americans are among the fastest-growing racial groups in the United States and are highly urbanised, with large communities in Los Angeles, New York City, the San Francisco Bay Area, Houston and Seattle. The population is diverse in income, education, religion and language; broad averages often conceal wide differences among ethnic subgroups.
