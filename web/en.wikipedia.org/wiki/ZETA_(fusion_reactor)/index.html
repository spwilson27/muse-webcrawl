ZETA, short for Zero Energy Thermonuclear Assembly, was an early fusion power research project built at the Atomic Energy Research Establishment at Harwell, Oxfordshire, in the United Kingdom. One of the first large-scale toroidal pinch devices, it began operation in 1957 and was, for a time, the largest and most powerful fusion experiment in the world. ZETA is best remembered for the celebrated — and mistaken — announcement in early 1958 that it had produced thermonuclear neutrons.

Design and operation

ZETA used the pinch effect: a powerful electric current driven through a low-pressure gas inside a torus created both the ionised plasma and a magnetic field that constricted, or "pinched", the plasma away from the vessel walls. The machine had a torus of about one metre bore and three metres overall diameter, and experiments began in August 1957. It reliably produced plasma temperatures on the order of a million degrees and significant bursts of neutrons.

The 1958 announcement and retraction

In January 1958, with the declassification of Anglo-American fusion research, Harwell's director John Cockcroft cautiously suggested at a press conference that he was "90% certain" the neutrons came from thermonuclear fusion reactions. The story made headlines worldwide as a possible breakthrough toward unlimited energy. Within months, however, further experiments showed the neutrons were produced by instabilities in the plasma accelerating deuterons into collisions, not by a thermonuclear plasma at fusion conditions. The claim was publicly withdrawn in May 1958 — a landmark episode in the history of science communication.

Legacy

ZETA continued operating until 1968 and remained a valuable tool for developing plasma diagnostics, including early uses of laser (Thomson) scattering to measure plasma temperature. Its instabilities confirmed that simple pinch confinement was too unstable for a power reactor, redirecting research toward magnetic configurations such as the stellarator and the Soviet tokamak, whose promising results at the end of the 1960s reshaped the field.
