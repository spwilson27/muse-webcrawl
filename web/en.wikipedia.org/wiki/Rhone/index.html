The Rhone (French: Rhône; German: Rhone) is a major river in Europe, rising in the Swiss Alps and flowing through south-eastern France to the Mediterranean Sea. It is about 813 kilometres long and one of the most important waterways of France.

Course

The river rises at the Rhone Glacier in the canton of Valais, flows west through Lake Geneva, and enters France near Lyon, where it is joined by the Saône. Turning south, it passes Vienne, Valence, Avignon and Arles before splitting into the Grand Rhône and Petit Rhône, forming the Camargue delta between them as it reaches the Mediterranean.

Etymology and history

The name derives from the Gaulish Rodonos (Latin Rhodanus). In antiquity the Rhone valley was a principal trade route between the Mediterranean and northern Europe, carrying wine, oil and amber, and the river marked the western frontier of Roman influence beyond Gallia Narbonensis.

Use

The Rhone is heavily engineered: a chain of hydroelectric dams and locks, operated largely by the Compagnie Nationale du Rhône, makes it navigable to large vessels as far as Lyon, generates electricity, and supplies cooling water to nuclear power stations on its banks. The Rhone valley is also a celebrated wine region, from Valais through the Côtes du Rhône.
