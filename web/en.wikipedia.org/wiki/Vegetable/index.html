Vegetable

Vegetables
A selection of vegetables at a market

Vegetables are parts of plants that are consumed by humans or other animals as food. The original meaning of the word is still commonly used and is applied to plants collectively to refer to all edible plant matter, including the flowers, fruits, stems, leaves, roots and seeds. An alternative definition is applied somewhat arbitrarily, often by culinary and cultural tradition: it may exclude foods derived from some plants that are fruits, flowers, nuts and cereal grains, but include savoury fruits such as tomatoes and courgettes, flowers such as broccoli, and seeds such as pulses.

Terminology

The word "vegetable" was first recorded in English in the early 15th century, from Old French, ultimately from Medieval Latin vegetabilis, "growing, flourishing". The culinary definition contrasts with the botanical one: some culinary vegetables, including tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers and squash, are botanically fruits, while some botanical vegetables, such as rhubarb stalks, are culinary treated as fruit. Legally, classifications have occasionally mattered for taxation, as in the 1893 United States Supreme Court case Nix v. Hedden, which held that the tomato is a vegetable for the purposes of customs law.

Types

Vegetables can be grouped by the part of the plant eaten: leaf vegetables (lettuce, spinach), stem vegetables (celery, asparagus), root vegetables (potatoes, carrots), bulb vegetables (onions, garlic), flower vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower), fruit vegetables (tomatoes, peppers), and seed vegetables (peas, beans, sweetcorn).

Nutrition

Vegetables are rich in vitamins, minerals, dietary fibre and phytochemicals, and are low in fat and calories. Health authorities commonly recommend eating at least five portions of fruit and vegetables a day. Some vegetables, such as potatoes, are important staple sources of starch worldwide.

Production

China is by far the world's largest producer of vegetables. Vegetables are grown commercially in fields and greenhouses and in home gardens; many are perishable and require careful harvesting, cooling and storage.
