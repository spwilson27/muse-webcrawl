Pelecaniformes

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Aves
Order: Pelecaniformes

The Pelecaniformes are an order of medium-sized and large waterbirds found worldwide. Traditionally the order included pelicans, cormorants, gannets, frigatebirds, and tropicbirds — birds united by having all four toes joined by webbing (totipalmate feet).

Classification

Molecular studies rearranged the group substantially. In modern classifications the Pelecaniformes include the pelicans (Pelecanidae), the shoebill, the hamerkop, herons and egrets (Ardeidae), and ibises and spoonbills (Threskiornithidae). The cormorants, gannets, frigatebirds, and anhingas were moved to the order Suliformes, and the tropicbirds to Phaethontiformes.

Characteristics

Members are mostly fish-eating birds associated with coasts, rivers, and wetlands. Pelicans are noted for their large throat pouches; herons for spear-like bills and patient stalking; the shoebill for its massive clog-shaped bill.
