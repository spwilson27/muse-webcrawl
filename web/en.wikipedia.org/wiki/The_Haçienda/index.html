The Haçienda was central to the rave and "Madchester" scenes.

The Haçienda (full name Fac 51 Haçienda) was a nightclub in Manchester, England, that became famous during the "Madchester" years of the late 1980s and early 1990s. It was opened by Factory Records and the band New Order, whose manager Rob Gretton championed the project, and was designed by Ben Kelly with industrial styling — traffic bollards, hazard stripes, and a warehouse-like dance floor.

History

Opened on 21 May 1982 in a former yacht showroom on Whitworth Street West, the club initially lost money as a live venue hosting acts such as Madonna (in her first UK performance), the Smiths, and the Stone Roses. Its fortunes transformed with the arrival of acid house in 1987–88: DJs including Mike Pickering and Graeme Park made nights such as "Nude" legendary, and by 1989 the Haçienda was the epicentre of British rave culture, closely associated with the Happy Mondays and the wider Madchester scene. Financial losses, gang violence, and licensing problems dogged the club in the 1990s, and it closed in 1997 despite its fame.

Legacy

The building was demolished in 2002 and replaced by apartments marketed as The Haçienda Apartments. The club's name, spelled with a cedilla to evoke a Mexican hacienda as filtered through Situationist slogans, and its FAC 51 catalogue number reflect Factory Records' practice of numbering everything in its output. The 2002 film 24 Hour Party People dramatizes its story.
