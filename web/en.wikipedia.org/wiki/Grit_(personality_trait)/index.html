Grit (personality trait)

Grit is a positive, non-cognitive personality trait based on an individual's perseverance of effort combined with the passion for a particular long-term goal or end state. The trait was defined and popularized by psychologist Angela Duckworth and colleagues from 2007 onward, and in her 2016 book Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance.

Definition and measurement

Duckworth's Grit Scale measures two facets — consistency of interest ("passion") and perseverance of effort — through self-report items. In studies of West Point cadets, National Spelling Bee contestants, and undergraduates, grit predicted retention and achievement beyond talent measures and IQ.

Research and criticism

Critics note that grit correlates strongly with the Big Five trait of conscientiousness, questioning its novelty; a 2017 meta-analysis by Credé, Tynan, and Harms found the perseverance facet useful but the overall construct weakly distinct from conscientiousness. Duckworth has responded that grit's focus on stamina toward a single top-level goal distinguishes it. The construct has been influential in education policy and "growth mindset" debates, with cautions raised about overstating it as a remedy for structural disadvantage.

Related concepts

Grit overlaps with psychological hardiness, resilience, and the need for achievement, and with the Finnish concept of sisu. Unlike short-term self-control, grit concerns sustained effort over years despite failure, adversity, and plateaus in progress.
