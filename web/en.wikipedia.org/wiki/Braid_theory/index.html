Braid theory

In mathematics, a braid is an abstract geometric object formed by strands intertwined without knotting, analogous to braided hair or woven threads. Braid theory is the branch of topology that studies these objects. The concept was formalized by Emil Artin in the 1920s.

Braids as groups

Braids with a fixed number of strands form a group under composition, known as the braid group Bn. The braid groups are closely related to the symmetric groups: each braid induces a permutation of its endpoints, and there is a natural short exact sequence connecting the two. Artin presented the braid group by generators σi with relations reflecting strands crossing only with adjacent strands.

Applications

Braid theory connects with knot theory (every knot can be represented as the closure of a braid, by Alexander's theorem), mathematical physics, cryptography, and robotics, where strands may model particle worldlines or robot paths.
