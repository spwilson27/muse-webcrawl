Yantra

A yantra (Sanskrit: यन्त्र, literally "machine, contraption, instrument") is a mystical diagram, mainly from the Tantric traditions of the Indian religions. Yantras are used for worship in temples or at home, as aids to meditation, and for the benefits believed to flow from their occult powers, based on Hindu astrology and tantric texts. A yantra is typically a geometric composition — centred on interlocking triangles, circles, lotus petals, and a square outer frame with four "gates" — that serves as a visual counterpart to a mantra: where the mantra is the deity in sound form, the yantra is the deity in visual form, and the two are used together in ritual.

Structure and use

The most famous yantra is the Sri Yantra (or Sri Chakra), composed of nine interlocking triangles — four pointing up (Shiva) and five pointing down (Shakti) — forming 43 smaller triangles around a central point (bindu), enclosed by lotus petals and a square with four doors. Yantras are drawn on paper, engraved on metal plates, or built in three-dimensional form (as in some temple architecture), consecrated by ritual, and then used as a focus for meditation and worship directed to the associated deity, such as Kali, Lakshmi, or Saraswati. In meditation, the practitioner may visualise the yantra internally, progressing inward from the outer square to the bindu as a map of the cosmos and of the self.

Related concepts

Yantras are closely related to mandalas (which are often more pictorial and serve similar meditative purposes, especially in Buddhism) and are embedded in the wider tantric practices of mantra, mudra, and nyasa.
