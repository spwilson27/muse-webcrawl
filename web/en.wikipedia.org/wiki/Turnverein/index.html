Turnverein
Meaning: "Gymnastics club/association" (German)
Founder of the movement: Friedrich Ludwig Jahn
First Turnplatz: 1811, Berlin

A Turnverein (German for "gymnastics association") is a gymnastics club organised under the tradition founded by Friedrich Ludwig Jahn, the "father of gymnastics" (Turnvater Jahn), who opened the first open-air gymnasium (Turnplatz) in Berlin in 1811. Jahn's movement combined physical exercise with German nationalist and liberal ideals.

Spread

Turnvereine multiplied across the German states in the 19th century and were suppressed during periods of political reaction because of their democratic associations. After the failed revolutions of 1848, many German emigrants ("Forty-Eighters") carried the movement abroad; the first American Turnverein was founded in Cincinnati in 1848, and Turner societies became important social, cultural and political institutions in German-American communities.

Legacy

The Turners contributed apparatus and exercises that fed into modern artistic gymnastics and promoted physical education in schools. Turner halls survive in many American cities, and the German Turner federation (Deutscher Turner-Bund) remains one of Germany's largest sports organisations.
