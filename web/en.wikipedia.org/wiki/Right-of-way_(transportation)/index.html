Right-of-way (transportation)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The right of way is the legal right, established by usage or grant, to pass along a specific route through grounds or property belonging to another. In traffic law, it is the right of a pedestrian, vehicle or ship to proceed with precedence over others in a particular situation or place. One construction of the right of way is that it requires the other party to yield the right of way to the other, rather than giving one party an absolute right to proceed regardless of circumstances.

Road traffic

Traffic codes assign priority at intersections, pedestrian crossings, roundabouts, merges and other conflict points. Typical rules include: vehicles must yield to pedestrians in marked and unmarked crosswalks; drivers turning across traffic yield to oncoming vehicles; at uncontrolled intersections the first vehicle to arrive proceeds first, ties being resolved in favour of the vehicle on the right; and all road users must yield to emergency vehicles displaying lights and sirens. Priority rules vary by jurisdiction in detail.

The Vienna Convention on Road Traffic (1968) harmonizes many of these rules internationally, including the priority-to-the-right system used in much of continental Europe.

Right of way as land

In transportation engineering and land use, a right-of-way also denotes the strip of land reserved for a road, railway, canal, pipeline or utility corridor, whether publicly owned or held as an easement. A "dedicated right-of-way" for transit means the vehicles operate on their own track or lane, separated from general traffic — the top category in classifications of transit quality.

Maritime and aviation

Collision regulations at sea assign stand-on and give-way vessels depending on crossing, head-on and overtaking situations, with sailing vessels and vessels restricted in their ability to manoeuvre receiving special treatment. In aviation, similar rules give priority, for example to aircraft in distress and, when two aircraft converge, to the aircraft on the other's right in defined circumstances.
