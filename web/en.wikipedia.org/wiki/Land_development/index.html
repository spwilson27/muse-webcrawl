Land development

Land development is the alteration of landscape in any number of ways such as:

  • Changing landforms from a natural or semi-natural state for a purpose such as agriculture or housing
  • Subdividing real estate into lots, typically for the purpose of building homes
  • Developing property or changing its purpose, for example by converting an unused factory complex into a condominium

History

Land has been developed since prehistoric times, as early farmers cleared land for crops and grazing. In the modern era, development became a major industry, particularly after the Second World War, with the growth of suburban housing estates in North America and Europe. Large-scale projects such as new towns, industrial parks, and shopping centres transformed landscapes and economies.

Economic aspects

Development involves converting raw land into serviced building sites, adding roads, water, sewerage, and other infrastructure. The value of developed land is typically much greater than that of undeveloped land, which is the source of profit for developers, but development is capital-intensive and risky because of planning controls, market cycles, and construction costs.

Environmental impacts

Land development can lead to habitat loss, soil erosion, increased runoff and flooding, and loss of agricultural land. Modern planning systems therefore require environmental impact assessments for major projects, and concepts such as sustainable development and smart growth seek to reduce the footprint of new development.
