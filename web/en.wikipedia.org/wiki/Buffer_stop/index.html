Buffer stop

A buffer stop prevents trains from running past the end of a track.

A buffer stop, bumper (in North America) or stopblock is a device installed at the end of a railway track to prevent trains from leaving the end of the track. It may be as simple as a pair of beams bolted to the rails with a crossbeam, or a massive concrete or friction-based energy-absorbing structure in a passenger terminus.

Design

The design of a buffer stop relates to the mass and speed of the trains expected to strike it. Simple stops are intended only to halt slow-moving shunting movements; larger terminus stops incorporate sliding friction elements or hydraulic buffers that dissipate the kinetic energy of an errant train over several metres. Buffer stops are fitted in terminal stations, sidings, depots and at the ends of headshunts; where a vehicle impacts at speed, the consequences can still be serious, as in the 1995 Gare de Montparnasse overrun — echoing the famous 1895 accident photographed there.
