1928 Okeechobee hurricane

The Okeechobee hurricane of 1928, also known as the San Felipe Segundo hurricane, was one of the deadliest hurricanes in recorded North Atlantic history. The Category 5 storm crossed the Caribbean in September 1928, devastating Guadeloupe and Puerto Rico before striking Florida, where its winds breached the low muck dikes around Lake Okeechobee and drowned thousands in the farming communities south of the lake. Total deaths are estimated at over 4,000, including at least 2,500 in Florida — making it the second-deadliest tropical cyclone in United States history after the 1900 Galveston hurricane.

Path and impacts

The storm developed off West Africa in early September, crossed the Atlantic, and struck Guadeloupe on September 12, killing about 1,200 people. It then hit Puerto Rico on September 13 — the feast of San Felipe, hence its Spanish name — as a Category 5 hurricane, causing enormous damage and hundreds more deaths. After crossing the Bahamas, it made landfall in Florida near West Palm Beach on September 17 at Category 4 strength.

The Lake Okeechobee disaster

As the hurricane crossed Lake Okeechobee, its winds piled the shallow lake's waters against the southern shore, overwhelming the low earthen dike protecting the reclaimed Everglades farmland. A wall of water swept over towns such as Belle Glade, South Bay and Pahokee. Most of the dead were Black migrant farmworkers, many of whom were buried in mass graves; the segregation of burial sites — whites in coffins in West Palm Beach, Black victims in a mass grave — became a lasting grievance and was memorialised in Zora Neale Hurston's novel Their Eyes Were Watching God. In response, the federal government built the massive Herbert Hoover Dike around the lake.
