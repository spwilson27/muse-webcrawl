Skinheads Against Racial Prejudice

Skinheads Against Racial Prejudice (SHARP) is an anti-racist organisation and identity adopted by skinheads who oppose racism and neo-Nazism within skinhead culture. SHARP originated in New York City in 1987 and spread internationally in the late 1980s and early 1990s, promoting the idea that the skinhead subculture — rooted in 1960s British working-class youth culture and Jamaican ska and reggae — was originally multiracial and distinct from the white-power skinhead movement.

History

SHARP began when a group of New York skinheads, associated with figures such as Marcus Pacheco, organised against racist skinhead groups and negative media portrayals. The SHARP name and logo — based on a Trojan Records-style helmet — were devised in part to distinguish anti-racist skinheads from racist ones, and to reclaim the style. The movement grew through punk and hardcore networks elsewhere in the United States and in Europe, often overlapping with anti-fascist activism, though SHARP chapters varied widely in their degree of political involvement.

Ideas and tensions

SHARP emphasised pride in skinhead culture's Jamaican and British roots, opposition to racism, and in many chapters an apolitical "neither left nor right" stance, which sometimes led to friction with explicitly left-wing groups such as Red and Anarchist Skinheads (RASH). SHARP skinheads typically maintain traditional skinhead fashion — shaved heads, boots, braces, and polo shirts — combined with anti-racist patches and symbols.
