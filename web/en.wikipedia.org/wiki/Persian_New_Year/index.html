Persian New Year

The Persian New Year, known as Nowruz (Persian: نوروز, "new day"), is the traditional festival marking the beginning of spring and the first day of the Iranian calendar. It falls on the vernal equinox, usually 20 or 21 March, and has been celebrated for over 3,000 years in Iran, Central Asia, the Caucasus, and the wider Persian cultural sphere.

Customs

Preparations include spring cleaning (khaneh tekani), buying new clothes, and setting the Haft-Sin table with seven items beginning with the Persian letter sin, symbolising rebirth, health, wealth, and other hopes for the year. Families visit elders, exchange gifts, and mark the festivities that continue with Sizdah Bedar, a picnic on the thirteenth day.

Recognition

Nowruz is a public holiday in Iran, Afghanistan, and many neighbouring countries, and is celebrated by diaspora communities worldwide. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed it on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and the United Nations proclaimed 21 March the International Day of Nowruz.
