Śāntarakṣita

Śāntarakṣita Born: c. 725 (India)
Died: c. 788 (Tibet)
Occupation: Buddhist monk, philosopher, abbot
Works: Tattvasaṃgraha; Madhyamakālaṃkāra
Tradition: Yogācāra-Svatantrika-Madhyamaka; founder figure of Tibetan Buddhism
Śāntarakṣita, the "Great Abbot" (Khenchen) of Samye.

Śāntarakṣita (c. 725 – c. 788) was an Indian Buddhist philosopher and monk from the great monastic university of Nalanda who played a decisive role in establishing Buddhism in Tibet. Invited by King Trisong Detsen, he travelled to Tibet and, together with the tantric master Padmasambhava — whose aid he advised the king to seek when local deities were said to obstruct the work — founded Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet, modelled on the Indian monastery of Odantapuri. There the first Tibetans were ordained as monks, and a vast programme of translating Sanskrit texts into Tibetan was begun.

Philosophy

Śāntarakṣita's system, later labelled the Yogācāra-Svatantrika-Madhyamaka, synthesised the Madhyamaka of Nāgārjuna and Bhāviveka, the Yogācāra of Asaṅga, and the logic and epistemology of Dharmakīrti. His verse treatise Madhyamakālaṃkāra ("Ornament of the Middle Way") argues that phenomena are ultimately neither one nor many, while conventionally accepting a mind-only account; his monumental Tattvasaṃgraha ("Compendium of Reality"), with the commentary of his disciple Kamalaśīla, examines and refutes the doctrines of rival Indian schools. Tradition remembers him as one of the "three scholars" — abbot, master and king — who planted Buddhism in Tibet.
