Ambisonics

Ambisonics
Type: Surround sound / spatial audio format
Developed: 1970s, by Michael Gerzon and colleagues

Ambisonics is a full-sphere surround sound format: in addition to the horizontal plane, it covers sound sources above and below the listener. Unlike channel-based formats, ambisonics represents a sound field using spherical harmonics and is independent of the loudspeaker layout used for playback, making it especially suitable for virtual reality and 360-degree video.

B-format

First-order ambisonics encodes a sound field in four channels known as B-format: W (omnidirectional pressure), and X, Y, Z (figure-of-eight components along three axes). The signals can be captured with a tetrahedral microphone array or synthesised from mono sources with an encoder. Higher-order ambisonics (HOA) adds further spherical harmonic components for greater spatial resolution.

History and revival

Ambisonics was developed in Britain in the 1970s, largely by Michael Gerzon, under the auspices of the NRDC, but achieved limited commercial success at the time, partly due to competing quadraphonic formats. It experienced a major revival in the 2010s when it was adopted as the standard spatial audio format for VR platforms, including YouTube 360 and Facebook 360 video.
