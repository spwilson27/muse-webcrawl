Bak Mei (Chinese: 白眉; "White Eyebrow") is a Chinese martial art, a style of kung fu traditionally attributed to the semi-legendary Taoist monk Bak Mei, said to have been one of the Five Elders who survived the destruction of the Shaolin Temple. The style is known for its explosive short-range power, close-quarters hand techniques, and fierce training methods. Modern Bak Mei was systematized in Guangzhou in the early 20th century by Cheung Lai Chuen.

Techniques

Bak Mei emphasizes power generated through coordinated whole-body mechanics, described by principles such as tun (swallow), tou (spit), fau (float), and cham (sink). Characteristic techniques include the phoenix-eye fist, clawing and grabbing hands, and low, rooted stances such as the triangular stance. Signature forms include Jik Bo (straight step), Sup Ji Kau Da, and Gau Bo Tui (Nine-Step Push).

History

According to legend, Bak Mei betrayed the Shaolin Temple to Qing forces and killed the abbot Jee Sin, becoming one of the great villains of southern Chinese martial-arts folklore, where the hero Fong Sai-yuk is his nemesis. Historically, the art was transmitted in Guangdong and was brought to prominence by Cheung Lai Chuen (1882–1964), who taught publicly in Guangzhou after learning from several masters. Cheung's lineage spread to Hong Kong, Vietnam, and overseas Chinese communities.

In popular culture

The character Bak Mei (played by Gordon Liu) appears as Pai Mei in the film Kill Bill: Volume 2, and the white-eyebrowed monk is a stock antagonist in kung fu cinema and wuxia fiction.
