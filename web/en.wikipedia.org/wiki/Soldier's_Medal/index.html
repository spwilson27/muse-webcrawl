The Soldier's Medal is a military decoration of the United States Army, established by Act of Congress on 2 July 1926. It is awarded to members of the US Armed Forces (and, in some cases, friendly foreign personnel) who distinguish themselves by heroism not involving actual conflict with an enemy — typically for acts of lifesaving at the risk of one's own life. It is the highest honour a soldier can receive for a non-combat act of valour.

Criteria and precedence

The standard for the award is comparable to that of the Distinguished Service Cross, but applied outside combat: the recipient must have voluntarily risked their life in saving or attempting to save others, for example in fires, floods, or accidents. In the order of precedence it ranks above the Bronze Star Medal and below the Silver Star among Army decorations in the personal awards hierarchy for heroism. Retired personnel awarded the medal for lifesaving heroism receive a 10% increase in retired pay, as with the Medal of Honor and service crosses.

Design and history

The medal, designed by sculptor Gaetano Cecere, is a bronze octagon bearing an eagle with raised wings standing on a fasces, with stars and the inscription "Soldier's Medal" and "For Valor" on the reverse, suspended from a ribbon of blue, white, and red stripes. It was created because, before 1926, no decoration existed for peacetime heroism by soldiers; early awards recognised rescues at sea, in fires, and during natural disasters. Equivalent decorations exist for the other services, including the Navy and Marine Corps Medal and the Airman's Medal.
