An e-book (electronic book) is a publication made available in digital form, consisting of text and sometimes images, readable on computers and handheld devices. E-books may be born-digital or digitised print titles, and are sold, lent by libraries, or distributed free by projects such as Project Gutenberg, founded by Michael S. Hart in 1971.

Formats and readers

The standard open format is EPUB, while Amazon's Kindle ecosystem uses its own formats and dedicated e-readers with E Ink screens that mimic paper. Debates around e-books concern pricing, digital rights management, ownership versus licensing, and the changing economics of bookselling and libraries related to publishing generally.
