Seven virtues

In Christian tradition, the seven virtues are the standard list of virtues that counter the seven deadly sins. In one common formulation they are the direct opposites of the capital vices: humility against pride, charity against greed, chastity against lust, gratitude against envy, temperance against gluttony, patience against wrath and diligence against sloth.

Two lists

The term "seven virtues" is used for two related lists:

  • The seven heavenly virtues: the four cardinal virtues (prudence, justice, temperance, fortitude) combined with the three theological virtues (faith, hope, charity).
  • The contrary virtues: seven qualities specifically opposed to the seven deadly sins, taught as remedies against temptation.

History

The tradition of matching virtues against vices began with the late antique poet Prudentius, whose Psychomachia depicted personified virtues battling vices for the soul. Pope Gregory the Great's list of seven capital sins in the 6th century fixed the pattern, and medieval catechisms, morality plays and works of art routinely paired each virtue with its opposing sin.
