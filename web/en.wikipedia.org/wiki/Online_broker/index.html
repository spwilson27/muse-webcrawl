Online broker

A typical online brokerage trading interface.

An online broker is a financial institution that allows customers to buy and sell financial securities — such as stocks, bonds, options and funds — over the internet through an electronic trading platform, rather than through a traditional full-service stockbroker.

History

Online trading grew rapidly in the 1990s with firms such as E*TRADE and Ameritrade, undercutting traditional commissions and fuelling the day-trading boom of the dot-com era. In 2019 most major US brokerages moved to zero-commission stock trading, following the model pioneered by Robinhood; revenue shifted to payment for order flow, margin lending and interest on cash.

Services

Modern online brokers offer mobile apps, fractional shares, research tools and retirement accounts, and are regulated in the United States by the SEC and FINRA, with customer assets protected by SIPC insurance up to set limits.
