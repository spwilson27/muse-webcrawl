Rudolf Nureyev

Nureyev in performance

Rudolf Khametovich Nureyev (17 March 1938 – 6 January 1993) was a Soviet-born ballet dancer and choreographer, widely regarded as the greatest male ballet dancer of his generation. His defection from the Soviet Union in 1961, despite KGB efforts to stop him, created an international sensation.

Early life and training

Nureyev was born on a Trans-Siberian train near Irkutsk, to a Tatar family, and grew up in Ufa, Bashkiria. He began dance lessons as a child and entered the Vaganova Academy in Leningrad at seventeen, an unusually late start, graduating into the Kirov Ballet as a soloist in 1958.

Defection and Western career

At Le Bourget airport in Paris on 16 June 1961, ordered to return to Moscow, Nureyev broke away from his KGB escorts and requested asylum. Within days he was dancing in the West. He formed a celebrated partnership with Margot Fonteyn at The Royal Ballet in London, and performed with companies worldwide, transforming the role of the male dancer from partner to star.

Later life

Nureyev directed the Paris Opera Ballet from 1983 to 1989, nurturing a generation of French dancers and staging his own productions of the classics. He died in Paris of AIDS-related complications in 1993 and is buried in the Russian cemetery at Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois.
