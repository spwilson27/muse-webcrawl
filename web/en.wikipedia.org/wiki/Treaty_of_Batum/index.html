Treaty of Batum

The Treaty of Batum was a treaty between the Democratic Republic of Armenia and the Ottoman Empire, signed in Batum on 4 June 1918. It was the first treaty signed by the newly proclaimed Armenian republic, concluded while Ottoman forces were advancing deep into Armenian territory in the closing months of the First World War.

Under its terms, Armenia was reduced to a small landlocked territory of roughly 11,000 square kilometres around Yerevan and Lake Sevan. The Ottoman Empire gained control of the Alexandropol (Gyumri) region, the railway lines through the Caucasus, and a corridor linking it to Baku and the Caspian Sea. Armenia was also required to demobilise much of its army and to allow the free movement of Ottoman troops across its territory.

Background

Following the Russian Revolution of 1917 and the collapse of the Caucasus Front, the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) had ceded the districts of Kars, Ardahan and Batum to the Ottoman Empire. Ottoman forces nevertheless continued their advance beyond those districts in May 1918. The Armenian victories at the battles of Sardarabad, Bash Abaran and Karakilisa at the end of May halted the drive on Yerevan but did not reverse the Ottoman occupation of western Armenia.

Terms and aftermath

The treaty recognised Armenian independence in name but left the republic in a precarious position, dependent on Ottoman goodwill and cut off from its natural trade routes. The terms were widely regarded as dictated under duress. The Ottoman defeat in the wider war later in 1918, sealed by the Armistice of Mudros on 30 October 1918, rendered the treaty void, and Armenia was able to recover some of the territory lost at Batum.
