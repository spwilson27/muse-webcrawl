The trumpet is a brass instrument commonly used in classical and jazz ensembles. The trumpet group ranges from the piccolo trumpet, with the highest register in the brass family, to bass trumpets. Trumpet-like instruments have historically been used as signalling devices in battle or hunting, with examples dating back to at least 1500 BC. They began to be used as musical instruments only in the late 14th or early 15th century.

Trumpets are used in art music styles, appearing in orchestras, concert bands, chamber music, and jazz ensembles, as well as in popular music. They are played by blowing air through closed lips (a "buzzing" sound), producing a vibrating column of air within the instrument. Since the Baroque era, most trumpets have been built in B♭, having a roughly 1.48 m (4 ft 10 in) tube; they are also built in C, D, E♭, E, F, G and A. A musician who plays the trumpet is called a trumpet player or trumpeter.

History

The earliest trumpets date back to 1500 BC and earlier. The bronze and silver trumpets from the tomb of Tutankhamun in Egypt, bronze lurs from Scandinavia, and metal trumpets from China are often considered the finest examples of early trumpets. The trumpet was used during the Middle Ages mainly as a signalling instrument in war and tournaments; its musical role grew during the Renaissance and Baroque eras, when composers such as Claudio Monteverdi and Henry Purcell wrote for the natural trumpet.

The invention of piston valves by Heinrich Stölzel and Friedrich Blühmel around 1814–1815 greatly expanded the trumpet's chromatic possibilities and led to the modern valved instrument.

Construction

The trumpet is constructed of brass tubing bent into an elongated oval shape. Most modern trumpets have three (or occasionally four) piston valves, each of which increases the length of tubing when engaged, thereby lowering the pitch. The player produces different pitches by combining valve positions with changes in embouchure and air speed, selecting notes from the harmonic series.

Types

The most common trumpet is the B♭ trumpet. Other types include the C trumpet, common in orchestral playing in some countries; the piccolo trumpet, usually in B♭ or A; the bass trumpet; and the natural trumpet, a valveless historical instrument revived for period performance. Related instruments include the cornet and the flugelhorn.
