A National Scenic Area (NSA) is a conservation designation used in Scotland for areas of exceptional scenic value. There are 40 National Scenic Areas covering roughly 13 percent of the land area of Scotland, including much of the Highlands and Islands and celebrated lowland and coastal landscapes. They are Scotland's equivalent in landscape terms of the Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty found in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, and many NSAs overlap with the two Scottish national parks.

History and protection

The areas were first identified in 1978 by the Countryside Commission for Scotland in its report Scotland's Scenic Heritage, and were formally designated in 1980. NSA status does not prohibit development, but planning authorities must give special attention to the scenic interest when deciding applications; certain permitted-development rights are restricted, and proposals of major impact may require particular justification.

Examples

Well-known NSAs include Ben Nevis and Glen Coe, Wester Ross, the Cairngorm Mountains, the Cuillin Hills on Skye, Loch Lomond, the Trossachs, and St Kilda. In 2010 NatureScot published "special qualities" descriptions for each area, identifying the attributes that make its scenery outstanding.
