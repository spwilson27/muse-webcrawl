Sesquiterpene

Sesquiterpenes are a class of terpenes consisting of three isoprene units, giving them the molecular formula C15H24. Like monoterpenes, sesquiterpenes may be acyclic or contain rings, and include many varied combinations of structures. Biochemically modified sesquiterpenes are termed sesquiterpenoids.

Biosynthesis

Sesquiterpenes are assembled from farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP), the C15 intermediate of the isoprenoid pathway. Terpene synthase enzymes fold and cyclise FPP into an enormous diversity of carbon skeletons, which may be further oxidised or rearranged by enzymes such as cytochrome P450 monooxygenases.

Occurrence and roles

They occur widely in plants, fungi and some insects, where they function as pheromones, defensive compounds and signalling molecules. Well-known examples include farnesene, caryophyllene (found in black pepper and cannabis), humulene in hops, and artemisinin, the antimalarial drug from Artemisia annua, which is a sesquiterpene lactone. The plant hormone abscisic acid is also derived from a sesquiterpenoid precursor.
