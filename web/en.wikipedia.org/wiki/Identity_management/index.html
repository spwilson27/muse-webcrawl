Identity management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Identity management (IdM), also known as identity and access management (IAM), is a framework of policies and technologies for ensuring that the proper people in an enterprise have the appropriate access to technology resources. It covers the creation, maintenance and deletion of digital identities, authentication of users, authorisation of access, and auditing of activity.

Functions

Core functions include identity governance and administration (provisioning and deprovisioning accounts, managing entitlements and role assignments), access management (single sign-on, multi-factor authentication, session management), directory services, and privileged access management for administrative accounts. The "joiner–mover–leaver" lifecycle is a standard model: accounts are created when a person joins, adjusted as their role changes, and disabled when they leave.

Technologies and standards

Common protocols and standards include LDAP for directories, Kerberos for network authentication, SAML and OpenID Connect for federated single sign-on, OAuth 2.0 for delegated authorisation, and SCIM for cross-domain provisioning. Federated identity allows a user's identity to be trusted across organisational boundaries, while customer identity and access management (CIAM) adapts the same ideas to consumer-facing services.

Challenges

Identity is a primary attack surface: credential theft features in a large share of data breaches, and over-provisioned accounts increase risk. Regulatory regimes such as GDPR and sector rules impose requirements on how identity data is stored and processed. Modern approaches promote least-privilege access, continuous verification ("zero trust"), and phishing-resistant authentication such as passkeys.
