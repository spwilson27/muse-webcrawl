Drill music

Drill music is a subgenre of hip hop that originated in Chicago in the early 2010s. It is characterised by dark, sparse trap-influenced production — sliding 808 bass, skittering hi-hats and ominous minor-key melodies — and by grim, unflinching lyrics about street life and gang conflict.

History

The Chicago scene coalesced around artists such as Chief Keef, Lil Durk, King Von and the producer Young Chop; Chief Keef's "I Don't Like" (2012) brought drill national attention. The sound then mutated abroad: UK drill, developed in London (especially Brixton) from about 2015, adopted faster, more intricate rhythms and sliding bass, and UK drill production in turn shaped Brooklyn drill, popularised by Pop Smoke, and drill scenes in Ireland, the Netherlands, Australia and New York.

Controversy

Drill has repeatedly been linked by police and politicians to gang violence; in London, videos have been removed from platforms at police request, and lyrics have been used as evidence in court — a practice criticised by academics and civil-liberties advocates as criminalising art.
