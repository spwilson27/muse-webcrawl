Line wrap and word wrap

Line wrap / word wrap
FieldText editing, typography, computing
AlgorithmsGreedy wrapping; Knuth–Plass (TeX)

Line wrapping is the process of breaking a continuous line of text into multiple display lines so that it fits within the available width of a page, screen or window. Word wrap is the common form in which breaks occur at word boundaries (spaces or hyphens) wherever possible, so words are not split arbitrarily.

Concepts

A hard wrap inserts actual line-break characters into the text data, as in many plain-text emails and source files; a soft wrap is purely presentational, computed by the editor or browser from the current window width and leaving the underlying text unchanged. Most modern word processors and text editors wrap softly, with options to toggle wrapping or break long words.

Algorithms

The simplest approach is greedy: place as many words on each line as fit, then break. This can yield uneven line lengths. The Knuth–Plass algorithm, used by TeX, considers the whole paragraph globally and minimises a "badness" measure of spacing to produce even, aesthetic line breaks, often combined with hyphenation. In East Asian text, wrapping must respect kinsoku shori rules about characters that may not start or end a line.
