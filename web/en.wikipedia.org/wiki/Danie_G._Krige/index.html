Danie G. Krige

Danie Gerhardus Krige (26 August 1919 – 3 March 2013) was a South African mining engineer and statistician who pioneered the field of geostatistics. His empirical work on estimating gold ore reserves in the Witwatersrand led to the statistical estimation techniques now known as kriging, named after him by the French engineer Georges Matheron.

Work

Working on South African gold mines in the 1950s, Krige confronted the problem that ore grades were highly variable and unevenly sampled, so simple averages systematically mis-estimated the gold content of mining blocks — rich samples tended to overstate, and poor samples understate, the true value (an early recognition of regression to the mean in spatial data). In a landmark 1951 paper he set out statistical methods, using the spatial correlation between samples, to obtain best unbiased estimates of block grades.

Georges Matheron formalised Krige's approach mathematically in the 1960s and named the resulting family of linear estimation methods "kriging" in his honour. Kriging is now used worldwide in mining, petroleum geology, hydrology, environmental science and meteorology. Krige later became a professor at the University of the Witwatersrand and continued publishing into old age.
