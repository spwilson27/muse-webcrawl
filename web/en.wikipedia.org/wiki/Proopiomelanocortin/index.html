Proopiomelanocortin

Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) is a precursor polypeptide with 267 amino acid residues. It is synthesized in the pituitary gland, hypothalamus, and other tissues, and is cleaved by prohormone convertases into a number of biologically active peptides, including ACTH, α-, β-, and γ-melanocyte-stimulating hormones (MSH), β-endorphin, and β-lipotropin.

Function

The products of POMC play central roles in stress response, energy homeostasis, pigmentation, and analgesia. In the anterior pituitary, POMC is cleaved mainly to ACTH, which stimulates cortisol production in the adrenal cortex. In the hypothalamus, α-MSH derived from POMC suppresses appetite through melanocortin receptors, notably MC4R, as part of the leptin–melanocortin pathway regulating body weight.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the POMC gene cause a syndrome of severe early-onset obesity, adrenal insufficiency (due to ACTH deficiency), and red hair pigmentation (due to lack of α-MSH signaling at the MC1R receptor). Excessive POMC production by pituitary adenomas causes Cushing's disease. See also adrenocorticotropic hormone and melanocortin.
