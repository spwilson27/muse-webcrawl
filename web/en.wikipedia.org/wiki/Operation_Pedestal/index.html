Operation Pedestal was a British convoy operation in August 1942 to carry vital supplies to the besieged island of Malta during the Second World War. Known in Malta as the Santa Marija Convoy, because its survivors arrived around the Feast of the Assumption on 15 August, it is remembered as one of the most heavily contested and costly convoy battles of the war.

Background

Malta's position in the central Mediterranean threatened Axis supply routes to North Africa, but by mid-1942 the island was close to starvation and running out of aviation fuel under sustained Axis bombing. Fourteen merchant ships sailed from Gibraltar, escorted by an unusually strong force including four aircraft carriers, two battleships and numerous cruisers and destroyers.

The battle

The convoy came under relentless attack from Axis aircraft, U-boats and E-boats. The carrier HMS Eagle was torpedoed and sunk on 11 August. On 13 August the tanker SS Ohio, carrying vital fuel, was badly damaged. Five merchant ships reached Malta, including the crippled Ohio, which was towed and supported into Grand Harbour and sank after discharging her cargo. Nine merchant ships, an aircraft carrier, two cruisers and a destroyer were lost in total.

Significance

Though the losses were severe, the supplies delivered — above all Ohio's fuel — kept Malta operational as an offensive base. From Malta, aircraft and submarines resumed attacks on Axis convoys to North Africa in the critical months before the Second Battle of El Alamein and Operation Torch. The island of Malta had earlier that year been awarded the George Cross, and the convoy is commemorated annually in Malta.
