Os Mutantes

Origin: São Paulo, Brazil

Genres: Tropicália, psychedelic rock, progressive rock

Years active: 1966–1978, 2006–present

Members: Sérgio Dias and others

Past members: Arnaldo Baptista, Rita Lee, Liminha, Dinho Leme

Os Mutantes (Portuguese for "The Mutants") are an influential Brazilian rock band that were linked with the Tropicália movement, a dissident musical movement during the Brazilian military dictatorship of the late 1960s. The band was formed in São Paulo in 1966 by brothers Arnaldo Baptista (bass, keyboards, vocals) and Sérgio Dias Baptista (guitar, vocals), with lead singer Rita Lee completing the classic core trio.

Their early albums, beginning with the self-titled Os Mutantes (1968), fused Brazilian music with psychedelic rock, musique concrète, and experimental sound effects, often using homemade electronic devices built by the brothers' older brother Cláudio César Dias Baptista. Songs such as "Panis et Circenses" and "A Minha Menina" became emblematic of Tropicália alongside the work of Caetano Veloso and Gilberto Gil.

History

After collaborating on the landmark 1968 collective album Tropicália: ou Panis et Circencis, the band released a run of increasingly progressive albums. Rita Lee departed in 1972, and after Arnaldo's departure in 1973 the group evolved into a progressive rock outfit before disbanding in 1978.

Largely overlooked outside Brazil for decades, Os Mutantes were rediscovered in the 1990s by admirers such as Kurt Cobain, David Byrne (whose Luaka Bop label reissued their work), Beck, and Sean Lennon. The band re-formed in 2006, with Sérgio Dias leading new line-ups, released the album Haih or Amortecedor in 2009, and has continued to tour and record.
