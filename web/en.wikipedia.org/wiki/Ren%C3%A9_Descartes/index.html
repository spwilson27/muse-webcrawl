René Descartes

Born31 March 1596, La Haye en Touraine, France
Died11 February 1650, Stockholm, Sweden
Era17th-century philosophy
Main interestsMetaphysics, epistemology, mathematics, physics

René Descartes (1596–1650) was a French philosopher, scientist, and mathematician, widely considered a seminal figure in the emergence of modern philosophy and science. Mathematics was paramount to his method of inquiry, and he connected the previously separate fields of geometry and algebra into analytic geometry.

Descartes is often considered the first thinker to emphasize the use of reason to develop the natural sciences. His Meditations on First Philosophy (1641) remains a standard text. His famous dictum cogito, ergo sum — "I think, therefore I am" — appears in his Discourse on the Method (1637).

Philosophy

Descartes employed methodical doubt, rejecting any idea that could be doubted, to arrive at a firm foundation for knowledge. He defended mind–body dualism, holding that mind and body are distinct substances that interact.

Mathematics and science

The Cartesian coordinate system is named for him (Cartesius is his Latinized name). He also made contributions to optics and laid groundwork later developed by Newton and Leibniz.
