Pea coat

A pea coat (also called a pea jacket, pilot jacket or reefer jacket) is an outer coat of heavy, coarse woolen cloth, usually navy blue, originally worn by sailors of European and later American navies. Pea coats are characterised by short length, broad lapels, double-breasted fronts with large buttons, often of metal or horn, vertical or slash pockets, and sometimes a small strap and button at the cuff.

Etymology

The name derives from the Dutch word pijjekker or pijjakker, from pij, a coarse twilled blue cloth with a nap on one side. The coat was worn by Dutch sailors by the early 18th century, and the term entered English usage in the 1720s. The British Royal Navy version was associated with petty officers and reefers (midshipmen), hence "reefer jacket".

Naval use

The United States Navy issued pea coats from the 19th century until 2020, when the garment was retired and replaced by a synthetic parka. The classic U.S. Navy pea coat was made of heavy (about 30-ounce) dark blue wool, originally Melton cloth and later Kersey wool, with anchor-embossed buttons, corduroy-lined pockets and a vent at the back. Officers' and chief petty officers' versions, sometimes called bridge coats when longer, carried gilt buttons and shoulder boards.

Fashion

The pea coat has been repeatedly adopted by civilian fashion for both men and women since the mid-20th century, valued for its durability, warmth and classic styling. Designers have produced versions in fabrics from traditional wool to cashmere, and the coat remains a staple of winter outerwear.
