Vicente Ramón Guerrero Saldaña (1782–1831) was a Mexican military leader and statesman who fought in the Mexican War of Independence and became the second president of Mexico in 1829. Of mixed African, Indigenous and Spanish descent, he was a mule-driver and merchant from Tixtla in what is now the state of Guerrero, which is named after him. He is remembered as a hero of independence and for abolishing slavery in Mexico during his presidency.

Independence war

Guerrero joined the insurgency of José María Morelos in 1810–11 and rose to command forces in the south after Morelos's execution in 1815. Operating from the mountains of the Sierra Madre del Sur, he kept the independence cause alive when it had collapsed elsewhere, refusing a royal pardon offered (legend says) through his own father — replying, in the famous phrase, "La patria es primero" ("The homeland comes first"). In 1821 he joined Agustín de Iturbide in the Plan of Iguala after their meeting at Acatempan, and the united Army of the Three Guarantees entered Mexico City in September 1821, completing independence.

Presidency and death

After Iturbide's empire fell, Guerrero was a leading figure of the liberal Yorkino faction. Following the disputed election of 1828 and a revolt, Congress installed him as president in April 1829. His government repelled a Spanish invasion attempt at Tampico and on 15 September 1829 decreed the abolition of slavery throughout Mexico. Overthrown by his vice-president Anastasio Bustamante's Plan of Jalapa in December 1829, Guerrero returned to arms in the south, but was betrayed and captured — delivered to government forces by a Genoese ship captain he trusted — tried by court-martial at Oaxaca, and executed by firing squad at Cuilapan on 14 February 1831.

Legacy

Guerrero is one of Mexico's national heroes; his name is inscribed in gold letters on the wall of honour of the Mexican Congress, and his remains rest in the Column of Independence in Mexico City. As an Afro-Mexican leader, he is also a symbol for Mexico's African-descended communities.
