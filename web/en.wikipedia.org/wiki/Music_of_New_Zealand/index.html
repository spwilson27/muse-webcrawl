Music of New Zealand

The music of New Zealand encompasses several traditions: the indigenous music of the Māori people, introduced musical styles of European settlers, and a contemporary popular music industry whose artists — from Split Enz and Crowded House to Lorde — have achieved international success.

Māori music

Traditional Māori music (puoro Māori) was primarily vocal and functional, encompassing waiata (songs), chants, laments and the haka, a posture dance popularised worldwide by the All Blacks rugby team. Traditional instruments (taonga pūoro) include the pūtātara (conch trumpet), kōauau (flute) and pūtōrino. The 20th and 21st centuries saw a major revival of traditional instruments and the widespread adoption of the guitar in Māori performance.

Popular music

New Zealand's recording industry developed through acts such as Johnny Devlin in the 1950s, Split Enz and their successor Crowded House, and the independent Dunedin sound of the Flying Nun label in the 1980s. Later international successes include Shihad, Fat Freddy's Drop, Kimbra and Lorde. Many popular songs, such as "How Bizarre" by OMC and "Don't Dream It's Over" by Crowded House, have become enduring international hits.
