Reform and opening up

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reform and opening up (Chinese: 改革开放) is the programme of economic reforms launched in the People's Republic of China in December 1978 under Deng Xiaoping. The reforms introduced market mechanisms into the planned economy in stages, opened China to foreign trade and investment, and are widely credited with lifting hundreds of millions of people out of poverty and transforming China into the world's second-largest economy.

Background and launch

After the death of Mao Zedong in 1976 and the end of the Cultural Revolution, Deng Xiaoping consolidated power and at the Third Plenary Session of the 11th Central Committee in December 1978 the Communist Party formally endorsed economic modernisation. Early reforms decollectivised agriculture through the household responsibility system, dramatically raising farm output.

Special economic zones and opening

In 1980 China established special economic zones in Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Shantou and Xiamen, offering tax incentives and streamlined rules to attract foreign capital. Coastal cities were opened in 1984, and Pudong in Shanghai followed in 1990. The reform process culminated in China's accession to the World Trade Organization in 2001, integrating it deeply into global supply chains.

Effects

China sustained average annual GDP growth near 10% for three decades after 1978. The World Bank estimates more than 800 million people were lifted out of extreme poverty. The reforms also produced rising inequality, environmental degradation and dependence on exports and investment, prompting later policy phases emphasising "common prosperity" and domestic consumption.
