Clef

Clef
Function: Fixes pitches to staff lines/spaces
Main types: Treble (G), Bass (F), Alto (C), Tenor (C)
Etymology: French clef, "key", from Latin clavis

A clef (from French clef "key") is a musical symbol used to indicate which notes are represented by the lines and spaces of a staff. Placing a clef at the beginning of a staff assigns a particular pitch to one of its lines, thereby fixing all the others.

Modern clefs

Three clef symbols are in common use, each a stylised letter naming the note it fixes:

  • The G clef (treble clef) curls around the second line, fixing it as G4 above middle C. It is used for high instruments and voices and for the right hand of keyboard music.
  • The F clef (bass clef) places two dots around the fourth line, F3 below middle C, and is used for low instruments and the keyboard left hand.
  • The C clef centres on the line that is middle C; placed on the third line it forms the alto clef (used by the viola), and on the fourth line the tenor clef (used by cello, bassoon and trombone in their upper registers).

History

Clefs developed in the later Middle Ages from letters written at the start of the staff to orient singers in plainchant notation. The G and F clefs gradually took their present decorative forms from handwritten letter shapes, while movable C clefs allowed music for any voice range to be written with few ledger lines.

Octave clefs and transposition

A small "8" above or below a clef indicates that the music sounds an octave higher or lower than written, as in guitar and tenor voice parts. Percussion instruments use a neutral clef whose lines represent instruments rather than pitches.
