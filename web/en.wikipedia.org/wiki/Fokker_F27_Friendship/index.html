The Fokker F27 Friendship is a turboprop airliner designed and built by the Dutch aircraft manufacturer Fokker. It was the most successful European airliner of the post-war era, with 586 built in the Netherlands and a further 207 built under license in the United States by Fairchild as the FH-227.

Design and development

Fokker designed the F27 in the early 1950s as a pressurized, high-wing replacement for piston-engined airliners such as the Douglas DC-3. It was powered by two Rolls-Royce Dart turboprops and seated initially 28–40 passengers, with later stretched versions seating up to 56. Innovative features included a high-aspect-ratio wing, extensive use of metal bonding in manufacture, and pneumatic de-icing. The prototype first flew on 24 November 1955, and the type entered service in 1958 with Aer Lingus.

Operational history

The Friendship was exported worldwide and flown by airlines in Europe, the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Australasia for regional passenger, freight, and military service. Production continued until 1987. Fokker developed the modernized Fokker 50 as its successor. Although most passenger operations had ended by the 2010s, a small number of F27s remained in cargo and military use into the 21st century.

Variants

Principal variants included the Series 100/200 (basic models), the Series 300/400 Combi, the Series 500 with a stretched fuselage and large cargo door, the Series 600 quick-change freighter, and the Maritime patrol versions. The Fairchild-built F-27 and stretched FH-227 served widely in the United States.

Specifications

The F27-500 carried about 52–56 passengers over a range of roughly 1,900 km, cruising at about 480 km/h (260 kn). Length was 25.06 m and wingspan 29.0 m for the stretched version, powered by two Rolls-Royce Dart Mk 532-7 turboprops of about 2,140 hp each.
