Johann Franz Encke

Born: 23 September 1791, Hamburg

Died: 26 August 1865 (aged 73), Spandau, Prussia

Occupation: Astronomer

Known for: Encke's Comet; Encke Division of Saturn's rings

Johann Franz Encke (23 September 1791 – 26 August 1865) was a German astronomer. He became director of the Berlin Observatory in 1825 and is best remembered for his work on comets and on the orbit of the planet Venus's transits.

Comet Encke

Working from observations of a comet seen in 1786, 1795, 1805 and 1818, Encke calculated that these were apparitions of a single short-period comet with an orbital period of about 3.3 years — the shortest then known — and predicted its return in 1822, which was duly observed from Australia. Comet Encke (2P/Encke) remains one of the most famous periodic comets.

Other work

Encke used the transits of Venus of 1761 and 1769 to compute the solar parallax and hence the astronomical unit, obtaining a value close to the modern one. The gap in Saturn's A ring known as the Encke Division is named for his observations of the rings. From 1830 he also directed the publication of the Berliner Astronomisches Jahrbuch.
