The Chamber of Deputies (Portuguese: Câmara dos Deputados) is the lower house of the National Congress of Brazil, together with the Federal Senate. It is composed of 513 deputies elected by proportional representation from the states and the Federal District for four-year terms.

Composition

Seats are apportioned among Brazil's 26 states and the Federal District roughly in proportion to population, with a minimum of 8 and a maximum of 70 deputies per state. Elections use open-list proportional representation.

Powers

The Chamber initiates most legislation, approves the budget, and has exclusive powers including authorizing proceedings against the President of the Republic and electing members of the Council of the Republic. Its president is second in the presidential line of succession after the Vice President.

History

The Chamber was created by Brazil's first constitution in 1824 during the Empire of Brazil and has continued, with interruptions, through the republican era since 1889.
