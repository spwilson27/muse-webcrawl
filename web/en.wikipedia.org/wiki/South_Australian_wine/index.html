South Australian wine

The South Australian wine industry is responsible for more than almost half of Australia's total wine production. South Australia has a venerable history in wine, with vines first planted in the 1830s and 1840s, shortly after the colony's founding. The state is home to some of the oldest continuously producing vineyards in the world, in part because it escaped the phylloxera epidemic that devastated vineyards elsewhere, and strict quarantine rules remain in place to protect it.

Regions

South Australia's wine zones include some of Australia's best-known regions:

  • Barossa Valley — famous for powerful old-vine Shiraz, including iconic labels such as Penfolds Grange (first made at Magill Estate near Adelaide) and Henschke's Hill of Grace in the neighbouring Eden Valley.
  • McLaren Vale — a Mediterranean-climate region south of Adelaide known for Shiraz, Grenache, and Cabernet Sauvignon.
  • Clare Valley — noted for dry Riesling and Shiraz.
  • Coonawarra — a small region on the Limestone Coast prized for Cabernet Sauvignon grown in its distinctive terra rossa soil.
  • Adelaide Hills and Langhorne Creek — cooler and bulk-producing regions respectively.
  • Riverland — a warm irrigated region producing a large share of Australia's everyday wine.

Grape varieties

Shiraz is the state's signature red grape, followed by Cabernet Sauvignon, Grenache, and Merlot. Leading whites include Riesling, Chardonnay, and Sauvignon Blanc. Old bush vines of Grenache and Mourvèdre, some planted in the nineteenth century, are a distinctive heritage resource.

Industry

Adelaide is a member of the Great Wine Capitals Global Network, and the University of Adelaide and the Australian Wine Research Institute at Waite are major centres of viticultural research. Major companies with South Australian roots include Penfolds (part of Treasury Wine Estates), Jacob's Creek, Wolf Blass, and Yalumba, Australia's oldest family-owned winery, founded in 1849 in the Barossa.
