Operation Hurricane

Country: United Kingdom

Date: 3 October 1952

Location: Montebello Islands, Western Australia

Yield: Approximately 25 kilotons

Device type: Plutonium implosion weapon

Operation Hurricane was the first test of a British atomic device. A plutonium implosion device was detonated in the lagoon at the Montebello Islands in Western Australia on 3 October 1952. The device was exploded inside the hull of the frigate HMS Plym, anchored in about 12 metres of water, partly to simulate the effects of a bomb smuggled into a harbour in a ship.

Background

Britain had contributed significantly to the wartime Manhattan Project, but the US McMahon Act of 1946 cut off nuclear cooperation. The Attlee government decided in 1947 to develop an independent British atomic weapon, under the codename HER (High Explosive Research), led by William Penney. The device tested in Hurricane used a design derived from the American Fat Man weapon, with improvements including a levitated pit.

The test

The explosion, with a yield of about 25 kilotons, vaporized the Plym and excavated a crater in the lagoon floor. The test made the United Kingdom the world's third nuclear power, after the United States and the Soviet Union. Personnel — some 15,000 British and Australian servicemen and scientists participated in the broader Australian test program that followed.

Aftermath

Hurricane was followed by further British test series in Australia (Totem, Mosaic, Buffalo, and Antler) at Emu Field and Maralinga, and later by thermonuclear tests at Christmas Island (Operation Grapple, 1957–58). The Australian tests later became controversial over radioactive contamination and the exposure of Aboriginal communities and service personnel; a Royal Commission examined the program in 1984–85, and the Maralinga site underwent major remediation.
