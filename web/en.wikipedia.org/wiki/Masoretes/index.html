Masoretes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Masoretes (Hebrew: בעלי המסורה, Ba'alei ha-Masora) were groups of Jewish scribe-scholars of the early Middle Ages, working between roughly the 6th and 10th centuries CE, who devoted themselves to preserving, standardising and annotating the text of the Hebrew Bible. Their work produced the Masoretic Text, the authoritative Hebrew text of the Tanakh used in Judaism to this day.

The Masorah

The name derives from the Hebrew word masorah, meaning "tradition" or "that which is handed down". Because the biblical text was written with consonants only and its correct reading depended on oral tradition, the Masoretes devised a system of diacritical marks — the niqqud or vowel points placed above and below the letters, together with cantillation marks (te'amim) indicating how the text was to be chanted — without altering the sacred consonantal text itself.

Around the text they wrote thousands of marginal notes, the Masorah parva and Masorah magna, recording such details as how often a word or spelling occurs, which forms are unique, and where a written word (ketiv) is to be read differently (qere). These safeguards were intended to prevent scribal error from ever creeping into the text.

Schools and families

The principal centres of Masoretic activity were Tiberias in Galilee, Jerusalem and Babylonia. Three systems of vocalisation competed — Tiberian, Babylonian and Palestinian — of which the Tiberian prevailed. In Tiberias the families of ben Asher and ben Naphtali became famous in the 9th and 10th centuries; the differences between their editions, though small, were carefully catalogued. Maimonides later pronounced the codex of Aaron ben Moses ben Asher the most accurate, and it is traditionally identified with the Aleppo Codex (c. 920 CE), the oldest surviving complete manuscript of the Hebrew Bible produced under Masoretic supervision. The Leningrad Codex (1008 CE), the oldest complete codex in existence, follows the ben Asher tradition and underlies modern printed editions.

Legacy

Thanks to the Masoretes, the biblical text was transmitted with a fidelity unmatched by any other ancient literature of comparable length: comparison with the Dead Sea Scrolls, written a thousand years earlier, shows a remarkable degree of stability. Their systems of vocalisation and accentuation also remain the basis for the study of biblical Hebrew pronunciation and grammar.
