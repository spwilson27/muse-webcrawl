Discourse analysis

Discourse analysis (DA), or discourse studies, is an approach to the analysis of written, vocal, or sign language use, or any significant semiotic event. It draws on disciplines including linguistics, anthropology, sociology, cognitive psychology, social psychology, international relations, and communication studies.

Overview

Unlike traditional linguistics, which focuses on the sentence as the largest unit, discourse analysis studies larger patterns of language in texts and conversations, and the social and cultural contexts in which language is used. The term was first used in a 1952 paper by Zellig Harris. Analysts examine how language constructs identities, relationships, and systems of knowledge.

Approaches

  • Conversation analysis — studies the structure of talk in interaction.
  • Critical discourse analysis (CDA) — examines how discourse reproduces power and inequality, associated with scholars such as Norman Fairclough and Teun van Dijk.
  • Foucauldian discourse analysis — draws on Michel Foucault's concept of discourse as systems of statements that construct objects and subjects.
  • Pragmatics and sociolinguistics — study language in context and social variation.
