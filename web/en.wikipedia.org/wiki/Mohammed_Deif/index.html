Mohammed Deif (born Mohammed Diab Ibrahim al-Masri; 12 August 1965 – 13 July 2024) was a Palestinian militant and the commander of the Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades, the military wing of Hamas, from 2002 until his death. One of Israel's most wanted men for three decades, he survived numerous assassination attempts and became a near-mythical figure to many Palestinians, known by the nom de guerre "Deif" — "the Guest" — for his habit of moving nightly between the homes of supporters to evade capture.

Early life and rise

Deif was born in the Khan Yunis refugee camp in the Egyptian-administered Gaza Strip in 1965 and studied science at the Islamic University of Gaza, where he was active in the Muslim Brotherhood milieu from which Hamas emerged in 1987. Trained in bomb-making in the early 1990s by Yahya Ayyash ("the Engineer"), he rose in the Qassam Brigades during the First and Second Intifadas. Israel and the United States accused him of planning and directing numerous attacks against Israelis, including suicide bombings in the 1990s and the development of the Qassam rocket programme and Gaza's tunnel network. He became overall commander after Israel killed his predecessor Salah Shehadeh in July 2002.

Assassination attempts and October 7 attack

Deif survived at least five Israeli assassination attempts between 2001 and 2014, sustaining serious injuries; an August 2014 airstrike during that year's Gaza war killed his wife and infant son. Rare photographs and audio recordings were almost his only public traces. On the morning of 7 October 2023, a recorded statement by Deif announced "Operation Al-Aqsa Flood", the Hamas-led attack on Israel in which about 1,200 people were killed and some 250 taken hostage; Israel, the US and the EU designated him one of the attack's principal planners. In May 2024 the prosecutor of the International Criminal Court sought an arrest warrant for Deif for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity.

Death

On 13 July 2024 Israel bombed a compound in the Al-Mawasi area of Khan Yunis in a strike it said targeted Deif; Gaza health authorities reported at least 90 Palestinians killed. Israel announced on 1 August 2024 that Deif had been killed in the strike. Hamas initially denied this but confirmed his death on 30 January 2025, hailing him as a martyr.
