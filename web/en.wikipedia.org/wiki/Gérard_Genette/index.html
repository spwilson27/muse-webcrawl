Gérard Genette, French literary theorist

Gérard Genette (7 June 1930 – 11 May 2018) was a French literary theorist, associated in particular with the structuralist movement and with figures such as Roland Barthes and Claude Lévi-Strauss, from whom he adapted the concept of bricolage. He is regarded as the founder of modern narratology, the systematic study of narrative structure.

Life and career

Genette studied at the École Normale Supérieure and taught at the Sorbonne. With Tzvetan Todorov he founded the journal Poétique in 1970 and directed the collection of the same name at Éditions du Seuil. He later became director of studies at the École des hautes études en sciences sociales.

Work

Narrative Discourse

His most influential work, Figures III (1972), translated as Narrative Discourse: An Essay in Method (1980), developed a systematic vocabulary for analysing narrative based on a close reading of Marcel Proust's À la recherche du temps perdu. Genette distinguished story (histoire), narrative (récit) and narrating (narration), and analysed narrative under the categories of order (analepsis and prolepsis), duration, frequency, mood (including focalization) and voice (including the levels of extradiegetic, intradiegetic and metadiegetic narration, and the distinction between homodiegetic and heterodiegetic narrators).

Transtextuality and the palimpsest

In Palimpsestes (1982) Genette mapped the relations between texts under the general term transtextuality: intertextuality (quotation, allusion, plagiarism), paratextuality (titles, prefaces and other thresholds), metatextuality (commentary), architextuality (genre) and hypertextuality (a text's transformation of an earlier hypotext, through parody, pastiche, sequel or translation). His Seuils (1987; Paratexts: Thresholds of Interpretation) remains the standard study of the paratext.
