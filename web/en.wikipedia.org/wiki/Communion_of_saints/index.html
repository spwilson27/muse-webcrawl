Communion of saints

The communion of saints (Latin: communio sanctorum) is a Christian doctrine describing the spiritual union of the members of the Church — the living, the dead in heaven, and, in Catholic teaching, the souls in purgatory — bound together in Christ. The phrase appears in the Apostles' Creed.

Part of Christian theology
Source: Apostles' Creed
Latin: communio sanctorum

Interpretations

In Roman Catholic theology, the communion comprises the Church Militant on earth, the Church Penitent in purgatory, and the Church Triumphant in heaven, united by charity and able to assist one another through prayer and intercession. The doctrine underlies prayers for the dead and the invocation of saints.

Most Protestant traditions interpret the communion primarily as the fellowship of believers, living and departed, in Christ, and many reject purgatory and saintly intercession while affirming a shared bond among Christians. Eastern Orthodox teaching similarly stresses the unity of the earthly and heavenly church, expressed especially in the Divine Liturgy.

Origins

The phrase entered the Apostles' Creed by about the 5th century. Sanctorum may originally have referred to holy persons, though some scholars have suggested it could also encompass holy things, notably shared participation in the Eucharist.
