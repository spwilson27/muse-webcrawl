Esketamine

Brand name: Spravato (antidepressant); Ketanest (anaesthetic)
Type: S-enantiomer of ketamine; NMDA receptor antagonist
Uses: Treatment-resistant depression; anaesthesia
Routes: Intranasal, intravenous
Legal status: Schedule III (US); prescription-only elsewhere

Esketamine, sold under the brand name Spravato among others, is the S(+) enantiomer of ketamine. It is a dissociative hallucinogen and NMDA receptor antagonist used as a general anaesthetic and, more recently, as an antidepressant for treatment-resistant depression. It is administered as a nasal spray for depression and by injection for anaesthesia.

Medical uses

Depression

In March 2019 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved esketamine nasal spray, in conjunction with an oral antidepressant, for adults with treatment-resistant depression (TRD) — major depressive disorder that has not responded to at least two other treatments. In 2020 the approval was extended to depressive symptoms in adults with major depressive disorder with acute suicidal ideation or behaviour. Because of risks of sedation, dissociation, and misuse, it is available only through a restricted distribution system (REMS) and must be administered in a certified healthcare setting with at least two hours of monitoring. The European Medicines Agency approved it for treatment-resistant depression in late 2019.

Anaesthesia

Esketamine has been used as a general anaesthetic (brand name Ketanest) in Germany and other countries since the 1990s, particularly in emergency medicine and for patients at risk of hypotension or bronchospasm, since it tends to maintain blood pressure and airway reflexes.

Pharmacology

Esketamine is the more potent enantiomer of ketamine at the NMDA receptor, with roughly twice the anaesthetic and analgesic potency of the racemic mixture. Blockade of NMDA receptors on inhibitory interneurons is thought to produce a glutamate surge, increased AMPA receptor activation, and release of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), rapidly promoting synaptogenesis — a proposed mechanism for its unusually fast antidepressant action, within hours rather than the weeks typical of conventional antidepressants. Its principal metabolite is noresketamine.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include dissociation, dizziness, nausea, sedation, vertigo, anxiety, and transient increases in blood pressure. Long-term or frequent use carries risks seen with ketamine, including urinary tract damage (ulcerative cystitis) and abuse liability; esketamine is a Schedule III controlled substance in the United States.

History

Esketamine was introduced for medical use as an anaesthetic in Germany in 1997. Its development as an antidepressant by Janssen Pharmaceuticals followed the discovery in the 2000s that subanaesthetic intravenous ketamine produced rapid antidepressant effects. Its approval in 2019 made it the first NMDA-antagonist antidepressant and the first new drug class for depression in decades, though debate continues over the strength of its trial evidence and long-term safety.
