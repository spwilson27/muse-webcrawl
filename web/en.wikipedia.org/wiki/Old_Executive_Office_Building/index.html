The Eisenhower Executive Office Building (EEOB), originally known as the Old Executive Office Building (OEOB) and earlier as the State, War, and Navy Building, is a U.S. government building situated just west of the White House in the U.S. capital of Washington, D.C. Maintained by the General Services Administration, the building currently houses the Executive Office of the President, including the Office of Management and Budget and the National Security Council, as well as the ceremonial office of the vice president.

Architecture and history

Built between 1871 and 1888 to house the State, War and Navy Departments, the granite building was designed by Alfred B. Mullett in the flamboyant French Second Empire style, with slate mansard roofs, 900 Doric columns and lavish interiors — including the ornate Indian Treaty Room, originally the Navy Department's library and reception room. At completion it was the largest office building in Washington. Mark Twain famously called it "the ugliest building in America," but tastes changed; it is now a National Historic Landmark and considered one of the finest surviving examples of its style.

The War and Navy Departments left for new headquarters in the 1930s and 1940s, and the White House staff, outgrowing the West Wing, progressively took over the rooms. President Dwight D. Eisenhower held the first televised presidential press conference in the Indian Treaty Room in 1955. In 1999 the building was renamed in Eisenhower's honour. The Vice President's ceremonial office, with its historic desk signed by past occupants, hosts bill signings and press events. A fire on December 19, 2007, damaged the vice-presidential suite but was contained.
