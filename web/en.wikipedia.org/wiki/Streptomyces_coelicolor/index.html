Streptomyces coelicolor is a soil-dwelling Gram-positive bacterium belonging to the genus Streptomyces, the filamentous actinomycetes famous for producing most of the antibiotics used in medicine. S. coelicolor strain A3(2) is the model organism for the genus and the best-studied streptomycete; its name refers to the "colour of the sky" (Latin coelicolor) from the blue pigment of one of its antibiotics.

Life cycle

Like other streptomycetes, S. coelicolor has a complex multicellular life cycle unusual among bacteria. A spore germinates to form a branching network of hyphae (the vegetative mycelium) that grows through soil. In response to nutrient limitation, the colony raises aerial hyphae that develop into chains of spores, while the vegetative mycelium undergoes programmed cell death to feed the aerial growth.

Antibiotics and secondary metabolism

S. coelicolor produces several well-characterized antibiotics, whose biosynthetic gene clusters serve as paradigms for the field: the blue-pigmented actinorhodin, the red undecylprodigiosin, the calcium-dependent antibiotic (CDA), and methylenomycin (encoded on the giant linear plasmid SCP1). Production of these compounds is coordinated with morphological differentiation through regulatory networks involving the bld and whi genes.

Genome

The complete genome of S. coelicolor A3(2) was published in Nature in 2002 (Bentley et al.). Its linear chromosome is 8,667,507 base pairs with a high G+C content (about 72%) and roughly 7,800 protein-coding genes — at the time one of the largest bacterial genomes known — including more than 20 gene clusters for secondary metabolites, most of them "cryptic" under laboratory conditions. The strain also carries the linear plasmids SCP1 and SCP2.

Importance

As the genetic model for antibiotic-producing bacteria, S. coelicolor has been central to understanding secondary metabolism, bacterial development, and the regulation of gene expression, and genome mining of streptomycetes continues to reveal new natural products of potential medical value.
