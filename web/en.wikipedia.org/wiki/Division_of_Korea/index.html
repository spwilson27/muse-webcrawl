Division of Korea

Began1945 (de facto); 1948 (two states founded)
Dividing line38th parallel; later the Korean Demilitarized Zone
StatesNorth Korea (DPRK) and South Korea (ROK)

The division of Korea began in 1945 at the end of World War II, when the Korean Peninsula was divided into two zones along the 38th parallel: a northern zone administered by the Soviet Union and a southern zone administered by the United States. The division became permanent in 1948, when two separate governments were established: the communist Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea) in the north and the Republic of Korea (South Korea) in the south.

Background

Korea had been annexed by the Empire of Japan in 1910. With Japan's defeat imminent in August 1945, the United States and the Soviet Union agreed to divide Korea at the 38th parallel, initially as a temporary measure for accepting the Japanese surrender. The Cairo Declaration of 1943 had promised that Korea would become free and independent "in due course."

Korean War

On 25 June 1950, North Korea invaded the South, beginning the Korean War. United Nations forces led by the United States intervened on behalf of the South, while China supported the North. The war ended in stalemate with the Korean Armistice Agreement of 27 July 1953, which established the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) near the original dividing line. No peace treaty was ever signed, so the two Koreas remain technically at war.

Aftermath

The division separated millions of families and produced two radically divergent states: South Korea developed into a prosperous democracy, while North Korea became an isolated, heavily militarized authoritarian state under the Kim dynasty. Reunification remains an official goal of both governments, though inter-Korean relations have fluctuated between engagement and confrontation.
