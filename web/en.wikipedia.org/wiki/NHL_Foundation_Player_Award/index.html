NHL Foundation Player Award


NHL Foundation Player Award
Sport: Ice hockey
League: National Hockey League
First awarded: 1997–98
Last awarded: 2016–17

The NHL Foundation Player Award was awarded annually to a National Hockey League (NHL) player "who applies the core values of hockey — commitment, perseverance and teamwork — to enrich the lives of people in his community". The award was presented by the NHL Foundation.

History

The award was first presented after the 1997–98 season, with Kelly Chase of the St. Louis Blues as the inaugural recipient. The winner received a grant of US$25,000 from the NHL Foundation to direct toward a charity or community organization of his choice.

Selection and discontinuation

Nominees were submitted by NHL clubs and the winner selected by a panel. Recipients included Vincent Lecavalier, Jarome Iginla, Ryan Miller, Mike Fisher, and P. K. Subban. After the 2016–17 season, the award was discontinued as part of a revamp of the NHL's awards presentation; its community-service recognition was folded into the King Clancy Memorial Trophy criteria.

Notable winners

Early winners included Adam Graves, known for extensive charitable work in New York, and Olaf Kölzig, recognized for work with children's hospitals. The final recipient was Travis Hamonic (2016–17), honoured for programs supporting children who had lost a parent.
