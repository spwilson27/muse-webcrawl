Bendix Trophy

The Bendix Trophy was a United States transcontinental air race that ran from 1931 to 1962 (with an interruption during the Second World War). Sponsored by industrialist Vincent Bendix, founder of the Bendix Corporation, the race was the centrepiece of the National Air Races at Cleveland, Ohio, and was intended to encourage the development of fast, reliable landplanes. Competitors typically flew from Burbank, California, to Cleveland, with some editions finishing onward at Bendix, New Jersey, or New York.

History

The first race, in 1931, was won by Jimmy Doolittle in a Laird Super Solution at an average speed of about 223 mph. Winners through the 1930s included many of the era's most famous aviators: Roscoe Turner won three times (1933, 1938, 1939), Jacqueline Cochran won in 1938, and Louise Thaden won the women's division in 1936. Frank Fuller won in 1937 and 1939 in the Seversky SEV-S2.

Postwar jet races

After the war the race was revived for jet aircraft, with routes from California to Cleveland and later to other finish points. Military pilots flying aircraft such as the F-84 Thunderjet and F-86 Sabre set transcontinental speed records in Bendix competition; in 1955 Captain John L. "Jack" Krings's generation of pilots flew the race in under four hours coast to coast. The last Bendix Trophy race was held in 1962.

Legacy

The trophy itself is displayed at the National Air and Space Museum. The Bendix race is remembered alongside the Thompson and Schneider trophies as one of the great speed competitions that drove interwar aviation technology.
