Refusal of work

Refusal of work is the behaviour in which a person refuses regular employment. With or without a political or philosophical program, it has been practiced by various subcultures and individuals. It is frequently engaged in by those who critique the concept of work, and it has a long history. Radical political positions have openly advocateded refusal of work. From within Marxism it was advocateded by Paul Lafargue and the Italian workerist/autonomists (e.g. Antonio Negri, Mario Tronti); within anarchism, especially by Bob Black and the post-left anarchist tendency (although technologically-oriented critics have disputed it), and within the Situationist International (e.g. Raoul Vaneigem).

Work refusal, work avoidance, and laziness all have long histories in human civilization, and have been observed in Buddhism, in Ancient Greek civilization, and during the 19th century in Paris, among the upper classes of young people.

History

The essay "In Praise of Idleness" by Bertrand Russell and the book The Right to Be Lazy by Paul Lafargue are classic treatments of the subject. Lafargue, a Cuban-born French revolutionary Marxist journalist, literary critic, political writer and activist, argued that laziness, combined with human creativity, is an important source of human progress.

The Italian workerist (operaist) movement of the 1960s and 1970s developed the refusal of work into a political strategy: rather than accepting the terms of capitalist production, workers should refuse work itself as a means of undermining the wage relation. Thinkers such as Mario Tronti argued that the working class's refusal to work is the driving force behind capitalist development, because capital responds to workers' struggles with technological innovation and reorganization.

Criticism

Critics of work refusal argue that it is impractical, that modern societies depend on widespread participation in labour, and that refusal strategies shift the burden of work onto others. Supporters respond that automation and productivity gains make a reduced working life increasingly feasible, and that refusal is a demand for a different organization of social life rather than simple idleness.
