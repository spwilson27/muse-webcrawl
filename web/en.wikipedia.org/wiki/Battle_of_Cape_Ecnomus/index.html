Battle of Cape Ecnomus

The Battle of Cape Ecnomus was a naval battle fought off the southern coast of Sicily in 256 BC during the First Punic War between the fleets of Carthage and the Roman Republic. A Roman fleet of about 330 warships, carrying an invasion army under the consuls Marcus Atilius Regulus and Lucius Manlius Vulso Longus, was intercepted by a Carthaginian fleet of about 350 ships under Hanno the Great and Hamilcar. The Romans won a hard-fought victory, sinking or capturing over a hundred Carthaginian vessels, and cleared the way to land in Africa. By the number of combatants involved — Polybius gives figures implying some 290,000 men — Ecnomus is often described as one of the largest naval battles in history.

Background

After eight years of war in Sicily, Rome resolved to carry the war to Africa and force Carthage to terms. The Romans built a great fleet of quinqueremes fitted with the corvus, a spiked boarding bridge that negated Carthaginian superiority in seamanship. The invasion fleet sailed from Sicily in a protective wedge formation: two squadrons in echelon forming the point, the horse transports towed behind, and a third and fourth squadron closing the base of the triangle.

Battle

The Carthaginians, deployed in a long line, executed a plan to split the Roman formation: their centre squadrons feigned retreat, drawing the leading Roman squadrons away, while the Carthaginian wings wheeled in against the Roman rear squadron and the transports. The battle thus fragmented into three separate engagements. The Romans, fighting with the corvus in close action, defeated the Carthaginian centre in the pursuit, then turned back: Regulus and Vulso relieved the hard-pressed rear squadron and trapped much of the Carthaginian left wing against the shore. Carthaginian losses, per Polybius, were 30 ships sunk and 64 captured with their crews; Rome lost 24 ships sunk.

Aftermath

The Roman fleet repaired in Sicily and then landed the invasion army near Clupea (Aspis) in Africa, beginning Regulus's campaign, which won initial victories before ending in disaster at the Battle of the Bagradas River in 255 BC. Ecnomus nevertheless confirmed Rome's emergence as a naval power capable of defeating Carthage in a full-scale fleet action.
