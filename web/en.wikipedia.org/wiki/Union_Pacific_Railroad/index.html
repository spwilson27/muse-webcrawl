The Union Pacific Railroad (reporting mark UP) is a Class I freight-hauling railroad that operates about 32,000 miles of track in 23 states west of Chicago and New Orleans, making it the largest railroad network in the United States. A subsidiary of the Union Pacific Corporation headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska, it links the Pacific Coast and Gulf Coast ports with the Midwest and carries chemicals, coal, agricultural products, intermodal containers, and automotive traffic.

History

The company was chartered by the Pacific Railroad Act of 1862, signed by President Abraham Lincoln, to build westward from the Missouri River as the eastern half of the first transcontinental railroad. Construction, much of it by Irish immigrant labour, raced the Central Pacific eastward from Sacramento; the lines met at Promontory Summit, Utah, on May 10, 1869, where the golden spike was driven. The construction was financed through the Crédit Mobilier company, whose stock-distribution scandal of 1872 became one of the great political scandals of the Gilded Age.

Bankrupt in the Panic of 1893, the railroad was reorganised by financier E. H. Harriman, who modernised it and briefly gained control of much of the western rail network. In the 1980s and 1990s Union Pacific absorbed the Missouri Pacific, Western Pacific, Chicago and North Western, and finally the Southern Pacific (1996), creating the present system.

Operations and heritage

Union Pacific is famed for its giant steam locomotives, including the "Big Boy" 4-8-8-4 class; one, No. 4014, was restored to excursion service in 2019. The railroad maintains a heritage fleet and operates Bailey Yard in North Platte, Nebraska — the world's largest rail classification yard. In 2019 it adopted Precision Scheduled Railroading principles under its Unified Plan 2020.
