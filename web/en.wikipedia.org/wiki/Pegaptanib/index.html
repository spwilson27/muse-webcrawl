Pegaptanib

Trade name: Macugen
Type: RNA aptamer
Target: VEGF165
Use: Neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration
Administration: Intravitreal injection

Pegaptanib (trade name Macugen) is an anti-angiogenic drug used to treat neovascular ("wet") age-related macular degeneration (AMD). Developed by NeXstar Pharmaceuticals (later Eyetech) and marketed by Eyetech and Pfizer, it was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in December 2004 — the first aptamer therapeutic approved for use in humans.

Mechanism

Pegaptanib is a pegylated RNA aptamer — a short, folded single-stranded nucleic acid selected (by the SELEX process) to bind with high affinity to the 165-amino-acid isoform of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF165), a protein that drives the growth and leakage of abnormal blood vessels beneath the retina in wet AMD. By blocking this isoform, pegaptanib slows the progression of vision loss. The attached polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains protect the aptamer from rapid degradation and clearance.

Clinical use

The drug is given by intravitreal injection (0.3 mg into the vitreous of the eye) every six weeks. In the pivotal VISION trials, about 70 per cent of treated patients lost fewer than 15 letters of visual acuity over a year, compared with about 55 per cent of controls — it slowed loss rather than improving vision. Adverse effects are mostly those of the injection procedure, including a small risk of endophthalmitis, retinal detachment and cataract.

Decline

Pegaptanib was rapidly eclipsed by ranibizumab (Lucentis) and off-label bevacizumab, antibody-based anti-VEGF drugs that bind all VEGF-A isoforms and can improve vision, and later by aflibercept. Macugen's sales fell sharply, and it is now little used, though it remains a landmark as the first approved aptamer drug.
