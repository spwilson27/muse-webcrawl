IDEO is an international design and consulting firm founded in Palo Alto, California, in 1991. The company uses a design thinking approach to design products, services, environments, and digital experiences. IDEO's headquarters are in San Francisco, with other major offices in Cambridge, Chicago, London, New York City, Shanghai, and Tokyo.

IDEO's San Francisco studio space.

History

IDEO was formed by the merger of three established design firms: David Kelley Design, founded by Stanford University professor David Kelley in 1978; ID Two, founded in San Francisco in 1980 by Bill Moggridge, co-designer of the first laptop computer, the GRiD Compass; and Matrix Product Design, founded in 1983 by Mike Nuttall. The merger was backed by the furniture company Steelcase.

The company gained widespread recognition through high-profile projects including the first manufacturable mouse for Apple (developed by David Kelley Design), the Palm V handheld computer, and the shopping cart redesign featured in a 1999 episode of ABC's Nightline, which became a widely cited demonstration of the design process. In 2016, IDEO joined the kyu collective, a group of creative companies.

Design approach

IDEO is closely associated with the popularization of "design thinking", a human-centred problem-solving methodology emphasizing observation of users, rapid prototyping, and interdisciplinary teams. Co-founder David Kelley also founded the Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford (the "d.school") in 2005. Former CEO Tim Brown's 2009 book Change by Design helped bring the methodology to a business audience.

Notable work

  • The first Apple mouse (1980) and the Apple Lisa mouse
  • The GRiD Compass laptop lineage through ID Two
  • Palm V personal digital assistant (1999)
  • Bank of America's "Keep the Change" savings programme
  • Design work for healthcare, education, and food systems, including OpenIDEO, an open innovation platform launched in 2010
