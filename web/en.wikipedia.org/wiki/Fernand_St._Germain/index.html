Fernand Joseph St. Germain (January 9, 1928 – August 16, 2014) was an American politician and attorney who served as a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from Rhode Island from 1961 to 1989. He is best remembered as the principal author of the Garn–St. Germain Depository Institutions Act of 1982, a major piece of banking deregulation legislation, and for his central role in the congressional events surrounding the savings and loan crisis.

Early life and education

St. Germain was born in Blackstone, Massachusetts, and raised in Woonsocket, Rhode Island, in a French-Canadian family. He attended parochial schools, served in the United States Army from 1946 to 1949, and later graduated from Providence College and Boston University School of Law. He was admitted to the Rhode Island bar and entered private legal practice in Woonsocket.

Political career

St. Germain served in the Rhode Island House of Representatives from 1953 to 1961. In 1960 he was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives, succeeding Aime Forand in Rhode Island's 1st congressional district. He was re-elected thirteen times, serving fourteen consecutive terms.

As chairman of the House Banking, Finance and Urban Affairs Committee's subcommittee on financial institutions, St. Germain helped steer the Depository Institutions Deregulation and Monetary Control Act of 1980 and co-authored the Garn–St. Germain Act of 1982, which deregulated the savings and loan industry and permitted interstate bank acquisitions of failing institutions.

Savings and loan controversy

In the late 1980s St. Germain faced ethics scrutiny over his close relationship with savings and loan executives and allegations concerning his personal finances. Although a House Ethics Committee inquiry did not result in formal discipline, the controversy damaged him politically. He lost the 1988 Democratic primary to Ronald Machtley, who went on to win the general election.

Later life and death

After leaving Congress, St. Germain worked as a lobbyist and attorney in Washington, D.C. He died in Newport, Rhode Island, on August 16, 2014, at the age of 86.
