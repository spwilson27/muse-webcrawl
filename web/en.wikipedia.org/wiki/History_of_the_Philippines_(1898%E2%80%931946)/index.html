History of the Philippines (1898–1946)

The history of the Philippines from 1898 to 1946 covers the transition from Spanish to American colonial rule and the achievement of independence. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence, but the Treaty of Paris transferred the islands to the United States.

Philippine–American War and colonial rule

The First Philippine Republic fought the United States in the Philippine–American War (1899–1902), which cost hundreds of thousands of Filipino lives. American administrations built schools, infrastructure, and colonial institutions while suppressing independence movements, and gradually introduced Filipino self-government through the Philippine Assembly (1907) and the Jones Law (1916).

Commonwealth and occupation

The Tydings–McDuffie Act (1934) created the Commonwealth of the Philippines under President Manuel L. Quezon as a ten-year transition to independence. Japan occupied the islands from 1942 to 1945; liberation devastated Manila.

Independence

On 4 July 1946, the United States recognised Philippine independence, with Manuel Roxas as the first president of the Third Republic. See also History of the Philippines.
