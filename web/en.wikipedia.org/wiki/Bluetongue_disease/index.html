CauseBluetongue virus (an orbivirus)

HostsSheep, cattle, goats, deer, other ruminants
TypeNon-contagious, insect-borne viral disease

Bluetongue disease is a non-contagious, insect-borne viral disease of ruminants, chiefly sheep, caused by bluetongue virus and transmitted by biting midges of the genus Culicoides.

Signs

In sheep the disease causes fever, swelling of the face and tongue, excessive salivation and lameness; the tongue may become swollen and cyanotic — "blue" — giving the disease its name. Cattle are usually infected without obvious illness but act as a reservoir. Mortality in sheep can be high in susceptible flocks.

Distribution and control

Bluetongue was historically confined to warmer regions where the midges thrive, but outbreaks have spread into northern Europe, notably from 2006 onward, aided by climate change and wind-borne midges. Control relies on vaccination, movement restrictions and vector monitoring; more than two dozen serotypes of the virus are known, complicating vaccine design.
