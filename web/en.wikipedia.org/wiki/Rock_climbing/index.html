Rock climbing

Rock climbing is a sport in which participants climb up, down or across natural rock formations or indoor climbing walls, using hands and feet and (usually) protective equipment. Styles include bouldering, sport climbing on fixed bolts, traditional climbing with removable gear, free soloing without protection, and big-wall and alpine climbing as part of mountaineering.

Modern rock climbing grew from Victorian mountaineering and from training grounds like the Lake District, Fontainebleau and the Elbe Sandstone, maturing in Yosemite's golden age (1950s–70s: Royal Robbins, Warren Harding, Lynn Hill's free ascent of The Nose, 1993). Grades (YDS 5.x, French 6a–9c) chart progress toward the current hardest ascents (Adam Ondra's Silence, 9c). Competition climbing debuted at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics; gyms worldwide have made the sport mainstream while outdoor crags manage access and conservation.
