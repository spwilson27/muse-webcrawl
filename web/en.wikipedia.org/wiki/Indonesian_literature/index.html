Indonesian literature

Languages: Indonesian, Javanese, Sundanese, Malay, Dutch and others

Notable figures: Pramoedya Ananta Toer, Chairil Anwar, Nh. Dini

Indonesian literature is literature written in the territory of modern Indonesia, in Indonesian and in the archipelago's many regional languages. It encompasses centuries of oral tradition, classical Malay and Javanese texts, and a modern literature that developed in the early 20th century.

Early modern period

The colonial publishing house Balai Pustaka (founded 1917) shaped early Indonesian novels, and the 1928 Youth Pledge elevated Malay-based Indonesian as the national language. Poets such as Chairil Anwar led the "Generation of 1945".

Modern literature

Pramoedya Ananta Toer, author of the Buru Quartet, is Indonesia's most internationally known novelist. Contemporary writers include Eka Kurniawan and Leila S. Chudori, whose works have been widely translated.
