Mitakshara

The Mitākṣarā is a celebrated commentary (vivaraṇa) on the Yājñavalkya Smṛti, written by the jurist Vijñāneśvara in the late 11th or early 12th century at the court of the Western Chalukya king Vikramaditya VI, likely at Kalyana in present-day Karnataka.

School of law

The Mitakshara gave its name to one of the two principal schools of Hindu law governing inheritance, the other being the Dāyabhāga of Jimutavahana, which prevailed in Bengal and Assam. Under the Mitakshara system, a son acquired an interest by birth in ancestral joint family property, and the joint family with its coparcenary structure was central to property law.

Influence

During British rule the Mitakshara was translated and applied by colonial courts across most of India, making it one of the most authoritative texts of classical Hindu law. Much of its inheritance law was superseded after independence by the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, though its concepts continue to influence Indian jurisprudence and scholarship.
