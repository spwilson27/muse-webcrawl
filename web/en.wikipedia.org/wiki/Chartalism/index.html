Chartalism

Chartalism
Key figuresGeorg Friedrich Knapp, Alfred Mitchell-Innes, Abba Lerner
Key workThe State Theory of Money (1905)
RelatedModern Monetary Theory, Post-Keynesian economics

Chartalism is a theory of money holding that money originates from the state's power to impose tax obligations payable in a token the state itself issues, and that money's value derives from that legal and fiscal authority rather than from any intrinsic commodity value. The name comes from the Latin charta (paper token), contrasted with metallism.

History

The theory was systematised by the German economist Georg Friedrich Knapp in The State Theory of Money (1905). Alfred Mitchell-Innes argued in articles of 1913–14 that money is fundamentally credit. Keynes endorsed aspects of chartalism in his Treatise on Money (1930), and Abba Lerner's "functional finance" (1943) built on it.

Modern development

Chartalism is a foundation of Modern Monetary Theory (MMT), developed by economists such as Warren Mosler, Bill Mitchell, and L. Randall Wray, which emphasises that a sovereign currency issuer spends first and taxes to create demand for its currency and control inflation. Critics dispute its account of money's origins and its policy conclusions.
