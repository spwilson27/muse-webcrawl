Fundamental plane (elliptical galaxies)

The fundamental plane is an empirical relationship between three physical properties of elliptical galaxies: their effective radius, their mean surface brightness within that radius, and their central velocity dispersion. In the three-dimensional space of these parameters, elliptical galaxies lie on a thin plane rather than filling the volume.


The fundamental plane relation

The relation

The relation is typically written as log Re = a log σ + b ⟨μ⟩e + c, with observed coefficients near a ≈ 1.2–1.5 and b ≈ 0.3, depending on the photometric band. It was established in 1987 by two groups, Djorgovski and Davis, and Dressler and collaborators (the "7 Samurai"). The small scatter about the plane (about 15–20% in radius) makes it useful as a distance indicator for galaxies, complementing the Faber–Jackson relation and the Tully–Fisher relation for spirals.

Interpretation

From the virial theorem, a plane is expected if ellipticals are homologous systems; the observed "tilt" of the plane relative to the virial prediction indicates systematic variation in mass-to-light ratio with galaxy mass, arising from differences in stellar populations, dark matter fraction, or structural non-homology. Studying how the plane and its tilt evolve with redshift probes the formation history of elliptical galaxies.
