Charles Martel (c. 688 – 22 October 741) was a Frankish political and military leader who, as Mayor of the Palace, was the de facto ruler of Francia from 718 until his death. He was a son of the Frankish statesman Pepin of Herstal and his mistress Alpaida. The name "Martel" means "the Hammer", a nickname earned in battle.

Rise to power

After the death of his father in 714, Charles fought a civil war against his stepmother Plectrude and the Neustrians, defeating them at Amblève (716), Vinchy (717) and Soissons (718/719), and thereafter ruling both Austrasia and Neustria. He campaigned successfully against the Saxons, Frisians, Bavarians and Aquitainians, restoring Frankish supremacy.

Battle of Tours

In 732 Charles defeated the Umayyad army of Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi at the Battle of Tours (also called the Battle of Poitiers), halting the northward expansion of the Umayyad Caliphate into Western Europe. The victory made Charles the champion of Christendom in Latin eyes and consolidated his position as the dominant figure in Francia. Modern historians debate the battle's long-term significance, but it remains one of the most famous engagements of the early Middle Ages.

Reforms and legacy

Charles reorganised the Frankish military, increasingly relying on mounted warriors supported by grants of land — including confiscated Church land — a practice often linked with the development of feudalism. He divided his realm between his sons Carloman and Pepin the Short; Pepin deposed the last Merovingian king in 751 and founded the Carolingian dynasty, whose most famous member was Charles's grandson, Charlemagne.
