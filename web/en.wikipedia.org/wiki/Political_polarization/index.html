Political polarization

Political polarization is the divergence of political attitudes toward ideological extremes, away from the political centre. Scholars distinguish ideological polarization (differences in policy beliefs), affective polarization (dislike and distrust of the opposing party), and partisan sorting, in which social identities align with party identity.

Causes and effects

Proposed causes include partisan media and social media, gerrymandering and primaries, geographic sorting, economic inequality, and elite polarisation that precedes mass polarisation. In the United States, congressional voting has polarised steadily since the 1970s. Effects debated by researchers include gridlock, democratic backsliding, political violence, and erosion of shared norms, though the degree to which ordinary citizens (versus elites) are polarised on issues is contested.
