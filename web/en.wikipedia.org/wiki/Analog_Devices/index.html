Analog Devices

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Analog Devices headquarters in Wilmington, Massachusetts.

Analog Devices, Inc. (ADI) is an American multinational semiconductor company headquartered in Wilmington, Massachusetts. Founded in 1965 by Ray Stata and Matthew Lorber in Cambridge, Massachusetts, it specializes in data conversion, signal processing, and power management integrated circuits that bridge the analog and digital worlds.

Business

ADI's catalog of tens of thousands of products includes analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog converters, amplifiers, MEMS sensors, radio-frequency ICs, digital signal processors, and power management chips. Its customers span industrial automation, automotive, communications infrastructure, healthcare, aerospace, and consumer electronics; the industrial segment is its largest. The company is listed on the Nasdaq (ticker: ADI) and is a member of the S&P 500.

Acquisitions

Analog Devices grew substantially through acquisitions, most notably Hittite Microwave (2014), Linear Technology (2017, about $14.8 billion), and Maxim Integrated (2021, about $21 billion), the latter two consolidating its position against its chief rival, Texas Instruments, in the analog chip market.
