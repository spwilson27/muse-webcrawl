Perry Mason

Perry Mason is a fictional character, an American criminal defense attorney, created by the author Erle Stanley Gardner. Mason is the protagonist of more than eighty novels and short stories, most involving his client being charged with murder. Mason is known for his courtroom skill, his relentless investigation of the facts, and his ability to expose the real culprit, often while a witness is on the stand during cross-examination.

Character

Mason is assisted by his confidential secretary Della Street and by private investigator Paul Drake, who heads the Drake Detective Agency. His chief courtroom adversary is Los Angeles County District Attorney Hamilton Burger, and the investigating homicide detective is often Lieutenant Arthur Tragg. Despite Burger's frequent defeats, the two men respect one another, and Mason is generally cooperative with the police when his client's interests permit.

Novels

The first Perry Mason novel, The Case of the Velvet Claws, was published in 1933. Gardner, himself a practicing trial lawyer, wrote the early books at a rapid pace, sometimes producing several novels a year. The series continued until Gardner's death in 1970; the final books were published posthumously. The novels were enormously popular and helped shape the public image of the American trial lawyer.

Adaptations

The character has been adapted many times. A radio series ran in the 1940s. The best-known adaptation is the CBS television series Perry Mason (1957–1966), starring Raymond Burr, which won Burr two Emmy Awards. Burr returned to the role in a long-running series of television films beginning in 1985. In 2020, HBO premiered a new Perry Mason series starring Matthew Rhys, reimagining the character as a private investigator in 1930s Los Angeles before he became a lawyer.

Legacy

Perry Mason has become a cultural archetype of the defense attorney. The phrase "Perry Mason moment" is used to describe a dramatic courtroom revelation, and the series is credited with popularizing the courtroom drama genre on television.
