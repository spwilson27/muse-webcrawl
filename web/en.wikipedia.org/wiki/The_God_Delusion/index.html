The God Delusion

The God Delusion is a 2006 bestselling book by English biologist Richard Dawkins, in which he argues that a supernatural creator almost certainly does not exist and that belief in a personal god qualifies as a delusion — a false belief held with strong conviction. The book became a central text of the "New Atheism" movement.

Content

Dawkins addresses the "God Hypothesis", arguing it should be treated as a scientific question. He attacks arguments for God's existence, including the argument from design, which he counters with his "Ultimate Boeing 747 gambit": a designer capable of creating complex life would itself have to be at least as complex, and therefore demands its own explanation. Darwinian natural selection, he argues, explains apparent design without a designer. Later chapters consider the roots of religion and morality in evolution, and argue that morality does not require religion, criticising the moral character attributed to God in scripture and the labelling of children by their parents' religion.

Reception and sales

The book sold millions of copies and was translated into dozens of languages, spending long periods on bestseller lists. It was praised by supporters for its vigour and criticised by theologians and philosophers — among them Alister McGrath, who replied in The Dawkins Delusion? — for allegedly misrepresenting theological arguments and treating a caricature of faith.
