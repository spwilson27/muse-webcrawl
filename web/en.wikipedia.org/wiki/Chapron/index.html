Henri Chapron was a prominent French automobile coachbuilder founded in 1919 in Levallois-Perret, near Paris. The firm became renowned for elegant custom bodies on chassis from Delahaye, Delage, Talbot-Lago and other luxury marques.

Citroën work

Chapron is best known for its factory-approved conversions of the Citroën DS, producing cabriolets and distinctive coupés such as the Le Croisette and Palm Beach in the 1960s, in cooperation with Citroën.

Later years

After Henri Chapron's death in 1978, the company continued under his widow Françoise, producing conversions based on cars such as the Citroën CX and Peugeot models, before production wound down in the 1980s. Chapron-bodied cars are highly prized by collectors.
