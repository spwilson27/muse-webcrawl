Roseola

Roseola, also known as exanthem subitum and sixth disease, is a common viral illness of infants and young children, caused mainly by human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) and occasionally by HHV-7. It is characterized by several days of high fever followed, as the fever breaks, by a pink rash. Nearly all children are infected by the age of two.

Signs and symptoms

After an incubation period of about 5–15 days, the child develops an abrupt high fever (often above 39.5 °C / 103 °F) lasting three to five days, sometimes with irritability, mild respiratory symptoms, or a febrile seizure. Strikingly, as the fever resolves, a rose-pink maculopapular rash appears, starting on the trunk and spreading to the neck and limbs, fading within one to two days. The child usually feels well once the rash appears.

Cause and management

HHV-6 spreads through saliva of healthy adult carriers and remains latent for life after infection. Diagnosis is clinical; treatment is supportive — fluids and antipyretics — and antibiotics are ineffective. Complications are uncommon but include febrile seizures in up to a third of cases and, rarely, encephalitis, especially in immunocompromised patients, in whom antivirals such as ganciclovir may be used.
