Seminole

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Seminole are a Native American people of Florida and Oklahoma, formed in the 18th century from Creek migrants and earlier peoples.

The Seminole are a Native American people who developed in Florida in the eighteenth century, principally from Lower Creek migrants from Georgia and Alabama who joined remnants of earlier Florida peoples and, notably, African Americans escaping slavery, known as Black Seminoles. Their name derives from the Spanish cimarrón ("runaway, wild one"), rendered simano-li in the Mikasuki language.

History

Seminole bands, speaking Mikasuki and Muscogee languages, settled the Florida peninsula as Spanish power waned. Conflict with the expanding United States produced the three Seminole Wars (1817–18, 1835–42, 1855–58). The Second Seminole War, the costliest Indian war in U.S. history, ended with most Seminoles removed to Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma), but several hundred under leaders such as Billy Bowlegs and later Abiaka (Sam Jones) held out in the Everglades; their descendants proudly describe themselves as the "Unconquered People".

Modern Seminole nations

Today there are three federally recognized Seminole entities: the Seminole Tribe of Florida, organized under a 1957 constitution; the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma, the larger nation descended from those removed west; and the Miccosukee Tribe of Indians of Florida, recognized separately in 1962. The Florida Tribe became a pioneer of tribal gaming, opening a high-stakes bingo hall in 1979 whose legal defence reached the U.S. Supreme Court, and in 2006 it purchased the Hard Rock International brand. Traditional culture — patchwork clothing, chickee dwellings, the Green Corn Dance, and clan organization — remains central to Seminole identity.
