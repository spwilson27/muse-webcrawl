Pokémon (short for Pocket Monsters) is a Japanese media franchise managed by The Pokémon Company, founded by Nintendo, Game Freak, and Creatures. It centres on fictional creatures called Pokémon, which human Trainers catch, train, and battle.

Origins

The franchise began with the video games Pokémon Red and Green, released in Japan in 1996 for the Game Boy and developed by Game Freak under director Satoshi Tajiri, whose childhood hobby of collecting insects inspired the concept. The games were a major success and were released internationally in 1998.

Video games

The main series of role-playing games, organised into generations, has sold more than 480 million units, making Pokémon one of the best-selling video game franchises in history. Core mechanics include catching wild Pokémon with Poké Balls, turn-based battles, trading between players, and completing the Pokédex. Spin-offs range from Pokémon Snap to the augmented-reality phenomenon Pokémon GO (2016).

Other media

The anime television series, following Ash Ketchum and his partner Pikachu for over 25 years, became one of the most successful adaptations of a video game. The Pokémon Trading Card Game is among the world's most popular card games, and more than twenty animated films and the live-action film Detective Pikachu (2019) have been released. By revenue, Pokémon is widely cited as the highest-grossing media franchise of all time.
