Enriched category

FieldCategory theory

In category theory, an enriched category generalises the notion of a category by replacing the sets of morphisms (hom-sets) with objects of another, monoidal, category. A category enriched over a monoidal category V is called a V-category.

Definition

A V-category consists of a collection of objects; for each pair of objects a, b a hom-object Hom(a,b) in V; a composition morphism in V combining hom-objects; and identity morphisms from the monoidal unit — all satisfying associativity and unit axioms expressed as commutative diagrams in V.

Examples

Ordinary categories are enriched over the category of sets. Categories enriched over abelian groups are preadditive categories; over topological spaces they give topologically enriched categories; over simplicial sets, simplicially enriched categories, important in higher category theory. Enrichment over a quantale yields generalised metric spaces, an observation due to Lawvere.
