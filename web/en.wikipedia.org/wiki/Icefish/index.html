Distinguishing traitColourless blood lacking haemoglobin

Icefish are a family (Channichthyidae) of perciform fish found in the cold waters of the Southern Ocean around Antarctica. They are famous as the only known vertebrates that lack haemoglobin in their blood as adults, so their blood is colourless and their bodies appear pale or translucent.

Physiology

Adult icefish also lack functional myoglobin in their heart muscle and have no red blood cells. They survive because Antarctic waters are cold and exceptionally oxygen-rich; oxygen dissolves directly in their blood plasma and is circulated by an unusually large heart and high blood volume. Icefish also produce antifreeze glycoproteins that prevent ice crystals from forming in their tissues at sub-zero temperatures.

Ecology and fishery

Icefish feed mainly on krill and smaller fish. The mackerel icefish (Champsocephalus gunnari) supported a significant commercial fishery around South Georgia and other sub-Antarctic islands, now regulated by CCAMLR after overfishing in the 1970s and 1980s.

The name "icefish" is also applied to unrelated groups, including some smelts and noodlefishes (Salangidae).
