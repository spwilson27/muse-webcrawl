Ardingly is a village and civil parish in the Mid Sussex district of West Sussex, England. It lies about 33 miles (53 km) south of London and 6 miles (10 km) north of Haywards Heath, in the High Weald Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The parish is noted for St Peter's Church, Ardingly College, the South of England Showground, and the nearby Ardingly Reservoir and Wakehurst botanical gardens.

History

Ardingly appears in Domesday Book (1086) as Erdingelega. The parish church of St Peter dates substantially from the 14th century, built of local sandstone. The village lay on routes through the Weald iron-working country, and its stocks and old inn, the Ardingly Inn, reflect its modest market and coaching past. In 1864 Ardingly College, an independent boarding school of the Woodard foundation, moved to its present site north of the village, its Gothic Revival buildings becoming a local landmark.

Landmarks and events

The South of England Showground, opened in the 1960s on the village's edge, hosts the annual South of England Show and numerous antiques fairs and events. Ardingly Reservoir, built in 1978 on a tributary of the River Ouse, supplies water to the region and supports sailing, fishing, and a nature reserve. Just south lies Wakehurst, the country estate of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, home to the Millennium Seed Bank.

The Bluebell Railway's East Grinstead line passes through the parish — the station at Horsted Keynes is nearby, and the railway once planned to reopen the closed Ardingly branch from Haywards Heath, a link long severed by the M23 motorway corridor. The village also hosts an annual vintage and steam rally traditions associated with Sussex agricultural life.
