George Lusk

George Akin Lusk (1839 – 15 October 1919) was a British builder and head of the Whitechapel Vigilance Committee during the Jack the Ripper murders.

Born: 1839
Died: 1919
Known for: Receiving the "From hell" letter

George Akin Lusk was a builder and decorator in Whitechapel, London, who served as chairman of the Whitechapel Vigilance Committee during the autumn of 1888, when the unidentified serial killer known as Jack the Ripper murdered women in the district.

Vigilance Committee

Lusk headed the committee of local businessmen formed in September 1888 to offer a reward and organise patrols amid public anger at the police's failure to catch the murderer. He wrote to the press and the Home Office pressing for a government reward, which was initially refused on the grounds that rewards encouraged false information.

The "From hell" letter

On 16 October 1888 Lusk received a small package containing half of a human kidney preserved in spirits, accompanied by a letter beginning "From hell" and signed "Catch me when you can Mishter Lusk". The writer claimed to have fried and eaten the other half of the kidney. The organ was examined by Dr Thomas Openshaw, who concluded it was part of a left kidney from a woman of about 45 who drank heavily — consistent with, though never proven to be, the kidney removed from the victim Catherine Eddowes. The original letter has been lost and is generally considered by Ripper historians to be among the few such letters possibly written by the killer, though opinion remains divided.

Later life

Lusk complained of threatening letters and of being followed, and received police protection for a time. He returned to relative obscurity after 1888 and died in 1919. He appears as a character in the graphic novel and film From Hell.
