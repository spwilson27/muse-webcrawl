Meaning (linguistics)

In linguistics, meaning is the information or concepts that a sender intends to convey, or does convey, in communication with a receiver. The study of meaning is the central concern of semantics, and it overlaps with pragmatics, which studies how context contributes to meaning.

Sense and reference

A foundational distinction, drawn by Gottlob Frege, separates the reference (denotation) of an expression — the thing in the world it picks out — from its sense, the mode of presentation of that referent. Thus "the morning star" and "the evening star" share a referent (Venus) but differ in sense. Theories of meaning also distinguish linguistic meaning from speaker meaning, and consider how words (lexical semantics), sentences, and whole discourses (discourse analysis) carry meaning.

Approaches

Major approaches include truth-conditional semantics, which identifies the meaning of a sentence with the conditions under which it would be true; prototype theory, which holds that concepts are organized around typical examples; and usage-based and cognitive approaches, which ground meaning in embodied experience and patterns of use. Philosophers of language from Ferdinand de Saussure to Ludwig Wittgenstein have debated whether meaning resides in reference, in mental representations, or in use.
