Tori Amos (born Myra Ellen Amos, 22 August 1963) is an American singer-songwriter and pianist. A classically trained musician, she is known for her confessional, allusive lyrics, virtuosic piano playing — often straddling the piano bench to play piano and keyboards simultaneously — and a fiercely loyal fanbase cultivated through relentless touring. She has sold more than 12 million albums worldwide.

Early life and Y Kant Tori Read

Born in North Carolina and raised largely in Maryland, Amos was admitted at age five to the Peabody Conservatory on a full scholarship — its youngest ever student — but was dismissed at eleven as her interests turned to rock and pop. After years playing piano bars and a move to Los Angeles, she fronted the synth-pop band Y Kant Tori Read, whose self-titled 1988 album flopped commercially but began her long association with Atlantic Records.

Solo career

Her solo debut Little Earthquakes (1992), featuring "Crucify", "Silent All These Years" and the a cappella "Me and a Gun", drew critical acclaim. Under the Pink (1994) reached number one in the UK and included "Cornflake Girl". Boys for Pele (1996), recorded in Ireland and Louisiana with harpsichord and harmonium, and From the Choirgirl Hotel (1998) expanded her sound into electronica. Later acclaimed releases include Scarlet's Walk (2002), the orchestral Gold Dust (2012) and Native Invader (2017). Amos co-founded the Rape, Abuse & Incest National Network (RAINN) in 1994, its first spokesperson. Later projects include the stage musical The Light Princess (2013, National Theatre, London) and her memoir Resistance (2020).
