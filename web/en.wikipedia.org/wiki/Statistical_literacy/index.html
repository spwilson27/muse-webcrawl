Statistical literacy

Statistical literacy is the ability to understand and reason about statistics and data. It includes the capacity to read, interpret and critically evaluate statistical information presented in graphs, tables, news reports and everyday argument. Statistical literacy is widely regarded by educators and statisticians as an essential component of an informed citizenry in modern societies.

Components

Statistical literacy involves familiarity with basic concepts such as averages, percentages, rates, risk, variation, sampling and correlation. A statistically literate person can recognize misleading graphs (for example, truncated axes), understand that correlation does not imply causation, appreciate the role of randomness and uncertainty, and judge whether a sample is representative of a population.

History and education

The concept gained prominence in the late 20th century as statistics educators — including Iddo Gal, Joan Garfield and others — argued that teaching statistical computation alone was insufficient. International assessments and programs such as the Quantitative Literacy movement and the Guidelines for Assessment and Instruction in Statistics Education (GAISE) have promoted statistical literacy as a goal of school and university curricula.

Related concepts

Statistical literacy is related to, but distinct from, numeracy (facility with numbers generally) and data literacy (working with datasets). It is also closely tied to critical thinking about risk communication in medicine, finance and public policy.
