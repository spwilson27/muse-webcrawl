Paul the Apostle

Paul, commonly known as Paul the Apostle and Saint Paul (c. 5 – c. 64/65 AD), was a Christian apostle who spread the teachings of Jesus to the Gentile world in the mid-1st century. Born Saul of Tarsus, a Pharisee and Roman citizen, he initially persecuted Christians before a conversion experience on the road to Damascus.

Bornc. 5 AD, Tarsus, Cilicia
Diedc. 64–65 AD, Rome
Feast29 June (with Peter)

Missionary journeys

Paul undertook journeys across Cyprus, Asia Minor, Greece and Rome, founding churches in Corinth, Philippi, Thessalonica and Ephesus. His letters (epistles) — Romans, Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians and others — form a major part of the New Testament; seven are widely regarded by scholars as authentically Pauline.

Theology and legacy

Paul emphasised justification by faith, salvation for Jew and Gentile alike without full observance of Mosaic law, and the resurrection. Tradition holds he was beheaded in Rome under Nero. He is patron saint of missionaries and theologians and, with Peter, of Rome.
