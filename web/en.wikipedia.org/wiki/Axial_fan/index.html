Axial Fan

An axial fan is a type of fan that causes air to flow through it in an axial direction, parallel to the shaft about which the blades rotate. Axial fans are the most common type of fan in use, found in applications ranging from small cooling fans for electronics to enormous fans used in wind tunnels and cooling towers.

Design

An axial-flow fan consists of a number of blades (usually two to thirteen) mounted on a hub driven by a motor, often housed in a cylindrical casing. Because the flow is parallel to the axis, axial fans move large volumes of air at relatively low pressure differences. Performance is governed by the fan laws relating flow rate, pressure, rotational speed, and power, and by the fan's characteristic curve; operating beyond the stall region causes surging and loss of efficiency. Guide vanes downstream of the rotor can recover swirl energy and improve efficiency in industrial designs.

Types and applications

Common types include propeller fans (used for general ventilation), tube-axial fans (mounted in a cylinder for ducted systems), and vane-axial fans (with guide vanes, for higher pressures). Axial fans cool computer electronics, ventilate buildings and mines, drive air through condensers in refrigeration and air conditioning, and move air in cooling towers at power stations. Large axial fans with variable-pitch blades are used in wind tunnels and as forced-draught fans in industry.

Comparison with centrifugal fans

Compared with centrifugal fans, axial fans deliver higher flow at lower pressure in a compact, usually cheaper package, but they are generally less suited to high-pressure ducted systems and tend to be noisier for a given duty.
