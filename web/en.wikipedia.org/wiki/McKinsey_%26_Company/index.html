McKinsey & Company is an American multinational strategy and management consulting firm founded in 1926 by University of Chicago professor James O. McKinsey. It is the oldest and largest of the "MBB" or "Big Three" management consulting firms, alongside Bain & Company and Boston Consulting Group.

History

McKinsey began as an accounting and management firm in Chicago. After James McKinsey's death in 1937, the firm was reshaped by Marvin Bower, who emphasized professionalism, a partnership structure, and serving only top executives. McKinsey pioneered the practice of hiring recent graduates from elite business schools rather than experienced industry managers.

Operations

The firm operates as a single global partnership with offices in more than 65 countries. It advises corporations, governments, and non-profit organizations on strategy, operations, technology, organization, and sustainability. Its alumni network includes many chief executives and public figures.

Criticism

McKinsey has faced scrutiny over its work for opioid manufacturers, authoritarian governments, and state-owned enterprises, as well as its role in corporate scandals such as the Enron collapse. The firm has paid substantial legal settlements, including hundreds of millions of dollars related to its opioid consulting work.
