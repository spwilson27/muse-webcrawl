Svabhava

Svabhāva (Sanskrit: स्वभाव, "own nature", "intrinsic nature") is a concept in Indian philosophy denoting the inherent, unchanging essence by which a thing is what it is. The term appears across Hindu, Buddhist and Jain thought, where it carries importantly different implications.

In Hindu thought

In schools such as Samkhya and in the Bhagavad Gita, svabhava refers to the intrinsic nature of beings and of primordial nature (prakriti) that determines their behaviour and qualities; living beings act according to their own nature, and spiritual practice must reckon with it. Later philosophical traditions debated whether svabhava is an independent explanatory principle — the idea that things happen simply "by their own nature" (svabhāvavāda) was associated with materialist schools such as the Lokayata.

In Buddhism

In Buddhism the concept became principally a target of critique. Early Abhidharma scholasticism, particularly the Sarvastivada school, used svabhava to characterise the intrinsic existence of dharmas (phenomena) through past, present and future. The Madhyamaka philosopher Nagarjuna (c. 2nd century CE) argued in the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā that nothing possesses svabhava: whatever arises dependently (pratitya-samutpada) is empty (shunya) of intrinsic nature, and an essence truly independent and uncaused could never arise or change. Emptiness of svabhava is therefore the centrepiece of Madhyamaka philosophy and a foundation of Mahayana Buddhism, though debates over whether Buddha-nature teachings reintroduce an essence recur throughout later Buddhist history.
