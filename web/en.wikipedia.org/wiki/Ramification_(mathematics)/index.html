Ramification (mathematics)

In mathematics, ramification is a geometric term used for "branching out", in the way that the square root function, for complex numbers, can be seen to have two branches differing in sign. The term is used from the opposite perspective (branches coming together) as when a covering map degenerates so that sheets come together over a point, as in a branched covering.

In complex analysis

In complex analysis, a point z0 in the domain of a holomorphic function f is called a branch point if there is no neighbourhood of z0 on which f is injective. For a rational map, ramification points are where the local degree exceeds 1; the Riemann–Hurwitz formula relates the ramification of a map between Riemann surfaces to their genera.

In algebraic number theory

In algebraic number theory, a prime ideal 𝔭 of the ring of integers of a number field is said to ramify in an extension field if, when the ideal generated by 𝔭 in the larger ring is factored into prime ideals, some prime factor appears with exponent greater than one. For example, the prime 2 ramifies in the Gaussian integers, since (2) = (1 + i)2 up to a unit. Ramification in the extension ℚ(i)/ℚ is described by the splitting of primes in extensions of number fields; the primes that ramify in a quadratic field are exactly those dividing its discriminant. Unramified extensions are central to class field theory.

In algebraic geometry

In algebraic geometry, ramification is defined for morphisms of schemes: a morphism is unramified if its sheaf of relative differentials vanishes. Ramified coverings of curves generalise the analytic notion of branched coverings.
