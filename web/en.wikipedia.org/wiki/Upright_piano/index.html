Upright piano

A vertical (upright) piano

An upright piano, also called a vertical piano, is a piano in which the strings and soundboard are oriented vertically rather than horizontally as in a grand piano. Because the strings run vertically, upright pianos occupy far less floor space, making them the most common type of piano found in homes, schools, and rehearsal rooms.

Types

Upright pianos are classified by height: the small spinet (under about 38 inches), the console, the studio piano, and the full-size upright (48 inches and taller), which approaches the tone of a small grand. Taller uprights have longer strings and larger soundboards and generally produce richer bass and greater volume.

Mechanism

The action of an upright differs from a grand's: hammers move horizontally and return by springs rather than gravity, and the repetition of notes is somewhat slower than on a grand piano. Dampers are typically operated from below by a mechanism connected to the keys and pedals.
