Siege of Wexford

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Wexford (October 1649) occurred during the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland, when the Parliamentarian New Model Army under Oliver Cromwell stormed the port town of Wexford in County Wexford after its Confederate Irish garrison's negotiations collapsed. The storming was accompanied by a massacre of soldiers and civilians that remains controversial.

Background

After taking Drogheda in September 1649, Cromwell marched south against Wexford, a major port for the Royalist-Confederate alliance and a base for privateers preying on Parliamentarian shipping. The town was defended by some 2,000–3,000 troops under David Sinnot, who stalled negotiations while hoping for relief from the Earl of Ormond's army.

The storm

On 11 October 1649 Cromwell's artillery breached the castle at the south of the town. While a parley was still underway, the castle's defenders suddenly fled, and Parliamentarian troops scaled the walls and poured into the town. In the ensuing chaos, some 2,000 Confederate soldiers were killed, many drowning in the River Slaney as they tried to escape across the harbour, along with a number of civilians and clergy. Cromwell reported that his own losses were small.

Aftermath

The port and its shipping were captured, depriving the Royalist cause of a key harbour. As at Drogheda, the severity at Wexford was justified by Cromwell as retribution and deterrence, and it has remained a byword in Irish historical memory of the conquest.
