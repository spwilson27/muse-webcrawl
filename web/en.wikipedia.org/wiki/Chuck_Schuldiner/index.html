Charles Michael Schuldiner (May 13, 1967 – December 13, 2001) was an American musician. He founded the death metal band Death in 1983, serving as its lead guitarist, vocalist, and principal songwriter, and is widely regarded as the "Godfather of death metal" for his pioneering role in the genre. Schuldiner was ranked No. 10 in Joel McIver's 2009 book The 100 Greatest Metal Guitarists and No. 20 in the March 2004 Guitar World readers' poll of the 100 greatest metal guitarists.

Early life

Schuldiner was born on Long Island, New York, to a Jewish father of Austrian descent and a mother from the American South who had converted to Judaism. In 1968, his family moved to Florida. He began playing guitar at the age of nine after his older brother Frank was killed in an automobile accident at sixteen; his parents bought him a guitar to help him cope with the loss. He took classical lessons briefly but soon taught himself, drawing heavy influence from Iron Maiden, Kiss, Billy Idol, and later Slayer and Possessed.

Career

In 1983, Schuldiner formed Mantas, which soon became Death. The band's demos, including Death by Metal and Reign of Terror, circulated through the tape-trading underground. Death's debut album Scream Bloody Gore (1987) is considered one of the first true death metal records. Subsequent albums — Leprosy (1988), Spiritual Healing (1990), Human (1991), Individual Thought Patterns (1993), Symbolic (1995), and The Sound of Perseverance (1998) — progressively moved toward technical and progressive territory while retaining extreme aggression. Schuldiner was the band's only constant member and its creative center.

After putting Death on hold, Schuldiner formed the progressive metal band Control Denied, whose debut The Fragile Art of Existence appeared in 1999. He also contributed to the Voodoocult project in 1994.

Illness and death

On his 32nd birthday, May 13, 1999, Schuldiner was diagnosed with a rare brainstem tumor (pontine glioma). After initial radiation shrunk the tumor, it returned in early 2000, and his insurance would not cover the surgery he needed. Members of the metal community organized fundraisers and auctions to support his medical bills. He died on December 13, 2001, at the age of 34.

Legacy

Schuldiner is remembered as one of the most influential figures in extreme metal. His mother, Jane Schuldiner, maintained his legacy through the official website Empty Words, and tribute concerts and benefit releases have been held in his name.
