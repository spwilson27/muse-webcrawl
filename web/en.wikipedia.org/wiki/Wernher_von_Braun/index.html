Wernher von Braun

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Von Braun at the base of a Saturn V.

Wernher Magnus Maximilian Freiherr von Braun (23 March 1912 – 16 June 1977) was a German-American aerospace engineer and the leading figure in the development of rocket technology in Nazi Germany and then the United States.

As technical director of the German Army's rocket programme at Peenemünde, von Braun led the team that created the V-2, the world's first long-range ballistic missile, built with concentration-camp labour under murderous conditions — a legacy that permanently shadows his reputation. Surrendering to American forces in 1945, he and much of his team were brought to the United States under Operation Paperclip. There he directed the team that launched America's first satellite, Explorer 1, in 1958, and then, as first director of NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center, led development of the Saturn V that carried Apollo 11 to the Moon.

Later career

After Apollo, von Braun advocateded crewed Mars missions without success, left NASA in 1972 for industry, and died of cancer in 1977 at age 65.
