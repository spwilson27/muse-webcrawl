Edward Woodward

Edward Albert Arthur Woodward, OBE (1 June 1930 – 16 November 2009) was an English actor and singer. He was best known for his starring roles in the British television spy drama Callan (1967–1972), the cult horror film The Wicker Man (1973), and the American television series The Equalizer (1985–1989), for which he won a Golden Globe Award.

Early life

Woodward was born in Croydon, Surrey, the son of a metal worker. He attended Kingston College of Technology and later the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art (RADA), graduating in 1946. He began his career on the stage, working in repertory theatre across England and Scotland before joining the Royal Shakespeare Company.

Career

Woodward's breakthrough came with the title role in Callan, a gritty espionage series in which he played a reluctant British intelligence assassin. In 1973 he played Sergeant Neil Howie, a devout Christian police officer, in The Wicker Man, opposite Christopher Lee; the film later achieved cult status and is frequently cited among the greatest British films ever made.

He also starred in the Australian film Breaker Morant (1980), playing Lieutenant Harry "Breaker" Morant, a performance widely praised by critics. In the United States he became a household name as Robert McCall in The Equalizer. Later roles included appearances in Hot Fuzz (2007) and the BBC soap opera EastEnders.

Personal life and death

Woodward was married twice, first to actress Venetia Barrett, and from 1987 to actress Michele Dotrice, who played Betty in the sitcom Some Mothers Do 'Ave 'Em. He was appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) in 1978. He died of pneumonia on 16 November 2009, aged 79.
