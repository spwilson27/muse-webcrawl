Matutinal

Matutinal, along with vespertine, is a term used in science to indicate an organism that is active particularly in the early morning, or before dawn. Matutinal animals are behaviorally most active in the early morning, a temporal niche that is intermediate between diurnal and crepuscular activity patterns. The word is derived from the Latin matutinus, "of or belonging to the morning."

Usage

The term is used in zoology and botany to describe daily activity patterns and phenology, alongside diurnal (day), nocturnal (night), crepuscular (twilight), and vespertine (evening). For example, some bees are described as matutinal, beginning to forage at dawn before other pollinators become active; certain flowers open in the early morning and are said to have matutinal anthesis. Matutinal activity may help animals avoid midday heat, predation peaks, or competition.

Related terms

In astronomy and meteorology, "matutinal" may describe events occurring just before sunrise, such as the morning visibility of a planet. In human chronobiology, morning-preferring individuals are sometimes described as having a matutinal chronotype, as distinct from vespertine ("evening person") tendencies.
