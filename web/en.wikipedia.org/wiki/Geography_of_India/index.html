Geography of India

Location: South Asia
Area: 3,287,263 km²
Coastline: 7,516.6 km
Highest point: Kangchenjunga (8,586 m)

The geography of India is extremely diverse, with landscapes ranging from snow-capped mountain ranges to deserts, plains, rainforests and coastlines. India lies on the Indian Plate, north of the equator between 8°4′ and 37°6′ north latitude, and is the seventh-largest country in the world, with an area of 3,287,263 km².

Physiographic divisions

India is conventionally divided into six physiographic regions. The northern mountains comprise the Himalayas, the highest mountain range in the world, which includes Kangchenjunga (8,586 m), the world's third-highest peak, on the border with Nepal. South of the mountains lie the Indo-Gangetic plains, formed by the basins of the Indus, Ganges and Brahmaputra rivers and among the most fertile and densely populated areas on Earth. The peninsular plateau includes the Deccan Plateau, flanked by the Western and Eastern Ghats along the coasts. The Thar Desert occupies much of Rajasthan in the northwest. India's coastline of 7,516 km borders the Arabian Sea, the Bay of Bengal and the Indian Ocean; its island territories include the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep.

Climate

India's climate is dominated by the monsoon: the southwest monsoon brings most of the annual rainfall between June and September, while the northeast (retreating) monsoon affects the southeast coast in autumn. Climate types range from alpine in the Himalayas to tropical in the south and arid in the Thar.

Rivers and resources

Major river systems include the Ganges, Brahmaputra, Indus, Godavari, Krishna, Mahanadi and Kaveri. India has significant reserves of coal (among the world's largest), iron ore, manganese, mica and bauxite, and renewable freshwater resources replenished by monsoon rains and Himalayan glaciers, though per-capita water availability is under pressure from a population exceeding 1.4 billion.
