Wilmer McLean


Born May 3, 1814, Alexandria, Virginia, U.S.
Died June 5, 1882 (aged 68), Alexandria, Virginia
Known for The Civil War "began in his front yard and ended in his front parlor"

Wilmer McLean (May 3, 1814 – June 5, 1882) was an American wholesale grocer whose houses were used at both the beginning and the end of the American Civil War, leading to the famous remark that the war began in his front yard and ended in his front parlor.

First Bull Run

McLean lived near Manassas, Virginia, and during the First Battle of Bull Run in July 1861 his house at Yorkshire served as headquarters for Confederate General P. G. T. Beauregard. Combat raged around the property — a Union shell famously burst in his kitchen — prompting McLean to move his family away from the war zone.

Appomattox

Seeking safety, McLean relocated to the quiet village of Appomattox Court House in south-central Virginia. There, on April 9, 1865, General Robert E. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia to Ulysses S. Grant in the parlor of McLean's brick house. Union officers afterwards carried off furniture as souvenirs, paying McLean for some items.

Later life

McLean afterwards struggled financially; his Appomattox house was sold and dismantled by speculators hoping to display it, and it was not rebuilt until the 1940s by the National Park Service. McLean worked for the Internal Revenue Service and died in Alexandria in 1882.
