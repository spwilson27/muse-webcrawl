Freeware

Freeware is software, most often proprietary, that is distributed at no monetary cost to the end user. There is no agreed-upon set of rights, license, or EULA that defines freeware unambiguously; each publisher defines its own rules for the freeware it offers. For instance, modification, redistribution by third parties, and reverse engineering are permitted by some publishers but prohibited by others. Unlike with free and open-source software, which are also often distributed free of charge, the source code of freeware is typically not made available.

History

The term freeware was coined in 1982 by Andrew Fluegelman, who wanted to sell a communications program named PC-Talk that he had created, outside of commercial distribution channels. Fluegelman distributed the program via a process now termed shareware. In the 1980s and 1990s, the term was commonly used for software released without source code.

Definition

Software classified as freeware may be used indefinitely without payment, but in many cases modification, reuse in other programs, and redistribution are restricted. Free software licences, by contrast, are defined by the freedoms to use, copy, study, modify and share the software.
