Yōrō Code

The Yōrō Code (養老律令, Yōrō-ritsuryō) is a compilation of laws of the ritsuryō system of imperial Japan, drawn up in the Yōrō era around 718 CE under the direction of Fujiwara no Fuhito and others, and promulgated in 757 during the reign of Empress Kōken.

Contents

Like its predecessor the Taihō Code (701), the Yōrō Code consisted of ritsu (penal laws) and ryō (administrative laws) modeled on Tang dynasty Chinese codes. It defined the structure of central government ministries, court ranks, provincial administration, taxation, land allotment under the handen-shūju system, and the legal status of households. Although its ritsu section is lost, most of the ryō survives through later commentaries such as the Ryō no Gige and Ryō no Shūge.

Significance

The Yōrō Code was the operative administrative code of the Japanese state for centuries, even as actual governance drifted away from its provisions with the rise of private estates (shōen) and warrior rule. Together with the Taihō Code it is the foundation of knowledge about the classical ritsuryō state, and its surviving text is a primary source for historians of early Japan.
