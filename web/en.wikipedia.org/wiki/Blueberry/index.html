Blueberries are perennial flowering plants with blue or purple berries, classified in the genus Vaccinium, section Cyanococcus. Native to North America, they have been gathered by Indigenous peoples for millennia and were domesticated in the early 20th century by Elizabeth Coleman White and botanist Frederick Coville in New Jersey.

Cultivation

Commercial blueberries require acidic, well-drained soil. Lowbush blueberries are harvested wild in Maine and Atlantic Canada, while highbush varieties are grown on farms across North and South America, Europe and Australasia. Peru has become a leading exporter, supplying counter-seasonal fruit to northern markets.

Nutrition and uses

Blueberries are eaten fresh and used in muffins, pies, jams and juices. They are a source of vitamin C, vitamin K, manganese and dietary fibre, and are rich in anthocyanin pigments that give the fruit its colour. See also Vaccinium and Bilberry.
