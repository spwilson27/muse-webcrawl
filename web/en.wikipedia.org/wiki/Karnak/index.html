Karnak

The Karnak Temple Complex, near Luxor in Egypt, comprises a vast mix of temples, pylons, chapels and other buildings built over some 2,000 years, from the Middle Kingdom (c. 2000 BCE) to the Ptolemaic period. It was the principal religious centre of the god Amun-Ra in ancient Thebes.

The complex

The largest precinct is that of Amun-Ra, with ten pylons, the Great Hypostyle Hall of Seti I and Ramesses II — 134 massive columns covering 5,000 m² — a sacred lake, and obelisks of Hatshepsut and Thutmose I. Separate precincts honour Mut and Montu.

History

Nearly every New Kingdom pharaoh added to Karnak. It was linked to Luxor Temple by an avenue of sphinxes, along which the Opet Festival procession travelled. The complex is among the most visited ancient sites in Egypt.
