Ritchey–Chrétien telescope

A Ritchey–Chrétien telescope (RCT) is a specialized variant of the Cassegrain reflector designed to eliminate off-axis coma, allowing a wide, sharp field of view. It uses hyperbolic primary and secondary mirrors.

Design

Invented by American astronomer George Willis Ritchey and French astronomer Henri Chrétien in the early 1910s, the design corrects spherical aberration and coma simultaneously, leaving only astigmatism and field curvature, which can be handled with field flatteners. The mirrors are difficult to fabricate and test because the hyperboloids have no simple null test along their axes of symmetry.

Use

The Ritchey–Chrétien design is used by most large professional telescopes, including the Hubble Space Telescope, the Keck telescopes, and the Very Large Telescope, as well as many advanced amateur instruments.
