The San Fernando Valley, known locally as the Valley, is an urbanised valley in Los Angeles County, California, lying largely within the city of Los Angeles, north-west of downtown across the Santa Monica Mountains.

Geography and history

Named for Mission San Fernando Rey de España (founded 1797), the Valley was transformed after the opening of the Los Angeles Aqueduct in 1913 and annexation by Los Angeles in 1915, which enabled large-scale irrigation and suburban development. It became a archetypal post-war suburb and a centre of the film and aerospace industries, with studios in Burbank and Warner Center's business district in Woodland Hills.

Communities

Besides Los Angeles neighbourhoods such as Van Nuys, Sherman Oaks, Encino, Tarzana, Reseda, Northridge and Granada Hills, the Valley includes the independent cities of Burbank, Glendale, San Fernando, Calabasas and Hidden Hills. The 1994 Northridge earthquake, centred in Reseda, was one of the costliest disasters in US history.
