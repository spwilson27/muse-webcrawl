Spacecraft

A spacecraft is a vehicle designed to fly and operate in outer space. Spacecraft are used for communications, Earth observation, meteorology, navigation, space colonisation, planetary exploration, and transportation of people and cargo. All spacecraft except single-stage-to-orbit vehicles use a launch vehicle to reach orbit. A spacecraft designed for human flight is called a crewed spacecraft or space ship, while one operated robotically is an uncrewed spacecraft, satellite or space probe.

History

The first artificial spacecraft was Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957. Yuri Gagarin's Vostok 1 (1961) was the first crewed spacecraft. Since then, spacecraft have visited every planet of the Solar System, landed on the Moon, asteroids and a comet, and the twin Voyager probes have entered interstellar space.

Subsystems

A spacecraft typically combines a structural bus with subsystems for propulsion; attitude determination and control; guidance and navigation; electrical power (usually solar panels and batteries); thermal control; communications; command and data handling; and, for crewed craft, life support. The payload — instruments, telescopes or passengers — is the part that performs the mission.

Types

Spacecraft are classified by function: communications satellites relay signals from geostationary or low orbits; Earth-observation and weather satellites image the planet; navigation constellations such as GPS provide positioning; space telescopes observe the universe above the atmosphere; space probes explore other bodies; and orbital stations such as the International Space Station serve as long-duration habitats. Reusable systems, including the Space Shuttle and modern partially reusable rockets, aim to lower the cost of reaching orbit.
