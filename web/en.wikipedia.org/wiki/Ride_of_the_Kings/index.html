Ride of the Kings

The Ride of the Kings (Czech: Jízda králů) is a folk festival held in the Moravian Slovak region of the Czech Republic, traditionally at Pentecost, in which a young boy dressed in women's clothing and holding a rose in his mouth rides through a village as the "king", accompanied by an entourage on decorated horses.

Description

The king, usually aged 10 to 15, wears an elaborate female folk costume and headdress, his face partly veiled, and remains silent throughout, holding a rose between his lips. His retinue of riders in folk dress sing, call out verses to spectators, and collect donations. The king's guards carry sabres to protect him.

Origins and locations

Legends link the custom to a fleeing Hungarian king Matthias Corvinus disguising himself to escape defeat, or to other medieval rulers. The most famous Ride is held in Vlčnov, with others in Hluk, Kunovice, and Skorocnice. In 2011 the Ride of the Kings was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
