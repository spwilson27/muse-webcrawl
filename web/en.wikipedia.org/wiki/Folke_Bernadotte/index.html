Folke Bernadotte

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Folke Bernadotte, Count of Wisborg (2 January 1895 – 17 September 1948) was a Swedish nobleman and diplomat. He is best known for negotiating the release of tens of thousands of prisoners from German concentration camps during the Second World War, and for serving as the first official mediator of the United Nations in Palestine, a post in which he was assassinated in 1948.

Early life

Bernadotte was born in Stockholm, a nephew of King Gustaf V of Sweden and the son of Prince Oscar Bernadotte. He became a cavalry officer and represented Sweden on ceremonial occasions. In 1928 he married Estelle Manville, an American heiress. He helped organise the Swedish participation in world exhibitions and became vice-chairman, and later acting head, of the Swedish Red Cross.

The White Buses

In the spring of 1945, Bernadotte negotiated with Heinrich Himmler and organised the expedition remembered as the White Buses: a fleet of white-painted buses and ambulances that evacuated Scandinavian prisoners from German concentration camps, eventually carrying some 20,000–30,000 prisoners — including Norwegians, Danes, Poles, French and Jews — to safety in Sweden as the war ended. His separate attempts to relay an offer of German surrender on the Western Front were rejected by the Allies.

United Nations mediator

In May 1948 the newly created United Nations appointed Bernadotte mediator in the Arab–Israeli war in Palestine. He negotiated two truces and drafted proposals for a settlement, including territorial adjustments and the return of Arab refugees, published in his posthumous report To Jerusalem. His plans angered extremists on both sides.

Assassination

On 17 September 1948 Bernadotte was shot dead in Jerusalem by gunmen of the Zionist paramilitary group Lehi (the "Stern Gang"), together with UN observer Colonel André Serot. The assassination was widely condemned; no one was convicted of the murders, though Lehi was disbanded and its members later amnestied. Bernadotte is commemorated in Israel by a forest planted in his memory, and the UN's mediation efforts were continued by his aide Ralph Bunche.
