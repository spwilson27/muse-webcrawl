Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act of 2010

The Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act of 2010 is a United States federal law signed by President Barack Obama on December 13, 2010. It reauthorised child nutrition programmes, funding school lunches and breakfasts, and gave the U.S. Department of Agriculture authority to set new nutrition standards for foods sold in schools.

Provisions

The act provided a $4.5 billion increase in funding over ten years, raised meal reimbursement rates, expanded after-school and summer feeding, and extended eligibility through direct certification and the Community Eligibility Provision, allowing high-poverty schools to serve free meals to all students. It aligned school meals with dietary guidelines by requiring more fruits, vegetables and whole grains and limits on sodium and calories.

Implementation and debate

The new standards, implemented from 2012, were championed by First Lady Michelle Obama as part of the Let's Move! campaign. They drew praise from public-health groups and criticism from some school food operators and students over cost, palatability and food waste; later administrations relaxed some requirements, such as sodium and whole-grain targets.
