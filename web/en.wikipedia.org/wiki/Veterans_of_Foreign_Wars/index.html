Veterans of Foreign Wars

The VFW emblem is based on the Cross of Malta.

The Veterans of Foreign Wars of the United States (VFW, or simply Veterans of Foreign Wars) is an American war veterans organisation headquartered in Kansas City, Missouri. The VFW's stated purpose is to speed rehabilitation of the nation's disabled and needy veterans, assist veterans' widows and orphans and the dependents of needy or disabled veterans, and promote Americanism by means of education in patriotism and by constructive service to local communities. Founded in 1899 by veterans of the Spanish–American War and the Philippine–American War, it is the nation's largest organisation of overseas combat veterans.

History

The VFW traces its roots to 1899, when veterans returning from the Spanish–American War and the Philippine Insurrection formed local societies to secure rights and benefits for their service. These groups merged and were chartered by Congress in 1936. Membership is restricted to citizens who have served honorably overseas in an expeditionary force or in a conflict, campaign, or occupation recognised by an armed forces service medal.

Programmes

The VFW operates a large network of local posts and departments in all fifty states and overseas. Its well-known programmes include the Buddy Poppy distribution, which raises funds for disabled veterans; the Voice of Democracy and Patriot's Pen scholarship competitions; advocacy before Congress on veterans' benefits; and assistance helping veterans file claims with the Department of Veterans Affairs. The organisation's membership peaked at about 2.1 million in the early 1990s and stands at roughly 1.4 million members in recent years.
