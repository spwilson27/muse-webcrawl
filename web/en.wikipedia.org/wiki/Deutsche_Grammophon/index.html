Deutsche Grammophon (German: [ˈdɔʏtʃə ɡʁamoˈfoːn]; DGG) is a German classical music record label that was the precursor of the corporation PolyGram. Headquartered in Berlin Friedrichshain, it is now part of Universal Music Group (UMG) since its merger in 1998, and is the world's oldest surviving established record company. Its famous yellow logo and yellow-bordered album sleeves are among the most recognisable trademarks in classical music.

History

Deutsche Grammophon Gesellschaft was founded in Hanover in 1898 by Emile Berliner, the inventor of the gramophone record, as the German branch of his record enterprise. The company recorded many of the great performers of the early 20th century, and its "His Master's Voice" trademark was used in some territories. Severed from its British and American affiliates during the First World War, the company later passed through the ownership of Siemens and Philips (via PolyGram) before joining Universal in 1998.

After the Second World War, Deutsche Grammophon rebuilt its catalogue around German orchestras and a new generation of stars. Its exclusive artists came to include conductors Herbert von Karajan, Claudio Abbado, Leonard Bernstein and Carlos Kleiber, the Berlin Philharmonic, and pianists such as Maurizio Pollini, Martha Argerich and Krystian Zimerman. Karajan's hundreds of recordings for the label made him the best-selling classical artist of the recording era.

Labels and series

The company's Archiv Produktion imprint, founded in 1947, became a leading label for historically informed performances of early and Baroque music. Deutsche Grammophon was also an early leader in digital recording and in the marketing of classical music on compact disc.

Present day

Today Deutsche Grammophon remains a flagship classical label of Universal Music, with a roster including Anne-Sophie Mutter, Lang Lang, Hilary Hahn, Daniil Trifonov and Víkingur Ólafsson, and initiatives such as the Yellow Lounge classical club nights and the STAGE+ streaming service.
