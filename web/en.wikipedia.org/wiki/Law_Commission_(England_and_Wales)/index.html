Law Commission (England and Wales)

The Law Commission is based at 52 Queen Anne's Gate, London

The Law Commission is an independent statutory body created by the Law Commissions Act 1965 to keep the law of England and Wales under review and to recommend reform where it is needed. Its purpose is to ensure that the law is as fair, modern, simple, and cost-effective as possible. It is an executive non-departmental public body of the Ministry of Justice.

Composition and work

The Commission consists of a Chairman, who is a High Court or Court of Appeal judge, and four other Commissioners appointed by the Lord Chancellor, supported by a chief executive and staff. Its work falls into four main areas: law reform projects set out in periodic programmes approved by the Lord Chancellor; references from government departments; repeal of obsolete legislation, producing Statute Law (Repeals) Bills; and consolidation of existing legislation into single Acts.

Output and implementation

Landmark work by the Commission includes proposals leading to reforms in contract law, criminal law, landlord and tenant law, and the law of homicide. Since 2010, a protocol between the Commission and the government has sought to improve the rate at which its recommendations are implemented, and a special parliamentary procedure allows uncontroversial Law Commission Bills to pass more quickly. Historically about two-thirds of its law reform recommendations have been enacted in whole or in part.
