Palantir Technologies Inc. is an American publicly traded software company that builds data integration and analytics platforms, headquartered in Denver, Colorado. It was founded in 2003 and takes its name from the palantíri, the "seeing stones" in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. Palantir's early investors included the CIA's venture capital arm, In-Q-Tel, and its first products were built for intelligence and counterterrorism work.

Products

Palantir's flagship platforms are Gotham, used by defence and intelligence agencies to integrate and analyse large, disparate datasets; Foundry, a commercial platform for data operations in industries such as manufacturing, healthcare and finance; Apollo, a deployment and continuous-delivery system; and, launched in 2023, its Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP), which connects large language models to enterprise and military data.

History

The company grew out of anti-fraud technology developed at PayPal. After 9/11, its founders adapted those human-analyst-centred data-mining ideas to counterterrorism. Palantir went public through a direct listing on the New York Stock Exchange in September 2020 (later moving to Nasdaq), and has expanded from government work into large commercial contracts, including with the NHS in England, Airbus, and US military programmes such as Maven and TITAN. Its rapid growth in the mid-2020s made it one of the most valuable software companies in the world.

Criticism

Palantir has attracted sustained criticism and protest over its contracts with immigration enforcement agencies, predictive policing deployments, and military targeting systems, with critics raising concerns about surveillance, civil liberties and algorithmic bias. The company maintains that its software supports privacy-protective, human-directed analysis.
