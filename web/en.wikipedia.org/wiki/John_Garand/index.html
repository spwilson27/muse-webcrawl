Jean Cantius Garand (January 1, 1888 – February 16, 1974), commonly known as John Garand, was a Canadian-American designer of firearms who created the M1 Garand, the first semi-automatic rifle to be generally issued to the infantry of any nation. General George S. Patton famously described the M1 as "the greatest battle implement ever devised".

Early life

Garand was born in St. Rémi, Quebec, and moved with his family to Connecticut as a child. Largely self-taught in engineering, he developed a passion for shooting and machinery, working in tool and machine shops and winning target-shooting competitions.

Work at Springfield Armory

In 1919 Garand went to work at the Springfield Armory in Massachusetts, where he was tasked with developing a semi-automatic infantry rifle. After years of refinement, his design — a gas-operated, .30-calibre rifle fed by an eight-round en-bloc clip — was adopted in 1936 as the Rifle, Caliber .30, M1. Millions were produced during the Second World War and the Korean War.

Legacy

Garand never received royalties for his design, having assigned his patents to the government; he was later awarded the Medal for Merit and the Meritorious Civilian Service Award. He retired from Springfield Armory in 1953.
