ALOHAnet

ALOHAnet
Developed: University of Hawaii
Operational: 1971
Key figure: Norman Abramson
Legacy: random-access protocols; influenced Ethernet and Wi-Fi

ALOHAnet, also known as the ALOHA System, was a pioneering computer networking system developed at the University of Hawaii. It became operational in 1971 and was the first wireless packet data network, connecting the university's computers across the Hawaiian islands by radio.

The ALOHA protocol

The network's medium access method, known as Pure ALOHA, allowed any station to transmit whenever it had data; if a collision corrupted two frames, both stations retransmitted after a random delay. The later Slotted ALOHA refinement, which synchronized transmissions to time slots, roughly doubled the channel's usable throughput.

Influence

ALOHAnet was connected to the ARPANET in 1972, becoming one of the earliest networks joined to it. Its random-access ideas directly influenced the design of Ethernet and later wireless local area networks and mobile packet systems.
