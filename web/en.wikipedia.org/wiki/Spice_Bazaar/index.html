The Spice Bazaar (Turkish: Mısır Çarşısı, meaning "Egyptian Bazaar") in Istanbul, Turkey, is one of the largest bazaars in the city. Located in the Eminönü quarter of the Fatih district, it is the second largest covered shopping complex in Istanbul after the Grand Bazaar. The bazaar forms part of the complex (külliye) of the New Mosque (Yeni Cami), and rents from its shops were intended to fund the mosque's upkeep and charitable services.

History

Construction of the building began in 1660 on the orders of Turhan Hatice Sultan, the valide sultan (queen mother) of Sultan Mehmed IV, as the final part of the New Mosque complex, whose construction had been interrupted decades earlier. The bazaar was completed around 1664 under the supervision of the court architect Mustafa Agha. Its Turkish name, Mısır Çarşısı ("Egyptian Bazaar"), reflects the fact that it was built with tax revenues from Egypt and that many of the spices and goods sold there arrived from Egypt; in English it became known as the Spice Bazaar because of its historic role as the city's centre for the spice trade, the last stop on the caravan and sea routes bringing spices from the East.

The building suffered fires in 1691 and 1755 and was repeatedly restored; the market was comprehensively renovated in the 1940s and again in the late 2010s.

Architecture

The bazaar is an L-shaped, domed stone structure adjoining the New Mosque at the southern end of the Galata Bridge on the Golden Horn. Its two arms contain rows of shops beneath barrel-vaulted and domed ceilings, pierced by windows. Around 85 shops operate inside today, selling spices, dried fruits and nuts, Turkish delight (lokum), caviar, cheeses, teas, herbal remedies, jewellery, and souvenirs. Historically, many shops were famous apothecaries stocked with herbs, roots, and plant extracts, and a number of traditional herbalists still trade there.
