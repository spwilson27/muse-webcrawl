Piper PA-24 Comanche

The Piper PA-24 Comanche is an American single-engine, low-wing, all-metal monoplane with retractable landing gear, designed and built by Piper Aircraft. Introduced in 1958, it was Piper's first high-performance single, competing with the Beechcraft Bonanza, and was produced until 1972, when flooding from Hurricane Agnes destroyed the tooling at Piper's Lock Haven, Pennsylvania plant.

Design and variants

The Comanche was offered with engines from 180 to 400 horsepower, including the Comanche 250 and 260, the turbocharged Turbo Comanche, and the rare 400 model with a large eight-cylinder Lycoming engine. A twin-engine derivative, the PA-30 Twin Comanche, was developed as a light twin using the same basic airframe. The aircraft is noted for its laminar-flow wing, stable handling, and efficient cruising performance.

Records and legacy

Comanches set several distance and speed records; in 1959 a Comanche 250 flew a record non-stop long-distance flight in its class, and in 1964 a Comanche flew around the world. Though succeeded in production by the Piper PA-28 Cherokee family and later models, the Comanche remains a sought-after classic among owner-pilots, supported by an active type club.
