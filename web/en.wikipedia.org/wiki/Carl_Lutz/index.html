Carl Lutz (30 March 1895 – 12 February 1975) was a Swiss diplomat who served as vice-consul in Budapest, Hungary, from 1942 to 1945. He is credited with saving the lives of tens of thousands of Jews during the Holocaust, in what is regarded as the largest civilian rescue operation of the Second World War.

Rescue work in Budapest

As head of the foreign interests section of the Swiss legation, Lutz negotiated an agreement permitting him to issue protective letters (Schutzbriefe) to 8,000 Jewish families for emigration to Palestine; he then interpreted the quota as applying to individuals per document number, reusing number ranges to protect far more people. He established 76 safe houses under Swiss protection, including the famous Glass House on Vadász Street, and repeatedly intervened personally, on one occasion jumping into the Danube to pull a wounded Jewish woman from the water as Arrow Cross militiamen fired.

Recognition

Lutz was recognised as Righteous Among the Nations by Yad Vashem in 1964. Despite this, he received little official gratitude in Switzerland during his lifetime, having exceeded his authority; a formal rehabilitation came only decades after his death.
