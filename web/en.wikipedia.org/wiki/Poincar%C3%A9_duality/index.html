Poincaré duality

Poincaré duality
FieldAlgebraic topology
Named afterHenri Poincaré
Statement (informal)For a closed orientable n-manifold, the k-th and (n−k)-th (co)homology groups are isomorphic

Poincaré duality is a central theorem of algebraic topology, first asserted by Henri Poincaré in the 1890s, expressing a fundamental symmetry in the homology of manifolds. For a closed, orientable n-dimensional manifold, the k-th cohomology group is isomorphic to the (n−k)-th homology group, realized concretely by cap product with the manifold's fundamental class.

Consequences

For a closed orientable surface of genus g, duality explains why the first Betti number is even: holes come in dual pairs. In general it implies that the Betti numbers satisfy bₖ = bₙ₋ₖ, that the Euler characteristic of a closed orientable odd-dimensional manifold is zero, and, in its bilinear form, that the intersection pairing on the middle-dimensional homology of a 4-manifold is a powerful invariant — the starting point of Donaldson's work on smooth 4-manifolds.

Extensions

Versions of the duality hold with twisted coefficients for non-orientable manifolds, for manifolds with boundary (Lefschetz duality), and in generalized cohomology theories; it also underlies the notion of a Poincaré complex and mirrors dualities in physics, such as electric–magnetic duality.
