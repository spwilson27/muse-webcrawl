Anthony Norman Albanese (born 2 March 1963) is an Australian politician serving as the 31st prime minister of Australia since 2022. He has been leader of the Australian Labor Party since 2019 and member of parliament for the New South Wales division of Grayndler since 1996.

Early life and career

Albanese was raised by a single mother in public housing in Camperdown, Sydney, and educated at St Mary's Cathedral College and the University of Sydney, where he studied economics and became involved in student politics and the Labor Left faction. Before entering parliament he worked for the Commonwealth Bank and as an adviser to New South Wales minister Tom Uren. Elected in 1996, he rose through the shadow ministry and, after Labor's 2007 victory under Kevin Rudd, became Minister for Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development and Local Government. In June 2013 he became deputy prime minister in Rudd's second ministry.

Opposition leader

After Labor's defeat in 2013, Albanese lost the leadership ballot to Bill Shorten despite winning the membership vote. Following Labor's loss at the 2019 election, he was elected leader unopposed. As opposition leader during the COVID-19 pandemic he campaigned on strengthening Medicare, action on climate change and the establishment of a constitutionally enshrined Indigenous Voice to Parliament.

Prime minister

Albanese led Labor to victory at the 21 May 2022 election, defeating Scott Morrison's Coalition. His government legislated a 43 per cent emissions reduction target for 2030, established the National Anti-Corruption Commission, and held the unsuccessful 2023 referendum on the Indigenous Voice. In foreign policy, the government pursued the AUKUS submarine agreement with the United States and United Kingdom and sought to stabilise relations with China. Albanese is the first Australian prime minister to have been raised by a single parent in public housing, and is known by the nickname "Albo."
