Birmingham New Street is the largest and busiest railway station in Birmingham, England, and the busiest station in the United Kingdom outside London. It is a central hub of the national rail network, with direct trains to destinations across England, Scotland, and Wales. The station lies beneath the Grand Central shopping centre in the city centre.

History

New Street opened in 1854 as a joint station of the London and North Western Railway and the Midland Railway, notable for what was then the largest single-span arched roof in the world, designed by E. A. Cowper. The station was heavily rebuilt in the 1960s as part of the West Coast Main Line electrification: the Victorian station was demolished and replaced by a concrete structure (opened 1967) with the platforms enclosed beneath a shopping centre, a widely criticised design that made the station dark and cramped.

Redevelopment

Between 2010 and 2015 the station was transformed by a £750 million redevelopment: a vast new atrium concourse with a light-filled roof replaced the old concourse, the exterior was wrapped in polished stainless steel with a large media eye screen, and the shopping centre above reopened as Grand Central, anchored by a John Lewis store. The platform layout of 12 through platforms and one bay was retained.

Services

Operators using New Street include Avanti West Coast, CrossCountry, West Midlands Trains, and Transport for Wales. The station is the focal point of the Cross City Line and of West Midlands local services and handles well over a thousand trains per day. It is close to Birmingham Moor Street and linked to the West Midlands Metro tram network.
