Dorsolateral prefrontal cortex

The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC or DL-PFC) is an area in the prefrontal cortex of the primate brain. It corresponds roughly to Brodmann areas 9 and 46 and is one of the most recently developed parts of the human brain. The DLPFC is not a precisely defined anatomical structure but a functional region involved in executive functions such as working memory, cognitive flexibility, planning, and abstract reasoning.

Function

The DLPFC is a central node of the central executive network. It is strongly implicated in holding and manipulating information in working memory, in task switching, decision-making, and the top-down regulation of attention and behaviour. It has extensive connections with the parietal cortex, thalamus, hippocampus, and the basal ganglia through the dorsolateral prefrontal circuit.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of the DLPFC is associated with several conditions. Hypoactivity of the region is a consistent finding in schizophrenia and relates to its cognitive deficits; abnormal activity is also implicated in depression. The left DLPFC is a standard target for repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) in the treatment of major depressive disorder. Damage to the area can produce a dysexecutive syndrome with poor planning and impaired working memory.
