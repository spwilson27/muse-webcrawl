Gainsborough Line

The Gainsborough Line is a rural branch line in Essex and Suffolk, England, running from Marks Tey on the Great Eastern Main Line to Sudbury. The line is 11 miles 53 chains (18.8 km) long and single-track throughout, with services operated by Greater Anglia. It takes its marketing name from the painter Thomas Gainsborough, who was born in Sudbury.

History

The line was opened by the Colchester, Stour Valley, Sudbury and Halstead Railway in stages in the late 1840s, reaching Sudbury in 1849. It was later absorbed into the Great Eastern Railway. The continuation beyond Sudbury toward Cambridge, via Long Melford and Haverhill, closed in 1967 under the Beeching cuts, leaving the branch to Sudbury as a stub worked as a single-line shuttle from Marks Tey.

Route and stations

From Marks Tey, the line serves Chappel and Wakes Colne (home of the East Anglian Railway Museum), Bures, and Sudbury. Its most striking feature is the Chappel Viaduct, one of the largest brick viaducts in England, carrying the line across the Colne Valley. The line is promoted by a community rail partnership and marketed to tourists visiting "Constable Country" and the Gainsborough's House museum in Sudbury.

Services

Trains generally run hourly between Marks Tey and Sudbury, connecting at Marks Tey with main-line services to Colchester, Ipswich, and London Liverpool Street. The branch is not electrified and is worked by diesel multiple units.
