Counties of Iran

The counties of Iran, called shahrestan (Persian: شهرستان, romanized: shahrestân) in Persian, are the second-level administrative subdivisions of the country, below the provinces (ostan) and above the districts (bakhsh). Each county is administered by a governor (farmandar) appointed by the Ministry of the Interior.

Structure

Iran is divided into 31 provinces, which are subdivided into counties. A typical county consists of one or more districts, each of which contains a city or a group of rural districts (dehestan). The county capital is usually its largest city, and many counties share their name with that city; for example, Tehran County is centred on the capital city of Tehran.

History

The shahrestan system has roots in older Persian administrative practice, but its modern form was established during the administrative reforms of the Pahlavi era in the 20th century and retained, with modifications, after the Iranian Revolution of 1979. As populations have grown, new counties have repeatedly been carved out of existing ones, steadily increasing the total number. Under the law on national divisions, the creation or division of counties is approved by the Council of Ministers.

Examples

Some of the most populous counties include Tehran County, Mashhad County in Razavi Khorasan, Isfahan County, Shiraz County in Fars, and Tabriz County in East Azerbaijan. The smallest counties by population are found in sparsely settled desert and mountain provinces.
