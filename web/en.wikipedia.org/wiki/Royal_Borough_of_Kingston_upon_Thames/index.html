Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames

The Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames is a borough in south-west London, England, forming part of Outer London on the south bank of the River Thames. It is one of only four royal boroughs in England and the oldest, its royal status descending from the ancient royal borough of Kingston upon Thames, where Saxon kings were crowned. The borough was created in 1965 by the merger of the municipal boroughs of Kingston upon Thames, Malden and Coombe, and Surbiton. Its administrative centre is the town of Kingston upon Thames, a major retail and educational centre.

History

Kingston's Coronation Stone, now outside the Guildhall, is traditionally the stone on which Saxon kings including Æthelstan (925) and Æthelred the Unready (978) were crowned. The town received its first charter in 1200 and was a Surrey market town and river crossing until absorbed into Greater London in 1965. The royal borough title, inherited from Kingston's ancient municipal borough, was confirmed for the new borough — one of a small number of local authorities permitted to use the "Royal Borough" style.

Districts and amenities

The borough includes Kingston town centre, Surbiton, New Malden (noted for its large Korean community), Chessington (home to Chessington World of Adventures), Tolworth, Norbiton, Old Malden and parts of Worcester Park. It is served by Kingston University and Kingston College, and its shopping centre around the Bentall Centre and the ancient market place is one of the largest retail destinations in south London. Transport links include Kingston, Surbiton, New Malden, Tolworth and Chessington stations, with the Thames riverside, Bushy Park and Richmond Park close by. The borough council is Kingston upon Thames London Borough Council, based at the Guildhall.
