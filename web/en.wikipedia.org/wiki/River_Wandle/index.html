River Wandle
LocationSouth London, England
SourceWaddon and Carshalton (chalk springs)

The River Wandle is a right-bank tributary of the River Thames in south London. Just under 9 miles (14 km) long, it rises from chalk springs at Waddon and Carshalton and flows north through Croydon, Sutton, Merton, and Wandsworth to join the Thames beside Wandsworth Bridge. Although short, it drops about 30 metres over its course and was once noted as one of the hardest-working rivers in England, powering dozens of mills, and later recognised as one of the most polluted; extensive cleanup since the late 20th century has restored it as a chalk stream fishery in the heart of the city.

Course

The Wandle has two principal headwaters that meet near Croydon: one branch rises at Waddon Ponds, fed by springs from the chalk of the North Downs, and the other flows from springs at Carshalton Ponds through Beddington Park. From Croydon the river runs north through Waddon, past the site of Croydon's mills, then through Beddington—where it passes the ornamental lakes of Beddington Park and the historic Carew Manor—Hackbridge, Mitcham, Morden Hall Park (a National Trust park where it drives a restored waterwheel), Colliers Wood, Earlsfield, and finally Wandsworth, where it enters the Thames.

Industrial history

The Wandle's steady chalk-fed flow in a short distance powered, at its peak, an estimated 68 mills, used for corn, snuff, copper, leather, paper, calico printing, and oil. William Morris established his Merton Abbey Mills works on the river in 1881, drawn by its clean water for dyeing and printing; Liberty's later printed fabrics at the same site, and the mills of Mitcham produced lavender and herbs for the pharmaceutical trade. Industrial effluent made the river notoriously polluted by the mid-20th century, and it was declared biologically dead in the 1960s. Major sewer improvements and volunteer cleanups transformed it; trout returned, and in 2007 a major pollution incident that killed fish led to fines and a large restocking programme.

Recreation

The Wandle Trail, a walking and cycling path, follows the river from Croydon to Wandsworth, linking a chain of parks. The river gives its name to Wandsworth ("Wendlesworth", Wendel's enclosure), the Wandle Valley, and local institutions including the Wandle Industrial Museum.
