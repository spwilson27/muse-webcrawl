Quantile

In statistics and probability, quantiles are cut points dividing the range of a probability distribution into continuous intervals with equal probabilities, or dividing the observations in a sample in the same way. There is one fewer quantile than the number of groups created. Common quantiles have special names, such as quartiles (four groups), deciles (ten groups), and percentiles (100 groups). The groups created are called halves, thirds, quarters, etc., though the quantile terms are sometimes used for the groups created rather than the cut points.

Definition

For a random variable X with cumulative distribution function F, the q-quantile for 0 < q < 1 is the value x such that

P(X ≤ x) ≥ q and P(X ≥ x) ≥ 1 − q,

equivalently the generalized inverse of F evaluated at q. The median is the 0.5-quantile.

Estimation

Estimating quantiles from a finite sample is a standard statistical problem; numerous methods exist, differing in how they interpolate between order statistics. Software packages such as R implement several definitions (Hyndman and Fan catalogued nine).

Applications

Quantiles are used in robust statistics, risk management (value at risk is a quantile of the loss distribution), and in summarizing distributions via quantile functions and Q–Q plots.
