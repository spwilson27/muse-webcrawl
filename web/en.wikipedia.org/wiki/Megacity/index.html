Megacity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A megacity is a very large city, typically with a population of more than 10 million people. The United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs applies the term to urban agglomerations exceeding 10 million inhabitants. In the mid-2020s there were roughly 35–45 megacities worldwide depending on the definition used, with the number expected to keep growing as urbanization proceeds.

Largest megacities

The largest urban agglomerations include Tokyo (the world's largest, at around 37 million), Delhi, Shanghai, Dhaka, São Paulo, Cairo, Mexico City, Beijing, Mumbai, and Osaka. Much of the recent growth has been in Asia and Africa: cities such as Lagos, Kinshasa, and Karachi have grown rapidly, and demographers project that most future megacities will emerge in the developing world.

Challenges

Megacities concentrate economic output, culture, and innovation, but also face challenges of housing (including large informal settlements), transport congestion, air and water pollution, waste management, water supply, and vulnerability to heat and flooding. Governance is complicated because the functional urban area often spans many municipalities and may cross provincial or national boundaries.

Related terms

A megalopolis is a chain or cluster of adjacent metropolitan areas merging into one urban region, such as the Boston–Washington corridor in the United States. A global city is defined less by population than by its influence in the world economy, finance, and culture.
