DFA Records

DFA Records is an American independent record label founded in 2001 by Mo' Wax co-founder Tim Goldsworthy, musician James Murphy, and manager Jonathan Galkin. The label grew out of the DFA production team — Murphy and Goldsworthy's Death from Above studio and production partnership — and became a defining force of the dance-punk movement of the 2000s.

History

Based in New York City, DFA first gained attention with productions and remixes for artists such as Le Tigre and Radio 4, and with the breakthrough singles of The Rapture, notably "House of Jealous Lovers" (2002). James Murphy's own band, LCD Soundsystem, released its recordings through the label, beginning with the single "Losing My Edge" (2002) and the self-titled debut album in 2005.

Artists

The DFA roster and associated acts have included LCD Soundsystem, The Rapture, Hot Chip (in some territories), Holy Ghost!, The Juan MacLean, Hercules and Love Affair, Shit Robot, Yacht, Planningtorock, and Gavin Russom. The label is recognized by its lightning-bolt logo and for bridging indie rock and electronic dance music.

Legacy

DFA's releases helped popularize dance-punk and indie dance in the 2000s, and the label continues to release records from its New York base.
