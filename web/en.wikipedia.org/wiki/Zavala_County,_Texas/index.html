Zavala County is a county located in the U.S. state of Texas. Its county seat and largest community is Crystal City. The county was created in 1858 and organised in 1884. It is named for Lorenzo de Zavala, a Mexican politician who served as the first vice president of the Republic of Texas.

Geography

Located in South Texas within the Winter Garden region, the county covers about 1,302 square miles of mostly flat to gently rolling terrain drained by the Nueces River and its tributaries. The area is known for irrigated agriculture, including spinach — Crystal City styles itself the "Spinach Capital of the World" and has a statue of the cartoon character Popeye.

History

The county was a centre of Chicano political organising in the 1960s and 1970s. In 1963, Mexican-American candidates backed by the Political Association of Spanish-Speaking Organizations won control of the Crystal City council in a landmark election, and in 1969-70 the Crystal City school walkouts helped spur the Chicano movement; the Raza Unida Party was founded there in 1970.

Demographics

As of the 2020 census, the county had a population of 9,670, the large majority of Hispanic or Latino origin. It is among the more rural and lower-income counties in Texas, with an economy based on agriculture, ranching and public services.
