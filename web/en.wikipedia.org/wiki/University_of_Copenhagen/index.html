University of Copenhagen

The University of Copenhagen (Danish: Københavns Universitet, abbreviated KU) is a public research university in Copenhagen, Denmark. Founded in 1479, it is the oldest university in Denmark and one of the oldest in Northern Europe. With roughly 37,000 students and about 10,000 employees, it is also the country's largest institution of research and education.

History

The university was inaugurated on 1 June 1479 by King Christian I with approval from Pope Sixtus IV, modelled on German universities. It was re-established in 1537 after the Reformation as a Lutheran institution. Over the centuries it expanded from the traditional four faculties of theology, law, medicine, and philosophy; today it has six faculties, including health and medical sciences, humanities, law, science, social sciences, and theology.

Research and alumni

The university is a member of the International Alliance of Research Universities and ranks among the leading universities in Europe. Associated researchers include astronomer Tycho Brahe, physicist Niels Bohr, and philosopher Søren Kierkegaard; a number of Nobel laureates have been affiliated with the university, among them Bohr, Niels Ryberg Finsen, and Morten Meldal, who won the 2022 chemistry prize.
