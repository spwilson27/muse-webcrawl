San Francisco Mime Troupe

The San Francisco Mime Troupe is a theatre company founded in 1959 in San Francisco by R. G. Davis. Despite its name, the troupe does not perform pantomime; "mime" is used in the ancient sense of mimicry. The company creates and performs free, outdoor, politically engaged musical comedies in parks around the San Francisco Bay Area and on tour, using the style of commedia dell'arte and melodrama to satirise American politics, militarism, racism, and corporate power.

History

The troupe began as an offshoot of the Actors' Workshop and developed under Davis into a guerrilla theatre ensemble. Its 1965 performance of A Minstrel Show, or Civil Rights in a Cracker Barrel led to Davis's arrest for performing in a public park without a permit; the resulting legal battle helped secure the right to perform free theatre in San Francisco's parks. Actor Peter Coyote was a member in the 1960s, developing the company that later spun off into the Diggers. The troupe adopted a collective structure in the early 1970s and continues to produce a new show each summer season.

Style and recognition

Productions combine original live music, broad comic types, topical scripts, and a signature passing of the hat. Notable productions include The Dragon Lady's Revenge (1971), False Promises (1976), and more recent works addressing housing, fracking, and war. In 1987 the company, together with playwright Tony Kushner having earlier ties to the Bay Area theatre scene, achieved institutional milestones; the Troupe received a regional Tony Award (Special Tony Award for Excellence in Regional Theatre) in 1987 — the first time the honour went to a company of its kind.
