Cinema of Nigeria

Cinema of Nigeria (Nollywood)
Hub: Lagos; also Asaba, Enugu, Kano (Kannywood)
Known for: High-volume direct-to-video era (1990s–2000s); New Nigerian Cinema
Landmark: Living in Bondage (1992)

The cinema of Nigeria, often called Nollywood, is one of the world's largest film industries by volume. Nigerian films are produced in English and in Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and other languages, and are watched across Africa and the diaspora.

History

Filmmaking in colonial Nigeria began in the early 20th century; indigenous features grew after independence, with directors such as Ola Balogun and Hubert Ogunde. Economic decline hit celluloid production in the 1980s, but the 1992 direct-to-video hit Living in Bondage launched a video-film boom — thousands of low-budget titles sold in markets across Africa.

New Nigerian Cinema

From around 2009, higher-budget films returned to cinemas, e.g. Kunle Afolayan's The Figurine, and later streaming-era titles such as The Wedding Party and Anikulapo. Challenges include piracy, financing and distribution; the industry remains a major employer and cultural export.
