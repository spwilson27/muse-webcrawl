Eighteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution

18th Amendment
ProposedDecember 18, 1917
RatifiedJanuary 16, 1919
EffectiveJanuary 17, 1920
RepealedDecember 5, 1933, by 21st Amendment
SubjectProhibition of alcohol

The Eighteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution established the prohibition of alcohol in the United States. Proposed by Congress on December 18, 1917, it was ratified on January 16, 1919. After one year from ratification, the manufacture, sale, or transportation of intoxicating liquors for beverage purposes was prohibited.

Text

Section 1: "After one year from the ratification of this article the manufacture, sale, or transportation of intoxicating liquors within, the importation thereof into, or the exportation thereof from the United States and all territory subject to the jurisdiction thereof for beverage purposes is hereby prohibited." Section 2 gave Congress and the states concurrent enforcement power. Section 3 required ratification within seven years.

Background

The amendment grew from the temperance movement, led by groups such as the Women's Christian Temperance Union and the Anti-Saloon League. The Volstead Act, passed October 28, 1919, provided enforcement.

Prohibition era

From 1920 to 1933, Prohibition reshaped American society, fostering speakeasies, bootlegging, and organized crime, while consumption initially fell then recovered. Enforcement was uneven and underfunded.

Repeal

Growing opposition, the Great Depression's need for tax revenue, and crime led to repeal by the Twenty-first Amendment in 1933 — the only amendment repealing another.
