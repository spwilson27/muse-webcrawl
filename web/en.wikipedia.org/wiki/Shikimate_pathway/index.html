Shikimate pathway

The shikimate pathway (shikimic acid pathway) is a seven-step metabolic pathway used by bacteria, archaea, fungi, algae, some protozoans, and plants to biosynthesise folates and the aromatic amino acids phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan. The pathway is absent from animals, which must obtain these amino acids from their diet — a fact that makes the pathway an attractive target for herbicides and antimicrobials.

Steps

The pathway begins with the condensation of phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) and erythrose 4-phosphate to form DAHP, catalysed by DAHP synthase. Subsequent steps proceed through 3-dehydroquinate, 3-dehydroshikimate, shikimate (the intermediate that gives the pathway its name), shikimate 3-phosphate, and EPSP, ending at chorismate — the branch-point precursor of the aromatic amino acids, folate, ubiquinone, and many secondary metabolites.

Herbicide target

The fifth/sixth enzyme of the pathway, EPSP synthase, is the target of glyphosate, the active ingredient in the herbicide Roundup. Glyphosate inhibits EPSP synthase, starving plants of aromatic amino acids. Because animals lack the enzyme, glyphosate's acute toxicity to them is low, though its environmental effects remain debated. Crops engineered with a glyphosate-insensitive EPSP synthase (such as the CP4 enzyme from Agrobacterium) are widely grown.

Regulation

In plants the pathway operates in plastids and is regulated by feedback inhibition of DAHP synthase isoforms. In microorganisms, enzymes may be fused into multifunctional complexes, such as the pentafunctional AROM complex of fungi.
