Fangataufa

Fangataufa is an uninhabited coral atoll in French Polynesia, used by France as a nuclear test site.

Fangataufa is a small, low-lying, uninhabited coral atoll in the Tuamotu Archipelago of French Polynesia in the South Pacific Ocean. Together with the neighbouring atoll of Moruroa (Mururoa), about 40 kilometres to the northwest, Fangataufa served as a French nuclear weapons test site.

Nuclear testing

Between 1966 and 1996, France conducted 193 nuclear tests at Moruroa and Fangataufa. The larger thermonuclear tests were carried out at Fangataufa, including France's first hydrogen bomb, code-named Canopus, detonated on 24 August 1968 with a yield of about 2.6 megatons — the largest device ever tested by France. After 1974, French testing moved underground, with devices detonated in shafts drilled beneath the atoll's lagoon and rim.

After testing

Testing ended in 1996 after the last series of underground tests provoked international protest, and France signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty. Fangataufa remains under military control and access is restricted; radiological surveys of the atolls continue to be monitored by French authorities and international observers.

Geography

The atoll consists of a narrow ring of coral islets surrounding a lagoon. Its total land area is about 5 square kilometres. There is no permanent civilian population.
