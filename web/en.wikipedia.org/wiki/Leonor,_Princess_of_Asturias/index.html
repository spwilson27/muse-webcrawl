Leonor, Princess of Asturias (Leonor de Todos los Santos de Borbón y Ortiz; born 31 October 2005) is the heir presumptive to the Spanish throne. She is the elder daughter of King Felipe VI and Queen Letizia and the granddaughter of King Juan Carlos I and Queen Sofía. Since her father's accession on 19 June 2014 she has held the traditional titles of the heir to the Crown: Princess of Asturias, Princess of Girona, Princess of Viana, Duchess of Montblanc, Countess of Cervera, and Lady of Balaguer. If she ascends the throne as expected, she will be Spain's first queen regnant since Isabella II, who reigned from 1833 to 1868.

Early life and education

Leonor was born at the Ruber International Hospital in Madrid during the reign of her grandfather, Juan Carlos I. She was educated at the Santa María de los Rosales school in Madrid, which her father and younger sister, Infanta Sofía, also attended. In 2021 she left Spain to study for the International Baccalaureate at UWC Atlantic College in Wales, graduating in 2023. Besides Spanish, she speaks English, Catalan, and some Galician and Basque, and has studied French, Arabic, and Mandarin.

She delivered her first public speech in October 2018, reading from the Spanish Constitution on its 40th anniversary, and has chaired the Princess of Asturias Awards ceremony in Oviedo each year since 2019.

Oath and military training

On 31 October 2023, her eighteenth birthday, Leonor swore the oath to the Constitution before the Cortes Generales, as her father had done in 1986, formally taking up her institutional role as heir. In August 2023 she had begun a three-year programme of military training designed to prepare her as future commander-in-chief of the armed forces: a year at the General Military Academy in Zaragoza (Army), followed by the Naval Military Academy in Marín — including service aboard the training ship Juan Sebastián de Elcano — and the General Air Academy in San Javier.

Succession

Under the Spanish Constitution, succession follows male-preference cognatic primogeniture, so Leonor is heir presumptive rather than heir apparent: a younger brother would displace her in the line. Successive governments have discussed reforming the rule to absolute primogeniture, but the amendment procedure is exceptionally demanding and no change has been enacted. As the monarchy's popularity has been rebuilt under Felipe VI after the scandals of Juan Carlos I's later reign, Leonor's public role is widely seen as central to the institution's future.
