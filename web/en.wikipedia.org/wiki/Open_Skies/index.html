Open Skies

Open Skies may refer to several related concepts in aviation and arms control. In air transport policy, "open skies" agreements liberalize the rules governing international aviation between countries, allowing airlines greater freedom over routes, capacity, and pricing. Separately, the Treaty on Open Skies was an arms-control agreement permitting unarmed aerial observation flights over the territories of its parties.

Open skies aviation agreements

The United States pioneered open skies bilateral agreements beginning in the early 1990s, removing government restrictions on which airlines could fly between the two countries, to which cities, and at what fares. A major multilateral example is the agreement between the United States and the European Union, signed in 2007 and applied from 2008, which opened transatlantic routes to any US or EU carrier. Similar liberalized regimes exist elsewhere, such as the European single aviation market and agreements in Southeast Asia.

Treaty on Open Skies

The Treaty on Open Skies, signed in 1992 and in force from 2002, allowed participating states to conduct unarmed reconnaissance flights over each other's territory using agreed sensors, in order to build mutual confidence and transparency about military forces. The United States withdrew from the treaty in 2020, and Russia withdrew in 2021, greatly weakening the regime.
