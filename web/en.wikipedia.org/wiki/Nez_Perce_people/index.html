Nez Perce people

The Nez Perce (autonym: Nimíipuu, "the people") are a Native American people of the Plateau region of the Pacific Northwest of the United States. Their name derives from the French nez percé ("pierced nose"), a misnomer applied by early explorers. Their traditional homeland centred on the Snake, Clearwater and Salmon river valleys in present-day Idaho, Oregon and Washington.

History

The Nez Perce encountered Lewis and Clark in 1805 and maintained generally friendly relations with the United States for decades. The treaties of 1855 and 1863 progressively reduced their reservation; the 1863 treaty, signed by only some bands, was rejected by the "non-treaty" bands.

In 1877, resisting forced removal, the non-treaty Nez Perce under leaders including Chief Joseph (Hinmatóowyalahtq̓it) undertook a fighting retreat of some 1,170 miles toward Canada, pursued by the US Army, before surrendering at Bear Paw, Montana, near the border. Joseph's surrender speech, ending "...I will fight no more forever," became famous.

Today

The federally recognised Nez Perce Tribe is headquartered at Lapwai, Idaho, with a reservation of roughly 750,000 acres in north-central Idaho; other descendants are among the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation in Washington.
