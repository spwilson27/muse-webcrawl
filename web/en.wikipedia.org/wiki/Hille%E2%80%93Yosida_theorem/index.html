Hille–Yosida theorem

The Hille–Yosida theorem is a result in functional analysis characterising the generators of strongly continuous one-parameter semigroups of linear operators on Banach spaces. It is named after Einar Hille and Kōsaku Yosida, who proved it independently in 1948.

Statement

A closed, densely defined linear operator generates a contraction semigroup if its resolvent set contains all sufficiently large positive real numbers and the resolvent satisfies a uniform bound on all its powers.

Applications

The theorem is a cornerstone of the theory of evolution equations, used to solve heat, wave, and Schrödinger equations as abstract Cauchy problems.
