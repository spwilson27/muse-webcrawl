Crossroads (folklore)

In folklore, a crossroads is a liminal place, between worlds, where supernatural beings may be met and where deals, burials and rituals are located. Crossroads appear in traditions worldwide.

Traditions

In ancient Greece, crossroads were associated with Hecate and Hermes, with shrines (herms) placed there. In European folklore, suicides and criminals were sometimes buried at crossroads, and crossroads were places to summon or meet the Devil — a motif central to the legend of blues musician Robert Johnson selling his soul. In Afro-Brazilian and Afro-Cuban traditions, Exu / Elegua guards crossroads; in Hoodoo, crossroads rituals are performed to gain skills.

Symbolism

As a place of choice and uncertainty, the crossroads symbolises a decision point, fate, and the meeting of the human and spirit worlds.
