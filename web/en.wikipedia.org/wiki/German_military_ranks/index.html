German military ranks

The ranks of the German Armed Forces (Bundeswehr) designate the military status, station, and pay grade of soldiers. German rank titles have a long continuity reaching back through the Wehrmacht, the Reichswehr, and the Imperial German Army, though insignia and some titles have changed over time.

Modern Bundeswehr ranks

The Bundeswehr (founded 1955) uses the following principal ranks:

The Navy (Deutsche Marine) uses parallel titles such as Kapitän zur See, Korvettenkapitän, and the specialist rank designations of its branches.

Historical ranks

In the Wehrmacht (1935–1945), the army's highest ranks included Generalfeldmarschall and Generaloberst, with the Waffen-SS using its own parallel titles (e.g., SS-Oberst-Gruppenführer). The East German National People's Army (1956–1990) adopted Soviet-influenced insignia while retaining German rank names, including Marschall der DDR, a rank created but never awarded. After reunification in 1990, only the Bundeswehr system continued.
