Transcription coregulator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In molecular biology, a transcription coregulator is a protein that regulates the transcription of genes by interacting with transcription factors. Unlike transcription factors, coregulators do not bind DNA directly in a sequence-specific manner; instead they are recruited to promoters and enhancers by protein–protein interactions with DNA-bound factors, and they activate or repress transcription by modifying chromatin or by contacting the basal transcription machinery. Coregulators that enhance transcription are called coactivators; those that reduce it are corepressors.

Mechanisms

Coregulators act through several mechanisms:

  • Chromatin modification: many coregulators are enzymes that add or remove chemical marks on histones — for example histone acetyltransferases (HATs) such as CBP/p300, which generally open chromatin and activate genes, and histone deacetylases (HDACs) recruited by corepressors such as NCoR and SMRT, which compact chromatin and repress genes. Others methylate or demethylate histones or remodel nucleosome positions using ATP.
  • Bridging: large complexes such as Mediator physically link enhancer-bound transcription factors to RNA polymerase II and the general transcription factors at the promoter.
  • Recruitment of further machinery: coregulators can recruit additional enzymes or the splicing and elongation apparatus, coupling transcription initiation to later steps in gene expression.

Nuclear receptor coregulators

Coregulators were characterised most fully for the nuclear hormone receptors (such as the estrogen, thyroid hormone and glucocorticoid receptors). In the absence of ligand, receptors bound to DNA recruit corepressors (NCoR, SMRT) and HDACs, keeping target genes off. Ligand binding changes the receptor's shape, ejecting corepressors and recruiting coactivators of the SRC/p160 family, which in turn bring in CBP/p300 and other HATs. Many coactivators recognise receptors through short LXXLL sequence motifs ("NR boxes").

Significance

Because a single transcription factor can produce opposite outputs depending on which coregulators are present in a cell, coregulators are central to tissue-specific and signal-dependent gene regulation. Mutations or altered expression of coregulators are implicated in cancers (for example, amplification of the coactivator AIB1/SRC-3 in breast cancer, and rearrangements of CBP/p300 in leukaemias), metabolic disease, and developmental disorders. More than 300 coregulators have been catalogued in humans.
