Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act

Dodd–Frank Act
Enacted: 21 July 2010
Signed by: President Barack Obama
Named for: Sen. Chris Dodd, Rep. Barney Frank
Public Law: 111–203

The Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, commonly referred to as Dodd–Frank, is a United States federal statute enacted in response to the financial crisis of 2008. It made sweeping changes to financial regulation.

Provisions

The Act created the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), the Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC), and an orderly liquidation authority for failing systemically important firms. The Volcker Rule restricted proprietary trading by banks. Derivatives were pushed toward central clearing and exchange trading, and banks faced higher capital and stress-testing requirements.

Legislative history

Passed by Congress in mid-2010 after extensive debate, it was signed on 21 July 2010. Implementation required hundreds of rulemakings by agencies including the Federal Reserve, SEC, and CFTC.

Amendments and debate

Supporters credit it with reducing systemic risk; critics argue it burdened community banks and entrenched large institutions. The Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act of 2018 rolled back some provisions for mid-sized banks.
