The Kobo eReader is a line of e-readers produced by Toronto-based Rakuten Kobo Inc. The first device was released in 2010 as an affordable alternative to Amazon's Kindle, and the range has since grown to include touchscreen models, waterproof devices, colour e-readers, and large-format note-taking tablets such as the Kobo Elipsa.

History

Kobo began as the e-book service of Canadian bookseller Indigo Books & Music ("Kobo" is an anagram of "book"); its first eReader launched in 2010 at a lower price than competitors and was sold through Indigo/Chapters stores in Canada and Borders in the United States. Japanese e-commerce company Rakuten acquired Kobo in 2012. Subsequent models included the Kobo Touch (2011), the backlit Kobo Glo (2012), the waterproof Aura H2O (2014), the 7.8-inch Aura One (2016), developed with input from the user community, and the Elipsa (2021) with stylus support. In 2024 Kobo released its first colour devices, the Libra Colour and Clara Colour, using E Ink Kaleido screens.

Software and ecosystem

Kobo devices are noted for supporting the open EPUB format (with or without Adobe DRM) and for library borrowing through OverDrive integration in many countries, as well as Pocket article syncing. The built-in Kobo Store sells e-books and audiobooks in numerous localised storefronts, and the devices track reading statistics and offer extensive typographic customisation.
