President of the Senate (Australia)

SeatSenate chamber, Parliament House, Canberra
AppointerElected by the Senate
Term lengthAt the pleasure of the Senate
Formation9 May 1901
First holderSir Richard Baker

The President of the Senate is the presiding officer of the Australian Senate, the upper house of the Parliament of Australia. Section 17 of the Australian Constitution requires the Senate to choose a senator to be its president before proceeding to any other business, and a new president must be chosen whenever the office becomes vacant. The president maintains order in the chamber, interprets and enforces the standing orders, and represents the Senate on formal occasions.

Election and tenure

The president is elected by secret ballot of senators when more than one candidate is nominated. By convention the president is a senator from the governing party, although the government rarely holds a majority in the Senate. Unlike the Speaker of the House of Representatives, the president retains an active party role, including voting in party room meetings, but in the chamber votes only in the ordinary way as a senator — the Australian Senate president has no casting vote, because Senate votes tied are resolved in the negative.

Functions

Within the chamber, the president calls senators to speak, puts questions for votes, rules on points of order, and may suspend or name disorderly senators. Outside it, the president is responsible, jointly with the Speaker, for the administration of Parliament House and the parliamentary departments. The president also performs ceremonial duties and hosts visiting parliamentary delegations. If both the Governor-General and the state governors are unavailable, the president is among the senior officeholders in the order of precedence.

Deputy President

The Senate also elects a Deputy President, who presides in the president's absence and chairs the Committee of the Whole. By convention since the 1990s, the Deputy President is drawn from a different party grouping than the president — commonly from the opposition — reflecting a broader practice of sharing the chamber's offices.

History

The first president, Sir Richard Baker of South Australia, was elected on 9 May 1901 at the first sitting of the federal Parliament in Melbourne. Early presidents were expected to be strictly impartial, modelling themselves on the Speaker of the House of Commons; over the twentieth century the office evolved toward a more partisan profile, balanced by strong conventions of fairness in chairing debates. The president moved with the Parliament to Canberra in 1927 and to the new Parliament House in 1988.
