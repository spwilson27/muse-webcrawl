Alexander Aronovich Pechersky (22 February 1909 – 19 January 1990) was a Jewish Soviet army officer who organised and led the mass escape of prisoners from the Sobibor extermination camp in German-occupied Poland on 14 October 1943. It was the most successful uprising by Jewish prisoners at a Nazi death camp during the Holocaust.

The Sobibor uprising

Captured by the Germans in 1941, Pechersky survived two years in camps before being deported to Sobibor in September 1943. Because of his military experience, prisoners led by Leon Feldhendler turned to him to organise a revolt. Under his plan, prisoners secretly killed eleven SS officers and several guards, and around 600 prisoners fled the camp; roughly 300 reached the forest, though most were later killed. Pechersky survived by joining Soviet partisans and later rejoined the Red Army, where he was wounded in combat.

Later life

After the war Pechersky lived in Rostov-on-Don, working in theatre administration. He testified about Sobibor at trials and wrote memoirs, but was prevented from travelling abroad to testify at the Sobibor trial in West Germany in 1965. His story reached a wide audience through the 1987 television film Escape from Sobibor, in which he was played by Rutger Hauer, and the 2018 Russian film Sobibor. He died in 1990 and has since been honoured with streets and memorials bearing his name.
