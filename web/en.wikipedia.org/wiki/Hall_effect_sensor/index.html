Hall effect sensor

A Hall effect sensor (also known as a Hall sensor or Hall probe) is a device that measures the magnitude and direction of a magnetic field using the Hall effect. Its output varies in response to changes in magnetic flux density around it, and with suitable signal conditioning it can serve as a switch, a linear position or angle sensor, or a current sensor.

Principle

The Hall effect was discovered by Edwin Hall in 1879. When a current-carrying conductor or semiconductor is placed in a magnetic field perpendicular to the current, charge carriers are deflected by the Lorentz force, producing a voltage — the Hall voltage — across the element, perpendicular to both the current and the field. In practical sensors the Hall element is a thin slab of semiconductor material such as silicon, gallium arsenide or indium antimonide; because the raw Hall voltage is only a few millivolts, commercial devices integrate amplifiers, temperature compensation, and often Schmitt-trigger or digital logic on the same chip.

Types

Linear (analogue) sensors output a voltage proportional to the field strength and are used to measure position, displacement and current. Switching (digital) sensors turn on or off when the field crosses a threshold; unipolar switches respond to one magnetic pole, bipolar (latching) switches change state on one pole and reset on the opposite pole, and omnipolar switches respond to either pole. Gear-tooth and rotary-encoder sensors count passing teeth or magnet poles to measure speed and position.

Applications

Hall sensors are ubiquitous because they are solid-state, contactless and immune to dirt and vibration. They are used for crankshaft and camshaft position sensing and wheel-speed (ABS) sensing in automobiles; for commutation in brushless DC motors; in current transducers that measure conductor current without breaking the circuit; as proximity and limit switches in industrial machinery; in laptop lids, smartphones and flip covers to detect closure; in joysticks and throttles; and in electronic compasses in combination with magnetometers. Hall-effect keyboards and switches use them for durable, wear-free actuation.
