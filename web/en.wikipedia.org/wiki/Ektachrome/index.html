Ektachrome

TypeColour reversal (slide) film
ManufacturerKodak
Introduced1946
ProcessE-6

Ektachrome is a brand name owned by Kodak for a range of transparency (reversal, or slide) photographic films. Introduced in 1946, it was for decades one of the standard professional colour films, known for its fine grain, accurate colour, and suitability for reproduction in publishing.

History

Unlike Kodachrome, whose complex processing required Kodak's own laboratories, Ektachrome could be processed by photographers and local labs, which made it popular with professionals and advanced amateurs. Successive versions used the E-1 through E-6 processes; the E-6 process, introduced in 1976, became the industry standard for colour transparency film. Ektachrome films were offered in formats from 35 mm to large-format sheet film, in daylight and tungsten balances and various speeds.

Discontinuation and revival

Kodak discontinued the last Ektachrome films in 2012 as demand for film collapsed. Following a resurgence of interest in analogue photography, Kodak Alaris and Kodak announced its return, and Ektachrome E100 began shipping again in 2018 in 35 mm and 120 formats, with sheet film following. A motion-picture version, Ektachrome 100D, has also been used by filmmakers seeking its distinctive look.
