Shizuo Kakutani

Shizuo Kakutani (Japanese: 角谷 静夫, August 28, 1911 – August 17, 2004) was a Japanese-American mathematician best known for his eponymous Kakutani fixed-point theorem, a fundamental result in functional analysis with major applications in economics and game theory.

Life

Kakutani was born in Osaka, Japan, and studied mathematics at Tohoku Imperial University, where he was influenced by Tatsujirō Shimizu. He visited the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton from 1940, where he met John von Neumann, and published his fixed-point theorem in 1941. He returned to Japan during the Second World War and taught at Osaka University, earning his doctorate, and in 1948 went back to Princeton before joining Yale University in 1949, where he spent the rest of his career, retiring in 1982. His daughter, Michiko Kakutani, is a noted literary critic.

Work

The Kakutani fixed-point theorem generalizes Brouwer's fixed-point theorem to set-valued functions and was famously used by John Nash in his proof of the existence of Nash equilibria in game theory. Kakutani contributed to many areas of analysis, including functional analysis, measure theory, ergodic theory, and probability; recognitions include the Kakutani–Yosida mean ergodic theorem and his work on Markov processes. He was elected to the Japan Academy and received the Imperial Prize of the Japan Academy in 1982.
