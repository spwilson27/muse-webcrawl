South Carolina State University (SC State) is a public, historically black, land-grant university in Orangeburg, South Carolina. Founded in 1896 as the state's sole public college for black youth, it is part of the Thurgood Marshall College Fund.

History

The university began in 1872 as part of Claflin University before becoming independent under the state's 1896 constitution as the Colored Normal, Industrial, Agricultural and Mechanical College. It gained university status in 1954. In 1968 the campus was the site of the Orangeburg massacre, in which Highway Patrol officers killed three students protesting segregation at a local bowling alley.

Academics

SC State offers bachelor's, master's, specialist, and doctoral degrees through colleges of arts and sciences, business, education, and graduate studies. It is known for its nuclear engineering programme — for years the only undergraduate programme of its kind at an HBCU — and for its transportation and speech pathology programmes.

Athletics

The Bulldogs compete in NCAA Division I in the Mid-Eastern Athletic Conference. The football programme has won numerous MEAC championships and Black college national titles, and the university's marching band, the Marching 101, is widely acclaimed.
