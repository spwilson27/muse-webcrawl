The Krishna River is the fourth-biggest river in terms of water inflows and river basin area in India, after the Ganges, Godavari, and Brahmaputra. The river, also called the Krishnaveni, is about 1,400 kilometres (870 mi) long and is a major source of irrigation for the states of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh. It is one of the most sacred rivers in Hinduism, named for the god Krishna.

Course

The Krishna rises in the Western Ghats near Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra, at an elevation of about 1,300 metres, just 64 km from the Arabian Sea. It flows eastward across the Deccan Plateau, joined by major tributaries including the Koyna, Bhima, Tungabhadra, Musi, and Ghataprabha. The river passes the cities of Sangli, Vijayawada, and Amaravati before emptying into the Bay of Bengal near Hamsaladeevi in Andhra Pradesh, forming a large delta.

Basin and irrigation

The Krishna basin covers about 258,948 square kilometres, roughly 8 per cent of India's geographical area. A chain of major dams — including Almatti, Srisailam, Nagarjuna Sagar, and the Prakasam Barrage — supports irrigation, hydroelectric generation, and drinking-water supply for cities such as Hyderabad. Water sharing between the riparian states has long been contested and is governed by the awards of the Krishna Water Disputes Tribunals.

Culture and religion

The river is revered at pilgrimage centres such as Sangam at Kudalasangama, Srisailam, and Vijayawada, where the Kanaka Durga Temple overlooks the river. The Pushkaram festival, held once every twelve years, draws millions of devotees to bathe in its waters. The historic diamond mines of Kollur on the river's banks were the source of famed gems including the Koh-i-Noor.
