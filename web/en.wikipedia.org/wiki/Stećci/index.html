Stećci

Stećci (singular: stećak, pronounced [stɛ̂tɕak]) are monumental medieval tombstones found across Bosnia and Herzegovina and in border regions of Croatia, Montenegro and Serbia. They are mostly carved from limestone and often bear decorative motifs and epitaphs written in Bosnian Cyrillic.

An estimated 60,000 to 70,000 stećci are found in more than 3,300 sites, with around 60,000 in Bosnia and Herzegovina alone. The tombstones date mainly from the 12th to the 16th century, reaching their peak in the 14th and 15th centuries. They are associated with the medieval Bosnian Church as well as Catholic and Orthodox populations, and their decoration includes crosses, crescents, hunting and battle scenes, dances such as the kolo, and stylised human figures.

In 2016, UNESCO inscribed 28 necropolises of stećci in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Montenegro and Serbia on the World Heritage List as the "Stećci Medieval Tombstone Graveyards". Prominent sites include Radimlja and Blidinje in Herzegovina.
