Penfolds is one of Australia's oldest and most famous wineries, founded in Adelaide in 1844 by Christopher Rawson Penfold, an English physician who had emigrated to the new colony of South Australia, and his wife Mary. It is now owned by Treasury Wine Estates. Its flagship wine, Grange, is widely regarded as Australia's most celebrated red wine.

History

The Penfolds bought land at Magill, at the foot of the Adelaide Hills, naming the property after Mary's home village, and planted vines with cuttings brought from the south of France. Christopher believed in wine's medicinal properties, and the young winery initially concentrated on fortified wines such as ports and sherries prescribed as tonics. After Christopher's death in 1870, Mary Penfold took over the business and expanded it considerably; by the 1880s Penfolds produced a large share of South Australia's wine.

Through the 20th century the company grew by acquisition, establishing the Kalimna vineyard in the Barossa Valley (1945) and operations at Nuriootpa and elsewhere. In the 1940s and 1950s, chief winemaker Max Schubert developed Grange Hermitage (first vintage 1951), modelled loosely on the great reds of Bordeaux but made principally from Shiraz; initially criticised and even ordered discontinued, it was reinstated and became an icon, with the 1955 vintage winning international gold medals.

Wines

Penfolds is known for its "bin" numbering system, originating in the storage bins of the cellar: Bin 28 Kalimna Shiraz, Bin 389 Cabernet Shiraz (called the "poor man's Grange" for its maturation in ex-Grange barrels), Bin 407 Cabernet Sauvignon, and Bin 707. Grange itself, a multi-district Shiraz blend matured in new American oak, commands some of the highest prices of any Australian wine and is classified as a Heritage Icon of South Australia.
