Quebec Secession Reference

Full nameReference re Secession of Quebec, [1998] 2 S.C.R. 217
CourtSupreme Court of Canada
DecidedAugust 20, 1998

The Quebec Secession Reference (Reference re Secession of Quebec, [1998] 2 SCR 217) is a landmark advisory opinion by the Supreme Court of Canada, delivered in 1998 in response to questions posed by the federal government following the near-success of the 1995 Quebec independence referendum.

Questions and answers

The Court was asked: (1) whether Quebec could secede unilaterally under the Canadian Constitution; (2) whether international law gave Quebec a right of unilateral secession; and (3) which would prevail in case of conflict. The Court answered that Quebec had no right to unilateral secession under either Canadian constitutional law or international law, since Quebecers are not a colonized or oppressed people and have meaningful access to government.

Duty to negotiate

Crucially, however, the Court held that a clear majority vote in Quebec on a clear question in favour of secession would give rise to a reciprocal duty to negotiate on the part of the federal government and the other provinces, grounded in the unwritten constitutional principles of federalism, democracy, constitutionalism and the rule of law, and protection of minorities. Secession would require a constitutional amendment, and negotiations would have to address the interests of all parties, including Indigenous peoples and linguistic minorities.

Legacy

The opinion led directly to the federal Clarity Act (2000) and Quebec's Bill 99, and is widely studied internationally as a framework for lawful secession.
