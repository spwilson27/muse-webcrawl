China–India relations

China–India relations, also called Sino-Indian relations, are the bilateral relations between the People's Republic of China and the Republic of India. The two countries share a border of about 3,488 kilometres (2,167 mi) and together account for more than a third of the world's population. Relations combine deep historical and cultural exchanges — including the spread of Buddhism from India to China — with a long-running territorial dispute that produced the Sino-Indian War of 1962 and periodic border standoffs.

History

India was one of the first countries to recognize the People's Republic of China, and the two states established diplomatic relations on 1 April 1950. In the 1950s the slogan Hindi-Chini bhai-bhai ("Indians and Chinese are brothers") and the 1954 Panchsheel agreement on five principles of peaceful coexistence marked a high point of cordiality. Disagreement over the Himalayan boundary — India claiming the McMahon Line in the east, China administering Aksai Chin in the west — led to the month-long border war of October–November 1962, won by China and leaving durable mistrust.

Border disputes and security

Clashes at Nathu La and Cho La in 1967, a standoff at Doklam in 2017, and deadly hand-to-hand fighting in the Galwan Valley in June 2020 — the first combat fatalities on the border since 1975 — kept the Line of Actual Control tense, with both sides building infrastructure and maintaining large deployments. In October 2024 the two sides announced a patrolling agreement and a partial disengagement, followed by the first Modi–Xi summit meeting in five years at Kazan. Strategic competition also plays out through China's partnership with Pakistan and India's participation in the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue.

Economic relations

Despite political friction, China has been among India's largest trading partners, with bilateral trade exceeding $100 billion annually in the early 2020s; India runs a large deficit and depends on Chinese imports of electronics, active pharmaceutical ingredients and machinery. After the 2020 clashes India restricted Chinese investment and banned numerous Chinese mobile applications.
