Football culture

TopicAssociation football (soccer) and its social practices
ElementsSupporter culture, chants, tifos, derbies, media, fashion

Football culture refers to the social practices, rituals and identities surrounding association football. As the world's most widely followed sport, football functions in many societies as a major focus of local and national identity, expressed through club allegiance, matchday rituals, songs and visual display.

Supporter culture

Organised supporters create atmosphere through chanting, banners and choreographed displays (tifos). In Europe and South America the ultras movement, emerging from the 1960s–70s, combines intense loyalty with elaborate visual shows and sometimes political expression; in Britain, terrace culture and the "casuals" subculture of the 1980s linked fandom with fashion. Football rivalries — derbies between neighbouring clubs such as Boca Juniors–River Plate or Celtic–Rangers — carry social, religious and political weight beyond sport.

Media and wider culture

Football shapes music (from club anthems to World Cup songs), film, literature and video games such as the FIFA/EA Sports FC series. Player transfers, tactics and results dominate sports media, while events like the FIFA World Cup final draw television audiences in the hundreds of millions. Negative aspects discussed alongside the culture include hooliganism, racism in stadiums and the commercialisation of the game.
