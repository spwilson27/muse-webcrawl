Field Marshal Douglas Haig, 1st Earl Haig, KT, GCB, OM, GCVO, KCIE (19 June 1861 – 29 January 1928) was a senior British Army officer. During the First World War he commanded the British Expeditionary Force (BEF) on the Western Front from late 1915 until the end of the war.


Douglas Haig commanded the BEF from 1915 to 1919.

First World War

Haig directed the BEF through its costliest battles, including the Somme (1916) and Third Ypres (Passchendaele, 1917), in which British casualties were enormous. In 1918 he commanded during the German spring offensives and the victorious Hundred Days Offensive that ended the war. His leadership remains deeply controversial: critics dubbed him the "Butcher of the Somme" for attritional tactics, while others argue he adapted the BEF into an effective all-arms force that won in 1918.

Later life

Created Earl Haig in 1919, he devoted his later years to ex-servicemen's welfare, helping found the Royal British Legion and promoting the poppy appeal; the Earl Haig Fund supports veterans to this day.
