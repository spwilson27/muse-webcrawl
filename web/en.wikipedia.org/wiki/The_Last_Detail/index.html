The Last Detail is a 1973 American comedy-drama film directed by Hal Ashby, with a screenplay by Robert Towne adapted from Darryl Ponicsan's 1970 novel. It stars Jack Nicholson and Otis Young as two career Navy petty officers escorting a young sailor (Randy Quaid) to military prison.

Plot

Signalman "Badass" Buddusky and gunner's mate "Mule" Mulhall are assigned shore-patrol duty: deliver 18-year-old Larry Meadows from Norfolk, Virginia, to the brig at Portsmouth, New Hampshire, where he faces an eight-year sentence for stealing forty dollars from a charity box. Sympathising with the naive boy, the two lifers detour through Washington, New York and Boston to show him a few days of drinking, fighting and tenderness before his confinement — only to deliver him anyway, the Navy's machinery rolling on regardless.

Reception

Nicholson won Best Actor at Cannes and received an Academy Award nomination, as did Quaid and Towne's screenplay; the film's profanity-laced realism initially troubled Columbia Pictures, which delayed its release. It is now regarded as one of the defining films of the New Hollywood era. A loose sequel, Last Flag Flying (2017), reunited Ponicsan's characters in a new story.
