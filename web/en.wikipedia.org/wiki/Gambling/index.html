Gambling

Gambling is the wagering of something of value ("the stakes") on a random event with the intent of winning something else of value, where instances of strategy are discounted. Gambling thus requires three elements to be present: consideration (an amount wagered), risk (chance), and a prize. The outcome of the wager is often immediate, such as a single roll of dice, a spin of a roulette wheel, or a horse crossing the finish line, but longer time frames are also common, allowing wagers on the outcome of a future sports contest or even an entire sports season.

History

Gambling dates back at least to the Paleolithic period, before written history. In Mesopotamia the earliest six-sided dice date to about 3000 BC. In China, gambling houses were widespread in the first millennium BC, and betting on fighting animals dates back as early as the second millennium BC. Lotto games and dominoes (precursors of Pai Gow) appeared in China as early as the 10th century.

Playing cards appeared in 9th-century China, and their spread to Europe in the late 14th century helped popularise card games such as poker. The first gambling houses, ancestral to the modern casino, appeared in 17th-century Italy; the Ridotto in Venice, established in 1638, is often cited as the first casino. Lotteries were used to finance public works in Europe and colonial America, funding institutions such as Harvard and Yale.

Types of gambling

Casino games

Casino games include table games such as blackjack, roulette, baccarat and craps, as well as slot machines and video poker. Because the rules of casino games are mathematically determined, the house retains a statistical advantage, known as the house edge, on nearly all wagers.

Sports betting

Sports betting involves wagering on the outcome of sporting events, commonly through a bookmaker who sets odds. Popular forms include point spreads, moneylines and over–under totals. See also sports betting.

Lotteries

A lottery is a form of gambling involving the drawing of numbers at random for a prize. Many governments operate state or national lotteries, and lotteries are among the most popular forms of gambling worldwide.

Poker

Poker is a family of card games in which players wager over which hand is best according to the game's rules. Although chance plays a role, poker involves substantial strategic skill, and professional players compete in tournaments such as the World Series of Poker.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions tax and regulate gambling through licensing regimes. Regulatory aims typically include preventing crime and money laundering, ensuring fairness and transparency, and collecting revenue. Some jurisdictions prohibit most forms of gambling, while others, such as Nevada and Macau, have built major industries around legalised casino resorts. The rise of the internet in the 1990s created online gambling, which many jurisdictions have since moved to license and regulate.

Problem gambling

Problem gambling is an urge to gamble continuously despite harmful negative consequences or a desire to stop. It is classified as an addictive disorder in diagnostic manuals such as the DSM-5. Treatment approaches include counselling, self-exclusion programmes, support groups such as Gamblers Anonymous, and, in some jurisdictions, mandatory responsible-gambling measures by operators.
