Hip flexors

The hip flexors are a group of muscles that act to flex the femur at the hip joint, bringing the knee toward the chest. The principal hip flexor is the iliopsoas, formed by the psoas major and iliacus, assisted by rectus femoris, sartorius, and tensor fasciae latae.

Function

These muscles are essential for walking, running, kicking, and maintaining posture. Prolonged sitting can shorten them, contributing to anterior pelvic tilt and lower back discomfort in some people.

Clinical significance

Hip flexor strains are common in sprinting and kicking sports. Strengthening and mobility exercises are widely used in rehabilitation.
