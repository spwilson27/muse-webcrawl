Oregon State University (OSU) is a public land-grant research university in Corvallis, Oregon, United States. Founded in 1868, it is one of only three universities in the United States — with Cornell and MIT — to be a land-grant, sea-grant, space-grant and sun-grant institution. Enrollment exceeds 35,000 students across its Corvallis campus, a branch campus in Bend (OSU-Cascades), and one of the nation's largest online programs, OSU Ecampus.

History

The school began in 1856 as a primary and preparatory academy affiliated with the Methodist Episcopal Church, South; in 1868 the Oregon Legislative Assembly designated it the state's agricultural college under the Morrill Land-Grant Act. It was long known as Oregon Agricultural College, then Oregon State College, before becoming Oregon State University in 1961.

Academics and research

OSU offers over 200 undergraduate and 100 graduate degree programs through colleges including Engineering, Agricultural Sciences, Forestry, Oceanography (the College of Earth, Ocean, and Atmospheric Sciences), and the Carlson College of Veterinary Medicine. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity", with research strengths in oceanography, forestry, agriculture, robotics and wave energy; its research vessels include the R/V Oceanus (retired 2021) and the new regional-class ships of the Taani class.

Athletics

The Oregon State Beavers compete in NCAA Division I. The football team plays at Reser Stadium; the baseball team has won three College World Series titles (2006, 2007, 2018). Following the collapse of the Pac-12 in 2024, OSU remained in the conference with Washington State as it rebuilds.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Linus Pauling, the only person to win two unshared Nobel Prizes; Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang; and engineer and inventor Douglas Engelbart, who earned his bachelor's degree at Oregon State.
