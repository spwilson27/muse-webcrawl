Sargam (music)

Sargam in performance
TypeVocal technique; solmisation system
TraditionsHindustani; Carnatic; Indian popular music

This article discusses sargam as a system and practice of music: the solmisation of Indian classical music, in which the notes of a melody are named and sung with the syllables sa re ga ma pa dha ni sa. It forms the foundation of ear-training and improvisation in both the Hindustani music of the north and the Carnatic music of the south.

Historical background

The syllables derive from the names of the seven svaras described in ancient treatises such as the Natya Shastra and the Sangita Ratnakara. The practice of singing scales and exercises (alankaras) to sargam has been central to the guru–shishya teaching tradition for centuries.

In performance

Beyond pedagogy, sargam is a display technique: in khyal and instrumental music, fast runs of named notes demonstrate mastery of the raga's grammar. In Carnatic music, kalpana svara passages are sung in sargam during improvisation. Compositions that set their lyrics to the names of the notes themselves are known as sargam geet.

Notation

Sargam is the basis of the Bhatkhande and Paluskar notation systems used for Hindustani music, in which Devanagari letters representing the syllables are written with marks indicating octave and altered notes.
