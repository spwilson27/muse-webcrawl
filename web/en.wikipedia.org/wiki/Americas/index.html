Americas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Americas, comprising the continents of North America and South America.

The Americas are the lands of the Western Hemisphere, comprising the continents of North America and South America together with their associated islands. They cover about 42.5 million square kilometres — roughly 28% of Earth's land area — and are home to more than one billion people, about 13% of the world's population.

Geography

North America includes Canada, the United States, Mexico, the countries of Central America, and the Caribbean islands; South America extends from Colombia and Venezuela to Tierra del Fuego. The two continents are joined by the Isthmus of Panama and separated artificially by the Panama Canal. The Americas stretch from the Arctic Ocean almost to Antarctica, encompassing nearly every climate zone, from Arctic tundra to the Amazon rainforest and the Atacama Desert.

History

Humans settled the Americas from Asia via Beringia during the last Ice Age, at least 15,000 years ago, and developed diverse civilizations including the Maya, Aztec, and Inca. The voyages of Christopher Columbus from 1492 opened the era of European colonization and the Columbian exchange, which transformed ecosystems, economies, and populations on both sides of the Atlantic. From the late 18th century, independence movements created the modern states of the hemisphere.

Name

The name derives from the Latinized first name of the explorer Amerigo Vespucci, applied to the new lands on Martin Waldseemüller's 1507 world map. In English usage "America" commonly denotes the United States, while "the Americas" refers to the hemisphere as a whole.
