Essential amino acid

Complete protein sources contain all nine essential amino acids.

An essential amino acid, or indispensable amino acid, is an amino acid that cannot be synthesised by the organism fast enough to supply its demand, and must therefore be obtained from the diet. Of the twenty-one amino acids common to all life forms, nine are classified as essential for humans.

A common mnemonic is "PVT TIM HaLL". Several other amino acids, such as arginine, cysteine and tyrosine, are conditionally essential—required in the diet under conditions such as infancy, illness or when their precursors are lacking.

Dietary sources

Animal products such as meat, eggs and dairy contain all essential amino acids in adequate proportions and are termed complete proteins. Most plant proteins are limiting in one or more essential amino acids—lysine in cereals, methionine in legumes—so varied plant diets combine complementary sources. Quinoa and soy are notable near-complete plant proteins.

Requirement and deficiency

The World Health Organization publishes recommended daily intakes per kilogram of body weight. Chronic deficiency contributes to protein-energy malnutrition syndromes such as kwashiorkor and marasmus, impaired growth and immune dysfunction. The branched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine) are of particular interest in muscle metabolism and sports nutrition.
