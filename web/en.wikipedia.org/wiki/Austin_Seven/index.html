The Austin Seven is an economy car that was produced from 1923 until 1939 in the United Kingdom by the Austin Motor Company. Dubbed the "Baby Austin", it was one of the most popular cars ever produced for the British market and sold well abroad. Its effect on the British market was similar to that of the Model T Ford in the US, replacing most other British economy cars and cyclecars of the early 1920s. It was also licensed and copied by companies all over the world.

History

Until the First World War, Austin built mainly large cars, but in 1909 it sold a small car, the single-cylinder Austin 7 hp, built by Swift of Coventry. Concluding after the war that it needed a small, cheap car for the mass market, Herbert Austin designed the Seven against the wishes of his board, working at his home, Lickey Grange, with an 18-year-old draughtsman, Stanley Edge. The car was launched in 1922 (production began in January 1923).

Design

The Seven was usually powered by a 747 cc four-cylinder side-valve engine producing 10.5 bhp (later raised to 13 bhp), driving the rear wheels through a three-speed (later four-speed) gearbox. The chassis was a simple A-frame with transverse leaf-spring front suspension and quarter-elliptic springs at the rear; four-wheel brakes were fitted from 1930. Early cars cost £225, but prices soon fell to £125, making the Seven a genuine four-seater within reach of the lower-middle-class family.

Licensed and derivative versions

The design was licensed to BMW in Germany, where it became the BMW Dixi—the company's first car; to Rosengart in France; to Nissan in Japan (as the Datsun Type 11); and in the United States it formed the basis of the American Austin and later the Bantam. William Lyons's Swallow Sidecar Company built Austin Seven Swallows, a forerunner of Jaguar cars. Bruce McLaren's first race car was a modified Austin Seven.

Motorsport

Sevens were raced extensively, in both standard and highly modified forms. Works and privateer specials, including supercharged "Ulster" models, achieved class wins at Brooklands and elsewhere, and the Seven became the foundation of British club racing; the 750 Motor Club, founded in 1939 around the model, exists to this day.

Legacy

Approximately 290,000 cars and chassis were built. The Austin Seven is credited with putting Britain on wheels, establishing the pattern of the small British family car, and launching the car industries of several other countries. Countless specials were built on Seven chassis after the war, including Colin Chapman's first Lotus cars.
