Tomás de Torquemada

Tomás de TorquemadaBorn: 14 October 1420, Torquemada, Castile
Died: 16 September 1498, Ávila, Spain
Office: First Grand Inquisitor of Spain

Tomás de Torquemada (1420–1498) was a Castilian Dominican friar who became the first Grand Inquisitor of the Spanish Inquisition. As confessor to Queen Isabella I of Castile, he was one of the most powerful and feared figures of his age.

Early life

Torquemada was born in the town of Torquemada in the Kingdom of Castile and entered the Dominican Order at the monastery of San Pablo in Valladolid, later becoming prior of the monastery of Santa Cruz la Real in Segovia.

The Spanish Inquisition

In 1483 Pope Sixtus IV appointed Torquemada inquisitor general. He organised the Inquisition's tribunals, drafted its procedural instructions, and directed campaigns against heresy, particularly against conversos accused of secretly practising Judaism. Thousands were executed during his tenure, and his name became a byword for religious persecution.

Death and reputation

Torquemada died at the monastery of Saint Thomas Aquinas in Ávila in 1498. Historians continue to debate the scale of the Inquisition's victims, but his severity was notorious even among contemporaries.
