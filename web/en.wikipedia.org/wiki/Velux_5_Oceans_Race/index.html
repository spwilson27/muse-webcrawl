Velux 5 Oceans Race

The Velux 5 Oceans Race was a single-handed, staged round-the-world yacht race for Open 50 and Open 60 monohulls. Originating in 1982 as the BOC Challenge and later known as the Around Alone, it was rebranded under Velux sponsorship for the 2006–07 and 2010–11 editions, with "ocean sprints" between stopovers on five continents.

History

The BOC Challenge began in 1982, inspired by the Sunday Times Golden Globe Race, with stopovers that distinguished it from non-stop events. As Around Alone, its 1998–99 edition featured the dramatic Southern Ocean rescue dynamics also seen elsewhere in solo racing. In 2006 the race became the Velux 5 Oceans; Bernard Stamm won the 2006–07 edition. The 2010–11 race, won by American Brad Van Liew in the Eco 60 class, started in La Rochelle and called at Cape Town, Wellington, Salvador, and Charleston before returning to La Rochelle.

Format

Unlike the non-stop Vendée Globe, the Velux 5 Oceans was sailed in legs ("ocean sprints") with rest and repair stopovers, and included in-port and coastal races at stopover cities. The Eco 60 class encouraged older, sustainably refitted boats.
