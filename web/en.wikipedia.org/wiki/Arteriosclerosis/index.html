Arteriosclerosis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Atherosclerotic plaque narrowing an artery

Arteriosclerosis, literally meaning "hardening of the arteries", is the generic term for a vascular disorder characterized by abnormal thickening, hardening, and loss of elasticity of the walls of arteries. It occurs when arteries are diseased in the form of atheroma, which can restrict blood flow due to narrowing and plaque buildup.

The main types of arteriosclerosis are atherosclerosis (the most common form, involving fatty plaques in large and medium arteries), arteriolosclerosis (affecting small arteries and arterioles, associated with hypertension and diabetes), and Mönckeberg's arteriosclerosis (calcification of the medial layer of arteries).

Signs and symptoms

Arteriosclerosis itself is usually asymptomatic until the blood supply to an organ is significantly reduced. Manifestations depend on the vessels involved and may include coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral artery disease, and aneurysm formation.

Risk factors and treatment

Risk factors include advancing age, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, and a family history of cardiovascular disease. Management involves lifestyle modification, lipid-lowering drugs such as statins, antihypertensive therapy, and in advanced cases surgical or endovascular revascularization.
