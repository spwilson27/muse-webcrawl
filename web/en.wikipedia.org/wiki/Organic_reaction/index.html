Organic reaction

Organic reactions are chemical reactions involving organic compounds. They are the foundation of organic synthesis — the construction of the molecules of drugs, polymers, agrochemicals, and materials — and are studied in terms of their mechanisms: the step-by-step movement of electrons by which bonds break and form, conventionally drawn with curly arrows.

Classification

By outcome, the basic types are addition reactions (two molecules combine), elimination reactions (a small molecule is lost, often forming a double bond), substitution reactions (one group replaces another), and rearrangement reactions (the skeleton of a molecule is reorganised). By mechanism, reactions are classed as polar (involving nucleophiles, electrophiles, acids, and bases), radical (involving unpaired electrons), or pericyclic (concerted reactions through a cyclic transition state, such as the Diels–Alder reaction).

Named reactions and conditions

Hundreds of transformations are known by name — the Grignard reaction, aldol condensation, Wittig reaction, Suzuki coupling, and many more — each a reliable tool for making a particular kind of bond. Organic reactions are controlled by reagents, solvents, temperature, and catalysts, and are evaluated by yield, selectivity (chemo-, regio-, and stereoselectivity), and increasingly by green-chemistry criteria such as atom economy and waste reduction.
