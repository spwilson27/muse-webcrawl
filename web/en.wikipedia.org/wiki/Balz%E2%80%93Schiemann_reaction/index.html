Balz–Schiemann reaction

Balz–Schiemann reaction (image placeholder)

The Balz–Schiemann reaction is a chemical reaction in which a primary aromatic amine is transformed into an aryl fluoride via an arenediazonium tetrafluoroborate intermediate. It is the classic practical method for introducing fluorine onto an aromatic ring and is named after the German chemists Günther Balz and Günther Schiemann, who developed it in 1927.

Mechanism and procedure

An aniline is diazotised with nitrous acid in the presence of fluoroboric acid (HBF4) to give an arenediazonium tetrafluoroborate salt, which is isolated and then heated; it decomposes, releasing nitrogen gas and boron trifluoride to yield the aryl fluoride. Aryltrifluoroborate and hexafluorophosphate salts have also been used in variants.

Limitations

Yields are often only moderate, and the isolated diazonium salts can be explosive, so the reaction demands care; it is typically used on a modest scale. Modern variants perform the diazotisation and fluorodediazoniation in one pot or use ionic liquids to improve safety and yield.
