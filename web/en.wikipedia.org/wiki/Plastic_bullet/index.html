Plastic bullet

A plastic baton round of the type used for riot control

A plastic bullet, or plastic baton round, is a large projectile made of PVC or similar polymer, developed as a less-lethal weapon for riot control. Plastic bullets were developed by the British security forces in the early 1970s as a replacement for rubber bullets during the Troubles in Northern Ireland, entering service in 1973.

Design

The standard L3A1 plastic baton round is about 89 mm long and 38 mm in diameter, weighing roughly 135 grams, and is fired from a 37 mm riot gun. It was intended to be fired at the lower body from a minimum range, skipping off the ground if necessary, to incapacitate rioters without the lethality of conventional ammunition.

Use and controversy

Plastic bullets were used extensively in Northern Ireland and issued to police forces elsewhere, including during public-order operations in Britain. They have been highly controversial: seventeen people were killed by plastic bullets in Northern Ireland, eight of them children, and many others suffered skull fractures and other serious injuries. Campaigners argued the rounds were lethal in practice, and in 2001 the British authorities replaced the older round with the L21A1 attenuated energy projectile, designed to be more accurate and less likely to cause unintended head injuries. Plastic baton rounds remain in use by police and military forces in several countries, and their use continues to be criticised by human rights organisations.
