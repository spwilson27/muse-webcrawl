Man o' War (March 29, 1917 – November 1, 1947) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse widely regarded as one of the greatest racehorses of all time. During his career, just after World War I, he won 20 of 21 races and earned US$249,465 in purses.

Racing career

Bred by August Belmont Jr. and sold as a yearling to Samuel D. Riddle for $5,000, Man o' War was trained by Louis Feustel and usually ridden by Johnny Loftus and later Clarence Kummer. His only defeat came in the 1919 Sanford Memorial Stakes, where a chaotic start left him badly positioned; the horse who beat him was named Upset, a result often (incorrectly) credited with popularizing the sports term "upset."

At three he won the Preakness and Belmont Stakes — his connections skipped the Kentucky Derby — and defeated the first Triple Crown winner, Sir Barton, in a match race in Canada. He routinely carried heavy weights and set multiple American records.

Stud career and legacy

Retired to stud in Kentucky, he sired the champion War Admiral and numerous other stakes winners, and his line influenced the breed for generations. He died in 1947 and was buried at Faraway Farm; his remains were later moved to the Kentucky Horse Park. In the Blood-Horse magazine ranking of the top 100 U.S. Thoroughbred champions of the 20th century, Man o' War was voted No. 1.
