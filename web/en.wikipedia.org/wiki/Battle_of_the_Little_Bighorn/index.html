Battle of the Little Bighorn

The Battle of the Little Bighorn, known to the Lakota and other Plains Indians as the Battle of the Greasy Grass, and commonly referred to in the United States as Custer's Last Stand, was an armed engagement between combined forces of the Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho tribes and the 7th Cavalry Regiment of the United States Army. It was fought on 25–26 June 1876 along the Little Bighorn River in present-day Montana, during the Great Sioux War of 1876.

Background

The war grew from the US government's attempt to force the Lakota and their allies onto reservations and to seize the Black Hills after gold was discovered there. A huge summer encampment — inspired by Sitting Bull's Sun Dance vision of soldiers falling into camp — gathered on the Little Bighorn.

The battle

Custer, fearing the village would scatter, attacked at once without waiting for reinforcements, dividing his regiment into three battalions. Reno's attack on the southern end of the village was repulsed and his men besieged on the bluffs; Custer's five companies, attacking the northern end, were overwhelmed and annihilated by warriors led by Crazy Horse and Gall. Reno and Benteen's surviving troops were relieved on 27 June.

Aftermath

The defeat, arriving during the nation's Centennial celebrations, shocked the American public and intensified the war; within a year most of the Lakota and Cheyenne had surrendered or fled to Canada. The battlefield is preserved as the Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument, which since 2003 has included an Indian Memorial alongside the obelisk to Custer's men.
