Les Misérables, colloquially known as Les Mis or Les Miz, is a sung-through musical based on the 1862 novel by Victor Hugo, with music by Claude-Michel Schönberg and original French lyrics by Alain Boublil and Jean-Marc Natel. The English-language version, adapted by Herbert Kretzmer, opened in London in 1985 and became the longest-running musical in the West End and the second longest-running musical in the world.

Synopsis

The story follows Jean Valjean, a former convict imprisoned for stealing bread, who breaks parole and reinvents himself as a factory owner and mayor, pursued relentlessly by the police inspector Javert. Valjean raises the orphaned Cosette; years later, amid the Paris student uprising of 1832, Cosette falls in love with the revolutionary Marius, while Javert, unable to reconcile mercy with the law, drowns himself in the Seine. Valjean dies at peace, reunited with the spirit of Cosette's mother Fantine.

History

The show began as a French concept album in 1980 and a Paris stage production. Producer Cameron Mackintosh mounted the English version with the Royal Shakespeare Company, directed by Trevor Nunn and John Caird. After a lukewarm critical response, audience word of mouth made it a phenomenon. The Broadway production (1987) won eight Tony Awards, including Best Musical. Famous songs include "I Dreamed a Dream," "On My Own," "One Day More," "Bring Him Home," and "Do You Hear the People Sing?" A film adaptation directed by Tom Hooper was released in 2012.

Reception and legacy

Les Misérables has been performed in dozens of countries and languages and seen by well over 100 million people. It is a landmark of the 1980s "megamusical" era and remains a staple of professional and amateur stages worldwide.
