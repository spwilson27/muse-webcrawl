CVS Pharmacy is an American retail pharmacy chain and a subsidiary of CVS Health. It is one of the largest pharmacy chains in the United States, with roughly 9,000 locations, and is known for its combination of prescription services, over-the-counter health products, and general retail merchandise.

A typical CVS Pharmacy store in the United States.

History

The company began in 1963 as the Consumer Value Store in Lowell, Massachusetts, founded by the Goldstein brothers and Ralph Hoagland, initially selling health and beauty products; the first pharmacies were added in 1967. It grew by acquisition over decades, absorbing chains including Revco (1997), Eckerd (2004), and the pharmacy operations of Albertsons (2006) and Longs Drugs (2008).

In 2014 CVS became the first major US pharmacy chain to stop selling tobacco products, rebranding its parent company as CVS Health. Following the parent company's 2018 acquisition of health insurer Aetna, many CVS stores added expanded "HealthHUB" clinic and wellness services, and CVS also operates MinuteClinic walk-in clinics inside many stores.

Operations

Beyond dispensing prescriptions, CVS Pharmacy is a major provider of immunizations, operates photo and convenience retail sections, and its parent runs the CVS Caremark pharmacy benefit manager. The chain is a principal competitor of Walgreens in the US drugstore market.
