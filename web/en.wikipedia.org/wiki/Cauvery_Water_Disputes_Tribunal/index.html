Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal

The Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal (CWDT) was constituted by the Government of India on 2 June 1990 under the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956, to adjudicate the long-standing water-sharing dispute over the Cauvery (Kaveri) river between the riparian states of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and the union territory of Puducherry.

Background

The dispute over the sharing of Cauvery waters dates back to agreements of 1892 and 1924 between the Madras Presidency and the Kingdom of Mysore. After the reorganisation of states in 1956, Karnataka (formerly Mysore) and Tamil Nadu contested the allocation of the river's waters, which support irrigation, drinking water, and hydroelectric generation across the basin. Negotiations failed repeatedly, and Tamil Nadu sought the constitution of a tribunal.

The award

After seventeen years of hearings, the tribunal delivered its final award on 5 February 2007, which was notified by the central government in 2013 following a direction of the Supreme Court of India. The tribunal assessed the total availability of water in the basin at 740 thousand million cubic feet (TMC) in a normal year and allocated 419 TMC to Tamil Nadu, 270 TMC to Karnataka, 30 TMC to Kerala, and 7 TMC to Puducherry, with the remainder reserved for environmental flows and inevitable escapages to the sea.

Supreme Court verdict and aftermath

All the parties appealed, and in February 2018 the Supreme Court modified the award, increasing Karnataka's share by 14.75 TMC (in view of Bengaluru's drinking-water needs) and correspondingly reducing Tamil Nadu's share. The Court directed the creation of an implementation mechanism, leading to the formation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA) and the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee in 2018.
