Positive train control (PTC) is a family of automatic train protection systems used in the United States that automatically slow or stop a train to prevent certain types of accidents: train-to-train collisions, overspeed derailments, incursions into work zones, and movement through a switch left in the wrong position. PTC combines GPS-based positioning, onboard computers, wayside equipment and radio data links to continuously calculate braking curves and enforce movement authorities, overriding the engineer if necessary.

History

The US National Transportation Safety Board had recommended train control systems for decades, and listed PTC on its "Most Wanted" list from 1990. Implementation was slow until the 2008 Chatsworth train collision in California, in which a Metrolink commuter train whose engineer had been texting ran a red signal and struck a freight train, killing 25. Congress responded with the Rail Safety Improvement Act of 2008, requiring PTC on main lines carrying passengers or toxic-by-inhalation freight by 31 December 2015. The deadline was extended to 2018, with further extensions available until the end of 2020; the Federal Railroad Administration announced on 29 December 2020 that PTC was in operation on all required route miles.

Implementation

Freight railroads largely adopted systems based on the Interoperable Train Control architecture, including BNSF and Union Pacific's I-ETMS, while commuter railroads used variants such as ACSES (Advanced Civil Speed Enforcement System) on the Northeast Corridor and I-ETMS-based systems elsewhere. Interoperability — the ability of a locomotive from one railroad to operate under the PTC system of another — was a major requirement and technical challenge, as was the acquisition of 220 MHz radio spectrum needed for data communications.

The industry reported spending more than $10 billion on deployment across roughly 58,000 route miles. PTC is credited with preventing accidents similar to the 2015 Amtrak derailment in Philadelphia, which occurred on a stretch where PTC had not yet been activated, and the 2017 Amtrak Cascades derailment near DuPont, Washington.
