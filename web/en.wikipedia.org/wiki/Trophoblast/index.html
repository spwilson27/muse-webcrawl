Trophoblast

The trophoblast (from Greek trophē, "nourishment", and blastos, "germinator") is the outer layer of cells of the blastocyst in early mammalian embryonic development. It is the first layer to differentiate from the fertilized egg and does not itself become part of the embryo proper; instead it forms the fetal contribution to the placenta and the membranes surrounding the embryo.

Development and function

The trophoblast mediates implantation into the uterine wall and develops into two layers: an inner cytotrophoblast of individual cells and an outer multinucleated syncytiotrophoblast, which invades the endometrium and later secretes human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) — the hormone detected by pregnancy tests. Extravillous trophoblast cells remodel the maternal spiral arteries so that maternal blood bathes the placental villi, enabling nutrient and gas exchange while keeping fetal and maternal blood separate.

Clinical significance

Abnormal trophoblast growth underlies gestational trophoblastic diseases, including hydatidiform moles and choriocarcinoma. Insufficient invasion of the spiral arteries is implicated in pre-eclampsia and fetal growth restriction.
