Tangent cone

In geometry, the tangent cone of a subset of a Euclidean space (or, more generally, of an algebraic variety or scheme) at a point is a cone formed by the limiting positions of secant lines through the point. It generalizes the notion of the tangent space to settings where the object is singular and no tangent space in the ordinary sense exists.

Definitions

For a subset S of Rn and a point x in S, the (Bouligand) tangent cone consists of all vectors v that are limits of sequences (xi − x)/ti with xi in S converging to x and ti decreasing to 0. In algebraic geometry, the tangent cone at a point of a variety is defined algebraically from the associated graded ring of the local ring at that point, using the lowest-degree terms of the defining equations. For a smooth point the tangent cone coincides with the tangent space; at singular points it records the multiplicity and local shape of the singularity.
