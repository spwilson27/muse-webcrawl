Vergina Sun

The Vergina Sun (Greek: Ήλιος της Βεργίνας, "Sun of Vergina"), also known as the Star of Vergina, is a rayed solar symbol appearing in ancient Greek art of the 6th to 2nd centuries BCE. It consists of a central disc surrounded by sixteen rays — eight wavy and eight straight with triangular tips in the fullest form — and is most famous from the golden larnax (casket) found in the tomb of Philip II of Macedon at Vergina (ancient Aigai) in Greek Macedonia, excavated by Manolis Andronikos in 1977.

Archaeology and meaning

The symbol appears on coins, pottery, shields and funerary objects across the ancient Greek world; related rayed stars with 8 or 12 rays are widespread. Its precise significance is debated — it may have been a royal emblem of the Argead dynasty of Macedon, a religious solar symbol, or a common decorative motif — and the twelve Olympian gods and other schemes have been proposed for the number of rays.

Modern dispute

After the Republic of Macedonia (now North Macedonia) adopted a flag bearing the Vergina Sun on a red field in 1992, Greece protested that the symbol was an exclusively Greek emblem appropriated along with the name "Macedonia". Under the 1995 Interim Accord, the republic changed its flag to a stylised eight-ray sun and removed the symbol from public monuments under the 2018 Prespa agreement, which settled the naming dispute. The Vergina Sun remains the emblem of the Greek region of Macedonia, appearing on its flag and on the Greek 100-drachma coin.
