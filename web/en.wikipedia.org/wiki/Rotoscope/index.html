Rotoscoping

Rotoscoping is an animation technique in which animators trace over live-action footage, frame by frame, to produce realistic motion. The technique was patented by Max Fleischer in 1917, and the device used was called a rotoscope.

History

Fleischer used rotoscoping for Koko the Clown in the Out of the Inkwell series. Disney used it extensively, notably for the human characters in Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937). Ralph Bakshi used rotoscoping in Wizards (1977) and The Lord of the Rings (1978). Richard Linklater's Waking Life (2001) and A Scanner Darkly (2006) used digital interpolated rotoscoping ("Rotoshop").

Modern use

In visual effects, rotoscoping ("roto") now refers to manually creating mattes by tracing elements in footage so they can be separated, replaced or composited — a foundational task in film post-production, performed with software such as Silhouette and Mocha.
