Chota Nagpur Plateau

The Chota Nagpur Plateau is a plateau in eastern India, which covers much of the state of Jharkhand, as well as adjacent parts of Chhattisgarh, Odisha, West Bengal and Bihar. The Indo-Gangetic plain lies to the north and east of the plateau, and the basin of the Mahanadi river lies to the south. The plateau has an area of approximately 65,000 square kilometres.

Geology and geography

The plateau is composed mainly of ancient Precambrian rocks, including gneiss and granite, and is one of the oldest land surfaces in India. It has an average elevation of about 700 metres, rising in steps from east to west. The Damodar River flows through the plateau in a rift valley and is an important source of hydroelectric power and irrigation.

Resources

The Chota Nagpur Plateau is exceptionally rich in minerals — it has been called the "Ruhr of India" — containing large deposits of coal, iron ore, mica, bauxite, copper, and limestone. Major industrial centres such as Jamshedpur, Bokaro Steel City, and Dhanbad grew up around these resources.

People

The region is home to many Adivasi (indigenous tribal) communities, including the Santhal, Munda, Oraon, and Ho peoples. The plateau gave its name to the Chota Nagpur Tenancy Act of 1908, which restricted the transfer of tribal land to non-tribals.
