Vidkun Abraham Lauritz Jonssøn Quisling (18 July 1887 – 24 October 1945) was a Norwegian military officer, politician and Nazi collaborator who headed the government of Norway during the country's occupation by Nazi Germany during World War II. He first came to international prominence as a humanitarian worker with Fridtjof Nansen during the Russian famine of 1921 in Ukraine, and in diplomacy. He served as Minister of Defence in the Agrarian Party governments of 1931 to 1933, then founded the fascist Nasjonal Samling (National Gathering) party.

Quisling, whose name became a synonym for traitor

Collaboration

When Germany invaded Norway on 9 April 1940, Quisling attempted a coup d'état against the Norwegian government and announced himself head of government, though the Germans initially did not support his administration. After a period of direct rule under Reichskommissar Josef Terboven, Quisling was installed as Minister President on 1 February 1942. His regime collaborated in the deportation of Norwegian Jews and the persecution of opponents. Membership in his party remained low, and he was widely despised by the Norwegian public.

Trial and execution

After Germany's surrender in May 1945, Quisling was arrested, tried for charges including embezzlement, murder and high treason, convicted, and executed by firing squad at Akershus Fortress in Oslo on 24 October 1945. His name has become an international eponym for a traitor who collaborates with an occupying enemy — the word "quisling" entered English and other languages during the war itself.
