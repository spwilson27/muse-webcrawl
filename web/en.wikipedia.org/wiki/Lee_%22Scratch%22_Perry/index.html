Lee "Scratch" Perry (born Rainford Hugh Perry; 20 March 1936 – 29 August 2021) was a Jamaican record producer and singer noted for his innovative studio techniques and production work. A pioneer of dub music in the 1970s, he produced recordings for Bob Marley and the Wailers, the Congos, Junior Byles and many others.

Early career

Perry began in music in the late 1950s working for Clement "Coxsone" Dodd's Studio One, then Joe Gibbs, before founding his own Upsetter label in 1968. His band the Upsetters scored an international hit with "Return of Django" in 1969. In the early 1970s he produced landmark early sessions by the Wailers.

The Black Ark

In 1973 Perry built the Black Ark studio behind his house in Kingston. Using a four-track recorder, phasers, echo and unconventional techniques — burying microphones, recording found sounds, and treating the mixing desk itself as an instrument — he created classic dub and roots reggae albums such as Super Ape and the Congos' Heart of the Congos. The studio burned down around 1979, an event Perry later said he caused himself.

Later years

Perry continued recording and touring for decades, collaborating with artists from the Clash to Adrian Sherwood and the Orb, and won a Grammy Award in 2003 for Jamaican E.T. He died in 2021 at the age of 85.
