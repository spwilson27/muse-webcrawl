Périgueux is a commune in southwestern France and the prefecture of the Dordogne department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine. It is the principal town of the Périgord, a region celebrated for its gastronomy — foie gras, truffles, and walnuts — and its dense concentration of prehistoric and medieval sites.

History

The site began as the Gallic settlement of the Petrocorii and became the Roman city of Vesunna, whose remains — including the Vesunna tower, an amphitheatre, and a Gallo-Roman villa housing the Vesunna museum — survive today. The medieval town grew around the abbey of Saint-Front, a major stop on the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela.

Landmarks

The cathedral of Saint-Front, built in the twelfth century and heavily restored in the nineteenth, is famed for its five Greek-cross domes and is said to have served as a model for Sacré-Cœur in Paris; it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Routes of Santiago de Compostela. The old town around it preserves Renaissance houses and hosts renowned food markets.
