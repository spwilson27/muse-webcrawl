Retezat Mountains

The Retezat Mountains (Romanian: Munții Retezat) are one of the highest massifs in Romania, part of the Southern Carpathians. The highest peak is Peleaga, at 2,509 metres. The massif is famous for its glacial landscape of cirques, U-shaped valleys and more than 80 glacial lakes, the largest of which is Lake Bucura, the biggest glacial lake in Romania.

Retezat National Park

Much of the range is protected by Retezat National Park, established in 1935 — the oldest national park in Romania — covering roughly 38,000 hectares. The park is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and contains one of the last remaining intact old-growth forest landscapes in Europe, with extensive primeval beech and spruce forest. It is home to chamois, brown bears, wolves, lynx and about a third of Romania's plant species, including many endemics. The name Retezat means "cut off" in Romanian, referring to the flat-topped appearance of some peaks.

Geology and tourism

The mountains are formed mainly of granite and crystalline rocks reshaped by Pleistocene glaciation. Marked hiking trails and mountain refuges make the Retezat one of Romania's most popular trekking areas, although its weather is notoriously changeable.
