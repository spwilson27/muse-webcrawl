Duty (tax)

For other uses, see Duty (disambiguation).

A duty is a form of taxation levied on certain goods, services, or other transactions. Historically, duties were taxes paid to a local lord or monarch for the use of a harbour, road, bridge or market, or for the privilege of trading. Today the term is most commonly used for customs duties on imported and exported goods and for excise duties on certain domestically produced goods such as alcohol, tobacco and fuel.

Types of duty

Customs duty

A customs duty, or import duty, is a tax imposed on goods when they are transported across international borders. Customs duties are usually levied to protect domestic industries, raise government revenue, or both. The rate may be ad valorem (a percentage of the value of the goods) or specific (a fixed amount per unit). Duties on exports are less common but were historically important.

Excise duty

Excise duties are inland taxes on the sale or use of specific goods, such as tobacco, alcohol, and motor fuel. Unlike customs duties they apply regardless of where the goods originate.

Stamp duty

Stamp duty is a tax on legal documents, historically evidenced by a physical stamp, and in many jurisdictions now applied to transactions such as property conveyances and share transfers.

History

Duties are among the oldest forms of taxation. In medieval England, the Crown levied customs on wool and wine, and parliamentary control of such taxation became a central issue in conflicts between king and Parliament, ultimately contributing to the English Civil War. In the modern era, trade agreements and organisations such as the World Trade Organization have progressively reduced customs duties between member states.

Duty-free

Goods sold without duties, typically to international travellers at airports and ports, are described as duty-free. Travellers are usually allowed to bring limited quantities of duty-free goods into a country before duties become payable.
