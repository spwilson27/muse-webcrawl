Siege of Mafeking

The Siege of Mafeking was a famous engagement of the Second Boer War, fought at the town of Mafeking (now Mahikeng) in South Africa from 13 October 1899 to 17 May 1900. A British garrison of about 2,000 men under Colonel Robert Baden-Powell held out for 217 days against a much larger Boer force, using deception and improvisation to exaggerate its strength. The relief of Mafeking triggered exuberant celebrations in Britain — "mafficking" entered the language — and made Baden-Powell a national hero, paving the way for his founding of the Scout movement.

Background

Mafeking was a railway town near the border of the South African Republic (Transvaal). Baden-Powell had been sent to raise two regiments of mounted infantry in Rhodesia and the Bechuanaland Protectorate to threaten the northern Transvaal if war came. When the Boer republics invaded in October 1899, Mafeking was among the first towns besieged, along with Kimberley and Ladysmith.

The siege

Boer forces under Piet Cronjé (later Jacobus Snyman) surrounded the town but, lacking heavy artillery for much of the siege, resorted mainly to bombardment and sniping; a big siege gun ("Long Tom," nicknamed "Creaky") shelled the town from late October. Baden-Powell's defence relied on bluff: dummy minefields (actually boxes of sand, whose "testing" was theatrically staged), fake barbed wire, an improvised armoured train, a howitzer ("The Wolf") built in the railway workshops, and cadets and townspeople manning trenches and carrying messages — the Mafeking Cadet Corps of boys aged 12–15 later inspired Baden-Powell's idea for the Boy Scouts. Rations declined to horse meat and "sowens" porridge; a besieged newspaper, the Mafeking Mail, and local postage stamps (depicting Baden-Powell and a cadet on a bicycle) were produced. The defence force included some 500 Barolong tribesmen defending their own quarter (the Stadt) under their chiefs; over 200 Africans died during the siege. The only major assault, on 12 May 1900 by Boer commandos under Sarel Eloff, briefly penetrated the defences before being captured.

Relief and aftermath

A flying column under Colonel Bryan Mahon, combined with a Rhodesian force under Herbert Plumer, relieved Mafeking on 17 May 1900. News of the relief provoked wild public rejoicing in London and across the British Empire — the verb "to maffick," meaning to celebrate with extravagant public demonstrations, derives from the celebrations. Baden-Powell was promoted to major-general and became a hero; his siege experience with the cadet corps directly informed Scouting for Boys (1908).
