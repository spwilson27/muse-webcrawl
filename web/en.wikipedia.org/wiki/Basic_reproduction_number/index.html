Basic reproduction number


One case generating further infections

In epidemiology, the basic reproduction number, commonly denoted R0 and pronounced "R nought" or "R zero", is the expected number of cases directly generated by one case in a population in which all individuals are susceptible to infection. It is a dimensionless number, not a rate, and it reflects the biology of the pathogen, the infectious period, the contact rate between people and the mode of transmission.

Threshold behaviour

If R0 > 1, each case generates on average more than one replacement, so an introduced infection can spread exponentially and cause an epidemic. If R0 < 1, the infection dies out. In a partially immune population the relevant quantity is the effective reproduction number, Re, and control aims to reduce it below 1; the classic herd-immunity threshold for vaccination is 1 − 1/R0. In compartmental models such as SIR, R0 can often be written as the product of transmission rate and mean infectious period.

Typical values

Estimated R0 values depend strongly on setting and population, but commonly cited ranges include about 12–18 for measles, 4–7 for poliomyelitis, 2–3 for the ancestral strain of SARS-CoV-2, and around 1.3 for seasonal influenza. Superspreading and heterogeneity mean real transmission is often far more dispersed than a single number suggests.
