Paramount Global was an American multinational mass media and entertainment conglomerate controlled by National Amusements and headquartered in New York City. It was formed on 4 December 2019 as ViacomCBS through the merger of CBS Corporation and the second incarnation of Viacom, reuniting assets that had been split in 2006; the company adopted the Paramount name on 16 February 2022.

Assets

Paramount's portfolio centred on the Paramount Pictures film studio and the CBS broadcast network, alongside cable channels MTV, Nickelodeon, Comedy Central, BET, VH1, Showtime and Paramount Network, and streaming services Paramount+ and the free, advertising-supported Pluto TV. Internationally it owned networks including Channel 5 (UK), Network 10 (Australia) and Telefe (Argentina). Its assets derived from some of the oldest brands in American media: CBS was founded in 1927 and Paramount Pictures in 1912.

History

The merger that created ViacomCBS was championed by Shari Redstone, whose family company National Amusements controlled both parties, ending years of separation and litigation. CEO Bob Bakish led a streaming-centred strategy, launching Paramount+ in March 2021. Despite a vast content library, the company struggled with cord-cutting, heavy debts and the costs of competing with larger streaming rivals, and its market value fell sharply.

Sale to Skydance

In July 2024, after months of on-and-off negotiations, Paramount agreed to merge with David Ellison's Skydance Media in a deal that installed Skydance's backers as owners of National Amusements' controlling stake. After a protracted regulatory review, the transaction closed in August 2025, creating a combined company (Paramount, a Skydance Corporation) led by Ellison, and ending both the Redstone era and Paramount Global as a standalone entity.
