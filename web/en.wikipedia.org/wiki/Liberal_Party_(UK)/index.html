Liberal Party (UK)

The Liberal Party was one of the two major political parties in the United Kingdom, along with the Conservative Party, during the 19th and early 20th centuries. Founded in 1859 from Whigs, Peelites and Radicals, it governed under leaders including William Gladstone, the Earl of Rosebery, H. H. Asquith and David Lloyd George.

History

Liberal governments enacted free trade, parliamentary reform and, in 1906–1914, the beginnings of the welfare state (the People's Budget, National Insurance). Split during the First World War between Asquith and Lloyd George, the party was displaced by Labour in the 1920s and declined to a handful of seats, before reviving modestly and merging with the SDP in 1988 to form the Liberal Democrats.
