Faxian (Chinese: 法顯; pinyin: Fǎxiǎn; c. 337 – c. 422 CE) was a Chinese Buddhist monk and translator who travelled by foot from China to India between 399 and 412 CE, visiting sacred Buddhist sites in Central, South, and Southeast Asia in search of authentic Vinaya (monastic discipline) texts. His travelogue, the Foguo Ji — A Record of Buddhist Kingdoms — is a foundational source for the history of Buddhism in India during the Gupta period.

Journey

Ordained after a childhood spent as a novice, Faxian set out in 399 with several companions, crossing the deserts of the Tarim Basin, the Hindu Kush, and the Indus valley to reach the Buddhist heartlands of the Ganges plain. He spent years at Pataliputra copying manuscripts, and two years in the port of Tamralipti, before sailing to Sri Lanka (where he spent two more years), then via Java back to China, surviving storms and shipwreck to land in Shandong in 412. Of his original companions, none completed the round trip with him.

Record and translations

Faxian's Record describes the flourishing condition of Buddhism under the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II, noting prosperous cities, charitable hospitals at Pataliputra, and the relative mildness of punishments — observations that historians use to reconstruct fifth-century India. On his return he settled in Nanjing and, with the Indian monk Buddhabhadra, translated the texts he had brought back, including the Mahayana Mahaparinirvana Sutra and a version of the Mahasanghika Vinaya.
