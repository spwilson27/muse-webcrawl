Black college football national championship

The black college football national championship is a title historically awarded to the best football team among historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs) in the United States. Because HBCUs were long excluded from the major bowls and national polls during segregation, Black newspapers, polls and classic bowl games selected their own national champions from the early 20th century onward.

History

Selectors such as the Pittsburgh Courier named Black college champions from the 1920s. Programs such as Grambling State under Eddie Robinson, Tennessee State under John Merritt, Florida A&M, Morgan State, Tuskegee and Prairie View A&M won multiple titles. Postseason "classics" — most famously the Orange Blossom Classic in Miami — often settled the championship. Since 2015 the Celebration Bowl has pitted the champions of the MEAC and SWAC against each other as a de facto Black college championship game.

Significance

The championship preserves the record of excellence achieved by HBCU programs whose players were barred from most predominantly white programs until the late 1960s and 1970s. HBCU teams produced many Pro Football Hall of Fame players, and several schools claim double-digit Black national titles.
