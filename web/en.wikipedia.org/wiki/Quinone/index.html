Quinone

A quinone is a class of organic compounds formally derived from aromatic compounds such as benzene or naphthalene by conversion of an even number of –CH= groups into –C(=O)– groups with any necessary rearrangement of double bonds, giving a fully conjugated cyclic dione structure. The prototypical member is 1,4-benzoquinone (cyclohexadienedione), often itself simply called "quinone".

Representative quinones, from benzoquinones to anthraquinone and naphthoquinone.

Properties and reactions

Quinones are typically coloured, oxidising compounds. Their characteristic reaction is reversible reduction to the corresponding hydroquinone (an aromatic diol), a two-electron, two-proton process central to their use as oxidants and electron carriers. They undergo Michael addition and can act as dienophiles in Diels–Alder reactions.

Occurrence and uses

Quinones are widespread in nature: ubiquinone (coenzyme Q) and plastoquinone shuttle electrons in respiration and photosynthesis, vitamin K compounds are naphthoquinones, and many natural pigments and antibiotics (such as the anthracyclines) contain quinone units. Industrially, anthraquinone is used in the production of hydrogen peroxide and as a dye precursor, and benzoquinone derivatives serve as polymerisation inhibitors and photographic developers.
