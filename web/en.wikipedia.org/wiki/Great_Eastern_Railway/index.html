Great Eastern Railway

The Great Eastern Railway (GER) was a pre-grouping British railway company, whose main line linked London Liverpool Street to Norwich and which operated an extensive network across East Anglia and the northeastern suburbs of London. Formed in 1862 by the amalgamation of several smaller companies, chiefly the Eastern Counties Railway, it became one of the largest railway companies in Britain before becoming part of the London and North Eastern Railway in the 1923 grouping.

Operations

The GER built up the world's largest suburban passenger service at Liverpool Street, carrying enormous numbers of commuters from Essex and northeast London, and famously ran trains at a penny a mile for workmen. Its locomotive works at Stratford built many engines to the designs of chief engineers including James Holden, whose "Claud Hamilton" 4-4-0s and Decapod experiment are well remembered. The company also served the ports of Harwich and Felixstowe with boat trains connecting to continental ferries.
