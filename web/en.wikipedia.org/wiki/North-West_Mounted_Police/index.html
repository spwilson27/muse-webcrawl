The North-West Mounted Police (NWMP) was a Canadian paramilitary police force established in 1873 by Prime Minister John A. Macdonald to maintain order in the North-West Territories, assert Canadian sovereignty over the prairies, and control the whisky trade and relations with Indigenous peoples.

The March West

In July 1874, some 275 officers and men set out from Manitoba on the gruelling "March West" to the foothills of the Rockies, prompted in part by the Cypress Hills Massacre of 1873. The force established posts including Fort Macleod, Fort Calgary and Fort Saskatchewan, earning a reputation — often contrasted with the American frontier experience — for comparatively orderly relations with First Nations.

Later history

The NWMP played major roles in suppressing the North-West Rebellion of 1885, policing the Klondike Gold Rush (1898), and patrolling the Arctic. Renamed the Royal North-West Mounted Police in 1904, it merged with the Dominion Police in 1920 to form the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP).
