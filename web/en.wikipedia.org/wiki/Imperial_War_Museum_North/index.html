Imperial War Museum North

IWM North
Opened 5 July 2002
Architect: Daniel Libeskind
Location: Trafford Park / Salford Quays, Greater Manchester

Imperial War Museum North (now styled IWM North) is a museum in the Metropolitan Borough of Trafford in Greater Manchester, England. One of five branches of the Imperial War Museum, it explores the impact of modern conflicts on people and society. It was the first branch of the museum to be located outside the south-east of England. The museum occupies a site overlooking the Manchester Ship Canal in Trafford Park, at the heart of an area heavily bombed during the Manchester Blitz.

Architecture

The building was designed by architect Daniel Libeskind and opened on 5 July 2002. It is composed of three interlocking "shards" of a shattered globe—the earth shard, air shard, and water shard—symbolising conflict on land, in the air, and at sea. The 55-metre air shard, a curved tapered tower clad in aluminium, contains a viewing platform reached by lift. Inside, the main exhibition space is a single vast hall with sloping floors and disorienting geometry intended to evoke the destabilising experience of war.

Exhibitions

The permanent exhibition is organised chronologically from the First World War to the present day, around a series of thematic "silos". A 360-degree audio-visual presentation, the Big Picture Show, is projected across the walls of the main hall at intervals. Large objects on display have included a Harrier jump jet, a T-34 tank, and the field gun that fired the British Army's first shot of the First World War.

Outside, the area around the museum forms part of the regenerated Salford Quays and MediaCityUK district, connected to the museum by the Media City footbridge across the canal.
