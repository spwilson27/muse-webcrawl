James I of Aragon

James I the Conqueror (Catalan: Jaume el Conqueridor; 2 February 1208 – 27 July 1276) was King of Aragon, Count of Barcelona and Lord of Montpellier from 1213 and King of Mallorca and Valencia, reigning 63 years, the longest of any Iberian monarch. He conquered Mallorca, Menorca, Ibiza and Valencia from Muslim rule and dictated the Llibre dels fets, one of the earliest royal autobiographies.

Born: 2 February 1208, Montpellier

Predecessor: Peter II
Successor: Peter III (Aragon), James II (Mallorca)

Early life

Son of Peter II of Aragon and Marie of Montpellier, James was held hostage by Simon de Montfort after his father's death at the Battle of Muret (1213) during the Albigensian Crusade. Released, he survived a turbulent minority of noble factions under the regency associated with the Knights Templar at Monzón.

Conquests

In 1229–1235 James conquered the Balearic Islands — Mallorca, Ibiza and Formentera, with Menorca becoming tributary (later annexed 1287). From 1232 he campaigned in Valencia, taking the city in 1238 and creating the Kingdom of Valencia with its own Furs law code. He also intervened in Murcia on behalf of Castile.

Government and culture

James promoted trade, codified law in the Furs de València, favoured the Corts, and patronised learning. His chronicle, the Llibre dels fets ("Book of Deeds"), narrates his life in Catalan. By the Treaty of Corbeil (1258) he renounced claims north of the Pyrenees in exchange for Louis IX renouncing claims to Catalonia. He divided his realms between his sons Peter and James, a division with lasting consequences for the Crown of Aragon.
