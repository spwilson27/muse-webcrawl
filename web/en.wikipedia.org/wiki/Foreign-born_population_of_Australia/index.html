Foreign-born population of Australia

Australia has one of the highest proportions of foreign-born residents of any major country. According to the 2021 census, about 27.6% of Australia's population — more than seven million people — was born overseas, and nearly half of all residents have at least one parent born abroad.

Largest birthplace groups

The largest foreign-born groups are those born in England (about 927,000 in 2021), followed by India, China, New Zealand and the Philippines. Since the 2000s, India and China have overtaken the older post-war European source countries such as Italy and Greece, whose populations are ageing and declining.

History

Immigration shaped Australia from British colonisation in 1788 through the gold rushes, the post-Second World War assisted-passage schemes that brought millions from Britain and continental Europe, and the dismantling of the White Australia policy between 1966 and 1973, which opened migration to Asia and the Middle East. Net overseas migration remains a major driver of population growth.
