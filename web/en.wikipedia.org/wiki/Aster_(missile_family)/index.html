Aster (missile family)

Aster
TypeSurface-to-air missile
ManufacturerEurosam / MBDA
VariantsAster 15, Aster 30
UsersFrance, Italy, United Kingdom, others

The Aster family of surface-to-air missiles, developed by the Eurosam consortium (MBDA and Thales), provides air defence for naval vessels and ground forces. The name comes from the Greek aster ("star") and an acronym for Aérospatiale terminale.

Variants

The Aster 15 is a medium-range weapon (over 30 km) for point and local area defence, while the Aster 30 extends range beyond 120 km and, in its Block 1 form, adds capability against short-range ballistic missiles of up to about 600 km range. The Aster 30 Block 1 NT (New Technology) adds a new Ka-band seeker for defence against longer-range ballistic threats. A land-based system, SAMP/T, pairs Aster 30 with the Arabel or Ground Fire radar and is used by France and Italy.

Design

Aster missiles are "hit-to-kill" weapons: a solid-fuel tandem booster provides vertical launch and rapid turn-over, and in the terminal phase a system of small lateral thrusters near the centre of gravity — the "PIF-PAF" (pilotage en force / pilotage aérodynamique fort) system — gives extreme agility to strike the target directly with the warhead. An active radar seeker homes on the target in the final phase.

Service

Aster arms French and Italian Horizon-class and FREMM frigates, the French carrier Charles de Gaulle, and British Type 45 destroyers as part of the Sea Viper system. Export customers include Singapore, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Morocco and Egypt. In 2023–2024, French, Italian and British warships used Aster missiles in the Red Sea to shoot down Houthi drones and ballistic missiles, the family's most prominent combat use.
