Barnabas

Barnabas (Greek: Βαρνάβας), born Joseph, was a prominent early Christian missionary and a companion of the apostle Paul. He is described in the Acts of the Apostles as "a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith." His name, given to him by the apostles, is traditionally interpreted as "son of encouragement" or "son of consolation."

Life

Barnabas was a Levite from Cyprus. After joining the early Jerusalem church, he sold a field he owned and laid the proceeds at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:36–37). He introduced the newly converted Paul to the apostles in Jerusalem, vouching for the sincerity of his conversion when others still feared him.

Barnabas was sent to Antioch, where a large Gentile church was growing, and there recruited Paul from Tarsus to help teach the new believers. Together they travelled to Cyprus and central Asia Minor on the journey often called Paul's first missionary journey, accompanied by Barnabas's cousin John Mark. A disagreement over Mark later caused Paul and Barnabas to part ways; Barnabas returned to Cyprus with Mark.

Veneration

Barnabas is venerated as a saint in many Christian traditions, with his feast day on 11 June. The apocryphal Epistle of Barnabas and the Gospel of Barnabas are pseudepigraphical works written under his name. Tradition holds that he was martyred at Salamis in Cyprus around AD 61.
