Anthony Braxton

Anthony Braxton (born June 4, 1945) is an American experimental composer, improviser, and multi-instrumentalist. A central figure in avant-garde jazz, he is known for his vast body of work, his idiosyncratic titles (often diagrams or formulas), and his "Tri-Axium" philosophical writings. Braxton plays a wide range of saxophones and clarinets, including the rare contrabass saxophone.

Born: June 4, 1945, Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Genres: Avant-garde jazz, free jazz, experimental
Instruments: Saxophones, clarinets, flute, piano
Labels: Delmark, Arista, hatART, Leo, Tzadik

Early life and career

Braxton grew up on the South Side of Chicago and studied philosophy at Roosevelt University. In the mid-1960s he joined the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM), performing with Muhal Richard Abrams and others. His 1968 debut album, For Alto, was a landmark recording of unaccompanied solo saxophone pieces.

Work

In the 1970s Braxton led a celebrated quartet with trumpeter Kenny Wheeler, bassist Dave Holland, and drummer Barry Altschul, and collaborated with Chick Corea in the group Circle. His compositions numbered in the hundreds employ a self-devised classification system. Later projects include the "Ghost Trance Music" ensembles and the operatic Trillium cycle. From 1990 to 2013 he taught at Wesleyan University.

Recognition

Braxton received a MacArthur Fellowship ("Genius Grant") in 1994 and was named an NEA Jazz Master in 2014.
