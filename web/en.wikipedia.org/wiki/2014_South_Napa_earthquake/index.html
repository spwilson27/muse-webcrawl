2014 South Napa earthquake

Earthquake details
Date24 August 2014, 03:20 PDT
Magnitude6.0 Mw
EpicentreNear Napa and American Canyon, California
FaultWest Napa Fault
InjuriesAbout 200; 1 death indirectly attributed
DamageEstimated US$362 million – $1 billion

The 2014 South Napa earthquake struck Northern California's wine country in the early hours of 24 August 2014. With a moment magnitude of 6.0 and an epicentre about 6 kilometres south-west of Napa, it was the largest earthquake in the San Francisco Bay Area since the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake. Rupture occurred on the West Napa Fault, part of the wider San Andreas Fault system. The quake injured around 200 people, damaged historic buildings in downtown Napa, and caused extensive losses to the region's wine industry.

Earthquake

The main shock occurred at 03:20 local time at a depth of about 11 kilometres. It produced strong ground motion across Napa and Solano counties, ruptured the ground surface along roughly 9 kilometres of the West Napa Fault, and was followed by dozens of aftershocks, the largest of magnitude 3.9. Instruments recorded peak ground accelerations exceeding 0.6 g near the epicentre. The quake was also a notable test of experimental earthquake early-warning systems, which detected the event seconds before shaking reached Berkeley and San Francisco.

Damage and casualties

About 200 people were treated for injuries, mostly cuts and bruises from falling objects; one victim died days later from a head injury sustained during the quake. In Napa, historic masonry buildings including the Goodman Library, the Napa County courthouse and the Alexandria Hotel building were badly damaged, and fires broke out in a mobile home park. Wineries across Napa Valley reported cracked tanks and toppled barrel stacks, with losses to wine and infrastructure estimated in the tens of millions of dollars. Total damage estimates ranged from about $362 million to $1 billion. Governor Jerry Brown declared a state of emergency, and federal disaster assistance followed.

Geological significance

The event was the first surface-rupturing earthquake in the Bay Area since 1989 and highlighted the seismic hazard of the West Napa Fault, previously considered less well characterised than neighbouring faults. Post-earthquake studies using GPS and satellite radar documented substantial afterslip — continued movement along the fault in the weeks after the main shock.
