Mirza Hasan Shirazi

Mirza Mohammad Hasan Shirazi (1815–1895), known as Mirza Shirazi or Mujaddid Shirazi, was an Iranian Twelver Shia marja' (source of emulation) and one of the most influential clerics of the 19th century. From his base in Samarra, in Ottoman Iraq, he led the Shia religious establishment, and he is remembered above all for the fatwa that defeated the Tobacco concession in Qajar Persia.

The Tobacco fatwa

In 1890 Naser al-Din Shah granted a British company a monopoly over the production, sale, and export of tobacco in Persia. Widespread protests culminated in a fatwa attributed to Shirazi (1891) declaring the use of tobacco tantamount to war against the Hidden Imam. Persians across the country stopped smoking — even the Shah's household was affected — and the government was forced to cancel the concession in 1892. The Tobacco Protest is often seen as a forerunner of the Persian Constitutional Revolution, demonstrating the political power the ulama could mobilise.

Life

Born in Shiraz, Shirazi studied in Isfahan and Najaf, becoming the leading student of Morteza Ansari, whom he succeeded as the pre-eminent marja'. He moved to Samarra, where he founded a seminary and taught a generation of scholars. He died in Samarra in 1895 and was buried in Najaf.
