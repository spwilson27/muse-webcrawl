John Rock (physician)

John Charles Rock (March 24, 1890 – December 4, 1984) was an American obstetrician and gynecologist. He is best known as one of the co-developers of the first oral contraceptive pill and as a pioneer of in vitro fertilisation research and fertility treatment.

Career

Rock was born in Marlborough, Massachusetts, and studied at Harvard Medical School. He established one of the first fertility clinics in the United States at the Free Hospital for Women in Brookline and became a professor at Harvard. In 1944, his associate Miriam Menkin achieved the first fertilisation of a human egg in a laboratory dish.

The Pill

In the 1950s Rock collaborated with biologist Gregory Pincus on clinical trials of the hormonal contraceptive Enovid. A devout Catholic, Rock campaigned for the pill's acceptance, arguing in his 1963 book The Time Has Come that it was a natural extension of the Church-approved rhythm method; the Church rejected his position in the 1968 encyclical Humanae vitae.

Legacy

Rock's clinical work made modern contraception practical and helped millions of patients with infertility. He died in Peterborough, New Hampshire, at the age of 94.
