Bad Kreuznach is a Landkreis (district) in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. It is bounded by (from the east and clockwise) the districts of Mainz-Bingen, Alzey-Worms, Donnersbergkreis, Kusel, Birkenfeld and Rhein-Hunsrück-Kreis. The district's administrative seat is the spa town of Bad Kreuznach, which itself is not part of the district's surrounding association municipalities but is administered together with them.

Geography

The district lies in the valley of the Nahe river, which flows from the southwest to the northeast and joins the Rhine at Bingen. The landscape is characterised by vineyards along the Nahe, the wooded hills of the Soonwald to the north, and the northern Palatinate uplands to the south. The Rotenfels near Bad Münster am Stein is one of the highest rock faces north of the Alps.

History

The district was established in the early 19th century when the region became part of Prussia after the Congress of Vienna. Its borders changed several times, notably in the administrative reforms of 1969, when parts of the neighbouring dissolved districts of Rockenhausen and Simmern were added. The town of Bad Kreuznach, famous for its brine springs and radon inhalation therapy, gave the district its name.

Towns and municipalities

The district comprises the association municipalities (Verbandsgemeinden) of Bad Kreuznach, Bad Sobernheim, Kirn-Land, Langenlonsheim-Stromberg, Meisenheim and Rüdesheim, together with the independent towns of Bad Kreuznach and Kirn. In all it contains well over one hundred local municipalities, many of them small wine-growing villages.

Economy

Wine production along the Nahe, with Riesling the most notable grape, is central to the local economy, together with tourism drawn by the spa towns and the valley's scenery. Historically the region was also known for agate cutting and gemstone processing centred on nearby Idar-Oberstein.
