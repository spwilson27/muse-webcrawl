Ireland–United Kingdom relations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The two states share a Common Travel Area dating to the 1920s

Ireland–United Kingdom relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Ireland and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Relations stretch back centuries of entanglement, from the Norman invasion of Ireland and the Acts of Union 1800, which incorporated Ireland into the United Kingdom, to Irish independence in 1922, the Northern Ireland conflict, and the peace process of the 1990s.

History

Following the Anglo-Irish War, the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 created the Irish Free State as a dominion; Northern Ireland remained in the UK, partitioning the island. The Free State became the Republic of Ireland in 1949. The Troubles (c. 1968–1998) in Northern Ireland strained relations, though cooperation deepened with the Anglo-Irish Agreement of 1985 and culminated in the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, under which Ireland amended its constitutional territorial claim and new North–South and East–West institutions were created. Queen Elizabeth II's state visit to Ireland in 2011 was widely seen as a milestone of reconciliation.

Cooperation and movement

The two countries share a Common Travel Area, predating both states' EU membership, which allows their citizens to travel, live, work and access services in each other's jurisdictions; bilateral trade is extensive, and Ireland's largest cities host large communities of each other's nationals. A memorandum of understanding in 2019 reaffirmed the Common Travel Area after Brexit.

Brexit

The United Kingdom's withdrawal from the European Union (completed 2020) created the central recent issue in relations: avoiding a hard border on the island of Ireland. The Northern Ireland Protocol (2019) and its successor, the Windsor Framework (2023), placed Northern Ireland in alignment with certain EU goods rules while remaining in the UK customs territory. The arrangements were controversial in unionist politics but broadly stabilised UK–Ireland and UK–EU relations.
