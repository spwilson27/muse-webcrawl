Chikungunya is an infection caused by the chikungunya virus (CHIKV), a mosquito-borne alphavirus transmitted to humans by infected Aedes mosquitoes, principally Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus. The disease is characterised by an abrupt onset of fever and severe, often debilitating joint pain. The name derives from a word in the Kimakonde language of southern Tanzania meaning "that which bends up", describing the stooped posture of sufferers caused by joint pain.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually begin 2–12 days after a bite and include high fever, polyarthralgia (pain in multiple joints, often symmetric and in the hands and feet), muscle pain, headache, fatigue, and a maculopapular rash. Most patients recover within a week or two, but joint pain can persist for months or years in a proportion of cases, particularly in older adults. Death is rare and occurs mainly in the elderly or those with underlying conditions. The disease closely resembles dengue fever, with which it is often confused.

Epidemiology

Chikungunya was first identified during an outbreak in southern Tanganyika (now Tanzania) in 1952. A major epidemic on Réunion Island in 2005–2006 infected roughly a third of the population and was associated with a viral mutation (E1-A226V) that improved transmission by Aedes albopictus. In December 2013 the virus was reported for the first time in the Americas, on the island of Saint Martin, and subsequently spread explosively through the Caribbean and Latin America. Outbreaks have also occurred across Africa and Asia, including large epidemics in India.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention relies on mosquito control and avoiding bites. There is no specific antiviral treatment; care is supportive, using rest, fluids, and analgesics/antipyretics. A live-attenuated vaccine, Ixchiq, was approved in the United States in 2023 for adults at increased risk of exposure.
