Eletrobras (Portuguese: Centrais Elétricas Brasileiras S.A.) is a Brazilian electric power holding company, the largest producer of electricity in Latin America and one of the largest utility companies in the world. Through its subsidiaries it has historically been responsible for a large share of Brazil's electricity generation — predominantly hydroelectric — and for much of the country's high-voltage transmission network.

History

Eletrobras was created by the federal government in 1962, during the presidency of João Goulart, to coordinate the expansion of Brazil's electricity sector. Its subsidiaries — including Furnas, Chesf, Eletronorte and Eletrosul — built many of Brazil's great hydroelectric plants and the national interconnected transmission system. The company held stakes in Itaipu Binacional, jointly with Paraguay, one of the largest hydroelectric dams in the world, and controlled the nuclear operator Eletronuclear, builder of the Angra plants.

Privatisation

After decades as a state-controlled company, Eletrobras was privatised in June 2022 through a share offering on the São Paulo and New York stock exchanges that diluted the federal government's voting stake, though the government kept a shareholding and a golden share with veto rights over certain decisions. The privatisation law required the separation of Itaipu and Eletronuclear, which remained under state control through a new holding, ENBPar.

Operations

The company's generation fleet is dominated by hydroelectricity, complemented by thermal, wind and solar plants, and it operates tens of thousands of kilometres of transmission lines interconnecting Brazil's regions.
