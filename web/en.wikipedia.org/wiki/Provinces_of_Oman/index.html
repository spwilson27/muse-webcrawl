The Sultanate of Oman is divided into eleven governorates (muhafazat, singular muhafazah), which are in turn subdivided into 63 wilayats (provinces or districts). The current administrative structure was established by a royal decree issued by Sultan Qaboos bin Said on 28 October 2011, which reorganised the country from five regions (mintaqah) and four governorates into a uniform system of eleven governorates.

History

Before the 2011 reorganisation, Oman was divided into five regions — Ad Dakhiliyah, Al Batinah, Al Wusta, Ash Sharqiyah and Ad Dhahirah — and four governorates: Muscat, Dhofar, Musandam and Al Buraimi. The decree of 28 October 2011 split Al Batinah into North and South Al Batinah, and Ash Sharqiyah into North and South Ash Sharqiyah, elevating the total to eleven governorates, all of equal administrative rank. Each governorate is headed by a governor appointed by royal decree, and the system is intended to promote decentralised development planning.

Geography

Musandam, on the tip of the Musandam Peninsula, is an exclave separated from the rest of Oman by territory of the United Arab Emirates; Dhofar, in the far south, is separated from the northern governorates by the vast, sparsely populated Al Wusta region. Muscat is the most populous governorate and the seat of government, while Al Wusta is the largest by area and among the least densely populated.
