Calennig

TypeNew Year custom
ObservedWales

Calennig is a Welsh New Year tradition, and also the name of the gift given at New Year. The word derives from the Latin calendae, the first day of the month — the same root as "calendar".

The custom

Traditionally on New Year's Day, children would go from house to house in their village early in the morning, singing rhymes and good wishes for the year ahead in exchange for small gifts of money or food — the calennig. The children often carried the decorated apple that gives the custom its most distinctive image: an apple mounted on three twig "legs", adorned with holly leaves, raisins, nuts, and cloves, sometimes with a sprig of box or rosemary and a candle, carried as a lucky emblem from door to door.

Old New Year

In a few Welsh communities — most famously the Gwaun Valley in Pembrokeshire — the New Year kept to the old Julian calendar date, Hen Galan, is still celebrated on 13 January, when children in the valley have a day off school to go out collecting calennig. The custom survives there as a living tradition rather than a revival.

Related customs

Calennig belongs to a family of British and Irish midwinter visiting customs involving song, luck-bringing, and gifts, comparable to first-footing in Scotland, wassailing in England, and the Welsh Mari Lwyd tradition of the Christmas season.
