Bluetooth Low Energy (Bluetooth LE, colloquially BLE, formerly marketed as Bluetooth Smart) is a wireless personal area network technology designed and marketed by the Bluetooth Special Interest Group (Bluetooth SIG) aimed at novel applications in the healthcare, fitness, beacons, security, and home entertainment industries. It is independent of classic Bluetooth (Bluetooth BR/EDR) and has no backward compatibility, but most modern devices support both in "dual mode".

History

The technology originated as Nokia's Wibree, announced in 2006, and was merged into the Bluetooth standard as part of Bluetooth Core Specification version 4.0, adopted in June 2010. The first smartphone to implement it was the iPhone 4S, released in October 2011. Bluetooth 5 (2016) doubled the speed and quadrupled the range in LE modes, and later versions added LE Audio and the LC3 codec.

Technical details

BLE operates in the 2.4 GHz ISM band using 40 channels with 2 MHz spacing and frequency hopping. It uses the Generic Attribute Profile (GATT) to organise data into services and characteristics. Devices advertise on three dedicated channels and can remain asleep for long intervals, enabling operation for months or years on a coin cell.

Applications

Typical uses include heart-rate monitors, fitness trackers, wireless medical devices, proximity beacons such as Apple's iBeacon and Google's Eddystone, item trackers, hearing aids using LE Audio, and mesh networks for building automation.
