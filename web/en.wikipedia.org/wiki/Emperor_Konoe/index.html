Emperor Konoe (近衛天皇, Konoe-tennō, May 16, 1139 – August 22, 1155) was the 76th emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. His reign spanned the years from 1142 through 1155.

Genealogy

Before his ascension to the Chrysanthemum Throne, his personal name (imina) was Narihito-shinnō (体仁親王). Konoe was the ninth son of Emperor Toba and his mother was Fujiwara no Tokushi (also known as Nariko), the daughter of Fujiwara no Tadamichi. He was raised in the household of his grandfather, the retired Emperor Toba, whose dominance over the imperial court continued through the system of cloistered rule (insei).

Events of Konoe's life

Konoe was proclaimed heir shortly after his birth, displacing his elder half-brother, the Emperor Sutoku, whose strained relationship with Toba made him politically inconvenient. Sutoku was pressured to abdicate in favor of the infant Konoe, and Konoe ascended the throne on January 5, 1142, at the age of two.

Throughout Konoe's reign, actual power remained in the hands of the retired Emperor Toba. In 1150, Konoe, then ten years old, was married to a daughter of the Fujiwara regent Fujiwara no Tadamichi. The young emperor suffered from chronic ill health, and persistent rumors held that he had been cursed by his deposed brother Sutoku, who was said to have performed rituals against him.

Death and succession

Konoe died on August 22, 1155, at the age of sixteen, without an heir. His death reopened the succession struggle between the lines of Toba and Sutoku. He was succeeded by his elder half-brother Go-Shirakawa. The following year, after Toba's death, the conflict erupted into open warfare in the Hōgen Rebellion of 1156, in which Sutoku's attempt to regain the throne was defeated.

The Imperial Household Agency designates Konoe's tomb as the Anrakuju-in no misasagi in Fushimi-ku, Kyoto.
