Philippine literature

Philippine literature is the literature associated with the Philippines, written in the archipelago's many languages — including Filipino (Tagalog), Spanish, English, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, and Kapampangan — from precolonial oral traditions to the present.

Precolonial period

Before Spanish colonization, Philippine peoples maintained rich oral literatures: epics such as the Ilocano Biag ni Lam-ang and the Maranao Darangen, riddles, proverbs, and folktales, some recorded in scripts such as Baybayin.

Spanish colonial period

Spanish rule brought the printing press and written works in Spanish and native languages, including religious pasyón texts. José Rizal's novels Noli Me Tángere (1887) and El Filibusterismo (1891) became foundational texts of Filipino nationalism.

American period and after

English-language writing flourished under American rule with writers such as Paz Márquez Benítez and José Garcia Villa. After independence, National Artists for Literature including Nick Joaquin, N.V.M. Gonzalez, and F. Sionil José shaped a modern canon, while contemporary writers publish in regional languages as well as Filipino and English.
