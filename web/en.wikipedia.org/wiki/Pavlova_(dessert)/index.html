Pavlova (dessert)

Pavlova
Type: Meringue dessert
Named after: Anna Pavlova, Russian ballerina
Main ingredients: Egg whites, sugar, whipped cream, fruit
[Image: A pavlova — a white meringue cake topped with whipped cream, strawberries, kiwi fruit and passionfruit]

Pavlova is a meringue-based dessert with a crisp crust and soft, light inside, usually topped with whipped cream and fruit. It is named after the Russian ballerina Anna Pavlova, who toured Australia and New Zealand in the 1920s. The dessert is a source of friendly trans-Tasman rivalry: both Australians and New Zealanders claim its invention, and research has traced similar recipes in New Zealand cookbooks to the late 1920s, while the name "pavlova" for meringue cakes also appeared in print in New Zealand by 1927.

Preparation

Egg whites are beaten to stiff peaks with caster sugar, then folded with cornflour and an acid such as vinegar or lemon juice; the mixture is shaped into a round and baked slowly at low temperature, then cooled in the oven. The additives produce the characteristic marshmallow-soft centre beneath a delicate crisp shell — a texture quite different from hard dry meringue. The base is topped just before serving with whipped cream and fresh fruit, most classically strawberries, kiwifruit and passionfruit.

Cultural role

Pavlova is strongly associated with summer and Christmas celebrations in Australia and New Zealand, and is served at barbecues and festive gatherings; "pav" is an affectionate abbreviation. Commercial meringue shells are sold ready-made, though home baking remains a point of pride, and the dessert's disputed origin is a stock example of the two nations' culinary rivalry.
