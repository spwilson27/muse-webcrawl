Nnedimma Nkemdili Okorafor (born 8 April 1974) is a Nigerian-American writer of science fiction and fantasy for children, young adults and adults. She is best known for the Binti trilogy, Who Fears Death, the Akata series, and the novella-to-novel cycle beginning with Lagoon. Her work, which she describes as "Africanfuturism" and "Africanjujuism", draws on Nigerian folklore, Igbo cosmology and West African settings. She has won the Hugo, Nebula, World Fantasy and Locus Awards.

Life

Okorafor was born in Cincinnati to Igbo Nigerian immigrant parents and made frequent childhood trips to Nigeria. A scoliosis surgery in her teens left her temporarily paralysed, during which she began writing stories. She earned a PhD in English from the University of Illinois Chicago and has taught creative writing at Buffalo State University and Arizona State University.

Works

Her debut novel Zahrah the Windseeker (2005) won the Wole Soyinka Prize. Who Fears Death (2010) won the World Fantasy Award for Best Novel. The novella Binti (2015) won both the Hugo and Nebula Awards for Best Novella, followed by Binti: Home and Binti: The Night Masquerade. The young-adult Akata Witch (2011) and its sequels founded her "Nsibidi Scripts" series. Later works include Remote Control (2021) and the memoir-in-graphic-form works. She has also written for Marvel Comics, including runs on Black Panther: Long Live the King and Shuri, and created the comic LaGuardia, which won a Hugo Award for Best Graphic Story.

Themes

Okorafor's fiction centres African places, languages and spiritual traditions, girlhood and transformation, technology fused with juju, and postcolonial futures. She is a leading voice arguing that African speculative fiction should be rooted in African worldviews rather than Western science-fiction defaults.
