Stream

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A stream is a body of water flowing in a channel on Earth's surface, driven by gravity from higher to lower ground. Streams range from ephemeral trickles to rivers of continental scale; in common usage "stream" implies the smaller end, and brooks, creeks, burns and runs are regional synonyms.

Behaviour

Streams begin in headwaters — springs, snowmelt, runoff — and grow by tributaries, organised by stream order (Strahler): two first-order streams join to make a second, and so on down the drainage basin to the mouth, often a delta. A stream's power to erode, transport and deposit sediment (see erosion) depends on its gradient and discharge: in flood it moves boulders and rewrites its floodplain; in drought it may shrink to disconnected pools.

Ecologically, streams are ribbons of high productivity — salmon runs, riparian forests, the drift of aquatic insects feeding fish — and their health is an early warning for their whole basin. Humanity's first farms, mills and cities grew on streams; their damming, channelling and pollution are among the oldest entries in our environmental ledger.
