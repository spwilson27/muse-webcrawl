Ferran Adrià i Acosta (born 14 May 1962) is a Spanish chef who was head chef of elBulli restaurant in Roses, Catalonia, widely regarded as one of the most influential chefs of the modern era. Under Adrià, elBulli held three Michelin stars and was voted the world's best restaurant a record five times by Restaurant magazine.

elBulli

Adrià joined elBulli in 1984 and became sole head chef in 1987. He transformed it into a laboratory of avant-garde cooking, opening only six months a year and devoting the rest to developing new techniques in his Barcelona workshop: culinary foams made with siphons, spherification, hot gelatins, and "deconstruction", in which classic dishes are taken apart and reassembled with altered textures and temperatures. The restaurant received up to two million reservation requests a year for some 8,000 seats.

After elBulli

Adrià closed elBulli in July 2011 and converted it into the elBullifoundation, which has worked on projects including the Bullipedia encyclopedia of gastronomy and the elBulli1846 museum on the restaurant's site. With his brother Albert he also ran the Tickets restaurant in Barcelona. His work helped establish Spain at the centre of modern gastronomy.
