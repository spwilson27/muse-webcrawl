The Kinks Are the Village Green Preservation Society is the sixth studio album by the English rock group the Kinks, released on 22 November 1968. It was the last album by the original quartet of Ray Davies, Dave Davies, Pete Quaife and Mick Avory, as Quaife left the band shortly after its release.

Written and recorded over a period of roughly two years, the album is a loose concept record celebrating English rural and small-town life, village greens, steam trains, draught beer and other vanishing customs. Ray Davies described it as a nostalgic reflection on the England of his childhood.

Background and recording

Sessions took place intermittently at Pye Studios in London between 1966 and 1968, alongside work on the albums Something Else by the Kinks and numerous singles. Session keyboardist Nicky Hopkins contributed extensively. Songs including "Village Green" and "Village Green Preservation Society" were accumulated gradually as Davies pursued the pastoral theme.

Release and reception

The album was released the same day as the Beatles' self-titled double album ("the White Album") and sold poorly on first release, failing to chart in either the UK or the US. Critical opinion, however, grew steadily over the following decades, and the record is now widely regarded as the Kinks' masterpiece and one of the greatest albums of the 1960s. A lavish 50th-anniversary box set was issued in 2018.
