Utrecht Psalter

The Utrecht Psalter is a 9th-century illuminated manuscript psalter, a key surviving work of Carolingian art, and probably the most valuable manuscript in the Netherlands. It is famous for its 166 lively ink illustrations, each accompanying a psalm, drawn in a spontaneous, animated style unusual for the period.

Description

The psalter was made, possibly in Reims, between about 816 and 835, most likely for the court of Louis the Pious. Its illustrations depict scenes from the psalms in a literal, energetic manner, with figures gesturing and landscapes sketched in swift pen strokes. The text, written in rustic capitals, includes the psalms, canticles, and other liturgical texts.

History and influence

The manuscript later came to England, where it profoundly influenced Anglo-Saxon art; copies and adaptations such as the Harley Psalter were made from it at Canterbury. It eventually entered the library of Utrecht University, where it remains. In 2015 it was included in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.
