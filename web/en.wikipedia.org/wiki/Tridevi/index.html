Tridevi

The Tridevi (Sanskrit: त्रिदेवी, "three goddesses") is a triad of supreme goddesses in Hinduism, the female counterpart to the male Trimurti. The Tridevi consists of Saraswati, goddess of knowledge, music and learning; Lakshmi, goddess of wealth and prosperity; and Parvati, goddess of power, love and devotion. They are the consorts — and are understood as the shakti (energy) — of Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer respectively.

Theology

In the Shakta tradition, the three goddesses are aspects of the single supreme goddess Adi Parashakti (Mahadevi). Saraswati corresponds to creation, Lakshmi to preservation and Parvati (as Durga/Kali in her fiercer forms) to destruction and transformation, mirroring the functions of the Trimurti. Texts such as the Devi Bhagavata Purana elaborate the doctrine that all goddesses are manifestations of one Devi.

Worship

The Tridevi is worshipped particularly during Navaratri, when nine nights of devotion are divided among the three goddesses and their nine forms (Navadurga). Temples and images of the three together are common in India, and the concept parallels triple-goddess figures in other religions.
