Mario Puzo

Mario Gianluigi Puzo (October 15, 1920 – July 2, 1999) was an American author and screenwriter. He wrote the crime novel The Godfather (1969), which became a bestseller and was adapted into the acclaimed film trilogy directed by Francis Ford Coppola. Puzo co-wrote the screenplays for the first two Godfather films, winning Academy Awards for both, and also worked on Superman: The Movie (1978).

Early life

Puzo was born in the Hell's Kitchen neighbourhood of New York City to Italian immigrants from Sicily. He served in the United States Army Air Forces in East Asia during the Second World War. After the war he studied at the New School for Social Research and Columbia University under the G.I. Bill, and worked as a civil servant while writing fiction.

Career

His first novels, The Dark Arena (1955) and The Fortunate Pilgrim (1965), were critically praised but sold poorly. In debt and wanting a commercial success, Puzo researched the Mafia for a novel about organized crime. The Godfather, published in 1969, spent 67 weeks on the bestseller list and became one of the best-selling novels of all time.

The 1972 film adaptation, co-written by Puzo and director Francis Ford Coppola, is frequently ranked among the greatest films ever made; Puzo and Coppola won the Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay, and repeated the win for The Godfather Part II (1974). Puzo also received an Oscar nomination for The Godfather Part III (1990). He wrote the original screenplay drafts for Superman (1978) and contributed to Superman II.

Later novels and death

Puzo's later novels include Fools Die (1978), The Sicilian (1984), The Fourth K (1990), The Last Don (1996), and Omertà, published posthumously in 2000. He died of heart failure on July 2, 1999, at his home in West Bay Shore, New York.
