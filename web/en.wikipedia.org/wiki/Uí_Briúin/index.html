Uí Briúin

The Uí Briúin were an Irish dynasty and ruling kindred of early medieval Ireland, who claimed descent from Brion, an elder half-brother of Niall of the Nine Hostages, the legendary ancestor of the Uí Néill. From the 7th century onward they established themselves as the dominant power in the province of Connacht in the west of Ireland, and their leading families supplied most of the kings of Connacht for the following thousand years.

Branches

The Uí Briúin divided into three major branches named for their territories:

  • Uí Briúin Aí, centred on the plain of Mag Aí around Rathcroghan in County Roscommon, who produced the ruling dynasties of the O'Connors (Uí Chonchobair), MacDermots, and O'Flanagans. The O'Connors became the pre-eminent family, and Tairrdelbach Ua Conchobair (Turlough O'Connor) and his son Ruaidrí (Rory O'Connor) rose to the High Kingship of Ireland in the 12th century.
  • Uí Briúin Bréifne, in the northwest around modern counties Leitrim and Cavan, ancestors of the O'Rourkes and O'Reillys.
  • Uí Briúin Seóla, in the region west of Lough Corrib in County Galway, ancestors of the O'Flahertys.

History

According to genealogical tradition, the Uí Briúin's ancestor Brion settled in Connacht in the 5th century. By the 690s the family's kings were competing successfully with the older Uí Fiachrach dynasty for the provincial kingship, and from the 8th century the Uí Briúin held it almost continuously. Their power survived the Viking age and the Anglo-Norman invasion, and the O'Connor kings of Connacht remained a force in the west until the late medieval period.
