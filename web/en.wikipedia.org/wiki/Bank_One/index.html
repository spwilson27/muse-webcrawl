Bank One Corporation was one of the largest bank holding companies in the United States, formed in October 1998 by the merger of Banc One Corporation of Columbus, Ohio, and First Chicago NBD Corporation. At the time of its creation it was among the five largest U.S. banks, with a major retail banking footprint across the Midwest and South and the country's largest credit card operation through its First USA subsidiary.

History

Banc One had grown from City National Bank of Columbus (founded 1868) through an aggressive programme of acquisitions under John B. McCoy, pioneering ATMs and the Visa card network (its ancestor, BankAmericard processing, passed through the Ohio bank). First Chicago NBD itself combined Chicago's First National Bank tradition with Detroit's National Bank of Detroit. The merged Bank One was chaired by McCoy, but earnings difficulties in the credit card business led to his resignation in 1999; Jamie Dimon, formerly of Citigroup, became chairman and CEO in 2000 and led a widely praised turnaround.

Merger with JPMorgan Chase

In January 2004 Bank One agreed to be acquired by JPMorgan Chase & Co. in a stock deal valued at about $58 billion, completed on 1 July 2004. The merger made Dimon president and chief operating officer of the combined firm — and, in 2006, its CEO — while the Bank One consumer and credit card operations were rebranded Chase. Many former Bank One systems and practices survived the merger, and the Columbus campus remained one of JPMorgan Chase's largest employment centres.
