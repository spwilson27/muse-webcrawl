Octahedron

In geometry, an octahedron (plural: octahedra or octahedrons) is a polyhedron with eight faces. The best-known is the regular octahedron, a Platonic solid composed of eight equilateral triangles, four of which meet at each vertex. It has 6 vertices and 12 edges.

Regular octahedron

The regular octahedron is the dual polyhedron of the cube: placing a vertex at the centre of each face of a cube and joining adjacent vertices yields an octahedron, and vice versa. Its symmetry group is the octahedral group, shared with the cube. For edge length a, the surface area is 2√3a², the volume is (√2/3)a³, and the radius of the circumscribed sphere is (√2/2)a.

Coordinates and structure

The vertices of a regular octahedron centred at the origin can be placed at (±1, 0, 0), (0, ±1, 0), and (0, 0, ±1), making it the three-dimensional analogue of the cross-polytope. It can also be seen as a square antiprism or as two square pyramids joined at their bases.

Occurrences

Octahedral geometry occurs in nature and chemistry: fluorite and magnetite crystals often grow as octahedra, and many transition-metal complexes have octahedral coordination geometry, in which six ligands surround a central metal atom. Alum crystals are a familiar classroom example. In games, the octahedron serves as the eight-sided die (d8).
