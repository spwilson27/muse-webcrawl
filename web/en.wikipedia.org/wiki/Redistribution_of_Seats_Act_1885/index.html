The Redistribution of Seats Act 1885 (48 & 49 Vict. c. 23) was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that redrew the boundaries of parliamentary constituencies to accompany the franchise extension of the Representation of the People Act 1884. Together the two acts — the product of a compromise between Gladstone's Liberal government and Lord Salisbury's Conservatives — created the framework of the modern British electoral map, based largely on equal, single-member districts.

Background

The 1884 Act extended household suffrage to the counties, adding roughly two million voters, but left the existing distribution of seats — which still reflected historic boroughs rather than population — untouched. Redistribution was negotiated between the parties, with Salisbury insisting on it as the price of letting the franchise bill pass the House of Lords; the details were worked out confidentially between Gladstone and Salisbury (through intermediaries including Sir Charles Dilke and Lord Carnarvon) in late 1884.

Provisions

The Act disenfranchised boroughs with populations under 15,000, merging them into county divisions; boroughs of 15,000–50,000 lost one of their two members; and large towns and the counties were divided into single-member divisions of roughly equal population (a target of about 50,000 inhabitants per seat). London's representation rose substantially, and new seats were created for growing industrial and suburban areas. Two-member seats survived in some medium-sized boroughs and for the universities. Overall the House of Commons grew slightly, to 670 members, of whom the great majority now sat for single-member constituencies.

Consequences

Single-member districts tied MPs closely to defined local communities, encouraged clearer two-party competition, and made suburban "villa Toryism" a durable feature of politics. The 1885 general election, the first fought on the new map, produced a Parliament in which the Irish Parliamentary Party held the balance of power, setting up the Home Rule crisis of 1886. The 1885 boundaries, with minor changes, endured until 1918.
