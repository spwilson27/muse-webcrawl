Ushba

Ushba (Georgian: უშბა) is one of the most notable peaks of the Caucasus Mountains, located in the Svaneti region of Georgia. Its distinctive double summit rises to 4,710 metres (15,453 ft) at the higher south peak, with the north peak at 4,690 metres. Ushba is nicknamed the "Matterhorn of the Caucasus" for its dramatic, steep profile.

Climbing history

The north peak was first climbed in 1888 by John Garford Cockin and Ulrich Almer. The south peak resisted attempts until 1903, when a party led by Adolf Schulze succeeded. Because of its steep granite faces and unpredictable weather, Ushba is regarded as one of the most demanding climbs in the Caucasus, and fewer ascents are recorded than for many higher peaks of the range.

Geography

The mountain stands just south of the main Caucasus watershed, near the border with Russia, above the village of Mazeri and the Enguri valley. Its glaciers feed streams that join the Enguri River.
