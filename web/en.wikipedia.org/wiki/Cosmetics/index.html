Cosmetics

Cosmetics are constituted mixtures of chemical compounds derived from either natural sources or synthetically created ones. Cosmetics have various purposes, including personal and skin care. They can also be used to conceal blemishes and enhance natural features (such as the eyebrows and eyelashes). Makeup can also add colour to a person's face, enhance a person's features or change the appearance of the face entirely to resemble a different person, creature or object.

The word cosmetics derives from the Greek kosmetikḗ tékhnē, "technique of dress and ornament". The global cosmetics industry is worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually; major manufacturers include L'Oréal, Unilever, Estée Lauder, Procter & Gamble and Shiseido.

History

Cosmetics have been used for thousands of years. Ancient Egyptians used kohl eyeliner, malachite eyeshadow and red ochre; castor oil was used as a protective balm. In ancient Rome, face whiteners contained lead — one of many historically toxic ingredients; white lead, mercury and arsenic compounds remained common in Western cosmetics into the 19th century. Regulations tightened in the 20th century: in the United States, the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act of 1938 followed scandals involving harmful products, and the European Union now bans or restricts many substances and prohibits animal testing of cosmetics.

Types

  • Decorative cosmetics (makeup) — foundation, lipstick, mascara, eyeshadow, blush, nail polish
  • Skin care — moisturisers, cleansers, sunscreens, serums
  • Hair care — shampoos, conditioners, dyes
  • Fragrances — perfumes, colognes, deodorants

Regulation and safety

Regulatory regimes differ: the EU requires pre-market safety assessment and ingredient labelling (INCI names), while US oversight is comparatively light, though the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 expanded FDA authority. Concerns include allergens, endocrine-disrupting chemicals, and "forever chemicals" (PFAS) in some products.
