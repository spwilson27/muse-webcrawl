arXiv (pronounced "archive" — the X represents the Greek letter chi, χ) is a free, open-access repository of electronic preprints (e-prints) in physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and systems science, and economics. It has transformed scholarly communication in these fields, making most new research freely available months before (or instead of) journal publication.

History

arXiv began in 1991 at Los Alamos National Laboratory as an email and FTP service (xxx.lanl.gov) created by physicist Paul Ginsparg to circulate preprints in high-energy physics, replacing the costly postal exchange of paper preprints. It moved with Ginsparg to Cornell University in 2001 and is now operated by Cornell Tech with support from member institutions and the Simons Foundation. Submissions passed two million in the early 2020s. Grigori Perelman famously posted his proof of the Poincaré conjecture only on arXiv, never submitting it to a journal.

Operation

Submissions are screened by moderators for topicality and obvious problems but are not peer reviewed; new authors may need endorsement from established users. Papers receive permanent identifiers (such as arXiv:0704.0001) and version numbers, and authors may post revised versions and journal references after publication. Most papers also appear later in peer-reviewed journals, but arXiv itself is a distribution service, not a journal.

Impact and criticism

arXiv pioneered the preprint culture now spreading through other disciplines via servers such as bioRxiv and medRxiv. Criticisms include the lack of peer review (papers are sometimes misreported in the press as established findings), moderation decisions, and the challenges of sustaining funding for a free service at very large scale.
