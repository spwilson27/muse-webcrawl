Church's Chicken is an American chain of fast food restaurants specialising in fried chicken, marketed outside the United States as Church's Texas Chicken (and Texas Chicken in some markets). Founded in San Antonio, Texas, in 1952 by George W. Church Sr., it became one of the largest fried chicken chains in the world, with roughly 1,500 franchised and company-operated restaurants in more than 25 countries.

[Image: A Church's Chicken restaurant exterior]

History

George W. Church Sr., a retired incubator salesman, opened Church's Fried Chicken To Go on April 17, 1952, in downtown San Antonio, across from the Alamo. The first store sold only fried chicken; french fries and jalapeños were added in 1955. The chain grew through franchising under Church's son, George W. "Bill" Church Jr., and went public in 1969.

After a series of ownership changes — including a merger with Popeyes in 1989 under Al Copeland (later separated) and ownership by AFC Enterprises, Arcapita and the private-equity firm Friedman Fleischer & Lowe — Church's was acquired in 2021 by High Bluff Capital Partners. Headquarters moved from San Antonio to Atlanta in 2005.

Products and markets

The menu centres on hand-battered fried chicken, honey-butter biscuits, fried okra, corn on the cob and jalapeño peppers. Internationally the chain operates in countries including Canada, Mexico, Russia (historically), Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and markets in the Middle East and Caribbean, in several of which the pork-free, halal-oriented menu of Texas Chicken differs from the US offering.
