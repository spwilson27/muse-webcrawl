Micius (Chinese: 墨子號), formally the Quantum Experiments at Space Scale (QUESS) mission, is a Chinese space science satellite launched on August 16, 2016 — the world's first quantum communications satellite. Operated by the Chinese Academy of Sciences under chief scientist Pan Jianwei, it was named after the ancient Chinese philosopher and scientist Mozi (Latinised as Micius), who is credited in Chinese tradition with early experiments in optics.

Mission

The satellite carries an entangled-photon source and telescopes that beam quantum signals to optical ground stations. Because quantum states cannot be cloned and are destroyed by measurement, distributing entangled pairs from orbit allows two distant ground stations to share cryptographic keys whose security is guaranteed by physics — satellite links avoid the exponential signal loss that limits ground-based optical fibre to a few hundred kilometres.

Achievements

In 2017 the Micius team reported a series of landmark results in Science and Nature: distribution of entangled photon pairs to ground stations separated by up to 1,203 km (Delingha and Lijiang), smashing previous records; satellite-to-ground quantum key distribution; and ground-to-satellite quantum teleportation of single-photon states over distances up to 1,400 km. In 2017 a secure intercontinental quantum-encrypted video call was demonstrated between Beijing and Vienna using Micius keys. In 2020 the mission achieved entanglement-based quantum key distribution over 1,120 km without trusted relays.

Significance

Micius made China the clear early leader in space-based quantum communication and spurred similar programmes in Europe, the United States, and elsewhere; it is regarded as a foundational step toward a future global quantum internet.
