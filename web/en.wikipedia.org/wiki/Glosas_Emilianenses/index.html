The Glosas Emilianenses (Spanish for "glosses of [San] Millán") are a set of handwritten annotations — interlinear and marginal glosses — in a Latin codex from the monastery of San Millán de la Cogolla in La Rioja, Spain. Written mainly in an early form of Ibero-Romance (Navarro-Aragonese or early Castilian), with two glosses in Basque, they are traditionally regarded as the earliest surviving written examples of both Spanish and Basque.

Content

The glosses explain difficult Latin words and phrases in the vernacular for monks reading the text. The longest is a prayer beginning "Cono aiutorio de nuestro dueno Christo, dueno salbatore..." ("With the help of our lord Christ, lord saviour..."). The Basque glosses, izioqui dugu and guec ajutu ez dugu, are usually interpreted as "we have not deserved it" and "we have not been helped".

Dating debate

The manuscript's codex dates from the ninth or tenth century, but scholars now generally place the glosses themselves in the late eleventh century rather than the traditionally claimed year 977. Their symbolic importance as the "cradle of Spanish" (cuna del castellano) nonetheless remains central to San Millán's identity, and the monasteries of San Millán are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
