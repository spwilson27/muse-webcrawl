Derivation (linguistics)

In linguistics, derivation is the process of forming a new word on the basis of an existing word, e.g. happiness from happy, or unhappy from happy. It is often, though not always, accomplished by means of a derivational affix: a bound morpheme added before, after, or within a base word. Derived words frequently differ in word class from their bases: for instance, the suffix -ness forms nouns from adjectives, and the prefix un- reverses meaning.

Derivational patterns

Derivational morphology often involves a change in the syntactic category or in the type of meaning of a word, and it can apply repeatedly, though the productivity of individual patterns varies: some affixes, such as English -ness and re-, are highly productive, while others survive only in a handful of words. Derivation is distinguished from inflection, which produces grammatical variants of the same lexeme without changing its core lexical identity.
