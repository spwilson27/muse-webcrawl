Amoghavarsha I (r. c. 814–878 CE) was a Rashtrakuta emperor of India, one of the longest-reigning and most celebrated rulers of early medieval India. Succeeding his father Govinda III while still a youth, he reigned for more than six decades from his capital at Manyakheta in the Deccan, in present-day Karnataka.

Reign

Amoghavarsha's long reign included campaigns against the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi and conflicts with the Pratiharas to the north, interspersed with long periods of peace during which the Rashtrakuta realm enjoyed prosperity. He is credited with founding or developing the capital city of Manyakheta, which remained the Rashtrakuta seat of power for the rest of the dynasty's history.

Religion and literature

Amoghavarsha was a devout Jain in his later years, influenced by the monk Jinasena, while continuing to patronise Hindu institutions. He is traditionally credited with authoring or sponsoring the Kavirajamarga (c. 850), the earliest surviving work on poetics in the Kannada language, and the Sanskrit work Prashnottara Ratnamalika is also attributed to him. His court made the Rashtrakuta empire a centre of Kannada and Sanskrit literature.

Legacy

Historians rank Amoghavarsha among the great monarchs of ancient India; the Arab traveller Sulaiman praised the Rashtrakuta king of his day as one of the four great sovereigns of the world. His reign is remembered for religious tolerance, administrative stability and literary patronage.
