World Aerobatic Championships

The World Aerobatic Championships (WAC) is the premier international competition in powered aerobatics, sanctioned by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) and its aerobatics commission, CIVA. Held every two years since 1960, it crowns world champions in the Unlimited category for individual men, women and teams.

Competition

Pilots fly a series of programmes: a known compulsory sequence, free sequences of their own design, and "unknown" sequences provided shortly before flight, all within a marked aerobatic box 1,000 metres on a side. Figures are drawn from the Aresti catalogue and scored by judges for precision, with difficulty coefficients (K-factors) weighting each manoeuvre. A separate four-minute freestyle programme rewards showmanship and innovation.

History

The first championships were held in Bratislava, Czechoslovakia, in 1960. Soviet pilots dominated early decades with aircraft such as the Yak-18 and later Sukhoi designs; in more recent eras French, Russian, German and American pilots have prevailed flying aircraft like the Extra 300, Sukhoi Su-26/Su-31 and MXS. Mikhail Mamistov of Russia and French pilots such as Renaud Ecalle and Aude Lemordant are among the most successful Unlimited champions.
