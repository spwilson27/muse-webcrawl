Yorkshire is a historic county in Northern England and the largest historic county in the United Kingdom. Because of its size, functions once exercised by the county as a whole were historically administered by its three ridings — the East Riding, North Riding, and West Riding — whose names derive from the Old Norse þriðjungr, meaning "third part". The county town is York, and its emblem is the white rose of the House of York.

Geography

Yorkshire's landscape is famously varied. The Pennine hills run down its western side; the Yorkshire Dales, designated a National Park in 1954, are a landscape of limestone valleys, dry-stone walls, and waterfalls. To the east lie the North York Moors, a high heather moorland plateau, and the chalk hills of the Yorkshire Wolds. The low-lying Vale of York and the Humberhead Levels occupy the centre and south. The North Sea coast includes the resorts of Scarborough and Whitby and the high cliffs of Flamborough Head.

History

The area was part of the Celtic kingdom of Elmet and then of Anglian Northumbria before falling to Viking settlers, who made York (Jórvík) a great trading city. After the Norman Conquest the county was devastated in the Harrying of the North (1069–70). In the Middle Ages Yorkshire's monasteries — Fountains, Rievaulx, and Kirkstall among them — grew rich on the wool trade. The Wars of the Roses took their name partly from the rival houses of York and Lancaster. During the Industrial Revolution the West Riding's woollen and worsted mills, Sheffield's steel and cutlery, and the coalfields of the south powered enormous urban growth in Leeds, Bradford, Sheffield, and Halifax.

Modern administration

Local government reforms, especially those of 1974, abolished the old ridings as administrative units and created the modern metropolitan and ceremonial counties of West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, North Yorkshire, and the East Riding of Yorkshire, with parts of historic Yorkshire passing to neighbouring counties. Yorkshire Day is celebrated on 1 August, the anniversary of the Battle of Minden and of the emancipation of slaves in the British Empire, championed by the Yorkshire MP William Wilberforce.

Culture

Yorkshire has a strong regional identity and a distinctive dialect. It is known for Yorkshire pudding, Wensleydale cheese, and parkin; for cricket (Yorkshire County Cricket Club, historically the most successful English county side); and for writers including the Brontë sisters of Haworth, Bram Stoker (who set part of Dracula in Whitby), Ted Hughes, and Alan Bennett. The county hosted the Grand Départ of the Tour de France in 2014.
