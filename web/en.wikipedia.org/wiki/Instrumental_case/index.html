Instrumental case

The instrumental case is a grammatical case used to indicate that a noun is the instrument or means by or with which the subject achieves or accomplishes an action. The noun may be a physical object, as in "written with a pen", or a more abstract concept.

Usage

The instrumental case appears in languages including Russian, Polish, Czech, Serbian, Sanskrit, Latin (where it merged into the ablative), and classical Armenian. In Russian, the instrumental answers the questions kem? ("with whom?") and čem? ("with what?"). It is also used for predicate nouns in some constructions, for the agent in passive clauses, and after certain prepositions.

Examples

In the English sentence "I wrote the letter with a pencil", the phrase "with a pencil" corresponds to the instrumental. In Russian, "perom" (пером) in "pisat' perom" ("to write with a pen") is in the instrumental case.

Related forms

Some languages express the same meaning with an instrumental-comitative case, combining the instrument and accompaniment senses, while others use prepositions instead of a dedicated case.
