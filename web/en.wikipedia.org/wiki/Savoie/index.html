Savoie (French pronunciation: [savwa]; Arpitan: Savouè) is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of south-eastern France, bordering Italy. Its prefecture is Chambéry. Together with the neighbouring department of Haute-Savoie, it forms the historical region of Savoy, which belonged to the Kingdom of Sardinia until its annexation by France in 1860 under the Treaty of Turin, confirmed by a plebiscite. The department's name comes from the Latin Sapaudia.

Geography

Savoie is one of the most mountainous departments of France: much of its territory lies in the French Alps, including part of the Mont Blanc massif's southern approaches, the Vanoise massif (protected by the Vanoise National Park, created in 1963), and major ski areas such as Val d'Isère, Tignes, Courchevel, Méribel, and Les Arcs. The Isère river and its tributary the Arc drain the great valleys of Tarentaise and Maurienne. Only the west of the department, around Chambéry and the Lac du Bourget — France's largest natural lake of glacial origin — is lowland.

History

Savoy was the original homeland of the House of Savoy, which ruled from the 11th century and eventually became the royal house of Italy. France occupied Savoy repeatedly before the definitive annexation of 1860, negotiated by Cavour and Napoleon III at Plombières in 1858 as the price of French support for Italian unification against Austria. The department of Savoie was created from the southern part of the annexed territory. The 1992 Winter Olympics were held in Albertville, spurring major infrastructure works including new motorways and the upgrading of ski stations.

Economy and culture

Tourism, especially winter sports and the thermal resorts of Aix-les-Bains and Brides-les-Bains, dominates the economy, alongside aluminium and chemical industries historically powered by hydroelectricity ("white coal"), and agriculture producing wines such as Apremont and cheeses including Beaufort and Reblochon (shared with Haute-Savoie). The traditional language is Savoyard, a dialect of Franco-Provençal (Arpitan), now spoken by a small minority.
