Fawlty Towers

Fawlty Towers is a British television sitcom written by John Cleese and Connie Booth, broadcast on BBC Two in 1975 and 1979. Two series of six episodes each were made. The show is set in a fictional hotel in the seaside town of Torquay on the "English Riviera" and follows the misadventures of its rude, disaster-prone owner Basil Fawlty, played by Cleese.

Premise

Basil Fawlty is a snobbish, misanthropic hotelier who aspires to raise the social tone of his establishment but is constantly thwarted by incompetent staff, impossible guests and his own temper. His formidable wife Sybil (Prunella Scales) keeps the hotel running, the put-upon Spanish waiter Manuel (Andrew Sachs), famous for the catchphrase "I know nothing", provides much of the physical comedy, and chambermaid Polly (Connie Booth) acts as the voice of reason.

Background

The character of Basil was inspired by Donald Sinclair, the proprietor of the Gleneagles Hotel in Torquay, where Cleese stayed with the other members of Monty Python while filming in 1970. Sinclair's brusque manner made a lasting impression. Although only twelve episodes were produced, the series is widely regarded as one of the greatest British sitcoms of all time; in a 2000 poll by the British Film Institute it was ranked first in the list of the 100 Greatest British Television Programmes, and the episode "The Germans" is particularly celebrated.

Legacy

The show has been remade in several countries and remains a touchstone of British comedy. In 2023 it was announced that Cleese was developing a revival with his daughter Camilla Cleese.
