Art model

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A model posing for a life drawing class

An art model is a person who poses, often nude, in part or in whole, for an artist as part of the creative process, serving as a visual reference for the human figure in a work of art. The act of posing for an artist is known as art modelling.

Art models are often paid professionals employed by art schools and classes, or by individual artists. Although commercial motives dominate over aesthetics in some types of illustration and photography, art models for life drawing are typically engaged to provide a visual reference for the human body rather than for erotic content.

History

The use of models in art dates to antiquity: in ancient Egypt and Greece, artists studied the human form from living subjects, and the practice became central to Western art training during the Renaissance, when life drawing from nude models became a standard part of academic instruction. In the 19th century, art schools in Paris and London institutionalized the life class, though female models and female students alike faced social controversy.

Posing

Poses range from short "gesture" poses of one to five minutes, used for warm-up sketches, to sustained poses held, with breaks, for hours or weeks for paintings and sculpture. Professional models are expected to hold still, return accurately to a pose after breaks, and devise expressive poses suitable for the class.
