Cappella Colleoni

LocationBergamo, Lombardy, Italy
TypeMausoleum chapel
Built1472–1476
ArchitectGiovanni Antonio Amadeo
Dedicated toSaints Bartholomew, Mark and John the Baptist

The Cappella Colleoni (Colleoni Chapel) is a church and mausoleum in Bergamo, northern Italy, attached to the Basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore. It was built between 1472 and 1476 as the funerary chapel of the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni, captain-general of the Republic of Venice, and is regarded as a masterpiece of early Renaissance architecture and sculpture in Lombardy.

History

Colleoni, who had demolished the basilica's sacristy to clear the site — an act the Venetian authorities had authorised but which local tradition long treated as high-handed — commissioned the chapel from the sculptor-architect Giovanni Antonio Amadeo. Colleoni died in 1475 and was buried in the chapel, though his sarcophagus was found empty during an investigation in 1969; his remains were eventually located under a different tomb slab in the same chapel.

Architecture

The facade is faced with polychrome marble inlay in white, pink, and black lozenges, with a rose window flanked by medallions of Julius Caesar and Trajan. The octagonal dome is crowned by a lantern. Inside, the chapel contains Colleoni's funerary monument by Amadeo — a sarcophagus with reliefs from the life of Christ, surmounted by a gilded wooden equestrian statue of the condottiero — and the tomb of his daughter Medea, who predeceased him. The presbytery holds an altarpiece by Bartolomeo Vivarini, and in the 18th century Giambattista Tiepolo added frescoes of the lives of the patron saints to the pendentives.
