Panamá Viejo (English: Old Panama) is the remaining part of the old Panama City, founded on 15 August 1519 by Pedro Arias Dávila. It was the first permanent European settlement on the Pacific Ocean and became the starting point of expeditions that conquered the Inca Empire, as well as a key transshipment point for Peruvian silver and gold bound for Spain via the Camino Real and the port of Nombre de Dios.

Destruction

On 28 January 1671 the Welsh privateer Henry Morgan attacked the city with some 1,200 men. During the battle a fire — its origin disputed between Morgan's men and a Spanish decision to blow up the powder magazines — destroyed the city, which was never rebuilt on the site. Panama City was refounded in 1673 on a peninsula about 8 km to the southwest, the present Casco Antiguo.

The site today

The ruins, covering about 28 hectares, include the cathedral tower, churches of several religious orders, convents, a hospital, bridges, and fortifications. Together with the Historic District of Panama City, Panamá Viejo was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997.
