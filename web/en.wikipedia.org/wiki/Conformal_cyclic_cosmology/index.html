Conformal cyclic cosmology

In CCC, the universe cycles through an infinite sequence of aeons.

Conformal cyclic cosmology (CCC) is a cosmological model in the framework of general relativity, advanced by the theoretical and mathematical physicist Roger Penrose. In CCC, the universe iterates through an infinite series of cycles, known as aeons, in which the future timelike infinity of each previous aeon is identified with the Big Bang singularity of the next.

Penrose set out the model in his 2010 book Cycles of Time: An Extraordinary New View of the Universe. Because the remote future of an expanding universe (dominated by massless radiation after all matter has decayed or fallen into black holes that then evaporate) is conformally equivalent to a Big Bang–like beginning, the boundary between aeons can be crossed by a conformal rescaling of the metric.

Predictions and evidence

CCC predicts that gravitational-wave signals from collisions of supermassive black holes in the previous aeon could appear as concentric circular patterns of temperature variation in the cosmic microwave background. Penrose and collaborators have also proposed "Hawking points" — spots of anomalous radiation attributed to the evaporation of black holes from a prior aeon. These claims remain controversial, and other researchers have argued that similar features can arise within the standard ΛCDM model and inflationary cosmology.
