EDTA

Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid
FormulaC10H16N2O8
Molar mass292.24 g/mol
AppearanceColourless crystals
TypeAminopolycarboxylic acid; chelating agent
First described1935, Ferdinand Münz

Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) is an aminopolycarboxylic acid with the formula [CH2N(CH2CO2H)2]2. This white, water-soluble solid is widely used as a chelating agent: it binds metal ions such as Ca2+ and Fe3+ in an octahedral complex, sequestering them so they cannot react as usual.

Uses

EDTA was first synthesised in 1935 by Ferdinand Münz at IG Farben, who sought a substitute for citric acid that would bind calcium in textile processing. Today the compound is produced on a scale of hundreds of thousands of tonnes per year. Industrial uses include water softening and detergents, pulp and paper bleaching, and food preservation. In medicine, calcium disodium EDTA is an approved treatment for lead poisoning, and EDTA is the standard anticoagulant in lavender-top blood collection tubes for haematology. It is also used in laboratories as a buffer component and to inhibit metal-dependent enzymes such as DNases.

Environmental behaviour

Because EDTA biodegrades only slowly, it is one of the most abundant synthetic organic compounds found in rivers; photolysis of its iron complex and newer biodegradable alternatives such as EDDS are researched as mitigations. Its strong metal binding can remobilise toxic metals from sediments, though it can also reduce metal bioavailability.
