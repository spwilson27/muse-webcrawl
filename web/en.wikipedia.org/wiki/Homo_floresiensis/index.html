Homo floresiensis

Homo floresiensis is an extinct species of small archaic human that inhabited the island of Flores in present-day Indonesia until the arrival of modern humans about 50,000 years ago. Its remains were discovered in 2003 in the Liang Bua cave; individuals stood about 1.1 m (3 ft 7 in) tall, leading to the popular nickname "the Hobbit" after the fictional characters of J. R. R. Tolkien.

Discovery

The first remains were unearthed in September 2003 by a joint Indonesian-Australian team excavating Liang Bua, a limestone cave on Flores. The nearly complete skeleton of a female, designated LB1, was described in 2004. Additional specimens, including a second tiny skeleton at Mata Menge, suggested an ancient population of small-bodied hominins on the island.

Anatomy

Despite a brain volume of roughly 380–417 cm³ — comparable to a chimpanzee's — H. floresiensis made and used stone tools and hunted animals such as the dwarf elephant Stegodon. Its small stature is attributed to insular dwarfism, an evolutionary response to limited island resources. Wrist bones, feet and shoulders retained primitive features unlike those of modern humans.

Classification and debate

Some researchers proposed that LB1 was simply a modern human with a pathology such as microcephaly, but most specialists regard the combined evidence as supporting a distinct species, probably descended from Homo erectus or an even earlier lineage. Stone tools at Soa Basin dated to about a million years ago suggest hominins reached Flores long before.
