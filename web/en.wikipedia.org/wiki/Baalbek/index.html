Baalbek

Baalbek
Country: Lebanon
Governorate: Baalbek-Hermel
Location: Beqaa Valley
Known for: Roman temples of Jupiter and Bacchus
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1984

Baalbek is a city in the Beqaa Valley of Lebanon, famous for its monumental Roman temple complex. Known in the Roman period as Heliopolis, it contains some of the largest and best-preserved Roman ruins in the world, including the Temple of Jupiter and the remarkably intact Temple of Bacchus.

History

The site was a Phoenician religious centre dedicated to Baal before Hellenistic and Roman rebuilding on a colossal scale from the 1st century BC. The Temple of Jupiter's podium incorporates megalithic stones, including the trilithon blocks weighing hundreds of tonnes, and the nearby quarry holds the unfinished Stone of the Pregnant Woman.

Modern city

Today Baalbek is a predominantly Shia Muslim city, a stronghold of Hezbollah, and hosts the Baalbeck International Festival of music and theatre among the ruins. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.
