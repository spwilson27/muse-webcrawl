Noble train of artillery

Noble train of artillery
Date: November 1775 – January 1776
Commander: Henry Knox
Cargo: ~60 tons of artillery
Result: Siege of Boston broken

The noble train of artillery was the expedition arranged by Colonel Henry Knox in the winter of 1775–76 to transport artillery captured at Fort Ticonderoga and Crown Point to the Continental Army besieging Boston. It is also known as the Knox Expedition.

Background

In May 1775, Ethan Allen and Benedict Arnold captured Fort Ticonderoga, gaining control of a large store of cannon and mortars. George Washington, commanding the siege of Boston, approved Knox's proposal to haul the guns overland to Massachusetts. Knox, a 25-year-old former Boston bookseller, reached Ticonderoga in November 1775 and selected 59 pieces — cannon, mortars, and howitzers weighing nearly 60 tons in total.

The journey

Using boats on Lake George and then ox-drawn sleds over snow-covered roads through the Berkshire Mountains and across Massachusetts, Knox's party covered roughly 300 miles in about ten weeks, contending with thin ice, thaws, and blizzards. The guns reached the Boston area by late January 1776.

Outcome

In early March 1776, Washington fortified Dorchester Heights overnight and emplaced the Ticonderoga cannon there, threatening the British position and fleet. General William Howe evacuated Boston on 17 March 1776, a date still celebrated as Evacuation Day in Suffolk County, Massachusetts.
