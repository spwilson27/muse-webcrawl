Hahnenkamm Races

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A racer on the Streif, one of the most feared downhills in the world.

The Hahnenkamm Races (German: Hahnenkammrennen) are annual alpine ski races held since 1931 in Kitzbühel, Austria, on the Hahnenkamm mountain above the town. They form one of the most prestigious weekends of the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup, headlined by the men's downhill on the Streif course and a slalom on the Ganslernhang. The combined events attract tens of thousands of spectators to Kitzbühel each January.

The Streif

The Streif downhill is considered the most demanding course on the World Cup circuit. Starting at 1,665 m, racers plunge down 3.3 km with an average gradient of about 27% and pitches — such as the Mausefalle ("mousetrap") near the start — of up to 85% (40 degrees), reaching speeds of 140 km/h. Notorious sections include the Steilhang, the Alte Schneise, and the Hausbergkante jump before the finishing schuss. Winning the Hahnenkamm downhill is, for many racers, second in prestige only to Olympic or world championship gold.

History

Races were first held on the Hahnenkamm in 1931, organised by the Kitzbühel Ski Club (founded 1902). After interruptions during the Second World War, the event grew into a fixture of the World Cup from the circuit's founding in 1967. Austrian heroes such as Karl Schranz made the race a national institution; Didier Cuche of Switzerland won the downhill a record five times. The weekend also traditionally includes a super-G and a slalom, with a combined trophy for the overall Hahnenkamm champion.

Notable incidents

The Streif's difficulty has produced severe crashes — among the most remembered are those of Hans Grugger (2011) and Daniel Albrecht (2009), both left in induced comas, though both survived. The course has been progressively re-profiled and netted, yet retains its reputation as ski racing's ultimate test of nerve.
