Olympic Stadium (London)

The Olympic Stadium in Stratford, London — since 2016 officially known for sponsorship and tenancy purposes as the London Stadium — is a multi-purpose stadium in the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park. Built between 2008 and 2011, it was the centerpiece stadium of the 2012 Summer Olympic and Paralympic Games, hosting the athletics events and the opening and closing ceremonies.

History

The stadium was designed by Populous with a capacity of about 80,000 for the Games, including a demountable upper tier intended to allow reduction to 25,000 seats afterward. It also hosted the 2015 Rugby World Cup matches, the 2017 World Athletics Championships, Major League Baseball's London Series, and concerts. After prolonged negotiations, West Ham United became the anchor tenant in 2016 under a 99-year lease, with capacity reconfigured to roughly 60,000 for football; the conversion costs and lease terms were the subject of sustained public controversy.

Design

The stadium sits on an island site between waterways in the Lower Lea Valley. Its design emphasized demountability and sustainability, using lightweight steel and recycled materials; the distinctive wrap around the exterior was made from surplus gas pipeline material. Since 2022 the venue has officially been named the London Stadium, with West Ham and UK Athletics as principal users.
