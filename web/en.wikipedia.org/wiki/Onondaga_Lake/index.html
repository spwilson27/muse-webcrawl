Onondaga Lake

Onondaga Lake is a lake in Central New York, northwest of and adjacent to the city of Syracuse. The lake is 4.6 miles (7.4 km) long, about a mile wide, and covers roughly 3,000 acres. It holds deep cultural significance for the Onondaga Nation, on whose traditional territory it lies, and tradition holds that the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy was founded on its shores.

Pollution and cleanup

During the 19th and 20th centuries the lake became one of the most polluted in the United States, contaminated by industrial waste — notably mercury and chemical by-products from soda ash production — and municipal sewage; swimming was banned in 1940 and fishing in 1970. Declared a federal Superfund site in 1994, the lake has undergone large-scale remediation, including dredging and capping of contaminated sediments completed in the 2010s, with water quality and wildlife gradually recovering.

Recreation

Onondaga Lake Park, along the eastern shore, features trails and the Salt Museum; bald eagles now winter at the lake, a widely cited symbol of its recovery.
