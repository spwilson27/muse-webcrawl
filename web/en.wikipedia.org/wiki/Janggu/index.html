Janggu

Hangul: 장구

Classification: Percussion (membranophone)

Origin: Korea

The janggu (Korean: 장구) is a traditional Korean hourglass-shaped drum. It is the most widely used drum in traditional Korean music and is used in a variety of contexts, including folk music (pungmul), court music (aak), and as an accompaniment for dance and song.

Construction

The janggu has a hollow wooden body shaped like an hourglass, with two drumheads made of animal skin — typically a thicker skin on the left side and a thinner skin on the right. The heads are held in place by leather straps that can be adjusted to change the pitch. The drum is played with two sticks: a thicker stick (gungchae) for the left side, played by hand or with the stick, and a thinner stick (yeolchae) for the right side.

Use

The janggu is a central instrument in pungmulnori (farmers' dance), samulnori, and ritual music. Its distinctive rhythms and timbres encode regional styles and form the rhythmic backbone of much Korean traditional music. The instrument was introduced to Korea from China during the Goryeo dynasty, where it evolved from the Chinese yaogu.
