Jewish Quarter (Prague)

The Jewish Quarter (Czech: Josefov) is a town quarter and the smallest cadastral area of Prague, in the Czech Republic. It lies between the Old Town Square and the Vltava river and preserves one of the most important ensembles of Jewish heritage in Europe, including six synagogues, the Old Jewish Cemetery and the Jewish Town Hall.

A street in Josefov, with the Old-New Synagogue in the distance.

History

Jews are documented in Prague from the 10th century, and a ghetto developed in the area from the 13th century. Under Emperor Joseph II, the quarter was renamed Josefov in his honour, and the walls were demolished in the 19th century. Between 1893 and 1913 much of the old quarter was cleared in a slum-clearance programme, but the main synagogues and the cemetery were spared. The community was devastated by the Holocaust; the surviving monuments were preserved in part because the Nazis intended them as a "Museum of an Extinct Race".

Sights

The Old-New Synagogue (c. 1270) is the oldest active synagogue in Europe. The Old Jewish Cemetery, used from the early 15th century to 1787, contains some 12,000 visible tombstones layered over many burials. Other sites include the Spanish Synagogue, the Pinkas Synagogue — now a memorial to the Czech victims of the Holocaust — the Maisel Synagogue and the Jewish Museum, founded in 1906.

Today

Josefov is now one of Prague's most visited districts, and its synagogues and museum are administered by the Jewish Museum in Prague. A small Jewish community still worships in the Old-New Synagogue.
