Stewart's theorem

Stewart's theorem gives a relation between the lengths of the sides of a triangle and the length of a cevian — a line segment from a vertex to the opposite side. It was published by the Scottish mathematician Matthew Stewart in 1746.

Statement

For a triangle with sides a, b, c, and a cevian of length d drawn to side a, dividing a into segments of lengths m and n (with m adjacent to side c and n adjacent to side b), the theorem states: b²m + c²n = a(d² + mn). A common mnemonic is "a man and his dad put a bomb in the sink": man + dad = bomb + sink.

Proof and applications

The result follows from applying the law of cosines to the two subtriangles and eliminating the angle term. Special cases give the length of a median (Apollonius's theorem) and of an angle bisector.
