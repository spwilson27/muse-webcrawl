Biostatistics

Biostatistics (also called biometry) is the development and application of statistical methods to a wide range of topics in biology. It encompasses the design of biological experiments, the collection and analysis of data from those experiments and the interpretation of the results. Biostatistics is central to medicine and public health, where it underpins clinical trials, epidemiological studies, genetics and health policy.

History

The field emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries from the work of Francis Galton, Karl Pearson and Ronald Fisher, who founded the journal Biometrika and developed methods such as correlation, regression, and the analysis of variance, largely motivated by questions of heredity and evolution. The randomised controlled trial, developed in the mid-twentieth century, became the standard for evaluating medical treatments.

Applications

Biostatisticians design clinical trials and analyse their results to determine the safety and efficacy of drugs and treatments. In epidemiology they model disease spread and identify risk factors. In genetics they analyse high-throughput data from genome-wide association studies and sequencing experiments. Other areas include survival analysis, longitudinal studies, spatial statistics for disease mapping, and increasingly the analysis of very large health datasets using machine learning.

Education and practice

Biostatistics is taught in schools of public health and statistics departments; practitioners collaborate with clinicians and biologists in universities, hospitals, government agencies such as the FDA and CDC, and the pharmaceutical industry. Software such as R, SAS and Stata is widely used.
