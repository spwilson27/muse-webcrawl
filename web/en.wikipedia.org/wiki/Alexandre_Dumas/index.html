Alexandre Dumas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Alexandre Dumas père: larger than life, wild grey hair, at a desk piled with manuscripts and a feast beside them]

Alexandre Dumas (born Dumas Davy de la Pailleterie, 24 July 1802 – 5 December 1870), also known as Alexandre Dumas père, was a French novelist and playwright of Haitian descent. He is one of the most widely read French authors in the world. Many of his historical novels of adventure were originally published as serials, including The Count of Monte Cristo, The Three Musketeers, Twenty Years After and The Vicomte of Bragelonne: Ten Years Later.

Grandson of a French marquis and an enslaved Haitian woman, and son of a Revolutionary general, Dumas conquered Paris first with Romantic drama (Henri III et sa cour, 1829) and then with feuilleton novels produced at industrial pace with collaborators, notably Auguste Maquet. His swashbuckling tales of d'Artagnan and of Edmond Dantès' revenge have never been out of fashion; in 2002 his ashes were transferred to the Panthéon.
