Navy bean

The navy bean, also called the haricot bean, pea bean or Boston bean, is a small, white, oval variety of the common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris). It takes its name from its use as a staple ration in the United States Navy in the early twentieth century.

Culinary uses

Navy beans are the traditional bean for Boston baked beans and are used in Senate bean soup, served daily in the United States Senate dining room. Because they break down when cooked, they are widely used in soups, stews and purées, and are the usual bean in commercial baked beans in several countries.

Cultivation

The beans are grown in temperate regions including the Great Lakes states of the United States and parts of Canada. They are harvested dry and valued for their mild flavour, dense texture and ability to absorb the flavours of other ingredients.
