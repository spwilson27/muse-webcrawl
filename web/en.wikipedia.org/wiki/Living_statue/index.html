Living statue

A living statue is a form of street performance in which a performer poses as a statue or mannequin, usually with realistic statue-like makeup, sometimes for hours at a time. When a passer-by tips the performer, the "statue" often responds with a small movement, pose change or interaction.

History

The art has roots in the tableau vivant ("living picture") tradition of theatre and pageantry, in which costumed actors posed silently in scenes from art or history. Modern living statues became a familiar feature of European city centres, festivals and tourist districts from the late 20th century; the World Living Statues Festival has been held in Arnhem, Netherlands, since 1996.

Technique

Performers use metallic or stone-effect body paint and costume, controlled breathing and muscular stillness to maintain the illusion. Many incorporate a plinth, props and a collection hat or tin; a common convention is that the statue only moves when a coin is dropped. The work demands considerable stamina, and performers may hold poses in extreme heat or cold.
