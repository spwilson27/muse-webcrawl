The Bōsō Peninsula (房総半島, Bōsō-hantō) is a peninsula that projects from the main island of Honshu into the Pacific Ocean in Chiba Prefecture, Japan. It forms the eastern and southern edge of Tokyo Bay, separating the bay from the open Pacific, and faces the Miura Peninsula across the Uraga Channel.

Geography

The interior of the peninsula is dominated by the Bōsō Hills, a range of low mountains of which Mount Atago, at 408 metres, is the highest point in Chiba Prefecture. The Pacific coast is known for its sandy beaches and surf spots around Katsuura and Onjuku, while the bay coast is more industrialised in the north, forming part of the Keiyō industrial zone. Cape Nojima at the southern tip is the southernmost point of the prefecture.

History

The peninsula historically comprised the provinces of Awa in the south, Kazusa in the centre, and part of Shimōsa in the north — the name "Bōsō" combines characters read from Awa and the two "-usa/-ōsa" provinces. The region was struck by the 1703 Genroku earthquake and its tsunami, and coastal defences were built during the Edo period against foreign ships. In the late Edo and Meiji periods, the peninsula was a significant whaling and sardine-fishing area.

Transport and tourism

The Tokyo Bay Aqua-Line, a bridge–tunnel combination opened in 1997, links Kisarazu on the peninsula with Kawasaki across the bay, tying the region closely to Tokyo. The peninsula is a popular weekend destination for its beaches, flower fields, hot springs, and the preserved Edo-period townscape of Sawara nearby, as well as Minamibōsō Quasi-National Park along its southern coast.
