Eugene Stoner

Eugene Morrison Stoner (November 22, 1922 – April 24, 1997) was an American firearms designer who is most associated with the development of the AR-15 rifle, adopted by the United States military as the M16. He is generally regarded, along with John Browning, Mikhail Kalashnikov and John Garand, as one of the most successful and influential small-arms designers of the twentieth century.

Career

Stoner served in the United States Marine Corps during the Second World War, working on aircraft ordnance, an experience that familiarised him with aluminium alloys and other aircraft materials. After the war he worked in the aviation industry before joining the newly formed ArmaLite company in 1954 as chief engineer.

At ArmaLite, Stoner applied aircraft practice — forged aluminium receivers, fibreglass furniture and a straight-line layout that directed recoil into the shoulder — to small arms. His AR-10, chambered in 7.62×51mm NATO, lost the United States Army rifle trials to the M14, but in 1957 he scaled the design down to the new .223 Remington cartridge, producing the AR-15. Colt acquired the rights and sold the rifle to the military, which standardised it as the M16 during the Vietnam War. Stoner received only modest royalties, as the patents were held by ArmaLite.

Later designs

Stoner's later work included the modular Stoner 63 weapons system, used in limited numbers by Navy SEALs and Marines in Vietnam; the Stoner 86 light machine gun at ARES Inc., which he co-founded; and the SR-25 precision rifle at Knight's Armament Company in the 1990s. He died of cancer in 1997.
