Abdominal aortic aneurysm

Abdominal aortic aneurysm

An abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a localized enlargement of the abdominal aorta such that the diameter is greater than 3 cm or more than 50% larger than normal. They usually cause no symptoms, except during rupture. Occasionally, abdominal, back, or leg pain may occur.

Signs and symptoms

Most aneurysms are asymptomatic and are found incidentally on imaging. Rupture presents with severe abdominal or back pain, hypotension, and a pulsatile abdominal mass, and carries a very high mortality rate.

Causes

The exact causes remain unclear, but risk factors include tobacco use, male sex, age over 65, high blood pressure, atherosclerosis, and a family history of the condition.

Screening and management

One-time ultrasound screening is recommended for men aged 65 to 75 who have ever smoked. Small aneurysms are monitored with periodic imaging; large or rapidly expanding aneurysms are treated surgically, either by open repair or by endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR).
