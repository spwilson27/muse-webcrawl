RAF officer ranks

The officer ranks of the Royal Air Force form the commissioned rank structure of the United Kingdom&rsquo;s air service. Many RAF rank titles are distinctive to the service and reflect its separate identity from the British Army and Royal Navy.

Structure

GroupExamples
Junior officersPilot officer, flying officer, flight lieutenant
Senior officersSquadron leader, wing commander, group captain
Air officersAir commodore, air vice-marshal, air marshal, air chief marshal
Senior appointmentMarshal of the Royal Air Force

Rank insignia are worn as braid on sleeves or shoulder boards depending on dress. The RAF system uses NATO rank codes for comparison with allied services, while retaining its own titles in daily use.

History

The RAF adopted its rank titles shortly after its creation in 1918. The titles squadron leader, wing commander and group captain evoke flying formations, while the marshal ranks parallel senior titles in other services.

These ranks were used across RAF stations including RAF Rudloe Manor.
