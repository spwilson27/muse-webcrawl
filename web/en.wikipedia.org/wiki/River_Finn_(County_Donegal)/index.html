The River Finn (Irish: Abhainn na Finne) is a river in the northwest of Ireland. It rises in Lough Finn in County Donegal and flows eastwards for about 64 kilometres (40 mi) through the Finn Valley, passing the twin towns of Ballybofey and Stranorlar, and on through Castlefin before crossing into County Tyrone in Northern Ireland. It joins the River Mourne near Strabane to form the River Foyle.

Course

From Lough Finn, set beneath the Blue Stack Mountains, the river runs east through a broad agricultural valley — the Finn Valley — one of the most fertile parts of Donegal. It is joined by tributaries including the River Reelan and the Burn Dennett before meeting the Mourne at the confluence near Lifford and Strabane, where the combined waters form the Foyle.

Fishing

The Finn is renowned as one of Ireland's finest salmon rivers, historically celebrated for its spring salmon run, with the tidal beats near Castlefin and the famous pools of the upper river attracting anglers from across Europe. It also supports sea trout and brown trout. In common with other Foyle system rivers, salmon numbers have declined, and the river has been subject to catch-and-release regulations and conservation measures administered by the Loughs Agency.
