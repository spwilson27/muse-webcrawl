Lee Valley Regional Park

Lee Valley Regional Park is a 10,000-acre (40 km²), 26-mile (42 km) long linear park, much of it green space, running through the northeast of Greater London, Essex and Hertfordshire in England. The park follows the course of the River Lea and the Lee Navigation from Ware in Hertfordshire to the banks of the River Thames at East India Docks Basin.

The park was established by the Lee Valley Regional Park Act 1966, following a campaign led by Professor Patrick Abercrombie, who had envisaged the valley as a "green lung" for Londoners in his 1944 Greater London Plan. It is managed by the Lee Valley Regional Park Authority.

Features

The park contains a chain of nature reserves, lakes, marshes and open spaces, including Walthamstow Wetlands — one of the largest urban wetland nature reserves in Europe — Rye Meads, Amwell Quarry, Cornmill Meadows, and the Waterworks Nature Reserve. It provides habitat for bitterns, otters and a wide variety of waterfowl, and several areas are designated Sites of Special Scientific Interest.

Sport and leisure

The park hosts major sporting venues, including the Lee Valley White Water Centre at Waltham Cross, built for the canoe slalom events of the London 2012 Olympic Games; the Lee Valley VeloPark and Lee Valley Hockey and Tennis Centre in Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park; Lee Valley Athletics Centre at Edmonton; and Lee Valley Ice Centre. Lee Valley Marina and a network of campsites, fisheries and cycleways serve visitors throughout the valley.

Management

The Lee Valley Regional Park Authority is funded principally by a levy on the council tax of London, Essex and Hertfordshire residents, supplemented by income from its venues and commercial activities.
