Clearchus (Greek: Κλέαρχος; c. 450–401 BC) was a Spartan general and mercenary commander, best known for leading the Greek mercenary force known as the Ten Thousand in the service of Cyrus the Younger during his revolt against the Persian king Artaxerxes II.

Clearchus first distinguished himself in the later years of the Peloponnesian War, commanding Spartan forces in the Hellespont. Exiled from Sparta after disobeying orders, he became a soldier of fortune and entered the service of Cyrus, recruiting a large Greek mercenary army in Thrace and Asia Minor.

The expedition of Cyrus

In 401 BC Clearchus commanded the right wing of Cyrus's army at the Battle of Cunaxa, where his Greeks routed the Persian forces opposite them. Cyrus, however, was killed charging his brother's position, leaving the mercenaries victorious but stranded deep inside the Persian Empire.

Death

After Cunaxa, the Persian satrap Tissaphernes invited Clearchus and the other Greek generals to a conference, seized them in violation of a truce, and sent them to the royal court, where they were executed. Their deaths left the Ten Thousand leaderless and set the stage for the election of new commanders, among them the Athenian Xenophon, whose Anabasis preserves the most vivid portrait of Clearchus: a stern, severe disciplinarian whom soldiers feared but trusted, "a man born for war."
