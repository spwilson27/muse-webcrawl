Iraqi Turkmen

The Iraqi Turkmen (also spelled Turkoman or Turkman) are an ethnically Turkic people native to northern Iraq. They form the country's third-largest ethnic group after Arabs and Kurds, with population estimates commonly ranging from about 600,000 to over two million. They are concentrated in a belt of territory often called Turkmeneli, stretching from Tal Afar and Mosul in the northwest through Kirkuk, Tuz Khurmatu, and Altun Kupri to districts south-east of Baghdad such as Mandali.

Language and culture

Iraqi Turkmen speak a variety of Oghuz Turkic closely related to Azerbaijani and Anatolian Turkish; many use Istanbul Turkish as a written standard. Culturally they preserve Ottoman-era traditions, and their community includes both Sunni and Shia Muslims, roughly divided along settlement lines, along with a small Catholic minority.

History

Turkic peoples settled in Iraq in successive waves: under the Abbasid caliphs, the Seljuks, and the Ottoman Empire, which governed the region for four centuries. Under the Ba'athist regime, Turkmen communities — particularly in Kirkuk — suffered Arabization policies that confiscated land, changed district boundaries, and suppressed Turkmen language and education. After 2003, the status of Kirkuk became a central dispute between the federal government, the Kurdistan Regional Government, and Turkmen representatives. The main Turkmen political organisation is the Iraqi Turkmen Front. Turkmen districts suffered heavily during the war against the Islamic State (2014–2017), including the siege of Amerli and the destruction of towns around Tal Afar.
