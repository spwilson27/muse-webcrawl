Dál Riata

Dál Riata or Dál Riada was a Gaelic kingdom that encompassed the western seaboard of Scotland and north-eastern Ireland, on each side of the North Channel. At its height in the 6th and 7th centuries it covered what is now Argyll and part of County Antrim.

Dál Riata
Capital: Dunadd (probable)
Language: Old Irish
Established: c. 498

History

Tradition traces its foundation to Fergus Mór mac Eirc around 498. Its kindreds are recorded in the Senchus fer n-Alban. Saint Columba, based on Iona from 563, played a central role in its politics.

Decline

After defeats by the Picts and Viking raids, Dál Riata's Scottish part merged with Pictland under Kenneth MacAlpin in the traditional account of the founding of the Kingdom of Alba.
