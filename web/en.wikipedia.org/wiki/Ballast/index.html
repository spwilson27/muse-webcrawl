Ballast

Crushed stone track ballast beneath railway sleepers

Ballast is material used to provide stability, weight or support to a vehicle, structure or electrical circuit. The word is used in several distinct technical senses.

Maritime ballast

In shipping, ballast is heavy material — today usually seawater carried in ballast tanks — taken aboard to lower a vessel's centre of gravity, improve stability, and adjust trim and draught, especially when sailing without cargo. Historically, stones, sand or iron were used as solid ballast. The transfer of ballast water between ports is a major vector for invasive aquatic species, and is regulated by the IMO Ballast Water Management Convention.

Railway ballast

Track ballast is the crushed stone bed on which railway sleepers (ties) rest. It distributes the load of passing trains to the subgrade, holds the sleepers in place, facilitates drainage and suppresses vegetation. Its angular, interlocking stones are periodically cleaned or renewed and compacted with tamping machines.

Other uses

Sailing ballast is the weight carried low in a yacht's keel to resist capsizing. An electrical ballast is a device that limits current through a load such as a fluorescent or discharge lamp. Hot air balloons and submersibles also manage ballast to control ascent and descent.
