Common tern

The common tern (Sterna hirundo) is a seabird in the family Laridae. This bird has a circumpolar distribution, breeding in temperate and sub-Arctic regions of Europe, Asia and North America. It is strongly migratory, wintering in coastal tropical and subtropical regions. Breeding adults have pale grey upperparts, black cap, orange-red legs, and a narrow pointed bill that is mainly red with a black tip in North American and European birds.

Description

The common tern is a medium-sized tern, 31–35 cm (12–14 in) long including a 6–9 cm tail fork, with a wingspan of 77–98 cm. It is very similar to the Arctic tern and roseate tern, from which it can be told by bill colour and pattern, leg length, and the pattern of dark and pale in the wing feathers. Its call is a harsh, descending "kee-ahr".

Behaviour and feeding

Like all Sterna terns, the common tern feeds by plunge-diving for fish, usually from hovering flight, and typically in shallow water near the shore. Its diet consists mainly of small fish, supplemented by crustaceans and insects. It nests in colonies on coasts and islands, and inland on suitable large freshwater lakes, laying two to four speckled eggs in a scrape on the ground. Both parents incubate the eggs and feed the chicks, and the adults defend the nest aggressively against predators, including humans.

Conservation

The species has a large global population and is classified as Least Concern by the IUCN, although some local populations have declined owing to habitat loss, disturbance, and predation by introduced mammals. In the nineteenth century the species was hunted for its feathers for the millinery trade, but recovered under protection in many areas.
