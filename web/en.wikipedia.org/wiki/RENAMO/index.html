RENAMO

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

RENAMO fighters in the Mozambican bush during the civil war
RENAMO fighters in the Mozambican bush during the civil war

RENAMO (Portuguese: Resistência Nacional Moçambicana, Mozambican National Resistance) is a Mozambican political party and former rebel military organisation. Founded in 1976–77 with Rhodesian backing to fight Mozambique's FRELIMO government, it waged a brutal civil war (1977–1992) increasingly sponsored by apartheid South Africa. Since the Rome General Peace Accords (1992) it has been the country's main opposition party, led until his death in 2018 by Afonso Dhlakama.

Origins and war

RENAMO was assembled by Rhodesia's Central Intelligence Organisation from ex-Portuguese security personnel and FRELIMO dissidents; its first leader, André Matsangaissa, was killed in 1979 and succeeded by Dhlakama. After Zimbabwean independence (1980), South Africa's security establishment took over sponsorship. The war devastated Mozambique — perhaps a million dead, millions displaced — and RENAMO was implicated in massacres such as Homoine (1987), which it denied ordering.

Peace and opposition politics

The Rome Accords (October 1992), mediated by the Community of Sant'Egidio, demobilised both armies and made RENAMO a political party; Dhlakama lost presidential elections in 1994, 1999 (narrowly and disputed), 2004, 2009 and 2014. Alleging exclusion from power and patronage, RENAMO remobilised armed units from 2013, attacking traffic on the main north–south highway; a 2019 peace accord under new leader Ossufo Momade provided for demobilisation and pensions.

Recent position

RENAMO remains the principal opposition in the north and centre, though its electoral share has declined and smaller parties (notably PODEMOS after the disputed 2024 election) have challenged its position. The demobilisation process concluded in 2023 with the closure of its last base.
