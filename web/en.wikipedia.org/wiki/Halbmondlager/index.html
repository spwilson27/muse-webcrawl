Halbmondlager
Location: Wünsdorf, near Berlin, Germany
Type: Prisoner-of-war camp
Period: First World War (1914–1918)

The Halbmondlager ("Half Moon Camp") was a prisoner-of-war camp in Wünsdorf, south of Berlin, during the First World War. It held Muslim prisoners captured from the Allied armies — principally British Indian, French North and West African, and Russian Tatar troops.

Purpose

Germany, allied with the Ottoman Empire, sought to recruit the prisoners for a jihad against the British, French and Russian empires. Prisoners received comparatively favourable treatment, religious instruction, and propaganda newspapers such as El Dschihad. The camp contained the Wünsdorf Mosque, the first mosque built in Germany, erected in 1915 in Ottoman style.

Legacy

Recruitment largely failed, and few prisoners joined Ottoman service. The camp's sound recordings of prisoners' voices, made by linguists, survive as an important archive. The mosque was demolished in the 1920s; a cemetery for prisoners who died remains at nearby Zehrensdorf.
