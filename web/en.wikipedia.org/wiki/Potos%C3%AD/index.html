Potosí

Potosí is a city in southern Bolivia, capital of the Potosí Department, lying at the foot of the Cerro Rico ("Rich Mountain") at an elevation of about 4,090 metres (13,420 ft), making it one of the highest cities in the world. Founded in 1545 after the discovery of enormous silver deposits, it became one of the largest and wealthiest cities of the Spanish Empire and the Americas.

Silver boom

Silver from Cerro Rico, extracted by an enormous forced labour system (the mita) of Indigenous workers under brutal conditions, flooded world markets and helped finance Spain's European wars. By the early 17th century Potosí's population rivalled that of London or Seville, at around 160,000–200,000. The Spanish phrase vale un Potosí — "worth a Potosí" — came to mean worth a fortune.

Decline and later history

Output declined in the 18th and 19th centuries as the richest ores were exhausted. Tin mining revived the regional economy in the 20th century, and mining — now largely by cooperatives in dangerous conditions — continues in the Cerro Rico today.

Heritage

The colonial city, with its mint (Casa de la Moneda), churches and the mountain itself, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987; it was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 2014 due to deterioration of the Cerro Rico.
