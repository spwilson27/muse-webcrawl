Nikolai Ivanovich Shakura (Russian: Николай Иванович Шакура; born 7 October 1945) is a Soviet and Russian astrophysicist, co-author with Rashid Sunyaev of the standard theory of accretion disks.

Nikolai Shakura

Nikolai Ivanovich Shakura (born 1945) is a Soviet and Russian astrophysicist known for his foundational work on accretion onto black holes and neutron stars. He has spent most of his career at the Sternberg Astronomical Institute of Moscow State University, where he headed the Department of Relativistic Astrophysics.

Accretion disk theory

In 1973 Shakura and Rashid Sunyaev published "Black holes in binary systems. Observational appearance" in Astronomy and Astrophysics, the paper that defined the standard model of geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disks. Unable to identify the physical origin of the turbulence that transports angular momentum, they parameterised the disk's viscosity with a dimensionless factor, universally known since as the Shakura–Sunyaev α parameter (alpha disk model). The model predicted the spectrum of X-ray binaries and remains the baseline against which observations of accreting black holes, neutron stars, white dwarfs and young stars are compared; it is one of the most cited papers in astrophysics.

Career

Shakura studied and worked in Moscow under the influence of Yakov Zeldovich's school of relativistic astrophysics. He was elected a corresponding member of the Russian Academy of Sciences and has received Russian state honours for his work on high-energy astrophysics. His later research includes accretion onto magnetised neutron stars and the theory of supercritical accretion.
