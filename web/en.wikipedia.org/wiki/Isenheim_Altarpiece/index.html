Isenheim Altarpiece

Isenheim Altarpiece
Artist: Matthias Grünewald (paintings); Nikolaus Hagenauer (sculpture)
Year: c. 1512–1516
Medium: Oil on wood, limewood sculpture
Location: Unterlinden Museum, Colmar, France

The Isenheim Altarpiece is a large polyptych altarpiece painted by Matthias Grünewald between about 1512 and 1516 for the Monastery of St. Anthony in Isenheim, Alsace. The sculpted sections are by Nikolaus Hagenauer. It is regarded as one of the masterpieces of German Renaissance art, renowned for the emotional intensity and expressive distortion of its Crucifixion.

Context

The Antonite monastery at Isenheim ran a hospital specialising in the treatment of skin diseases, including ergotism ("St Anthony's fire"). The altarpiece's imagery — Christ's afflicted, diseased body on the cross, and saints associated with healing such as Saint Anthony and Saint Sebastian — was intended to offer patients identification with Christ's suffering and hope of salvation.

Views

The altarpiece has multiple hinged wings giving three configurations. Closed, it shows the Crucifixion flanked by Saint Sebastian and Saint Anthony, with the Lamentation on the predella. The first opening reveals the Annunciation, the Concert of Angels with the Madonna and Child, and the Resurrection. The second opening exposes the carved shrine of Saint Anthony flanked by painted scenes of the saint's life, including the Temptation of Saint Anthony, a panel famous for its grotesque demons.

Reception

Neglected for centuries, Grünewald was rediscovered by German Expressionists, who saw a precursor in his distortions. Paul Hindemith based his opera and symphony Mathis der Maler (1935) on Grünewald's life. The altarpiece is now displayed at the Unterlinden Museum in Colmar.
