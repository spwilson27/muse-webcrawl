GUM (department store)

GUM (Russian: ГУМ, pronounced "goom") is a famous department store on Red Square in Moscow, Russia, facing the Kremlin and Lenin's Mausoleum. The name is an acronym of Gosudarstvenny universalny magazin — "State Department Store" — its designation during the Soviet era; after privatisation it was reinterpreted as Glavny universalny magazin ("Main Department Store"). Its glass-roofed arcades make it as much an architectural monument and tourist attraction as a shop.

History

Trading rows had occupied the site since the 17th century. The present building was erected in 1890–1893 by architect Alexander Pomerantsev, with a revolutionary steel-and-glass vaulted roof engineered by Vladimir Shukhov spanning the three parallel arcades. At its opening the Upper Trading Rows comprised over a thousand shops. After the Russian Revolution the store was nationalised and became GUM in 1921, a showcase of Soviet consumer goods; in 1930 Stalin closed it and the building housed government offices, but it reopened as a department store in 1953 and became one of the few Soviet stores not plagued by visible shortages.

Architecture and present day

The façade, nearly 250 metres long, is built in a pseudo-Russian style with marble, granite, and decorative towers; inside, three light-filled arcades are joined by bridges and crowned by Shukhov's arched glass roof, famous for its fountain at the centre. Privatised after 1991 and majority-owned since 2004 by the Bosco di Ciliegi group, GUM today houses luxury boutiques, cafés, a historic cinema, and the celebrated Stolovaya No. 57 canteen; seasonal skating rinks and fairs on Red Square stand at its door.
