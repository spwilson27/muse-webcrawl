Bielefeld conspiracy

The Bielefeld conspiracy (German: Bielefeldverschwörung) is a satirical conspiracy theory that claims the existence of the German city of Bielefeld is an illusion, fabricated by an obscure organization referred to only as "THEM" (SIE in German). It is one of the best-known jokes of German internet culture.

[Image: A road sign pointing toward Bielefeld, playfully annotated by conspiracy fans]

Origin

The joke began in 1993 at a student party, when a friend of computer science student Achim Held claimed never to have met anyone from Bielefeld. Held posted the theory to the Usenet newsgroup de.talk.bizarre in May 1994. The theory asks three questions: Do you know anybody from Bielefeld? Have you ever been to Bielefeld? Do you know anybody who has ever been to Bielefeld? Most people answer "no" to all three, which the satire treats as "proof" of the conspiracy.

Spread and response

The conspiracy became a running gag in German media; even Chancellor Angela Merkel jokingly referenced it in 2012, saying of a Bielefeld event, "...if it exists at all." The city of Bielefeld has repeatedly played along: in 1999 it issued an April Fools' press release assuring the public of its existence, which was taken as further "evidence" by fans. In 2019 the city offered a €1 million prize to anyone who could provide irrefutable proof that Bielefeld does not exist; hundreds of humorous entries were received and the city declared its existence "proven."

Cultural significance

The Bielefeld conspiracy is frequently cited as an early internet meme and as a harmless parody used in media-literacy education to illustrate how conspiracy reasoning works.
