Endogeneity (econometrics)

Endogeneity: an explanatory variable correlated with the error term

Endogeneity is a property of an econometric model in which an explanatory variable is correlated with the error term. In the presence of endogeneity, ordinary least squares (OLS) estimators are biased and inconsistent — the estimated coefficients do not converge to the true parameter values even as the sample size grows — so causal interpretation of the estimates is not possible.

Causes

Endogeneity typically arises from three sources:

  • Omitted variables: a relevant variable that affects both the dependent variable and an included regressor is left out of the model, so its influence is absorbed into the error term.
  • Simultaneity / reverse causality: the dependent variable and an explanatory variable determine each other, as in supply-and-demand systems where price and quantity are jointly determined.
  • Measurement error: an explanatory variable is measured with error, which places part of the true variable into the error term (errors-in-variables), biasing estimates — for classical measurement error, typically toward zero (attenuation bias).

Exogeneity

The opposite condition is exogeneity: an explanatory variable is exogenous if it is uncorrelated with the error term. In time-series and panel settings a distinction is drawn between strict exogeneity (uncorrelated with errors in all periods) and predeterminedness or sequential exogeneity.

Remedies

The standard remedy is instrumental variables (IV) estimation, including two-stage least squares (2SLS): the researcher finds one or more instruments that correlate with the endogenous regressor (relevance) but are uncorrelated with the error term (exclusion restriction). Other strategies include panel-data fixed effects, control functions, natural experiments, regression discontinuity designs, and randomised experiments where feasible. Tests such as the Durbin–Wu–Hausman test can compare OLS and IV estimates to detect endogeneity, though they have limited power.
