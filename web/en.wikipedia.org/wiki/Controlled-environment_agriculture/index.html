Controlled-environment agriculture

Interior of a vertical farm operating as a controlled environment

Controlled-environment agriculture (CEA) is a technology-based approach toward food production. The aim of CEA is to provide protection from the outdoor elements and maintain optimal growing conditions throughout the development of the crop. Production takes place within an enclosed growing structure such as a greenhouse or a fully indoor facility, often called a vertical farm.

Plants are frequently grown using hydroponic, aeroponic, or aquaponic methods rather than soil, and environmental parameters such as light, temperature, humidity, carbon dioxide concentration, and nutrient delivery are monitored and adjusted, increasingly with the help of sensors and automation.

History

Controlled growing environments have ancient precedents, including Roman forcing houses and the orangeries of early modern Europe. Modern CEA developed alongside commercial glasshouse production in the Netherlands in the twentieth century, and expanded rapidly in the 2010s with the falling cost of LED lighting and investment in indoor vertical farms.

Methods

  • Greenhouses and glasshouses, which use natural sunlight supplemented by artificial light
  • Indoor vertical farms, in which crops are stacked in layers under LED lighting
  • Growth chambers used for research and seedling production
  • Container farms housed in shipping containers

Advantages and disadvantages

Supporters cite year-round production, reduced water use compared with field agriculture, elimination of most pesticide applications, local production near urban consumers, and protection from extreme weather. Critics point to high capital costs and the large energy demand of artificial lighting and climate control, which can make CEA uncompetitive for staple crops such as grains.
