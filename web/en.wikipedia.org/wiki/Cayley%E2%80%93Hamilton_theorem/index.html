Cayley–Hamilton theorem

In linear algebra, the Cayley–Hamilton theorem states that every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation: if p(λ) = det(λI − A) is the characteristic polynomial of an n×n matrix A, then p(A) = 0, the zero matrix. The theorem is named after Arthur Cayley and William Rowan Hamilton.

Example

For a 2×2 matrix A, the characteristic polynomial is λ² − (tr A)λ + det A, so the theorem asserts A² − (tr A)A + (det A)I = 0.

Applications

The theorem expresses A−1 as a polynomial in A when A is invertible, lets one reduce high powers of a matrix to polynomials of degree less than n, and links the minimal polynomial to the characteristic polynomial: the minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial. It is used in control theory, matrix function computation, and the theory of algebraic integers.
