Degree (music)

In music theory, the degree is the position of a particular note on a scale relative to the tonic, as well as the name given to that note. In the diatonic scale, the seven degrees have traditional names expressing their function in tonal music, while scale degrees are also commonly identified by number (often written with a caret, e.g. 1̂, 2̂, 3̂) or by solfège syllables.

Function

The names of the degrees derive from their positions relative to the tonic and dominant: the mediant lies midway between tonic and dominant, the submediant midway between tonic and subdominant, and so on. In functional harmony, the scale degree on which a chord is built determines much of its behaviour; chords are labelled with Roman numerals corresponding to the degree of their root. Degrees 2 and 7 are sometimes described as unstable, tending to resolve to 1 and 3 or 1 respectively. In minor keys the sixth and seventh degrees are variable, being raised or lowered in the harmonic and melodic minor scales.
