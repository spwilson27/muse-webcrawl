The rainbow flag or pride flag is a symbol of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer (LGBT) pride and LGBT social movements. Its colours reflect the diversity of the LGBT community and the spectrum of human sexuality and gender. The most widely used version consists of six stripes — red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet — but the original 1978 design had eight.

History

The flag was designed by San Francisco artist Gilbert Baker at the request of Harvey Milk, one of the first openly gay elected officials in the United States. It first flew at the San Francisco Gay Freedom Day Parade on 25 June 1978. Baker's original eight stripes each carried a meaning: hot pink for sex, red for life, orange for healing, yellow for sunlight, green for nature, turquoise for magic and art, indigo for serenity, and violet for spirit.

Hot pink was dropped because the fabric was unavailable, and indigo and turquoise were merged into royal blue to allow the flag to be split evenly along a parade route, yielding the familiar six-stripe version adopted in 1979.

Variants

Many variants have been created to represent specific communities and causes. The Progress Pride Flag, designed by Daniel Quasar in 2018, adds a chevron of white, pink, light blue, brown and black, representing trans people, people of colour and those lost to HIV/AIDS. Other variants incorporate the black and brown stripes of the 2017 Philadelphia flag, the intersex circle, or national and regional flags.

Use and recognition

The flag is flown at pride parades, LGBT venues and supportive institutions worldwide, and is rendered as consumer products, illuminated landmarks and emoji (🏳️‍🌈, added in 2016). Baker declined to trademark the design, calling it a symbol for everyone. In 2015 the Museum of Modern Art in New York added the rainbow flag to its permanent design collection.
