Interpersonal communication

Interpersonal communication is the process by which people exchange information, feelings and meaning through verbal and non-verbal messages. It is a central subject in communication studies and psychology.

Elements

Communication involves senders and receivers exchanging messages through channels, subject to noise and interpreted within contexts. Feedback — the receiver's response — distinguishes interpersonal communication from one-way mass communication.

Non-verbal communication

A large share of interpersonal meaning is conveyed non-verbally through facial expression, gesture, posture, eye contact, tone of voice and use of space and time.

Theories

Key theories include social penetration theory, which describes how relationships develop through increasing self-disclosure, and uncertainty reduction theory, which explains how people seek information about new acquaintances.
