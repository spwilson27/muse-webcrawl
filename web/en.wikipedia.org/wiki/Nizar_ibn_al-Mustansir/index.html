Nizar ibn al-Mustansir

Abu Mansur Nizar ibn al-Mustansir (1045 – 1095) was a Fatimid prince, the eldest son of the eighth Fatimid caliph, al-Mustansir Billah. After his father's death in 1094, the succession was seized by his younger brother al-Musta'li through the vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah, despite Nizar having been widely regarded as the designated heir. Nizar's revolt in Alexandria was defeated and he was executed in Cairo. The succession dispute caused the great schism of Ismaili Islam into the Nizari branch, which recognises Nizar and his descendants as the rightful imams, and the Musta'li branch.

Succession dispute

Al-Mustansir reigned for 58 years, the longest of any Muslim caliph. By longstanding expectation, his eldest son Nizar, governor of Alexandria, would succeed him. On the caliph's death in December 1094, the powerful Armenian vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah instead enthroned Nizar's youngest brother, Ahmad, as al-Musta'li, and demanded the princes' homage. Nizar refused and fled to Alexandria, where the local governor and populace proclaimed him caliph with the title al-Mustafa li-Din Allah.

Revolt and death

Nizar's forces initially defeated an army sent from Cairo, but al-Afdal regrouped, bribed away Nizar's allies, and besieged Alexandria. In 1095 the city fell; Nizar and his supporters were taken to Cairo, where he was put to death — according to tradition, immured in a wall. His sons were imprisoned or fled; Nizari tradition holds that a son or grandson, al-Hadi, was smuggled to the Ismaili stronghold of Alamut in Persia, preserving the line of imams recognised by the Nizaris.

The Nizari–Musta'li schism

The dispute split the Ismaili da'wa permanently. The Ismailis of Persia, Syria and Central Asia, led from Alamut by Hassan-i Sabbah, rejected al-Musta'li and upheld Nizar's imamate — becoming the Nizari Ismailis, today the largest Ismaili community, led by the Aga Khan, who claims descent from Nizar. The Musta'li branch, dominant in Fatimid Egypt and Yemen, later subdivided into Hafizi and Tayyibi lines.
