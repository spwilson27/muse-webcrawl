Lake Pontchartrain

Lake Pontchartrain is a brackish estuary in southeastern Louisiana, United States. Covering about 1,630 square kilometres (630 square miles), it is one of the largest wetlands along the Gulf Coast and the second-largest inland saltwater lake in the United States, after the Great Salt Lake. Its average depth is only about 3.7 metres.

Geography

The lake is bordered by New Orleans to the south and by communities of the North Shore, including Mandeville and Slidell. It connects to Lake Borgne and the Gulf of Mexico through the Rigolets and Chef Menteur Pass, and to the Mississippi River via the Bonnet Carré Spillway, which is opened during high river stages to relieve flooding pressure on New Orleans.

Causeway

The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway, a pair of parallel bridges crossing the lake north–south, is among the longest bridges over water in the world, at about 38.4 kilometres (23.9 miles).

Hurricane Katrina

In August 2005, Hurricane Katrina's storm surge overwhelmed floodwalls along New Orleans' drainage canals connected to the lake, causing catastrophic flooding of much of the city. Subsequent repairs rebuilt the region's hurricane protection system.
