East Java

East Java (Indonesian: Jawa Timur) is a province of Indonesia located in the eastern third of the island of Java. Its capital and largest city is Surabaya, the second-largest city in Indonesia. The province has a population of roughly 41 million, making it one of the most populous provinces in the country.

East Java includes the island of Madura, connected to Surabaya by the Suramadu Bridge, the longest bridge in Indonesia, as well as Bawean Island and the Kangean Islands in the Java Sea.

Geography and history

The province is known for its volcanoes, including Mount Bromo in the Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park and Mount Semeru, the highest mountain in Java. Historically, East Java was the heartland of great Javanese kingdoms, including Singhasari and the Majapahit empire, whose capital was near modern Trowulan. The region was central to the Indonesian National Revolution; the Battle of Surabaya in November 1945 is commemorated by Heroes' Day.

Economy

Surabaya is a major port and industrial centre, and the province is important for agriculture (rice, sugar cane, tobacco, coffee), fisheries, shipbuilding, and manufacturing.
