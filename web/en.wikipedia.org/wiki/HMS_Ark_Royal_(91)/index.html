HMS Ark Royal (pennant number 91) was an aircraft carrier of the Royal Navy that served in the Second World War. Designed in the 1930s to exploit the tonnage allowed Britain under the naval treaties, she was the first large purpose-built carrier completed for the Royal Navy, and her design — with two hangar decks, a full-length flight deck and a heavy anti-aircraft armament — influenced all subsequent British carriers. In barely two years of war service she became one of the most famous warships in the world; German propaganda claimed to have sunk her so often that "Where is the Ark Royal?" became a byword in Britain.

Design

Ark Royal was constrained by the Washington and London naval treaties to about 22,000 tons, yet the Admiralty required a large air group, so the design adopted two superimposed hangars, giving capacity for some 60 to 72 aircraft — more than any contemporary carrier. Her flight deck, 800 feet long, was an integral strength deck, and she was protected against 6-inch gunfire and 500-pound bombs, though her underwater protection, with a single bulkhead system, proved a fatal weakness. She carried sixteen 4.5-inch dual-purpose guns in twin turrets, pompoms, three lifts, and catapult and arrester gear, and her three shafts gave her a speed of 31 knots.

War service

At the outbreak of war in September 1939, Ark Royal joined the Home Fleet hunting U-boats; on 14 September she was attacked by U-39, whose magnetic torpedoes exploded prematurely, and her escorting destroyers sank the U-boat — the first U-boat kill of the war. A week later her Skua dive-bombers shot down a German flying boat, the first British aerial victory of the war. German radio repeatedly announced her sinking after a near miss by bombs on 26 September, to British amusement.

In late 1939 she hunted the pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee in the South Atlantic, her mere approach helping to convince Captain Langsdorff to scuttle his ship at Montevideo. In 1940 she covered the Norwegian campaign, provided air cover for the evacuation from France, and joined Force H at Gibraltar, taking part in the attack on the French fleet at Mers-el-Kébir. Through 1940–41 she was the backbone of Force H in the Mediterranean: escorting Malta convoys, raiding Italian targets in Sardinia, and fighting at the Battle of Cape Spartivento.

Her most famous action came in May 1941, when her Fairey Swordfish torpedo bombers, flying from south of the Azores in atrocious weather, attacked the German battleship Bismarck. A torpedo jammed Bismarck's rudders, leaving her circling helplessly and allowing the Home Fleet to destroy her the next day — arguably the most consequential carrier strike of the European war.

Loss

On 13 November 1941, returning to Gibraltar after flying off fighters to Malta, Ark Royal was torpedoed by the German submarine U-81, commanded by Friedrich Guggenberger. A single torpedo struck her starboard side amidships, causing extensive flooding that her damage-control organisation initially seemed to contain; but progressive flooding, loss of power and a growing list forced Captain Maund to order abandonment. Only one crewman, Able Seaman Edward Mitchell, was killed — the rest of her company of over 1,400 was taken off, mostly by the destroyer HMS Legion. Despite desperate efforts to tow her to Gibraltar, only some ten miles away, the carrier capsized and sank in the early hours of 14 November 1941.

Her loss was a severe blow and led to a court of inquiry that criticised aspects of her damage control and design. Her wreck was located in 2002 by a BBC-funded expedition, lying upright in two main pieces at a depth of about 1,060 metres off Gibraltar.
