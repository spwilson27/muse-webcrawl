Eraser

An eraser (in British English usually a rubber) is an article of stationery used to remove marks from paper or other surfaces. Most erasers are used to remove pencil marks; specialist types remove ink, typewriter writing, or marks from chalkboards and whiteboards.

History

Before rubber erasers, wax, sandstone, and even soft bread were used to remove pencil marks. In 1770 the English engineer Edward Nairne is credited with developing the first marketed rubber eraser, after Joseph Priestley described the ability of natural rubber (caoutchouc) to rub out pencil marks—the origin of the British name "rubber". In 1839 Charles Goodyear's vulcanisation process made rubber more durable, and in 1858 Hymen Lipman patented a pencil with an eraser attached to the end.

Types

Common types include pink pearl (rubber) erasers, plastic/vinyl erasers, kneaded erasers favoured by artists because they lift graphite without residue, gum erasers, pen-shaped and cap erasers, and electric erasers. Eraser material is consumed in use, shedding crumbs that carry away the pigment particles.
