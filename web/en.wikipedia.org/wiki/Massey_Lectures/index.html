The CBC Massey Lectures are an annual series of lectures on a political, cultural or philosophical topic, given in Canada by a noted scholar, writer or public figure. The lectures were created in 1961 by the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation together with Massey College at the University of Toronto, and are named for the Massey Foundation, established by the family of Hart Massey.

Format

Each year a single lecturer delivers a series of five lectures, usually in the autumn, often touring several Canadian cities. The lectures are broadcast nationally on the CBC Radio program Ideas and are published in book form by House of Anansi Press. The series is regarded as one of Canada's most prestigious public intellectual platforms, and many of the published lectures have become significant works in their own right.

Notable lecturers and lectures

Past lecturers include some of the most prominent thinkers of the past half-century. Among the best known are:

  • Northrop Frye, The Educated Imagination (1962)
  • Martin Luther King Jr., Conscience for Change (1967)
  • Claude Lévi-Strauss, Myth and Meaning (1977)
  • Doris Lessing, Prisons We Choose to Live Inside (1985)
  • Charles Taylor, The Malaise of Modernity (1991, published in the US as The Ethics of Authenticity)
  • John Ralston Saul, The Unconscious Civilization (1995)
  • Margaret Atwood, Payback: Debt and the Shadow Side of Wealth (2008)
  • Thomas King, The Truth About Stories (2003)
  • Wade Davis, The Wayfinders (2009)
  • Neil Turok, The Universe Within (2012)
  • Adam Gopnik, Winter: Five Windows on the Season (2011)
  • Tanya Talaga, All Our Relations (2018)
