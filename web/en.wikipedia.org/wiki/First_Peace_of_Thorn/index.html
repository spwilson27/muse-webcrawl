Peace of Thorn (1411)

The First Peace of Thorn was a peace treaty formally ending the Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War (1409–1411) between allied Poland and Lithuania on one side and the Teutonic Knights on the other. It was signed on 1 February 1411 in Thorn (Toruń), one of the southernmost cities of the Monastic State of the Teutonic Knights in Prussia. In Polish and Lithuanian historiography it is known as the First Peace of Toruń (Pierwszy pokój toruński).


Prussia after the Peace of Thorn: the Teutonic Order retained most of its territory.

Background

The war had begun with a Lithuanian uprising in Samogitia in 1409 and climaxed at the Battle of Grunwald (Tannenberg) on 15 July 1410, where a joint Polish–Lithuanian army under King Władysław II Jagiełło and Grand Duke Vytautas destroyed the army of the Teutonic Order and killed Grand Master Ulrich von Jungingen. Despite the scale of the victory, the allies failed to take the Order's capital at Marienburg (Malbork), which withstood a two-month siege under Heinrich von Plauen, and the war wound down inconclusively.

Terms

  • The Order returned the Dobrzyń Land to Poland and made only small territorial concessions.
  • Samogitia was ceded to Lithuania, but only for the lifetimes of Jagiełło and Vytautas; thereafter it would revert to the Order.
  • The Order agreed to pay a large war indemnity — some 100,000 kopas of Prague groschen, paid in instalments — intended, together with the ransom of prisoners, to cripple its finances.
  • Both sides confirmed the existing frontier and agreed to release prisoners.

Consequences

Contemporaries often judged the peace surprisingly mild given Grunwald: Poland–Lithuania gained little territory. Yet the indemnity forced the Order to raise taxes, which alienated its Prussian cities and landed elite, accelerated the emergence of organised opposition (the Prussian Confederation, founded 1440), and fed the grievances that erupted in the Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466), ended by the far harsher Second Peace of Thorn (1466), in which the Order lost western Prussia and became a Polish vassal. The Samogitian question was settled in Lithuania's favour by the Treaty of Melno (1422).
