Chester Arthur Burnett (June 10, 1910 – January 10, 1976), better known by his stage name Howlin' Wolf, was an American blues singer and guitarist. A towering figure — physically imposing at nearly 6 ft 6 in — with a booming, gravelly voice, he was one of the foundational artists of Chicago blues and a major influence on rock and roll. His best-known songs include "Smokestack Lightning", "Spoonful", "Killing Floor", "Back Door Man" and "Little Red Rooster".

Early life

Burnett was born in Mississippi and learned guitar from Delta blues pioneer Charley Patton and harmonica from Sonny Boy Williamson II. He took his name from tales of wolves and performed around the Delta in the 1930s, farming between engagements. After army service in the Second World War he settled in West Memphis, Arkansas, where he formed a band (featuring guitarist Hubert Sumlin from 1954) and broadcast on radio.

Chess Records years

Recorded first by Sam Phillips in Memphis ("Moanin' at Midnight", 1951), Burnett moved to Chicago in 1953 for Chess Records, beginning a famous chart rivalry with Muddy Waters. With Willie Dixon supplying songs and Sumlin's biting guitar, Wolf cut a string of classics. His 1962 album Howlin' Wolf (the "Rocking Chair Album") is regarded as a masterpiece. In 1971 he recorded The London Howlin' Wolf Sessions with Eric Clapton, Steve Winwood and members of the Rolling Stones, who idolised him.

Legacy

Howlin' Wolf was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1991. His primal vocals and stagecraft influenced artists from Led Zeppelin and The Doors to Tom Waits and Captain Beefheart; "Spoonful" and "Killing Floor" became standards of the rock repertoire.
