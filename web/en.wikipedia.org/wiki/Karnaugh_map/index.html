Karnaugh map

The Karnaugh map (K-map) is a method of simplifying Boolean algebra expressions, introduced by Maurice Karnaugh in 1953 as a refinement of Edward Veitch's chart. It represents the truth table of a function as a grid in which adjacent cells differ in exactly one variable, so that groups of adjacent 1s can be read off directly as simplified product terms.

Method

Cells are arranged in Gray-code order, and the map wraps around at the edges, forming a torus. Adjacent 1s (or 0s) are grouped in blocks whose sizes are powers of two; each group yields a term in which any variable that changes within the group is eliminated. The largest possible groups give the simplest sum-of-products (or product-of-sums) expression.

Use

K-maps are practical for functions of up to about six variables and remain a standard teaching tool in digital logic design. For larger problems, algorithmic methods such as Quine–McCluskey and Espresso are preferred.
