Stun gun

A contact stun gun; the electrodes must touch the target

A stun gun is an electroshock weapon — an incapacitating weapon that discharges an electric current into a target's body. Unlike the Taser, which fires projected probes and can be used at a distance, a stun gun is a contact weapon: two electrodes on the device itself must be pressed against the target.

Operation

An internal oscillator circuit steps battery power up to high voltage (advertised figures run from tens of thousands to over a million volts, with low current). When the electrodes touch the body, the discharge causes pain and involuntary muscle contractions at the point of contact; a sustained application of several seconds can disorientate or incapacitate. Because the current path is confined to the tissue between the electrodes, effects are local, and the devices are primarily used as pain-compliance and self-defence tools.

Legality

Stun guns are restricted or prohibited for civilian possession in many jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and several European countries, while in the United States legality varies by state and locality. Their use by police is less common than that of conducted energy weapons with ranged probes, but they are widely marketed for personal protection.
