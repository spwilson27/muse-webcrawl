Commutator subgroup

In mathematics, more specifically in abstract algebra, the commutator subgroup or derived subgroup of a group is the subgroup generated by all the commutators of the group.

Definition

The commutator of two elements g and h of a group G is [g, h] = g−1h−1gh (under one common convention). The commutator subgroup, denoted G′ or [G, G], is the subgroup generated by all commutators.

Properties

The commutator subgroup is a characteristic subgroup, hence normal in G. The quotient group G/G′ is always abelian and is called the abelianization of G; it is the largest abelian quotient of G. A group is perfect if it equals its own commutator subgroup.

Derived series

Repeatedly taking commutator subgroups yields the derived series, used to define solvable groups: a group is solvable if its derived series reaches the trivial group in finitely many steps.
