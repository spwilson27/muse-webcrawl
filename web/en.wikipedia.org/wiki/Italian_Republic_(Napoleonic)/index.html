Italian Republic (Napoleonic)

The Italian Republic (Italian: Repubblica Italiana) was a short-lived republic in northern Italy that existed from 1802 to 1805. A client state of the French Republic, it succeeded the Cisalpine Republic and was itself replaced by the Kingdom of Italy when Napoleon Bonaparte crowned himself king in 1805.

History

The republic was proclaimed on 26 January 1802 at an extraordinary assembly in Lyon, where delegates adopted a constitution modelled on French lines. Napoleon was chosen as president, with Francesco Melzi d'Eril as vice-president and the effective head of government in Milan, the capital. The state's territory covered much of Lombardy, Emilia-Romagna and the former Cisalpine lands east of Piedmont.

Legacy

Although dependent on France, the republic introduced lasting administrative, legal and military reforms and gave Italians experience of a unified northern Italian state. Its green-white-red tricolour, adopted from the Cisalpine standard, eventually became the national flag of Italy. In March 1805, following Napoleon's coronation as Emperor of the French, the republic was transformed into the Kingdom of Italy with Napoleon as king and Eugène de Beauharnais as viceroy.
