Battery management system

A battery management system (BMS) is an electronic system that manages a rechargeable battery pack, protecting it from operating outside its safe area, monitoring its state, computing secondary data, reporting that data, controlling its environment, and balancing the individual cells within the pack. BMS units are essential in modern lithium-ion applications — electric vehicles, grid-scale energy storage, aerospace systems and consumer electronics — because lithium chemistries can be damaged, or become dangerous, if overcharged, over-discharged or overheated.

A BMS board monitoring a multi-cell lithium-ion battery pack.

Functions

Key BMS functions include: monitoring cell voltages, pack current and temperatures; protection against over-voltage, under-voltage, over-current, short circuits and thermal runaway by disconnecting the pack; estimating state of charge (SOC) and state of health (SOH) using coulomb counting and voltage models; cell balancing, either passive (bleeding charge through resistors) or active (shuttling charge between cells) to equalise cell voltages; and communicating with chargers, inverters and vehicle controllers via buses such as CAN.

Architectures

BMS designs range from simple protection boards in small devices to centralised systems with a single controller sampling all cells, and distributed or modular designs in which each cell or module has its own monitoring circuit reporting to a master unit. Large stationary storage and EV packs employ hierarchical designs. Safety standards for BMS include ISO 26262 for road vehicles and IEC 62619 and UL 1973 for stationary and industrial batteries.
