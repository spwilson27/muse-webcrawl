Shahu I

Reign: 1707 – 15 December 1749
Predecessor: Shivaji II
Successor: Rajaram II
Born: 18 May 1682, Raigad
Died: 15 December 1749, Satara

Shahu I (18 May 1682 – 15 December 1749), also spelled Sahu, was the fifth Chhatrapati of the Maratha Empire. He was the son of Sambhaji and grandson of Shivaji, the empire's founder. Captured as a child by the Mughals after the fall of Raigad in 1689, he spent seventeen years in Mughal captivity at the court of Aurangzeb. His release in 1707 — under terms intended to sow discord among the Marathas — instead led, after a civil war with his aunt Tarabai, to his consolidation of power and a long reign that saw the Marathas expand across much of India.

Captivity and release

Shahu was seven when Mughal forces captured Raigad, and he grew up in Aurangzeb's camp, receiving the title Raja and a mansab, and marrying into Mughal-connected families. Aurangzeb hoped a Mughalized prince would never rally the Marathas; his successors, calculating that a released Shahu would split the Maratha kingdom, freed him in 1707 after Aurangzeb's death.

Reign

Shahu defeated Tarabai's forces at the Battle of Khed (1707) with the help of the Dhangar general Dhanaji Jadhav's defection, and was crowned at Satara in 1708. Tarabai established a rival court at Kolhapur, beginning the Kolhapur–Satara split of the Maratha royal house. Ground down by court intrigues at his own court, Shahu delegated executive power to his Peshwa (chief minister). In 1713 he appointed Balaji Vishwanath as Peshwa, whose diplomacy secured recognition from Delhi of Maratha rights to collect chauth and sardeshmukhi in the Deccan (1719).

Under subsequent Peshwas — above all Baji Rao I (1720–1740) — Maratha armies raided as far as Delhi and established dominion in Malwa, Gujarat and Bundelkhand, while Shahu's reign provided the stable legitimacy that held the growing Maratha Confederacy together. Shahu, childless, adopted Rajaram II before his death at Satara in December 1749.
