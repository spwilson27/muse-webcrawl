Silesia (Polish: Śląsk; German: Schlesien; Czech: Slezsko) is a historical region of Central Europe, most of which lies in modern southwestern Poland, with smaller parts in the Czech Republic and Germany. It is drained by the upper and middle Oder river and bounded to the south by the Sudetes mountains. Its historic capital is Wrocław.

History

In the early Middle Ages Silesia was settled by Slavic tribes and came under the Polish Piast dynasty, fragmenting into numerous small duchies. From the fourteenth century the Silesian duchies passed to the Bohemian crown and thus to the Habsburgs. Frederick the Great of Prussia seized most of Silesia from Austria in the Silesian Wars (1740–1763), making it one of Prussia's richest provinces, noted for coal mining and industry in its eastern (Upper Silesian) basin. After the Second World War, the Potsdam Agreement transferred nearly all of Silesia to Poland; its German population was expelled westward and the region was resettled with Poles, many from territories annexed by the Soviet Union.

Geography and economy

Lower Silesia in the northwest is centred on Wrocław; Upper Silesia in the southeast is a dense industrial conurbation around Katowice built on coal and steel. Czech Silesia, around Ostrava and Opava, forms part of the Moravian-Silesian Region. The region retains a distinct identity, and the Silesian language/dialect is spoken by several hundred thousand people in Upper Silesia.
