Babur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Babur supervising the laying out of a garden in Kabul, from a Baburnama miniature]
Babur supervising the laying out of a garden in Kabul, from a Baburnama miniature

Babur (Zahir-ud-din Muhammad Babur, 14 February 1483 – 26 December 1530) was a Timurid prince from Fergana who founded the Mughal Empire in India. Descended from Timur on his father's side and Genghis Khan on his mother's, he lost and won Samarkand three times, settled for Kabul, and in 1526 defeated the Delhi Sultanate at the First Battle of Panipat with field artillery — a first in India. His memoir, the Baburnama, is a masterpiece of Chagatai Turkish literature.

From Fergana to Kabul

Babur inherited the small kingdom of Fergana at eleven and spent his youth in the Timurid princes' ruinous wars against the Uzbeks of Shaybani Khan. Driven south, he took Kabul in 1504 and made it his base, eyeing both Samarkand (briefly recovered in 1511 with Persian help, at the price of a hated Safavid alliance) and the wealth of Hindustan, which he raided repeatedly from 1519.

Conquest of Hindustan

At Panipat (21 April 1526), Babur's 12,000–25,000 men with matchlocks and cannon, laagered behind carts, destroyed Ibrahim Lodi's vastly larger army. He then defeated the Rajput confederacy of Rana Sanga at Khanwa (1527) and the eastern Afghans at the Ghaghra (1529), founding an empire he had no time to organise — that task fell to his son Humayun and grandson Akbar.

The Baburnama and character

Babur's memoirs are disarmingly frank — about battles and gardens, wine parties and tears for a bowl of melons from home — and rank among the world's great autobiographies. A poet in two languages, a gardener (his Kabul garden holds his tomb), a swimmer of the Ganges, he died in Agra in 1530, worn out at 47; a famous legend has him offering his life for the ailing Humayun's.
