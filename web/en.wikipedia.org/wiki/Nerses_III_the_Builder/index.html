Nerses III the Builder

Nerses III the Builder (Armenian: Ներսես Գ Շինող, Nerses G Shinogh) was the Catholicos of the Armenian Apostolic Church between 641 and 661. He is remembered above all for his extensive church-building programme, which earned him the epithet "the Builder" (Shinogh), and for the cathedral he erected at Zvartnots.

Early life and election

Nerses was from the village of Tayots in the canton of Arsharunik. He served as Bishop of Tayk before being elected Catholicos in 641, succeeding Ezra I. His tenure coincided with the Arab conquest of Armenia, a period in which the Church had to negotiate its position between the Byzantine Empire and the new Arab rulers.

Zvartnots Cathedral

Nerses's most famous project was the cathedral of Zvartnots, near Etchmiadzin, begun in 643 and completed around 652. Dedicated to Saint Gregory, it was a vast three-tiered circular church, some 49 metres high, decorated with carved pomegranates, vines, and relief figures, and it became one of the masterpieces of early medieval Armenian architecture. The cathedral stood for more than three centuries before collapsing, probably in an earthquake, in the 10th century; its ruins were excavated in the early 20th century.

Other works

Nerses is also credited with churches at Dvin and Khor Virap, with restoring older sanctuaries, and with translating Greek theological works into Armenian, including writings associated with Proclus and Dionysius the Areopagite.

Politics and death

Suspected by the Byzantines of sympathy toward the Arabs, Nerses spent part of his catholicosate in exile at Tayk and in Constantinople before returning to Armenia. He died in 661 and was buried at Zvartnots, the cathedral he had built.
