Kazan Federal University

The university's main building in Kazan

Kazan (Volga region) Federal University (Russian: Каза́нский (Приво́лжский) федера́льный университе́т) is a public research university located in Kazan, Tatarstan, Russia. Founded in 1804 as Kazan Imperial University, it is one of the oldest universities in Russia and was granted federal university status in 2010.

History

The university was founded by decree of Emperor Alexander I on 17 November 1804. Under its first rector, the mathematician Nikolai Lobachevsky — rector from 1827 to 1846 — Kazan became famous as the birthplace of non-Euclidean geometry: Lobachevsky presented his revolutionary "imaginary geometry" there in 1826. The chemist Alexander Butlerov developed his theory of chemical structure at Kazan, and the university's chemistry school produced figures such as Nikolai Zinin and Alexander Arbuzov.

Among the university's most famous students was Vladimir Ulyanov (Lenin), who was expelled in 1887 after participating in student protests, and the writer Leo Tolstoy, who studied law and Oriental languages there in the 1840s without graduating.

Modern university

In 2010 Kazan University was reorganised as one of Russia's federal universities, merging with several other regional institutions. The university today comprises numerous institutes covering mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, engineering, economics, law, humanities and information technologies, and enrols tens of thousands of students, including a large international contingent. It operates an astronomical observatory, founded in 1814, and extensive museum collections, including the Lobachevsky Library and the Ethnographic Museum.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include Lobachevsky, Butlerov, Zinin, the physicist Evgeny Zavoisky (who discovered electron paramagnetic resonance at Kazan in 1944), and the orientalist-scholars of the Kazan school of Turkology.
