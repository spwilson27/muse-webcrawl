William and Flora Hewlett Foundation

The William and Flora Hewlett Foundation, commonly called the Hewlett Foundation, is an American private foundation established by Hewlett-Packard co-founder William Redington Hewlett and his wife Flora Lamson Hewlett in 1966. Headquartered in Menlo Park, California, it is one of the largest grant-making foundations in the United States, with assets of roughly thirteen billion dollars.

History

The foundation began as a family foundation; William Hewlett served as its first board chair, and his son Walter B. Hewlett later chaired the board. In 1994 the foundation broadened its governance beyond the family, and in 2001, upon the death of David Packard, the foundation received a large bequest that made it one of the wealthiest philanthropic institutions in the country.

Programs

The foundation makes grants in several areas: global development and population; environment, with a major focus on climate change (it is a significant funder of ClimateWorks Foundation); education, including early support for open educational resources and the OpenStax initiative; performing arts in the San Francisco Bay Area; and effective philanthropy. Under president Paul Brest (2000–2012) the foundation became known for its emphasis on strategy and outcome-oriented philanthropy. Larry Kramer served as president from 2012 to 2023; Amber D. Miller became president in 2024.
