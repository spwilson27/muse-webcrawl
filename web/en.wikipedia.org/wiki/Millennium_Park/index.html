Millennium Park is a public park located in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois, United States. It is a prominent civic center near the shoreline of Lake Michigan, covering 24.5 acres (9.9 ha) of the northwestern section of Grant Park. The park, which honors the third millennium, was opened in a ceremony on July 16, 2004, four years behind schedule.

History

The site was formerly occupied by rail yards and parking lots of the Illinois Central Railroad. Planning for the park began in 1998 under Mayor Richard M. Daley, and construction proceeded from 2000 to 2004. The park was conceived as a rooftop garden over a parking garage and the Metra Millennium Station, making it one of the largest green roofs in the world.

Features

The park's best-known attraction is Cloud Gate, a highly polished stainless-steel sculpture by Anish Kapoor nicknamed "The Bean" for its shape. Other major features include the Jay Pritzker Pavilion, an outdoor concert venue designed by Frank Gehry; the Crown Fountain by Jaume Plensa; the Lurie Garden, designed by Gustafson Guthrie Nichol with Piet Oudolf and Robert Israel; and the BP Pedestrian Bridge, also designed by Gehry. In winter, the McCormick Tribune Plaza hosts an ice skating rink.

Reception

Millennium Park was an immediate critical and popular success and became one of Chicago's top tourist destinations. It has received numerous awards for its design, accessibility and green features, and is often cited as one of the most successful public-private partnerships in American urban design.
