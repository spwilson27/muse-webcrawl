Anti-Western sentiment

Anti-Western sentiment, also known as Anti-Westernism or Westernophobia, refers to broad opposition, bias, or hostility toward the people, culture, values, or policies of the Western world. It has historical roots in reactions to European colonialism and imperialism, and in the modern era is often intertwined with opposition to the foreign policies of the United States and its allies.

Historical development

In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, anti-Western movements arose in societies confronting Western expansion, including the Boxer Rebellion in China and various pan-Islamic and pan-Asian movements. During the Cold War, anti-Western rhetoric was a prominent feature of communist and Non-Aligned Movement discourse. Intellectual currents within the West itself — including critiques of capitalism, consumerism, and cultural homogenization — have also contributed to anti-Western thought.

Contemporary expressions

Today anti-Western sentiment appears in varied forms: state rhetoric in countries such as Russia, China, and Iran; Islamist movements that frame the West as morally corrupt; and populist or nationalist movements elsewhere. Polls such as the Pew Global Attitudes surveys have tracked fluctuations in favorability toward Western countries, often falling after events such as the 2003 invasion of Iraq. Critics distinguish between criticism of specific policies and generalized hostility toward Western peoples and liberal values.
