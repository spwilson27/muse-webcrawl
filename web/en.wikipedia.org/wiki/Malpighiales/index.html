The Malpighiales comprise one of the largest orders of flowering plants, containing about 36 families and roughly 16,000 species — around 7.8% of the eudicots. The order belongs to the fabid clade of the rosids. Its members are strikingly diverse in form, ranging from tiny herbs to rainforest trees, and the grouping was not recognised on morphological grounds: it emerged only from molecular phylogenetic studies beginning in the 1990s.

Families and notable members

Well-known families in the order include the Euphorbiaceae (spurges, rubber tree, cassava, poinsettia, castor oil plant), Salicaceae (willows and poplars, greatly enlarged in modern classifications), Violaceae (violets and pansies), Passifloraceae (passion flowers), Malpighiaceae (the type family, including acerola), Linaceae (flax), Clusiaceae (mangosteen family), Rhizophoraceae (red mangroves), Phyllanthaceae and Hypericaceae (St John's wort family). Economically important plants in the order include rubber, cassava, flax, coca, tea relatives and many timber and ornamental species.

Classification

In the APG IV system (2016) the Malpighiales are placed within the fabids, as part of a clade sometimes called the COM clade together with Celastrales and Oxalidales. Relationships among the families inside the order have proved difficult to resolve because the group appears to have undergone a rapid ancient radiation, leaving short internal branches in its phylogeny.
