Toilet

A toilet is a piece of sanitary hardware that collects human urine and faeces, and sometimes toilet paper, usually for disposal. Flush toilets use water and connect to a sewer or septic system, while dry toilets such as pit latrines do not use flush water.

Modern flush toilets consist of a ceramic bowl with a hinged seat, a cistern supplying flush water, and a trap that prevents sewer gases entering. About 2 billion people worldwide lack access to basic sanitation. Toilet design varies culturally, including sitting and squatting types.

History

Ancient sanitation existed in the Indus Valley Civilisation. John Harington invented an early flush toilet in 1596, and Alexander Cumming patented the S-trap in 1775. Widespread adoption followed Victorian sanitation reforms.
