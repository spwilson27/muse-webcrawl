Elastic cartilage

Elastic cartilage, also called yellow fibrocartilage, is a type of cartilage that provides strength and elasticity to parts of the body, most notably the external ear. It is one of the three main types of cartilage, alongside hyaline cartilage and fibrocartilage. Elastic cartilage is distinguished by a dense network of elastin fibres within its extracellular matrix, which give it the ability to bend and spring back into shape.

Structure

The matrix of elastic cartilage contains type II collagen fibres (as in hyaline cartilage) together with abundant branching elastic fibre bundles, which can be demonstrated with special stains such as Verhoeff's stain or orcein. Chondrocytes lie in lacunae, often in small groups, within the fibre-rich matrix. Like most cartilage, elastic cartilage is avascular and is covered by a perichondrium, from which it receives nutrients by diffusion; unlike hyaline cartilage, it does not calcify with age.

Locations

Elastic cartilage is found in the pinna (auricle) of the external ear and the wall of the external acoustic meatus, the epiglottis, the corniculate and cuneiform cartilages of the larynx, and the auditory (Eustachian) tubes. These locations require a tissue that maintains a definite shape while remaining flexible — the ear returns to form after being folded, and the epiglottis must flex repeatedly to cover the laryngeal inlet during swallowing.
