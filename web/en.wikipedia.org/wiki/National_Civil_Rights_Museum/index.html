National Civil Rights Museum

Location450 Mulberry Street, Memphis, Tennessee, U.S.
SiteLorraine Motel
OpenedSeptember 28, 1991
TypeHistory museum

The National Civil Rights Museum is a complex of museums and historic buildings in Memphis, Tennessee, tracing the history of the civil rights movement in the United States from the 17th century to the present. Its centrepiece is the Lorraine Motel, the site of the 1968 assassination of Martin Luther King Jr., whose facade and Room 306 — where King spent his final hours — are preserved as they were.

History

After King's assassination on April 4, 1968, the motel fell into decline and foreclosure. A group of Memphis citizens formed the Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial Foundation to save the site, purchasing it in 1982. The museum opened in 1991, making it one of the first major museums devoted to the civil rights movement. In 2002 it expanded across the street into the former Young and Morrow building — the boarding house from which the fatal shot was fired — adding exhibits on the assassination investigation. A major renovation completed in 2014 added new galleries covering slavery, the sit-ins, Freedom Rides and Black Power.

Exhibits

Immersive exhibits include a slave ship hold, a Montgomery bus boycott bus, lunch-counter sit-in scenes, and the sanitation workers' strike of 1968 that brought King to Memphis. The museum presents the Freedom Award annually to individuals who have made significant contributions to civil and human rights.
