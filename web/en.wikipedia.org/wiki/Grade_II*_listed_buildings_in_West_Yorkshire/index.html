Grade II* listed buildings in West Yorkshire

There are over 1,500 listed buildings in the county of West Yorkshire, England. Buildings of special architectural or historic interest are graded I, II* or II by Historic England; Grade II* denotes "particularly important buildings of more than special interest", accounting for about 5.5% of listed buildings nationally. This article lists the Grade II* buildings in West Yorkshire, organised by district: Leeds, Bradford, Kirklees, Calderdale and Wakefield.

Background

Listing, under the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990, protects a building from demolition or unsympathetic alteration. West Yorkshire's listed stock reflects its history of medieval churches and monasteries, country houses, and — above all — the textile industry of the Industrial Revolution: woollen and worsted mills, warehouses, exchanges, workers' housing and the model industrial village of Saltaire.

Notable examples

Notable Grade II* buildings in the county include the Corn Exchange and Kirkgate Market buildings in Leeds, the Piece Hall in Halifax (Grade I), textile mills such as Dean Clough in Halifax, the Victoria Quarter arcades in Leeds, numerous parish churches and chapels, and country houses such as Lotherton Hall and Nostell Priory's stable block. The full statutory list is maintained in the National Heritage List for England.
