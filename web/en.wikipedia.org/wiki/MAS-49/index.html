The MAS-49 is a French semi-automatic rifle designed and manufactured by the Manufacture d'Armes de Saint-Étienne. It was the culmination of a series of French semi-automatic prototypes developed between the wars and during the 1940s, and it served as the standard rifle of the French armed forces through the First Indochina War and the Algerian War.

Development

France had experimented with semi-automatic rifles since the First World War. The MAS-44, produced in small numbers at the end of the Second World War, led to the MAS-49, adopted in 1949 and entering wide service in the early 1950s. The rifle uses a direct-gas-impingement system in which propellant gas is channelled through a tube above the barrel to act directly on the bolt carrier, combined with a tilting bolt.

Features

The MAS-49 is chambered for the 7.5×54mm French cartridge and feeds from a detachable ten-round magazine, though the magazine was generally reloaded in place with stripper clips. All MAS-49 rifles were built with an integral device for firing rifle grenades, including a flip-up grenade-launching sight. The later MAS-49/56 variant, introduced in 1957, was shorter and lighter, had a redesigned muzzle device and bayonet, and became the most widely issued version.

Service history

The MAS-49 family served extensively in Indochina and Algeria and equipped French forces until replaced by the FAMAS assault rifle at the end of the 1970s. Some rifles were converted to 7.62×51mm NATO and sold on the surplus market, including a quantity imported into the United States. The type was also exported to several Francophone states and Syria, whose contract rifles are among the more commonly encountered variants.
