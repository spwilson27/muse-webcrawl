Star party

A star party is a gathering of amateur astronomers for the purpose of observing the sky at night. Local astronomy clubs often host monthly star parties at dark sites, while larger regional and national events draw hundreds or thousands of participants for several nights of observing, talks and equipment trading.

Description

Star parties are typically scheduled around the new moon, when skies are darkest, and held away from city light pollution. Attendees set up telescopes — from small refractors to large Dobsonians — and spend the night observing deep-sky objects, planets and double stars, often sharing views and comparing equipment. Red flashlights are used to preserve night vision, and many events enforce strict white-light discipline after dark. Socializing, astrophotography and informal mentoring of newcomers are central features; organized events often include daytime lectures, vendor exhibits and door prizes.

Notable star parties

The Texas Star Party, held since 1979 near Fort Davis, Texas, is one of the largest in the United States. Stellafane, the annual convention of amateur telescope makers in Springfield, Vermont, dates to 1926 and is the oldest event of its kind. The Winter Star Party in the Florida Keys, the Oregon Star Party, Starfest in Ontario, Canada, and the South Pacific Star Party in Australia are other long-running gatherings. Astronomy clubs also hold public outreach star parties in parks and schools to introduce astronomy to the general public.
