Davos (Romansh: Tavau) is a town and municipality in the Prättigau/Davos Region of the Swiss canton of Graubünden. Situated at 1,560 metres above sea level in the Landwasser valley, it is the highest town in the Alps and is known worldwide as the venue of the annual meeting of the World Economic Forum.

History

The valley was settled by Romansh speakers in the early Middle Ages and colonised by German-speaking Walsers in the 13th century. For centuries Davos was a quiet farming community and a centre of mining. Its modern fame began in the 1860s–70s, when physicians such as Alexander Spengler promoted its dry, sunny high-altitude air as a cure for tuberculosis. Grand sanatoria multiplied, attracting wealthy patients from across Europe; Robert Louis Stevenson completed Treasure Island there in 1881–82, Arthur Conan Doyle wrote and skied there, and Thomas Mann set his novel The Magic Mountain (1924) in a Davos sanatorium, modelled on his wife's stay in 1912.

Sport

As medical tourism declined after antibiotics, winter sports took its place. Davos claims some of the earliest organised skiing in Switzerland, hosted pioneering ice hockey, and is home to HC Davos, one of Switzerland's most successful hockey clubs, and to the Spengler Cup, the world's oldest invitational ice hockey tournament, held every year between Christmas and New Year since 1923. Its ski areas include Parsenn, Jakobshorn and the freeride mountain Pischa.

World Economic Forum

Since 1971 Davos has hosted the annual meeting of the World Economic Forum, founded by Klaus Schwab, which each January brings several thousand business leaders, heads of government, central bankers, journalists and activists to the town. "Davos" has become shorthand in the press for the global political-economic elite and for the meeting itself, which requires a massive Swiss security operation and regularly draws protests and criticism over globalisation and inequality.

Geography and climate

The municipality, one of the largest in Switzerland by area, comprises the fractions of Davos Platz and Davos Dorf along the Landwasser river, plus outlying villages. It enjoys a cold, sunny alpine climate with long snowy winters, reached by the Rhaetian Railway's Landquart–Davos and Filisur lines over famous viaducts.
