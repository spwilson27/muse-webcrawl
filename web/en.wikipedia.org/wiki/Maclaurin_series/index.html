Maclaurin series

In mathematics, a Maclaurin series is a Taylor series expansion of a function about 0:

f(x) = f(0) + f′(0)/1! · x + f″(0)/2! · x² + f‴(0)/3! · x³ + …

The series is named after the Scottish mathematician Colin Maclaurin, who made extensive use of this special case of Taylor series in his Treatise of Fluxions (1742). Maclaurin credited the general result to Brook Taylor.

Examples

  • Exponential function: ex = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + …, convergent for all x.
  • Geometric series: 1/(1 − x) = 1 + x + x² + x³ + …, for |x| < 1.
  • Sine: sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …, for all x.
  • Cosine: cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − …, for all x.
  • Natural logarithm: ln(1 + x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − …, for −1 < x ≤ 1.
  • Binomial series: (1 + x)α = 1 + αx + α(α−1)/2! x² + …, for |x| < 1.

Use

Maclaurin series provide polynomial approximations to functions that are used throughout calculus, physics and engineering: truncating the series gives approximations valid near 0, and differentiating or integrating a series term by term yields series for related functions. A function equal to its Maclaurin series in an interval is called analytic there; some infinitely differentiable functions, such as e−1/x² (defined as 0 at 0), have Maclaurin series that fail to converge to the function except at 0 itself.
