Nobadia

Nobadia was a late antique kingdom in Lower Nubia, in what is now northern Sudan and southern Egypt. It emerged after the decline of the kingdom of Meroë and the fragmentation of the region among peoples including the Nobatae, from whom its name derives. Its capital was Pachoras, modern Faras.

History

The Nobatae were settled in the region by the Roman emperor Diocletian in the late 3rd century to guard the frontier against the Blemmyes. By the 5th century a distinct Nobadian kingdom had formed. In about 543 the kingdom officially converted to Christianity through missions sent by the Byzantine empress Theodora, adopting the Miaphysite (Coptic) confession. Nobadia resisted the Arab invasions of the 7th century and around the early 8th century was absorbed into the larger southern Nubian kingdom of Makuria, though its bishops and eparchy retained a distinct identity for centuries.

Culture

Nobadia is famed for the cathedral of Faras, excavated during the UNESCO salvage campaign before the flooding of Lake Nasser, whose vivid wall paintings of kings, bishops and saints are now divided between museums in Warsaw and Khartoum.
