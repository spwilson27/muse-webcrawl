Tower and stockade

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tower and stockade (Hebrew: חומה ומגדל, Homa u'Migdal, literally "wall and tower") was a method of settlement used by Zionist settlers in Mandatory Palestine during the 1936–39 Arab revolt and afterwards. Between December 1936 and October 1939, some 52 new Jewish settlements were established in a single day (or night) each, using prefabricated wooden structures: a watchtower and a defensive stockade wall enclosing a small compound with huts for the settlers.

Background

During the Arab revolt, attacks on Jewish settlements and transport were common, and the British Mandatory authorities restricted the establishment of new settlements. However, under a provision of Ottoman land law still in force, a building that had a roof could not be demolished without a court order, and obtaining one took time. The settlement movement therefore developed a system by which a defensible settlement could be erected in a single day, presenting the authorities with a fait accompli.

Method

The components — a tall wooden watchtower with a searchlight, a double wooden stockade wall filled with gravel, barbed wire, and huts — were prefabricated in advance and held in readiness. On the appointed day, convoys of trucks carried the materials and dozens or hundreds of volunteers to the site. The stockade and tower were assembled first, giving the settlement immediate defensibility, followed by tents or huts inside the compound. The first settlement built by this method was Tel Amal (now Nir David) in the Beit She'an Valley, founded on 10 December 1936.

Settlements founded

Tower and stockade settlements were placed strategically to create contiguous Jewish presence in areas such as the Beit She'an Valley, the Jezreel Valley, the Hula Valley, the Jordan Valley and the northern Negev. Among them were Hanita in western Galilee (1938), whose establishment became a celebrated symbol of the movement; Tirat Zvi; Maoz Haim; Ein Gev on the Sea of Galilee; and Negba in the south (1939). Hanita's founding was staffed in part by the Special Night Squads' Jewish supernumerary police and involved figures such as Yitzhak Sadeh and Moshe Dayan.

Legacy

The tower and stockade system became one of the iconic images of Zionist settlement, commemorated in museums, including the Tower and Stockade Museum at Nir David and a reconstructed site at Kibbutz Hanita. The model of rapid prefabricated settlement influenced later settlement operations, including the "11 points in the Negev" of October 1946.
