Architecture of Morocco

Moroccan architecture reflects the country's diverse history and geography, drawing on Berber (Amazigh), Arab, Andalusi, and later French colonial influences. It is characterised by carved plaster and cedar wood, colourful zellige tilework, horseshoe arches, and fortified earthen structures.

History

Indigenous Amazigh builders developed rammed-earth (pisé) and mud-brick fortifications, including the ksar and kasbah of the pre-Saharan valleys. With the arrival of Islam in the 7th–8th centuries, mosque and madrasa architecture took root. The Almoravid, Almohad, and Marinid dynasties produced landmarks such as the Koutoubia Mosque in Marrakech and the Bou Inania Madrasa in Fez, blending Andalusi refinement with local craftsmanship.

Features

Traditional houses and palaces are organised around interior courtyards (riads), with walls decorated in carved stucco (tadelakt plaster), painted cedar ceilings, and geometric zellige mosaics. The horseshoe arch, muqarnas vaulting, and crenellated towers recur across religious and domestic buildings.

Regional styles

The earthen architecture of the Draa and Ziz valleys, the whitewashed coastal towns influenced by Portuguese and Spanish occupation, and the modernist experiments of the French Protectorate era (1912–1956) in Casablanca all contribute distinct regional vocabularies.
