Forward engineering

FieldSoftware engineering
DirectionAbstraction → implementation
ContrastReverse engineering

Forward engineering is the traditional process of moving from high-level abstractions and logical, implementation-independent designs to the physical implementation of a system. In this sense, forward engineering is the "normal" development process: requirements are refined into designs, and designs into code and deployed products.

Relation to other processes

Forward engineering is the counterpart of reverse engineering, which recovers design information from an existing implementation, and of reengineering, which combines reverse engineering, rethinking, and forward engineering to rebuild a system. In model-driven engineering, forward engineering takes the form of model transformations that generate lower-level artifacts from platform-independent models.

In practice

Because most software is built top-down from specifications, forward engineering describes the bulk of day-to-day development work. The term is most often used when contrasting a clean-slate build with efforts to understand or migrate a legacy system.
