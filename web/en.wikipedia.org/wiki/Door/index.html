Door

A door is a hinged or otherwise movable barrier that allows ingress into and egress from an enclosure. Its primary purpose is to provide security by combining it with an organization system of locks and keys to be opened and closed only by people authorized to use the entry. Doors normally consist of a panel or slab that can be swung open, and fit into a frame. Doors are generally made of a material suited to the door's task: they are frequently made from wood, metal, and composites.

History

The earliest recorded doors from antiquity were depicted in Egyptian paintings around 4000 years ago, and later generations of doors were made of stone, bronze, and wood. Doors giving access to homes, temples, and tombs were considered symbols of status and protection. In ancient Greece and Rome, doors were described in literature such as the Odyssey, and Roman deities such as Janus were associated with doorways and passages.

Types

Common door types include hinged doors, sliding doors, revolving doors, folding doors, and overhead doors. Doors may be classified by their construction, such as panel doors, flush doors, and louvered doors, or by their function, such as fire doors, which are rated to resist fire for a period of time. Specialized forms include the trapdoor, the hatch, and the portcullis.

Elements

A door assembly typically includes the door leaf, the frame, hinges, a handle or knob, and a lock or latch. Additional fittings may include hinges, closers, weatherstripping, thresholds, and door stops. Modern doors may incorporate glass panels, security hardware, or automatic openers for accessibility.
