Papa John's International, Inc., trading as Papa Johns, is an American pizza restaurant chain. It was founded in 1984 by "Papa" John Schnatter, who began making pizzas in a converted broom closet behind his father's tavern in Jeffersonville, Indiana. It runs the fourth largest pizza delivery restaurant chain in the United States, with thousands of franchised and company-owned stores in dozens of countries.

History

Schnatter famously sold his 1971 Camaro Z28 to buy pizza-making equipment; the chain grew rapidly through franchising in the 1990s and went public in 1993 under the NASDAQ ticker PZZA. The company markets fresh, never-frozen dough and signature garlic sauce and pepperoncini included with each pizza.

Recent years

Schnatter stepped down as CEO in late 2017 and resigned as chairman in July 2018 following controversy over his remarks. The company later rebranded (dropping the apostrophe from "Papa John's" in 2021), opened a global headquarters in Atlanta, and has been led since 2024 by CEO Todd Penegor.
