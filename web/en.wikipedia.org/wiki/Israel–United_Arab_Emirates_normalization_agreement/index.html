Israel–United Arab Emirates normalization agreement

The Israel–United Arab Emirates normalization agreement, officially the Abraham Accords Peace Agreement: Treaty of Peace, Diplomatic Relations and Full Normalization Between the United Arab Emirates and the State of Israel, was agreed on 13 August 2020 and signed at the White House on 15 September 2020. It made the UAE the third Arab country, after Egypt (1979) and Jordan (1994), to formally normalise relations with Israel.

The signing ceremony at the White House, September 2020.

Terms

Under the agreement the two countries established full diplomatic relations, embassies, direct flights and cooperation in trade, technology, energy, tourism and security. Israel agreed to suspend plans to annex parts of the West Bank. The deal was brokered by the United States under President Donald Trump and formed the centrepiece of the Abraham Accords, signed the same day alongside a declaration with Bahrain, and followed later in 2020 by normalisation agreements with Sudan and Morocco.

Reactions

Palestinian leaders condemned the agreement as a betrayal, while many Western and Gulf governments welcomed it as a step toward regional stability. Trade between Israel and the UAE grew rapidly in subsequent years.
