Literary studies

Literary studies examines written works across periods, genres and cultures.

Literary studies is the academic discipline devoted to the study of literature: its history, forms, genres, authors, and the methods by which texts are interpreted and evaluated. Sometimes used interchangeably with literary scholarship or as a close relative of comparative literature and English studies, the field brings together close reading, literary theory, philology, and cultural history.

Scope

Literary studies encompasses the analysis of poetry, drama, fiction, and non-fiction prose, as well as oral literatures and, increasingly, film, digital media, and other narrative forms. Scholars in the field study how texts are produced and received, how they relate to their historical moments, and how meaning is made by readers. Major subfields include literary history, genre studies, narratology, stylistics, and the study of national and regional literatures.

History

The systematic study of literature has roots in classical rhetoric and poetics, notably Aristotle's Poetics, and in the philological traditions of the medieval and Renaissance periods. Literary studies emerged as a distinct university discipline in the nineteenth century, alongside the rise of national literatures and the professionalization of the humanities. In the twentieth century the field was transformed by successive movements: New Criticism, structuralism, post-structuralism, Marxist and feminist criticism, postcolonial studies, and New Historicism each reshaped what scholars asked of texts and how they read them.

Methods and approaches

  • Close reading — detailed analysis of a text's language, structure, and imagery.
  • Literary theory — frameworks (formalist, psychoanalytic, deconstructive, ecocritical, and others) that guide interpretation.
  • Historical and contextual study — situating works within their periods, movements, and material conditions of production.
  • Comparative approaches — studying literatures across languages and cultures, as in comparative literature and world literature.
  • Reception and book history — examining how texts circulate, are read, and are canonized.

Relationship to other disciplines

Literary studies overlaps with linguistics, philosophy, history, anthropology, and media studies. Its methods of textual analysis have been widely adopted across the humanities, and its scholars contribute to debates about canon formation, translation, censorship, and the cultural value of reading.
