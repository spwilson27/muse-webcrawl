Great Malvern is the historic centre of the spa town of Malvern in Worcestershire, England, lying at the foot of the Malvern Hills. It grew around the Benedictine priory founded in 1085 and became famous in the nineteenth century as a fashionable spa, noted for the purity of Malvern water and for the hydropathic "water cure" practised there.

History

Priory

According to tradition, a hermitage existed on the hills before the Norman Conquest. In 1085 Aldwyn founded a priory under Westminster Abbey; the surviving priory church contains Norman work, celebrated medieval stained glass, and a notable set of fifteenth-century encaustic tiles. At the Dissolution the church was bought by the parishioners for the reputed sum of £20.

The spa town

The medicinal reputation of Malvern's springs — particularly St Ann's Well and the Holy Well at Malvern Wells — grew in the eighteenth century; a 1756 analysis by Dr John Wall praised the water as containing "nothing at all", its virtue lying in its purity. In 1842 Drs James Wilson and James Manby Gully established hydropathic clinics in Great Malvern, applying the water cure of Vincent Priessnitz. The town boomed: patients and visitors included Charles Darwin, Florence Nightingale, Alfred Tennyson and Thomas Carlyle. Darwin's daughter Annie died in Malvern in 1851 and is buried in the priory churchyard.

The railway reached Great Malvern in 1859–60, and hotels, villas, the Winter Gardens, assembly rooms and Malvern College (founded 1865) followed. Bottled Malvern Water was produced commercially from 1850 until 2010.

Geography and sights

The town climbs the eastern slopes of the hills, with Church Street and Bellevue Terrace at its heart. Landmarks include the Priory Church, the Abbey Gateway (now housing the Malvern Museum), the Victorian railway station designed by Edmund Wallace Elmslie with its painted ironwork, and St Ann's Well café on the path to the Worcestershire Beacon. The composer Edward Elgar lived in and around Malvern for much of his life, drew inspiration from the hills (the Enigma Variations were composed at Forli in Alexandra Road), and is buried at Little Malvern.
