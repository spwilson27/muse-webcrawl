Dutasteride, sold under the brand name Avodart among others, is a medication used to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia (enlarged prostate) in men and, in some countries or off-label, male pattern hair loss. Taken by mouth, it works by blocking the conversion of testosterone into the more potent androgen dihydrotestosterone (DHT).

Medical uses

Dutasteride is approved for the treatment of symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia, alone or in combination with the alpha blocker tamsulosin (sold as Jalyn). By reducing DHT, it shrinks the prostate over months of treatment, improving urinary symptoms and reducing the risk of acute urinary retention and prostate surgery. In South Korea and Japan it is also approved for androgenetic alopecia, and it is used off-label elsewhere for that purpose.

Mechanism of action

The drug inhibits all three isoforms of the enzyme 5α-reductase, which converts testosterone to DHT. This distinguishes it from finasteride, which inhibits mainly types 2 and 3. Dutasteride reduces serum DHT levels by more than 90% at standard doses. It has an unusually long elimination half-life of about five weeks, so its effects persist for months after stopping.

Side effects

Common side effects include decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, ejaculation disorders and breast tenderness or enlargement. Dutasteride is teratogenic in principle — it can interfere with the development of male genitalia in a fetus — and must not be handled by women who are or may become pregnant; men taking it are also deferred from donating blood for a period after the last dose.
