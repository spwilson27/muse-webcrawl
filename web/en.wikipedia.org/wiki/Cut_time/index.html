Cut time

Cut time is notated with a C with a vertical line through it.

Cut time (also known as alla breve) is a musical meter notated with the time signature 2/2, in which the half note receives one beat and each measure contains two beats. It is symbolised by a capital C with a vertical line through it. Cut time is equivalent to 4/4 with every note value halved: a measure that would contain four quarter notes in common time contains two half notes in cut time.

The term alla breve derives from mensural notation, in which the breve was shortened — "cut" — so that the semibreve took its place as the beat unit. Cut time is especially common in marches, polkas, and other brisk music in two, and in much Baroque and Classical fast-movement writing, where it encourages a lighter, two-in-a-bar feel rather than a heavier four-in-a-bar tread.

Usage

Conductors typically beat cut time in two, and performers think of the half note as the pulse. Although any 2/2 passage could in principle be written in 4/4 with doubled note values, cut time conventionally signals a faster, more buoyant tempo. It is frequently found in the works of composers from Palestrina through to modern band and orchestral march repertoire.
