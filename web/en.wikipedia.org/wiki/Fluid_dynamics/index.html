Fluid dynamics

Fluid dynamics is a subdiscipline of fluid mechanics describing the flow of fluids — liquids and gases. Its branches include aerodynamics (gases, especially air) and hydrodynamics (liquids). Applications range from aircraft design and weather prediction to blood flow and petroleum engineering.

Fluid dynamics
Field: Fluid mechanics
Key equations: Navier–Stokes, Bernoulli, continuity
Concepts: Turbulence, viscosity, Reynolds number

Equations

Flow is governed by conservation of mass (the continuity equation), momentum (the Navier–Stokes equations) and energy. Bernoulli’s principle relates pressure and velocity in inviscid flow. Because the Navier–Stokes equations are nonlinear, analytical solutions exist only for simple geometries; computational fluid dynamics (CFD) solves them numerically.

Flow regimes

The Reynolds number predicts laminar flow — smooth, layered — versus turbulent flow with eddies and mixing. Other key ideas include boundary layers, viscosity, compressibility and Mach number, lift and drag, and instabilities such as vortex shedding.

History

Contributors include Archimedes, Leonardo da Vinci, Daniel Bernoulli, Leonhard Euler, Claude-Louis Navier, George Gabriel Stokes and Osborne Reynolds. Turbulence remains incompletely understood and is a major open problem in classical physics.
