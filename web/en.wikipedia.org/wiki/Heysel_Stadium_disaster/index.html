Heysel Stadium disaster

The Heysel Stadium disaster (Italian: Strage dell'Heysel) occurred on 29 May 1985 when Juventus fans escaping from an attack by Liverpool fans were pressed against a collapsing retaining wall in the Heysel Stadium in Brussels, Belgium, before the start of the 1985 European Cup Final between the Italian and English clubs. Thirty-nine people — mostly Italians and Juventus fans — were killed and 600 were injured.

Background and events

The stadium was in poor condition and the terracing crumbling. About an hour before kick-off, Liverpool fans charged a section holding predominantly Juventus supporters, breaching the thin line of police and fencing separating them. Fleeing fans were crushed against a wall, which collapsed. Despite the deaths, the match was played after a delay of over an hour, with Juventus winning 1–0, in part to avert further disorder.

Aftermath

UEFA banned all English clubs from European competition indefinitely; the ban was lifted in 1990, with Liverpool serving an additional year. Fourteen Liverpool fans were convicted of involuntary manslaughter in Belgium in 1989. The disaster led to major reforms in stadium safety and crowd management across Europe. The rebuilt stadium on the site was later renamed the King Baudouin Stadium, and a memorial sundial was unveiled for the twentieth anniversary in 2005.
