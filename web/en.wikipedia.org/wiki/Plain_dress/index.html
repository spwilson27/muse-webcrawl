Plain dress

Plain dress is a practice among some religious groups — notably Anabaptists such as the Amish, Old Order Mennonites, and some Hutterites, as well as historically many Quakers — of wearing modest, simple clothing without ornamentation, as an expression of humility, nonconformity to the world, and communal identity.

Characteristics

For women, plain dress typically means a long dress of a solid, dark or subdued colour, often with a cape and apron, and a head covering such as a prayer cap or bonnet. For men it typically means plain trousers with suspenders rather than a belt, a plain shirt and coat without lapels or buttons of certain kinds, a broad-brimmed hat, and in many groups a beard without a moustache. Specific rules are set by each congregation's Ordnung or discipline.

Rationale

Adherents cite scriptural injunctions to modesty and separation from worldly fashion. Plain dress avoids fasteners, patterns, and cuts considered prideful or wasteful, signals membership in the community, and removes clothing as a source of status competition.
