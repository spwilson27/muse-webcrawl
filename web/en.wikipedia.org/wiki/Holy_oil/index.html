Holy oil

Holy oil is oil that has been consecrated for religious use, most prominently in the anointing rites of Judaism and Christianity. In the Hebrew Bible, a specially compounded holy anointing oil (described in Exodus 30) was used to consecrate the Tabernacle, its vessels and the priests, and later to anoint kings of Israel. Christian churches use blessed oils in several sacraments and sacramentals.

In Christianity

The Roman Catholic Church uses three holy oils, blessed by the bishop at the Chrism Mass in Holy Week:

  • Sacred Chrism — olive oil mixed with balsam, used in baptism, confirmation and holy orders, and to consecrate altars and churches.
  • Oil of Catechumens — used in preparing candidates for baptism.
  • Oil of the Infirm — used in the anointing of the sick.

Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches consecrate myron (chrism) in an elaborate rite performed by patriarchs and hierarchs, sometimes only once in many years, using dozens of aromatic ingredients. Anglican and Lutheran churches also retain chrism and oils for baptism, confirmation and the anointing of the sick. In British coronations, the monarch is anointed with holy oil blessed for the ceremony; the anointing of Charles III in 2023 used oil consecrated in Jerusalem.

Other traditions

Anointing with oil also appears in Hindu practice (abhisheka), in ancient Egyptian temple ritual, and in modern movements such as Rastafari and some Pentecostal churches, where small vials of blessed oil are used in prayer for healing.
