Matvey Isaakovich Blanter (Russian: Матвей Исаакович Блантер; 10 February 1903 – 27 September 1990) was a Soviet composer, one of the most prominent creators of Soviet popular songs. He is best known as the composer of "Katyusha" (1938), one of the most famous Russian songs of the Second World War, with lyrics by Mikhail Isakovsky.

Life and career

Blanter was born in Pochep, in the Chernigov Governorate of the Russian Empire, into a Jewish family. He studied piano and violin in Kursk and Moscow, and composition at the Gnessin School of Music. During the 1920s he wrote popular dance music, foxtrots and early Soviet jazz-influenced songs, before turning to the mass song genre in the 1930s.

His song "Katyusha", about a girl singing for her beloved serving on the frontier, became immensely popular during the Great Patriotic War and gave its name to the Katyusha rocket launchers. Blanter composed numerous other well-known songs, including "The Football March" (still played at Russian football matches), "In the Forest by the Front Line", "My Beloved", and "Song of the United Nations" contenders. He also wrote music for films and operettas.

Honours

Blanter was named People's Artist of the USSR in 1975, received the Stalin Prize in 1946, and was awarded the title Hero of Socialist Labour in 1983.
