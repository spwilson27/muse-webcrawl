Buland Darwaza

The Buland Darwaza ("Door of Victory") is a massive gateway at Fatehpur Sikri, Uttar Pradesh, India, built in 1601 by the Mughal emperor Akbar to commemorate his victory over Gujarat. At about 54 metres (177 ft) high, it is among the largest gateways in the world and a celebrated example of Mughal architecture.

Description

Built of red and buff sandstone with marble inlay, the gateway forms the southern entrance to the Jama Masjid courtyard. A broad flight of steps leads up to its central arched iwan, flanked by smaller arches and topped by chhatris. An inscription quotes Jesus (Isa) in the Islamic tradition: "The world is a bridge; pass over it, but build no houses upon it."

History

Akbar founded Fatehpur Sikri as his capital in 1571 and added the Buland Darwaza after the Gujarat campaign of 1573, though its completion is dated to 1601. The city was later abandoned, partly over water supply, but the gateway remains its most imposing monument.
