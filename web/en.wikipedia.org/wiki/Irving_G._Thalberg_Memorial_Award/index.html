Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award

The Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award is an honorary Academy Award presented periodically by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences to "creative producers, whose bodies of work reflect a consistently high quality of motion picture production". It is named after Irving Thalberg, the legendary head of production at Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer in the 1920s and 1930s.

History

The award was first presented at the 10th Academy Awards in 1938 to Darryl F. Zanuck. Unlike the Oscar statuette given in competitive categories, the Thalberg Award takes the form of a bust of Thalberg himself. It is not awarded every year; recipients are chosen by the Academy's Board of Governors.

Recipients

Recipients have included David O. Selznick, Walt Disney, Hal B. Wallis, William Wyler, Alfred Hitchcock, Ingmar Bergman, Billy Wilder, Steven Spielberg, George Lucas, Clint Eastwood, John Calley, Dino De Laurentiis and Francis Ford Coppola.
