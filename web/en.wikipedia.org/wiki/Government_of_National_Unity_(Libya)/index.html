The Government of National Unity (Arabic: حكومة الوحدة الوطنية, Ḥukūmat al-Waḥdah al-Waṭanīyah) is the internationally recognised interim government of Libya, formed in 2021 under a United Nations-sponsored political process to unify the institutions divided since the Second Libyan Civil War.

Formation

The Libyan Political Dialogue Forum, convened by the UN Support Mission in Libya, selected a new executive authority in February 2021: Mohamed al-Menfi as head of a three-member Presidential Council and Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh as prime minister. On 10 March 2021 the House of Representatives, meeting in Sirte, granted the Government of National Unity a vote of confidence, formally replacing the Tripoli-based Government of National Accord and the rival administration in the east.

Mandate and challenges

The government was tasked with administering the country and preparing for national elections originally scheduled for 24 December 2021. Those elections were postponed indefinitely amid disputes over electoral laws and candidate eligibility, and the unity government remained in office beyond its intended transitional period. In 2022 the eastern-based House of Representatives appointed a rival Government of National Stability under Fathi Bashagha (later Osama Hamada), reviving the division of Libya between competing administrations.
