James Macpherson

James Macpherson (Gaelic: Seumas MacMhuirich; 27 October 1736 – 17 February 1796) was a Scottish writer, poet, literary collector and politician, known as the “translator” of the Ossian cycle of epic poems, which he claimed to have translated from ancient Gaelic sources.

Ossian

In 1760 Macpherson published Fragments of Ancient Poetry, followed by Fingal (1762) and Temora (1763), attributed to the bard Ossian. The works were an international sensation, influencing Goethe, Napoleon and Romanticism, but scholars including Samuel Johnson demanded the originals and accused Macpherson of forgery. Modern scholarship holds he wove genuine Gaelic ballad material into largely his own compositions.

Later life

Macpherson became an MP, defended his works until death, and was buried in Westminster Abbey. The controversy helped spur serious study of Gaelic oral tradition.
