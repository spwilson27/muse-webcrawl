Barnard College

Barnard College is a private women's liberal arts college in New York City, founded in 1889. It is one of the Seven Sisters colleges and is affiliated with Columbia University, whose undergraduate curriculum and degree it shares while maintaining its own faculty, administration, and campus in Morningside Heights, Manhattan.

History

Named for Columbia president Frederick A. P. Barnard, an advocate of coeducation, the college was founded after Columbia refused to admit women. Annie Nathan Meyer led the campaign for its charter. Barnard women have been able to take Columbia courses since the early 20th century; a 1983 affiliation agreement preserved Barnard when Columbia College went coeducational.

Academics and life

Students complete Barnard's Foundations curriculum and may cross-register across Columbia, join Columbia societies, and compete on Columbia's NCAA teams through the Columbia–Barnard Athletic Consortium. Notable alumnae include anthropologist Margaret Mead, writer Zora Neale Hurston, and choreographer Twyla Tharp. See also Seven Sisters.
