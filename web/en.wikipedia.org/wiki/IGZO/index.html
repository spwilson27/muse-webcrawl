IGZO

IGZO (indium gallium zinc oxide) is a semiconducting material composed of indium (In), gallium (Ga), zinc (Zn), and oxygen (O). It is used as the channel material in thin-film transistors (TFTs) for flat-panel displays, notably in the backplanes of LCD and OLED screens.

An IGZO-TFT display backplane; the material's transparency and mobility allow very high pixel densities.

Properties

IGZO is an amorphous oxide semiconductor. Its electron mobility of roughly 10–50 cm2/(V·s) is an order of magnitude higher than that of amorphous silicon, the traditional TFT material, while its off-state leakage current is extremely low. These properties allow displays to be made with smaller transistors (hence higher resolution and aperture ratio) and to hold a static image with far fewer refreshes, reducing power consumption.

History

The material was developed from research by Hideo Hosono and colleagues at the Tokyo Institute of Technology, with crystalline IGZO TFTs reported in 2003 and amorphous IGZO work published in Nature in 2004. Sharp Corporation began the first mass production of IGZO display panels in 2012 at its Kameyama plant and has marketed the technology under the IGZO name. The technology is widely licensed to other panel makers.

Applications

IGZO backplanes are used in tablet computers, smartphones, notebook and monitor displays, and professional reference monitors, as well as in some OLED televisions. The low leakage enables variable and very low refresh rates for static content, a feature exploited in power-saving "Idling Stop" LCD modes.
