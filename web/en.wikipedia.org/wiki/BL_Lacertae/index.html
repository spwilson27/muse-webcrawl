BL Lacertae

BL Lacertae or BL Lac is a highly variable, aggressive active galactic nucleus (AGN) located in the constellation Lacerta. It was discovered by Cuno Hoffmeister in 1929, who mistook it for an irregular variable star because of its rapid brightness changes and featureless optical spectrum; it accordingly received a variable-star designation. Only in 1968 was it identified with a bright radio source, and its true extragalactic nature recognised.

Characteristics

BL Lacertae is the prototype of the class of AGN known as BL Lacertae objects (or BL Lacs), a subclass of blazars. These objects are characterised by rapid and large-amplitude flux variability, strong optical polarisation, and nearly featureless spectra lacking strong emission or absorption lines. The observed emission is dominated by a relativistic jet pointed almost directly at the Earth, so that relativistic beaming amplifies the radiation and compresses its variability timescales.

Host galaxy

The AGN is embedded in a bright elliptical host galaxy with an absolute magnitude of about −22.9. The galaxy's redshift, z = 0.069, corresponds to a distance of roughly 900 million light-years. Long-term monitoring shows flares across the electromagnetic spectrum, from radio waves to gamma rays.
