An athletic field, also called a playing field or pitch, is an outdoor area of ground on which field sports are played. Fields are typically surfaced with natural grass or artificial turf, and marked with lines according to the laws of the relevant sport. Indoor equivalents include courts (for basketball and tennis), rinks (for ice hockey) and arenas.

Types

Different sports require fields of different shapes and dimensions:

  • Association football — a rectangular pitch, internationally 105 m × 68 m.
  • American football — a field 120 yards long including end zones, 53⅓ yards wide.
  • Baseball — a diamond-shaped infield with an outfield whose dimensions vary by park.
  • Cricket — an oval ground with a rectangular pitch 22 yards long at its centre.
  • Rugby — a rectangular field up to 144 m long including in-goal areas.
  • Field hockey and lacrosse — rectangular fields with sport-specific markings.

Surface

Natural grass remains the preferred surface for many professional sports, but it requires intensive maintenance, irrigation and rest periods. Artificial turf, introduced at the Houston Astrodome in 1966 under the name AstroTurf, offers durability and all-weather play; modern third-generation turf uses longer fibres and rubber infill to approximate grass. Hybrid systems combine natural grass with synthetic reinforcement.

Maintenance

Groundskeeping includes mowing, aeration, overseeding, fertilisation, line marking and drainage management. In professional venues, heating and grow-light systems may be used to sustain grass quality through winter.
