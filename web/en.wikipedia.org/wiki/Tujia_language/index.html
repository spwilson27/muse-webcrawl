Tujia language

Tujia
Native to: China
Region: Hunan, Hubei, Chongqing, Guizhou
Family: Sino-Tibetan (Tujia)

The Tujia language (Tujia: Bifzivkar) is a language spoken by the Tujia people in the Wuling Mountains of south-central China, at the junction of Hunan, Hubei, Chongqing and Guizhou. Although the Tujia ethnic population numbers several million, fluent speakers of the language are estimated at only around 70,000, and the language is considered severely endangered.

Classification

Tujia is a Sino-Tibetan language, but its heavy borrowing from Chinese and possible substrate influences make its precise position within the family difficult to establish; it is usually placed in its own Tujia branch. Two mutually unintelligible varieties, Northern and Southern Tujia, are recognised, with Northern Tujia having the great majority of speakers.

Status

Most Tujia now speak Southwestern Mandarin. Language loss accelerated during the 20th century, and documentation and revitalisation efforts have been undertaken by Chinese linguists and local cultural authorities.
