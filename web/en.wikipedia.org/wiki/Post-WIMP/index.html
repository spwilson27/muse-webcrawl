Post-WIMP

Post-WIMP user interfaces are interfaces that depart from the classic WIMP paradigm — Windows, Icons, Menus, and Pointer — which dominated personal computing from the Xerox Alto and Apple Macintosh onward. The term gained prominence with a 1999 special issue of ACM Interactions and Andries van Dam's essay "Post-WIMP User Interfaces," arguing that the desktop metaphor had reached its limits for 3D graphics, collaboration, and new input devices.

Motivations

Critics noted that WIMP interfaces assume a single user, a 2D screen, sequential mouse input, and tasks modelled on office documents. They scale poorly to three-dimensional data, large information spaces, multiple simultaneous users, and mobile or immersive contexts. Post-WIMP research therefore explored direct manipulation of 3D objects, multitouch and gesture, speech recognition, tangible user interfaces, wearable computing, and virtual and augmented reality.

Characteristics

Post-WIMP interfaces typically rely on one or more of: non-command-based or continuous interaction, simultaneous multi-device input, recognition-based input (speech, handwriting, gesture), embedded and context-aware computing, and collaboration support. The multitouch interfaces popularized by the iPhone (2007) are often cited as the mass-market arrival of post-WIMP ideas in mobile computing, though desktop computing has largely retained WIMP conventions.

Criticism

Skeptics observe that WIMP has proven remarkably durable, that gesture vocabularies lack standards and discoverability, and that many "post-WIMP" designs remain niche. The term now functions mainly as a research banner for interaction beyond the desktop metaphor.
