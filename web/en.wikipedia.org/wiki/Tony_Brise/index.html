Anthony William Brise (28 March 1952 – 29 November 1975) was an English racing driver who competed in Formula One during the 1975 season. Regarded as one of the most promising British drivers of his generation, he was killed, along with team owner Graham Hill and several members of the Embassy Hill team, in a plane crash in November 1975.

Career

Brise began racing in karting as a teenager and quickly moved into single-seaters, winning British Formula Ford and Formula Three championships. His performances attracted the attention of Frank Williams, who gave him his Formula One debut at the 1975 Spanish Grand Prix at Montjuïc, substituting for the injured Jacques Laffite. Brise finished seventh in a chaotic, shortened race.

He soon joined Graham Hill's Embassy Hill team, driving the Hill GH1. He scored his first and only World Championship point with sixth place at the Swedish Grand Prix at Anderstorp, and impressed with competitive qualifying performances and a fine drive in the wet at the Dutch Grand Prix, where he ran as high as third before spinning.

Death

On 29 November 1975, Brise was travelling aboard a Piper Aztec aircraft piloted by Graham Hill, returning from a test session at the Circuit Paul Ricard in France. In thick fog, the plane crashed into trees while attempting to land at Elstree Airfield in Hertfordshire, killing all six people on board: Hill, Brise, team manager Ray Brimble, designer Andy Smallman, and mechanics Terry Richards and Tony Alcock. Brise was 23 years old. The accident effectively ended the Embassy Hill team.
