Saho people

The Saho are a Cushitic ethnic group native to the Horn of Africa. They inhabit the coastal lowlands and escarpment country of Eritrea — particularly the Southern Red Sea and Northern Red Sea regions — and extend into northern Ethiopia. Their language, Saho, is a Lowland East Cushitic language closely related to Afar, and the two are sometimes treated as a Saho–Afar dialect continuum.

Society and livelihood

Traditionally the Saho are semi-nomadic pastoralists herding cattle, sheep, goats and camels, moving seasonally between the coastal plains and the highland escarpment; some groups practise agriculture on terraced slopes. Society is organised into tribes and sub-clans, historically eleven principal tribal groupings, each with its own grazing territories and customary law (mada). Elders and elected chiefs mediate disputes.

Religion and history

Most Saho are Sunni Muslims, Islam having spread through trade with Arabia across the Red Sea; there is also a Christian minority, including converts associated with the Irob community of the border highlands. The Saho homeland lies astride the ancient trade routes between the Red Sea port of Massawa and the Ethiopian highlands, and Saho groups served as caravan traders and middlemen. The Eritrea–Ethiopia border war (1998–2000) cut through Saho territory and displaced many families.

Language

Saho is written in both Latin and Ge'ez scripts. It is one of the working languages of Eritrea used in primary education and media in Saho areas.
