Geoff Johns

Geoff Johns
Born: January 25, 1973, Detroit, Michigan, U.S.
Occupation: Comic book writer, screenwriter, film and television producer
Areas: Writer, producer

Geoff Johns (born January 25, 1973) is an American comic book writer, screenwriter, and film and television producer. He is best known for his work with DC Comics, including long runs on The Flash, Green Lantern, Teen Titans, Justice Society of America, Aquaman and Superman, and for the crossovers Infinite Crisis, Blackest Night, Flashpoint and Doomsday Clock. Johns served as Chief Creative Officer and later President of DC Entertainment, and co-founded the production company Mad Ghost Productions.

Early life and career

Johns was born in Detroit, Michigan, and grew up in the suburbs of Clarkston and Gross Pointe. He studied media arts, screenwriting, film production and film theory at Michigan State University, graduating in 1995. After moving to Los Angeles he interned for director Richard Donner, working on films such as Conspiracy Theory and Lethal Weapon 4; Donner's production of Superman: The Movie had been a formative influence. His first comics work came with Stars and S.T.R.I.P.E. (1999) for DC, featuring the character Stargirl, whom he based on his late sister Courtney.

Major works

Johns took over The Flash in 2000, writing Wally West for five years and building up the Rogues. His 2005–2013 run on Green Lantern, beginning with Green Lantern: Rebirth, restored Hal Jordan and expanded the mythology with the emotional spectrum and the Sinestro Corps War, culminating in Blackest Night (2009). He wrote the sequels and events Infinite Crisis (2005), Flashpoint (2011) — which rebooted the DC Universe into the New 52 — and Doomsday Clock (2017–2019), a sequel to Watchmen. His runs on Aquaman, Justice League and Batman: Earth One were also widely acclaimed.

Film and television

Johns has written episodes of Smallville, Arrow and The Flash, and was a co-developer of the television series The Flash (2014). In film he co-wrote Wonder Woman (2017), Aquaman (2018) and Wonder Woman 1984 (2020), and created the Stargirl television series. In 2018 he stepped down as DC's chief creative officer to focus on writing and producing through Mad Ghost Productions, including work with his own imprint at Image Comics, the "Ghost Machine" collective launched in 2023.
