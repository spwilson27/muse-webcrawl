Complexometric titration

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A complexometric titration is a form of volumetric analysis in which the formation of a coloured complex is used to indicate the end point of a titration. Complexometric titrations are particularly useful for the determination of metal ions in solution. The titrant is a chelating agent that binds the metal ion in a well-defined stoichiometry, usually 1:1.

EDTA titrations

The most widely used titrant is ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), a hexadentate ligand that forms stable, water-soluble chelates with most metal ions. EDTA titrations were developed in the 1940s, notably by Gerold Schwarzenbach, and made the routine quantitative analysis of metals such as calcium, magnesium, zinc and lead straightforward. Because the fraction of EDTA present as the fully deprotonated, metal-binding form depends on pH, titrations are performed in buffered solutions, and conditional formation constants are used to judge feasibility.

Indicators

Metallochromic indicators change colour when they bind or release the metal ion. Eriochrome Black T, used for calcium and magnesium (water hardness) determinations, is wine-red when complexed with metal ions and blue when free. Other common indicators include murexide, calcein, xylenol orange and PAN.

Types of titration

  • Direct titration — the analyte is titrated directly with EDTA.
  • Back titration — an excess of EDTA is added and the remainder titrated with a standard metal solution; used when the analyte reacts slowly or precipitates.
  • Replacement (displacement) titration — the analyte displaces a metal from its EDTA complex, and the freed metal is titrated.
  • Indirect titration — used for anions, such as sulfate, that can be precipitated with a measured excess of a metal ion.

Masking

Masking agents such as cyanide, fluoride or triethanolamine can be added to bind interfering ions so that only the analyte is titrated, allowing selective determinations in mixtures.

Applications

The best-known application is the determination of water hardness (Ca²⁺ + Mg²⁺). Complexometric titrations are also standard in pharmaceutical analysis, electroplating bath control, and food and clinical laboratories.
