Clyde Dam

Clyde Dam
Location: Clyde, Otago, New Zealand
Construction began: 1977
Opening date: 1993
Type: Concrete gravity dam
Impounds: Clutha River
Creates: Lake Dunstan
Installed capacity: 464 MW

The Clyde Dam, on the Clutha River (Mata-Au) near the town of Clyde in Otago, New Zealand, is a concrete gravity hydroelectric dam. It is the third largest hydroelectric station in New Zealand, with an installed capacity of 464 megawatts from four 116 MW turbines.

Construction

Construction began in 1977 as part of the "Think Big" energy projects but was plagued by delays and cost escalation, including the discovery of an active fault beneath the site that required a large slip joint in the dam structure. The reservoir, Lake Dunstan, was filled in stages between 1992 and 1993, flooding the original township area of Cromwell and orchards and forcing the relocation of State Highway 8. The project was controversial for its environmental impact and expense, and contributed to the introduction of the Resource Management Act 1991. The dam is operated by Contact Energy.
