Vienna uprising

The Vienna uprising of October 1848 was the last revolutionary insurrection of the Austrian Revolution of 1848. On 6 October 1848, troops intended for Hungary mutinied and Viennese workers, students and National Guards prevented their departure; War Minister Count Theodor Baillet von Latour was lynched by the mob.

Course

Emperor Ferdinand I and the court fled to Olmütz. Imperial forces under Prince Alfred of Windischgrätz and Croatian Ban Josip Jelačić besieged the capital; after heavy bombardment and fighting, Vienna capitulated on 31 October. Robert Blum, a deputy of the Frankfurt Parliament, was executed on 9 November despite parliamentary immunity.

Aftermath

The suppression ended the revolution in Austria proper. Ferdinand abdicated on 2 December in favour of his nephew Franz Joseph I, inaugurating a decade of neo-absolutism.
