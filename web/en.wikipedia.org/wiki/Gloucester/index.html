Gloucester

Gloucester is a cathedral city and the county town of Gloucestershire in south-west England, on the River Severn, with a population of about 120,000–130,000.

History

Founded by the Romans as Glevum around AD 97, Gloucester became an important Saxon town and the burial place of Edward II in its abbey, now Gloucester Cathedral — famous for its fan-vaulted cloisters used as a filming location for Hogwarts corridors. Robert Raikes pioneered Sunday schools here in the 1780s.

Economy and landmarks

The historic docks, the most inland port in England via the Gloucester and Sharpness Canal, have been regenerated with the National Waterways Museum. The city is also known for Gloucester Rugby (Kingsholm), Double Gloucester cheese, and the tale of Dr Foster from the nursery rhyme.
