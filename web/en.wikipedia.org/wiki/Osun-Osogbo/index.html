Osun-Osogbo

Osun-Osogbo refers to the worship of the Yoruba goddess (orisha) Osun in the city of Osogbo, capital of Osun State, Nigeria, and most famously to the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a dense forest along the Osun River on the outskirts of the city.

The grove, one of the last surviving areas of primary high forest in southern Nigeria, contains shrines, sanctuaries, sculptures and artworks dedicated to Osun and other Yoruba deities. It is the site of the annual Osun-Osogbo Festival, held in August, which draws thousands of devotees and tourists and culminates in processions to the grove led by the Ataoja of Osogbo and a maiden carrying a sacred calabash.

In the 20th century the Austrian-born artist and priestess Susanne Wenger (Adunni Olorisha) worked with local artists of the New Sacred Art movement to restore the grove's shrines with monumental sculptures, helping protect it from encroachment. The Sacred Grove was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005.
