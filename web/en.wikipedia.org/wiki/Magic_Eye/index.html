Magic Eye

Magic Eye is a series of books and images that use autostereograms — two-dimensional patterns that, when viewed with the eyes diverged or converged in a particular way, reveal a hidden three-dimensional scene. The books, first published in the early 1990s, became a popular cultural phenomenon, with millions of copies sold worldwide.

How it works

A Magic Eye image is a random-dot or textured pattern in which a depth map is encoded by horizontal shifts. When the viewer relaxes the eyes as if looking through the image at a distant point (wall-eyed viewing), the brain fuses shifted elements and perceives shapes floating at different depths. Some viewers instead use the cross-eyed method on images designed for it.

History

The underlying technique descends from random-dot stereograms developed by Béla Julesz in the 1960s and single-image stereograms refined by Christopher Tyler and others. The Magic Eye books, beginning in 1993 from N.E. Thing Co. and publisher Andrews McMeel, popularised the autostereogram for a mass audience; the images appeared in newspapers, posters and merchandise throughout the mid-1990s.
