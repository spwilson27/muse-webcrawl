The main mound at Knowth, ringed by satellite passage tombs

Knowth (Irish: Cnogba) is a Neolithic passage grave complex in the Boyne Valley, County Meath, Ireland. It consists of one very large mound and seventeen smaller satellite tombs, and forms part of the Brú na Bóinne archaeological landscape together with Newgrange and Dowth, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

Description

The great mound, about 67 metres across and 12 metres high, covers two independent passages, one entering from the east and one from the west, each leading to its own cruciform or undifferentiated chamber. Unusually, the two passages do not meet. Knowth contains more than a third of all known megalithic art in Europe — some 200 decorated stones, including kerbstones carved with spirals, lozenges and serpentiform motifs. Finds from excavations led by George Eogan from 1962 onward included a carved flint macehead and evidence of Grooved Ware settlement.

History

The tombs were built around 3200 BC, in the same great burst of activity as Newgrange. The site remained significant for millennia: it was used in the early Christian period, fortified as a ringfort, adopted as a royal seat of the kings of Brega, and occupied and reworked by the Normans, after which the mound gradually fell into obscurity until modern excavation revealed its scale.

Access

Knowth is managed by the Office of Public Works and is visited from the Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre; visitors can walk around and onto the main mound and enter part of the eastern passage area, but the chambers themselves are not open to the public.
