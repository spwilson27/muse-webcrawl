The Conservatoire de Paris (French: [kɔ̃sɛʁvatwaʁ də paʁi]), officially the Conservatoire national supérieur de musique et de danse de Paris (CNSMDP), is a French grande école for music and dance, founded in 1795. It is one of the most prestigious conservatories in the world, located in the Parc de la Villette in the 19th arrondissement of Paris.

History

The Conservatoire was created in 1795 by merging the École Royale de Chant (founded 1784) with the music school of the National Guard, as a revolutionary institution to train musicians for public service. Bernard Sarrette was its founding administrator, and François-Joseph Gossec among its early inspectors. Luigi Cherubini became director in 1822 and raised its standards during a long tenure.

The Conservatoire's famous competition system, the concours, awarded premiers prix by instrument. Its Prix de Rome in composition sent laureates to study in Rome; winners included Berlioz, Gounod, Massenet, Debussy and Ravel's rivals — Ravel himself famously failed to win despite five attempts, a scandal that led to the director's resignation in 1905. In 1946 the drama section split off to form a separate conservatory (CNSAD). In 1990 the music conservatory moved to a purpose-built complex at La Villette designed by Christian de Portzamparc.

Teaching

Admission is by competitive audition and is tuition-free for admitted students, consistent with French public grandes écoles. Studies cover instrumental and vocal performance, composition, conducting, musicology, pedagogy, jazz, early music, sound engineering (formation supérieure aux métiers du son) and dance (classical and contemporary). Degrees follow the bachelor–master–doctorate structure in partnership with universities.

Notable people

Directors have included Cherubini, Auber, Ambroise Thomas, Théodore Dubois, Gabriel Fauré, Henri Busser, Claude Delvincourt, Marcel Dupré and, in the modern era, Alain Louvier and Bruno Mantovani. Alumni include composers Hector Berlioz, Claude Debussy, Maurice Ravel (as student), Olivier Messiaen, Pierre Boulez, and many leading performers and conductors.
