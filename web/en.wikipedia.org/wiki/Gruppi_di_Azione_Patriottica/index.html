The Gruppi di Azione Patriottica (GAP; "Patriotic Action Groups") were small urban guerrilla units of the Italian resistance during the Second World War, operating mainly in cities of German-occupied northern and central Italy between the armistice of September 1943 and the liberation of April 1945. They were formed on the initiative of the Italian Communist Party but included fighters of other anti-fascist political allegiances.

Operations

GAP units — typically cells of three or four fighters, known as gappisti — carried out bombings, sabotage, and assassinations of German officers and Fascist officials in cities such as Rome, Milan, Turin, Genoa, Bologna, and Florence. Their best-known action was the Via Rasella attack of 23 March 1944 in Rome, in which a GAP group led by Rosario Bentivegna and Carla Capponi detonated a bomb against a column of German military police, killing 33; the Germans retaliated with the Ardeatine massacre of 335 prisoners. In Turin and Milan, GAP and the related Squadre di Azione Patriottica (SAP) supported the general insurrection of April 1945 that liberated the northern cities before the arrival of Allied forces.

Organisation and legacy

The GAP operated in strict clandestinity, coordinated by provincial commands under the political direction of the Committee of National Liberation. Many gappisti were captured, tortured, and executed. Their actions, and especially Via Rasella, remained subjects of historical and political debate in postwar Italy.
