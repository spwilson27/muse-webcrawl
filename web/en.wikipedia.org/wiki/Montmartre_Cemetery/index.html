Montmartre Cemetery

The Cemetery of Montmartre (French: Cimetière de Montmartre) is a cemetery in the 18th arrondissement of Paris, France, opened in 1825. Occupying about 11 hectares in a former quarry below the Butte Montmartre, it is the third largest necropolis in Paris after Père Lachaise and Montparnasse, and is known for its shady avenues, elaborate funerary monuments, and population of semi-feral cats.

History

During the French Revolution the site's gypsum quarries served as mass graves. The cemetery was formally established in 1825 on land north of the old Farmers-General wall. A bridge, the Pont Caulaincourt (built 1888), crosses its western end, a feature unusual among Parisian cemeteries.

Notable burials

Interments include the composers Hector Berlioz and Jacques Offenbach, the writers Alexandre Dumas fils, Stendhal and Émile Zola (whose remains were later transferred to the Panthéon), the painter Edgar Degas, the dancer Vaslav Nijinsky, the singer Dalida, and the filmmaker François Truffaut. The tomb of Alphonsine Plessis, the real-life inspiration for La Dame aux Camélias and La traviata, is among its most visited.
