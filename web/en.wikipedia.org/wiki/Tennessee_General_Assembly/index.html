Tennessee General Assembly

The Tennessee General Assembly is the state legislature of the U.S. state of Tennessee. It is a bicameral body consisting of the 33-member Tennessee Senate and the 99-member Tennessee House of Representatives. The General Assembly meets in the Tennessee State Capitol in Nashville.

Composition and terms

Senators serve four-year terms, with roughly half the chamber elected every two years; representatives serve two-year terms. Members must be at least 21 years old for the House and 30 for the Senate (under the state constitution), citizens of the United States, residents of Tennessee for three years, and residents of their county or district for one year before election. The presiding officers are the Speaker of the Senate — who also holds the title Lieutenant Governor of Tennessee — and the Speaker of the House.

Sessions

The General Assembly convenes annually, typically from January into the spring. Under the Tennessee Constitution, organisational and legislative days are limited, making Tennessee's legislature effectively part-time. Each two-year assembly is numbered sequentially; the 114th General Assembly convened in 2025.

Powers

The General Assembly enacts state laws, passes the state budget, levies taxes, and may propose amendments to the Tennessee Constitution, which must then be approved by voters. It can override a governor's veto by a simple majority of both houses — a lower threshold than in many states, a consequence of the state constitution's veto provisions.

History

Tennessee's first General Assembly met in 1796, the year of statehood. The 1870 constitution, still in force (heavily amended), established much of the legislature's present structure. Following the 2010 elections, Republicans gained durable control of both chambers, holding supermajorities for much of the subsequent decade.
