Data erasure

Data erasure (also called data clearing or data wiping) is a software-based method of data destruction that aims to completely destroy all electronic data residing on a hard disk drive or other digital media by overwriting it with zeroes and ones, so that the data can no longer be recovered. Unlike physical destruction, erasure leaves the device functional and reusable, and unlike simple deletion or formatting, it removes data from all addressable locations, including remapped sectors where possible.

Methods

Erasure methods include overwriting with fixed patterns or random data, cryptographic erase (destroying the encryption keys of a self-encrypting drive), and firmware-based commands such as ATA Secure Erase. Older standards prescribed multiple overwrite passes — the once-famous "Gutmann method" proposed 35 — but modern guidance, such as NIST Special Publication 800-88, holds that a single overwrite pass is generally sufficient for modern magnetic media. Solid-state drives require different handling because wear levelling and over-provisioning hide data from the host; block erase and crypto erase are preferred.

Verification and regulation

Certified erasure produces an auditable record of the process for each device serial number, supporting compliance with data protection laws such as the GDPR, which grants a "right to erasure." Verified erasure enables safe resale and donation of IT equipment, reducing electronic waste compared with shredding.
