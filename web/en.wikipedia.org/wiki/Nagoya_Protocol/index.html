Nagoya Protocol

The Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization to the Convention on Biological Diversity, also known as the Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS), is a 2010 supplementary agreement to the 1992 Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD). It was adopted on 29 October 2010 in Nagoya, Japan, and entered into force on 12 October 2014, ninety days after the fiftieth ratification.

The CBD's tenth Conference of the Parties (COP 10) in Nagoya, where the Protocol was adopted.

Objective and mechanism

The Protocol implements the CBD's third objective: the fair and equitable sharing of benefits from the use of genetic resources, which developing and biodiversity-rich countries argued was essential to counter "biopiracy." Provider countries must establish clear, transparent rules for access to genetic resources, under prior informed consent (PIC), while user countries must ensure that resources used in their jurisdiction were accessed under mutually agreed terms (MAT). Monetary benefits may include royalties and licence fees; non-monetary benefits include research collaboration, technology transfer and training. The Protocol also covers traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources held by Indigenous and local communities, and created the Access and Benefit-sharing Clearing-House for information exchange.

Implementation and debate

More than 140 countries have ratified the Protocol; the United States, which never ratified the CBD, is not a party. Implementing measures include the EU's Regulation (EU) No 511/2014 and national ABS laws in countries such as Brazil, India and South Africa. Compliance checkpoints and due-diligence declarations are required of companies developing products from genetic resources, notably in pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, agriculture and biotechnology. Critics in research communities argue that bureaucratic compliance burdens basic and non-commercial science, including taxonomy and pathogen sharing — a concern sharpened during debates over access to influenza and SARS-CoV-2 samples, and by discussions of extending benefit-sharing to digital sequence information (DSI), on which parties agreed in 2022 to develop a multilateral mechanism, operationalised as the "Cali Fund" at COP 16 in 2024.
