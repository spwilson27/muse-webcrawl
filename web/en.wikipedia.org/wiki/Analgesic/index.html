An analgesic drug, also called simply a painkiller, is any member of the group of drugs used to achieve relief from pain. Analgesic drugs act in various ways on the peripheral and central nervous systems. They are distinct from anaesthetics, which temporarily affect, and in some instances completely eliminate, sensation.

Classification

  • Paracetamol (acetaminophen) — widely used for pain and fever; its mechanism is incompletely understood but it acts mainly centrally, with little anti-inflammatory effect.
  • Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) — including ibuprofen, aspirin and naproxen; they inhibit cyclo-oxygenase (COX) enzymes, reducing prostaglandin synthesis, and relieve pain, fever and inflammation. Side effects include gastrointestinal irritation and cardiovascular risk.
  • Opioids — including morphine, codeine, oxycodone and fentanyl; they act on opioid receptors and are used for moderate to severe pain. They carry significant risks of tolerance, dependence and respiratory depression.
  • Adjuvants — drugs such as certain antidepressants and anticonvulsants (gabapentinoids) used chiefly for neuropathic pain.

Use

The World Health Organization's analgesic ladder, originally developed for cancer pain, guides stepwise escalation from non-opioids to opioids according to pain severity. Combination products pairing paracetamol with an opioid or caffeine are common. Overuse of analgesics can itself cause harm, including medication-overuse headache and paracetamol-related liver toxicity.
