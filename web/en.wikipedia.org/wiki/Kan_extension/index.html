Kan extension

Kan extensions are universal constructions in category theory, introduced by Daniel Kan in 1958. Given functors K: C → D and F: C → E, a left Kan extension of F along K is a functor LanKF: D → E together with a natural transformation η: F ⇒ (LanKF)∘K, universal among such pairs; a right Kan extension is defined dually. Intuitively, the Kan extension is the best possible extension of F to the larger category D.

Formulas and examples

When E is (co)complete, Kan extensions can be computed pointwise as (co)limits over comma categories: the left Kan extension is a colimit ("integration") over all ways an object of D is approximated by images of objects under K. Many standard constructions are Kan extensions in disguise: left and right adjoints are Kan extensions along the identity-like functors of the adjunction's unit/counit diagrams, tensor products of functors, sheafification, and the Yoneda extension of a functor to presheaf categories.

Importance

Saunders Mac Lane famously remarked in Categories for the Working Mathematician that "all concepts are Kan extensions", reflecting the notion's unifying role: limits, colimits, adjoints and many universal properties in mathematics can be expressed through Kan extensions.
