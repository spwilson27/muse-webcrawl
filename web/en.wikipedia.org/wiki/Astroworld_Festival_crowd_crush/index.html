Astroworld Festival crowd crush

Astroworld Festival crowd crush
Date: November 5, 2021
Location: NRG Park, Houston, Texas, US
Deaths: 10
Injuries: hundreds (approx. 300 treated on site)

The Astroworld Festival crowd crush was a fatal crowd incident that occurred on November 5, 2021, at the Astroworld music festival in NRG Park in Houston, Texas, United States. Ten people died from compressive asphyxiation during a performance by the festival's headliner and organiser, the American rapper Travis Scott, and hundreds more were injured.

Background

Astroworld was an annual festival founded by Scott in 2018 and named after a former Houston amusement park. The 2021 edition, the festival's third, was attended by roughly 50,000 people. Earlier in the day, attendees had breached an entrance and overwhelmed security, and concerns about crowd management had been raised before the headline set began.

The incident

During Scott's headline performance, which began at about 9 p.m., the crowd surged toward the stage, compressing people in the front sections so tightly that many could not breathe or move. Concertgoers attempted to alert staff and climbed camera platforms to signal for help. The performance continued for more than half an hour after a mass casualty event was declared by emergency responders before the show was ended. The victims who died ranged in age from 9 to 27.

Aftermath

The second day of the festival was cancelled. Investigations by Houston authorities and extensive litigation followed; hundreds of lawsuits were consolidated in Texas courts, and the wrongful-death suits were later settled. In June 2023 a Harris County grand jury declined to indict Scott or other festival organisers on criminal charges. The incident prompted renewed scrutiny of crowd-safety planning, barricade design, and show-stop procedures at large music events.
