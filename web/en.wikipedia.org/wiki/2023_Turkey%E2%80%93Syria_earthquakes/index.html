2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes
Date6 February 2023
Magnitude7.8 Mw and 7.5 Mw
EpicentreNear Gaziantep, Turkey
Affected areasSouthern Turkey, northern Syria
CasualtiesMore than 59,000 deaths

The 2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes struck southern and central Turkey and northern and western Syria on 6 February 2023. The first earthquake, with a moment magnitude of 7.8, occurred west of the city of Gaziantep at 04:17 local time, causing widespread damage. A second earthquake of magnitude 7.5 struck roughly nine hours later, centred near Elbistan in Kahramanmaraş Province.

Damage and casualties

More than 59,000 deaths were confirmed: over 50,500 in Turkey and more than 8,400 in Syria, making the event one of the deadliest earthquakes of the twenty-first century. Tens of thousands of buildings collapsed across the affected region, including in Kahramanmaraş, Hatay, Adıyaman, Malatya and Gaziantep provinces, and in the Syrian cities of Aleppo and Idlib.

Tectonic setting

The earthquakes occurred along the East Anatolian Fault zone, a major strike-slip fault system accommodating the westward motion of the Anatolian Plate relative to the Eurasian and Arabian plates.

Response

Turkey declared a state of emergency in ten provinces, and an international search-and-rescue effort drew teams from dozens of countries. Humanitarian access to opposition-held areas of northwestern Syria was severely complicated by the ongoing Syrian civil war.
