Fontaine du Palmier

LocationPlace du Châtelet, Paris, France
Built1806–1808
DesignerFrançois-Jean Bralle (engineer); Gabriel Davoud (sculptor of the Victory)
Heightabout 22 m (72 ft)

The Fontaine du Palmier (1806–1808) is a monumental fountain in the Place du Châtelet in the 1st arrondissement of Paris, France. It was built under Napoleon Bonaparte to provide drinking water to the neighborhood and to commemorate his victories.

Description

The fountain consists of a circular basin about six meters across, from which rises a column modeled on a palm tree trunk, with palm fronds at its capital. Atop the column stands a gilded figure of Victory by the sculptor Gabriel Davoud, holding laurel wreaths. The column is ringed with bas-reliefs naming Napoleon's battles, including the Siege of Danzig, the Battle of Ulm, the Battle of Austerlitz, the Battle of the Pyramids, and the Battle of Jena–Auerstedt.

Later additions

Four statues representing Vigilance, Justice, Strength, and Prudence, by Louis-Simon Boizot, stand at the base. During the Second Empire, under Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris, the fountain was moved to make room for the widened boulevards, and four sphinx statues spouting water were added at the corners by Gabriel Davioud. It is the only surviving fountain of the fifteen built under Napoleon's 1806 decree still in its original purpose on its (slightly shifted) site.
