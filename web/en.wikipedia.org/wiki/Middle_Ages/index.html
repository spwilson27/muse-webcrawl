Middle Ages

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A medieval illuminated manuscript page with knights in armour, beside a Gothic cathedral interior]

In European history, the Middle Ages or medieval period lasted roughly from the late 5th to the late 15th century, from the fall of the Western Roman Empire to the beginning of the Renaissance and the Age of Discovery.

Historians commonly divide the period into the Early, High and Late Middle Ages. Population decline, invasions and de-urbanisation marked the Early Middle Ages; from about the year 1000 population and towns grew again in the High Middle Ages; the Late Middle Ages saw crises including the Great Famine and the Black Death.

Society and culture

Medieval society was dominated by the Roman Catholic Church, the feudal order of lords and vassals, monarchies, and later rising towns, guilds and universities (Bologna, Oxford, Paris). Scholastic philosophy, Gothic architecture and chivalric literature are lasting cultural products of the age.

End of the period

Events often used to mark the end include the fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Turks (1453), the invention of the movable-type printing press by Gutenberg (c. 1440) and the first voyage of Columbus (1492). The term 'Middle Ages' was coined by Renaissance humanists looking back.
