The Göta Canal (Swedish: Göta kanal) is a Swedish canal constructed in the early 19th century. The canal is 190.5 km (118.4 mi) long, of which 87.3 km (54.2 mi) were dug or blasted through the ground, with a width varying between 7 and 14 m (23–46 ft) and a maximum depth of about 3 m (10 ft). It has 58 locks and can accommodate vessels up to 32 m (105 ft) long and 7 m (23 ft) wide.

Together with the Trollhätte Canal and the Göta älv river, the Göta Canal forms a 614 km (382 mi) long waterway — the so-called "Blue Ribbon of Sweden" (Sveriges blå band) — linking the east coast at Mem on the Baltic Sea with Gothenburg and the Kattegat on the west coast, via Lakes Vänern and Vättern.

History

The idea of a canal across southern Sweden was first put forward in 1516 by Hans Brask, Bishop of Linköping. However, it was not until the start of the 19th century that Brask's proposals were put into action by Baltzar von Platen, a German-born former officer in the Swedish Navy. He organised the project and obtained the necessary financial and political backing. His plans attracted the enthusiastic support of the government and the new king, Charles XIII, who saw the canal as a way of kick-starting the modernisation of Sweden.

Construction began in 1810 and involved some 58,000 soldiers over 22 years. The canal was officially opened on 26 September 1832 by King Charles XIV John. Von Platen himself did not live to see the opening, having died in Norway three years earlier. The canal cost 9 million riksdaler, and was built with expertise partly supplied by Thomas Telford, the renowned Scottish civil engineer.

Economic impact

The canal never achieved the commercial importance its promoters expected, because the advent of the railway soon provided faster and cheaper transport. It was, however, briefly important for transporting iron ore, timber and other bulk goods across the country. Today the canal is primarily a tourist and recreational attraction; approximately two million people visit it in some form each summer, and around 4,000 boats pass through each year, mainly pleasure craft.

Description

The canal runs from Mem, near Söderköping on the Baltic Sea, westward through the lakes Roxen, Boren and Vättern to Sjötorp on Lake Vänern. Its highest point, 91.8 m (301 ft) above sea level, is at Lake Viken. The most famous flight of locks is at Berg, north of Linköping, where seven locks raise or lower boats 18.8 m (62 ft).
