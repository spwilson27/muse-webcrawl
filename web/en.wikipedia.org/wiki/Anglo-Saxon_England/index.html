Anglo-Saxon England

Anglo-Saxon England was a period in the history of England from the 5th century to the Norman Conquest in 1066, during which Germanic-speaking settlers — Angles, Saxons, Jutes and others — established kingdoms in post-Roman Britain and developed a distinctive culture, language (Old English) and eventually a unified kingdom of England.

Periodc. 410–1066
Major kingdomsWessex, Mercia, Northumbria, East Anglia, Kent, Sussex, Essex
LanguageOld English

Settlement and kingdoms

After Roman withdrawal, Germanic migration reshaped lowland Britain. By the 7th century a Heptarchy of kingdoms had formed. The Gregorian mission from 597 and Irish monks Christianized the Anglo-Saxons; Bede's Ecclesiastical History (731) is the key narrative source. Mercia dominated in the 8th century under Offa.

Vikings and unification

Viking raids from 793 and the Great Heathen Army from 865 destroyed most kingdoms except Wessex, defended by Alfred the Great. Alfred's successors reconquered the Danelaw, and Æthelstan is regarded as the first king of England from 927. Danish rule returned under Cnut (1016–1035).

Society and end

Society was organized around kings, ealdormen, thegns, ceorls and slaves, with shires, hundreds, witan councils, coinage and law codes. The period ends with Edward the Confessor's death, Harold Godwinson's brief reign, and William the Conqueror's victory at Hastings in 1066.
