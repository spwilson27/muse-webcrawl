The Nakdong River or Nakdonggang (Korean: 낙동강, pronounced [nak̚t͈oŋɡaŋ]) is the longest river in South Korea, flowing 510 kilometres from the Taebaek Mountains to the Korea Strait that separates Korea from Japan. It passes through major cities such as Daegu and Busan.

Course

The river originates at Eundae Peak in the Taebaek range near Taebaek city, Gangwon Province, and flows generally southward through the Gyeongsang provinces. Major tributaries include the Yeong River, Geumho River, Miryang River, and Nam River. Near its mouth it splits around the island of Eulsukdo in Busan, where an important migratory bird sanctuary and the Nakdong River Estuary Eco Center are located.

History

The Nakdong valley has been inhabited since prehistoric times and played a major role in the history of the Silla kingdom and the Nakdong fiefdoms of the Gaya confederacy. The river served as a vital transport artery throughout the Joseon dynasty. During the Korean War, the river formed a key defensive line: in the summer of 1950, United Nations and South Korean forces held the Pusan Perimeter along the Nakdong against North Korean assaults, including the Battle of the Nakdong Bulge, preserving the foothold that allowed the war effort to continue.

Ecology and development

The estuary wetlands support large numbers of migratory birds, including swans, spoonbills, and storks. In 2009–2011, the Four Major Rivers Project constructed a series of weirs on the Nakdong, intended for water management and flood control; the project has been controversial because of changes to water quality and ecosystems, including algal blooms.
