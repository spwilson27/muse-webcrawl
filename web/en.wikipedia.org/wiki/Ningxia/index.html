Ningxia

Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region
Capital: Yinchuan
Area: 66,400 km²
Population: about 7.2 million (2020)
Government: Autonomous region

Ningxia, officially the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, is an autonomous region of the People's Republic of China in the northwest of the country. It is one of the smallest provincial-level divisions in China by area, bordered by Inner Mongolia to the north and west, Shaanxi to the east, and Gansu to the south. Its capital and largest city is Yinchuan.

Geography

Ningxia is a largely arid region traversed by the Yellow River, which flows through its northern part and supports an irrigated agricultural plain often described as a "pearl beyond the Great Wall" in contrast to the surrounding desert. The region includes the Helan Mountains in the northwest, the Loess Plateau in the south, and parts of the Tengger and Mu Us deserts. The Western Xia Imperial Tombs — the "pyramids of China" — lie at the foot of the Helan range.

History

The area was the heartland of the Western Xia (Tangut) empire from 1038 until its destruction by the Mongols in 1227. It later formed part of Gansu province; Ningxia was created as a separate province in 1928 and abolished in 1954, then re-established in 1958 as an autonomous region for the Hui people, one of China's largest Muslim ethnic groups, who make up roughly a third of the population.

Economy

Ningxia's economy rests on irrigated agriculture — noted for goji (wolfberries), wheat, rice, and grapes for wine production — as well as coal mining and energy generation around Shizuishan and the Ningdong energy base. The region has become a significant producer of wine, and desert-control and photovoltaic projects in the Tengger Desert have drawn national attention.
