Additive group

An additive group is a group of which the operation is to be thought of as some sort of addition. It is usually an abelian group, and is typically denoted using the symbol "+" for its operation and the symbol "0" for its identity element. The inverse of an element x is then written −x, and repeated operation x + x + ⋯ + x (n times) is abbreviated nx.

This terminology is widely used with structures equipped with several operations, for referring to the structure obtained by forgetting all operations except addition. Examples include:

  • the additive group of the integers Z, of a vector space, of a ring, or of any other structure considered as a group with the operation of addition;
  • the additive group of a field, for example the rational numbers under addition;
  • the additive group underlying an elliptic curve, where the operation is customarily written with a "+" sign as well.

Underlying group of a ring

For any ring (R, +, ·), forgetting the multiplication operation yields the additive group (R, +), which is necessarily abelian because addition in a ring is commutative. Similarly, every module has an underlying additive abelian group, and every vector space is, by forgetting scalar multiplication, an additive group over its field — often a product of copies of the field's own additive group.

Notation

Writing a group additively rather than multiplicatively is a matter of convention that signals commutativity: multiplicative notation is the default for general, possibly non-abelian groups, while additive notation is reserved for abelian groups. The translation between the two conventions replaces the product xy with the sum x + y, the identity 1 with 0, inverses x−1 with negatives −x, and powers xn with multiples nx. Under this correspondence, direct sums of abelian groups generalize constructions first met for integers and vector spaces.

Examples

The integers under addition form the prototypical infinite cyclic additive group, generated by 1. The rational numbers under addition form an additive group that is not finitely generated. The group Z/nZ of integers modulo n is the standard finite example. Plane vectors under vector addition form the additive group R2, and the circle group can be viewed as the additive group R/Z of real numbers modulo integers.
