William Martin Murphy (6 January 1845 – 26 June 1919) was an Irish businessman, newspaper publisher, and nationalist politician. He built an extensive business empire that included tramways, department stores, hotels, and railways, and he owned the Irish Independent, Ireland's largest-circulation newspaper. He is best remembered as the employers' leader during the Dublin Lock-out of 1913, one of the most severe industrial disputes in Irish history.

Early life and business career

Murphy was born in Castletownbere, County Cork, and educated at Belvedere College in Dublin. Starting as a building contractor, he expanded into railway construction and then into electric tramways, unifying Dublin's tram system under the Dublin United Tramways Company. He promoted exhibitions, including the Irish International Exhibition of 1907, and developed interests in newspapers, acquiring the Irish Independent in 1904 and turning it into a mass-market daily.

Political career

A supporter of the Irish Parliamentary Party, Murphy sat in the House of Commons as MP for St Patrick's, Dublin, from 1885 to 1892. He was a moderate nationalist who favoured Home Rule achieved by constitutional means and opposed the more militant labour and republican movements of his day.

The Dublin Lock-out

In 1913 Murphy led more than 400 Dublin employers in locking out members of James Larkin's Irish Transport and General Workers' Union who refused to renounce the union. The dispute lasted from August 1913 to January 1914, left thousands of workers destitute, and ended without formal recognition of the union, though it permanently changed Irish industrial relations. Murphy was vilified by labour supporters and defended by business and much of the press he controlled.

Later life

Murphy opposed the Easter Rising of 1916 and the Irish Independent's editorial line calling for severe punishment of its leaders was widely resented. He declined a knighthood and other honours. He died in Dublin in 1919, leaving one of Ireland's largest business empires, including the Independent newspaper group, which his family continued to control.
