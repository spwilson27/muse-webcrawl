The Bronfman family is a Canadian family, known for its extensive business holdings. The family fortune originated with Seagram, the distilled beverage empire built by Samuel Bronfman (1889–1971) and his brothers. Descendants of Jewish immigrants who fled anti-Semitic pogroms in Czarist Russia, the Bronfmans became one of the most prominent and wealthy families in Canada.

Samuel Bronfman and Seagram

Samuel Bronfman and his brothers made their early fortune selling liquor, including during Prohibition in the United States, when Canadian distillers legally exported alcohol that was then smuggled across the border. In 1928 Samuel acquired the Joseph E. Seagram & Sons distillery and built it into one of the world's largest distillers, with brands including Crown Royal, Chivas Regal and Captain Morgan.

Later generations

Samuel's sons Edgar and Charles took over the business. Edgar Bronfman Sr. became a prominent public figure and served as president of the World Jewish Congress from 1981 to 2007. Charles Bronfman was a co-founder of the Montreal Expos baseball team. Edgar Bronfman Jr. diversified into entertainment, acquiring MCA/Universal, but the family's controlling stake was greatly diminished after Seagram's 2000 merger with Vivendi, a deal that ended in major losses.

Philanthropy

Members of the family have been major philanthropists, supporting Jewish causes, education and the arts through foundations such as the Andrea and Charles Bronfman Philanthropies and the Samuel and Saidye Bronfman Family Foundation.
