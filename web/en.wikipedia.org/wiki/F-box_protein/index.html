F-box protein

F-box proteins are proteins containing at least one F-box domain. The F-box domain is a protein structural motif of about 50 amino acids that mediates protein–protein interactions. It was first identified in cyclin F, hence its name. The motif interacts directly with the protein Skp1 of the SCF (SKP1–Cullin–F-box protein) ubiquitin ligase complex.

Function

F-box proteins function primarily as substrate adaptors of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes, which mark specific proteins with ubiquitin for degradation by the proteasome. By recognising distinct substrates, different F-box proteins confer specificity on the ubiquitination machinery and thereby regulate processes such as the cell cycle, signal transduction, transcription and development. Well-known examples include Skp2, β-TrCP (FBXW1) and Fbw7 (FBXW7), the last of which is a tumour suppressor that targets oncoproteins such as c-Myc and cyclin E for degradation.

Classification

In humans, F-box proteins are divided into three subfamilies based on their additional protein-interaction domains: FBXW proteins (containing WD40 repeats), FBXL proteins (containing leucine-rich repeats) and FBXO proteins (containing other or no recognisable domains). The human genome encodes roughly 70 F-box proteins.

History

The F-box motif was described in 1996 following the identification of sequence similarity between cyclin F and the Skp2-related proteins. Plant genomes encode especially large numbers of F-box proteins, where they play roles in hormone signalling, including auxin perception by the TIR1 F-box protein.
