Blues Breakers with Eric Clapton

The "Beano album", 1966

Blues Breakers, informally known as The Beano Album, is the debut album by the English blues rock band John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers, released in July 1966. It is credited to John Mayall with Eric Clapton and is widely regarded as one of the most influential British blues albums ever recorded.

Background

Guitarist Eric Clapton joined the Bluesbreakers in 1965 after leaving the Yardbirds, seeking a purer blues sound. On this album he played a 1960 Gibson Les Paul through a Marshall amplifier turned up to full volume, creating a thick, overdriven tone that became a blueprint for rock guitar. The band included John McVie on bass and Hughie Flint on drums, with Mayall on vocals, harmonica, and organ.

Track listing

The album mixes Mayall originals such as "All Your Love" with covers of Chicago blues standards including "Parchment Farm", "Ramblin' on My Mind" (Robert Johnson), "Hideaway" (Freddie King), and "Steppin' Out". Clapton also sang lead on "Ramblin' on My Mind", his first recorded vocal.

Legacy

The album reached number six on the UK charts and helped ignite the British blues boom. Its nickname comes from the cover photograph, in which Clapton reads a copy of the children's comic The Beano. In 2003 it was ranked among Rolling Stone's 500 greatest albums of all time. Clapton left soon after its release to form Cream.
