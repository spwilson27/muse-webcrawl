Anne Bonny

Bornc. 1697–1702, possibly County Cork, Ireland
DiedUnknown; after 1720
OccupationPirate
Piratical careerCaribbean, aboard the sloop of John "Calico Jack" Rackham

Anne Bonny (c. 1700 – unknown) was an Irish pirate who operated in the Caribbean in the early 18th century. She is one of the most famous female pirates of the Golden Age of Piracy, remembered for sailing with John "Calico Jack" Rackham alongside fellow pirate Mary Read.

Early life

The principal source for Bonny's life is Captain Charles Johnson's A General History of the Pyrates (1724), which mixes fact with embellishment. According to that account, she was born near Cork as the illegitimate daughter of a lawyer, emigrated with her family to South Carolina, and married a poor sailor before running away to Nassau in the Bahamas, then a pirate haven.

Piratical career

In Nassau, Bonny took up with Rackham, whose crew she joined — reportedly dressed as a man — after Rackham seized a sloop in 1720. Their brief cruise took several prizes around Jamaica. In October 1720 their ship was captured by a pirate hunter commissioned by the Governor of Jamaica. Bonny and Read were said to have fought on deck while the male crew hid below.

Trial and fate

The crew was tried in Jamaica and sentenced to hang, but Bonny and Read "pleaded their bellies" — both were pregnant — and received stays of execution. Read died in prison, probably of fever; Bonny's fate after 1720 is not recorded. She became, with Read, a lasting popular image of the female pirate, inspiring plays, novels and modern screen portrayals.
