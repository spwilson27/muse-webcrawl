Constructed script

Constructed scripts range from fictional alphabets to practical shorthand systems.

A constructed script is a new writing system specifically created by an individual or group, rather than having evolved naturally as part of a language or culture. Constructed scripts may be alphabets, syllabaries, logographies, featural scripts or ciphers, and are often created to accompany constructed languages.

Famous fictional scripts include J. R. R. Tolkien's Tengwar and Cirth for his Elvish languages, the Klingon script pIqaD from Star Trek, and the scripts of the video game world of Myst. Other constructed scripts were designed for practical purposes: Hangul was promulgated by King Sejong of Korea in the 15th century; Sequoyah created the Cherokee syllabary in the 19th century; and shorthand systems such as Pitman and Gregg, the Deseret alphabet, and the Shavian alphabet were deliberate inventions intended for real-world use.

Many constructed scripts have been encoded in the Unicode ConScript Unicode Registry, a voluntary registry assigning code points in the Private Use Areas for artificial scripts.
