The International Slavery Museum is a museum located in Liverpool, England, that explores the history and legacy of the transatlantic slave trade and slavery. It is part of National Museums Liverpool and is situated on the third floor of the Merseyside Maritime Museum building at the Albert Dock.

The museum opened on 23 August 2007, a date chosen to coincide with the annual International Day for the Remembrance of the Slave Trade and its Abolition, and with the bicentenary of the British abolition of the slave trade under the Slave Trade Act 1807. The opening formed part of Liverpool's commemorations of that bicentenary; the city had been a major slaving port in the eighteenth century, with Liverpool ships carrying a large share of Britain's enslaved African captives.

Exhibits

The museum's displays are organised around themes including life in West Africa before the slave trade, the Middle Passage across the Atlantic, enslavement and resistance in the Americas, abolition, and the continuing legacies of slavery, including racism and campaigns for human rights. Objects on display include shackles, branding irons, trade goods, ship models and contemporary artworks. A full-size reconstruction evokes the cramped conditions below decks on a slave ship.

The museum also addresses modern forms of slavery and human trafficking and hosts educational programmes, community exhibitions and events marking Slavery Remembrance Day each August.

Development

The galleries grew out of the Transatlantic Slavery Gallery opened in the Merseyside Maritime Museum in 1994, which was among the first permanent displays in a British national museum devoted to the subject. Plans were announced to expand the International Slavery Museum into the neighbouring Dr Martin Luther King Jr Building; in 2023 National Museums Liverpool secured funding toward the redevelopment of both the museum and the Maritime Museum as part of the transformation of Liverpool's waterfront.
