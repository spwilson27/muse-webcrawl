Brief (law)

A brief is a written legal document used in various legal adversarial systems that is presented to a court arguing why one party to a particular case should prevail. In modern practice the word is used in several distinct senses: a document submitted to a court by a party, a memorandum of law supporting a motion, or, in England and Wales, the instructions given by a solicitor to a barrister.

Appellate briefs in the United States

In American practice, a brief is the principal written argument filed in appellate litigation. The appellant's opening brief typically contains a table of contents, a table of authorities, a statement of the case and of the facts, a summary of argument, the argument itself — applying the law to the facts and contending that the court below erred — and a conclusion. The appellee files an answering brief, and the appellant may file a reply brief. Courts impose strict rules on length, formatting, typeface, and filing deadlines; under the Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure, a principal brief is generally limited to 13,000 words.

The cover colour of briefs filed in the Supreme Court of the United States follows a long-standing convention: the petitioner's brief on the merits has a blue cover, the respondent's a red cover, and an amicus curiae brief supporting neither party a green cover.

Amicus briefs

An amicus curiae ("friend of the court") brief is filed by a person or organization that is not a party to the case but has a strong interest in the subject matter. Amicus briefs supply the court with information, data, or argument beyond that provided by the parties, and are common in appellate and constitutional litigation. In the United States they must ordinarily be filed with the consent of the parties or by leave of the court.

Trial briefs and memoranda of law

At trial level, lawyers file briefs or memoranda of law in support of, or in opposition to, motions — for example a motion to dismiss or a motion for summary judgment. Such documents set out the relevant facts, identify the legal standard, and argue for the requested order.

United Kingdom usage

In England and Wales, a "brief" traditionally denotes the document by which a solicitor instructs a barrister (counsel) to appear in court or to advise, summarizing the facts, evidence, and the client's objectives. A barrister so instructed is said to be "briefed", and the fee endorsed on the document is the "brief fee".
