Ramelteon

Ramelteon, sold under the brand name Rozerem among others, is a medication used to treat difficulty falling asleep (onset insomnia). It was developed by Takeda Pharmaceutical Company and approved in the United States in 2005.

Mechanism

Unlike most hypnotics, which act on GABA receptors, ramelteon is a melatonin receptor agonist with high affinity for the MT1 and MT2 receptors in the suprachiasmatic nucleus, which regulate the sleep–wake cycle and circadian rhythms. Because it does not act on GABAergic systems, it lacks the dependence, rebound insomnia, and abuse potential associated with benzodiazepines and Z-drugs, and it is not a controlled substance in the United States.

Use and effects

Ramelteon is taken shortly before bedtime and reduces sleep latency modestly. Common side effects include somnolence, dizziness, fatigue, and nausea. It is metabolised primarily by CYP1A2, and its exposure is greatly increased by the antidepressant fluvoxamine, with which it should not be combined.
