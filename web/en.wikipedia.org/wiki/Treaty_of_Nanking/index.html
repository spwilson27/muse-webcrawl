Treaty of Nanking

Treaty of Nanking
Signed: 29 August 1842, Nanjing, aboard HMS Cornwallis
Parties: United Kingdom and Qing China
Context: End of First Opium War

The Treaty of Nanking (Nanjing) was an unequal treaty between the United Kingdom and Qing dynasty China, signed on 29 August 1842, ending the First Opium War and inaugurating China's 'century of humiliation' in later Chinese historiography.

Terms

China ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain in perpetuity, opened five treaty ports — Canton (Guangzhou), Amoy (Xiamen), Foochow (Fuzhou), Ningpo (Ningbo) and Shanghai — to British trade and residence, abolished the Cohong monopoly, agreed a fair tariff, and paid an indemnity of 21 million silver dollars, including compensation for destroyed opium. A supplementary treaty (the Bogue, 1843) added extraterritoriality and most-favoured-nation status.

Legacy

The treaty began the treaty-port system and was followed by similar treaties with the US, France and others. Hong Kong was returned to China in 1997. See First Opium War and Unequal treaties.
