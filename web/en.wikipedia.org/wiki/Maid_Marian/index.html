Maid Marian

Maid Marian is the heroine of the Robin Hood legend in English folklore, often portrayed as his lover. She did not appear in the earliest medieval ballads of Robin Hood, but became firmly attached to the legend in the early modern period, in part through her role in May Games festivities, where "Maid Marian" was a traditional character.

Origins

The name Marian may derive from May Day traditions and from French pastoral romances, notably Adam de la Halle's Jeu de Robin et Marion (c. 1283), in which a shepherdess named Marion is loved by a knight and by the shepherd Robin. A Marian figure appeared in English May Games by the late Middle Ages, and in the sixteenth century she was increasingly associated with Robin Hood's company in ballads, plays and pageants, including works connected with Anthony Munday's plays The Downfall and The Death of Robert, Earl of Huntington (1598).

Character

In later retellings Marian is often a noblewoman — sometimes identified as Matilda Fitzwalter — who joins the outlaws in Sherwood Forest, or a ward of the Sheriff of Nottingham who aids Robin from within the castle. Victorian and modern versions frequently make her an active, skilled archer and fighter in her own right, and she remains the principal female figure of the legend, appearing in nearly all film and television adaptations.
