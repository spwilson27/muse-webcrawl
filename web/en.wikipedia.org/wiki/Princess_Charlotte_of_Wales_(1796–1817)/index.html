Princess Charlotte Augusta of Wales (7 January 1796 – 6 November 1817) was the only child of the Prince Regent, the future King George IV, and his wife Caroline of Brunswick. She was second in line to the British throne and the expected future queen; her death in childbirth at the age of 21 threw the succession into crisis and led, indirectly, to the birth of Queen Victoria.

Early life

Charlotte was born at Carlton House, London, into the famously unhappy marriage of her parents, who separated within weeks of her birth. She was raised mainly at Warwick House and Windsor under governesses appointed by her grandfather George III, becoming a pawn in her parents' disputes. High-spirited and popular with the public, she was briefly engaged to William, Hereditary Prince of Orange, before breaking the engagement in 1814.

Marriage

On 2 May 1816 Charlotte married Prince Leopold of Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld at Carlton House. The marriage was happy; the couple settled at Claremont House in Surrey, and Leopold's calm influence steadied the princess. In a period of wartime austerity the couple's domestic contentment made them enormously popular.

Death and aftermath

After two miscarriages, Charlotte became pregnant again in 1817. On 3 November she went into labour; after a prolonged delivery of some 50 hours she gave birth to a stillborn son on 5 November and died in the early hours of 6 November, probably from postpartum haemorrhage. The nation went into deep mourning — shops closed, and it was said that every household in London had someone in black. Her accoucheur, Sir Richard Croft, shot himself three months later; the "triple obstetrical tragedy" prompted lasting reforms in obstetric practice.

Charlotte's death left George III without any legitimate grandchildren of marriageable generation, prompting the middle-aged royal dukes to rush into marriages in the "race for the crown" that produced Queen Victoria, born in 1819. Her widower Leopold later became the first King of the Belgians and advised his niece Victoria throughout her early reign.
