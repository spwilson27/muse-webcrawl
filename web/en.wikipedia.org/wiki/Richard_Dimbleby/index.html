Richard Dimbleby

Richard Dimbleby
Born: 25 May 1913, Richmond, Surrey
Died: 22 December 1965, London
Occupation: BBC journalist and broadcaster
Children: David Dimbleby, Jonathan Dimbleby

Frederick Richard Dimbleby (25 May 1913 – 22 December 1965) was an English journalist and broadcaster, widely acknowledged as one of the greatest figures in the history of British broadcasting. He was the BBC's first war correspondent and the corporation's leading commentator on state occasions and election nights.

War reporting

Dimbleby joined the BBC in 1936 and reported from the major crises of the late 1930s. During the Second World War he accompanied the RAF on bombing raids and landed with troops in Normandy; in April 1945 his harrowing report from the liberated Bergen-Belsen concentration camp, initially doubted by BBC managers, became one of the most famous broadcasts ever made.

Commentator

After the war Dimbleby became the voice of royal and national events, commentating on the funeral of George VI and the coronation of Elizabeth II in 1953, and anchoring the BBC's general election results programmes from 1950. From 1955 he chaired the current affairs programme Panorama. He fronted the famous 1957 April Fool "spaghetti tree" hoax broadcast with straight-faced aplomb.

Death and legacy

Dimbleby died of cancer in 1965, aged 52, having concealed his illness while continuing to broadcast. His sons David and Jonathan Dimbleby both became leading broadcasters, and the annual Richard Dimbleby Lecture was established in his memory.
