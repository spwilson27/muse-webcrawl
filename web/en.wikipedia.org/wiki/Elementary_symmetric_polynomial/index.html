Elementary symmetric polynomial

In mathematics, specifically in commutative algebra, the elementary symmetric polynomials are one type of basic building block for symmetric polynomials, in the sense that any symmetric polynomial can be expressed as a polynomial in elementary symmetric polynomials. That is, any symmetric polynomial P can be given by an expression involving only additions and multiplications of constants and elementary symmetric polynomials. There is one elementary symmetric polynomial of degree d in n variables for each non-negative integer d ≤ n, formed by adding together all distinct products of d distinct variables.

Examples

For variables X1, X2, X3, the elementary symmetric polynomials are: e1 = X1 + X2 + X3; e2 = X1X2 + X1X3 + X2X3; and e3 = X1X2X3. By convention e0 = 1.

Relation to polynomial roots

By Viète's formulas, the coefficients of a monic polynomial are (up to sign) the elementary symmetric polynomials in its roots: (x − r1)···(x − rn) = xn − e1xn−1 + e2xn−2 − ··· + (−1)nen. The fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials—that the elementary symmetric polynomials generate the ring of all symmetric polynomials and are algebraically independent—is a cornerstone of the classical theory, with the proof typically proceeding via a lexicographic-ordering algorithm related to Newton's identities, which connect the ek with power-sum symmetric polynomials.

Applications

Elementary symmetric polynomials arise in Galois theory (as the natural invariants of the symmetric group acting on roots), in algebraic geometry, in the theory of Schur polynomials and representation theory, and in invariant theory generally.
