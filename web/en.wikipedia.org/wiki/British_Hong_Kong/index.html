British Hong Kong was a colony and later a dependent territory of the United Kingdom from 1841 to 1997, apart from a period of Japanese occupation during the Second World War. The colony began with Hong Kong Island, ceded by Qing China under the Treaty of Nanking in 1842, and grew to include the Kowloon Peninsula (1860) and the New Territories, leased for 99 years in 1898. On 1 July 1997 sovereignty over the whole territory was transferred to the People's Republic of China, and Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region.

History

British forces occupied Hong Kong Island during the First Opium War, and the island was formally ceded in perpetuity by the Treaty of Nanking. The Convention of Peking added Kowloon and Stonecutters Island in 1860, and the Second Convention of Peking in 1898 leased the New Territories until 1997. Under British administration Hong Kong developed into a major free port and trading entrepôt.

Japan occupied Hong Kong from December 1941 until 1945. After the war, and especially after the Communist victory in mainland China, refugees flooded into the colony, fuelling rapid industrialisation in textiles and manufacturing from the 1950s. Serious riots occurred in 1967. From the 1970s, under Governor Murray MacLehose, social reforms expanded public housing, education and anti-corruption measures (including the creation of the ICAC in 1974), and Hong Kong grew into a global financial centre.

Handover

The impracticality of separating the ceded and leased portions led Britain and China to negotiate the territory's future. The Sino-British Joint Declaration of 1984 provided for the transfer of the whole of Hong Kong in 1997 under the principle of "one country, two systems", preserving its capitalist system and way of life for 50 years. The last governor, Chris Patten, introduced democratic reforms that were reversed after the handover ceremony on 30 June–1 July 1997.

Government

The colony was administered by a Governor appointed by London, advised by Executive and Legislative Councils. Direct elections to the Legislative Council were introduced only in 1991. The legal system was based on English common law, and the Hong Kong dollar was issued by commercial banks under the supervision of a currency board system from 1983.
