The Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture is a research library and cultural institution of the New York Public Library (NYPL), located at 515 Malcolm X Boulevard (Lenox Avenue) in Harlem, Manhattan. One of the world's leading repositories of material on the African diaspora, it holds more than 10 million items across five divisions.

History

The Center originated in 1925 as the Division of Negro Literature, History and Prints at the 135th Street branch library, a focus of the Harlem Renaissance. In 1926 the Carnegie Corporation purchased the private collection of Arturo Alfonso Schomburg — the Afro-Puerto Rican bibliophile and historian — containing some 5,000 books, 3,000 manuscripts, and artworks, and donated it to the library. Schomburg served as curator from 1932 until his death in 1938. The division was renamed in his honour in 1940, and the present facility opened in 1980, with a major renovation completed in 2017.

Collections

  • Research and Reference Division — general collections on the African diaspora
  • Jean Blackwell Hutson Research and Reference Division
  • Manuscripts, Archives and Rare Books Division — papers of figures including Malcolm X, Maya Angelou, and Ralph Ellison
  • Moving Image and Recorded Sound Division
  • Photographs and Prints Division — extensive photographic documentation of Black life
  • Art and Artifacts Division — including works from the Harlem Renaissance and beyond

Among its treasures are items associated with Frederick Douglass, books from the libraries of historic Black institutions, and the Malcolm X assassination artifacts used in research. The floor of the Center's lobby contains the "Rivers" cosmogram honouring Langston Hughes, whose ashes are interred beneath it.

Programs

The Center hosts exhibitions, lectures, the Schomburg's annual Black Liberation List, the Junior Scholars Program for youth, and the Lapidus Center for the Historical Analysis of Transatlantic Slavery. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2017 as part of the Schomburg Collection.
