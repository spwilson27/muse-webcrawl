Coywolf


An eastern coyote

A coywolf is a canid hybrid offspring of coyotes, eastern wolves, grey wolves, and dogs. Genetic studies show that most North American canids classified as coyotes — in particular the eastern coyote of the north-eastern United States and south-eastern Canada — carry substantial wolf and some domestic dog ancestry, acquired as coyotes expanded eastward through the Great Lakes region during the twentieth century.

Genetics and appearance

Eastern coyotes are typically larger than western coyotes, with broader skulls and stronger jaws reflecting wolf ancestry, enabling them to prey on white-tailed deer. Studies of specimens from the north-eastern United States have estimated ancestry on the order of 60–85% coyote, 8–25% wolf, and 8–11% dog, varying by region. The term "coywolf" is popular but not a formal taxonomic designation; scientists generally refer to the animals as eastern coyotes.

Behaviour and range

Coywolves occupy forests, farmland, suburbs, and even large cities such as Toronto and New York. They are adaptable omnivores and predators, hunting in small family groups for deer in winter while also taking rodents, rabbits, and carrion. Hybridisation debates continue over the taxonomic status of the eastern wolf and red wolf, both of which show coyote admixture.
