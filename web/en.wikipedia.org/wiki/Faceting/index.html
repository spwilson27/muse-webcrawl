Faceting

Faceting is the process of cutting a gemstone with flat faces (facets) at specific angles to maximise its brilliance, fire, and scintillation. A faceted stone is typically cut on a faceting machine, where a mast or handpiece holds the stone against a rotating lap charged with abrasive.

Process

The cutter first shapes a rough preform, then grinds and polishes the crown (top) and pavilion (bottom) facets according to a cutting diagram suited to the material's refractive index. Common styles include the round brilliant cut for diamonds and a wide variety of designs for coloured stones. Faceting contrasts with cabochon cutting, in which stones are polished into smooth domes.
