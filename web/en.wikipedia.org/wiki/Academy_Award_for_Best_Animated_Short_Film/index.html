Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film

The Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film is an Oscar presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences to the best animated short film of the year. The category has been awarded since the 5th Academy Awards in 1932, when it was known as Best Short Subject (Cartoon).

History

Walt Disney dominated the category in its first decade, winning eight consecutive awards beginning with Flowers and Trees (1932), the first cartoon produced in three-strip Technicolor. In the 1940s the Tom and Jerry cartoons of MGM, produced by Fred Quimby and directed by William Hanna and Joseph Barbera, won seven Oscars. Later decades rewarded a wider international field, including works from the National Film Board of Canada, Nick Park's Aardman Animations, Pixar, and independent filmmakers using stop-motion, CGI and hand-drawn techniques.

Notable winners

  • The Three Little Pigs (1933)
  • The Old Mill (1937)
  • Gerald McBoing-Boing (1950)
  • The Hole (1962)
  • Creature Comforts (1989)
  • The Wrong Trousers (1993)
  • Geri's Game (1997)
  • Piper (2016)

Rules

Eligible films must run no more than 40 minutes including credits and qualify through theatrical exhibition, festival awards or student competition wins. Since 2012 the award has gone to the film's director and key creative producers.
