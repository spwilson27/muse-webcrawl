Krk

Krk (pronounced [kr̩̂k]; Italian: Veglia; Latin: Curicta) is a Croatian island in the northern Adriatic Sea, located near Rijeka in the Bay of Kvarner and part of Primorje–Gorski Kotar County. With an area of about 405.78 km², Krk is roughly tied with Cres as the largest island in the Adriatic, and it is the most populous Adriatic island, with some 19,000–20,000 inhabitants in its seven municipalities. Its main settlement and namesake is the town of Krk.

Geography and climate

The island has a Mediterranean climate with mild winters and warm summers, modified by the cold bora wind in winter. The north-western part is relatively green and fertile, while the south-east is bare karst. The island is connected to the mainland by the Krk Bridge (formerly Tito's Bridge), a 1,430-metre reinforced-concrete arch bridge opened in 1980, then the longest concrete arch in the world, linking Krk to the coast near Kraljevica. Rijeka Airport is located on the island, near Omišalj.

History

Krk has been inhabited since prehistoric times and was known to the Romans as Curicta; the island was the site of a naval battle in 49 BC during Caesar's civil war. It later passed through Byzantine, Croatian, Venetian, Habsburg and Italian hands. Krk is of particular importance in Croatian cultural history as a centre of the Glagolitic script: the Baška tablet (c. 1100), one of the oldest surviving monuments of Croatian written language in Glagolitic script, was found near Baška on the island. The powerful Frankopan family, one of the great noble houses of medieval Croatia, had their seat on Krk.

Today the island's economy is dominated by tourism, with resorts at Malinska, Njivice, Baška and the town of Krk itself. An LNG terminal off Omišalj began operation in 2021.
