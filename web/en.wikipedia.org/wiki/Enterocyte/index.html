Enterocyte

Enterocytes, or intestinal absorptive cells, are simple columnar epithelial cells which line the inner surface of the small and large intestines. They are by far the most numerous cells of the intestinal epithelium, and their principal function is the absorption of nutrients — including sugars, amino acids, peptides, fatty acids, vitamins, and ions — from the gut lumen, together with the reabsorption of water and electrolytes.

Structure

The apical surface of an enterocyte is covered by densely packed microvilli forming a "brush border" that increases the absorptive surface area some twenty-fold. The microvillus membrane carries digestive enzymes such as lactase, sucrase-isomaltase, and peptidases that perform the final stages of digestion at the cell surface, and transporters including SGLT1 (sodium–glucose cotransporter) and GLUT5. Adjacent enterocytes are bound by tight junctions, which regulate paracellular movement and maintain the barrier between lumen and blood. Beneath the microvilli, a terminal web of actin anchors the structures.

Function

Enterocytes take up monosaccharides, amino acids, and small peptides apically and export them across the basolateral membrane into the portal blood. Dietary lipids are absorbed as fatty acids and monoglycerides, re-esterified inside the cell, packaged into chylomicrons, and discharged into the lacteals of the lymphatic system. Enterocytes also absorb vitamin B12 (in the terminal ileum, bound to intrinsic factor), bile salts for enterohepatic recirculation, iron regulated by the hormone hepcidin, and they secrete immunoglobulin A and antimicrobial peptides as part of gut immunity.

Life cycle

Enterocytes are born from stem cells in the crypts of Lieberkühn and migrate up the villus over three to five days, maturing as they go, before being shed from the villus tip into the lumen. This rapid turnover makes the intestinal epithelium one of the most quickly renewed tissues in the body; in disease states such as coeliac disease, immune-mediated destruction of villi and their enterocytes causes malabsorption.
