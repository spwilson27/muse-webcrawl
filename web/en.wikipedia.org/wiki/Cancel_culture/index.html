Cancel culture

Image placeholder — social media and public reaction

Cancel culture is a phrase contemporary to the late 2010s and early 2020s used to describe a cultural phenomenon in which an individual deemed to have acted or spoken in an unacceptable manner is ostracized, boycotted, or shunned. It has also been referred to as cancellation, call-out culture, and consequence culture. The practice is often carried out on social media, where groups pressure employers, venues or platforms to withdraw support from a public figure or organisation.

History of the term

The idea of "cancelling" a person or product circulated in African-American slang and on Black Twitter in the mid-2010s before entering mainstream usage. The phrase "cancel culture" became prominent in public debate from about 2019, featuring in discussions about free speech, accountability, and the power of online mobs. Commentators drew parallels with earlier practices such as boycotts, blacklisting and public shaming.

Public debate

Supporters argue that cancelling is a form of free expression and a tool for holding powerful people accountable when institutions fail to act. Critics argue that it amounts to disproportionate punishment, chills speech, and can harm people who lack the resources of celebrities. The term is frequently contested: many commentators note that figures described as "cancelled" often retain or rebuild their careers, and prefer terms such as "accountability" or "consequences". The concept became a significant theme in political campaigns and "culture war" debates in the United States, United Kingdom and elsewhere.

Academic and media analysis

Surveys, such as those by the Pew Research Center, find the public divided: some associate calling out perceived offensive behaviour with accountability, others with punishment of speech that need not be offensive. Scholars have compared cancel culture to historical forms of ostracism and to consumer boycotts, and debate over the term overlaps with debates about deplatforming, online harassment, and media bias.
