Scientific theory

A scientific theory is an explanation of an aspect of the natural world that has been repeatedly tested and confirmed through observation and experiment, and that can be used to make falsifiable predictions about future observations. Theories are the main product and goal of science: they organise facts, laws and hypotheses into a coherent explanatory framework.

Characteristics

Well-established theories — such as the theory of evolution by natural selection, germ theory, plate tectonics, atomic theory and general relativity — are supported by many independent lines of evidence and have survived extensive attempts at falsification. In scientific usage "theory" therefore does not mean a guess or hunch, as it often does colloquially; a theory is among the most reliable forms of scientific knowledge.

Theories and laws

Theories differ from scientific laws: a law describes a regular pattern in nature, often mathematically, while a theory explains why the pattern occurs. Theories do not "become" laws with more evidence; they answer different questions. Hypotheses are proposed explanations that, if supported and integrated into a broader framework, may contribute to a theory.

Change

Theories are revised or replaced when new evidence accumulates that they cannot explain, as Newtonian mechanics was superseded by relativity and quantum mechanics, though older theories often remain accurate within limited domains.
