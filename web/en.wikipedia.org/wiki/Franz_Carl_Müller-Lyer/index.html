Franz Carl Müller-Lyer

Franz Carl Müller-Lyer (5 February 1857 – 29 October 1916) was a German psychiatrist, sociologist and author. He is best known in psychology for the Müller-Lyer illusion, an optical illusion he described in 1889, and in sociology for his works on the evolution of culture and the family.

Born: 5 February 1857, Baden-Baden, Germany
Died: 29 October 1916, Munich, Germany
Fields: Psychiatry, sociology
Known for: Müller-Lyer illusion

The illusion

In the illusion, two line segments of identical length appear different: the segment ending in fins pointing outward (like arrowheads) looks longer than the segment ending in fins pointing inward (like arrow tails). Müller-Lyer published the effect in 1889 in the Archiv für Anatomie und Physiologie. The illusion has become one of the most studied phenomena in perceptual psychology. Cross-cultural research — notably the 1966 study by Segall, Campbell and Herskovits — found that susceptibility varies between populations, giving rise to the "carpentered world" hypothesis that experience with rectangular built environments tunes the perception involved.

Sociology

Müller-Lyer practised psychiatry in Munich while writing ambitious evolutionary sociology. In works such as Die Entwicklungsstufen der Menschheit ("The Developmental Stages of Humanity") and his studies of the family and of eugenics and sexual reform, he sought laws of cultural development and advocated progressive social reform, including changes to marriage law. His sociological writings were widely read in German-speaking countries before the First World War but are now largely forgotten outside the history of the discipline.
