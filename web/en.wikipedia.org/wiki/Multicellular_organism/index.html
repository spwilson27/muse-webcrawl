Multicellular organism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Volvox, a colonial alga with differentiated cells, illustrates a simple form of multicellular organization.

A multicellular organism is an organism that consists of more than one cell, in contrast to a unicellular organism. In complex multicellular organisms the cells are differentiated to perform specialized functions and are organized into tissues, organs, and organ systems, with cell adhesion, cell signalling, and regulated cell division coordinating development from a single fertilized egg. All land plants, animals, and most fungi are multicellular, as are many algae and some slime moulds.

Evolution of multicellularity

Multicellularity has evolved independently many times — by some counts at least 25 times among eukaryotes — in lineages including animals, land plants, red and brown algae, and fungi. It is thought to have arisen through cells failing to separate after division, or through aggregation of free-living cells, followed by specialization and the evolution of mechanisms to suppress "cheating" cells (cancer being a breakdown of this cooperation). Fossil evidence suggests multicellular eukaryotes existed nearly two billion years ago, and complex macroscopic multicellular life is well documented from the Ediacaran biota onward. Choanoflagellates, the single-celled sister group of animals, and experimental evolution of multicellular yeast ("snowflake yeast") are widely used models for the transition.

Organization

True multicellularity requires more than a colony of identical cells: cells communicate via signalling pathways, adhere with specialized junctions, and often divide labour, with only germ cells contributing genes to the next generation while somatic cells support reproduction. Extracellular matrix, programmed cell death, and developmental gene regulatory networks (such as Hox genes in animals) build and maintain the body plan. Intermediate forms — colonies with partial specialization, like Volvox or the cellular slime moulds that aggregate into a fruiting body — illustrate gradations between unicellular and fully multicellular life.
