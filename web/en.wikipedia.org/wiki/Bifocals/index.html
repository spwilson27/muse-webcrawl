Bifocals

Bifocals are eyeglasses with two distinct optical powers, correcting both distance vision and near vision in a single lens. The upper part of the lens is generally for distance viewing and the lower part — often a visible segment — for reading and close work. They are commonly prescribed for people with presbyopia.

History

The invention of bifocals is traditionally credited to Benjamin Franklin in the 1780s; tired of switching between two pairs of glasses, he had the lenses cut in half and combined in one frame, calling them "double spectacles". Earlier composite designs may have existed, but Franklin's version popularised the concept. Trifocals, adding an intermediate segment, came later, as did progressive lenses with a gradual power change and no visible line.

Design

In the most common design, a small D-shaped segment in the lower lens provides near correction. Wearers look straight ahead for distance and lower their eyes to read through the segment. Some users experience an "image jump" as the line of sight crosses the segment boundary, and adaptation may take days or weeks.
