Compatibility

This article is about the general concept. Compatibility is the capacity for two systems to work together without having to be altered to do so.

In computing

In computing, compatibility describes whether hardware or software components can function together. Backward compatibility means a newer system can work with inputs, data, or devices designed for an older one; forward compatibility means a system is designed to accept inputs intended for later versions. Binary compatibility and source compatibility distinguish whether programs can run unchanged or must be recompiled. Compatibility layers and emulators let software built for one platform run on another.

In engineering

Mechanical and electrical compatibility concerns standardised dimensions, voltages, connectors, and protocols, so that parts from different manufacturers interoperate. Electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) is the branch of engineering concerned with ensuring devices neither emit excessive interference nor are unduly susceptible to it.

In biology and medicine

Histocompatibility governs whether transplanted tissue will be accepted by a recipient's immune system. Blood compatibility determines safe transfusion pairings between donor and recipient blood types. In biochemistry, enzyme–substrate compatibility underlies molecular recognition.

In other fields

In astrology and popular psychology, "compatibility" loosely denotes how well two people's traits suit a relationship. In chemistry, compatibility charts indicate which substances can be stored or mixed safely.
