Pulp (tooth)

The dental pulp is the soft connective tissue at the centre of a tooth, housed in the pulp chamber and root canals. It contains nerves, blood vessels, lymphatics and odontoblasts, the cells that produce dentine.

Functions and disease

The pulp nourishes the tooth, forms dentine throughout life, and senses pain, temperature and pressure. Inflammation of the pulp (pulpitis), usually from deep decay or trauma, can cause severe toothache; irreversible cases are treated by root canal therapy or extraction.
