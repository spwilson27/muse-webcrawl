Susenyos I

Susenyos I (Ge'ez: ሱስንዮስ Sūsinyōs; c. 1571/1575 – 17 September 1632), also known by the throne name Seltan Seged I, was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1607 to 1632. A member of the Solomonic dynasty, his reign is most remembered for his conversion to Roman Catholicism and his violent attempt to impose the Catholic faith on Ethiopia, which provoked civil war and ended with his abdication.

Early life and accession

Susenyos was the grandson of Emperor Dawit II through his father Fasilides, a prince who had been exiled. Raised among the Oromo, who knew him as a formidable warrior, he rose through successful military campaigns in the south and, after defeating rival claimants, was crowned in 1607. He consolidated his power with marriages into leading families and campaigns against rebellious provinces.

Conversion and civil war

Influenced by the Portuguese Jesuit Pedro Páez, Susenyos converted privately to Catholicism in 1612 and publicly proclaimed it in 1622, subordinating the Ethiopian Church to Rome. The reforms — including changes to fasting, Sabbath observance, and rebaptism — provoked widespread rebellion led by his own son Fasilides and clergy of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. After years of bloody conflict, Susenyos relented in 1632, issued a proclamation allowing his subjects to return to their traditional faith, and abdicated in favour of Fasilides, dying a few months later. His successor expelled the Jesuits and closed Ethiopia to Catholic missionaries for over two centuries.
