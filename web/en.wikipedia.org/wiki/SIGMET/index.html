SIGMET

SIGMET, or Significant Meteorological Information, is a weather advisory that contains meteorological information concerning the safety of all aircraft. A SIGMET is issued by a meteorological watch office for its flight information region (FIR) when specified significant weather phenomena are observed or forecast. SIGMETs are distinct from AIRMETs, which address less severe phenomena of particular concern to smaller aircraft, and from convective SIGMETs used in the United States for severe thunderstorms.

Phenomena covered

Under ICAO Annex 3, SIGMETs are issued for phenomena including:

  • Thunderstorms (obscured, embedded, frequent, or in squall lines), with or without hail;
  • Tropical cyclones;
  • Severe icing and severe turbulence;
  • Mountain waves marked as severe;
  • Dust storms and sandstorms;
  • Volcanic ash clouds and radioactive clouds.

Messages are written in abbreviated plain language following a fixed ICAO template and normally have a validity of up to four hours (six hours for volcanic ash and tropical cyclone SIGMETs). Volcanic ash SIGMETs are coordinated with the global network of Volcanic Ash Advisory Centres (VAACs).

Format

An example of a SIGMET message:

EFIN SIGMET 3 VALID 171200/171600 EFKL-
EFIN FINLAND FIR SEV TURB FCST AT 1240Z
S OF LINE N6140 E02300 - N6325 E02630 FL260/380
MOV NE 20KT NC=

The message identifies the FIR, the phenomenon (severe turbulence), whether it is observed (OBS) or forecast (FCST), the area and flight levels affected, and the expected movement and change in intensity.

Convective SIGMET (United States)

In the United States, the Aviation Weather Center issues convective SIGMETs for severe convective activity: tornadoes, lines of thunderstorms, embedded thunderstorms, and thunderstorms with hail of 3/4 inch or greater, among other criteria. They cover regions rather than individual FIRs and are valid for up to two hours. Non-convective SIGMETs in the US cover severe icing, severe or extreme turbulence, dust storms, sandstorms and volcanic ash.
