Washer (hardware)

Summary
TypeFastener component
FormThin disc with a central hole
Common materialsSteel, stainless steel, brass, nylon, rubber
Main typesPlain, spring (lock), fender, sealing, Belleville

A washer is a thin plate (typically disk-shaped, but sometimes square) with a hole (typically in the middle) that is normally used to distribute the load of a threaded fastener, such as a bolt or nut. Other uses are as a spacer, spring (Belleville washer, wave washer), pre-load indicating device, locking device, to reduce vibration (rubber washer), or to seal against fluid leakage.

Uses

Placed under a nut or bolt head, a plain washer spreads the clamping load over a wider area of the joined material, protecting soft surfaces such as wood or aluminium from being crushed or scored as the fastener is tightened and rotated. Washers also bridge oversized holes, act as shims to adjust spacing or level, and provide a smooth bearing surface that gives more consistent torque-tension behaviour.

Types

Plain washers are simple flat discs, standardised in series by diameter and thickness; fender washers have an unusually large outside diameter for distributing load over thin sheet. Spring washers, including split lock washers, wave washers and toothed washers, exert axial force and are intended to resist loosening under vibration, though their effectiveness for locking is debated and standards bodies have withdrawn some recommendations. Belleville washers are conical disc springs that can be stacked to tune stiffness and deflection. Sealing washers combine a metal disc with a bonded rubber ring to make a watertight joint, common in roofing screws and plumbing. Tab washers and lock plates, bent after fitting, physically prevent rotation in critical assemblies.

Materials and standards

Washers are produced in plain and galvanised steel, stainless steel, brass, copper, aluminium, nylon and fibre, chosen for strength, corrosion resistance and electrical insulation. Dimensions are governed by standards such as ISO 7089, DIN 125 and ASME B18.2.6, which define nominal sizes matching standard bolt diameters.
