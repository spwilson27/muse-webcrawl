Al-Ukhdud (Arabic: الأخدود, "the Trench") is an archaeological site in what is now south-western Saudi Arabia, near the modern city of Najran close to the Yemeni border. It preserves the remains of an ancient South Arabian walled city, including ruined fortifications, houses, tombs and rock inscriptions in the ancient South Arabian script.

The People of the Ditch

The site is traditionally identified with the story of the Aṣḥāb al-Ukhdūd, the "People of the Ditch", mentioned in Surah Al-Buruj (85:4–8) of the Quran: persecutors who burned believers in trenches of fire. In Islamic tradition and historical scholarship the story is linked to events in sixth-century Najran, where the Himyarite king Dhu Nuwas — a convert to Judaism — persecuted the Christian population around 523 CE, an atrocity that provoked the Aksumite invasion of Yemen.

Archaeology

Excavations have revealed occupation spanning many centuries, with city walls, a citadel, cemeteries and carved inscriptions attesting to the city's role on the incense trade routes between Yemen and the north. The site is protected as one of Saudi Arabia's significant heritage locations.
