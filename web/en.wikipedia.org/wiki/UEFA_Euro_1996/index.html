The 1996 UEFA European Football Championship, commonly referred to as Euro 96, was the tenth UEFA European Championship and was held in England from 8 to 30 June 1996. It was the first European Championship contested by sixteen teams, double the previous eight, and the first in which the "golden goal" rule was used to decide knockout matches.

Tournament summary

Hosts England reached the semi-finals on a wave of home support, powered by the partnership of Alan Shearer and Teddy Sheringham, but were eliminated by Germany on penalties at Wembley — Gareth Southgate missing the decisive kick. The Czech Republic, surprise finalists featuring Pavel Nedvěd, Karel Poborský and Patrik Berger, had earlier beaten Italy, Portugal and France to reach the final.

Final

In the final at Wembley, Patrik Berger's penalty gave the Czechs the lead before substitute Oliver Bierhoff equalised. Five minutes into extra time Bierhoff scored again — the first golden goal in a major international final — giving Germany a 2–1 victory and their third European title, the first as a reunified country.

Cultural impact

The tournament is remembered in England for the anthem "Three Lions" by Baddiel, Skinner and the Lightning Seeds, with its refrain "football's coming home" — a reference to England being the birthplace of the modern game and host of a major tournament for the first time since the 1966 World Cup.
