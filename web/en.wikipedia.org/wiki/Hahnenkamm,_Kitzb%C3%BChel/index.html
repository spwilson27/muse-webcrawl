The Hahnenkamm is a mountain peak at 1,712 metres above sea level in the Kitzbühel Alps, overlooking the town of Kitzbühel in the Austrian state of Tyrol. It is best known for the Hahnenkamm Races, held since 1931 and among the most prestigious events on the alpine skiing World Cup circuit.

The Streif

The downhill race is run on the Streif, widely regarded as the most demanding course in World Cup skiing, with gradients of up to about 85 percent at the Mausefalle ("mousetrap") jump near the start. A slalom on the neighbouring Ganslern slope completes the traditional race weekend, which draws tens of thousands of spectators each January.

Access

A gondola lift, the Hahnenkammbahn, connects Kitzbühel town to the summit area, serving skiers in winter and hikers in summer.
