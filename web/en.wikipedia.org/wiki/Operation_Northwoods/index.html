Operation Northwoods was a proposed false-flag operation against American citizens that was drafted by the United States Department of Defense and the Joint Chiefs of Staff in 1962. The proposals called for the Central Intelligence Agency and other operatives to stage acts of terrorism against American military and civilian targets, blame them on the Cuban government of Fidel Castro, and thereby manufacture public and international support for a war against Cuba.

Proposals

The memorandum, formally titled "Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba", suggested a series of incidents, including sinking a US ship in Guantánamo Bay ("Remember the Maine"), staging a mock or real attack on American aircraft using a drone painted as a Cuban MiG, orchestrating a terror campaign in Miami and Washington blamed on Cuban agents, and faking an attack on a chartered civilian airliner. Several scenarios contemplated actual American casualties.

Rejection and disclosure

The plan was signed by Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Lyman Lemnitzer and presented to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara in March 1962, but President John F. Kennedy rejected it, and it was never adopted. Lemnitzer was subsequently not reappointed as chairman. The documents remained classified until they were released by the Assassination Records Review Board in 1997 and published in 2001, attracting wide attention and frequent citation in discussions of false-flag operations.
