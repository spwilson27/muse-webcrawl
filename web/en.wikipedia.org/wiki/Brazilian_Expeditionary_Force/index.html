The Brazilian Expeditionary Force (Portuguese: Força Expedicionária Brasileira, FEB) consisted of about 25,900 men from the Brazilian Army and Air Force who fought alongside the Allies in the Mediterranean Theatre of World War II. Brazil was the only South American country to send troops to fight in the conflict.

History

Brazil declared war on Germany and Italy in August 1942 after German U-boats sank Brazilian merchant ships. The FEB was organised with American assistance and deployed to Italy in 1944, where it served under the U.S. Fifth Army. Its soldiers — called pracinhas — fought in the Gothic Line campaign and the 1945 spring offensive, taking part in the battles of Monte Castello, Castelnuovo and Montese, and accepting the surrender of the German 148th Infantry Division at Fornovo di Taro.

Legacy

The FEB's shoulder insignia, a snake smoking a pipe, answered Axis propaganda that Brazil would fight only "when snakes smoke". Its soldiers are commemorated at the Brazilian Military Cemetery in Pistoia, Italy, and by the Monumento aos Pracinhas in Rio de Janeiro.
