Marxism–Leninism is the political and economic ideology that synthesized the thought of Karl Marx with Vladimir Lenin's organizational and revolutionary theory. As the official doctrine of the Soviet Union and the international communist movement, it shaped the politics of much of the world during the Cold War.

Core tenets

The ideology rests on dialectical and historical materialism, the dictatorship of the proletariat exercised through a disciplined vanguard party, collective ownership of the means of production, and opposition to capitalist imperialism. It holds that a socialist phase must precede the eventual withering away of the state.

Historical role

Adopted by the Comintern parties in the 1920s and 1930s, Marxism–Leninism justified one-party rule in the USSR and its allies. Its interpretation varied by country, producing friction such as the Sino-Soviet split.
