Cuticle


Leaf cross-section with cuticle (placeholder image)

A cuticle, or cuticula, is a protective, waterproof outer layer produced by organisms across many groups of life. The term is used for the waxy layer over plant leaves, the exoskeleton of arthropods, the outer covering of nematodes, the shell material of molluscs, and in human anatomy the skin at the base of a fingernail (the eponychium) and the outer layer of a hair shaft.

Plant cuticle

The plant cuticle is a layer of cutin and waxes covering the epidermis of leaves and young shoots. It limits water loss, helps protect against ultraviolet radiation and pathogens, and is a key adaptation that allowed plants to colonize land.

Animal cuticles

Arthropods secrete a cuticle of chitin and protein that forms their exoskeleton and must be shed during growth (ecdysis). Nematodes have a tough collagenous cuticle. In humans, the nail cuticle seals the gap between nail plate and skin, protecting the nail matrix from infection.
