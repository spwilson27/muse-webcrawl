Full-size car

A full-size car is a vehicle-size class for automobiles originating in North America, denoting the largest size of car available. After World War II, most full-size cars adopted sedan and station wagon body styles; by the 2010s, the segment had largely given way to large SUVs and crossovers.


Ford Crown Victoria

Definition

The United States Environmental Protection Agency defines the "large car" class by interior volume: sedans with a combined passenger and cargo volume of more than 3,400 litres (120 cubic feet). Historically, full-size cars were built on wheelbases of 110 inches (2.79 m) or more, typically used large V8 engines and body-on-frame construction, and seated six passengers on two bench seats.

History

Full-size cars dominated the American market from the 1940s through the 1970s, with models such as the Chevrolet Impala, Ford Galaxie and Plymouth Fury. The fuel crises of 1973 and 1979 prompted downsizing, and front-wheel-drive unibody designs gradually replaced the traditional formula. The Ford Crown Victoria, Chevrolet Caprice and Buick Roadmaster were among the last body-on-frame examples; the Crown Victoria survived in fleet service until 2011, largely as a police car and taxi. In Europe and Australia, the equivalent segment was occupied by "large cars" such as the Ford Falcon and Holden Commodore.
