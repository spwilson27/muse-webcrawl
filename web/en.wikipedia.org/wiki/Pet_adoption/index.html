Pet adoption

Pet adoption is the process of taking responsibility for a pet that a previous owner has abandoned or released to a shelter or rescue organization. People adopt pets for companionship and, in adopting, provide a home to animals that might otherwise be euthanized due to shelter overcrowding. Common sources for adoptable pets are animal shelters, rescue groups, and individuals rehoming animals.

Process

The adoption process typically involves an application, an interview or home check, and an adoption fee, which commonly helps cover the costs of spaying or neutering, vaccinations, microchipping, and veterinary care already provided. Prospective adopters may be screened to assess their suitability and to match animals with appropriate homes. Some organizations offer foster arrangements or trial periods before finalizing an adoption.

Adoption versus buying

Animal welfare organizations promote adoption with slogans such as "Adopt, don't shop," arguing that adopting from shelters reduces the animals killed in overcrowded facilities and avoids supporting puppy mills and irresponsible breeding. In the United States, several million dogs and cats enter shelters each year, and a substantial fraction are adopted, though historically large numbers were euthanized; adoption, spay-neuter programs, and no-kill initiatives have reduced shelter euthanasia substantially since the early 2010s.

Types of adoption

Beyond dogs and cats, shelters and rescues place rabbits, guinea pigs, birds, reptiles, and farm animals. Breed-specific rescues focus on particular dog or cat breeds, and senior-pet and special-needs adoption programs encourage placement of harder-to-adopt animals. Online services such as Petfinder aggregate listings from thousands of shelters and rescue groups.
