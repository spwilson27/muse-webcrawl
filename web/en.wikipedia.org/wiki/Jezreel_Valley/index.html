The Jezreel Valley (Hebrew: עמק יזרעאל, Emek Yizra'el, "God sows"), sometimes called the Plain of Esdraelon from its Greek name, is a large fertile plain in northern Israel between the Galilee and Samaria, stretching from the Carmel range and Haifa Bay in the west to the Jordan Valley in the east. Drained by the Kishon River, it forms the most important overland corridor between the Mediterranean coast and the interior — the route of the ancient Via Maris — and is studded with strategic sites including Megiddo, Mount Tabor, Mount Gilboa and the Harod Valley. Today it is one of Israel's most productive agricultural regions, centred on the town of Afula.

History

The valley's position made it a battlefield of antiquity: the Battle of Megiddo (15th century BCE) between Pharaoh Thutmose III and a Canaanite coalition is the first battle in history recorded in reliable detail, and Megiddo (Armageddon) later gave its name to the prophesied end-times battle of the Book of Revelation. Gideon defeated the Midianites in the valley, Saul fell to the Philistines on Mount Gilboa, and King Josiah was killed at Megiddo in 609 BCE. The plain took its name from the city of Jezreel, the winter capital of the northern kingdom of Israel under Ahab, site of the story of Naboth's vineyard. Battles continued into the modern era: Napoleon defeated an Ottoman army near Mount Tabor in 1799, and in September 1918 General Allenby's forces won the decisive Battle of Megiddo of the First World War in the valley.

Settlement and agriculture

In the late Ottoman period much of the valley floor was marshy and malarial, owned in large part by the absentee Sursock family of Beirut. From 1920 the Jewish National Fund purchased the Sursock lands — among the most consequential land acquisitions of the Zionist movement — and Jewish settlers drained the swamps and founded a dense network of kibbutzim and moshavim, beginning with Nahalal (1921), the first moshav ovdim. Arab fellahin tenants displaced by the purchases became a grievance in the Arab–Zionist conflict. The modern valley is intensively farmed — field crops, cotton, fodder, dairy cattle and fish ponds — and crossed by the restored Jezreel Valley railway (Haifa–Beit She'an, reopened 2016).
