In the Heat of the Night (film)

In the Heat of the Night is a 1967 American mystery drama film directed by Norman Jewison, based on John Ball's 1965 novel of the same name. It stars Sidney Poitier as Virgil Tibbs, a Black homicide detective from Philadelphia who is drawn into a murder investigation in a racially hostile small town in Mississippi, and Rod Steiger as the town's police chief, Bill Gillespie. The film confronts American racism directly and is remembered for the famous line "They call me Mister Tibbs!"

Virgil Tibbs arrives in Sparta, Mississippi, and is first arrested, then enlisted, as a suspect-turned-investigator.

Plot

After a wealthy industrialist is found murdered in Sparta, Mississippi, police round up Tibbs at the train station simply because he is a Black man with money in his wallet. When his identity as a Philadelphia detective emerges, his chief orders him to assist the investigation. Gillespie, resentful and prejudiced, gradually comes to respect Tibbs's forensic skill as Tibbs exposes the first arrest as baseless and works through a web of suspects — the victim's widow, a wealthy racist landowner (whom Tibbs famously slaps back after being struck), a nervous diner counterman, and a young man fleeing with his pregnant girlfriend — before identifying the true killer.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, winning five Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Actor for Steiger, and Best Adapted Screenplay. It spawned two sequels starring Poitier — They Call Me Mister Tibbs! (1970) and The Organization (1971) — and a long-running television series (1988–1995) with Carroll O'Connor and Howard Rollins. In 2002 the film was selected for the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, and aesthetically significant.
