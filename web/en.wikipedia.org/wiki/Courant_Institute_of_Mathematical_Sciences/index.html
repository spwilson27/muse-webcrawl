Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences

The Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences (CIMS) is the mathematics research institute of New York University (NYU). Founded in 1935, it is one of the world's leading centres for research and advanced training in mathematics and computer science, particularly renowned for applied mathematics and partial differential equations.

History

The institute grew out of the graduate mathematics programme built at NYU by Richard Courant, a German émigré mathematician who had directed the mathematics institute at Göttingen. In 1935 Courant established the Institute for Mathematics and Mechanics, which expanded during the Second World War through defence-related research. In 1946 it was renamed the Institute for Mathematical Sciences, and in 1964 it took Courant's name.

Research and teaching

Courant houses NYU's departments of Mathematics and Computer Science and research groups in applied mathematics, scientific computing, fluid dynamics, mathematical physics, probability, geometry and computer science. Its faculty and alumni include Abel Prize laureates Peter Lax, S. R. Srinivasa Varadhan, Louis Nirenberg and Martin Hairer (affiliated), and Fields Medalist Akshay Venkatesh received his training in the broader NYU orbit; the institute's graduate programmes are consistently ranked among the best in the United States, with applied mathematics frequently ranked first.
