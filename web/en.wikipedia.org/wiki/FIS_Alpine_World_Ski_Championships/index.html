FIS Alpine World Ski Championships

The FIS Alpine World Ski Championships are an alpine skiing competition organized by the International Ski Federation (FIS). First held in 1931 in Mürren, Switzerland, the championships are now held every two years in odd-numbered years, separate from the Winter Olympic Games.

History

The first championships included only downhill and slalom. The combined event was added in 1932, giant slalom in 1950, super-G in 1987, and the team parallel event in later editions. From 1948 through 1980, the Olympic races also counted as world championship events. Since 1985 the championships have been held in years independent of the Winter Olympics.

Events

Men and women compete in downhill, super-G, giant slalom, slalom, alpine combined, and parallel events, along with a mixed team parallel event. Medals are awarded to the top three finishers in each discipline.

Host venues

Notable hosts include St. Moritz (multiple times), Garmisch-Partenkirchen, Åre, Vail/Beaver Creek, St. Anton, Kitzbühel-area venues, Cortina d'Ampezzo (2021), Méribel/Courchevel (2023), and Saalbach (2025).

Most successful nations

Austria has historically dominated the medal table, followed by Switzerland, France, Germany, Italy, and the United States. Individual greats include Toni Sailer, Jean-Claude Killy, Ingemar Stenmark, Pirmin Zurbriggen, Hermann Maier, Lindsey Vonn, and Mikaela Shiffrin.
