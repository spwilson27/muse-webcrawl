Oroville Dam crisis

Erosion below the emergency spillway during the February 2017 crisis.

The Oroville Dam crisis occurred in February 2017 at Oroville Dam, the tallest earthfill dam in the United States, on the Feather River in Northern California. Heavy rainfall filled Lake Oroville to capacity, and damage to the dam's main spillway forced operators to use its never-before-used emergency spillway. Erosion beneath that spillway threatened to undermine it, prompting the evacuation of nearly 190,000 people downstream.

Events

On 7 February 2017 a large crater was discovered in the concrete chute of the main spillway. Operators reduced releases while inspecting the damage, but the reservoir kept rising. On 11 February water began flowing over the emergency spillway — a broad concrete weir leading onto a wooded hillside — for the first time in the dam's history. The hillside eroded far faster than expected, and on 12 February authorities ordered the immediate evacuation of Oroville and other downstream communities in Butte, Yuba and Sutter counties. Increased releases down the damaged main spillway lowered the lake level and ended the emergency spillway flow; the evacuation order was lifted two days later.

Aftermath

Repair of the main spillway cost on the order of a billion dollars and was completed in phases by 2019. An independent forensic investigation attributed the failure to long-term design, construction and maintenance deficiencies in the spillway's concrete slab and drainage system, and criticised the regulatory and organisational culture surrounding dam safety. No lives were lost.
