Censure

Censure is a formal statement of disapproval, usually by a legislature or other governing body, of a person's conduct. It is a rebuke falling short of removal from office or expulsion.

In the United States

In Congress, censure is a disciplinary measure available to both the House and Senate. A censured member typically must stand in the well of the chamber while the resolution is read. Notable cases include the Senate's censure of Joseph McCarthy in 1954. President Andrew Jackson was censured by the Senate in 1834 over the Bank War; the resolution was later expunged. Censure differs from impeachment, which can lead to removal, and from a motion of no confidence in parliamentary systems.

Elsewhere

Ecclesiastical censure in canon law includes penalties such as excommunication and interdict. Professional bodies also censure members for misconduct.
