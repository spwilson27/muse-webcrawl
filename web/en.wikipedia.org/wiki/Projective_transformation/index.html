Projective transformation

In mathematics, a projective transformation (or projectivity) is a bijective map of a projective space to itself that sends lines to lines and preserves the cross-ratio of four collinear points. Projective transformations form a group, the projective linear group, and are the fundamental symmetries studied in projective geometry.

Description

In coordinates, a projectivity of the projective plane is represented by an invertible 3×3 matrix acting on homogeneous coordinates, defined up to a nonzero scalar multiple. Projective transformations generalise affine transformations: translations, rotations and scalings are projectivities that fix the line at infinity, while a general projectivity can move that line, which is why parallel lines can be made to meet in an image of a scene.

Properties and applications

Any four points in general position can be mapped to any other four, a property exploited in computer vision for perspective correction of photographs and in computer graphics, where the perspective projection of a 3D scene is implemented with projective transformations. In the classification of geometries, projective transformations preserve incidence and cross-ratio but not distance, angle or parallelism.
