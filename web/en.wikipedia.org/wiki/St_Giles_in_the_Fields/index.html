St Giles in the Fields

St Giles in the Fields Location: St Giles High Street, London WC2
Denomination: Church of England
Founded: 1101 (original chapel)
Current church: 1731–1733
Architect: Henry Flitcroft
Style: Palladian

St Giles in the Fields is the parish church of St Giles, a district on the western edge of the City of London's neighbouring borough of Camden, once notorious for its slum "rookery". The present Palladian church, built 1731–1733 to designs by Henry Flitcroft, is the third church on the site.

The church from St Giles High Street

History

A chapel attached to a hospital for lepers, founded by Matilda of Scotland in 1101, stood here outside the city walls. After the Dissolution the chapel became a parish church. The second church (1629–31) was one of the earliest purpose-built Anglican churches. The parish became infamous in the 18th and 19th centuries for gin shops and overcrowded slums — Hogarth set his Gin Lane print in St Giles — and its churchyard was an early burial ground for victims of the Great Plague of 1665.

The church today

The church is known for its music, including a historic organ and weekly recitals, and for its association with poets: John Milton's first wife is buried here, and the poet Andrew Marvell's memorial is in the church.
