Gerlachovský štít (Slovak pronunciation: [ˈɡerlaxow.skiː ʃciːt]; German: Gerlsdorfer Spitze) is the highest peak in the High Tatras, in Slovakia, and in the whole 1,500 km (930 mi) long Carpathian mountain chain. At 2,654.4 metres (8,709 ft) above sea level, it is a prominent, rugged peak rising above the village of Gerlachov.

History

The peak was first climbed in 1834 by Ján Still, a teacher from Nová Lesná, with companions. For centuries it was mistakenly assumed to be an average mountain; precise measurements in the 19th century established its supremacy in the Tatras. The peak's name and symbolism changed repeatedly with political regimes: it was briefly called Štít Františka Jozefa, then — controversially — Stalin's Peak (1949–1959), before reverting to Gerlachovský štít.

Access

Unlike many Tatra peaks, Gerlach may only be climbed with a certified mountain guide, or by members of national climbing associations via designated routes. Popular approaches run from the Sliezsky dom hotel up the Velická valley, or via the Batizovská valley. Sudden weather changes and rockfall make the ascent demanding despite its modest altitude.
