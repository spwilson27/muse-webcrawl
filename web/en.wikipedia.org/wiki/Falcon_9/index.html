Falcon 9

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Falcon 9 booster landing on a drone ship.

Falcon 9 is a partially reusable two-stage medium-lift launch vehicle designed and manufactured by SpaceX. Named for its nine Merlin engines on the first stage, it first flew in 2010 and has become the most frequently launched rocket in the world, carrying satellites, cargo and crews to orbit.

In December 2015 a Falcon 9 first stage landed vertically at Cape Canaveral — the first time an orbital-class booster had been recovered — and ship-based landings on autonomous drone ships followed in 2016. Reflight of recovered boosters, once considered impractical, became routine; individual boosters have flown more than twenty times. The current Block 5 version, introduced in 2018, carries SpaceX's Dragon spacecraft to the International Space Station and deploys the Starlink satellite constellation in batches.

Variants

Falcon Heavy, first flown in 2018, straps three Falcon 9 cores together for heavy payloads. Falcon 9's reliability and low cost — driven by reusability and high flight rate — reshaped the global launch industry and pushed competitors toward reusable designs of their own.
