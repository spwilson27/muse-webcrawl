Amarapura (Burmese: အမရပူရ, from Pali Amarapura, "City of Immortality") is a former capital of Myanmar (Burma) and now a township of Mandalay. It lies on the left bank of the Irrawaddy River, about 11 kilometres south of Mandalay city centre. Amarapura served twice as the royal capital of the Konbaung dynasty — from 1783 to 1821 and again from 1842 to 1859 — before King Mindon moved the court to the newly founded city of Mandalay.

Today Amarapura is famous for the U Bein Bridge, a 1.2-kilometre teak footbridge across Taungthaman Lake, built around 1850 from wood salvaged from the old royal palace and believed to be the oldest and longest teak bridge in the world. The township is also a centre of traditional silk and cotton weaving, and home to Mahagandayon Monastery, where more than a thousand monks study and where visitors observe the morning alms meal.

History

King Bodawpaya founded Amarapura in 1783 and made it his capital, receiving there the first British embassy to Burma in 1795. His grandson Bagyidaw moved the capital back to Ava (Inwa) in 1821, but King Tharrawaddy returned the court to Amarapura in 1842. King Mindon, who succeeded in 1853, decided to build a new capital at the foot of Mandalay Hill; the move took place in 1857–59, and materials from Amarapura's palace were reused, including the teak posts of the U Bein Bridge.

Sights

Besides the U Bein Bridge and Mahagandayon Monastery, notable sites include the Pahtodawgyi Pagoda (built by Bodawpaya in 1816), the Kyauktawgyi Pagoda, and the remains of the royal palace and city walls. The nearby village of Yadanabon and the weaving workshops of Amarapura's quarters demonstrate traditional longyi production on hand looms.

Economy

Weaving is the township's signature industry: hundreds of workshops produce silk and cotton longyis and the elaborate acheik patterns for which Amarapura is known throughout Myanmar. Tourism, drawn by the bridge and monastery, is also significant.
