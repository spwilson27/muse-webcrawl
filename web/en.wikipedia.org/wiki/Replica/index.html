Replica

A replica is an exact (or near-exact) copy or reproduction of an object, work of art, vehicle, or building. Replicas range from museum-grade reproductions sanctioned by the original maker to commercial imitations and kit-built copies.

Uses

Museums use replicas to display fragile or unique originals safely, to complete collections, or to allow handling; famous examples include the replica of the Rosetta Stone exhibited in place of the original's display context, and Lascaux II, the facsimile cave built so visitors could experience the Lascaux paintings without damaging them. Replica vehicles (such as kit cars copying classic sports cars), replica firearms, replica watches, and reproduction furniture serve collectors and hobbyists.

Replica and related terms

A replica made by the original creator or workshop is sometimes distinguished from a reproduction (a copy made by others) and a facsimile (an exact copy, especially of a document). In art law and collecting, undisclosed replicas passed off as originals amount to forgery or counterfeiting.
