Lahore Fort

Lahore Fort
Location: Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan
Built: 1566 onwards (present form)
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1981)

The Alamgiri Gate of Lahore Fort

The Lahore Fort (Urdu: شاہی قلعہ, Shahi Qila, "Royal Fort") is a citadel in the Walled City of Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan. Together with the adjacent Shalamar Gardens it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1981 as an outstanding example of Mughal architecture. The fort occupies the northwestern corner of the old city, overlooking the Badshahi Mosque and the Ravi river plain.

History

A fortress existed on the site in some form from at least the eleventh century, but the present structure was founded when the Mughal emperor Akbar rebuilt it in brick and masonry from 1566. His successors added many of its most famous buildings: Jahangir completed the great Picture Wall and his own quadrangle; Shah Jahan added the marble Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience), the Moti Masjid (Pearl Mosque) and the Sheesh Mahal (Palace of Mirrors) in the 1630s; and Aurangzeb built the monumental Alamgiri Gate in 1674. After the Mughal decline the fort passed to the Sikh Empire of Maharaja Ranjit Singh and then, in 1849, to the British, who used it as a garrison.

Layout and monuments

The fort is a roughly rectangular complex of palaces, audience halls, mosques and gardens arranged in two main courts. Notable features include the Picture Wall, the world's largest mural of its kind, decorated with glazed tile mosaics; the Naulakha Pavilion, inlaid with semi-precious stones; and the Moti Masjid. A museum within the fort displays Mughal and Sikh artefacts.
