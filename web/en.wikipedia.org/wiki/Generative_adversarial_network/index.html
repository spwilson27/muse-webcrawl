Generative adversarial network

Generative adversarial network
TypeMachine learning framework; generative model
Introduced2014
InventorIan Goodfellow and colleagues
ComponentsGenerator and discriminator networks

A generative adversarial network (GAN) is a class of machine learning frameworks in which two neural networks — a generator and a discriminator — are trained simultaneously in an adversarial, zero-sum game. The framework was introduced by Ian Goodfellow and his colleagues at the University of Montreal in 2014.

Method

The generator learns to produce samples (such as images) from random noise, while the discriminator learns to distinguish the generator's samples from real data drawn from the training set. The generator is trained to maximize the probability that the discriminator mistakes its outputs for real data. At the theoretical optimum, the generator recovers the data distribution and the discriminator outputs probability 1/2 everywhere.

Variants and applications

Many variants have been proposed, including Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGAN), conditional GANs, CycleGAN (for unpaired image-to-image translation), and NVIDIA's StyleGAN family, which produced highly realistic images of human faces. GANs have been used for image synthesis, super-resolution, style transfer, data augmentation, video prediction, and generating deepfake media, raising concerns about misuse.

Training difficulties

GAN training is notoriously unstable. Common problems include mode collapse (the generator produces a limited variety of outputs), vanishing gradients when the discriminator becomes too strong, and non-convergence. Techniques such as Wasserstein GAN loss, spectral normalization, and progressive growing were developed to stabilize training.
