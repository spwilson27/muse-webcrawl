Haematoxylum campechianum

Haematoxylum campechianum, commonly known as logwood, bloodwood, bluewood, Campeche wood and Jamaica wood, is a species of flowering tree in the legume family Fabaceae, native to southern Mexico, particularly Campeche, and northern Central America.

Description

It is a slow-growing, gnarled tree reaching 3–14 m, with dark bark and pinnate leaves. The heartwood is heavy and dense and yields a deep red-purple dye.

Uses

Logwood was a historically important source of natural dye. Haematoxylin extracted from the heartwood is used in histology as a stain, notably in haematoxylin and eosin staining. Logwood extracts were used to dye textiles black, blue and purple, and trade in logwood drove settlement in Belize and conflicts between Spanish and British loggers in the 17th and 18th centuries.

History

Maya peoples used logwood dye before European contact. Spanish monopolies and British cutting in the Bay of Campeche and Honduras made it a valuable colonial commodity.
