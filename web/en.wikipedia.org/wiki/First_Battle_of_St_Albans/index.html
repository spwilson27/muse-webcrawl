The First Battle of St Albans, fought on 22 May 1455 at St Albans, 22 miles (35 km) north of London, traditionally marks the beginning of the Wars of the Roses in England. Richard, Duke of York, and his allies, the Neville Earls of Salisbury and Warwick, defeated a royal army commanded by Edmund Beaufort, Duke of Somerset, who was killed in the fighting.

Background

Frustrated by the influence of Somerset over the mentally ill King Henry VI, York marched south with an army to press his demands. Negotiations failed, and the Yorkists stormed the town's barricades. Archers under Warwick entered the town through unguarded gardens and attacked the Lancastrians in the market square.

Aftermath

Henry VI was captured and York was restored as Lord Protector. Several senior Lancastrian nobles — Somerset, the Earl of Northumberland, and Lord Clifford — were killed. The battle was small by later standards but set the pattern of armed conflict between the houses of York and Lancaster that continued intermittently until 1487.
