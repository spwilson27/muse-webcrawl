Died9 January 1998 (aged 87), Netanya, Israel

Imi Lichtenfeld (Hebrew: אימי ליכטנפלד; 26 May 1910 – 9 January 1998), also known by the Hebrew name Imi Sde-Or, was a Hungarian-born Israeli martial artist who founded Krav Maga, the self-defense and fighting system developed for the Israel Defense Forces and now taught worldwide to military, law-enforcement, and civilian practitioners.

Lichtenfeld developed Krav Maga from street fighting and military training experience.

Early life

Lichtenfeld was born in Budapest and raised in Bratislava (then Pressburg), where his father Samuel was a chief police inspector and former circus acrobat who taught self-defense. Imi excelled as a boxer, wrestler, and gymnast, winning national and international titles in the late 1920s. In the 1930s, as antisemitic violence rose in Bratislava, he led groups of Jewish youths who fought fascist gangs in the streets — experience that convinced him sporting techniques needed drastic adaptation for real violence.

Emigration and military service

In 1940 Lichtenfeld fled Europe aboard the refugee ship Pentcho, which sank in the Aegean; rescued, he reached Mandatory Palestine in 1942 after serving with the Czechoslovak Legion under British command in North Africa. In Palestine he taught hand-to-hand combat to the Haganah and the Palmach's frogmen units, and after 1948 became the IDF's chief instructor for physical fitness and close combat. Over two decades of military service he systematized Krav Maga — "contact combat" in Hebrew — around instinctive movements, simultaneous defense and counterattack, and rapid neutralization of threats.

Civilian teaching and legacy

After retiring from the IDF in 1964, Lichtenfeld adapted Krav Maga for civilians, founding schools in Netanya and Tel Aviv. His senior students, notably Eli Avikzar and Eyal Yanilov, helped spread the system abroad in the late 1970s and 1980s. Lichtenfeld remained active in teaching until shortly before his death in 1998. Krav Maga organizations worldwide trace their lineage to him, though disputes among successor organizations over curriculum and authority have been frequent.
