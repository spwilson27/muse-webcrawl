Behemoth

Behemoth and Leviathan, as depicted in William Blake's illustrations to the Book of Job.

Behemoth (Hebrew: בְּהֵמוֹת, bəhēmōṯ) is a beast from the biblical Book of Job, described in Job 40:15–24 as a mighty land creature created by God, ranked "first among the works of God". Alongside the sea monster Leviathan and the bird Ziz, it forms a trio of primordial creatures in Jewish tradition.

Biblical description and interpretation

The passage portrays a grass-eating beast with bones like bars of iron, a tail like a cedar, and strength in its loins, unmoved by the raging Jordan River. Interpreters have variously identified Behemoth with the hippopotamus or elephant, treated it as a purely mythological chaos creature, or, in some creationist readings, proposed a dinosaur. In Jewish eschatology, Behemoth, Leviathan and Ziz are preserved to be served at the messianic banquet of the righteous.

Later usage

The word has entered general usage meaning anything enormous or powerful. Thomas Hobbes titled his history of the English Civil War Behemoth, as a counterpart to Leviathan; the creature also appears in literature, video games, and the naming of the heavy metal band Behemoth.
