Assassination of Martin Luther King Jr.

Date: 4 April 1968, 6:01 p.m. CST

Location: Lorraine Motel, Memphis, Tennessee

Target: Martin Luther King Jr.

Perpetrator: James Earl Ray (convicted)

Martin Luther King Jr., an American civil rights activist and Baptist minister, was assassinated on Thursday, 4 April 1968, in Memphis, Tennessee, at the age of 39. He was shot at 6:01 p.m. while standing on the balcony of Room 306 at the Lorraine Motel, and died an hour later at St. Joseph's Hospital. King had travelled to Memphis to support striking Black sanitation workers.

Assassin and capture

James Earl Ray, a fugitive from the Missouri State Penitentiary, was identified by fingerprints on a rifle and other items abandoned near the scene. After a two-month international manhunt, Ray was arrested at London Heathrow Airport on 8 June 1968. He pleaded guilty in March 1969 and was sentenced to 99 years in prison, recanting three days later and maintaining until his death in 1998 that he was a scapegoat in a wider conspiracy — claims investigated repeatedly but never substantiated in court findings of fact beyond the House Select Committee's 1979 suggestion of a likelihood of conspiracy.

Aftermath

King's death sparked riots ("Holy Week uprisings") in more than 100 American cities; Robert F. Kennedy's speech in Indianapolis that night is credited with preventing unrest there. The Memphis sanitation strike was settled on 16 April. The assassination accelerated passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1968 (the Fair Housing Act), signed on 11 April. The Lorraine Motel is now the National Civil Rights Museum.
