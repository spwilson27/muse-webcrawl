Mimika Regency is a regency (kabupaten) in the Indonesian province of Central Papua, on the southern coast of the island of New Guinea. Its capital is the town of Timika. The regency extends from the swampy lowlands along the Arafura Sea northward into the high mountains of the Sudirman Range.

Mining

Mimika contains the Grasberg mine, one of the world's largest gold mines and second-largest copper mines, operated by PT Freeport Indonesia. Mining around the town of Tembagapura has been the dominant economic activity since the early 1970s and has brought rapid population growth, infrastructure development and continuing disputes over environmental damage and the rights of the indigenous Amungme and Kamoro peoples.

Geography and people

The indigenous inhabitants include the Kamoro (Mimika) people of the coast, known for their woodcarving, and the Amungme of the highlands. The regency's terrain ranges from mangrove swamps and sago forests to alpine valleys near Puncak Jaya, the highest mountain in Indonesia and Oceania, whose glaciers lie near the regency's northern border.

Administration

The regency is subdivided into districts (distrik), including Mimika Baru, which contains Timika, and the highland mining district of Tembagapura.
