Issyk kurgan

The Issyk kurgan, in south-eastern Kazakhstan, is a royal Saka burial mound of the 4th–3rd century BC, excavated in 1969–1970 near the town of Issyk, about 53 kilometres east of Almaty.

Discovery

Although the main chamber had been robbed in antiquity, a side chamber contained the undisturbed burial of a young person in rich golden attire — some 4,000 gold ornaments in total — together with weapons, vessels and a silver cup bearing an inscription in an undeciphered script. The figure, reconstructed wearing a tall pointed hat and gold-embroidered clothing, became known as the "Golden Warrior" (Altyn Adam).

Significance

The Golden Warrior is a national symbol of Kazakhstan, reproduced on monuments and the state emblem's imagery. The finds are held in Kazakh museums, and a replica of the costume is widely exhibited.
