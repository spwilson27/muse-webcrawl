2019 Australian federal election

The 2019 Australian federal election was held on Saturday 18 May 2019 to elect members of the 46th Parliament of Australia. The incumbent Coalition government, led by Prime Minister Scott Morrison of the Liberal Party, defeated the opposition Labor Party led by Bill Shorten, in a result widely described as a shock upset, as nearly all opinion polls had predicted a Labor victory.

Campaign

Morrison had become Prime Minister in August 2018 after a leadership spill removed Malcolm Turnbull. Labor campaigned on an ambitious policy platform including changes to negative gearing and dividend imputation credits, action on climate change and large tax offsets. The Coalition attacked Labor's plans as a "big taxing" agenda and focused heavily on Morrison's persona and Shorten's unpopularity, while Clive Palmer's heavy advertising spend for the United Australia Party also influenced preferences in Queensland.

The Coalition won a narrow majority of 77 seats, performing especially strongly in Queensland and Tasmania. Shorten resigned as Labor leader on election night and was succeeded by Anthony Albanese. The failure of published polling to anticipate the result prompted a formal review by the polling industry.
