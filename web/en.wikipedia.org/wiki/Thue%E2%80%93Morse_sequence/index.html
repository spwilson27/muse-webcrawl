Thue–Morse sequence
Named afterAxel Thue, Marston Morse
First terms0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 0, 0, 1, ...
GenerationRepeatedly append the bitwise complement

The Thue–Morse sequence (or Prouhet–Thue–Morse sequence) is the binary sequence obtained by starting with 0 and successively appending the Boolean complement of the sequence so far: 0, 01, 0110, 01101001, and so on. Equivalently, its n-th term is the parity of the number of 1s in the binary expansion of n.

Properties

The sequence is cube-free in the sense that it contains no three consecutive repetitions of the same block, a result central to Axel Thue's early work on combinatorics on words (1906–1912). It also never contains the pattern 01010 or 10101 in certain overlap senses, making it "overlap-free."

Applications

The sequence arises in fair division and the "fair sharing" of turns (the basis of the Thue–Morse ordering used to mitigate first-move advantage), in fractal geometry, and in the study of automatic sequences. Marston Morse rediscovered it in 1921 in work on differential geometry.
