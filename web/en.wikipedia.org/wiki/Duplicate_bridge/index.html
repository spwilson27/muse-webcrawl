Duplicate bridge

Duplicate bridge is the most common form of contract bridge played in clubs and tournaments. The same hands are played by several pairs of players at different tables, so that results can be compared fairly and the element of luck in the deal is greatly reduced: skill in bidding and play, rather than the luck of receiving good cards, determines the outcome.

Players4 (two partnerships) per table
EquipmentBoards, duplicate wallets, bidding boxes
Governing bodyWorld Bridge Federation
ScoringMatchpoints or IMPs

How it works

Each deal is placed in a numbered board with four slots holding the North, South, East and West hands. After a board is played at one table, the cards are returned to their slots and the board passes to the next table, so every pair plays the identical deals. Players move between tables according to a movement schedule directed by the tournament director.

Scoring

In matchpoint scoring (typical of pairs events), each pair's score on a board is compared with all other pairs who played the same board in the same direction: a pair receives two matchpoints for every pair it beats and one for every pair it ties. In teams events, scores are converted to International Match Points (IMPs) by comparing the two results achieved on the same board at two tables.

Organisation

Duplicate bridge is organised worldwide by the World Bridge Federation and national bodies such as the American Contract Bridge League and the English Bridge Union, which award masterpoints and ranks. Major events include the Bermuda Bowl world teams championship. Duplicate scoring and pre-dealt boards are also standard in online bridge.
