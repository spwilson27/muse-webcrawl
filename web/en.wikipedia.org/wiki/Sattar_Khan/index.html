Sattar Khan

Sattar Khan (Persian: ستارخان; 20 October 1866 – 15 November 1914), honoured with the title Sardar-e Melli ("National Commander"), was a hero of the Persian Constitutional Revolution. A luti and horse-trader from Tabriz of Azerbaijani origin, he led the defence of Tabriz against royalist forces in 1908–1909, becoming the most famous popular commander of the constitutional cause.

Constitutional Revolution

When Mohammad Ali Shah bombarded the Majlis (parliament) in June 1908 and moved to crush the constitution, Tabriz rose in revolt. Sattar Khan, together with Baqer Khan, organised the defence of the city's quarters, holding out for eleven months against royalist troops and a blockade that brought famine; foreign consuls and journalists made his stand famous across Europe. Constitutional forces eventually marched on Tehran and deposed the Shah in July 1909.

Later life

The restored government, fearing his popularity, moved to disarm his followers in Tehran. In a clash at Atabak Park in August 1910 Sattar Khan was wounded; he never recovered his influence and died in Tehran in 1914. His tomb in the Shah Abdol-Azim shrine complex in Ray remains a site of commemoration, and he is remembered in Iran and Azerbaijan as a folk hero of constitutionalism.
