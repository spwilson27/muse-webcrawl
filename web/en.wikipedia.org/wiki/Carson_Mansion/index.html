The Carson Mansion is a large Victorian house in Eureka, California, widely considered one of the finest examples of American Queen Anne architecture. It was built in 1884–1886 for lumber baron William Carson and has been described as the most grand Victorian home in California.

Architecture

Designed by San Francisco architects Samuel and Joseph Cather Newsom, the mansion combines Queen Anne, Stick and Eastlake styles in an exuberant composition of gables, turrets, balconies and carved ornament. It was built largely of redwood, with some 18 rooms, and employed more than 100 workers — a deliberate job-creation project by Carson during a lumber downturn. Stained glass, exotic woods and stencilled patterns enrich the interior. The house was famously praised (and half-mocked) by architectural historian Vincent Scully, and it influenced the look of "haunted house" attractions and gingerbread Victorian imagery across America.

Later history

The mansion remained in the Carson family until 1950, when it was sold to the Ingomar Club, a private social club that has maintained it since. The nearby Pink Lady, a smaller Queen Anne house, was built by Carson as a wedding present for his son in 1889. The mansion stands above Humboldt Bay and is Eureka's best-known landmark, though it is not open to the general public.
