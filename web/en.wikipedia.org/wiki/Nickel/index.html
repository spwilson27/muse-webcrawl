Nickel is the chemical element with the symbol Ni and atomic number 28. It is a hard, silvery-white, corrosion-resistant transition metal, one of the four elements ferromagnetic at room temperature (with iron, cobalt and gadolinium).

Most nickel goes into stainless steel and other alloys; it also coats coins (the US five-cent 'nickel'), forms the electrodes of NiCd and nickel-metal-hydride batteries, and is increasingly used in lithium-ion battery cathodes for electric vehicles.

History

German miners who found a red ore that yielded no copper blamed a mischievous sprite, 'Kupfernickel' (devil's copper). Axel Fredrik Cronstedt isolated the new metal from that ore in 1751 and named it nickel.

Properties

Nickel is only moderately reactive and resists corrosion by forming a protective oxide film. Earth's core is thought to be largely an iron–nickel alloy.
