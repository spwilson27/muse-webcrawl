Nā Pali Coast


Nā Pali Coast
Location: Northwestern Kauai, Hawaii, US
Length: about 17 miles (27 km)
Designation: Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park

The Nā Pali Coast is a rugged stretch of coastline on the northwestern shore of the Hawaiian island of Kauai, renowned for its towering sea cliffs (pali), which rise as much as 4,000 feet (1,200 m) above the Pacific Ocean, together with lush hanging valleys, waterfalls, and remote beaches. In Hawaiian, nā pali means "the cliffs".

Access

No road reaches the coast. It can be experienced by hiking the Kalalau Trail, an 11-mile trail from Keʻe Beach along the coast to Kalalau Beach, by sea kayak or boat tours, or by helicopter. The area is protected as the Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park, and camping requires permits.

History and culture

The valleys of the coast — including Kalalau, Hanakoa, and Miloliʻi — were inhabited by Native Hawaiians for centuries, who farmed taro in terraced fields and built heiau (temples) and house platforms whose remains survive. The coast has appeared in numerous films and television productions, including Jurassic Park and King Kong (1976).

Geography

The cliffs are the eroded edge of a shield volcano, carved by high winter surf and heavy rainfall descending from Mount Waiʻaleʻale, one of the wettest spots on Earth.
