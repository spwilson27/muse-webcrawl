Quiberon expedition

The Quiberon expedition (also called the invasion of France, 1795) was a landing of French royalist émigrés on the Quiberon peninsula in Brittany in the summer of 1795, supported by Great Britain, aimed at sparking a counter-revolution against the French Republic during the War in the Vendée.

Course

In late June 1795, a British fleet landed several thousand émigré troops under the comte de Puisaye and the comte d'Hervilly. The expedition secured the peninsula and Fort Penthièvre and expected support from local Chouan rebels, but disagreements between the two commanders paralysed operations. Republican general Lazare Hoche contained the bridgehead, then stormed Fort Penthièvre on the night of 20–21 July, forcing the royalists back to the tip of the peninsula.

Aftermath

Thousands of émigrés were evacuated by the Royal Navy, but many were captured; under laws against émigrés who had borne arms, several hundred — commonly given as around 750 — were shot after summary trials at Auray and Vannes, an event remembered as a royalist martyrdom. The failure crippled the émigré military effort and strengthened Hoche's pacification of the west.
