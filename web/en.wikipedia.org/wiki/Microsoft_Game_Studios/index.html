Microsoft Game Studios

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Former logo of Microsoft Game Studios

Microsoft Game Studios was the video game publishing division of Microsoft, responsible for first-party games for the Xbox consoles and Windows. It operated under this name from 2002 until 2011, when it was renamed Microsoft Studios; in 2019 the division was rebranded again as Xbox Game Studios, the name under which Microsoft's first-party publishing continues.

History

Microsoft entered serious games publishing in the late 1990s with PC titles such as Age of Empires and Microsoft Flight Simulator. With the launch of the original Xbox in 2001, the company formed Microsoft Game Studios in 2002 to publish games from its internal studios and partners, beginning an era defined by franchises such as Halo, Fable, Forza Motorsport and Gears of War.

The division oversaw a shifting portfolio of studios, acquiring developers such as Bungie (2000) and Rare (2002), and later closing or spinning off others; Bungie regained its independence in 2007 while retaining a publishing relationship for Halo titles. As Microsoft Studios, and subsequently Xbox Game Studios, the organisation continued to expand through major acquisitions in the late 2010s and beyond.
