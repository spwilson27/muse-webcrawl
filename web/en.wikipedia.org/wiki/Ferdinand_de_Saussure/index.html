Ferdinand de Saussure (26 November 1857 – 22 February 1913) was a Swiss linguist, semiotician and philosopher whose ideas laid the foundation for many significant developments in both linguistics and semiotics in the 20th century. He is widely considered one of the founders of modern linguistics and semiotics, sometimes alongside Charles Sanders Peirce.

His posthumous Course in General Linguistics (1916), compiled by students from his Geneva lectures, introduced structuralism: language as a system (langue) distinct from speech (parole); the sign as the union of signifier (sound-image) and signified (concept), whose link is arbitrary; and the analysis of language synchronically (a state) versus diachronically (its history). His youthful Mémoire (1878) predicted the 'laryngeal' consonants of Proto-Indo-European, spectacularly confirmed decades later by deciphered Hittite.
