Arava

The Arava valley, looking east toward Jordan.

The Arava (Hebrew: הָעֲרָבָה, HaAravah; Arabic: وادي عربة, Wādī ʿAraba), sometimes called the Arabah, is a valley in the rift system of the Levant, running roughly north–south between the Dead Sea in the north and the Gulf of Aqaba (Gulf of Eilat) in the south. It forms part of the border between Israel to the west and Jordan to the east, and is part of the Great Rift Valley.

Geography

The valley is about 166 km (103 mi) long. Its northern section descends below sea level near the Dead Sea, the lowest land on Earth, while the southern Arava rises gently to a watershed before descending again toward the Red Sea. The climate is extremely arid and hot: rainfall is scant and erratic, and summer temperatures frequently exceed 40 °C.

Settlements and nature

Despite the harsh climate, the Israeli side of the Arava hosts a string of kibbutzim and moshavim — including Yotvata, Ketura, and Ein Yahav — that practice advanced desert agriculture, exporting peppers, melons, dates, and flowers. The valley also contains the Timna Valley Park, site of ancient copper mines, and is an important flyway for migratory birds travelling between Europe and Africa. The main highway, Route 90, runs its length, connecting Eilat with the rest of Israel.
