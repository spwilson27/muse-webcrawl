Circumventricular organs

Locations of the circumventricular organs around the ventricles.

Circumventricular organs (CVOs) are structures in the brain characterised by their position around the ventricular system and by an incomplete blood–brain barrier: their capillaries are fenestrated, allowing neurons there to sense substances circulating in the blood — hormones, ions, toxins — and to secrete substances such as hormones directly into the circulation or cerebrospinal fluid. They thus form an interface between the blood, the brain, and the cerebrospinal fluid, central to neuroendocrine control and homeostasis.

The organs

The circumventricular organs usually recognised are:

  • The median eminence of the hypothalamus, where releasing hormones enter the hypophyseal portal system
  • The neurohypophysis (posterior pituitary), which releases oxytocin and vasopressin into the blood
  • The pineal gland, which secretes melatonin
  • The subfornical organ, involved in thirst and blood-pressure regulation
  • The organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis (OVLT), sensing plasma osmolality and circulating pyrogens in fever
  • The area postrema, the chemoreceptor trigger zone that induces vomiting in response to blood-borne toxins
  • The subcommissural organ, an ependymal gland secreting glycoproteins into the cerebrospinal fluid

Functions

Sensory CVOs such as the subfornical organ, OVLT, and area postrema monitor sodium, osmolality, angiotensin II, and toxins, driving thirst, salt appetite, vasopressin release, and nausea. Secretory CVOs — the median eminence, posterior pituitary, and pineal gland — release hypothalamic and other hormones into portal or systemic blood. Because they lie outside the blood–brain barrier, CVOs are also potential routes for circulating pathogens and signals to influence the brain, and they feature in research on fever, fluid balance, and cardiovascular regulation.
