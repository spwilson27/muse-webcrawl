Buffalo is a city in the U.S. state of New York, the seat of Erie County, situated at the eastern end of Lake Erie where the Niagara River begins, across from Ontario, Canada. It is the second-most populous city in New York State after New York City, with about 278,000 residents in the 2020 census — the first census in decades to record growth.

History

Laid out around 1801 by Joseph Ellicott of the Holland Land Company, Buffalo was burned by British forces in the War of 1812 and rebuilt. The opening of the Erie Canal in 1825, with Buffalo as its western terminus, made the city a great transshipment point for grain and goods between the Great Lakes and the Atlantic. It hosted the Pan-American Exposition of 1901, during which President William McKinley was assassinated. Heavy industry, especially steel and grain milling, declined in the later 20th century as shipping shifted away from the Great Lakes, halving the city's population from its 1950s peak.

Culture and landmarks

Buffalo is noted for its park system designed by Frederick Law Olmsted, the Albright-Knox Art Gallery, Frank Lloyd Wright's Darwin Martin House, and the Art Deco Buffalo City Hall. It is the namesake of Buffalo wings, created at the Anchor Bar in 1964, and of the Buffalo Bills and Sabres. Recent decades have brought reinvestment in the waterfront (Canalside), a medical campus, and modest population recovery.
