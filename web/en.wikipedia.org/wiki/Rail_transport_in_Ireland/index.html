Rail transport in Ireland comprises passenger and freight services on the island of Ireland, operated by Iarnród Éireann (Irish Rail) in the Republic of Ireland and NI Railways in Northern Ireland. Unusually for Europe, the network uses a broad track gauge of 1,600 mm (5 ft 3 in).

History

Ireland's first railway, the Dublin and Kingstown Railway, opened in 1834. By the early 20th century the island had an extensive network of main lines and narrow-gauge branches; after partition in 1921, cross-border operation continued, but many lines closed in the 1950s and 1960s, leaving the present radial network centred on Dublin and Belfast.

Services

Intercity services link Dublin with Cork, Galway, Limerick, Waterford and Belfast (the Enterprise service, jointly operated). Dublin has DART electric suburban services along the coast and diesel commuter lines, and Luas light rail. Freight traffic is small compared with passenger volumes.
