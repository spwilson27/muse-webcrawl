Walter Behrmann

Walter Behrmann (May 22, 1882 – May 3, 1955) was a German geographer best remembered for the map projection that bears his name and for his work in New Guinea and on the regional geography of Germany.

Life and work

Behrmann studied geography in Berlin and took part in a 1912–1913 expedition to German New Guinea, exploring the Sepik (then Kaiserin-Augusta) River region; his account of the journey, Im Stromgebiet des Sepik, appeared in 1917. He later held teaching posts in Berlin and, from 1929, at Bucharest, and after the Second World War at the Free University of Berlin.

Behrmann projection

In 1910 Behrmann published a modification of the cylindrical equal-area projection with standard parallels at 30°N and 30°S. The Behrmann projection preserves area while reducing the extreme stretching near the poles found in Lambert's cylindrical equal-area projection with standard parallels at the equator. It belongs to the family of cylindrical equal-area projections differing only in their standard parallels.
