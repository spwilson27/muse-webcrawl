Stabilizer (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, a stabilizer is a fixed or movable surface on an aircraft that provides stability and helps keep the aircraft in controlled flight. Stabilizers are a fundamental part of the empennage (tail assembly) of most fixed-wing aircraft, and their proper function is essential for the longitudinal and directional stability of the aeroplane.

Types

Horizontal stabilizer

The horizontal stabilizer is the fixed horizontal surface at the tail of an aircraft. It provides pitch (longitudinal) stability by generating a downward or upward force that balances the pitching moment produced by the wing. Attached to its trailing edge, or forming its movable portion, is the elevator, which the pilot uses to control pitch. On many aircraft the entire horizontal stabilizer is movable — an arrangement called a stabilator or all-flying tailplane — which increases control authority, particularly at transonic speeds.

Vertical stabilizer

The vertical stabilizer, often called the fin, is the fixed vertical surface that provides yaw (directional) stability, keeping the nose of the aircraft pointed into the relative wind. The rudder is hinged to its trailing edge and is used by the pilot to control yaw, especially during crosswind take-offs and landings and to coordinate turns.

Function

Stabilizers work on the same aerodynamic principle as wings: they are airfoils which, when the aircraft deviates from its trimmed attitude, experience a change in angle of attack that generates a restoring force. Because the tail surfaces are located far behind the centre of gravity, even modest aerodynamic forces produce large restoring moments. The size, position, and airfoil section of the stabilizers are chosen by designers so that the aircraft is statically and dynamically stable in normal flight, while still remaining manoeuvrable.

On some aircraft the horizontal stabilizer is mounted partway up the vertical fin (a cruciform tail) or at the top of the fin (a T-tail), arrangements that keep the stabilizer clear of the wing wake and engine exhaust at the cost of greater structural weight and, in the T-tail case, a susceptibility to deep stall.

Adjustable stabilizers

Many aircraft, especially airliners, allow the incidence of the whole horizontal stabilizer to be adjusted in flight for trim purposes. This trimmable stabilizer relieves the pilot of sustained elevator forces and accommodates changes in centre of gravity, speed, and configuration. Trim is normally controlled electrically, with a manual backup.
