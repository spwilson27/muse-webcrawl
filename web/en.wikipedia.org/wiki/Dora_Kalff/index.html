Dora Kalff

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dora Maria Kalff (née Gattiker; 6 April 1904 – 15 January 1990) was a Swiss Jungian analyst who founded sandplay therapy, a method of psychotherapy in which clients create scenes in a tray of sand using miniature figures, described in her book Sandplay: A Psychotherapeutic Approach to the Psyche (German original 1966).

Life and work

Kalff was born in Richterswil, Switzerland, studied piano and languages in Europe, and after the Second World War trained in analytical psychology in Zurich, where Carl Jung encouraged her interest in child therapy. In London she studied with Margaret Lowenfeld, originator of the "world technique" using toys and sand trays with children, and with D. W. Winnicott.

Sandplay

Combining Lowenfeld's technique with Jungian psychology and Eastern contemplative thought, Kalff developed sandplay as a non-verbal therapy for children and adults. In a "free and protected space" provided by the therapist, the client arranges miniatures in a wooden tray partly filled with sand; the resulting series of images is understood as a symbolic unfolding of the psyche and of the individuation process. Kalff taught internationally, and the International Society for Sandplay Therapy, founded under her influence, continues her work.
