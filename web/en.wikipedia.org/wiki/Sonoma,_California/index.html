Sonoma is a city in Sonoma County, California, in the Sonoma Valley wine country. It is known for its historic town plaza — the largest in California — its role in the Bear Flag Revolt of 1846, and its place at the heart of California's wine industry.

History

The site was home to Indigenous peoples including the Coast Miwok and Pomo. Mission San Francisco Solano, the last and northernmost of the California missions, was founded here in 1823. In 1835, General Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo laid out the town as a Mexican pueblo. On 14 June 1846, American settlers proclaimed the short-lived California Republic in the Bear Flag Revolt, raising their flag in Sonoma Plaza before California was annexed by the United States.

Wine

Sonoma is often called the birthplace of California wine. Hungarian-born Ágoston Haraszthy founded Buena Vista Winery here in 1857, importing European grape varieties. The surrounding Sonoma Valley AVA remains a major wine-producing region alongside neighbouring Napa Valley.

Geography and tourism

The city sits in the Sonoma Valley between the Mayacamas and Sonoma Mountains. Its plaza, surrounded by historic adobe buildings, restaurants, and tasting rooms, is the centre of a substantial tourism economy.
