Christen Schiellerup Købke (26 May 1810 – 7 February 1848) was a Danish painter and one of the most celebrated artists of the Danish Golden Age. His meticulously observed landscapes, architectural views and portraits are noted for their clarity of light and quiet, unidealised naturalism.

[Hero image: Købke, View of Lake Sortedam (1838) — image placeholder]

Life and training

Købke was born in Copenhagen, the son of a baker. He studied at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts from the age of twelve, first under Christian August Lorentzen and then under Christoffer Wilhelm Eckersberg, whose emphasis on direct observation shaped his art. In 1838 he travelled to Italy, visiting Rome and Naples with his fellow painter Constantin Hansen; the journey produced relatively few of his major works.

Work

Among his best-known paintings are View of Lake Sortedam from the Dosseringen near Nørrebro (1838), views of Frederiksborg Castle, and intimate portraits of family members and fellow artists such as the decorator Georg Hilker and the painter Frederik Sødring. Despite the quality of his work, Købke failed to be elected to the Academy shortly before his early death from pneumonia at the age of 37. He is buried in Assistens Cemetery, Copenhagen.

Legacy

Købke's works are held principally by the National Gallery of Denmark and the Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek; The Forum, Pompeii, with Vesuvius in the Background is in the Getty Museum, Los Angeles.
