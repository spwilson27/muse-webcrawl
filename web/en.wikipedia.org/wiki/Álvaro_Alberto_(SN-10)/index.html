Álvaro Alberto (pennant number SN-10) is a nuclear-powered attack submarine under construction for the Brazilian Navy. She is the culminating vessel of the PROSUB submarine development programme and would make Brazil the seventh country in the world — and the only one besides the five permanent members of the UN Security Council and India — to operate nuclear-powered submarines. The boat is named after Admiral Álvaro Alberto da Motta e Silva, a pioneer of nuclear science in Brazil.

Development

Brazil's naval nuclear programme dates to the 1970s. The Navy mastered the uranium enrichment fuel cycle in 1988, and the land-based prototype reactor, LABGENE, is being built at the Aramar Experimental Center in Iperó, São Paulo. Under a 2008 strategic partnership with France, Brazil acquired Scorpène-class technology for four conventional submarines (the Riachuelo class) and assistance with the non-nuclear design of the nuclear boat.

Design

Álvaro Alberto is expected to displace around 6,000 tonnes submerged, considerably larger than the conventional Riachuelo-class boats, and to be powered by a pressurized water reactor developed domestically, designated RENAP-11. Construction of the pressure hull sections began at the Itaguaí naval complex in Rio de Janeiro state, and launch has been projected for the early 2030s, though schedules have slipped repeatedly.

Significance

Brazil frames the programme as a deterrent for protecting the "Blue Amazon" — its offshore oil reserves, sea lanes, and maritime resources in the South Atlantic. Because Brazil is a non-nuclear-weapon state party to the Treaty of Tlatelolco and the NPT, its naval fuel arrangements with the IAEA have attracted sustained international attention as a precedent for safeguards on naval nuclear propulsion.
