Abdullah ibn Muhammad Al Khalifa (Arabic: عبد الله بن محمد آل خليفة) was a member of the Al Khalifa family, the Sunni ruling house of Bahrain, which has governed the islands since the family's conquest of Bahrain from Persian control in 1783. The Al Khalifa were a branch of the Bani Utbah tribal confederation that had earlier taken control of Kuwait and Zubarah in Qatar.

Historical context

During the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, authority in Bahrain was frequently shared or contested among senior Al Khalifa sheikhs, often ruling jointly from Muharraq and Riffa. Figures named Abdullah within the family appear in the dynastic record in this period of co-rulership, civil conflict and growing British involvement in the Gulf, which culminated in the treaties of 1861 and 1880–92 that made Bahrain a British protected state while leaving the Al Khalifa in internal power.

Notes on the name

The form "Ibn-Mohammed Al-Khalifa", with hyphenated transliteration, reflects nineteenth- and early twentieth-century British gazetteer and India Office usage for members of the Bahraini ruling family. Modern transliteration more commonly renders the name Abdullah bin Muhammad Al Khalifa.
