Martyr

A martyr is someone who suffers persecution and death for advocating, renouncing, or refusing to renounce a religious belief or other cause. The word derives from Greek martys, "witness". Most martyrs are considered holy or heroic in their traditions.

EtymologyGreek martys — witness
TraditionsChristianity, Islam, Judaism, others

In Christianity

Stephen is regarded as the first Christian martyr (protomartyr). Early Christians persecuted under Rome, commemorated in martyrologies, shaped ideals of witness; types include red martyrdom (death) and white martyrdom (ascetic life).

In other traditions

In Islam a shahid is a martyr who dies in the path of God. Martyrdom also appears in Judaism, Sikhism, Hinduism, and secular contexts, where people die for political or national causes and are memorialised as martyrs.
