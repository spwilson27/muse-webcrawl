The University of Pavia (Università degli Studi di Pavia) is a public university in Pavia, Lombardy, Italy. Founded in 1361 by charter of Emperor Charles IV at the request of Galeazzo II Visconti, it is one of Europe's oldest universities, with origins in a school of law documented as early as the 9th century.

History

The university flourished under Milanese and later Spanish and Austrian rule. In the 18th and 19th centuries it was home to scholars such as Alessandro Volta, inventor of the electric battery, anatomist Antonio Scarpa, and mathematician Eugenio Beltrami. Nobel laureates Camillo Golgi and Giulio Natta also worked at Pavia.

Structure

Pavia is famous for its system of residential colleges (collegi), including the historic Almo Collegio Borromeo and Collegio Ghislieri, which combine housing with academic and cultural life. The university teaches across the sciences, medicine, humanities, law, economics, and engineering.
