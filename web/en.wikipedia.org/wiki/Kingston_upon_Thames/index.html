Kingston upon Thames

Borough: Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames
Region: London
Historic county: Surrey

Kingston upon Thames, often simply Kingston, is a town in southwest London, England, on the River Thames, and is the administrative centre of the Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames. It was historically the county town of Surrey.

History

Kingston is one of England's oldest royal boroughs. Several Saxon kings are traditionally said to have been crowned there in the 10th century, including Athelstan in 925 and Ethelred the Unready in 978; the Coronation Stone, now outside the town's Guildhall, is associated with these ceremonies. Kingston received its first charter from King John in 1200 and was an important market town and Thames crossing, with a bridge documented from the 13th century.

Today

The town is a major retail and commercial centre for southwest London, with a historic market square, the Bentall Centre shopping mall, and a riverside with pubs and walks. Kingston University is based in the town. It is served by Kingston railway station, with trains to London Waterloo.
