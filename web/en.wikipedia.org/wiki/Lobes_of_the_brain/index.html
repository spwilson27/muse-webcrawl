Lobes of the brain

Lobes of the brain
Part ofCerebrum
ComponentsFrontal, parietal, temporal, occipital lobes (traditionally four); sometimes insular and limbic lobes
SystemCentral nervous system

The lobes of the brain are the major divisions of the cerebral cortex in each hemisphere of the brain. Mammals are traditionally described as having four main lobes in each hemisphere: the frontal lobe, parietal lobe, temporal lobe, and occipital lobe, named after the bones of the skull that overlie them. The insular cortex, buried within the lateral sulcus, is sometimes counted as a fifth lobe, and the limbic lobe as a further division.

Frontal lobe

The frontal lobe is the largest lobe, lying at the front of the brain, separated from the parietal lobe by the central sulcus (fissure of Rolando) and from the temporal lobe by the lateral sulcus (Sylvian fissure). It contains the primary motor cortex and the prefrontal cortex, and is involved in voluntary movement, expressive language (Broca's area, usually in the left hemisphere), executive function, planning, decision-making and aspects of personality.

Parietal lobe

The parietal lobe lies behind the central sulcus and above the lateral sulcus. It integrates somatosensory information — touch, proprioception, temperature and pain — in the primary somatosensory cortex of the postcentral gyrus, and plays a major role in spatial awareness and navigation. Damage can produce neglect of one side of space.

Temporal lobe

The temporal lobe sits beneath the lateral sulcus on each side of the brain. It contains the primary auditory cortex and is essential for processing sound, understanding language (Wernicke's area, typically left-sided), and forming long-term memories through the hippocampus and associated structures on its medial surface.

Occipital lobe

The occipital lobe is the smallest and most posterior lobe, devoted primarily to vision. The primary visual cortex (striate cortex, V1) lies along the calcarine sulcus on its medial surface; visual information is organised retinotopically and passed forward into the dorsal ("where") and ventral ("what") streams.

Insular and limbic lobes

The insular cortex, folded deep within the lateral sulcus, is involved in interoception, taste, autonomic control and aspects of emotion and consciousness. The limbic lobe, an arc of cortex on the medial surface including the cingulate and parahippocampal gyri, is associated with emotion and memory as part of the limbic system.
