Lebanon–Syria relations

Lebanon–Syria relations are the bilateral relations between the Lebanese Republic and Syria. The two countries emerged from Ottoman rule and the French Mandate, and their histories remain deeply intertwined. Syria long treated Lebanon as within its sphere of influence; formal diplomatic relations were only established in 2008, decades after both states' independence.

History

Syrian forces entered Lebanon in 1976 during the Lebanese Civil War and remained for 29 years, a period often described in Lebanon as the Syrian occupation. The 2005 assassination of former prime minister Rafic Hariri triggered the Cedar Revolution and Syria's withdrawal that year under international pressure. In 2008 Syria recognized Lebanese sovereignty and the two states exchanged ambassadors for the first time.

Syrian civil war era

The Syrian civil war (from 2011) brought over a million Syrian refugees into Lebanon and split Lebanese politics between pro- and anti-Assad camps. After the fall of the Assad regime in December 2024, Lebanon's new leadership sought to reset relations, addressing border security, smuggling, missing persons from the occupation era, and the return of refugees.

Issues

Outstanding issues include demarcation of the border, the Shebaa Farms dispute, and trade and transit ties, Syria historically being Lebanon's only land outlet to the wider Arab world.
