JVC

JVC (Victor Company of Japan)

Native name: 日本ビクター株式会社

Founded: September 13, 1927, Yokohama, Japan

Headquarters: Yokohama, Japan

Parent: JVCKenwood

Known for: VHS format

JVC (short for Japan Victor Company, in Japanese Nihon Bikuta Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese consumer and professional electronics brand, best known for developing the VHS video recording format, which defeated Sony's Betamax in the videotape format war of the late 1970s and 1980s. Since 2008 it has been part of JVCKenwood, following a merger with Kenwood Corporation.

History

JVC was founded in 1927 in Yokohama as the Japanese subsidiary of the American Victor Talking Machine Company, initially making phonographs and records. It became independent during the Second World War and after the war grew into a major manufacturer of televisions, radios, and audio equipment. Under the Victor brand in Japan it used the famous "His Master's Voice" dog-and-gramophone trademark (Nipper).

VHS and video

In 1976 JVC introduced the HR-3300, the first VCR using the VHS (Video Home System) format it had developed. By licensing VHS widely to other manufacturers while Sony initially guarded Betamax, JVC built an alliance that captured the market; by the late 1980s VHS accounted for the overwhelming majority of home video sales. JVC went on to develop S-VHS, the VHS-C camcorder format, Digital VHS, and contributed to the HDV and Everio camcorder lines.

Later years

Faced with losses in flat-panel televisions and the decline of physical video, JVC merged with Kenwood in 2008 to form JVC Kenwood Holdings, renamed JVCKenwood in 2011. The JVC brand continues on projectors (noted for high-contrast D-ILA home-theater models), headphones, car audio, and professional broadcast equipment.
