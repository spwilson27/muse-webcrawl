Rotavirus vaccine

Rotavirus vaccine is a live attenuated oral vaccine used to protect infants against rotavirus infections, the leading cause of severe dehydrating diarrhoea in young children worldwide.

Use

The World Health Organization recommends that rotavirus vaccine be included in all national immunisation programmes. Two widely used vaccines are Rotarix (a monovalent human strain vaccine, two doses) and RotaTeq (a pentavalent bovine–human reassortant vaccine, three doses), given orally beginning at about six weeks of age. Additional vaccines such as Rotavac and Rotasiil are used in India and other countries.

Effectiveness and safety

In high-income countries the vaccines prevent roughly 80–90% of severe rotavirus disease; effectiveness is lower but the public-health impact greater in low-income settings, where rotavirus mortality is concentrated. Introduction of vaccination has sharply reduced hospitalisations for childhood diarrhoea in many countries. A small increased risk of intussusception (around 1–7 extra cases per 100,000 infants) has been observed; this risk led to withdrawal of an earlier vaccine, RotaShield, in 1999, but is judged far outweighed by benefits for current vaccines.
