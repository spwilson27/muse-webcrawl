Lebesgue number

In topology, the Lebesgue covering lemma states that for a compact metric space and any open cover of it, there exists a number δ > 0, called a Lebesgue number of the cover, such that every subset of the space having diameter less than δ is contained in some member of the cover. The lemma is named after the French mathematician Henri Lebesgue.

Statement

Let (X, d) be a compact metric space and let {Uα} be an open cover of X. Then there exists δ > 0 such that for every subset A ⊆ X with diameter less than δ, there is an index α with A ⊆ Uα.

Proof idea

Because the cover is open, around each point x there is a ball contained in some member of the cover. These balls cover X; taking balls of half the radius and extracting a finite subcover by compactness, the minimum of the finitely many radii serves as a Lebesgue number.

Applications

The Lebesgue number lemma is a standard tool in proving that a continuous function on a compact metric space is uniformly continuous, and it underlies the proof of the Heine–Borel theorem and results in simplicial approximation in algebraic topology.
