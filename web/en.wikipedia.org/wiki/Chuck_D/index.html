Chuck D (born Carlton Douglas Ridenhour; 1 August 1960) is an American rapper, author, and lecturer, best known as the leader and frontman of the hip hop group Public Enemy, which he co-founded in Long Island, New York, in 1982 (as 1985 with Flavor Flav and the Bomb Squad production team). His powerful baritone delivery and politically charged lyrics made Public Enemy one of the most influential acts in hip hop history.

Career

Ridenhour grew up in Roosevelt, Long Island, and studied graphic design at Adelphi University, where he promoted parties and worked at the campus radio station WBAU. Signed to Def Jam Recordings, Public Enemy released Yo! Bum Rush the Show (1987), followed by It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back (1988), widely ranked among the greatest albums of all time, Fear of a Black Planet (1990) — including "Fight the Power," written for Spike Lee's film Do the Right Thing — and Apocalypse 91... The Enemy Strikes Black (1991).

Activism and later work

Chuck D has been an outspoken advocate on issues including civil rights, the music industry, and file-sharing, which he notably defended in the Napster era. He hosted political talk programming, narrated documentaries, published books including Fight the Power: Rap, Race and Reality, and helped pioneer online music distribution through Rapstation. In 2016 he formed the supergroup Prophets of Rage with members of Rage Against the Machine and Cypress Hill. Public Enemy was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2013.
