Duke of Devonshire is a title in the Peerage of England held by members of the Cavendish family. It was created in 1694 for William Cavendish, 4th Earl of Devonshire, one of the "Immortal Seven" who invited William of Orange to take the English throne. Despite the title, the family's estates and seat are centred on Derbyshire, at Chatsworth House.

History

The Cavendish family descends from Sir William Cavendish, a courtier of Henry VIII. Members of the family have included the 7th Duke, a noted industrialist and university chancellor; the 8th Duke, Marquess of Hartington, a leading Liberal statesman; and Spencer Cavendish, whose refusal of the premiership is a famous episode in Victorian politics. Deborah Mitford, the youngest of the Mitford sisters, was Duchess of Devonshire as wife of the 11th Duke.

The current holder is Peregrine Cavendish, 12th Duke of Devonshire. The heir apparent is William Cavendish, Earl of Burlington.
