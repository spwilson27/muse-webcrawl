Cluster munition

A cluster munition (or cluster bomb) is a form of air-dropped or ground-launched explosive weapon that releases or ejects smaller submunitions, commonly called bomblets. Because cluster bombs release many small bomblets over a wide area, they pose risks to civilians both during attacks and afterwards: unexploded bomblets can kill or maim civilians long after a conflict has ended and function like land mines.

History

Cluster munitions were first used in the Second World War and were employed extensively in the Vietnam War, the Gulf War, Kosovo, Iraq, and Afghanistan. Laos is the most heavily bombed country per capita in history, with millions of unexploded bomblets remaining from U.S. bombing during the Laotian Civil War.

Convention on Cluster Munitions

The Convention on Cluster Munitions, adopted in Dublin in 2008 and in force since 2010, prohibits the use, production, transfer, and stockpiling of cluster munitions for its states parties, more than 110 of which have joined. Major military powers including the United States, Russia, and China are not parties, and the weapons have continued to be used in conflicts such as those in Syria and Ukraine.
