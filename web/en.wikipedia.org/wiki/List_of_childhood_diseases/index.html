List of childhood diseases

This is a list of diseases that occur most commonly, or exclusively, in childhood. Many are infectious diseases that were once near-universal experiences of childhood but have become far less common in countries with high vaccination coverage.

Common infectious diseases of childhood

  • Chickenpox (varicella) — itchy blistering rash caused by varicella-zoster virus; now vaccine-preventable.
  • Measles — highly contagious viral illness with fever and rash; prevented by the MMR vaccine.
  • Mumps — viral infection causing swollen parotid (salivary) glands.
  • Rubella (German measles) — mild rash illness, dangerous to unborn babies if caught in pregnancy.
  • Whooping cough (pertussis) — bacterial respiratory infection with characteristic coughing fits.
  • Scarlet fever — streptococcal infection with a sandpaper rash.
  • Fifth disease (erythema infectiosum) — parvovirus B19 infection with a "slapped cheek" rash.
  • Hand, foot, and mouth disease — usually caused by coxsackievirus A16.
  • Roseola (sixth disease) — high fever followed by rash in infants, caused by HHV-6.
  • Croup — barking cough from viral swelling of the larynx and trachea.
  • Bronchiolitis — most often caused by respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in infants.
  • Impetigo — contagious bacterial skin infection with honey-coloured crusts.
  • Conjunctivitis ("pink eye"), ear infections (otitis media), and threadworms (pinworms) are also extremely common.

Other conditions of childhood

  • Kawasaki disease — inflammation of blood vessels in young children
  • Febrile seizures — convulsions triggered by fever in children aged 6 months to 5 years
  • Growing pains, Osgood–Schlatter disease, and Sever's disease
  • Intussusception and pyloric stenosis (surgical conditions of infancy)
  • Genetic and congenital disorders such as cystic fibrosis, Down syndrome, and congenital heart defects
  • Developmental conditions such as autism spectrum disorder and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder
