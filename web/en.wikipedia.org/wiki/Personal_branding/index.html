Personal branding

Personal branding
Term popularised1997, by Tom Peters ("The Brand Called You")
FieldMarketing, career development
Key mediaSocial media, personal websites, public speaking
Personal branding is largely conducted through online profiles and media

Personal branding is the conscious, strategic effort to create and shape a public perception of an individual by positioning them as an authority in their industry, elevating their credibility, and differentiating them from the competition, usually to advance their career or business. It treats the person much as commercial branding treats a product: a managed identity, message and reputation directed at a target audience.

History

Ideas of deliberate self-presentation are older — Napoleon Hill wrote about a pleasing personality as a career asset in Think and Grow Rich (1937), and positioning theory (Ries and Trout, 1981) was applied to individuals in Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind. The term itself was popularised by management writer Tom Peters in his 1997 Fast Company cover article "The Brand Called You", which argued that in a changing labour market every worker is effectively the chief executive of their own company, "Me Inc." The rise of social media in the 2000s made personal branding a routine practice for professionals, freelancers, executives and online creators alike.

Practice

Personal branding typically involves defining a niche and value proposition, maintaining a consistent visual and verbal identity across channels, publishing content that demonstrates expertise, and monitoring one's search results and reputation. It overlaps with self-promotion and impression management but is framed as aligning an authentic public identity with professional goals.

Criticism

Critics argue that personal branding commodifies identity, blurs the line between private life and work, rewards self-promotion over substance, and pressures workers to perform unpaid marketing labour. Others note authenticity tensions: a heavily managed brand can read as inauthentic, undermining the trust it aims to build.
