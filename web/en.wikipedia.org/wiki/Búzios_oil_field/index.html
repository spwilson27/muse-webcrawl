The Búzios oil field (Portuguese: Campo de Búzios), formerly known as the Franco field, is a giant deepwater oil field in the pre-salt layer of the Santos Basin, about 200 kilometres off the coast of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Operated by the state-controlled company Petrobras, Búzios is one of the largest oil fields in the world by reserves and production, and the flagship of Brazil's pre-salt province, which has turned the country into one of the world's leading oil producers.

Discovery and geology

The accumulation was discovered in 2010 by the well 2-ANP-1-RJS, drilled in an area ceded to Petrobras by the Brazilian government under the "transfer of rights" (cessão onerosa) regime. The reservoir lies some 5,000–7,000 metres below sea level, beneath roughly 2,000 metres of water and a thick layer of evaporite salt about 2,000 metres deep. The oil is held in microbial carbonate reservoirs of the Barra Velha formation, characterised by exceptionally high productivity wells; the crude is light (around 28–30° API) with a high gas-oil ratio and significant associated CO2 content, which is reinjected into the reservoir.

Development

Production began in April 2018 with the FPSO P-74. Development proceeds in phases, each centred on a large floating production, storage and offloading unit (FPSO) capable of producing roughly 150,000–225,000 barrels of oil per day. Output climbed rapidly: Búzios passed one million barrels per day of production capacity in the mid-2020s, making it Brazil's largest producing field, ahead of Tupi. In November 2019 Brazil auctioned the field's surplus transfer-of-rights volumes in one of the world's largest oil auctions; Petrobras, with Chinese partners CNODC and CNOOC holding minority stakes, won the Búzios block with a signing bonus of about R$68 billion.

Significance

Búzios symbolises the transformation of Brazil's oil industry by the pre-salt discoveries announced from 2006 onward. Its high-productivity wells give it among the lowest lifting costs of any deepwater province, but its development also raises debates in Brazil over climate commitments, the role of Petrobras, and the distribution of oil rents.
