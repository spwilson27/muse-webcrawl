Townsend Thoresen

Founded: 1968 (merger)
Defunct: 1987
Fate: Renamed P&O European Ferries
Services: Cross-Channel and other ferry routes

Townsend Thoresen was a British ferry operator formed in 1968 by the merger of Townsend Brothers Ferries and Thoresen Ferries (Atlantic Steam Navigation Company). It operated roll-on/roll-off car ferry services across the English Channel and on other routes around the British Isles, recognizable by its bright orange hulls and the slogan "the orange funnel line".

History

Townsend Brothers had pioneered drive-on ferry services between Dover and Calais in the 1930s and 1950s, while Thoresen introduced car ferries to Southampton routes. After the merger, the combined company became one of the largest cross-Channel operators. Its parent from 1971 was the European Ferries group, which in 1985 was taken over by P&O.

Herald of Free Enterprise disaster

On 6 March 1987 the company's ferry MS Herald of Free Enterprise capsized shortly after leaving Zeebrugge, Belgium, with the loss of 193 lives, because her bow doors had been left open. The official inquiry found fault at every level of the company, describing a "disease of sloppiness". The disaster destroyed the brand's reputation: Townsend Thoresen was renamed P&O European Ferries later in 1987, and the case led to major reforms in ferry safety and, in law, renewed debate over corporate manslaughter in the United Kingdom.
