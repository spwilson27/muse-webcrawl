French aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle

Charles de Gaulle is the flagship of the French Navy. The ship, commissioned in 2001, is the tenth French aircraft carrier, the first French nuclear-powered surface vessel, and the only nuclear-powered carrier completed outside the United States Navy. She carries a complement of Dassault Rafale M fighters and E-2C Hawkeye airborne early warning aircraft, and displaces about 42,500 tonnes.

History

The carrier replaced the conventionally powered Foch. Construction began in 1989 at Brest; the ship was launched in 1994 and commissioned in 2001 after delays, including problems with her nuclear propulsion derived from the reactors of the Triomphant-class submarines. She has deployed in support of operations in Afghanistan, Libya, and against the Islamic State from the eastern Mediterranean and the Persian Gulf.
