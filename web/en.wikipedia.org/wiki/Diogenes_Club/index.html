Diogenes Club

The Diogenes Club is a fictional gentlemen's club created by Arthur Conan Doyle and featured in several of his Sherlock Holmes stories, notably "The Adventure of the Greek Interpreter". It is described as a place for men who have no wish for company but still enjoy comforts, and its most famous member is Mycroft Holmes, the elder brother of Sherlock Holmes.

According to Sherlock Holmes, the club was founded for "the most unsociable and unclubable men in London". No talking is permitted in the club, except in the Stranger's Room, and members who break the rule three times can be expelled. The club is located in Carlton House Terrace, near Pall Mall.

Mycroft Holmes

Mycroft, one of the club's co-founders, is integral to the British government and is said by Sherlock to sometimes be the British government. The club serves as a meeting place where Holmes first introduces Dr. Watson to Mycroft.

In other works

The Diogenes Club has been widely referenced in later fiction. In Kim Newman's Anno Dracula series it is reimagined as a front for a secret government agency. It also appears in works by other authors writing pastiches and expansions of the Holmes canon, and in television adaptations such as the BBC series Sherlock, where it appears as a quiet retreat for government figures.
