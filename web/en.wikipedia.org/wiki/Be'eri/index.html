Be'eri

Country: Israel
District: Southern District
Founded: 1946
Type: Kibbutz

Be'eri (Hebrew: בְּאֵרִי) is a kibbutz in southern Israel, in the north-western Negev desert a few kilometres east of the Gaza Strip. Founded in 1946 as one of the "11 points in the Negev", it is named after Berl Katznelson's Hebrew pen name, Be'eri. The kibbutz is known for its large printing house, Be'eri Printers, and for its cycling culture and surrounding anemone fields.

History

Be'eri was established on the night of 6 October 1946 by members of the HaNoar HaOved and scouting movement groups, as part of a plan to establish a Jewish presence in the Negev. The community developed agriculture — orchards, field crops, dairy — and in the 1950s founded a printing press that became one of Israel's largest, printing government forms, ID cards and lottery tickets.

October 2023 attack

On 7 October 2023, during the Hamas-led attack on Israel, militants infiltrated Be'eri and killed more than 130 residents and defenders — roughly a tenth of the community — kidnapped others to Gaza, and set many homes ablaze. The massacre at Be'eri was one of the deadliest single incidents of the attack. Surviving residents were evacuated, and the community has since centred on rebuilding and the return of hostages.

Geography and nature

Near the kibbutz lies the Be'eri Badlands (battarot, or loess craters) and the Be'eri Forest, planted by the Jewish National Fund; each winter the area draws visitors for the "Red South" (Darom Adom) festival celebrating the blooming of red anemones.
