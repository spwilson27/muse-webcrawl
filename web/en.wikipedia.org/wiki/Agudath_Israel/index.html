Agudath Israel

Agudath Israel (Hebrew: אגודת ישראל, "Union of Israel"), also called Agudah, is an international organisation of Orthodox Jews, founded in 1912 in Kattowitz (Katowice), then in the German Empire, to unite Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) Jewry in defence of traditional Judaism against secularising movements, Reform Judaism, and political Zionism. Its affiliated bodies include Agudath Israel of America and the Agudat Yisrael political party in Israel, which represents Haredi interests in the Knesset, usually in alliance with Degel HaTorah in the United Torah Judaism list.

History

Agudath Israel was established in May 1912 by leading rabbis and lay leaders from Germany, Eastern Europe, and elsewhere, partly in reaction to the Tenth Zionist Congress's turn toward cultural work in 1911. Its German founders, influenced by Samson Raphael Hirsch's neo-Orthodoxy, joined with Eastern European traditionalists despite differing outlooks. Between the world wars, Agudath Israel grew strongly in Poland, where it ran schools—including the Beis Yaakov network for girls founded by Sarah Schenirer—published newspapers, and won seats in the Sejm. Much of its European base was destroyed in the Holocaust, after which the movement rebuilt in Israel and the United States.

In Israel

Agudat Yisrael participated in Israel's provisional government and signed the 1947 "status quo" letter with David Ben-Gurion concerning Shabbat, kashrut, marriage, and education. In the Knesset it advocates for yeshiva funding, military-service exemptions for yeshiva students, and religious legislation, and has joined many governing coalitions. Its constituency is principally Hasidic, guided by rebbes through the Council of Torah Sages.

In the United States

Agudath Israel of America, founded in 1922 and revitalised by refugees from Europe, serves as a major advocacy and service organisation for the Haredi community, operating government-affairs lobbying, social services, and organising the Siyum HaShas celebrations of the Daf Yomi cycle, which have filled stadiums. Its rabbinic leadership is the Moetzes Gedolei HaTorah of America.

Ideology

The movement is guided by Da'as Torah—deference to the rulings of senior rabbis on public as well as religious questions. Historically non-Zionist or anti-Zionist in differing degrees, Agudath Israel has generally adopted pragmatic cooperation with the State of Israel while maintaining theological reservations about secular Zionism.
