Makati

Makati, officially the City of Makati (Filipino: Lungsod ng Makati), is a highly urbanized city in the National Capital Region of the Philippines. It is the country's financial center, home to the Makati Central Business District, and has the highest concentration of multinational and local corporations in the Philippines. Major banks, corporations, and department stores are located in the city. Makati is also home to the Philippine Stock Exchange (though its trading floor has since moved) and forms one of the key hubs of Metro Manila. Its population was about 630,000 in the 2020 census, swelling to several million during the workday.

History

The name Makati is said to derive from the Tagalog phrase kumakati ang tubig, referring to the ebbing tides of the Pasig River. Before the Spanish era the area was part of the Kingdom of Namayan. In the 1950s the Ayala family began developing their hacienda into a planned financial and commercial district — the nucleus of modern Makati, centred on Ayala Avenue and the Ayala Triangle. Makati became a city in 1995.

Economy and landmarks

Makati hosts the headquarters of many of the Philippines' largest companies and most of its foreign embassies and consulates are nearby. Landmarks include the Ayala Museum, Greenbelt and Glorietta shopping complexes, the PBCom Tower (among the country's tallest buildings), and the historic Nuestra Señora de Gracia Church in Guadalupe. The city is governed from Makati City Hall and is divided into barangays, including Bel-Air, San Lorenzo, Urdaneta, Forbes Park, and Dasmariñas "villages" — exclusive residential enclaves.
