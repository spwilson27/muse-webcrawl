Penetration pricing

Penetration pricing is a marketing strategy in which a firm sets a deliberately low initial price for a new product or service in order to enter a market quickly, attract a large customer base, and build market share, expecting to raise prices later or to profit from volume, complementary sales, and customer lock-in. It is the opposite of price skimming, in which a high initial price is charged and lowered over time.

Rationale

The strategy is attractive when demand is price-elastic, when production costs fall rapidly with volume (economies of scale or experience-curve effects), when network effects or switching costs reward early adoption, and when a low price can deter potential competitors from entering. By sacrificing short-term margin, the firm hopes to establish brand loyalty, distribution, and an installed base before rivals can respond.

Risks

Penetration pricing can fail if customers anchor on the low introductory price and resist later increases, if competitors match the price and trigger a price war, or if low prices signal low quality. It also requires sufficient financial resources to sustain losses during the penetration phase, and in some jurisdictions pricing below cost to eliminate competitors may raise predatory pricing concerns under competition law.

Examples

Commonly cited examples include subscription services offering low introductory or free tiers, streaming platforms pricing aggressively to build subscriber bases, and new consumer brands undercutting incumbents in supermarkets. Microsoft's pricing of early Xbox consoles and the low launch prices of many software-as-a-service products are frequently analysed as penetration strategies.
