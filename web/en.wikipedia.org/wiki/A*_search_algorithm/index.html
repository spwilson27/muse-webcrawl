A* (pronounced "A-star") is a graph traversal and pathfinding algorithm which is used in many fields of computer science due to its completeness, optimality and efficiency. It was first published in 1968 by Peter Hart, Nils Nilsson and Bertram Raphael of Stanford Research Institute, who developed it for the Shakey the Robot project.

Description

A* extends Dijkstra's algorithm by steering the search toward the goal with a heuristic estimate. For each node n it deploys f(n) = g(n) + h(n), where g(n) is the exact cost from the start to n, and h(n) is a heuristic estimate of the cost from n to the goal. Nodes are expanded from a priority queue in order of increasing f. If h is admissible (never overestimates the true remaining cost), A* is guaranteed to find a least-cost path; if h is also consistent, no node need be expanded more than once.

Applications

A* is the standard pathfinding method in video games, robotics and route planning, and is widely taught in artificial-intelligence courses. Variants include IDA* (memory-bounded), D* and LPA* (dynamic replanning), and weighted A* for faster, near-optimal results. Its practical performance depends heavily on the quality of the heuristic: a perfect heuristic makes A* expand only nodes on the solution path, while h = 0 degenerates to Dijkstra's algorithm.
