Dwarfism

Dwarfism may be disproportionate, as in achondroplasia, or proportionate, as in growth-hormone deficiency.

Dwarfism is a condition of unusually short stature, generally defined as an adult height of 147 centimetres (4 ft 10 in) or less, resulting from a medical or genetic condition that slows growth. More than 300 conditions can cause dwarfism; most are genetic disorders of skeletal development.

Types and causes

Disproportionate dwarfism involves some body parts being shorter relative to others, usually because of abnormal bone growth. Its most common cause is achondroplasia, a mutation in the FGFR3 gene that impairs cartilage formation at the growth plates; most cases arise as new mutations. Other causes include spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia and diastrophic dysplasia. Proportionate dwarfism results when the whole body is small in proportion, typically from deficiency of growth hormone or other hormonal and metabolic problems such as congenital hypothyroidism; growth-hormone treatment can be effective when deficiency is the cause.

Terminology and society

Many people with dwarfism prefer the terms "short stature", "little person" or simply identification by name; the word "midget", once applied to proportionate cases, is now widely regarded as offensive. Advocacy organisations such as Little People of America campaign on accessibility, employment discrimination and medical issues, and against the use of people with dwarfism as comic spectacle in entertainment.
