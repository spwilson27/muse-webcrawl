Prima pratica

Prima pratica (Italian for "first practice") is a term used in music history for the older style of composition at the turn of the 17th century: the vocal polyphony of the Renaissance, in which the rules of counterpoint and the controlled treatment of dissonance govern, and the text is one element among several in the musical fabric. It is contrasted with the seconda pratica, in which the expression of the text was declared master of the harmony.

The Artusi–Monteverdi controversy

The terms arose from the famous controversy between the theorist Giovanni Maria Artusi and Claudio Monteverdi. In L'Artusi, overo Delle imperfettioni della moderna musica (1600, 1603), Artusi attacked passages in Monteverdi's madrigals for breaking the contrapuntal rules codified by Gioseffo Zarlino. In the preface to his Fifth Book of Madrigals (1605), Monteverdi answered that there existed a seconda pratica distinct from the first, in which departures from strict counterpoint were justified by the demands of the words — "the text is the mistress of the harmony." His brother Giulio Cesare elaborated the distinction in 1607.

Usage

The prima pratica is associated with composers such as Palestrina, and was later called the stile antico ("old style"), cultivated for church music for centuries after 1600. The debate marks a central episode in the transition from Renaissance to Baroque music.
