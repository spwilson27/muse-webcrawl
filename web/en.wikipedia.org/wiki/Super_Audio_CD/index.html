The Super Audio CD (SACD) is a high-resolution audio disc format introduced by Sony and Philips in 1999. Instead of the pulse-code modulation (PCM) of the ordinary compact disc, it uses Direct Stream Digital (DSD), a one-bit pulse-density modulation system sampled at 2.8224 MHz — 64 times the CD sampling rate — offering a theoretical dynamic range of about 120 dB and frequency response extending to 100 kHz.

Format

An SACD can hold stereo and up to six channels of surround sound, plus optional text and graphics. Most commercial releases are hybrid discs: a standard CD layer readable by any CD player bonded to the high-density SACD layer, a design intended to ease adoption. Pure SACD discs play only in SACD players. The format includes copy-protection measures, including a modulated watermark and restrictions on digital output, which long prevented computer drives from reading the SACD layer (though later workarounds via certain PlayStation 3 models became popular with collectors).

History and market

SACD competed directly with DVD-Audio in a high-resolution "format war" during the early 2000s. Neither displaced the CD, and both were overtaken commercially by downloads and streaming. SACD found a durable niche in classical music and jazz reissues, audiophile labels, and the Japanese market; thousands of titles were released, including landmark reissues of albums by Pink Floyd, the Rolling Stones, and Miles Davis. Sony's support for DSD later continued through downloadable DSD files, and new SACD releases continue on specialist labels, particularly in Japan and Hong Kong.

DSD

Direct Stream Digital stores audio as a stream of single bits whose density follows the waveform's amplitude. Proponents credit it with a smooth, analogue-like sound; critics note that its advantages over well-implemented high-rate PCM are difficult to demonstrate in controlled listening tests, and that heavy noise shaping pushes quantisation noise into the ultrasonic band. The debate mirrors wider audiophile controversies over high-resolution audio.
