Eilenberg–Moore category

In category theory, the Eilenberg–Moore category of a monad is the category of algebras for the monad. Named after Samuel Eilenberg and John Coleman Moore, who developed the theory in the 1960s, it provides the universal or "maximal" way of turning a monad into an adjunction: every monad arises from an adjunction, and the Eilenberg–Moore construction gives the terminal such adjunction, while the Kleisli category gives the initial one.

Definition

Let T = (T, η, μ) be a monad on a category C, with unit η and multiplication μ. A T-algebra is a pair (A, α) consisting of an object A of C and a morphism α: TA → A, called the structure map, satisfying two axioms: α ∘ ηA = idA (unit law) and α ∘ μA = α ∘ Tα (multiplication law). A morphism of algebras f: (A, α) → (B, β) is a morphism f: A → B such that β ∘ Tf = f ∘ α. The category of T-algebras and their morphisms is the Eilenberg–Moore category CT.

The canonical adjunction

The forgetful functor UT: CT → C has a left adjoint FT sending an object X to the free algebra (TX, μX). The monad induced by this adjunction is the original monad T. Eilenberg and Moore showed that any adjunction inducing T factors uniquely through this one, making the Eilenberg–Moore category the largest category of "algebras" associated with the monad. Beck's monadicity theorem gives conditions under which a functor is equivalent to such a forgetful functor.

Examples

For the free-group monad on sets, the Eilenberg–Moore category is the category of groups. Similarly, familiar algebraic categories such as rings, modules over a ring, and compact Hausdorff spaces (from the ultrafilter monad) arise as Eilenberg–Moore categories of monads on more basic categories.
