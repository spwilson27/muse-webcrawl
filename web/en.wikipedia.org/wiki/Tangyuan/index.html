Tangyuan

Tangyuan (traditional Chinese: 湯圓; simplified Chinese: 汤圆; pinyin: tāngyuán, "soup ball") is a Chinese dessert made of glutinous rice flour mixed with a small amount of water to form balls, which are then cooked and served in boiling water, sometimes with a sweet soup. Fillings commonly include black sesame paste, peanut, red bean paste, or meat in savoury regional variants. The dish is traditionally eaten during the Lantern Festival and at the winter solstice (Dongzhi), as well as at weddings and family gatherings.

History and symbolism

Tangyuan dates back at least to the Song dynasty. In southern China the filled, rolled balls are called tangyuan, while in the north similar festival balls traditionally made by coating filling in dry rice flour are called yuanxiao. The round shape and the sound of the name (echoing tuanyuan, "reunion") symbolize wholeness and family togetherness, making the dish a staple of the Lantern Festival, which closes the Chinese New Year celebrations.

Preparation

Glutinous rice flour is kneaded with warm water into a dough, divided, wrapped around filling, and rolled into smooth balls. They are boiled until they float and served in the cooking water, a sweetened broth (often ginger), or occasionally deep-fried. Modern fillings range from chocolate and matcha to salted egg yolk, and frozen packaged tangyuan are sold year-round across East and Southeast Asia.
