Harmonic minor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In music theory, the harmonic minor scale is a type of minor scale in which the seventh degree is raised by a semitone compared with the natural minor scale. In A harmonic minor, the notes are A, B, C, D, E, F and G♯. The raised seventh creates a leading tone that pulls strongly back to the tonic, allowing a major (or dominant seventh) chord to be built on the fifth degree — the dominant — which is essential to tonal harmony in minor keys.

Characteristics

The interval between the flattened sixth and the raised seventh is an augmented second, which gives the scale a distinctive colour sometimes described as "Middle Eastern." This awkward melodic interval is one reason composers also use the melodic minor scale, which raises the sixth as well when ascending. Chords derived from the harmonic minor include the minor-major seventh chord (imaj7) and the fully diminished seventh chord on the seventh degree (vii°7).

Use

The harmonic minor is ubiquitous in Baroque and Classical harmony, flamenco, and heavy metal — particularly neoclassical metal — and its fifth mode underlies the Phrygian dominant scale used in many musical traditions. Compare the related harmonic major scale.
