Chagai-II

Chagai-II is the codename of a single underground nuclear weapons test conducted by Pakistan on 30 May 1998 in the Kharan Desert of Balochistan province. It followed the five tests of Chagai-I, conducted two days earlier on 28 May 1998 at Ras Koh. Together, the six announced tests were Pakistan's public answer to India's Pokhran-II tests of 11 and 13 May 1998, and they established Pakistan as a declared nuclear-weapon state.

Background

Pakistan's weapons programme, led by the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission (PAEC) and the Khan Research Laboratories, accelerated after India's 1974 "Smiling Buddha" test. When India conducted five further tests in May 1998, the government of Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif came under intense domestic pressure to respond despite the threat of international sanctions.

The test

Unlike Chagai-I, which involved five devices detonated simultaneously in a horizontal tunnel at Ras Koh, Chagai-II consisted of a single device detonated in a vertical shaft roughly 1,000 feet deep at a prepared site in the Kharan Desert, about 115 km southwest of Ras Koh. The PAEC announced a yield of about 15–18 kilotons and described the device as a boosted fission weapon using solid fuel; Western seismic estimates placed the yield somewhat lower. The test was carried out at 13:10 PKT on 30 May 1998.

Aftermath

Following the tests, Pakistan declared a unilateral moratorium on further nuclear testing. The United States and other states imposed economic sanctions on both India and Pakistan, most of which were lifted after 2001. The anniversary of the Chagai tests, 28 May, is observed in Pakistan as Youm-e-Takbir ("the day of greatness").
