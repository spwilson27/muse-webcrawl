The German Army (German: Heer, lit. 'army') is the land component of the Bundeswehr, the armed forces of the Federal Republic of Germany. Founded in 1955 as part of West Germany's post-war rearmament within NATO, it united with the East German National People's Army's land forces after reunification in 1990.

History

The Heer was established on 12 November 1955, the 200th birthday of Prussian reformer Gerhard von Scharnhorst. During the Cold War it formed the backbone of NATO's defence of the North German Plain, fielding twelve divisions by the 1980s. After 1990 it shrank drastically and reoriented toward crisis-management deployments, including IFOR/SFOR in the Balkans, KFOR in Kosovo and ISAF in Afghanistan. Conscription was suspended in 2011.

Structure

The Army is commanded by the Inspector of the Army through Army Command (Kommando Heer) in Strausberg. Its major formations include armoured and mechanised divisions, the Rapid Forces Division (airborne and special forces, including the KSK), mountain and Franco-German units, and the multinational 1 (German/Netherlands) Corps.

Equipment

Main equipment includes the Leopard 2 main battle tank, Puma and Marder infantry fighting vehicles, Boxer armoured transport vehicles, Panzerhaubitze 2000 self-propelled artillery, and NH90 and Tiger helicopters.
