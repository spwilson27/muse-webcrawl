Ottoman cuisine

Ottoman cuisine is the cuisine of the Ottoman Empire and its successor states, forming the basis of modern Turkish cuisine and strongly influencing the cuisines of the Balkans, the Levant, the Caucasus and North Africa. It developed in the palace kitchens of Istanbul — notably those of Topkapı Palace, where hundreds of specialised cooks worked in separate confectioneries, soup kitchens and kebab houses — and combined Central Asian, Anatolian, Persian, Arab, Greek and Balkan elements.

Characteristic dishes include kebabs, dolma (stuffed vegetables and vine leaves), pilafs, börek pastries, köfte, soups such as lentil and tripe soup, and rich syrup-based desserts including baklava, kadayıf, sütlaç and lokum (Turkish delight). Yoghurt, aubergine, lamb and dried fruits and nuts feature prominently, and coffee, introduced through Yemen, became central to Ottoman social life in the world's first coffeehouses.

The empire's guild-organised palace kitchen system produced a refined, codified culinary tradition that spread with Ottoman rule; regional cuisines across the former empire still share the great majority of these dishes, often under similar names.
