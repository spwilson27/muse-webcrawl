Herstmonceux Castle

LocationHerstmonceux, East Sussex, England
Built1441 – c.1449
BuilderSir Roger Fiennes
StyleRed-brick fortified manor

Herstmonceux Castle is a brick-built Tudor castle near Herstmonceux, East Sussex, England, one of the oldest significant brick buildings still standing in England.

History

Built from 1441 by Sir Roger Fiennes, Treasurer of the Household to Henry VI, the moated castle passed to the Dacre family and later owners; its interior was dismantled in the 1770s, leaving a romantic shell. Admiral Robert Beatty and others restored it in the early 20th century, notably under Colonel Claude Lowther and Sir Paul Latham.

Royal Greenwich Observatory

From 1957 to 1988 the estate housed the Royal Greenwich Observatory; its telescopes, including the Isaac Newton Telescope before its move to La Palma, stood in the grounds, now the Observatory Science Centre.

Today

Owned by Queen's University at Kingston, Canada, as the Bader International Study Centre, the castle and gardens are open to visitors seasonally.
