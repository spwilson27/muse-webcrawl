Borel–Cantelli lemma

The Borel–Cantelli lemma is a theorem in probability theory about sequences of events, named after Émile Borel and Francesco Paolo Cantelli. Informally, it says that if the probabilities of a sequence of events are summable, then almost surely only finitely many of them occur.

Statement

Let (En) be a sequence of events in a probability space. If Σ P(En) < ∞, then the probability that infinitely many of the En occur — the limit superior of the sequence — is 0.

The second Borel–Cantelli lemma gives a partial converse: if the events are independent and Σ P(En) = ∞, then the probability that infinitely many of them occur is 1. Together the lemmas are a fundamental tool for proving almost-sure results, including the strong law of large numbers, and they underlie the classic "infinite monkey" illustration: an event of fixed positive probability, retried independently forever, occurs infinitely often with probability one.
