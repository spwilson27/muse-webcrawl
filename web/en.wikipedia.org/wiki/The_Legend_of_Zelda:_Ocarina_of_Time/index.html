The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is a 1998 action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo 64. It is the fifth entry in The Legend of Zelda series and the first with 3D graphics. The player controls Link, who journeys through the kingdom of Hyrule to stop the evil king Ganondorf from obtaining the Triforce, travelling back and forth seven years in time with the aid of the magical Ocarina of Time.

Gameplay

Ocarina of Time introduced features that became standard in 3D action games, including a context-sensitive action button and Z-targeting, a lock-on combat system. Link explores Hyrule Field on foot or on his horse Epona, clearing dungeons first as a child and then as an adult, learning songs on the ocarina that warp him across the world, change the time of day, or summon rain.

Development

Development began with the hardware of the Super Nintendo before shifting to the Nintendo 64, where director and producer Shigeru Miyamoto's team experimented with first-person and third-person prototypes, settling on a view influenced by chanbara sword-fighting films. Koji Kondo composed the score, centred on the twelve learnable ocarina melodies.

Reception and legacy

The game received near-universal acclaim and holds the highest aggregate score on Metacritic of any video game. It sold over seven million copies on the Nintendo 64, won numerous Game of the Year awards, and is widely cited as one of the greatest video games ever made. A remake, Ocarina of Time 3D, was released for the Nintendo 3DS in 2011.
