University of Toronto

The University of Toronto (U of T) is a public research university in Toronto, Ontario, Canada, located on the grounds surrounding Queen's Park. Founded in 1827 as King's College, it is Canada's largest university by enrolment and research funding and is consistently ranked among the world's top universities. It comprises three campuses — St. George (downtown), Mississauga, and Scarborough — and a collegiate system modelled partly on Oxford and Cambridge.

History

King's College received its royal charter in 1827 under Anglican bishop John Strachan and was secularized in 1850 as the University of Toronto. The university grew by federating with denominational colleges — including Trinity College and Victoria University — while retaining its colleges as constituent parts. University College, the founding college, opened its Romanesque Revival building in 1859.

Research achievements

Toronto researchers made several landmark discoveries: Frederick Banting and Charles Best isolated insulin here in 1921–22, one of the most important medical advances of the twentieth century; stem cells were identified by Ernest McCulloch and James Till in the 1960s; and the university was a cradle of modern deep learning, where Geoffrey Hinton's group pioneered neural network research that underpins contemporary artificial intelligence. U of T affiliates include eleven Nobel laureates, and its affiliated teaching hospitals form one of North America's largest academic health science networks.

Colleges and programs

Arts and science students on the St. George campus belong to one of seven colleges: Innis, New, St. Michael's, Trinity, University, Victoria, and Woodsworth. Professional faculties include Medicine (Temerty), Law, Engineering (Applied Science and Engineering), Rotman Commerce and management, Dentistry, and Pharmacy. The university's library system is among the largest in North America.
