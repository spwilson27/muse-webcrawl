The Church in Wales (Welsh: Yr Eglwys yng Nghymru) is the Anglican church in Wales, composed of six dioceses. It is a province of the Anglican Communion and, since disestablishment in 1920, independent of the Church of England, though closely related to it.

History

Christianity in Wales dates to Roman Britain and the Age of Saints (David, Teilo, Dyfrig). The Welsh dioceses were absorbed into the province of Canterbury at the English Reformation and remained part of the Church of England for nearly four centuries. Following decades of campaigning — Nonconformist chapel culture had made Anglicanism a minority faith in Wales — the Welsh Church Act 1914 provided for disestablishment and partial disendowment, taking effect on 31 March 1920 after wartime suspension. The Church in Wales then elected its own bishops and convened its own Governing Body; the first Archbishop of Wales was A. G. Edwards of St Asaph.

Structure and worship

The church is episcopal and synodical: the Governing Body includes clergy and laity. Women were ordained priests from 1997 and consecrated bishops following 2013 legislation (first, Joanna Penberthy, Bishop of St Davids, in 2017). Services are in Welsh and English, using the Book of Common Prayer (1984 Welsh/English editions). Membership has declined with wider secularisation — compare Canterbury, primatial seat of the Church of England.
