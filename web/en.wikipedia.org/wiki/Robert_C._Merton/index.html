Robert Cox Merton (born July 31, 1944) is an American economist, Nobel Memorial Prize laureate in Economic Sciences, and a professor at the MIT Sloan School of Management. He is best known for his contributions to continuous-time finance, including the extension and generalization of option pricing theory in the Black–Scholes–Merton model.

Early life and education

Merton was born in New York City, the son of sociologist Robert K. Merton. He earned a B.S. in engineering mathematics from Columbia University in 1966, an M.S. in applied mathematics from Caltech in 1967, and a PhD in economics from MIT in 1970 under Paul Samuelson.

Career and research

Merton taught at MIT Sloan until 1988, then at Harvard Business School until 2010. His 1973 papers on rational option pricing generalized the work of Fischer Black and Myron Scholes, deriving the pricing formula via dynamic hedging and continuous-time stochastic calculus. He also developed the intertemporal capital asset pricing model (ICAPM) and the Merton model for corporate debt and credit risk.

Nobel Prize and LTCM

In 1997 Merton shared the Nobel Memorial Prize with Scholes; Black, who had died in 1995, was acknowledged in the award announcement. Merton was a founding partner of Long-Term Capital Management, the hedge fund whose spectacular 1998 collapse he later analyzed in case studies and writings on financial innovation and risk.
