Direct deposit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Direct deposit is the electronic transfer of a payment directly into the bank account of the recipient, in place of payment by paper cheque or cash. The term is used most commonly for the payment of wages and salaries by employers, and for government benefits, pensions, and tax refunds. The recipient supplies the payer with their bank's routing number and account number; the payer then sends payments through an electronic network such as the Automated Clearing House (ACH) in the United States.

Use

Direct deposit is the dominant method of payroll payment in many countries. In the United States, federal law (the Electronic Fund Transfer Act and Treasury regulations) requires most federal payments — including Social Security benefits — to be made electronically, and most large employers pay staff by direct deposit, often allowing employees to split pay among several accounts. In the United Kingdom, salaries are usually paid via Bacs Direct Credit, a closely related system.

Advantages

Payments arrive on a predictable date without a trip to a bank, cannot be lost or stolen like paper cheques, and are usually available sooner because there is no cheque-clearing hold. For payers, direct deposit is cheaper per transaction than printing and mailing cheques, and simplifies reconciliation. It also reduces the environmental cost of paper payment instruments.

Requirements and safety

Because bank details must be shared with the payer, direct deposit enrolment is done through secure payroll or government portals. Unlike direct debit, direct deposit gives the payer no ability to withdraw funds from the recipient's account; the recipient's bank details alone do not authorise debits in most banking systems.
