Casimir III the Great

Casimir III the Great (Polish: Kazimierz III Wielki; 30 April 1310 – 5 November 1370) reigned as the King of Poland from 1333 to 1370. He was the last king of the Piast dynasty and is regarded as one of Poland's greatest monarchs.

Reign

A saying held that Casimir "found Poland built of wood and left it built of stone": he founded numerous towns and castles, reformed the legal system (the Statutes of Casimir), founded the University of Kraków in 1364 — one of Europe's oldest universities — and roughly doubled the kingdom's territory, extending it into Galicia–Volhynia (Red Ruthenia).

Diplomacy and policy

He pursued diplomacy over war with the Teutonic Order and Bohemia, confirming arrangements at Kalisz (1343) and Namysłów (1348). He codified protections for Jews in Poland, extending earlier privileges, which encouraged Jewish settlement.

Death and succession

Casimir died from injuries sustained in a hunting accident in 1370. Having no legitimate son, he was succeeded by his nephew Louis I of Hungary, ending the Piast royal line.
