Baozi

Freshly steamed baozi in a bamboo steamer.

Baozi (Chinese: 包子), also known simply as bao, is a type of yeast-leavened filled bun in Chinese cuisine. A baozi consists of a soft, white wheat-flour dough wrapped around a savoury or sweet filling and steamed. Fillings commonly include seasoned minced pork, pork with chive or cabbage, beef, lamb, vegetables, preserved greens, or sweet pastes such as red bean, lotus seed, or custard.

History

Steamed filled buns are traditionally said to have been invented by the strategist Zhuge Liang in the third century CE, though filled steamed breads were already recorded under the name mantou in earlier centuries; over time mantou came to denote the plain bun and baozi the filled one. The word baozi itself gained currency in the Song dynasty (tenth–thirteenth centuries), when shops in the capital Kaifeng sold named varieties of filled buns.

Varieties

Well-known regional types include the large fluffy dabao of the north, Tianjin's Gou Bu Li baozi, the soup-filled tangbao of Jiangsu, and small steamed buns known in some regions as xiaobao. Pan-fried variants such as shengjian bao and water-fried shuijianbao are cooked in a skillet so the bottom crisps while the top steams. Cousins across Asia include Japanese nikuman, Korean jjinppang-mandu/hoppang, Mongolian buuz, Tibetan momo-adjacent steamed buns, and the baked or steamed buns of Central Asia. The flat, folded Taiwanese gua bao is an unsealed relative of the baozi.
