An aquarium (plural: aquariums or aquaria) is a vivarium of any size having at least one transparent side in which aquatic plants or animals are kept and displayed. Fishkeepers use aquaria to keep fish, invertebrates, amphibians, aquatic reptiles such as turtles, and aquatic plants. Large public aquariums exhibit great numbers of specimens for the public, and often play roles in conservation, research, and education.

History

The keeping of fish in confined water is ancient: Sumerians, Egyptians, and Romans kept fish in ponds and in some cases in containers, and selective breeding of carp and goldfish was practised in China and Japan for centuries. The modern glass aquarium emerged in the 19th century from advances in understanding oxygen balance between animals and plants. Robert Warington's experiments around 1850 showed that plants could oxygenate water for fish, and Jeanne Villepreux-Power had earlier built glass "cages" to observe marine life in Sicily. Naturalist Philip Henry Gosse popularized the pastime in Britain and coined the word "aquarium" in 1854; the first public aquarium opened at London Zoo in 1853, the so-called "Fish House".

Home aquariums

Home aquaria range from small bowls (widely discouraged for welfare reasons) to large custom installations. A functioning system must manage the nitrogen cycle: ammonia excreted by fish is converted by nitrifying bacteria to nitrite and then to less toxic nitrate, a process supported by filtration — mechanical, biological, and chemical. Other equipment includes heaters, lighting, aeration, and, for marine "reef" aquariums, protein skimmers, calcium reactors, and powerful lights to support corals. Aquascaping treats the layout of plants, rock, and wood as an art form.

Public aquariums

Public aquariums are often affiliated with zoos or marine institutes. Notable examples include the Georgia Aquarium in Atlanta, Monterey Bay Aquarium in California, the Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium in Japan, and the Dubai Aquarium. Modern exhibits use thick acrylic tunnels and viewing windows to immerse visitors, and many institutions run breeding, rescue, and rehabilitation programs. The world's largest aquarium by water volume has changed over time; Georgia Aquarium's roughly 38,000 cubic metres of water made it the largest in the world when it opened in 2005, and larger facilities have since been built in China.

Welfare and conservation

Aquariums are debated in animal-welfare terms: critics question the keeping of large, wide-ranging species, while institutions point to research, conservation breeding of threatened species, and public education. Captive breeding in public aquariums has helped sustain some species of seahorses, corals, and freshwater fishes that are threatened in the wild.
