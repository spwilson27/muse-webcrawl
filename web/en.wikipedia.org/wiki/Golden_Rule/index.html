Golden Rule

The Golden Rule is the principle of treating others as one would want to be treated by them. It is sometimes called the ethic of reciprocity. It can appear in either a positive form — "treat others as you would like others to treat you" — or a negative form, sometimes called the Silver Rule: "do not treat others in ways you would not like to be treated".

Versions of the principle appear in the teachings of almost every major religion and ethical tradition, and it is often regarded as a foundational expression of empathy and of the idea that one's own interests and the interests of others are morally comparable.

History

Early formulations appear in ancient Egyptian wisdom literature and in Babylonian sources. In ancient Greece, versions are attributed to figures such as Pittacus and Thales, and the principle was developed by later philosophers. Confucius gave a classic negative statement in the Analects: "Do not impose on others what you do not wish for yourself" — his one-word summary of a lifetime's teaching being shu, reciprocity.

In religions

In Judaism, Hillel the Elder summarised the Torah with the negative form: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow." In Christianity, Jesus gave the positive form in the Sermon on the Mount: "So in everything, do to others what you would have them do to you, for this sums up the Law and the Prophets" (Matthew 7:12). Parallels occur in Islam, Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism, Taoism and Zoroastrianism, among others.

Philosophical discussion

Philosophers have both praised and criticised the rule. Immanuel Kant argued that it is a derivative and limited principle, unable by itself to ground duties to oneself or strict duties of justice, and developed the categorical imperative partly in response. Others, such as the ethicist Harry Gensler, have formalised the rule and defended it as a consistent and demanding standard of impartiality. Critics note that people differ in what they want, so a refined formulation — treating others as they would wish to be treated — is sometimes proposed (the so-called "platinum rule").
