Badwater Ultramarathon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A runner on the road through Death Valley in extreme summer heat]

The Badwater Ultramarathon is a 135-mile (217 km) ultramarathon in California, billed as "the world's toughest foot race". It starts at Badwater Basin in Death Valley, the lowest point in North America at 85 m below sea level, and finishes at Whitney Portal, the trailhead to Mount Whitney, at about 2,530 m elevation.

Course

Runners cross three mountain ranges and accumulate about 4,450 m of ascent. Daytime temperatures in Death Valley in July can approach 55 °C (130 °F); runners wear white clothing, run on the painted road lines to protect their shoes, and are supported continuously by crews with ice and water.

History

Al Arnold attempted the course in 1974 and succeeded on his third try in 1977; Jay Birmingham ran from Badwater to Whitney and back in 1981. The organised race dates from 1987. Notable champions include Scott Jurek, who won twice, and Pam Reed, twice overall winner. Course records of under 22 hours have been set in the men's division in recent decades.
