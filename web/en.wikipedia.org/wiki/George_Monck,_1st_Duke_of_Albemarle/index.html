George Monck, 1st Duke of Albemarle

George Monck, 1st Duke of Albemarle KG JP PC (6 December 1608 – 3 January 1670) was an English soldier and politician, a key figure in the Restoration of 1660.

Born: Potheridge, Devon
Died: The Cockpit, Whitehall, London
Allegiance: Royalist, then Parliament/Commonwealth, then Crown
Commands: Commander-in-Chief, Scotland; General at Sea

George Monck, 1st Duke of Albemarle was an English soldier and statesman whose actions in 1659–60 brought about the Restoration of the monarchy under Charles II. A professional soldier of great caution and few words, he served the Royalists, then the Commonwealth, before turning kingmaker.

Early life and the Civil Wars

Monck was born at Potheridge in Devon, the second son of Sir Thomas Monck. After assaulting an official who had wronged his father, he fled abroad and served as a volunteer in Spanish and Dutch service, learning his trade in the Low Countries. Returning to England, he fought for Charles I in the First English Civil War, serving in Ireland and then England, until his capture at the Battle of Nantwich in January 1644. He spent two years imprisoned in the Tower of London, where he wrote a military treatise, Observations upon Military and Political Affairs.

Service under Cromwell

Released to serve Parliament in Ireland, Monck later joined Cromwell's invasion of Scotland, fought at Dunbar (1650), and completed the subjugation of the Highlands, becoming commander-in-chief in Scotland in 1654. As a General at Sea he fought the Dutch in the First Anglo-Dutch War, distinguishing himself at the battles of Portland and the Gabbard (1653). He remained outwardly loyal to the Protectorate while keeping his own counsel.

The Restoration

Following Oliver Cromwell's death in 1658 and the political chaos of 1659, Monck preserved his army's discipline in Scotland, marched south across the Tweed at Coldstream on 1 January 1660, and entered London in February. He forced the readmission of the secluded MPs, demanded a newly elected parliament, and opened secret negotiations with Charles II. The Convention Parliament accepted Charles's Declaration of Breda and proclaimed him king in May 1660.

Later career and death

Monck was created Duke of Albemarle, Earl of Torrington, and a Knight of the Garter, and received an enormous pension and the palace of New Hall. In the Second Anglo-Dutch War he shared naval command with Prince Rupert, fighting the Four Days' Battle and the St James's Day Battle in 1666. During the Great Plague of 1665 he stayed in London maintaining order, and during the Great Fire he helped organise firefighting. He died on 3 January 1670 and was given a state funeral in Westminster Abbey.
