USS Stark (FFG-31) was an Oliver Hazard Perry-class guided-missile frigate of the United States Navy, named for Admiral Harold Rainsford Stark, Chief of Naval Operations at the start of the Second World War. She is best known for surviving an Iraqi missile attack in the Persian Gulf in 1987.

The Stark incident

On 17 May 1987, during the Iran–Iraq War's "Tanker War", Stark was patrolling in the Persian Gulf when an Iraqi aircraft — later identified as a modified Dassault Falcon 50 business jet — fired two Exocet AM39 anti-ship missiles at her. The ship's sensors detected the aircraft but the missiles were not engaged; both struck the frigate's port side, though only one warhead detonated. The attack killed 37 sailors and wounded 21. Heroic damage control saved the ship, which was escorted to Bahrain and later returned to the United States for extensive repairs. Iraq apologized, claiming mistaken identity, and the incident led to investigations of the ship's readiness and command decisions, as well as eventual Iraqi compensation payments.

Later service

After repairs, Stark returned to service and made several further deployments before decommissioning in 1999. She was scrapped in 2006. A memorial at Naval Station Mayport, Florida, commemorates the 37 crewmen who died.
