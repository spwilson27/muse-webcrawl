Shi Le (Chinese: 石勒; 274 – 17 August 333), courtesy name Shilong (世龍), formally Emperor Ming of (Later) Zhao, was the founding emperor of the Jie-led Chinese Later Zhao dynasty, one of the Sixteen Kingdoms of the fourth century. Rising from slavery, he became a bandit leader and warlord, destroyed the rival Han-Zhao state, and by 329 ruled most of northern China. He is notable as one of very few emperors in Chinese history who began life as a slave, and for establishing the Later Zhao state that his nephew Shi Hu would inherit.

Shi Le founded Later Zhao and unified most of the north by 329.

Early life

Shi Le was a Jie tribal leader's son from Wuxiang in Shangdang (modern Shanxi). During the chaos of the War of the Eight Princes he was kidnapped and sold into slavery in Shandong; freed in a slave revolt, he became a mounted bandit, then joined the rebel leader Gongshi Fan and later the Xiongnu prince Liu Yuan, founder of Han-Zhao, who gave him high commands. An oft-retold anecdote has the literatus Wang Yan, captured by Shi Le, failing to talk his way out of execution.

Rise and foundation of Later Zhao

Shi Le built an independent power base around Xiangguo (in modern Hebei), gathering Chinese advisers, notably the strategist Zhang Bin, whose counsel he followed throughout his career. In 319 he declared himself Prince of Zhao and broke with Han-Zhao. After a decade of campaigning, his general Shi Kan captured the Han-Zhao emperor Liu Yao in 328, and in 329 Later Zhao annexed the remnant state. In 330 Shi Le took the imperial title. He organised a dual administration for Jie and Han Chinese populations, founded schools, standardised examinations for local officials in some accounts, and settled his capital at Xiangguo.

Death and succession

Shi Le died in 333 and was succeeded by his son Shi Hong, who within a year was deposed and killed by Shi Hu, ushering in a far more brutal phase of the dynasty.
