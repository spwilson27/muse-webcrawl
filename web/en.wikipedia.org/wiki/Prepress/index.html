Prepress

Prepress is the term used in the printing and publishing industries for the processes and procedures that occur between the creation of a print layout and the final printing. The prepress procedure includes the manufacture of printing plates, image carriers, or other materials used to transfer an image to a printed substrate, along with file preparation, proofing, and imposition.

Stages

Modern digital prepress typically involves receiving electronic files (often PDF), preflighting them for missing fonts, images, and colour problems, colour management and conversion to CMYK, trapping, imposition of pages into press-sheet layouts, and output of proofs and plates, commonly through computer-to-plate (CTP) systems.

History

Before digitisation, prepress work was performed by camera operators, strippers, and platemakers using photographic film and manual assembly. Desktop publishing in the 1980s and the PDF workflow in the 1990s transformed the field, merging design and production tasks.
