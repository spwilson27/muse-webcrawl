Kings River (California)

The Kings River drains the southern Sierra Nevada into the San Joaquin Valley.

The Kings River is a 125-mile (201 km) river draining the southwestern slope of the Sierra Nevada in central California. It rises in Kings Canyon National Park, where its Middle and South Forks carve one of the deepest canyons in North America, and flows southwest past Pine Flat Dam into the San Joaquin Valley.

History and use

Spanish explorers named the river Río de los Santos Reyes ("River of the Holy Kings") in 1805, after the Epiphany. In the valley, its waters are almost entirely diverted for irrigation of some of the most productive farmland in the United States; historically the river fed Tulare Lake, once the largest freshwater lake west of the Mississippi, which reappears in exceptionally wet years. The North Fork and main stem are also popular whitewater rafting runs.
