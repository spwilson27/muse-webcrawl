Thrombosis

Thrombosis
SpecialtyHaematology, vascular medicine
TypesVenous thrombosis, arterial thrombosis
Risk factorsImmobility, surgery, cancer, pregnancy, thrombophilia
TreatmentAnticoagulants, thrombolysis, thrombectomy

Thrombosis is the formation of a blood clot, known as a thrombus, within a blood vessel, obstructing the flow of blood. When a thrombus breaks free and travels it becomes an embolus, causing an embolism.

Causes

Virchow's triad summarises the causes: endothelial injury, stasis or turbulence of blood flow, and hypercoagulability. Venous thromboembolism includes deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism; arterial thrombosis, usually on ruptured atherosclerotic plaque, causes myocardial infarction and stroke.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention includes mobilization, compression stockings, and prophylactic anticoagulants such as heparin, warfarin and direct oral anticoagulants. Acute treatment may involve anticoagulation, thrombolytic drugs, catheter-directed therapy or surgical thrombectomy; antiplatelet agents such as aspirin are central for arterial events.
