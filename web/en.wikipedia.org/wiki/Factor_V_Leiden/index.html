Factor V Leiden

Factor V Leiden is a variant of human factor V, a protein of the coagulation system, caused by a point mutation in the F5 gene. It makes factor V resistant to inactivation by activated protein C, producing a hypercoagulable state and increased risk of venous thromboembolism. It is the most common hereditary thrombophilia in people of European descent.

Factor V Leiden
Gene: F5 (G1691A, Arg506Gln)
Discovered: 1994, Leiden, Netherlands
Risk: Deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism

Mechanism and epidemiology

The mutation replaces arginine with glutamine at position 506, removing a cleavage site. Heterozygosity (present in ~5% of Europeans) raises venous thrombosis risk roughly 3–8 fold; homozygosity raises it far more. Risk compounds with oral contraceptives, pregnancy, surgery and immobility.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is by DNA test or activated protein C resistance assay. Most carriers never clot and are not routinely anticoagulated; treatment follows standard thrombosis care, with counselling on risk factors.
