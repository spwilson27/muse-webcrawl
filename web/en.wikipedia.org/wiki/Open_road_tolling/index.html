Open road tolling

Open road tolling (ORT), also called all-electronic tolling (AET), cashless tolling, or free-flow tolling, is the collection of tolls on toll roads without the use of toll booths. An electronic toll collection system is usually used instead. The major advantage to ORT is that users are able to drive through the toll collection area at highway speeds without having to stop or slow down to pay the toll.

Operation

In an ORT system, vehicles pass beneath overhead gantries equipped with electronic readers and cameras. Vehicles equipped with a transponder, such as E-ZPass in the United States, are charged electronically to the account associated with the transponder. For vehicles without a transponder, cameras record the license plate and a bill is mailed to the registered owner, often at a higher rate.

History and adoption

One of the earliest ORT systems was installed on the Dallas North Tollway in 1989. The Highway 407 ETR in Ontario, Canada, opened in 1997 as one of the first all-electronic, open-access toll highways. Many toll roads in the United States, Australia, Europe, and elsewhere have since converted from cash toll booths to fully electronic collection, including the Massachusetts Turnpike in 2016 and the Pennsylvania Turnpike in 2020.

Advantages and disadvantages

Open road tolling reduces traffic congestion at toll plazas, lowers operating costs, and decreases vehicle emissions from idling. Disadvantages include the cost of billing unregistered vehicles, toll evasion, and privacy concerns over plate-tracking systems.
