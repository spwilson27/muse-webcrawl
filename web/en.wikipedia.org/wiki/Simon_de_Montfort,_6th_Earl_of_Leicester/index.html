Simon de Montfort, 6th Earl of Leicester (c. 1208 – 4 August 1265), also known as Simon V de Montfort, was an English nobleman of French origin and a member of the English peerage. Having inherited the lordship and earldom of Leicester from his father, he led the baronial opposition to the rule of King Henry III of England, culminating in the Second Barons' War. Following his victory at the Battle of Lewes in 1264, de Montfort ruled England for some fifteen months; he is remembered in part for summoning the parliament of 1265, which included representatives of the boroughs alongside knights of the shires. He was killed by forces loyal to Prince Edward at the Battle of Evesham in 1265.

Early life

De Montfort was the younger son of Simon de Montfort, 5th Earl of Leicester, a French nobleman who led the Albigensian Crusade, and Alix de Montmorency. As a younger son he came to England in 1229 seeking to recover the family earldom of Leicester, which Henry III granted to him; in 1238 he secretly married the king's sister, Eleanor, widow of William Marshal the younger — a marriage that caused lasting controversy because Eleanor had taken a vow of chastity and because a royal sister's marriage was a matter of state.

Conflict with Henry III

Relations between de Montfort and the king soured over debts, grievances in Gascony — where de Montfort served as seneschal from 1248 to 1252 — and the wider baronial discontent with Henry's rule. In 1258 de Montfort was a leading figure in the movement that imposed the Provisions of Oxford on the king, establishing a council of barons to oversee government. When Henry repudiated the Provisions, civil war followed.

Rule and death

At the Battle of Lewes (14 May 1264) de Montfort defeated and captured Henry III and his son Edward, becoming the effective ruler of England. His parliament of January 1265 summoned, for each county, two knights, and for each borough, two burgesses — often cited as a formative step in the development of the House of Commons. Edward escaped captivity in May 1265, and on 4 August de Montfort was defeated and killed at the Battle of Evesham, his body mutilated by royalist forces. He was later venerated by many as a saint and martyr, though never canonised.

Legacy

Historians continue to debate whether de Montfort acted from principle, ambition, or both. A memorial marks the site of his death at Evesham, and a statue of de Montfort appears on the Haymarket Memorial Clock Tower in Leicester.
