K Foundation Burn a Million Quid
Date: 23 August 1994
Location: Ardfin boathouse, Isle of Jura, Scotland
Participants: Bill Drummond and Jimmy Cauty (The K Foundation)
Sum burned: £1,000,000 in £50 notes

On 23 August 1994 the K Foundation burned one million pounds in cash on the island of Jura.

K Foundation Burn a Million Quid was an action on 23 August 1994 in which Bill Drummond and Jimmy Cauty, formerly pop duo The KLF and operating as the art organization the K Foundation, burned £1 million in cash in a disused boathouse on the Ardfin Estate on the Scottish island of Jura. The money, mostly in £50 notes, represented the bulk of the fortune the pair had earned as The KLF, then among the best-selling singles acts in the world.

Background

The KLF had retired from the music business in 1992, deleting their catalogue. Re-emerging as the K Foundation, Drummond and Cauty staged a series of provocations around art and money, including the 1993 K Foundation art award, which gave £40,000 — twice the Turner Prize amount — to Rachel Whiteread as the "worst artist of the year" on the night she also won the Turner Prize. The pair then attempted to exhibit and dispose of a million pounds, at one point nailing it to a board as the artwork Money: A Major Body of Cash, before deciding to burn it.

The burning

The money was withdrawn in cash and transported to Jura. In the early hours of 23 August 1994, before a small audience including journalist Jim Reid and the island's ferrymaster, Drummond and Cauty fed bundles of notes into a fire in the boathouse over the course of about an hour. The event was filmed; the resulting film, Watch the K Foundation Burn a Million Quid, was toured for years afterwards, with the pair asking audiences to debate whether the burning had been justified, under a self-imposed moratorium on explaining their motives.

Reactions and legacy

The burning provoked outrage, bafflement, and intense debate about art, wealth, and waste; some on Jura were angered that the money had not been given to charity, and the pair themselves later expressed ambivalence, with Drummond acknowledging the act's lasting hold on their lives. The event remains one of the most notorious gestures in British art and popular music, and inspired later works and investigations, including attempts to locate the ashes and the 2017 KLF "re-emergence" events in Liverpool.
