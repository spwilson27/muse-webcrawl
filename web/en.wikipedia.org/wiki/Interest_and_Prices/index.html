Interest and Prices

Interest and Prices: A Study of the Causes Regulating the Value of Money (Swedish: Geldzins und Güterpreise, 1898) is a book by the Swedish economist Knut Wicksell. It is widely regarded as one of the most important works in the history of monetary economics.

Summary

In the book, Wicksell developed his theory of the natural rate of interest — the rate of interest that would be neutral with respect to commodity prices and would not tend to raise or lower them. He distinguished this from the market rate of interest charged by banks. When the market rate falls below the natural rate, prices rise cumulatively; when it rises above, prices fall. This mechanism became known as the cumulative process.

Influence

Wicksell's ideas influenced later economists including John Maynard Keynes, the Austrian School (notably Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek), and the Stockholm School. The distinction between natural and market rates remains an ancestor of modern concepts such as the neutral rate of interest used by central banks.
