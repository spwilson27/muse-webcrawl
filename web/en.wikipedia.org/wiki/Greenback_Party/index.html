Greenback Party

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greenback Party (known successively as the Independent Party, the National Independent Party and the Union Labor Party) was an American political party with an anti-monopoly ideology active between 1874 and 1889. The party ran candidates in three presidential elections — in 1876, 1880, and 1884 — and at its height elected 14 members to Congress in 1878.

The party took its name from the "greenbacks", the paper currency issued by the Union during the Civil War. Farmers suffering from falling prices and deflation after the Panic of 1873 demanded that the government keep greenbacks in circulation and issue more, rather than returning to the gold standard, arguing that a larger money supply would raise prices and make debts easier to repay.

Platform and decline

Beyond currency expansion, Greenbackers supported an eight-hour day, a graduated income tax, workplace safety regulation, and (in many states) women's suffrage. Presidential candidates included Peter Cooper (1876), James B. Weaver (1880, winning over 300,000 votes), and Benjamin Butler (1884). The party faded as prosperity returned and the currency issue shifted to free silver, many of its members and ideas passing into the Populist Party of the 1890s.
