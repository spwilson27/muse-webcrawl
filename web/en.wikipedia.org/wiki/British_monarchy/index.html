Monarchy of the United Kingdom

The monarchy of the United Kingdom, commonly referred to as the British monarchy, is the form of government in which a hereditary monarch is the sovereign and head of state of the United Kingdom, the Crown Dependencies and the British Overseas Territories. The current monarch is Charles III, who ascended the throne on 8 September 2022 upon the death of his mother, Elizabeth II.

Constitutional role

The United Kingdom is a constitutional monarchy. The monarch appoints the prime minister (by convention, the person able to command the confidence of the House of Commons), summons and dissolves Parliament, grants royal assent to legislation, and is commander-in-chief of the armed forces, who swear allegiance to the Crown. In practice, executive power is exercised by ministers responsible to Parliament — the monarch "reigns but does not rule", acting on ministerial advice. Walter Bagehot summarised the sovereign's rights as "the right to be consulted, the right to encourage, the right to warn".

History

The office descends from the monarchies of England and Scotland, united in the Crown in 1603 and in Parliament in 1707. Its powers were progressively curtailed after the Glorious Revolution of 1688 and the Bill of Rights 1689, through the growth of cabinet government in the 18th century and democratic reform in the 19th and 20th. Elizabeth II reigned for 70 years (1952–2022), the longest of any British monarch, and was head of state of 15 Commonwealth realms at her death — a role Charles III continues.

Succession and finances

Succession is governed by the Act of Settlement 1701 and the Succession to the Crown Act 2013, which introduced absolute primogeniture and ended the disqualification for marrying a Roman Catholic (though the monarch must be in communion with the Church of England, of which they are Supreme Governor). The monarchy is funded principally by the Sovereign Grant, a percentage of the profits of the Crown Estate, supplemented by the private incomes of the Duchies of Lancaster and Cornwall.
