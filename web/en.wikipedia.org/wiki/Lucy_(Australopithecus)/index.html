Overview

Lucy is the common name of AL 288-1, several hundred pieces of bone fossil representing about 40% of the skeleton of a female Australopithecus afarensis, discovered in 1974 at Hadar in Ethiopia's Afar Triangle by palaeoanthropologists Donald Johanson and Tom Gray. Dated to about 3.2 million years old, Lucy was, at discovery, the oldest and most complete early hominin skeleton known. Her Ethiopian name is Dinknesh ("you are marvellous").

Importance

Lucy's pelvis and knee showed that A. afarensis walked upright habitually, while her small brain (~400–500 cm³) and curved fingers showed ape-like traits persisted — proving bipedalism preceded brain enlargement in human evolution. The name Lucy came from the Beatles' "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds", played in camp that night. Her species was named in 1978; debate continues over whether afarensis is a single variable species, and a 2016 study argued Lucy died from a fall from a tree — an interpretation others dispute. The original fossils are kept in the National Museum of Ethiopia in Addis Ababa.
