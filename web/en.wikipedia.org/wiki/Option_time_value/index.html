Option time value

Option time value
FieldFinance / derivatives
Defined asOption premium − intrinsic value
Also calledExtrinsic value
Main driversTime to expiration, implied volatility, interest rates, dividends

In finance, the time value (extrinsic or instrumental value) of an option is the premium a rational investor would pay over its current exercise value (intrinsic value), based on the probability that the option will increase in intrinsic value before expiration. An option's total premium equals its intrinsic value plus its time value.

Determinants

Time value is greatest for at-the-money options and declines as an option moves deeper in or out of the money. It decreases as expiration approaches — a phenomenon known as time decay, measured by the Greek theta — and this decay accelerates in the final weeks of an option's life, reaching zero at expiration. Higher implied volatility raises time value, because greater price movement increases the chance that the option finishes in the money. Interest rates and expected dividends also enter pricing models such as Black–Scholes.

Example

If a call option with a strike price of 100 trades at a premium of 6 while the underlying stock trades at 103, the intrinsic value is 3 (the amount by which the option is in the money) and the time value is 6 − 3 = 3. An out-of-the-money option has zero intrinsic value, so its entire premium is time value.
