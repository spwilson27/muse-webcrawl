Sporran

The sporran (Scottish Gaelic for "purse") is a traditional part of Scottish Highland dress: a pouch worn at the front of the kilt, hanging from a chain or leather strap around the waist. Because the kilt itself has no pockets, the sporran serves as a wallet and container for personal items such as money, keys, and a phone.

Styles

Day sporrans are plain, usually of leather with minimal decoration, worn for everyday and informal occasions. Dress sporrans are made of fur — historically sealskin, now often synthetic or bovine fur — with an ornate metal top, or cantle, and hanging tassels or bells. Semi-dress sporrans combine leather bodies with a fur front. Military and pipe-band sporrans may be of horsehair, with long strands hanging over the kilt.

History

The sporran descends from the simple belt pouches worn across medieval Europe. As Highland dress developed, the pouch became standardised and increasingly decorative, especially after the nineteenth-century romantic revival of Scottish tradition. Regimental sporrans became distinctive badges of identity for Highland regiments. Modern controversy has surrounded sporrans made from seal skin, which is restricted under wildlife-protection rules in some jurisdictions, and Scottish guidance has encouraged alternatives.
