Arcadia is a city in Los Angeles County, California, United States, located about 13 miles (21 km) northeast of downtown Los Angeles in the San Gabriel Valley, at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains. It is known for the Santa Anita Park racetrack and the Los Angeles County Arboretum and Botanic Garden.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Tongva (Gabrielino) people. Much of the land formed part of Rancho Santa Anita, and its most famous owner, Elias Jackson "Lucky" Baldwin, acquired the rancho in 1875, raising thoroughbred horses and promoting development. Arcadia incorporated in 1903 after disputes over saloon licensing and local control. Baldwin's Santa Anita Park opened in 1934 and became one of America's premier horse-racing venues, home of the Santa Anita Derby and multiple Breeders' Cup runnings. During the Second World War the racetrack grounds served as a temporary assembly centre for Japanese Americans before relocation — a dark chapter later memorialised on the site.

Geography and community

Arcadia is a largely residential, affluent suburb with highly rated public schools and a large Asian American community, which grew rapidly from the 1980s onward. Landmarks include the Arboretum — Baldwin's former estate grounds, with peafowl descended from birds he imported — the Santa Anita mall (now The Shops at Santa Anita), and the historic Santa Anita train depot. The city is served by the Metro A Line at Arcadia station. See also Santa Anita Park and San Gabriel Valley.
