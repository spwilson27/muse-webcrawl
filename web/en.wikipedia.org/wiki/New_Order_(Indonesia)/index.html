New Order (Indonesia)

The New Order (Indonesian: Orde Baru, abbreviated Orba) is the term coined by Indonesian president Suharto for his regime, which held power from 1966 until 1998. It succeeded Sukarno's "Old Order" (Orde Lama) and was characterised by authoritarian rule, military dominance in politics, anti-communism, and a development-oriented economic programme.

Rise

Following the abortive 30 September Movement of 1965 and the ensuing mass killings of communists and alleged sympathisers, Major General Suharto manoeuvred Sukarno into signing the Supersemar letter of 11 March 1966, transferring effective authority. Suharto became acting president in 1967 and president in 1968.

Rule

The regime combined rapid economic growth, price stability and expanded education and infrastructure with strict political control: parties were fused into approved blocs, the armed forces held a dual function (dwifungsi) in society, the press was censored, and dissent — in Aceh, Papua, and occupied East Timor — was met with repression. The state ideology Pancasila was enforced as the sole foundation of organisations. Foreign investment and Western-aligned technocrats (the "Berkeley Mafia") underpinned development.

Fall

The Asian financial crisis of 1997–98 collapsed the rupiah and the regime's legitimacy. Amid riots and student protests in May 1998, Suharto resigned on 21 May 1998, ushering in the Reformasi era of democratisation.
