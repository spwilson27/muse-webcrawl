Christian Boehmer Anfinsen Jr. (26 March 1916 – 14 May 1995) was an American biochemist who shared the 1972 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Stanford Moore and William Howard Stein for his work on ribonuclease, especially concerning the connection between the amino acid sequence and the biologically active conformation of proteins.

Ribonuclease experiments

At the National Institutes of Health, Anfinsen studied the enzyme ribonuclease A from bovine pancreas. He showed that the enzyme could be unfolded (denatured) by breaking its four disulphide bonds with urea and mercaptoethanol, and that removing the denaturants allowed the enzyme to spontaneously refold into its active form, restoring full catalytic activity. The experiment demonstrated that all the information needed for a protein's three-dimensional structure is encoded in its amino acid sequence.

Anfinsen's dogma

From this work came "Anfinsen's dogma": under normal physiological conditions, the native structure of a protein is the unique, stable, kinetically accessible minimum of free energy determined by the amino acid sequence. The idea — sometimes called the thermodynamic hypothesis of protein folding — remains a cornerstone of structural biology, even as later work recognised the roles of molecular chaperones and exceptions such as prions.
