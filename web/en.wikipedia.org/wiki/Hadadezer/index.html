Hadadezer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hadadezer (Aramaic: הֲדַדְעֶזֶר, "Hadad is help"), son of Rehob, was king of Zobah, an Aramean kingdom in Syria, in the tenth century BC. He is known from the Hebrew Bible as the principal northern opponent of King David of Israel.

Biblical accounts

According to 2 Samuel 8, David defeated Hadadezer as the latter marched to restore his power at the Euphrates River, capturing large numbers of his chariots, horsemen and foot soldiers and hamstringing most of the chariot horses. The spoils included shields of gold carried by Hadadezer's officers and a great quantity of bronze from his cities of Betah and Berothai.

When the Ammonites hired Aramean troops from Beth-Rehob, Zobah and elsewhere against David (2 Samuel 10), Hadadezer sent forces under his commander Shobach (or Shophach) to assist them; these were defeated by David's general Joab and then by David himself at Helam, east of the Jordan, where Shobach was killed. After this defeat Hadadezer's vassals made peace with Israel and became subject to it, and the Arameans of Damascus, who had come to Hadadezer's aid, likewise became David's tributaries.

Hadadezer also appears in connection with the rise of Rezon, one of his former officers, who (1 Kings 11) escaped, gathered a band of raiders and seized Damascus, reigning there as an adversary of Solomon.

Historical context

Zobah, centred in the Beqaa region between the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges, was among the most powerful of the early Aramean states, and Hadadezer's attempt to extend his authority to the Euphrates put him in competition with both Israel under David and, later, the expanding Neo-Assyrian Empire, whose annals mention subsequent kings of the region. Scholars debate the scale and historicity of the biblical campaigns, but the account preserves a memory of Zobah's prominence before its eclipse by Damascus.
