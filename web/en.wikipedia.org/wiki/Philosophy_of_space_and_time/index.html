Philosophy of space and time

The philosophy of space and time is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of space, time, and spacetime: whether they exist independently of objects and events, how time passes, and whether the past and future are as real as the present.

Absolutism and relationism

Isaac Newton defended absolute space and time, a fixed stage on which events occur. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz countered that space and time are merely systems of relations between objects and events. Their dispute, aired in the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence, set the agenda for later debate.

Relativity and spacetime

Einstein's theories of relativity unified space and time into a four-dimensional spacetime and challenged simultaneity, feeding debates between presentism, eternalism (the "block universe"), and the growing block theory.

The flow of time

Philosophers also debate J.M.E. McTaggart's A-series and B-series, the reality of temporal passage, and the direction of time's arrow in thermodynamics and cosmology.
