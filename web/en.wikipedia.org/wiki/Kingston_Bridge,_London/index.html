Kingston Bridge is a road bridge over the River Thames in south-west London, connecting Kingston upon Thames on the south bank with Hampton Wick and Bushy Park on the north. It carries the A308 and forms a key link in the local road network, with footways and cycle paths on both sides.

History

A crossing at Kingston is of great antiquity: for centuries Kingston Bridge was the first bridge upstream of London Bridge, and a wooden bridge existed here from at least the 12th century. The present stone bridge, built of Portland stone with five arches, was designed by Edward Lapidge and opened in 1828, slightly downstream of the old medieval bridge. A toll was charged until 1870.

The bridge was widened between 1912 and 1914, when the downstream side was extended, and further widened in 2000–2001 as part of works that added bus lanes and improved facilities for pedestrians and cyclists. The bridge and its approach are Grade II* listed. Downstream is Kingston Railway Bridge; upstream is Hampton Court Bridge.
