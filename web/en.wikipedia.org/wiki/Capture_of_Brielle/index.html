Capture of Brielle

Date: 1 April 1572
Location: Brielle (Den Briel), Holland
Conflict: Eighty Years' War
Attackers: Sea Beggars (Watergeuzen)
Result: Brielle taken; general revolt in Holland follows

The Capture of Brielle (Dutch: Inname van Den Briel) took place on 1 April 1572, when the Sea Beggars (Watergeuzen), Dutch rebel privateers, seized the coastal town of Brielle in the name of William of Orange. The event is traditionally regarded as the turning point of the Dutch Revolt against Spanish rule and one of the founding moments of the Dutch Republic.

Background

The Sea Beggars were a confederacy of Calvinist exiles and adventurers who, licensed by William of Orange, preyed on Spanish shipping. Having been expelled from English ports by Elizabeth I — who came under Spanish diplomatic pressure — the fleet under Willem van der Marck, Lord of Lumey, was driven by storms toward the Dutch coast and arrived before Brielle at the mouth of the Meuse.

The capture

Brielle's Spanish garrison was absent, and the town's ferry master signalled that the place was weakly held. A landing party demanded the town's surrender; when the town council hesitated, the Beggars broke down the northern gate with a battering ram and entered. The town was taken with little fighting. In a famous Dutch rhyme, "Op 1 april verloor Alva zijn bril" — "On 1 April, Alva lost his glasses" (bril punning on Brielle) — the Spanish commander, the Duke of Alva, is mocked for the loss. The Beggars also hanged a number of Catholic clergy from Brielle and nearby Gorinchem — the Martyrs of Gorkum — an act that damaged their cause.

Consequences

Against expectation, the rebels decided to hold Brielle rather than plunder and abandon it. Within weeks, town after town in Holland and Zeeland declared for the revolt, and William of Orange was reinstated as stadtholder of Holland. Brielle thus became the first liberated town — "Libertatis Primitiae", the first fruits of liberty — and 1 April is still celebrated annually in the town with a street festival re-enacting the capture.
