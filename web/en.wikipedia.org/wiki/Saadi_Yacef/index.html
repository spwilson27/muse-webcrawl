Saadi Yacef

Saadi Yacef (January 20, 1928 – September 10, 2021) was an Algerian independence fighter who led the National Liberation Front (FLN) in Algiers during the Battle of Algiers in the Algerian War, and later became a film producer and politician.

BornJanuary 20, 1928, Algiers, French Algeria
DiedSeptember 10, 2021, Algiers, Algeria
Known forBattle of Algiers; producing The Battle of Algiers (film)

Revolutionary activity

Born in the Casbah of Algiers, Yacef worked as a baker's apprentice and joined nationalist movements. During 1956–57 he commanded the FLN's Autonomous Zone of Algiers, organising guerrilla attacks and bombings by networks that included women such as Zohra Drif, Djamila Bouhired and Hassiba Ben Bouali. He was captured by French paratroopers in September 1957 with Zohra Drif, after Ali La Pointe's network was hunted down; sentenced to death, he was spared and freed after independence in 1962 under the Évian Accords.

Film and later life

Yacef founded the production company Casbah Film and produced Gillo Pontecorvo's acclaimed 1966 film The Battle of Algiers, in which he played a character based on himself (El-Hadi Jafar). He wrote memoirs of the battle and served in Algeria's Council of the Nation (Senate).
