Electro-optics

Electro-optics is a branch of electrical engineering, electronic engineering, materials science, and physics involving components, electronic devices such as lasers, laser diodes, LEDs, waveguides, etc. which operate by the propagation and interaction of light in various materials. It is closely associated with photonics.

Electro-optic effects

The field is named for the electro-optic effect: the change in the optical properties (especially refractive index) of a material in response to an electric field. The linear Pockels effect occurs in non-centrosymmetric crystals such as lithium niobate and gallium arsenide, while the quadratic Kerr effect occurs in all materials. Applying a voltage to such a material allows the phase, polarization, or amplitude of light passing through it to be modulated at very high speeds.

Devices and applications

Electro-optic modulators are essential components of fibre-optic communication systems, converting electrical data signals into modulated light. Other applications include laser Q-switching, optical beam deflectors and scanners, tunable filters, and voltage sensors. In military usage, "electro-optics" also refers to thermal imaging, night-vision, and laser rangefinding and targeting systems.
