Art in Architecture

Art in Architecture (also written Art-in-Architecture) is a program of the United States General Services Administration (GSA) that commissions fine artists to create works of art for new federal buildings and courthouses. Established in 1963, the program reserves a portion of the estimated construction cost of a federal building — traditionally one-half of one percent — for the commissioning of artworks that are integrated into the building's design and accessible to the public.

History

The modern program grew out of earlier federal art initiatives. During the New Deal era of the 1930s, the Treasury Department's Section of Painting and Sculpture commissioned murals and sculptures for post offices and courthouses across the United States, providing work for artists during the Great Depression. After a lapse during and after the Second World War, the GSA revived the idea in 1963 under the name Art in Architecture, following the recommendations of a presidential ad hoc committee on government office space chaired by Nathaniel Owings.

Process

For each eligible project, the GSA works with the building's design architect and a panel that includes art professionals — often convened with advice from the National Endowment for the Arts — to select an artist early in the design process. The artist collaborates with the architect so that the artwork becomes part of the building rather than an afterthought: commissioned works range from monumental sculpture and murals to integrated features such as floors, glass, earthworks and light installations.

Notable commissions

Among the best-known Art in Architecture commissions are Alexander Calder's stabile Flamingo (1974) for the plaza of the Kluczynski Federal Building in Chicago, Isamu Noguchi's sculpture garden works, and numerous site-specific pieces by artists such as Ellsworth Kelly, Martin Puryear, Maya Lin and Sol LeWitt for federal courthouses and office buildings across the country. The program has commissioned more than 500 works of art since its founding.

Related programs

Many state and local governments operate similar "percent for art" programs, dedicating a fixed percentage of public construction budgets to art. The concept has parallels in programs such as the Federal Transit Administration's art guidelines and in international percent-for-art schemes in Europe.
