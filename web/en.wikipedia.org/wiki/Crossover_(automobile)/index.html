Crossover (automobile)

Crossover SUV
Body styleSUV-like, unibody construction
ExamplesToyota RAV4, Honda CR-V, Nissan Qashqai

A crossover, or crossover SUV, is a type of automobile with a sport utility vehicle (SUV) body style built on a unibody (monocoque) car platform, rather than the body-on-frame chassis used by traditional SUVs. Crossovers combine the high seating position, raised ground clearance, and cargo space of an SUV with the ride comfort, handling, and fuel economy of a passenger car.

Most crossovers are front-wheel drive or offer optional all-wheel drive, and they are not generally intended for serious off-road use. The segment grew rapidly from the 1990s, with models such as the Toyota RAV4 (1994) and Honda CR-V (1995), and came to dominate new-car sales in North America and Europe by the late 2010s.

Classification

Crossovers are sold in sizes from subcompact (B-segment) to full-size, and the boundary between crossovers and SUVs is largely one of construction: unibody vehicles are classed as crossovers, while frame-based vehicles are classed as SUVs. In Europe, many models are marketed simply as SUVs regardless of construction.
