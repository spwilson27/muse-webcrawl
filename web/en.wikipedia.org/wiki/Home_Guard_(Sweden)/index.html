Home Guard (Sweden)

The Swedish Home Guard (Swedish: Hemvärnet), formally the Home Guard – National Security Forces (Hemvärnet – Nationella skyddsstyrkorna), is a volunteer military reserve force forming part of the Swedish Armed Forces. Founded on 29 May 1940 during the Second World War, it consists of part-time soldiers who can be mobilised rapidly to guard infrastructure, support the regular forces and assist civil society in emergencies.

Organisation and role

The Home Guard is organised into battalions across Sweden's military regions, including rapid-response companies, intelligence, CBRN, transport and support units, as well as Home Guard bands and youth activities. Members sign a service agreement, train for a set number of days per year, and keep basic equipment at home or in local depots so units can assemble within hours. Wartime tasks include protecting mobilisations, guarding key installations and terrain, and supporting the army's manoeuvre forces; in peacetime the Home Guard regularly assists in floods, forest fires, storms and searches for missing persons.

History

Created in 1940 as German forces occupied neighbouring Denmark and Norway, the Home Guard quickly grew to over 100,000 volunteers, including the women's auxiliary Lottorna. It remained a mass organisation through the Cold War before shrinking after the 1990s, then regrew as Sweden rebuilt its total defence in the 2010s and 2020s, a process accelerated by Russia's invasion of Ukraine and Sweden's accession to NATO in 2024.
