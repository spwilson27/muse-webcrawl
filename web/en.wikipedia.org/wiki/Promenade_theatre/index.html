Promenade theatre

Promenade theatre is a form of theatre in which the audience is not seated but moves with the action, following performers from place to place within a venue or through a landscape. Productions may occupy a whole building, a street, or a series of rooms, with scenes unfolding around, among and sometimes involving the spectators.

Characteristics

Because the audience is mobile, promenade productions can stage large-scale, multi-location narratives impossible on a single stage, and each audience member's experience differs according to the route taken. The form overlaps with site-specific theatre and immersive theatre; British company Punchdrunk's masked productions, in which roaming spectators explore detailed environments, are prominent modern examples.

History

Processional and mystery plays of the medieval period, performed on pageant wagons before moving crowds, are early ancestors. The modern promenade format was developed by experimental companies from the 1960s onward and became a staple of fringe and community theatre, including large-cast promenade productions at venues such as the National Theatre in London.
