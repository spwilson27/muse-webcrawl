Deccan sultanates

The Deccan sultanates were five late medieval and early modern kingdoms of the Deccan Plateau in south-central India: Bijapur, Golkonda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar and Berar.

They emerged from the fragmentation of the Bahmani Sultanate in the late 15th and early 16th centuries. Frequently at war with each other, they famously united at the Battle of Talikota in 1565 to defeat the Vijayanagara Empire. One by one they were absorbed into the Mughal Empire during the 17th century, Golkonda and Bijapur falling to Aurangzeb in 1686–87.

Culture

The sultanates developed a distinctive Deccani culture blending Persian, Turkish and local Telugu, Marathi and Kannada influences, seen in monuments such as the Gol Gumbaz and Charminar.
