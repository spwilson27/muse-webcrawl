Hydrazoic acid

Chemical formulaHN3
Molar mass43.028 g/mol
AppearanceColorless, volatile liquid
Boiling point37 °C (310 K)
Acidity (pKa)4.6
HazardsHighly toxic; explosive

Hydrazoic acid, also known as hydrogen azide or azoimide, is a compound with the chemical formula HN3. It is a colorless, volatile, and explosive liquid at room temperature and pressure. Hydrazoic acid is a compound of nitrogen and hydrogen and is therefore a pnictogen hydride. It was first isolated in 1890 by Theodor Curtius.

Hydrazoic acid is a weak acid (pKa 4.6), comparable in strength to acetic acid. Its salts, the azides, include industrially important compounds such as sodium azide, used in automobile airbags, and dangerously explosive heavy-metal azides such as lead azide, used in detonators.

Production

Hydrazoic acid is usually prepared by acidifying an azide salt such as sodium azide with a strong acid, though the product must be handled with extreme care because of its toxicity and explosiveness. An older route oxidizes hydrazine with nitrous acid. Aqueous solutions can also be generated by treating barium azide with dilute sulfuric acid and filtering off the barium sulfate.

Structure and reactions

The molecule is nearly linear, with an H–N–N angle of about 109° and differing N–N bond lengths reflecting resonance between two principal structures. Hydrazoic acid reacts with metals to liberate hydrogen and form azide salts, and with carbonyl compounds in the Schmidt reaction to give amides and amines. Its conjugate base, the azide ion N3−, is a pseudohalide and a useful ligand and click-chemistry reagent through the azide–alkyne cycloaddition popularized by Sharpless and Meldal.

Health hazards

Hydrazoic acid vapor is highly toxic: it is a potent vasodilator, and exposure causes headache, irritation, and potentially fatal hypotension. Its toxicity is comparable to that of hydrogen cyanide. Sodium azide solutions left in contact with metal plumbing can deposit shock-sensitive copper or lead azides, a recognized laboratory and hospital hazard.
