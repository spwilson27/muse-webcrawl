Thomas Berry

Thomas Berry (November 9, 1914 – June 1, 2009) was an American Passionist priest, cultural historian, and self-described "geologian". He became a leading voice in the movement for an ecological spirituality, arguing that humanity's relationship with the Earth must be grounded in the scientific story of the universe.

Life and career

Born in Greensboro, North Carolina, Berry joined the Passionist order and was ordained in 1942. He earned a doctorate in European intellectual history from the Catholic University of America, studied Asian religions and languages extensively, and taught at several universities, including Fordham, where he directed the graduate program in the history of religions. He founded the Riverdale Center for Religious Research in New York.

Thought

Influenced by Teilhard de Chardin and by Asian traditions, Berry argued that the primary sacred community is the Earth community itself. His best-known works include The Dream of the Earth (1988), The Universe Story (1992, with Brian Swimme), and The Great Work: Our Way into the Future (1999).

Key ideas:

  • The Great Work — each generation's defining task; for the present one, moving from a human-centred to an Earth-centred mode of civilization.
  • The New Story — the scientific account of cosmic evolution as a shared sacred narrative for all peoples.
  • Ecozoic era — Berry's proposed name for a hoped-for future period of mutually enhancing human–Earth relations, succeeding the destructive Cenozoic.
  • Earth jurisprudence — his thought inspired legal movements recognizing rights of nature.
