Alexandroupolis (Greek: Αλεξανδρούπολη) is a city in northeastern Greece, the capital of the Evros regional unit and the principal port of Greek Thrace, situated on the Aegean Sea close to the Evros river delta and the border with Turkey.

History

The modern city grew from the 19th-century fishing village of Dedeagach under Ottoman rule, which developed after the construction of the railway line from Thessaloniki towards Constantinople in the 1870s. The town changed hands several times during the Balkan Wars and the First World War; assigned briefly to Bulgaria, it was transferred to Greece by treaty in 1920 and renamed in honour of King Alexander I of Greece during his visit that year. Its landmark lighthouse, built in 1880, still guides shipping into the port.

Geography and economy

Alexandroupolis lies at the eastern end of the Egnatia motorway and is connected by rail and road to Thessaloniki and to Turkey. The nearby Evros Delta is an important protected wetland for migratory birds. The city's port and a floating liquefied natural gas terminal have given it a growing role as an energy and logistics hub for the Balkans and Eastern Europe, a role that expanded after 2022 when the port became a significant transit point for allied forces in the region.
