The Mai Tai is a cocktail made of rum, Curaçao liqueur, orgeat syrup, and lime juice. It is one of the characteristic cocktails in tiki culture.

History

The cocktail was purportedly invented at the Trader Vic's restaurant in Oakland, California, in 1944. Trader Vic's rival, Donn Beach, claimed to have instead first created it in 1933, although a longtime employee and historian argues the Mai Tai was based on Victor J. Bergeron's (Trader Vic's) own 1934 creation, the Q.B. Cooler.

Victor Bergeron claimed to have invented the Mai Tai in 1944 at his restaurant, Trader Vic's, in Oakland. According to Bergeron, he made the drink for friends visiting from Tahiti, Ham and Carrie Guild. After tasting it, Carrie reportedly exclaimed, "Maita'i roa a'e", which is Tahitian for "out of this world, the best!" — hence the name.

Recipe

The original 1944 recipe called for 2 ounces of 17-year-old J. Wray and Nephew rum, juice of one fresh lime, 1/2 ounce of Holland DeKuyper Orange Curaçao, 1/4 ounce of Trader Vic's Rock Candy Syrup, and 1/2 ounce of French Garier orgeat, shaken with shaved ice, garnished with half the lime shell and a sprig of mint.

The modern International Bartenders Association recipe combines amber Jamaican rum and Martinique molasses rum with orange curaçao, orgeat, fresh lime juice, and simple syrup, shaken with ice cubes, strained into a double old-fashioned glass over ice, and garnished with a pineapple spear, mint leaves, and a lime peel.

In popular culture

The Mai Tai became so popular that it supposedly depleted world rum supplies in the 1940s and 1950s. It was featured in the Elvis Presley film Blue Hawaii, which is credited for helping to popularize it.
