William Adelin (5 August 1103 – 25 November 1120), sometimes called William Ætheling, was the only legitimate son of Henry I of England by his wife Matilda of Scotland, and thus heir apparent to the English throne. His early death in the White Ship disaster caused the succession crisis that led to the Anarchy.

Life

William was invested as Duke of Normandy in 1115 and received the homage of the English barons as heir in 1116. In 1119 he married Matilda, daughter of Fulk V of Anjou. In November 1120 he sailed from Barfleur for England aboard the White Ship; the vessel struck a rock and sank. William initially escaped in a small boat but returned to rescue his half-sister Matilda FitzRoy, Countess of Perche, and was drowned when the overloaded boat capsized. His widow became a nun and later abbess of Fontevraud.

Consequences

With no legitimate son remaining, Henry I required his barons to swear to accept his daughter, the Empress Matilda, as successor. After Henry's death in 1135, Stephen of Blois seized the throne, plunging England and Normandy into civil war.
