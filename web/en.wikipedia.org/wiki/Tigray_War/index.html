The Tigray War was an armed conflict from November 2020 to November 2022 between the Ethiopian federal government and allied forces (including Eritrea) against the Tigray People's Liberation Front governing Ethiopia's northern Tigray Region.

Origins

Tensions between Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government and the TPLF—dominant in the former ruling coalition—escalated after postponed elections and Tigray's unilateral regional vote in September 2020. Fighting began on 3–4 November 2020 after reported TPLF attacks on federal Northern Command bases.

Course

Federal and Eritrean forces captured Mekelle within weeks, but Tigrayan forces retook most of Tigray in mid-2021 and advanced into Amhara and Afar before being driven back. The war caused mass displacement, famine conditions under a de facto blockade, and widespread reports of atrocities—including the Mai Kadra and Axum massacres and systematic sexual violence—by all sides. Casualty estimates range into the hundreds of thousands including famine deaths.

Peace agreement

African Union-mediated talks in Pretoria produced a cessation of hostilities on 2 November 2022, providing for TPLF disarmament, restoration of services and federal authority, and Eritrean withdrawal, though implementation and accountability remained incomplete.
