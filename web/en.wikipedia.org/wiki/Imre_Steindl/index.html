Imre Steindl

Born: 29 October 1839, Pest, Kingdom of Hungary

Died: 31 August 1902 (aged 62), Budapest, Austria-Hungary

Occupation: Architect

Notable work: Hungarian Parliament Building

Imre Steindl (29 October 1839 – 31 August 1902) was a Hungarian architect, best known as the designer of the Hungarian Parliament Building in Budapest, one of the most famous landmarks of the city. He was a leading representative of Gothic Revival architecture in Hungary and a professor at the Technical University of Budapest.

Life and career

Steindl was born in Pest and studied first at the Joseph Technical University in Budapest and then at the Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna, where he was a pupil of Friedrich von Schmidt, a prominent Gothic Revival architect. Returning to Hungary, he became a teacher and later professor of medieval architecture at the Technical University, training a generation of Hungarian architects in the study and restoration of historic monuments.

The Parliament Building

In 1882 Steindl won the competition for a permanent home for the Hungarian parliament. Construction of his neo-Gothic design began in 1885 on the bank of the Danube in Pest. The building, richly decorated with spires, statues of Hungarian rulers, and a great central dome, was inaugurated in 1896 for the millennium of the Hungarian state, though it was not fully completed until 1904 — two years after Steindl's death. Steindl himself went blind before the building was finished and died shortly before its vaults were closed.

Other works

Among Steindl's other works are the New Town Hall of Budapest, the Church of St Elizabeth in the Rózsák tere, and numerous restorations of medieval churches, including work connected with the Vajdahunyad Castle and the cathedral at Kassa (Košice). His restorations followed the purist Gothic principles of his Viennese training.
