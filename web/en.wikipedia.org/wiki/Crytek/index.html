Crytek

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crytek's games and CryEngine technology are known for demanding, photorealistic graphics.

Crytek GmbH is a German video game developer and software company based in Frankfurt am Main. Founded in 1999 by the brothers Cevat, Avni, and Faruk Yerli, Crytek is best known for the Far Cry and Crysis game series and for CryEngine, its proprietary game engine. The company gained a reputation for pushing the limits of real-time graphics on personal computers.

History

Crytek began as a technology demonstration group producing demos for Nvidia graphics cards. A tech demo of a dinosaur island, X-Isle, evolved into the company's first game, Far Cry (2004), published by Ubisoft. Its open-ended tropical environments and advanced graphics were critically acclaimed, and its engine, CryEngine, became a licensing product.

In 2007 Crytek released Crysis, a first-person shooter whose system requirements were so far ahead of typical gaming PCs that the phrase "But can it run Crysis?" became an enduring internet benchmark joke. Sequels Crysis 2 (2011) and Crysis 3 (2013) followed, along with the standalone expansion Crysis Warhead (2008). Other Crytek titles include Ryse: Son of Rome (2013), an Xbox One launch title, and the multiplayer survival shooter Hunt: Showdown (2019), which became a durable commercial success.

Financial difficulties and restructuring

In 2014 Crytek suffered a widely reported financial crisis, with staff reporting delayed wages; the company sold its UK studio (which became Dambuster Studios) and the Homefront intellectual property, and later closed or sold several international studios, including its studios in Budapest, Sofia, Seoul, Shanghai, and Istanbul. It refocused on its Frankfurt headquarters, its Kiev studio (which became independent as Blackwood Games), CryEngine, and live-service titles.

CryEngine

CryEngine has been licensed to other developers and used outside games, in film, architecture, and simulation. The engine pioneered techniques such as per-pixel lighting, depth of field, and large-scale vegetation rendering in commercial games. Since 2016 Crytek has distributed the engine under a pay-what-you-want and later royalty-based model.
