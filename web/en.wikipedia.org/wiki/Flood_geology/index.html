Flood geology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Flood geology (also called diluvial geology) is a creationist, pseudoscientific interpretation of the geological record based on a literal reading of the Genesis flood narrative, in which a worldwide flood described in the Book of Genesis is held to be responsible for most of the Earth's sedimentary rock layers and fossil record. It was developed by Seventh-day Adventist geologist George McCready Price in the early 20th century and popularised by John C. Whitcomb and Henry M. Morris in their 1961 book The Genesis Flood.

History

Before the development of modern geology in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, many naturalists attributed sedimentary strata to the biblical Deluge. However, as field evidence accumulated for an ancient Earth and gradual processes, flood geology was abandoned by mainstream geologists, including religious ones, in favour of uniformitarianism and an old Earth. Price revived the idea in works such as The New Geology (1923), arguing that the geological column was a creationist misinterpretation and that fossils had been sorted by the Flood.

Claims and scientific response

Flood geologists attribute phenomena such as the Grand Canyon, fossil graveyards, coal seams and evaporite deposits to a single year-long global inundation a few thousand years ago. Mainstream geologists and the broader scientific community reject these claims: radiometric dating, ice cores, varves, dendrochronology, the ordered succession of fossils, and the physical impossibility of the required heat and water volumes all contradict the model. Flood geology is classified as pseudoscience and is not accepted as part of geology by any major scientific organisation.
