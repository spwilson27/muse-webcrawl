Mandal Commission

The Mandal Commission, officially the Socially and Educationally Backward Classes Commission (SEBC), was a commission established in India in 1979 by the Janata Party government under Prime Minister Morarji Desai, with the mandate to "identify the socially or educationally backward classes" of India. It was chaired by B. P. Mandal, a former chief minister of Bihar.


Student protests against implementation, 1990.

Report

Using caste as an indicator of class, the commission's report of 1980 identified some 3,743 castes and communities as socially and educationally backward, estimated at about 52% of India's population (excluding Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes). Its central recommendation was that 27% of posts in central government services and public-sector undertakings, and places in higher education, be reserved for the Other Backward Classes (OBCs) — keeping total reservation under the 50% ceiling later affirmed by the Supreme Court.

Implementation and protests

The report was shelved for a decade until Prime Minister V. P. Singh announced its implementation in August 1990. The announcement provoked widespread protests, particularly by upper-caste students, including a wave of self-immolations (most famously by Rajeev Goswami), and reshaped Indian politics by accelerating caste-based mobilisation. In Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) the Supreme Court upheld 27% OBC reservation subject to exclusion of the "creamy layer".
