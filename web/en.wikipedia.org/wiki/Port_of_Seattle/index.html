The Port of Seattle is a public port authority in King County, Washington, United States, governed by an elected five-member commission. Created by voters in 1911 as part of the Progressive Era movement for public ownership of waterfront facilities, it operates Seattle's seaport terminals on Elliott Bay and Puget Sound, the Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (Sea-Tac), Fishermen's Terminal, marinas, and cruise ship berths. Since 2015 its container cargo operations have been managed jointly with the Port of Tacoma through the Northwest Seaport Alliance.

History

Seattle's waterfront was long dominated by private railroad interests. The port's creation in 1911, authorised by Washington's Port District Act, returned the harbour to public control. The port built public piers and, in 1914, the Bell Street Terminal and the great Smith Cove terminal; grain elevators and cold-storage plants served the Alaska and trans-Pacific trades. Fishermen's Terminal (1914) on Salmon Bay became home port to the North Pacific fishing fleet. The port developed Sea-Tac Airport, opened in 1944 and transferred to scheduled service in 1949. Containerisation from the 1960s transformed the working waterfront on Harbor Island and the Duwamish Waterway, whose industrial pollution later made the Lower Duwamish a federal Superfund site in which the port is a principal responsible party for cleanup.

Operations

Seattle's container terminals (Terminals 5, 18, 30, and 46) on Elliott Bay are operated under the Northwest Seaport Alliance, created in 2015 to end direct competition with Tacoma and to present a unified Puget Sound gateway for trans-Pacific trade; the alliance ranks among the largest container gateways in North America. Breakbulk and project cargo, grain at Terminal 86, refrigerated cargo, and cruise operations round out seaport business: the Smith Cove Cruise Terminal (Pier 91) and Pier 66 serve Alaska cruises, making Seattle one of the busiest cruise homeports on the US West Coast. Sea-Tac Airport is the region's dominant airport and a hub for Alaska Airlines and Delta Air Lines.

Governance and issues

The port is a special-purpose government with taxing authority, funded largely by operating revenues, airport fees, and a King County property tax levy. Recurring public issues include airport noise and expansion, electrification and air-quality goals for drayage trucks and shore power for cruise ships, equity programmes, and periodic corruption or ethics controversies, including a state audit in the late 2000s that criticised port construction contracting and led to governance reforms.
