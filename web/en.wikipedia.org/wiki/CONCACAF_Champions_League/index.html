CONCACAF Champions League

The CONCACAF Champions League, known from 2024 under its restored name the CONCACAF Champions Cup, is an annual continental club football competition organised by CONCACAF for the top clubs of North America, Central America and the Caribbean. It is the successor to the CONCACAF Champions' Cup, first held in 1962, and the winner qualifies for the FIFA Club World Cup (now the FIFA Intercontinental Cup and the expanded Club World Cup).

History

The original Champions' Cup ran from 1962 to 2008 in various formats, often with long gaps and finals decided in centralised mini-tournaments. In 2008 it was relaunched as the Champions League with a group stage modelled on the UEFA Champions League, expanding the field to 24 teams. In 2018 the format shifted to a 16-team straight knockout tournament played February–May, with regional competitions (the CONCACAF League and Caribbean Club Championship) feeding into it. From 2024 the competition reverted to the name Champions Cup with an expanded 27-team knockout format.

Dominance of Mexican clubs

Liga MX clubs dominated the Champions League era, winning every edition from 2009 through 2021. The streak was broken by the Seattle Sounders of MLS in 2022, the first American club to win the modern tournament. Mexican club América is the most successful side in the competition's overall history, with seven titles.

Qualification

Slots are allocated by league and cup results in member associations, with additional places earned through regional tournaments for Central American, MLS-affiliated and Caribbean clubs.
