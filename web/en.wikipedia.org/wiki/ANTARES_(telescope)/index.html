ANTARES (telescope)

ANTARES detected high-energy neutrinos using the Mediterranean Sea as a target volume.
TypeNeutrino telescope (Cherenkov detector)
LocationMediterranean Sea, 40 km off Toulon, France
Depth2,475 m
Completed30 May 2008
DecommissionedFebruary 2022
Predecessor/successorSucceeded by KM3NeT

ANTARES (Astronomy with a Neutrino Telescope and Abyss Environmental Research) was an underwater neutrino telescope located in the Mediterranean Sea off the coast of Toulon, France, at a depth of about 2,475 metres. It detected Cherenkov light produced when high-energy neutrinos interact in the water or the seabed below the detector, and was the first neutrino telescope operating in the Northern Hemisphere, giving it a view of the Galactic Centre through the Earth.

Design

The detector consisted of twelve vertical lines, each about 450 metres tall, anchored to the seabed and held taut by buoys. The lines carried 885 optical modules — glass spheres containing 10-inch photomultiplier tubes — arranged in triplets on 25 storeys per line. A 40-km electro-optical cable carried data and power to shore. In addition to neutrino astronomy, the deep-sea infrastructure (the AMADEUS acoustic array and oceanographic instruments) supported marine and earth sciences.

Results and legacy

ANTARES took data from 2008 until its decommissioning in February 2022, searching for cosmic neutrino sources such as active galactic nuclei, gamma-ray bursts, and the Galactic plane, and contributing to multi-messenger alerts. Its technology and collaboration formed the basis for the much larger KM3NeT telescope now under construction at sites off France, Italy, and Greece.
