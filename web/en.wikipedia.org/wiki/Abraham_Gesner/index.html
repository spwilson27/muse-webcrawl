Abraham Pineo Gesner (2 May 1797 – 29 April 1864) was a Canadian physician and geologist best known as the inventor of kerosene. His distillation of a clean-burning lamp oil from coal and bitumen launched the modern petroleum industry before the first oil wells were drilled.

Geological work

Trained in medicine in London, Gesner returned to Nova Scotia and was appointed provincial geologist of New Brunswick in 1838, producing the colony's first geological surveys and opening a museum of his collections in Saint John — one of Canada's earliest public museums. Financial disputes over the museum ruined him, and he returned to farming and medicine before resuming scientific work.

Kerosene

By 1846 Gesner had publicly demonstrated a new lamp fuel distilled from coal, which he named kerosene (from Greek keroselaion, "wax oil"). He obtained patents in 1854 and co-founded the North American Kerosene Gas Light Company on Long Island, New York, whose refinery mass-produced the fuel. Lacking the capital to defend his patents and compete after the 1859 Pennsylvania oil discoveries, he returned to Halifax, where he taught natural history at Dalhousie University.

Legacy

Gesner died in relative poverty in 1864, but was later recognised as a founder of the petroleum industry; he was inducted posthumously into the Canadian Petroleum Hall of Fame. A monument in Chipman Corner, Nova Scotia, marks his birthplace.
