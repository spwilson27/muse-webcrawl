Hechsher

A hechsher (Hebrew: הכשר, "prior approval"; plural hechshers) is a rabbinical product certification confirming that a food product complies with the Jewish dietary laws of kashrut. It also refers to the symbol printed on packaging, such as the OU (Ⓤ) of the Orthodox Union or the OK, Star-K and Kof-K marks.

Certification process

Certifying agencies inspect ingredients, production equipment and processes, and employ supervisors (mashgichim) to oversee manufacturing. Certification may distinguish categories such as meat, dairy and pareve, and Passover certification (e.g. OU-P) applies stricter seasonal rules about leavened grain.

Scope and market

Kosher certification is a large global industry: many packaged foods sold in the United States carry a hechsher, purchased by consumers including Jews, some Muslims, vegetarians and people seeking certain allergen assurances. Standards differ between agencies and communities, particularly between Orthodox and other denominations, and disputes over reliability have a long history.
