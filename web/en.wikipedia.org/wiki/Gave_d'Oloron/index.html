The Gave d'Oloron is a river in south-western France, in the department of Pyrénées-Atlantiques. It is formed at Oloron-Sainte-Marie by the confluence of two Pyrenean torrents, the Gave d'Aspe and the Gave d'Ossau. Together with the Gave de Pau, which it joins at Peyrehorade to form the short Gaves réunis, it is a left tributary of the Adour.

Course

From Oloron-Sainte-Marie the river flows north-west through the Béarn countryside, passing Navarrenx, Sauveterre-de-Béarn and Saint-Palais country before reaching Peyrehorade in the Landes department, where it meets the Gave de Pau. Its main tributaries include the Saison (also called the Gave de Mauléon), the Joos and the Saleys.

Hydrology and wildlife

The Gave d'Oloron has a pluvio-nival regime, with high water in spring from Pyrenean snowmelt and in autumn from Atlantic rainfall. It is renowned as one of France's finest Atlantic salmon and sea trout rivers, and its valley supports a notable population of the Pyrenean desman. The river is also used for canoeing and rafting near its upper reaches.
