Snowmobile

A snowmobile, also known as a snowmachine, motor sled, motor sledge or skimobile, is a motorised vehicle designed for winter travel and recreation on snow. It is designed to be operated on snow and ice and does not require a road or trail, but most are driven on open terrain or trails. Snowmobiling is a sport many people take part in as a hobby.

Design

A snowmobile is propelled by a continuous rubber track at the rear and steered by skis at the front, which the rider turns with handlebars. The engine — traditionally a two-stroke, increasingly a four-stroke — drives the track via a continuously variable transmission. Riders sit astride the machine like a motorcycle and shift their weight to steer. Modern machines include electric-start, reverse gears, heated grips and, increasingly, fuel injection and turbocharging.

History

Early motorised sledges appeared in the early 20th century. The modern recreational snowmobile was created by Joseph-Armand Bombardier of Valcourt, Quebec, whose company launched the Ski-Doo in 1959; within a decade dozens of manufacturers were selling hundreds of thousands of machines annually across Canada and the northern United States. Major manufacturers today include BRP (Ski-Doo), Polaris, Arctic Cat and Yamaha.

Uses and issues

Snowmobiles are essential transport for many Arctic and rural communities, and are used by police, military, trappers, utility workers and ski patrols, as well as for trail riding, mountain riding and racing (including snocross and the Iron Dog race). Environmental concerns include noise, two-stroke emissions and disturbance to wildlife, which have led to restrictions in some protected areas; avalanche risk and collisions are the principal safety hazards.
