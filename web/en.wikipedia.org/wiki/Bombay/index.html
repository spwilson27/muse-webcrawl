Bombay

Bombay is the former official name of the city now called Mumbai. For the modern city, see Mumbai.

Bombay was the official name until 1995 of the city now known as Mumbai, the capital of the Indian state of Maharashtra and the country's financial and entertainment capital. The name derives from the Portuguese Bom Bahia ("good bay") or from Bombaim, the name used during Portuguese rule from 1534 to 1661, when the islands were ceded to England as part of the dowry of Catherine of Braganza on her marriage to Charles II.

History of the name

Under British rule Bombay grew from a group of seven fishing islands into India's premier port and commercial metropolis, capital of the Bombay Presidency and later of Bombay State (1947–1960). In 1995 the Shiv Sena-led state government officially renamed the city Mumbai, after Mumbadevi, the patron goddess of the indigenous Koli fishing community, as part of a broader policy of replacing colonial-era names. "Bombay" persists in institutions founded under the old name, including the Bombay High Court, the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, and the Bombay Stock Exchange, and in the name of the Hindi film industry's portmanteau "Bollywood."
