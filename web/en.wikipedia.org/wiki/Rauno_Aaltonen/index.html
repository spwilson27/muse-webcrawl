Rauno Aaltonen

Rauno August Aaltonen (born 7 January 1938) is a Finnish former rally driver, one of the original "Flying Finns" of motorsport. He is best known for winning the Monte Carlo Rally in 1967 in a Mini Cooper S, and is credited with perfecting and popularizing left-foot braking in rallying.

Career

Aaltonen began his career in speedboat racing and motorcycle competition before switching to rallying. With the BMC works team he became a star in the Mini Cooper S, winning the European Rally Championship in 1965 and the RAC Rally the same year. His 1967 Monte Carlo victory, with co-driver Henry Liddon, remains one of the Mini's most famous wins. He later competed for Saab, Datsun, Fiat, Opel, and BMW, contesting events into the 1980s and achieving strong results on the Safari Rally.

Technique and legacy

Aaltonen was renowned for his car control and for developing left-foot braking — braking with the left foot while keeping the right on the throttle — to balance front-wheel-drive cars through corners, a technique now fundamental to rally driving. He was inducted into the Rally Hall of Fame in 2011.
