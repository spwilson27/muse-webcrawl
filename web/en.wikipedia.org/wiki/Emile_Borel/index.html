Félix Édouard Justin Émile Borel (7 January 1871 – 3 February 1956) was a French mathematician and politician. A founder of modern measure theory and a pioneer of probability theory and game theory, he was among the most influential French mathematicians of the first half of the 20th century. (The surname is commonly rendered "Emile Borel" in plain text; in French it is written Émile Borel.)

Mathematical work

Borel made foundational contributions to the theory of divergent series, functions of a real variable, and measure theory: the concepts of Borel sets and the Borel measure bear his name, as does the Heine–Borel theorem on compactness in Euclidean space. With René Baire and Henri Lebesgue he helped create the modern theory of functions of a real variable. In the 1920s Borel published early work on the theory of games, introducing ideas of mixed strategy in papers that preceded von Neumann's minimax theorem, and he wrote extensively on probability, including the famous image of monkeys typing at random (the infinite monkey theorem) to illustrate very unlikely events.

Public life

Borel served as a deputy in the French Chamber, as Minister of the Navy in 1925, and was a leading figure in French scientific administration, helping to create institutions that preceded the CNRS. He founded the Institut Henri Poincaré in 1928. During the Second World War, by then in his seventies, he was active in the French Resistance and was decorated for his service.
