Vajont Dam

The Vajont Dam (or Vaiont Dam) is a disused dam in the valley of the Vajont River under Monte Toc, in the municipality of Erto e Casso, 100 km north of Venice, Italy. It is one of the tallest dams in the world, at 262 metres (860 ft). The dam was conceived in the 1920s and constructed between 1957 and 1960 by SADE (Società Adriatica di Elettricità).

The 1963 disaster

On 9 October 1963, at 22:39 local time, a massive landslide of about 270 million cubic metres of rock slid from Monte Toc into the reservoir at high speed. The displaced water generated a megatsunami that overtopped the dam by some 250 metres and roared down the Piave valley, destroying the town of Longarone and the villages of Pirago, Rivalta, Villanova and Faè. Around 1,917 people were killed (estimates range up to about 2,500), making it one of the worst man-made disasters in European history.

Causes and aftermath

Geologists had warned that Monte Toc's slopes were unstable, and earlier smaller slides and creep movements in 1960–1963 were monitored but underestimated; the reservoir level was being lowered in a controlled attempt to manage the slide when it suddenly accelerated. Remarkably, the dam itself — a slender double-curvature arch designed by Carlo Semenza — survived almost undamaged. Criminal proceedings found SADE and state officials partly responsible, and the disaster became a landmark case in engineering ethics and risk assessment. The reservoir was never refilled.
