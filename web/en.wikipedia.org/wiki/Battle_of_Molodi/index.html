The Battle of Molodi was fought in July–August 1572 between the forces of the Crimean Khanate under Khan Devlet I Giray and the army of Tsar Ivan IV of Russia, commanded by Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky and Prince Dmitry Khvorostinin. It is regarded as one of the most important battles in Russian history, ending the threat of Crimean invasion of the Russian heartland.

Background

In 1571 Devlet Giray had burned Moscow and carried off tens of thousands of captives. The following year he returned with a much larger army, intending — in alliance with Ottoman ambitions — to conquer the Russian state outright and restore Tatar dominion over the Volga region. The Russians, their forces weakened by the Livonian War and internal strife, assembled south of Moscow behind the Oka river line near Serpukhov.

The battle

The Russian army employed the gulyay-gorod, a mobile fortress of wagons fitted with wooden shields, behind which musketeers and artillery could fire on the Tatar horse archers. After preliminary clashes from 30 July, the main assaults fell on the wagon fort near the village of Molodi. Khvorostinin's regiment, feigning retreat, drew the Tatars onto the guns of the gulyay-gorod, and on 2 August Vorotynsky led a flank attack into the Tatar rear. The Crimean army broke and fled, suffering catastrophic losses; the Khan's sons were among the dead.

Aftermath

The victory at Molodi ended large-scale Crimean invasions for a generation and secured Russia's southern frontier, allowing expansion toward the steppe. Vorotynsky was briefly celebrated as the saviour of Russia, though within a year Ivan IV had him arrested and executed on suspicion of treason.
