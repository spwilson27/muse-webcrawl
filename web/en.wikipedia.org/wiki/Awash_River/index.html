The Awash River (Amharic: አዋሽ ወንዝ, Awaš weniz; Afar: We'ayot) is a major river of Ethiopia. Its course is entirely contained within the boundaries of Ethiopia and it empties into a chain of interconnected lakes that begin with Lake Gargori and end with Lake Abhe on the border with Djibouti, rather than reaching the sea—an endorheic basin in the Afar Depression.

Course

The Awash rises in the highlands near Ginchi, west of Addis Ababa, flows east past the city of Awash and through the Great Rift Valley, then turns northeast through the Afar lowlands. Sugar estates at Wonji and Metehara, cotton plantations and the Tendaho scheme irrigate from its waters, and the Koka Dam (1960) regulates its upper flow for hydroelectric power and irrigation.

Palaeontology

The Middle Awash valley is one of the richest fossil localities in the world: deposits several million years old have yielded Ardipithecus, early Australopithecus—including finds near Hadar such as "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis) in the wider Awash basin—early Homo, and some of the oldest known stone tools. The lower valley is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
