Scotland Yard

Scotland Yard (officially New Scotland Yard) is a metonym for the headquarters of the Metropolitan Police Service, the territorial police force responsible for policing Greater London. The name derives from the location of the original headquarters of the Metropolitan Police, at 4 Whitehall Place, which had a rear entrance on a street called Great Scotland Yard.

History

The Metropolitan Police was founded in 1829 by Home Secretary Sir Robert Peel. Its first commissioners, Charles Rowan and Richard Mayne, occupied offices at 4 Whitehall Place, backing onto Great Scotland Yard — a street whose name may recall a medieval residence of Scottish royalty or, alternatively, a former owner named Scott. The back premises at Great Scotland Yard became the public entrance, and "Scotland Yard" quickly entered popular usage for the force itself.

In 1890 the headquarters moved to a new building by Norman Shaw on the Victoria Embankment, named New Scotland Yard. It moved again in 1967 to 10 Broadway, Westminster, and in 2016–2017 back to a refurbished Curtis Green Building on the Embankment, once more called New Scotland Yard. The famous rotating triangular sign outside the Broadway building — turning on its axis since 1968 — was moved to the new site.

In popular culture

Scotland Yard, and its Criminal Investigation Department (CID), are famous worldwide through detective fiction: Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes stories feature the hapless Inspector Lestrade of Scotland Yard, and generations of novels, films and television series have treated the name as synonymous with British police detective work.
