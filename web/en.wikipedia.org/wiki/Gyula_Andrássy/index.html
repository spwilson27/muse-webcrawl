Gyula Andrássy

Count Gyula Andrássy de Csíkszentkirály et Krasznahorka (3 March 1823 – 18 February 1890) was a Hungarian statesman who served as Prime Minister of Hungary (1867–1871) and subsequently as Foreign Minister of Austria-Hungary (1871–1879). He was one of the principal architects of the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 and of the Dual Alliance with Germany in 1879.

Early life and revolution

Born into one of Hungary's great magnate families, Andrássy entered the Diet in 1847 as a supporter of Lajos Kossuth's liberal nationalism. In the Hungarian Revolution of 1848 he served the revolutionary government as an emissary to Constantinople. After the revolution's defeat he was sentenced to death in absentia and hanged in effigy, living in exile in London and Paris, where he moved in fashionable society.

Compromise and premiership

Pardoned in 1857, Andrássy returned to Hungary and, working with Ferenc Deák, helped negotiate the Compromise of 1867 that created the Dual Monarchy. He became the first prime minister of the Hungarian half of the monarchy, modernizing its administration and army (the Honvéd).

Foreign minister

As foreign minister of the Dual Monarchy from 1871, Andrássy oriented Austria-Hungary toward Germany and away from Russia. He attended the Congress of Berlin in 1878, securing the occupation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, and in 1879 concluded the Dual Alliance with the German Empire — the cornerstone of European alignments before the First World War. He resigned the same year.

Legacy

Budapest's grand Andrássy Avenue is named after him. His son, Gyula Andrássy the Younger, was the last foreign minister of Austria-Hungary in 1918.
