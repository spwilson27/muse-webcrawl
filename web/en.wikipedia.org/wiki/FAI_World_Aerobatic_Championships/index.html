FAI World Aerobatic Championships

The FAI World Aerobatic Championships is a competition in aerobatics organized by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI), the world air sports federation. The championships are held every two years and determine individual and team world champions in powered aerobatic flying.

A competition aerobatic aircraft in flight.

History

The first World Aerobatic Championships were held in 1960 in Bratislava, Czechoslovakia. The event has since been staged at venues around the world. Pilots fly a series of compulsory and freestyle sequences inside a defined aerobatic "box", a cube of airspace 1,000 metres on each side, and are scored by judges on the precision and difficulty of their manoeuvres.

Format

The competition includes known (compulsory) programmes, free programmes devised by the pilots themselves, and unknown programmes given to competitors shortly before flying. Results combine into overall individual standings, with the Aresti Cup awarded to the men's world champion and team medals awarded to the best national squads. Aircraft such as the Extra 300, Sukhoi Su-26 and Su-31, and the MXS have been prominent in modern editions of the event.
