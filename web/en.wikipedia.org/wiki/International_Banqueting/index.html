International Banqueting

Banqueting is the practice of holding formal banquets — ceremonial feasts with set rituals of seating, courses, and toasts — and its customs vary considerably between cultures and countries. This article surveys international banqueting traditions.

Traditions

In Britain, formal banquets often follow the customs of livery companies and civic dinners, with a loyal toast to the monarch. French banqueting emphasizes long multi-course meals and the service à la française historically gave way to service à la russe. In China, banquets such as the Manchu–Han Imperial Feast showcase regional cuisines and are structured around round tables and shared dishes, with ganbei toasts. Japanese kaiseki meals, Korean royal court cuisine, and Ethiopian communal feasting each have their own ceremonial forms.

Modern practice

International state banquets remain an instrument of diplomacy, with protocol offices regulating precedence, menus, and entertainment. Corporate and wedding banqueting is a major part of the hospitality industry worldwide.
