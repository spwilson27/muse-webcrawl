The Kokemäenjoki (Swedish: Kumo älv) is a river in south-western Finland. It begins at Lake Liekovesi in Sastamala and flows generally westward for about 121 kilometres through Huittinen, Kokemäki and Harjavalta to the Bothnian Sea at Pori. With a catchment of about 27,000 km², the largest of any Finnish river basin together with its tributary systems, it drains much of the Pirkanmaa lake district, including the waters of Lake Pyhäjärvi near Tampere via the Tammerkoski rapids and the Nokianvirta.

Course and use

The river's main tributaries include the Loimijoki and the Kauvatsanjoki. Several hydroelectric power stations exploit its drop, notably at Harjavalta (completed 1939) and at Kolsi and Tyrvää upstream. The river's estuary at Pori is one of Finland's most important bird wetlands and its delta is still advancing into the sea because of post-glacial land uplift.

History

The lower valley was among the earliest settled and farmed regions of Finland; the medieval parish and trading site at Kokemäki gave the river its name. Salmon and whitefish fisheries were historically significant, and restoration of migratory fish passage has been a continuing aim of river management.
