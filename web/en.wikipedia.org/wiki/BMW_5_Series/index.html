The 5 Series is BMW's executive saloon, now in its eighth generation.

The BMW 5 Series is an executive car manufactured and marketed by the German company BMW since 1972. It sits between the 3 Series and 7 Series in BMW's range and is the second-best-selling BMW model after the 3 Series. The "5" designation replaced the New Class saloons and inaugurated the naming convention — three digits denoting series and engine size — later applied across the whole BMW range.

Generations

The eight generations are known by their internal codes: E12 (1972–1981), E28 (1981–1988), E34 (1988–1996), E39 (1995–2003), E60/E61 (2003–2010), F10/F11 (2010–2017), G30/G31 (2017–2024) and G60 (from 2023). Estate versions, badged "Touring", joined the range with the E34. High-performance M5 versions, beginning with the E28 M5 of 1984, have been a fixture of most generations; the M5 became a plug-in hybrid V8 with the G90 of 2024. The E39 is frequently cited in surveys as one of the finest executive cars of its era, while the sharply styled Bangle-era E60 was the best-seller of the line to its time.

Current model and electrification

The eighth-generation 5 Series (G60, launched 2023) is offered with petrol, diesel, plug-in hybrid and — for the first time — as the fully electric BMW i5, including the high-performance i5 M60. Production takes place principally at Dingolfing in Germany, with additional assembly in several other countries including China for long-wheelbase local models. Principal rivals throughout the model's life have been the Mercedes-Benz E-Class and Audi A6.
