Liability

A liability is a legal obligation or responsibility arising from civil actions, debts, or other duties enforceable by law. The term is used in several distinct senses in law and accounting.

Legal liability

In law, liability is the state of being responsible for damages or for performing an obligation. Civil liability arises in tort and contract, where a liable party may be required to compensate the injured party. Criminal liability requires, in most systems, both a prohibited act (actus reus) and a culpable mental state (mens rea). Doctrines such as strict liability impose responsibility without proof of fault, while vicarious liability holds one party (such as an employer) responsible for the acts of another. Joint and several liability allows a claimant to recover the full amount from any one of several liable parties.

Accounting liability

In financial accounting, a liability is a present obligation of an entity arising from past transactions, the settlement of which is expected to result in an outflow of resources. Liabilities appear on the balance sheet opposite assets and are classified as current (due within one year) or non-current. Common examples include accounts payable, loans, bonds payable, accrued expenses and deferred revenue.
