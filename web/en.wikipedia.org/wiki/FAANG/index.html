FAANG

FAANG is an acronym that refers to the stocks of five prominent American technology companies: Meta (formerly Facebook), Amazon, Apple, Netflix, and Google (now Alphabet). The term was popularized by Jim Cramer, the television host of CNBC's Mad Money, in 2013, when the group was originally called FANG, comprising Facebook, Amazon, Netflix, and Google. Apple was added in 2017, expanding the acronym to FAANG.

The five FAANG companies are among the most valuable publicly traded companies in the world.

History

The original FANG stocks were selected for their dominant market positions and rapid growth in revenue and earnings. Facebook (now Meta Platforms), Amazon, Netflix, and Google each led their respective fields of social media, e-commerce, streaming video, and online search. The addition of Apple in 2017 reflected its growing importance in the technology sector and its market capitalization surpassing $1 trillion in 2018, the first American public company to reach that milestone.

Market significance

Collectively, the FAANG companies have accounted for a substantial share of the market capitalization of the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq-100 indices. Their stock performance has been a major driver of broad market returns, and shifts in their valuation often move the wider market. The term later inspired successors and variants, including FAAMG (replacing Netflix with Microsoft) and the "Magnificent Seven".
