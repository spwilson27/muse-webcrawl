Public holidays in Canada

Public holidays in Canada, known as statutory holidays, stat holidays, or simply stats, consist of a variety of cultural, nationalistic, and religious holidays that are legislated at the federal or provincial/territorial level.

Nationwide statutory holidays

Five holidays are observed nationwide under federal and provincial law: New Year's Day (January 1), Good Friday, Canada Day (July 1), Labour Day (the first Monday in September), and Christmas Day (December 25). Federal employees and federally regulated industries additionally observe Easter Monday, Victoria Day, the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (September 30), Thanksgiving (the second Monday in October), Remembrance Day (November 11), and Boxing Day (December 26).

Provincial holidays

Provinces and territories add their own holidays, such as Family Day (in February, under various names), Saint-Jean-Baptiste Day in Quebec (June 24), the August Civic Holiday, and Discovery Day in Yukon and Newfoundland and Labrador. Altogether, Canadians typically receive between 9 and 13 statutory holidays per year depending on jurisdiction.

Observance

When a holiday falls on a weekend, it is commonly observed on the adjacent Friday or Monday. Statutory holidays generally entitle employees to a day off with pay or premium pay if worked, as set out in employment standards legislation.
