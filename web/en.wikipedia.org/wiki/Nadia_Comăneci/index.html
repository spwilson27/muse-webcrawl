Nadia Elena Comăneci Conner (born 12 November 1961) is a Romanian former gymnast and five-time Olympic gold medallist. At the 1976 Summer Olympics in Montreal, at the age of fourteen, she became the first gymnast ever awarded a perfect score of 10.0 at the Olympic Games, an achievement that transformed the sport and made her a global celebrity.

Early career

Comăneci was discovered tumbling in a schoolyard by coach Béla Károlyi, who with his wife Márta developed the Romanian centralised training system at Onești and later Deva. She won her first Romanian national title at nine and dominated the 1975 European Championships with four gold medals.

Montreal 1976

Competing on the uneven bars in the team event on 18 July 1976, Comăneci performed a flawless routine. The scoreboard, built by Omega, had been programmed on the assumption that a perfect ten was impossible and displayed "1.00" — the crowd understood before the officials did. She earned seven perfect tens at those Games, winning gold in the all-around, balance beam and uneven bars, plus team silver and floor bronze.

Later career and defection

Comăneci won further titles at the 1978 and 1979 World Championships and two more golds at the 1980 Moscow Olympics, though her career was curtailed by growth, injuries and the controlling environment of Ceaușescu's Romania. In November 1989, weeks before the Romanian Revolution, she defected across the Hungarian border and reached the United States. She married American gymnast Bart Conner in 1996 in Bucharest and became a US citizen in 2001.

Legacy

Comăneci is credited with popularising gymnastics worldwide and shifting it toward younger, more acrobatic athletes. She received the Olympic Order twice and is routinely ranked among the greatest gymnasts of all time. With Conner she runs a gymnastics academy in Oklahoma and is active in the Special Olympics and Laureus movements.
