University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory

Department
Founded: 1937 (as Mathematical Laboratory)
Location: William Gates Building, Cambridge, England
Parent: University of Cambridge

The University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory, now part of the Department of Computer Science and Technology, is the computer science department of the University of Cambridge. It is one of the oldest computer science departments in the world and a pioneer of practical computing.

History

The laboratory was founded in 1937 as the University Mathematical Laboratory, under the influence of Charles Darwin's son Leonard and later directed by Maurice Wilkes. In 1949 its EDSAC computer became the world's first practical stored-program computer to enter regular service. EDSAC 2 followed in 1958, and the laboratory's Titan and CAP systems advanced time-sharing and capability-based security. It was renamed the Computer Laboratory in 1970.

Research and teaching

Research groups work on systems, security, machine learning, natural language processing, graphics, and theory. The department teaches the Computer Science Tripos, one of the first undergraduate computer science degrees (formalized in 1978). Notable projects include the Xen hypervisor, the Raspberry Pi (conceived by laboratory members), and the first webcam — the Trojan Room coffee pot camera.

Building

Since 2001 the department has occupied the William Gates Building on the West Cambridge site, funded in part by a gift from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation.
