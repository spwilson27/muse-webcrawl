Cleveland Stadium

Location: 1085 West 3rd Street, Cleveland, Ohio, U.S.

Opened: July 1, 1931

Closed: December 1995 (Browns); September 1993 (Indians)

Demolished: November 4, 1996

Capacity: 77,813 (football); approx. 74,400 (baseball, final)

Tenants: Cleveland Indians (MLB), Cleveland Browns (NFL), Cleveland Rams (NFL)

Cleveland Stadium, commonly called Municipal Stadium or "the Mistake by the Lake", was a multi-purpose stadium located in Cleveland, Ohio. It was one of the early multi-purpose stadiums, built to accommodate both baseball and football. The stadium opened in 1931 and is best known as the long-time home of the Cleveland Indians of Major League Baseball (from 1932 to 1993) and the Cleveland Browns of the National Football League (from 1946 to 1995).

History

The stadium was built on landfill on the shore of Lake Erie at the urging of city manager William R. Hopkins, partly in the hope of attracting the 1932 Summer Olympics, which were ultimately awarded to Los Angeles. It was one of the largest stadiums in the world when it opened, seating more than 78,000 spectators. The Indians first played there in 1932 but did not make it their full-time home until 1947, splitting games for years with the smaller League Park.

The Browns began play in the stadium in 1946 in the All-America Football Conference and joined the NFL in 1950. The stadium hosted the 1935, 1954, 1963 and 1981 Major League Baseball All-Star Games, as well as numerous championship games.

Demolition

The Indians moved to the new Jacobs Field (now Progressive Field) for the 1994 season, and the Browns played their final game at the stadium in December 1995 before the franchise's controversial relocation to Baltimore. Demolition began on November 4, 1996; much of the rubble was dumped into Lake Erie to create artificial reefs. The site is now occupied by the Browns' current stadium, Huntington Bank Field, which opened in 1999 on part of the old stadium's footprint.
