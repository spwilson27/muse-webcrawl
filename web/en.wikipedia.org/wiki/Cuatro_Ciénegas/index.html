Cuatro Ciénegas (Spanish for "four marshes") is a municipality and town in the northern Mexican state of Coahuila, set in an intermontane basin of the Chihuahuan Desert. The town of Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza — named for revolutionary leader Venustiano Carranza, who was born there — serves as the municipal seat.

The basin is internationally known for its unusual system of spring-fed pools, streams and wetlands, known locally as pozas, which sustain a remarkable number of endemic species despite being surrounded by arid desert.

Ecology

The Cuatro Ciénegas basin covers roughly 84,000 hectares and contains more than 200 pools fed by underground aquifers. Its waters support living stromatolites, endemic fish such as the Cuatro Ciénegas pupfish, aquatic box turtles and other organisms found nowhere else on Earth. Because of this, scientists often compare the valley to the Galápagos Islands as a natural laboratory of evolution.

In 1994 the Mexican government designated the valley a protected flora and fauna area. The ecosystem is threatened by groundwater extraction for alfalfa irrigation in surrounding valleys, which lowers the water table feeding the pools.

History

The settlement was founded in the colonial era near the marshes that gave it its name. It was renamed in honour of Venustiano Carranza (1859–1920), the Coahuila-born leader of the Constitutionalist faction during the Mexican Revolution and later President of Mexico.
