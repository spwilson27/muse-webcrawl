Owain Glyndŵr

Owain ap Gruffydd (c. 1359 – c. 1415), commonly known as Owain Glyndŵr, was a Welsh nobleman and soldier who led a fierce revolt against English rule in Wales in the early 15th century. He was the last native Welshman to hold the title Prince of Wales.

Revolt

A descendant of the princes of Powys and Deheubarth, Glyndŵr had studied law in London and served in English armies before a land dispute with his neighbour Lord Grey of Ruthin helped spark his rising in 1400. Proclaimed Prince of Wales, he captured castles including Conwy, Harlech and Aberystwyth, won a victory at the Battle of Bryn Glas (Pilleth) in 1402, and allied with the Mortimer family and France. He held parliaments at Machynlleth and Harlech and set out a vision of an independent Wales with its own church and universities in the Tripartite Indenture of 1405.

Decline and legacy

From 1406 the revolt waned under English pressure; Harlech fell in 1409 and Glyndŵr became a fugitive. He was never captured and his burial place is unknown, giving rise to the legend that he sleeps, like a once and future king, awaiting Wales's need. He remains a central figure of Welsh national identity.
