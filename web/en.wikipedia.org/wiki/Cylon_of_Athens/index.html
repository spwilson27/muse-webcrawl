Cylon of Athens

Cylon of Athens (Greek: Κύλων) was an Athenian aristocrat and former Olympic victor who attempted to seize power in Athens around 632 BC, in what is often considered the first reliably recorded event in Athenian history. His failed coup and the sacrilegious killing of his supporters produced a hereditary curse said to haunt the Alcmaeonid family for generations.

The coup

Cylon had won at the Olympic Games and married the daughter of Theagenes, the tyrant of Megara. Encouraged by his father-in-law and an ambiguous Delphic oracle, he seized the Acropolis during a festival with a band of followers, hoping to become tyrant of Athens. The Athenians rallied under the archons — led by Megacles of the Alcmaeonid family — and besieged the Acropolis.

Aftermath

Starving, Cylon and his brother escaped, but his remaining supporters took refuge as suppliants at the altar of Athena. Promised their lives if they surrendered, they were nevertheless killed — some while still clinging to sacred statues. The killing was a grave religious offense: the Alcmaeonids were cursed and exiled, and the pollution (miasma) was cited centuries later against Alcmaeonid descendants such as Pericles. Athens brought in the Cretan purifier Epimenides to cleanse the city.

Sources

The episode is recounted by Herodotus and Thucydides, and the episode illustrates the aristocratic factionalism that preceded the reforms of Draco and Solon.
