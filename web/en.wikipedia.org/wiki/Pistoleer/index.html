Pistoleer

A pistoleer is a soldier armed with a pistol, especially a cavalryman of the early modern period. The term is most closely associated with the heavy cavalry tactics that developed in sixteenth-century Europe as firearms began to displace the lance on the battlefield.

Historical usage

German Schwarze Reiter ("black riders"), mercenary cuirassiers of the later sixteenth century, were the archetypal pistoleers. Riding in deep formations, successive ranks trotted up to the enemy, discharged their wheellock pistols at close range, and wheeled away — a manoeuvre known as the caracole — while the next rank advanced. Pistoleers wore a cuirass and helmet and usually carried a sword as a secondary weapon.

Decline

The caracole proved vulnerable to determined charges and disciplined infantry fire. Commanders such as Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden in the Thirty Years' War restricted pistol fire and restored the sword charge, and by the later seventeenth century the pistoleer had evolved into the more versatile cuirassier and dragoon.

Other meanings

In looser usage, "pistoleer" can denote anyone skilled with a pistol, including the gunfighters of the American frontier.
