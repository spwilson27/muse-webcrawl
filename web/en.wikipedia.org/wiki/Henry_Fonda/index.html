Henry Jaynes Fonda (May 16, 1905 – August 12, 1982) was an American actor whose career spanned five decades on Broadway and in Hollywood. He cultivated an everyman screen image in several films considered to be classics, including his performances as Tom Joad in The Grapes of Wrath (1940), the Juror No. 8 in 12 Angry Men (1957), which he also co-produced, and the villain Frank in Once Upon a Time in the West (1968). Fonda won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his final film role, in On Golden Pond (1981). He is the patriarch of a family of actors, including daughter Jane Fonda, son Peter Fonda, granddaughter Bridget Fonda, and grandson Troy Garity.

Early life and stage career

Fonda was born in Grand Island, Nebraska, into a family of Dutch descent (the name derives from an ancestor who emigrated to New Netherland in the 17th century). Raised in Omaha, he studied journalism briefly at the University of Minnesota before turning to acting with the Omaha Community Playhouse, encouraged by family friend Dorothy Brando — mother of Marlon Brando. He worked his way to Broadway, achieving success in 1934 in The Farmer Takes a Wife, whose 1935 film adaptation with Janet Gaynor brought him to Hollywood.

Film career

In the late 1930s Fonda established himself in films such as Jezebel (1938) with Bette Davis, Young Mr. Lincoln (1939) and Drums Along the Mohawk (1939) for John Ford. His portrayal of the dispossessed farmer Tom Joad in Ford's The Grapes of Wrath (1940), from Steinbeck's novel, earned an Academy Award nomination and became one of the defining roles of his career. He starred in Preston Sturges's The Lady Eve (1941), William Wellman's The Ox-Bow Incident (1943), and Ford's My Darling Clementine (1946), in which he played Wyatt Earp.

After a period back on Broadway — originating the title role in Mister Roberts (1948) — he returned to the screen in the 1950s with 12 Angry Men (1957), Sidney Lumet's courtroom drama, and Alfred Hitchcock's The Wrong Man (1956). In Sergio Leone's Once Upon a Time in the West (1968), he played memorably against type as the blue-eyed killer Frank. His last film, On Golden Pond (1981), co-starring Katharine Hepburn and his daughter Jane, won him the Best Actor Oscar months before his death from heart disease in Los Angeles on 12 August 1982.

Personal life and politics

Fonda served in the U.S. Navy during the Second World War, earning a Bronze Star. Politically a liberal Democrat, he was known for his quiet, laconic manner and strained relations, at times, with his children — a dynamic dramatized in On Golden Pond. He was married five times; his third wife, Susan Blanchard, was mother to Jane and Peter's younger adopted sibling. Fonda's career and persona — the decent, plainspoken American standing up for conscience — made him an enduring figure of American cinema; the American Film Institute ranked him sixth on its list of the greatest male screen legends.
