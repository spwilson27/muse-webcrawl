Zhang Liao (張遼)

Born: 169, Mayi, Yanmen (present-day Shuozhou, Shanxi)

Died: 222, Jiangdu

Courtesy name: Wenyuan (文遠)

Allegiance: Cao Cao / Cao Wei

Famous battle: Battle of Xiaoyao Ford (215)

Zhang Liao (169–222), courtesy name Wenyuan, was a Chinese military general serving under the warlord Cao Cao and later the state of Cao Wei during the late Eastern Han dynasty and early Three Kingdoms period. He is ranked among the Five Elite Generals of Wei.

Early career

Born in Yanmen Commandery, Zhang Liao served successively under Ding Yuan, the general He Jin, Dong Zhuo, and Lü Bu, rising to command cavalry. After Cao Cao defeated Lü Bu at the Battle of Xiapi in 198, Zhang Liao surrendered and was taken into Cao Cao's service — tradition credits Guan Yu with persuading him to submit honourably. He distinguished himself in the campaigns against Yuan Shao's heirs and the Wuhuan at White Wolf Mountain (207), where he led the vanguard.

Battle of Xiaoyao Ford

Zhang Liao's fame rests on his defence of Hefei in 215. With about 7,000 men, he faced Sun Quan's army of perhaps 100,000. Following Cao Cao's written instructions, Zhang Liao led a picked force of 800 volunteers in a dawn charge that smashed into Sun Quan's camp and threw the Wu army into confusion; he then held Hefei through a siege of more than ten days until Sun Quan withdrew. His name became so feared that, according to tradition, children in Jiangdong would stop crying when told "Zhang Liao is coming." He died of illness in 222 while on campaign against Wu and was posthumously honoured by Emperor Cao Pi.
