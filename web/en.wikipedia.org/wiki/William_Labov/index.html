William Labov

William Labov (born December 4, 1927) is an American linguist widely regarded as the founder of variationist sociolinguistics.

Born: December 4, 1927, Rutherford, New Jersey
Field: Sociolinguistics
Institutions: Columbia University, University of Pennsylvania

Work

His Columbia PhD and classic studies — Martha's Vineyard (1963) and New York City department stores (1966) — showed language variation is orderly and socially patterned. At the University of Pennsylvania he directed the Linguistic Data Consortium work and the Atlas of North American English.

Major contributions

Labov developed the sociolinguistic variable, quantitative methods, narrative analysis, and principles of language change in Principles of Linguistic Change (1994–2010). His work on African American Vernacular English influenced education policy and the 1979 Ann Arbor decision. See Sociolinguistics and Language change.
