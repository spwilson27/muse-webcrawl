Art museum

An art museum or art gallery is a building or space for the display of art, usually from the museum's own collection. It might be in public or private ownership and may be accessible to all or have restrictions on access.

History

Though primarily concerned with visual art, art museums are often used as a venue for other cultural exchanges and artistic activities. The first public art museum was arguably Basel's Kunstmuseum (1661 collection opened). The Louvre opened as a museum in 1793. The 19th and 20th centuries saw foundations such as the Metropolitan Museum of Art (1870), Tate, and MoMA (1929).

Functions

Museums collect, preserve, research and exhibit works, run education programmes, and mount temporary exhibitions. Debates concern provenance, repatriation of looted or colonial-era objects, deaccessioning and inclusivity.

Major museums

By visitor numbers and collection size, leading institutions include the Louvre in Paris, the Vatican Museums, the British Museum (multi-disciplinary), the Met in New York, and the National Gallery in London.
