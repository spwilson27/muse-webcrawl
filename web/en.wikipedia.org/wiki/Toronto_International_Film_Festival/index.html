The Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF) is one of the most publicly attended film festivals in the world, held each September in Toronto, Canada. Founded in 1976 as the "Festival of Festivals," it has grown into a major launchpad for the autumn awards season.

Character

Unlike Cannes or Venice, TIFF is non-competitive in its main slate; its top honour is the audience-voted People's Choice Award, whose winners — including Chariots of Fire, American Beauty, Slumdog Millionaire, and 12 Years a Slave — have frequently gone on to Academy Award success.

Venues and organization

Since 2010 the festival has been headquartered at the TIFF Bell Lightbox in the city's entertainment district, with screenings also at venues such as Roy Thomson Hall and the Princess of Wales Theatre. Annual public attendance has been estimated in the hundreds of thousands.
