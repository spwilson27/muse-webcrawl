Constantine I (Greek: Κωνσταντῖνος Αʹ; 2 August 1868 – 11 January 1923) was King of Greece from 1913 to 1917 and from 1920 to 1922. As commander-in-chief of the Hellenic Army during the Balkan Wars of 1912–13, he earned immense popularity as the victorious "King-Soldier".

Early life and Balkan Wars

The eldest son of King George I, Constantine was born in Athens and educated in Germany. He commanded Greek forces in the disastrous Greco-Turkish War of 1897 but redeemed his reputation in the Balkan Wars, leading the Army of Thessaly in the capture of Salonica and the Army of Epirus in the capture of Ioannina. He ascended the throne after his father's assassination in March 1913.

First World War and the National Schism

Constantine's insistence on Greek neutrality in the First World War — he was married to Sophia of Prussia, sister of Kaiser Wilhelm II — brought him into bitter conflict with Prime Minister Eleftherios Venizelos, who favoured joining the Entente. The resulting "National Schism" split Greece in two; under Allied pressure Constantine abdicated in June 1917 in favour of his second son, Alexander.

Restoration and Asia Minor Catastrophe

After Alexander's death and a plebiscite, Constantine returned to the throne in December 1920. He resumed command in Asia Minor in 1921 during the Greco-Turkish War, but the Greek offensive collapsed in the Asia Minor Catastrophe of 1922. Following a military revolt, Constantine abdicated a second time in September 1922 and went into exile in Sicily, where he died in Palermo in 1923.

Legacy

Constantine remains a polarising figure: remembered as the triumphant commander of 1912–13 and, by his detractors, as the king whose neutrality policy and return in 1920 led Greece to disaster.
