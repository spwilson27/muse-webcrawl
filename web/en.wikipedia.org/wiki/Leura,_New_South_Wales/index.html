Leura is a village in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, about 100 kilometres west of Sydney and adjacent to the larger town of Katoomba. Situated at an elevation of about 985 metres on the Great Dividing Range, it is known for its cool-climate gardens, heritage streetscapes, and views into the Jamison Valley and the Grose Valley gorges. Its population is roughly 4,600.

History

The Leura area was home to the Gundungurra and Darug peoples for thousands of years. European subdivision began in the late 19th century after the railway crossed the mountains, and the village developed as a retreat for wealthy Sydney residents seeking the clean mountain air. The name "Leura" is believed to derive from an Aboriginal word, though its precise origin is debated.

Attractions

Leura's main street, Leura Mall, is lined with cafés, galleries, and gardens, and is noted for its avenue of cherry trees that blossom in spring. The village hosts the annual Leura Garden Festival, when residents open their cool-climate gardens — many designed in the English style with exotic imports suited to the altitude — to the public. Nearby attractions include the Everglades House and Gardens, a National Trust property with grounds designed by Paul Sorensen, the cliff-edge lookouts at Sublime Point and Gordon Falls, and the Leuralla toy and railway museum. Leura Cascades provides access to walking tracks into the valley below. The village is a stop on the Blue Mountains railway line and a gateway to the Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area.
