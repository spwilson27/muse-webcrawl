Eugene Aserinsky

Eugene Aserinsky (May 6, 1921 – July 22, 1998) was an American physiologist who, as a graduate student at the University of Chicago, co-discovered rapid eye movement (REM) sleep — one of the landmark discoveries in modern sleep science. His work with his advisor Nathaniel Kleitman opened the scientific study of sleep and dreaming.

Discovery of REM sleep

In 1952–53, Aserinsky was assigned to observe the eyelids of sleeping infants, and then of adults, for slow rolling eye movements. While monitoring a sleeping subject — his eight-year-old son Armond was among his first subjects — he noticed periods in which the subject's eyes moved rapidly beneath closed lids while the electroencephalogram showed a pattern resembling wakefulness. With Kleitman he reported these "rapid eye movements" in a brief 1953 paper in Science. Follow-up work with William C. Dement showed that subjects awakened from REM sleep usually reported vivid dreams, linking the physiological state to dreaming.

Aserinsky struggled financially throughout the research, working with unreliable equipment that he reportedly repaired himself, and the discovery initially brought him little recognition; he left the University of Chicago, worked in several posts — including research on electric fish and a position with the Bureau of Commercial Fisheries in Seattle — and later taught physiology at Jefferson Medical College in Philadelphia. Only in later decades, as sleep medicine grew into a major field, was his foundational role fully acknowledged. He died in a car accident in 1998, reportedly after falling asleep at the wheel.
