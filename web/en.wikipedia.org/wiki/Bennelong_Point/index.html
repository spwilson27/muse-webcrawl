Bennelong Point is a headland on the southern shore of Sydney Harbour in Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. It is best known as the site of the Sydney Opera House, one of the most recognisable buildings in the world. The point lies between Sydney Cove to the west and Farm Cove to the east, at the northern end of the Sydney central business district, adjacent to Circular Quay and The Rocks.

History

The point is named after Bennelong (c. 1764–1813), a senior man of the Eora people who served as an interlocutor between the Aboriginal people and the British colonists after the arrival of the First Fleet in 1788. Governor Arthur Phillip had a small hut built for Bennelong on the eastern side of the point, where he lived for a time; the location consequently became known as Bennelong Point.

During the early colonial period the point was known to the local Gadigal people as Dubbagullee. A fort, Fort Macquarie, was constructed on the point in the early nineteenth century. In 1901 the site became home to the Fort Macquarie Tram Depot, a large tram shed that served Sydney's tram network. The depot was demolished in 1958 to make way for the Sydney Opera House, designed by Danish architect Jørn Utzon and completed under an Australian design team led by Peter Hall. The Opera House was officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 20 October 1973 and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007.

Geography and transport

Bennelong Point is connected to the city by Macquarie Street and is a short walk from Circular Quay railway station and ferry wharf. A car park, the Opera House car park, is built underground beneath the point's forecourt approach. The Royal Botanic Garden lies to the east across Farm Cove.
