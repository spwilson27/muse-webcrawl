Osvaldo Bayer

Osvaldo Bayer (18 February 1927 – 24 December 2018) was an Argentine writer, historian and journalist, and a lifelong adherent of anarchist ideas. He is best known for La Patagonia rebelde ("Rebellious Patagonia", published 1972–1974), a four-volume history of the Patagonia Uprising of 1920–1922, in which striking rural workers in Santa Cruz were suppressed by the army with the execution of hundreds of labourers. The work was banned under the military government and formed the basis of the award-winning 1974 film La Patagonia rebelde.

Life

Born in Santa Fe to German immigrant parents, Bayer studied history and worked as a journalist and trade unionist. His reporting on the expropriator Severino Di Giovanni and on the massacres of indigenous peoples and workers made him a prominent, and at times persecuted, voice on the Argentine left. During the 1976–1983 dictatorship he lived in exile in West Germany, returning afterwards to Buenos Aires, where he continued writing a long-running column and defending human rights causes until his death aged 91.
