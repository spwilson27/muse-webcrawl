Colonial Williamsburg

Location: Williamsburg, Virginia
Founded/restored: Restoration began 1920s
Operator: Colonial Williamsburg Foundation

Colonial Williamsburg is a living-history museum and private foundation in Williamsburg, Virginia, presenting a restored 301-acre historic district of the city as it was in the 18th century, when Williamsburg was the capital of the Colony of Virginia (1699–1780).

Restoration

The restoration was led from the 1920s by Reverend W. A. R. Goodwin and funded by philanthropist John D. Rockefeller Jr. Hundreds of later buildings were removed, colonial-era buildings restored, and many others reconstructed on original foundations, including the Capitol and the Governor's Palace. Duke of Gloucester Street is its main thoroughfare.

Interpretation

Costumed interpreters portray colonial tradespeople, politicians, enslaved people and townsfolk; historic trades such as blacksmithing and printing are demonstrated. Over recent decades interpretation has expanded to address slavery and the lives of the enslaved population, who made up about half of 18th-century Williamsburg's residents.
