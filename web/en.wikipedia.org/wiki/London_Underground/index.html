London Underground

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Tube train at a station platform.

The London Underground (commonly called the Tube) is a rapid transit system serving Greater London. It opened in 1863 as the Metropolitan Railway, the world\'s first underground railway, and today has 272 stations and about 402 km (250 miles) of track across 11 lines. It is operated by Transport for London (TfL) and is an icon of the city, from Harry Beck\'s diagrammatic map to the roundel logo.

History

Steam-hauled trains first ran between Paddington and Farringdon in 1863. The Circle line followed, then deep-level electric \'tube\' lines such as the Central London Railway (1900) and the Northern line. The lines were unified under the London Passenger Transport Board in 1933.

Network

Lines include the Metropolitan, Circle, District, Hammersmith & City, Northern, Piccadilly, Victoria, Central, Bakerloo, Jubilee and Waterloo & City. Much of the network is actually above ground in the suburbs; only about 45% is in tunnel. The Elizabeth line (Crossrail), opened in 2022, is a separate TfL railway that interchanges with the Tube.

Operation

Trains are driven manually on most lines, with automatic operation on the Victoria, Jubilee, Northern and Central lines in part. Fares use Oyster and contactless payment. The network carries up to about five million passenger journeys on its busiest days.
