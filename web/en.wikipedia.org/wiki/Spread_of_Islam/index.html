Spread of Islam

The spread of Islam began in the 7th century CE in the Arabian Peninsula with the preaching of the prophet Muhammad (c. 570–632) in Mecca and Medina. Within a century of his death, Muslim rule extended from the Iberian Peninsula in the west to Central Asia and the Indus Valley in the east. In subsequent centuries, Islam spread further — to sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia, and beyond — through trade, scholarship, Sufi missionary activity, and intermarriage as much as through conquest. Today Islam is the world's second-largest religion, with roughly two billion adherents.

Early conquests

Under the Rashidun Caliphate (632–661), Arab armies defeated the Byzantine and Sasanian empires, bringing Syria, Egypt, Persia, and Iraq under Muslim rule. The Umayyad Caliphate (661–750) expanded the realm to North Africa, Spain (from 711), and Sindh and Transoxiana (from around 705–715). Conquered peoples were not generally compelled to convert; conversion proceeded gradually over centuries, driven by social, economic, and institutional factors, while Christians, Jews, and others retained protected (dhimmi) status in return for a tax.

Trade and missionary expansion

From the 8th century onward, Muslim merchants and Sufi orders carried Islam along the trans-Saharan trade routes into West Africa, down the East African coast, and across the Indian Ocean to India, the Malay world, and Indonesia — today the most populous Muslim-majority country — as well as to parts of China. Under the Abbasids, Delhi Sultanate, and later the Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal empires, Islamic institutions, law, and learning were consolidated across vast regions.

Later history

Between the 16th and 19th centuries, Islam continued to expand in Africa and Asia even as European colonialism reshaped Muslim societies. In the modern era, migration has established significant Muslim communities in Europe and the Americas, and Islam is among the fastest-growing religions globally, primarily due to demographic factors.
