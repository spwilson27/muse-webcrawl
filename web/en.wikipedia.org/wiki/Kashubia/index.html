Kashubia

Kashubia (Kashubian: Kaszëbë; Polish: Kaszuby) is a cultural and linguistic region in north-central Poland, in the eastern part of Pomerania west of Gdańsk, inhabited by the Kashubian people, a West Slavic ethnic group whose Kashubian language is recognised in Poland as a regional language.

Geography

The region covers a rolling, lake-dotted landscape of forests and farmland, often called "Kashubian Switzerland" around Kartuzy and Wieżyca, the highest point in northern Poland. Major towns include Kartuzy, Kościerzyna, Bytów, Wejherowo, and Puck; Gdańsk, at the region's edge, is its major city and historic port.

Culture and language

Kashubian culture is distinguished by its language, distinctive multi-colour embroidery with characteristic floral motifs, ceramics, horn snuffboxes, and a strong Catholic tradition. The Kashubian-Pomeranian Association promotes the region's heritage, and Kashubian is taught in some schools and used in local media and signage. The Kashubian Unity Day is celebrated on 19 March.

History

Historically contested between Polish and German states, Kashubia belonged for long periods to the Teutonic Order, Royal Prussia within the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, and later Prussia and the German Empire, before returning to Poland after the First World War. Kashubians endured Germanisation pressures in the 19th century and Nazi persecution during the Second World War, including the Piaśnica massacres.
