Furuichi Kofun Group

The Furuichi Kofun Group (古市古墳群, Furuichi-kofungun) is a cluster of ancient burial mounds (kofun) in the cities of Habikino and Fujiidera in Osaka Prefecture, Japan. Together with the Mozu Kofun Group in Sakai, it forms the Mozu–Furuichi Kofun Group, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in July 2019 — the first World Heritage site in Osaka Prefecture.

Overview

The group spreads over a plateau about 2.5 km east–west and 4 km north–south, and originally comprised some 123 mounds. Twenty-six survive as component parts of the World Heritage listing, including some of the largest keyhole-shaped tombs (zenpō-kōen-fun) ever built. The mounds are attributed to the imperial line and its court: the largest, the Kondagobyōyama Kofun, is traditionally regarded as the tomb of Emperor Ōjin, and the Nakatsuyama Kofun as that of his empress.

Major mounds

The Kondagobyōyama Kofun (誉田御廟山古墳), at roughly 425 metres long, is the second-largest kofun in Japan after the Daisenryō Kofun (tomb of Emperor Nintoku) in the Mozu group. Other major mounds include the Okamisanzai Kofun, the Ichinoyama Kofun, and the Tsudōshiroyama Kofun, whose moat yielded celebrated grave goods. Because most of the great mounds are administered as imperial tombs by the Imperial Household Agency, excavation is restricted and much knowledge comes from moat excavations and finds from smaller mounds, including rich assemblages of iron armour, weapons, horse trappings, and Sue ware pottery.

World Heritage listing

The Mozu–Furuichi Kofun Group was inscribed under UNESCO criteria (iii) and (iv) in 2019 as an outstanding testimony to the kofun culture of ancient Japan and its burial traditions, comprising 49 component mounds selected from the two clusters.
