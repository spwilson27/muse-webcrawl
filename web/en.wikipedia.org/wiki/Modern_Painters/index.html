Modern Painters

AuthorJohn Ruskin
Published1843–1860 (five volumes)
SubjectArt criticism

Modern Painters is a five-volume work of art criticism by the British writer and artist John Ruskin, published between 1843 and 1860. It began as a defence of the landscape paintings of J. M. W. Turner against critics who had disparaged them, written when Ruskin was only 24 and published anonymously as the work of "a Graduate of Oxford".

Argument and scope

Ruskin argued that great art must be founded on truth to nature — close observation of natural facts such as the forms of clouds, water, trees and mountains — and that Turner surpassed the Old Masters in landscape precisely because of this fidelity. Across the later volumes the work expanded into a sweeping theory of art, beauty and landscape, discussing the "truths" of space, light, colour and composition, and turning to moral and social critique; the final volume (1860) reached far beyond its original brief.

Influence

Modern Painters established Ruskin as the leading art critic of Victorian Britain, reshaped taste toward Turner and the Pre-Raphaelites, whom he later championed, and influenced generations of artists, designers and reformers.
