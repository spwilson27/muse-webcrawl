"Ah! vous dirai-je maman"

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The melody of "Ah! vous dirai-je maman"

"Ah! vous dirai-je maman" (French, "Oh! Shall I tell you, Mama") is a popular French folk song and nursery melody, first published in the eighteenth century. Its simple tune is one of the most widely recognized melodies in the world: it is used, with different lyrics, for the English-language children's songs "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star", "Baa, Baa, Black Sheep", and the "Alphabet song".

The song

The original French lyrics are not a nursery rhyme but a light pastoral complaint: a young shepherdess confesses to her mother that she has been distracted from her flock by a young man's attentions and gifts of sweets. The melody appeared in print in 1761, and the lyrics were published a few years later; several verses exist in different versions.

Musical adaptations

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart composed his Twelve Variations on "Ah vous dirai-je maman", K. 265/300e (c. 1781–82), which made the tune famous in classical music. Many other composers have used it, including Adolphe Adam (in his opera Le toréador), Camille Saint-Saëns (in The Carnival of the Animals, where it underpins "Fossils"), Franz Liszt, and Ernő Dohnányi, who wrote his Variations on a Nursery Song on the tune.

Derivative songs

The English poem "The Star" by Jane Taylor (1806), sung to this melody, became "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star". The same tune carries "Baa, Baa, Black Sheep" and the ABC alphabet song; German children sing it as "Morgen kommt der Weihnachtsmann".
