The Old Catholic Church is a group of Christian churches that originated with groups that split from the Roman Catholic Church over certain doctrines, most importantly papal infallibility, defined at the First Vatican Council in 1870. The term Old Catholic was adopted by those who held to the faith and order of the early church without the later Roman doctrines.

History

The Dutch Church of Utrecht had been effectively independent since 1723, when it elected its own archbishop without papal approval. After 1870, Catholics in Germany, Austria and Switzerland who rejected papal infallibility — including the theologian Ignaz von Döllinger — organised separate parishes and received bishops consecrated by Utrecht. In 1889 the bishops issued the Declaration of Utrecht, founding the Union of Utrecht of Old Catholic Churches.

Beliefs and practice

Old Catholics retain the seven sacraments, apostolic succession, and the creeds of the ecumenical councils, while rejecting papal infallibility and universal jurisdiction. Clerical celibacy is optional, the liturgy is celebrated in the vernacular, and member churches have progressively approved the ordination of women and blessings of same-sex unions. Since the Bonn Agreement of 1931 the Union of Utrecht has been in full communion with the Anglican Communion.
