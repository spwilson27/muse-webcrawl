The Jeep Cherokee is a line of sport utility vehicles manufactured by Jeep over five generations. The nameplate was introduced in 1974 as a full-size SUV based on the Wagoneer, and is best known for the compact XJ generation (1984–2001), whose lightweight unibody construction helped define the modern SUV segment and influenced virtually every competitor that followed.

The XJ (1984–2001)

Developed by American Motors with input from Renault, the XJ Cherokee was revolutionary: a compact, four-door wagon with unitised body construction that was roughly 450 kg lighter than the full-size model it effectively replaced. Offered with four-wheel drive systems including the part-time Command-Trac and full-time Selec-Trac, and powered most famously by the 4.0-litre AMC inline-six, the XJ sold in the millions worldwide and remained in production in China, as the Jeep 2500, well into the 2000s. Automobile journalists have repeatedly named it one of the most important SUV designs of all time.

Discontinuation

Production of the KL Cherokee ended in 2023 at the Belvidere, Illinois plant, leaving the nameplate dormant. Jeep has indicated the Cherokee name may return on a future model.
