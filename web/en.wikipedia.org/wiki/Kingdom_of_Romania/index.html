The Kingdom of Romania (Romanian: Regatul României) was a constitutional monarchy that existed from 1881, when the Principality of Romania was proclaimed a kingdom under Carol I, until 1947, when King Michael I was forced to abdicate and the Romanian People's Republic was proclaimed under communist rule.

Early kingdom

Romania had gained full independence from the Ottoman Empire in the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878, recognised by the Treaty of Berlin. Carol I, a prince of the Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen line who had ruled as Domnitor since 1866, was crowned king in 1881. Under Carol and his successor Ferdinand I, Romania industrialised and fought in the Second Balkan War (1913), gaining Southern Dobruja.

Greater Romania

After initially remaining neutral in the First World War, Romania joined the Allies in 1916, suffered occupation, but emerged in 1918–1920 with its territory more than doubled: Transylvania, Bukovina, and Bessarabia united with the "Old Kingdom" to form Greater Romania. The interwar kingdom, ruled by Ferdinand and then Carol II, was a multi-ethnic state of some 18 million people; Carol II established a royal dictatorship in 1938.

Second World War and abolition

In 1940 Romania lost Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina, Northern Transylvania, and Southern Dobruja under Axis and Soviet pressure. Carol II abdicated; his son Michael I became king while Marshal Ion Antonescu ruled as dictator and took Romania into the war against the Soviet Union alongside Germany. On 23 August 1944 King Michael's coup removed Antonescu and switched Romania to the Allied side. Despite this, Soviet influence grew; in 1945 a communist-dominated government was imposed, and on 30 December 1947 Michael was forced to abdicate. Romania became a people's republic, ending 66 years of monarchy.
