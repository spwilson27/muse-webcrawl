Pickwick Lake is a reservoir in the Tennessee River in the U.S. states of Tennessee, Alabama and Mississippi. It was created by the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) with the completion of Pickwick Landing Dam in 1938, and extends about 53 miles (85 km) from Pickwick Landing Dam upstream to Wilson Dam at Florence, Alabama.

Geography and use

The lake has roughly 490 miles (790 km) of shoreline and a surface area of about 43,100 acres at full pool. It forms part of the Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway system via the divide cut near Pickwick, allowing barge traffic between the Tennessee River and the Gulf of Mexico. The lake lies adjacent to the Shiloh National Military Park, site of the 1862 Battle of Shiloh, and Pickwick Landing State Park in Tennessee.

Recreation and fishing

Pickwick Lake is nationally known for smallmouth bass fishing and also supports largemouth bass, crappie, catfish and striped bass. It hosts major bass fishing tournaments and is a popular boating and water-sports destination, with marinas and resorts along its shores near Counce, Tennessee, and Florence, Alabama.
