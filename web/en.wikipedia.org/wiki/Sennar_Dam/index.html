The Sennar Dam is an irrigation dam on the Blue Nile near the town of Sennar in east-central Sudan. Completed in 1925 under the Anglo-Egyptian condominium government, it was built to provide gravity irrigation water to the Gezira Scheme, one of the largest irrigation projects in the world, which transformed the plain between the Blue and White Niles into a major cotton-producing region.

Construction and operation

The masonry dam is about 3 kilometres long and impounds a reservoir used to regulate the Blue Nile's strongly seasonal flow, filling during the flood season and releasing water through main canals to the Gezira and Managil extensions. A hydroelectric station at the dam generates a modest amount of power (about 15 MW). The dam's design was overseen by Scottish engineer Murdoch MacDonald, and construction was carried out by the Sudan Construction Company using large numbers of Sudanese labourers.

Issues

Siltation of the reservoir has progressively reduced the dam's storage capacity — a recurring problem for Nile dams — requiring repeated dredging and raising schemes. The dam remains a key piece of Sudanese agricultural infrastructure, supplying water to roughly two million feddans of irrigated land.
