Sakai (Japanese: 堺市, Sakai-shi) is a city in Osaka Prefecture, Japan, on Osaka Bay south of the city of Osaka. It is the second-largest city in the prefecture and one of Japan's designated cities. Historically one of Japan's greatest ports and trading centres, Sakai remains renowned for its handcrafted knives and blades.

History

Sakai grew as a port during the medieval period and by the Sengoku era had become a wealthy, self-governing merchant city — sometimes called the "Venice of the East" — protected by moats and ruled by a council of merchants (egōshū). It traded with Ming China and, after 1543, with Portuguese merchants who introduced firearms; Sakai became a major centre of gunsmithing, as well as of incense, textiles and metalwork. Tea master Sen no Rikyū, a Sakai native, shaped the Japanese tea ceremony under the patronage of Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Oda Nobunaga ended the city's autonomy in 1569. The city was heavily damaged by air raids in 1945 and rebuilt as an industrial and residential hub of the Osaka metropolitan area.

Kofun tombs

Sakai contains part of the Mozu–Furuichi Kofun Group, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019. It includes the Daisenryō Kofun, the tomb traditionally attributed to Emperor Nintoku — one of the largest tombs in the world by area, a keyhole-shaped mound about 486 metres long surrounded by moats.

Industry

Sakai's knife-making tradition, begun with tobacco knives in the Edo period and encouraged by the shogunate's monopoly, continues today: hand-forged Sakai knives are prized by professional chefs in Japan and abroad. The city is also associated with bicycles (Shimano was founded in Sakai in 1921) and with modern coastal industrial zones.
