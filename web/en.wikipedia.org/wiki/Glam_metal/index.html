Glam metal

Glam metal
Other namesHair metal, pop metal
Stylistic originsHeavy metal, hard rock, glam rock, pop
Cultural originsLate 1970s – early 1980s, Los Angeles, United States
Peak years1983–1992
Typical instrumentsElectric guitar, bass, drums, vocals, keyboards

Glam metal is a subgenre of heavy metal that features pop-influenced hooks and guitar riffs, upbeat rock anthems, and slow power ballads. It borrows heavily from the fashion and image of 1970s glam rock, including teased hair, makeup, and flamboyant clothing. It is also often referred to as hair metal, a term coined by critics, and pop metal.

History

Glam metal emerged on the Los Angeles Sunset Strip club scene in the early 1980s, led by bands such as Mötley Crüe, Quiet Riot, Ratt and Dokken. Quiet Riot's Metal Health (1983) was the first heavy metal album to top the Billboard 200. A second wave including Poison, Cinderella, Warrant and Skid Row achieved massive success in the late 1980s, with Def Leppard's Hysteria and Bon Jovi's Slippery When Wet selling tens of millions of copies.

Characteristics

Musically, glam metal features virtuosic guitar solos, catchy choruses designed for arena sing-alongs, and a mix of hard-rocking anthems and sentimental power ballads. The visual image — big teased hair, spandex, leather, and makeup — was integral to the genre's MTV-driven success.

Decline and revival

The genre collapsed commercially in the early 1990s with the rise of grunge and alternative rock. Since the 2000s, nostalgia tours, festivals, and new bands such as Steel Panther have kept glam metal alive, and several classic acts returned to large-scale touring.
