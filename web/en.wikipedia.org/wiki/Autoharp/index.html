Autoharp

Classification: Board zither
Inventor: Charles F. Zimmermann (attributed)
Related: Zither, chorded zither

The autoharp or chord zither is a stringed instrument in the zither family. It features a series of chord bars attached to dampers which, when pressed, mute all strings other than those forming the desired chord. The player strums the strings, typically with the right hand, while pressing chord bars with the left, producing full chordal accompaniment with relatively little technique.

History

The instrument was popularised in the United States in the late 19th century, notably through the Oscar Schmidt company. It became closely associated with American folk, country and Appalachian music. Performers such as Maybelle Carter of the Carter Family brought it wide recognition, playing melodies by plucking individual strings while using the chord bars.

Construction

A typical autoharp has 36 or 37 strings stretched across a flat wooden body, with 15 to 21 chord bars offering major, minor and seventh chords. Modern instruments may use fine tuners and felt dampers. Tuning follows a chromatic layout across roughly three octaves.

Playing technique

Players hold the instrument on the lap, on a table, or against the chest. Techniques include strumming, fingerpicking individual melody strings, and the "Carter scratch" style. Bryan Bowers became known for a virtuosic melodic style in the 20th century.
