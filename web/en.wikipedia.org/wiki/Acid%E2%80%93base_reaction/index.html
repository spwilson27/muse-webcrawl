Acid–base reaction

An acid–base reaction is a chemical reaction that occurs between an acid and a base. Several theoretical frameworks describe the mechanisms of such reactions and their applications, including the Arrhenius, Brønsted–Lowry, and Lewis definitions of acids and bases.

Theories

Under the Arrhenius definition, an acid increases the concentration of hydrogen ions in aqueous solution, while a base increases hydroxide ions. The Brønsted–Lowry theory generalizes this: an acid is a proton donor and a base a proton acceptor. The Lewis theory goes further, defining an acid as an electron-pair acceptor and a base as an electron-pair donor.

Neutralization

In a typical neutralization reaction, an acid and a base react to form water and a salt; for example, hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide produce sodium chloride and water. Acid–base titrations are a standard analytical technique for determining unknown concentrations, and pH indicators signal their endpoints.

Applications

Acid–base chemistry underlies buffer solutions, digestion, soil chemistry, industrial synthesis and much of biochemistry, where enzymes operate within narrow pH ranges.
