BRICS

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BRICS is an intergovernmental organisation originally comprising Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa — large emerging economies identified by Goldman Sachs economist Jim O'Neill's 2001 acronym "BRIC" (South Africa joined in 2010). Foreign ministers began meeting in 2006 and leaders' summits from 2009. In 2024 the grouping expanded, inviting and admitting new members including Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and the United Arab Emirates, with Indonesia joining as a full member in 2025 and a wider circle of "partner countries" — making BRICS a significant, if heterogeneous, pole in debates about global governance reform.

Institutions and aims

BRICS has created the New Development Bank (headquartered in Shanghai, lending since 2016) and a Contingent Reserve Arrangement, and its declarations call for greater emerging-economy voice in the IMF and World Bank, trade in local currencies, and a "multipolar" order. Members diverge sharply — democracies and authoritarian states, rivals India and China, energy exporters and importers — which shapes both the group's appeal (non-Western but not a formal alliance) and the scepticism of analysts about its coherence. Summits at Kazan (2024) and Rio de Janeiro (2025) addressed expansion, development finance and conflicts, with wording negotiated case by case.
