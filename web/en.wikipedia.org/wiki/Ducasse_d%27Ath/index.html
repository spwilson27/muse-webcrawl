Ducasse d'Ath

Ducasse d'Ath
LocationAth, Hainaut, Belgium
DateFourth weekend of August
RecognitionUNESCO Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage (2005)

The Ducasse d'Ath is an annual folk festival held in the town of Ath, in the province of Hainaut, Belgium, on the fourth weekend of August. Dating to the 15th century, it is famous for its procession of giants — large wicker-and-cloth figures carried through the streets — including the celebrated giant Goliath (Gouyasse).

The festival

Highlights include the wedding of Goliath on Saturday, when the giant is ceremonially married to Madame Goliath amid crowds at the town hall, and on Sunday the traditional combat between Goliath and the shepherd David on the steps of the church of Saint-Julien, in which David throws a ball through an opening in the giant's costume. Other giants include Samson, Ambiorix, Mademoiselle Victoire and the Cheval Bayard, a huge horse carried by many porters.

Recognition

In 2005 the Ducasse d'Ath was proclaimed by UNESCO as one of the Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, together with the giants and dragons of Belgium and France; it was inscribed on the Representative List in 2008.
