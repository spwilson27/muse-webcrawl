Public economics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Public economics (or the economics of the public sector) is the study of government policy through the lens of economic efficiency and equity. It analyses how taxation, government expenditure, and regulation affect the allocation of resources and the distribution of income. Public economics provides a framework for thinking about whether the government should intervene in the economy and, if so, in what way.

Scope

Historically, public economics was narrowly the study of public finance — taxation and public expenditure. The modern subject is broader, including:

  • Externalities — costs or benefits imposed on third parties, and corrective instruments such as Pigouvian taxes and subsidies.
  • Public goods — goods that are non-rival and non-excludable, such as national defence and street lighting, and the associated free-rider problem.
  • Social insurance and redistribution — pensions, unemployment insurance, and welfare programmes.
  • Tax analysis — issues of incidence, excess burden (deadweight loss), optimal taxation, and tax evasion and avoidance.
  • Public choice — how political processes determine policy outcomes.

First and second fundamental theorems

The subject is organised around the two fundamental theorems of welfare economics. Under ideal conditions a competitive market equilibrium is Pareto efficient (the first theorem); under further conditions any desired efficient allocation can be achieved by markets after suitable lump-sum redistribution (the second theorem). Departures from these conditions — market failures — provide the standard rationale for government intervention, while the practical impossibility of lump-sum taxes and the costs of government failure shape real policy design.

Key contributions

Richard Musgrave's The Theory of Public Finance (1959) organised the field around the allocation, distribution, and stabilisation branches of the public household. Later landmarks include James Mirrlees's theory of optimal income taxation and the development of mechanism design for public decision-making.
