Erwin Panofsky

Born: 30 March 1892, Hanover, Germany

Died: 14 March 1968, Princeton, New Jersey

Erwin Panofsky was a German-born Jewish art historian who emigrated to the United States in 1933 and became the leading exponent of iconology — the interpretation of artworks' meanings — and a central figure in the sociology and history of art.

Iconology

In Studies in Iconology (1939) and Meaning in the Visual Arts (1955), Panofsky distinguished three levels of analysis: pre-iconographical description, iconographical identification of subjects and stories, and iconological interpretation of a work's intrinsic meaning within its culture.

Works

His books include a celebrated study of Albrecht Dürer, Gothic Architecture and Scholasticism linking building and thought, Early Netherlandish Painting, and essays on perspective as a symbolic form. At Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study he shaped generations of art historians.
