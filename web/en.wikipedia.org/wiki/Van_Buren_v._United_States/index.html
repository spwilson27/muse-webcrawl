Van Buren v. United States

Van Buren v. United States, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), is a United States Supreme Court case interpreting the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA). The Court held that an individual "exceeds authorized access" under the CFAA only when he accesses information he is not entitled to obtain at all — not when he misuses information he is otherwise permitted to access.

Background

Nathan Van Buren, a Georgia police sergeant, accepted money from an FBI informant to run a license-plate search on a law-enforcement database he was authorised to use only for police business. He was convicted under the CFAA for exceeding authorised access. The Eleventh Circuit upheld the conviction, deepening a circuit split over whether improper purpose makes authorised access criminal.

Decision

Justice Amy Coney Barrett's majority opinion adopted a "gates-up-or-down" reading: the statute bars entering parts of a system that are off limits to the user, but does not criminalise improper use of information the user may access. The ruling narrowed the CFAA and was welcomed by security researchers and civil-liberties groups who had warned that the broader reading would criminalise commonplace computer activity such as violating website terms of service.
