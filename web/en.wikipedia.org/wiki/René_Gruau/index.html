René Gruau (born Renato Zavagli Ricciardelli delle Caminate; February 4, 1909 – March 31, 2004) was an Italian-born French fashion illustrator whose elegant, expressive drawings defined the image of postwar Parisian haute couture. His work is especially associated with the House of Dior.

Career

Gruau moved to Paris in the 1930s and produced illustrations for magazines such as Marie Claire, Femina, and L'Officiel. In 1947 he created the iconic imagery for Christian Dior's "New Look", including advertising for the Miss Dior perfume, and became the house's artistic collaborator for decades. He also designed posters for the Moulin Rouge and the Lido and created campaigns for Balmain, Balenciaga, Givenchy, and many others.

Style

His style combined minimal, bold line work, strong black-and-white contrasts, and accents of red, influenced partly by Japanese woodblock prints and by Toulouse-Lautrec. His drawings are held by museums including the Musée des Arts Décoratifs in Paris.
