Verb phrase

In linguistics, a verb phrase (VP) is a syntactic unit composed of a verb and its arguments and modifiers. The term is used differently in traditional grammar and in generative grammar.

Traditional usage

In traditional English grammar, the verb phrase is the part of the predicate consisting of the main verb together with any auxiliary verbs — for example, will have been finished in "The work will have been finished by Friday".

Generative usage

In phrase structure grammar, VP is a constituent headed by a verb that includes its complements — objects, predicative phrases and obligatory adverbials — but excludes the subject, which sits outside the VP under a higher node (TP/IP). Postulating a VP constituent is supported by tests such as VP-ellipsis ("Maria read the report, and Juan did too") and VP-fronting.

Verb phrase in other traditions

In dependency grammar the notion is less central, since the finite verb is the root of the whole clause. In some frameworks a distinction is drawn between the inner lexical VP and a functional vP ("little v") shell in the VP-shell/ Larsonian analysis.
