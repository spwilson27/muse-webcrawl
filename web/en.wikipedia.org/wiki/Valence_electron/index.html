Valence electron

In chemistry and physics, a valence electron is an electron in the outermost shell of an atom that can participate in the formation of chemical bonds. The number of valence electrons determines many of an element's chemical properties and its position in the periodic table.

Role in bonding

Atoms tend to gain, lose or share valence electrons so as to achieve a filled outer shell — commonly an octet — which underlies the formation of ionic and covalent bonds. Main-group elements in the same group of the periodic table have the same number of valence electrons and hence similar reactivity: the alkali metals have one, the halogens seven, and the noble gases a complete shell that makes them largely inert.

Electrical properties

In solids, valence electrons occupy the valence band. In metals they are delocalised and free to conduct electricity, while in semiconductors the small energy gap to the conduction band governs conductivity and doping behaviour.
