Top of the Pops

Top of the PopsFirst broadcast: 1 January 1964
Final regular episode: 30 July 2006
Network: BBC One

Top of the Pops (often abbreviated TOTP) was a British music chart television programme produced by the BBC. For over four decades it showcased the week's best-selling singles, performed in the studio by the artists or accompanied by dance troupes.

History

The first edition aired on New Year's Day 1964, hosted from a converted church in Manchester. Early presenters included Jimmy Savile, Alan Freeman, Pete Murray and David Jacobs. The show moved to London, adopted colour in 1969, and became a Thursday-night institution.

Format

Acts mimed (and later increasingly sang live) to their chart hits; the programme culminated in the number one single of the week. Dance troupes such as Pan's People and Legs & Co provided routines for songs whose artists could not appear.

End and afterlife

Falling ratings, attributed to fragmented music media, led the BBC to end the weekly show in 2006. Christmas specials have continued annually, and archive editions are regularly repeated.
