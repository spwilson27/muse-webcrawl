Umm Al Quwain

Umm Al Quwain (Arabic: أم القيوين) is the capital and largest city of the Emirate of Umm Al Quwain in the United Arab Emirates. The city sits on a peninsula on the Khor Al Bidiyah creek along the Persian Gulf coast, between Sharjah to the southwest and Ras Al Khaimah to the northeast.

History

The emirate is ruled by the Al Mualla dynasty, founded in the mid-18th century. The town grew around a fort established by Sheikh Rashid bin Majid Al Mualla, who moved the seat of rule from Al Sinniyah island to the mainland peninsula around 1820. As with other Trucial States, Umm Al Quwain signed maritime treaties with Britain in the 19th century, and it became a founding member of the United Arab Emirates in December 1971.

Geography and economy

The city is one of the least populous emirate capitals in the UAE. Its economy historically depended on fishing and the pearl trade; today it includes a free trade zone, the Ahmed Bin Rashid Port, aquaculture research, and attractions such as the Dreamland Aqua Park and the Al Dur island archaeological site, where a settlement dating to the third millennium BCE has been excavated. A large lagoon studded with mangrove islands separates the old town from newer developments.
