Haram

Haram (Arabic: حَرَام, ḥarām) is an Arabic term meaning "forbidden" or "prohibited" under Islamic law (sharia). It denotes acts, foods, drinks and behaviours that are sinful for Muslims to engage in, in contrast to halal, which is permissible. The term also relates to the sacred precincts known as haram, such as the sanctuary around Mecca.

Categories of action

In Islamic jurisprudence, human actions are commonly classified into five categories (al-ahkam al-khamsa): obligatory (fard), recommended (mustahabb), neutral (mubah), reprehensible (makruh), and forbidden (haram). Committing a haram act is considered a sin, with severity varying by the act.

Examples

Things widely classified as haram include consuming pork and alcohol, gambling, theft, murder, adultery, and charging or paying interest (riba). The classification derives from the Quran and the hadith, interpreted through the schools of Islamic jurisprudence (madhhab).
