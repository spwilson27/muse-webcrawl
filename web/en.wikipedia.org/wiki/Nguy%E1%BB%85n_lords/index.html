Nguyễn lords

The Nguyễn lords (Vietnamese: Chúa Nguyễn, 主阮) were the rulers of southern Vietnam (Đàng Trong, "Inner Region") from 1558 to 1777, formally loyal to the Later Lê emperors but in practice independent. Their domains rivalled those of the Trịnh lords of the north (Đàng Ngoài), with whom they fought the Trịnh–Nguyễn Civil War.

History

Nguyễn Hoàng, founder of the line, was sent to govern the southern frontier provinces of Thuận Hóa and Quảng Nam in 1558 and built a base at Huế (Phú Xuân). His successors fortified the border at the Gianh River and Linh Giang, repelling repeated Trịnh invasions during the war of 1627–1672, after which a long truce divided Vietnam.

The Nguyễn lords promoted the southward expansion (Nam tiến) into Cham and Khmer lands and developed the port of Hội An as an international trading hub. Their rule ended with the Tây Sơn rebellion: Phú Xuân fell in 1775 and the last Nguyễn lord was killed in 1777. Nguyễn Phúc Ánh, a surviving prince, later reunified Vietnam and founded the Nguyễn dynasty in 1802.
