The Rudolfinum is a neo-Renaissance building in Prague, on the bank of the Vltava at Jan Palach Square. Opened in 1885, it is one of the oldest concert halls in Europe and the home of the Czech Philharmonic.

History

Designed by architects Josef Zítek and Josef Schulz — who also designed the National Theatre — the building was named in honour of Rudolf, Crown Prince of Austria. Between 1919 and 1938, and again briefly after the Second World War, it served as the seat of the Czechoslovak parliament before reverting to the arts.

Venues

Its principal hall, the Dvořák Hall, is celebrated for its acoustics and named for Antonín Dvořák, who conducted the Czech Philharmonic's first concert there in 1896. The smaller Suk Hall hosts chamber music. The building also houses the Rudolfinum Gallery (Galerie Rudolfinum), a major venue for contemporary art exhibitions.
