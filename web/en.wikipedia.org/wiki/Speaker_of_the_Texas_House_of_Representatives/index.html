Speaker of the Texas House of Representatives

OfficeSpeaker of the Texas House of Representatives
SeatTexas State Capitol, Austin
TermTwo years, renewable; elected each session
SelectionElected by the members of the House

The speaker of the Texas House of Representatives is the presiding officer of the Texas House of Representatives, the lower house of the Texas Legislature. The speaker is chosen by a majority of the 150 House members at the beginning of each regular session and is one of the most powerful officials in Texas government, ranking with the governor and the lieutenant governor (who presides over the Texas Senate) in influence over legislation and the state budget.

Powers

The speaker presides over the House, maintains order, recognises members to speak, rules on points of order, and signs bills and resolutions. Crucially, the speaker appoints the chairs and members of all House committees (subject to seniority rules for some seats), refers bills to committee, and appoints members of conference committees — giving the office decisive control over which legislation advances. The speaker also serves as a member or appointee to powerful interim bodies such as the Legislative Budget Board, which drafts the state budget when the legislature is not in session.

Election and politics

Unlike the U.S. Speaker of the House, the Texas speaker is formally elected by the whole membership rather than chosen by a party caucus, and candidates traditionally assemble bipartisan pledges of support; in practice the majority party's candidate normally prevails. Because Texas legislators serve part-time biennial sessions of 140 days, a strong speaker's control of the calendar and committees is especially consequential. Notable long-serving speakers include Pete Laney (1993–2003), Tom Craddick (2003–2009), the longest-serving member in House history and its first Republican speaker since Reconstruction, and Joe Straus (2009–2019). Recent holders include Dennis Bonnen (2019–2021) and Dade Phelan (2021–2025).

Succession

The speaker pro tempore, appointed by the speaker, presides in the speaker's absence. The speaker is not in the line of succession to the governorship; that order runs from the lieutenant governor.
