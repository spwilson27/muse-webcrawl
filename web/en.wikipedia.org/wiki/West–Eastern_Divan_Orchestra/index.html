West–Eastern Divan Orchestra

The West–Eastern Divan Orchestra is a youth orchestra based in Seville, Spain, bringing together musicians from Arab countries and Israel. It was founded in 1999 by conductor Daniel Barenboim and the Palestinian scholar Edward Said. The orchestra's name derives from the West–östlicher Divan, a collection of poems by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe.

History

The orchestra grew out of a 1999 workshop in Weimar, Germany, where Barenboim and Said gathered young Arab and Israeli musicians to make music together and to engage in dialogue. The project settled in Seville with support from the Andalusian regional government. The orchestra has performed at major venues and festivals worldwide, including the BBC Proms, the Salzburg Festival and Carnegie Hall, and in 2005 it gave a celebrated concert in Ramallah.

Aims

The orchestra is not a political organisation, but it seeks to promote understanding between Israelis and Palestinians and to model a space where musicians listen to one another as equals. Its members, aged roughly 14 to 25, meet each summer for rehearsals, workshops and discussion before an international tour. The Barenboim-Said Akademie in Berlin grew out of the project.
