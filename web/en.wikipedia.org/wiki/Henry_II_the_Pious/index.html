Henry II the Pious (Polish: Henryk II Pobożny; c. 1196 – 9 April 1241) was Duke of Silesia and High Duke of Poland from 1238 until his death fighting the Mongols at the Battle of Legnica.

Reign

Son of Henry the Bearded, he co-ruled from 1226 and succeeded his father over a realm stretching over Silesia, Kraków and Greater Poland, continuing policies of German-law town settlement and administrative reform.

Mongol invasion and death

During the first Mongol invasion of Poland, Henry gathered Polish knights, military orders and allies at Legnica. On 9 April 1241 his army was destroyed and he was killed; tradition holds his body was identified by his wife Anne. The Mongols withdrew from Hungary the following year.

Legacy

His death ended the temporary reunification of Poland, deepening fragmentation for decades. He is remembered in Polish tradition as a Christian martyr-prince.
