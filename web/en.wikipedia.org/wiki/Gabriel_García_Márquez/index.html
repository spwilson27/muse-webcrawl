Gabriel García Márquez

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Gabriel García Márquez in later life: white guayabera, grey moustache, yellow butterflies hovering in the caption of fame]

Gabriel José de la Concordia García Márquez (6 March 1927 – 17 April 2014) was a Colombian novelist and journalist, known affectionately as Gabo throughout Latin America. Considered one of the most significant authors of the 20th century, particularly in the Spanish language, he was awarded the 1972 Neustadt International Prize for Literature and the 1982 Nobel Prize in Literature.

A journalist in Bogotá, Paris and Caracas before settling in Mexico City, García Márquez achieved fame with One Hundred Years of Solitude (1967), the defining novel of magical realism, written in eighteen months while his family fell into debt. Later works include The Autumn of the Patriarch (1975), Chronicle of a Death Foretold (1981) and Love in the Time of Cholera (1985). His political friendships, notably with Fidel Castro, drew controversy; he died in Mexico City in 2014.
