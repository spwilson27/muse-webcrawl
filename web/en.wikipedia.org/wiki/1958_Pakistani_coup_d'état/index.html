1958 Pakistani coup d'état

Date7–27 October 1958
LocationPakistan
Key figuresPresident Iskander Mirza; General Ayub Khan
ResultConstitution abrogated; martial law imposed; Ayub Khan becomes President

The 1958 Pakistani coup d'état was the first military coup in the history of Pakistan. On 7 October 1958, President Iskander Mirza abrogated the 1956 constitution, dissolved the national and provincial assemblies, and declared martial law, appointing Army Commander-in-Chief General Ayub Khan as Chief Martial Law Administrator. Less than three weeks later, on 27 October, Ayub Khan forced Mirza into exile and assumed the presidency himself.

Background

Pakistan's first decade after independence in 1947 was marked by chronic political instability: seven prime ministers in eleven years, factional intrigue between the bureaucracy, politicians, and the military, and disputes over the balance of power between East and West Pakistan. Mirza, a former civil servant and defence secretary, justified his intervention by declaring that the constitution was unworkable and that politicians had brought the country to the brink of ruin.

Course of events

The takeover was bloodless. Mirza initially expected to retain power with the army's support, but Ayub Khan moved swiftly: Mirza was arrested at his residence, flown to Quetta, and then exiled to London, where he died in 1969. Ayub consolidated power, later promulgating the 1962 constitution with a presidential system and the "Basic Democracies" scheme of indirect elections.

Legacy

The 1958 coup established the precedent of direct military intervention in Pakistani politics, followed by further periods of military rule under Ayub Khan (1958–69), Yahya Khan, Zia-ul-Haq, and Pervez Musharraf. It is widely seen as a turning point that entrenched the armed forces as the dominant institution of the Pakistani state.
