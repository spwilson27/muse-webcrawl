Aizawl

Aizawl spread across its ridge-top hills

Aizawl is the capital and largest city of Mizoram, a state in northeastern India. Perched on steep ridges at an average elevation of about 1,132 metres (3,714 feet) above sea level, the city is the political, cultural and commercial centre of the Mizo people. Its name is often said to derive from ai, a type of turmeric once abundant in the area, and zawl, meaning a flat land or field.

History

The settlement grew around Fort Aijal, established by the British in 1890 during their expeditions into the Lushai Hills. Aizawl developed as an administrative outpost and mission centre; Welsh Presbyterian missionaries arrived in the 1890s, and Christianity became the dominant faith, as it remains in Mizoram today. The city was heavily affected during the Mizo insurgency of 1966, when it was bombed by the Indian Air Force — the only instance of aerial attack on civilians in India. After the Mizoram Peace Accord of 1986, Aizawl became the capital of the new state of Mizoram in 1987.

Geography and climate

Aizawl lies in the northern part of Mizoram, west of the Tlawng river valley. The city experiences a humid subtropical climate with heavy monsoon rainfall between May and September and mild, dry winters. Because of its hilltop setting, landslides are a recurring hazard during the rainy season.

Culture and transport

The city is known for its strong church-centred community life, choral singing tradition and football culture; Aizawl FC made national headlines by winning the I-League in 2017. Lengpui Airport, about 32 km away, connects the city to Kolkata and other Indian cities, and National Highway 54 links Aizawl to Silchar in Assam.
