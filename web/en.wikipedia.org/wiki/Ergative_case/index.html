Ergative case

In linguistics, the ergative case is the grammatical case that identifies a nominal phrase as the subject of a transitive verb in ergative–absolutive languages. In such languages, the subject of an intransitive verb and the object of a transitive verb share the absolutive case, while the transitive subject receives the ergative case — a pattern contrasting with the nominative–accusative alignment of languages such as English.

Distribution

Ergative case marking is found in Basque, Georgian and other Caucasian languages, many Australian Aboriginal languages such as Dyirbal, Mayan languages, Eskimo–Aleut languages, and a number of Indo-Iranian languages including Kurdish and (in perfective constructions) Hindi–Urdu. Many languages show split ergativity, in which ergative marking appears only in certain tenses, aspects, or pronoun classes.

Marking

The ergative marker takes many forms: in Basque it is the suffix -(e)k, while in Australian languages suffixes such as Dyirbal -ŋgu are typical. Some languages mark ergativity on the verb rather than (or in addition to) the noun phrase.
