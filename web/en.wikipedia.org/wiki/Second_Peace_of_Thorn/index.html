The Second Peace of Thorn (Polish: Drugi pokój toruński; German: Zweiter Friede von Thorn) was a peace treaty signed on 19 October 1466 in Thorn (Toruń) between the Kingdom of Poland and the State of the Teutonic Order, ending the Thirteen Years' War. It is distinguished from the First Peace of Thorn of 1411, which had followed the Battle of Grunwald.

Background

In 1454 the Prussian Confederation — an alliance of Prussian cities and nobility resisting the Teutonic Order's taxation — renounced allegiance to the Order and sought incorporation into Poland under King Casimir IV Jagiellon. The ensuing Thirteen Years' War saw Polish and Confederate forces gradually wear down the Order, whose finances collapsed; the decisive Polish victory at the Battle of Świecino (1462) and the naval victory on the Vistula Lagoon (1463) left the Order unable to continue, while its mercenaries sold Marienburg Castle to Poland in 1457.

Terms

Under the treaty, the Order ceded western Prussia to Poland: Pomerelia (Gdańsk Pomerania), the Chełmno Land, Marienburg, Elbing (Elbląg), and the Prince-Bishopric of Warmia. This territory became the Polish province of Royal Prussia, giving Poland durable access to the Baltic Sea. The Order retained eastern Prussia (later Ducal Prussia) but its Grand Master became a vassal obliged to swear fealty to the Polish king and provide military aid; the Order's capital moved from Marienburg to Königsberg. Papal and imperial confirmation was refused by Rome and the Empire, but the settlement stood.

Significance

The treaty permanently weakened the Teutonic Order, restored Polish access to the sea lost in 1308, and reshaped the political map of the southern Baltic for three centuries. In 1525 the Order's last Grand Master in Prussia, Albert of Hohenzollern, secularized the remaining territory as the Duchy of Prussia under Polish suzerainty.
