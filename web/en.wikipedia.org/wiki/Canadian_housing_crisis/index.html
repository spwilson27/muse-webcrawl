Canadian housing crisis

The Canadian housing crisis refers to a persistent shortage of affordable housing and steep increases in house prices and rents in Canada, particularly in large metropolitan areas such as Toronto and Vancouver. By the early 2020s Canada had among the highest house-price-to-income ratios in the developed world, and housing affordability became a dominant issue in federal and provincial politics.

Causes

Economists attribute the crisis principally to a long-running shortfall in housing construction relative to population growth. Canada's housing completions per capita lagged peer countries for decades, while population growth accelerated through immigration in the 2010s and 2020s. Restrictive zoning that reserved most urban residential land for detached houses, lengthy approval processes, development charges, and construction-labour shortages all constrained supply.

Demand-side factors included a long period of low interest rates following the 2008 financial crisis, tax treatment favouring owner-occupied housing, and purchases by investors, who accounted for a growing share of sales in some markets.

Consequences

Average house prices roughly tripled in many cities between 2005 and the 2022 peak, and rents rose sharply as would-be buyers remained in the rental market. Younger Canadians delayed homeownership and household formation, homelessness and encampments became more visible, and intergenerational wealth gaps widened between homeowners and renters.

Policy responses

Federal responses included the National Housing Strategy of 2017, the Housing Accelerator Fund to reward municipalities that liberalize zoning, restrictions on foreign buyers, and tax measures against house flipping and underused housing. Provinces such as British Columbia and Ontario legislated overrides of municipal zoning to permit multiplexes and transit-oriented density. The Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation estimated in 2022 that Canada needed 3.5 million homes beyond projected construction by 2030 to restore affordability.
