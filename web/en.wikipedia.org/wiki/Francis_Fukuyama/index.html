Francis Fukuyama

Francis Fukuyama (born 27 October 1952) is an American political scientist, political economist and international relations scholar. A senior fellow at Stanford University, he is best known for his book The End of History and the Last Man (1992).

The End of History

Expanding his 1989 essay, Fukuyama argued that the end of the Cold War marked the endpoint of ideological evolution, with liberal democracy as the final form of government — a thesis widely debated and frequently revisited after events from 9/11 to the rise of populism, about which he has written extensively and with qualifications.

Other work

His books include Trust (1995) on social capital, Our Posthuman Future (2002) on biotechnology, State-Building (2004), the two-volume The Origins of Political Order (2011) and Political Order and Political Decay (2014), and Identity (2018) on the demand for recognition in contemporary politics. He previously taught at Johns Hopkins SAIS and served on the US State Department's Policy Planning Staff.
