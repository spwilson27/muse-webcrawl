János Kádár (born János József Czermanik; 26 May 1912 – 6 July 1989) was a Hungarian Communist leader who governed Hungary from 1956 until his forced retirement in 1988 — 32 years, the longest tenure of any Hungarian leader of the 20th century. His comparatively mild later rule was nicknamed "Goulash Communism."

Rise

Kádár joined the illegal Communist Party in 1931, was repeatedly arrested, and after World War II became a senior figure in the party, serving as Minister of the Interior (1948–1950) before himself falling victim to a show trial under Mátyás Rákosi and being imprisoned until 1954.

1956 and after

During the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, Kádár first joined Imre Nagy's government but then defected to the Soviets and was installed as head of the new regime after the uprising was crushed; Nagy was later executed. Kádár's early rule included reprisals against thousands, but from the 1960s he pursued reconciliation under the slogan "whoever is not against us is with us," amnestying prisoners and prioritizing living standards.

Goulash Communism

The New Economic Mechanism of 1968 introduced market elements unusual in the Soviet bloc, and Hungary became relatively prosperous and liberal by Eastern-bloc standards, though dependent on Western loans. Kádár was removed by younger party reformers in May 1988 and died in July 1989, weeks before the system he led began to collapse.
