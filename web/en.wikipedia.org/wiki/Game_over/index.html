Game over

OriginArcade games, 1950s–1970s
MeaningEnd of gameplay, usually following defeat

A classic arcade "GAME OVER" screen

"Game over" is a message in video games that signals the end of the game, most commonly when the player has been defeated or has exhausted all available lives or continues. The phrase originated in electromechanical and arcade gaming and became a universal convention of the medium.

History

Variants of the phrase appeared on pinball machines from the 1950s, where "game over" lit up when the last ball drained. Early arcade video games such as Space Invaders (1978) popularised the on-screen text, and because arcade machines profited from short sessions, game over screens typically prompted players to insert another coin to continue. Home games inherited the phrase even after the commercial rationale disappeared.

Usage and variations

Modern games frequently replace or soften the phrase with alternatives such as "You Died" (Dark Souls), "Wasted" (Grand Theft Auto), or mission-failed screens, and roguelike games treat game over — "permadeath" — as a core design element. The term has entered general English as a metaphor for any definitive failure or ending.
