The WB

The WB Television Network (short for Warner Bros.) was an American television network launched on January 11, 1995, as a joint venture between the Warner Bros. Entertainment division of Time Warner and the Tribune Broadcasting subsidiary of the Tribune Company, with Warner Bros. as the majority owner.

Programming

Aimed principally at teenagers and young adults, The WB became known for signature dramas and comedies including 7th Heaven, Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Dawson's Creek, Gilmore Girls, Charmed, Smallville, One Tree Hill, Felicity and the Kids' WB Saturday-morning block. Its mascot was Michigan J. Frog, borrowed from the 1955 Warner Bros. cartoon One Froggy Evening.

Closure and legacy

Struggling in the ratings against larger networks, The WB ceased operations on September 17, 2006, and merged with UPN to form The CW, which launched the following day and carried over several WB series. Many of its affiliate stations became CW or MyNetworkTV affiliates.
