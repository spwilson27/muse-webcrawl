Santa Ana (Spanish for "Saint Anne") is a city in and the county seat of Orange County, California, United States. Located in the Greater Los Angeles region, it is the second most populous city in Orange County and, with a population of about 310,000 in 2020, one of the densest large cities in California. Founded in 1869, the city is the namesake of the Santa Ana winds, the hot, dry offshore winds that periodically sweep Southern California.

History

The area was inhabited by the Tongva and Acjachemen peoples before Spanish colonization; Mission San Juan Capistrano was founded nearby in 1776. In 1810 the land was granted as Rancho Santiago de Santa Ana to José Antonio Yorba. William H. Spurgeon founded the town of Santa Ana in 1869, and it became the county seat when Orange County was created in 1889. The city grew with agriculture — celery, walnuts, and sugar beets — and later with military installations during the Second World War. The Santa Ana Army Air Base and nearby Marine Corps Air Station El Toro spurred postwar development.

Demographics and culture

Santa Ana has one of the largest Spanish-speaking populations of any U.S. city; a large majority of residents are of Mexican origin, and the downtown Calle Cuatro (Fourth Street) is a centre of Mexican-American commerce and culture. Cultural institutions include the Bowers Museum, the Discovery Cube Orange County science centre, and the Santa Ana Zoo. John Wayne Airport, serving Orange County, lies at the city's southwestern edge.
