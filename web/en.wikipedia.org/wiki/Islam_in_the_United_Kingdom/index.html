Islam in the United Kingdom

Islam is the second-largest religion in the United Kingdom. According to the 2021 census, Muslims numbered about 3.9 million in England and Wales, around 6.5% of the population, with further communities in Scotland and Northern Ireland. The majority of British Muslims are of South Asian descent, particularly from Pakistan, Bangladesh and India, alongside Arab, Turkish, Somali and other communities.

History

Muslim presence in Britain grew from the 18th and 19th centuries through lascars — sailors from British India employed on merchant ships — who settled in port cities such as London, Cardiff, Liverpool and South Shields. The Shah Jahan Mosque in Woking, opened in 1889, was the first purpose-built mosque in Britain. Large-scale migration followed the Second World War, driven by labour demand and Commonwealth ties.

Communities and organisations

Most British Muslims are Sunni, with Deobandi and Barelvi traditions prominent among South Asian communities; there are also Shia, Ahmadi and Sufi groups. The Muslim Council of Britain, founded in 1997, is a major umbrella body. There are over 1,500 mosques in the UK. Cities with large Muslim populations include London, Birmingham, Bradford, Manchester and Leicester.

Politics and society

Muslims have been elected to Parliament in increasing numbers since 1997, and Sadiq Khan became Mayor of London in 2016. British Muslim life has also been shaped by debates over integration, counter-terrorism policy, and incidents of Islamophobia.
