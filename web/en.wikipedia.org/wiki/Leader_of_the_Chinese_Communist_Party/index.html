The leader of the Chinese Communist Party is the highest-ranking official of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and, since the party's victory in the Chinese Civil War in 1949, generally the paramount leader of the People's Republic of China. The office is currently held by the General Secretary of the Central Committee.

History

The title and structure of the party leadership changed repeatedly in the party's first decades. Chen Duxiu, a co-founder of the party in 1921, served as its first leader under the title of Secretary of the Central Bureau. In later years the top post was variously styled General Secretary and, from 1943 to 1982, Chairman of the Central Committee. Mao Zedong held the chairmanship from 1945 until his death in 1976, dominating the party and state for three decades.

After Mao, Hua Guofeng briefly succeeded as Chairman. Deng Xiaoping became the de facto paramount leader from 1978 despite never holding the top party or state office, and in 1982 the chairmanship was abolished in favour of the General Secretary post, partly to discourage a recurrence of one-man rule. Hu Yaobang was the first to hold the restored title.

Role and powers

The General Secretary presides over the Politburo Standing Committee, the party's top decision-making body, and convenes meetings of the Politburo and Central Committee. Since the 1990s the party leader has conventionally also held the posts of President of the People's Republic of China and Chairman of the Central Military Commission, concentrating party, state, and military authority in one person. Xi Jinping assumed the general secretaryship in November 2012 and is frequently described as the "core" of the party leadership.

List of leaders

Holders of the top party post include Chen Duxiu, Mao Zedong, Hua Guofeng, Hu Yaobang, Zhao Ziyang, Jiang Zemin, Hu Jintao, and Xi Jinping. Zhang Wentian served as general secretary during part of the 1930s while Mao consolidated power during and after the Long March.
