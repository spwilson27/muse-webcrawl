Cretan lyra

ClassificationBowed string instrument
Related instrumentsByzantine lyra, kemenche, gadulka

The Cretan lyra is the dominant folk instrument of Crete.

The Cretan lyra (Greek: Κρητική λύρα) is a Greek pear-shaped, three-stringed bowed musical instrument, central to the traditional music of Crete and the Dodecanese. It is a descendant of the medieval Byzantine lyra and is played held upright on the knee, with the strings stopped by the fingernails from the side rather than pressed from above.

Description

The lyra has a shallow, pear-shaped body carved from a single piece of wood, traditionally mulberry or walnut, with a thin soundboard and small sound holes. It usually has three strings tuned in fifths, commonly A–D–A. The bow may have small bells (gerakokoudouna) attached, which jingle as the player moves the bow, adding a rhythmic accompaniment.

Music

The lyra is the lead instrument in Cretan folk music, usually accompanied by the laouto (Cretan lute), which provides rhythm and harmony. Its repertoire includes dances such as the syrtos, pentozali and sousta, as well as narrative songs and improvised couplets known as mantinades. Noted players include Kostas Mountakis, Antonis Xylouris (Psarantonis), and Ross Daly, who helped popularise the instrument internationally.
