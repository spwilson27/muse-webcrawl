Limiter

In electronics and audio engineering, a limiter is a circuit or device that allows signals below a specified threshold to pass unaffected while attenuating the peaks of stronger signals that exceed it. Limiting is a type of dynamic range compression with a fast attack and a very high compression ratio, often treated as effectively infinite.

Uses

In broadcasting, limiters protect transmitters from overmodulation and keep audio levels within legal limits. In recording and mastering, brick-wall limiters raise perceived loudness while preventing digital clipping. Limiters are also used in live sound to protect loudspeakers and in hearing conservation.

Types

Limiters may be implemented in analogue hardware, in digital signal processors, or as software plug-ins. A "look-ahead" limiter delays the audio slightly so peaks can be detected before they reach full amplitude, enabling true brick-wall limiting with no overshoot.
