National Water Development Agency

The National Water Development Agency (NWDA) is an autonomous society of the Government of India under the Ministry of Jal Shakti, established on 17 July 1982. It was set up to study the optimal utilisation of the water resources of peninsular India and to prepare feasibility reports for the interlinking of rivers under the National Perspective Plan for water resources development.

Functions

The NWDA carries out detailed surveys and investigations of reservoir sites and link alignments, prepares detailed project reports for inter-basin water transfer links, and conducts studies on the Himalayan rivers development component added to its mandate in 1990. It also maintains data on interstate river basins and assists states in preparing project reports for intra-state links.

Interlinking of rivers

The National Perspective Plan, formulated in 1980, envisages 30 major links: 14 under the Himalayan component and 16 under the Peninsular component. The NWDA has identified priority links such as the Ken–Betwa link, the first to move toward implementation with a detailed project report and inter-state agreement, and the Parbati–Kalisindh–Chambal and Godavari–Cauvery links. The programme is intended to transfer water from surplus to deficit basins to increase irrigation potential and mitigate floods and droughts, though it has also attracted environmental and interstate controversies.
