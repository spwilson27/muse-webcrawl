The Marquisate of Saluzzo was a historical state in Piedmont, Italy, centred on the city of Saluzzo and ruled by the Aleramici marquises from the 12th century until its annexation by France in the 16th century.

History

The marquisate originated in the inheritance of Boniface del Vasto. Its marquises balanced between the Holy Roman Empire, Savoy and France, extending influence into the Alpine passes. Under Ludovico I and Ludovico II (15th–16th centuries) Saluzzo enjoyed a cultural flowering, though Ludovico II's defeat by France led to French occupation.

End

The last marquis, Gabriele, was deposed in 1548 and the territory annexed to France; by the Treaty of Lyon (1601) it passed to the Duchy of Savoy in exchange for Bresse and other lands. The marquisate figures in Boccaccio's Decameron tale of patient Griselda.
