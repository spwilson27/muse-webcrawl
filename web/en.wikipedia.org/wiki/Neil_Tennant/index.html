Neil Francis Tennant (born 10 July 1954) is an English musician, singer and songwriter. He is the co-founder and lead singer of the synth-pop duo Pet Shop Boys, formed in London in 1981 with Chris Lowe. The duo have sold more than 100 million records worldwide.

Early life and career

Tennant grew up in North Shields and Newcastle upon Tyne and studied history at North London Polytechnic. Before music he worked in publishing, including as an editor at Marvel Comics' UK branch and as deputy editor of the pop magazine Smash Hits, where he worked during the early 1980s. He met Lowe in an electronics shop on the King's Road, Chelsea, in 1981.

Pet Shop Boys

As the duo's lyricist and vocalist, Tennant supplied the deadpan delivery and literate, ironic lyrics of hits including "West End Girls" (1985), "It's a Sin", "What Have I Done to Deserve This?" (with Dusty Springfield), "Rent", "Heart" and "Go West". Pet Shop Boys have released a continuous run of studio albums from Please (1986) to Nonetheless (2024). Tennant has also collaborated with Electronic, Robbie Williams and others, and co-wrote the musical Closer to Heaven (2001).

Personal life

Tennant came out as gay in a 1994 interview with Attitude magazine. He is a patron of the Elton John AIDS Foundation and is known for his interest in history, architecture and art.
