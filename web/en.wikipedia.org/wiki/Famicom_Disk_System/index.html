The Family Computer Disk System, commonly shortened to Famicom Disk System or simply the Disk System, is a peripheral for Nintendo's Family Computer home console, released only in Japan on 21 February 1986. It connects through the cartridge slot via a RAM adapter and plays games on proprietary double-sided floppy disks called Disk Cards.

Technology

Disk Cards are 2.8-inch quick disks in a hard plastic shell, holding about 56 kilobytes per side (112 KB total). Because disks were rewritable, games could save progress without passwords or expensive battery-backed cartridges, and an additional RAM sound channel gave Disk System titles richer audio. Nintendo also installed Disk Writer kiosks in shops across Japan, where customers could copy a new game onto a disk for ¥500 — far less than a cartridge — and later Disk Fax kiosks let players submit high scores in contests.

Games

The launch line-up included The Legend of Zelda, whose save feature was revolutionary for a console game. Other notable Disk System titles include Metroid, Kid Icarus, Castlevania, Super Mario Bros. 2 (the Japanese version), and Zelda II: The Adventure of Link. Some games, such as Doki Doki Panic, were Disk System exclusives. In total, roughly 200 games were released for the system.

Decline

The system's advantages faded as cartridge ROM prices fell and cartridges gained battery saves and larger capacities. The disks were also easy to pirate, and the unshuttered early disks were vulnerable to dust and magnetic fields. Nintendo quietly wound the format down; Disk Writer services ended in 2003 alongside Famicom support. The Disk System was never released outside Japan, though its mascot, Disk-kun, remains a familiar Nintendo character in Japan.
