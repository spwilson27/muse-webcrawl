Cimabue

Cimabue (c. 1240 – 1302), also known as Cenni di Pepo, was an Italian painter and designer of mosaics from Florence. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern Italian painting, the last great painter in the Byzantine tradition and the first to move toward the naturalism that flowered in the Renaissance. Tradition holds that he was the teacher of Giotto.

Life and work

Little is documented about Cimabue's life. He is recorded in Rome in 1272 and was working in Pisa in 1301–02, where he died. Giorgio Vasari's biography of him opens the Lives of the Artists, treating him as the painter who began the revival of art in Italy after centuries of the "Greek manner" (Byzantine style).

Major works

Surviving works attributed to Cimabue include the large Maestà (Santa Trinita Madonna) in the Uffizi, Florence; the Crucifix in Santa Croce (badly damaged in the 1966 Florence flood); frescoes in the Upper Church of San Francesco at Assisi; and the mosaic of Christ enthroned in Pisa Cathedral, completed in 1302. His Madonna figures show softer modelling and a greater sense of volume than his Byzantine predecessors.

Legacy

Dante mentions Cimabue in the Purgatorio as a painter whose fame was eclipsed by his pupil Giotto — an early comment on artistic reputation. In 2019, a panel attributed to Cimabue, The Mocking of Christ, was discovered in a house in France and sold at auction for over €24 million.
