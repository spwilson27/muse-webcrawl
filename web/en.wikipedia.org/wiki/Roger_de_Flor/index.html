Roger de Flor (c. 1267 – 30 April 1305) was an Italian-born military adventurer and mercenary commander in the service of the Crown of Aragon. He led the Catalan Company of Almogavar mercenaries on its celebrated expedition to the Byzantine Empire in 1303.

Life

Born in Brindisi to a German falconer of Emperor Frederick II and an Italian mother, Roger went to sea as a squire of the Knights Templar, eventually commanding a Templar galley and profiting from evacuations during the fall of Acre in 1291. Expelled from the order, he became a pirate and then a mercenary for Frederick of Sicily, who made him vice-admiral. After the Peace of Caltabellotta (1302) left the Almogavars unemployed, Roger offered their services to the Byzantine emperor Andronikos II Palaiologos, who was threatened by the Ottoman Turks.

The Catalan Company and death

Arriving in Constantinople in 1303 with some 6,500 men, Roger was given the title of megas doux and married the emperor's niece. The Company won striking victories against the Turks in Anatolia, but its ravaging of Byzantine territory and demands for pay made it deeply unpopular. In 1305 Roger was lured to Adrianople and murdered with many of his men by Alan mercenaries on the orders of the co-emperor Michael IX. The Company's survivors took the famous "Catalan vengeance", ravaging Thrace and Macedonia before seizing the Duchy of Athens in 1311.
