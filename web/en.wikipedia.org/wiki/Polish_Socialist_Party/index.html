The Polish Socialist Party (Polish: Polska Partia Socjalistyczna, PPS) was a socialist political party in Poland, founded in Paris in 1892. It was one of the most important Polish political movements of the first half of the 20th century: a left-wing party that uniquely combined the struggle for socialism with the struggle for Polish national independence, holding that social emancipation required the restoration of a sovereign Polish state.

History

Partition era

Founded by Polish émigrés and activists from all three partitions, the PPS published the newspaper Robotnik ("The Worker") and organized workers in the Russian and Austrian partitions. Its leading figure in the early decades was Józef Piłsudski, editor of Robotnik and organizer of the party's Combat Organization, which carried out strikes, demonstrations, and armed actions — including the famous Bezdany raid of 1908. In 1906 the party split over tactics and the primacy of independence, into the PPS–Revolutionary Faction (Piłsudski) and the PPS–Left.

Second Republic

The PPS was a pillar of the reborn Polish state: Ignacy Daszyński headed the provisional Lublin government of November 1918, and PPS governments under Jędrzej Moraczewski enacted universal suffrage (including women's suffrage), the eight-hour day, and social insurance. The party remained a major parliamentary force in the interwar Sejm, opposed Piłsudski's post-1926 Sanacja regime from within the Centrolew opposition, and defended democracy; its leaders were imprisoned in the Brest trials.

War and suppression

Under German occupation the party continued underground as PPS–WRN (Wolność, Równość, Niepodległość — "Freedom, Equality, Independence"), contributing leaders such as Kazimierz Pużak to the Polish Underground State. After 1944 a communist-backed "Lublin" PPS was constructed, and in December 1948 the party was forcibly merged with the Polish Workers' Party to form the Polish United Workers' Party (PZPR), ending independent Polish socialism; an exile PPS continued abroad. A revived PPS was re-established in Poland in 1987–1990 and has existed since as a small democratic-socialist party.

Ideology

The PPS combined Marxist-influenced socialism with Polish patriotism, supporting an independent, democratic republic with extensive social reforms, land reform, and workers' rights, and opposing both the internationalist SDKPiL of Rosa Luxemburg (which rejected Polish independence) and later the communists' subordination to Moscow.
