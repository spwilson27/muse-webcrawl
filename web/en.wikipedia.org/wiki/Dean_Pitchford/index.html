Dean Pitchford (born July 29, 1951) is an American songwriter, screenwriter, stage actor and director. He won an Academy Award for the song "Fame" and wrote the screenplay and most of the songs for the 1984 film Footloose, one of the defining musical dramas of the 1980s.

Early life and stage career

Pitchford was born in Honolulu, Hawaii. He studied drama at Yale University and began his career on stage, appearing in productions including the original Broadway cast of Godspell and the revival of Pippin, in which he performed the title role. His acting background informed his later work writing for performers rather than simply for the page.

Songwriting and film

With composer Michael Gore, Pitchford co-wrote the songs for the 1980 film Fame; the title song won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. For Footloose (1984), Pitchford wrote the screenplay and collaborated on all of the film's songs, including the title track performed by Kenny Loggins, "Let's Hear It for the Boy", "Holding Out for a Hero" and "Almost Paradise". The soundtrack spent ten weeks at number one on the Billboard 200. Pitchford later adapted Footloose for the stage; the Broadway musical opened in 1998 and received four Tony Award nominations.

His other songwriting credits include "All the Man That I Need" for Whitney Houston, "Don't Cry Out Loud", and songs for The Slipper and the Rose, for which he also co-wrote the screenplay with the film's directors. He has been inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame.
