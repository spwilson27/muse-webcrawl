Newtonian telescope

The Newtonian telescope, also called the Newtonian reflector, is a type of reflecting telescope invented by Isaac Newton in 1668. It uses a concave parabolic primary mirror to collect light and a flat diagonal secondary mirror to reflect the image to an eyepiece at the side of the tube, near the top.

Design

Newton built his first reflector to avoid the chromatic aberration that plagued refracting telescopes of his day: because mirrors reflect all colours equally, the image has no coloured fringes. The primary mirror focuses light back up the tube; before the focal point, the small flat secondary, tilted at 45°, diverts the beam through the side of the tube to the focuser. Spherical mirrors can be used in slow (high f-ratio) instruments, but fast Newtonians require parabolisation to avoid spherical aberration, and show coma off-axis.

Use

The design remains the most popular for amateur telescopes because it offers the largest aperture per unit cost. The Dobsonian mount, popularised in the 1970s by John Dobson, made very large Newtonian reflectors affordable. See also Newton's law of universal gravitation for Newton's other famous work.
