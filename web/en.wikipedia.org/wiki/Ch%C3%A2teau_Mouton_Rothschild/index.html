Château Mouton Rothschild

Mouton Rothschild, famous for its artist-designed labels.

Château Mouton Rothschild is a wine estate in Pauillac, in the Médoc region of Bordeaux, France, owned by the Rothschild family. Originally classified as a Second Growth in the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855, it was elevated to Premier Grand Cru Classé (First Growth) status in 1973 — the only change ever made to the classification — after decades of lobbying by its owner, Baron Philippe de Rothschild, whose motto on the label changed from "Premier ne puis, second ne daigne, Mouton suis" ("First I cannot be, second I do not deign to be, Mouton I am") to "Premier je suis, second je fus, Mouton ne change" ("First I am, second I was, Mouton does not change").

History

The estate, formerly Château Brane-Mouton, was bought by Baron Nathaniel de Rothschild in 1853 and renamed Mouton Rothschild. The estate's modern era began in 1922, when the twenty-year-old Philippe de Rothschild took control. Among his innovations were château bottling of the entire crop from 1924, the invention of the second wine concept with Mouton Cadet, and, from 1945, commissioning a different artist each year to design the label of the grand vin — a series that has included Jean Cocteau, Georges Braque, Salvador Dalí, Joan Miró, Marc Chagall, Pablo Picasso, Andy Warhol and Francis Bacon.

Vineyard and wines

The vineyard covers about 90 hectares on gravel slopes near the Gironde, planted to roughly 80% Cabernet Sauvignon with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and a little Petit Verdot. The grand vin is typically more opulent and exotic in style than its Pauillac neighbours, with a high proportion of Cabernet Sauvignon, and is aged in new oak. The second wine is Le Petit Mouton de Mouton Rothschild, and the estate also produces a white wine, Aile d'Argent, from Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon planted in the Pauillac appellation.
