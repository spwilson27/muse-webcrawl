Information warfare

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Modern information operations combine cyber, electronic, and psychological tools.

Information warfare (IW) is the battlespace use and management of information and communication technology (ICT) in pursuit of a competitive advantage over an opponent. It encompasses the interdiction of enemy information systems, the protection of one's own, and the shaping of perceptions through propaganda, deception, and influence operations — across cyber, electronic-warfare, psychological-operations, and media domains.

History

Deception and propaganda are ancient arts, but the term entered doctrine in the 1990s as militaries networked their sensors and command systems. The 1991 Gulf War showcased precision strikes guided by information dominance. Estonia (2007) and Georgia (2008) saw state-linked cyber attacks accompany political and military crises, and the Stuxnet worm (2010) demonstrated cyber-physical sabotage.

Components

U.S. doctrine groups information operations into electronic warfare, computer network operations, psychological operations (now military information support operations), military deception, and operations security. Adversaries' concepts are often broader: Russian doctrine treats the information space as a continuous sphere of confrontation, and election-interference campaigns and state troll farms exemplify peacetime influence operations, while Ukraine's conflicts since 2014 have combined all of these with kinetic war.

Legal and ethical issues

The Tallinn Manual process has attempted to map international law onto cyber operations, but attribution problems, the dual-use character of networks, and the targeting of civilian cognition make information warfare a contested grey zone below the threshold of armed conflict.
