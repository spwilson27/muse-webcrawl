Music of Newfoundland and Labrador

The music of Newfoundland and Labrador is a distinctive folk tradition shaped by the province's Irish, English, Scottish, and French settlers, its seafaring and fishing heritage, and the Indigenous cultures of the Innu, Inuit, and Mi'kmaq peoples.

Folk traditions

Traditional Newfoundland music centres on fiddle, accordion (especially the single-row button accordion), guitar, mandolin, bodhrán, and the "ugly stick", a homemade percussion instrument. Sea shanties, ballads, and jigs and reels reflect centuries of outport life. Well-known traditional songs include "I's the B'y", "The Ryans and the Pittmans (We'll Rant and We'll Roar)", "Jack Was Every Inch a Sailor", and "Ode to Newfoundland", the provincial anthem, written in 1902.

Notable performers

Singer Harry Hibbs popularized Newfoundland folk in the 1960s–70s. The folk-rock band Great Big Sea achieved national fame in the 1990s–2000s with rollicking arrangements of traditional songs. Other prominent acts include The Irish Descendants, Buddy Wasisname and the Other Fellers, Ron Hynes ("Sonny's Dream"), Shanneyganock, Amelia Curran, and Hey Rosetta!, led by Tim Baker. Rufus Guinchard and Émile Benoit were celebrated fiddlers.

Indigenous music

In Labrador, Inuit music includes throat singing and drum dancing, while Innu and Mi'kmaq traditions include drumming and song ceremonies. Kashtin, an Innu duo, gained prominence in the 1980s–90s singing in Innu-aimun.
