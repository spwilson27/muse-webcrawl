Kingdom of Mysore

Years: 1399 – 1947

Capital: Mysore (Srirangapatna 1610–1799)

Dynasty: Wadiyar

Notable rulers: Hyder Ali, Tipu Sultan

The Kingdom of Mysore was a kingdom in southern India founded in about 1399 by the Wadiyar dynasty near the city of Mysore. Initially a vassal state of the Vijayanagara Empire, it became independent as that empire declined, and grew into one of the major powers of the subcontinent before becoming a princely state under British paramountcy.

Expansion and the Anglo-Mysore Wars

Under Chikka Devaraja (r. 1673–1704) the kingdom acquired Bangalore and Salem. In 1761 real power passed to the military commander Hyder Ali, who modernised the army and expanded Mysore across the Malabar Coast and the Deccan. Hyder and his son Tipu Sultan fought four Anglo-Mysore Wars against the British East India Company and its allies; their forces pioneered the military use of iron-cased rockets. Tipu was killed defending his capital Srirangapatna in 1799, after which the British restored a child of the Wadiyar line to a reduced, subordinate state.

Princely state and modernisation

From 1831 to 1881 Mysore was administered directly by British commissioners; the "rendition" of 1881 returned it to Wadiyar rule. Under maharajas such as Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV and able dewans including Mirza Ismail, Mysore became a model princely state, building hydroelectric power, the University of Mysore (1916) and industries around Bangalore. In 1947 the state acceded to the Dominion of India and was later reorganised as part of Karnataka.
