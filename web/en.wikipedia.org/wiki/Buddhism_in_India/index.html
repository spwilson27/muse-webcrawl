Buddhism in India

Buddhism in India began in the 6th–5th century BCE with the enlightenment of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, at Bodh Gaya. It spread across the subcontinent under royal patronage, notably of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, and was transmitted abroad along trade routes, becoming a major world religion even as it declined in its homeland after the 12th century CE.

History

After the Buddha's death, monastic communities and councils preserved his teachings. Ashoka erected pillars and sent missions abroad. Centres such as Nalanda and Vikramashila flourished under the Guptas and Palas, attracting scholars like Xuanzang. Buddhism declined through loss of patronage, absorption into Hindu practice, and invasions that destroyed the great monasteries.

Revival

In 1956, B. R. Ambedkar led a mass conversion of Dalits to Navayana Buddhism in Nagpur, making Maharashtra home to most Indian Buddhists today. Tibetan refugees, including the Dalai Lama at Dharamshala, also revitalised Buddhism in the Himalayan regions. Compare Buddhism in Mongolia.
