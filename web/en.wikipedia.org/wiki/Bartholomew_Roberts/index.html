Bartholomew Roberts

Bartholomew Roberts (17 May 1682 – 10 February 1722), born John Roberts and known as Black Bart, was a Welsh pirate who raided ships off the coasts of the Americas and West Africa between 1719 and 1722. He was the most successful pirate of the Golden Age of Piracy, having captured an estimated 470 vessels in his career.

Career

Roberts was serving as third mate aboard a slave ship when it was captured by the pirate Howell Davis off West Africa in 1719. Forced to join the pirates, he was elected captain within weeks of Davis's death, quickly taking revenge on the settlement that had ambushed his predecessor.

His boldest exploits included sailing into Trepassey harbour, Newfoundland, capturing dozens of vessels, and seizing a forty-two-gun Dutch warship off Brazil. His pirate articles — a strict shipboard code — and his personal black flag, showing him standing on the skulls of a Barbadian and a Martinican, made him legendary.

Death

Roberts was killed by grapeshot during a battle with HMS Swallow off Cape Lopez, Gabon, in February 1722. His crew threw his weighted body overboard, as he had wished. His capture and the trial of his men marked a turning point in the suppression of Caribbean piracy.
