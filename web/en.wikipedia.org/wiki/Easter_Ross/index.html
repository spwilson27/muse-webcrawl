Easter Ross

Easter Ross (Scottish Gaelic: Ros an Ear) is a loosely defined area in the east of Ross, Highland, Scotland. It forms the eastern, low-lying portion of the historic county of Ross and Cromarty, centred on the Cromarty Firth and Dornoch Firth peninsulas, and contrasts with the mountainous Wester Ross on the Atlantic coast.

Geography and settlements

The main towns of Easter Ross are Tain, Alness, Invergordon and Dingwall (on the western fringe), along with villages such as Portmahomack, Balintore, Hilton and Fearn. The three villages of Balintore, Hilton and Johnstown are known as the Seaboard Villages. The Tarbat Peninsula, ending at Tarbat Ness lighthouse, is notable for its Pictish archaeology, including the carved stones at Nigg, Shandwick and Hilton of Cadboll.

History

The area was a heartland of the Picts, and the monastery at Portmahomack was an important early Christian centre. Tain is one of Scotland's oldest royal burghs and became a place of pilgrimage to the shrine of St Duthus. In the 20th century Invergordon served as a Royal Navy anchorage — the scene of the Invergordon Mutiny of 1931 — and later became a centre for North Sea oil-rig fabrication and repair at Nigg, as well as a cruise-ship port. Whisky distilleries at Glenmorangie, Dalmore, Balblair and Invergordon are significant employers.

Economy

Agriculture on the fertile coastal plain, whisky distilling, energy-sector fabrication at Nigg Energy Park, tourism and public services sustain the local economy. The area is served by the Far North railway line and the A9 trunk road, which crosses the firths by the Cromarty and Dornoch Firth bridges.
