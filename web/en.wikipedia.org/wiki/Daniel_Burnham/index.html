Daniel Burnham

Daniel Hudson Burnham (4 September 1846 – 1 June 1912) was an American architect and urban planner. A proponent of the Beaux-Arts movement, he directed construction of the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893 and authored the influential 1909 Plan of Chicago.


Born: 4 September 1846, Henderson, New York
Died: 1 June 1912, Heidelberg, Germany
Occupation: Architect, urban planner
Notable works: Flatiron Building, Union Station (Washington), Plan of Chicago

Career

Burnham partnered with John Wellborn Root as Burnham and Root in Chicago, pioneering skyscrapers including the Monadnock Building and the Rookery. After Root's death he formed D. H. Burnham & Co. His New York Flatiron Building (1902) became an icon. As a planner he produced designs for Washington, D.C. (McMillan Plan), Cleveland, San Francisco, and Manila and Baguio in the Philippines.

Plan of Chicago

Co-authored with Edward Bennett, the 1909 Plan of Chicago proposed lakefront parks, diagonal avenues, and civic improvements, much of which was realised. Burnham is famously associated with the exhortation "Make no little plans."

Legacy

Burnham helped establish American city planning as a profession and the City Beautiful movement's vision of ordered, classical urban design.
