The United States of the Ionian Islands (Greek: Ἡνωμένον Κράτος τῶν Ἰονίων Νήσων) was a Greek state and protectorate of the United Kingdom between 1815 and 1864. Established under the Treaty of Paris (1815) following Napoleonic-era changes of control, it comprised seven principal islands — Corfu, Paxos, Lefkada, Cephalonia, Ithaca, Zakynthos and Kythira — together with their dependencies, off the western coast of Greece.

History

The Ionian Islands had been Venetian possessions until 1797, after which they passed between French, Russo-Ottoman (as the Septinsular Republic, 1800–1807), and again French control. British forces occupied most of the islands between 1809 and 1814. Under the 1815 settlement, the islands formed a single federal state — described as a free and independent state — under the exclusive protection of the British Crown, exercised through a Lord High Commissioner.

The islands were governed under constitutions granted in 1817 and revised later, with an Ionian Senate and Legislative Assembly at Corfu, though the Lord High Commissioner retained extensive powers. Ionian politics were dominated by the question of enosis (union with Greece), and islanders participated in the Greek War of Independence despite official neutrality.

Cession to Greece

Following the selection of the Danish prince George as King of Greece and British diplomacy aimed at strengthening the new kingdom, the Treaty of London (1864) ceded the islands to Greece. British forces withdrew and the union was proclaimed at Corfu on 21 May 1864; the islands became three prefectures of the Kingdom of Greece.
