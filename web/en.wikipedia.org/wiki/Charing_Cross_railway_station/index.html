Charing Cross railway station (also known as London Charing Cross) is a central London railway terminus between the Strand and Hungerford Bridge. It is one of 20 stations managed by Network Rail and the London terminal of the South Eastern Main Line. The station takes its name from the nearby Charing Cross junction, the notional centre of London from which distances are measured, marked by the Eleanor cross replica in its forecourt.

History

The station was opened by the South Eastern Railway on 11 January 1864, on the site of the former Hungerford Market. Trains cross the Thames on Hungerford Bridge to reach it. In 1905 part of the original arched trainshed roof collapsed, killing six people; the roof was replaced. The station was rebuilt in the late 1980s–1990s, when the Embankment Place office development was built over the platforms on a masonry-clad structure.

Services

Southeastern trains serve destinations in south-east London and Kent, including Hastings, Ramsgate, Dover, and Tunbridge Wells. The station connects with Charing Cross and Embankment Underground stations (Bakerloo and Northern lines), and is a short walk from the West End theatres.
