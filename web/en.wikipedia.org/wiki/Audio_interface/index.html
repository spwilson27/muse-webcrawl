Audio interface

An audio interface is a device that connects microphones, musical instruments, and other audio equipment to a computer, converting analogue audio signals into digital data the computer can record and process, and converting digital audio from the computer back to analogue signals for monitoring through speakers or headphones. Audio interfaces are a core component of home and professional recording studios and are used with digital audio workstation (DAW) software.

Functions

A typical audio interface provides:

  • Analogue-to-digital and digital-to-analogue conversion (ADC/DAC) at sample rates commonly ranging from 44.1 kHz to 192 kHz and bit depths of 16 or 24 bits.
  • Microphone preamplifiers with gain control, often with switchable +48 V phantom power for condenser microphones.
  • Inputs for line-level signals and high-impedance (Hi-Z/DI) inputs for electric guitars and basses, often via combination XLR/TRS "combo" jacks.
  • Outputs for studio monitor loudspeakers and one or more headphone outputs, often with direct (zero-latency) monitoring.
  • Digital connections such as MIDI, S/PDIF, ADAT optical, or AES3, and computer connections via USB, Thunderbolt, or PCIe cards.

Form factors

Interfaces range from small bus-powered desktop units with one or two inputs, aimed at podcasters and solo musicians, to rackmount units with dozens of channels for professional studios. Historically, professional systems used proprietary PCI or FireWire connections; USB and Thunderbolt now dominate the market. Manufacturers include Focusrite, Universal Audio, PreSonus, MOTU, RME, Steinberg, Native Instruments, and Apogee.

Latency

Because audio passes through buffers in the computer, interfaces introduce latency—the delay between input and output. Low round-trip latency (under roughly 10 milliseconds) is important when monitoring through software effects while recording. Many interfaces offer direct hardware monitoring, which routes the input signal straight to the outputs to avoid this delay, and modern drivers and faster connections have reduced latency substantially.
