The Taunus is a mountain range in Hesse, Germany, located north of Frankfurt am Main. Part of the Rhenish Massif, it is bounded by the Rhine, Main and Lahn river valleys and rises to 878 metres at the Großer Feldberg. The range is heavily forested and forms a popular recreation area for the Rhine-Main metropolitan region.

Geography and history

The Taunus consists mainly of Devonian slates and quartzites. Its mineral springs gave rise to famous spa towns on its southern slopes, including Wiesbaden, Bad Homburg, Bad Nauheim and Bad Soden. In Roman times the frontier works of the Limes ran along the ridge; the reconstructed fort of the Saalburg near Bad Homburg is now a museum and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Wealthy suburbs of Frankfurt, such as Königstein and Kronberg, lie on the southern foothills, and the range lends its name to the Ford Taunus car produced by Ford Germany. Two nature parks, Taunus and Rhein-Taunus, protect much of the upland.
