Yule Lads

The Yule Lads (Icelandic: Jólasveinar) are characters in Icelandic Christmas folklore. They are the thirteen sons of the ogress Grýla and her husband Leppalúði, mountain-dwelling trolls who, in modern tradition, visit children across Iceland on the thirteen nights leading up to Christmas.

In earlier folklore the Yule Lads were frightening figures used to scare children into good behaviour, much like their mother, who was said to cook disobedient children in her cauldron. Their numbers and names once varied widely — dozens of regional variants existed. In the twentieth century they were softened into mischievous but mostly harmless pranksters, and since the mid-1900s Icelandic children have left a shoe on their windowsill each night: good children receive a small gift from the visiting Lad, while naughty ones may find a raw potato.

The first Lad arrives on the night of 11–12 December and the last on the night of 23–24 December; after Christmas they depart one per night, the final one leaving on Epiphany, 6 January, the last day of the Icelandic Christmas season.
