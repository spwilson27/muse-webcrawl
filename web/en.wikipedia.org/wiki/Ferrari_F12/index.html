The Ferrari F12berlinetta (badged simply as the F12) is a front mid-engine, rear-wheel-drive grand tourer produced by the Italian manufacturer Ferrari from 2012 to 2017. Unveiled at the 2012 Geneva Motor Show as the successor to the 599, it was at its launch the most powerful and fastest road-going Ferrari ever built.

Specifications

The F12berlinetta is powered by a 6,262 cc (6.3 L) naturally aspirated 65° V12 derived from the F140 engine family, producing 740 PS (544 kW; 730 hp) at 8,250 rpm and 690 N·m of torque. Power is sent to the rear wheels through a 7-speed dual-clutch automatic gearbox. Ferrari quoted a 0–100 km/h time of 3.1 seconds and a top speed of over 340 km/h (211 mph). At its introduction, the F12 lapped Ferrari's Fiorano test circuit in 1 minute 23 seconds, faster than any previous road Ferrari.

Design

The body was designed by the Ferrari Styling Centre in collaboration with Pininfarina. Aerodynamic features include the "Aero Bridge", which channels air from the bonnet through openings in the flanks to generate downforce, and active brake cooling ducts. The car uses an aluminium space-frame chassis co-developed with Scaglietti.

F12tdf

In 2015 Ferrari introduced the limited-production F12tdf, named in honour of the Tour de France road race. Its engine was uprated to 780 PS, weight was reduced by about 110 kg, and production was limited to 799 units.

Replacement

The F12berlinetta was replaced in 2017 by the 812 Superfast, presented at that year's Geneva Motor Show.
