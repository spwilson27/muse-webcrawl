Mide

Mide (also spelled Mida or Mide') may refer to the Midewiwin, the Grand Medicine Society of the Ojibwe (Anishinaabe) people and related Algonquian-speaking nations, as well as to its practitioners and ceremonies. The word derives from the Ojibwe language and is associated with sacred knowledge, healing and spiritual power.

The Midewiwin society

The Midewiwin is a religious and ceremonial society whose members, known as mide, are initiated through a series of degrees or grades. Initiates learn healing practices, herbal medicine, songs, and the interpretation of sacred birch bark scrolls (wiigwaasabakoon) that record migration stories, ritual instructions and cosmological teachings in pictographic form.

Ceremonies

Mide ceremonies traditionally take place in a purpose-built lodge, the midewigaan. Rituals include the use of sacred objects such as the water drum, rattles, and the megis shell, a small white cowrie-like shell said to have been given to the Anishinaabe by the Creator. A central rite symbolically involves "shooting" the megis shell into the initiate, representing the infusion of spiritual power and the renewal of life.

History

Scholars debate the antiquity of the Midewiwin; some traditions hold it to be of great age, while others associate its fluorescence with the upheavals of the fur trade era and the displacement of Anishinaabe communities in the 17th and 18th centuries. The society remains a living tradition among Ojibwe communities in Canada and the United States.
