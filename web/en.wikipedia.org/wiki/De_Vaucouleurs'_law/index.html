De Vaucouleurs's law

Surface-brightness profile of an elliptical galaxy, which falls steeply in the centre and fades gradually outward.

De Vaucouleurs's law, also known as the r1/4 law, describes how the surface brightness of an elliptical galaxy varies with distance from its centre. It was formulated by the French-American astronomer Gérard de Vaucouleurs in 1948 and remains a standard empirical description of the light distribution of elliptical galaxies and the bulges of spiral galaxies.

Formulation

The law states that the surface brightness I at a projected radius r is given by:

I(r) = Ie · exp{ −7.67 [ (r/re)1/4 − 1 ] }

where re is the effective radius — the radius enclosing half of the galaxy's total light — and Ie is the surface brightness at that radius. The factor 7.67 is chosen so that this definition of re holds. Equivalently, the logarithm of the surface brightness decreases linearly with r1/4, which is why the relation is called the r1/4 law.

Relation to the Sérsic profile

De Vaucouleurs's law is a special case of the more general Sérsic profile, corresponding to a Sérsic index of n = 4. Lower-mass and late-type systems are often better fitted by smaller values of n, while the exponential profile (n = 1) describes the discs of spiral galaxies. Despite this generalisation, the r1/4 profile remains a benchmark against which the structure of early-type galaxies is measured.
