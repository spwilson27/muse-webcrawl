Rigsdagen

The Rigsdag (Danish: Rigsdagen) was the name of the national legislature of Denmark from 1849 to 1953. It was a bicameral parliament consisting of two houses: the Folketing (the lower house) and the Landsting (the upper house). The Rigsdag was seated at Christiansborg Palace in Copenhagen.

History

The Rigsdag was established by the first Danish constitution, the June Constitution of 1849, which ended the absolute monarchy and introduced representative government. Both chambers were elected, though the Landsting was chosen by a narrower, wealthier electorate and included members appointed by the Crown for much of its existence.

Constitutional development

The constitution of 1866 tightened the franchise for the Landsting and strengthened its position relative to the Folketing, contributing to the long constitutional struggle (forfatningskampen) of the late nineteenth century. The 1915 constitution broadened suffrage, extending the vote to women and servants. The present constitution of 1953 abolished the Landsting, making the Folketing a unicameral legislature, and the name Rigsdag fell out of use.
