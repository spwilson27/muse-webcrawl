The Chuukese language (also rendered Trukese in older sources) is a Micronesian language of the Austronesian language family, spoken on the islands of Chuuk in the Federated States of Micronesia. It is the most widely spoken of the Chuukic languages and serves as a lingua franca across Chuuk State.

Classification

Chuukese belongs to the Chuukic subgroup of the Micronesian languages, which in turn form part of the Oceanic branch of Austronesian. It is closely related to languages such as Mortlockese, Puluwatese, Namonuito and Carolinian, and the varieties spoken across the Chuuk Lagoon and outer islands form a dialect continuum.

Phonology

Chuukese has a distinctive consonant inventory that includes a series of plain and labialized consonants and a set of geminate (doubled) consonants that contrast in meaning. Its vowel system includes nine vowel qualities, and both consonant and vowel length are phonemic. The language is notable among Oceanic languages for allowing geminate initial consonants.

Grammar

Chuukese is an isolating-to-agglutinative language with subject–verb–object word order in many constructions. It has a rich system of possessive classifiers: different possessive markers are used depending on the nature of the possessed noun, such as food, drink, or general possessions. Verbs are marked for aspect, and the language uses reduplication for distributive and habitual meanings.

Writing system

Chuukese is written in a Latin-based alphabet introduced during the period of missionary activity and German administration, when the islands were known as Truk. Orthographic conventions were standardized in the 20th century, and a Chuukese–English dictionary has been published to support literacy and education.

Status

Chuukese is the language of everyday life, local government and primary education in Chuuk State, while English is used as an official language at the national level. A substantial Chuukese-speaking diaspora lives in Guam, Hawaii and the continental United States.
