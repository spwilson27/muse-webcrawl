Steady-state model

In cosmology, the steady-state model is an alternative to the Big Bang theory. The universe is expanding but remains statistically unchanged over time because matter is continuously created as old matter moves beyond the observable horizon — embodying the perfect cosmological principle.

Steady-state model
Proposed: 1948
Proponents: Hermann Bondi, Thomas Gold, Fred Hoyle
Status: Superseded by Big Bang / ΛCDM

History

Bondi and Gold proposed the model in 1948 from the perfect cosmological principle; Hoyle independently proposed a version with a creation field (C-field) modifying general relativity. Fred Hoyle coined the term "Big Bang" in a 1949 radio broadcast.

Decline

Evidence against it accumulated: radio source counts showing cosmic evolution, and especially the discovery of the cosmic microwave background in 1965, a relic the steady-state model could not naturally explain. Most cosmologists abandoned it, though Hoyle and collaborators proposed a quasi-steady-state variant in the 1990s.
