Kavala

Kavala (Greek: Καβάλα) is a city in northern Greece, the principal seaport of eastern Macedonia and the capital of the Kavala regional unit. Built amphitheatrically around its bay on the Aegean Sea, facing the island of Thasos, it stands on the ancient Via Egnatia and on the site of ancient Neapolis, the port where the Apostle Paul first landed in Europe.

History

Neapolis was founded in the 7th century BC by colonists from Thasos and became a port of nearby Philippi. According to the Acts of the Apostles, Paul sailed from Troas and landed at Neapolis on his first journey to Europe around AD 49–50, proceeding to Philippi. In Byzantine times the city was refortified and renamed Christoupolis.

Ottoman rule lasted from 1387 to 1912. Under the Ottomans, Kavala was the birthplace (c. 1769) of Muhammad Ali, the Albanian commander who became ruler of Egypt; his birthplace and the Imaret he endowed — a vast charitable complex of 1817–21 — survive in the old town. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries Kavala grew rich on tobacco: its warehouses processed Macedonian leaf for export, employing thousands, and tobacco workers made the city a stronghold of the early Greek labour and communist movements. The city was occupied by Bulgaria in both Balkan and World War periods before integration into Greece. Refugees from Asia Minor after 1922 reshaped its population.

Landmarks and economy

Landmarks include the medieval castle on the Panagia peninsula, the Kamares aqueduct rebuilt by Suleiman the Magnificent around 1550, the Imaret, the Muhammad Ali house, and the Tobacco Museum. Today the port handles ferries to Thasos and the north Aegean islands; the economy rests on trade, fishing, marble, oil installations at Prinos offshore, and tourism, with the archaeological site of Philippi — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — inland.
