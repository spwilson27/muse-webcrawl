Lake Memphremagog (French: Lac Memphrémagog) is a long, narrow glacial lake that straddles the Canada–United States border between the province of Quebec and the state of Vermont. About three quarters of its 51-kilometre length and most of its watershed lie in Quebec, while its southern end reaches Newport, Vermont.

Geography

The lake's name comes from the Abenaki language and is generally interpreted as "where there is a big expanse of water". Its northern outlet is the Magog River at Magog, Quebec, which flows to the Saint-François River. The lake contains several islands, including Province Island, through which the international border runs. Owl's Head mountain rises above its western shore and hosts a ski area.

Folklore and recreation

Like Scotland's Loch Ness, Lake Memphremagog has a legendary lake monster, "Memphre", reported since the nineteenth century and now a fixture of local tourism. The lake is heavily used for sailing, fishing, swimming, and ice fishing, and its shores are lined with cottages and the resort town of Magog. Water quality, particularly phosphorus levels and cyanobacteria blooms, is a shared management concern for Quebec and Vermont.
