Iron Mountain Road, officially designated as a portion of U.S. Route 16A in the Black Hills of South Dakota, is a 17-mile scenic highway linking Custer State Park with Mount Rushmore National Memorial. Designed by Cecil Clyde Gideon, superintendent of Custer State Park, the road was laid out in 1933 to showcase the Black Hills scenery and to frame views of Mount Rushmore through its famous "pigtail" bridges and tunnels.

Design

The road is renowned for its engineering: corkscrew "pigtail" bridges that loop over and under themselves to climb steep grades, wooden trestle-style bridges, and narrow one-lane tunnels blasted through granite. The three tunnels were deliberately aligned so that, when driving north towards Mount Rushmore, the monument is framed in the tunnel opening — a deliberate theatrical effect in the spirit of the era's scenic park roads.

The road climbs over the summit of Iron Mountain itself (5,430 ft / 1,655 m) and passes through dense ponderosa pine forest, granite spires and mountain meadows, with numerous turnouts for viewing wildlife, including the bison herds of Custer State Park.

Recreation and events

Iron Mountain Road forms part of the annual Sturgis Motorcycle Rally routes and is a popular drive for motorcyclists and tourists, often combined with Needles Highway (SD 87). Because of its sharp curves, low clearances and narrow tunnels, large vehicles and trailers are discouraged.
