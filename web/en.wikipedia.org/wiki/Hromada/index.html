Hromada

Hromada (Ukrainian: громада, literally "community") is a term used in Ukraine for a territorial community — the basic unit of local self-government and of the country's administrative-territorial structure — and, historically, for Ukrainian community organisations more generally.

Territorial communities

Under the decentralisation reform launched in 2014 and completed in 2020, Ukraine's local government was reorganised into territorial communities (територіальні громади), formed by amalgamating neighbouring villages, settlements and cities. The 2020 reform established 1,469 hromadas, each electing a council and a head, and each responsible for local services, budgets, education, healthcare facilities and development within its territory. Hromadas are grouped into raions (districts), which in turn form oblasts.

Historical usage

In the 19th century, hromadas were Ukrainian civic and cultural societies — secret or semi-secret networks of intellectuals in Kyiv, Poltava and other cities of the Russian and Austro-Hungarian empires that promoted Ukrainian language, publishing and education. The Kyiv Hromada included figures such as Volodymyr Antonovych and Mykhailo Drahomanov. In the Ukrainian People's Republic period (1917–1921), hromada also served as a term for rural communes and their assemblies.
