Pickup (music technology)

A pickup is a transducer that captures mechanical vibrations produced by musical instruments — particularly stringed instruments such as the electric guitar, electric bass, and violin — and converts them into an electrical signal that can be amplified and recorded.

Magnetic pickups

The most common type, the magnetic pickup, consists of a permanent magnet wrapped with coils of fine copper wire. The vibration of a steel string above the magnet disturbs its field and induces a small alternating current in the coil. Single-coil pickups produce a bright tone but pick up electromagnetic hum; humbuckers, patented in the 1950s, use two coils wired to cancel that noise.

Piezoelectric pickups

Piezo pickups use crystals that generate a voltage when vibrated by the instrument body itself. They are common on acoustic-electric guitars and bowed instruments because they work with non-metallic strings.
