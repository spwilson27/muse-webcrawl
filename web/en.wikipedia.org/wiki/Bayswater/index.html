Bayswater is an area of west London in the City of Westminster, lying north of Hyde Park and Kensington Gardens and west of Paddington. It is characterised by grand mid-Victorian terraces and garden squares of white stucco-fronted houses, many now divided into flats or used as hotels, and by the shopping street of Queensway. The district's postcode is W2.

History

The name derives from "Baynard's watering," a place where horses were watered, recorded in the medieval period, associated with the Baynard family and the Westbourne stream. Bayswater remained rural until the nineteenth century, when, following the development of Paddington and the opening of the railway terminus, it was built up rapidly in the 1850s and 1860s as a fashionable residential suburb. St Sophia's Cathedral on Moscow Road, consecrated in 1882, reflects the area's long-standing Greek community; Whiteley's department store on Queensway opened in 1889 and was once among the largest shops in London. After the Second World War many of the large houses were converted into hotels and flats, giving Bayswater a cosmopolitan population.

Transport and landmarks

The area is served by Bayswater and Queensway Underground stations (Circle and District, and Central lines respectively) and by Lancaster Gate and Royal Oak nearby. Landmarks include the former Whiteley's building — redeveloped as a luxury residential and hotel complex — St Sophia's Cathedral, and the terraces of Hyde Park Gardens and Lancaster Gate bordering the park.
