Occurrence record

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An occurrence record is a documented piece of evidence that a particular organism — usually identified to a taxon such as a species — was present at a particular place at a particular time. Occurrence records are the fundamental data units of biodiversity informatics and are used to map species distributions, model responses to environmental change, and detect the spread of invasive species.

Types of evidence

An occurrence record may be based on a preserved specimen in a museum or herbarium, a photograph, a sound recording, a sample of DNA or environmental DNA, or a recorded human observation (for example from citizen-science platforms such as iNaturalist or eBird). Standards such as Darwin Core use the field basisOfRecord to distinguish these categories, including PreservedSpecimen, HumanObservation and MachineObservation.

Data standards and aggregators

Occurrence data are commonly exchanged using the Darwin Core standard and published through the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), which aggregates billions of records from museums, herbaria, surveys and citizen-science projects. A typical record includes the taxon name, geographic coordinates and their uncertainty, the date of the event, the collector or observer, and the institution holding the evidence.

Quality issues

Occurrence datasets are subject to taxonomic misidentification, georeferencing error, duplicate records and strong sampling biases toward accessible places, charismatic species and wealthy countries. Data-cleaning tools and flags for issues such as coordinates falling in the sea or in capital cities are widely used before records are employed in species distribution modelling.
