The Arrow Cross Party (Hungarian: Nyilaskeresztes Párt) was a far-right, ultranationalist party in Hungary led by Ferenc Szálasi, which formed a German-backed government in October 1944. It modelled its name on the German swastika's role as a party emblem and combined National Socialist ideas with Szálasi's doctrine of "Hungarism".

History

Szálasi, a former army staff officer, founded the Party of National Will in 1935; banned and re-formed several times, its successor the Arrow Cross Party contested the 1939 elections and, riding widespread discontent and antisemitism, won the second-largest vote share and 29 seats, becoming the principal opposition to Miklós Horthy's regime. The party drew support across classes and agitated for Hungarian revisionism, land reform and the removal of Jews from public life.

In power, 1944–45

After Germany occupied Hungary in March 1944 and then forced out Horthy in Operation Panzerfaust, Szálasi became head of government on 16 October 1944. During its roughly six months in power the Arrow Cross resumed the deportation of Jews, set up the Budapest ghetto, and its militias murdered thousands of Jews along the Danube embankment — commemorated today by the Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial — as well as deserters and opponents, while the Siege of Budapest destroyed much of the city.

After the war

The party was outlawed in 1945. Szálasi and other leaders were tried by a people's court and executed in 1946. The arrow cross remains a prohibited extremist symbol in Hungary, and the party is remembered as the most violent episode of Hungary's wartime collaboration.
