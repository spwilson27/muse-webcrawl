The Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata (Spanish: Virreinato del Río de la Plata) was the last of the four viceroyalties of the Spanish Empire in the Americas, created in 1776 from territories detached from the Viceroyalty of Peru. Its territory covered present-day Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay and Uruguay, with Buenos Aires as its capital.

History

King Charles III created the viceroyalty as part of the Bourbon Reforms, to strengthen defences against Portuguese expansion from Brazil and British smuggling, and to give the growing port of Buenos Aires direct administrative standing. Pedro Antonio de Cevallos was its first viceroy. The viceroyalty included the silver-rich region of Upper Peru (Potosí), whose output had previously flowed through Lima; trade regulations of 1778 opened Buenos Aires to direct commerce with Spain and other colonial ports, fuelling rapid economic growth.

End of the viceroyalty

British invasions of the River Plate in 1806–07 were repelled largely by locally raised militias, strengthening creole confidence. In May 1810 the May Revolution deposed Viceroy Baltasar Hidalgo de Cisneros and installed the Primera Junta, beginning the Argentine war of independence. Paraguay declared independence in 1811, Upper Peru remained contested until 1825, and the viceroyalty's territory eventually formed the independent states of Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay and Uruguay.
