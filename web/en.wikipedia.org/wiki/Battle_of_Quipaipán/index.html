Battle of Quipaipán

The Battle of Quipaipán was fought in April 1532 near Cusco in the Inca Empire, during the Inca Civil War between the half-brothers Atahualpa and Huáscar. Atahualpa's veteran northern armies, led by generals Quizquiz and Chalcuchímac, crushed Huáscar's forces and captured Huáscar himself, ending the civil war — months before the Spanish invasion.

Background

The death of Huayna Capac (c. 1527), probably of disease introduced from Europe, left the empire divided: Huáscar held Cusco, Atahualpa the northern army at Quito. War followed, with Atahualpa's experienced generals winning a series of battles as they advanced south, despite an earlier setback at Ambato.

Battle

After victories at Chimborazo and elsewhere, Quizquiz and Chalcuchímac met Huáscar's last army at Quipaipán, west of Cusco. Huáscar's forces were routed; Huáscar was captured attempting to escape. Cusco was occupied and Huáscar's family and supporters were massacred on Atahualpa's orders.

Aftermath

Atahualpa was undisputed Sapa Inca — but the empire was exhausted and divided by civil war when Francisco Pizarro landed. Within months Atahualpa was captured at Cajamarca (November 1532), and Huáscar was murdered on his brother's orders while in captivity.
