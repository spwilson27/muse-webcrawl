1888–89 in English football

The 1888–89 season was the 18th season of competitive football in England and a landmark in the sport's history: it saw the first season of the Football League, the world's first national league competition, founded by Aston Villa director William McGregor. Preston North End won the inaugural league championship without losing a match and added the FA Cup without conceding a goal, becoming the first team to complete "the Double".

The Football League

Frustrated by the disruption of informal friendly schedules and cup ties, William McGregor proposed a league of leading clubs playing guaranteed home-and-away fixtures. Twelve founder clubs — Accrington, Aston Villa, Blackburn Rovers, Bolton Wanderers, Burnley, Derby County, Everton, Notts County, Preston North End, Stoke, West Bromwich Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers — began competition in September 1888. Two points were awarded for a win and one for a draw.

Preston North End, managed by William Sudell and captained by players drawn largely from Scotland, dominated the season: they won 18 and drew 4 of their 22 matches, finishing 11 points clear of Aston Villa, with John Goodall among the league's leading scorers. Their unbeaten record earned them the enduring nickname "The Invincibles".

FA Cup

Preston completed the Double by defeating Wolverhampton Wanderers 3–0 in the 1889 FA Cup Final at the Kennington Oval, with goals from Fred Dewhurst, Jimmy Ross and Sam Thomson — remarkably, without conceding a single goal in any round of the competition.

National teams

England, Scotland and Wales contested the British Home Championship; Scotland won the title, while Ireland lost all three matches, conceding 15 goals.
