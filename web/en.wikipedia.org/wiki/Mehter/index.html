Mehter

Mehter (Ottoman Turkish: مهتر) refers to the Ottoman military bands, among the oldest known military marching bands in the world. The mehterân ensembles accompanied the Janissary corps and the Ottoman army into battle, playing music intended to embolden their own troops and intimidate the enemy. Their distinctive percussion-heavy sound strongly influenced European classical composers of the 18th century.

Instruments

A mehter ensemble typically includes the zurna (a loud double-reed wind instrument), boru (trumpet), nakkare (small kettledrums), davul (large bass drum), kös (giant kettledrums), zil (cymbals) and the çevgan (Turkish crescent, known in Europe as the "jingling Johnny"). The size of the band reflected the rank of the commander it served.

History and influence

Mehter bands were abolished along with the Janissaries in 1826 during the Auspicious Incident, but were revived in the early 20th century. Their influence on European music is heard in the "Turkish" style (alla turca) of Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven. Today mehter ensembles perform ceremonially in Turkey, notably at military museums and state occasions.
