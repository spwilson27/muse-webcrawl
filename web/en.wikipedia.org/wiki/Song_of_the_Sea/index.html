Song of the Sea

Song of the Sea (Irish: Amhrán na Mara) is a 2014 animated fantasy film directed by Tomm Moore and produced by the Irish studio Cartoon Saloon. Inspired by the selkie folklore of Ireland and Scotland, it tells the story of Ben, a young Irish boy, and his mute sister Saoirse, the last seal-child, who must journey to free the faerie folk from the goddess Macha. The film was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Plot

Ben lives in a lighthouse on an island off Ireland with his father Conor and his little sister Saoirse, whose mother Bronagh — a selkie — disappeared the night Saoirse was born. On her sixth birthday, Saoirse discovers her mother's selkie coat and plays a shell flute whose music attracts the fairies, revealing that she is the last seal-child whose song can free the spirit world from the spells of the owl witch Macha, who turns beings to stone to spare them pain. Taken to the city by their grandmother, the children escape and journey home through a fading magical Ireland, pursued by Macha's owls, before Saoirse sings the song of the sea and the faerie folk depart for Tír na nÓg.

Production and style

The film is hand-drawn, with a distinctive style influenced by Irish illuminated manuscripts, Celtic art, and mid-century illustration. It is the second part of Moore's informal "Irish folklore trilogy", following The Secret of Kells (2009) and preceding Wolfwalkers (2020). The voice cast includes David Rawle, Brendan Gleeson, Fionnula Flanagan, Lisa Hannigan, and Jon Kenny. The music was composed by Bruno Coulais in collaboration with the Irish group Kíla, with songs performed by Lisa Hannigan and Lucy O'Connell.

Reception

Song of the Sea premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2014 and was widely praised for its visuals and emotional storytelling, winning the Satellite Award for Best Animated Feature and receiving an Oscar nomination, where it lost to Big Hero 6.
