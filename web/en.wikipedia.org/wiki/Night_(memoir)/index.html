Night (memoir)

AuthorElie Wiesel
Original titleUn di velt hot geshvign (Yiddish, 1956)
French editionLa Nuit (1958)
English editionNight (1960)

Night is a memoir by Elie Wiesel, published in English in 1960, describing his experiences as a teenage prisoner in the Nazi concentration camps at Auschwitz and Buchenwald in 1944–1945, at the height of the Holocaust. It is the first book in a trilogy—Night, Dawn, and Day—reflecting Wiesel's transition from darkness to day.

Background and publication

Wiesel, born in Sighet in Transylvania, was deported with his family in 1944. His mother and younger sister were murdered at Auschwitz; his father died at Buchenwald shortly before liberation. After the war Wiesel wrote a long Yiddish manuscript, published in abridged form in Buenos Aires in 1956 as Un di velt hot geshvign ("And the World Remained Silent"). A shortened French version, La Nuit, appeared in 1958 with a foreword by François Mauriac, followed by the English translation in 1960. A new English translation by his wife Marion Wiesel appeared in 2006, and the book was selected for Oprah's Book Club the same year.

Content

Written in a spare, fragmentary style, the memoir follows the narrator's loss of faith, his strained relationship with his dying father, and the dehumanising routine of the camps. Its title reflects the darkness—literal and spiritual—that descends on the prisoners.
