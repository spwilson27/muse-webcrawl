Disaster risk reduction

Disaster risk reduction (DRR) is an approach to identifying, assessing and reducing the risks of disaster, aiming to reduce socio-economic vulnerability to hazards and to deal with the environmental and other causes of disasters. It encompasses prevention, mitigation and preparedness, complementing emergency response and recovery.

International frameworks

The modern international framework developed through the UN International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction in the 1990s, the Yokohama Strategy (1994), the Hyogo Framework for Action 2005–2015, and the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030, adopted at the Third UN World Conference in Sendai, Japan. The Sendai Framework sets seven global targets, including substantial reductions in disaster mortality, numbers of affected people and economic losses relative to GDP, and increased availability of multi-hazard early warning systems.

Practice

DRR measures include hazard and vulnerability mapping, land-use planning, resilient building codes and retrofitting, ecosystem-based approaches such as mangrove protection, early warning systems, evacuation planning, and community-based preparedness. Because disasters arise from hazards interacting with vulnerability, DRR is closely linked to climate change adaptation and sustainable development; the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR) coordinates system-wide work.
