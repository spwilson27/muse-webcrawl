Slab-on-grade foundation

A slab-on-grade foundation is a shallow foundation in which a concrete slab is poured directly at ground level ("on grade") to serve simultaneously as the foundation and the ground floor of a structure. It is one of the most common and economical foundation types for houses and light buildings, especially in warm climates where frost depth is shallow.

Construction

The site is graded and a layer of compacted gravel laid, often over a vapour barrier. Formwork defines the slab edges, which are thickened into an integral grade beam or footing around the perimeter to carry wall loads and, in cold climates, extend below the frost line. Steel reinforcement — rebar or welded wire mesh — and embedded plumbing and conduit are placed before the concrete is poured in a single operation, giving the "monolithic slab." Post-tensioned slabs use steel cables tightened after curing to resist cracking from soil movement.

Advantages and disadvantages

Slab foundations are inexpensive, fast to build, and resistant to termites and moisture problems of crawl spaces. Their drawbacks include poor access to under-slab plumbing, which is costly to repair; susceptibility to cracking and heaving on expansive or frost-prone soils; and, in cold regions, heat loss at the slab edge unless insulated, as required by modern energy codes (for example with frost-protected shallow foundation designs).
