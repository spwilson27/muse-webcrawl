Cryptocurrency fraud

Cryptocurrency fraud encompasses schemes that use digital currencies and related technologies to defraud victims. Common forms include Ponzi and pyramid schemes presented as investment programmes, fraudulent initial coin offerings, fake exchanges and wallets, phishing and "pig butchering" romance-investment scams, and "rug pulls" in which developers abandon a token project after taking investors' funds.

Notable cases

Among the largest schemes were OneCoin, promoted as a cryptocurrency but operated as a centralised Ponzi scheme that took in billions of dollars before its founder Ruja Ignatova disappeared in 2017, and BitConnect, whose lending platform collapsed in 2018. The collapse of the FTX exchange in 2022, followed by founder Sam Bankman-Fried's conviction for fraud, was treated as both a corporate fraud and a defining event for the industry.

Regulation and enforcement

Regulators such as the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission and Commodity Futures Trading Commission have brought numerous enforcement actions, while the pseudonymity of blockchain transfers and the cross-border nature of platforms complicate recovery of assets for victims.
