First Buddhist Council

The First Buddhist Council was a gathering of senior Buddhist monks held, according to tradition, shortly after the death (parinirvana) of the Buddha, around the 5th century BCE, at Rājagṛha (modern Rajgir) in India, sponsored by King Ajatashatru and presided over by Mahākāśyapa.

Proceedings

Tradition holds that 500 arahants assembled to preserve the Buddha's teachings. Ānanda, the Buddha's attendant, recited the discourses (sūtras), and Upāli recited the monastic discipline (vinaya). The recitations were collectively verified and accepted, forming the basis of the early Buddhist canon transmitted orally for centuries before being written down.

Historicity

Modern scholars debate the historicity of the council as described, noting that the accounts differ between traditions and were recorded later. Many accept, however, that communal recitation gatherings played a real role in preserving the early texts.
