Perameles

Perameles is a genus of marsupials of the order Peramelemorphia, commonly known as the "true" bandicoots. Members of the genus are found in Australia and include some of the best-known bandicoot species, such as the long-nosed bandicoot.

Species

Depending on classification, the genus contains around four living species: the western barred bandicoot (Perameles bougainville), the eastern barred bandicoot (P. gunnii), the long-nosed bandicoot (P. nasuta), and the golden bandicoot (P. eremiana is sometimes placed here, though often assigned to Isoodon). A fifth species, the desert bandicoot (P. eremiana in some schemes, or P. papillon), is extinct.

Characteristics

Bandicoots are small to medium-sized nocturnal omnivores with long pointed snouts, compact bodies, and strong foreclaws for digging conical holes in soil as they forage for insects, larvae, and plant material. Females have a backward-opening pouch, an adaptation that keeps soil out while digging. Gestation in bandicoots is among the shortest of any mammal — about 12 days in some species.

Conservation

Several Perameles species have suffered steep declines since European settlement owing to habitat loss and predation by introduced foxes and cats. The eastern barred bandicoot is extinct on the Australian mainland in the wild and survives through captive breeding and reintroduction programs in predator-proof reserves.
