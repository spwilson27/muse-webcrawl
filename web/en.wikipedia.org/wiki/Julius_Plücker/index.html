Julius Plücker

Julius Plücker (16 June 1801 – 22 May 1868) was a German mathematician and physicist. He made fundamental contributions to analytic geometry and was a pioneer in the study of cathode rays, which culminated in the discovery of the electron.

Work

A professor at the University of Bonn, Plücker introduced homogeneous coordinates and the method of abridged notation, developed Plücker coordinates for lines in space, and advanced the duality principle in projective geometry. In physics he studied gas discharges in vacuum tubes, observing fluorescence and the deflection of cathode rays by magnets, work continued by his student Johann Hittorf. He received the Royal Society's Copley Medal in 1866.
