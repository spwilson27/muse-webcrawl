Siegel upper half-space

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Domain of symmetric complex matrices with positive-definite imaginary part.

In mathematics, the Siegel upper half-space of degree g (or genus g) is the set of g × g symmetric matrices over the complex numbers whose imaginary part is positive definite. It was introduced by Carl Ludwig Siegel in 1939. In the case g = 1, the Siegel upper half-space is the familiar upper half-plane of complex analysis.

Definition

The Siegel upper half-space of degree g is

Hg = { Z ∈ Mg×g(ℂ) : ZT = Z, Im(Z) positive definite }

It is a complex manifold of complex dimension g(g + 1)/2.

Group action

The symplectic group Sp(2g, ℝ) acts transitively on Hg by generalized Möbius transformations, Z ↦ (AZ + B)(CZ + D)−1. The quotient of Hg by the Siegel modular group Sp(2g, ℤ) is the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension g.

Applications

Siegel upper half-space is the parameter space for abelian varieties with a principal polarization and the domain of Siegel modular forms; it also serves as a prominent example of a Hermitian symmetric space and a bounded symmetric (Siegel) domain.
