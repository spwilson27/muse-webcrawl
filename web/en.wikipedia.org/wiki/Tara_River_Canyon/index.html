Tara River Canyon

Tara River Canyon
Location: Northern Montenegro
Length: approx. 82 km
Maximum depth: approx. 1,300 m
Protection: Durmitor National Park, UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Tara River Canyon (Montenegrin: Kanjon Tare), also known as the Tara River Gorge, is the river canyon of the Tara River in Montenegro. It is roughly 82 kilometres long and reaches a depth of about 1,300 metres, making it the deepest river canyon in Europe and one of the deepest in the world, often compared with the Grand Canyon in the United States.

Geography

The Tara is formed by the confluence of the Opasanica and Veruša rivers beneath the Komovi mountains and flows northwestward before joining the Piva to form the Drina. Large parts of the canyon lie within Durmitor National Park, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1980, and the river's basin is also protected as a UNESCO biosphere reserve. The Tara's water is famously clear, earning it the nickname "the tear of Europe".

Tourism

The canyon is a popular destination for white-water rafting, with the most travelled stretch running from Brštanovica to Šćepan Polje. The canyon is spanned by the Đurđevića Tara Bridge, a five-arched concrete bridge completed in 1940 that once ranked among the largest of its kind in Europe.

Conservation

Plans to dam the Tara for hydroelectric power in the 2000s were abandoned after public protests, and the Montenegrin parliament adopted a declaration protecting the river in 2004.
