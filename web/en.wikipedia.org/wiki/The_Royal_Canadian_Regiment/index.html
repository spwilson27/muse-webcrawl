The Royal Canadian Regiment (RCR) is an infantry regiment of the Canadian Army, and the country's senior regular-force infantry regiment. Formed on 21 December 1883 as the Infantry School Corps — originally to teach militia soldiers at schools of infantry in Fredericton, St. Jean and Toronto — it passed through several titles (the Canadian Regiment of Infantry; the Royal Regiment of Canadian Infantry) before becoming The Royal Canadian Regiment in 1901. Its members are commonly called "Royals" or "RCRs," and the regiment is known by the motto Pro Patria. It was with this regiment that the Canadian Army's first Victoria Cross of the Boer War era and many later distinctions were won.

History

Companies of the regiment served in the North-West Rebellion of 1885, and it provided the core of the 2nd (Special Service) Battalion sent to the South African War in 1899, fighting notably at Paardeberg in February 1900. In the First World War the regiment served in the 7th Brigade, 3rd Canadian Division, fighting at Mount Sorrel, the Somme, Vimy Ridge, Passchendaele, Amiens and the Hundred Days. One of its soldiers, Lieutenant Milton Gregg, won the Victoria Cross in 1918; the regiment's battle honours for the war include "Ypres, 1915, '17" and "Vimy, 1917."

In the Second World War the 1st Battalion landed in Sicily in July 1943 with the 1st Canadian Division and fought up the Italian peninsula — including the Moro River and Ortona — before moving to North-West Europe in 1945. Royals served in the Korean War, where the regiment's stand at Hill 187 in May 1953 became one of its defining actions, and subsequently in Cyprus and other peacekeeping missions, the Balkans, and Afghanistan, where its battlegroups formed the backbone of several Canadian rotations in Kandahar province.

Structure

The regiment today comprises three regular battalions and one reserve battalion: 1 RCR, a mechanized infantry battalion at Petawawa, Ontario; 2 RCR, at Gagetown, New Brunswick; 3 RCR, a light infantry battalion at Petawawa that includes a parachute company; and 4 RCR, a Primary Reserve battalion headquartered in London, Ontario, which perpetuates the regiment's historical home — the regimental museum is at Wolseley Barracks in London. The regiment's colonel-in-chief was Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, from 1953 until his death in 2021; Princess Anne, the Princess Royal, succeeded in the role. Its affiliated and allied regiments include the Royal Regiment of Fusiliers in Britain.
