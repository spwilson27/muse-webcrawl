Holy trinity (cuisine)

The holy trinity in Cajun and Louisiana Creole cuisine is a combination of three aromatic vegetables — onion, bell pepper and celery — used as the flavour base for many dishes, including gumbo, jambalaya, étouffée and sauce piquante. The name is a playful reference to the Christian doctrine of the Trinity and to the Catholic heritage of French Louisiana.

Use

The vegetables are diced and cooked in oil, butter or rendered fat, often as the first step before adding a roux or other ingredients. A typical proportion is roughly two parts onion to one part each of bell pepper and celery, though recipes vary. Green bell pepper is traditional. Chef Paul Prudhomme popularised the term "holy trinity" beyond Louisiana; when garlic is added, cooks sometimes joke that it is the "pope".

Related bases

Similar aromatic foundations exist in many cuisines: French mirepoix (onion, carrot, celery), Italian soffritto, Spanish sofrito, German Suppengrün, and the Polish włoszczyzna. The Cajun trinity differs from mirepoix chiefly in substituting bell pepper for carrot, reflecting the ingredients of the Louisiana bayous.
