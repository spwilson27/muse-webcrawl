Merrie Monarch Festival

Merrie Monarch Festival
Location: Hilo, Hawai'i
First held: 1964
Held: week after Easter Sunday

The Merrie Monarch Festival is a week-long cultural festival that takes place annually in Hilo, Hawaii during the week after Easter. It honors King David Kalākaua, who was called the "Merrie Monarch" for his patronage of the arts, and is credited with reviving hula and other Hawaiian cultural practices during his reign. The festival's centerpiece is an internationally acclaimed hula competition.

History

The festival was begun in 1964 by the Hawaii Island Chamber of Commerce as an economic boost for Hilo after the devastating 1960 tsunami. Initially a civic celebration, it was transformed in the early 1970s under organizers including "Auntie" Dottie Thompson into a serious cultural event centered on hula, coinciding with the Hawaiian Renaissance. It is widely credited with helping revive interest in the Hawaiian language, chant, and traditional hula.

The competition

Hula hālau (schools) from Hawaii and the mainland United States compete over three nights at the Edith Kanaka'ole Stadium: a solo competition for Miss Aloha Hula on Thursday, group hula kahiko (ancient-style hula) on Friday, and group hula 'auana (modern-style hula) on Saturday, followed by awards. Performances are judged on chant, dance technique, expression, costuming, and adornments made from native materials.

Other events

The festival week includes a ho'olaule'a (celebration), an invitational Hawaiian arts fair, cultural demonstrations, and the Royal Parade through downtown Hilo. Tickets for the competition are in very high demand and are distributed by mail lottery.
