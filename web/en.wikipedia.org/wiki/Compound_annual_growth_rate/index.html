Compound annual growth rate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Compound annual growth rate (CAGR) is a business, economics and investing term representing the mean annualised growth rate of a quantity over a specified period of time longer than one year. It is the constant rate at which an investment (or revenue, population, or other measure) would have had to grow, compounded annually, to move from its beginning value to its ending value.

Formula

CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)1 / Number of Years − 1

For example, an investment that grows from 100 to 161 over five years has a CAGR of (161/100)1/5 − 1 ≈ 10% per year.

Uses

CAGR is widely used to compare the historical returns of investments, mutual funds, and businesses over common periods, and to summarise growth in revenue, users, or GDP. Because it is based only on the start and end values, it gives a smooth, single-figure summary suitable for presentations and league tables.

Limitations

CAGR ignores everything that happened between the endpoints: volatility, interim losses, and additional contributions or withdrawals do not affect it. It therefore does not describe risk, and it can be manipulated by choice of period. For cash-flow streams with deposits and withdrawals, measures such as the internal rate of return or money-weighted return are more appropriate. CAGR also assumes reinvestment and compounding at a constant rate, which actual returns never follow exactly.
