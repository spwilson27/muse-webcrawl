House of Tudor

House of Tudor
Country: England, Wales, Ireland
Founded: 1485
Founder: Henry VII
Final ruler: Elizabeth I (died 1603)
Monarchs: Henry VII, Henry VIII, Edward VI, Mary I, Elizabeth I

The House of Tudor was an English and Welsh royal house of Welsh origin that ruled the Kingdom of England from 1485 until 1603, succeeding the House of Plantagenet. Its first monarch, Henry VII, descended through his mother from the House of Lancaster and married Elizabeth of York, uniting the rival claims after the Wars of the Roses; the Tudor rose symbolises that union.

Reigns

Henry VII restored royal finances and authority. His son Henry VIII broke with Rome, beginning the English Reformation, and dissolved the monasteries. After the short reign of Edward VI and the Catholic restoration under Mary I, Elizabeth I's 45-year reign saw the defeat of the Spanish Armada (1588), the flourishing of English drama, and consolidation of a Protestant settlement.

End of the dynasty

Elizabeth died childless in 1603; the crown passed to James VI of Scotland, beginning Stuart rule and the union of the crowns.
