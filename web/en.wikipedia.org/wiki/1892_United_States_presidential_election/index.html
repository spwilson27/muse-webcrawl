The 1892 United States presidential election was the 27th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 8, 1892. Former Democratic President Grover Cleveland defeated incumbent Republican President Benjamin Harrison, making Cleveland the first — and for more than a century the only — president to serve two non-consecutive terms.

Background

Cleveland had narrowly lost the 1888 election in the Electoral College despite winning the popular vote. The rematch of 1892 was fought largely over the tariff: Republicans defended the high protective McKinley Tariff of 1890, while Democrats attacked it as a burden on consumers. The campaign was unusually subdued, in part because Harrison's wife Caroline was gravely ill and died two weeks before the election; both candidates curtailed personal campaigning.

Third parties

The newly formed People's Party (Populists), nominating James B. Weaver, campaigned on free silver, a graduated income tax, and government ownership of railroads. Weaver won over a million popular votes and carried five western states, the strongest third-party showing since the Civil War. The Prohibition Party and Socialist Labor Party also fielded candidates.

Results and significance

Cleveland won with 277 electoral votes to Harrison's 145, carrying the Solid South and key swing states including New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, and Indiana. His second administration would be dominated by the Panic of 1893 and the bitter fight over the gold standard, which split the Democratic Party and paved the way for William Jennings Bryan's nomination in 1896.
