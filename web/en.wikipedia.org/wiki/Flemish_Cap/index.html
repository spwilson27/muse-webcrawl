Flemish Cap

The Flemish Cap is an area of shallow waters in the North Atlantic Ocean approximately 560 km (350 miles) east of St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada. It is a plateau of about 200 m depth situated on the North American continental shelf, separated from the Grand Banks by the Flemish Pass, a channel some 1,000 m deep.

Location of the Flemish Cap in the North Atlantic

Geography

The Flemish Cap covers an area of roughly 57,000 km². Its shallow waters, warmed by the mixing of the cold Labrador Current and the warm North Atlantic Current, create conditions favourable to marine life. Because the centre of the Cap is a region of relatively weak circulation, particles and larvae tend to be retained there, which supports productive ecosystems.

Fisheries

The Flemish Cap has long been an important fishing ground for Atlantic cod, redfish, American plaice, Greenland halibut, and northern shrimp. Lying largely beyond Canada's 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone, it is regulated internationally by the Northwest Atlantic Fisheries Organization (NAFO). Overfishing in the late 20th century severely depleted several stocks, prompting moratoria and quota regimes.

Cultural references

The Flemish Cap was mentioned in the 2000 film The Perfect Storm as a fishing ground of the swordfishing fleet out of Gloucester, Massachusetts.
