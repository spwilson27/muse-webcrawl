The baptism of Jesus is an event in the life of Jesus described in the canonical Gospels, in which Jesus was baptised in the River Jordan by John the Baptist. It marks the beginning of Jesus's public ministry. Most modern scholars regard the baptism as a historical event, partly because of the criterion of embarrassment: later Christians found it awkward that the sinless Jesus submitted to John's baptism of repentance, a tension visible in Matthew's account, where John protests that he should be baptised by Jesus instead.

Gospel accounts

In Mark, the earliest account, Jesus comes from Nazareth, is baptised by John, and sees the heavens torn open, the Spirit descending like a dove, and hears a voice: "You are my Son, the Beloved". Matthew adds John's objection and Jesus's reply that it is fitting "to fulfil all righteousness"; Luke emphasises Jesus praying after the baptism; John's Gospel omits the act itself but has the Baptist testify that he saw the Spirit descend and remain on Jesus. The synoptic accounts are followed by the temptation in the wilderness.

Site

Churches traditionally located the baptism at "Bethany beyond the Jordan" on the east bank. The site of Al-Maghtas in Jordan, with Byzantine church remains, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015; the competing site of Qasr el Yahud lies on the west bank.

Commemoration

Eastern Christianity celebrates the baptism at Theophany (Epiphany), with the Great Blessing of Waters; in the Roman Catholic calendar the Feast of the Baptism of the Lord closes the Christmas season. The event is a major subject of Christian art, from Byzantine icons to Verrocchio's Baptism of Christ, on which the young Leonardo da Vinci worked.
