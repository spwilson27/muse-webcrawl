Object (grammar)

In linguistics, an object is any of several types of arguments that typically appear in the position immediately following a transitive verb. In traditional grammar, the object is the noun, pronoun, or noun phrase that is acted upon by the verb: in "She read the book", the book is the object of the verb read.

Types of object

A direct object is the entity directly acted upon by the subject (in "The dog bit the man", the man). An indirect object is an entity indirectly affected, typically the recipient or beneficiary, as in "She gave him the book", where him is the indirect object and the book the direct object. The object of a preposition (as in "on the table") is sometimes called an oblique object.

Objects across languages

Languages mark objects in different ways: by word order, by case marking (for instance the accusative case in Latin, German and Russian), or by agreement on the verb. In ergative–absolutive languages, the object of a transitive clause patterns grammatically with the subject of an intransitive clause. The distinction between subject and object is a central topic in syntax, where objects are commonly analysed as complements of the verb within the verb phrase.
