Greyhound racing

Greyhound racing is a competitive sport in which greyhounds are raced around a track, chasing a mechanically propelled lure. The sport developed from coursing in the early twentieth century and became a major gambling and spectator sport in countries including the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, and the United States.

Racing

Races are typically run over distances from about 300 to 800 metres on oval sand or grass tracks. Dogs start from traps and follow a mechanical lure resembling a hare or bone. Betting, including parimutuel wagering, has historically been central to the sport's economics.

History

The first commercial track opened in Emeryville, California, in 1919, using the mechanical lure invented by Owen Patrick Smith. The sport was introduced to Britain in 1926 at Belle Vue, Manchester, and grew rapidly between the wars.

Welfare and decline

The sport has declined in many countries amid concerns over animal welfare, injuries, and the fate of retired dogs, leading to bans or phase-outs in several jurisdictions, including much of the United States and, announced in 2024, New Zealand.
