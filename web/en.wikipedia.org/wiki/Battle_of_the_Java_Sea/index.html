Battle of the Java Sea

The Battle of the Java Sea was a decisive naval battle of the Pacific theatre of the Second World War, fought on 27 February 1942 in the Java Sea between an Allied striking force of Dutch, American, British and Australian warships and the Imperial Japanese Navy. The Allied force, commanded by Dutch Rear Admiral Karel Doorman, attempted to intercept a Japanese convoy invading Java and was virtually annihilated.

Background

With Malaya and the Philippines falling, the ABDA command scraped together its remaining ships to defend Java, the last major Allied position in Southeast Asia. Doorman's squadron sortied from Surabaya to attack the eastern invasion convoy approaching from the Makassar Strait.

The battle

The fleets sighted each other on the afternoon of 27 February. In a prolonged gunnery and torpedo action, Japanese "Long Lance" torpedoes proved devastating: the destroyer Kortenaer was sunk, and during the confused night action the light cruisers De Ruyter (Doorman's flagship) and Java were torpedoed and lost with most of their crews, including Doorman.

Aftermath

The surviving Allied ships were destroyed piecemeal in the following days at the Battle of Sunda Strait and the Second Battle of the Java Sea. Java surrendered on 8 March 1942, completing the Japanese conquest of the Dutch East Indies. The wrecks of De Ruyter and Java were discovered in 2002, but were later found to have been largely removed by illegal salvage.
