Quarter days

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In British and Irish tradition, the quarter days are the four dates in each year on which servants were hired, school terms started, and rents and other quarterly payments fell due. They correspond roughly to the solstices and equinoxes and to traditional religious festivals.

The English quarter days

DayDateFestival
Lady Day25 MarchFeast of the Annunciation
Midsummer Day24 JuneNativity of St John the Baptist
Michaelmas29 SeptemberFeast of St Michael and All Angels
Christmas Day25 DecemberChristmas

Lady Day was the first day of the legal year in England until the calendar reform of 1752, which is why the United Kingdom tax year still begins in early April (Lady Day plus the eleven days dropped from the calendar, plus one for the leap year 1800). Michaelmas gave its name to the autumn academic and legal term.

Scotland and Ireland

The traditional Scottish term days were Candlemas (2 February), Whitsunday (15 May), Lammas (1 August) and Martinmas (11 November). In Ireland the quarter days followed the old Celtic festivals: Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasa and Samhain. The intermediate cross-quarter days fall between the quarter days proper.
