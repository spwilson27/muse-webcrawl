The Israel Defense Forces (IDF; Hebrew: צְבָא הַהֲגָנָה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, Tsva ha-Hagana le-Yisra'el) are the combined military forces of the State of Israel, consisting of three service branches: the Israeli Ground Forces, the Israeli Air Force, and the Israeli Navy. It is the sole military wing of the Israeli security forces.

History

The IDF was formally established on 26 May 1948, two weeks after the proclamation of the State of Israel, by order of Defense Minister David Ben-Gurion. It was built principally on the Haganah, the main Jewish paramilitary organisation of the Mandate period, and absorbed or disbanded rival groups such as the Irgun and Lehi after confrontations including the Altalena affair. The IDF fought in all of Israel's major wars: the 1948 War of Independence, the 1956 Sinai campaign, the Six-Day War of 1967, the War of Attrition, the Yom Kippur War of 1973, and the Lebanon wars of 1982 and 2006, as well as repeated operations in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank.

Organisation

The IDF is a conscript military with a small standing force and a large reserve, mobilised in emergencies. It is headed by the Chief of the General Staff (Ramatkal), who reports to the Minister of Defense and is subordinate to the cabinet. Regional commands cover the north (Northern Command), centre (Central Command), and south (Southern Command), alongside the Home Front Command created after the 1991 Gulf War. Military intelligence is provided by Aman, and elite units include Sayeret Matkal, Shayetet 13, and Unit 8200, noted for signals intelligence and cyber operations.

Conscription and society

Most Jewish Israeli citizens, and Druze and Circassian men, are conscripted at age 18 — men serving about 32 months and women about 24 — followed by reserve duty. Exemptions for ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) yeshiva students and the non-conscription of most Arab citizens have been persistent subjects of political controversy. The IDF describes itself as a "people's army," and military service plays a major role in Israeli society and in immigrant absorption.

Equipment and doctrine

The IDF operates largely US-supplied aircraft such as the F-35, alongside domestically developed systems including the Merkava main battle tank, the Iron Dome air-defence system, and a substantial unmanned aerial vehicle fleet. Israel is widely believed to possess nuclear weapons, which it neither confirms nor denies under a policy of deliberate ambiguity. Its doctrine emphasises early warning, rapid mobilisation, transferring combat to enemy territory, and achieving decisive results in short campaigns.
