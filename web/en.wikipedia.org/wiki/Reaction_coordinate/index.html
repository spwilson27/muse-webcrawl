Reaction coordinate

In chemistry, a reaction coordinate is an abstract one-dimensional coordinate that represents progress along a reaction pathway. It is typically a geometric parameter — such as a bond length, bond angle, or some combination of them — that changes monotonically during the conversion of reactants into products.

Description

A reaction coordinate diagram plots the potential energy (or free energy) of a system against the reaction coordinate. Reactants and products appear as minima (valleys) on this curve, while the highest point along the minimum-energy path between them corresponds to the transition state. The energy difference between the reactants and the transition state is the activation energy of the reaction.

In molecular dynamics and computational chemistry, identifying a good reaction coordinate is central to methods such as umbrella sampling and metadynamics, which compute free-energy profiles along a chosen collective variable. In multidimensional systems the reaction coordinate traces the minimum energy path on the potential energy surface, connecting reactant and product valleys through a first-order saddle point.

Related concepts

Reaction coordinates are closely related to the concept of the intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC), defined in mass-weighted Cartesian coordinates as the steepest-descent path from the transition state. For multi-step reactions, the diagram shows a sequence of minima (intermediates) and maxima (transition states).
