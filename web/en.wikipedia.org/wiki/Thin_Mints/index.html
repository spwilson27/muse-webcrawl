Thin Mints

Thin Mints are chocolate-mint cookies sold by the Girl Scouts of the USA as part of the annual Girl Scout Cookies programme. A Thin Mint is a round, thin, crisp chocolate wafer cookie flavoured with peppermint oil and enrobed in a dark chocolate coating. Thin Mints are consistently the best-selling Girl Scout cookie variety, accounting for roughly a quarter of all Girl Scout cookie sales, and are one of the best-selling cookie brands in the United States overall.

Description

The two licensed Girl Scout cookie bakers — ABC Bakers and Little Brownie Bakers — produce Thin Mints to slightly differing recipes under the same name, one of the only varieties (along with the caramel-coconut Samoas/Caramel deLites) made by both bakers under a shared name. The cookies are sold in the characteristic green box during the cookie season, traditionally early in the year. The recipe is vegan under current formulations, having been reformulated when the bakers removed partially hydrogenated oils and later adjusted ingredients.

History

Girl Scout cookies have been sold since 1917, and the programme became commercial in the 1930s. A chocolate mint cookie appeared in the line-up by 1939 under the name "Cooky-Mints"; it passed through the names "Chocolate Mint" and "Thin Mint" before settling on "Thin Mints" in 1963. Their popularity has made them a cultural fixture: they are frequently eaten frozen, a habit endorsed in polls and marketing, and they have inspired numerous retail imitations and a Keebler counterpart (Grasshopper cookies). Each year Americans buy on the order of 200 million boxes of Girl Scout cookies, with Thin Mints leading the varieties, ahead of Samoas/Caramel deLites and Tagalongs/Peanut Butter Patties.

Nutrition

A serving of four Thin Mints (about 32 g) contains roughly 160 kilocalories, 8 g of fat, 22 g of carbohydrate and 10 g of sugars, values that vary slightly between the two bakers.
