ArtistsJean-Michel Basquiat and Al Diaz
Active1977–1979 (tag used into 1980)
LocationLower Manhattan, New York City
Meaning"Same old shit" (from "same old")

SAMO (pronounced "sam-oh") was a graffiti tag and conceptual art project created in New York City in the late 1970s by teenagers Jean-Michel Basquiat and Al Diaz. Written in stark marker and spray paint across walls in SoHo and the Lower East Side of Manhattan, the SAMO texts consisted of cryptic, poetic aphorisms signed "SAMO©" — an abbreviation of the phrase "same old shit," coined from the pair's joking slang for marijuana ("same old").

The epigrams

Unlike the elaborate lettering of subway graffiti, SAMO pieces were terse philosophical one-liners, such as "SAMO© AS AN ALTERNATIVE 2 PLAYING ART BY THE 'RADICAL CHIC' SECT ON DADDY'S $ FUNDS," "SAMO© SAVES IDIOTS AND GONE-UHDS," and "SAMO© IS DEAD" — the last sprayed around the city in 1979–80 when Basquiat ended the project. The tag's mystery attracted attention from the downtown art press; an article in The Village Voice in December 1978 first identified the authors, helping launch Basquiat's transition from street writer to gallery artist.

Aftermath

Basquiat went on to become one of the most famous painters of the 1980s, retaining the epigrammatic text and crown imagery of SAMO in his canvases. Diaz continued in music and art, and in later decades publicly revisited and re-inscribed SAMO texts. The project is now seen as a bridge between graffiti, conceptual art, and the downtown scene that produced the East Village art boom.
