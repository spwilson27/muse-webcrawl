Erasmus+

Established: 2014 (successor to Erasmus, 1987)
Administered by: European Commission / EACEA
Budget (2021–27): €26.2 billion

Erasmus+ is the European Union's programme to support education, training, youth and sport in Europe. Launched in 2014, it brought together the earlier Erasmus student exchange scheme (founded in 1987 and named after the humanist Desiderius Erasmus), the Comenius, Leonardo da Vinci and Grundtvig programmes, Youth in Action, and several international cooperation schemes under a single framework.

Structure

The programme is organised around key actions covering learning mobility of individuals, cooperation among organisations and institutions, and support for policy development. It funds study and traineeship periods abroad for higher-education students, exchanges for school pupils and vocational learners, staff mobility, volunteering through the European Solidarity Corps' related actions, and partnerships between institutions. The 2021–2027 programme has a budget of €26.2 billion, nearly double that of its predecessor, with emphasis on inclusion, digital and green transitions.

Impact

By its 30th anniversary in 2017, the Erasmus programmes had supported more than nine million participants. Studies associate participation with improved employability and language skills, and the programme is widely regarded as one of the EU's most popular and symbolic initiatives.
