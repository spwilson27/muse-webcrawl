Nevado del Huila is a stratovolcano in the Cordillera Central of the Colombian Andes, located at the junction of the departments of Huila, Tolima, and Cauca. At 5,364 metres (17,598 ft), it is the highest volcano in Colombia. Its snow-capped summit comprises several volcanic cones aligned along a north–south trend, and it lies within the Nevado del Huila National Natural Park.

Eruptive history

The volcano was long considered dormant, with no confirmed eruptions in historical times before the 21st century. Seismic activity increased in 2007, and eruptions occurred in April 2007 and again in November 2008. The 2008 eruption generated lahars (volcanic mudflows) that swept down the Páez River valley, prompting the evacuation of thousands of residents of the surrounding region, including the town of Belalcázar. The area had previously suffered catastrophe in 1994, when an earthquake-triggered landslide buried the village of Páez.

The volcano is monitored by the Colombian Geological Service (Servicio Geológico Colombiano) through its observatory in Popayán.
