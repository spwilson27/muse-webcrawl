Rangefinder

A rangefinder (also range finder or telemeter) is a device used to measure the distance from the observer to a target. Originally developed for surveying and for military gunnery, rangefinders are now widely used in photography, golf, hunting, forestry and construction. Methods range from optical triangulation to time-of-flight measurement using lasers or sound.

A modern laser rangefinder

Optical rangefinders

Coincidence and stereoscopic rangefinders, developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries (notably by Barr and Stroud), use triangulation: two windows at the ends of a long base tube view the target, and the parallax angle yields the distance. Such instruments directed naval and coastal artillery in both World Wars, with bases up to tens of metres long on battleships. Smaller versions were built into rangefinder cameras such as the Leica and Contax, where a coupled rangefinder focuses the lens by aligning two superimposed images.

Laser and other electronic rangefinders

Laser rangefinders emit a pulse of laser light and compute distance from the time the reflection takes to return. They range from pocket devices accurate to a millimetre for surveying and golf to military systems capable of measuring targets many kilometres away. Radar, sonar and ultrasonic devices use the same time-of-flight principle in other wavebands. Phase-shift and interferometric instruments achieve very high precision over shorter distances.

Applications

Rangefinders are used to lay guns and designate targets in the military; by golfers and hunters to judge shot distance; by surveyors and foresters for measurement without physical access; and in autofocus and robotics systems. Stadiametric rangefinders estimate distance from the apparent size of a target of known height and are still printed on many rifle scopes and binocular reticles.
