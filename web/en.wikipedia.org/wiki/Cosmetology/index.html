Cosmetology

[Image: Cosmetology]

Cosmetology (from Greek kosmētikos, "beautifying") is the study and application of beauty treatment. Branches of speciality include hairstyling, skincare, cosmetics, manicures and pedicures, non-permanent hair removal such as waxing and sugaring, and permanent hair removal processes such as electrology and intense pulsed light (IPL).

Cosmetologists

A cosmetologist is an expert in applying cosmetic procedures, techniques and treatments to enhance a person's appearance. In many jurisdictions, including most states in the United States, cosmetologists must be licensed after completing an accredited course of training, typically between 1,000 and 1,600 hours, and passing examinations administered by a state board of cosmetology.

Careers

Cosmetologists work in salons, spas, hotels, resorts and cruise ships, and in the entertainment and fashion industries. Specialisations include hair stylists and colourists, barbers, manicurists, estheticians (skincare specialists) and make-up artists. Related occupations include shampooers, who assist stylists, and electrologists.

History

The use of cosmetics dates to ancient Egypt and other early civilisations. The modern beauty industry developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries with figures such as Madam C. J. Walker and Elizabeth Arden, who built businesses around hair and skincare products and salon services. Professional licensing and cosmetology schools became widespread in the twentieth century.
