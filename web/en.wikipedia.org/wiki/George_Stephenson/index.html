George Stephenson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: George Stephenson's Rocket locomotive of 1829 with its angled cylinder and tall chimney]
Stephenson's Rocket.

George Stephenson (1781–1848) was an English civil and mechanical engineer who built the first public inter-city railway line in the world to use steam locomotives — the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830). Renowned as the \'Father of Railways\', he developed the standard gauge of 4 ft 8½ in used by most of the world\'s railways, and with his son Robert built the celebrated locomotive Rocket.

Early life

Born to an illiterate mining family in Wylam, Northumberland, Stephenson worked as a colliery brakesman and taught himself to read as an adult. By 1812 he was engine-wright at Killingworth colliery, where he built his first locomotive, Blücher (1814), and a miners\' safety lamp.

Railway pioneer

Stephenson surveyed and engineered the Stockton and Darlington Railway (1825) — the first public steam railway — and then the Liverpool and Manchester (1830), overcoming Chat Moss bog and the Rainhill Trials (1829), where Rocket\'s multi-tubular boiler design won decisively. He advised railways across Britain and Europe.

Legacy

Stephenson\'s gauge choice, locomotive works in Newcastle (Robert Stephenson and Company, 1823 — the first locomotive factory), and advocacy shaped the railway age. He retired to Tapton House, Chesterfield, dying in 1848; a statue stands at the National Railway Museum, York.
