Ford GT40

Ford GT40
Manufacturer: Ford Motor Company
Production: 1964–1969
Class: Sports prototype / sports car
Engine: 4.2–7.0 L V8

The Ford GT40 is a high-performance endurance racing car built by Ford in the 1960s. It was created after Henry Ford II's failed attempt to buy Ferrari, with the goal of defeating Ferrari at the 24 Hours of Le Mans. The GT40 won Le Mans four consecutive times, from 1966 to 1969.

Development

The car was developed in England by Ford Advanced Vehicles under Roy Lunn, drawing on the Lola Mk6. Its name derives from its height of just 40 inches (1.02 m). Early cars used 4.2- and 4.7-litre V8 engines; the Mk II of 1966 used a 7.0-litre (427 cu in) big-block V8. Carroll Shelby's team took over the racing programme and brought the car its first Le Mans victory in 1966, a famous 1-2-3 finish.

Racing record

Bruce McLaren and Chris Amon won the rain-affected 1966 race; Dan Gurney and A. J. Foyt won in the Mk IV in 1967, the only all-American victory — American car, team and drivers. John Wyer's Gulf-sponsored team then won in 1968 and 1969 with the same chassis, 1075, driven in 1969 by Jacky Ickx and Jackie Oliver.

Legacy

The GT40 became one of the most celebrated racing cars ever built. Ford revived the concept with the Ford GT of 2005–2006 and again in 2016, the latter winning its class at Le Mans fifty years after the original triumph.
