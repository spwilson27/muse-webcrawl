William IV

William IV (William Henry; 21 August 1765 – 20 June 1837) was King of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland and of Hanover from 26 June 1830 until his death.

Reign: 26 June 1830 – 20 June 1837
Predecessor: George IV
Successor: Victoria
Born: 21 August 1765, Buckingham Palace
Spouse: Adelaide of Saxe-Meiningen

Naval career

The third son of George III, William served in the Royal Navy from age 13, seeing action in the American Revolutionary War and rising to admiral. Known as the Sailor King, he lived for many years with actress Dorothea Jordan, with whom he had ten children.

Reign

Succeeding his brother George IV, his reign saw the Reform Act 1832, the abolition of slavery in most of the British Empire (1833), factory reform, and reform of the Poor Law. He was the last British king of Hanover in personal union, as Hanover's Salic law barred his niece Victoria from its throne.
