Imam Shamil

Imam Shamil (Avar: Шейх Шамил; 26 June 1797 – 4 February 1871) was the political, military, and spiritual leader of the Muslim tribes of the Northern Caucasus who led armed resistance against the Russian Empire during the Caucasian War. He is commonly referred to as the third Imam of the Caucasian Imamate (1840–1859), succeeding Ghazi Muhammad and Hamzat Bek.

Early life

Shamil was born in the village of Gimry, in present-day Dagestan, into an Avar family. He studied Islamic law, Arabic, logic and rhetoric, and became a follower of the Naqshbandi Sufi order. He fought beside his friend Ghazi Muhammad, the first imam, and was wounded at the siege of Gimry in 1832, escaping when nearly all his companions were killed.

Leadership of the Imamate

Elected imam in 1834, Shamil built the Imamate into a disciplined state in the mountains of Dagestan and Chechnya, organising a standing army of murids, taxation, courts and administration under Islamic law. For twenty-five years his guerrilla forces resisted vastly larger Russian armies, winning notable victories such as the relief of Akhulgo (1839) — from which he escaped after a famous siege — and the 1845 campaign that destroyed a Russian column under Prince Vorontsov at Dargo.

Capture and later life

Worn down by Russia's massive scorched-earth offensives under Prince Baryatinsky, and losing support among war-weary mountaineers, Shamil surrendered at Gunib on 25 August 1859. He was received with honour in St. Petersburg by Tsar Alexander II and later settled in Kaluga. In 1869 he was allowed to make the pilgrimage to Mecca; he died in Medina in 1871 and was buried in the Jannat al-Baqi cemetery.

Shamil remains a national hero in Dagestan and Chechnya, and his resistance was romanticised across Europe, where he was often compared to earlier anti-colonial leaders.
