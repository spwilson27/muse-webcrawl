Paramita

SanskritPāramitā
MeaningPerfection; that which has reached the other shore
TraditionsMahayana (six or ten), Theravada (ten pāramī)

Pāramitā (Sanskrit; Pali: pāramī) is a Buddhist term often translated as "perfection". It describes the virtues perfected by a bodhisattva on the path to Buddhahood. In Mahayana Buddhism the six paramitas are central to the bodhisattva path.

The six paramitas

The six perfections are: (1) dāna, generosity; (2) śīla, morality and discipline; (3) kṣānti, patience; (4) vīrya, energy and diligence; (5) dhyāna, meditation and concentration; and (6) prajñā, wisdom — the perfection of understanding emptiness (śūnyatā), elaborated in the Prajñāpāramitā sutras.

The ten paramitas

Some Mahayana lists, such as in the Daśabhūmika Sūtra, add four more: skilful means (upāya), aspiration (praṇidhāna), power (bala) and knowledge (jñāna), each associated with one of the ten bodhisattva grounds (bhūmis). Theravada Buddhism lists ten pāramī, including renunciation, truthfulness, determination, loving-kindness and equanimity.

Interpretation

Folk etymology derives pāramitā from pāra (the far shore) and ita (gone), suggesting "gone to the other shore" — crossing from samsara to nirvana. Scholars note the term may derive from parama, "highest".
