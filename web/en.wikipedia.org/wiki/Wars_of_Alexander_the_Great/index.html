The wars of Alexander the Great were fought by King Alexander III of Macedon, known as Alexander the Great, first against the Achaemenid Persian Empire under Darius III, then against local chieftains and warlords as far east as Punjab, India. By the time of his death, Alexander had conquered the entire Persian Empire, the largest empire in the ancient world up to that point, in addition to the territories of his father, Philip II, which already included Macedonia and much of mainland Greece.

Background

In 336 BC, Alexander succeeded his father Philip II, who had united most of Greece under Macedonian hegemony in the League of Corinth and was preparing an invasion of Persia. After suppressing revolts in Thrace, Illyria, and Greece — including the destruction of Thebes in 335 BC — Alexander crossed the Hellespont into Asia in the spring of 334 BC with an army of roughly 40,000 infantry and 5,000 cavalry.

Conquest of the Persian Empire

Alexander's first victory in Asia came at the Battle of the Granicus (334 BC), after which much of western Anatolia submitted. He next defeated Darius III in person at the Battle of Issus (333 BC), capturing the Persian royal family. Turning south, Alexander besieged and captured Tyre (332 BC) after a seven-month siege and took Gaza, after which Egypt welcomed him as a liberator; there he founded Alexandria. In 331 BC he marched into Mesopotamia and decisively defeated Darius again at the Battle of Gaugamela, effectively ending Achaemenid rule. Babylon, Susa, and Persepolis fell in succession, and Darius was murdered by his own satrap Bessus in 330 BC.

Central Asia and India

Alexander pursued Bessus into Bactria and Sogdiana, fighting a difficult guerrilla war from 329 to 327 BC, during which he founded numerous garrison cities named Alexandria and married the Bactrian noblewoman Roxana. In 326 BC he invaded India, defeating King Porus at the Battle of the Hydaspes. His exhausted troops mutinied at the Hyphasis (Beas) River, refusing to march farther east, forcing Alexander to turn back. He led part of the army through the Gedrosian Desert while his fleet under Nearchus sailed along the coast.

Death and aftermath

Alexander died in Babylon in June 323 BC, aged 32, leaving no clear successor. His empire was divided among his generals, the Diadochi, whose wars produced the Hellenistic kingdoms of the Ptolemies in Egypt, the Seleucids in Asia, and the Antigonids in Macedon.
