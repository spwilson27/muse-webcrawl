William Lamb (May 7, 1835 – March 23, 1909) was an American newspaper editor, politician, and Confederate Army officer who commanded the garrison of Fort Fisher, North Carolina, during the American Civil War. Under his direction Fort Fisher grew into the largest earthwork fortification in the Confederacy, protecting the port of Wilmington and the vital blockade-running trade that supplied the Army of Northern Virginia.

Early life

Lamb was born in Norfolk, Virginia, and educated at the College of William & Mary. He returned to Norfolk and became editor of the Southern Argus newspaper, and was active in the Virginia militia before the war.

Civil War service

Lamb entered Confederate service with the Woodis Rifles of Norfolk, then was appointed major of artillery and assigned to the defences of the Cape Fear River. In July 1862 he took command of the small battery at Confederate Point that became Fort Fisher. Over the next two and a half years Lamb supervised its transformation into a massive L-shaped earthwork mounting more than forty heavy guns, earning it the nickname the "Gibraltar of the South".

Lamb's garrison repelled the first Union attack in December 1864 (the First Battle of Fort Fisher), in which the Union expedition — hampered by the failure of a powder-boat explosion and the hesitation of General Benjamin Butler — withdrew. In January 1865 a much larger Union force under General Alfred Terry and Admiral David Dixon Porter attacked again; after a three-day bombardment and desperate hand-to-hand fighting in the Second Battle of Fort Fisher, the fort fell on January 15, 1865. Lamb, wounded during the final assault, was captured. The loss of Fort Fisher closed Wilmington, the Confederacy's last major port.

Later life

After the war Lamb returned to Norfolk, where he became a prominent civic figure: he served as mayor of Norfolk, was active in the Democratic Party, and worked as a journalist and orator. He wrote and lectured extensively on the defence of Fort Fisher and is commemorated at the Fort Fisher State Historic Site.
