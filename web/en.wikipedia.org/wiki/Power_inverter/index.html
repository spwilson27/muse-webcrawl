Power inverter

A power inverter, or simply inverter, is a power electronic device or circuitry that changes direct current (DC) to alternating current (AC). Inverters are essential wherever AC equipment must run from DC sources such as batteries or solar panels.

Operation

An inverter uses semiconductor switches (transistors such as MOSFETs or IGBTs) to chop the DC input and alternate its polarity, producing an AC waveform. Filters smooth the output. Depending on design, the output may approximate a square wave, a modified sine wave, or a pure sine wave suitable for sensitive electronics.

Applications

Inverters are used in solar photovoltaic systems to feed power into homes and the grid, in uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), in electric vehicle drivetrains (where they also control the motor), in variable-frequency drives for industrial motors, in induction heating, and in portable units that let vehicle batteries power household appliances.

Types

Stand-alone inverters power isolated loads from batteries; grid-tie inverters synchronise with the utility supply and must match its voltage, frequency and phase, shutting down when the grid fails (anti-islanding). Microinverters are mounted on individual solar panels, performing conversion and maximum power point tracking per module.
