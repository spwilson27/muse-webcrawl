Cartwheel penny

The cartwheel penny and cartwheel twopence were large copper coins struck in 1797 at Matthew Boulton's Soho Mint in Birmingham under contract to the British government. Their nickname comes from their great size and the broad raised rim that surrounds the design like a cartwheel.

Design and production

The penny weighed one ounce (about 28 grams) and measured roughly 36 mm across; the twopence weighed two ounces. Their intrinsic copper value was intended to match their face value in order to deter counterfeiting, a chronic problem with earlier regal copper coinage. The coins bear the bust of George III and a figure of Britannia, and were among the first British coins produced by steam power.
