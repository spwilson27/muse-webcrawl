City of London

The City of London, widely referred to simply as the City, is a city, ceremonial county, and local government district that contains the historic centre of London, England. It is known colloquially as the Square Mile, containing the United Kingdom's financial services industry, centred historically on the Bank of England.

History

The City occupies the site of Roman Londinium, founded around AD 47–50. It survived the Norman Conquest with its privileges intact and grew into England's principal port and commercial centre. Much of the medieval City was destroyed in the Great Fire of London in 1666 and rebuilt, with Sir Christopher Wren's St Paul's Cathedral as its centrepiece.

Governance

The City is governed by the City of London Corporation, headed by the Lord Mayor of London, and retains its own police force, the City of London Police. Its electoral system preserves a business vote: non-residential organisations appoint voters, so workers outnumber residents on the electoral roll.

Finance

The City is one of the world's leading financial centres, hosting the London Stock Exchange, Lloyd's of London, and hundreds of banks and insurers. Its daytime working population is several hundred thousand, vastly exceeding its small resident population.
