Alpha Mensae (abbreviated α Men) is the brightest star in the southern constellation of Mensa. With an apparent visual magnitude of about 5.09, it is only just visible to the naked eye under dark skies. Based on parallax measurements, it lies about 33 light-years from the Sun, making it one of the nearer solar-type stars.

Properties

Alpha Mensae is a main-sequence star of spectral type G7 V, very similar to the Sun but slightly cooler and less massive, with roughly 96% of the Sun's mass and a somewhat smaller radius. Its luminosity is about 80% of solar. Spectroscopic estimates place its age at roughly ten billion years, considerably older than the Sun, and its rotation period is slow, around 32 days. The star has a modest level of chromospheric activity consistent with its age.

Companion

Alpha Mensae has a common-proper-motion companion, a red dwarf of spectral class M3.5–M6.5 (designated Gliese 231 B), separated from the primary by several arcseconds — a projected distance of a few tens of astronomical units. The pair forms a wide binary system moving together through the galaxy.
