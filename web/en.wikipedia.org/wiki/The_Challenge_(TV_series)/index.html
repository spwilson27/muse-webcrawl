The Challenge is an American reality competition television series on MTV that is a spin-off of the reality shows The Real World and Road Rules. It premiered on April 20, 1998, as Road Rules: All Stars, was retitled Real World/Road Rules Challenge, and took its current shortened title in 2010. It is one of the longest-running reality series in television history.

Format

Each season, cast members — originally drawn from The Real World and Road Rules, and later from other MTV and international reality franchises — live together in a foreign location and compete in physically and mentally demanding team, pair, or individual challenges. Losing players face elimination rounds, and the season typically culminates in a multi-day final challenge with a large cash prize, historically up to $1 million. Since 2005 the show has been hosted by BMX rider T. J. Lavin, noted for his blunt catchphrase that he "hates quitters."

Notable contestants and spin-offs

Long-running competitors include Johnny "Bananas" Devenanzio, the show's most decorated champion; Chris "C.T." Tamburello; Wes Bergmann; and Jordan Wiseley. The franchise has spawned spin-offs including The Challenge: Champs vs. Stars, the Paramount+ series The Challenge: All Stars, and international editions in the United Kingdom, Australia, and Argentina, as well as the CBS edition The Challenge: USA.

Production

The series is produced by Bunim/Murray Productions. Later seasons have incorporated elaborate stunt work and international locations from Iceland to South Africa, and the show is frequently cited — like other long-running competition formats — as a defining example of the reality-competition genre that reshaped cable television in the 2000s.
