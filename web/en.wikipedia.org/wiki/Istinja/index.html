Istinja

Istinja (Arabic: استنجاء) is an Islamic term for the act of cleansing the private parts with water after urinating or defecating, and by extension a part of Islamic toilet etiquette and hygienic jurisprudence (fiqh). It is performed so that the worshipper is in a state of purity before prayer.

Practice

Istinja is conventionally performed with water using the left hand. Where water is not available, cleansing with solid materials such as stones, pebbles, or toilet tissue is called istijmar. Islamic sources recommend an odd number of stones (at least three) for istijmar and discourage the use of certain materials such as bones or dung. Entering the toilet with the left foot, avoiding facing the qibla, and refraining from speech while relieving oneself are among the associated etiquettes.

Rulings

Jurists differ over whether istinja is obligatory or recommended, and over whether tissue alone is sufficient when water is available. The requirement of ritual purity in Islam, elaborated in works on tahara (purification), makes the practice a routine part of daily life in Muslim-majority societies, where toilets are commonly fitted with a water sprayer or a jug (often called a lota in South Asia).
