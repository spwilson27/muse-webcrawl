Thévenin's theorem

Thévenin's theorem states that any linear electrical network containing only voltage sources, current sources and resistances can be replaced, at terminals A–B, by an equivalent combination of a voltage source Vth in series with a resistance Rth.

Calculation

Vth is the open-circuit voltage at the terminals. Rth is the equivalent resistance at the terminals with all independent sources turned off (voltage sources short-circuited, current sources open-circuited). The theorem was derived by Hermann von Helmholtz in 1853 and independently by Léon Charles Thévenin in 1883, for whom it is named.

Applications

The theorem simplifies circuit analysis, especially for studying how a network responds to different loads, and is fundamental in electronics and power engineering. Its dual is Norton's theorem, which uses an equivalent current source in parallel with a resistance.
