Bata Corporation (in the Czech Republic and Slovakia styled Baťa) is a multinational footwear, apparel, and fashion accessories manufacturer and retailer headquartered in Lausanne, Switzerland. Founded in 1894 in Zlín, Moravia (then part of Austria-Hungary), it grew into one of the world's largest shoemakers, selling more than a million pairs of shoes per day at its peak through thousands of retail stores in dozens of countries.

History

The company was founded by Tomáš Baťa with his siblings Anna and Antonín, using mechanized production at a time when shoemaking was still largely a craft trade. Tomáš Baťa pioneered the mass production of affordable canvas shoes, especially during the economic hardship of the First World War, and built entire industrial towns — "Bata towns" — around his factories, complete with worker housing, schools, and hospitals.

Expansion and exile

After Tomáš Baťa's death in a plane crash in 1932, his half-brother Jan Antonín Baťa led the company. Anticipating the Second World War and Nazi occupation, the family shifted operations abroad, notably to Canada, where Thomas J. Bata, the founder's son, rebuilt the business from Batawa, Ontario. The Czechoslovak assets were nationalized after the war.

Modern company

Today Bata operates retail networks across Europe, Asia, Africa, and Latin America, with strong positions in markets such as India. The company remains family-owned and is known for affordable, durable footwear and for the satellite towns and modernist architecture associated with its Zlín origins.
