Alexander I (medieval Gaelic: Alaxandair mac Maíl Coluim; c. 1078 – 23 April 1124), posthumously nicknamed "the Fierce", was King of Scotland from 1107 until his death. He was the fifth son of Malcolm III and Saint Margaret of Scotland, and succeeded his brother Edgar.

Reign

Alexander was a devout king who continued his mother's work of church reform. He founded the Augustinian priory at Scone, the coronation place of Scottish kings, and established an Augustinian house on the island of Inchcolm in the Firth of Forth, according to tradition after being driven ashore there in a storm. He appointed bishops including his mother's chaplain Turgot to St Andrews, although his attempts to resist the metropolitan claims of Canterbury and York over the Scottish church brought him into disputes with the English church.

His epithet "the Fierce" reflects vigorous defence of his kingdom: he campaigned against rebellious magnates in Moray and the north and suppressed unrest ruthlessly. Under the terms of his succession arrangements, his brother David held extensive southern territories as an appanage, effectively dividing the kingdom during Alexander's reign.

Marriage and death

Around 1107 Alexander married Sybilla of Normandy, an illegitimate daughter of Henry I of England. The marriage was childless. Alexander died at Stirling in April 1124 and was buried at Dunfermline Abbey; he was succeeded by his brother David I.
