Crown-of-thorns starfish

The crown-of-thorns starfish (Acanthaster planci and related species) is a large sea star covered in long, venomous spines, found on coral reefs throughout the Indo-Pacific. Adults may reach 25–35 cm in diameter and commonly have more than a dozen arms. It feeds on coral polyps and is notorious for population outbreaks that devastate reefs.

Feeding

The starfish everts its stomach through its mouth onto living coral, digesting the tissue externally and leaving behind a white scar of bare skeleton. A single adult can consume several square metres of coral per year; during outbreaks, thousands of starfish can strip most of the coral cover from a reef.

Outbreaks and control

Outbreaks on Australia's Great Barrier Reef have occurred in waves since the 1960s and are a major cause of coral loss alongside bleaching. Proposed drivers include nutrient run-off increasing larval survival and removal of predators such as the giant triton. Control programmes cull starfish by injecting them with bile salts or vinegar. The spines can inflict painful stings on humans.
