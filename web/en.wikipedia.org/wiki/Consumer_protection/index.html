Consumer protection

Consumer protection is the practice of safeguarding buyers of goods and services, and the public, against unfair practices in the marketplace. Measures are designed to prevent businesses from engaging in fraud or specified unfair practices, and to protect the weaker party in transactions where information and bargaining power are unequal.

History

Modern consumer protection grew with industrialisation and mass markets: food and drug safety laws (e.g., the U.S. Pure Food and Drug Act 1906), the consumer movement associated with Ralph Nader in the 1960s, President Kennedy's 1962 "Consumer Bill of Rights" (safety, information, choice, and to be heard), and later EU consumer law.

Areas

  • Product safety and liability; recalls
  • Unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices; advertising standards — see comparative advertising
  • Consumer credit, debt collection, and financial services (e.g., the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, created 2011)
  • Privacy and data protection
  • Cooling-off periods, warranties, and redress mechanisms

Institutions

Enforcers include the U.S. Federal Trade Commission, the UK Competition and Markets Authority and Trading Standards, and national bodies coordinated internationally through ICPEN. In the UK the Consumer Rights Act 2015 is a central statute.
