Feigenbaum constants

The Feigenbaum constants are two mathematical constants that appear in the study of bifurcations of nonlinear dynamical systems. They are named after the physicist Mitchell Feigenbaum, who discovered them in 1975 while studying the logistic map.

The first constant δ

As the parameter of a system such as the logistic map increases, its behaviour undergoes a cascade of period-doubling bifurcations on the way to chaos. Feigenbaum found that the ratio of the intervals between successive bifurcations approaches a universal limit,

δ = 4.669201609102990671853203821578…

regardless of the particular one-dimensional map (provided it has a single quadratic maximum). The same constant governs real physical systems — fluid convection, electronic oscillators, chemical reactions — that follow the period-doubling route to chaos.

The second constant α

The second constant,

α = 2.502907875095892822283902873218…

gives the universal ratio by which the width of the "forks" in the bifurcation diagram shrinks from one level of the cascade to the next. Feigenbaum explained the universality using renormalisation-group methods, showing that the cascade is governed by a fixed-point function of a rescaling operator. The constants are believed to be transcendental, though this has not been proved.
