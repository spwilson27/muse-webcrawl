Change of basis

In mathematics, a change of basis is the conversion of the coordinates of a vector, or of the matrix of a linear map, from one basis of a vector space to another. A vector itself is independent of the basis used to describe it, but its coordinate representation changes, and the change-of-basis matrix relates the two descriptions.

Coordinates of a vector

Let B = (b1, …, bn) and C = (c1, …, cn) be two bases of a vector space V. The change-of-basis matrix from C to B is the matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors of C expressed in the basis B. If [v]C denotes the coordinate vector of v in basis C, then [v]B = P[v]C, where P is that matrix. The matrix is invertible, and its inverse performs the change in the opposite direction.

Matrix of a linear map

If a linear map T : V → V has matrix A in basis B and matrix A′ in basis C, then A′ = P−1AP, where P is the change-of-basis matrix from C to B. Matrices related in this way are called similar, and they share eigenvalues, determinant, trace and characteristic polynomial.

Importance

Choosing a convenient basis can greatly simplify a problem: for a diagonalizable operator there is a basis of eigenvectors in which the matrix is diagonal, and for a normal operator on a complex inner-product space there is an orthonormal eigenbasis. Change of basis is also the mathematical content of many coordinate transformations in geometry, physics and computer graphics.
