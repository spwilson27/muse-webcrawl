Meat pie (New Zealand)

The meat pie in New Zealand is a hand-sized baked pastry dish containing diced or minced meat and gravy, sometimes with onion, cheese, mushrooms or other ingredients, encased in a flaky pastry shell. It is widely regarded as an iconic item of New Zealand cuisine, sold at bakeries, dairies (corner stores), petrol stations, school tuck shops, and sporting venues across the country.

Varieties and consumption

Classic fillings include mince (ground beef), mince and cheese, steak, steak and cheese, bacon and egg, and potato-top pies; newer bakeries offer gourmet and fusion fillings. Annual per-capita pie consumption in New Zealand is among the highest in the world, with over 100 million pies eaten per year. Pies are traditionally eaten by hand, often with tomato sauce, and are famously best blown on first — a joke popularized by a 2011 police safety campaign advising that one should "always blow on the pie".

Awards

The Bakels NZ Pie Awards, held annually since the 1990s, judge thousands of entries across categories and confer a Supreme Pie Award that brings the winning bakery wide publicity. Notable commercial brands include Big Ben, Mrs Mac's, and Georgie Pie, the McDonald's-owned chain revived in the 2010s after its original 1990s run.
