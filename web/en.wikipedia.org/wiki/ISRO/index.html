ISRO

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is India's national space agency, headquartered in Bengaluru. Founded in 1969 under Vikram Sarabhai, who insisted the programme serve development — communications, weather, remote sensing — ISRO built a reputation for frugal, pragmatic engineering, culminating in headline achievements on small budgets: the Mars Orbiter Mission (2013) reached Mars on its first attempt for a reported cost below that of a Hollywood space film, and Chandrayaan-3 made India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon in 2023, and the first near the lunar south pole.

ISRO's workhorse PSLV rocket flies from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre at Sriharikota and has launched hundreds of foreign satellites commercially. The heavier LVM3 launched Chandrayaan-2 and -3 and is being human-rated for the Gaganyaan crewed programme.

Crewed ambitions

Gaganyaan aims to fly Indian astronauts ('vyomnauts') on an Indian vehicle from Indian soil; uncrewed test flights and an abort demonstration preceded the crewed attempt, now expected in the mid-2020s. India has also announced plans for a national space station (Bharatiya Antariksha Station) in the 2030s and a crewed lunar landing by 2040.
