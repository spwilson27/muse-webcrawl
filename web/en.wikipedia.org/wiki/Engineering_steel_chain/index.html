Engineering steel chain

Engineering steel chain (also called engineered class chain or engineering-class conveyor chain) is a category of heavy-duty power transmission and conveying chain built from steel sidebars, pins, bushings and rollers, designed for demanding industrial service such as bulk material handling, elevating and conveying in harsh environments.

Construction

Unlike precision roller chain built to tight dimensional standards for high-speed drives, engineering steel chain prioritises strength, wear resistance and tolerance of shock loads, abrasive materials and poor lubrication. Links are typically assembled from flat steel sidebars — straight or offset ("cranked") — joined by press-fit or cottered pins, with through-hardened or case-hardened components. Large-pitch versions are often built with cast or fabricated links and can be fitted with attachments for buckets, scrapers, flights and slats.

Applications

Typical uses include bucket elevators in cement plants and grain terminals, drag and apron conveyors in mining and steelworks, forest products handling, wastewater treatment collectors, and drive duties in sugar mills and asphalt plants. Standardised series are produced to manufacturer and industry standards (such as those of the American chain industry for engineered steel conveyor chains), allowing interchangeability between makers.

Selection and maintenance

Selection is governed by working load, chain pull, speed, sprocket size and environment. Because these chains commonly run slowly under heavy load, service life is dominated by joint wear (pin and bushing abrasion), which manifests as pitch elongation; chains are usually replaced when elongation reaches a few percent. Lubrication, alignment and protection from corrosive or abrasive ingress substantially extend life.
