Ultra-high-temperature processing

UHT processing
Temperature135–150 °C (275–302 °F)
Time1–4 seconds
Shelf life6–9 months unopened, unrefrigerated
Common productsMilk, cream, juice, plant-based drinks

Ultra-high-temperature processing (UHT), ultra-heat treatment, or ultra-pasteurization is a food processing technology that sterilizes liquid food by heating it above 135 °C (275 °F) — the temperature required to kill bacterial endospores — for 1 to 4 seconds. UHT is most commonly used in milk production, but the process is also applied to fruit juices, cream, soy milk, yogurt, wine, soups, honey, and stews.

Process

In indirect UHT systems, the product is heated through heat exchangers; in direct systems, culinary steam is injected into the product or the product is infused into steam, followed by flash cooling in a vacuum chamber. After treatment, the product is packaged aseptically, typically in pre-sterilized Tetra Pak cartons or similar containers, which prevent recontamination and allow storage at ambient temperature for months.

Effects on the product

The high heat slightly alters the taste of milk: some milk proteins denature, and Maillard reactions give UHT milk a somewhat sweeter, "cooked" flavour compared with conventionally pasteurized milk. UHT milk has a shelf life of six to nine months unopened without refrigeration, which makes it advantageous where refrigeration is scarce. It was first developed in Switzerland in the 1960s and became widespread commercially in the 1970s, particularly in Europe, where in some countries it accounts for the large majority of milk sold.
