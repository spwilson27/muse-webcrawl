Acrylic fiber

Acrylic fibers are synthetic fibers made from a polymer (polyacrylonitrile) with an average molecular weight of about 100,000, containing at least 85% acrylonitrile monomer by definition in the United States. They were developed by DuPont in the 1940s and commercialised under the trade name Orlon beginning in 1950.

Properties

Acrylic is lightweight, soft and warm, with a wool-like feel, and it resists moths, oils, chemicals and sunlight better than many natural fibres. It is, however, less breathable than natural fibres and can pill and melt rather than burn. The fibres are solution-spun, either by wet or dry spinning, from polyacrylonitrile dissolved in a solvent.

Uses

Acrylic yarns are widely used in sweaters, blankets, carpets, upholstery and craft knitting as an inexpensive alternative to wool. Acrylic fiber is also the principal precursor for carbon fiber production.
