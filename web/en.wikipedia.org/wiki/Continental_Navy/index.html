The Continental Navy was the navy of the Thirteen Colonies (later the United States) during the American Revolutionary War. Founded on 13 October 1775 by the Continental Congress, it is regarded as the ancestor of the modern United States Navy.

Creation

Congress authorized two armed vessels in October 1775 to intercept British transports, then ordered thirteen frigates in December 1775. Esek Hopkins was appointed commander in chief; converted merchantmen formed its early strength.

Operations

Always outnumbered by the Royal Navy, the Continental Navy raided commerce, captured supplies (including the 1776 raid on Nassau), and carried diplomats to Europe. Its most famous fight was John Paul Jones's victory of Bonhomme Richard over HMS Serapis off Flamborough Head in 1779 ("I have not yet begun to fight!"). Most of its ships were lost, captured or blockaded; state navies and privateers arguably contributed more to the war at sea.

Disbandment

With the war over and finances strained, Congress sold the last frigate, Alliance, in 1785. A permanent navy was re-established by the Naval Act of 1794.
