Young adult fiction

Young adult fiction (YA) is a category of fiction published for readers in their youth. The Young Adult Library Services Association defines young adults as ages 12 to 18, and the term is typically used for books aimed at ages 12 and up, with protagonists of similar age.

Young adult fiction
Audience: Ages 12–18
Landmarks: The Outsiders (1967), Harry Potter, The Hunger Games
Genres: All, esp. fantasy, romance, dystopia

History

The category emerged after World War II; Maureen Daly's Seventeenth Summer (1942) is an early example. S. E. Hinton's The Outsiders (1967), written by a teenager, brought gritty realism. The 1970s–80s saw Judy Blume and Robert Cormier, and the 2000s boom was driven by Harry Potter, Twilight, and The Hunger Games, drawing many adult readers.

Characteristics

YA fiction typically features adolescent protagonists, first-person or close third-person voice, coming-of-age themes, identity, first love, and conflict with authority — across every genre from contemporary realism to fantasy and verse novels.

Criticism and diversity

Debates concern dark content, crossover adult readership, and representation; movements such as We Need Diverse Books have pushed for broader voices.
