Side-scrolling video game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A side-scrolling video game (also called a side-scroller) is a video game in which the gameplay action is viewed from a side-view camera angle and the screen follows the player character as they move, usually from left to right, through a level. Side-scrollers were one of the dominant formats during the golden age of arcade games and the 8-bit and 16-bit console eras.

History

Early examples include the scrolling race and shooting games of the late 1970s and early 1980s. Williams Electronics' Defender (1981) allowed free horizontal scrolling in both directions, while Irem's Kung-Fu Master (1984) established the scrolling beat-'em-up, and Nintendo's Super Mario Bros. (1985) defined the side-scrolling platform game for a generation. Side-scrolling shooters such as Gradius and R-Type, run-and-gun games like Contra, and exploration-oriented "Metroidvania" games such as Super Metroid and Castlevania: Symphony of the Night expanded the format.

Techniques

Side-scrollers commonly use parallax scrolling, in which background layers move at different speeds to create an illusion of depth. Tile-based graphics allowed large levels to be built from small reusable elements, fitting the tight memory limits of early hardware. As 3D graphics came to dominate in the mid-1990s, side-scrollers declined in mainstream popularity, but the format survived and later flourished in independent games — such as Braid, Limbo, Hollow Knight, and Celeste — and in "2.5D" games rendered with 3D models but played on a 2D plane.
