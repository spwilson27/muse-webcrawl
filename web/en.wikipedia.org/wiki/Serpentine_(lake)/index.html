The Serpentine (also known as the Serpentine River) is a 40-acre (16 ha) recreational lake in Hyde Park, London, England, created in 1730 at the instigation of Queen Caroline. The river Westbourne originally fed the lake. Although it is common to refer to the entire body of water as the Serpentine, strictly the name refers only to the eastern half; the western half is called the Long Water. The Serpentine Bridge, built in the 1820s, marks the boundary between Hyde Park and Kensington Gardens.

History

The lake's curving, naturalistic shape was innovative at a time when formal straight canals were the fashion in garden design, and it became a model for many later park lakes. The north bank features the Italian Gardens, ornamental fountains created in the 1860s.

Recreation

The Serpentine is used for rowing, pedalo hire, and open-water swimming. The Serpentine Lido, on the south bank, has been a popular swimming spot since the 1930s, and the Serpentine Swimming Club holds a Christmas Day race for the Peter Pan Cup, a tradition dating from 1864. The lake hosted the triathlon and marathon swimming events of the 2012 Summer Olympics.
