The Boeing 777X is the latest series of the long-range, wide-body Boeing 777 family, developed to succeed the 777-300ER. It features new GE9X engines — the largest turbofans ever built — new composite wings with folding wingtips, a wider cabin, and improved fuel efficiency. The initial variant, the 777-9, seats around 426 passengers in two classes and first flew on January 25, 2020; the smaller 777-8 and the 777-8F freighter follow.

Development

Boeing launched the 777X in November 2013 at the Dubai Airshow with record orders from Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Lufthansa, the launch customer. The aircraft combines the 777's fuselage with a new carbon-fiber wing of 71.8 m (235 ft) span; because the span exceeds airport gate limits for its class, the outer 3.4 m of each wing folds upward on the ground, a first for commercial airliners. The GE9X engine, with a fan diameter of 134 inches, promises large gains in efficiency, and the cabin gains larger windows at a lower cabin altitude.

Delays and certification

The program has been repeatedly delayed — by engine-development issues, a 2019 cargo-door failure during ground testing, the pandemic, and heightened FAA scrutiny after the 737 MAX crisis — pushing planned entry into service from 2020 into the mid-2020s and beyond. Flight testing of the 777-9 resumed and progressed through FAA type-inspection authorization phases, with deliveries to Lufthansa and Emirates expected as the first.

Variants and orders

The 777-9 is the stretched flagship; the 777-8 is a shorter, longer-range version roughly matching the 777-200LR's size; the 777-8F, launched in 2022 with Qatar Airways as launch customer, targets the large freighter market as 747-8F and MD-11F fleets retire. Total orders exceed 500 aircraft, with Emirates the largest customer.
