Maurice André

Maurice André (21 May 1933 – 25 February 2012) was a French classical trumpeter. He is widely regarded as the musician who established the trumpet as a respected solo instrument in the concert hall during the second half of the 20th century, particularly through his championing of the piccolo trumpet and the Baroque trumpet repertoire.

Early life

André was born in Alès, in the Cévennes region of southern France, into a family of miners. His father, an amateur trumpet player, gave him his first lessons. André himself worked in the coal mines as a teenager before his talent was recognised; he entered the Paris Conservatoire, where he studied with Raymond Sabarich and won first prizes in both cornet and trumpet.

Career

André won major international competitions, including the Geneva International Music Competition in 1955 and the ARD International Music Competition in Munich in 1963. He became professor of trumpet at the Paris Conservatoire in 1967, a post he held until 1978, and he taught a generation of leading trumpeters.

His enormous discography — more than 250 recordings — includes the concertos of Haydn, Hummel, Telemann, Bach, Handel, Vivaldi and Albinoni. Working with instrument makers, notably the Selmer company in Paris, he helped develop the modern four-valve piccolo trumpet in B-flat and A, which made the high clarino register of Baroque music playable with security and brilliance.

Legacy

Before André, the trumpet was seldom heard as a solo instrument in serious concert programmes; after him, a solo trumpet career became a realistic ambition. He was made a Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur and is remembered for a singing, effortless tone and apparently inexhaustible breath control. He died in Bayonne in 2012, aged 78.
