Hockey Hall of Fame

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hockey Hall of Fame (French: Temple de la renommée du hockey) is a museum and hall of fame in Toronto, Ontario, Canada, dedicated to the history of ice hockey. It holds exhibits about players, teams, and National Hockey League (NHL) records, and houses the Stanley Cup for much of the year.

History

The Hall was established in 1943 through the efforts of former NHL referee and Kingston mayor James T. Sutherland, who promoted Kingston, Ontario as hockey's birthplace. The first class of inductees was named in 1945, though a permanent building did not open in Toronto until 1961, at Exhibition Place. In 1993 the Hall moved to its current home in the former Bank of Montreal building at Brookfield Place, at the corner of Yonge and Front streets.

Induction

Members are inducted in three categories: players, builders, and on-ice officials; a media honourees section recognizes broadcasters and writers. An 18-member selection committee votes annually; players are normally eligible three years after retirement, though the waiting period was famously waived for stars such as Wayne Gretzky and Mario Lemieux. As of the mid-2020s, more than 400 individuals have been honoured. The Hall also serves as the official hall of fame of the IIHF for international hockey.
