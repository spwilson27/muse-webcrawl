Roman numerals

Roman numerals are a numeral system that originated in ancient Rome and remained the usual way of writing numbers throughout Europe well into the Late Middle Ages. Numbers are written with combinations of letters from the Latin alphabet, each with a fixed integer value.

Symbols are added together, with the subtractive principle used for values just below a larger symbol: IV for 4, IX for 9, XL for 40, CM for 900. Thus 2026 is written MMXXVI. There is no symbol for zero.

Modern use

Roman numerals are still used in many contexts: numbering monarchs and popes, clock faces, book chapters and volume numbers, the years of the Olympic Games, Super Bowl numbering in American football, and film and book copyright dates. They were gradually displaced for arithmetic and commerce by the Hindu–Arabic numeral system because they lack a place value and a zero, making calculation cumbersome.
