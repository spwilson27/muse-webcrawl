Propionic acid

Propionic acid (also propanoic acid) is a naturally occurring carboxylic acid with chemical formula CH3CH2CO2H. It is a liquid with a pungent, disagreeable odor resembling body odor. The anion CH3CH2CO2− is called a propionate (or propanoate).

Production and properties

Propionic acid was first described in 1844 by Johann Gottlieb. Industrially it is produced mainly by the oxo process (hydrocarboxylation of ethylene) and by oxidation of propionaldehyde. It miscible with water and has a melting point of −20.5 °C and boiling point of 141 °C. In biology, it is produced by Propionibacterium in the gut and in Swiss cheese, contributing to flavor.

Uses

Propionic acid and its salts (calcium and sodium propionate) inhibit mold and some bacteria and are widely used as food preservatives in baked goods and animal feed. Esters of propionic acid are used in fragrances and in the production of herbicides and pharmaceuticals.
