The demolition of the Babri Masjid occurred on 6 December 1992, when a large crowd of Hindu nationalist activists, known as kar sevaks, demolished the Babri Masjid, a 16th-century mosque in Ayodhya, in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh. The demolition was the culmination of the Ayodhya dispute: Hindu organisations, led by the Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP) and supported by the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), claimed the mosque stood on the exact birthplace of the deity Rama (Ram Janmabhoomi) and had been built in 1528 by Mir Baqi, a commander of the Mughal emperor Babur, after destroying a temple.

Background

The site had been contested since the 19th century. In 1949, idols of Rama were placed inside the mosque, leading to its closure by court order. The dispute intensified in the 1980s as the VHP launched a campaign to build a temple at the site, and BJP leader L. K. Advani undertook a rath yatra (chariot procession) across India in 1990 to mobilise support. On 6 December 1992, despite pledges to the Supreme Court that the mosque would be protected, a rally of some 150,000 kar sevaks overwhelmed security forces, scaled the structure, and tore it down within hours.

Aftermath

The demolition triggered widespread Hindu–Muslim rioting across India, most severely in Mumbai, where over 900 people died in December 1992 and January 1993, followed by retaliatory bombings in March 1993 that killed 257 people. Violence also spread to Pakistan and Bangladesh, where Hindus and their temples were attacked. The central government of Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao dismissed the BJP-led Uttar Pradesh state government and banned several organisations. The Liberhan Commission, appointed to investigate, reported in 2009, indicting senior BJP and VHP leaders.

In 2019, the Supreme Court of India awarded the disputed site for the construction of a Hindu temple, while allotting alternative land for a mosque; the court called the 1992 demolition an "egregious violation of the rule of law". In 2020, a special court acquitted all 32 accused in the criminal demolition case, including Advani. The Ram Mandir built at the site was consecrated in January 2024.
