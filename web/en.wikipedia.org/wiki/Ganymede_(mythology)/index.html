Ganymede (mythology)

Ganymede (Greek: Γανυμήδης, Ganymēdēs) is a divine hero in Greek mythology whose homeland was Troy. He was the youngest son of King Tros of Dardania and was described by Homer as the most beautiful of mortals. Because of his beauty, he was abducted by Zeus — in later versions, carried off by Zeus in the form of an eagle — to Mount Olympus, where he became the cupbearer of the gods in place of Hebe.

Myth

In the Iliad, Ganymede is taken up by the gods to pour nectar for Zeus, and his father receives a gift of divine horses in compensation. In later tradition, Zeus himself desires Ganymede and sends his eagle (or transforms into one) to snatch the youth as he tends flocks on Mount Ida. Ganymede is associated with the constellation Aquarius, the water-bearer, and with the eagle constellation Aquila. In Roman mythology he was known as Catamitus.

Cultural depictions

The myth became one of the principal classical sources for the social and erotic motif of pederasty in Greek culture and was cited by Plato in the Phaedrus and Laws. Ganymede has been depicted by artists from ancient vase painters to Correggio, Michelangelo, Rubens, and Rembrandt, and referenced by Shakespeare, Marlowe, and Goethe. Jupiter's largest moon, Ganymede — the largest moon in the Solar System — is named after him.
