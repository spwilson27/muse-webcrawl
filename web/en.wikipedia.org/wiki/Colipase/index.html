Colipase

Colipase
Gene: CLPS
Organ: Pancreas
Function: Cofactor for pancreatic lipase
Size: ~112 amino acids (human procolipase)

Colipase is a small protein secreted by the pancreas that acts as a cofactor for pancreatic lipase, the principal enzyme responsible for digesting dietary triglycerides in the small intestine. It is produced as a precursor, procolipase, which is activated by trypsin in the intestinal lumen.

Function

In the presence of bile salts, which would otherwise coat fat droplets and exclude lipase from the oil–water interface, colipase anchors pancreatic lipase to the surface of emulsified lipid droplets and helps it retain activity. Colipase binds both to lipase and to lipid micelles, restoring effective lipolysis under physiological conditions.

Structure

Colipase is a compact protein stabilised by five disulfide bonds, with hydrophobic surface loops that interact with lipid interfaces and a binding site for lipase. The lipase–colipase complex has been a model system in structural studies of interfacial enzyme activation.

Clinical relevance

Activation of procolipase releases a peptide called enterostatin, which has been studied for possible roles in appetite regulation. Deficiency of effective lipase–colipase function contributes to fat malabsorption in pancreatic insufficiency.
