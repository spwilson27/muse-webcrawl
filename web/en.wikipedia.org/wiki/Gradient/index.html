Gradient

Gradient
Notation∇f, grad f
TypeVector differential operator

In vector calculus, the gradient of a scalar-valued differentiable function f of several variables is the vector field whose value at a point is the vector of the partial derivatives of f at that point. Written ∇f and called "grad f" or "del f", the gradient points in the direction of the greatest rate of increase of the function, and its magnitude is that maximum rate of change.

Definition

For a function f(x1, …, xn), the gradient is ∇f = (∂f/∂x1, …, ∂f/∂xn). The directional derivative of f in the direction of a unit vector u is the dot product ∇f · u. The gradient is everywhere perpendicular to the level sets (contour lines) of f.

Applications

Gradients are fundamental in physics: electric and gravitational fields are the negative gradients of their potentials, and heat flows down the temperature gradient. In machine learning, gradient descent and its variants minimise loss functions by stepping in the direction of the negative gradient, a process enabled by the backpropagation algorithm.

Generalisation

The gradient is one of the three basic operations of vector calculus alongside divergence and curl, and generalises to functions on Riemannian manifolds, where it is defined using the metric tensor.
