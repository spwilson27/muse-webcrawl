Worth Avenue is an upscale shopping district in Palm Beach, Florida, United States. Running east–west for about four blocks from the Atlantic Ocean inland, the avenue is lined with luxury boutiques, art galleries, jewellers, restaurants and Mediterranean Revival architecture, and is frequently compared to Rodeo Drive in Beverly Hills and Fifth Avenue in New York.

History

The avenue developed in the 1920s during Palm Beach's resort boom. Architect Addison Mizner, whose Everglades Club opened at the avenue's eastern end in 1919, shaped much of its distinctive look, designing arcaded courtyards and pedestrian alleys known as "vias" that branch off the main street, including Via Mizner and Via Parigi. The vias contain small shops, fountains and apartments, and were among the earliest planned shopping environments in the United States designed around the pedestrian.

Shopping

Worth Avenue hosts flagship and boutique stores of international luxury brands in fashion, jewellery and watches, alongside long-established local retailers. The avenue's western end is anchored by The Esplanade, a two-storey open-air shopping centre. A clock tower at the eastern end, where the avenue meets the ocean, is a local landmark.

Revitalisation

In 2010 the avenue underwent a streetscape renovation of roughly $15 million that added palm trees, wider sidewalks and new lighting, funded jointly by the town and local merchants.
