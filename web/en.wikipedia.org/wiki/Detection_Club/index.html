Detection Club

The Detection Club is a group of British mystery writers founded in 1930, during the Golden Age of detective fiction. Its members included many of the most celebrated crime novelists of the era, and the club still holds regular dinners and meetings in London.

Foundation and oath

The club was founded by Anthony Berkeley and others; G. K. Chesterton served as its first president, succeeded after his death by E. C. Bentley. New members were initiated with a mock-solemn oath, written by Dorothy L. Sayers, swearing to adhere to fair play in their detection: the reader must have equal opportunity with the detective to solve the mystery, and the writer must never use Divine Revelation, Feminine Intuition, Mumbo Jumbo, or coincidence to resolve it.

Collaborative works

Members collaborated on round-robin novels and radio serials, most famously The Floating Admiral (1931), in which each author contributed a chapter; also Ask a Policeman (1933) and Six Against the Yard (1936).

Fair play rules

The club's ethos overlapped with Ronald Knox's "Ten Commandments" of detective fiction and S. S. Van Dine's rules, codifying the puzzle-mystery conventions of the Golden Age.
