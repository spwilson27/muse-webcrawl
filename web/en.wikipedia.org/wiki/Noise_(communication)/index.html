Noise (communication)

In communication theory, noise is anything that distorts or interferes with a signal between sender and receiver, reducing the fidelity of the transmitted message. In Claude Shannon's mathematical theory of communication, noise is a fundamental limit on channel capacity.

Types of noise

Thermal (Johnson–Nyquist) noise arises from random motion of charge carriers in conductors. Other types include shot noise, flicker (1/f) noise, crosstalk from adjacent channels, and environmental interference. In human communication, "noise" extends to semantic and psychological interference.

Signal-to-noise ratio

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), usually expressed in decibels, measures the level of a desired signal relative to background noise. The Shannon–Hartley theorem states that channel capacity grows with bandwidth and with the logarithm of (1 + SNR).

Mitigation

Engineers combat noise with shielding, filtering, error-correcting codes, redundancy, modulation schemes and digital regeneration, which prevents noise from accumulating over repeated transmission hops.
