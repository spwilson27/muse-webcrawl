Doxxing

Doxxing (also spelled doxing) is the act of publicly providing personally identifiable information about an individual or organisation, usually via the internet and typically with malicious intent. The word derives from "dropping dox" — hacker slang of the 1990s for compiling and releasing a dossier of documents ("docs") on someone, often to expose a rival's real identity.


Doxxing publishes private information to expose or intimidate a target.

Methods and motives

Doxxers combine publicly available records, social media, data breaches and social engineering to assemble home addresses, phone numbers, workplaces, financial details and family members' identities. Motives include harassment, revenge, vigilantism, and political intimidation; consequences for victims range from unwanted contact to swatting, stalking and identity theft.

Legality and policy

Publishing truthful, lawfully obtained information is protected speech in some jurisdictions, but many countries have moved to criminalise malicious doxxing: Hong Kong amended its privacy ordinance in 2021, the Netherlands made doxxing an offence in 2024, and several U.S. states have enacted anti-doxxing statutes. Major platforms prohibit posting private personal information under their harassment policies. Journalists and researchers debate a distinction between doxxing private individuals and identifying public figures in the public interest.
