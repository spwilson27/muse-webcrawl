Anti-Zionism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anti-Zionism is opposition to Zionism, the movement for the self-determination and statehood of the Jewish people in a homeland in the Land of Israel. Anti-Zionism has been expressed in many forms since Zionism emerged in the late nineteenth century, on religious, political, national and ideological grounds.

History and varieties

Before 1948, opposition to Zionism came from several directions: Orthodox Jewish groups that held a Jewish state should await the Messiah; Reform Jewish movements that defined Judaism purely as a religion; assimilationist Jews who feared dual-loyalty charges; Arab and Palestinian opponents who objected to Jewish immigration and statehood in Palestine; and British officials and others concerned with imperial interests. After Israel's establishment in 1948, anti-Zionism became a central plank of Arab nationalism and of Soviet and communist policy, notably in the UN General Assembly's 1975 "Zionism is racism" resolution (Resolution 3379), revoked in 1991. Contemporary anti-Zionism includes Palestinian national movements, some forms of Islamist and far-left activism, and Jewish anti-Zionist groups such as Neturei Karta and Jewish Voice for Peace.

Debates

The relationship between anti-Zionism and antisemitism is contested. Critics of Israel and defenders of anti-Zionism argue that opposition to a political ideology or to a state's policies is distinct from hostility to Jews. Others, including the IHRA working definition of antisemitism, argue that some expressions of anti-Zionism — for example denying the Jewish people a right to self-determination afforded to others, or applying standards not expected of other democratic nations — cross into antisemitism. The debate intensified following the Gaza war that began in 2023.
