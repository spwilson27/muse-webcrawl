British Chinese

British Chinese (also known as Chinese British or British-born Chinese) are people of Chinese ancestry who were born in or have migrated to the United Kingdom. The Chinese community is one of the oldest ethnic minority communities in Britain, with the first recorded arrivals dating to the 17th century and a settled community forming from the mid-19th century.

The Chinatown gate on Gerrard Street, London.

History

Early Chinese settlers were seamen employed by the East India Company who settled around the docklands of London (Limehouse) and Liverpool, which claims Europe's oldest Chinatown. Large-scale migration followed in the 1950s and 1960s, much of it from the New Territories of Hong Kong, driven by post-war demand for labour and upheaval in farming communities; many families entered the catering trade, establishing the Chinese restaurants and takeaways that became a feature of British high streets. Later arrivals included students and professionals from mainland China, Malaysia, Singapore and Vietnam.

Demographics

According to the 2021 census, around 445,000 people in England and Wales identified as ethnically Chinese. The largest concentrations are in London, Manchester, Birmingham, Liverpool and Edinburgh. British Chinese pupils consistently record among the highest levels of educational attainment in the United Kingdom.

Culture

Chinese New Year celebrations in London are among the largest outside Asia. Community life centres on Chinatowns, Chinese-language schools, churches and associations, while Cantonese remains the most widely spoken variety among the older generations, with Mandarin growing among recent arrivals.
