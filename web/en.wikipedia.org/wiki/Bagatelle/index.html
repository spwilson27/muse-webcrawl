Bagatelle

Bagatelle (image placeholder)

Bagatelle (from the French château de Bagatelle) is a billiards-derived indoor table game, a precursor of the modern pinball machine and the Japanese game pachinko. It is played on a table with nine pins (skittles) arranged in a particular pattern, and the player uses a cue to strike a ball past the pins into numbered cups at the far end of the board.

History

The game became popular in France during the reign of Louis XVI; the name is associated with a party given in 1777 by the Count of Artois at his Château de Bagatelle in the Bois de Boulogne, where the game was a featured entertainment. It spread to Britain, where it became a popular parlour and pub game in the 19th century, and to America. Victorian bagatelle tables were often elaborate pieces of furniture.

Gameplay

Traditional bagatelle is played on a semicircular-ended table with nine cups at the far end, each numbered 1 to 9, guarded by nine pins. Players score by sending balls into the cups; hitting a pin or failing to reach the scoring zone loses points. Variants include Bar billiards and Russian bagatelle.
