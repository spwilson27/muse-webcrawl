Semidirect product

In mathematics, specifically in group theory, the semidirect product is a construction that generalizes the direct product of two groups. It describes a group G as a product of a normal subgroup N and a subgroup H, where H may act on N by automorphisms. The resulting group is written N ⋊ H (or H ⋉ N, depending on convention) when such an action is specified.

Definition

Formally, given groups N and H and a group homomorphism φ : H → Aut(N), the semidirect product N ⋊φ H is the set of pairs (n, h) with multiplication (n, h)(n′, h′) = (n φ(h)(n′), hh′). When φ is trivial, this reduces to the direct product N × H. Unlike the direct product, N is normal in the semidirect product but H need not be.

Examples

The dihedral group of symmetries of a polygon is a semidirect product of the cyclic group of rotations (normal) with a group of order 2 generated by a reflection. The Euclidean group of rigid motions of the plane is a semidirect product of the translation group with the orthogonal group. Semidirect products are also central to the classification of groups of small order and to the structure theory of Lie groups.
