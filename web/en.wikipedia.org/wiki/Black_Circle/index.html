Black Circle is a painting by the Russian avant-garde artist Kazimir Malevich, one of the foundational works of his Suprematist movement. The motif — a solid black circle floating on a plain white background — was first exhibited in 1915 at the Last Futurist Exhibition of Paintings 0.10 in Petrograd, alongside the celebrated Black Square; the version most often reproduced was painted around 1923 with the participation of Malevich's students.

Suprematism

Malevich announced Suprematism as the "supremacy of pure feeling" in art, discarding depiction of the visible world in favour of elementary geometric forms — squares, circles, crosses, rectangles — arranged on white grounds. Together with the Black Square and Black Cross, the Black Circle formed a triad of "zero degree" forms from which Malevich proposed to rebuild painting. He wrote that these forms were to art what a new beginning point is to perception: a liberation from the "ballast" of objectivity.

Versions and legacy

Like many Suprematist works, the Black Circle exists in more than one autograph version, since Malevich and his circle replicated the key motifs for later exhibitions, including the 1927 Berlin exhibition. The principal version is held by the State Russian Museum in St Petersburg. Along with the Black Square, it became an icon of geometric abstraction, influencing Constructivism, De Stijl, the Bauhaus and later Minimal art.
