The Allahabad Address was the presidential address delivered by the philosopher-poet Sir Muhammad Iqbal to the annual session of the All-India Muslim League at Allahabad on 29 December 1930. In it, Iqbal proposed the consolidation of India's Muslim-majority north-western provinces into a single autonomous state, and the speech is widely regarded in Pakistan as the ideological seed of the country's creation.

Content

Iqbal declared: "I would like to see the Punjab, North-West Frontier Province, Sind and Baluchistan amalgamated into a single State. Self-government within the British Empire, or without the British Empire, the formation of a consolidated North-West Indian Muslim State appears to me to be the final destiny of the Muslims, at least of North-West India." He argued that Islam in India needed a territorial centre to preserve its religious and cultural life, and that such a state would safeguard both Indian security and communal peace.

Interpretation and legacy

Historians debate whether Iqbal envisaged a sovereign state independent of India or an autonomous unit within an Indian federation; the address itself left the matter ambiguous, and Iqbal spoke chiefly of the north-west, not Bengal. Nevertheless, Pakistani national tradition treats the address as the founding vision of Pakistan, and Iqbal as its spiritual father. The idea fed into the Muslim League's politics of the 1930s and, in altered form, into the Lahore Resolution of 1940.
