Maple leaf

The maple leaf is the distinctive leaf of trees and shrubs of the genus Acer, typically palmately lobed with three to nine pointed lobes, though some species have pinnate or unlobed leaves. Maples are famous for their brilliant autumn foliage — reds, oranges and golds — as chlorophyll breaks down and other pigments are revealed.

Symbolism

The maple leaf is the national symbol of Canada. It appeared on the arms of Ontario and Quebec in 1868, on Canadian coins from 1876, and as the central element of the national flag adopted in 1965, which bears a stylised red 11-pointed maple leaf. The leaf has long served as an emblem of Canadian troops, appearing on cap badges in both world wars. A golden maple leaf worn by the Governor General and the number of points on the flag's leaf have no specific symbolic count — the design was chosen for visibility and distinctiveness.

Other uses

Maple leaves and seeds (samaras, or "helicopters") appear in heraldry and logos worldwide. The sugar maple's leaf marks the source of maple syrup; the Japanese maple is widely planted as an ornamental, and autumn leaf-viewing (momijigari) is a traditional pastime in Japan.
