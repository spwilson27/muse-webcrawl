Alpha shape

In computational geometry, an alpha shape, or α-shape, is a family of piecewise linear simple curves in the Euclidean plane associated with the shape of a finite set of points. It was first introduced by Edelsbrunner, Kirkpatrick and Seidel in 1983 as a generalization of the convex hull of a point set.

Definition

Intuitively, imagine rolling a disk of radius 1/α around the outside of a point set. The boundary traced by the disk carves away material from the convex hull; the resulting region is the alpha shape. For α = 0 the alpha shape is the point set itself, while as α → ∞ it approaches the convex hull. Intermediate values capture concavities, holes and even disconnected components of the "shape" of the data.

Formally, alpha shapes are subcomplexes of the Delaunay triangulation: a Delaunay simplex belongs to the alpha complex when its circumradius is below the threshold determined by α. The boundary of the alpha complex is the alpha shape.

Applications

Alpha shapes are used to reconstruct surfaces from unorganized point clouds in 3D scanning, to model molecular shapes in computational biology (for example the pockets and voids of proteins), and in geographic information systems for estimating the territory of point observations.
