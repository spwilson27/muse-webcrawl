Delaunay triangulation

In computational geometry, a Delaunay triangulation of a set of points in the plane is a triangulation such that no point lies inside the circumcircle of any triangle. It is named after Boris Delaunay, who described it in 1934.

Properties

Among all triangulations of a point set, the Delaunay triangulation maximizes the minimum angle, avoiding long skinny triangles as much as possible. It is unique when no four points are cocircular. The Delaunay triangulation is the dual graph of the Voronoi diagram: connecting the circumcenters of adjacent Delaunay triangles yields the Voronoi cells.

Relation to the convex hull

Lifting the points onto a paraboloid maps the Delaunay triangulation to the lower part of a three-dimensional convex hull, giving a classical O(n log n) construction. In higher dimensions the concept generalizes to Delaunay tessellations, defined by the empty-sphere condition.

Algorithms and applications

Algorithms include the incremental Bowyer–Watson algorithm, edge-flipping methods, and divide-and-conquer schemes. Delaunay triangulations are used to generate meshes for finite element analysis, to interpolate scattered data in terrain modelling, and in surface reconstruction from point clouds.
