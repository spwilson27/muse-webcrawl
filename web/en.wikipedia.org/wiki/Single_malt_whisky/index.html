Single malt whisky is malt whisky from a single distillery. Single malts are typically associated with single malt Scotch, though they are also produced in various other countries, including Japan, Ireland, the United States, India and Taiwan. Under Scotch Whisky Regulations, a "single malt Scotch whisky" must be made exclusively from malted barley (although caramel colouring may be added), distilled using pot stills at a single distillery, and aged for at least three years in oak casks of a capacity not exceeding 700 litres.

Production

Single malt whisky is produced from a mash of 100 per cent malted barley. The barley is steeped in water, allowed to germinate, and dried — traditionally over peat smoke in some regions, which imparts a smoky character. The dried malt is ground, mixed with hot water to extract sugars, fermented with yeast into a beer-like wash, and distilled twice (occasionally three times) in copper pot stills. The spirit is then matured in oak casks, often previously used for bourbon or sherry.

Scotch whisky regions

Scotland's single malts are conventionally grouped by region: Lowland, Highland, Speyside (often considered part of the Highlands), Campbeltown and Islay. Islay malts such as Laphroaig and Ardbeg are known for heavy peat smoke; Speyside malts such as Glenfiddich and The Macallan are typically fruitier and often sherry-cask matured.

Age statements

The age statement on a bottle refers to the youngest whisky in the bottle. Common ages include 10, 12, 15, 18 and 21 years. Whisky does not mature in the bottle, so only time spent in the cask counts toward the age statement.
