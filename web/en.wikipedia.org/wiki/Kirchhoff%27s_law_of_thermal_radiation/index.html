Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation

Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation, formulated by Gustav Kirchhoff in 1859–1860, states that for a body in thermodynamic equilibrium with its surroundings, the emissivity of the body at a given wavelength and temperature equals its absorptivity at that wavelength and temperature. In other words, good absorbers are good emitters.

Statement

For radiation of wavelength λ at temperature T, ε(λ,T) = α(λ,T), where ε is emissivity and α is absorptivity. A perfect blackbody, which absorbs all incident radiation, is therefore also the most efficient possible emitter, radiating according to Planck's law.

Importance

The law underlies the concept of the blackbody and paved the way for Planck's quantum theory. It is applied throughout radiative heat transfer, astrophysics and climate science.
