Material

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A material is a substance or mixture of substances that constitutes an object. Materials can be pure or impure, living or non-living matter; they are classified on the basis of their physical and chemical properties, their geological or biological origin, or their function. In engineering and industry, a material is what things are made from — the input to manufacturing and construction — and the study of their structure and properties is the field of materials science.

Classification

Materials are commonly grouped into families with shared characteristics:

  • Metals — such as steel, aluminium and copper; generally strong, ductile, and good conductors of heat and electricity, with a crystalline structure held by metallic bonding.
  • Ceramics — such as glass, brick, porcelain and cement; typically hard, stiff, heat-resistant and chemically inert, but brittle.
  • Polymers — plastics and rubbers made of long-chain molecules; generally light, flexible and insulating, with properties tuned by composition and processing.
  • Composites — combinations of two or more materials, such as fibreglass, carbon-fibre reinforced polymer and reinforced concrete, designed to achieve properties no single constituent has.
  • Semiconductors — such as silicon, whose electrical conductivity can be controlled, forming the basis of electronics.
  • Biomaterials and natural materials — wood, leather, textiles, bone and stone, used since prehistory.

Properties

Selection of a material for an application depends on its properties: mechanical (strength, stiffness, hardness, toughness, fatigue and creep resistance), thermal (conductivity, expansion, melting point), electrical and magnetic behaviour, optical properties, density, corrosion and chemical resistance, cost, availability and environmental impact. Structure at every scale — atomic bonding, crystal structure, microstructure and macroscopic shape — determines these properties, a relationship summarised in the materials science "tetrahedron" of processing, structure, properties and performance.

Raw materials

In economics and industry, raw materials are the unprocessed or minimally processed inputs — ores, crude oil, timber, agricultural produce — from which materials and products are made, and their supply is a central concern of trade and resource policy.
