Quotient topology

The quotient topology is the topology placed on a quotient set so that the natural projection from the original space is continuous. Given a topological space X and a surjective map p: X → Y, a subset U of Y is open in the quotient topology exactly when p−1(U) is open in X.

This definition makes the quotient topology the finest topology on Y for which p is continuous. It also has a universal property: a function out of Y is continuous precisely when its composition with p is continuous.

Use in constructions

The quotient topology is used when a space is described by gluing parts together. Identifying boundary points of a disk, collapsing a subspace to a point, or forming an orbit space under a group action all lead to quotient topologies. Such descriptions are compact, but topological properties of the result can be subtle.

Comparison

Not every continuous surjection is a quotient map, although every quotient map is continuous and surjective. Open or closed surjections are common sufficient examples. The distinction matters when maps are used to recognize the topology of a target space from a simpler domain.
