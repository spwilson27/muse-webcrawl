Yuri Mikhailovich Lotman (28 February 1922 – 28 October 1993) was a Russian and Estonian literary scholar, semiotician, and historian of Russian culture. A professor at the University of Tartu in Estonia, he founded the Tartu–Moscow Semiotic School, one of the most influential movements in 20th-century semiotics and cultural theory.

Life

Lotman was born in Petrograd to a Jewish intelligentsia family and studied philology at Leningrad State University. He served in the Red Army during the Second World War. Unable to obtain a post in Leningrad amid the anti-Semitic campaigns of the late Stalin era, he moved in 1950 to Estonia, where he spent the rest of his career at the University of Tartu. From 1964 he edited the journal Sign Systems Studies (Trudy po znakovym sistemam), the flagship publication of the Tartu school.

Work

Lotman's early work applied structuralist methods to literature, notably in The Structure of the Artistic Text (1970). He later developed a semiotics of culture, treating culture as a system of sign systems ("texts") that stores and generates information. His best-known concept is the semiosphere — by analogy with the biosphere, the total semiotic space within which communication and meaning-making are possible, outlined in works such as Universe of the Mind (1990) and Culture and Explosion (1992). He also wrote extensively on Pushkin, Karamzin, Russian noble culture, and the semiotics of cinema.

Legacy

The Tartu school's summer schools and journals kept semiotic research alive in the Soviet Union and shaped cultural semiotics worldwide. The Lotman Institute and the Juri Lotman Semiotics Repository at the University of Tartu preserve his archive, and his later works on unpredictability and cultural dynamics have been influential in cultural studies and media theory.
