The Kingdom of Holland (Dutch: Koninkrijk Holland) was a client state of Napoleonic France established in 1806, replacing the Batavian Commonwealth. Napoleon placed his younger brother Louis Bonaparte on the throne, expecting him to subordinate Dutch interests to those of France. The kingdom took its name from Holland, the historic dominant province of the Dutch Republic.

Reign of Louis Bonaparte

Louis surprised his brother by governing in the Dutch interest: he learned Dutch, took the Dutch form of his name, Lodewijk, reorganised the administration, promoted legal codification modelled only partly on French law, founded cultural institutions including the Royal Museum (the predecessor of the Rijksmuseum), and responded energetically to disasters such as the Leiden gunpowder explosion of 1807 and the floods of 1809. His reluctance to enforce the Continental System against British trade brought him into repeated conflict with Napoleon.

Annexation

In 1810 Napoleon forced Louis to cede territory south of the Rhine and then to abdicate on 1 July 1810. Days later the kingdom was annexed outright into the French Empire, where the Dutch departments remained until French rule collapsed in 1813. The kingdom was a precursor to the modern Kingdom of the Netherlands, established in 1815, and was the first time the Dutch state was formally a monarchy.
