Don "Campbellock" Campbell (8 January 1951 – 30 March 2020) was an American dancer and choreographer who originated locking, one of the foundational funk and street-dance styles that fed into hip-hop dance. He founded the pioneering dance crew The Lockers in Los Angeles in 1973.

Career

Campbell developed locking around 1969–1970 while trying to imitate dances such as the Funky Chicken in Los Angeles clubs; when he froze mid-move trying to remember the next step, the crowd loved the pause, and he built a style around sudden "locks", points, wrist rolls and comedic character. His performances on Soul Train made the style famous. The Lockers — including Toni Basil, Shabba Doo and Fred Berry — performed with Frank Sinatra, on television variety shows and in the opening of concerts, bringing street dance to mainstream audiences.

Legacy

Locking influenced breakdancing, popping and generations of choreographers; Campbell is widely regarded as one of the founding fathers of street dance. He spent later decades teaching and judging internationally, and was honoured by hip-hop and dance organisations worldwide.
