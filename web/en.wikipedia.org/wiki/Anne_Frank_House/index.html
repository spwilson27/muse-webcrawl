Anne Frank House

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Anne Frank House (Dutch: Anne Frank Huis) is a museum and biographical house in Amsterdam, the Netherlands, dedicated to the Jewish wartime diarist Anne Frank. It occupies the canal house at Prinsengracht 263–265, where Anne, her family, and four others hid from Nazi persecution during the German occupation of the Netherlands in the Second World War.

The hiding place

In July 1942 the Frank family went into hiding in rooms at the rear of Otto Frank's business premises — the "Secret Annex" (Achterhuis), concealed behind a movable bookcase. Eight people lived there for just over two years, helped by trusted employees including Miep Gies. On 4 August 1944 the hiding place was raided and its occupants arrested and deported; only Otto Frank survived the camps. Anne's diary, saved by Gies, was published in 1947 as The Diary of a Young Girl.

Museum

After the war the building was threatened with demolition, but public campaigns led to the creation of the Anne Frank Foundation in 1957, and the museum opened in 1960. Otto Frank insisted the annex rooms remain unfurnished. The museum displays Anne's original diary and other documents, presents exhibitions on discrimination and human rights, and runs educational programmes worldwide. Extensions, including a modern entrance building, were added as visitor numbers grew; the museum now receives well over a million visitors a year and is one of Amsterdam's most visited attractions.
