The history of philosophy is the story of systematic inquiry into fundamental questions — the nature of reality, knowledge, the mind, morality and meaning — across the world's civilisations, from the Pre-Socratics, Confucius and the Upanishads to contemporary analytic and continental philosophy.

Raphael's School of Athens (1509–1511) imagines the philosophers of ancient Greece gathered together.

Ancient philosophy

Greek philosophy began in 6th-century BC Ionia with Thales and the Pre-Socratics, who sought natural explanations of the cosmos. Socrates turned philosophy toward ethics and definition; his student Plato founded the Academy and developed the theory of Forms; Plato's student Aristotle systematised logic, metaphysics, ethics, politics and the natural sciences. Hellenistic schools — Stoicism, Epicureanism, Skepticism and Neoplatonism — followed. In parallel, Indian philosophy developed in the Vedas and Upanishads, the systems of Hindu thought, Buddhism and Jainism; in China, Confucius, Mencius, the Daoists Laozi and Zhuangzi, and the Legalists debated society and the good life during the Hundred Schools of Thought.

Medieval philosophy

Medieval thinkers wrestled with faith and reason. In the Islamic world, Al-Kindi, Al-Farabi, Avicenna (Ibn Sina) and Averroes (Ibn Rushd) preserved and extended Greek philosophy, profoundly influencing Jewish thinkers such as Maimonides and Christian scholastics such as Anselm, Aquinas and Duns Scotus, who built vast syntheses in the universities. In India and China, Buddhist logic, Vedanta, Neo-Confucianism (Zhu Xi) and Zen developed richly.

Early modern philosophy

The Scientific Revolution reshaped philosophy. Descartes founded modern epistemology with his cogito; the rationalists (Spinoza, Leibniz) debated the empiricists (Locke, Berkeley, Hume) over the sources of knowledge. Hume's skepticism awoke Kant, whose critical philosophy — the Critique of Pure Reason (1781) — sought to ground knowledge and morality. Political philosophy flourished from Hobbes and Locke to Rousseau and Mill, alongside the Enlightenment's critique of authority.

19th and 20th centuries

Hegel's idealism, Marx's historical materialism, Kierkegaard's and Nietzsche's challenges to system, and the pragmatism of Peirce and James marked the 19th century. In the 20th, philosophy split into broad "analytic" (Frege, Russell, Wittgenstein, logical positivism, philosophy of language and mind) and "continental" (phenomenology, existentialism, hermeneutics, critical theory, poststructuralism) traditions. Contemporary philosophy addresses ethics of technology, consciousness, identity and global justice, in growing dialogue with non-Western traditions.
