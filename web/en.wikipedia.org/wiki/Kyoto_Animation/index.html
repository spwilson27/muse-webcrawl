Kyoto Animation

Kyoto Animation Co., Ltd. (Japanese: 株式会社京都アニメーション), abbreviated KyoAni, is a Japanese animation studio located in Uji, Kyoto Prefecture, founded in 1981 (incorporated 1985) by Yoko Hatta and Hidekazu Hatta. Renowned for exceptionally polished, emotionally resonant productions and for employing salaried in-house staff rather than relying on freelancers, the studio is known for The Melancholy of Haruhi Suzumiya, Clannad, K-On!, Free!, A Silent Voice, and Violet Evergarden. In July 2019 the studio suffered a devastating arson attack that killed 36 people.

History and working model

Yoko Hatta, a former Mushi Production painter, began the studio with a circle of local animators in Kyoto — unusual in an industry concentrated in Tokyo. For years KyoAni did subcontract work for studios such as Sunrise and Toei Animation before producing its own series from 2003 (Full Metal Panic? Fumoffu). Its adaptations of visual novels and light novels — Air, Kanon, Clannad — and the phenomenon of Haruhi Suzumiya (2006) established its reputation for cinematic quality on television schedules. The studio trains its own animators through an in-house school and publishes light novels under its KA Esuma Bunko imprint, several of which it adapts, such as Violet Evergarden.

2019 arson attack

On 18 July 2019, a man set fire to KyoAni's Studio 1 building in Fushimi, Kyoto, killing 36 employees — including directors Yasuhiro Takemoto and Yoshiji Kigami — and injuring dozens more, in Japan's deadliest mass killing in decades. The attacker, who claimed the studio had plagiarised his novel, was convicted and sentenced to death in 2024. Donations exceeding ¥3 billion poured in from fans worldwide; the studio demolished the building, erected a memorial, and gradually resumed production, releasing Violet Evergarden: The Movie (2020) to acclaim.
