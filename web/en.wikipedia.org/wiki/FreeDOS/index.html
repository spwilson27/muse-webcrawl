FreeDOS

FreeDOS (formerly Free-DOS and PD-DOS) is a free and open-source operating system for IBM PC compatible computers. It aims to provide a complete, MS-DOS-compatible environment for running legacy software, playing classic games, supporting embedded systems and installing on new hardware for firmware updates.

History

The project was announced on 29 June 1994 by Jim Hall, a physics student and programmer who, after Microsoft announced it would stop selling MS-DOS, posted a manifesto proposing a public-domain DOS developed collaboratively over the Internet. The first alpha release followed within months. Hall coordinated the project and wrote many of its tools; the FreeDOS kernel (FreeCOM and the kernel itself) was developed by contributors including Pat Villani and Bart Oldeman.

FreeDOS 1.0 was released on 3 September 2006, followed by 1.1 (2012), 1.2 (2016) and 1.3 (2022). Releases are distributed as bootable CD and USB images with an installer and a large set of utilities.

Features

FreeDOS includes a DOS kernel compatible with MS-DOS, the FreeCOM command interpreter, and dozens of utilities replicating and extending classic DOS commands, together with additional open-source tools for networking, editing and development. It is licensed under the GNU General Public License. Manufacturers such as Dell, HP and Lenovo have shipped FreeDOS preinstalled on some machines as a low-cost base operating system, and it remains popular for BIOS flashing and retrocomputing.
