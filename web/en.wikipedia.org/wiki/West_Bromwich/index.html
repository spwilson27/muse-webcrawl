West Bromwich

West Bromwich (locally /wɛst ˈbrɒmɪtʃ/) is a market town in the Metropolitan Borough of Sandwell, West Midlands, England, about 7 miles (11 km) northwest of Birmingham. Historically part of Staffordshire, it had a population of around 103,000 at the 2021 census. The town's name derives from Old English for the "west broom village".

History

West Bromwich originated as a village on the London-to-Shrewsbury coach road; a Benedictine priory was founded there in the 12th century. Coal mining and ironworking transformed it during the Industrial Revolution, and it became a municipal borough in 1882. Heavy industry declined in the late 20th century, and the town has since diversified into services and retail.

Sport and culture

The town is home to West Bromwich Albion Football Club, founded in 1878 and based at The Hawthorns since 1900—one of the highest grounds in English football. Notable people from West Bromwich include rock musician Robert Plant (born in the town), comedian Frank Skinner, and Thin Lizzy guitarist Brian Robertson. Dartmouth Park and Sandwell Valley Country Park are major green spaces on its edge.
