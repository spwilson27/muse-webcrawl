North Yemen

North Yemen is a term used to describe the northern part of Yemen during the period when the country was divided into two separate states, from the early 20th century until Yemeni unification in 1990. It refers first to the Mutawakkilite Kingdom of Yemen (1918–1962) and then to the Yemen Arab Republic (1962–1990), whose capital was Sanaa.

History

Following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire after the First World War, the northern highlands of Yemen came under the rule of Imam Yahya Muhammad Hamid ed-Din, who established the Mutawakkilite Kingdom. The kingdom remained an isolated, theocratic monarchy ruled by Zaydi imams until 1962.

Yemen Arab Republic

On 26 September 1962, republican officers led by Abdullah al-Sallal overthrew the imamate shortly after the accession of Imam Muhammad al-Badr, proclaiming the Yemen Arab Republic. The ensuing North Yemen Civil War (1962–1970) pitted republicans, supported by Egypt, against royalist tribes backed by Saudi Arabia. The republic survived and was formally recognised after the withdrawal of Egyptian forces and a reconciliation settlement in 1970.

Subsequent decades saw a succession of military rulers, including Ibrahim al-Hamdi (1974–1977) and Ali Abdullah Saleh, who became president in 1978 and governed until unification — and beyond, as president of the unified Republic of Yemen.

Unification

On 22 May 1990, the Yemen Arab Republic merged with the southern People's Democratic Republic of Yemen (South Yemen) to form the Republic of Yemen, with Saleh as president. Tensions between north and south later erupted in the 1994 civil war, after which northern dominance of the unified state increased.

Geography and demographics

North Yemen comprised the mountainous western highlands, the Tihama coastal plain on the Red Sea and northern desert fringes. Its population was predominantly Muslim, divided between Zaydi Shia in the highlands and Shafi'i Sunni in the lowlands, and its economy was based largely on agriculture, including qat cultivation, and remittances from workers abroad.
