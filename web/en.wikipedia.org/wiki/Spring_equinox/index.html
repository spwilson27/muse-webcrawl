Spring equinox

The spring equinox — the March equinox in the Northern Hemisphere and the September equinox in the Southern Hemisphere — is the moment when the Sun crosses the celestial equator moving northward, and day and night are of approximately equal length across the planet. In the Northern Hemisphere it marks the beginning of astronomical spring; in the Southern Hemisphere it begins autumn.

Date and astronomy

The March equinox usually falls on 20 March, occasionally on 19 or 21 March. At the equinox the subsolar point leaves the Southern Hemisphere and crosses the equator, and the Sun rises due east and sets due west. Because of atmospheric refraction and the Sun's apparent size, the day of exactly equal light and darkness (the equilux) occurs a few days before the spring equinox at mid-latitudes.

Cultural significance

The spring equinox has long been celebrated as a time of renewal and rebirth. It anchors the calculation of Easter in Western Christianity, determines the Persian New Year (Nowruz) in Iran and Central Asia, and coincides approximately with festivals such as Holi and the vernal equinox holiday in Japan. Sites including Chichen Itza are noted for equinoctial alignments and light effects.
