Plainfield is a village in Waushara County, Wisconsin, United States. The population was 899 at the 2020 census. The village is located within the Town of Plainfield. It lies in the Central Sands region of the state, an area of sandy soils formed by glacial Lake Wisconsin, and the local economy is strongly influenced by irrigated agriculture, including potatoes and vegetables.

History

The community was originally known as Norwich and was renamed Plainfield. A post office has operated under the name Plainfield since 1850. The village grew as a local trading point for the surrounding farm country and was served by a railroad line through the Central Sands.

Ed Gein

Plainfield is widely known as the home and burial place of murderer and body snatcher Ed Gein. Gein's crimes, discovered in 1957 when police searched his farmhouse and found human remains and objects made from skin and bone, attracted worldwide notoriety and influenced horror fiction and film, including the characters of Norman Bates in Psycho and Leatherface in The Texas Chain Saw Massacre. Gein's house burned down in 1958, but his grave in Plainfield Cemetery remains a point of macabre interest; his headstone was repeatedly vandalised and was stolen in 2000 before being recovered.

Geography

According to the United States Census Bureau, the village has a total area of about 1.61 square miles (4.18 km²), all of it land. Wisconsin State Highway 73 passes through the village, and Interstate 39 runs nearby to the west.

Demographics

As of the census of 2020, there were 899 people residing in the village. The population is predominantly White, with a growing Hispanic or Latino community associated with agricultural employment in the region.
