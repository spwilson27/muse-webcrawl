Star Trek: The Next Generation (TNG) is an American science fiction television series created by Gene Roddenberry. It originally aired from September 28, 1987, to May 23, 1994, in syndication, spanning 178 episodes over seven seasons. The third series in the Star Trek franchise, it is set in the 24th century, about a century after The Original Series, and follows the crew of the starship USS Enterprise-D under Captain Jean-Luc Picard (Patrick Stewart).

Premise and cast

The crew explores the galaxy under the banner of the United Federation of Planets, encountering phenomena and species such as the omnipotent Q, the cybernetic Borg, and the Ferengi and Klingons in new roles. Regular characters include Commander William Riker, the android Lt. Commander Data, counselor Deanna Troi, chief engineer Geordi La Forge, security chief Worf, and Dr. Beverly Crusher.

Production and reception

Roddenberry initially resisted repeating the original series' format, insisting on new characters and a more utopian tone. After uneven early seasons, the show matured under producers such as Michael Piller, becoming one of the most acclaimed science fiction series on television; the series finale, "All Good Things...", won the Hugo Award for Best Dramatic Presentation. TNG launched a wave of spin-offs including Deep Space Nine and Voyager, and four feature films: Generations (1994), First Contact (1996), Insurrection (1998), and Nemesis (2002). Much of its cast returned for Star Trek: Picard (2020–2023).
