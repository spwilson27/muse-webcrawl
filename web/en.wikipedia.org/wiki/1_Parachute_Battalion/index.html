1 Parachute Battalion (1 Bn Para), commonly known as the Parabats, is the only full-time airborne battalion of the South African Army. It was established on 1 April 1961 after a group of volunteers from the South African Air Force Regiment and other units were sent to the United Kingdom for parachute training at RAF Abingdon.

History

The battalion grew out of the parachute company formed in 1960. Based at Tempe Military Base in Bloemfontein, it became the nucleus of the South African Parachute Brigade. During the South African Border War, paratroopers of the battalion took part in numerous operations in Angola and Namibia, including, as part of larger battle groups, the airborne assault at Cassinga in 1978 (Operation Reindeer), one of the largest parachute operations undertaken by South African forces.

Role and training

The battalion provides the South African National Defence Force with a parachute and air-landing capability. Selection and training are famously demanding: candidates undergo an arduous selection course followed by basic parachute training, and qualified members wear the distinctive maroon beret and parachute wings. The unit has deployed on peacekeeping missions in Africa, including in Burundi and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where paratroopers fought at the Battle of Bangui in 2013 as part of a broader South African deployment.
