Siege of Halicarnassus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Halicarnassus (334 BC) was fought between the forces of Alexander the Great and the Achaemenid Persian Empire at Halicarnassus, capital of Caria in southwestern Asia Minor. The city, commanded by the satrap Orontobates and the Rhodian mercenary general Memnon, was the strongest Persian naval base in the Aegean and the first serious obstacle Alexander faced after crossing into Asia.

The siege

Alexander, having dismissed most of his fleet, lacked naval support and attacked the landward walls with siege towers, rams and catapults. The defenders conducted aggressive sorties, at one point nearly driving the Macedonians back to their camp; in the fiercest fighting two of Alexander's veteran battalions broke a sallied force at a collapsed bridge over the defensive ditch, killing many leading Persians. Repeated Macedonian assaults gradually carried the outer defences.

Outcome

Memnon and Orontobates finally abandoned the city by night, setting fire to parts of it, and withdrew to the citadels of Salmacis and the island fortress, which Alexander could not take without a fleet. He occupied and partly demolished the city, installed Ada of Caria as satrap, and left a force to contain the citadels, which held out for about a year. Memnon carried the war into the Aegean until his death in 333 BC.
