Turul

The Turul is a mythological bird of prey, mostly depicted as a falcon, in Hungarian tradition and a national symbol of modern Hungary. It is said to have been the leader and forerunner of the Hungarians on their migration into the Carpathian Basin, and traditions about it are preserved in medieval Hungarian chronicles.

Mythology

According to legend recorded in the Gesta Hungarorum and the Chronicon Pictum, the Turul appeared in a dream to Emese, the wife of Ügyek and mother of Álmos, the first leader of the Hungarian tribes. In the dream the bird impregnated her (or, in other versions, a river of glory flowed from her womb), prophesying that her descendants would be great kings. Álmos's line, the Árpád dynasty, ruled Hungary for centuries.

A second legend tells how the Turul guided the seven Hungarian chieftains during the conquest of the Carpathian Basin (the Honfoglalás, c. 895). When the Hungarians' sacred bird dropped the sword it was carrying, the tribes settled at that spot; a similar tale links the bird to the founding of the country's settlements. The name Turul is thought to derive from a Turkic word for a bird of prey, possibly related to the Old Turkic toɣrïl or to Altaic falconry terms.

Symbolism and monuments

The Turul remains a potent national and military symbol in Hungary. It appears on the coat of arms of the Hungarian Defence Forces and of the Office of National Security. Monumental Turul statues stand in several cities; the largest, erected in 1896 for the Hungarian millennium at Bánhida (now part of Tatabánya), depicts the bird with a wingspan of about 15 metres, holding the sword of Attila — it is among the largest bird statues in the world. Another prominent Turul monument stands in Turul Park in Budapest, and a controversial statue stands in Mukachevo (Munkács), in modern-day Ukraine.
