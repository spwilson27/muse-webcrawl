Uniting for Consensus (UfC), nicknamed the Coffee Club, is a coalition of United Nations member states, formed in 1995, that opposes the bid of the G4 nations (Brazil, Germany, India, and Japan) for permanent seats on the UN Security Council. The group instead proposes expanding only the Council's non-permanent, elected membership, arguing that new permanent seats would entrench inequality and that regional representation is better served by longer-term renewable seats.

Position

UfC advocates enlarging the Security Council from 15 to about 25–26 members by adding elected seats with longer terms (three to five years) and the possibility of immediate re-election, distributed among regional groups including Africa, Asia-Pacific, Latin America and the Caribbean, and Eastern Europe. It opposes creating new permanent members or extending the veto.

Members

The movement originated in a 1995 meeting convened by the Italian permanent representative Francesco Paolo Fulci. Its core group has included Italy, Pakistan, Mexico, Egypt, Argentina, Canada, Colombia, Costa Rica, Malta, San Marino, Spain, South Korea, and Turkey, with China and Indonesia among its supporters.
