Jacob Marley

Jacob Marley

First appearance: A Christmas Carol (1843)

Created by: Charles Dickens

Died: Seven years before the story opens (Christmas Eve, 1836)

Occupation: Money-lender and businessman; Scrooge's partner

Jacob Marley is a fictional character in Charles Dickens's 1843 novella A Christmas Carol. The deceased business partner of Ebenezer Scrooge, Marley returns as a ghost on Christmas Eve, seven years to the day after his death, to warn Scrooge that his own miserly course will lead to damnation unless he changes his ways.

Role in the novella

The story opens with its famous insistence — "Marley was dead: to begin with" — and Scrooge, Marley's sole executor, mourner, and friend, has never painted out his partner's name above the counting-house door. On Christmas Eve, Scrooge first sees Marley's face in the door knocker, then, in his chambers, the ghost himself appears, clad in a heavy chain:

"It was a chain he had forged in life... made of cash-boxes, keys, padlocks, ledgers, deeds, and heavy purses wrought in steel."

Marley explains that every spirit who neglects humankind in life must wander the earth after death witnessing what it might have shared. He announces that Scrooge will be visited by three spirits — the Ghosts of Christmas Past, Present, and Yet to Come — offering him "a chance and hope of escaping my fate." Marley's visitation sets the entire plot in motion.

Interpretation and adaptations

Marley embodies the novella's moral: wealth hoarded and humanity ignored become literal bondage. Dickens drew the name in part from Dr. Miles Marley, a physician he knew. The character has been portrayed in countless adaptations of A Christmas Carol on stage, film, and television — notably by Leo G. Carroll, Alec Guinness, Frank Finlay, and Gary Oldman in A Christmas Carol (1999) — and Marley's ghost is often staged with elaborate rattling chains and special effects. In some versions, such as The Muppet Christmas Carol, the role is doubled as Jacob and Robert Marley.
