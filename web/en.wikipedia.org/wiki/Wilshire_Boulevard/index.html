Wilshire Boulevard

Wilshire Boulevard is a prominent 15.83 mi (25.48 km) boulevard in the Los Angeles area of Southern California, extending from Ocean Avenue in the city of Santa Monica east to Grand Avenue in Downtown Los Angeles. It is one of the principal east-west arterial roads in the Los Angeles metropolitan area, traversing five of the city's districts and the incorporated city of Beverly Hills.

History

The boulevard was named for Henry Gaylord Wilshire, a wealthy developer who in 1895 began developing an elite residential subdivision on a barley field west of downtown. Wilshire donated a strip of land for the boulevard that would bear his name, on the condition that it be used only for passenger traffic and that no freight railroads be built along it. Over the twentieth century the street developed into a major commercial corridor, lined with department stores, office towers, and theaters.

Notable stretches

The segment between Fairfax Avenue and Highland Avenue, densely lined with Art Deco commercial buildings, is known as the Miracle Mile. Further west, Wilshire passes through Beverly Hills, Westwood, and the Brentwood area before terminating at Ocean Avenue in Santa Monica. The easternmost portion runs through downtown Los Angeles, where it is home to several historic office buildings, including the Wilshire Grand Center, the tallest building in the western United States at its completion.

Transit

The Los Angeles Metro's D Line (formerly Purple Line) subway runs beneath Wilshire Boulevard for much of its length, with extensions westward planned and under construction toward Westwood. Metro bus lines 20 and 720 (Rapid) also serve the corridor.
