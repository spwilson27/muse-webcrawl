S&H Green Stamps

Founded1896, by Thomas Sperry and Shelley Byron Hutchinson
OperatorSperry & Hutchinson Company
TypeTrading-stamp loyalty programme

S&H Green Stamps were a popular American trading-stamp loyalty programme operated by the Sperry & Hutchinson Company from 1896 into the late 20th century. Retailers — chiefly supermarkets and petrol stations — bought the stamps and gave them to customers in proportion to their purchases; customers pasted them into saver books and redeemed filled books for merchandise from S&H catalogues and "Green Stamp" redemption stores.

Peak and decline

At their peak in the 1960s, S&H issued roughly three times as many stamps as the U.S. Post Office issued postage stamps, and its catalogue was among the largest publications in the country. The 1970s energy crises, price competition from discount stores and changing retail economics caused most retailers to drop stamps, and the programme faded; the brand passed through several owners, ending as an online points scheme before finally disappearing.

Cultural legacy

Green Stamps became a byword for mid-century American thrift, commemorated in songs and advertising and collected in countless kitchen drawers. Competitors included Top Value Stamps, Gold Bond Stamps and Blue Chip Stamps.
