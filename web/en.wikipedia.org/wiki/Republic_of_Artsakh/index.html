Republic of Artsakh

The Republic of Artsakh (Armenian: Արցախի Հանրապետություն), also known as the Nagorno-Karabakh Republic, was a breakaway state in the South Caucasus whose territory was internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan. It controlled most of the former Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast and surrounding districts from the end of the First Nagorno-Karabakh War in 1994 until its dissolution in 2023–2024.

History

The Nagorno-Karabakh conflict began in 1988 as Armenians of the autonomous oblast sought unification with Armenia. After the collapse of the USSR, the First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1992–1994) left ethnic Armenian forces in control; a Russian-brokered ceasefire followed. The Second Nagorno-Karabakh War in 2020 returned large territories to Azerbaijan and stationed Russian peacekeepers along the Lachin corridor. Following a blockade beginning in December 2022, Azerbaijan launched an offensive on 19–20 September 2023; Artsakh authorities capitulated, and President Samvel Shahramanyan signed a decree dissolving all state institutions by 1 January 2024. Nearly the entire ethnic Armenian population — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia within days.

Politics

Artsakh held regular elections and operated as a de facto state closely integrated with Armenia, though it was never recognised by any UN member state, including Armenia itself. Its presidents included Robert Kocharyan (later president of Armenia), Arkadi Ghukasyan, Bako Sahakyan and Arayik Harutyunyan.
