Youth

Youth is the time of life when one is young. The word, youth, can also mean the time between childhood and adulthood (maturity), but it is also often used to refer to being in the early part of life, or being in the state of being a young person. An individual's actual maturity may not correspond to their chronological age, as immature individuals can exist at all ages.

Throughout the world, the terms youth, adolescent, teenager, kid, and young person are often used interchangeably, though they may have slightly different meanings in particular contexts. Youth is also defined as a subculture or a period of increased social and economic dependence. The United Nations defines youth as persons between the ages of 15 and 24 years, while the World Health Organization groups adolescents (10–19) and youth (15–24) together as "young people" aged 10 to 24.

Definitions

There is no universally agreed international definition of the youth age group. The UN Secretariat uses the range 15–24 for statistical purposes, while the African Youth Charter defines youth as persons aged 15–35, and many national definitions extend into the late twenties or early thirties. The transition from childhood to adulthood — marked by events such as leaving school, entering work, leaving the parental home, marriage and parenthood — has lengthened in many societies, giving rise to the concept of "emerging adulthood" for the late teens and twenties.

History

The idea of youth as a distinct stage of life is relatively modern. In pre-industrial societies, children moved more directly into adult work roles. Compulsory schooling, child labour laws, and urbanisation in the 19th and 20th centuries created a prolonged transitional period. Thinkers such as G. Stanley Hall, who published Adolescence in 1904, helped establish the scientific study of the age group, and the post-war decades saw the emergence of distinctive youth cultures, from rock and roll to hip hop.

Development

Puberty triggers physical maturation, while brain development — particularly of the prefrontal cortex, involved in planning and impulse control — continues into the mid-twenties. Psychologists describe identity formation as the central developmental task of the period, following Erik Erikson's stage of "identity versus role confusion". Social relationships shift in importance from family toward peer groups.

Health

Youth is generally a period of good physical health, but it carries distinctive risks: road traffic injuries are a leading cause of death among young people worldwide, and most mental health conditions have their onset by the mid-twenties. Public health programmes target issues such as substance use, sexual health, and, increasingly, the effects of social media and sedentary lifestyles.

Education and employment

Global secondary-school enrolment has risen steadily, and more than 40% of the relevant age cohort in many countries now enters tertiary education. At the same time, young people face higher unemployment rates than adults — roughly three times the adult rate globally according to the International Labour Organization — and a substantial share are NEET (not in education, employment, or training).

Youth culture

Young people have been at the centre of successive cultural movements: the flappers of the 1920s, the counterculture of the 1960s, punk, hip hop, rave and gaming cultures, and today's internet-native meme and streaming cultures. Youth culture is closely tied to music, fashion, slang, and new media technologies.

Rights and policy

The UN General Assembly adopted its first resolutions on youth in 1965, and the World Programme of Action for Youth (1995) set a framework for national youth policies. The UN Security Council Resolution 2250 (2015) recognised young people's role in peace and security. Many countries maintain ministries or agencies for youth, and voting-age debates frequently centre on whether the franchise should extend to 16- and 17-year-olds.
