Ngô Đình Diệm (3 January 1901 – 2 November 1963) was a Vietnamese politician who served as the first president of South Vietnam (the Republic of Vietnam) from 1955 until his assassination in a military coup in 1963. A Catholic from a mandarin family, he had been the last prime minister of the State of Vietnam under Emperor Bảo Đại.

Presidency

Diệm consolidated power after a rigged 1955 referendum, refused the nationwide elections envisaged by the Geneva Accords, and ruled with heavy reliance on his family — notably his brother Ngô Đình Nhu and sister-in-law Madame Nhu. His regime suppressed communists and, disastrously, discriminated against the Buddhist majority; the 1963 Buddhist crisis, sparked by shootings in Huế and the self-immolation of Thích Quảng Đức, destroyed American confidence in him.

Death

On 1–2 November 1963, with tacit U.S. approval, ARVN generals overthrew Diệm; he and Nhu were arrested and killed in an armoured personnel carrier. His fall deepened the Vietnam War's escalation.
