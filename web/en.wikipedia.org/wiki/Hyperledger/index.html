Hyperledger

Hyperledger (now known as Hyperledger Foundation) is an umbrella project of open source blockchains and related tools for developing blockchain-based distributed ledgers, hosted by the Linux Foundation. It was announced in December 2015 with founding members including IBM, Intel, Cisco, JPMorgan Chase and the Linux Foundation itself.

Projects

The best-known Hyperledger framework is Hyperledger Fabric, originally contributed by IBM, a permissioned blockchain platform designed for enterprise use, supporting modular consensus and smart contracts ("chaincode") written in general-purpose languages. Other projects have included Hyperledger Sawtooth (contributed by Intel), Hyperledger Besu (an Ethereum client), and tools such as Hyperledger Caliper for benchmarking. Unlike public cryptocurrencies, Hyperledger frameworks generally target known, permissioned participants and do not require a native cryptocurrency.

Governance and membership

The foundation is a multi-stakeholder collaboration whose members include technology companies, banks and startups; Brian Behlendorf served as its first executive director. Its stated goal is to advance cross-industry blockchain technologies through open governance and neutral hosting. In 2024 the Linux Foundation consolidated its decentralised trust projects, with Hyperledger becoming part of the LF Decentralized Trust initiative.
