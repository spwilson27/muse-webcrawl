A visa (from the Latin charta visa, meaning "paper that has been seen") is a conditional authorization granted by a polity to a foreigner that allows them to enter, remain within, or leave its territory. Visas typically include limits on the duration of the foreigner's stay, areas within the country they may enter, the dates they may enter, the number of permitted visits, or if the individual has the ability to work in the country.

Historically, immigration officials were empowered to permit or reject entry of visitors on arrival at the frontiers. If permitted entry, the official would sometimes issue a visa, normally in the form of a stamp in the passport. Today, travellers wishing to enter another country must often apply in advance for what is also called a visa.

History

In western Europe in the late 19th century and early 20th century, passports and visas were not generally necessary for moving from one country to another. The relatively high speed and large movements of people travelling by train would have caused bottlenecks if regular controls had been used. Passports and visas became usually necessary as travel documents only after World War I.

Until the end of World War I, a visa was primarily an endorsement note in the passport, later being a stamp or a glued sheet. The modern visa may be a sticker or stamp in the passport, an electronic record of the authorization, or a separate document.

Types of visa

  • Tourist visa — for a limited period of leisure travel, with no business activities allowed.
  • Business visa — for engaging in commerce in the country; usually does not include employment.
  • Student visa — allows the holder to study at an institution of higher learning.
  • Work visa — based on a labor certification or employment contract.
  • Transit visa — for passing through the country to a destination outside that country.
  • Refugee and asylum visas — for persons fleeing persecution, war, or natural disaster.
  • Immigrant visa — for those intending to immigrate permanently.

Visa policy

Countries have visa restrictions in order to check and control the flow of visitors in and out of the country and to prevent illegal immigration and other criminal activities. Forcing travellers to apply for a visa allows the authorities to vet potential visitors. Countries may also have reciprocal arrangements: many states are party to agreements such as the Schengen Area in Europe, whose citizens enjoy freedom of movement between member states. Some countries operate waiver schemes such as the United States Visa Waiver Program, which allow nationals of selected countries to enter without a visa for short stays.

Electronic visas

An increasing number of countries issue e-visas, applied for online and recorded electronically against the traveller's passport number rather than as a physical sticker. Australia pioneered the electronic travel authority model, dispensing with visa labels entirely.
