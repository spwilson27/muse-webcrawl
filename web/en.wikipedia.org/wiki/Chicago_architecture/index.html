Architecture of Chicago

The architecture of Chicago has influenced and been reflected in the history of American architecture. The skyline of Chicago is among the world's tallest and densest. Rebuilt after the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, the city became the birthplace of the skyscraper and a laboratory for architectural innovation, from the steel-frame buildings of the Chicago school to the Prairie School of Frank Lloyd Wright and the modernism of Ludwig Mies van der Rohe.

Early development and the first Chicago school

Following the 1871 fire, which destroyed much of the central city, architects including William Le Baron Jenney, Daniel Burnham, John Wellborn Root and Louis Sullivan developed steel-skeleton construction that allowed buildings to rise higher on Chicago's clay soil. Landmark works of the era include the Monadnock Building, the Rookery and Sullivan's Carson, Pirie, Scott Building. The World's Columbian Exposition of 1893, coordinated by Burnham, promoted neoclassical planning and inspired the City Beautiful movement, culminating in Burnham's 1909 Plan of Chicago.

Prairie School and Wright

In the first decades of the 20th century, Frank Lloyd Wright and his contemporaries developed the Prairie School, emphasising horizontal lines, low-pitched roofs and integration with the landscape. Wright's Robie House (1910) in Hyde Park is a canonical example, and his studio in Oak Park anchored a community of like-minded architects.

Second Chicago school and modernism

After the Second World War, Mies van der Rohe, teaching at the Illinois Institute of Technology, reshaped Chicago building with minimalist steel-and-glass towers such as the Lake Shore Drive Apartments and the Federal Center. Firms including Skidmore, Owings & Merrill advanced structural innovations — bundled-tube construction in the Willis Tower (1973) and the John Hancock Center's X-bracing — that kept Chicago at the forefront of tall-building design.

Contemporary architecture

Recent landmarks include Millennium Park (2004), with Frank Gehry's Jay Pritzker Pavilion and Anish Kapoor's Cloud Gate, and supertall additions to the skyline such as the St. Regis Chicago (2020) by Studio Gang. The Chicago Architecture Center and the annual Open House Chicago festival reflect the city's architectural culture.
