Lucy is a NASA space probe on a twelve-year journey to study eight different asteroids, principally the Jupiter trojans — swarms of primitive bodies that share Jupiter's orbit around the Sun. Launched on 16 October 2021 from Cape Canaveral aboard an Atlas V rocket, it is the first mission to the Trojan asteroids.

Mission

The mission, selected under NASA's Discovery Program and led by principal investigator Hal Levison of the Southwest Research Institute, is named after the fossilised australopithecine skeleton "Lucy": just as the fossil illuminated human origins, the Trojan asteroids are thought to be fossils of planetary formation. Lucy's trajectory uses three Earth gravity assists to reach the L4 "Greek camp" in 2027–2028 and the L5 "Trojan camp" in 2033.

Encounters

Targets include the main-belt asteroid 52246 Donaldjohanson (2025) and the Trojans Eurybates, Polymele, Leucus, Orus, and the binary pair Patroclus and Menoetius. En route, in November 2023, Lucy flew past the small main-belt asteroid 152830 Dinkinesh and discovered that it has a satellite, Selam, which proved to be a contact binary. Instruments include the L'LORRI high-resolution imager, the L'Ralph colour imager and infrared spectrometer, and the L'TES thermal emission spectrometer.

Spacecraft

Built by Lockheed Martin, Lucy carries two enormous circular solar arrays, 7.3 metres in diameter, needed to operate at Jupiter's distance from the Sun. Shortly after launch, one array failed to fully latch open, though it produced nearly full power and the mission continued as planned.
