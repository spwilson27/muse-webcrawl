Dame Barbara Mary Quant (11 February 1930 – 13 April 2023) was a British fashion designer who became a central figure in the London-based Mod and youth fashion movements of the 1960s. She was one of the designers credited with popularising the miniskirt and hot pants, and she helped define the look of "Swinging London."

Career

Quant studied illustration at Goldsmiths College and in 1955 opened her first boutique, Bazaar, on the King's Road in Chelsea with her husband Alexander Plunket Greene and Archie McNair. Finding the available clothes too staid, she began designing her own: short, simple, colourful garments made from inexpensive fabrics, worn with coloured tights and flat shoes. Her trademark daisy logo and her own Vidal Sassoon geometric bob haircut made her as recognisable as her clothes.

The miniskirt

Quant raised hemlines steadily, and by the mid-1960s her skirts ended well above the knee. She named the miniskirt after her favourite car, the Mini, and insisted that it was young women themselves — the girls on the King's Road — who drove the style. Whether or not she invented it, she made it the emblem of a liberated, youthful femininity and exported it worldwide through licensing, mass production, and a successful cosmetics line.

Honours and legacy

Quant was appointed OBE in 1966 and a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2015. A major retrospective at the Victoria and Albert Museum in 2019 drew huge crowds. She is remembered for democratising fashion — bringing stylish clothes within reach of ordinary young women and challenging the dominance of Paris couture.
