Richard Whalley Anthony Curtis (born 8 November 1956) is a British screenwriter, film director and producer. He is one of Britain's most successful comedy writers, known for the television series Blackadder and Mr. Bean (co-created with Rowan Atkinson) and for a string of internationally successful romantic comedy films including Four Weddings and a Funeral (1994), Notting Hill (1999), Bridget Jones's Diary (2001) and Love Actually (2003), his directorial debut. He is also a co-founder of the charity Comic Relief.

Early life and education

Curtis was born in Wellington, New Zealand, to Czechoslovak-born parents and grew up in several countries, including the Philippines and Sweden, before the family settled in England. He was educated at Papplewick School and Harrow School, and read English Language and Literature at Christ Church, Oxford, where he met Rowan Atkinson and began writing and performing comedy.

Television career

Curtis began writing for the satirical series Not the Nine O'Clock News before co-writing, with Atkinson and others, the historical sitcom Blackadder (1983–1989), whose four series are regularly ranked among the finest British comedies. With Atkinson he also created Mr. Bean (1990–1995), whose near-silent slapstick achieved enormous international success. He later wrote The Vicar of Dibley for Dawn French.

Film career

Four Weddings and a Funeral, directed by Mike Newell, became the highest-grossing British film of its time and earned Curtis an Academy Award nomination for Best Original Screenplay. He followed it with Notting Hill and the Bridget Jones films. As writer-director he made Love Actually (2003), an ensemble romantic comedy that became a durable seasonal favourite, followed by The Boat That Rocked (2009), About Time (2013) and Yesterday (2019), written by him and directed by Danny Boyle. He also co-wrote Steven Spielberg's War Horse (2011).

Charity work

In 1985 Curtis co-founded Comic Relief with comedian Lenny Henry, in response to the Ethiopian famine. Its Red Nose Day telethons have raised more than £1 billion for charitable causes in the UK and abroad. He has also been active in campaigns such as Make Poverty History and co-founded Project Everyone, promoting the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals.

Personal life

Curtis lives in London with broadcaster Emma Freud, his long-term partner, with whom he has four children. His sister-in-law connections aside, he has often spoken about using his writing to support his charitable interests, regarding popular entertainment and campaigning as complementary pursuits.
