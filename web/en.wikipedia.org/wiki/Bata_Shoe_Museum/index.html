Bata Shoe Museum

The Bata Shoe Museum is a museum of footwear and shoemaking in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Located at 327 Bloor Street West, at the southwest corner of Bloor and St. George streets, the museum houses a collection of more than 13,000 shoes and related artefacts spanning some 4,500 years of history. It is the only museum in North America dedicated solely to the history and anthropology of footwear.

The Bata Shoe Museum on Bloor Street West, Toronto.

History

The collection was begun by Sonja Bata, whose family founded the Bata shoe company in 1894 in Zlín, in what is now the Czech Republic. Travelling widely on business, Sonja Bata collected traditional footwear from the regions she visited. In 1979 the Bata family established the Bata Shoe Museum Foundation to operate the collection and create a centre for footwear research. The museum opened to the public in its present purpose-built home on 6 May 1995.

Building

The museum building was designed by Canadian architect Raymond Moriyama. Its limestone-clad form is intended to evoke an opening shoebox, and the structure includes a dramatic multi-storey glass atrium. The building contains four permanent galleries, temporary exhibition space, a seminar room and a gift shop.

Collection and exhibitions

The permanent exhibition "All About Shoes" traces the social and cultural roles of footwear from ancient Egyptian sandals and Chinese foot-binding shoes to Renaissance chopines, Indigenous moccasins, and twentieth-century designer heels. The museum also holds celebrity footwear, including shoes worn by Queen Victoria, Elton John, Marilyn Monroe and Terry Fox. Rotating exhibitions have explored topics such as footwear in the Arctic, fashion and crime, and the history of sneakers.
