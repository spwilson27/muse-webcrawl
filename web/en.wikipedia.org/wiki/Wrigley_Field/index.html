Wrigley Field is a baseball stadium in the Lakeview neighbourhood of Chicago, Illinois, United States. It is the home of the Chicago Cubs of Major League Baseball. Opened in 1914, it is the second-oldest ballpark in the majors, after Fenway Park in Boston, and is famed for its ivy-covered outfield walls, hand-operated scoreboard and rooftop seating on neighbouring buildings.

History

The park was built in 1914 as Weeghman Park for the Chicago Whales of the Federal League. When the league folded, the Cubs moved in for the 1916 season. It was renamed Cubs Park in 1920 and Wrigley Field in 1927, after chewing-gum magnate William Wrigley Jr., the club's owner. The outfield ivy was planted in 1937, and the iconic hand-turned scoreboard was installed the same year.

For decades the Cubs played only day games at home because the park had no lights; the first scheduled night game took place on 8 August 1988. Wrigley Field hosted its first World Series since 1945 in 2016, when the Cubs won their first championship in 108 years, though the decisive Game 7 was played in Cleveland.

Features

Distinctive features include the ivy on the outfield walls, the centre-field scoreboard topped by flags showing the league standings, the red marquee over the main entrance reading "Wrigley Field, Home of Chicago Cubs", and the flag flown after each game — a white flag with a blue "W" for a win, or a blue flag with a white "L" for a loss. Fans historically watched from rooftops across Waveland and Sheffield Avenues, a tradition now formalised in licensed rooftop clubs.

Other events

The stadium has hosted American football (it was home to the Chicago Bears from 1921 to 1970), soccer, ice hockey — including the 2009 NHL Winter Classic — and major concerts.
