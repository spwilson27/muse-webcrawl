El filibusterismo

AuthorJosé Rizal
LanguageSpanish
Published1891, Ghent, Belgium
Preceded byNoli Me Tángere (1887)

El filibusterismo ("The Filibusterism"), known in English as The Reign of Greed, is the second novel written by the Philippine national hero José Rizal. Published in Ghent in 1891, it is the sequel to Noli Me Tángere and, like it, exposed the abuses of Spanish colonial rule and the clergy in the Philippines.

Plot

Thirteen years after the events of the first novel, Crisóstomo Ibarra returns to the Philippines disguised as Simoun, a wealthy jeweller and confidant of the Governor-General. Embittered and seeking revenge for the injustices he suffered and the death of María Clara, Simoun plots revolution: he foments abuses to provoke the people to rise, and prepares a bomb hidden in a pomegranate-shaped lamp to be detonated at a wedding feast attended by colonial officials. The plot unravels when the student Basilio, whom Simoun had drawn into the conspiracy, warns the guest Isagani, who throws the lamp into the river. Simoun flees, takes poison, and confesses to a priest before dying.

Themes and legacy

Darker than its predecessor, the novel debates reform against revolution and the corrupting effect of vengeance. Rizal dedicated it to the three executed Filipino priests Gómez, Burgos, and Zamora (Gomburza). Together with Noli Me Tángere, it helped awaken Filipino nationalism and remains required reading in Philippine schools.
