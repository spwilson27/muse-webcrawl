Polaris (UK nuclear programme)

The United Kingdom Polaris programme, officially the British Polaris Force, provided Britain's submarine-based nuclear deterrent from the late 1960s until 1996, when it was replaced by Trident. Following the Nassau Agreement of December 1962 between Prime Minister Harold Macmillan and President John F. Kennedy, the United States agreed to supply the United Kingdom with Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missiles, while Britain built its own submarines and nuclear warheads.

The force

Four Resolution-class ballistic missile submarines — HMS Resolution, Repulse, Renown, and Revenge — were built in Britain, each armed with sixteen Polaris A-3 missiles fitted with British warheads. The force was based at Faslane (HMNB Clyde) on the Gareloch, with the armament depot at nearby Coulport. The first deterrent patrol sailed in June 1968, and from 1969 the Royal Navy maintained continuous at-sea deterrence (CASD), with at least one boat always on patrol; operational control of the deterrent passed from RAF Bomber Command's V-force to the Royal Navy.

Chevaline

In the 1970s and early 1980s the British warhead was extensively upgraded under the secret Chevaline programme, which replaced the original three warheads with two more sophisticated ones plus a suite of penetration aids (decoys and other countermeasures) to defeat Soviet anti-ballistic missile defences around Moscow, which limited Britain's targeting under the ABM Treaty. Chevaline was publicly revealed in 1980 and entered service in 1982, at a cost of around £1 billion, far above initial estimates.

Replacement

Polaris remained in service longer than originally planned. The decision to replace it with the US Trident system was announced in 1980 (Trident I C4, changed to Trident II D5 in 1982). The last Polaris patrol ended in 1996, when HMS Revenge was paid off and the Vanguard-class submarines armed with Trident II D5 took over the deterrent role.
