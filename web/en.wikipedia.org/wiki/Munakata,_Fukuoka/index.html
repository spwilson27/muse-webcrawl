Munakata (宗像市, Munakata-shi) is a city in Fukuoka Prefecture, Japan, situated on the coast of the Genkai Sea between Fukuoka City and Kitakyushu. As of recent estimates it has a population of roughly 97,000. The modern city was formed in 1981 and expanded by merging with neighbouring towns in the 2000s.

History

The Munakata region was historically a centre of maritime trade and seafaring between Japan and the Korean Peninsula and China. The Munakata clan, a powerful local family of seafaring shrine officials, administered the area and its great shrine for centuries. The city developed as a commuter and industrial area in the modern period and absorbed the town of Genkai in 2003 and the village of Ōshima in 2005.

Munakata Taisha

Munakata is home to Munakata Taisha, one of Japan's oldest Shinto shrines, dedicated to the three Munakata goddesses (the Munakata Sanjoshin), daughters of Amaterasu and Susanoo, revered as protectors of seafarers and traffic safety. The shrine comprises three sites: Hetsu-gū on the Kyushu mainland, Nakatsu-gū on the island of Ōshima, and Okitsu-gū on the remote sacred island of Okinoshima, where rituals have been performed since the 4th century and entry is traditionally restricted.

World Heritage

In 2017, the "Sacred Island of Okinoshima and Associated Sites in the Munakata Region" was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognising the exceptionally preserved ritual sites and votive deposits on Okinoshima, which document exchanges between Japan and the rest of East Asia from the 4th to the 9th centuries.
