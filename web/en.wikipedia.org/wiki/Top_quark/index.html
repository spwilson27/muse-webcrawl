The top quark, sometimes also referred to as the truth quark (symbol: t), is the most massive of all observed elementary particles. It derives its mass from its coupling to the Higgs boson. The top quark is the third-generation up-type quark, with an electric charge of +2/3 e, and is the partner of the bottom quark.

History

In 1973, Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa predicted the existence of a third generation of quarks to explain observed CP violations in kaon decays. The top quark was discovered in 1995 by the CDF and DØ experiments at Fermilab, using the Tevatron collider.

Properties

With a mass of about 172.7 GeV/c² — roughly that of a gold atom's nucleus — the top quark is by far the heaviest known elementary particle. Its extremely short lifetime of about 5×10−25 seconds means it decays, almost always into a bottom quark and a W boson, before it can hadronise. It is the only quark that decays before forming hadrons, allowing physicists to study a "bare" quark.

Production

Top quarks are produced mainly in top–antitop pairs through the strong interaction at hadron colliders such as the Tevatron and the Large Hadron Collider, and also singly through the weak interaction.
