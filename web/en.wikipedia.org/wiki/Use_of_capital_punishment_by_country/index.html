Use of capital punishment by country

Capital punishment, also known as the death penalty, is a state-sanctioned practice of killing a person as a punishment for a crime. The legality and practice of capital punishment varies widely around the world. As of the 2020s, a majority of the world's countries have abolished it in law or in practice, while a minority retain and use it.

Overview

Countries are commonly classified into four categories: abolitionist for all crimes; abolitionist for ordinary crimes only; abolitionist in practice (retentionist in law but no executions for at least ten years); and retentionist. More than two-thirds of the world's nations fall into the abolitionist categories. All member states of the European Union and the Council of Europe have abolished the death penalty.

Retentionist countries

Countries that continue to carry out executions include China, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and the United States, among others. Recorded execution figures are highest in China, where numbers are treated as a state secret and are estimated in the thousands. Methods used worldwide include lethal injection, hanging, shooting, and beheading.

Trends

The global trend over recent decades has been toward abolition, with new countries abolishing the penalty nearly every year. International treaties such as the Second Optional Protocol to the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights commit signatories to abolition. Debates continue over deterrence, wrongful convictions, and the offences — such as drug trafficking and terrorism — for which the penalty is applied in some jurisdictions.
