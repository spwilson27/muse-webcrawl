Robert Fulton

Robert Fulton (November 14, 1765 – February 24, 1815) was an American engineer and inventor widely credited with developing the world's first commercially successful steamboat, the North River Steamboat (also known as the Clermont), which in 1807 carried passengers between New York City and Albany on the Hudson River.

Early life and Europe

Born in Pennsylvania, Fulton first trained as a portrait painter, studying in London under Benjamin West. In England and France he turned to engineering, promoting canal designs, experimenting with a submarine (the Nautilus, offered to Napoleon in 1800), and conducting steamboat trials on the Seine in 1803 in partnership with Robert R. Livingston.

Steamboat success

Returning to the United States, Fulton built the Clermont with a Boulton and Watt engine; its 1807 voyage inaugurated commercial steamboat service. Fulton and Livingston's monopoly spurred steamboat development on American rivers, and Fulton went on to design the first steam warship, Demologos. He died in 1815 and is commemorated in numerous place names, statues, and Fulton County and Fulton, Missouri.
