Rocket stove

A rocket stove is an efficient and hot-burning portable stove that uses small-diameter wood fuel. Fuel is burned in a simple combustion chamber containing an insulated vertical chimney, which ensures almost complete combustion before the flames reach the cooking surface. Rocket stove designs are most often used for portable stoves for cooking and for space heating.

A basic rocket stove made from metal cans.

History and design

The rocket stove was developed in the 1980s by Ianto Evans and Larry Winiarski at the Aprovecho Research Center in Oregon, drawing on earlier work on efficient biomass cookstoves. Its name comes from the roaring sound of the strong draft through the insulated riser. Key principles include an insulated elbow-shaped combustion chamber, a fuel magazine that feeds sticks horizontally, and a heat riser that promotes a strong chimney draught so that hot gases are drawn around the pot skirt.

Use

Because it burns twigs and small branches efficiently with little smoke, the rocket stove is widely promoted in development work as an improvement on open three-stone fires, reducing fuel consumption and indoor air pollution. Larger versions, called rocket mass heaters, couple the same combustion unit to a thermal mass bench for building heating. Commercial and homemade variants serve campers and off-grid households.
