PBY Catalina

The Consolidated PBY Catalina is a flying boat and amphibious aircraft produced by Consolidated Aircraft in the 1930s and 1940s. It was one of the most widely used seaplanes of World War II, serving with the United States Navy, Royal Air Force, Royal Canadian Air Force, and many other forces.

Design and role

The Catalina had a parasol wing, twin radial engines, and a distinctive hull for water landings; later PBY-5A variants added retractable tricycle landing gear. Crews used it for maritime patrol, anti-submarine warfare, convoy escort, search and rescue, and torpedo and bombing missions.

Service history

Catalinas are credited with sighting the Japanese fleet before the Battle of Midway and with helping locate the German battleship Bismarck. More than 3,300 were built, including licence production in Canada and the Soviet Union. After the war many served as water bombers for aerial firefighting and as civilian transports; some still fly as warbirds.
