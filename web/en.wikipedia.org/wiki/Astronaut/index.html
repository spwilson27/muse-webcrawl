An astronaut is a person trained, equipped and deployed by a human spaceflight program to serve as commander or crew member aboard a spacecraft. Yuri Gagarin became the first in 1961; Valentina Tereshkova the first woman (1963); the Apollo 11 crew the first to land on another world.

Life and work

Astronauts conduct science aboard stations, maintain spacecraft on spacewalks, and endure years of training for months of microgravity — which weakens bone and muscle (see Exercise countermeasures). Commercial crew and private astronaut missions have widened access in the 2020s, alongside the uncrewed probes at Europa and Titan.
