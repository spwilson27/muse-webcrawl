Fine Gael

Fine Gael (Irish: Fine Gael, meaning "Family of the Gaels" or "Tribe of the Gaels") is a political party in Ireland. It is generally positioned on the centre to centre-right of the political spectrum and is associated with liberal conservatism, Christian democracy, and pro-Europeanism.

History

Fine Gael was founded on 3 September 1933 through the merger of Cumann na nGaedheal, the National Centre Party, and the National Guard (the "Blueshirts"). Its first leader was Eoin O'Duffy, followed by W. T. Cosgrave, the first head of government of the Irish Free State. The party descends from the pro-Treaty side of the Irish Civil War.

Historically the second-largest party in the state behind Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael became the largest party in the Dáil after the 2011 general election, leading coalition governments under Enda Kenny and later Leo Varadkar, who became Taoiseach in 2017. Simon Harris became party leader and Taoiseach in 2024.

Ideology

Fine Gael describes itself as a party of the "progressive centre". It supports free enterprise, lower taxes, fiscal responsibility, and Ireland's membership of the European Union. In Europe it is a member of the European People's Party.
