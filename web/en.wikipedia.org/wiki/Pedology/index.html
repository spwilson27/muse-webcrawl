Pedology (from Greek πέδον, pedon, "soil") is the study of soils in their natural environment. It is one of two main branches of soil science, the other being edaphology, which focuses on soil in relation to plant growth. Pedology deals with pedogenesis (soil formation), soil morphology, and soil classification, treating soil as a natural body shaped by climate, organisms, relief, parent material and time.

History

The Russian geologist Vasily Dokuchaev founded modern pedology in the late 19th century, recognising soil as an independent natural body rather than merely weathered rock. His work on the Russian chernozem established the zonal concept of soil distribution. In 1941 Swiss-American scientist Hans Jenny formalised the factors of soil formation in his "clorpt" equation: soil = f(climate, organisms, relief, parent material, time).

Concepts

Pedologists describe soil profiles as sequences of horizons (O, A, E, B, C, R) and classify soils using systems such as the USDA Soil Taxonomy and the World Reference Base (WRB). Applications include land-use planning, agriculture, archaeology, and modelling the global carbon cycle—soils store more carbon than the atmosphere and vegetation combined, partly in peatlands (see Peat).
