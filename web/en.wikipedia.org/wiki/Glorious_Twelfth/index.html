Glorious Twelfth

The Glorious Twelfth is the twelfth day of August, the start of the shooting season for red grouse (Lagopus lagopus scotica) in Great Britain and Northern Ireland. It is one of the busiest days of the shooting calendar, and the first grouse shot on the day are traditionally flown to London restaurants by helicopter or airline so that they can be served that evening.

Because grouse cannot be reared intensively, the season's prospects depend on natural breeding success, and the number of birds shot is closely tied to the condition of heather moorland managed for the sport. The season runs until 10 December. If 12 August falls on a Sunday, shooting does not begin until the following day.

The day is also of cultural significance in parts of Northern Ireland and Scotland, though the term is sometimes satirised, and driven grouse shooting remains controversial because of its effects on raptors, peatland management and heather burning.
