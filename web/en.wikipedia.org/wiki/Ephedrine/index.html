Ephedrine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ephedrine is a medication and stimulant of the phenethylamine and amphetamine chemical classes. It is a sympathomimetic amine that acts on the adrenergic receptor system, both directly and by promoting the release of norepinephrine. It is used to prevent low blood pressure during anesthesia, and has historically been used for asthma, narcolepsy, and obesity, though these uses have largely been superseded.

History

Ephedrine is a naturally occurring alkaloid of plants in the genus Ephedra, notably Ephedra sinica (ma huang). It was isolated by the Japanese chemist Nagai Nagayoshi in 1885, and its pharmacology was brought to Western attention in the 1920s by K. K. Chen and Carl F. Schmidt. It became a popular treatment for asthma before safer selective agents were developed.

Effects and regulation

Common side effects include trouble sleeping, anxiety, tremor, headache, and increased blood pressure and heart rate; serious cardiovascular events have been reported at high doses. Ephedrine is also a precursor chemical in the manufacture of methamphetamine, so its sale is tightly controlled in many countries, and ephedra dietary supplements were banned in the United States in 2004. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
