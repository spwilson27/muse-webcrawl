Tarvos Trigaranus

NameTarvos Trigaranus
CultureGallo-Roman (Celtic)
Attested onPillar of the Boatmen (Paris); reliefs at Trier
Name meaning"Bull with three cranes"

Tarvos Trigaranus ("the bull with three cranes") is a Celtic deity or divine figure known from Gallo-Roman iconography. He is depicted as a bull carrying three birds—usually identified as cranes or egrets—on his back and head. The figure appears on two monuments of the early Roman imperial period: the Pillar of the Boatmen in Paris and a relief found at Trier in Germany.

Depictions

On the Pillar of the Boatmen, a column erected in Lutetia (modern Paris) by a guild of boatmen during the reign of Tiberius (14–37 CE) and dedicated to Jupiter, one block bears the inscribed label TARVOS TRIGARANVS above the image of a bull standing before a tree. Three long-necked birds perch on the bull: two on its back and one on its head. On the Trier relief the bull, likewise accompanied by three birds, stands in a wooded setting near a figure cutting wood with an axe, a scene also associated with the god Esus.

Interpretation

The Gaulish name is transparent: tarvos means "bull" (compare Welsh tarw) and trigaranus combines tri-, "three," with garanus, "crane." Scholars have linked the imagery to Irish mythology, in particular to tales in which cranes or herons are associated with transformation and otherworld beings, and to the Welsh and Irish motif of the bull of abundance. Some interpreters read the Paris block as one episode of a myth in which the woodcutter Esus fells a tree and the bull with its cranes is somehow involved, but the narrative remains speculative because no accompanying text survives.
