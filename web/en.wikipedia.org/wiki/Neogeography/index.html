Neogeography

Neogeography (literally "new geography") is the use of geographical techniques and information by non-specialists, outside the bounds of traditional geographic information systems (GIS), enabled by consumer web mapping tools. The term was popularized around 2006–2007 in connection with Web 2.0 mapping services such as Google Maps and Google Earth, user mashups, and volunteer-contributed mapping projects.

Description

Neogeography combines intuitive, consumer-oriented tools with user-generated geographic content: geotagged photographs, GPS tracks, collaborative crisis maps and place annotations. Andrew Turner, in Introduction to Neogeography (2006), defined it as "a bunch of map hacks" built by people using and creating their own maps in a blend of personal and shared contexts. Related concepts include volunteered geographic information (VGI), a term coined by Michael Goodchild, and crowdsourced mapping through projects such as OpenStreetMap.

Relation to GIS

Professional geographers debated whether neogeography threatened established cartographic standards or democratized mapmaking; in practice the two have converged, with professional GIS adopting web services and cloud hosting while citizen mapping feeds professional datasets, particularly in disaster response and humanitarian mapping.
