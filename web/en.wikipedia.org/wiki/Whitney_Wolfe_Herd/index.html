Whitney Wolfe Herd

Whitney Wolfe Herd (born July 1, 1989) is an American entrepreneur. She is the founder and chief executive officer of Bumble, a social networking and online dating application in which women make the first move when messaging matches. She is also a co-founder of the dating app Tinder.

Early life and education

Wolfe Herd was born in Salt Lake City, Utah. She attended Southern Methodist University in Dallas, Texas, where she majored in international studies. While in college she started a business selling bamboo tote bags to raise money for areas affected by the BP oil spill, and later worked in Southeast Asia with orphanages.

Career

After graduating, Wolfe Herd joined Hatch Labs in Los Angeles. Through Hatch Labs she became involved with the startup Cardify and then with the dating app Tinder, which launched in 2012. She was a co-founder of Tinder and its vice president of marketing, and is credited with helping popularize the app on college campuses.

Wolfe Herd left Tinder in 2014 and subsequently filed a lawsuit against the company alleging sexual harassment and discrimination; the suit was settled. Later that year, with backing from Andrey Andreev, founder of the social network Badoo, she founded Bumble, which launched in December 2014. Bumble distinguishes itself by allowing only women to initiate contact in heterosexual matches.

In February 2021, Bumble went public on the Nasdaq. The initial public offering made Wolfe Herd, at age 31, the youngest woman to take a company public in the United States and one of the world's youngest self-made female billionaires at the time. In November 2023 it was announced she would step down as CEO, transitioning to executive chair, with Lidiane Jones succeeding her; she later returned to the chief executive role in 2025.

Personal life

Wolfe Herd married Michael Herd, a Texas oil and hospitality heir, in 2017 in Italy. The couple have children and live in Austin, Texas.
