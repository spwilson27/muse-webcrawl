Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban (1 May 1633 – 30 March 1707), commonly known simply as Vauban, was a French military engineer who revolutionised the art of siege warfare and defensive fortification. He designed some of the most efficient military fortifications in Europe and was an early proponent of the use of artillery in siegecraft.

Military career

Vauban served under Louis XIV, taking part in 48 sieges and designing or improving more than 300 fortresses. His systematic method of siege warfare, the attaque en règle, remained the standard approach to besieging fortresses for nearly a century. He was made a Marshal of France in 1703.

Fortifications

His works include the Citadel of Lille, the fortifications of Belfort, Fort Carré at Antibes, and the ring of frontier fortresses known as the pré carré. In 2008, UNESCO inscribed twelve of his fortified sites as the Fortifications of Vauban World Heritage Site.

Later life and writings

Toward the end of his life Vauban wrote works on economics and taxation, including the Dîme Royale (1707), which criticised France's inequitable tax system. The book was banned, and Vauban died shortly afterwards.
