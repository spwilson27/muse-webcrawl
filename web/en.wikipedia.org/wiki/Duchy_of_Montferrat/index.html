The Duchy of Montferrat was a state in north-western Italy, in the hilly Montferrat region of present-day Piedmont. It originated as the Margraviate (March) of Montferrat, founded in the 10th century under Aleramo, and was ruled successively by the Aleramici, the Byzantine Palaiologos family and the Gonzaga dukes of Mantua before passing to Savoy.

History

Montferrat's marquises played an outsized role in the Crusades — members of the family became kings of Jerusalem and Thessalonica — and its court at Casale Monferrato was a notable Renaissance centre. In 1536, after the extinction of the Palaiologos line, Emperor Charles V granted Montferrat to Federico II Gonzaga of Mantua, and in 1574 it was raised to a duchy.

Decline and partition

The duchy was devastated during the War of the Mantuan Succession (1628–1631), when Casale endured famous sieges. In 1708, during the War of the Spanish Succession, the House of Savoy annexed most of Montferrat, and the settlement was confirmed by the Treaty of Utrecht (1713), ending the duchy as a separate state.
