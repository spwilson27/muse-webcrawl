Terra Nova Expedition

The Terra Nova Expedition, officially the British Antarctic Expedition 1910–1913, was led by Captain Robert Falcon Scott aboard the ship Terra Nova. Its principal aims were scientific research and being the first to reach the geographic South Pole.

The polar journey

Scott's five-man polar party — Scott, Edward Wilson, Henry Bowers, Lawrence Oates, and Edgar Evans — reached the South Pole on 17–18 January 1912, only to find that Roald Amundsen's Norwegian expedition had arrived 34 days earlier. All five men perished on the return journey: Evans died in February, Oates walked out of the tent into a blizzard in March ("I am just going outside and may be some time"), and Scott, Wilson, and Bowers died in their tent around 29 March 1912, eleven miles from One Ton Depot.

Legacy

The expedition's extensive scientific work — in geology, biology, and meteorology — remained valuable, and the party's story became one of the most famous tragedies of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration.
