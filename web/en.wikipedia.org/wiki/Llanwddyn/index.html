Llanwddyn is a village and community in Montgomeryshire, Powys, Wales, standing at the southern end of Lake Vyrnwy (Llyn Efyrnwy). The present village is a planned settlement built in the 1880s to rehouse the inhabitants of the original Llanwddyn, a small community in the Vyrnwy valley whose church, school, chapels and homes were demolished and submerged when the valley was flooded to create the reservoir that supplies Liverpool with water.

History

The old village centred on the church of St Wddyn; when Liverpool Corporation built the great masonry dam of Lake Vyrnwy — the first large stone dam in Britain, completed in 1889 — the valley floor was cleared, the dead were reinterred in a new churchyard, and the replacement village was laid out above the new shoreline with a new church (St Wddyn's), school and houses in Victorian style. The drowned village has become one of Wales's best-known lost communities, and in severe droughts the remains of old roads and buildings in the reservoir have re-emerged, as in the dry summers of 1976 and 2022. Today Llanwddyn serves visitors to the lake, a popular centre for walking, cycling, birdwatching (including an RSPB reserve) and the annual Lake Vyrnwy half marathon.
