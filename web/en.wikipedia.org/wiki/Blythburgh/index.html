Blythburgh is a village and civil parish in the East Suffolk district of Suffolk, England, situated on the estuary of the River Blyth. It is known for its large parish church, Holy Trinity, often called the "Cathedral of the Marshes".

History

Blythburgh was an important port and market town in medieval times, and the site of a battle in 653 in which Anna of East Anglia was killed. Its prosperity declined as the river silted up. On 4 August 1577, during a storm, lightning struck Holy Trinity Church — an event associated with the legend of the Black Shuck, a spectral black dog.

Holy Trinity Church

The present church dates mainly from the 15th century, with a distinctive flushwork tower and carved angels in the roof. It was restored in the 19th and 20th centuries. See also Southwold and River Blyth.
