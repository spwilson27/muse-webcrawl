Mrs Danvers is a fictional character in Daphne du Maurier's 1938 novel Rebecca and its many adaptations. She is the housekeeper of Manderley, the great Cornish estate of Maxim de Winter, and serves as the novel's principal antagonist. Tall, gaunt and dressed in black — the narrator compares her face to a skull — Mrs Danvers is obsessively devoted to the memory of Maxim's first wife, Rebecca, and wages a quiet campaign of psychological warfare against the shy, unnamed second Mrs de Winter.

Role in the novel

Mrs Danvers had served Rebecca since childhood and idolised her. She keeps Rebecca's bedroom in the west wing as a shrine, preserved exactly as it was, and continually measures the new mistress of the house against her predecessor, finding her wanting. Her manipulations culminate in the costume-ball episode, in which she tricks the narrator into wearing the same dress Rebecca wore — provoking Maxim's fury — and then, in the aftermath, nearly persuades the despairing narrator to take her own life by jumping from the window of Rebecca's room.

When the truth about Rebecca's death emerges and Manderley is doomed, Mrs Danvers disappears; the novel closes with the house in flames, a conflagration the narrative strongly implies she set before fleeing or perishing in it.

Portrayals and legacy

The character was memorably played by Judith Anderson in Alfred Hitchcock's 1940 film adaptation, a performance that earned an Academy Award nomination and fixed the image of the sinister housekeeper in popular culture. Later portrayals include Anna Massey in the 1979 television adaptation and Kristin Scott Thomas in the 2020 film. Mrs Danvers is frequently cited as one of the great villains of English literature, and her name has become shorthand for the forbidding, obsessive housekeeper — echoed, for instance, in the name of the housekeeper in the television series Downton Abbey.
