Nirvana

Nirvana
TraditionsBuddhism, Hinduism, Jainism
MeaningExtinction of craving and suffering; awakening; liberation
[Image: A serene Buddha statue at dawn with soft light, evoking nirvana]

Nirvana (Sanskrit nirvāṇa, 'blowing out') is the ultimate goal of Buddhism: the extinction of the fires of greed, hatred and delusion, the end of suffering (dukkha) and of rebirth. The Buddha taught it as attainable in this life through the Noble Eightfold Path; his own final passing into parinirvana at death ended all remaining conditions of existence. What nirvana is positively like, the Buddha characteristically left in large part unsaid — it is not annihilation, since there is no permanent self to annihilate.

Interpretations

Theravada emphasises the arhat's nirvana; Mahayana exalts the bodhisattva who postpones final entry to liberate all beings, and in some schools (notably with Buddha-nature teaching) nirvana and samsara are declared non-dual. In the West the word entered popular culture — and a famous band name — as shorthand for blissful peace, a loose borrowing from its precise soteriological sense.
