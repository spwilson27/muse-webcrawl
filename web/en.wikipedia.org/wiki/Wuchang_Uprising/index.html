LocationWuchang, Hubei, Qing China (now part of Wuhan)
ResultRevolutionary victory; triggered the Xinhai Revolution

The Wuchang Uprising was an armed revolt that began on 10 October 1911 in Wuchang, Hubei (now part of Wuhan, China), and ignited the Xinhai Revolution that overthrew China's last imperial dynasty, the Qing, ending more than two thousand years of imperial rule.

Background

Revolutionary societies, notably the Literary Society and the Progressive Association, had infiltrated the Qing dynasty's modernized New Army units in Hubei for years. The immediate trigger was the Railway Protection Movement, a popular protest against the Qing court's nationalization of local railways and their mortgaging to foreign bankers, which had drawn troops away from Wuchang to suppress unrest in Sichuan.

The uprising

On 9 October a bomb being assembled by revolutionaries in the Russian concession in Hankou exploded accidentally, exposing the plot; police seized membership lists and arrested plotters. Facing arrest, soldiers of the engineering battalion mutinied on the night of 10 October, seized the arsenal, and captured the city after fierce fighting; the viceroy fled. The revolutionaries, lacking their senior leaders, persuaded the reluctant brigade commander Li Yuanhong to head a military government, which proclaimed the Republic of China and called on other provinces to secede.

Consequences

Within weeks, province after province declared independence from the Qing. Sun Yat-sen returned from exile and was elected provisional president on 29 December; the Republic of China was proclaimed on 1 January 1912, and the last emperor, Puyi, abdicated in February 1912. The date of the uprising, 10 October — the "Double Tenth" — is still celebrated as the national day of the Republic of China on Taiwan.
