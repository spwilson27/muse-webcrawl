Inspector

Inspector is both a police rank and a generic term for an official charged with examination and oversight. As a rank, it is used in many police forces around the world, typically ranking above a sergeant and below a superintendent or chief inspector, though the precise hierarchy varies by jurisdiction.

Police rank by country

In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth police forces, an inspector is a senior rank — the third supervisory rank above constable and sergeant — and inspectors often command shifts, neighbourhood teams or specialist units. Uniformed inspectors wear two Bath stars ("pips") on their epaulettes. Above inspector sit the ranks of chief inspector, superintendent and chief superintendent.

In the United States, usage varies: some departments, such as the New York City Police Department, use inspector as a senior command rank above captain, roughly equivalent to a colonel, while in others it is a civil-service title for a senior detective. In India, an inspector of police commonly commands a police station or a unit within one, ranking above sub-inspector. In many European services, cognate titles (German Inspektor, French inspecteur) denote mid-level officer grades.

Other usages

"Inspector" is also the title of officials who examine and enforce standards: health and safety inspectors, building inspectors, tax inspectors, factory inspectors and school inspectors (such as those of Ofsted in England). Government oversight bodies are often headed by an inspector general, an official responsible for auditing and investigating an agency's conduct. In transport, ticket inspectors verify passengers' fares. The figure of the police inspector is a staple of detective fiction, from Inspector Lestrade in the Sherlock Holmes stories to Inspector Morse and Inspector Gadget in popular culture.
