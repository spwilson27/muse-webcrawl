Maranello

Maranello (Modenese: Maranèl) is a town and comune in the province of Modena, Emilia-Romagna, northern Italy, with a population of about 17,000. It is world-famous as the home of Ferrari, the luxury sports car manufacturer, and of the Scuderia Ferrari Formula One team.

Ferrari

Enzo Ferrari moved his operations from Modena to Maranello in 1943, and the Ferrari factory has been located there ever since, producing every Ferrari road car. The town hosts the Museo Ferrari, which displays road and racing cars and draws hundreds of thousands of visitors a year, and the Fiorano Circuit test track, just over the border in Fiorano Modenese. The town's identity is inseparable from the company: streets, squares and even the parish church bells — rung to celebrate Ferrari victories — reflect the connection.

Geography and history

Maranello lies at the foot of the Apennine foothills south of Modena, along the ancient road toward the mountains. Settled since the Bronze Age and Roman times, it developed as an agricultural town before Ferrari's arrival transformed it in the post-war decades. The Biblioteca Mabic, a public library designed by Arata Isozaki and opened in 2011, is a notable modern building.
