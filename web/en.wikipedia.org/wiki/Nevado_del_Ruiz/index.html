Nevado del Ruiz (pronounced [neˈβaðo ðel ˈrwis]), also known as La Mesa de Herveo, is a volcano on the border of the departments of Caldas and Tolima in Colombia, about 129 kilometres (80 mi) west of Bogotá. It is the northernmost and highest of several ice-capped stratovolcanoes in the Los Nevados National Natural Park. The volcano has been erupting for some 2 million years, and its summit glacier has shrunk markedly in recent decades.

The volcano is notorious for its eruption of 13 November 1985, in which pyroclastic flows melted part of the summit ice cap and generated lahars (volcanic mudflows) that buried the town of Armero, killing more than 23,000 people — the deadliest lahar in recorded history and one of the worst volcanic disasters of the twentieth century.

Geology

Nevado del Ruiz lies in the Cordillera Central of the Colombian Andes, formed by subduction of the Nazca Plate beneath the South American Plate. It is a broad stratovolcano built of alternating lava flows and pyroclastic deposits of andesitic to dacitic composition, topped by the Arenas crater, about 1 kilometre wide. Its glaciers cover several square kilometres, and glacial meltwater feeds the headwaters of rivers including the Gualí, Azufrado and Lagunilla.

Eruption history

Major historical eruptions occurred in 1595 and 1845, both producing destructive lahars. After decades of quiet, seismic unrest began in late 1984, and volcanologists warned that an eruption could melt the ice cap and flood the valleys — hazard maps were published in the weeks before the disaster. The eruption of 13 November 1985 began at 9:08 p.m.; within hours lahars swept down the river valleys, reaching Armero around midnight and obliterating the town. The nearby town of Chinchiná also suffered heavy losses. The tragedy, and the ordeal of the trapped teenager Omayra Sánchez, whose image became world-famous, led to sweeping reforms in Colombian disaster management and to the creation of a national system for disaster prevention, as well as influencing volcanic-hazard policy internationally.

The volcano erupted again in 2012 after 27 years of dormancy, with ashfall affecting Manizales and other cities, and intermittent ash emissions have continued since.
