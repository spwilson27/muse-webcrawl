Active electronically scanned array

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An active electronically scanned array (AESA) is a type of phased array radar antenna in which each radiating element is driven by its own solid-state transmit/receive module. By electronically controlling the phase (and often amplitude) of hundreds or thousands of these modules, the radar beam can be steered almost instantaneously without any moving parts.

How it works

In an AESA, the many small transmit/receive modules each generate and receive their own signals; computer control of their relative phases shapes and points the beam, allows several beams to operate in different modes nearly simultaneously, and varies the operating frequency from pulse to pulse. Gallium arsenide and, increasingly, gallium nitride semiconductors provide the modules' power amplifiers.

Advantages

AESA radars offer agile beam steering, multiple simultaneous functions (search, tracking, mapping, electronic warfare support), low probability of intercept through frequency agility and sidelobe control, graceful degradation — the failure of some modules barely reduces performance — and higher reliability than mechanically scanned or passive phased arrays. Their disadvantages are high cost, cooling demands and processing complexity.

Applications

AESA radars equip modern fighter aircraft such as the F-22 (AN/APG-77), F-35 (AN/APG-81), Eurofighter Typhoon (Captor-E) and Rafale (RBE2-AA), naval air-defence systems, missile-defence radars, and increasingly also weather, space and communications systems.
