Penguin Random House LLC is a British-American multinational conglomerate publishing company formed on 1 July 2013 by the merger of the Penguin Group and Random House. It is the world's largest trade book publisher.

The merger brought together Random House, owned by the German media group Bertelsmann, and Penguin, owned by the British company Pearson. Bertelsmann initially held a 53% stake and Pearson 47%; Bertelsmann bought out Pearson's share in stages, completing full ownership in 2020.

History

Penguin Books was founded in 1935 by Allen Lane in London, pioneering the inexpensive paperback. Random House was founded in 1927 in New York by Bennett Cerf and Donald Klopfer. The combined company was led first by Markus Dohle, the former Random House chief executive; he stepped down in 2022 and was succeeded by Nihar Malaviya.

In 2020 Penguin Random House agreed to acquire Simon & Schuster from Paramount Global for more than $2 billion, but the deal was blocked in 2022 by a U.S. federal judge on antitrust grounds, and the acquisition was abandoned.

Imprints

The company publishes through more than 300 imprints and brands, including Alfred A. Knopf, Doubleday, Viking, Riverhead, Crown, Ballantine, Del Rey, Puffin, Ladybird, DK, and Vintage. It employs more than 10,000 people worldwide and publishes books in all formats — print, digital audio and e-book.

Operations

Penguin Random House operates publishing divisions in more than 20 countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, India, South Africa, and across Europe, Latin America and Asia through Penguin Random House Grupo Editorial and other groups.
