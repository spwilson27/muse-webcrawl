The 1986 FIFA World Cup was the thirteenth FIFA World Cup, held in Mexico from 31 May to 29 June 1986, after original hosts Colombia withdrew for financial reasons. Argentina won their second title, beating West Germany 3–2 in the final at the Estadio Azteca. The tournament is remembered above all as Diego Maradona's World Cup: the Argentine captain dominated the competition, most famously in the quarter-final against England.

The Maradona quarter-final

On 22 June 1986 at the Azteca, Argentina met England in a match charged with symbolism four years after the Falklands War. Four minutes into the second half, Maradona opened the scoring by punching the ball past Peter Shilton — the "Hand of God" goal, as he later called it. Four minutes later he scored the "Goal of the Century", dribbling past five English opponents from his own half. Gary Lineker pulled one back, but Argentina won 2–1; Lineker's six goals made him the tournament's top scorer.

Knockout rounds and final

Belgium eliminated the Soviet Union and Spain in surprise runs to the semi-finals, where Argentina beat them 2–0 with two Maradona goals. France, winners of a classic quarter-final against Brazil on penalties, fell to West Germany in the other semi. In the final, Argentina led 2–0 through José Luis Brown and Jorge Valdano; West Germany levelled late through Karl-Heinz Rummenigge and Rudi Völler, but Jorge Burruchaga's winner, set up by Maradona, sealed a 3–2 Argentine triumph.

Legacy

Mexico became the first country to host the World Cup twice. The tournament popularised the "Mexican wave" (la ola) worldwide, introduced a straight knockout format after the group stage that endured until 1994, and cemented Maradona's reputation as the outstanding player of his generation: he was directly involved in ten of Argentina's fourteen goals.
