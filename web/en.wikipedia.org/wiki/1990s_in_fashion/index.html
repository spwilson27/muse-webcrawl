Fashion in the 1990s was defined by a return to minimalist fashion, in contrast to the more elaborate and colourful trends of the 1980s. The decade saw the rise of grunge and alternative subcultures, the global spread of hip-hop fashion, the dominance of supermodels early in the decade, and, by its end, a revival of retro and futuristic styles influenced by technology and the approaching millennium.

Early 1990s (1990–1993)

The decade began with a continuation of late-1980s styles — neon colours, leggings and oversized tops — but the recession and the grunge movement centred on Seattle bands such as Nirvana popularised thrift-store aesthetics: flannel shirts, ripped jeans, combat boots and layered, deliberately unkempt clothing. Designers such as Marc Jacobs brought grunge to the runway, initially to controversy.

Mid 1990s (1994–1996)

Minimalism dominated high fashion, with Calvin Klein, Jil Sander and Helmut Lang favouring clean lines and neutral palettes. The slip dress, worn alone or over a T-shirt, became emblematic. The "heroin chic" look associated with models such as Kate Moss provoked public debate. Sportswear brands — Adidas, Nike, Fila — moved into everyday dress, and hip-hop fashion popularised baggy silhouettes, Timberland boots and oversized logos.

Late 1990s (1997–1999)

The murder of Gianni Versace in 1997 marked a turning point. Tom Ford's Gucci, John Galliano at Dior and Alexander McQueen brought theatricality back to fashion. Street trends included platform shoes popularised by the Spice Girls, low-rise trousers, futuristic metallics and shiny fabrics anticipating the year 2000, and the beginnings of fast fashion's expansion through retailers such as Zara and H&M.

Hairstyles and makeup

Popular hairstyles included "the Rachel", inspired by Jennifer Aniston's character in Friends, chunky highlights, crimped hair, butterfly clips and, for men, curtain haircuts and frosted tips. Makeup trended toward matte skin, brown and nude lip colours, and thin eyebrows.
