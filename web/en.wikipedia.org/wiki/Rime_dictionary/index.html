Rime dictionary

A rime dictionary (Chinese: 韻書, yùnshū) is a type of ancient Chinese dictionary that categorizes Chinese characters by their pronunciation — organized by tone and rime (rhyme) rather than by radical. Rime dictionaries were essential references for composing regulated verse, whose metres depend on tonal patterns and rhyming categories.

History

The most important early example is the Qieyun (601 CE), compiled by Lu Fayan under the Sui dynasty, which codified the reading pronunciations of literary Chinese. Its expanded Tang–Song descendant, the Guangyun (1007–1008), arranges characters into 206 rimes grouped under the four tones. Later works such as the Jiyun and the rime tables of the Yunjing tradition further systematized the categories.

Linguistic value

Because the Qieyun system preserves distinctions lost in all modern dialects, rime dictionaries are the primary evidence used to reconstruct Middle Chinese phonology, notably in the systems of Karlgren and later scholars using the fanqie spellings given for each character.
