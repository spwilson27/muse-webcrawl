Namesake

A namesake is a person, geographic location, or other entity bearing the name of another. More specifically, it may refer to the person or entity after which something is named, or to the thing that receives the name. For example, a child named after a grandparent is the grandparent's namesake, and conversely the grandparent is the child's namesake in the other usage.

Etymology

The word is believed to derive from the phrase "for [the] name's sake" — that is, something done or given "for the sake of" a person's name. The term appears in English by the mid-17th century, originally in religious contexts ("for his name's sake").

Usage

Namesakes are common in personal naming, where children are named for relatives, saints, or admired figures; in geography, where towns, streets, and institutions are named for founders or honorees; and in commerce, where ships, buildings, and products are named after people or places. Naval tradition gives many ships namesakes in earlier vessels of the same name.

Related concepts

An eponym is the person or thing for which something is named. A cognomen or surname shared by unrelated people does not by itself make them namesakes in the intentional sense; the term generally implies that one was named in reference to the other.
