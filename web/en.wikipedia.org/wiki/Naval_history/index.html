Naval history is the study of naval warfare, navies, ships, and seafaring from ancient times to the present. It encompasses the development of warships and tactics, famous battles and commanders, and the strategic role of sea power in the rise and fall of states.

Ancient and medieval

Organized naval warfare began in the ancient Mediterranean: the Battle of Salamis (480 BC), in which Greek triremes defeated a larger Persian fleet, is often cited as a decisive moment. Rome built fleets to fight Carthage in the Punic Wars, and Byzantine, Arab and Viking seafarers dominated regional seas in the early medieval period. The Battle of Lepanto (1571), where a Holy League fleet defeated the Ottoman navy, marked the last great battle of galleys.

Age of sail

From the 16th to the 19th centuries, ocean-going sailing warships armed with broadside cannon projected European power worldwide. Key events include the defeat of the Spanish Armada (1588), the Anglo-Dutch Wars, and the Napoleonic Wars, culminating at Trafalgar (1805), where Admiral Nelson's Royal Navy secured British naval supremacy for a century.

Steam, iron and the world wars

Steam propulsion, ironclad hulls and rifled artillery transformed navies in the 19th century. The dreadnought revolution, submarines, and naval aviation reshaped fleets before and during the World Wars; carrier battles such as Midway (1942) decided the Pacific War.

Modern era

Since 1945 nuclear-powered submarines and aircraft carriers, guided missiles and satellite surveillance have defined naval power, with the United States Navy as the dominant global fleet of the late 20th and early 21st centuries.
