Body transfer illusion

Body transfer illusion
FieldCognitive neuroscience, psychology
Key paradigmsRubber hand illusion (1998); full-body illusion; enfacement illusion
Key researchersMatthew Botvinick, Jonathan Cohen, Henrik Ehrsson, Mel Slater

Body transfer illusion is the experience of owning a body or body part other than one's own, typically induced in the laboratory by presenting correlated multisensory signals — for example, seeing a fake body being touched while feeling touch on one's real, hidden body. Such illusions show that the sense of body ownership is constructed by the brain from visual, tactile, and proprioceptive cues and can be manipulated experimentally.

The rubber hand illusion

The classic paradigm was reported by Matthew Botvinick and Jonathan Cohen in 1998. A participant's real hand is hidden while a rubber hand is placed in view; when both hands are stroked synchronously, most participants soon feel the touches on the rubber hand and report a sense that it belongs to them, even flinching when it is threatened. Asynchronous stroking abolishes the effect, showing that temporal congruence of sight and touch drives the illusion. Participants also misjudge the position of their real hand toward the fake one ("proprioceptive drift").

Full-body illusions

Extensions using head-mounted displays and mannequins let participants experience ownership of an entire artificial or another person's body, sometimes combined with a shift in perceived self-location. Henrik Ehrsson's group demonstrated "body swap" experiments in which participants shook hands with themselves, apparently from the other body's perspective. Related variants include the enfacement illusion (for faces) and illusions of owning smaller, larger, or differently featured bodies, which can transiently change behavior and attitudes.

Mechanisms and uses

Neuroscientific work implicates the premotor cortex, intraparietal sulcus, and insula in integrating multisensory bodily signals. Body transfer illusions are used to study bodily self-consciousness and disorders such as depersonalization, and have practical applications in virtual reality design (embodiment of avatars), prosthetics (making artificial limbs feel like one's own), and teleoperation.
