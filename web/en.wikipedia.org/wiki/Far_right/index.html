Far right

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Far right is a political term for ideologies and movements positioned to the right of mainstream conservatism on the Political spectrum, characterised by ultranationalism, authoritarianism, nativism and hostility to liberal democracy or to some of its elements. The far right includes neo-fascist and neo-Nazi currents, radical-right populist parties, and various identitarian and supremacist movements; scholars distinguish the extreme right (which rejects democracy itself) from the radical right (which accepts elections but challenges liberal-democratic norms such as minority rights and judicial independence).

After 1945 the far right was marginal in most Western democracies, tainted by fascism's defeat; from the 1980s radical-right parties (the French National Front/Rally, Austria's FPÖ and others) achieved durable electoral success, campaigning chiefly against immigration and European integration, and in the 2010s–2020s entered or supported governments in several countries. Governments and security agencies also treat violent far-right extremism — lone-actor and organised terrorism — as a major threat. Classification of specific parties is contested by the parties themselves, and usage of the term varies between academic, journalistic and polemical contexts.
