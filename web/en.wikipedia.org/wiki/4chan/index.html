4chan is an anonymous English-language imageboard website launched in 2003 by Christopher Poole, then a teenager posting as "moot". Modelled on Japanese imageboards such as Futaba Channel (2chan), it allows users to post images and comments without registering; most posts are anonymous, threads are ephemeral and are deleted as new ones displace them.

Boards and culture

The site is divided into boards by topic, denoted by letters — /a/ (anime), /b/ ("random"), /v/ (video games), /pol/ (politically incorrect) among dozens of others. 4chan has been an influential source of internet memes and subculture, associated with phenomena such as lolcats, Rickrolling, the hacktivist collective Anonymous (which emerged from /b/), and numerous viral hoaxes and controversies.

Ownership and moderation

Poole retired as administrator in 2015 and sold the site to Hiroyuki Nishimura, founder of the Japanese textboard 2channel. The site has repeatedly drawn criticism for hosting extremist and harassing content, and some boards have been linked to real-world violence; it has also been praised as a space for freewheeling anonymous speech. In April 2025 the site was taken offline following a hack that leaked moderator data, returning several days later.
