Cello repertoire

The cello concerto is a centrepiece of the instrument's repertoire.

InstrumentVioloncello
Earliest worksLate 17th century (Gabrielli, Jacchini)
CornerstonesBach's six suites; concertos by Haydn, Dvořák, Elgar; sonatas by Beethoven, Brahms

The cello repertoire comprises the body of music written for the violoncello as a solo instrument, with piano, or with orchestra, from the late seventeenth century to the present. Although smaller than the violin or piano repertoire, it includes some of the most beloved works in classical music.

Baroque era

The earliest solo cello music appeared in Bologna in the 1680s and 1690s, in ricercars and sonatas by Domenico Gabrielli and Giuseppe Jacchini. Luigi Boccherini, himself a virtuoso cellist, wrote twelve concertos and many sonatas in the following century. Towering over the period are J. S. Bach's six Suites for Unaccompanied Cello (c. 1717–1723), rediscovered and popularised by Pablo Casals in the early twentieth century; they are now the foundation of every cellist's repertoire.

Classical and Romantic eras

Haydn's two concertos, especially the C major concerto rediscovered in 1961, anchor the Classical repertoire, and Beethoven's five cello sonatas established the modern duo sonata. The nineteenth century produced concertos by Schumann, Saint-Saëns, Lalo, and Tchaikovsky (whose Variations on a Rococo Theme is a staple), the two Brahms sonatas, and the Dvořák Concerto in B minor (1895), often regarded as the greatest of all cello concertos.

Twentieth century and beyond

Elgar's elegiac Concerto in E minor (1919) became central after Jacqueline du Pré's celebrated recordings. Mstislav Rostropovich inspired an enormous expansion of the repertoire, premiering works by Prokofiev, Shostakovich (two concertos), Britten (the Cello Symphony and three solo suites), Lutosławski, and Dutilleux. Kodály's Solo Sonata, Op. 8 (1915) is the other great monument of the unaccompanied literature. Contemporary composers continue to write prolifically for the instrument, aided by virtuosi such as Yo-Yo Ma.
