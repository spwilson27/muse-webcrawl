Li Zicheng

Li Zicheng (22 September 1606 – 1645), born Li Hongji, also known by the nickname the Dashing King, was a Chinese peasant rebel leader who overthrew the Ming dynasty in 1644 and briefly ruled China as the founder of the short-lived Shun dynasty.

Rise

Born in Mizhi County, Shaanxi, Li worked as a shepherd, ironworker and post-station attendant before deserting and joining the great peasant rebellions that swept northern China during the famine-stricken final years of the Ming. After the death of the rebel leader Gao Yingxiang, Li inherited his title and following, building a disciplined army and a programme of land equalisation and tax remission summarised in the slogan "welcome the Dashing King, pay no grain tax."

Fall of the Ming

Li captured Luoyang and Xi'an, where he proclaimed the Shun dynasty in early 1644. In April his army entered Beijing almost unopposed; the Chongzhen Emperor hanged himself on Coal Hill, ending the Ming dynasty.

Defeat and death

Li's rule in the capital lasted only weeks. After the Ming general Wu Sangui allied with the Manchu Qing, Li was defeated at the Battle of Shanhai Pass in May 1644 and forced to abandon Beijing. He died in 1645 — killed, according to most accounts, by local militia in Hubei — as Qing armies consolidated their conquest.
