The United Fruit Company (later United Brands) was an American multinational corporation that traded in tropical fruit, primarily bananas, grown on plantations in Latin America and sold in North America and Europe. Formed in 1899 by the merger of Minor C. Keith's banana interests with the Boston Fruit Company, it became one of the most powerful corporations in Central America.

United Fruit controlled vast landholdings, railways, ports, and a large shipping fleet (the "Great White Fleet") in countries such as Guatemala, Honduras, Costa Rica, and Colombia. Its dominance gave rise to the term "banana republic" for politically unstable countries dependent on a single export crop.

Controversies

The company was implicated in the 1928 Banana Massacre in Colombia and in lobbying that contributed to the 1954 CIA-backed coup in Guatemala against President Jacobo Árbenz, whose land reforms threatened its holdings. In 1970 it merged to form United Brands, which later became Chiquita Brands International.
