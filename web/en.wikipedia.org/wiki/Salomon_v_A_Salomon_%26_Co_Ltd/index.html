Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd


Court: House of Lords
Decided: 1896
Citation: [1897] AC 22
Area: Company law — separate legal personality

Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] AC 22 is a landmark judgment of the House of Lords in United Kingdom company law. The unanimous decision upheld the doctrine of corporate personality: a duly incorporated company is a legal person entirely separate from its shareholders, even where one person holds nearly all of its shares.

Background

Aron Salomon, a leather merchant, incorporated his business as A Salomon & Co Ltd, taking shares and secured debentures, with family members holding the remaining shares. When the company went into liquidation, its unsecured creditors argued that Salomon and the company were the same person and that his secured claim should rank behind theirs.

Judgment

The House of Lords held that the company had been validly formed under the Companies Acts; it was at law a different person altogether from its subscribers, and Salomon's debentures were valid. The motives of those forming a company, the court said, were irrelevant to its legal existence.

Significance

The case remains a foundation of modern company law throughout the common-law world, establishing separate legal personality and underpinning limited liability for one-person companies.
