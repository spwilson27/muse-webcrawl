Space (punctuation)

Space
UnicodeU+0020 SPACE (and numerous width variants)
FunctionSeparates words and sentences in writing

In writing, a space ( ) is a blank area that separates words, sentences, syllables and other written or printed glyphs. Conventions for spacing vary among languages and scripts: in most modern written languages spaces separate words, whereas historically many scripts — including classical Latin and Greek — were written in scriptio continua without word separation. Word spaces entered European writing through Insular (Irish and Anglo-Saxon) scribes in the early Middle Ages and spread with print.

Types of space

Digital typography recognises many space characters: the ordinary space (U+0020), the non-breaking space (U+00A0), which prevents a line break, and fixed-width spaces derived from metal type, such as the em space, en space, thin space and hair space. French typographic practice places a narrow non-breaking space before certain punctuation marks such as the colon and question mark.

Sentence spacing

Whether one or two spaces should follow a full stop became a well-known typographic debate in the typewriter era: monospaced typewriter fonts encouraged double spacing, while professional typesetting had generally used a single (if sometimes enlarged) space. Modern style guides overwhelmingly prescribe a single space, and a 2020-era body of readability research has been cited on both sides of the question.
