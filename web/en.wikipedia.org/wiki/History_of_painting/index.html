The history of painting spans from prehistoric cave art to the present day and encompasses independent traditions across the world, including Western, Chinese, Indian, and Japanese painting. For most of human history, painting has served religious, narrative, and decorative functions.

Prehistoric and ancient painting

The oldest known paintings are cave images in Indonesia and at Chauvet and Lascaux in France, depicting animals and hand stencils tens of thousands of years old. Egyptian tomb painting, Greek vase painting, and Roman wall painting at Pompeii developed sophisticated conventions of representation.

Asian traditions

Chinese painting developed ink-and-brush landscape (shanshui) and figure traditions over two millennia, while Japanese painting produced screen and scroll formats, and Indian art flourished in murals such as those at Ajanta and in later Mughal and Rajput miniature painting.

Medieval and Renaissance painting

Medieval Western painting was dominated by religious panels, icons, and illuminated manuscripts. The Italian Renaissance of the 15th–16th centuries brought linear perspective, anatomical study, and oil painting to maturity in the works of Masaccio, Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael, alongside Northern masters such as Jan van Eyck.

Baroque to the 19th century

Baroque drama (Caravaggio, Rembrandt, Rubens) gave way to Rococo, Neoclassicism, and Romanticism. The 19th century saw Realism and then Impressionism, whose broken color and plein air technique transformed painting; Post-Impressionists such as Van Gogh and Cézanne opened the way to modern art.

Modern and contemporary painting

The 20th century produced an explosion of movements—Fauvism, Cubism, Surrealism, Abstract Expressionism, Pop art—challenging the very definition of painting. Painting remains a vital medium in contemporary art worldwide.
