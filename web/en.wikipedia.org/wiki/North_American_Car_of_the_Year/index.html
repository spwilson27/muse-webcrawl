Overview

The North American Car of the Year is an automobile award announced annually in January, historically at the North American International Auto Show in Detroit. It is chosen by a jury of automotive journalists from the United States and Canada, and is considered one of the most prestigious awards in the industry.

History

The award was established in 1994, modelled on the long-running European Car of the Year. From the beginning it was administered by a jury of journalists rather than a single publication. In 2017 the programme expanded to three categories: Car of the Year, Truck of the Year, and Utility of the Year, reflecting the growing share of sport utility vehicles in the market.

Selection

Eligible vehicles must be all-new or substantially changed for the model year. Jurors evaluate vehicles on innovation, design, safety, performance, technology, driver satisfaction, and value, and test finalists together before a final vote. Winners are announced during the Detroit auto show period.
