The notion of a fibration, introduced by Jean-Pierre Serre in his 1951 thesis, generalises the idea of a fibre bundle in algebraic topology. A fibration is a continuous map satisfying the homotopy lifting property, which allows homotopies in the base space to be lifted to the total space.

Definition

A Hurewicz fibration is a continuous map p : E → B such that for every space X, every homotopy in B beginning at a map that lifts to E can itself be lifted to a homotopy in E. The space E is called the total space, B the base space, and the preimage p−1(b) of a point the fibre over b.

Examples

  • Every fibre bundle is a fibration; in particular, a covering space is a fibration with discrete fibre.
  • The Hopf fibration: the map S3 → S2 with fibre S1, fundamental to the study of homotopy groups of spheres.
  • The path-space fibration: evaluation at the endpoint maps the space of paths in X onto X, with fibre the loop space.
  • Any projection B × F → B.

Long exact sequence

A fibration with fibre F induces a long exact sequence of homotopy groups

… → πn(F) → πn(E) → πn(B) → πn−1(F) → …

which is one of the principal computational tools of homotopy theory. For the Hopf fibration it yields, for example, that π3(S2) ≅ ℤ.

Serre and Hurewicz fibrations

A Serre fibration satisfies the weaker lifting property only for homotopies of cubes (equivalently, of CW complexes); this suffices for the long exact sequence and for Serre's spectral sequence. In model category language, fibrations are one of the three distinguished classes of maps, dual to cofibrations.
