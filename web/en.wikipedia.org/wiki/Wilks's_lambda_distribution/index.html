Wilks's lambda distribution

Field: Multivariate statistics

Named after: Samuel S. Wilks

In statistics, Wilks's lambda distribution (named for Samuel Stanley Wilks) is a probability distribution used in multivariate hypothesis testing, especially with regard to the likelihood-ratio test and multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA).

Wilks's lambda, denoted Λ, is defined as the ratio of the determinant of the error covariance matrix to the determinant of the total covariance matrix: Λ = det(E) / det(E + H), where H is the hypothesis sum-of-squares-and-cross-products matrix and E is the error matrix. Values near 0 indicate that group means differ strongly, while values near 1 indicate little difference.

Use

The statistic arises in tests of whether several response variables differ across groups, in discriminant analysis, and in testing the significance of subsets of variables. Because the exact distribution of Λ is complicated, it is usually converted to an approximate F distribution (Rao's approximation) or a chi-square statistic (Bartlett's approximation) for practical testing.

Relations

In special cases Wilks's lambda reduces to familiar statistics: with two groups it corresponds to Hotelling's T², and with a single response variable it is a monotone function of the usual F statistic of one-way ANOVA. Together with Pillai's trace, Hotelling's trace, and Roy's greatest root, it is one of the four standard test statistics reported by MANOVA software.
