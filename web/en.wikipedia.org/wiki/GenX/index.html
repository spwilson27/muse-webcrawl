GenX

[Image placeholder: GenX chemical structure]

GenX is a trade name for a chemical process and the family of fluorinated compounds associated with it, used in the production of fluoropolymers such as Teflon-like coatings. The principal compound is hexafluoropropylene oxide dimer acid (HFPO-DA), and its ammonium salt. GenX technology was developed by DuPont (later Chemours) as a replacement for perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA), which was phased out over health and environmental concerns.

Controversy and contamination

GenX chemicals came to wide public attention in 2017, when studies and journalism reported that HFPO-DA had been detected in the Cape Fear River in North Carolina and in drinking water supplies downstream of Chemours' Fayetteville Works plant. The revelation prompted state and federal investigations, litigation by local water utilities and residents, and a 2019 consent order requiring Chemours to dramatically reduce emissions.

Health and regulation

GenX chemicals belong to the broader class of PFAS ("forever chemicals"). Animal studies found effects including liver and developmental toxicity, and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency issued a toxicity assessment for GenX chemicals in 2021 and later included HFPO-DA among the PFAS regulated under the Safe Drinking Water Act, setting a maximum contaminant level in 2024. The case became a prominent example in debates over whether replacement chemicals are adequately tested before introduction — critics describe GenX as a "regrettable substitution".
