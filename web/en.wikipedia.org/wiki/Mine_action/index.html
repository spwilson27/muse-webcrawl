Mine action

Mine action is the humanitarian and development activity aimed at reducing the social, economic, and environmental impact of landmines and explosive remnants of war. It is conventionally described as resting on five complementary pillars: (1) mine and explosive-ordnance risk education; (2) humanitarian demining — the survey, mapping, marking, and clearance of contaminated areas; (3) victim assistance; (4) destruction of stockpiled mines; and (5) advocacy in support of a total ban on anti-personnel mines.

Framework

The field's modern framework emerged in the 1990s, catalyzed by the International Campaign to Ban Landmines and the adoption of the 1997 Ottawa Treaty (Mine Ban Treaty), which prohibits the use, production, transfer, and stockpiling of anti-personnel mines. The United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS), established in 1997, coordinates mine action within the UN system, and international mine action standards (IMAS) govern safety and quality in clearance and education.

Practice

Humanitarian demining combines manual clearance by trained deminers with prodders and metal detectors, mechanical assets such as flails and tillers, and mine-detection dogs, verified through survey and quality management before land is released for use. Manual clearance remains slow and dangerous work. Dozens of countries remain contaminated, including Cambodia, Afghanistan, Angola, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and, following recent conflicts, Ukraine. Casualties from mines and unexploded ordnance number in the thousands annually, with civilians — many of them children — making up the large majority.
