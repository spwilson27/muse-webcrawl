A television pilot (also called a pilot episode or simply a pilot) is a standalone episode of a television series that is used to sell the show to a television network, streaming service, or other distributor. At the time of its creation, the pilot is meant to be the testing ground to gauge whether a series will be successful. It is, therefore, a test episode for the intended television series, an early step in the series development, much like pilot studies in other fields.

In the case of a successful television series, the pilot is commonly the first episode that is aired of the specific series in question. It is often (although not always) also the episode from which the regular series is substantially descended; characters, settings, and tone introduced in the pilot are carried into the series itself. Some pilots are retooled and reshot, or never air at all when a series is not picked up — such unaired pilots are sometimes later released as curiosities.

Development process

During the traditional broadcast development cycle, writers pitch concepts, networks commission scripts, and a small number of scripts are ordered to pilot. Pilots are cast, produced, and screened for network executives, test audiences, and advertisers. Based on this evaluation, a network may order a full season ("pick up" the series), order additional scripts, request changes and a reshoot, or pass on the project entirely. The vast majority of pilots produced each year are not ordered to series.

Cable and streaming services have altered the process: some order series "straight-to-series" without a pilot, based on the strength of the creative team or underlying material, or commission a short presentation rather than a full episode.

Types of pilots

Premise pilot

A premise pilot introduces the characters and the series' world and sets up the central situation — for example, showing how a group of characters first meets or how an unusual circumstance arises. Most successful series open with premise pilots.

Standalone episode pilot

Rather than establishing an origin, this type of pilot presents a typical episode of the show as it would ordinarily run, demonstrating the series' regular format to buyers.

Backdoor pilot

A backdoor pilot is an episode of an existing series (or a television film) that introduces characters or a setting intended to spin off into a new series, so named because it enters the schedule "through the back door" of an established program. If the parent show's audience responds well, the spin-off may be ordered; if not, the cost of the pilot is partly absorbed by the existing production.

Put pilot

A put pilot is a contractual arrangement in which a network agrees to air a pilot or pay a substantial penalty if it does not, giving the project a strong chance of production.

Unaired pilots

For every series ordered, many pilots are produced and never broadcast. Some become notable in their own right — for example, the original unaired pilot of Star Trek, "The Cage", whose footage was later incorporated into the series — while others circulate only within the industry or among collectors.
