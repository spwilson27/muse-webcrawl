DreamWorks Pictures (also known as DreamWorks SKG) is an American film studio and distribution label of Amblin Partners. It was founded in 1994 by Steven Spielberg, Jeffrey Katzenberg and David Geffen — the "SKG" of its earlier name — as an ambitious multi-media studio spanning live-action film, animation, television and music.

History

The studio's early releases included The Peacemaker (1997), Amistad (1997) and Saving Private Ryan (1998). DreamWorks won three consecutive Academy Awards for Best Picture with American Beauty (1999), Gladiator (2000) and A Beautiful Mind (2001, with Universal). Its animation arm, DreamWorks Animation, created franchises such as Shrek, Madagascar and Kung Fu Panda before becoming a separate public company in 2004 and being acquired by NBCUniversal in 2016.

In 2005 the live-action studio was sold to Viacom, then relaunched in 2008 as an independent company in partnership with India's Reliance Group, with distribution through Disney's Touchstone Pictures. In 2015 the studio became part of Amblin Partners, with distribution handled by Universal Pictures; its releases under that arrangement include Bridge of Spies, The Post, Green Book (Best Picture winner), 1917 and The Fabelmans.
