The Birdsville Track is a famous outback road in Australia, running 517 kilometres between Marree in northern South Australia and Birdsville in south-western Queensland. One of the country's most iconic remote routes, it crosses the Tirari–Sturt Stony Desert and the Channel Country and is traversed today mainly by four-wheel-drive tourists and local station traffic.

History

The track originated in the 1860s as a stock route for droving cattle from the Channel Country of Queensland to the railhead at Marree (originally the railhead was further north as the line extended). Drovers such as the celebrated — and notoriously hard — drovers of the era moved herds down the track to market. From the 1930s to the 1960s the route was served by legendary mailman Tom Kruse, whose fortnightly mail runs in his Leyland Badger truck through floods and breakdowns were immortalised in the 1954 documentary The Back of Beyond. Supplies along the route historically came from bores sunk into the Great Artesian Basin and from isolated stations such as Mungerannie.

The route today

The track passes landmarks including the Cooper Creek crossing (impassable when flooded), the Mungerannie Hotel — one of the few sources of fuel and supplies — and the ruins and bores of the desert fringe. Although graded regularly and suitable for high-clearance vehicles in dry conditions, the Track remains remote: travellers are advised to carry water, fuel, spare tyres, and communications equipment, and roads close after rain. Birdsville, at the northern end, is famous for the Birdsville Races each September and the Big Red sand dune of the Simpson Desert nearby.
