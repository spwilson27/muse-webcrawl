Gríma Wormtongue

Gríma, son of Gálmód, called Wormtongue, is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's epic novel The Lord of the Rings. He is introduced in The Two Towers as the chief adviser to King Théoden of Rohan, but has secretly been corrupted by the wizard Saruman. Through Gríma's counsel, Théoden is weakened and Rohan left exposed to Saruman's attack until Gandalf breaks the spell. Gríma flees to Saruman, accompanies him in his ruin, and in the end kills him during the Scouring of the Shire, before being shot by Hobbit archers. Critics have compared Gríma to figures such as Unferth in Beowulf and have read the character — the treacherous counsellor whose words poison a king — as a study in the destructive power of flattery and deceitful language, closely tied to Tolkien's themes about speech and corruption.

Biography

Gríma, son of Gálmód, became the trusted counsellor of King Théoden of Rohan. At some point he entered the service of the wizard Saruman of Isengard, who promised him wealth and Théoden's niece Éowyn, whom Gríma lusted after. Acting on Saruman's instructions, Gríma worked to weaken Rohan: he undermined Théoden's health and will, encouraged the king's distrust of his son Théodred and nephew Éomer, and contrived Éomer's imprisonment and banishment. Aragorn's later judgment was that Théoden had been "in bondage to [Gríma's] counsel" and aged before his time.

When Gandalf, Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli arrived at Edoras in The Two Towers, Gandalf exposed Gríma as Saruman's spy and freed Théoden from his influence. Offered a final chance to renounce Saruman, Gríma refused and fled to Isengard. He was present when Saruman's fortress was broken by the Ents, and accompanied his master into wandering exile. By the time of the Scouring of the Shire, Gríma — by then half-starved and treated with contempt, called "Worm" by Saruman — was implicated in the murder of Lotho Sackville-Baggins, whom Saruman taunted him for having possibly eaten. In the final confrontation on the steps of Bag End, Gríma suddenly cut Saruman's throat, and was immediately shot dead by Hobbit archers. He is the last casualty of the War of the Ring within the story.

Name

The name Gríma derives from Old English grīma, "mask, helmet, ghost," related to the English word "grim." His epithet "Wormtongue" — given by Gandalf in the hall of Meduseld — refers to his smooth, forked, poisonous speech; Gandalf calls him "a witless worm" and observes that he has long been "a snake" in Théoden's household. In Tolkien's conception of Rohirric as represented by Old English, such wordplay is characteristic.

Interpretation

Scholars have discussed Gríma as the type of the evil counsellor. Tom Shippey and others compare him to Unferth, the hostile courtier in Beowulf, and note the parallel scene in which a visitor (Gandalf, like Beowulf) must verbally defeat a hostile court spokesman before reaching the king. Criticism has also explored the psychology of Gríma's servitude — his self-abasement before Saruman resembles the dynamics of addiction or an abusive relationship — and Tolkien's broader theme of language corrupted: Gríma's fall parallels Saruman's own weaponization of persuasive voice. In Peter Jackson's film adaptation, Gríma (played by Brad Dourif) is given an expanded role, including a scene in which he sheds a tear at Saruman's army, a nuance some viewers read as residual humanity.
