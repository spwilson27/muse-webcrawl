Eastern gorilla

The eastern gorilla (Gorilla beringei) is a critically endangered species of gorilla and the largest living primate, endemic to the mountainous forests of east-central Africa. The species is divided into two subspecies: the mountain gorilla (G. b. beringei) of the Virunga volcanic mountains and Bwindi Impenetrable Forest, and the eastern lowland gorilla or Grauer's gorilla (G. b. graueri) of the lowland forests of the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Description and behaviour

Eastern gorillas are robust and powerful, with long arms, a broad chest, and black fur that is longer and thicker in the mountain subspecies. They live in stable family groups led by a dominant silverback male, are primarily herbivorous — eating leaves, stems, fruit, and bark — and build nests each night. Males may weigh over 200 kg in the wild.

Conservation

The species is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN, owing to habitat loss, poaching, civil unrest, and disease. Grauer's gorilla numbers crashed dramatically during and after the Congo wars, while mountain gorilla numbers have slowly recovered to just over 1,000 individuals through intensive conservation, anti-poaching patrols, and regulated tourism.
