Modern United States Navy carrier air operations

Modern United States Navy carrier air operations are the methods and procedures used aboard US Navy aircraft carriers to launch, recover and manage fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters. Operations are organised around the carrier air wing (CVW) — typically about 70 aircraft including F/A-18E/F Super Hornet strike fighters, F-35C Lightning II fighters, EA-18G Growler electronic-warfare jets, E-2D Hawkeye airborne early warning aircraft, MH-60 helicopters and CMV-22B Osprey logistics aircraft — embarked aboard a nuclear-powered carrier of the Nimitz or Gerald R. Ford class.

Launch and recovery

Fixed-wing aircraft are launched by steam catapults (or electromagnetic EMALS on the Ford class) and recovered by arresting wires, in a cycle of launch and recovery "events" lasting roughly 90 minutes, known as cyclic operations. The Landing Signal Officer (LSO) guides pilots during final approach using the Fresnel lens optical landing system ("the ball"), and every landing is graded. Flight deck crews are identified by colour-coded jerseys: yellow for aircraft handling officers and directors, green for catapult and arresting gear crews, red for ordnance, purple for aviation fuel ("grapes"), blue for plane handlers, brown for squadron plane captains and white for safety and medical personnel.

Command and control

Flight operations are directed from "Pri-Fly" (Primary Flight Control) in the island by the Air Officer ("Air Boss") and his assistant ("Mini Boss"). Aircraft are marshalled in holding patterns — including the Case I, II and III recovery patterns used in good, marginal and poor weather or at night — and emissions control (EMCON) states govern how much the carrier and its aircraft radiate electronically. Underway replenishment and vertical replenishment sustain operations at sea for weeks at a time.

Qualification and training

Naval aviators must complete carrier qualifications (CQ) aboard a carrier before joining a fleet squadron, and must periodically requalify; night carrier landings are considered among the most demanding tasks in aviation. The Naval Air Training and Operating Procedures Standardization (NATOPS) programme codifies procedures for each aircraft type.
