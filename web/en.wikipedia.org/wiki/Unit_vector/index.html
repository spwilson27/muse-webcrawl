Unit vector

In mathematics, a unit vector in a normed vector space is a vector whose length, or norm, is one. Unit vectors are used to specify direction independently of magnitude.

Normalization

Any nonzero vector v can be converted into a unit vector in the same direction by dividing by its norm:

û = v / ||v||

This process is called normalization. The zero vector cannot be normalized because it has no direction and division by its norm is impossible.

Standard basis

In three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates, the standard unit basis vectors are often denoted i, j and k, pointing along the positive x, y and z axes respectively. A vector can be decomposed into components along these directions. In polar, cylindrical and spherical coordinate systems, local unit vectors point in the directions of increasing coordinate values and may vary from point to point.

Applications

Unit vectors appear throughout geometry and physics: direction cosines express the components of a unit vector relative to coordinate axes; normal vectors are often normalized to simplify lighting, surface and reflection calculations; and vector fields such as velocity direction, force direction and electric field direction are commonly separated into magnitude and unit direction. In higher dimensions, the set of unit vectors forms the unit sphere.
