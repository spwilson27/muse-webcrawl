Companies House is the executive agency of the British Government that maintains the register of companies in the United Kingdom. It incorporates and dissolves limited companies, registers company information, and makes it available to the public. The register contains records for more than four million companies, and since 2015 basic company information — including accounts, officers, and filings history — has been available free of charge through the Companies House Service website.

Functions

Every limited company in the UK must be registered with Companies House. Companies must file annual confirmation statements, annual accounts, and notifications of changes to directors, people with significant control (PSC), registered office, and share capital. Companies House historically played a largely passive, record-keeping role: it checked that documents were properly completed but did not verify the accuracy of the information supplied — an arrangement long criticised for facilitating fraud, shell companies, and joke or erroneous entries.

Reform

The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 gave the Registrar substantial new powers, including to query and reject suspicious information, require identity verification for directors and PSCs, and share data with law enforcement, transforming Companies House from a passive registry into an active gatekeeper. Identity verification for new directors began to be rolled out from 2025. Companies House also operates registers for limited liability partnerships and overseas companies with a UK establishment, and maintains the official company name index under the Companies Act 2006.
