Slash (musician)

Slash performing with his signature top hat and Les Paul

Saul Hudson (born 23 July 1965), better known by his stage name Slash, is a British-American musician who serves as the lead guitarist of the hard rock band Guns N' Roses, with whom he achieved worldwide success in the late 1980s and early 1990s. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest guitarists in rock history and is recognized by his signature top hat, long curly hair, and use of Gibson Les Paul guitars.

Early life

Hudson was born in London to an English father, Anthony Hudson, an artist who created album covers, and an African-American mother, Ola J. Hudson, a fashion designer who worked with artists such as David Bowie. He was raised in Stoke-on-Trent before moving to Los Angeles at age 11. He took up guitar in his mid-teens, influenced by Aerosmith, Led Zeppelin, and AC/DC, and played in several Los Angeles bands before co-founding Guns N' Roses in 1985.

Career

With Guns N' Roses, Slash recorded Appetite for Destruction (1987) — featuring "Sweet Child o' Mine," whose opening riff he wrote — as well as G N' R Lies and the Use Your Illusion albums. He left the band in 1996 amid conflict with Axl Rose, formed Slash's Snakepit, and co-founded the supergroup Velvet Revolver with Scott Weiland in 2002. His first solo album, Slash (2010), featured numerous guest vocalists; he then formed Slash featuring Myles Kennedy and the Conspirators. In 2016 he rejoined Guns N' Roses for the hugely successful Not in This Lifetime... Tour.

Legacy

Slash was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2012 with Guns N' Roses. Magazines such as Time and Rolling Stone have ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time. He has signature Gibson and Epiphone guitar models, and has worked in wildlife conservation and addiction-recovery advocacy, having been diagnosed with cardiomyopathy in 2001 attributed to years of drug and alcohol use.
