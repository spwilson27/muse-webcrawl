Greenish warbler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The greenish warbler (Phylloscopus trochiloides) is a widespread leaf warbler with a breeding range in northeastern Europe and temperate to subtropical Asia. It is strongly migratory and winters in India. It is a small passerine, greenish-grey above and off-white below, with a prominent pale supercilium and (usually) a single wing bar.

Ring species

The greenish warbler is a celebrated example of a ring species. Populations spread around the Tibetan Plateau along two routes — a western one through the Himalaya's west and a longer eastern route through Siberia — gradually diverging in song and genetics. Where the two forms meet again in central Siberia (P. t. viridanus and P. t. plumbeitarsus), they behave as separate species and rarely interbreed, even though they are connected by a chain of intergrading populations around the plateau. Taxonomic authorities differ on whether to split the complex into several species.

Behaviour

Like most leaf warblers, it is insectivorous, foraging actively in tree canopies. It nests on the ground in low shrub, laying four to six eggs. The song is a high, jerky trill, differing markedly between populations across the ring.
