Jinhua

Jinhua is a prefecture-level city in central Zhejiang province in eastern China. It is known throughout China for Jinhua ham, a dry-cured ham with a long history, and for its proximity to Yiwu, the world's largest wholesale market for small commodities.

Geography

Jinhua lies in the Jinqu Basin, drained by the Jinhua River, a tributary of the Qiantang River. The city administers districts including Wucheng and Jindong, the county-level cities of Lanxi, Yiwu, Dongyang and Yongkang, and the counties of Wuyi, Pujiang and Pan'an.

History

The area was settled in antiquity and came under the administration of Kuaiji Commandery during the Qin dynasty. Jinhua prefecture was established under the Tang dynasty. It was an important centre of trade and scholarship in the Song dynasty, and the philosopher and neo-Confucian thinker Lü Zuqian was a native.

Economy and culture

The wider Jinhua area is a major centre of light industry, hardware and small-commodity trade, anchored by Yiwu's markets and Yongkang's hardware manufacturing. The city is also famed for Double Dragon Cave (Shuanglongdong), a karst cave scenic area, and for its cured ham, produced for over a thousand years and prized in Chinese cuisine.
