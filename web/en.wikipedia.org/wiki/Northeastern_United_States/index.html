The Northeastern United States, often simply the Northeast, is one of the four census regions of the United States. As defined by the U.S. Census Bureau it comprises the six New England states and the three Mid-Atlantic states of New York, New Jersey and Pennsylvania. It is the most densely populated and economically developed region of the country, home to the megalopolis stretching from Boston to Washington, D.C.

Geography

The region extends from the Atlantic coast to the Great Lakes and the Ohio River. Its terrain includes the coastal plain, the Appalachian Mountains, and the rolling farmland of the interior. Major rivers include the Hudson, Connecticut, Susquehanna and Delaware. The climate is humid continental in the north and humid subtropical toward the south of the region, with cold, snowy winters in New England and upstate New York.

History

The Northeast was one of the earliest areas of European colonisation in North America and the cradle of the American Revolution, with events such as the Boston Tea Party and the battles of Lexington and Concord. It became the centre of American industrialisation in the 19th century, with textile mills, shoe factories and steel works, and received waves of immigrants through ports such as New York and Boston.

Economy and culture

The region is a centre of finance (Wall Street), higher education (the Ivy League), medicine, publishing and technology. If it were a separate country, the Northeast would rank among the largest economies in the world. Culturally it is known for its historic cities, distinct accents, and professional sports rivalries.
