Mid-Atlantic Ridge

The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is a mid-ocean ridge (a divergent plate boundary) located along the floor of the Atlantic Ocean, and part of the longest mountain range in the world. It separates the Eurasian and North American plates in the North Atlantic, and the African and South American plates in the South Atlantic.

Geology

The ridge runs from north of the Arctic Circle to Bouvet Island in the South Atlantic, mostly underwater, though it rises above sea level in Iceland. New oceanic crust is continually formed at the ridge by magma rising between the separating plates, driving seafloor spreading; the Atlantic widens by roughly 2.5 cm per year.

Discovery

The ridge's existence was first inferred from temperature and depth soundings in the 19th century and was mapped in detail in the 1950s by Marie Tharp and Bruce Heezen, providing key evidence for plate tectonics.
