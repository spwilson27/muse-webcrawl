The Canadian province of Manitoba has several official emblems, established by legislation and royal grant. The province's armorial bearings were granted by royal warrant of King Edward VII on 10 May 1905, and its flag — a Red Ensign bearing the provincial shield — was adopted in 1965 and first raised on 12 May 1966, after the national flag debate.

Coat of arms

The shield shows a chief bearing the cross of St. George, recalling Manitoba's Hudson's Bay Company heritage, above a plains bison standing on a rock. The augmented arms of 1993 added a crest (a beaver holding a prairie crocus, beneath the Crown), supporters — a unicorn and a horse — and the motto Gloriosus et liber ("Glorious and free"), drawn from "O Canada."

Provincial symbols

Manitoba's floral emblem is the prairie crocus (Pulsatilla patens), adopted in 1906; its bird is the great grey owl (1987), its tree the white spruce (1984), and its fish the walleye. The Manitoba tartan, registered in 1962, uses colours representing the province's agriculture and natural features. The bison also appears on the provincial flag's shield and is a widely used symbol of Manitoba.
