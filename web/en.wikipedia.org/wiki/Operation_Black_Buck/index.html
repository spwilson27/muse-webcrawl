Operation Black Buck

Operation Black Buck
Date1 May – 12 June 1982
LocationPort Stanley Airport and Mount Pleasant area, Falkland Islands
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom (Royal Air Force) vs. Argentina
AircraftAvro Vulcan B.2 bombers supported by Handley Page Victor tankers
ResultDamage to Stanley runway; strategic and psychological effect debated

Operation Black Buck was a series of seven very-long-range ground attack missions flown by Royal Air Force Avro Vulcan bombers against Argentine positions in the Falkland Islands during the 1982 Falklands War. The raids, flown from Ascension Island, were at the time the longest bombing missions in history, covering some 7,600 nautical miles (14,100 km) on a round trip of nearly 16 hours, supported by an elaborate chain of Victor aerial refuelling tankers — each Vulcan required multiple refuellings, and the tankers themselves had to be refuelled in relay.

The missions

The first raid, Black Buck One, on the night of 30 April–1 May 1982, targeted the runway at Port Stanley Airport: Vulcan XM607, flown by Flight Lieutenant Martin Withers, dropped twenty-one 1,000 lb bombs across the runway, one of which cratered it, preventing its use by fast jets (Argentine transports continued to use it). Subsequent raids attacked the runway again and, in Black Buck Five and Six, targeted Argentine Westinghouse TPS-43 radar installations with American-supplied Shrike anti-radiation missiles. Black Buck Six's Vulcan, XM597, suffered a broken refuelling probe and diverted to Rio de Janeiro, where it was briefly impounded by Brazil. The final raid, Black Buck Seven, on 12 June again bombed Stanley airfield.

Assessment

The material damage inflicted was modest relative to the enormous logistical effort — a single bomber's load required the better part of the RAF's tanker fleet. Nevertheless, the raids demonstrated that the British mainland could strike the islands, compelled Argentina to retain air-defence fighters on the mainland rather than deploying them forward, and had significant psychological and political impact. The Black Buck missions held the record for the longest bombing raids in history until surpassed by USAF B-52 sorties in the 1991 Gulf War.
