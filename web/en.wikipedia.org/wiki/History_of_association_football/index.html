History of association football

The history of association football, more commonly known as football or soccer, stretches back through folk games played for centuries to the codification of the modern game in England in 1863. Although ball games involving kicking were played in many ancient cultures, the history of the modern sport begins with the formation of the Football Association.

Early ball games

The Chinese game cuju, played as early as the 3rd century BC, involved kicking a leather ball through a small opening and is recognised by FIFA as the earliest form of football for which there is evidence. Other antecedents include the Greek episkyros, the Roman harpastum, Japanese kemari, and the often violent folk football games of medieval Britain, which were repeatedly banned by authorities.

Codification

In the 19th century, football was played in English public schools under varying local rules. Attempts to unify them led to the Cambridge Rules and the Sheffield Rules. On 26 October 1863 representatives of London clubs founded the Football Association (FA) at the Freemasons' Tavern; Ebenezer Morley's Laws of the Game were agreed in December 1863. Clubs favouring handling of the ball broke away, a split that eventually produced rugby football.

Spread and development

The FA Cup, first played in 1871–72, was the first national competition, and the English Football League, founded in 1888, the first league championship. Professionalism was legalised in 1885. The game spread worldwide through British sailors, engineers and expatriates; FIFA, the international governing body, was founded in Paris in 1904. The first FIFA World Cup was held in Uruguay in 1930 and won by the hosts.

Modern era

The laws of the game are maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB), founded in 1886. Major developments of the 20th and 21st centuries include the growth of European club competitions such as the European Cup (now the UEFA Champions League, from 1955), the rise of women's football after bans were lifted in the 1970s, the Bosman ruling of 1995, huge television revenues, and the introduction of video assistant referees (VAR) in the 2010s. Association football is the world's most popular sport, with an estimated 3.5 billion fans and over 250 million players.
