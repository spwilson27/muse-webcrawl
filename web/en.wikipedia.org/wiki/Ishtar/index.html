Overview

Ishtar is the Mesopotamian goddess of love, sex, beauty, political power and war, the Akkadian, Babylonian and Assyrian counterpart of the Sumerian Inanna. She was the most prominent female deity of ancient Mesopotamia; her principal city was Uruk (later Nineveh and Arbela also), and her symbols were the eight-pointed star (the planet Venus, of which she was the embodiment) and the lion.

Myths

Ishtar's descent into the underworld (Kur) — passing seven gates at each of which she is stripped of a garment, dying, and being revived — is one of the great Sumerian-Akkadian myths, explaining the seasons of fertility and the death of her lover Tammuz (Dumuzi). In the Epic of Gilgamesh she propositions the hero; spurned, she sends the Bull of Heaven against Uruk. Babylon's Ishtar Gate (c. 575 BC, under Nebuchadnezzar II), glazed blue brick with bulls and dragons, is her most famous monument — reconstructed in Berlin's Pergamon Museum.
