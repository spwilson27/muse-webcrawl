Spanish Armed Forces

Spanish Armed Forces
Spanish: Fuerzas Armadas Españolas
Founded: 15th–16th centuries
Service branches: Army, Navy, Air and Space Force
Headquarters: Madrid
Commander-in-chief: King of Spain

The Spanish Armed Forces are the military forces of the Kingdom of Spain. They consist of the Army (Ejército de Tierra), the Navy (Armada), and the Air and Space Force (Ejército del Aire y del Espacio), together with joint bodies such as the Military Emergencies Unit and the Royal Guard. The King of Spain is the commander-in-chief, while day-to-day direction rests with the Ministry of Defence.

History

The Spanish military has roots in the forces of the Catholic Monarchs and the famed tercios of the 16th and 17th centuries, when Spain was a leading European military power. After the transition to democracy in the late 1970s, the armed forces were professionalized; compulsory military service was suspended in 2001. Spain joined NATO in 1982.

Structure and missions

The armed forces number roughly 120,000 active personnel. Spanish forces contribute to NATO, European Union, and United Nations missions abroad, as well as domestic emergency response through the Military Emergencies Unit (UME). Major equipment includes Eurofighter Typhoon aircraft, Leopard 2 tanks, F-100 class frigates with Aegis combat systems, and the aircraft carrier Juan Carlos I.
