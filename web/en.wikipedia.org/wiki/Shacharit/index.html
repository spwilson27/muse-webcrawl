Shacharit

Shacharit (Hebrew: שַׁחֲרִית šaḥăriṯ, "dawn") is the morning tefillah (prayer) in Judaism, one of the three daily services prescribed by Jewish tradition. It is recited after dawn and, according to halakha (Jewish law), should be completed within the first portion of the morning, ideally finishing the central Amidah by the fourth seasonal hour of the day.

Tradition attributes the institution of Shacharit to the patriarch Abraham, based on the verse "And Abraham rose early in the morning to the place where he had stood before the LORD" (Genesis 19:27). The morning service corresponds to the daily morning sacrifice (tamid) offered in the Temple in Jerusalem.

Structure

The weekday Shacharit service is the longest of the three daily services and consists of several main sections:

  • Morning blessings (Birkhot Hashachar) — a series of blessings thanking God for the basic gifts of the new day.
  • Pesukei Dezmira ("verses of song") — introductory psalms and passages, including Psalm 145 (Ashrei) and the Song of the Sea.
  • Barekhu — the formal call to prayer, opening the central part of the service.
  • Shema and its blessings — the recitation of the Shema Yisrael with blessings before and after it.
  • Amidah — the standing prayer of nineteen blessings, recited silently and then repeated aloud by the cantor (on weekdays when a minyan is present).
  • Tachanun — supplicatory prayers, omitted on festive days.
  • Torah reading — on Mondays, Thursdays, Shabbat, and festivals, a portion is read from a Torah scroll.
  • Concluding prayers — including Aleinu and the Mourner's Kaddish.

Time

The earliest time for Shacharit is dawn (alot hashachar), though the preferred time (zman tefillah) begins at sunrise (netz hachama). Rabbinic authorities hold that the Amidah should be recited by the fourth hour of the day; the Shema must be said by the third hour. On Shabbat and festivals the service is lengthened by the Musaf additional service and, in many communities, a more leisurely pace, so synagogue services often run two to three hours.

Objects used

During weekday Shacharit, observant Jewish men (and, in many egalitarian communities, women) wear a tallit (prayer shawl) and tefillin (phylacteries), small leather boxes containing Torah passages worn on the arm and head. On Shabbat and major festivals tefillin are not worn.
