Bank account

A bank account is a financial account maintained by a bank or other financial institution in which the financial transactions between the bank and a customer are recorded. Each financial institution sets the terms and conditions for each type of account it offers, which are classified in commonly understood types, such as deposit accounts, credit card accounts, current accounts, loan accounts or many other types of account. A customer may have more than one account.

Types

  • Transaction accounts — called current accounts in the United Kingdom and much of the Commonwealth, and checking accounts in the United States; designed for frequent deposits and withdrawals, often with a debit card and cheque facility.
  • Savings accounts — pay interest and may restrict the number of withdrawals.
  • Deposit accounts — time deposits or certificates of deposit where funds are locked for a fixed term in exchange for higher interest.
  • Money market accounts — hybrid accounts combining limited cheque-writing with money-market interest rates.
  • Joint accounts — held by two or more persons, common for married couples.

Account numbers

In most countries, accounts are identified by a bank account number; internationally, the IBAN (International Bank Account Number) standard combines the country, bank and account identifiers, and is used with a SWIFT/BIC code for cross-border payments. In the United States, a routing transit number identifies the institution alongside the account number.

Opening and regulation

Opening an account generally requires identity verification under know-your-customer (KYC) and anti-money-laundering rules. Deposits in many jurisdictions are protected by deposit insurance up to a statutory limit — for example by the FDIC in the United States and the FSCS in the United Kingdom. People without any account are described as "unbanked", and those with limited access as "underbanked".
