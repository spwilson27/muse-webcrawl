Dalkeith Palace

Location: Dalkeith, Midlothian, Scotland
Built: 1702–1711 (present palace)
Architect: James Smith, after John Vanbrugh
Owner: Buccleuch Living Heritage Trust

Dalkeith Palace in Dalkeith, Midlothian, Scotland, is a seat of the Duke of Buccleuch. The present classical palace was built between 1702 and 1711 for Anna Scott, 1st Duchess of Buccleuch, on the site of Dalkeith Castle, a stronghold of the Douglas Earls of Morton.

History

The castle passed to the Scott family in 1642. The duchess, widow of the executed Duke of Monmouth, commissioned architect James Smith to model a grand new house partly on William III's palace of Het Loo in the Netherlands. King George IV stayed at the palace during his 1822 visit to Scotland — the first visit of a reigning British monarch to Scotland since the 17th century — and Queen Victoria also visited.

Today

The Buccleuch family moved out in 1914, and the palace was later leased as offices and, from 1985, as a study-abroad centre for the University of Wisconsin system. The surrounding Dalkeith Country Park is open to the public, with woodland walks along the River Esk, an adventure playground and the restored 18th-century orangery and stables courtyard housing shops and a restaurant.
