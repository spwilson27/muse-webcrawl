Vietnam Veterans Memorial

LocationNational Mall, Washington, D.C., United States
DesignerMaya Lin
DedicatedNovember 13, 1982

The Vietnam Veterans Memorial is a U.S. national memorial in Washington, D.C., honouring American service members who died or were declared missing in action in the Vietnam War. Its centrepiece is the Memorial Wall, two walls of polished black granite sunk into the earth, inscribed with the names of more than 58,000 dead and missing.

The black granite wall of the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, reflecting visitors among the names.

Design

The memorial was designed by Maya Lin, a 21-year-old Yale undergraduate, who won a national competition with a minimalist design: two 246-foot (75 m) walls meeting at an angle of 125 degrees, one pointing toward the Washington Monument and the other toward the Lincoln Memorial. The names are listed in chronological order of casualty, so the story of the war unfolds along the wall. Lin described the design as a wound in the earth.

The abstract design was controversial at first, and two additions were made: Frederick Hart's bronze statue The Three Soldiers (1984) and the Vietnam Women's Memorial by Glenna Goodacre (1993).

Significance

The memorial was dedicated on November 13, 1982. Visitors commonly make pencil rubbings of names and leave offerings — letters, photographs, medals, and personal effects — which are collected daily by the National Park Service and preserved in an archive. It is one of the most visited memorials on the National Mall, receiving several million visitors a year, and its design profoundly influenced later memorial architecture.
