Policy debate

Policy debate is a form of debate competition in the United States in which teams of two advocate for and against a resolution that typically calls for a change in the policy of the United States federal government. It is also referred to as cross-examination debate (CX) because of the cross-examination periods that follow each constructive speech.

Structure

A round consists of eight speeches: four constructive speeches of eight minutes (in high school) or nine minutes (in college), each followed by a three-minute cross-examination, and four five- or six-minute rebuttals. The affirmative presents a plan to implement the resolution and argues it produces net benefits; the negative refutes the case and may present disadvantages, counterplans, and critiques (kritiks). Debaters read large quantities of quoted evidence at very high speed — a delivery style known as "spreading" — and judges decide rounds on a framework of stock issues such as inherency, solvency, topicality and harms.

Organisations

Policy debate is governed at the high school level by organisations such as the National Speech and Debate Association, and at the college level by the Cross Examination Debate Association and the National Debate Tournament. A single resolution is debated for the entire academic year.
