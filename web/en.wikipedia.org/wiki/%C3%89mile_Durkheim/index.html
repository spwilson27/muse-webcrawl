Émile Durkheim

David Émile Durkheim (15 April 1858 – 15 November 1917) was a French sociologist, widely regarded as a principal architect of modern social science and, with Karl Marx and Max Weber, one of the founding figures of sociology. He formally established sociology as an academic discipline in France.

Major works

In The Division of Labour in Society (1893) he analysed social solidarity. The Rules of Sociological Method (1895) set out his programme of studying "social facts". Suicide (1897) famously argued that suicide rates reflect levels of social integration and regulation, identifying egoistic, altruistic, anomic and fatalistic types. The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life (1912) treated religion as a fundamental social institution.

Legacy

Durkheim founded the journal L'Année sociologique and the first European department of sociology at the University of Bordeaux. Concepts such as anomie and collective consciousness remain central to sociological theory.
