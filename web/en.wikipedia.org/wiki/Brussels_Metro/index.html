The Brussels Metro (French: Métro de Bruxelles; Dutch: Brusselse metro) is a rapid transit system serving a large part of the Brussels-Capital Region of Belgium. It consists of four conventional metro lines (1, 2, 5 and 6) and is complemented by underground premetro tram sections.

History

The first metro line opened on 20 September 1976 between De Brouckère and Merode, splitting into two branches. The network expanded steadily: line 2, a loop around the city centre, was completed in 2009, and line 6 was created by rebranding part of line 2. Premetro tunnels, such as the North–South Axis, carry trams but were designed for later conversion to heavy metro.

Network and service

Operated by STIB/MIVB, the metro has 59 stations and about 40 km of metro route. A fifth line (line 3), converting the North–South premetro axis and extending it northwards to Bordet, has been under construction and planning for many years, with the new northern section the subject of prolonged debate and delay.
