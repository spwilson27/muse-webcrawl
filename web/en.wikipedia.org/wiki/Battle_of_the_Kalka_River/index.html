Battle of the Kalka River

The Battle of the Kalka River on 31 May 1223 was the first clash between the Mongol Empire and the principalities of Kievan Rus'. A Mongol reconnaissance-in-force under Subutai and Jebe, returning from a great raid around the Caspian and Caucasus, annihilated a larger but divided Rus'–Cuman coalition near the Kalka River (in modern Ukraine).

Background

After defeating the Georgians and Cumans in the Caucasus, the Mongols raided the steppe. The Cumans appealed to their Rus' in-laws; a coalition of princes — from Galicia, Kiev, Chernigov and others — marched south, winning an initial skirmish against the Mongol rearguard and ignoring warnings about Mongol feigned retreats.

Battle

The Rus' advanced eagerly across the Kalka without coordinating: the Galician and Cuman vanguard attacked first and was routed by a planned Mongol withdrawal and counterattack, fleeing back through the still-deploying Chernigov troops and into the Kievans' position. Mstislav of Kiev, who had not joined the battle, fortified a hill camp; after three days he surrendered on terms — and was executed anyway, the princes famously suffocated beneath boards while Mongol generals feasted on top, keeping the letter of a promise not to shed their blood.

Aftermath

The Mongols raided toward the Dnieper but withdrew east, suffering a defeat by the Volga Bulgars, and returned to Mongolia. The Rus' principalities, having lost many princes and warriors, failed to unite — fourteen years later Batu's full invasion began the conquest that subjected Rus' to Mongol rule for two centuries.
