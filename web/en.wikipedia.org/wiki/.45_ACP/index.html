.45 ACP

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The .45 ACP (Automatic Colt Pistol), also known as .45 Auto or 11.43×23mm, is a rimless straight-walled handgun cartridge designed by John Moses Browning in 1904, for use in his prototype Colt semi-automatic pistol. After successful United States Army trials, it was adopted as the standard chambering for the Colt M1911 pistol.

History

The cartridge arose from US cavalry experience in the Philippine–American War, where .38-calibre revolvers were judged inadequate in stopping power against Moro fighters. The Army's Thompson–LaGarde tests of 1904 favoured a bullet of at least .45 calibre, and Browning adapted his design accordingly. The .45 ACP served as the standard US military pistol and submachine-gun cartridge through both World Wars, Korea, and Vietnam, until the M1911 was largely replaced by the 9mm Beretta M9 in 1985.

Performance

The standard military load fires a 230-grain full metal jacket bullet at roughly 835 feet per second. The round is noted for its heavy bullet, modest muzzle velocity, and — because of its low pressure — its suitability for suppressed weapons. It remains one of the most popular handgun cartridges in the United States for self-defence, target shooting, and competition, chambered in a vast range of pistols.
