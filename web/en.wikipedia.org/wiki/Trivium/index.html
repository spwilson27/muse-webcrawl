The trivium (Latin for "the place where three roads meet") is the lower division of the seven liberal arts and comprises grammar, logic (dialectic) and rhetoric. It was the foundation of a medieval university education, preceding the quadrivium of arithmetic, geometry, music and astronomy.

Content

Grammar studies the structure and correct use of language; logic (or dialectic) provides the mechanics of thought and reasoning; rhetoric is the art of persuasive expression and communication of what has been analysed. Sister Miriam Joseph summarised the three as concerned respectively with the thing as it is symbolised, the thing as it is known, and the thing as it is communicated.

History

The scheme of the liberal arts was systematised in late antiquity by writers such as Martianus Capella and Boethius, and became the standard curriculum of medieval schools and universities. In the modern era the term "trivial" — from the comparatively elementary trivium — came to mean "of little importance", though the trivium has been revived in the classical education movement of the 20th and 21st centuries.
