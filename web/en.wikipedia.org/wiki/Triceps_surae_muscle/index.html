Triceps surae muscle

Triceps surae
DetailsMuscle group of the posterior leg
OriginDistal femur (gastrocnemius); tibia and fibula (soleus)
InsertionCalcaneus via the Achilles tendon
ArteryPosterior tibial artery
NerveTibial nerve (S1–S2)
ActionsPlantar flexion of the foot at the ankle

The triceps surae is a pair of muscles located at the calf – the two-headed gastrocnemius and the soleus. These muscles both insert into the calcaneus, the bone of the heel of the foot, and form the major part of the muscle of the posterior leg, commonly known as the calf muscle.

Structure

The triceps surae is connected to the foot through the Achilles tendon, and has three bellies ("heads" from the Latin caput): the medial and lateral heads of the gastrocnemius, which arise from the femur, and the soleus, which arises from the tibia and fibula. All three bellies merge as they descend, joining into a single tendon.

Function

The main function of the triceps surae is plantar flexion of the foot at the ankle joint, the movement used in walking, running and jumping. It also assists in stabilising the leg over the foot while standing.

Clinical significance

The triceps surae is vulnerable to injury, including Achilles tendinitis and rupture of the Achilles tendon. The small plantaris tendon, running between the gastrocnemius and soleus, can also rupture and mimic a more serious injury.
