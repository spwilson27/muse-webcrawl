Coenzyme A

Coenzyme A (CoA) is a coenzyme essential to the synthesis and oxidation of fatty acids and to the citric acid cycle. It carries acyl groups as thioesters, most importantly in the form of acetyl-CoA.

Coenzyme A
Derived fromPantothenic acid (vitamin B5), cysteamine, ATP
Key formAcetyl-CoA
Discovered1945 (Fritz Lipmann)

Function

CoA's reactive thiol group forms high-energy thioester bonds with carboxylic acids, activating them for transfer. Acetyl-CoA, produced from pyruvate, fatty acids and amino acids, feeds the citric acid cycle and provides acetyl groups for biosynthesis and protein acetylation. Fritz Lipmann received the Nobel Prize in 1953 partly for discovering CoA.
