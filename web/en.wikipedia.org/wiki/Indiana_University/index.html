Indiana University

Indiana University (IU) is a public university system in the U.S. state of Indiana, with a flagship campus at Bloomington and campuses in Indianapolis and several regional centres. Founded in 1820 as the State Seminary, it became Indiana College in 1828 and Indiana University in 1838, making it one of the oldest public universities in the United States. The system enrolls more than 100,000 students across its campuses.

Campuses

Indiana University Bloomington (IUB), founded in 1820, is the flagship and largest campus, enrolling around 47,000 students and known for the Kelley School of Business, the Jacobs School of Music, the Maurer School of Law and strong programmes in languages and area studies. Indiana University Indianapolis (IU Indianapolis, formerly IUPUI) was reorganised in 2024 when Purdue University's participation ended; it hosts the IU School of Medicine — the largest medical school in the United States — along with schools of law, dentistry, nursing and public health. Regional campuses include IU East (Richmond), IU Kokomo, IU Northwest (Gary), IU South Bend, IU Southeast (New Albany), IU Columbus and IU Fort Wayne.

Academics and athletics

IU offers hundreds of degree programmes and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity". Indiana's varsity teams, the Hoosiers, compete in the Big Ten Conference; the Bloomington men's basketball programme has won five NCAA championships, and the university has a distinguished swimming and diving tradition. The Little 500 bicycle race, held since 1951, is a famous campus tradition.

Governance

The university is governed by a nine-member Board of Trustees and led by a system president; it is distinct from Purdue University, Indiana's other major public research university system, though the two formerly shared the Indianapolis campus and the Fort Wayne campus.
