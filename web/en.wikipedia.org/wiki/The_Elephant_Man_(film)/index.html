The Elephant Man (film)

The Elephant Man is a 1980 American-British historical drama film directed by David Lynch and starring John Hurt as Joseph Merrick, a severely deformed man in Victorian London, and Anthony Hopkins as the surgeon Frederick Treves. Filmed in black and white and produced by Mel Brooks's Brooksfilms, it was Lynch's second feature, following Eraserhead.

Plot

Treves discovers Merrick being exhibited as a freak-show attraction, the "Elephant Man," and brings him to the London Hospital, where, beneath his deformities, Merrick proves gentle, literate and intelligent. He is visited by London society, including the actress Madge Kendal, but his exploitation by the showman Bytes and a night porter shows he cannot escape being displayed. Merrick completes a model of St Philip's Church and dies in his sleep after lying down flat like "ordinary" people, suffocating under the weight of his head.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success, nominated for eight Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director and Best Actor. Its makeup by Christopher Tucker, which had no Oscar category at the time, directly prompted the Academy to create the Best Makeup award the following year. Hurt's performance — delivered under prosthetics modelled on casts of Merrick's body — is widely considered among the finest of his career.
