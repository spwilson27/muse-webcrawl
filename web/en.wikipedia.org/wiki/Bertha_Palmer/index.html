Bertha Matilde Honoré Palmer (May 22, 1849 – May 5, 1918) was an American socialite, businesswoman, and philanthropist, the leading society figure of Chicago and a pioneering collector of French Impressionist art.

Life in Chicago

She married merchant Potter Palmer in 1870. After the Great Chicago Fire, the Palmers' castle-like mansion on Lake Shore Drive became the centre of Chicago society.

World's Columbian Exposition

Palmer chaired the Board of Lady Managers for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition, promoting women's work internationally. She commissioned Mary Cassatt's mural for the Woman's Building and assembled a major collection of Monet, Renoir, and Degas, much of it now at the Art Institute of Chicago.

Florida

Widowed in 1902, she became a major landowner and agricultural innovator in Sarasota County, Florida, where she died in 1918.
