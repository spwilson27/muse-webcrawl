STCW Convention

Full nameInternational Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers
Signed7 July 1978, London
Effective28 April 1984
DepositaryInternational Maritime Organization (IMO)

The International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers (STCW), adopted in 1978 by the International Maritime Organization, sets minimum qualification standards for masters, officers and watch personnel on seagoing merchant ships. It was the first convention to establish basic requirements on training, certification and watchkeeping for seafarers on an international level; previously, standards were set by individual governments without reference to practices in other countries.

History and amendments

The convention entered into force in 1984. A major revision in 1995 (the STCW-95 amendments) greatly expanded its requirements, adding the detailed STCW Code, which specifies mandatory standards of competence, and giving the IMO greater powers to check parties' compliance. A further comprehensive revision, the Manila Amendments, was adopted in 2010 and entered into force in 2012; it updated training requirements for modern technology, introduced new requirements on hours of rest, prevention of drug and alcohol abuse, security training, and certification for able seafarers and electro-technical officers.

Structure

The convention consists of articles and an annex, supplemented by the STCW Code. Part A of the Code is mandatory and contains standards of competence organised in tables by function (navigation, cargo handling and stowage, controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board, marine engineering, electrical and electronic control engineering, radiocommunications, and maintenance and repair). Part B is recommendatory guidance. All seafarers must complete basic safety training covering personal survival techniques, fire prevention and fire fighting, elementary first aid, and personal safety and social responsibilities.

Application

STCW applies to seafarers serving on seagoing ships entitled to fly the flag of a party state, with exceptions including warships and most fishing vessels (covered by the separate STCW-F Convention of 1995). Parties issue certificates of competency and endorsements attesting that national requirements meet the convention's standards, and "white list" parties are those confirmed by the IMO as giving full effect to the convention.
