Grundtvig

Nikolai Frederik Severin Grundtvig
Born: 8 September 1783, Udby, Denmark
Died: 2 September 1872, Copenhagen
Pastor, poet, historian, educator

Nikolai Frederik Severin Grundtvig (1783–1872) was a Danish pastor, theologian, poet, historian, and educator whose influence on Danish national identity, church life, and education has been immense. He wrote hundreds of hymns—more than any other Danish hymnwriter—and his works form a large part of the Danish Hymn Book.

Ideas

Grundtvig championed "the living word" over doctrinal rigidity, a patriotic yet open Danish nationalism grounded in history, language, and Norse mythology, and the concept of folkelighed—a sense of belonging to a people and culture. His educational ideas, summarized as "the school for life", inspired the Danish folk high school movement, beginning at Rødding in 1844, which spread across Scandinavia and beyond as a model of non-examined adult education.

Legacy

Grundtvigian movements reshaped the Danish Lutheran church (including the "Grundtvigian" free congregations), Danish politics, and national song culture through communal singing. His historical and mythological writings, including translations of Beowulf and Saxo Grammaticus, helped define the Danish Golden Age's national consciousness.
