"Carol of the Bells" is a popular Christmas carol based on the Ukrainian New Year song "Shchedryk", composed by Mykola Leontovych in 1914. Its English lyrics, about the joyful ringing of bells announcing Christmas, were written by Peter J. Wilhousky in 1936.

The original "Shchedryk"

Leontovych's piece is built on a four-note ostinato motif drawn from a traditional Ukrainian chant associated with the swallow heralding spring and a prosperous New Year. It was first performed in Kyiv in 1916 and introduced to American audiences in 1922, when Alexander Koshetz's Ukrainian National Chorus performed it at Carnegie Hall during a concert tour.

The English carol

Wilhousky, an American composer of Ukrainian descent, heard the melody and wrote new Christmas lyrics centred on bells ("Hark! how the bells, sweet silver bells..."), publishing his arrangement in 1936. The piece's hypnotic repeating figure has made it one of the most recorded and arranged of all carols, in versions ranging from classical choirs to the Trans-Siberian Orchestra's rock arrangement "Christmas Eve/Sarajevo 12/24".

Cultural significance

The carol has become a symbol of Ukrainian cultural heritage, performed worldwide and widely shared following the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022, a century after its New York debut.
