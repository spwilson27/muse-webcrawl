Connectivism

Connectivism pictures learning as the formation and navigation of networks of people, resources and ideas.

Connectivism is a learning theory developed by George Siemens and Stephen Downes for the digital age, first presented in Siemens's 2004 article "Connectivism: A Learning Theory for the Digital Age" and elaborated by both authors thereafter. It holds that learning is the process of creating connections and developing a network, and that knowledge is distributed across a network of connections — in other people, organisations, databases and devices — rather than residing solely in an individual mind.

Principles

Siemens listed eight principles, including: learning and knowledge rest in a diversity of opinions; learning is a process of connecting specialised nodes or information sources; learning may reside in non-human appliances; the capacity to know more is more critical than what is currently known; nurturing and maintaining connections is needed for continual learning; the ability to see connections between fields is a core skill; currency (accurate, up-to-date knowledge) is the intent of connectivist activities; and decision-making is itself a learning process.

Context and applications

Downes and Siemens applied connectivism in designing the first massive open online courses (MOOCs) in 2008 (the course "Connectivism and Connective Knowledge", CCK08), whose distributed, network-based format contrasted with later content-delivery ("xMOOC") platforms. Connectivist ideas have influenced networked learning, personal learning environments and rhizomatic learning (Dave Cormier).

Criticism

Critics have questioned whether connectivism is a standalone learning theory at the level of behaviourism, cognitivism and constructivism, or rather a pedagogical stance describing networked phenomena; others have disputed its account of knowledge and its treatment of interaction versus information retrieval. Defenders reply that established theories predate networked digital technology and fail to account for knowledge stored and manipulated by technology.
