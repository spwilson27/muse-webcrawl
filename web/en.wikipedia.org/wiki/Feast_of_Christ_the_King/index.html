The Feast of Christ the King, formally the Solemnity of Our Lord Jesus Christ, King of the Universe, is a feast in the liturgical year of the Roman Catholic Church that emphasises the kingship of Christ over all creation. It was instituted by Pope Pius XI in 1925 with the encyclical Quas primas, in response to growing secularism and nationalist claims on allegiance.

History

Pius XI fixed the feast on the last Sunday of October. In the 1969 revision of the General Roman Calendar, Pope Paul VI moved it to the last Sunday of the liturgical year, the Sunday before the First Sunday of Advent, and raised it to the rank of a solemnity. Many Lutheran, Anglican, and other Protestant churches also observe the day as Christ the King Sunday.

Observance

The feast closes the Church year and presents Christ's reign as one of truth, life, holiness, grace, justice, love, and peace. Processions and acts of consecration of the human race to the Sacred Heart are traditional devotions.
