Parathyroid hormone

Parathyroid hormone (PTH), also called parathormone or parathyrin, is a peptide hormone secreted by the chief cells of the parathyroid glands. It plays a central role in regulating serum calcium levels through its actions on bone, kidney and intestine.

Function

When blood calcium falls, the parathyroid glands release PTH. PTH stimulates osteoclast activity, releasing calcium and phosphate from bone; increases renal reabsorption of calcium while promoting phosphate excretion; and stimulates the kidney to convert vitamin D to its active form, calcitriol, which increases intestinal absorption of calcium. Together these actions raise blood calcium toward normal.

Structure and secretion

Human PTH is a single-chain polypeptide of 84 amino acids, synthesised as a larger preprohormone. Its secretion is controlled by the calcium-sensing receptor on parathyroid chief cells in a negative feedback loop.

Clinical significance

Excess PTH secretion causes hyperparathyroidism, leading to hypercalcaemia, bone loss and kidney stones. Deficient secretion causes hypoparathyroidism and hypocalcaemia. Synthetic PTH analogues such as teriparatide are used in the treatment of osteoporosis.
