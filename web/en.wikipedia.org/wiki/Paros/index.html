Paros

Location: Cyclades, Aegean Sea, Greece
Area: 196 km²
Population: c. 13,700 (2021)
Capital: Parikia
Highest point: Mt. Profitis Ilias (771 m)
Known for: Parian marble; beaches; Panagia Ekatontapiliani church

Paros (Greek: Πάρος) is a Greek island in the central Aegean Sea, one of the Cyclades group, lying west of Naxos, from which it is separated by a channel about 8 km wide. It was famous in antiquity for its fine white marble, which gave rise to the term "Parian" for especially valued marble and porcelain-like stone; the Venus de Milo and the Winged Victory of Samothrace were carved from Parian marble.

History

Paros was settled in the Neolithic period (the settlement of Saliagos is among the earliest in the Cyclades) and flourished in the Archaic period, colonising Thasos and producing the lyric poet Archilochus (7th century BC), inventor of iambic invective. The island was conquered by Athens' rivals in the Persian Wars, joined the Delian League, and later passed through Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine hands. The great Byzantine church of Panagia Ekatontapiliani ("Church of the Hundred Doors") in Parikia, traditionally dated to the 4th–6th centuries, is one of the best-preserved early Christian monuments in Greece. After the Fourth Crusade, Paros belonged to the Duchy of the Archipelago, then to the Ottoman Empire, and joined the modern Greek state after the War of Independence.

Geography and economy

The island's main town is the port of Parikia on the west coast; the fishing village of Naoussa in the north is a major tourist centre. Tourism is now the dominant industry, drawn by beaches such as Kolymbithres and Santa Maria, windsurfing conditions in the strait with Antiparos, and traditional Cycladic villages such as Lefkes. The marble quarries at Marathi, worked since antiquity, can still be visited.
