Roanoke River

Roanoke River
Location: Virginia and North Carolina, United States
Length: approx. 660 km (410 miles)
Source: Confluence of North and South Forks, near Lafayette, Virginia
Mouth: Albemarle Sound, North Carolina

The Roanoke River is a river in southern Virginia and northeastern North Carolina in the United States, about 660 kilometres (410 miles) long. It begins near Lafayette in Montgomery County, Virginia, at the confluence of its North and South Forks, and flows generally southeast through the city of Roanoke, across the Virginia Piedmont, and into North Carolina, emptying into Albemarle Sound near Plymouth. In its upper reaches it is also known as the Staunton River, a name commonly applied to the stretch between Roanoke and its mouth at Kerr Lake area; both names refer to the same watercourse.

Course and dams

The river passes the cities of Roanoke and Salem, Virginia, then flows past Altavista and Brookneal. Several major hydroelectric dams impound the river, creating Smith Mountain Lake, Leesville Lake, Kerr Lake (Buggs Island Lake), and Lake Gaston. Below the dams the river enters North Carolina, passing Roanoke Rapids and Weldon before widening into a floodplain of bottomland hardwood forests and reaching Albemarle Sound.

Ecology

The lower Roanoke River floodplain is one of the largest intact bottomland hardwood wetland ecosystems on the U.S. East Coast and is protected in part by the Roanoke River National Wildlife Refuge. The river is noted for its spring run of striped bass (rockfish), which migrate from Albemarle Sound to spawning grounds near Weldon, and for hickory shad. Roanoke Rapids Lake and the other reservoirs support recreational fishing, boating, and lakeside development.

History

The river took its name from an Algonquian word. In the colonial era the Roanoke valley was an important route westward. The river was the scene of Civil War actions, and its waters powered textile mills at Roanoke Rapids, where one of the earliest hydroelectric developments in the American South was built in the 1890s.
