Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598)

The Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598), known in Korea as the Imjin War, were two invasions of Korea launched by Toyotomi Hideyoshi with the intent of conquering the Korean Peninsula and ultimately Ming China. Japan quickly captured much of the peninsula in 1592, but Korean naval victories under Admiral Yi Sun-sin, Korean guerrilla (righteous army) resistance, and Ming Chinese intervention turned the tide.

First invasion (1592)

Japanese forces landed at Busan in April 1592 and advanced rapidly to Seoul and Pyongyang. The Korean navy, using turtle ships, disrupted Japanese supply lines, while Ming forces intervened in early 1593 and recaptured Pyongyang.

Second invasion (1597)

After failed peace talks, Japan invaded again in 1597. Korean and Ming forces held the Japanese in the south, and the war ended with Hideyoshi's death in 1598 and the withdrawal of Japanese troops.

Legacy

The war devastated Korea, caused immense loss of life, and led to the transfer of Korean artisans and technology to Japan. Admiral Yi Sun-sin remains a national hero in Korea.
