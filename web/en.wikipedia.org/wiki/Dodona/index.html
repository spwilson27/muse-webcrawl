Dodona (Greek: Δωδώνη) was an ancient Greek sanctuary and oracle dedicated to Zeus and the Mother Goddess Dione, located in Epirus in northwestern Greece. It was considered the oldest Hellenic oracle, perhaps dating to the second millennium BC, and ranked second in prestige only to Delphi.

The oracle

According to Herodotus and Homer, the oracle's prophecies were interpreted from the rustling of leaves of a sacred oak tree, the flight of doves, and the sounds of bronze cauldrons. Priests called Selloi, who were said never to wash their feet and to sleep on the ground, delivered responses. Excavations have unearthed thousands of small lead tablets on which consultants inscribed questions to the oracle — a uniquely rich record of everyday ancient concerns.

Site and history

The sanctuary flourished under the Molossian kings of Epirus; King Pyrrhus built extensively in the early 3rd century BC, including one of the largest theatres in Greece, seating some 18,000. The site was destroyed by the Aetolians in 219 BC, rebuilt, and finally suppressed with the Christianisation of the Roman Empire in the late 4th century AD. The theatre was restored in modern times and hosts performances.
