Churchill War Rooms

The Cabinet War Room, preserved as it was in 1945

The Churchill War Rooms is a museum in London, England, one of the five branches of the Imperial War Museum. It comprises the Cabinet War Rooms, a historic underground complex that housed a British government command centre throughout the Second World War, and the Churchill Museum, a museum exploring the life of Winston Churchill.

History

The War Rooms, located beneath the Treasury building in Whitehall, became operational on August 27, 1939, a week before Britain declared war on Germany. The complex included the Cabinet War Room, where the War Cabinet met over a hundred times, and the Map Room, staffed around the clock, as well as bedrooms, offices, and a transatlantic telephone room disguised as a private lavatory, from which Churchill spoke directly with President Franklin D. Roosevelt.

The rooms were closed in August 1945 after Japan's surrender, and their contents were largely preserved. The site was opened to the public in 1984, and the Churchill Museum was added in 2005.

The museum today

Visitors can see the rooms essentially as they were left in 1945, including the map-covered walls of the Map Room and Churchill's bedroom-office. The adjoining museum uses documents, photographs, and personal objects to trace Churchill's life and political career.
