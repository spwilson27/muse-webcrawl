Movement disorder

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Movement disorders affect the speed, fluency, and control of voluntary movement.

Movement disorders are clinical syndromes with either an excess of movement or a paucity of voluntary and automatic movements, unrelated to weakness or spasticity. They are broadly divided into hyperkinetic disorders (too much movement) and hypokinetic disorders (too little), and are most often associated with dysfunction of the basal ganglia and their connections. The subspecialty of neurology devoted to them is also called movement disorders.

Classification

  • Hypokinetic: Parkinson's disease and other parkinsonian syndromes, with slowness (bradykinesia), rigidity, and rest tremor.
  • Hyperkinetic:
    • Essential tremor and other tremor disorders
    • Dystonia — sustained twisting postures
    • Chorea — as in Huntington's disease
    • Myoclonus — brief shock-like jerks
    • Tics — as in Tourette syndrome
    • Ballism, athetosis, and stereotypies
  • Others: ataxia (cerebellar), restless legs syndrome, stiff-person syndrome, and functional (psychogenic) movement disorders.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the phenomenology of the abnormal movement, history (including drug exposures such as antipsychotics, which can cause tardive dyskinesia), neurological examination, brain imaging, and genetic testing where indicated. Treatments include medications that modulate dopamine and other neurotransmitters, botulinum toxin injections for focal dystonia and tremor, and deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, and dystonia.
