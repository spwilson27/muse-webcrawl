Digital Visual Interface

Image: DVI connector

Digital Visual Interface (DVI) is a video display interface developed by the Digital Display Working Group (DDWG). The digital interface is used to connect a video source, such as a video display controller, to a display device, such as a computer monitor. It was developed with the intention of creating an industry standard for the transfer of uncompressed digital video content.

Overview

The DVI specification, released in 1999, uses transition-minimized differential signaling (TMDS) to carry pixel data. A single DVI link supports resolutions up to 1920 × 1200 at 60 Hz; a dual-link connector adds a second TMDS link, supporting resolutions up to 2560 × 1600. Connector variants include DVI-D (digital only), DVI-A (analog only), and DVI-I (integrated digital and analog, compatible with VGA via a passive adapter).

History and successors

DVI replaced analog VGA connections on many LCD monitors and graphics cards in the 2000s. It has in turn been largely superseded by HDMI (with which single-link DVI video is electrically compatible) and DisplayPort, though DVI ports remained common on business displays and projectors for many years.
