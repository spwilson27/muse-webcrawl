The Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI; English: International Canine Federation) is the largest international federation of national kennel clubs and purebred registries. Founded in 1911, it is headquartered in Thuin, Belgium, and works to promote cynology — the study and breeding of domestic dogs — and to safeguard the health and welfare of purebred dogs worldwide.

History

The FCI was founded on 22 May 1911 by the kennel clubs of Germany, Austria, Belgium, France and the Netherlands. It was dissolved during the First World War and re-established in 1921. Over the following century it grew into a global body encompassing dozens of full and associate members across Europe, Asia, the Americas, Africa and Oceania.

Activities

The FCI maintains an international nomenclature recognising several hundred dog breeds, grouped into ten categories ranging from sheepdogs and cattle dogs to retrievers, companion dogs and sighthounds. It sanctions international dog shows, including the annual World Dog Show, and coordinates judging standards, pedigree recognition and working trials in disciplines such as agility, obedience and herding.
