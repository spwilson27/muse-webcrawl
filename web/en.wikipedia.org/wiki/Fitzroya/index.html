Fitzroya is a monotypic genus in the cypress family Cupressaceae, containing the single species Fitzroya cupressoides, commonly known as the alerce or Patagonian cypress. It is a large, long-lived conifer native to the Valdivian temperate rainforests of the Andes in southern Chile and adjacent parts of Argentina.

Description

The alerce is one of the largest and longest-lived trees in the world. Mature trees can exceed 60 metres in height and live for more than 3,600 years, making the species one of the oldest-living organisms known. Its reddish, fibrous bark and small scale-like leaves form a pyramidal to irregular crown. The wood is highly resistant to decay, which made it intensely sought after for shingles and building timber.

Conservation

Centuries of logging depleted the species across much of its range. It is now protected by law in Chile, listed in CITES Appendix I, and classified as Endangered. Significant stands survive in protected areas such as Alerce Andino National Park and Alerce Costero National Park, and in Argentina's Los Alerces National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
