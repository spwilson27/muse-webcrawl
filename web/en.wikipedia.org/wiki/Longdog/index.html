A longdog is a type of dog: strictly, a crossbreed between two sighthound breeds. The term is most often used in Britain and Ireland, where longdogs were developed as hunting and coursing dogs. A longdog differs from a lurcher, which is a cross between a sighthound and a non-sighthound working breed (such as a terrier, collie or gundog); a longdog has two sighthound parents.

Common crosses

Popular longdog crosses include Greyhound × Saluki (combining speed with endurance and a weather-resistant coat), Greyhound × Borzoi, Greyhound × Scottish Deerhound, and Greyhound × Whippet. Saluki crosses in particular are valued on rough or open ground and for longer runs, where pure Greyhounds' bursts of speed may not be sustained; deerhound blood adds size and bone for larger quarry.

Characteristics

Longdogs share the sighthound build — deep chest, tucked waist, long legs — with the temperament varying according to the parent breeds. They retain a strong instinct to chase by sight. Like lurchers, they are not recognised as a breed by kennel clubs but are bred, shown and raced within the working-dog and country-sports community, with lurcher and longdog shows held across Britain and Ireland.
