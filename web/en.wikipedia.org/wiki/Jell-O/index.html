Jell-O

Product type: Gelatin dessert
Owner: Kraft Heinz
Introduced: 1897
Origin: Le Roy, New York, United States

Jell-O is an American brand of gelatin desserts, puddings, and no-bake cream pies, owned by Kraft Heinz. The original gelatin dessert — powdered gelatin sweetened and flavored — was patented in 1845 by Peter Cooper, but the Jell-O brand itself was created in 1897 in Le Roy, New York, and popularized through pioneering advertising that made it a staple of American home cooking.

History

In 1897, cough-syrup manufacturer Pearle Bixby Wait and his wife May trademarked the name "Jell-O" for a flavored gelatin powder in strawberry, raspberry, orange, and lemon. Lacking marketing success, Wait sold the business in 1899 to the Genesee Pure Food Company of Orator Francis Woodward for $450. Woodward's aggressive advertising — including free recipe booklets, traveling salesmen, and ads in magazines such as the Ladies' Home Journal — made Jell-O a household name, advertised as "America's Most Famous Dessert." The brand passed to Postum (later General Foods) in 1925 and then to Kraft through corporate mergers.

Product and culture

Jell-O is prepared by dissolving the powder in hot water and chilling until set. It became a cultural icon of mid-20th-century American cuisine, used in molded salads, parfaits, and desserts, and later in "Jell-O shots." Utah, with its large Mormon population, is famously among the highest per-capita consumers of lime Jell-O; the snack was named the official state snack of Utah in 2001. The town of Le Roy hosts the Jell-O Gallery museum. Comedian Bill Cosby served as the brand's longtime spokesperson beginning in the 1970s.

Jigglers and varieties

Flavors have expanded far beyond the original four, and the line includes sugar-free versions, pudding mixes, and gelatin snacks. The Jell-O trademark's distinctive hyphenated name arose partly to ease trademark registration.
