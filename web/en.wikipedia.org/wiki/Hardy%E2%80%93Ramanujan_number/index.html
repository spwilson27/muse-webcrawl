The Hardy–Ramanujan number is 1729. It is the smallest number that can be expressed as the sum of two positive cubes in two different ways: 1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³.

The anecdote

The number is famous because of an anecdote involving the British mathematician G. H. Hardy and the Indian mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan. Hardy recalled visiting Ramanujan in hospital and remarking that the number of the taxi cab he had ridden in, 1729, seemed rather dull. Ramanujan immediately replied that it was a very interesting number, being the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways.

Taxicab numbers

The anecdote inspired the concept of taxicab numbers. The n-th taxicab number, Ta(n), is defined as the smallest number that can be expressed as the sum of two positive cubes in n distinct ways. By definition, 1729 is Ta(2). Larger taxicab numbers have been found with computer searches; Ta(3) is 87539319.

Other properties

1729 is also a Zeisel number and a Harshad number in base 10, since it is divisible by the sum of its digits (1 + 7 + 2 + 9 = 19, and 1729 = 19 × 91). It is the third Carmichael number, after 561 and 1105.
