Rose (symbolism)

The rose has served as a symbol for many thousands of years and across many cultures. In Western tradition it is above all an emblem of love and beauty, but it has also symbolised secrecy, martyrdom, political factions, socialism, and the Virgin Mary, among many other things. The meanings attached to roses also vary by colour: red for romantic love, white for purity, yellow for friendship (or historically jealousy), and black for mourning or death.

Classical mythology

In Greek and Roman myth the rose was associated with Aphrodite (Venus), goddess of love. One legend held that red roses sprang from the blood of Adonis, or from Aphrodite's own blood when she pricked herself on a thorn rushing to him. The rose also belonged to Dionysus and to the cults of Isis. The Latin phrase sub rosa — "under the rose" — meaning in secrecy, derives from the practice of hanging a rose over a council table to pledge the confidentiality of what was said beneath it; roses were carved on the ceilings of Roman dining rooms and later on confessionals for the same reason.

Religious symbolism

In Christianity the rose became a symbol of the Virgin Mary, called the "Mystical Rose" and "Rose without thorns." Five-petalled roses suggested the five wounds of Christ, and red roses symbolised the blood of martyrs. In Islam and Sufism the rose is associated with divine beauty and the Prophet Muhammad; in Persian literature the rose and the nightingale (gol-o-bolbol) are a classic pairing for the beloved and the lover. The Rosicrucian movement combined the rose with the cross as its central emblem.

Heraldry and politics

The rose is one of the most common heraldic flowers. In England the red rose of the House of Lancaster and the white rose of the House of York gave the Wars of the Roses their name; Henry VII united them in the Tudor rose. The red rose was adopted as an emblem of socialism and social democracy in the late nineteenth century — "bread and roses" — and remains the symbol of parties such as the British Labour Party. A white rose gave the German student resistance group Die Weiße Rose its name in the Second World War. The rose is the national flower of England, the United States, Iran, and several other countries.
