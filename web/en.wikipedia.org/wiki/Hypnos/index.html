Hypnos (Ancient Greek: Ὕπνος, "sleep") is the personification and god of sleep in Greek mythology. In Hesiod's Theogony he is a child of Nyx (Night) born without a father, and the twin brother of Thanatos (Death) — an association reflecting the ancient view of sleep as death's counterpart. His Roman equivalent is Somnus.

Mythology

Hypnos lived in a cave in the underworld (or, in some accounts, on Lemnos) through which flowed the river Lethe, surrounded by poppies and other soporific plants; no light or sound entered, and his sons the Oneiroi — Morpheus, Phobetor and Phantasos — brought dreams to mortals. In the Iliad, Hera bribes Hypnos with marriage to the Grace Pasithea to lull Zeus to sleep so that Poseidon can aid the Greeks; Hypnos, recalling how he was nearly hurled from Olympus after a previous such trick, at first refuses and hides in the form of a bird in the branches of a pine on Mount Ida. On another occasion he and Thanatos together carried the body of the fallen hero Sarpedon back to Lycia — a scene famous in Greek vase painting. He also put the Argonauts' dragon and, at Hera's request in later myth, various watchmen to sleep.

Legacy

The word hypnosis and terms such as hypnotic and hypnagogic derive from his name, as does the poppy's scientific associations with sleep. Hypnos appears in modern popular culture, including the Percy Jackson novels and the video game Hades (2020), in which he is a cheerful attendant of the House of Hades.
