James Lee Massey (February 11, 1934 – June 16, 2013) was an American information theorist and cryptographer whose work on coding theory, block ciphers, and cryptanalysis was foundational to digital communications and information security.

Career and contributions

Massey graduated from the University of Notre Dame and earned his PhD from MIT in 1962 with a thesis on convolutional codes; his 1963 paper on threshold decoding was highly influential. He taught at Notre Dame, UCLA, and MIT, and from 1980 until his retirement held the chair of digital communications at ETH Zurich in Switzerland. In 1968–69 he developed, independently of Elwyn Berlekamp, the Berlekamp–Massey algorithm for finding the shortest linear feedback shift register for a given output sequence, a result essential to the decoding of Reed–Solomon and BCH codes used in CDs, DVDs, and deep-space communications. In cryptography he co-designed the IDEA block cipher (with Xuejia Lai), used in early versions of PGP; co-invented the Massey–Omura cryptosystem; and proposed (with Jim Omura) and analysed ring signature and related schemes. His work on randomness and the "Massey" characterisations of entropy is also widely cited.

Honours

Massey received the Claude E. Shannon Award (1988), the IEEE Alexander Graham Bell Medal (1992), and the Marconi Prize (1999), and was a member of the U.S. National Academy of Engineering. He co-founded Codex Corporation, which commercialised error-correction equipment for telecommunications.
