Electron gun

Electron gun
FunctionProduces a narrow, collimated electron beam
UsesCRTs, electron microscopes, particle accelerators, welding

An electron gun (also called an electron emitter) is an electrical component in vacuum tubes that produces a narrow, collimated electron beam with precise kinetic energy. It is best known as the source in cathode-ray tubes (CRTs) used in older televisions and monitors.

Construction

A typical gun has a heated cathode (thermionic emission), a control grid (Wehnelt cylinder) modulating beam current, and anode electrodes that accelerate and focus the beam. Magnetic or electrostatic lenses refine focus; deflection plates or coils steer the beam.

Applications

Beyond CRTs, electron guns are central to scanning and transmission electron microscopes, electron-beam welding and lithography, linear accelerators, and microwave tubes such as klystrons and travelling-wave tubes. Photoemission and field-emission guns provide brighter beams for advanced research.

Characteristics

Key parameters include beam current, accelerating voltage, brightness, energy spread, and spot size.
