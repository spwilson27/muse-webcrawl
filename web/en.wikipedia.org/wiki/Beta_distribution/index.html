Beta distribution

[Image placeholder: Beta distribution PDF curves]

In probability theory and statistics, the beta distribution is a family of continuous probability distributions defined on the interval [0, 1] with two positive shape parameters, denoted by α and β, that appear as exponents of the variable and its complement with respect to 1.

The beta distribution is a suitable model for the random behavior of percentages and proportions. In Bayesian statistics it is the conjugate prior for the Bernoulli, binomial, negative binomial and geometric distributions, making it central to Bayesian inference about probabilities. Its density is f(x) ∝ xα−1(1−x)β−1, normalized by the beta function B(α, β).

Properties

The mean is α/(α+β). Special cases include the uniform distribution (α = β = 1) and the arcsine distribution (α = β = 1/2). The beta distribution of the first kind is related to the gamma distribution and to order statistics of uniform random variables.
