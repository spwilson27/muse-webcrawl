Osteomyelitis

Osteomyelitis is an infection and inflammation of bone and bone marrow, most commonly caused by bacteria — above all Staphylococcus aureus — and occasionally by fungi. Microbes reach the bone through the bloodstream (hematogenous osteomyelitis, typical in children, where it attacks the metaphyses of long bones), by spread from nearby infected tissue, or directly through open fractures, wounds or surgery, as in diabetic foot infections.

Signs, diagnosis and treatment

Acute osteomyelitis presents with pain, swelling, redness and warmth over the affected bone, fever and malaise; chronic disease may produce draining sinus tracts and fragments of dead bone (sequestra) surrounded by new bone (involucrum). Diagnosis combines blood tests, cultures, MRI and sometimes bone biopsy. Treatment requires prolonged antibiotics — often weeks of intravenous followed by oral therapy — and, in chronic or complicated cases, surgical debridement of dead tissue. Untreated infection can lead to abscess, pathological fracture, sepsis or, rarely, squamous-cell carcinoma in a long-standing sinus.
