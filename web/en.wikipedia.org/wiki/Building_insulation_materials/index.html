Building insulation materials

Building insulation materials are materials used in buildings to reduce heat transfer and improve energy efficiency, acoustic comfort and fire safety. Insulating performance is measured by thermal resistance (the R-value): the higher the R-value per unit thickness, the better the material insulates. Most insulation works by trapping pockets of still air or gas within a fibrous or cellular structure.

Common materials

  • Fibreglass (glass wool) — batts, rolls and loose fill made from spun glass fibres; inexpensive and widely used, but an irritant to skin and lungs during installation.
  • Mineral (rock/stone) wool — made from molten rock or slag; excellent fire resistance and sound absorption.
  • Cellulose — loose-fill insulation made from recycled paper treated with borate fire retardants.
  • Expanded polystyrene (EPS) and extruded polystyrene (XPS) — rigid foam boards used in walls, roofs and below slabs.
  • Polyurethane and polyisocyanurate foams — rigid boards and spray-applied foams with high R-value per unit thickness.
  • Spray polyurethane foam — applied as a liquid that expands and hardens, sealing air leaks; available in open-cell and closed-cell forms.
  • Natural materials — sheep's wool, cork, wood fibre, hemp, straw bales and cotton (denim) batts, used increasingly in low-impact construction.
  • Aerogel and vacuum insulated panels — very high-performance, thin insulations used where space is at a premium.
  • Reflective insulation and radiant barriers — foil-faced materials that reduce radiant heat transfer, mainly in roofs in hot climates.

Considerations

Choice of insulation depends on application, climate, moisture behaviour, fire performance, cost, durability and environmental impact. Some foam insulations historically used blowing agents with high global-warming potential, prompting regulation and a shift to low-GWP alternatives. Correct installation — free of gaps and compression — is essential to achieving the rated performance, since convection and air leakage can bypass insulation entirely.
