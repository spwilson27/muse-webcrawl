Municipalities of Sweden

The municipalities of Sweden (Swedish: Sveriges kommuner) are the country's lower-tier local government entities. There are 290 municipalities, responsible for a large share of public services such as schools, social services, elderly care, water supply, waste management and local planning.

Organisation

Each municipality is governed by an elected municipal assembly (kommunfullmäktige), which appoints an executive board (kommunstyrelse). Municipalities may levy income taxes, which form their main source of revenue. Municipalities are grouped into 21 counties and belong to one of Sweden's regions for healthcare matters.

History

The modern system traces to the local government reforms of 1862, which created rural and urban municipalities based on the old parishes. Major amalgamation reforms in 1952 and between 1962 and 1974 drastically reduced the number of municipalities from several thousand to under 300.

The largest municipality by population is Stockholm; the largest by area is Kiruna, which is also one of the largest municipalities in Europe.
