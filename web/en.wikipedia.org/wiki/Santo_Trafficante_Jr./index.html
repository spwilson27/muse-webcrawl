Santo Trafficante Jr.

Born: November 15, 1914, Tampa, Florida
Died: March 17, 1987, Houston, Texas
Occupation: Crime boss of the Trafficante crime family

Santo Trafficante Jr. (November 15, 1914 – March 17, 1987) was an American mobster and the boss of the Trafficante crime family of Tampa, Florida, succeeding his father Santo Trafficante Sr. in 1954. He was one of the most powerful Mafia bosses in the United States at the time, controlling organized crime operations in Florida and pre-Castro Cuba.

Criminal career

Trafficante maintained links with the Bonanno crime family in New York and Sam Giancana of the Chicago Outfit. He operated casinos in Havana, Cuba, including the Hotel Habana Riviera and interests in the Sans Souci and Hotel Nacional, until Fidel Castro's revolution closed and seized the mob's Cuban operations in 1959.

CIA plots and investigations

Trafficante was recruited into early Central Intelligence Agency plots to assassinate Fidel Castro, working with figures such as John Roselli and Sam Giancana; he later testified about the plots before congressional committees under grants of immunity. He was named in some theories surrounding the assassination of John F. Kennedy; Carlos Marcello and Trafficante were said by a congressional committee to have had the motive, opportunity, and means, though the committee stopped short of concluding a conspiracy involving them. Trafficante was never convicted of a major crime and died in 1987 in Houston, where he had gone for heart surgery.
