Ultrabook

Developer: Intel
Introduced: 2011
Type: Trademark and specification for thin, light laptops

Ultrabook is a marketing term and trademark, originated and trademarked by Intel, for a category of high-end subnotebook computers designed to be thin, light and relatively powerful. Announced at Computex in 2011, the Ultrabook initiative was widely seen as Intel and PC makers' response to the success of Apple's MacBook Air.

Specifications

Intel's successive specifications limited thickness (initially to about 21 mm), required fast resume from sleep, solid-state storage, long battery life — at least five hours, later nine — and the use of low-power Intel Core processors. Later generations added requirements for touchscreens, voice recognition and security features such as anti-theft technology.

History

The first Ultrabooks, including the Asus Zenbook and Acer Aspire S3, reached the market in late 2011. Early models were criticised for high prices, but costs fell and thin-and-light designs became the mainstream of laptop design. Intel phased out the Ultrabook branding in later years in favour of programmes such as Intel Evo, launched in 2020.
