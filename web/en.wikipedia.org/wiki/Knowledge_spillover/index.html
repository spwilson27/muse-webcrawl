Knowledge spillover

Knowledge created by one firm or researcher spills over to benefit others nearby.

A knowledge spillover is an external benefit that arises when knowledge created by one economic agent — a firm, university, or individual — is used by others without full compensation to the creator. Because knowledge is partially non-rival and hard to exclude others from, its spillovers are a classic positive externality and a central explanation for why innovation clusters geographically and why private investment in research and development (R&D) tends to fall below the socially optimal level.

Mechanisms

Spillovers travel through several channels: labour mobility, as skilled employees carry know-how between firms; informal communication and professional networks; publications and patents, which disclose inventions; reverse engineering of products; and spin-off firms founded by employees of incumbents or universities. Alfred Marshall described the phenomenon for industrial districts, noting that in such places the "mysteries of the trade" are "in the air".

Evidence and geography

Empirical studies using patent citations (notably Jaffe, Trajtenberg and Henderson) show that citations are geographically localised, indicating that spillovers decay with distance — a finding central to urban economics and to policies promoting clusters such as Silicon Valley and science parks. The knowledge spillover theory of entrepreneurship (Acs, Audretsch and co-authors) argues that knowledge created in incumbent firms and universities, but not commercialised by them, creates opportunities that entrepreneurs exploit by founding new firms.

Policy implications

Because spillovers mean that inventors capture only part of the value they create, they justify public support for R&D: subsidies, tax credits, public research funding, and the patent system (which trades disclosure — itself a spillover — for temporary monopoly). Regional and cluster policies likewise aim to thicken the local flows of knowledge among firms, universities, and workers.
