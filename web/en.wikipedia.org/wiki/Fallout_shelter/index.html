Fallout shelter

A fallout shelter is an enclosed space specially designed to protect occupants from radioactive debris ("fallout") resulting from a nuclear explosion. Distinct from a blast shelter, its main purpose is shielding from radiation rather than from the explosion itself; fallout radiation intensity decays rapidly, dropping to a small fraction of its initial level within two weeks.

Design

Effective shelters use dense shielding materials — earth, concrete, or brick — between occupants and the fallout outside; a packed-earth thickness of about one metre reduces gamma radiation to a small fraction. Shelters require filtered ventilation, stocks of water and food for roughly two weeks, sanitation, and radiation monitoring instruments.

History

During the Cold War, the United States and other nations built and stocked public fallout shelters. Under President John F. Kennedy, the Community Fallout Shelter Program surveyed and stocked buildings, identified by distinctive yellow-and-black signs, with water, food rations, and medical kits. Switzerland built an extensive network of shelters sufficient for its entire population. Private backyard shelters were promoted in civil defense literature such as the U.S. government's Nuclear War Survival Skills. Most public shelter programs lapsed after the Cold War, though some countries, including Switzerland and Finland, maintain theirs.
