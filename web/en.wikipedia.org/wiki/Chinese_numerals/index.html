Chinese numerals

Chinese numerals are words and characters used to denote numbers in Chinese. Two systems coexist in modern usage: the native Chinese character numerals, still used in formal, financial and everyday contexts, and the Arabic numerals introduced from the West, which dominate in science and commerce.

Structure

Chinese is a base-10 system, but large numbers are grouped by myriads (powers of 10,000) rather than by thousands: units include 萬 (10⁴), 億 (10⁸) and 兆 (10¹²). Two characters exist for "two" — 二 (èr) for counting and 兩 / 两 (liǎng) used before measure words. For financial documents, a set of complex "formal" numerals (壹, 貳, 參…) is used to prevent forgery by alteration. Traditional Suzhou numerals, a positional system derived from rod numerals, were once common in markets and are still seen in some price tags in Hong Kong and Macau.
