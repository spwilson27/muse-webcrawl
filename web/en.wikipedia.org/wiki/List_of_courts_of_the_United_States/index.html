The courts of the United States are closely linked through the Constitution and federal law. There are two kinds of courts in the United States — federal courts and state courts. The federal court system is created by the United States Constitution and federal statute, while the 50 state court systems are created by the individual state constitutions and state statutes.

The list below includes the principal federal courts of the United States and examples of the highest courts of each state.

State supreme courts

Each state has a court of last resort, usually called the state supreme court (in New York and Maryland, the highest court is called the Court of Appeals, and the "supreme court" is a trial court). Examples include:

  • Supreme Court of California
  • New York Court of Appeals
  • Supreme Court of Texas, with the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals for criminal matters
  • Florida Supreme Court
  • Illinois Supreme Court
  • Supreme Court of Pennsylvania
  • Ohio Supreme Court
  • Supreme Judicial Court of Massachusetts — the oldest appellate court in continuous existence in the Western Hemisphere
