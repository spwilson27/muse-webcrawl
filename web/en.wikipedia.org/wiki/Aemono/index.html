Aemono

Aemono
CourseSide dish
Place of originJapan
Main ingredientsVegetables, seafood; dressing of soy sauce, vinegar, miso, sesame, or tofu

Aemono (Japanese: 和え物) is a category of Japanese side dishes consisting of ingredients — typically vegetables, and sometimes seafood or other items — tossed or mixed with a dressing (ae-jiru). The verb aeru means "to dress" or "to mix", and aemono is a standard component of a traditional Japanese meal, served in small bowls as an accompaniment to rice, soup, and a main dish.

Dressings

The dressing defines the dish. Common varieties include:

  • Goma-ae — ground sesame seeds with soy sauce and sugar, often used with spinach or green beans.
  • Shira-ae — a smooth dressing of mashed tofu with sesame and miso.
  • Karashi-ae — flavoured with Japanese mustard (karashi).
  • Sumiso-ae — sweetened vinegar-miso dressing, classically paired with squid and wakame.
  • Nuta — a miso-vinegar dressing with green onion, served with seafood such as tuna or squid.

Preparation

Vegetables are usually blanched briefly and squeezed dry before being combined with the dressing just before serving, so the flavours remain fresh and the textures crisp. The ingredients are cut to uniform bite size. Aemono is related to, but distinct from, sunomono (vinegared dishes) and namul-like seasoned preparations in neighbouring cuisines.

In Japanese meals

Within the ichijū-sansai pattern (one soup, three dishes), aemono commonly fills one of the side-dish roles, contributing seasonal produce and a contrasting flavour — nutty, sharp, or sweet-sour — to the meal.
