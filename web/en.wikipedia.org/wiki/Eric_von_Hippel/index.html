Eric von Hippel

Eric von Hippel
Born: 27 August 1941
Alma mater: Harvard University; MIT; Carnegie Mellon University (PhD)
Known for: User innovation; lead user method
Fields: Innovation management, economics

Eric von Hippel (born August 27, 1941) is an American economist and professor at the MIT Sloan School of Management, specialising in the nature and economics of distributed and open innovation. He is best known for originating the concept of user innovation — the finding that many important products and processes are first developed by users rather than manufacturers.

Research

Von Hippel's empirical studies of scientific instruments, semiconductor process equipment, and sports equipment showed that the functional source of innovation often lies with users who face needs ahead of the general market. His lead user method, developed from this work, is a market research technique used by firms such as 3M to identify advanced users and co-develop breakthrough products with them. His cycle of publications began with "The Dominant Role of Users in the Scientific Instrument Innovation Process" (1976) and was consolidated in the book The Sources of Innovation (1988).

Democratizing innovation and free innovation

In Democratizing Innovation (2005), von Hippel argued that users increasingly innovate for themselves, aided by cheap design tools and communication, and that public policy and business practice should support user innovation communities, toolkits, and open sharing. His later book Free Innovation (2017) documented household-sector innovation by consumers — tens of millions of people in national surveys — who develop products at their own expense and often give the designs away freely. He has also studied innovation by patients and user entrepreneurship, where users commercialise their own designs.
