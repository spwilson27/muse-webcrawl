Post Office Limited, trading as Post Office, is a retail post office company in the United Kingdom that provides a wide range of products including postage stamps and banking services to the public, businesses and government departments through its nationwide network of around 11,500 branches — the largest retail network in the UK. Most branches are run by franchise partners or subpostmasters as agencies, alongside a small number of directly managed "Crown" branches.

History

The Post Office was historically part of the General Post Office and then of the Post Office Corporation. The British Telecommunications Act 1981 and subsequent reorganizations separated telecommunications from postal services, and in 1986 the counters business became Post Office Counters Ltd, later Post Office Ltd. Under the Postal Services Act 2011, the Post Office was separated from Royal Mail in April 2012 and remained in public ownership, with a statutory commitment to maintain a network of at least 11,500 branches meeting national access criteria covering distance and population coverage.

Services

Branches sell stamps and handle mail and parcels on behalf of Royal Mail and Parcelforce, and provide services including banking deposits and withdrawals for most UK banks, bill payments, government services (such as passport checking, driving-licence renewals and fishing licences), travel money, insurance, and the Post Office Card Account successor products. The Post Office also operates a travel money business and broadband and phone services at times in partnership with other providers.

Horizon scandal

From 1999 the Post Office rolled out the Horizon accounting system supplied by Fujitsu. Shortfalls reported by the system were treated as evidence of theft or false accounting by subpostmasters, and between 1999 and 2015 more than 900 subpostmasters were prosecuted; many were imprisoned, bankrupted or died before exoneration. In 2019 the High Court ruled in group litigation (Bates & Others v Post Office Ltd) that Horizon was not reliably accurate, and hundreds of convictions have since been overturned; Parliament passed the Post Office (Horizon System) Offences Act 2024 to quash convictions en masse. A statutory public inquiry chaired by Sir Wyn Williams examined the scandal, widely described as one of the worst miscarriages of justice in British history, and compensation schemes for victims have run to over a billion pounds.
