Pitcher

In baseball and softball, the pitcher is the player who throws the ball from the pitcher's mound toward the catcher to begin each play, with the goal of retiring a batter, who attempts either to become a base runner by hitting the thrown ball or to draw a walk. In the numbering system used to record defensive plays, the pitcher is assigned the number 1. In the course of a game, pitchers are commonly categorized by their roles and throwing hand.

Roles

A starting pitcher begins the game and is generally expected to pitch several innings. Starting pitchers typically work in a rotation, starting a game every few days. When a starter is removed, one or more relief pitchers take over. Relievers include middle relievers, setup pitchers, and the closer, who specializes in finishing close games and earning saves. Some pitchers are used situationally against batters of a particular handedness.

Pitching

Pitchers throw a variety of pitches, each with characteristic speed, movement, and grip. Common pitches include the fastball, curveball, slider, changeup, and knuckleball. Pitching demands both velocity and control: a pitch outside the strike zone is a ball, while one within it (or swung at and missed) is a strike; three strikes retire the batter and four balls award a walk. Because pitching places great stress on the arm, pitchers' workloads are carefully managed, and injuries such as ulnar collateral ligament tears — often repaired by "Tommy John surgery" — are common.

Other duties

Besides throwing, the pitcher covers first base on certain ground balls, backs up throws, fields bunts and comebackers, and in leagues without a designated hitter also bats. Pitchers also work with the catcher, who usually calls the pitches, and attempt to hold base runners close to their bases to discourage stolen bases, including by throwing pickoff attempts.
