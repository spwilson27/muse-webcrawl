Indian Police Service

Abbreviation: IPS

Formed: 1948

Country: India

Controlling authority: Ministry of Home Affairs

The Indian Police Service (IPS) is one of the three All India Services, alongside the Indian Administrative Service and the Indian Forest Service. Its officers provide senior leadership to the police forces of the states and union territories of India and to central police organisations. The service was constituted in 1948, succeeding the Imperial Police of British India.

Recruitment and training

Most IPS officers are recruited through the Civil Services Examination conducted by the Union Public Service Commission, with a smaller number promoted from state police services. Probationers train at the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy in Hyderabad before being allotted to state cadres, where they begin as assistant superintendents of police.

Roles

IPS officers head district police as superintendents of police, lead ranges and zones, and serve as directors general of police at the state level. They also staff central agencies such as the Central Bureau of Investigation, the Intelligence Bureau, the Border Security Force, and the Central Reserve Police Force. The senior-most post held by an IPS officer is typically Director General of Police of a state or director of a central agency.

History

The service descends from the Indian (Imperial) Police created under the Police Act of 1861. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, independent India's first home minister, defended the All India Services in the Constituent Assembly as the "steel frame" of the country. The first woman IPS officer, Kiran Bedi, joined in 1972.
