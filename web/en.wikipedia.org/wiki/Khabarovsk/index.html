Khabarovsk (Russian: Хабаровск) is the largest city and administrative centre of Khabarovsk Krai, Russia, located about 30 kilometres (19 mi) from the China–Russia border, at the confluence of the Amur and Ussuri rivers. Founded in 1858 as a military outpost, it is one of the principal cities of the Russian Far East and a major stop on the Trans-Siberian Railway.

History

The city was founded on May 31, 1858 by Captain Yakov Dyachenko as a military post named Khabarovka, after the 17th-century explorer Yerofey Khabarov, whose expeditions had first brought the Amur region to Russian attention. Following the Treaty of Aigun (1858), Russia consolidated its hold on the left bank of the Amur. The settlement received town status in 1880 and was renamed Khabarovsk in 1893. During the Russian Civil War the city was contested by Red, White and Japanese forces. In the Soviet era it grew as an industrial and military centre of the Far East.

Geography and climate

Khabarovsk stands on the right bank of the Amur, which here reaches over a kilometre in width; the Khabarovsk Bridge, carrying the Trans-Siberian Railway and highway traffic, spans the river downstream. The city has a humid continental climate strongly influenced by the East Asian monsoon: winters are very cold and dry, summers warm and humid.

Economy and transport

Industry includes machine building, shipbuilding on the Amur, oil refining and food processing. Khabarovsk Novy Airport offers international connections, and the city is a hub for river traffic on the Amur. Its main street, Muravyov-Amursky Street, is lined with early-20th-century merchant buildings leading to Komsomolskaya Square and the golden-domed Transfiguration Cathedral, one of the tallest churches in Russia.
