Jebel Barkal

Jebel Barkal

Location: Karima, Northern State, Sudan
Height: ~98 m above plain
Heritage: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2003, with Napata region)

Jebel Barkal (Arabic: جبل بركل) is a small, flat-topped sandstone mountain in northern Sudan, on the Nile near modern Karima, sacred in ancient Egyptian and Kushite religion as the dwelling of the god Amun.

History

Pharaoh Thutmose III founded a temple of Amun at its foot around 1450 BCE, identifying the mountain — whose distinctive pinnacle was read as a rearing uraeus or phallic form — with Karnak's primeval mound. The site became the religious capital Napata of the Kingdom of Kush; its kings, including Piye and Taharqa, who ruled Egypt as the 25th Dynasty, greatly expanded the temple complex (B500) and cut rock temples into the cliff.

Archaeology

The ruins include palaces, at least 13 temples and a field of pyramids in the surrounding cemeteries. Excavated by George Reisner in 1916–1920 and later by Timothy Kendall, Jebel Barkal and the Napatan region were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003. See Napata and Kingdom of Kush.
