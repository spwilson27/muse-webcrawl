Retirement home

ResidentsOlder adults, typically 55+ or 65+
Care levelIndependent to assisted living
RelatedNursing home, Assisted living

A retirement home is a residential facility designed for older adults who are generally able to care for themselves, offering private apartments or rooms along with shared services such as meals, housekeeping, social activities and varying degrees of support. Retirement homes form part of a spectrum of senior housing that ranges from independent-living communities for active retirees to assisted living facilities and skilled nursing homes providing round-the-clock medical care.

Types

Independent-living communities serve healthy older adults who want freedom from home maintenance and access to communal amenities. Assisted living facilities add help with activities of daily living — bathing, dressing, medication management — while still offering private accommodation. Continuing care retirement communities (CCRCs) combine several levels on one campus, allowing residents to move from independent living to nursing care as their needs change without leaving the community.

Services and amenities

Typical provision includes dining rooms serving regular meals, cleaning and laundry services, transport, fitness and social programmes, libraries, gardens and on-site staff available for emergencies. Many homes organise outings, clubs and classes intended to combat isolation, a recognised risk factor for declining health in old age.

Costs and funding

Costs vary widely by country and by the level of care. In many systems residents pay monthly fees from pensions, savings or the proceeds of selling a family home, with public subsidies available in some countries for those with limited means. In the United Kingdom, local authorities may fund places in care homes after a needs and means assessment; in the United States, Medicare generally does not pay for long-term custodial care, while Medicaid may assist eligible low-income residents.

History

Institutional care for the elderly has roots in almshouses and charitable hospices. The modern retirement home developed in the twentieth century as rising life expectancy, urbanisation and smaller households made family-based care less universal, and has since become a large sector of the housing and care industries in many countries. Issues debated around retirement homes include affordability, quality and inspection standards, staffing, and residents' autonomy and rights.
