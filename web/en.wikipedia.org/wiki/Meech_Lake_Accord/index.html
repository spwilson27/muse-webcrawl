Meech Lake Accord

The Meech Lake Accord (French: Accord du lac Meech) was a series of proposed amendments to the Constitution of Canada negotiated in 1987 by Prime Minister Brian Mulroney and all ten provincial premiers. It was intended to secure Quebec's formal acceptance of the Constitution Act, 1982, which Quebec had refused to sign.

Terms

The Accord would have recognised Quebec as a "distinct society", increased provincial powers over immigration, required provincial input on federal appointments to the Senate and Supreme Court, limited federal spending power in provincial jurisdictions, and given provinces a veto over certain constitutional changes.

Failure

Because unanimity was required, approval was needed from all provincial legislatures within three years. Opposition grew over the closed-door process, the distinct-society clause and the exclusion of Indigenous peoples. In June 1990 the Accord died when Manitoba — where MLA Elijah Harper famously withheld the unanimous consent needed for a rushed vote — and Newfoundland failed to ratify it. Its failure deepened Quebec separatist sentiment, leading to the 1995 Quebec referendum.
