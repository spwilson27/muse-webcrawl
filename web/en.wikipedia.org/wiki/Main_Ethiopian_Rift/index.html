Main Ethiopian Rift

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Main Ethiopian Rift is a geological rift in Ethiopia, the central sector of the East African Rift system. It runs northeast–southwest between the Afar Depression to the northeast and the Turkana Depression to the southwest, separating the Ethiopian Plateau into northwestern and southeastern highlands.

Geology

Rifting began in the Miocene as the Nubian and Somali plates began to diverge. Present-day extension is on the order of several millimetres per year, concentrated along a narrow axial zone of young faults and volcanic centres known as the Wonji Fault Belt. The rift floor is dotted with Quaternary silicic volcanoes — including Aluto, Corbetti and Shala — and is bounded by large border faults with escarpments rising more than a kilometre above the floor.

Lakes and significance

The rift contains the Ethiopian Rift Valley lakes, including Ziway, Langano, Abijatta, Shala, Awasa, Abaya and Chamo, several of which are endorheic. The region is of major palaeoanthropological importance: its sediments and volcanic tuffs preserve hominin fossils and datable ash layers that anchor the East African fossil record, and the rift's geothermal resources are exploited for electricity at Aluto-Langano.
