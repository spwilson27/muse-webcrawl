1975 Banqiao Dam failure

Date8 August 1975
LocationZhumadian, Henan, China
CauseExtreme rainfall from Typhoon Nina; design and construction deficiencies
Deaths~26,000 (flood) + up to ~145,000–230,000 (subsequent famine and epidemics)
Damage62 dams failed; ~11 million people affected; millions of homes destroyed

The 1975 Banqiao Dam failure was the collapse of the Banqiao Dam and dozens of other dams in Henan province, China, in August 1975, under the impact of Typhoon Nina. Around 62 dams failed in a chain reaction, releasing a wall of water that inundated vast areas of the plains around Zhumadian. It is regarded as the deadliest dam failure and one of the deadliest technological disasters in history, though the Chinese government kept details secret for many years.

Background

The Banqiao Dam on the Ru River was built in the early 1950s, with Soviet assistance, as part of a programme to control the Huai River after its catastrophic floods. Hydrologist Chen Xing warned that the project's emphasis on water storage over safe discharge, and the proliferation of upstream dams during the Great Leap Forward, created dangerous risks; his recommendations for more sluice gates were overridden, and he was sidelined during political campaigns. Banqiao was designed for a "1-in-1,000-year" flood; it was subsequently raised and reinforced in ways that reduced its discharge capacity.

Collapse

Typhoon Nina stalled over Henan in early August 1975, colliding with cold air to produce rainfall of extraordinary intensity: 1,060 mm (41.7 in) fell in three days near Linzhuang, including 830 mm in six hours — more than a typical year's rain in a day. Communications failed (a key telegraph line was down), requests to open Banqiao's sluices came too late, and evacuation orders could not be transmitted. In the early hours of 8 August the Shimantan Dam failed first; Banqiao, its reservoir far beyond capacity, collapsed at about 1 a.m. A surge some 10 m high and up to 11 km wide raced down the valley at nearly 50 km/h. Daowenpeng and other downstream communities were obliterated; whole counties lay under metres of water for up to two weeks, trapping survivors on rooftops and dikes without food.

Death toll and aftermath

Official declassified figures state approximately 26,000 people drowned in the flood itself and a further 145,000 died in the ensuing epidemics and famine, for a total of about 171,000; other estimates run as high as 230,000. Some 11 million people were affected and nearly 6 million buildings destroyed. The People's Liberation Army conducted major relief and rescue operations, and some dams were deliberately breached by bombing to drain the floodwater towards less populated areas.

The disaster was concealed in China for over a decade; it began to be openly discussed in the 1990s, including in the book The Great Floods in Chinese History and a 2005 Chinese television ranking of catastrophic dam failures. The Banqiao Dam was rebuilt in the 1990s to modern standards.
