Iron Pagoda

The Iron Pagoda (Chinese: 鐵塔, pinyin: Tiě Tǎ) is a Buddhist pagoda in Kaifeng, Henan Province, China, built in 1049 during the Song dynasty. Despite its name it is not made of iron: it is constructed of glazed brown-purple bricks whose dark, metallic sheen gave it the popular name "Iron Pagoda". The pagoda belongs to the Youguo Temple (佑國寺) complex.

History

An earlier wooden pagoda on the site, built in the 980s to house relics, burned down after a lightning strike in 1044. The architect Yu Hao had designed the original. Emperor Renzong ordered its reconstruction in brick in 1041, and the new pagoda was completed in 1049. It has since withstood earthquakes, Yellow River floods that buried the surrounding temple, and wartime bombing.

Architecture

The pagoda stands 56.88 metres (about 186 feet) tall with thirteen storeys. Its octagonal plan tapers as it rises, and each storey is faced with glazed tiles in dozens of moulded patterns — figures of the Buddha, lions, dragons, musicians, and floral designs. Inside, a spiral brick staircase of 168 steps climbs to the top, with windows providing light and ventilation. Because silt from repeated Yellow River floods buried the temple grounds, visitors today enter at what was effectively a higher level of the original site.
