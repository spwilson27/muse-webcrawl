Thomas Posey (July 9, 1750 – March 19, 1818) was an American soldier and politician who served as the second governor of Indiana Territory from 1813 to 1816, and earlier as a United States senator from Louisiana and lieutenant governor of Kentucky. A veteran of the American Revolutionary War, he rose to the rank of brigadier general.

Early life and military career

Posey was born in Fairfax County, Virginia, near the home of George Washington, under whose patronage he received his early education. He served in the French and Indian War era militia and, during the Revolution, fought at the Battle of Saratoga and served under General Anthony Wayne. After the war he settled in Kentucky, where he became a prominent militia leader and state senator.

Political career

Posey served as lieutenant governor of Kentucky from 1806 to 1808. He later moved to Louisiana, where he was appointed to the United States Senate in 1812 to fill a vacancy. In 1813 President James Madison appointed him governor of Indiana Territory, succeeding William Henry Harrison. Posey's administration saw the territory's transition toward statehood, and he supported the move of the capital to Corydon.

Death and legacy

Posey died in Shawneetown, Illinois, in 1818. Posey County, Indiana, and Posey Township in several states are named in his honor.
