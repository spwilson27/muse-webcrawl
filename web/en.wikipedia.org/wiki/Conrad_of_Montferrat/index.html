Conrad of Montferrat

Conrad of Montferrat (Italian: Corrado del Monferrato; c. 1146 – 28 April 1192) was a north Italian nobleman of the Aleramici family, Marquis of Montferrat, who became a leading figure of the Kingdom of Jerusalem during the Third Crusade. Renowned for his successful defence of Tyre against Saladin in 1187–88, he married the heiress Isabella and was elected king of Jerusalem in 1192, but was assassinated by members of the Order of Assassins (Hashshashin) days before his coronation.

Early career

Conrad was a son of William V of Montferrat and brother of William Longsword, whose marriage to Sibylla of Jerusalem had first tied the family to the crusader kingdom. Conrad spent much of his early career in the orbit of the Byzantine Empire: he served the emperor Manuel I Komnenos, married Manuel's niece Theodora, helped suppress a revolt at Constantinople, and after a falling-out with the regime of Isaac II Angelos killed a leading courtier and fled to the Levant in 1187, arriving at Tyre almost by accident when his ship was diverted there.

Defence of Tyre and the Third Crusade

Arriving shortly after Saladin's destruction of the kingdom's army at Hattin, Conrad took command of Tyre — nearly the only major port still in Christian hands — strengthened its defences, and repelled Saladin's sieges, famously refusing to surrender even when Saladin paraded his captured father, William V, before the walls. Tyre became the bridgehead that enabled the Third Crusade. Conrad quarrelled bitterly with King Guy of Lusignan over the leadership of the kingdom; backed by Philip II of France, while Guy was backed by Richard I of England. Conrad's marriage to Isabella, Sibylla's half-sister and heir, after the controversial annulment of her first marriage, gave him a claim to the throne. In April 1192 the barons of the kingdom unanimously elected Conrad king.

Assassination

On 28 April 1192, days after his election and before his coronation, Conrad was stabbed to death in Tyre by two Assassins who had insinuated themselves into his household. Responsibility for the murder has been debated ever since: contemporary and later writers variously blamed Saladin, Richard I, Humphrey of Toron, or the Assassin master Rashid al-Din Sinan acting on his own motives. Isabella quickly remarried Henry of Champagne, who succeeded as ruler of the kingdom. Conrad's posthumous daughter, Maria of Montferrat, later became queen of Jerusalem.
