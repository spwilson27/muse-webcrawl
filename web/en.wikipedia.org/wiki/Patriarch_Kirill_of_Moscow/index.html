Kirill, Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus'.

Kirill (Russian: Кирилл, secular name Vladimir Mikhailovich Gundyayev, born 20 November 1946) is a Russian Orthodox bishop who became Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus' — primate of the Russian Orthodox Church — on 1 February 2009, succeeding Alexy II.

Early life and career

Born in Leningrad into a family of clergy, Kirill was tonsured a monk in 1969, ordained, and studied at the Leningrad Theological Academy. From 1974 to 1984 he served as rector of that academy, and from 1976 as bishop. He later became Archbishop, then Metropolitan, of Smolensk and Kaliningrad, and from 1989 chaired the Moscow Patriarchate's Department for External Church Relations, making him the church's chief diplomat for two decades.

Patriarchate

Kirill was elected patriarch by a Local Council of the church on 27 January 2009 and enthroned days later. His tenure has emphasised the church's public role in Russian society, close relations with the Russian state, and the concept of the "Russian world" (Russkiy mir). His support for Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine — which he framed in spiritual and civilisational terms — drew condemnation from other churches, deepened the break with the Ecumenical Patriarchate over Ukrainian church independence, and led to sanctions against him by several Western governments.
