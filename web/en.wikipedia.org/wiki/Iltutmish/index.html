Shams ud-Din Iltutmish (died 30 April 1236) was the third ruler of the Delhi Sultanate and the first to rule from Delhi itself. A former slave (mamluk) of Turkic origin purchased by Qutb ud-Din Aibak, whose daughter he married, Iltutmish is generally regarded as the effective founder of the Sultanate, consolidating Turkish rule in northern India.

Reign

After Aibak's death in 1210, Iltutmish displaced the ineffective Aram Shah and spent two decades subduing rival claimants and rebellious governors. He defeated Taj al-Din Yildiz of Ghazni and Nasir ad-Din Qabacha of Sindh, conquered Bengal and Bihar, and recovered Ranthambore and Gwalior from Rajput rulers. In 1221 he prudently avoided battle with the Mongols of Genghis Khan by refusing asylum to the Khwarazmian prince Jalal al-Din.

Administration and legacy

Iltutmish organised the iqta system of land assignments, introduced a standard silver coinage (the tanka) and copper jital, and obtained a letter of investiture from the Abbasid caliph in Baghdad in 1229, legitimising his rule. He completed the Qutb Minar begun by Aibak and built his own tomb in the Qutb complex. Judging his sons unfit, he nominated his daughter Razia Sultana as his successor — a choice the nobles initially overturned before Razia took the throne in 1236.
