The 2013 Daulat Beg Oldi incident was a three-week military standoff between India and China in the spring of 2013, when a platoon of China's People's Liberation Army pitched tents about 19 km inside territory India regards as its own, near Daulat Beg Oldi in the Depsang Bulge area of Ladakh, close to the Line of Actual Control (LAC).

Standoff

On the night of 15 April 2013, some 50 PLA soldiers established a camp of tents at an altitude of about 16,300 feet in the Raki Nala area. Indian troops of the Indo-Tibetan Border Police set up a rival camp a few hundred metres away. India accused China of the deepest incursion in years; China denied crossing the LAC, saying its troops were on the Chinese side of a border whose alignment the two countries perceive differently. Daulat Beg Oldi hosts one of the world's highest airstrips, reactivated by India in 2008 near the strategic Karakoram Pass.

Resolution

After several flag meetings and intense diplomacy, the two sides reached an agreement on 5 May 2013 — just before a planned visit to India by Chinese Premier Li Keqiang — under which both withdrew to their positions of before 15 April. India reportedly agreed to dismantle some structures at Chumar further south as part of the face-saving settlement.

Significance

The incident was the most serious LAC crisis in decades at the time and foreshadowed later, larger confrontations at Doklam (2017) and in the Galwan Valley (2020).
