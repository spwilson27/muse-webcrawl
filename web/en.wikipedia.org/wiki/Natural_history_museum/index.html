Natural history museum

A natural history museum or museum of natural history is a scientific institution with collections of animals, plants, fungi, ecosystems, geology, palaeontology and related subjects, maintained for exhibition, education and research. Such museums are typically supported by governments or universities, and their collections underpin work in taxonomy, evolution and conservation biology.

History

The origins of natural history museums lie in the cabinets of curiosities of Renaissance collectors and in early scientific societies. The Ashmolean Museum at Oxford (opened 1683) and the British Museum in London (1759), whose natural history departments later became the Natural History Museum, London, were among the first public institutions of the kind. The nineteenth century saw a great expansion, driven by exploring expeditions and the rise of evolutionary science: major foundations include the Museum für Naturkunde in Berlin, the Muséum national d'histoire naturelle in Paris, the American Museum of Natural History in New York (1869) and the Smithsonian's National Museum of Natural History in Washington, D.C. (1910).

Collections and research

Natural history collections contain billions of specimens worldwide: pinned insects, herbarium sheets, skins, skeletons, fossils, minerals and, increasingly, frozen tissue and DNA samples. Only a small fraction is on display; the remainder forms a scientific archive used to document biodiversity, track environmental change and describe new species — hundreds of new species are still described each year from museum specimens collected long ago. Type specimens, the reference examples on which species names are based, are among the most scientifically valuable holdings.

Exhibitions

Public galleries typically feature dinosaur skeletons and other fossils, animal dioramas, gems and minerals, and exhibits on evolution, human origins and the environment. Iconic exhibits include the blue whale model in New York and "Dippy" the Diplodocus, long displayed in London.
