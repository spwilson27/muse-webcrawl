Pediment

A pediment is a triangular gable found above the horizontal structure of a classical building, typically resting above a portico of columns. It is considered one of the most characteristic elements of classical architecture, originating in ancient Greek temples such as the Parthenon, whose pediments were filled with sculpture.

Forms

The triangular field within the pediment is called the tympanum. Variations developed over time include the broken pediment, in which the raking cornice is interrupted at the apex; the swan's-neck pediment, with S-shaped curves; and the segmental or arched pediment, whose top follows a curve rather than straight raking lines.

History

Used extensively in Greek and Roman temples, the pediment was revived in Renaissance architecture by architects such as Andrea Palladio and became a staple of Neoclassical and Classical Revival buildings — courthouses, museums, banks, and country houses — throughout Europe and the Americas.
