Human rights in Ethiopia

Human rights in Ethiopia
Legal basis1995 FDRE Constitution (Chapter Three, fundamental rights and freedoms)
Key bodyEthiopian Human Rights Commission (re-established 2019)
OversightUN Human Rights Council; African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights
Press freedomRated constrained; journalists periodically detained

Human rights in Ethiopia are guaranteed on paper by the 1995 constitution of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, but their enjoyment has been persistently limited by successive governments, ethnic federalism tensions, and armed conflict. International organizations including Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, and the United States Department of State have documented abuses across the imperial, Derg, EPRDF, and post-2018 periods.

Historical background

Under Haile Selassie, political dissent was suppressed; under the Marxist Derg (1974–1991), the "Red Terror" campaign killed tens of thousands. The EPRDF government (1991–2018) was credited with development gains but criticized for the 2009 anti-terrorism and civil-society laws used against journalists and opposition figures, and for lethal crackdowns on protests in Oromia and Amhara in 2015–2018.

Reform period and conflict

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's accession in 2018 brought the release of political prisoners, legal reforms, and a re-established Ethiopian Human Rights Commission under Daniel Bekele. However, the Tigray War (2020–2022) produced mass atrocities documented by the EHRC and the UN International Commission of Human Rights Experts on Ethiopia, including massacres, sexual violence, and ethnic cleansing allegations in Western Tigray, committed by multiple parties. Conflicts in Amhara (from 2023, with the Fano militia) and the long-running Oromia insurgency have been accompanied by drone strikes on civilians, mass arrests, states of emergency, and restrictions on media and humanitarian access.

Issues

Recurring concerns include freedom of expression and the press, arbitrary detention, the rights of ethnic minorities and internally displaced people (numbering in the millions), female genital mutilation and child marriage (illegal but still practised), and the rights of LGBT people, homosexuality remaining criminalized. Ethiopia retains the death penalty, though executions are rare.
