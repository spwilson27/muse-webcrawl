Reichenau Island

Reichenau Island (German: Insel Reichenau) is an island in Lake Constance in southern Germany. It lies almost due west of the city of Konstanz, between the Gnadensee and the Untersee branches of the lake, and is connected to the mainland by a causeway. The island is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2000 as "Monastic Island of Reichenau".

History

The Benedictine abbey of Reichenau was founded in 724 by the itinerant monk Saint Pirmin and became one of the great centres of learning and art of the Carolingian and Ottonian periods. Its scriptorium produced some of the most celebrated illuminated manuscripts of the 10th and 11th centuries, including works of the Reichenau School now held in libraries across Europe.

Churches

The island preserves three notable Romanesque churches: the minster of St. Mary and Mark at Mittelzell, St. George's at Oberzell — famous for its Ottonian wall paintings — and the church of Saints Peter and Paul at Niederzell. Today the island is also known for vegetable growing, vineyards, and fishing.
