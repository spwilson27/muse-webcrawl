Convex optimization

Convex optimization is a subfield of mathematical optimization that studies the problem of minimizing convex functions over convex sets. The convexity property guarantees that any local minimum is also a global minimum, which makes convex problems fundamentally more tractable than general nonlinear optimization problems.

Standard form

A convex optimization problem can be written as minimizing a convex objective function f0(x) subject to convex inequality constraints fi(x) ≤ 0 and affine equality constraints. Important special cases include linear programming, quadratic programming, second-order cone programming, and semidefinite programming, each admitting specialized algorithms.

Methods

Algorithms for convex optimization include gradient descent and its accelerated variants, the ellipsoid method, and interior-point methods, which solve many convex problems to high accuracy in polynomial time. For problems with special structure, first-order methods such as the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) and proximal algorithms are widely used in machine learning and signal processing.

Applications

Convex optimization underlies portfolio optimization, control systems, signal and image processing, circuit design, statistics (for example, the lasso and support vector machines), and many machine learning formulations. Modeling systems and disciplined convex programming tools allow practitioners to specify problems in natural mathematical form and have them automatically transformed for solvers.
