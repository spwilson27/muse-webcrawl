Thread (network protocol)

Thread is an IPv6-based, low-power wireless mesh networking technology designed for Internet of Things (IoT) products, particularly in home automation. It operates over the IEEE 802.15.4 radio standard in the 2.4 GHz band and was developed by the Thread Group, founded in 2014 by companies including Nest Labs (Google), Samsung and ARM.

Characteristics

Thread builds a self-healing mesh in which devices can route messages for one another, extending range and reliability while battery-powered "sleepy" end devices conserve energy. The protocol uses 6LoWPAN to carry IPv6 over low-power links and secures communications with AES encryption and network-wide keys. Unlike protocols tied to a single vendor hub, Thread networks can include multiple border routers connecting the mesh to a home's Wi-Fi or Ethernet network.

Relation to Matter

Thread is one of the two principal wireless transports (with Wi-Fi) for Matter, the smart-home interoperability standard launched by the Connectivity Standards Alliance in 2022. Major platform devices — including smart speakers and hubs from Apple, Google, Amazon and Samsung — ship with Thread border router functionality.

History

The Thread Group released the specification to members in 2015 and opened certification thereafter. OpenThread, Google's open-source implementation released in 2016, accelerated adoption across chip vendors.
