Turn-based strategy

Turn-based strategy (TBS) is a genre of strategy game in which players take turns making decisions and issuing commands, as opposed to real-time strategy games in which all players act simultaneously. The format descends from board and tabletop wargames such as chess and Risk.

Characteristics

In a turn-based strategy game, play is divided into discrete turns. During a turn, a player moves units, builds structures, conducts research or diplomacy, and plans attacks; the game state then advances, often through an end-turn phase in which opponents' actions and game mechanics are resolved. The absence of time pressure allows for deliberation, long-term planning and complex systems.

Subgenres and examples

The 4X subgenre (explore, expand, exploit, exterminate) is exemplified by the Civilization series, Master of Orion and Endless Legend. Turn-based tactics games such as XCOM, Fire Emblem and Advance Wars focus on small squads and combat manoeuvres. Grand strategy titles such as Crusader Kings use pausable or hybrid systems, while purely turn-based examples include Heroes of Might and Magic and Total War's campaign layer.

History

Computer TBS games grew out of mainframe wargames of the 1970s. The genre achieved mass popularity in the 1990s with Civilization (1991), Master of Orion (1993) and Heroes of Might and Magic. Although real-time strategy dominated the late 1990s, turn-based games retained a dedicated audience and experienced renewed success in the 2010s with revivals such as XCOM: Enemy Unknown and Civilization VI.
