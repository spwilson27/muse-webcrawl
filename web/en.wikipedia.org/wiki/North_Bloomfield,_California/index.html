Overview

North Bloomfield is an unincorporated community in Nevada County, California, United States. It began as a hydraulic mining town during the California Gold Rush and is now best known as the centre of Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park, which preserves the site of one of the largest hydraulic mines in California.

History

The town was founded in the 1850s under the name Humbug, after nearby Humbug Creek, and was renamed North Bloomfield in 1857. Hydraulic mining — blasting away hillsides with high-pressure water cannons called monitors — washed enormous quantities of gravel through sluices to recover gold, but also sent vast amounts of debris into downstream rivers.

In 1884, Judge Lorenzo Sawyer's landmark decision in Woodruff v. North Bloomfield Gravel Mining Company effectively ended large-scale hydraulic mining in California by prohibiting the discharge of mining debris into waterways.

Today

Much of the townsite lies within Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park, established in 1965. Surviving and restored buildings include a schoolhouse, church, and general store, and the eroded cliffs of the diggings remain a striking landscape feature.
