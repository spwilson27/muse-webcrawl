Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Inc. (ΔΣΘ) is an African American Greek-lettered sorority founded on January 13, 1913, by 22 women at Howard University in Washington, D.C. It is the largest African American Greek-lettered organization and a member of the National Pan-Hellenic Council, commonly known as the Divine Nine.

History

The sorority's 22 founders were former members of Alpha Kappa Alpha who sought to reorganize their chapter into an organization placing greater emphasis on social and political action. In March 1913, members of Delta Sigma Theta participated in the Women's Suffrage March in Washington, D.C., the organization's first public act.

Programs

Delta Sigma Theta's service programs are organized around its Five-Point Programmatic Thrust: economic development, educational development, international awareness and involvement, physical and mental health, and political awareness and involvement. Signature initiatives include Delta Academy and Delta GEMS, which mentor adolescent girls, and financial literacy and health programs.

Membership

The sorority has initiated more than 350,000 members worldwide, organized into over 1,000 collegiate and alumnae chapters in the United States, Canada, England, Japan, Germany, the Virgin Islands, Bermuda, Jamaica, the Republic of Korea, and West Africa. Notable members have included civil rights activist Dorothy Height, who served as national president from 1947 to 1956, congresswoman Shirley Chisholm, and actress Cicely Tyson.

Symbols

The sorority's official colors are crimson and cream. Its symbol is Fortitude, a statue on the Howard University campus, and the elephant is an unofficial symbol associated with strength and determination. The African violet is its official flower.
