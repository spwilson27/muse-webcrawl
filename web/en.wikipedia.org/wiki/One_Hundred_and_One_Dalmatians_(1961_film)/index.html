One Hundred and One Dalmatians (1961 film)

One Hundred and One Dalmatians is a 1961 American animated adventure comedy-drama film produced by Walt Disney Productions and based on Dodie Smith's 1956 children's novel The Hundred and One Dalmatians. The 17th Disney animated feature film, it tells the story of a litter of Dalmatian puppies who are kidnapped by the villainous Cruella de Vil, who wants to use their fur to make coats, and of their parents' quest to rescue them, aided by animals across England via the "Twilight Bark."

Plot

Songwriter Roger Radcliffe and his Dalmatian Pongo meet Anita and her Dalmatian Perdita; the couples marry and the dogs produce fifteen puppies. Anita's old schoolmate Cruella de Vil offers to buy the litter and, when refused, hires Jasper and Horace to steal them, gathering 99 puppies in total at her decaying mansion. With help from the Old English Sheepdog Colonel, Sergeant Tibbs the cat, and other animals, Pongo and Perdita rescue all the puppies, who roll in soot to disguise themselves as Labradors, and the family adopts the whole group — 101 Dalmatians.

Production and legacy

The film pioneered the use of Xerox photocopying in animation, transferring animators' drawings directly to cels and giving the film its distinctive scratchy line style; the technique saved the studio's animation department, which had been threatened with closure after the costly Sleeping Beauty. Released on January 25, 1961, it was a box office success, and Cruella de Vil — voiced by Betty Lou Gerson — is consistently ranked among the greatest animated villains. It spawned a 1996 live-action remake, a 2003 animated sequel, and the 2021 prequel Cruella.
