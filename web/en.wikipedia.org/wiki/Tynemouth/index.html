Tynemouth

Tynemouth is a coastal town in North Tyneside, Tyne and Wear, England, at the mouth of the River Tyne on the North Sea, about 13 km (8 mi) east of Newcastle upon Tyne. It is known for its beaches, priory and castle.

History

The headland was settled in the Iron Age and guarded the Tyne's entrance under the Romans. Tynemouth Priory, founded in the Anglo-Saxon period and refounded as a Benedictine house after the Norman Conquest, became one of the richest monasteries of northern England; its fortified ruins, together with Tynemouth Castle, still dominate the headland. From the 18th century the town developed as a fashionable bathing resort, and Longsands beach remains a popular surfing spot.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the priory and castle (English Heritage), the Tynemouth Volunteer Life Brigade watch house and museum, the Grand Hotel, and the weekend market held in the restored Tynemouth Metro station.
