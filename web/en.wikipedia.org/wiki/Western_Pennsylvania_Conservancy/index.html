The Western Pennsylvania Conservancy (WPC) is a private, nonprofit conservation organization based in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1932, it works to protect and restore natural areas in western Pennsylvania and to enhance the region's quality of life through land and water conservation, community greening and heritage preservation.

Work

The conservancy has protected more than 250,000 acres of natural landscapes through land acquisition and conservation easements, helping to establish or expand ten state parks in the region. Its scientists also operate a watershed conservation program and the Pennsylvania Natural Heritage Program, which inventories threatened species and ecosystems.

The WPC is best known as the steward of Fallingwater, the famous house designed by Frank Lloyd Wright in 1935 for the Kaufmann family at Bear Run in Fayette County. Edgar J. Kaufmann Jr. entrusted the house and its land to the conservancy in 1963, and Fallingwater opened as a museum in 1964. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019 as part of "The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright".
