Month

A month is a unit of time, used with calendars, that is approximately as long as a natural orbital period of the Moon; the words month and Moon are cognates. The traditional concept of the month arose with the cycle of Moon phases; such lunar months ("lunations") are synodic months, lasting about 29.53 days.

Types of month in astronomy

  • Synodic month — the time between successive new moons: about 29.53 days. This is the basis of most lunar and lunisolar calendars.
  • Sidereal month — the Moon's orbital period relative to the fixed stars: about 27.32 days.
  • Tropical month — relative to the vernal equinox: about 27.32 days.
  • Anomalistic month — perigee to perigee: about 27.55 days.
  • Draconic month — node to node: about 27.21 days, governing eclipse cycles.

The Gregorian calendar is a solar calendar whose twelve months no longer track the Moon's phases. Other calendars — such as the Islamic calendar — are purely lunar, with twelve months of 29 or 30 days making a year of about 354 days, while lunisolar calendars such as the Hebrew and Chinese calendars insert intercalary months to stay aligned with the solar year.
