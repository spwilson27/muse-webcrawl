Michele Karen Dougherty (born 1962) is a South African-born British space physicist, professor of space physics at Imperial College London. She is best known as principal investigator of the magnetometer instruments carried aboard the NASA–ESA Cassini–Huygens mission to Saturn and the European Space Agency's JUICE mission to Jupiter's icy moons.

Career and discoveries

Dougherty was inspired by astronomy through her father's telescope and studied at the University of Natal, taking her PhD on magnetised plasmas. At Imperial, her Cassini magnetometer team made the celebrated discovery that Saturn's moon Enceladus has a plume of water vapour venting from its south pole — evidence of a subsurface ocean. Her group also studies the magnetospheres of Jupiter's moons, particularly Ganymede, the only moon known to generate its own internal magnetic field, a focus of the JUICE mission launched in 2023.

Astronomer Royal

In 2025 Dougherty was appointed the sixteenth Astronomer Royal, succeeding Martin Rees and becoming the first woman to hold the 350-year-old post, created in 1675. Previous holders include Martin Ryle and Arnold Wolfendale. She also took up the role of Executive Chair of the Science and Technology Facilities Council. She was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2012 and appointed DBE for services to physical sciences.
