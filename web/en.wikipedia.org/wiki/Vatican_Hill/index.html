Vatican Hill

Vatican Hill (Latin: Mons Vaticanus; Italian: Colle Vaticano) is a hill in Rome, Italy, lying on the west bank of the Tiber River, opposite the traditional seven hills of Rome. It gives its name to Vatican City, the sovereign city-state of the Holy See, most of which lies upon and around the hill.

Etymology

The name "Vatican" predates Christianity. It may derive from the Etruscan language or from the Latin vates, meaning soothsayer or prophet, since the area was associated in antiquity with a settlement of fortune-tellers. In Roman times the Ager Vaticanus was a low-lying, marshy district considered unhealthy, used for vineyards and for the circuses of Caligula and Nero.

History

According to Christian tradition, Saint Peter was martyred and buried on Vatican Hill under the emperor Nero, beneath or near the present site of St Peter's Basilica. The emperor Constantine the Great built the first basilica over the apostle's tomb in the 4th century. In the Middle Ages the popes built their palace beside the basilica, and after the return from Avignon the Vatican became the principal papal residence.

The modern St Peter's Basilica, built in the 16th and 17th centuries, crowns the hill; the Vatican Museums, the Vatican Gardens, and the Apostolic Palace occupy its slopes. The hill's highest point is about 75 metres above sea level.

Geography

Vatican Hill is composed largely of Pliocene marine clays and sands. It is one of the hills of Rome but is not counted among the canonical seven hills of the ancient city, which lay on the east bank of the Tiber.
