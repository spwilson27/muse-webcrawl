Apollo of Veii
Datec. 510–500 BC
MediumPainted terracotta
Height1.81 m
LocationMuseo Nazionale Etrusco, Villa Giulia, Rome

The Apollo of Veii is a life-size painted terracotta statue of the god Apollo from the late 6th century BC, a masterpiece of Etruscan art. It was made for the rooftop of the Temple of Minerva at Portonaccio in the Etruscan city of Veii (now Veio, near Rome), where it formed part of a group depicting Apollo confronting Heracles over the Ceryneian Hind.

Discovery and attribution

The statue was excavated in fragments in 1916 and reconstructed. It is attributed to the workshop of Vulca of Veii, the only Etruscan artist named in Roman sources, whom Pliny the Elder records as having been summoned to Rome to make the cult statue of Jupiter Capitolinus.

Description

Apollo strides forward vigorously, his archaic smile and finely modelled drapery still bearing traces of paint. Together with the companion statues of Heracles, Leto and Hermes, the group is the finest surviving example of the Etruscan tradition of large-scale terracotta roof sculpture.
