Quit India Movement

The Quit India Movement was a mass civil disobedience movement launched by Mohandas Gandhi and the Indian National Congress in August 1942, demanding an immediate end to British rule in India. The Congress resolution of 8 August 1942 called on the British to "Quit India", and Gandhi gave the call of "Do or Die" in his speech at Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay.

Course

Within hours, the British arrested Gandhi and the entire Congress leadership. Leaderless protests spread across India: strikes, attacks on government buildings, railways, and telegraph lines, and in some districts, such as Ballia, Midnapore, and Satara, parallel governments were briefly established. The colonial government responded with mass arrests — over 100,000 people — collective fines, and lethal force; official figures record over a thousand killed.

Outcome

The movement was suppressed by 1943–44, and the Muslim League, which opposed it, expanded its influence in the interim. Historians debate its direct effect, but Quit India demonstrated that British rule could no longer continue without Indian consent and set the stage for independence in 1947. The failure of the Cripps Mission earlier in 1942 had convinced Congress that wartime promises of post-war self-government were insufficient.
