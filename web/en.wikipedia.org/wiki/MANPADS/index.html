Man-portable air-defense systems (MANPADS or MPADS) are portable surface-to-air missiles. They are guided weapons and are a threat to low-flying aircraft, especially helicopters. They were developed in the 1950s to provide military ground forces with protection from jet aircraft. They have received a great deal of attention, partly because armed groups have used them against commercial airliners. These missiles, affordable and widely available through a variety of sources, have been used successfully over the past three decades both in military conflicts and by terrorist organizations.

Development and generations

The first systems, introduced in the 1960s, included the American FIM-43 Redeye and the Soviet 9K32 Strela-2 (SA-7 Grail). These first-generation weapons used simple uncooled infrared seekers that could only engage targets from behind, tracking the hot exhaust of a jet engine. Second-generation systems such as the American FIM-92 Stinger and Soviet 9K34 Strela-3 used cooled seekers with improved sensitivity and some all-aspect capability. Third-generation systems, including the French Mistral, Soviet 9K38 Igla and later Stinger variants, added multi-element seekers and flare counter-countermeasures. Fourth-generation systems, such as the Russian 9K333 Verba, use multispectral seekers operating in several infrared and ultraviolet bands to resist decoys.

Operation

A typical MANPADS consists of a launch tube with gripstock, a battery coolant unit that powers and cools the seeker, and the missile itself. The operator acquires the target visually, uncages the seeker so it locks onto the target's heat signature, and fires; the missile is typically a "fire-and-forget" weapon. Most systems are effective against targets at altitudes below roughly 3,800 metres and ranges of a few kilometres.

Proliferation concerns

Because a complete system weighs as little as 13–18 kg and can be carried by one person, MANPADS are difficult to control once transferred. Thousands are believed to be outside state control. Their use against civilian aircraft — including failed attacks on an Israeli charter jet at Mombasa in 2002 and the shootdown of cargo and passenger aircraft in conflict zones — has driven international export-control efforts, including agreements within the Wassenaar Arrangement, and programs to buy back or destroy surplus stocks.

Countermeasures

Aircraft defend against MANPADS with flare dispensers that eject hot decoys, directed infrared countermeasure (DIRCM) systems that jam the seeker with modulated laser energy, missile approach warning systems, and operational measures such as steep climb-out profiles over threatened areas.
