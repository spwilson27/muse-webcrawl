"Rock Around the Clock" is a rock and roll song written by Max C. Freedman and James E. Myers (the latter under the pseudonym "Jimmy De Knight") in 1952. The best-known and most successful rendition was recorded by Bill Haley & His Comets in 1954. When the song was used in the film Blackboard Jungle (1955), it became a number-one hit and is widely credited with bringing rock and roll into mainstream popular culture.

Background

The song was first recorded by the Italian-American novelty act Sonny Dae and His Knights in 1952, but it was Haley's version — recorded for Decca Records on 12 April 1954 after earlier attempts at other labels — that succeeded. Released initially as a B-side to "Thirteen Women", it sold modestly at first. Its prominent placement under the opening credits of Blackboard Jungle, a film about juvenile delinquency, transformed it: re-released in 1955, it topped the American charts for eight weeks and reached number one in Britain as well.

The recording

The record features Haley's vocal and rhythm guitar, Danny Cedrone's celebrated two-part guitar solo (borrowed from his earlier work on "Rock the Joint"), Franny Beecher's guitar, Marshall Lytle's slapped bass and Rudy Pompilli's saxophone. The count-in lyrics — "One, two, three o'clock, four o'clock rock" — became one of rock's most recognisable openings.

Impact and legacy

"Rock Around the Clock" is often described as the song that launched the rock and roll era and youth culture's musical identity; it provoked excitement (and occasional riots) at screenings and concerts. It has sold an estimated 25 million copies in various editions and re-charted repeatedly, including in 1974 when it featured in the film American Graffiti and the sitcom Happy Days. The recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry.
