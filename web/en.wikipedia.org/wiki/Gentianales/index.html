Gentianales

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

KingdomPlantae
CladeAngiosperms; Eudicots; Asterids
OrderGentianales Juss. ex Bercht. & J.Presl
FamiliesApocynaceae; Gelsemiaceae; Gentianaceae; Loganiaceae; Rubiaceae

Gentianales is an order of flowering plants in the asterid clade of the eudicots. It comprises more than 20,000 species in about 1,100 genera distributed among five families. The order contains some of the most economically important plants in the world, most notably the coffee family, Rubiaceae, one of the largest families of flowering plants.

Families

Under the APG IV system, the order includes the following families:

  • Apocynaceae — the dogbane family, including milkweeds, oleander, and periwinkles; often rich in alkaloids and cardiac glycosides.
  • Gelsemiaceae — a small family that includes the poisonous yellow jessamine.
  • Gentianaceae — the gentian family, mostly herbs with showy, often blue, flowers.
  • Loganiaceae — a family including Strychnos, the source of strychnine.
  • Rubiaceae — the coffee or madder family, with some 13,000 species including coffee, quinine-producing Cinchona, and gardenia.

Characteristics

Members of the order typically have opposite leaves, often with interpetiolar stipules (notably in Rubiaceae), and flowers with a contorted corolla aestivation. Many members produce secondary compounds, especially indole alkaloids, which underlie both their toxicity and their medicinal uses; quinine, vincristine, and reserpine are famous examples.
