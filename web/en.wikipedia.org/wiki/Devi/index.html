Devi

Devī (Sanskrit: देवी, "goddess") is the Sanskrit word for goddess in Hinduism. Devi is the divine feminine, worshipped in many forms and central to the tradition of Shaktism, in which she is regarded as the supreme being — the dynamic energy (shakti) underlying the universe. In other Hindu traditions she is the complementary feminine aspect of the divine, associated with energy, fertility, protection and power.

Forms

Devi manifests in benevolent forms such as Parvati (consort of Shiva), Lakshmi (goddess of wealth, consort of Vishnu) and Saraswati (goddess of learning), and in fierce forms such as Durga, the warrior goddess who slays the buffalo demon Mahishasura, and Kali, the terrifying goddess of time and destruction. The ten Mahavidyas are a group of ten aspects of the Divine Mother prominent in Tantric worship. Local and village goddesses (gramadevata) are worshipped across India, and the Ganges is personified as the goddess Ganga.

Texts and worship

The most important scripture of goddess worship is the Devi Mahatmyam (c. 5th–6th century CE), part of the Markandeya Purana, which celebrates the goddess's victories over demons and is recited during the autumn festival of Navaratri and Durga Puja. The Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Lalita Sahasranamam are also central texts. Temples to the goddess, including the 51 Shakti Pithas marking where parts of the goddess Sati fell to earth, are spread across the Indian subcontinent. See also Hinduism, Shakti and Dalit.
