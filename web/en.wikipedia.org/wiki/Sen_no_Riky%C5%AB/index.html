Sen no Rikyū (千利休; 1522 – 21 April 1591) is considered the historical figure with the most profound influence on chanoyu, the Japanese Way of Tea. He perfected the rustic, austere style of tea known as wabi-cha and served as tea master to Toyotomi Hideyoshi.

Life

Born in the merchant city of Sakai, Rikyū studied tea under Takeno Jōō. He entered the service of Oda Nobunaga and then of Hideyoshi, for whom he designed the golden tea room. His aesthetic favoured rough Raku tea bowls, bare earthen walls and modest tea houses such as the Tai-an at Myōki-an.

Death

In 1591, for reasons still debated, Hideyoshi ordered Rikyū to commit seppuku, which he did at his residence in Kyoto. His legacy continued through the three Sen schools of tea: Omotesenke, Urasenke and Mushakōjisenke.
