Muscle Shoals is a city in Colbert County, Alabama, United States, on the southern bank of the Tennessee River. It is the largest city in the Florence–Muscle Shoals metropolitan area, known locally as "the Shoals". The city takes its name from the dangerous shoals in the river, whose name may derive from the mussels once abundant there or from the effort ("muscle") needed to navigate the rapids.

Music

Despite its small size, Muscle Shoals became one of the most important recording centres in American popular music. In 1959 Rick Hall founded FAME Studios, where Arthur Alexander's "You Better Move On" (1961) launched the "Muscle Shoals sound". FAME's house band and its successors recorded classics with Aretha Franklin ("I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You"), Wilson Pickett, Percy Sledge ("When a Man Loves a Woman"), Otis Redding and Etta James. In 1969 four former FAME session players — Barry Beckett, Roger Hawkins, Jimmy Johnson and David Hood — founded the rival Muscle Shoals Sound Studio in nearby Sheffield, recording the Rolling Stones, Paul Simon, Bob Dylan, Lynyrd Skynyrd and many others. The backing musicians, known as "The Swampers", were immortalised in Lynyrd Skynyrd's "Sweet Home Alabama".

History

The area was long inhabited by Native American peoples, including the Cherokee and Chickasaw. Development accelerated in the 20th century with the construction of Wilson Dam (completed 1924) across the Tennessee River and the Tennessee Valley Authority's nitrate and fertiliser plants, which made the region a centre of the aluminium and chemical industries.
