Comb filter

In signal processing, a comb filter is a filter implemented by adding a delayed version of a signal to itself, causing constructive and destructive interference. Its frequency response consists of a series of regularly spaced notches (or, in the feedback form, peaks), giving the appearance of a comb — hence the name.

Magnitude response of a feedforward comb filter, showing regularly spaced notches.

Forms

The feedforward (FIR) comb filter computes y[n] = x[n] + α·x[n − K], where K is the delay in samples and α controls the depth of the notches. Its notches occur at multiples of fs/K, where fs is the sampling frequency. The feedback (IIR) comb filter computes y[n] = x[n] + α·y[n − K]; its response instead exhibits regularly spaced resonance peaks, and the feedback coefficient must satisfy |α| < 1 for stability.

Applications

Comb filters are ubiquitous in digital signal processing. They are used to separate the luminance and chrominance components of composite video signals in PAL and NTSC television decoders; as the structural element of cascaded integrator-comb (CIC) filters in sample-rate conversion; in audio effects such as flanging, phasing, and physical-modeling synthesis (the Karplus–Strong algorithm uses a feedback comb filter with a low-pass filter); and in radar and communications for suppressing or selecting periodic signal components. Comb filtering also occurs unintentionally in rooms when direct sound mixes with a delayed reflection, colouring the perceived sound.
