Royal Gunpowder Mills

Surviving buildings of the mills at Waltham Abbey

The Royal Gunpowder Mills are a former industrial site at Waltham Abbey in Essex, England, which manufactured gunpowder and later other explosives and propellants for the British state for over 300 years. The site is now a heritage attraction and nature reserve.

History

Powder milling on the River Lea at Waltham Abbey dates from the 1560s, when John Tamworth established mills there using water power. In 1787 the Crown purchased the mills from the Walton family, placing gunpowder production under direct government control. The site expanded greatly during the Napoleonic Wars and the two World Wars, producing gunpowder, guncotton, nitroglycerine, cordite and later plastic explosives and rocket propellants.

Production of explosives ended in 1945; the site then became a research establishment for propellants and explosives, finally closing in 1991. The 175-acre site contains more than 200 listed buildings, a canal network used to move explosives safely by barge, and extensive woodland that had been planted to absorb the shock of accidental explosions.

The site today

The mills are managed as a visitor attraction with exhibitions on explosives manufacture, a narrow-gauge railway and wildlife habitats including fallow deer herds.
