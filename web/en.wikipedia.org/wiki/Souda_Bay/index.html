Souda Bay

Souda Bay (Greek: Κόλπος Σούδας) is a bay and natural harbour near the town of Souda on the northwest coast of Crete, Greece. Some 15 kilometres long, it is one of the deepest natural harbours in the Mediterranean and has been of major strategic importance since antiquity. The bay is formed between the Akrotiri peninsula and Cape Drapano and is overlooked by the ancient site of Aptera.

History

The harbour was used by the Venetians and later the Ottomans, who built the fortress on Souda islet guarding the bay's entrance. During the Second World War Souda Bay was a key Allied anchorage. In March 1941 Italian explosive motor boats of the Decima MAS seriously damaged the British heavy cruiser HMS York in the bay. The area saw heavy fighting during the Battle of Crete in May 1941, and Souda Bay War Cemetery, maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission, lies at the head of the bay.

Naval facilities

Today the bay hosts the Crete Naval Base of the Hellenic Navy and Naval Support Activity Souda Bay, a United States naval facility that is one of the few deep-water ports in the Mediterranean able to accommodate aircraft carriers. The base supports Greek, US and NATO operations in the eastern Mediterranean. The commercial port of Souda provides ferry services to Piraeus for the nearby city of Chania.
