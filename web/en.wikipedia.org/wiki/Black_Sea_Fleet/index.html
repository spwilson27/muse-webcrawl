Black Sea Fleet

The Black Sea Fleet is the fleet of the Russian Navy in the Black Sea, the Sea of Azov and the Mediterranean Sea. It traces its history to a squadron founded by Prince Grigory Potemkin on 13 May 1783, built up around the naval base at Sevastopol in Crimea, which the Russian Empire had annexed from the Ottoman Empire that year.

History

The fleet was created after Russia annexed Crimea in 1783, ending the threat of Ottoman and Tatar raids and giving Russia a warm-water naval presence in the south. Under commanders such as Fyodor Ushakov, it won notable victories over the Ottoman navy during the Russo-Turkish War of 1787–1792, including at Tendra and Kaliakra.

During the Crimean War (1853–1856), Black Sea Fleet sailors under Admiral Pavel Nakhimov won the Battle of Sinop in 1853, destroying an Ottoman squadron, but the fleet was scuttled in Sevastopol harbour during the allied siege to block the entrance, and the Treaty of Paris (1856) temporarily demilitarised the Black Sea.

Soviet era

After the Russian Revolution and Civil War, the fleet was rebuilt by the Soviet Union. In the Second World War it supported the defence of Odessa and Sevastopol, the Kerch landings and operations along the Caucasus coast. After 1945 it became one of the Soviet Navy's four main fleets, operating into the Mediterranean through the Turkish Straits under the Montreux Convention.

After 1991

The dissolution of the USSR led to a long dispute between Russia and Ukraine over the division of the fleet and the status of Sevastopol. A 1997 treaty split the ships and allowed Russia to lease its Sevastopol facilities until 2017, extended by the 2010 Kharkiv Pact to 2042. After Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, Russia took control of Ukrainian naval assets in Crimea. Following the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the fleet lost its flagship, the missile cruiser Moskva, in April 2022, and Ukrainian missile and drone attacks on Sevastopol forced much of the fleet to relocate eastward to Novorossiysk and other ports.

Composition

The fleet has traditionally included frigates, corvettes, diesel-electric submarines of the improved Kilo class, landing ships, minesweepers and a large support and coastal-defence establishment, including naval infantry and coastal missile units.
