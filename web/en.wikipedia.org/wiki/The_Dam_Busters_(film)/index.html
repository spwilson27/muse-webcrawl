The Dam Busters (film)

The Dam Busters is a 1955 British epic war film starring Richard Todd and Michael Redgrave, adapted from Paul Brickhill's 1951 book of the same name and Guy Gibson's memoir Enemy Coast Ahead. The film portrays the true story of Operation Chastise, the Royal Air Force's 1943 attack on German dams using Barnes Wallis's bouncing bombs.

Plot

Engineer Barnes Wallis (Michael Redgrave) struggles against official indifference to develop a bouncing bomb capable of skipping across water to strike the great dams of the Ruhr valley. Meanwhile Wing Commander Guy Gibson (Richard Todd) assembles the specially formed No. 617 Squadron and trains its Lancaster crews in precise low-level flying. On the night of 16–17 May 1943 the squadron attacks the Möhne, Eder and Sorpe dams, breaching the first two at the cost of eight of the nineteen aircraft and 53 aircrew.

Production and reception

The film was made by Associated British Picture Corporation and shot in black and white, using genuine wartime techniques for the bomb experiments and real Lancaster bombers for the flying sequences. It was the most successful British film at the British box office in 1955 and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Special Effects.

Eric Coates's "The Dam Busters March" became famous in its own right and remains strongly associated with the operation. The film's attack sequences are often cited as an influence on the Death Star trench run in Star Wars. Gibson's dog's name, a racial slur, has led to the word being dubbed, edited, or contextualised in modern broadcasts, and has been a point of debate in discussions of a long-planned remake.
