Neutron star

Neutron star
TypeCollapsed core of a massive star
DiameterAbout 20 km
DensityA teaspoon would weigh about a billion tonnes
[Image: A neutron star with beams of radiation sweeping from its magnetic poles]

A neutron star is the collapsed core of a massive star after a supernova, composed almost entirely of neutrons packed to nuclear density. A city-sized neutron star can outweigh the Sun; its matter is the densest known short of a black hole.

Pulsars

Rapidly rotating, magnetised neutron stars emit beams of radiation detected as pulsars — the first, in 1967, was briefly nicknamed LGM-1 ('little green men'). Colliding neutron stars (observed in gravitational waves in 2017) forge heavy elements such as gold and platinum. Their physics extends the story of Antimatter and nuclear matter.
