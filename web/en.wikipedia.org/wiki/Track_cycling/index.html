Track cycling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Track cycling is a bicycle racing sport usually held on specially built banked tracks or velodromes using track bicycles. Track bicycles are optimised for the sport: they have a fixed gear (the rider cannot coast), no brakes, and a stiff, aerodynamic frame. Velodromes are oval circuits, usually 250 m long at Olympic standard (historically 333.33 m or other lengths), with banking of up to about 45 degrees in the bends.

History

Track cycling has been practised since at least the 1870s, when racing on wooden indoor tracks drew large crowds. It has been part of the Olympic Games since the first modern Olympics in 1896, with the exception of 1912, and women's track events were added in 1988. Six-day racing, in which teams of two competed almost continuously over six days, was hugely popular in Europe and North America in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries; the Madison race is named after Madison Square Garden in New York, where the format was devised.

Race formats

Sprint events

The sprint is a short race over 2–3 laps in which riders jockey for position, often nearly stationary in a "track stand", before a final explosive lap. The team sprint involves teams of three (men) or two (women) racing over the same distance. The keirin, originating in Japan as a betting sport, has riders follow a pacing motorbike (derny) that gradually accelerates before pulling off, leaving a sprint finish. The time trial (1 km for men, 500 m for women historically) is a solo race against the clock.

Endurance events

The individual pursuit pits two riders starting on opposite sides of the track over 4 km (3 km for women in earlier formats), aiming to catch the opponent or record the faster time; the team pursuit uses teams of four. The points race awards points for intermediate sprints and laps gained. The Madison is a two-rider relay in which partners hand-sling each other into the race. The scratch race is a straightforward first-across-the-line mass start race, while the elimination race (or "devil take the hindmost") removes the last rider every other lap. The omnium combines several events into a single competition.

Governing body and major events

The sport is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). Major competitions include the Olympic Games, the UCI Track Cycling World Championships (first held in 1893 as the ICA championships), the UCI Track Champions League and the Nations Cup series. Famous velodromes include the Vélodrome de Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines, the Lee Valley VeloPark in London, and the Manchester Velodrome.

Records and notable riders

The hour record on the track has been a prestigious mark since the days of Henri Desgrange. Successful track riders have included Chris Hoy, Jason Kenny and Laura Kenny (née Trott) of Great Britain, Victoria Pendleton, Anna Meares of Australia, and sprint legends such as Harrie Lavreysen of the Netherlands. Track cycling also serves as a talent pathway to professional road racing; riders such as Mark Cavendish and Bradley Wiggins began on the track.
