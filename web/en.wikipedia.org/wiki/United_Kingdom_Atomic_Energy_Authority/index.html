United Kingdom Atomic Energy Authority

The United Kingdom Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA) is a British government research organization responsible for nuclear fusion research and related science and technology. It is an executive non-departmental public body sponsored by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero. UKAEA is headquartered at Culham Campus in Oxfordshire.

History

UKAEA was established in 1954 to take over Britain's atomic energy research and development from the Ministry of Supply. It played a central role in early civil and military nuclear programmes and operated research sites including Harwell, Winfrith, Dounreay, Windscale and Culham. Many of its industrial and production functions were later transferred to other bodies, notably British Nuclear Fuels and the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority.

Fusion research

Today UKAEA's principal mission is to lead the delivery of sustainable fusion energy and maximize the scientific and economic benefit from fusion research. At Culham it operated the European JET tokamak until the end of its scientific operations in 2023 and continues to run the British MAST Upgrade spherical tokamak. Programmes include robotics and remote handling at RACE, materials research, tritium handling, fusion technology facilities and work supporting the planned STEP prototype fusion power plant.

Sites and role

UKAEA manages the Culham Campus as a centre for fusion, robotics and advanced technology businesses, and retains responsibilities connected with some former nuclear sites and pension schemes. It collaborates with universities, industry and international projects including ITER. Its work has shifted decisively from the fission research and production duties of its early decades toward fusion power and associated engineering.
