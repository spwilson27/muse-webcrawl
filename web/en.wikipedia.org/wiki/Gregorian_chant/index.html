Gregorian chant is the central tradition of Western plainchant, a form of monophonic, unaccompanied sacred song in Latin of the Roman Catholic Church. It developed mainly in western and central Europe during the 9th and 10th centuries, from the unification of Roman and Gallican chant under the Carolingians. Tradition attributes its codification to Pope Gregory I (the Great, r. 590–604), often depicted receiving the chant from the Holy Spirit in the form of a dove; modern scholarship treats this as legend and dates the repertoire's standardisation to the Carolingian period.

Characteristics

Gregorian chant is sung in free rhythm, following the natural accentuation of the Latin text rather than a regular metre. Melodies are organised in the system of eight church modes. Textures range from syllabic settings (one note per syllable), through neumatic (small groups of notes), to highly melismatic passages in which a single syllable may carry dozens of notes, as in the great Alleluias. The repertoire is written in the chant books — the Gradual for the Mass and the Antiphoner for the Divine Office — using square notation on a four-line staff, developed from earlier unheighted neumes.

Repertoire

The chant of the Mass divides into the Proper (texts varying by feast: Introit, Gradual, Alleluia, Offertory, Communion) and the Ordinary (fixed texts: Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus, Agnus Dei). Famous items include the Easter sequence Victimae paschali laudes, the funeral chant Dies irae from the Requiem Mass, and the Advent antiphons known as the "O Antiphons".

Later history

Chant was the foundation of Western art music: polyphony grew up around it, and its melodies remained a source for composers for centuries. After a long decline, the 19th-century monks of Solesmes led a restoration of chant performance and scholarship, issuing the editions adopted after the Second Vatican Council, which affirmed that Gregorian chant should be given "pride of place" in the Roman liturgy. A popular revival came in the 1990s, when recordings such as the Benedictine monks of Santo Domingo de Silos' album Chant became international best-sellers.
