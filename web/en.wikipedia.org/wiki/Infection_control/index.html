Infection control

Infection control addresses factors related to the spread of infections within the healthcare setting, whether among patients, from patients to staff, from staff to patients, or among staff. This includes preventive measures such as hand washing, cleaning, disinfecting, sterilizing, and vaccinating. Other aspects include monitoring and managing outbreaks of infection in hospitals.

Principles

Infection control relies on the concept of the chain of infection: an infectious agent, a reservoir, a portal of exit, a mode of transmission, a portal of entry, and a susceptible host. Interventions aim to break one or more links in this chain. Standard precautions treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious.

Hand hygiene

Hand hygiene is widely considered the single most important practice for reducing healthcare-associated infections. Alcohol-based hand rubs and washing with soap and water are promoted by the World Health Organization's "Five Moments for Hand Hygiene".

Isolation precautions

Transmission-based precautions — contact, droplet, and airborne — supplement standard precautions when the route of transmission is not fully interrupted by standard practice alone. Personal protective equipment such as gloves, gowns, masks, and N95 respirators is used according to the anticipated exposure.

Sterilization and disinfection

Medical devices are classified by the Spaulding system as critical, semi-critical, or non-critical, determining whether sterilization, high-level disinfection, or low-level disinfection is required.
