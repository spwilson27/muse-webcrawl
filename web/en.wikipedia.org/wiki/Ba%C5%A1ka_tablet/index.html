Baška tablet

The Baška tablet (Croatian: Bašćanska ploča) is one of the earliest and most important monuments of Croatian literacy. Carved in stone around 1100 CE, it is inscribed in the Glagolitic script and records a donation of land by the Croatian king Zvonimir to a Benedictine abbey. It was found in 1851 in the Church of St. Lucy in Jurandvor near Baška on the island of Krk, Croatia.

Content

The inscription, written in the Croatian recension of Church Slavonic mixed with elements of the vernacular, states that King Zvonimir granted a piece of land to the Abbey of St. Lucy during the time of Abbot Držiha, and names witnesses and officials. It is the earliest document to mention the name of a Croatian ruler in the Croatian language, referring to Zvonimir as "king of the Croats".

Significance

The tablet is considered a foundational document of Croatian culture and statehood, sometimes called the "birth certificate" of the Croatian people. The original is preserved in the Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts in Zagreb, while a replica remains in the Church of St. Lucy. A fragment of its text appears in Croatian passport designs.
