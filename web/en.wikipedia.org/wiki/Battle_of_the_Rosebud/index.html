Battle of the Rosebud

The Battle of the Rosebud, known to the Lakota as the Battle Where the Girl Saved Her Brother, was fought on June 17, 1876, in the Montana Territory during the Great Sioux War of 1876. A United States Army column under Brigadier General George Crook was attacked by Lakota Sioux and Northern Cheyenne warriors led by Crazy Horse. Crook claimed victory but withdrew to his supply base, removing his column from the campaign at the moment the other Army columns were converging — eight days before the Battle of the Little Bighorn.

Background

In the summer of 1876 the U.S. Army launched a three-pronged campaign against Lakota and Cheyenne bands who had refused to move to reservations. Crook's column advanced north from Fort Fetterman in Wyoming with about a thousand soldiers supported by Crow and Shoshone scouts. On the morning of June 17 the column was resting along Rosebud Creek when its scouts came racing back, pursued by Lakota and Cheyenne warriors.

The battle

The fighting lasted about six hours across several miles of broken, hilly terrain. Crazy Horse coordinated a series of charges and feigned retreats unusual in Plains warfare. Crook's troops held their ground and he sent a detachment to attack the enemy village, but the raid found no village nearby and was recalled. The Lakota name for the battle commemorates Buffalo Calf Road Woman, who rode into the fighting to rescue her wounded brother — an act witnessed by warriors on both sides.

Aftermath

Crook withdrew south to Goose Creek to await reinforcements and resupply, where he remained for six weeks. His absence meant that the columns of Terry and Custer advanced toward the Little Bighorn without his support — and without warning of the strength and confidence of the Lakota and Cheyenne force they would meet on June 25.
