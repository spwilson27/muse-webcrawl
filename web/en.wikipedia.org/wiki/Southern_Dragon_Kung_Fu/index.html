Southern Dragon Kung Fu

Southern Dragon Kung Fu (Chinese: 龍形摩橋; also known as Lung Ying, "Dragon Shape") is a Chinese martial art of Hakka origin, closely related to Southern Praying Mantis and Bak Mei. It is one of the styles grouped under the Southern Shaolin tradition and is notable for techniques that imitate the movements of the mythical Chinese dragon, emphasising flowing, coiling body motion and short-range power.

History

According to tradition, the style was developed by the Buddhist monk Daai Yuk (also rendered Dai Yuk) in the late nineteenth century and taught in the Huizhou region of Guangdong. Its most influential teacher was Lam Yiu Kwai (1874–1949), who learned from several masters and founded the Dragon Style association in Hong Kong, spreading the art widely. Lam's students and descendants carried the style to Vietnam, Malaysia, Australia, Europe, and North America.

Characteristics

Southern Dragon emphasises the waist as the source of power, with techniques "floating, sinking, swallowing, and spitting" in accordance with internal martial arts principles. Practitioners use an upright stance with knees bent and the body slightly leaned, generating force through rotation and snapping of the torso. Hand techniques favour the fist, palm, claw, and "phoenix-eye" fist for striking pressure points, delivered at close range with low kicks. The style's movements are described as hard and soft combined — outwardly flowing but transmitting short, explosive force (ging) on impact.

Forms

The curriculum traditionally includes hand forms such as Sup Ji Kuen (Cross Pattern Fist), Sam Tong Kuen, and the advanced Lung Ying Mor Kiu (Dragon Shape Rubbing Bridges), along with weapons forms for the staff, butterfly swords, and bench. Bridge-hand (kiu sau) drills develop sensitivity and trapping skill in a manner similar to related Hakka arts.
