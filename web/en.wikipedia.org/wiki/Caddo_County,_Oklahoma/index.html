Caddo County, Oklahoma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Caddo County is a county located in the U.S. state of Oklahoma. Its county seat is Anadarko. The county was created in 1901 as part of Oklahoma Territory and named for the Caddo tribe, who were settled in the area. As of the 2020 census, the population was roughly 27,000.

History

The land that became Caddo County was assigned in the 19th century to the Wichita, Caddo, Kiowa, Comanche, and Apache tribes. After the Jerome Agreement and federal allotment policy, the territory was opened to non-Indian settlement by lottery in August 1901, shortly before Oklahoma statehood in 1907. Anadarko grew up around the agency for the Kiowa, Comanche, and Wichita Agency.

Geography

The county covers about 1,290 square miles in west-central Oklahoma. The Washita River crosses the county, and Fort Cobb Lake, in the north, provides water supply and recreation. The terrain transitions from the Red Bed Plains toward the eastern edge of the Wichita Mountains region.

Communities

Besides Anadarko, communities include Apache, Binger, Carnegie, Cement, Cyril, Eakly, Fort Cobb, Gracemont, Hinton, Hydro (partly), Lookeba, and Washita. Agriculture — wheat, cotton, peanuts, and cattle — remains the economic mainstay, alongside tribal enterprises associated with the Caddo Nation, whose headquarters is at Binger, and other tribal nations.
