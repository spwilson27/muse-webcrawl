Tony Blair

Anthony Charles Lynton Blair
Born: 6 May 1953, Edinburgh
Prime Minister: 1997–2007
Party: Labour
Predecessor: John Major; Successor: Gordon Brown

Tony Blair (born 6 May 1953) is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1997 to 2007 and Leader of the Labour Party from 1994 to 2007. He led Labour to three consecutive general election victories, the party's longest period in government.

Early life and rise

Educated at Fettes College and St John's College, Oxford, Blair became a barrister and MP for Sedgefield in 1983. After John Smith's death in 1994 he won the Labour leadership, rebranding the party as 'New Labour', revising Clause IV and moving it to the centre.

Premiership

Domestically his governments introduced the National Minimum Wage, devolution to Scotland and Wales, the Human Rights Act 1998, civil partnerships, and large increases in health and education spending, and brokered the 1998 Good Friday Agreement in Northern Ireland. In foreign policy he ordered interventions in Kosovo (1999) and Sierra Leone (2000), and after 9/11 aligned closely with US President George W. Bush, joining the invasions of Afghanistan (2001) and Iraq (2003). The Iraq War — and claims about weapons of mass destruction — badly damaged his popularity; the 2016 Chilcot Report criticised the decision-making.

Later career

Blair resigned in 2007, served as Quartet envoy to the Middle East until 2015, and founded the Tony Blair Institute for Global Change. See Labour Party and Iraq War.
