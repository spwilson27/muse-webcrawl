Maroons (people)

Maroons are descendants of Africans in the Americas and Indian Ocean who escaped enslavement and formed free, independent settlements, often in remote mountains, swamps, and forests. The word derives from the Spanish cimarrón, meaning wild or untamed.

Communities

Notable Maroon peoples include the Jamaican Maroons, who fought the British to treaties in 1739 under leaders such as Nanny of the Maroons and Cudjoe; the Saramaka, Ndyuka, and other peoples of Suriname and French Guiana, among the largest Maroon populations; the Palenqueros of San Basilio de Palenque in Colombia; and the Black Seminoles of Florida and Oklahoma. Maroon societies preserved African languages, religions, and governance, and some, such as the Surinamese groups, maintain recognised autonomy today.
