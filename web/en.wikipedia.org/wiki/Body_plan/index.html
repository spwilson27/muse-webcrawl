Body plan

A body plan, or Bauplan (German plural: Baupläne), is a set of morphological features common to many members of a phylum of animals. The concept originated in vertebrate morphology, and was subsequently generalized to all levels of biological organization. A body plan encompasses aspects such as symmetry, segmentation, and the disposition of limbs and organ systems.

Origin

Of the 30+ animal phyla, most arose during the Cambrian explosion around 541 million years ago, when the basic body plans of modern animals first appear in the fossil record. The Ediacaran biota of the preceding period show a smaller range of body plans, some of which resemble later forms only distantly.

Genetic basis

Body plans are laid down during embryonic development under the control of regulatory genes, notably the Hox genes, which determine the identity of body segments along the anterior–posterior axis in most bilaterian animals. Changes in the expression patterns of these "toolkit" genes during evolution are thought to underlie major changes in body plan, a central theme of evolutionary developmental biology (evo-devo).

Examples

The vertebrate body plan features a hollow dorsal nerve cord, a notochord, pharyngeal slits and a post-anal tail at some stage of development. Arthropods are characterised by a segmented exoskeleton and jointed appendages, while molluscs share a mantle, a muscular foot and a visceral mass despite their great diversity of form.
