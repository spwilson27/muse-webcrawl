Fayalite

Fayalite is the iron-rich end-member of the olivine solid solution series.

FormulaFe2SiO4
Crystal systemOrthorhombic
Hardness6.5–7 (Mohs)
GroupOlivine group

Fayalite (Fe2SiO4, commonly abbreviated to Fa) is the iron-rich end-member of the olivine solid solution series. In common with all minerals in the olivine group, fayalite crystallises in the orthorhombic system (space group Pbnm) with cell parameters a 4.82 Å, b 10.48 Å and c 6.09 Å.

Name and occurrence

Fayalite is named after Faial Island in the Azores, where it was first described in 1840. It forms a complete solid solution with the magnesium olivine forsterite; natural olivines are typically intermediate in composition, and fayalite-rich varieties occur in iron-rich igneous rocks, metamorphosed iron formations, and slags. Pure fayalite is relatively rare in nature but is common in man-made metallurgical slag.

Properties and stability

Fayalite melts at atmospheric pressure at about 1,205 °C. Under high pressure it undergoes a phase transition to a spinel-structured polymorph. Fayalite reacts with oxygen to produce magnetite and quartz, a reaction that defines the fayalite–magnetite–quartz (FMQ) oxygen buffer widely used in experimental petrology to control oxygen fugacity.

Uses in research

Because its phase transitions mimic those occurring at greater depth in the Earth's mantle, fayalite has been extensively used in high-pressure mineral physics as an analogue for the denser spinel phase of (Mg,Fe)2SiO4 olivine in the mantle transition zone.
