The Lotus Seven is a small, lightweight two-seater open-top sports car produced by the British manufacturer Lotus Cars between 1957 and 1973. Designed by Lotus founder Colin Chapman according to his philosophy of performance through low weight, the Seven was sold both as a complete car and as a kit to be assembled by the buyer, which reduced purchase tax in the United Kingdom. After Lotus ceased production, Caterham Cars bought the rights in 1973 and has continued building the design ever since as the Caterham 7.

Design and development

The Seven followed Chapman's earlier trials and club-racing cars and applied the formula of a tubular steel space frame clothed in aluminium panels, cycle wings, a live rear axle on the early cars, and a succession of tuned engines from Ford, BMC and Cosworth. The Series 1 (1957–1960) used a Ford side-valve or Coventry Climax engine; the Series 2 (1960–1968) simplified the chassis with more Ford components; the Series 3 (1968–1970) brought a return to a stiffer frame and options such as the Lotus Twin Cam engine in the potent Seven S and SS models; the Series 4 (1970–1973) introduced a moulded fibreglass body shell and more weather equipment, but proved less popular with traditionalists.

With kerb weights around 500 kg (1,100 lb), even modestly powered Sevens offered vivid acceleration and exceptional handling, and the cars became mainstays of club racing, hill climbing and sprinting, winning innumerable events in the hands of private owners.

Legacy

The Seven is one of the most copied designs in motoring. Caterham's continuation models, from the basic 170 to the extreme 620R, preserve the original concept, while dozens of manufacturers worldwide have produced Seven-inspired "Locost" and replica cars. The Seven also achieved television fame as the car driven by Patrick McGoohan's character in the opening sequence of the 1967 series The Prisoner. Chapman's maxim — "Simplify, then add lightness" — is nowhere more evident than in the Seven, and the car is regularly cited among the most influential sports cars ever built.
