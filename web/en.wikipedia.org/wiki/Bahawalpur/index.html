Bahawalpur

Bahawalpur (Urdu: بہاولپور) is a city in the Punjab province of Pakistan. Located south of the Sutlej River near the edge of the Cholistan Desert, it was the capital of the former princely state of Bahawalpur, founded in 1748 by Nawab Bahawal Khan I of the Abbasi family, from whom the city takes its name. The city has a population of around 800,000, making it one of the largest cities in southern Punjab.

History

The Abbasi nawabs ruled Bahawalpur as a princely state under first Afghan and then British paramountcy. The state became prosperous through irrigation works on the Sutlej. At the partition of India in 1947, the Nawab acceded to Pakistan, and the state was merged into West Pakistan in 1955. The ruling family built several notable palaces in and around the city, including Noor Mahal (1872), Darbar Mahal and Gulzar Mahal.

Landmarks and economy

Nearby attractions include the ancient fort of Derawar in the Cholistan Desert, the shrines of Uch Sharif, and Lal Suhanra National Park, a UNESCO-recognised biosphere reserve known for its blackbuck and Indian rhinoceros. Bahawalpur is a commercial centre for cotton, sugarcane and other agricultural produce of the surrounding irrigated plains, and is home to Islamia University.
