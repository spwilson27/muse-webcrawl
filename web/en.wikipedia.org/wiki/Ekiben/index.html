Ekiben

Ekiben
OriginJapan, late 19th century
TypeRailway station bento box lunch

Ekiben (駅弁, literally "station bento") are special bento box meals sold at railway stations and on trains throughout Japan, Taiwan and elsewhere. Each station's ekiben typically showcases local specialities and ingredients, making them a form of edible regional tourism.

History

Ekiben appeared with Japan's railways in the 1880s; Utsunomiya station is often credited with an early ekiben around 1885. Initially simple rice balls and pickles sold by platform vendors, they evolved into elaborate lacquered, wooden, ceramic or novelty containers — some shaped like trains or local landmarks — with menus reflecting regional cuisine, from Hokkaido seafood to Gunma's Tōge no Kamameshi (hot-pot rice served in a clay pot, sold at Yokokawa since the 1950s).

Culture

Ekiben fairs (ekiben taikai) at department stores draw crowds; collectors seek regional varieties. Shinkansen services offer trolley and station sales. Taiwan adapted the tradition as railway bento (鐵路便當).
