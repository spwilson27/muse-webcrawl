Reliability engineering

Reliability engineering is a sub-discipline of systems engineering that emphasises the ability of equipment to function without failure. Reliability is defined as the probability that a product, system or service will perform its intended function adequately for a specified period of time, or operate in a defined environment without failure.

Scope

Reliability engineering deals with the prediction, prevention and management of failures over the lifecycle of a product. Its tools include failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA), fault tree analysis, reliability block diagrams, accelerated life testing and statistical models such as the Weibull distribution. Common metrics include mean time between failures (MTBF), mean time to failure (MTTF), failure rate and availability.

History

The discipline grew out of military and aviation needs during and after the Second World War, when the unreliability of vacuum-tube electronics in complex systems such as ballistic missiles prompted systematic study. It later spread to aerospace, nuclear power, automotive and consumer electronics industries.

Related concepts

Reliability is one of the "ilities" of systems engineering, related to maintainability, availability and safety. The characteristic pattern of failure rates over a product's life is often illustrated by the "bathtub curve": early failures, a period of roughly constant random failures, and finally wear-out failures.
