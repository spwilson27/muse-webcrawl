Channel (geography)

In physical geography, a channel is a type of landform consisting of the outline of a path of relatively shallow and narrow body of water, most commonly the confine of a river, river delta or strait. The word is cognate with canal, and sometimes takes this form in place names; for example, the Hood Canal is not a canal, but a fjord.

Formation

Channel formation is dominated by the movement of water and sediment. A channel is typically formed and continually reshaped by flowing water carrying sediment; where the flow is fast, it erodes the bed and banks, and where it slows, it deposits material as bars and floodplains. Over time this produces characteristic planforms described as straight, meandering, braided or anastomosing.

Natural channels

Natural channels include the beds of rivers and streams, tidal channels through marshes and mudflats, and submarine channels cut into continental shelves and slopes by turbidity currents. The deepest line of a channel, where the flow is typically fastest, is called the thalweg.

Navigation channels

In navigation, a channel is the deeper, dredged or naturally navigable route through a harbour, bay or river, often marked by buoys and beacons. Maintaining such channels may require periodic dredging to remove accumulated sediment. The term is also applied to straits used by shipping, such as the English Channel.
