The Fouta Djallon (Fula: Fuuta Jaloo) is a highland region in the center of Guinea, West Africa. A heavily eroded sandstone plateau with deep gorges and valleys, it is the source of several of West Africa's great rivers, including the Niger, the Senegal, and the Gambia, and is often called the "water tower" of West Africa.

History

Between the 1730s and 1750s, Muslim Fula pastoralists established the Imamate of Fouta Djallon, a theocratic state organized into nine provinces, which became a major regional power and center of Islamic scholarship before French colonization in the late nineteenth century. The city of Timbo served as its capital.

Geography and people

The highlands rise to about 1,515 metres at Mount Loura near Mali. The population is predominantly Fula, whose language, Pular, dominates the region. Cattle herding, farming, and trade structure the local economy, and the cool climate supports fruit and vegetable growing.
