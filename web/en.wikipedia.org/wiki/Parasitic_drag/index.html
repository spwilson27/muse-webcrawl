Parasitic drag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parasitic drag, also known as profile drag, is a type of aerodynamic drag that acts on any object when the object is moving through a fluid. Parasitic drag is a combination of form drag and skin friction drag. It is named as such because it is not a result of the production of lift, and hence is considered "parasitic" from an aerodynamic standpoint.

The other component of total drag on an aircraft in flight, besides parasitic drag, is lift-induced drag. In a simplified model, the total drag of an aircraft in level flight is the sum of parasitic drag and induced drag. Parasitic drag increases with the square of airspeed, while induced drag decreases with the square of airspeed; the total drag curve is therefore U-shaped, and the speed at which total drag is minimum is important for endurance and range calculations.

Form drag

Form drag arises because of the shape of the object. The general size and shape of the body are the most important factors in form drag; bodies with a larger presented cross-section will have a higher drag than thinner bodies. Smooth, streamlined shapes reduce form drag by delaying flow separation and reducing the size of the wake. Form drag follows the drag equation, meaning that it increases with the square of velocity.

Skin friction drag

Skin friction drag arises from the friction of the fluid against the "skin" of the object that is moving through it. The friction is a result of fluid viscosity acting within the boundary layer. It depends on the wetted surface area, surface roughness, and whether the boundary layer flow is laminar or turbulent. Streamlining and smooth surface finishes reduce skin friction.

Interference drag

Interference drag arises where flows around different parts of an aircraft — such as the wing and fuselage junction — interact in a way that produces more drag than the sum of the parts. It is often grouped with parasitic drag and is minimised by fairings and careful junction design.

Drag at transonic and supersonic speeds

At transonic speeds, wave drag appears as a further component related to shock waves and is also unrelated to lift generation; it is sometimes treated as part of parasitic drag in simplified analyses. Streamlined area ruling and swept surfaces are used to control it.
