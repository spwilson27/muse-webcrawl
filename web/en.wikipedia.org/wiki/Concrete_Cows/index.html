The Concrete Cows in Milton Keynes, England are an iconic work of sculpture, created in 1978 by Canadian-born artist Liz Leyh. There are three cows and three calves. They were constructed from scrap materials — concrete over a steel and glass fibre frame — with the assistance of a group of artists at Stony Stratford in a programme funded by the Milton Keynes Development Corporation.

History

The Development Corporation commissioned public art to give the new city an identity. Leyh modelled the cows on local Friesian cattle. They were placed in a field at Bancroft, near the A5, where generations of drivers have taken them for real animals. The cows quickly became the best-known symbol of Milton Keynes, affectionately mocked and fiercely defended by residents alike; they feature on souvenirs, in local folklore, and in the nickname given by football fans to the city's club.

Vandalism and restoration

The sculptures have repeatedly been painted, dressed, given zebra stripes, pyjamas, and even a BSE-related paint attack in 2000. In 2013 the deteriorating originals were taken into the care of Milton Keynes Museum, and new replica cows, made using the original moulds and techniques, took their place in the Bancroft field in 2016.
