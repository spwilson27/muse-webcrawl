Frederick Henry, Prince of Orange

Frederick Henry (Dutch: Frederik Hendrik; 29 January 1584 – 14 March 1647) was the sovereign prince of Orange and stadtholder of Holland, Zeeland, Utrecht, Guelders and Overijssel in the Dutch Republic from 1625 until his death in 1647. The third son of William the Silent, he succeeded his half-brother Maurice of Nassau as stadtholder and captain-general of the Union.

Stadtholderate

Frederick Henry proved a master of siege warfare in the later stages of the Eighty Years' War against Spain. His captures of 's-Hertogenbosch (1629), Maastricht (1632), Breda (1637) and Sas van Gent (1644) pushed the Republic's frontier southward and earned him the nickname "the city forcer" (Stedendwinger). His campaigns laid the territorial groundwork for the settlement reached at the Peace of Münster in 1648, a year after his death.

Court and patronage

With his wife Amalia of Solms-Braunfels, Frederick Henry transformed the stadtholder's court at The Hague into a centre of European culture, building palaces including Huis ten Bosch and patronising artists and architects such as Jacob van Campen and Constantijn Huygens, his secretary. His dynastic ambitions were crowned by the marriage of his son William II to Mary, Princess Royal of England, in 1641.

Death and legacy

Frederick Henry died in The Hague in 1647 and was buried in the Nieuwe Kerk in Delft. He was succeeded as stadtholder by his son William II. Historians regard his stadtholderate as the high point of the Dutch Golden Age in military, diplomatic and cultural terms.
