The Antonov An-2 (NATO reporting name: Colt) is a Soviet and later Ukrainian single-engine biplane utility/agricultural aircraft designed and manufactured by the Antonov Design Bureau. First flown in 1947, it is the largest single-engine biplane ever produced and one of the longest-serving aircraft designs in history, with production continuing in China as the Shijiazhuang Y-5.

Design

The An-2 is an all-metal sesquiplane powered by a 1,000 hp Shvetsov ASh-62 radial engine. Its fixed tailwheel landing gear, large wing area and full-span automatic leading-edge slats give it exceptional short take-off and landing performance and safe handling at extremely low speeds — it has no documented stall speed and can remain controllable at around 40 km/h, even flying slowly backwards relative to the ground in a strong headwind.

Service history

More than 18,000 An-2s were built in the USSR, Poland (under licence by PZL) and China. The type has served as a 12-seat airliner, crop duster, parachute trainer, water bomber, floatplane and military transport in dozens of countries. Thousands remain in use, particularly in Russia and Central Asia, for work from remote airstrips.

Variants

Numerous variants exist, including the An-2T transport, An-2P passenger version, An-2S agricultural sprayer and the turboprop-powered An-3 conversion. North Korea's use of the type as a low-observable infiltration aircraft has drawn particular attention from defence analysts.
