Telecommunications in France

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Telecommunications in France covers the telephone, mobile, internet, broadcasting and postal-related communications systems of France. The sector is regulated by ARCEP (Autorité de régulation des communications électroniques, des postes et de la distribution de la presse).

History

France was a pioneer in telecommunications: Claude Chappe's optical semaphore telegraph network in the 1790s was the first of its kind. In the 1980s, France Télécom's Minitel videotex service reached millions of households years before the consumer web. The state monopoly was progressively liberalized in the 1990s; France Télécom was rebranded Orange in 2013.

Market

The principal operators are Orange, SFR (Altice), Bouygues Telecom, and Free (Iliad), whose aggressive pricing from 2012 reshaped the mobile market. France has extensive fibre-to-the-home deployment under the Plan France Très Haut Débit, and metropolitan France has near-universal 4G coverage with ongoing 5G rollout.

Broadcasting and internet

Public broadcasting is provided by France Télévisions and Radio France. France has one of Europe's largest internet user populations, with country code top-level domain .fr administered by AFNIC.
