Drought in Australia

Drought in Australia is a recurring and defining feature of the continent's climate. Australia is the driest inhabited continent, and rainfall variability driven by the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, the Indian Ocean Dipole, and the Southern Annular Mode produces prolonged dry periods with severe effects on agriculture, water supplies, and ecosystems.

Major droughts

Significant historical droughts include the Federation Drought (1895–1903), the World War II drought (1937–1947), the droughts of the early 1980s, and the Millennium Drought (c. 1997–2009), which devastated the Murray–Darling Basin and forced major water restrictions in Australian cities. The late-2010s drought in eastern Australia was among the most intense and widespread on record and contributed to the conditions of the 2019–20 bushfire season.

Responses

Responses have included drought relief payments to farmers, investment in desalination plants and water recycling, and reforms to water markets in the Murray–Darling Basin.
