Souvlaki

Souvlaki
Place of originGreece
Main ingredientsPork, chicken or lamb; olive oil, lemon, oregano

Souvlaki (Greek: σουβλάκι) is a popular Greek fast food of small pieces of meat, and sometimes vegetables, grilled on a skewer. It is usually eaten straight from the skewer, in a pita sandwich with garnishes and sauces, or on a plate with fried potatoes.

History

Excavations in Greece and Cyprus have found stone and terracotta skewer supports dating to the Bronze Age and antiquity. The term derives from souvla, meaning skewer. In Athens, souvlaki as street food became widespread after World War II.

Preparation

Meat — traditionally pork in Greece, also chicken, lamb and beef — is marinated in olive oil, lemon juice, oregano and salt, skewered and grilled over charcoal. Kalamaki is the Athenian name for a plain skewer, to distinguish it from the wrapped sandwich.

Variations

In Cyprus, souvlaki is served in a large oval pita with cabbage and is often combined with sheftalia. Related dishes include gyro, kebab and shashlik.
