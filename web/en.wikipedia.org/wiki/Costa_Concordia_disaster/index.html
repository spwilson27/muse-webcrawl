The Costa Concordia disaster occurred on 13 January 2012, when the Italian cruise ship Costa Concordia struck a rock formation off Isola del Giglio in the Tyrrhenian Sea, tearing a 70-metre gash in her hull. The ship took on water, lost power, and capsized onto her starboard side in shallow water just off the island. Of the 4,252 people aboard, 32 died.

The grounding

Captain Francesco Schettino had deviated from the planned route to perform a "sail-by salute" close to Giglio, a practice previously carried out with company knowledge. At 21:45 the ship, travelling at about 16 knots, struck the Scole rocks. Despite the severity of the flooding, a general emergency was not declared for over an hour, and the coast guard learned of the ship's plight from other sources; recorded exchanges in which Livorno port captain Gregorio de Falco ordered the captain — who had left the ship — to "get back on board" ("Vada a bordo, cazzo!") became notorious. The evacuation of more than 4,000 people, mostly by boat to Giglio, was completed in difficult conditions; the last survivors were rescued from the wreck in the following days.

Salvage

The wreck was removed in the largest maritime salvage operation of its kind. In September 2013 the ship was righted by parbuckling onto an underwater platform; in July 2014 she was refloated and towed to Genoa for dismantling, completed in 2017.

Legal consequences

Schettino was convicted in 2015 of multiple manslaughter, causing a shipwreck and abandoning ship, and sentenced to 16 years in prison, a sentence upheld on final appeal in 2017. Several other officers and the company's crisis manager received shorter sentences through plea bargains, and Costa Crociere paid a €1 million fine and reached civil settlements.
