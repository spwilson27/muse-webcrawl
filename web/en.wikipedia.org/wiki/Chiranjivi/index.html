Overview

The Chiranjivi (from Sanskrit cira, 'long', and jivin, 'living') are a group of immortals in Hindu tradition who are believed to remain alive on Earth until the end of the current Kali Yuga. The best-known grouping, the Sapta Chiranjivi or seven immortals, is recalled in a popular Sanskrit verse recited for longevity.

The seven

The seven are usually listed as Ashvatthama, the warrior of the Mahabharata cursed to wander immortal; King Bali (Mahabali), the generous asura king granted annual return to his people, celebrated in Kerala's Onam festival; Vyasa, the sage credited with compiling the Vedas and composing the Mahabharata; Hanuman, the devoted servant of Rama; Vibhishana, Ravana's righteous brother crowned king of Lanka; Kripa, the teacher of the Kuru princes; and Parashurama, the warrior-sage avatar of Vishnu. The boy-sage Markandeya, blessed by Shiva, is sometimes added as an eighth.

Cultural role

Accounts of encounters with the Chiranjivi appear in regional folklore, and the remembrance verse is recited in daily prayer. Their stories are read as teachings on duty, devotion, generosity and the consequences of action across Hindu epics and Puranas.
