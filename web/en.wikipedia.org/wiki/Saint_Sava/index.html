Overview

Saint Sava (1174/75–1236), born Rastko Nemanjić, was a Serbian prince and monk who founded the independent Serbian Orthodox Church and became its first archbishop in 1219. The youngest son of Grand Prince Stefan Nemanja, he renounced the court for Mount Athos (taking monastic vows at Vatopedi), then worked with his father (the monk Simeon) to found the Hilandar monastery on Athos, the enduring centre of Serbian spirituality.

Legacy

Sava secured autocephaly for the Serbian Church from the exiled Byzantine patriarch at Nicaea, organised its dioceses, crowned his brother Stefan the First-Crowned king, and authored foundational texts (the Life of St Simeon, the Karyes Typikon, the Nomocanon translation). His relics, kept at Mileševa, were burned by the Ottoman Sinan Pasha on Vračar hill in Belgrade in 1595 to punish Serb rebels — the act only deepened his cult; the colossal Church of Saint Sava now rises on that site, one of the largest Orthodox churches in the world. He is Serbia's patron saint, and his feast (27 January, Savindan) is the school holiday.
