Hibatullah Akhundzada

Mawlawi Hibatullah Akhundzada (Pashto: هبت الله آخندزاده; born 19 October 1967) is an Afghan cleric who is the supreme leader of Afghanistan and head of the Taliban. He became the Taliban's third supreme leader in May 2016, following the death of Akhtar Mansour in a US drone strike, having served as a deputy leader and previously as the movement's chief justice.

Early life and Taliban career

Akhundzada was born in the Panjwayi District of Kandahar Province and studied in Pakistani madrasas. After the Soviet invasion he joined the mujahideen; during the first Taliban government in the 1990s he sat on military courts and headed courts in Nangarhar and Kandahar. In the insurgency after 2001 he served as head of the Taliban's shadow court system and issued many of its fatwas and rulings.

Supreme leader

Known as a religious rather than military figure, Akhundzada has maintained a notably reclusive profile, rarely appearing in public. Following the Taliban's return to power in August 2021 he was proclaimed Amir al-Mu'minin of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan, ruling from Kandahar and issuing decrees — including those restricting the education and employment of women — that are implemented by the cabinet in Kabul.
