Photoaging

Photoaging (also known as dermatoheliosis) is premature aging of the skin caused by repeated exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation, primarily from the sun but also from artificial tanning sources. It differs from chronological aging in its clinical and histological features.

Signs

Photoaged skin shows deep wrinkles, leathery texture, mottled pigmentation and lentigines ("liver spots"), loss of elasticity, telangiectasia, and a yellowish, rough appearance — most prominently on the face, neck, and the backs of the hands.

Mechanism

UV radiation generates reactive oxygen species and induces matrix metalloproteinases that degrade collagen and elastin in the dermis, while also damaging DNA — a process linked to increased skin cancer risk.

Prevention and treatment

Photoprotection with sunscreen, clothing, and shade is the main preventive measure. Topical retinoids such as tretinoin are the best-studied treatment for reversing some features of photoaging.
