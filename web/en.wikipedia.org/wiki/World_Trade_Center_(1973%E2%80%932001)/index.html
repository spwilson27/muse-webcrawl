World Trade Center (1973–2001)

World Trade Center
Location: Lower Manhattan, New York City
Opened: 1973 (North Tower 1970)
Destroyed: 11 September 2001
Height: 417 m (North Tower)

The original World Trade Center was a complex of seven buildings in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan, New York City, dominated by the landmark Twin Towers designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki. The 110-storey towers were the tallest buildings in the world on completion in the early 1970s.

History

Built by the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey, the complex housed tens of thousands of workers and firms in international trade and finance, the Windows on the World restaurant and an observation deck. It survived a bombing in its parking garage in 1993 that killed six people.

Destruction

On 11 September 2001, al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked two airliners and flew them into the towers; both collapsed within hours, and the whole complex was destroyed, with nearly 3,000 people killed in the attacks overall. The site was rebuilt with the National September 11 Memorial & Museum and a new One World Trade Center. See also September 11 attacks.
