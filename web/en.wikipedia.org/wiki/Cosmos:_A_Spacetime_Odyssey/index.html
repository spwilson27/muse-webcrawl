Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey is a 2014 American science documentary television series, a follow-up to Carl Sagan's landmark 1980 series Cosmos: A Personal Voyage. It is hosted by astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson, who as a young man was inspired by Sagan, and was produced by Sagan's widow Ann Druyan, Steven Soter, and Seth MacFarlane, whose advocacy helped bring the series to the Fox network.

Format and content

The 13 episodes reprise signature devices of the original: the Ship of the Imagination, redesigned for the new series, and the Cosmic Calendar, compressing the history of the universe into one year. Topics range across the Big Bang, evolution by natural selection, the electromagnetic spectrum, gravity and black holes, the lives of scientists such as Giordano Bruno, Isaac Newton, Edmond Halley, Clair Patterson, and Cecilia Payne, and climate change. Historical stories are told through animated sequences, with voices by actors including MacFarlane, Patrick Stewart, and Kirsten Dunst; the score was composed by Alan Silvestri.

Episodes

Notable episodes include the premiere “Standing Up in the Milky Way”, “Some of the Things That Molecules Do” on evolution, “The Clean Room” on Clair Patterson's campaign against leaded petrol, and the finale “Unafraid of the Dark”, which closes with Sagan's “Pale Blue Dot” reflection from the original series.

Reception

The series premiered simultaneously across Fox-owned channels in the United States and was broadcast in more than 180 countries. It was widely praised for its production values and science communication, winning four Primetime Emmy Awards from twelve nominations, including for Tyson's narration being nominated and wins for writing and theme music. A further season, Cosmos: Possible Worlds, premiered in 2020.
