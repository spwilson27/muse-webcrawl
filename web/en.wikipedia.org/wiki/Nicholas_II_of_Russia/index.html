Nicholas II, last Emperor of Russia.

Nicholas II (18 May 1868 – 17 July 1918) was the last Emperor of Russia, ruling from 1894 until his abdication in 1917 during the February Revolution. His reign saw the expansion and then the collapse of the Russian Empire: the disastrous Russo-Japanese War, the Revolution of 1905, Russia's entry into the First World War, and finally the fall of the Romanov dynasty after more than three centuries.

Early life and accession

Nicholas was the eldest son of Emperor Alexander III and Maria Feodorovna (Dagmar of Denmark). Ill-prepared by his father's standards and reluctant to rule, he succeeded on Alexander's sudden death in 1894 and married Princess Alix of Hesse within weeks. His coronation in 1896 was marred by the Khodynka Tragedy, in which nearly 1,400 people were crushed in a stampede at the coronation festivities.

Reign

Nicholas believed fervently in autocracy and resisted reform. Defeat in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) and the Bloody Sunday massacre of January 1905 triggered the Revolution of 1905, forcing him to issue the October Manifesto and create the Duma, whose powers he then curtailed. At home, the influence of the faith healer Grigori Rasputin over the Empress — who sought relief for the Tsarevich Alexei's haemophilia — badly damaged the monarchy's prestige.

During the First World War Nicholas took personal command of the army in 1915, tying himself to its failures while leaving the government in Petrograd to Alexandra and Rasputin. In March 1917, amid food shortages, strikes and military mutiny, he abdicated for himself and his son.

Death and canonisation

The former imperial family was held under house arrest and eventually exiled to Yekaterinburg, where Nicholas, Alexandra, their five children and four retainers were murdered by Bolshevik guards on the night of 16–17 July 1918. Their remains were recovered and formally identified decades later. Nicholas and his family were canonised as passion bearers by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000.
