Pisa Cathedral

Pisa Cathedral, with the Leaning Tower behind

Pisa Cathedral (Italian: Cattedrale Metropolitana Primaziale di Santa Maria Assunta) is a medieval Roman Catholic cathedral in the Piazza dei Miracoli (the "Cathedral Square") in Pisa, Italy, dedicated to the Assumption of the Virgin Mary. It is a masterpiece of Romanesque architecture and the centrepiece of one of the world's most famous monumental ensembles, together with the Baptistery, the Camposanto cemetery and the campanile — the Leaning Tower of Pisa.

History

Construction began in 1063 (1064 by the Pisan calendar) under the architect Buscheto, funded by spoils from the Pisan victory over the Saracens at Palermo. The cathedral was consecrated in 1118 by Pope Gelasius II, and was enlarged in the 12th century by the architect Rainaldo, who extended the nave and built the present façade of tiers of open arcades. A fire in 1595 destroyed much of the interior, which was redecorated under the Medici grand dukes of Tuscany.

Architecture and art

The cathedral is built in white and coloured marble in the distinctive Pisan Romanesque style, combining classical columns, blind arcading and Islamic and Byzantine influences from Pisa's maritime trade. The bronze south-transept doors by Bonanno Pisano (c. 1180) depict scenes from the life of Christ. Inside are Giovanni Pisano's great carved pulpit (1302–1310), a mosaic of Christ Pantocrator in the apse completed by Cimabue in 1302, and, in the nave, the great bronze lamp traditionally (though almost certainly apocryphally) said to have inspired Galileo's observations on pendulums. Galileo's tomb and the sarcophagus of Emperor Henry VII, carved by Tino di Camaino, are also associated with the cathedral.

Leaning

Like its famous bell tower, the cathedral itself leans slightly — by roughly half a degree — because of the soft alluvial soil of the square.
