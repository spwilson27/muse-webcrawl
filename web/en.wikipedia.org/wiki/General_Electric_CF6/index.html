The General Electric CF6 is a family of high-bypass turbofan engines produced by GE Aviation. Introduced in the early 1970s to power the McDonnell Douglas DC-10, the CF6 became one of the most widely used engines on wide-body airliners, and its core was adapted into the CFM56 and marine and industrial gas turbines.

A CF6-80 series engine on a Boeing 747.

Development

The CF6 grew out of the TF39, the first high-bypass turbofan, developed for the Lockheed C-5 Galaxy military transport in the 1960s. General Electric commercialised the design as the CF6, which first entered service on the DC-10 in 1971. Successive variants — the CF6-6, CF6-50, CF6-80A, CF6-80C2, and CF6-80E1 — raised thrust and efficiency for aircraft including the Boeing 747, 767, Airbus A300, A310 and A330, and military types such as the E-4B and KC-10 Extender.

Design

The CF6 is a two-spool, axial-flow turbofan with a single-stage fan driven by a multi-stage low-pressure turbine and a 14-stage high-pressure compressor driven by a two-stage high-pressure turbine. Later -80 series engines introduced full authority digital engine control (FADEC). The engine's gas generator also underpins the LM2500 and LM6000 industrial and marine turbines.
