Dante Giacosa

Dante Giacosa (3 January 1905 – 31 March 1996) was an Italian automobile designer and engineer, widely regarded as one of the most influential car designers of the 20th century. He spent virtually his entire career at Fiat, where he was responsible for many of the company's most successful models.

Career

Giacosa joined Fiat in 1928 after graduating in mechanical engineering from the Polytechnic University of Turin. He rose to become head of the company's technical office and later its engineering director. His early masterpiece was the Cisitalia 202 of 1947, whose flowing bodywork, designed in collaboration with Pinin Farina, is exhibited at the Museum of Modern Art in New York as a landmark of automotive design.

Notable designs

Giacosa designed the Fiat 500 "Topolino", the post-war Fiat 1400 and 1500, the Fiat 600 of 1955 — which pioneered the rear-engined layout widely copied across Europe — and the Nuova 500 of 1957, which became an icon of Italian motoring. His Fiat 128 of 1969, with its transverse engine and front-wheel drive combined with an end-on gearbox, established the layout that dominates small-car design to this day; the arrangement is sometimes called the "Giacosa layout". He also worked on the Fiat 130 and the Autobianchi Primula, an early testbed for the transverse front-drive concept.
