Ulcerative colitis

SpecialtyGastroenterology
SymptomsBloody diarrhea, abdominal pain, urgency
Usual onsetAges 15–30, or 50–70
TypeInflammatory bowel disease

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that causes inflammation and ulcers in the mucosal lining of the colon and rectum. Unlike Crohn's disease, which can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract, ulcerative colitis is confined to the large intestine and affects the inner lining in a continuous pattern beginning at the rectum.

Signs and symptoms

Typical symptoms include bloody diarrhea, abdominal pain and cramping, rectal urgency, and tenesmus (the feeling of needing to defecate despite an empty bowel). The disease course is marked by periods of flare-up and remission. Extraintestinal manifestations can include inflammation of the joints, eyes, skin, and liver, such as primary sclerosing cholangitis. Long-standing disease increases the risk of colorectal cancer.

Cause and diagnosis

The cause is unknown but is thought to involve genetic susceptibility, immune dysregulation, and environmental factors including the gut microbiome. Diagnosis is typically made by colonoscopy with biopsy, after excluding infectious causes.

Treatment

Treatment aims to induce and maintain remission. Medications include aminosalicylates such as mesalamine, corticosteroids for flares, immunomodulators, and biologic agents such as anti-TNF drugs and vedolizumab. In severe or refractory cases, surgical removal of the colon (colectomy) is curative of the intestinal disease.
