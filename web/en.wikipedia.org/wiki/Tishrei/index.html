Tishrei

Tishrei (Hebrew: תִּשְׁרֵי, Tishri) is the first month of the Jewish civil year and the seventh month of the ecclesiastical year on the Hebrew calendar. It is a month of 30 days, falling in September or October of the Gregorian calendar, and is distinguished by a concentration of major festivals unmatched in any other month — the High Holy Days and the autumn pilgrimage festival all occur within its first three weeks.

Festivals of Tishrei

  • Rosh Hashanah (1–2 Tishrei): the Jewish New Year, a day of judgement and coronation of God as king, marked by hearing the shofar (ram's horn).
  • Fast of Gedalia (3 Tishrei): a minor fast commemorating the assassination of Gedaliah, governor of Judah under the Babylonians.
  • Yom Kippur (10 Tishrei): the Day of Atonement, a 25-hour fast and the holiest day of the Jewish year, concluding the Ten Days of Repentance that began on Rosh Hashanah.
  • Sukkot (15–21 Tishrei): the Festival of Booths, commemorating the Israelites' wilderness wanderings and the autumn harvest, followed immediately by Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah (22 Tishrei in Israel; 22–23 in the diaspora), celebrating the completion and recommencement of the annual Torah reading cycle.

Name and significance

Like most Hebrew month names, Tishrei derives from the Babylonian calendar adopted during the exile; it may be connected to an Akkadian root meaning "beginning". Because the month contains Rosh Hashanah, Judaism counts years from 1 Tishrei, even though the religious calendar in the Torah begins in Nisan. Tradition holds that the patriarchs Abraham and Jacob were born in Tishrei. In Israel the density of holidays makes Tishrei a month of many public holidays, school breaks, and reduced working days.
