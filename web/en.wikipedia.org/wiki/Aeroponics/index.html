Aeroponics

TypeSoilless cultivation
MediumAir; roots suspended and misted with nutrient solution
Related methodsHydroponics, aquaponics, fogponics
Research useNASA plant-growth studies for spaceflight
Roots suspended in air are periodically misted with nutrient solution

Aeroponics is the process of growing plants in an air or mist environment without soil or an aggregate growing medium. The plants' roots hang suspended in a (usually enclosed) chamber and are sprayed at intervals with a fine mist of nutrient-rich water, while the foliage grows above. Aeroponics is a branch of hydroponics distinguished by delivering nutrients as an aerosol rather than a liquid stream or bath.

History

Suspended-root culture was used for plant research from the early twentieth century: L. J. Klotz described a misting method for citrus studies in the 1940s, and F. W. Went named the technique "aeroponics" in 1957 while growing coffee and tomato plants with roots in air, misted with nutrients. Richard Stoner commercialised aeroponic hardware in the 1980s, and NASA funded extensive aeroponics research in the 1990s for growing food in space, where the method's low water mass and absence of loose media are advantages.

Advantages and drawbacks

Because roots receive abundant oxygen and nutrients directly, aeroponic plants often grow faster than in soil or hydroponic systems, with greatly reduced water and nutrient use and minimal risk of soil-borne disease; the enclosed root zone also simplifies monitoring. The method is especially used for producing disease-free potato minitubers and for cloning plants from cuttings. Its weaknesses are dependence on pumps, nozzles, and power — a failure can dry roots within hours — plus clogging from salt build-up and higher equipment cost.

Variants

High-pressure systems atomise solution to droplets of roughly 20–50 micrometres; low-pressure systems use simpler soaker pumps; ultrasonic "fogponics" generates a still finer fog. Aeroponics is a mainstay of research greenhouses and some commercial vertical farms.
