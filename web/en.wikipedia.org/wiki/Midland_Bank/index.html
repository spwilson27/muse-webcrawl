Midland Bank

Midland Bank was a British clearing bank that became one of the "Big Four" UK high-street banks. Founded in 1836 as the Sheffield and Hallamshire Bank's successors in various forms, the institution took the name Midland Bank in 1923 after a series of amalgamations, the largest being the merger of the London City and Midland Bank with the London Joint Stock Bank in 1918, which made it at the time the largest deposit bank in the world.

History

The bank grew by absorbing dozens of regional banks across England and Wales. Its grand headquarters on Poultry in the City of London, designed by Edwin Lutyens with interiors by Joseph Singer (completed in the 1930s), is one of the finest bank buildings of its era.

In the 20th century Midland expanded internationally and into merchant banking, and in 1981 it acquired a majority stake in the California bank Crocker National. Losses at Crocker contributed to later difficulties. Midland became a member of the HSBC Group in 1992, when HSBC Holdings acquired it in one of the largest banking mergers of the time, partly on condition that HSBC move its domicile to London.

Disappearance of the name

In 1999, Midland Bank branches were rebranded as HSBC, and the Midland name disappeared from the high street after more than 160 years. The name survived in a few subsidiaries for a time, and HSBC's UK ring-fenced bank introduced in 2018 was initially expected to revive it, though it ultimately traded simply as HSBC UK.
