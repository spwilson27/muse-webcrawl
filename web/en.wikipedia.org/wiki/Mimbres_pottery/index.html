Mimbres pottery

Mimbres pottery is a style of prehistoric ceramic art produced by the Mimbres branch of the Mogollon culture in the Mimbres Valley of southwestern New Mexico, flourishing roughly between 1000 and 1150 CE. Its black-on-white bowls are celebrated for finely painted geometric designs and naturalistic figures of humans, animals and mythic beings.

Style

The classic Mimbres style (Style III) features precise, thin-line painting in dark mineral or vegetal pigment on a white slip, usually inside hemispherical bowls. Imagery includes fish, birds, rabbits, insects, and human figures engaged in daily or ceremonial activities; many bowls were "killed" — punctured through the base — and placed over the heads of the dead in burials.

Legacy

Mimbres pottery is a highlight of museum collections of Southwestern archaeology and has influenced modern Pueblo and studio potters.
