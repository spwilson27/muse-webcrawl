Conditional sentence

Conditional sentences are sentences that discuss hypothetical situations and their consequences. In traditional grammar, a complete conditional sentence contains two clauses: the condition or protasis (often introduced by "if") and the result or apodosis (often introduced by "then"). Cross-linguistically, conditionals are expressed by a wide variety of moods, tenses, and constructions.

Types in English

English pedagogy commonly distinguishes: the zero conditional for general truths ("If you heat ice, it melts"); the first conditional for real future possibilities ("If it rains, we will stay home"); the second conditional for present or future hypotheticals ("If I had a million dollars, I would travel"); and the third conditional for counterfactual past situations ("If you had studied, you would have passed"). Mixed conditionals combine patterns.

Other forms

Conditions may also be expressed with "unless", "provided that", "in case", or inversion ("Had I known..."). Conditional clauses interact with modality, counterfactuality, and politeness in complex ways, and the semantics of conditionals is a major topic in philosophy of language and logic, related to the material conditional of formal logic.
