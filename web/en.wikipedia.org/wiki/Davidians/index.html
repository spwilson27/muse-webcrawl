Davidians

The Davidians, also called the Shepherd's Rod or the Davidian Seventh-day Adventists, are a religious movement founded in 1929 by Victor Houteff, a Bulgarian immigrant to the United States and a former member of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Taking their name from the biblical King David and their emblem from Houteff's book The Shepherd's Rod, the Davidians preached an imminent restoration of the Davidic kingdom in Palestine and a final "cleansing" of the Adventist church. A schism in 1955 produced the Branch Davidians, the group later led by David Koresh at Waco, Texas.

History

Victor Houteff (1885–1955) published The Shepherd's Rod in 1929, arguing that the Seventh-day Adventist Church had fallen into apostasy and that the prophecies of the 144,000 and the kingdom of Ezekiel and Hosea were about to be fulfilled. Rejected by the church, he founded the movement in 1929 and in 1935 established a community at Mount Carmel, near Waco, Texas. Davidians lived communally, observed the seventh-day Sabbath and Adventist health practices, and published extensive prophetic literature.

Branch Davidians

After Houteff's death in 1955, his widow Florence announced a prophecy that the kingdom would be established in 1959. When it failed, the movement fragmented; Benjamin Roden's faction, the Branch Davidians, gained control of Mount Carmel. Roden's group eventually came under the leadership of Vernon Howell, who took the name David Koresh. The Branch Davidian standoff of 1993 — a 51-day siege ending in a fire that killed Koresh and 75 followers — is often wrongly conflated with the original Davidian movement, whose surviving associations continue separately to this day.
