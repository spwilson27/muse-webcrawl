Deposition is the phase transition in which gas transforms into solid without passing through the liquid phase. Deposition is a thermodynamic process. The reverse of deposition is sublimation, and hence sometimes deposition is called desublimation.

Examples

One example of deposition is the process by which, in sub-freezing air, water vapour changes directly to ice without first becoming a liquid. This is how frost and hoar frost form on the ground or other surfaces. Another example is when frost forms on a leaf. For deposition to occur, thermal energy must be removed from a gas. When the air becomes cold enough, water vapour in the air surrounding the leaf loses enough thermal energy to change into a solid.

Another example of physical deposition is the artificial process of physical vapour deposition, used to deposit thin films of various materials onto various surfaces. In the atmosphere, soot particles can deposit onto surfaces in a process sometimes loosely called deposition, though strictly that is settling rather than a phase transition.

Applications

Deposition processes are exploited industrially in chemical vapour deposition and physical vapour deposition to coat tools, electronics and optics, and occur naturally in the formation of snow in clouds when ice crystals grow directly from vapour under suitable conditions.
