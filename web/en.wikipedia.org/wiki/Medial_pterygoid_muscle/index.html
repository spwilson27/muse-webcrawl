Medial pterygoid muscle

OriginLateral pterygoid plate and palatine bone
InsertionMedial surface of ramus and angle of mandible
NerveNerve to medial pterygoid (from mandibular nerve, V3)
ActionsElevates mandible, protrudes mandible, lateral movement

The medial pterygoid muscle (or internal pterygoid muscle) is a thick, quadrilateral muscle of mastication located on the inside of the ramus of the mandible. It mirrors the masseter muscle on the medial side of the jaw, forming with it a muscular sling that supports the mandible at its angle.

Structure

The muscle has two heads: a deep head arising from the medial surface of the lateral pterygoid plate and the pyramidal process of the palatine bone, and a smaller superficial head from the tuberosity of the maxilla. Its fibers pass downward, laterally, and backward to insert on the medial surface of the mandible near its angle. It is supplied by the nerve to medial pterygoid, a branch of the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve.

Function

Acting bilaterally, the medial pterygoids elevate and protrude the mandible; acting unilaterally, they move the jaw toward the opposite side, contributing to the grinding movements of chewing. Dysfunction of the muscle is implicated in temporomandibular joint disorders and myofascial pain.

References: Gray's Anatomy; Moore, Clinically Oriented Anatomy.
