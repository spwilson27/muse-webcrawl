Fujairah

Fujairah (Arabic: الفجيرة‎) is the capital of the Emirate of Fujairah in the United Arab Emirates, located on the Gulf of Oman. It is the only emirate capital situated on the UAE's east coast.


Fujairah Fort

Geography

The city lies between the Hajar Mountains and the Gulf of Oman. Its coastal position, outside the Strait of Hormuz, gives the emirate strategic significance: the Port of Fujairah is a major bunkering and oil storage hub, and the Habshan–Fujairah oil pipeline, opened in 2012, allows Abu Dhabi crude to be exported without passing through the Strait.

History

Fujairah was historically part of the territory contested between the rulers of Sharjah and Muscat. It became a separate emirate in 1952, when the British recognised the Al Sharqi sheikh as its ruler, and it joined the United Arab Emirates at the federation's formation in 1971–72. Landmarks include Fujairah Fort, dating from the 16th–17th centuries, and the Al-Hayl Castle.

Economy

Besides the port and oil terminals, Fujairah hosts a free zone and Fujairah International Airport. Tourism is growing, drawn by beaches, diving in the Gulf of Oman and the nearby mountains and wadis.
