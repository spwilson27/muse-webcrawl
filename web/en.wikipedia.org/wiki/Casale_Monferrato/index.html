Casale Monferrato is a town in the Piedmont region of north-western Italy, in the province of Alessandria, on the right bank of the river Po. Historically it was the capital of the Marquisate of Montferrat and one of the most strongly fortified towns in Italy.

History

Casale grew around an early medieval settlement and a monastery dedicated to Saint Evasius. It became a free commune in the 12th century, passed to the marquises of Montferrat, and was contested by Milan, Savoy, Spain and France. The Gonzaga of Mantua inherited Montferrat in the 16th century. The town endured famous sieges in 1628–1630 during the War of the Mantuan Succession and again in the 1640s and 1650s. It passed to the House of Savoy in the early 18th century and became part of the Kingdom of Sardinia and then of united Italy.

Main sights

The Romanesque cathedral of Sant'Evasio, consecrated in 1107, is noted for its unusual narthex. The town also preserves a castle of the Paleologi, the church of San Domenico, and one of the oldest synagogues in Piedmont, built in 1595. In the 20th century Casale was a centre of cement and asbestos-cement production; the Eternit plant became the focus of a major public-health scandal and trial over asbestos-related disease.
