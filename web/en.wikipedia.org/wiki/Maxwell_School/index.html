Maxwell School of Citizenship and Public Affairs

The Maxwell School of Citizenship and Public Affairs (commonly known as the Maxwell School) is Syracuse University's home for professional degree programs in public administration and international relations, as well as for undergraduate, master's, and doctoral degrees in the social sciences. It is located in Syracuse, New York.

History

The Maxwell School was founded in 1924 after a large donation by George Holmes Maxwell, a Syracuse alumnus and Boston patent attorney. It was the first professional school of public administration in the United States, and its Master of Public Administration (M.P.A.) program, founded by William E. Mosher, is the country's oldest continuously accredited M.P.A. program. The school's founding dean was William E. Mosher.

The school is housed in Maxwell Hall and Eggers Hall on the Syracuse University campus. Maxwell Hall, dedicated in 1937, includes a reproduction of the Athenian agora and an inscription from Pericles' funeral oration.

Academics

The Maxwell School's public affairs programs have consistently ranked among the best in the United States; its M.P.A. program has frequently been ranked number one in the U.S. News & World Report rankings of public affairs graduate schools. The school offers programs in public administration, international relations, economics, political science, history, anthropology, geography, sociology, and social science, as well as interdisciplinary centers and institutes such as the Campbell Public Affairs Institute, the Lerner Center for Public Health Promotion, and the Institute for Veterans and Military Families (housed within the university).

Notable alumni and faculty

Alumni include numerous members of Congress, governors, ambassadors, and public officials. The school's faculty have included prominent scholars across the social sciences, and its alumni network is particularly strong in Washington, D.C., and in international organizations.
