Grammos

The Grammos massif, one of the wildest and least populated areas of mainland Greece.

Grammos (Greek: Γράμμος; Albanian: Gramoz) is a mountain range in northwestern Greece, on the border with Albania. Part of the northern Pindus range, it spans the regional units of Ioannina (Epirus) and Kastoria (Western Macedonia). Its highest summit, Tsouka Petsik, rises to 2,520 metres, making it among the highest mountains in Greece. The Aliakmonas, Greece's longest river, rises in the Grammos area, and the massif's dense forests of black pine, beech and fir form part of one of the country's most important refuges for brown bears, wolves and other wildlife, adjacent to the Northern Pindus National Park.

Geography and settlements

The range is remote and sparsely populated. Villages on its slopes include Grammousta, Aetomilitsa (once among the largest Aromanian (Vlach) villages in Greece), Pyrsogianni and Plikati. Seasonal transhumance shaped the area's culture for centuries. The mountain lake Gistova, near the summit, is one of the highest alpine lakes in Greece. Grammos is also known for the Arrenes lakes and for World War II-era and Civil War-era remains scattered across its ridges.

Greek Civil War

Grammos occupies a central place in the history of the Greek Civil War (1946–1949). Together with neighbouring Mount Vitsi, it formed the last stronghold of the communist Democratic Army of Greece (DSE), which depended on supply routes from Albania and Yugoslavia. The Greek National Army's attempts to clear the mountain in Operation Coronis (1948) failed at heavy cost; the guerrillas temporarily withdrew into Albania and returned. In August 1949, the government forces' final offensive, Operation Torch (Pyrsos), employing artillery, air power including napalm, and large troop concentrations, captured Vitsi and then Grammos. The fall of Grammos at the end of August 1949 effectively ended the war, and thousands of fighters and villagers fled across the border into Albania and the Eastern Bloc.

Nature protection

Much of the massif is included in the Natura 2000 network, and it supports significant populations of large mammals and raptors. The relative absence of roads and development has made Grammos a destination for mountaineers and a focus of conservation debate, including proposals for a cross-border protected area with Albania.
