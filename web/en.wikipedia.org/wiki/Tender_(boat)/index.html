Tender (boat)


A ship's tender carrying passengers ashore

A tender is a small boat used to service or support a larger vessel, generally by transporting people or supplies to and from shore or another ship. The term covers several related usages in recreational boating, commercial shipping and naval service.

Ship's tender

A ship's tender — often called a dinghy when small and carried aboard — ferries crew, passengers and stores between the parent vessel and the shore when the ship is at anchor or too large to dock. Cruise ships use sizeable lifeboat-tenders ("tender boats") to land passengers at ports lacking deep-water berths, a process known as tendering; such craft are typically enclosed, carry a hundred or more passengers, and double as lifeboats.

Support vessels

Larger dedicated vessels that support others are also called tenders: submarine tenders and destroyer tenders supply and repair warships; lighthouse tenders maintained aids to navigation; and seaplane tenders supported patrol aircraft. Historically, colliers and stores ships performed analogous roles.

Yacht tenders

On yachts, the tender is the runabout, often a rigid inflatable boat, used for shore excursions, watersports and provisioning, stored on davits, in a garage, or towed. Small sailing dinghies carried aboard cruising yachts serve the same everyday function of reaching shore from an anchorage.
