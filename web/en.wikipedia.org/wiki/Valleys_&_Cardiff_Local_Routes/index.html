Valleys & Cardiff Local Routes

LocaleCardiff and the South Wales Valleys, Wales
Transit typeCommuter rail
OperatorTransport for Wales Rail
Core hubCardiff Central and Cardiff Queen Street

Valleys & Cardiff Local Routes (often called the Valley Lines) is the network of suburban and commuter railway lines radiating from Cardiff into the South Wales Valleys and the Vale of Glamorgan. The network is one of the busiest regional rail systems in the United Kingdom relative to its size and forms the core of the planned South Wales Metro.

Lines

The network includes the Rhymney line, the Merthyr line (with its Aberdare and Treherbert branches), the Rhondda line, the City line, the Coryton line, the Cardiff Bay line, the Ebbw Valley Railway, and the Vale of Glamorgan line to Barry, Penarth, and Bridgend. Services converge on Cardiff Queen Street and Cardiff Central stations, linked since the 2010s under a unified timetable.

History

Most of the lines were built in the nineteenth century to carry coal from the Valleys to the docks at Cardiff, Barry, and Newport. Passenger services survived the decline of the coal industry and were revitalised from the 1980s with new stations and reopened lines, including the Ebbw Valley line in 2008.

South Wales Metro

Under the South Wales Metro programme, the core Valley Lines were transferred from Network Rail to Transport for Wales in 2020 for electrification and conversion to light-metro style operation with new tram-train and tri-mode rolling stock.
