List of most massive stars

This is a list of the most massive stars that have been discovered, in units of solar masses (M☉). The masses listed are initial masses — the mass of the star when it formed — which for the most extreme stars can substantially exceed their present-day masses, since very massive stars lose enormous amounts of material through stellar winds.

Mass estimates for the most massive stars are model-dependent and uncertain. The most massive known stars are found in dense young star clusters such as the Arches Cluster and the Tarantula Nebula's R136 cluster in the Large Magellanic Cloud. Theoretical arguments suggest an upper limit to stellar mass of roughly 150–300 solar masses, related to the Eddington luminosity at which radiation pressure overcomes gravity.

Mass measurement

Stellar masses are most reliably measured in binary systems, where Kepler's laws yield masses directly. For single massive stars, masses are inferred by fitting atmospheric models to spectra and computing luminosities, then comparing with evolutionary tracks. Luminous blue variables such as the Pistol Star and Eta Carinae are among the most massive stars known in the Milky Way, though their initial masses have been revised as models improved.
