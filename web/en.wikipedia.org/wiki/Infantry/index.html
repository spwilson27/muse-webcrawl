Infantry

Infantry is a military specialisation of soldiers who engage in fighting on foot, as the primary combat arm of most armies. Distinguished from cavalry, artillery and armoured forces, infantry has been the core of armies throughout history — from the hoplites of ancient Greece and the legions of Rome to modern mechanised and airborne units. Although they make up a relatively small share of modern armed forces' personnel, infantry typically suffer the heaviest casualties and undertake the most physically demanding duties, including holding ground, clearing terrain and fighting in close combat.

History

Foot soldiers dominated warfare from antiquity: Egyptian, Assyrian and Persian armies fielded massed spearmen and archers, while Greek hoplites fought in the dense phalanx formation and Rome built an empire on the disciplined heavy infantry of its legions. In the Middle Ages, improving infantry — Swiss pikemen, English longbowmen — eroded the supremacy of mounted knights. The introduction of gunpowder weapons transformed infantry tactics again: lines of musketeers with bayonets, drilled in formations such as the tercio and later line and column, became the decisive arm of early modern armies.

The 20th century brought dispersed, firepower-centred infantry tactics: infiltration tactics in the First World War, combined-arms mechanisation in the Second, and extensive use of light, airborne and special-purpose infantry thereafter. Helicopters gave rise to air assault infantry, and armoured personnel carriers and infantry fighting vehicles to mechanised infantry.

Modern infantry

A modern infantryman carries a substantial load — rifle, ammunition, body armour, communications and navigation equipment, rations and water often totalling 30–50 kg. The basic tactical unit is the squad or section, grouped into platoons, companies, battalions and brigades. Contemporary doctrine emphasises networked communications, night-fighting capability, support from crew-served weapons such as machine guns and mortars, and integration with armour, artillery, engineers and air support.

Types of infantry

  • Line infantry — the historical backbone of armies, fighting in formed bodies.
  • Light infantry — lightly equipped, highly mobile troops, historically skirmishers.
  • Mechanised and motorised infantry — transported by armoured fighting vehicles or trucks.
  • Airborne and air assault infantry — delivered by parachute or helicopter.
  • Marine infantry (naval infantry) — sea-based forces for amphibious operations.
  • Mountain infantry — trained and equipped for high-altitude warfare.
