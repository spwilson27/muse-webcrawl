Occupation (profession)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An occupation is a person's job or profession — the work or principal activity that someone does to earn a living or to occupy their time, such as being a teacher, carpenter, physician, farmer or software engineer. The term is used in statistics, censuses and labour economics to classify the working population, and in everyday language it is roughly synonymous with "job", "trade" or "profession", though a profession usually implies specialised training and a recognised qualification.

Classification

National statistical agencies group occupations into standard classification systems, such as the International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO) maintained by the International Labour Organization, the Standard Occupational Classification in the United States and the United Kingdom, and similar schemes elsewhere. These systems typically divide work into major groups — managers, professionals, technicians, clerical workers, service and sales workers, agricultural workers, craft and trades workers, plant and machine operators, and elementary occupations — each subdivided into more detailed categories.

Occupation and identity

Beyond economics, occupation has long been a component of social identity. Historical surnames often derive from ancestors' occupations (Smith, Baker, Taylor, Schmidt, Lefèvre). Censuses have recorded occupation since the nineteenth century, and occupational information is widely used in genealogy, epidemiology (to study occupational health and disease) and sociology (to measure social class and mobility).

Occupational health

Different occupations carry different risks and health profiles, and the study of these is the field of occupational health and safety. Regulators set standards for exposure to hazards — noise, dust, chemicals, repetitive strain — according to the occupations affected.
