Wii U GamePad

The Wii U GamePad is the main controller for Nintendo's Wii U home video game console. It is a tablet-like controller featuring an embedded 6.2-inch touchscreen display, which can be used to supplement or substitute the display on a television set, along with traditional analog sticks, buttons, and motion sensing.

Features

The GamePad communicates wirelessly with the console using a proprietary variant of Wi-Fi, designed for low latency so that the image streamed to its screen is effectively lag-free. It includes a front-facing camera, a microphone, stereo speakers, a sensor strip for Wii Remotes, an NFC reader/writer, and a stylus. A notable use is "Off-TV Play", in which supported games can be played entirely on the GamePad screen while the television is used for something else.

History

Nintendo unveiled the GamePad alongside the Wii U at E3 2011 and demonstrated it again in revised form at E3 2012, when the analog sliders of the prototype were replaced with full analog sticks. The console and controller launched in November 2012. Critics praised the low-latency streaming and the versatility of the second screen, though the GamePad's short battery life (around three to five hours) was criticised; Nintendo later released a higher-capacity battery pack.
