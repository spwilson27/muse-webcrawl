Baltica

Baltica is a palaeocontinent that formed in the late Proterozoic and now constitutes much of northern and eastern Europe: the East European Craton, including Scandinavia, the Baltic states, and European Russia west of the Urals. It takes its name from the Baltic Sea region.

Formed~2 billion years ago (cratonic cores older)
Today part ofNorthern and Eastern Europe
Key eventsCollision with Avalonia and Laurentia; Caledonian orogeny

History

Baltica assembled from three ancient cratonic fragments — Fennoscandia, Sarmatia, and Volgo-Uralia — sutured by about 1.8–1.7 billion years ago, and for a time formed part of the supercontinents Columbia, Rodinia, and Pannotia. In the early Palaeozoic it drifted as an independent continent separated from Laurentia by the Iapetus Ocean. During the Silurian–Devonian Caledonian orogeny it collided with Avalonia and then Laurentia, forming the Old Red Sandstone continent (Euramerica/Laurussia).

Later evolution

Laurussia later joined Gondwana to form Pangaea. Since Pangaea's breakup, Baltica's core has remained relatively stable beneath the East European Platform, fringed by the Scandinavian Caledonides and the Ural Mountains, the latter marking its suturing to Siberia.
