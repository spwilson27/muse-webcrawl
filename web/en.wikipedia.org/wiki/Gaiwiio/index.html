Gaiwiio

The Gaiwiio (Seneca: "the Good Word" or "Good Message") is the religious code of the Longhouse Religion, revealed to the Seneca prophet Handsome Lake (Sganyadaiyo) beginning in 1799 and still recited and practised among the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) peoples of New York and Ontario. It forms one of the teachings through which Handsome Lake revitalised Seneca society after the devastation of the American Revolutionary War era.

The visions

In 1799, after years of illness and alcoholism, Handsome Lake experienced a series of visions in which messengers of the Creator (the Four Angels) instructed him to preach a moral code combining traditional Haudenosaunee ceremonies — the seasonal thanksgiving festivals, the Great Feather Dance, and confession and repentance — with new teachings against alcohol, witchcraft, abortion and the sale of land, and in favour of family stability and limited adoption of settler agriculture and schooling.

The code

The Gaiwiio is preserved orally and recited in full over several days at longhouse gatherings, traditionally in the Seneca language. A written version, the Code of Handsome Lake, was recorded by the anthropologist Arthur C. Parker and published in 1913. Adherents, called Longhouse people, continue to hold ceremonies at longhouses on the Seneca, Onondaga, Cayuga and other territories, and the code remains a living scripture and cultural foundation.
