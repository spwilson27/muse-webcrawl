Missional living

A missional community gathering

Missional living is a Christian concept, often described as "adopting the posture, thinking, behaviours, and practices of a missionary in everyday life" in order to engage one's local community with the gospel. The term is associated with the missional church movement that emerged from discussions of missiology and the church in post-Christendom Western culture in the late 20th century.

Concept

Rooted in the idea of the missio Dei (mission of God), missional living frames every believer, not just professional missionaries, as sent into their neighbourhoods and workplaces. Practices commonly emphasised include hospitality, incarnational presence in a particular place, service, and forming small "missional communities" that organise their shared life around outreach.

Background

The movement draws on theologians such as Lesslie Newbigin and writers including Alan Hirsch and Michael Frost (The Shaping of Things to Come, 2003), and is related to the emerging church conversation.
