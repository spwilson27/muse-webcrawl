Svetambara

Śvetāmbara (Sanskrit: श्वेतांबर, "white-clad") is one of the two main traditions of Jainism, the other being Digambara ("sky-clad"). Svetambara monks and nuns wear white robes, in contrast to Digambara monks, who practise nudity as a sign of complete renunciation. The division is traditionally traced to a schism in the centuries following Mahavira, though it hardened gradually over the first millennium CE.

Beliefs and practices

Svetambaras differ from Digambaras on several doctrinal points: they hold that women can attain liberation (moksha) in a female body, that the 19th Tirthankara Mallinatha was female, that omniscient beings still take food, and that the canonical scriptures (the Agamas) preserved in their tradition are authentic. Monks wear simple white garments, carry an alms bowl and a soft broom (rajoharana) to avoid harming insects, and often wear a mouth shield (muhapatti) to avoid inhaling small organisms. Unlike Digambara monks, they may possess multiple sets of clothing and scriptures.

Sub-traditions

The tradition includes image-worshipping Murtipujaka communities — the majority — and the non-image-worshipping Sthanakavasi and Terapanth orders that arose in later reform movements. Svetambaras are concentrated historically in Gujarat and Rajasthan, though communities now exist worldwide. Major festivals include Paryushana, shared with Digambaras though observed on different dates.
