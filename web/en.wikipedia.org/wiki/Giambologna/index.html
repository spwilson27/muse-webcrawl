Giambologna (1529 – 13 August 1608), also known as Jean de Boulogne (French), Jehan de Boulogne (Flemish) and Giovanni Bologna (Italian), was the last great Italian Renaissance sculptor, with a large workshop producing large and small works in bronze and marble in a late Mannerist style.

Life

Born in Douai, then in the Habsburg Netherlands, Giambologna studied in Antwerp before travelling to Rome in 1550 to study classical statuary. On his way back north in 1552 he passed through Florence, where he found employment and remained for the rest of his life, becoming court sculptor to the Medici grand dukes Francesco I and Ferdinando I. The Medici discouraged him from leaving, and he never saw Flanders again.

Works

His most famous works include the marble Rape of the Sabine Women (1579–1583) in the Loggia dei Lanzi, a tour de force of intertwined figures composed in a spiral (figura serpentinata) that rewards viewing from every angle; the equestrian monuments to Cosimo I and Ferdinando I in Florence, which established a model followed across Europe; the Fountain of Neptune in Bologna (1563–1566); and numerous bronze statuettes, including the flying Mercury, which were produced in multiple versions by his workshop and sent as diplomatic gifts.

Legacy

Giambologna's workshop system, with assistants casting and finishing his models, disseminated his inventions throughout Europe, and his multi-figure compositions influenced Baroque sculpture, notably Gian Lorenzo Bernini.
