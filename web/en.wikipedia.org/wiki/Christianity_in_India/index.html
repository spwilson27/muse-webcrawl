Christianity in India

Christianity in India is the third-largest religion in India, following Hinduism and Islam, with roughly 2.3 percent of the population — about 28 million followers according to the 2011 census. Its roots in the subcontinent are traditionally traced to the arrival of the apostle Thomas in the first century AD, making Indian Christianity among the oldest Christian traditions in the world.

History

According to tradition, St. Thomas landed at Muziris (near modern Kodungallur, Kerala) in AD 52 and founded seven churches along the Malabar Coast. The community he founded, known as the Saint Thomas Christians or Nasrani, maintained links with the Church of the East in Persia for over a millennium. By the fourth century, organized Christian communities existed in southwest India, and the sixth-century traveller Cosmas Indicopleustes described churches in the region.

European colonialism brought new forms of Christianity. Portuguese missionaries arrived after Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498, and Roman Catholic missions spread along the western coast, notably in Goa. The arrival of Francis Xavier in 1542 marked a major phase of evangelization. Protestant missions followed in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, with figures such as Bartholomäus Ziegenbalg in Tranquebar (1706) and William Carey in Bengal translating the Bible into numerous Indian languages and promoting education and social reform.

Demographics

Christians form majorities in the northeastern states of Nagaland, Mizoram, and Meghalaya, and significant minorities in Kerala, Goa, Tamil Nadu, and Andhra Pradesh. The largest denominations include the Roman Catholic Church, the Church of South India, the Church of North India, the Syro-Malabar Church, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, and numerous Pentecostal and independent churches, which have grown rapidly since the late twentieth century.

Contributions

Indian Christians have contributed prominently to education, healthcare, and social services, operating many of the country's best-known schools, colleges, and hospitals. Christian institutions were pioneers of girls' education, printing, and modern nursing in India.
