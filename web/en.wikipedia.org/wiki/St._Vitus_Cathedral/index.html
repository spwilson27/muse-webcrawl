St. Vitus Cathedral

St. Vitus Cathedral Location: Prague Castle, Prague, Czech Republic
Denomination: Roman Catholic
Founded: 1344 (current church)
Consecrated: 1929
Style: Gothic and Neo-Gothic
Architects: Matthias of Arras, Peter Parler

The Metropolitan Cathedral of Saints Vitus, Wenceslaus and Adalbert — commonly St. Vitus Cathedral — is a Roman Catholic cathedral within Prague Castle and the seat of the Archbishop of Prague. It is the largest and most important church in the Czech Republic and a pre-eminent example of Gothic architecture.

St. Vitus Cathedral from the south, dominating the Prague Castle complex

History

A rotunda dedicated to St Vitus was founded on the site by Duke Wenceslaus I around 930. The present church was begun in 1344 under Emperor Charles IV, with Matthias of Arras and then Peter Parler as master builders. Construction stalled during the Hussite wars and the cathedral stood half-finished for centuries; it was completed only in the 19th–20th century neo-Gothic campaigns and consecrated in 1929.

Features

Notable elements include the St Wenceslas Chapel, housing the relics of the saint and the door to the Crown Chamber where the Bohemian Crown Jewels are kept; the mosaic of the Last Judgment over the Golden Gate; the great south tower; and, in the newer part, Alphonse Mucha's stained-glass window depicting Slavic history. The cathedral contains the tombs of many Bohemian kings and Holy Roman Emperors.
