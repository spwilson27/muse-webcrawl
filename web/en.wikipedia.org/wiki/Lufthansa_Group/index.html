The Lufthansa Group is a German aviation group led by Deutsche Lufthansa AG, one of the world's largest airline groups by revenue and passengers carried. Its passenger-airline segment comprises the network carriers Lufthansa Airlines, SWISS, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, and Eurowings, together with stakes in other carriers; the group also operates major businesses in cargo (Lufthansa Cargo), maintenance and repair (Lufthansa Technik), and catering (LSG Group).

History

The present company was founded in Cologne in 1953 as a successor — though not the legal heir — to the pre-war Deutsche Luft Hansa, and began scheduled flights in 1955. It grew with West Germany's economy, rejoined long-haul networks in the 1960s, and was fully privatised by 1997. Lufthansa co-founded the Star Alliance in 1997 with United Airlines, Air Canada, SAS, and Thai Airways. From the 2000s it built its multi-hub group through the acquisitions of Swiss International Air Lines (2005), Austrian Airlines (2009), and Brussels Airlines (completed 2016), and in 2024–2025 agreed to take a minority stake in Italy's ITA Airways.

Operations

The group's network airlines run hubs at Frankfurt and Munich (Lufthansa), Zurich (SWISS), Vienna (Austrian), and Brussels, while Eurowings concentrates on European point-to-point services. Miles & More, the group's frequent-flyer programme, is among Europe's largest loyalty schemes. The fleet numbers several hundred aircraft, centred on Airbus and Boeing widebodies for long-haul routes.
