James of St George (c. 1230 – 1309), also known as Master James of Saint George (French: Maître Jacques de Saint-Georges), was a master mason and military architect from Savoy. Brought to England by Edward I, he designed the great concentric castles built to secure the conquest of Wales — including Conwy, Harlech, Caernarfon and Beaumaris — which are among the finest works of medieval military architecture in Europe.

Origins

James came from Saint-Prex on Lake Geneva, in the county of Savoy, where he worked on castles for Peter II and Philip I of Savoy, including Yverdon and Chillon. His Savoyard background is visible in details of his Welsh castles, such as spiral staircases rising in one direction and distinctive window and latrine arrangements.

Works in Wales

Edward I employed James from 1278, first on the castles of the first Welsh war, Flint and Rhuddlan. After the conquest of 1282–83 he was master mason of the king's works in Wales, commanding enormous resources — thousands of labourers and craftsmen drawn from across England. His masterpieces are the great castles and town walls of Conwy (1283–87), Harlech, Caernarfon with its polygonal towers inspired by Constantinople, and Beaumaris (begun 1295), the most perfectly symmetrical concentric castle of the age. He also worked in Scotland and on Gascon castles late in life.

Legacy

Four of his Welsh castles form the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd". James of St George is regarded as possibly the greatest military architect of the Middle Ages, and his career illustrates how expertise moved across thirteenth-century Europe.
