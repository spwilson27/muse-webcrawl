Küy

A küy (Kazakh: күй; also küi or kyui) is a traditional instrumental composition of the Kazakh and Kyrgyz peoples, performed on the dombra (a long-necked two-stringed lute) or the kobyz. Küys are programmatic pieces: each carries a title and often an associated legend or story that the performer relates before playing.

Characteristics

Küys are short but structurally rich solo pieces, typically in lively rhythms with passages imitating the sounds of nature, galloping horses, birdsong or human emotion. The tradition holds that a küyshi (küy performer-composer) narrates the story behind the piece — tales of heroes, historical events, laments and celebrations — so that the music functions as a form of oral history for a nomadic culture.

Famous küyshi

The most celebrated composer of küys is Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly (1818–1889), whose compositions such as "Saryarka" and "Adai" remain staples of the repertoire. Other major figures include Dauletkerei Shygaiuly, Tattimbet Kazangapuly, and in the 20th century Nurgisa Tlendiyev. Kurmangazy's image appears on the Kazakh 5-tenge note and he is honoured by the Kazakh National Conservatory in Almaty, which bears his name.

Heritage

The traditional art of the Kazakh dombra küy was inscribed in 2014 on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
