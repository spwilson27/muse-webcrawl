Seagram Company Ltd. was a Canadian multinational beverage and entertainment conglomerate, for much of the 20th century the world's largest distiller of alcoholic beverages. Founded on a Waterloo, Ontario distillery established in 1857 and named for Joseph E. Seagram, the company was built into an empire by Samuel Bronfman, whose Distillers Corporation acquired it in 1928. Seagram's brands — among them Seagram's VO, Seagram's 7 Crown, Crown Royal and Chivas Regal — dominated the post-Prohibition North American whisky market.

History

The Bronfmans, a Jewish immigrant family from Bessarabia, made their fortune supplying liquor during Canadian and American Prohibition; Samuel Bronfman's Distillers Corporation–Seagrams Ltd. then positioned itself for the legal U.S. market when Prohibition was repealed in 1933, building huge inventories of aged whisky and cultivating an image of respectable moderation ("Drink Seagram's and be sure"). The company diversified into oil and gas, and from 1981 held a major stake in DuPont, for a time its most valuable asset.

Entertainment pivot and breakup

Under Edgar Bronfman Jr., Seagram sold its DuPont stake in 1995 to buy control of MCA/Universal, then acquired PolyGram in 1998, transforming itself into an entertainment company. In 2000, Seagram was sold to Vivendi in an all-stock merger creating Vivendi Universal; the drinks business was split between Pernod Ricard and Diageo, ending the 143-year-old enterprise. The family's stewardship of Seagram, and the deal's destruction of Bronfman wealth as Vivendi shares fell, is a celebrated case study in corporate history.

Legacy

Seagram's New York headquarters, the Seagram Building (1958) on Park Avenue, designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe with Philip Johnson, is a landmark of International Style architecture. Many Seagram brands survive under Diageo and Pernod Ricard ownership.
