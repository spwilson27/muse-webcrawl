St. Clair's defeat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

St. Clair's defeat, also known as the Battle of the Wabash, the Battle of Wabash River or (in American Indian accounts) the victory of the Western Confederacy, was fought on 4 November 1791 in the Northwest Territory, in present-day Ohio, between the United States Army under Major General Arthur St. Clair and a confederacy of Native American forces led by Little Turtle of the Miami and Blue Jacket of the Shawnee.

Battle

St. Clair's poorly supplied expedition, advancing to establish forts in Indian country, was surprised at dawn in its encampment near the headwaters of the Wabash River. Over 600 of the roughly 1,000 American soldiers were killed, together with many camp followers — proportionally the worst defeat the United States Army ever suffered at Native American hands, with a casualty rate far exceeding that of Little Bighorn.

Aftermath

The disaster prompted the first congressional investigation in American history, the expansion of the army as the Legion of the United States under Anthony Wayne, and eventually Wayne's victory at Fallen Timbers in 1794 and the Treaty of Greenville.
