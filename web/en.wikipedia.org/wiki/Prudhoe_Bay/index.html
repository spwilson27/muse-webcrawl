Prudhoe Bay
Location: North Slope, Alaska, US
Coordinates: about 70.3° N, 148.7° W
Named: 1826, after Cape Prudhoe
Known for: Largest oil field in North America

Prudhoe Bay is a bay on the northern coast of Alaska, on the Arctic Ocean, and the name given to the unincorporated community and oil-production complex beside it on Alaska's North Slope. The bay was named in 1826 by the British explorer Frederick William Beechey after Cape Prudhoe.

Oil field

The Prudhoe Bay Oil Field, discovered on 12 March 1968 by the Atlantic Richfield Company (ARCO) and Humble Oil, is the largest oil field in North America, originally estimated to hold some 25 billion barrels of oil, of which roughly 13 billion were considered recoverable. Production began in 1977 following the completion of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, which carries crude oil 800 miles (1,300 km) south to the ice-free port of Valdez.

Output peaked at about 2 million barrels per day in 1988 — then roughly a quarter of United States production — and has since declined to a few hundred thousand barrels per day. The field is now operated by Hilcorp, which acquired BP's stake in 2020, with ConocoPhillips and ExxonMobil as major partners.

Community and environment

The resident workforce, housed in industrial camps at Deadhorse near the Dalton Highway, numbers in the thousands but consists mostly of rotating shift workers. In 2006 corrosion in a transit pipeline caused the largest oil spill recorded on the North Slope, about 200,000 gallons, prompting major pipeline replacement. The surrounding coastal plain is habitat for caribou, grizzly bears and migratory birds.
