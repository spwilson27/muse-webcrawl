Æthelstan or Athelstan (c. 894 – 27 October 939) was King of the Anglo-Saxons from 924/925 and, after his conquest of York in 927, the first king to rule the whole of England directly. Grandson of Alfred the Great and son of Edward the Elder, he is regarded by many historians as effectively the first King of England and one of the greatest Anglo-Saxon monarchs.

Reign

Raised in Mercia by his aunt Æthelflæd, Æthelstan succeeded his father in 924, though his half-brother Ælfweard briefly held Wessex. Crowned at Kingston upon Thames in 925, he married one sister to Sihtric, the Viking king of York; when Sihtric died in 927, Æthelstan seized York and Northumbria, bringing all England under one ruler. At Eamont Bridge the kings of Scotland, Strathclyde and Wales submitted to him. When a coalition under Constantine II of Scotland and Olaf Guthfrithson of Dublin invaded in 937, Æthelstan crushed it at the Battle of Brunanburh, a victory celebrated in an Old English poem in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle.

Government and law

Æthelstan's court was a major centre of learning and religion, hosting scholars from Britain and the Continent. He issued extensive law codes, standardised the coinage (requiring a mint in every borough), and used grandiloquent titles such as rex totius Britanniae ("king of all Britain"). His diplomacy married his many half-sisters into the ruling houses of Europe, including to the future Holy Roman Emperor Otto I. Unmarried and childless, he was buried at Malmesbury Abbey, whose patron saint Aldhelm he venerated.
