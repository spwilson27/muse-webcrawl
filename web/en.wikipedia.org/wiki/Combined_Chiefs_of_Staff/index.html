Combined Chiefs of Staff

The Combined Chiefs of Staff (CCS) was the supreme Allied staff body of the United States and the United Kingdom during World War II. Set up by President Franklin D. Roosevelt and Prime Minister Winston Churchill at the Arcadia Conference in Washington in December 1941 – January 1942, it coordinated the military strategy of the two nations for the remainder of the war.

Organization

The CCS comprised the British Chiefs of Staff Committee and the United States Joint Chiefs of Staff. It was headquartered in Washington, D.C., where the British maintained a permanent Joint Staff Mission. The committee reported to Roosevelt and Churchill acting jointly as heads of government.

Key figures included Admiral William D. Leahy, General George C. Marshall, Admiral Ernest J. King, and General Henry H. Arnold for the United States, and Field Marshal Sir Alan Brooke, Admiral Sir Dudley Pound (later Sir Andrew Cunningham), and Air Chief Marshal Sir Charles Portal for the United Kingdom, with Field Marshal Sir John Dill serving as the senior British representative in Washington until his death in 1944.

Role

Meeting at the great Allied conferences — Casablanca, Quebec, Tehran, Yalta, and Potsdam — the CCS settled grand strategy: the "Germany first" policy, the timing of the cross-Channel invasion (Operation Overlord), the allocation of landing craft and shipping, and the direction of the war against Japan. Subordinate combined bodies handled munitions assignments, shipping, and raw materials.

The CCS was a notable experiment in coalition warfare: unlike in the First World War, the Western Allies maintained a genuinely integrated strategic command structure throughout the conflict. It effectively ceased to function with the end of the war in 1945.
