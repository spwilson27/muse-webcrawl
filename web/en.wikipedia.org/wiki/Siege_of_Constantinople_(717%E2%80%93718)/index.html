Siege of Constantinople (717–718)

Date15 August 717 – 15 August 718
LocationConstantinople
ResultByzantine–Bulgar victory

The Siege of Constantinople of 717–718 was a major Arab attempt by the Umayyad Caliphate to conquer the Byzantine capital. A huge army under Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik crossed into Anatolia, while a fleet of some 1,800 ships sailed to blockade the city by sea.

Course of the siege

Emperor Leo III defended the city behind the Theodosian Walls, using Greek fire to destroy Arab ships and a great chain across the Golden Horn. A severe winter, disease, and starvation devastated the besiegers, and Leo's Bulgar allies under Tervel inflicted further losses on Arab foraging parties. After exactly a year, the caliph Umar II ordered a retreat; storms wrecked much of the returning fleet.

Significance

The failure ended large-scale Arab attempts on Constantinople and is often compared in importance to the Battle of Tours in halting early Islamic expansion into Europe.
