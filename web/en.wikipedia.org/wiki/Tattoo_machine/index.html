Tattoo machine

[Image: A coil tattoo machine with needle bar and tube]

A tattoo machine (often called a tattoo gun) is a hand-held device generally used to create a tattoo, a permanent marking of the skin with indelible ink. Modern tattoo machines use electromagnetic coils, a rotary motor, or pneumatic pressure to move a needle bar up and down, driving pigment into the dermis at frequencies ranging from about 50 to 3,000 times per minute.

History

The precursor of the tattoo machine was the electric pen invented by Thomas Edison in 1876, intended for creating stencils. In 1891 Samuel O'Reilly patented the first electric tattoo machine based on Edison's design, adding an ink reservoir. In 1929 Percy Waters patented a design recognisably close to the modern coil machine, and in 1979 Manfred Kohrs patented the rotary machine, reviving an older concept.

Types

Coil machines use one or two electromagnetic coils and a spring-mounted armature bar; they are commonly configured as liners, which use tighter needle groupings to draw lines, and shaders, which lay in colour and shading. Rotary machines use a small electric motor to drive the needle in a smoother cycle and are quieter and lighter. Pneumatic machines are powered by compressed air. Pen-style rotary cartridges, popular since the 2010s, combine motor and disposable needle cartridge in a single handpiece.

Operation

The artist controls depth and speed so that needles deposit ink roughly 1–2 mm into the skin, in the dermis, where pigment particles are too large for the immune system to remove. Machines are powered through a clip cord or RCA cable from a bench power supply, often with a foot switch. Modern practice pairs them with sterile, single-use needles and strict hygiene procedures.
