The Panzerkampfwagen IV (Pz.Kpfw. IV), commonly known as the Panzer IV, was a German medium tank developed in the late 1930s and used extensively in World War II. It was the most-produced German armoured fighting vehicle of the war and the only German tank to remain in continuous production throughout the conflict.

Development and service

Designed by Krupp as an infantry-support tank armed with a short-barrelled 75 mm howitzer, the Panzer IV was up-gunned from 1942 with the long-barrelled 7.5 cm KwK 40 after encounters with the Soviet T-34 and KV-1, becoming Germany's principal battle tank. Later variants added side skirts (Schürzen) and thicker armour.

Variants and legacy

Its chassis served as the basis for the Sturmgeschütz IV, Jagdpanzer IV, Brummbär, Wirbelwind, and other vehicles. After the war, Panzer IVs saw service with Finland, Spain, Bulgaria, and Syria — Syrian examples fought in the 1967 Six-Day War on the Golan Heights.
