The Río de la Plata (Spanish for "River of Silver") is the wide estuary formed by the confluence of the Paraná and Uruguay rivers between Argentina and Uruguay, opening into the Atlantic Ocean. Bordered by Buenos Aires on its southern shore and Montevideo near its mouth, it is often described as the widest river estuary in the world, reaching about 220 km across at its mouth.

Geography

The estuary extends about 290 km from the Paraná Delta northwest to the Atlantic. Its waters are shallow and heavy with sediment carried from the Paraná basin, giving them a characteristic brown colour; the channels to the ports of Buenos Aires and Montevideo require constant dredging.

History

Sebastian Cabot explored the river in the 1520s searching for a fabled source of silver, which gave the estuary its name and, indirectly, Argentina ("land of silver"). In December 1939 the estuary was the scene of the Battle of the River Plate, the first major naval battle of the Second World War, ending with the scuttling of the German pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee.
