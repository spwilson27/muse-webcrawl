Murray–Darling Basin

The Murray–Darling Basin is a large geographical area in the interior of southeastern Australia, encompassing the drainage basin of the tributaries of the Murray River and Darling River, Australia's two longest rivers. Covering about 1.06 million square kilometres — roughly one-seventh of the Australian continent — the basin spans most of New South Wales and Victoria, parts of Queensland and South Australia, and all of the Australian Capital Territory.

Agriculture and water use

The basin is Australia's most important agricultural region, producing a large share of the nation's food and fibre, including cotton, rice, wheat, dairy, fruit and wine grapes. Its rivers are among the most heavily regulated in the world, with major storages such as the Dartmouth and Hume dams and the Snowy Mountains Scheme diverting water for irrigation.

Environmental issues

Over-allocation of water, drought, rising salinity and algal blooms have severely stressed the basin's wetlands, including Ramsar-listed sites and the Coorong estuary at the Murray mouth. The Millennium Drought of the 2000s prompted the Water Act 2007 and the Murray–Darling Basin Plan of 2012, which set sustainable diversion limits and a programme of buying back water entitlements for the environment, administered by the Murray–Darling Basin Authority.
