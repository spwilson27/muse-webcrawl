Yeouido

Yeouido (여의도)
District: Yeongdeungpo, Seoul
Location: Island in the Han River
Known for: National Assembly, finance, broadcasting

Yeouido (Korean: 여의도, also spelled Youido or Yŏŭido) is a large island on the Han River in Seoul, South Korea, within Yeongdeungpo District. Sometimes called "Korea's Wall Street" or the nation's political heart, it hosts the National Assembly, the headquarters of major broadcasters and financial firms, and some of Seoul's tallest buildings.

Development

The low-lying island was used as pasture and, in the 1920s, as Seoul's first airfield. Large-scale development began in the late 1960s under Mayor Kim Hyun-ok: the riverbanks were embanked, a ring road built, and broad apartment and office blocks laid out. The National Assembly Building, with its distinctive domed roof, opened in 1975, and the island became the seat of the Korea Stock Exchange and broadcaster KBS. Landmarks include the 63 Building (1985), for a time Korea's tallest, the IFC Seoul complex, and the Parc.1 tower (2020). The headquarters of MBC and the giant Yeouido Full Gospel Church are also on the island.

Public life

Yeouido Park and the Han River parks (Hangang Park) make the island a leisure centre: its riverside cherry-blossom festival each spring and the Seoul International Fireworks Festival each autumn draw enormous crowds. The island's broad ceremonial square, Yeouido Plaza, hosted mass rallies and papal and state events. Subway Lines 5 and 9 serve the island, and the name "Yeouido" is used in Korean media as shorthand for the political establishment, much as "Westminster" is in Britain.
