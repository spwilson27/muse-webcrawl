Al-Amir bi-Ahkam Allah

Al-Āmir bi-Aḥkām Allāh
Reign: 1101 – 1130
Predecessor: al-Musta'li Billah
Successor: al-Hafiz (regent, then caliph)
Born: 31 December 1096, Cairo
Died: 7 October 1130, Cairo
Dynasty: Fatimid

Abū ʿAlī al-Manṣūr ibn al-Mustaʿlī (Arabic: أبو علي المنصور بن المستعلي), better known by his regnal name al-Āmir bi-Aḥkām Allāh (Arabic: الآمر بأحكام الله, lit. "The One Who Directs by God's Orders"), was the tenth Fatimid caliph, ruling from 1101 to his death in 1130, and the 20th imam of Musta'li Isma'ilism.

Reign

Al-Amir succeeded his father al-Musta'li Billah at the age of five. For the first twenty years of his reign, effective power lay with the viziers, first al-Afdal Shahanshah (assassinated in 1121) and then al-Ma'mun al-Bata'ihi. After al-Afdal's death, al-Amir increasingly exercised power himself. His reign saw continued Fatimid conflict with the Crusader states in the Levant, including the loss of Tyre in 1124.

Assassination and succession crisis

Al-Amir was assassinated on 7 October 1130, traditionally attributed to Nizari agents. His death without a surviving adult heir triggered a succession crisis: his cousin Abd al-Majid ruled as regent (later becoming caliph al-Hafiz), while the infant son al-Tayyib was proclaimed by his supporters, giving rise to the Tayyibi branch of Musta'li Isma'ilism, whose followers hold that al-Tayyib went into occultation.
