Inconel


Inconel components

Inconel is a family of austenitic nickel-chromium-based superalloys, a registered trademark of Special Metals Corporation. Inconel alloys are oxidation- and corrosion-resistant materials well suited to extreme environments subject to high pressure and temperature; they retain strength over a wide temperature range where aluminium and steel would succumb to creep.

Properties

On heating, Inconel forms a thick, stable, passivating oxide layer that protects the surface from further attack. Depending on the grade, strengthening comes from solid-solution effects of elements such as molybdenum and niobium or from precipitation hardening by intermetallic phases such as gamma double prime. The alloys are notoriously difficult to machine because they work harden rapidly.

Common grades and uses

Inconel 600 is used in chemical processing and furnace components. Inconel 625, strengthened by molybdenum and niobium, serves in marine and aerospace applications. Inconel 718, a precipitation-hardened grade, is one of the most widely used superalloys in jet engines, rocket motors, and gas turbines, and was used in the skin of the North American X-15 rocket plane. Inconel X-750 is used in turbines and nuclear reactors. Modern applications include 3D-printed rocket engine components and exhaust systems in high-performance motorsport.
