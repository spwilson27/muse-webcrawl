The politics of Mozambique take place in a semi-presidential republic in which the President is head of state and the Prime Minister heads the government. Legislative power is held by the unicameral Assembly of the Republic, with 250 deputies elected by proportional representation in provincial constituencies.

The Assembly of the Republic in Maputo.

FRELIMO dominance

Since independence from Portugal in 1975, Mozambique has been governed by FRELIMO (Mozambique Liberation Front), first as a Marxist one-party state under Samora Machel and then, after the 1990 constitution introduced multiparty politics, through regular elections. The main opposition has historically been RENAMO, the former rebel movement that fought FRELIMO in the civil war of 1977–1992 and returned to sporadic armed conflict between 2013 and 2016 before a final peace agreement in 2019.

Recent developments

President Filipe Nyusi (2015–2025) presided over the discovery and development of large offshore natural gas fields in the Rovuma Basin, though an Islamist insurgency in Cabo Delgado province from 2017 disrupted projects and displaced hundreds of thousands. FRELIMO's Daniel Chapo won the October 2024 general election, whose disputed results and the strong showing of independent candidate Venâncio Mondlane triggered months of nationwide protests.
