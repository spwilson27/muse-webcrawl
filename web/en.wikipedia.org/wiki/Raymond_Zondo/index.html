Raymond Zondo

Raymond Mnyamezeli Mlungisi Zondo (born 4 May 1960) is a South African jurist who served as Chief Justice of South Africa from 1 April 2022 until his retirement at the end of August 2024. He is widely known for chairing the Judicial Commission of Inquiry into State Capture, universally called the Zondo Commission.

Career

Born in Ixopo in Natal (now KwaZulu-Natal), Zondo worked in retail while studying law part-time, later practising labour law before joining the judiciary in 1997. He served in the Labour Court — including as its Judge President — and the North Gauteng High Court, and was appointed to the Constitutional Court in 2012, becoming Deputy Chief Justice in 2017.

State Capture Commission

Appointed in 2018 to investigate allegations of corruption and "state capture" during Jacob Zuma's presidency, Zondo presided over nearly four years of televised hearings. The commission's multi-volume report (2022) documented extensive corruption and recommended prosecutions and reforms. In 2021 the Constitutional Court, with Zondo prominent, sentenced Zuma to imprisonment for contempt for refusing to testify. Zondo succeeded Mogoeng Mogoeng as Chief Justice in April 2022 and retired at the mandatory age in 2024.
