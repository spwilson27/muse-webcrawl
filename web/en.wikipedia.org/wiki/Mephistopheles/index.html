Mephistopheles

Mephistopheles is a demon familiar from German folklore, best known as the devil with whom Faust makes his pact in the Faust legend, Christopher Marlowe's Doctor Faustus, and Goethe's Faust.

Origins

The name first appears in the 1587 Faustbuch. Etymology is uncertain; possibly from Hebrew mephitz (destroyer) and tophel (liar), or Greek meaning not loving light.

In Goethe

Goethe's Mephistopheles describes himself as part of that power which eternally wills evil and eternally works good. He is witty, cynical, and serves as Faust's companion for 24 years in exchange for his soul.

In culture

Appears in operas by Gounod, Boito and Berlioz, Liszt's Mephisto Waltzes, and countless modern adaptations.
