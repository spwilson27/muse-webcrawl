Wittenbergplatz is a square in the central Schöneberg district of Berlin, Germany, near the border with Charlottenburg, at the eastern end of Tauentzienstraße. One of the best-known squares in the "New West" (Neuer Westen) of Berlin, it is dominated by the Kaufhaus des Westens (KaDeWe), the largest department store in continental Europe, and by the ornate entrance building of the Wittenbergplatz U-Bahn station in the middle of the square.

History

The square was laid out between 1889 and 1892 and named after the Battle of Wittenberg of 1813 during the Wars of Liberation against Napoleon. It developed into a fashionable commercial centre of the expanding western suburbs of Berlin. The underground station, designed by Alfred Grenander, opened in 1902 as part of Berlin's first U-Bahn line; its grand entrance hall, completed in 1912–1913, is one of the most recognisable in the city.

The square's most famous landmark, the KaDeWe, opened in 1907 on the square's northern side and became a symbol of luxury retailing in Berlin. During the Nazi era, the department store's Jewish owners were forced out; the building was badly damaged by bombing in the Second World War — an American bomber crashed into it in 1943 — and was rebuilt after the war. In the divided city, Wittenbergplatz lay in West Berlin and KaDeWe became a showcase of western consumer goods.

Sights

KaDeWe's gourmet food department on the sixth floor is a famous attraction. Since 1990, the square has carried a memorial plaque at the U-Bahn station listing the names of Nazi concentration camps with the words "Places of terror that we must never forget" (Orte des Schreckens, die wir niemals vergessen dürfen). The station entrance has long been decorated with signs in the style of the London Underground given by the British sector authorities in the postwar period.

Transport

Wittenbergplatz station is served by U-Bahn lines U1, U2, and U3, making the square a significant interchange in Berlin's public transport network. Numerous bus lines also serve the square and Tauentzienstraße, one of Berlin's principal shopping streets, which runs from the square toward the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.
