Carl Andrew Spaatz (28 June 1891 – 14 July 1974) was an American military officer best known for his key role in the strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany during the Second World War, and as the first Chief of Staff of the independent United States Air Force.

Second World War

Spaatz commanded the Eighth Air Force in England from 1942, then the Northwest African Air Forces, the Fifteenth Air Force in Italy, and from January 1944 the US Strategic Air Forces in Europe, directing American daylight bombing alongside RAF Bomber Command under the Combined Bomber Offensive. In 1945 he transferred to the Pacific, where he directed the strategic bombing of Japan, including — on the orders of President Truman — the atomic bomb attacks.

Post-war

After serving as commanding general of the Army Air Forces, Spaatz became the first Chief of Staff of the newly independent US Air Force in 1947, retiring in 1948. He added the second "a" to the spelling of his surname (originally Spatz) to clarify its pronunciation.
