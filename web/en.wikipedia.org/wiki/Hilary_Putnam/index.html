Hilary Putnam

Hilary Whitehall Putnam (July 31, 1926 – March 13, 2016) was an American philosopher, mathematician, and computer scientist, and a central figure in analytic philosophy from the 1960s onward, especially in philosophy of mind, philosophy of language, and philosophy of science. He taught for most of his career at Harvard University.

Philosophy of mind

Putnam is associated with the rise of functionalism: in the 1960s he argued, using the idea of multiple realizability, that mental states are functional states rather than identical with particular brain states, drawing an analogy with Turing machines. He later became a prominent critic of the computational theory of mind he had helped found.

Philosophy of language

In "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975) Putnam defended semantic externalism with the Twin Earth thought experiment, arguing that "meanings just ain't in the head": what a term refers to depends partly on the speaker's environment and linguistic community. His division of linguistic labour and the causal theory of reference influenced a generation of philosophers of language.

Later thought

Putnam's views evolved repeatedly: he defended scientific realism, then developed "internal realism" in Reason, Truth and History (1981), and later moved toward a pragmatic, "second naïveté" realism. With Martha Nussbaum he criticised the fact–value dichotomy. His work on the brain-in-a-vat sceptical hypothesis remains widely discussed.
