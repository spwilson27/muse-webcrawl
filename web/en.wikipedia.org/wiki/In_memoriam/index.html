In memoriam

In memoriam
LanguageLatin
Literal meaning"Into the memory (of)"
UsageEpitaphs, obituaries, dedications, memorial segments

In memoriam is a Latin phrase meaning "in memory of", used to honour a person who has died. It appears in epitaphs and gravestones, obituary notices, dedications of books and works of art, and in the titles of memorial compositions and poems.

Uses

The phrase commonly introduces names in obituary columns and memorial services, and is used for endowment dedications — for example, scholarships, lectureships and library collections established in a person's memory. Award ceremonies such as the Academy Awards include an "In Memoriam" segment honouring industry figures who died in the preceding year.

In literature and music

The most famous literary use is Alfred, Lord Tennyson's long elegy In Memoriam A.H.H. (1850), written over seventeen years for his friend Arthur Henry Hallam, which popularised the phrase in English. Numerous composers have titled memorial works In memoriam, particularly for victims of war and disaster.
