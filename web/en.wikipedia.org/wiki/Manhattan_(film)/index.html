Manhattan is a 1979 American romantic comedy-drama film directed by Woody Allen and produced by Charles H. Joffe. Written by Allen and Marshall Brickman, it follows Isaac Davis (Allen), a twice-divorced television comedy writer whose tangled romantic life involves his teenage girlfriend Tracy (Mariel Hemingway), his intellectual friend Mary (Diane Keaton), and his best friend's infidelity.

Production

The film was shot in black and white by cinematographer Gordon Willis, opening with a celebrated montage of New York City set to Gershwin's Rhapsody in Blue. The score throughout draws on Gershwin compositions performed by the New York Philharmonic and the Buffalo Philharmonic.

Reception

Manhattan was a critical and commercial success and was nominated for two Academy Awards, including Best Supporting Actress for Hemingway. It is frequently cited among Allen's greatest works and one of the defining cinematic love letters to New York City.
