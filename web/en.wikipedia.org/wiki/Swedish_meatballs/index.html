Swedish meatballs

Swedish meatballs (Swedish: köttbullar, pronounced [ˈɕœtːˌbɵlːar]) are a traditional Swedish dish consisting of small meatballs made from a mixture of ground beef and pork (or sometimes veal), breadcrumbs soaked in milk, finely chopped onion, and seasonings such as salt, white pepper and allspice.

Serving

Swedish meatballs are traditionally served with creamy mashed potatoes, a rich brown cream gravy (gräddsås), lingonberry jam, and sometimes pickled cucumber. They are a staple of the Swedish smörgåsbord and of everyday home cooking (husmanskost).

History

Meatballs have a long history in Sweden. A popular (and contested) account holds that King Charles XII brought a version of the dish back from his exile in the Ottoman Empire in the early 18th century; in 2018 the official Swedish Twitter account acknowledged Turkish origins for the recipe, sparking debate. Regardless of origin, köttbullar became firmly established in Swedish cuisine during the 19th century, aided by the spread of the meat grinder. In the late 20th and 21st centuries, the furniture retailer IKEA popularised Swedish meatballs worldwide by serving them in its store restaurants.
