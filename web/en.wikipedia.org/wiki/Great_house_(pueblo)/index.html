Great house (pueblo)

Pueblo Bonito in Chaco Canyon, the largest of the great houses.

A great house is a large, multi-storied Ancestral Puebloan structure built during the height of the Chacoan culture of the American Southwest, roughly between AD 850 and 1150. Some 200 great houses are known, concentrated in Chaco Canyon in present-day New Mexico, with outlying examples across the San Juan Basin in Colorado, Utah and Arizona. They are distinguished from ordinary dwellings by their monumental scale, planned layout, core-and-veneer masonry, and association with great kivas and engineered roads.

Architecture

The largest great house, Pueblo Bonito in Chaco Canyon, rose four or five storeys and contained more than 600 rooms arranged in a D-shaped arc around a plaza with multiple great kivas. Builders used shaped sandstone blocks laid in distinctive banded styles, vast quantities of timber beams carried from mountain forests 50 miles or more away, and T-shaped doorways that became a Chacoan signature. Great houses were connected to outlying communities by the Chaco road system, broad straight roadways cut across the desert, sometimes with stairways carved into cliffs.

Purpose

Why the great houses were built remains debated. Relatively few burials and hearths suggest many were not ordinary residences for large populations; proposed functions include elite residences, ceremonial and pilgrimage centres, redistribution hubs storing maize and turquoise, astronomical observatories aligned to solar and lunar cycles, and public architecture expressing the integrative power of the Chacoan regional system. Construction ceased amid drought in the mid-12th century, and the Chaco world reorganised and dispersed.
