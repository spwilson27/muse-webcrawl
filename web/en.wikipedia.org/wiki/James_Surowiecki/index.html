Career

James Michael Surowiecki (born 30 April 1967) is an American journalist and author, long the business columnist of The New Yorker, where his "Financial Page" column ran from 2000 to 2021. Educated at the University of North Carolina and Yale (PhD work in American history), he previously wrote for New York magazine, Fortune, and Slate, and contributed to the Atlantic. His surname reflects Polish ancestry (pronounced roughly "sur-oh-WIK-ee").

The Wisdom of Crowds

Surowiecki's bestseller The Wisdom of Crowds (2004) argued that, under the right conditions — diversity of opinion, independence of judgement, decentralisation, and a means of aggregating views — large groups are remarkably intelligent, often outperforming their smartest members at estimation, prediction, and problem-solving. Its opening example is Francis Galton's 1906 observation that the median guess of a crowd at a county fair estimated an ox's weight almost exactly. The book popularised ideas behind prediction markets and collective intelligence, and is a standard reference in behavioural economics.
