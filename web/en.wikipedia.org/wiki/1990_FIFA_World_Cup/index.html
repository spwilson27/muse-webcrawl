The 1990 FIFA World Cup was the 14th edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in Italy from 8 June to 8 July 1990. West Germany defeated defending champions Argentina 1–0 in the final at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome, winning its third title in what was the West German team's last tournament before reunification.

Tournament summary

The tournament is often remembered as defensive and low-scoring — it averaged just 2.21 goals per game, a record low — and its negative tactics prompted FIFA to introduce the back-pass rule and three points for a win in subsequent competitions. Nevertheless, it produced celebrated stories: Cameroon's veteran striker Roger Milla led the "Indomitable Lions" to the quarter-finals, the first African team to reach that stage, after stunning Argentina in the opening match; host Italy's surprise hero Salvatore "Totò" Schillaci finished as top scorer and best player; and England's run to the semi-finals, ended by a penalty shoot-out defeat to West Germany, featured Paul Gascoigne's famous tears.

Final

The final, a rematch of 1986, was a fractious game in which Argentina had two players sent off — the first dismissals in a World Cup final. Andreas Brehme converted a controversial late penalty in the 85th minute to give Franz Beckenbauer's West Germany victory, making Beckenbauer the first person to win the World Cup as both captain and coach.

Legacy

Italia '90 is fondly recalled despite its football: the "Nessun dorma" theme performed by the Three Tenors, the tournament's design and atmosphere, and its role in reviving the English game's image after a decade of hooliganism all contributed to its enduring reputation as a cultural watershed for the sport.
