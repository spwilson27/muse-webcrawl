Small nuclear RNA

Small nuclear RNA (snRNA) is a class of small RNA molecules found in the nucleus of eukaryotic cells. They are transcribed by either RNA polymerase II or RNA polymerase III. snRNAs are involved in a variety of important processes such as RNA splicing (removal of introns from pre-mRNA), regulation of transcription factors and RNA polymerase II activity, and maintaining telomeres.

snRNAs are always found in association with specific proteins, and the complexes are referred to as small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNP, often pronounced "snurps"). Each snRNP particle is composed of one (or in the case of U4/U6, two) snRNA molecule(s) and several snRNP-specific proteins. The best-characterised snRNAs are the spliceosomal U RNAs: U1, U2, U4, U5 and U6, which assemble into the spliceosome, the large ribonucleoprotein machine that catalyses pre-mRNA splicing.

A second, minor (U12-type) spliceosome uses the analogous snRNAs U11, U12, U4atac and U6atac to remove a rare class of introns.

Other classes

Beyond the spliceosomal RNAs, the snRNA family includes the U7 snRNA, which directs the unique endonucleolytic processing of histone pre-mRNAs, and the nucleolar small RNAs (snoRNAs) — a related but distinct class that guide chemical modification (methylation and pseudouridylation) of ribosomal RNAs. Many snoRNAs are processed from introns of protein-coding genes.

Clinical significance

Autoantibodies against snRNP components (anti-Sm and anti-U1-RNP antibodies) are hallmarks of autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus and mixed connective tissue disease. Mutations affecting snRNA genes themselves, such as in the RNU4-2 gene (a U4atac component, ReNU syndrome), have been linked to neurodevelopmental disorders. Defects in the survival motor neuron (SMN) protein, which is required for snRNP assembly, cause spinal muscular atrophy.
