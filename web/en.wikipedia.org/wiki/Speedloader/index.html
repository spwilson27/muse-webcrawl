Speedloader

A speedloader is a device used to reduce the time and effort for reloading firearms. Speedloaders come in a variety of forms for reloading revolvers, or the magazines used with other types of firearms such as rifles and shotguns. Generally, speedloaders are used for loading all chambers of a revolver simultaneously; speedloaders are most commonly used for revolvers as manual-loading the chambers one by one is slower.

Revolver speedloaders

The classic revolver speedloader is a circular carrier holding a full cylinder's worth of cartridges in the same geometric arrangement as the chambers. The shooter opens the cylinder, inserts the tips of all cartridges at once, and releases them — depending on design, by twisting a knob, pressing a plunger, or flexing the carrier — then closes the cylinder. Well-known manufacturers include HKS and Safariland, whose push-type Comp loaders were long popular in police revolver use and revolver competition.

Variants

Related devices include the speed strip, a flat rubber or plastic strip holding cartridges in a line, which is slower but flatter and easier to carry; moon clips, thin steel rings that hold rimless cartridges for extraction and permit very fast reloads; and magazine loaders ("mag loaders") that ease loading pistol and rifle magazines by pushing cartridges down against spring pressure. Tube speedloaders serve some shotguns and lever-action rifles.

Use

Speedloaders became popular with the widespread adoption of double-action revolvers by police forces in the 20th century, and speed-loading technique remains central to revolver competition shooting disciplines such as ICORE and the revolver divisions of practical shooting sports.
