Gaza Strip

The Gaza Strip is a narrow strip of land on the eastern coast of the Mediterranean Sea, bordered by Israel to the north and east and Egypt to the southwest. Together with the West Bank, it forms the State of Palestine. It is about 41 km long and 6–12 km wide, with an area of about 365 km² and a population of over two million Palestinians, making it one of the most densely populated places on Earth.

History

Gaza has been inhabited for millennia and ruled in turn by Egyptians, Philistines, Assyrians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, Arab caliphates, Crusaders, Mamluks and Ottomans. After World War I it fell under the British Mandate for Palestine. Following the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Egypt administered the Strip; Israel captured it in the Six-Day War of 1967.

Under the Oslo Accords (1993–95), the Palestinian Authority took civil control of most of Gaza. Israel withdrew its settlements and forces in 2005. In 2007 Hamas took control after winning the 2006 elections and a conflict with Fatah. Israel and Egypt then imposed a blockade, citing security concerns.

Gaza war

After the Hamas-led attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, Israel launched a large-scale military campaign in Gaza, causing extensive destruction, mass displacement and a severe humanitarian crisis, and drawing international legal and diplomatic proceedings.

Geography and economy

The terrain is flat to rolling, with sand dunes near the coast. The economy has been severely constrained by blockade, conflict and high unemployment; many residents depend on humanitarian aid, largely coordinated by UNRWA. Governorates include North Gaza, Gaza, Deir al-Balah, Khan Yunis and Rafah.
