Twaron

Twaron is a trade name for a para-aramid synthetic fibre developed by the Dutch company AkzoNobel and now produced by Teijin Aramid. Chemically it is poly(paraphenylene terephthalamide) (PPTA), the same class of polymer as DuPont's Kevlar, developed independently in the 1970s. Twaron is a light, golden-yellow, high-strength fibre used where strength, heat resistance, and low weight are required.

History

Dutch researchers at Akzo developed the fibre in the 1970s under the name Arenka; it was commercialized in the 1980s as litigation with DuPont over aramid patents was resolved. Production is centred in the Netherlands, at Emmen and Delfzijl. The business passed to Teijin in 2000 as part of Teijin Aramid.

Properties and uses

Twaron has a tensile strength about five times that of steel at equal weight, excellent resistance to heat and abrasion, and no melting point (it decomposes above about 500 °C). Applications include ballistic vests and helmets, tires, belts and hoses, fibre-optic cables, ropes, composites for boats and aircraft, and protective gloves.
