The Russian Far East (Russian: Дальний Восток России, Dalny Vostok Rossii) is the easternmost part of Russia, comprising the Far Eastern Federal District between Lake Baikal/Yakutsk in the west and the Pacific Ocean in the east. It covers about 6.95 million square kilometres — roughly 40 per cent of Russia's territory — but is home to only about 8 million people. Its principal cities are Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Yakutsk, Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk, Blagoveshchensk, Magadan, Birobidzhan, Anadyr, and Komsomolsk-on-Amur.

Geography

The region spans the Pacific coast from Chukotka and Kamchatka in the northeast to Primorsky Krai in the south, and includes the island of Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. Major rivers include the Amur, which forms much of the border with China, and the Lena in the northwest. The terrain ranges from Arctic tundra and the volcanic Kamchatka Peninsula to the temperate forests of the Sikhote-Alin range, habitat of the Amur tiger and Amur leopard. Most of the region lies within taiga and permafrost zones and experiences monsoon-influenced summers in the south and extreme continental cold in the interior.

History

Russian explorers reached the Pacific in the 1630s–40s, founding Okhotsk in 1647. The Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689) fixed the border with Qing China along the Stanovoy range; the Treaties of Aigun (1858) and Peking (1860) transferred the left bank of the Amur and the lands east of the Ussuri to Russia, and Vladivostok was founded in 1860. The Trans-Siberian Railway (completed 1916) and its Chinese Eastern Railway shortcut across Manchuria tied the region to European Russia.

During the Russian Civil War the area saw Japanese, American, and other foreign intervention, and the buffer Far Eastern Republic existed from 1920 to 1922. In the Soviet period the region was industrialised and militarised — the Baikal–Amur Mainline was built largely by Gulag labour — and it saw fighting against Japan in 1938–39 (Khalkhin Gol, in neighbouring Mongolia) and the Soviet invasion of Manchuria in August 1945, after which southern Sakhalin and the Kurils were annexed.

Economy

The Far East is rich in natural resources: gold, diamonds (in Yakutia), coal, oil and natural gas (Sakhalin projects), timber, and fisheries in the Sea of Okhotsk and Bering Sea. Since the 2010s the Russian government has promoted development through the Eastern Economic Forum in Vladivostok, "Far Eastern hectare" free land grants, and advanced special economic zones. Population decline since 1991 remains a central concern.

Administration

The Far Eastern Federal District includes eleven federal subjects: the Amur, Jewish Autonomous, Magadan, and Sakhalin oblasts; the republic of Sakha (Yakutia) and Buryatia; the Zabaykalsky, Kamchatka, Primorsky, and Khabarovsk krais; and the Chukotka Autonomous Okrug. In December 2018 the district's administrative centre was moved from Khabarovsk to Vladivostok.
