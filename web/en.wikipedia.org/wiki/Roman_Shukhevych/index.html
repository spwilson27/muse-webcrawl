Roman Shukhevych

Roman-Taras Yosypovych Shukhevych (30 June 1907 – 5 March 1950) was a Ukrainian nationalist political and military leader. He was a leading figure in the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN) and, from 1943, commander-in-chief of the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA), which fought against both Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union during and after the Second World War. He operated under the pseudonym "Taras Chuprynka".

Early life

Shukhevych was born in Krakovets in Austrian Galicia into a family of Ukrainian activists. He studied civil engineering at Lviv Polytechnic and was an accomplished athlete and musician. As a youth he joined the Ukrainian Military Organization and later the OUN, participating in sabotage and assassination operations against the Polish state; he was imprisoned in the Bereza Kartuska detention camp and tried in the 1930s.

Second World War

In 1941 Shukhevych served as an officer in the German-sponsored Nachtigall Battalion, which entered Lviv ahead of the Wehrmacht. After the Germans arrested the OUN leadership for proclaiming Ukrainian statehood, he went underground. In 1943 he became head of the OUN's leadership on Ukrainian lands and commander of the UPA, directing its insurgency against the returning Soviet forces.

Death and legacy

Shukhevych was killed by Soviet MGB troops on 5 March 1950 in the village of Bilohorshcha near Lviv. He remains a deeply contested figure: honoured in Ukraine as a fighter for independence — he was posthumously awarded the title Hero of Ukraine in 2007, later annulled in court proceedings — while historians and neighbouring countries criticize the OUN and UPA for collaboration with Nazi Germany and for the massacres of Poles in Volhynia.
