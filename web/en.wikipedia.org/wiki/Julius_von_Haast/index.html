Julius von Haast

Sir Johann Franz Julius von Haast (1 May 1822 – 16 August 1887) was a German-born New Zealand explorer, geologist, naturalist and museum director. He carried out extensive geological surveys of the South Island of New Zealand and founded the Canterbury Museum in Christchurch.

Early life

Haast was born in Bonn, then part of the Kingdom of Prussia, and studied geology and mineralogy at the University of Bonn without completing a degree. He travelled in Europe as a collector and dealer in geological and zoological specimens. In 1858 he was commissioned to investigate emigration prospects in New Zealand, arriving at Auckland in December 1858 aboard the Evening Star.

Exploration in New Zealand

In New Zealand Haast met Ferdinand von Hochstetter, geologist to the Austrian Novara expedition, and assisted him in surveys around Auckland and Nelson. Appointed provincial geologist of Canterbury in 1861, Haast undertook systematic surveys of the province, exploring the Southern Alps, crossing passes, and mapping river systems and coal and gold resources. In 1863 he became the first European to cross the pass that now bears his name — Haast Pass — reaching the West Coast.

Haast named many features of the Southern Alps for eminent scientists, including the Franz Josef Glacier, which he named for Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria. His reports laid the foundation of Canterbury geology, and he speculated on the former extent of glaciers and on the extinct moa, publishing important work on the bird's remains.

Canterbury Museum and later life

Haast founded the Canterbury Museum, which opened in 1870, and served as its first director, building its collections through international exchanges — famously trading moa skeletons for other specimens. He was also the first professor of geology at Canterbury College. He was made a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1867 and was knighted in 1886. He died in Christchurch in 1887.

Several New Zealand species and places bear his name, including Haast's eagle (Hieraaetus moorei), the largest eagle known to have existed, the Haast tokoeka kiwi, the town of Haast, and the Haast River.
