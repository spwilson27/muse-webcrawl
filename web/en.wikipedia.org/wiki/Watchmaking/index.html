Watchmaking

Watchmaking is the art and craft of making and repairing watches and clocks.

Watchmaking is the art, craft and industry of designing, manufacturing, assembling and repairing watches and clocks. A practitioner is a watchmaker (or, for clocks, a clockmaker); the broader science of timekeeping is horology.

History

Portable timepieces emerged in the early 16th century with spring-driven clocks and the "Nuremberg eggs" associated with Peter Henlein. The invention of the balance spring in the 17th century greatly improved accuracy. Watch production became centred in Switzerland, where the farm-based établissage system of specialised part-makers developed, and later in England, France, Germany and the United States. Industrialised American production with interchangeable parts challenged the Swiss in the 19th century; the "quartz crisis" of the 1970s, when cheap Japanese quartz watches flooded the market, forced a restructuring of the Swiss industry around luxury mechanical watches.

The craft

A traditional watchmaker is trained to manufacture and finish components — gears, pinions, escapements, balance wheels and hairsprings — and to assemble, regulate and repair complete movements. Complications such as chronographs, perpetual calendars, minute repeaters and tourbillons represent the highest levels of the craft. Modern training combines hand skills with CNC machining, and independent watchmakers and large manufactures alike sustain the trade.
