Sujud

Sujud
Arabicسُجود (sujūd)
MeaningProstration
Part ofSalah (Islamic prayer)

Sujud (Arabic: سُجود, sujūd), or sajdah, is the act of prostration to God in Islam, performed by placing the forehead, nose, both hands, knees, and toes on the ground. It is an integral part of the daily prayers (salah): each unit (rak'ah) of prayer contains two prostrations, performed after bowing (ruku).

Description

While in sujud, the worshipper recites glorifications such as Subhana rabbiyal a'la ("Glory be to my Lord, the Most High"), typically three times. According to a well-known hadith, the worshipper is closest to God while prostrating, and personal supplications are encouraged at this point. Prostration is directed toward the Kaaba in Mecca, the qibla.

Other forms

Beyond the obligatory prayer, Islamic practice includes the prostration of gratitude (sujud al-shukr), performed on receiving good news, and the prostration of recitation (sujud al-tilawa), performed when certain verses of the Quran — the ayat al-sajdah — are recited. In Shia practice, prostration is performed on a small clay tablet (turbah), often made from the soil of Karbala.
