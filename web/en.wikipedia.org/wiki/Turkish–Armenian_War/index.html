Turkish–Armenian War

The Turkish–Armenian War was fought between the Democratic Republic of Armenia and the Turkish National Movement from 24 September to 2 December 1920, during the Turkish War of Independence. It ended with the defeat of Armenia and the Treaty of Alexandropol.

Background

After the First World War, the Treaty of Sèvres (August 1920) promised Armenia a large state in eastern Anatolia, with its western boundary to be arbitrated by U.S. President Woodrow Wilson. The Ankara-based Turkish Nationalists under Mustafa Kemal rejected Sèvres, and General Kâzım Karabekir commanded forces in the east.

Course of the war

Turkish forces advanced in late September, capturing Sarikamish, then Kars on 30 October, and Alexandropol (Gyumri) on 7 November. The Armenian army, weakened and diplomatically isolated, could not sustain resistance; Soviet Russia simultaneously pressured Armenia. On 2 December 1920 Armenia signed the Treaty of Alexandropol, renouncing Sèvres and ceding Kars and surrounding territory. Days later Armenia was sovietised, and the later Treaty of Kars (1921) between Turkey and the Soviet republics confirmed the frontier.
