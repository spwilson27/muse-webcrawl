R. Murray Schafer

Raymond Murray Schafer (18 July 1933 – 14 August 2021) was a Canadian composer, writer, music educator and environmentalist perhaps best known for his World Soundscape Project, his concern for acoustic ecology, and his book The Tuning of the World (1977). He coined the term soundscape and popularised terms such as schizophonia and soundmark.

Career

Born in Sarnia, Ontario, Schafer studied at the Royal Conservatory of Music and the University of Toronto, and later in Vienna and London. In 1965 he joined Simon Fraser University in British Columbia, where he founded the World Soundscape Project, a research group that documented acoustic environments and produced pioneering studies of the sonic environment, including The Vancouver Soundscape. The project grew into the discipline of acoustic ecology and the World Forum for Acoustic Ecology.

Compositions

Schafer's large-scale compositional cycle Patria, composed over several decades, comprises works combining music, theatre and environment, several of them performed outdoors at dawn in wilderness settings. His string quartets and choral works are also widely performed. In 1987 he became the first winner of the Glenn Gould Prize.

Legacy

Schafer's ideas about listening, noise pollution and the designed sound environment influenced composers, architects and environmental thinkers worldwide. He died in 2021 at the age of 88.
