Aymara language

Aymara

Native to: Bolivia, Peru, Chile
Speakers: ~1.7 million
Family: Aymaran
Official status: Bolivia and Peru

Aymara is an Aymaran language spoken by the Aymara people of the Bolivian Andes, with roughly 1.7 million speakers in Bolivia, Peru and northern Chile. It is an official language of Bolivia and Peru alongside Spanish and Quechua.

Features

Aymara is agglutinative and suffixing, with a three-vowel system /a i u/ whose realisation varies with adjacent uvular consonants. It grammatically marks evidentiality — whether the speaker personally witnessed information — and famously analyses time with the future conceptualised as behind the speaker and the past in front.

Status

Intercultural bilingual education programmes in Bolivia and Peru support Aymara literacy, and the language has a growing media and online presence, though Spanish dominance pressures younger generations.
