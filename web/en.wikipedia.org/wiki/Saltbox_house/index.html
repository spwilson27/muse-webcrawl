Saltbox house

Saltbox house
Style: American colonial vernacular
Region: New England
Period: c. 1650–1820s
Features: Two storeys front, one storey rear, long sloping rear roof ("catslide")

A saltbox house is a traditional New England style of house with a distinctive asymmetric gable roof: two storeys at the front and a single storey at the back, covered by a long, pitched roof that slopes down to the rear. The shape resembles the wooden boxes in which salt was once kept, which gives the style its name.

Origins

Saltboxes originated in colonial New England in the mid-17th century. The sloping rear roof — called a catslide roof — is often explained as the result of adding a lean-to at the back of a two-storey house, though many were built in one piece with integral lean-tos. Timber-framed with clapboard siding and a central chimney, they were well suited to the cold climate.

Examples

Notable examples include the Thomas Lee House in Connecticut and the houses preserved at historic sites such as Old Sturbridge Village.
