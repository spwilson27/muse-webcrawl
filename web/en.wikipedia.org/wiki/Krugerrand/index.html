The Krugerrand (Afrikaans: [ˈkry.ərˌrant]) is a South African coin, first minted on 3 July 1967 to help market South African gold and produced by Rand Refinery and the South African Mint. The name is a compound of Paul Kruger, the former President of the South African Republic (depicted on the obverse), and the rand, the South African unit of currency. The reverse bears a springbok antelope, the national animal, designed by Coert Steynberg.

History

The Krugerrand was the first modern bullion coin. When introduced, South Africa dominated world gold production, and the coin was intended to give private investors a convenient way to own gold; it was legal tender in South Africa, but its value derives from its gold content rather than any face value. By 1980 the Krugerrand accounted for some 90% of the world gold coin market, and fractional sizes (1/2, 1/4 and 1/10 troy oz) were introduced that year.

During the 1970s and 1980s, economic sanctions against apartheid South Africa led several countries, including the United States (1985), to ban Krugerrand imports. The bans were lifted after the end of apartheid in the early 1990s. Though surpassed by coins such as the Canadian Maple Leaf and American Gold Eagle, the Krugerrand remains one of the world's most recognised bullion coins; over 60 million have been sold.

Design and composition

Krugerrands are struck in a durable 22-karat gold–copper alloy (916.7 fine), giving them a slightly orange hue and greater resistance to scratching than pure gold coins. The one-ounce coin contains exactly one troy ounce of fine gold. The obverse bust of Kruger is by Otto Schultz; the springbok reverse derives from Coert Steynberg's design first used on the 1947 five-shilling piece.

Variants

Proof Krugerrands are struck for collectors. Silver and platinum Krugerrands were introduced in 2017 for the coin's 50th anniversary, the first non-gold issues in the series.
