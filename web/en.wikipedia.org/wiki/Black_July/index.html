Black July (Tamil: கறுப்பு யூலை) is the name given to the anti-Tamil pogrom of July 1983 in Sri Lanka. Sinhalese mobs attacked Tamil civilians, homes and businesses across the island, killing hundreds — estimates range from around 400 to 3,000 — and destroying thousands of properties. The riots are widely regarded as the outbreak of the Sri Lankan Civil War.

Background and outbreak

Ethnic tension between the Sinhalese majority and Tamil minority had built for decades over language policy, university admissions and demands for a separate Tamil state, Eelam. On 23 July 1983 the LTTE ambushed an army patrol at Thirunelveli in Jaffna, killing 13 soldiers. The soldiers' funerals in Colombo the next day triggered rioting that rapidly became organised violence; mobs used electoral lists to identify Tamil homes, and survivors reported the involvement or passivity of elements of the security forces.

Consequences

Tens of thousands of Tamils fled abroad, beginning the global Sri Lankan Tamil diaspora, while thousands more moved to the north and east of the island. Militant Tamil groups swelled with recruits, and the conflict escalated into a civil war that lasted until 2009. The government of J. R. Jayewardene was criticised for its delayed and inadequate response. Black July is commemorated annually by Tamil communities worldwide.
