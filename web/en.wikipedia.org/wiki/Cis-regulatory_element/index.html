Cis-regulatory element

Cis-regulatory elements (CREs) are regions of non-coding DNA which regulate the transcription of neighbouring genes. They are vital components of gene regulatory networks and are studied in evolutionary developmental biology.

Cis-regulatory element
Examples: Promoters, enhancers, silencers, insulators
Act on: Same DNA molecule (in cis)

Types

Promoters lie immediately upstream of genes; enhancers can act over long distances and in either orientation; silencers repress transcription; insulators block enhancer–promoter interactions or halt heterochromatin spread. Transcription factors bind specific sequences within CREs.

Evolution

Changes in CREs alter where, when and how much a gene is expressed without changing proteins, and are thought to drive much morphological evolution — a central theme of evo-devo.

Identification

CREs are found by comparative genomics, DNase hypersensitivity, ChIP-seq and reporter assays such as those catalogued by the ENCODE project.
