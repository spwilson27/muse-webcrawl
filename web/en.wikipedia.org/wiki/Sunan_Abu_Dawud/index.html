Sunan Abu Dawud

Sunan Abu Dawud (Arabic: سنن أبي داود) is one of the six major hadith collections (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam, compiled by the Persian scholar Abu Dawud al-Sijistani (817/818–889 CE). The collection contains approximately 4,800 hadith (with repetitions) selected from some 500,000 that Abu Dawud examined, arranged in 43 books organised chiefly by legal topic.

Author: Abu Dawud al-Sijistani
Language: Arabic
Subject: Hadith; Islamic law

Compilation

Abu Dawud compiled his Sunan in Basra after travelling widely through Iraq, the Hijaz, Syria, Egypt and Khurasan in search of traditions. In a famous letter to the people of Mecca he explained his method: he included hadith on which jurists had based rulings, indicated where a narration was weak, and noted that anything he passed over in silence was sound enough to be usable. The work is noted for its juristic focus — more than half of its contents concern ritual and law — and for its relatively critical stance towards weak narrations compared with other collections.

Position among the six books

Scholars rank the Sunan of Abu Dawud after the Sahihayn of al-Bukhari and Muslim and alongside the collections of al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah. It has attracted numerous commentaries, most famously 'Awn al-Ma'bud, and is widely translated and taught in madrasas around the world.
