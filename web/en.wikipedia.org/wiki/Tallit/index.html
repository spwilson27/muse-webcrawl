Tallit

A tallit (Hebrew: טַלִּית, plural tallitot) is a fringed garment worn as a prayer shawl by observant Jews during morning prayers (Shacharit) and on certain other occasions. The tallit's most notable feature is the fringes, called tzitzit, attached to its four corners in fulfilment of the biblical commandment in Numbers 15:37–40.

Types

The tallit gadol ("large tallit") is the large shawl worn over outer clothing during prayer. The tallit katan ("small tallit") is a poncho-like undergarment with tzitzit worn throughout the day by many observant Jewish men (and some women). A tallit is traditionally made of wool or linen; mixing the two fibres in one garment is prohibited by the law of shaatnez, and most traditional tallitot are wool.

Customs

Before donning the tallit gadol, a blessing is recited. Customs vary widely between Ashkenazi and Sephardi communities regarding the age at which the tallit is first worn — Ashkenazim traditionally begin after marriage (or from bar mitzvah in some communities), while Sephardim often begin at bar mitzvah. Decorative elements include stripes (often blue or black), an embroidered collar (atarah), and corner reinforcements. In many synagogues the tallit is draped over the head during particularly solemn prayers.
