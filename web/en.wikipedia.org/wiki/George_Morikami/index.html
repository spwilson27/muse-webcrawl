George Morikami

The Japanese gardens of the Morikami Museum in Delray Beach, Florida.

George Sukeji Morikami (1886–1976) was a Japanese immigrant to the United States who became a successful farmer in Palm Beach County, Florida, and a philanthropist remembered for donating the land that became the Morikami Museum and Japanese Gardens in Delray Beach, Florida.

Life

Morikami was born in Miyazu, Japan, and emigrated to the United States at the age of 19, arriving in 1906 as part of the Yamato Colony, an agricultural settlement of Japanese farmers founded in south Florida (near present-day Boca Raton) by Jo Sakai. Unlike most colonists, who left as the colony faltered—particularly after the Florida land boom and the Great Depression—Morikami stayed, acquiring land and farming around Delray Beach. Over decades of hard work growing pineapples and vegetables he prospered, accumulating substantial acreage in what was then rural Palm Beach County.

Legacy

In the 1970s, late in life, Morikami donated much of his land—about 200 acres—to Palm Beach County, wishing to preserve the memory of the Yamato Colony and create a park expressing Japanese culture. He died in 1976, a year before the Morikami Museum opened in 1977. The site, which he wanted to bridge Japanese and American cultures, now comprises a museum of Japanese art and history, extensive roji-en gardens, a bonsai collection, and a popular annual programme of festivals. A school in Parkland, Florida, and the site itself bear his name; he is buried on the grounds.
