Jewish Christian

Jewish Christians were the original Jewish followers of Jesus, forming the earliest Christian community in Jerusalem in the first century CE. They continued to observe Jewish law and Temple worship while believing Jesus to be the Jewish Messiah.

History

Led at first by James the Just, "the brother of the Lord," the Jerusalem church combined faith in Jesus with observance of Torah, including circumcision and dietary laws. The question of whether Gentile converts needed to follow Jewish law was debated at the Council of Jerusalem (c. 50 CE), which under the influence of Paul of Tarsus ruled that they did not.

Later groups

As Christianity became predominantly Gentile, Jewish Christian communities such as the Ebionites and Nazarenes — who maintained Jewish observance and in some cases denied Jesus's divinity — were increasingly marginalized and declared heretical by the mainstream church, and they disappeared by late antiquity. In modern usage, the phrase "Jewish Christian" may also refer to people of Jewish heritage who have converted to Christianity, or to members of the contemporary Messianic Judaism movement.
