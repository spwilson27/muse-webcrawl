Interlibrary loan

Interlibrary loan (abbreviated ILL, and sometimes called interloan, document delivery, or document supply) is a service that enables patrons of one library to borrow books, DVDs, music, and other materials owned by another library, or to obtain copies of articles and book chapters held elsewhere.

Procedure

A patron makes a request at their local library, which acts as an intermediary, identifying libraries holding the item and placing a request on the patron's behalf. The lending library sends the material (or a scan/copy) to the borrowing library, which makes it available to the patron and returns it when due. Systems such as OCLC's WorldShare ILL and the open-source software project reShare help automate these transactions.

History

Informal lending between libraries dates back centuries; formal codes governing the practice were developed in the early 20th century, and in the United States the first interlibrary loan code was adopted in 1917. Digital delivery of articles has greatly reduced turnaround times.
