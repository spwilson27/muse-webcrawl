Flight management system

A flight management system (FMS) is a fundamental component of a modern airliner's avionics. It is a specialised computer system that automates a wide variety of in-flight tasks, reducing the workload of the flight crew to the point that modern civilian aircraft no longer require flight engineers or navigators. A primary function is the in-flight management of the flight plan.

The control display unit (CDU) of an FMS

Operation

Using various sensors — including GPS receivers, inertial reference systems, and radio navigation aids such as VOR and DME — the FMS determines the aircraft's position and guides it along the flight plan entered by the crew. Pilots interact with the system chiefly through a control display unit (CDU) with a small screen and alphanumeric keys.

Flight plan and navigation database

Before departure, the crew loads a route made up of waypoints, airways, and standard instrument departures and arrivals from a periodically updated navigation database. The FMS computes the lateral and vertical profiles of the route, predicts times and fuel consumption at each waypoint, and can command the autopilot to follow the route. It also calculates optimum speeds and altitudes for economical flight and manages step climbs as fuel is burned off.

Performance management

The FMS stores performance data for the aircraft type, allowing it to predict climb, cruise, and descent profiles, manage autothrottle settings, and provide the crew with advisory information about fuel, time, and altitude constraints.
