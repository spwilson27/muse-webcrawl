Political movement

A political movement is a coordinated group action focused on a political issue or set of issues, undertaken by actors — ranging from small voluntary groups to large organized networks — seeking to influence or change public policy, political institutions, or social values. Political movements differ from political parties in that movements do not necessarily contest elections or seek to hold office, though the two often overlap: a movement may give rise to a party, and parties may present themselves as the electoral arm of a movement.

Characteristics

Political movements are typically organized around a shared grievance, ideology, or goal. They may pursue their aims through demonstrations, petitions, lobbying, boycotts, strikes, civil disobedience, or digital campaigning. Some movements are reformist, seeking incremental change within an existing system; others are revolutionary, aiming to overthrow it. Movements can also be reactionary, seeking to reverse previous changes.

Relation to social movements

The term overlaps heavily with social movement. Sociologists generally classify movements by the target and extent of the change sought: alterative movements target individual behavior, redemptive movements seek total change in individuals, reformative movements seek partial change in society, and revolutionary movements seek total transformation of the social order. Political movements are distinguished mainly by their orientation toward state power and public policy.

Lifecycle

Movements often pass through stages: emergence around a shared grievance, coalescence into organization and strategy, bureaucratization, and then decline — whether through success, failure, repression, or institutionalization and co-optation into mainstream politics. Resource mobilization theory emphasizes the role of organization, funding, and leadership, while political opportunity theory stresses openings created by divisions among elites or changes in the political environment.

Examples

  • The civil rights movement in the United States
  • The labour movement
  • The environmental movement and climate movement
  • Suffrage movements for women's voting rights
  • Anti-colonial independence movements
