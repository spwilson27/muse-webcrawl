The Jews (Hebrew: יְהוּדִים, Yehudim) are an ethnoreligious group and nation originating from the Israelites of ancient Israel and Judah. Jewish ethnicity, religion and culture are strongly interrelated, and Judaism is the ethnic religion of the Jewish people, although its observance varies from strict to none.

Jews originated as a national and religious group in the Middle East during the second millennium BCE, in the part of the Levant known as the Land of Israel. After conquests by Assyria, Babylon, and Rome, the Jewish diaspora spread across Europe, North Africa and Asia, developing distinct communities such as the Ashkenazi, Sephardi and Mizrahi Jews.

History

According to tradition and archaeological evidence, Israelite society coalesced in the highlands of Canaan in the late Bronze and early Iron Ages. The kingdoms of Israel and Judah were destroyed by Assyria (722 BCE) and Babylon (586 BCE) respectively; the Second Temple period ended with Rome's destruction of Jerusalem in 70 CE. Over the following centuries Jewish communities lived, often under persecution, throughout the Mediterranean world, Europe and the Islamic world, contributing to philosophy, science, commerce and culture.

In the 19th and 20th centuries, emancipation in Europe, mass emigration to the Americas, and the rise of Zionism transformed Jewish life. During the Second World War, Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered approximately six million Jews in the Holocaust. In 1948 the State of Israel was established; today Israel and the United States together host the great majority of the world's Jewish population.

Identity and religion

Jewish identity can be religious, ethnic, cultural or ancestral. Traditional Jewish law defines a Jew as one born to a Jewish mother or converted to Judaism. Judaism's central text is the Torah, supplemented by the Talmud and rabbinic tradition. Major movements today include Orthodox, Conservative, Reform and Reconstructionist Judaism, alongside a large secular population.

Population

Demographers estimate the world's Jewish population at roughly 15–16 million as of the early 2020s, below its pre-Holocaust peak of about 16.7 million in 1939. The largest communities are in Israel and the United States.
