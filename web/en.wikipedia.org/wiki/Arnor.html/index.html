Arnor

The ruins of Amon Sûl (Weathertop), once a watchtower of Arnor

Arnor is a fictional kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. Founded by Elendil after the fall of Númenor, it was the northern of the two Kingdoms in Exile of the Dúnedain in Middle-earth, counterpart to Gondor in the south.

History

Arnor's capital was Annúminas on Lake Evendim, and its chief fortress was Fornost. After the death of its last king, Eärendur, in the year 861 of the Third Age, the realm split into three rival kingdoms — Arthedain, Cardolan, and Rhudaur. The Witch-king of Angmar gradually destroyed them; Arthedain, the last survivor, fell in 1974. The Dúnedain of the North dwindled into the wandering Rangers.

Restoration

At the end of the Third Age, Aragorn II Elessar, chieftain of the Rangers and heir of Elendil, restored the united Reunited Kingdom of Arnor and Gondor after the War of the Ring. Its symbol was the seven stars and crown of Elendil, and its treasures included the Sceptre of Annúminas and the shards of Narsil, reforged as Andúril.
