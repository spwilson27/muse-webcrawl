Grimms' Fairy Tales

AuthorsJacob and Wilhelm Grimm
Original titleKinder- und Hausmärchen
Published1812 (first edition); seven editions to 1857
GenreFairy tale collection, folklore

Grimms' Fairy Tales, originally Children's and Household Tales (Kinder- und Hausmärchen), is a German collection of fairy tales by Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, first published on 20 December 1812. It contains many of the best-known fairy tales in the world, including "Cinderella", "Snow White", "Hansel and Gretel", "Rapunzel", "Rumpelstiltskin", and "The Frog Prince".

Composition

The brothers, scholars of language and law, gathered tales largely from oral sources in Hesse — notably the storyteller Dorothea Viehmann — as part of a scholarly project to preserve German folk tradition. Across seven editions (1812–1857), Wilhelm progressively edited the tales, softening violence and adding Christian moral tones for a child audience.

Influence

The collection has been translated into more than 100 languages and has profoundly shaped children's literature and popular culture; its tales have been adapted countless times in theatre, film — including Disney animations — and literature. UNESCO inscribed the collection in its Memory of the World Register in 2005.
