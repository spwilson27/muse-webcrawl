Mount Kazbek (Georgian: მყინვარწვერი, Mqinvartsveri, "Glacier Peak"; Ossetian: Урсхох, "White Mountain") is a dormant stratovolcano and one of the major mountains of the Caucasus, rising 5,047 metres (16,558 ft) on the border between Georgia and the Russian republic of North Ossetia–Alania. It is the third-highest peak in Georgia.

In Georgian mythology, Kazbek is associated with Amirani, the Georgian counterpart of Prometheus, who was said to have been chained on the mountain for stealing fire. The Gergeti Trinity Church, built in the fourteenth century at 2,170 metres on the mountain's southern slopes, is one of the most recognisable landmarks of Georgia.

Climbing

Kazbek was first climbed in 1868 by an English party led by Douglas Freshfield. Today the standard route starts from the town of Stepantsminda (Kazbegi), passes the Gergeti Trinity Church and the Gergeti Glacier, and overnight shelter at the Meteo station (Bethlemi Hut) at about 3,650 metres. Although not technically difficult, the climb involves glacier travel and is graded around PD+ on the Alpine scale.

Glaciers

The mountain is heavily glaciated, with a total glacier area of roughly 135 square kilometres. The largest glacier, Devdaraki, descends on the north-eastern side toward the Georgian Military Highway; surges and collapses of this glacier, notably in 2014, have temporarily blocked the Darial Gorge road below.
