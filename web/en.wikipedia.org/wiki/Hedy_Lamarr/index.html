Hedy Lamarr

Hedy Lamarr
Born: Hedwig Eva Maria Kiesler, 9 November 1914, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
Died: 19 January 2000, Casselberry, Florida, U.S.
Occupations: Actress, inventor

Hedy Lamarr (born Hedwig Eva Maria Kiesler; November 9, 1914 – January 19, 2000) was an Austrian-born American actress and inventor. A film star during Hollywood's Golden Age, she appeared in films including Algiers (1938), Boom Town (1940), White Cargo (1942) and Samson and Delilah (1949).

Inventing

At the beginning of World War II, Lamarr and composer George Antheil developed a radio guidance system using frequency-hopping spread spectrum technology intended to prevent jamming of Allied torpedoes. They received a U.S. patent in 1942. The principles of their work were later incorporated into Bluetooth and Wi-Fi technology, and Lamarr was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2014.

Later life

Lamarr became a U.S. citizen in 1953 and lived her later years in Florida. Her contributions as an inventor gained wide recognition only late in her life.
