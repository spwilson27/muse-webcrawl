Cape Fear River

LocationNorth Carolina, United States
SourceConfluence of the Deep and Haw rivers
MouthAtlantic Ocean at Cape Fear
Length191.08 mi (307.51 km)
Basin size9,164 sq mi (23,730 km²)

The Cape Fear River is a 191.08-mile (307.51 km) long blackwater river in east-central North Carolina. It flows into the Atlantic Ocean near Cape Fear, from which it takes its name. The river is formed by the confluence of the Deep River and the Haw River just below Jordan Lake. The Cape Fear is the largest river system entirely within North Carolina, draining a basin of more than 9,000 square miles.

Course

From its headwaters confluence near Moncure, the river flows southeast past Fayetteville and Elizabethtown before reaching Wilmington, the largest city on its banks. Below Wilmington the river widens into a tidal estuary, joined by the Northeast Cape Fear River and the Brunswick River, and empties into the Atlantic between Bald Head Island and Oak Island near Southport.

History

The river was explored by Giovanni da Verrazzano in 1524. Its name dates to 1585, when Sir Richard Grenville's expedition ran aground at Tilbury on Cape Fear. During the colonial era the river was a vital artery for the naval stores trade — tar, pitch, and turpentine — that gave North Carolinians the nickname "Tar Heels." In the American Civil War, the river was defended by Fort Fisher, whose fall in January 1865 closed the Confederacy's last major port at Wilmington.

Navigation and industry

Three locks and dams — William O. Huske Lock and Dam, Lock and Dam No. 2 at Elizabethtown, and Lock and Dam No. 1 at Kings Bluff — maintain navigation to Fayetteville. The lower river supports the Port of Wilmington, one of the busiest ports on the U.S. Atlantic coast. The river also supplies drinking water and cooling water to communities and industries throughout the basin.
