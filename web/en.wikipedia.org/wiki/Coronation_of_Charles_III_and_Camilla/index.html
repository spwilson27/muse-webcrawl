Coronation of Charles III and Camilla

Coronation of Charles III and Camilla
Date6 May 2023
LocationWestminster Abbey, London
ParticipantsCharles III; Camilla; Archbishop of Canterbury Justin Welby; ~2,200 guests

The coronation of Charles III and his wife, Camilla, as king and queen of the United Kingdom and the other Commonwealth realms, took place on Saturday, 6 May 2023, at Westminster Abbey. Charles acceded to the throne on 8 September 2022, upon the death of his mother, Elizabeth II. It was the first coronation held in Britain since 1953 and the 40th to be held at Westminster Abbey since 1066.

The service

The ceremony followed the traditional Anglican order of service, shortened and modernised from that used in 1953. Charles was presented to the congregation, swore the coronation oath, and was anointed with holy oil — chrism consecrated in Jerusalem — behind a screen. He was invested with the regalia, including the Orb, the Sceptres and the Ring, and crowned with St Edward's Crown by the Archbishop of Canterbury, Justin Welby. Camilla was crowned with Queen Mary's Crown in a shorter rite. For the first time, leaders of other faiths participated in the service, and the homage of the people replaced the traditional homage of peers.

Celebrations and reactions

The coronation weekend included a concert at Windsor Castle and a national volunteering initiative, the Big Help Out. The ceremony was watched by a peak UK television audience of around 20 million. It also drew protests from republican campaigners, some of whom were arrested, and criticism of its cost during a cost-of-living crisis. Planning for the event had been known by the codename Operation Golden Orb.
