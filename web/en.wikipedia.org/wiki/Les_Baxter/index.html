Les Baxter

Les Baxter (14 March 1922 – 15 January 1996) was an American musician, composer, and conductor. With his orchestral albums of the 1950s he became a founding figure of exotica, the lush, escapist easy-listening genre evoking far-off places, and he later composed many film scores.

Career

Born in Mexia, Texas, Baxter studied piano at the Detroit Conservatory and sang with Mel Tormé's group the Mel-Tones before arranging for swing bands. His breakthrough came with the album Music Out of the Moon (1947), one of the earliest theremin recordings, and the groundbreaking Ritual of the Savage (Le Sacre du Sauvage) (1951), whose "Quiet Village" became an exotica standard later made famous by Martin Denny. His single "The Poor People of Paris" reached number one in the US in 1956.

Film and later work

In the 1960s Baxter turned to film scoring, producing scores for Roger Corman's Edgar Allan Poe adaptations such as House of Usher and The Pit and the Pendulum, and for numerous beach-party and horror films for American International Pictures. He also composed the whistled theme for the television series Lassie's early seasons. Baxter's exotica recordings were rediscovered during the 1990s lounge revival.
