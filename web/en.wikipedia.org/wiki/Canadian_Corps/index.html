Canadian Corps

The Canadian Corps was a World War I corps formed from the Canadian Expeditionary Force in September 1915 after the arrival of the 2nd Canadian Division in France. By mid-1916 it had expanded to four divisions. The corps was commanded successively by Lieutenant-Generals Edwin Alderson, Julian Byng, and Arthur Currie. It is best known for its victory at the Battle of Vimy Ridge in April 1917 and its role in the Hundred Days Offensive of 1918, during which it earned a reputation as an elite assault formation.

Formation and early battles

The First Canadian Division arrived in France in February 1915 and fought at the Second Battle of Ypres, where it endured the first successful poison gas attack of the war. The corps fought at the Somme in 1916, suffering heavy losses in attacks around Courcelette and the Regina Trench. Unlike many British formations, the Canadian divisions were kept together under a single national corps headquarters for nearly the entire war, giving the corps an unusual continuity and cohesion.

Vimy Ridge

At Vimy Ridge (9–12 April 1917), the four divisions of the corps attacked together for the first time, capturing the ridge from the German army in one of the most thoroughly prepared operations of the war. Under Byng, with Currie and British officer Julian Byng's staff, the attack used detailed maps issued down to platoon level, tunnelled approaches, and a creeping barrage. Over 10,000 Canadians were killed or wounded, and four Victoria Crosses were awarded. The battle became a defining moment in Canadian national identity, and the site is now crowned by the Canadian National Vimy Memorial.

Hundred Days

Under Currie, the corps became one of the spearhead formations of the final Allied offensives of 1918, fighting at Amiens, Arras, the Canal du Nord, and Cambrai, and liberating Mons on 11 November 1918. In the last hundred days of the war the corps suffered some 45,000 casualties. In total, about 620,000 Canadians served in the war and more than 60,000 died.
