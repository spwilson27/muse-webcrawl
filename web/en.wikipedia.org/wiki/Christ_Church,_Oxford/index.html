Christ Church (Latin: Aedes Christi) is a constituent college of the University of Oxford and the cathedral church of the Diocese of Oxford. Founded by Cardinal Wolsey as Cardinal College in 1524 and refounded by Henry VIII in 1546, it is the only Oxford (or Cambridge) college that is also a cathedral, and its head is the Dean of Christ Church. It is one of the largest and wealthiest Oxford colleges.

Buildings

The Great Quadrangle (Tom Quad) is the largest quad in Oxford; its gatehouse, Tom Tower, designed by Christopher Wren, houses the Great Tom bell, rung each night. The college hall — with its hammerbeam roof — was the model for the Great Hall of Hogwarts in the Harry Potter films, and parts of the college appeared in several films. The college chapel is the cathedral.

Academic life and notable members

Alumni include thirteen British prime ministers — including Anthony Eden, Harold Macmillan and William Gladstone — as well as the philosopher John Locke, the scientist Robert Hooke, and Charles Dodgson ("Lewis Carroll"), who set Alice's Adventures in Wonderland at the college. The college choir maintains a distinguished musical tradition.
