Pistacia
FamilyAnacardiaceae (cashew family)
Type genus memberPistacia vera (pistachio)
DistributionMediterranean, Asia, North and Central America, Africa

Pistacia is a genus of flowering plants in the cashew family, Anacardiaceae. It contains about ten to twenty species of dioecious shrubs and small trees native to the Mediterranean region, the Canary Islands, North and East Africa, southern Europe, central and western Asia, and parts of North and Central America.

Notable species

The best-known species is Pistacia vera, cultivated for the pistachio nut. Pistacia lentiscus, the mastic tree of the Mediterranean, yields the resin mastic, long chewed and used in varnishes and liqueurs. Pistacia terebinthus (terebinth) is a source of turpentine, and Pistacia chinensis (Chinese pistache) is widely planted as an ornamental shade tree valued for its autumn colour. Pistacia atlantica serves as a hardy rootstock for pistachio orchards.

Description

Plants are usually evergreen or deciduous with pinnate leaves and small, inconspicuous flowers borne in panicles; male and female flowers grow on separate plants. The fruit is a drupe, often reddish ripening to blue or black in ornamental species, though in the pistachio the edible seed sits inside a splitting hull.

Uses

Beyond nuts and resin, Pistacia species provide timber, tannin, and traditional remedies, and are ecologically important in dry Mediterranean scrubland.
