Slow loris

Slow lorises are a group of several species of nocturnal strepsirrhine primates that make up the genus Nycticebus. Found in Southeast Asia and bordering areas, they range from Bangladesh and Northeast India in the west to the Sulu Archipelago in the Philippines in the east, and from Yunnan province in China in the north to the island of Java in the south.

Description

Slow lorises have round heads, narrow snouts, large forward-facing eyes and distinctive colouration patterns that vary by species. Their arms and legs are roughly equal in length, and their torsos are long, allowing them to twist with great flexibility. They have a vice-like grip in their hands and feet, with an opposable thumb that diverges from the other fingers at nearly 180 degrees, enabling them to cling to branches for hours.

Uniquely among primates, slow lorises are venomous. A brachial gland on the inner elbow produces a secretion that, when licked and mixed with saliva, forms a toxin delivered through a toothcomb bite. The bite can cause pain, swelling and, rarely, anaphylactic shock in humans.

Behaviour and diet

Slow lorises are nocturnal and arboreal, moving slowly and deliberately through the canopy. They are omnivorous, eating small animals, fruit, tree gum and other vegetation. Gum is an important food source, gouged from tree trunks with the toothcomb. Slow lorises are generally solitary foragers, though they maintain social contact through scent marking and vocalisations, including a soft whistling call.

Conservation

All slow loris species are threatened by habitat loss and capture for the exotic pet trade and traditional medicine. They are listed in CITES Appendix I, prohibiting international commercial trade. Videos of pet lorises popularised online have driven demand; conservationists note that animals in trade often have their teeth clipped and suffer high mortality. Some species, such as the Javan slow loris, are assessed as critically endangered.

Species

Recognised species include the Sunda slow loris (N. coucang), Bengal slow loris (N. bengalensis), pygmy slow loris (N. pygmaeus, sometimes placed in the genus Xanthonycticebus), Javan slow loris (N. javanicus), Bornean slow loris (N. borneanus), and several others recognised since the early 2010s.
