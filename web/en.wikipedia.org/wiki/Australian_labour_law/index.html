Australian labour law


Image placeholder: workplace relations

Australian labour law is the area of law governing employment, industrial relations and workplace health and safety in Australia. It is principally regulated by federal legislation, most notably the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), administered through the Fair Work Commission and the Fair Work Ombudsman, supplemented by state and territory laws.

History

A distinctive feature of the Australian system was the early adoption of compulsory conciliation and arbitration. The Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration, established in 1904, set wages and conditions through legally binding awards. The 1907 Harvester case, delivered by Justice H. B. Higgins, established the concept of a "fair and reasonable" basic wage sufficient for a man, his wife and three children to live in "frugal comfort".

The Howard government's Workplace Relations Amendment (Work Choices) Act 2005 deregulated the system, expanding individual statutory agreements and restricting unfair-dismissal protections. Following the 2007 federal election, the Rudd government replaced WorkChoices with the Fair Work Act 2009, which restored a safety net of ten National Employment Standards and modern awards, reinstated unfair-dismissal rights for most workers and re-emphasised collective bargaining at the enterprise level.

Key institutions

  • Fair Work Commission — the national workplace relations tribunal, responsible for setting the minimum wage, administering modern awards and approving enterprise agreements.
  • Fair Work Ombudsman — enforces compliance with workplace laws and provides information and assistance.
  • Fair Work (Registered Organisations) Commission — regulates registered unions and employer associations.

Minimum standards

The National Employment Standards prescribe minimum conditions including maximum weekly hours, requests for flexible working arrangements, parental leave, annual leave, personal and carer's leave, community service leave, long service leave, public holidays, notice of termination and redundancy pay. Since 2010 a national minimum wage order applies to most employees, reviewed annually by the Fair Work Commission.
