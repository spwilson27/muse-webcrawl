The Los Angeles Rams are a professional American football team based in the Los Angeles metropolitan area. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) West division, and play their home games at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, which they share with the Los Angeles Chargers.

History

The franchise was founded in 1936 as the Cleveland Rams and won the NFL championship in 1945 before moving to Los Angeles in 1946, becoming the city's first major professional sports team. The Rams played in Los Angeles and Anaheim for nearly five decades, reaching Super Bowl XIV in the 1979 season, before moving to St. Louis in 1995. Led by quarterback Kurt Warner and the high-scoring "Greatest Show on Turf" offence, the St. Louis Rams won Super Bowl XXXIV over the Tennessee Titans.

Owner Stan Kroenke returned the team to Los Angeles in 2016 after NFL owners approved the relocation and his privately financed stadium project in Inglewood. Under head coach Sean McVay, hired in 2017, the Rams reached Super Bowl LIII (losing to the New England Patriots) and then, after trading for quarterback Matthew Stafford, won Super Bowl LVI on their home field in February 2022, defeating the Cincinnati Bengals 23–20; wide receiver Cooper Kupp was named the game's MVP.

Identity and rivalries

The team's colours are royal blue and athletic gold ("Rams Royal" and "Sol"). Historic rivals include the San Francisco 49ers, the Seattle Seahawks and the Arizona Cardinals of the NFC West, as well as the Dallas Cowboys from their earlier Los Angeles era.
