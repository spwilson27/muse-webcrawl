Mark Neary Donohue Jr. (1937–1975) was an American race car driver and engineer, nicknamed "Captain Nice", whose technical approach to racing made him the cornerstone of Roger Penske's early success.

Career

A Brown University engineering graduate, Donohue won SCCA championships before joining Penske Racing. He dominated the Trans-Am Series in Camaros, won the USRRC and Can-Am titles, and drove the fearsome Porsche 917-30 — the "Can-Am killer" — to the 1973 championship.

Indianapolis and Formula One

Donohue won the 1972 Indianapolis 500 in a McLaren-Offenhauser, Penske's first Indy victory. His autobiography, The Unfair Advantage, became a classic of racing literature. He came out of retirement for Penske's Formula One effort.

Death

Donohue crashed during practice for the 1975 Austrian Grand Prix at the Österreichring and died two days later from head injuries. He was inducted into the Motorsports Hall of Fame of America.
