German cruiser Admiral Graf Spee

Launched30 June 1934
Commissioned6 January 1936
ClassDeutschland-class "pocket battleship"
Armament6 × 28 cm guns
FateScuttled 17 December 1939, Montevideo

Admiral Graf Spee was a Deutschland-class heavy cruiser (dubbed a "pocket battleship" by the British press) that served with the Kriegsmarine of Nazi Germany during World War II. The vessel was named after Admiral Maximilian von Spee, victor of the Battle of Coronel in 1914.

Design

Built within the nominal limits of the Treaty of Versailles, the class combined cruiser speed with near-battleship armament: six 28 cm guns, diesel propulsion for long range, and electric welding to save weight. The ships were intended to outgun any faster ship and outrun any stronger one.

Raiding cruise and the River Plate

Sailing before the war began, Captain Hans Langsdorff raided Allied shipping in the South Atlantic and Indian Ocean, sinking nine merchant ships without loss of life. On 13 December 1939 the cruiser fought the British cruisers Exeter, Ajax and Achilles in the Battle of the River Plate. Damaged, Graf Spee took refuge in neutral Montevideo, Uruguay.

Scuttling

Deceived into believing a large British force awaited him, Langsdorff sailed out on 17 December 1939 and scuttled his ship in the Plate estuary before an enormous crowd of spectators; he shot himself three days later in Buenos Aires. Much of the crew was interned in Argentina.
