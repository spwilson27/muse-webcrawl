Gävleborg County (Swedish: Gävleborgs län) is a county (län) on the Baltic Sea coast of Sweden, in the southern part of the Norrland region. It borders the counties of Uppsala, Västmanland, Dalarna, Jämtland and Västernorrland. The county capital and largest city is Gävle. Gävleborg comprises the provinces of Gästrikland and Hälsingland, together with a small part of Dalarna, and its land area of about 18,200 km² is heavily forested.

Administration

The County Administrative Board (Länsstyrelsen), headed by a governor appointed by the Swedish government, represents the state in the county, while the elected regional council, Region Gävleborg, is responsible chiefly for health care and public transport. The county was established in 1762, when it was separated from Västernorrland County.

Municipalities

Gävleborg has ten municipalities: Gävle, Sandviken, Hofors and Ockelbo in Gästrikland; and Hudiksvall, Ljusdal, Bollnäs, Söderhamn, Nordanstig and Ovanåker in Hälsingland. Other towns include the steel town of Sandviken, home of the Sandvik engineering group, and the paper and forestry centres along the coast.

Geography and economy

The terrain rises from a low Baltic coastal plain to forested uplands in the west. Forestry, pulp and paper, steel and engineering have long dominated the economy; the port of Gävle is one of Sweden's largest for container and bulk traffic. The county contains the decorated farmhouses of Hälsingland, a UNESCO World Heritage Site of seven large timber farmhouses noted for their painted interiors, and parts of the Höga Kusten region's post-glacial landscape lie just to the north. The Gävle goat (Gävlebocken), a giant straw Yule goat erected annually in Gävle, is internationally known — not least for repeatedly being burned down by vandals.
