Number format

A number format is the convention by which numbers are written and displayed in a given culture, language, or computing context. It covers the choice of decimal separator, the digit grouping separator, grouping sizes, and the treatment of signs, percentages, currencies, and dates.

Decimal and grouping separators

Countries differ in whether the decimal separator is a point (used in the United States and most of East Asia) or a comma (used in much of Europe and South America). Likewise, thousands may be grouped with commas (1,000,000), points (1.000.000), thin spaces (1 000 000), or apostrophes (Swiss style). The international standard ISO 80000 recommends using a space for digit grouping to avoid ambiguity. In South Asia, the Indian numbering system groups digits in the lakh–crore pattern (e.g., 10,00,000), while East Asian systems historically group by ten thousands (myriads).

In computing

In computing, a number format may refer to the binary representation of numbers in hardware — such as integer and floating-point formats defined by IEEE 754 — or to display formats in spreadsheets and programming languages, where format codes control decimals, currency symbols, and scientific notation. Locale settings in operating systems and libraries (for example the Unicode CLDR data) determine which convention is applied for a given user.
