Horace Vere, 1st Baron Vere of Tilbury (1565 – 2 May 1635) was an English military leader during the Eighty Years' War and the Thirty Years' War, commander of the English forces fighting for the Dutch Republic against Spain. Together with his elder brother Sir Francis Vere, under whom he first served, he was regarded as one of the finest English soldiers of his age, and the regiments he trained in the Dutch service became a school of arms for a generation of English officers, including many future commanders of the English Civil War.

Early career

Vere was a younger son of Geoffrey Vere of Crepping Hall, Essex, a cadet branch of the de Vere earls of Oxford. He joined his brother Francis in the Low Countries in the 1590s, was knighted for his part in the Battle of Nieuwpoort (1600), and served through the siege of Ostend (1601–1604) and the campaigns against the Spanish Army of Flanders. After Francis's retirement in 1606 and death in 1609, Horace succeeded to the command of the English regiments in Dutch pay.

Commander in the Netherlands

Vere defended the Palatinate in the early Thirty Years' War (1620–1622), holding Mannheim until forced to surrender on terms; he then returned to Dutch service, notably commanding the English contingent at the siege of Breda (1624–1625) and at the successful siege of 's-Hertogenbosch (1629) under Frederick Henry, Prince of Orange. In 1625 Charles I raised him to the peerage as Baron Vere of Tilbury. His residence in England was at Clapton and later Whitehall.

Family and death

In 1607 he married Mary Tracy, widow of William Hoby; of their five daughters, several married into prominent families, and the co-heiresses included the ancestors of later noble houses. Vere died suddenly of apoplexy at Whitehall on 2 May 1635, and was buried in Westminster Abbey near his brother Francis. As he left no sons, the barony became extinct at his death.
