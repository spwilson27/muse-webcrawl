Durdle Door

Durdle Door (sometimes written Durdle Dor) is a natural limestone arch on the Jurassic Coast near Lulworth in Dorset, England. Formed by the erosion of a band of resistant Portland limestone, it is one of Dorset's most photographed and iconic landmarks, and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geology

The arch formed where the sea eroded through a vertical band of Portland limestone — rock layers that run parallel to the coast here, tipped almost vertically by the tectonic movements that built the Alps some 25 million years ago. Waves cut through weaknesses in the limestone, creating first caves, then the arch; the softer rocks behind have been swept away to form the bay. The arch will eventually collapse, leaving a stack.

Name

"Durdle" derives from the Old English thirl, meaning to pierce or drill — the same root as "thrill" in its older sense of piercing. The name is recorded as early as the late 18th century.

Tourism and culture

The beach below, reached by steep steps from the cliff-top car park, is a popular swimming and sightseeing spot drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors a year. The arch has appeared in films, television, music videos and album artwork.
