Volvo Cars

Volvo Cars (Swedish: Volvo Personvagnar) is a Swedish multinational manufacturer of luxury vehicles headquartered in Torslanda, Gothenburg. The company manufactures SUVs, station wagons, and sedans, and is widely associated with an emphasis on automotive safety.

Founded1927 (as part of AB Volvo)
HeadquartersGothenburg, Sweden
OwnerGeely Holding Group (majority, since 2010)
ProductsLuxury cars, SUVs

History

Volvo was founded in 1927 in Gothenburg as a subsidiary of the ball-bearing manufacturer SKF; the first production car, the ÖV 4 ("Jakob"), rolled off the line that year. Volvo Cars was separated from AB Volvo in 1999, when it was sold to Ford Motor Company, becoming part of Ford's Premier Automotive Group. In 2010, Ford sold Volvo Cars to China's Geely Holding Group.

Safety

Volvo engineer Nils Bohlin invented the modern three-point safety belt in 1959, and the company made the patent available to other manufacturers. Volvo's Vision 2020 initiative aimed for no one to be killed or seriously injured in a new Volvo car, and the company has committed to an all-electric line-up with models such as the EX30 and EX90.
