Marriage A-la-Mode (Hogarth)

Marriage A-la-Mode is a series of six pictures painted by William Hogarth between 1743 and 1745, with engravings published in 1745. The series is a sharp satire on the arranged marriages of the upper classes, in which a bankrupt earl marries his gouty son to the daughter of a wealthy but miserly merchant, with disastrous consequences for the young couple.

The scenes

The six scenes are: The Marriage Settlement, in which the contract is signed while the betrothed couple ignore each other; The Tête à Tête (shortly after the marriage), showing the bored, estranged pair at home; The Inspection, in which the young viscount visits a quack doctor; The Toilette, where the countess receives guests including the lawyer Silvertongue; The Bagnio, in which the husband is murdered by Silvertongue after discovering the lovers; and The Suicide of the Countess, the tragic final scene.

Reception

The paintings, now in the National Gallery, London, are considered among Hogarth's finest works — densely packed with symbolic detail, from spotted skin hinting at disease to the chained dogs in the first scene mirroring the couple's bond. The series influenced later narrative art and remains a classic commentary on mercenary marriage.
