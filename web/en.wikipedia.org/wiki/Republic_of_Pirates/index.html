Republic of Pirates

The Republic of Pirates is the name given to the loose confederacy of pirates and privateers who used Nassau, on New Providence Island in the Bahamas, as their base roughly between 1706 and 1718, during the later part of the Golden Age of Piracy. At its height, more than a thousand pirates were based there, effectively beyond the control of colonial authorities.

History

Nassau's harbour, defensible and suitable for shallow-draft vessels, attracted former privateers left unemployed by the end of the War of the Spanish Succession. Figures associated with the "Flying Gang" of pirates included Benjamin Hornigold, Charles Vane, Edward Teach (Blackbeard), "Calico Jack" Rackham, Anne Bonny and Mary Read. The pirates organised themselves under informal articles and, in popular accounts, elected magistrates among themselves; Hornigold was a leading figure until he accepted a royal pardon.

Suppression

In 1718 the British government appointed Woodes Rogers as Governor of the Bahamas and sent a naval expedition offering a royal pardon to pirates who surrendered. Rogers reoccupied Nassau, fortified it, and pursued those — such as Vane — who refused the pardon. Within a few years organized piracy in the Bahamas was effectively ended, and Rogers adopted the motto Expulsis Piratis – Restituta Commercia ("Pirates expelled, commerce restored").

In popular culture

The "republic" has been popularised by Colin Woodard's 2007 history The Republic of Pirates, and appears in the television series Black Sails and the video game Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag.
