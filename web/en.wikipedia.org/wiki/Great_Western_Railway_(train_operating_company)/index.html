Great Western Railway (GWR) is a British train operating company owned by FirstGroup that has operated the Greater Western passenger railway franchise since 2006 (under successive direct awards from the Department for Transport). Services run from London Paddington to the Thames Valley, the Cotswolds, Bristol, South Wales, the West Country (Exeter, Plymouth, and Penzance), and the south coast, as well as regional and branch-line services across the south-west. The company revived the name and, from 2015, the dark green livery of the historic Great Western Railway (1833–1947).

History

The operation began at privatisation in 1996 as Great Western Trains, a management buy-out later acquired by FirstGroup, which renamed it First Great Western in 1998. In 2006 the Greater Western franchise combined First Great Western with First Great Western Link (Thames Valley) and Wessex Trains services. The September 2015 rebrand restored the GWR identity, coinciding with the Great Western electrification programme and the introduction of Hitachi-built Class 800/802 Intercity Express Trains from October 2017, which replaced the beloved High Speed Trains on long-distance services.

Services and fleet

Principal routes include Paddington–Bristol Temple Meads–Cardiff–Swansea, Paddington–Exeter–Plymouth–Penzance, Paddington–Oxford–Worcester–Hereford, and North Downs Line services to Gatwick Airport. The fleet combines Class 800/802 bi-mode multiple units, Class 387 electrics in the Thames Valley, Class 165/166 Networker Turbos, and Class 150/158 diesel units on regional work; the "Night Riviera" sleeper to Penzance is one of only two sleeper services in Britain. GWR operates over 270 stations and carries on the order of 80–100 million passengers per year in normal times.
