Diptych

A diptych (from the Greek díptychos, "folded in two") is any object with two flat plates which form a pair, often attached by a hinge. In art, the term usually refers to a painting or carving made of two panels joined together, designed to be displayed as a single work and often to be folded shut for protection. Works of three panels are called triptychs, and larger works polyptychs.

History

In late antiquity, ivory diptychs were produced recording the names of consuls and other officials, and folding writing tablets of waxed wood or ivory were in everyday use. In the medieval church, diptychs listed the names of the living and the dead commemorated in prayer, and small portable ivory or panel diptychs served as private devotional objects. Panel diptychs flourished in Early Netherlandish painting, with examples by artists such as Jan van Eyck and Rogier van der Weyden.

Modern use

Contemporary artists and photographers use the diptych format to juxtapose two images that comment on one another. The word is also used metaphorically in literature and film for a pair of related works.
