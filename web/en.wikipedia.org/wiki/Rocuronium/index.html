Rocuronium bromide (brand names Zemuron and Esmeron) is an aminosteroid non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker, or muscle relaxant, used in modern anaesthesia to facilitate tracheal intubation and to provide skeletal muscle relaxation during surgery or mechanical ventilation.

Chemical structure of rocuronium.

Pharmacology

Rocuronium acts as a competitive antagonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction, preventing acetylcholine from triggering muscle contraction. It is a derivative of vecuronium designed for a faster onset: at standard intubating doses it takes effect in about one minute, the fastest onset of the non-depolarizing agents, though with a longer duration than succinylcholine.

Clinical use

In higher doses, rocuronium is used for rapid-sequence induction of anaesthesia as an alternative to succinylcholine where that drug is contraindicated. Its effects can be reversed by acetylcholinesterase inhibitors such as neostigmine, or specifically by sugammadex, a modified cyclodextrin that encapsulates rocuronium molecules. Side effects are generally few; allergic reactions, including rare anaphylaxis, are the most significant risk.

History

Rocuronium was introduced into clinical practice in 1994. It later became known outside medicine through its use, together with other agents, in lethal injection protocols in the United States, a use opposed by its manufacturers.
