Derbent

Derbent is a city in Dagestan, Russia, on the Caspian Sea, near the border with Azerbaijan. It occupies a narrow strip of land between the Caspian and the Caucasus Mountains that has served for millennia as a strategic gateway between Europe and the Middle East. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Russia and the world, with a history spanning some 5,000 years of settlement.

History

The name Derbent is Persian for "locked gate". The Sasanian Empire fortified the pass in the 5th–6th centuries CE, building two parallel walls running from the citadel (Naryn-Kala) down to the sea, with a further "mountain wall" extending far into the Caucasus to block steppe nomads. Derbent was later held by Arabs, Seljuks, Mongols, Safavid Persia, and the Ottoman Empire before passing permanently to Russia under the Treaty of Gulistan in 1813.

Heritage

The citadel, ancient city, and fortress buildings of Derbent were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003; they include one of the oldest mosques in the former Soviet Union, the Juma Mosque, founded in the 8th century. Today the city is ethnically diverse — Lezgins, Azerbaijanis, Russians, Tabasarans, and others — and is known for viticulture and brandy production.
