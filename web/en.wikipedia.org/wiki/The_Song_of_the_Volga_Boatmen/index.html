Overview

The Song of the Volga Boatmen is a well-known traditional Russian song sung by the burlaks — the barge-haulers who, from the seventeenth into the early twentieth century, pulled laden vessels upstream along the Volga River by rope. Its famous refrain, 'Ey, ukhnyem!' ('Yo, heave ho!'), timed the haulers' synchronized pulls.

The song was collected and popularized from folk tradition; Mily Balakirev published an arrangement after hearing a version on the Volga, and the great bass Feodor Chaliapin made it famous worldwide in the early 1900s. It has since been arranged for orchestras and choirs and recorded countless times.

Cultural legacy

The song became an emblem of Russian folk music and of the hardships of pre-industrial labor; Ilya Repin's celebrated painting Barge Haulers on the Volga (1870-1873) depicts the same world. In 1941, Glenn Miller's big-band arrangement, titled 'Volga Boatman', reached number one in the United States. The melody has been used in film scores and cartoons whenever Russian or labor imagery is invoked.
