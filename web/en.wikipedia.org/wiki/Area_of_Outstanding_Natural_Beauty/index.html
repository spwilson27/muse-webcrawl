Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty

An Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONB), formally designated a National Landscape since 2023, is an area of countryside in England, Wales or Northern Ireland that has been designated for conservation due to its significant landscape value. AONBs are designated by the relevant government conservation bodies and are afforded protection similar in many respects to that of national parks.

Designation and protection

The concept was created by the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949, following John Dower's 1945 report on national parks. The first AONB, the Gower Peninsula in Wales, was designated in 1956. Unlike national parks, AONBs are generally administered by local authorities, in many cases through conservation boards or partnerships, and planning policy gives their landscapes great weight. Since November 2023 all AONBs in England and Wales have been rebranded as "National Landscapes", though the legal name is unchanged. Scotland uses the equivalent designation National Scenic Area.

Extent

The 46 areas cover about 18% of the land area of England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Well-known examples include the Cotswolds, the Chiltern Hills, the South Downs (later a national park), the Isles of Scilly, the North Pennines, the Causeway Coast and the Mourne Mountains in Northern Ireland, and the Wye Valley, which straddles the England–Wales border.

Purpose

The primary purpose of designation is to conserve and enhance natural beauty, with regard also to the needs of agriculture, forestry and rural communities. Following the 2019 Landscapes Review (the Glover Review), the government committed to strengthening AONBs' purposes and funding, implemented partly by the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023, which placed a stronger duty on public bodies to further the purposes of protected landscapes.
