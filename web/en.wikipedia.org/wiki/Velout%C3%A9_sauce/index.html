Velouté sauce

TypeSauce
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsLight stock, blond roux

Velouté sauce (French pronunciation: [vəluˈte]) is one of the five "mother sauces" of French cuisine in the classification codified by Auguste Escoffier. Its name derives from the French word velour, "velvet", referring to its smooth texture.

Preparation

A velouté is made from a light stock — chicken, veal or fish — thickened with a blond roux (equal parts butter and flour cooked briefly so it stays pale). The stock is added gradually to the roux and simmered until the sauce is smooth and lightly coating in consistency, then skimmed and seasoned.

Derivative sauces

Velouté is the base for a family of derivative ("small") sauces. Well-known examples include suprême sauce (chicken velouté enriched with cream and mushroom cooking liquor), allemande sauce (veal velouté finished with egg yolk and cream), and fish velouté enriched with cream, as well as sauces flavoured with wine, herbs or mushroom essence.
