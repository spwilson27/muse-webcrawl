Old Summer Palace

The Old Summer Palace, known in Chinese as Yuanmingyuan (圓明園, "Garden of Perfect Brightness"), was a vast complex of palaces and gardens in the Haidian District of Beijing, China. Built and expanded over the 18th and early 19th centuries by the Qing dynasty emperors, it was renowned as a masterpiece of Chinese landscape garden design.

History

Construction began under the Kangxi Emperor and reached its height under the Qianlong Emperor, who added the Xiyanglou — a group of European-style palaces and fountains designed with the help of Jesuit artists such as Giuseppe Castiglione. The gardens covered some 350 hectares (860 acres) and housed an enormous collection of art and antiquities, including the bronze zodiac fountainheads.

Destruction

In October 1860, during the Second Opium War, British and French troops looted the palace, and British forces under Lord Elgin ordered its destruction by fire in retaliation for the torture and death of Anglo-French envoys. The palace burned for days. Further looting and decay followed, and most wooden structures have vanished.

Legacy

The ruins are preserved as a park and a memorial of national humiliation in China. Efforts to recover looted artefacts, such as the zodiac heads, continue to attract public attention.
