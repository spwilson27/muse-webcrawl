Good Friday

Good Friday is a Christian holy day commemorating the crucifixion of Jesus and his death at Calvary. It is observed during Holy Week as part of the Paschal Triduum, on the Friday preceding Easter Sunday, and it frequently coincides with the Jewish observance of Passover. The day is marked by fasting, mourning and somber services in most Christian traditions; in many countries it is a public holiday. The word "Good" is generally understood in the older sense of "holy" or "pious".

Observance

In the Roman Catholic Church, Good Friday is a day of fasting and abstinence; the Celebration of the Passion of the Lord includes readings from Isaiah and John's Gospel, the solemn intercessions, veneration of the Cross and a communion service — uniquely, no Mass is celebrated. Many churches hold the Three Hours' Agony service from noon to 3 p.m., the hours traditionally assigned to the Crucifixion, and devotions such as the Stations of the Cross. Lutheran, Anglican, Methodist and other traditions hold their own Passion services; the Moravian Church is known for its graveyard cleaning and services.

Customs

Customs include hot cross buns in Britain and the Commonwealth, passion plays (notably at Oberammergau), and public processions carrying crosses or statues in Spain, Latin America, the Philippines and elsewhere. In Bermuda, kites are traditionally flown on Good Friday. In some traditions church bells are silenced and altars stripped from Maundy Thursday until the Easter Vigil.

Date

Good Friday's date moves with Easter, computed from the first full moon after the spring equinox; Western and Eastern churches often observe it on different dates because of their different calendars and computus.
