Characteristic impedance

Characteristic impedance
SymbolZ0
UnitOhm (Ω)
Common values50 Ω, 75 Ω, 300 Ω

The characteristic impedance or surge impedance (usually written Z0) of a uniform transmission line is the ratio of the amplitudes of voltage and current of a single wave propagating along the line; that is, a wave travelling in one direction in the absence of reflections in the other direction. Equivalently, it can be defined as the input impedance of a transmission line when its length is infinite.

Formula

For a line with resistance R, inductance L, conductance G, and capacitance C per unit length, the characteristic impedance is Z0 = √((R + jωL)/(G + jωC)). For a lossless line (R = G = 0) this reduces to Z0 = √(L/C), a real value independent of frequency. A source and load matched to Z0 transfer maximum power without reflections; mismatches cause reflected waves and standing waves, quantified by the VSWR.

Applications

Standard values include 50 Ω for radio-frequency coaxial cable, 75 Ω for television cable, and around 100 Ω for twisted-pair data cabling. The characteristic impedance of free space is approximately 377 Ω.
