Chaat

Chaat (from Hindi cāṭ, "to lick" or "taste") is a family of savoury snacks that originated in India and is now eaten across South Asia, typically served as street food or appetisers. Chaat is characterised by a lively combination of salty, sour, sweet, spicy and crunchy elements, usually seasoned with chaat masala, a tangy spice blend featuring dried mango powder (amchoor) and black salt (kala namak).

Origins

Chaat is generally traced to the royal kitchens of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in the seventeenth century, where, by tradition, court physicians recommended light, spicy snacks made with yogurt and chutneys to aid digestion. From Delhi the genre spread along trade routes to Uttar Pradesh — cities such as Lucknow, Agra, Varanasi and Mathura remain famed for their chaat — and then across the subcontinent.

Common varieties

  • Aloo chaat — fried potato cubes tossed with spices and chutneys
  • Aloo tikki — spiced potato patties, crisped on a griddle
  • Pani puri / golgappa — hollow crisp puris filled with spiced water, chickpeas and potato
  • Bhel puri — puffed rice with vegetables and tamarind sauce, associated with Mumbai beaches
  • Samosa chaat — crushed samosa layered with chickpeas, yogurt and chutneys
  • Papri chaat — crisp wafers with potato, chickpeas, yogurt and tamarind
  • Dahi puri — puris filled with yogurt and chutneys

Characteristics

The unifying grammar of chaat is contrast: a starchy base, something crisp (sev, papri, puri), something creamy (whisked yogurt), sour tamarind and green mint-coriander chutneys, fresh onion and coriander, and a dusting of chaat masala and chilli. It is assembled to order and eaten immediately, before the crisp elements soften.
