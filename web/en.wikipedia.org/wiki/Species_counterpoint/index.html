Species counterpoint

Species counterpoint is a graded pedagogical method for teaching counterpoint, in which a student writes lines against a given melody, the cantus firmus, under progressively loosened rhythmic rules. The system was codified by Johann Joseph Fux in his treatise Gradus ad Parnassum (1725) and remains a standard foundation of music theory training.

The five species

  1. First species — one note against each note of the cantus firmus (note against note).
  2. Second species — two notes against each note of the cantus firmus, introducing passing dissonances on weak beats.
  3. Third species — four (or three) notes against each note, allowing a wider variety of melodic figures.
  4. Fourth species — syncopated notes tied across beats, introducing suspensions that must resolve by step.
  5. Fifth species — "florid" counterpoint, combining the techniques of the previous species freely.

Rules

Species exercises enforce the voice-leading norms of Renaissance polyphony: only perfect consonances at the beginning and end, careful handling of perfect intervals (avoiding parallel fifths and octaves), predominantly stepwise melodic motion with limited, well-shaped leaps, and strict control of dissonance — permitted only as passing tones, neighbor tones, or prepared suspensions resolving downward by step.

Exercises are first written in two voices and then in three and four voices, with correspondingly adjusted rules for the treatment of intervals between the added parts.
