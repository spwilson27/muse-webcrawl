Photonic-crystal fiber


Cross-section of a photonic-crystal fiber

Photonic-crystal fibers (PCF) are a class of optical fiber based on the properties of photonic crystals. Because of its ability to confine light in hollow cores or with confinement characteristics not possible in conventional optical fiber, PCF is finding applications in fiber-optic communications, fiber lasers, nonlinear devices, high-power transmission, highly sensitive gas sensors, and other areas. More specific categories of PCF include photonic-bandgap fiber (PCF that confines light by bandgap effects, including hollow-core fibers) and holey fiber (PCF using air holes in its cross-section), microstructured optical fiber, and Bragg fiber.

History

The term photonic-crystal fiber was coined by Philip Russell in 1995–1997 (he states the idea dates to 1991). The first PCF was demonstrated at the University of Bath in 1996, and the first hollow-core photonic bandgap fiber followed in 1998.

Description

Optical fibers conventionally guide light by total internal reflection from a solid core of higher refractive index than the surrounding cladding. Photonic-crystal fibers instead contain a regular array of air holes running the length of the fiber, which both lowers the average cladding index (index-guiding PCF) and can create a photonic bandgap that confines light even in a hollow core (bandgap-guiding PCF).

Index-guiding PCFs can be designed to be endlessly single-mode, guiding only one mode at all wavelengths, and to have unusual dispersion properties, including very high or anomalous dispersion at wavelengths where silica fiber is normally dispersive. Air-core bandgap fibers guide most of the light in air, reducing nonlinear effects and allowing high-power delivery and low-latency transmission.

Applications

PCFs are used for supercontinuum generation, in which a short intense pulse broadens into a wide spectrum of light; for gas and liquid sensing using hollow cores filled with the analyte; for high-power fiber lasers and amplifiers; and for particle guidance and atom optics in hollow cores.
