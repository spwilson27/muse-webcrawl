Collegiate sport
Level: University and college athletics
Largest system: United States (NCAA, NAIA)
Prominent sports: American football, basketball, rowing, ice hockey

Collegiate sport, also called college athletics, is sport organised between teams of students at universities and colleges. It exists in many countries, but it is most highly developed and commercially significant in the United States, where university teams attract large crowds, television audiences, and substantial revenue.

United States

American college sports are governed chiefly by the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA), which organises championships in dozens of sports across three divisions, and by smaller bodies such as the NAIA. American football and men's and women's basketball are the largest spectator sports; major football stadiums at universities such as Michigan and Ohio State hold over 100,000 spectators. Athletic scholarships fund many students' education, and the system has long been debated over amateurism, athlete compensation, and name, image, and likeness rights, which student-athletes have been permitted to monetise since 2021.

Other countries

In the United Kingdom, university sport is coordinated by British Universities and Colleges Sport (BUCS), with the Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race among the oldest and most famous fixtures. Canada's U Sports, Australia's university games, and varsity systems in the Philippines and Japan also organise inter-university competition, generally on a smaller commercial scale than in the United States.

History

Intercollegiate competition began in the nineteenth century with rowing contests, including the first Harvard–Yale regatta in 1852 and the Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race first held in 1829, before spreading to football codes, athletics, and many other sports.
