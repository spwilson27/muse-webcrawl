Dame Anna Wintour (born 3 November 1949) is a British-American media executive who served as editor-in-chief of American Vogue from 1988 to 2025, and as Condé Nast's global chief content officer and global editorial director of Vogue. Widely regarded as one of the most powerful figures in fashion, she is known for her signature pageboy bob haircut and large sunglasses, her support of young designers, and her transformation of Vogue into a dominant cultural and commercial force.

Early life and career

Wintour was born in London, the daughter of Charles Wintour, editor of the Evening Standard. She began her career in fashion journalism in London in 1970 at Harpers & Queen, later moving to New York, where she worked at Harper's Bazaar, Viva, Savvy and New York magazine. In 1986 she became editor of British Vogue, and in 1987 briefly edited House & Garden before taking over American Vogue in 1988.

Vogue and influence

Her first cover, in November 1988, mixed a Christian Lacroix couture top with inexpensive jeans — a then-unusual blend of high and low fashion. Under Wintour, Vogue put celebrities rather than only models on its covers and expanded into a global brand. Since the 1990s she has chaired the annual Met Gala in New York, turning it into one of the world's most prominent fashion fundraisers. In 2025 she announced she would step down as editor-in-chief of American Vogue while retaining her global Condé Nast roles.

In popular culture

Wintour is widely believed to be the model for Miranda Priestly, the formidable editor in Lauren Weisberger's 2003 novel The Devil Wears Prada and its 2006 film adaptation.
