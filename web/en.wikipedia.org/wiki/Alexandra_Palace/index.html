Alexandra Palace is a Grade II listed entertainment and sports venue in Alexandra Park, London, situated on a hill overlooking the city between Wood Green and Muswell Hill. Intended as a North London counterpart to the Crystal Palace in South London, it is affectionately known as "Ally Pally".

History

The palace opened in 1873 but burned down just sixteen days later. It was rebuilt and reopened in 1875, containing a concert hall, theatre, museum, library and the Great Hall with one of the largest pipe organs of its day. In 1935 the BBC leased part of the building and installed two television studios; on 2 November 1936 the BBC launched the world's first scheduled high-definition television service from Alexandra Palace. The studios remained in use until 1956.

Modern use

A second devastating fire in 1980 destroyed much of the building, but it was restored and reopened in 1988. The palace now hosts exhibitions, concerts, an ice rink and sporting events, most famously the PDC World Darts Championship. Its East Court and Victorian theatre, the latter reopened in 2018 after decades of disuse, are major event spaces.
