Great hall

TypeArchitectural space
EraMedieval to early modern
ExamplesWestminster Hall, Penshurst Place

A great hall is the main room of a royal palace, castle, or large manor house in the medieval and early modern periods, and the country houses of the 16th and early 17th centuries. Great halls were found especially in France, England, and Scotland, but similar rooms were also found in some other European countries.

Layout and use

A typical great hall was rectangular, two or three times as long as it was wide, with a high ceiling. At one end was a raised dais for the lord's high table; at the other, a screens passage separated the hall from the kitchens and service rooms. A central open hearth heated the room until chimneys and wall fireplaces became common. The hall was the centre of household life: meals were taken there communally, business was conducted, guests were received, and servants often slept there.

Decline

From the later Middle Ages, lords increasingly withdrew to private chambers and great chambers, and by the 17th century the great hall had declined in ceremonial importance, becoming an entrance hall in later country houses — though the term and form persist in institutional settings such as college dining halls and livery company halls.
