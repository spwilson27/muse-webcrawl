Hidden Armenians

Hidden Armenians (Turkish: Gizli Ermeniler) or Crypto-Armenians is an "umbrella term" describing Turkish citizens who conceal their full or partial Armenian ancestry from the wider society. Most are descendants of Armenians who converted to Islam and adapted Turkish or Kurdish identities, often to survive the Armenian genocide of 1915, including children adopted into Muslim families and women taken into households.

History

Following the deportations and massacres of 1915, tens of thousands of Armenians — especially women and children — remained in Anatolia under new identities. Their descendants, estimated by some researchers to number in the hundreds of thousands or more, grew up as Muslims, often preserving only fragmentary memories: a grandmother's habits, food customs, or guarded family stories of Armenian origin.

Rediscovery

Since the early 2000s, public discussion of hidden Armenians has grown in Turkey, encouraged by works such as Fethiye Çetin's memoir My Grandmother (2004) and by growing numbers of people researching their roots, learning Armenian, or openly reclaiming an Armenian identity, though many still face social pressure and discrimination.
