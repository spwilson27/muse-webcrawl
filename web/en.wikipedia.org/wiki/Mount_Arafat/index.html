Mount Arafat (Arabic: جَبَل عَرَفَات) is a granite hill east of Mecca in Saudi Arabia, rising about 70 metres above the surrounding Plain of Arafat. It is the site of the wuqūf, the "standing" vigil that forms the central rite of the Hajj pilgrimage.

Religious significance

On the ninth day of Dhu al-Hijjah, pilgrims gather on the plain from noon until sunset in prayer and reflection; a Hajj without presence at Arafat on that day is considered invalid, following the Prophet Muhammad's saying "Hajj is Arafat". It was here that Muhammad delivered his Farewell Sermon during his final pilgrimage in 632 CE. Islamic tradition also associates the hill with Adam and Eve, who are said to have been reunited on Earth at Arafat after their expulsion from Paradise, giving the mount its alternative name, Mount of Mercy.

A white pillar marks the summit where Muhammad is believed to have stood. The Namirah Mosque stands at the western edge of the plain, where the combined noon and afternoon prayers are led on the Day of Arafat, which many non-pilgrim Muslims observe with fasting.
