Mecha anime and manga


A giant robot typical of the "super robot" tradition

Mecha, also known as mecha anime and manga, is a genre of Japanese animation and comics featuring or focusing on mechanical innovations such as robots, cybernetics, androids, and powered exoskeletons. Giant robots are often piloted by human characters and serve as central plot devices, vehicles, and metaphors.

History

The genre traces its roots to Mitsuteru Yokoyama's manga Tetsujin 28-go (1956), whose giant remote-controlled robot appeared in anime in 1963. Go Nagai's Mazinger Z (1972) established the trope of a pilot riding inside the robot. Yoshiyuki Tomino's Mobile Suit Gundam (1979) then inaugurated the "real robot" subgenre, treating mecha as mass-produced military hardware within a gritty war story, and spawned one of the largest media franchises in Japan.

Subgenres

Commentators commonly divide mecha fiction into super robot works, in which unique, near-mythical machines battle monsters, and real robot works, emphasising logistics, politics, and realistic physics. Influential titles include Super Dimension Fortress Macross, Neon Genesis Evangelion, Code Geass, and Gurren Lagann. The genre is closely tied to model kits ("Gunpla") and toy merchandising.

Influence

Mecha design has influenced science fiction worldwide, from Hollywood films such as Pacific Rim to Japanese popular culture more broadly, and full-scale statues of the Gundam have become landmarks in Japan.
