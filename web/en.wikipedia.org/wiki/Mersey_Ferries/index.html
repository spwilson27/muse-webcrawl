Mersey Ferries

Mersey Ferries is a ferry service operating on the River Mersey in North West England, between Liverpool and the Wirral Peninsula. Crossings run between Pier Head in Liverpool and Seacombe and Woodside in Wirral. The service is operated by Merseytravel, and the ferry across the Mersey is a celebrated Liverpool icon, immortalised by the 1964 Gerry and the Pacemakers song "Ferry Cross the Mersey".

History

Ferries have crossed the Mersey since at least the 12th century, when monks of Birkenhead Priory ran a service. Municipal ferries from Birkenhead and Wallasey were eventually consolidated; the famous vessels Royal Iris of the Mersey and Royal Daffodil served for decades. Today the fleet includes Royal Daffodil and Snowdrop, the latter painted in a "dazzle" colour scheme designed by Sir Peter Blake.

Services

A direct commuter service runs at peak times, while a 50-minute River Explorer cruise operates during the day, calling at the terminals and offering views of the Liverpool skyline, a UNESCO-listed waterfront for much of the modern era.
