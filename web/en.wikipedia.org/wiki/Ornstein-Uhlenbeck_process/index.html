Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is a stochastic process with applications in financial mathematics and the physical sciences. It is named after the physicists Leonard Ornstein and George Eugene Uhlenbeck, who introduced it in 1930 as a model of the velocity of a massive Brownian particle under the influence of friction. The process is a continuous-time, continuous-state Gaussian process that is stationary and mean-reverting: its values tend to drift back towards a long-run mean.

Sample paths of an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (θ = 1, σ = 1, μ = 0), showing mean reversion.

Definition

The process Xt is defined by the stochastic differential equation

dXt = θ(μ − Xt) dt + σ dWt,

where θ > 0 is the rate of mean reversion, μ is the long-term mean, σ > 0 is the volatility, and Wt is a Wiener process. It can be solved explicitly: Xt equals its initial value decayed exponentially toward μ, plus a stochastic integral term. Conditional on the past, Xt is normally distributed with mean μ + (X0 − μ)e−θt and variance (σ2/2θ)(1 − e−2θt). As t → ∞ the stationary distribution is normal with mean μ and variance σ2/2θ.

Properties

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is the only non-trivial process that is simultaneously stationary, Gaussian and Markovian. Unlike Brownian motion, whose variance grows without bound, its variance saturates, reflecting the restoring "spring" force pulling it toward μ. It is a special case of the Langevin equation used for Brownian motion with friction.

Applications

In finance the process underlies the Vasicek model of interest rates (1977) and is widely used to model mean-reverting quantities such as commodity prices, volatility, and the spread between cointegrated assets in pairs trading. In physics it describes the velocity distribution of a Brownian particle, and in evolutionary biology the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model describes trait evolution under stabilising selection toward an optimum, in contrast to unbounded Brownian-motion trait evolution.
