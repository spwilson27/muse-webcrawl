Unit square

In mathematics, a unit square is a square whose sides have length one. It is commonly taken to be the Cartesian product [0,1] × [0,1] in the Euclidean plane, with corners at (0,0), (1,0), (0,1) and (1,1).

Properties

The unit square has area 1, perimeter 4 and diagonal length √2. Whether its boundary is included depends on context; analysts often write it as a product of closed unit intervals, while other definitions may use an interior or a square centered at the origin. These variants share the same scale and basic geometric role.

Uses

The unit square is a standard domain for double integrals, probability models involving two independent uniform quantities, transformations in computer graphics, texture coordinates and numerical methods. A point (x,y) in the square can represent a pair of fractions or probabilities. Subdividing it into congruent smaller squares underlies grid methods, image pixels and approximations to area and integration.

It is the two-dimensional analogue of the unit interval in one dimension and the unit cube in three dimensions. General rectangles can often be mapped to the unit square by affine scaling, simplifying calculations while preserving ratios along each coordinate direction.
