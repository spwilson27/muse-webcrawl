Class number formula

In number theory, the class number formula is a formula relating many important invariants of an algebraic number field to a special value of a Dedekind zeta function. It was first proved by Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet for quadratic fields and later generalized.

Statement

The formula expresses the residue of the Dedekind zeta function of a number field K at s = 1 in terms of the class number hK, the regulator RK, the number of roots of unity wK, the discriminant of the field, and the numbers r1 and r2 of real and complex embeddings. Schematically: Ress=1 ζK(s) = (2r1 (2π)r2 hK RK) / (wK √|dK|).

Significance

The class number formula is one of the foundational results of algebraic number theory. It connects the analytic behavior of zeta functions with purely algebraic invariants, and it implies that the class number of a number field is finite. Generalizations lead to conjectures such as the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for elliptic curves.
