Science and technology in Brazil

Science and technology in Brazil has a long history, with organised research expanding greatly in the second half of the 20th century through federal funding agencies and public universities. Brazil produces a significant share of Latin America's scientific publications and has internationally recognised strengths in agriculture, tropical medicine, deep-water petroleum engineering, aviation and biofuels.

Institutions

The main federal funding bodies are the National Council for Scientific and Technological Development (CNPq) and the Coordination for the Improvement of Higher Education Personnel (CAPES), founded in 1951. Major research universities include the University of São Paulo, the State University of Campinas and the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro. Specialist institutes include Embrapa, the agricultural research corporation whose work transformed tropical farming in the cerrado; Fiocruz, in public health; and Embraer, one of the world's largest aircraft manufacturers.

Fields

Brazil is a world leader in ethanol fuel from sugarcane, in offshore oil production in the pre-salt fields, and in research on tropical diseases such as Chagas disease, dengue and Zika. It operates the Alcântara Launch Center near the equator and the INPE space research institute, which monitors deforestation of the Amazon by satellite. Funding has fluctuated sharply with economic cycles, and chronic underinvestment and bureaucracy are frequently cited as obstacles.
