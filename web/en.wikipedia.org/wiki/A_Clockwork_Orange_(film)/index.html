A Clockwork Orange is a 1971 dystopian crime film adapted, produced, and directed by Stanley Kubrick, based on Anthony Burgess's 1962 novel of the same name. It employs disturbing, violent images to comment on psychiatry, juvenile delinquency, youth gangs, and other social, political, and economic subjects in a dystopian near-future Britain.

Plot

Alex DeLarge (Malcolm McDowell), a charismatic young delinquent and leader of a gang of "droogs", commits acts of "ultra-violence" before being imprisoned. He volunteers for the Ludovico Technique, an experimental aversion therapy that leaves him unable to commit violence — or to enjoy Beethoven's music — and is released, defenceless against his former victims.

Reception and legacy

The film was controversial on release for its graphic violence; Kubrick withdrew it from distribution in the United Kingdom after reported copycat crimes, and it was not re-released there until 2000. It was nevertheless a critical success, nominated for four Academy Awards including Best Picture, and is now widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made.
