Sabre Corporation

Type: Public
Traded as: Nasdaq: SABR
Industry: Travel technology
Founded: 1960 (as Sabre); 2006 (privatized); 2014 (re-listed)
Headquarters: Southlake, Texas, United States
Products: Global distribution system, airline and hospitality software

Sabre Corporation is an American travel technology company headquartered in Southlake, Texas. It operates the Sabre global distribution system (GDS), one of the world's largest marketplaces connecting travel suppliers — airlines, hotels, car rental companies and rail providers — with travel buyers such as agencies and corporations. The company also provides software solutions to airlines and hotels for reservations, operations, revenue management and guest management.

History

The Sabre system originated in the 1950s as a collaboration between American Airlines and IBM. The name SABRE stood for "Semi-Automated Business Research Environment". The system, which entered service in the early 1960s, was one of the first computerized airline reservation systems and could process thousands of bookings per day. In 1976 the system was installed in a travel agency for the first time, beginning its evolution into a general distribution platform.

Sabre was spun off from AMR Corporation, American Airlines' parent, in 2000. In 2007 the company was taken private by an affiliate of TPG Capital and Silver Lake Partners in a transaction valued at about $5 billion. Sabre returned to public markets with an initial public offering on the Nasdaq in April 2014.

Operations

Sabre's business is organized around its Travel Solutions segment, which includes the GDS marketplace and airline IT solutions, and its Hospitality Solutions segment, which provides reservation and property-management technology to hotel chains. Its principal GDS competitors are Amadeus and Travelport.
