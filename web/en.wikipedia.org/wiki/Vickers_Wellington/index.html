Vickers Wellington

The Vickers Wellington was a British twin-engined, long-range medium bomber designed in the mid-1930s at Brooklands by Vickers-Armstrongs. It served throughout the Second World War, at first as one of RAF Bomber Command's principal night bombers and later in anti-submarine, transport and training roles.

Design

The Wellington was famous for its geodetic airframe construction, devised by Barnes Wallis: a lattice of aluminium members formed a basket-like structure covered in doped fabric, which proved exceptionally tough and able to survive heavy battle damage. Affectionately called the "Wimpy" after the cartoon character J. Wellington Wimpy, it carried a crew of five or six and, in early marks, twin-gun turrets in nose and tail. A total of 11,461 were built — more than any other British bomber — in marks powered chiefly by Bristol Pegasus or Hercules radial engines.

Service

Wellingtons flew on the first RAF raids of the war, shifted to night bombing after costly daylight losses, and later served with Coastal Command hunting U-boats with radar and the Leigh light, in the Mediterranean and Far East, and as trainers and transports into the 1950s.
