Ivar Aasen

Ivar Andreas Aasen (5 August 1813 – 23 September 1896) was a Norwegian philologist, lexicographer, playwright, and poet. Largely self-taught, he created Landsmål (later called Nynorsk), a written standard for Norwegian built from rural dialects, which remains one of Norway's two official written languages.

Born5 August 1813, Ørsta, Norway
Died23 September 1896, Christiania (Oslo), Norway
Known forCreating Landsmål/Nynorsk
Major worksGrammar of the Norwegian Dialects (1848), Dictionary of the Norwegian Dialects (1850)

Work

Supported by a grant from the Storting, Aasen travelled across Norway in the 1840s collecting dialect speech. His grammar (1848) and dictionary (1850) synthesised the dialects into a national language alternative to the Danish-influenced written norm of the cities.

Legacy

Aasen also wrote poems and songs in his new language, including "Nordmannen" ("The Norwegian"). The Ivar Aasen Centre in Ørsta is devoted to Nynorsk culture, and his project remains central to Norway's language politics.
