According to the Hebrew Bible, the Twelve Tribes of Israel (Hebrew: שִׁבְטֵי־יִשְׂרָאֵל, Shivtei Yisra'el) were the descendants of the twelve sons of the patriarch Jacob, also known as Israel: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Joseph, and Benjamin. Together they formed the people of Israel, and each tribe was allotted a portion of the Land of Israel after the Exodus from Egypt.

Tradition and territory

In the biblical account, the descendants of Joseph were divided into two tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh, so that with Levi (which received no landed inheritance, its members serving as priests) the number of landed tribes remained twelve. The Book of Joshua describes the distribution of the land by lot among the tribes, west and east of the Jordan River.

Division and loss

After the death of Solomon, the united monarchy split into the northern Kingdom of Israel (ten tribes) and the southern Kingdom of Judah (Judah, Benjamin, and part of Levi). The northern kingdom was destroyed by the Neo-Assyrian Empire in c. 722 BC, and its population deported, giving rise to the tradition of the "Ten Lost Tribes". The tribes of Judah, Benjamin, and Levi largely constitute the ancestors of modern Jews; other groups, including Samaritans, Beta Israel, and various communities and churches, also claim descent from the ancient tribes.
