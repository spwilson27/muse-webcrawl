Traffic congestion
CausesDemand exceeding road capacity, incidents, roadworks, weather
EffectsDelays, fuel waste, air pollution, driver stress
MitigationCongestion pricing, public transport, demand management, ITS

Traffic congestion is a condition in transport that is characterised by slower speeds, longer trip times, and increased vehicular queueing. Traffic congestion on urban road networks occurs when traffic demand exceeds the available capacity of the road, or when incidents such as collisions and breakdowns temporarily reduce capacity.

Causes

Congestion is classically divided into recurrent congestion — the predictable rush-hour overload at bottlenecks — and non-recurrent congestion caused by crashes, roadworks, special events and weather. Small disturbances can grow into "phantom traffic jams" (stop-and-go waves) through car-following behaviour even without any visible obstruction. Induced demand complicates remedies: expanding road capacity tends to attract additional trips, eroding the relief over time.

Impacts and measurement

Congestion costs economies billions in lost time and wasted fuel and increases emissions and local air pollution. Analysts such as INRIX and TomTom publish annual rankings of the world's most congested cities; commuters in cities like London, Mexico City, Bangalore and several US metros have been measured losing more than 100 hours a year to congestion.

Countermeasures

Responses include congestion pricing (as in Singapore, London and Stockholm), high-occupancy vehicle lanes, ramp metering, improved public transport, cycling infrastructure, teleworking, and intelligent transportation systems that adapt signals and inform drivers in real time.
