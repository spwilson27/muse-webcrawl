Expo 67

The International and Universal Exposition of 1967, known as Expo 67, was a general exhibition held in Montreal, Quebec, Canada, from 27 April to 29 October 1967, as the centrepiece of Canada's centennial celebrations. It is considered one of the most successful world's fairs of the 20th century, with over 50 million visitors and 62 participating nations.

Expo 67 was built largely on islands in the Saint Lawrence River — Saint Helen's Island (expanded) and the artificial Île Notre-Dame — served by the new Montreal Metro. Its theme, "Man and His World" (Terre des Hommes), was drawn from Antoine de Saint-Exupéry. Landmark structures included the United States pavilion, a geodesic dome by Buckminster Fuller (now the Montreal Biosphere), and Moshe Safdie's Habitat 67 housing complex.
