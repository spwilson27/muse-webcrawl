Einbeck

Einbeck is a town in the district of Northeim in southern Lower Saxony, Germany. It has a population of around 30,000 and is famed for its well-preserved medieval old town of half-timbered houses and for its brewing tradition: the town gave its name to Bock beer, whose name derives from a corruption of "Einbeck" (via the Bavarian pronunciation "Oanpock").

History

The area was settled in the early Middle Ages around a settlement on the river Ilme. Einbeck grew prosperous in the Middle Ages as a member of the Hanseatic League, exporting beer far beyond the region — Einbeck beer was drunk at the Bavarian court in Munich, where brewers sought to imitate it, creating the Bock style in the 17th century. A devastating town fire in 1540 destroyed most of the town on the feast day of St Anne; Einbeck was rebuilt with the ornately decorated half-timbered burgher houses that characterise it today. The Einbecker Brauhaus continues the town's brewing tradition.

Sights

Landmarks include the market church of St James (St. Jakobi) with its leaning tower, the Münsterkirche St. Alexandri, one of the largest Gothic churches in northern Germany, and the old town hall. The Eicke'sches Haus, with its carved half-timbered façade, houses the tourist information. The town is also home to the PS.SPEICHER, a museum devoted to the history of motor vehicles, which opened in 2014.

Transport and economy

Einbeck lies near the A7 motorway between Hanover and Göttingen. Besides brewing, the town is known for KWS Saat, one of the world's leading plant-breeding companies, which has its headquarters in Einbeck.
