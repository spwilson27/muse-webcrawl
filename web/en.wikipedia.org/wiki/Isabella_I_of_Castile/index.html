Isabella I (Spanish: Isabel I; 22 April 1451 – 26 November 1504), also called Isabella the Catholic, was Queen of Castile and León from 1474 until her death. Her marriage to Ferdinand II of Aragon in 1469 joined the two largest Iberian crowns in a dynastic union that laid the foundation of modern Spain, though the two kingdoms retained separate institutions.

Reign

Isabella's accession was disputed by supporters of her niece Joanna la Beltraneja, whom she defeated in the War of the Castilian Succession (1475–1479). As queen she reformed Castile's administration and finances, curbed the nobility, reorganised the military orders and codified royal justice. With Ferdinand — jointly styled the Catholic Monarchs by Pope Alexander VI — she completed the Reconquista with the conquest of the Emirate of Granada in January 1492.

1492 and the Inquisition

That same year the monarchs issued the Alhambra Decree expelling practising Jews from Spain and sponsored Christopher Columbus's first voyage, which reached the Americas and began Spanish colonisation. Earlier, in 1478, Isabella had obtained papal authorisation for the Spanish Inquisition, which operated under crown control and targeted converted Jews and Muslims suspected of heresy. Her religious policies and the forced conversions that followed remain central to debates over her legacy; a cause for her canonisation in the Catholic Church has been promoted but not completed.

Death and succession

Isabella died in 1504 and was buried in the Royal Chapel of Granada. She was succeeded in Castile by her daughter Joanna, whose marriage to Philip of Habsburg brought the Spanish crowns into the Habsburg inheritance of Charles V.
