Train Protection & Warning System

The Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS) is a train protection system used throughout the British mainline network, and in Victoria, Australia. It was developed following a series of accidents in which trains passed signals at danger (SPADs), most notably the Southall rail crash (1997) and the Ladbroke Grove rail crash (1999).

The UK government Health and Safety Executive (HSE) recommended that TPWS be installed across the network at locations where the consequences of a SPAD could be severe. The system was fitted at thousands of signals, level crossings, buffer stops and permanent speed restrictions, and became fully operational across the mainline network in the early 2000s.

How it works

TPWS uses pairs of electronic loops placed between the rails at specified distances on the approach to a signal or speed restriction. Each loop transmits at a different frequency. A receiver on the train detects the loops in sequence; by timing the interval between them, the system determines whether the train is travelling too fast for the location.

Overspeed sensors

Overspeed sensor (OSS) loops are installed on the approach to selected stop signals and permanent speed restrictions. If a train passes over the second loop before a set time has expired — indicating it is travelling above the permitted approach speed — the system applies an emergency brake application and sounds an audible warning in the cab.

Train stop sensors

Train stop sensor (TSS) loops are installed at the signal itself. If a train passes a signal at danger, the loops cause an immediate emergency brake application, bringing the train to a stop.

Components

  • Trackside loops — pairs of wire loops laid between the running rails, energised at distinct frequencies (typically in the 60–66 kHz range).
  • Train-borne equipment — a receiver aerial under the locomotive or multiple unit, a TPWS control panel in the cab, and an interface to the brake system.
  • Control panel — provides the driver with visual and audible indications, and a reset arrangement following an intervention (with a lockout period before reset is possible).

Coverage and limitations

TPWS was fitted at over 10,000 signals, hundreds of buffer stops and permanent speed restrictions in Britain. It is designed to stop a train travelling at up to about 70 mph (113 km/h) within the signal overlap if it passes a signal at danger. It does not provide the continuous speed supervision of a full automatic train protection (ATP) system, and at higher speeds a train may overrun significantly, though the severity of any collision is reduced.

TPWS+

An enhanced version, TPWS+, extends effective overspeed protection to higher speeds for certain locations by placing the overspeed loops further from the signal.
