Southwest Airlines

Southwest Airlines Co. is an American low-cost airline headquartered in Dallas, Texas. It is the fourth-largest airline in North America by passengers carried and operates an extensive domestic network, historically distinguished by a single aircraft type (the Boeing 737), open seating, free checked baggage, and a point-to-point route model. The airline was founded in 1967 by Herb Kelleher and Rollin King as Air Southwest Co. and began scheduled service on June 18, 1971, between Dallas, Houston, and San Antonio.

History

Southwest began as an intrastate Texas airline, which exempted it from federal Civil Aeronautics Board fare regulation. Rival carriers sued to keep it grounded for over three years before it won the right to fly. The Wright Amendment of 1979 restricted flights from its home at Dallas Love Field to nearby states, limits fully repealed only in 2014. The airline pioneered practices later copied across the industry: quick aircraft turnarounds, no assigned seats, no-frills service, and extensive employee profit-sharing. It acquired AirTran Airways in 2011, completing integration in 2014, and began international service to the Caribbean and Central America.

Business model

Southwest's model relies on a single fleet type to reduce training and maintenance costs, high aircraft utilization, and direct sales through its own website. Its "Bags Fly Free" policy long set it apart from competitors; in 2024–2025, under pressure from an activist investor, the company announced major changes including assigned seating, premium seating options, and an end to universally free checked bags for tickets purchased from May 2025 onward.

Incidents

The airline experienced a massive operational meltdown during the December 2022 winter storm Elliott, cancelling roughly 17,000 flights and incurring a record $140 million U.S. Department of Transportation penalty. Notable accidents include Flight 1248 (2005) and Flight 1380 (2018), the latter its only accident with a passenger fatality.
