Gong Li

Gong Li (Chinese: 巩俐; born 31 December 1965) is a Chinese actress. Regarded as one of the greatest actresses in Chinese cinema, she helped bring Chinese film to international attention in the late 1980s and 1990s through her collaborations with director Zhang Yimou. She is widely known in China by the honorific title "Queen Gong" and was the first Chinese actress to achieve sustained international fame.

Early life and breakthrough

Gong was born in Shenyang, Liaoning, and grew up in Jinan, Shandong. After twice failing to enter music schools, she was admitted in 1985 to the Central Academy of Drama in Beijing. While still a student she was cast by Zhang Yimou in his directorial debut Red Sorghum (1987), which won the Golden Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival and launched both of their careers.

Career

Her films with Zhang Yimou — including Ju Dou (1990), Raise the Red Lantern (1991), The Story of Qiu Ju (1992), for which she won the Volpi Cup for Best Actress at Venice, To Live (1994) and Shanghai Triad (1995) — made her the emblematic face of China's Fifth Generation cinema. She also starred in Chen Kaige's Farewell My Concubine (1993), which won the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

Gong later worked internationally, appearing in Chinese Box (1997), The Emperor and the Assassin (1999), Wong Kar-wai's 2046 (2004), Memoirs of a Geisha (2005), Miami Vice (2006), Hannibal Rising (2007), Zhang Yimou's Curse of the Golden Flower (2006) and Coming Home (2014), Disney's live-action Mulan (2020), and Lou Ye's Saturday Fiction (2019).

Honours

She has served on the juries of the Berlin, Venice and Cannes film festivals, chairing the Venice jury in 2002 — the first Chinese person to do so. She was appointed a UNESCO Artist for Peace and a goodwill ambassador for the UN Food and Agriculture Organization, and France made her a Commandeur of the Ordre des Arts et des Lettres.
