Penobscot Building

The Greater Penobscot Building, commonly called the Penobscot Building, is a 47-storey Art Deco skyscraper in downtown Detroit, Michigan. Completed in 1928 and standing 175 metres (574 feet) tall — 199 metres (654 feet) to the top of its beacon — it was the tallest building in Michigan for many years and remains one of the most prominent landmarks on the Detroit skyline.

Designed by architect Wirt C. Rowland of the firm Smith, Hinchman & Grylls, the building is clad in limestone with rich Art Deco ornamentation, including motifs inspired by Native American art, reflecting the name Penobscot, taken from a Native American tribe of Maine. It is the third building in a complex of three Penobscot buildings on Fort Street.

For decades the tower's peak carried a large red aviation beacon, a familiar feature of the night-time skyline. The building is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as a contributing property of the Detroit Financial District.
