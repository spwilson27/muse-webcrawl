TXS 0506+056 is a very high-energy blazar — an active galactic nucleus with a relativistic jet pointing close to Earth's line of sight — located in the constellation Orion, roughly four billion light-years away. It became famous in 2018 as the first identified source of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos, marking a milestone in multimessenger astronomy.

The 2017 neutrino event

On 22 September 2017, the IceCube Neutrino Observatory at the South Pole detected a high-energy neutrino, IceCube-170922A, with an energy of about 290 trillion electron volts. Its arrival direction coincided with TXS 0506+056, which the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope found to be in a flaring state, its gamma-ray output several times above normal. The association, published in July 2018 with a statistical significance of about 3 sigma, was strengthened when IceCube researchers searched archival data and found an earlier burst of neutrinos from the same direction in 2014–2015, during a period when the blazar was not gamma-ray flaring.

Significance

The detection showed that blazar jets accelerate protons to extreme energies, producing both neutrinos and gamma rays — long theorised but never before directly demonstrated — and established neutrinos as a practical tool for locating cosmic-ray sources. TXS 0506+056 remains an intensely studied target for neutrino, gamma-ray, and optical observatories.
