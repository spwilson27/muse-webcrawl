Submarine sandwich

A submarine sandwich, commonly known as a sub, is a type of American sandwich made from a cylindrical bread roll split lengthwise and filled with meats, cheeses, vegetables, and condiments. The sandwich has many regional names across the United States — including hoagie (Philadelphia area), hero (New York), grinder (New England), wedge (parts of New York), spukie (Boston), and po' boy (Louisiana, a related but distinct tradition).

History

The submarine sandwich is generally credited to Italian-American communities of the northeastern United States in the early 20th century. One popular account attributes the name to Dominic Conti of Paterson, New Jersey, who in 1910 saw a recovered submarine, the Fenian Ram, in a museum and named his sandwich after its shape; another associates the "sub" name with the naval base at Groton, Connecticut, during the Second World War. The Philadelphia "hoagie" is likewise variously derived from the Hog Island shipyard workers who ate them.

Composition

The classic Italian sub layers cured meats such as salami, capicola, ham, and pepperoni with provolone, shredded lettuce, tomato, onion, oregano, and oil and vinegar on a long roll. Subs are served cold or toasted ("baked"), and sandwich-shop chains such as Subway — the world's largest restaurant chain, named after the sandwich — have popularised the format internationally.
