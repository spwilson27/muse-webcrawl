Peripheral myelin protein 22 (PMP22), also known as growth arrest-specific protein 3 (GAS-3), is a 22-kDa transmembrane glycoprotein and a major structural component of the myelin sheath in the peripheral nervous system, where it is produced by Schwann cells. Although it makes up only a few percent of peripheral myelin protein, PMP22 dosage is exquisitely critical: both too much and too little cause hereditary demyelinating neuropathies, making the gene a textbook example of a dosage-sensitive locus.

Structure and function

PMP22 is a small tetraspan membrane protein with four transmembrane domains, one N-linked glycosylation site and intracellular amino and carboxyl termini. In compact myelin it is thought to contribute to the adhesion and stability of the wrapped Schwann-cell membrane layers and to regulate Schwann cell growth and differentiation. Before reaching the myelin sheath, much of newly synthesized PMP22 is degraded by the proteasome, and misfolded mutant protein can be retained in the endoplasmic reticulum — a mechanism contributing to some point-mutation forms of disease.

Clinical significance

The PMP22 gene lies in a 1.4–1.5 megabase region of chromosome 17p12 flanked by low-copy repeats, which predisposes to unequal crossing over. A duplication of the segment produces three copies of PMP22 and causes Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease type 1A (CMT1A) — the single most common cause of hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy, with slow conduction velocities, distal weakness and wasting, pes cavus and "onion bulb" Schwann-cell pathology. The reciprocal deletion of one copy causes hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies (HNPP), in which minor compression produces transient focal palsies and nerves show sausage-like focal myelin thickenings (tomacula). Certain point mutations instead cause more severe early-onset phenotypes such as Dejerine–Sottas syndrome (CMT3) or congenital hypomyelinating neuropathy.

There is no disease-modifying treatment for CMT1A; management is supportive, and therapies under investigation include lowering PMP22 expression with antisense, siRNA and small-molecule approaches, informed by transgenic rat and mouse models that overexpress the gene.
