Musique concrète

Musique concrète
OriginParis, 1948
Key figurePierre Schaeffer
TechniquesTape splicing, loops, speed manipulation

Musique concrète is a type of music composition that utilises recorded sounds as raw material. It was developed in the 1940s by the French composer Pierre Schaeffer at the Studio d'Essai of French radio in Paris.

Techniques

Composers manipulate recordings by splicing tape, changing playback speed and direction, layering sounds, and applying loops and filtering. The approach treats any recorded sound — trains, voices, instruments — as potential musical material, listened to for its sonic qualities rather than its source, a practice Schaeffer called "reduced listening."

Influence

Schaeffer's collaborators included Pierre Henry; their Symphonie pour un homme seul (1950) is a landmark work. Musique concrète profoundly influenced electronic music, sampling, and hip hop production.
