Buchenwald concentration camp

Buchenwald was a Nazi concentration camp established in July 1937 on the Ettersberg hill near Weimar in Germany. It became one of the largest camps in the German Reich. Its gate bore the motto Jedem das Seine ("to each his own").

Prisoners and conditions

Between 1937 and 1945 about 280,000 people from across Europe were imprisoned at Buchenwald and its subcamps: Jews, political prisoners, Roma, Jehovah's Witnesses, prisoners of war, homosexual men, and others persecuted by the regime. Prisoners were subjected to forced labour in armaments and quarries, starvation, disease, medical experiments, and executions. An estimated 56,000 people died. The camp was not an extermination camp with gas chambers, but mass murder occurred there, including of Soviet prisoners of war.

Liberation and aftermath

As American forces approached in April 1945, an underground prisoner resistance seized control of the camp shortly before the U.S. Army arrived on 11 April 1945. From 1945 to 1950 the Soviet occupation authorities reused the site as Special Camp No. 2, in which thousands more died. Today Buchenwald is a major memorial and museum.
