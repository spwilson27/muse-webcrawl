Regurgitalite

Category: Bromalite (fossilised digestive material)
Produced by: Regurgitation of indigestible remains
Modern analogue: Owl pellets

A regurgitalite is a type of bromalite — fossilised material from the digestive tract of an organism — consisting of food remains that were regurgitated rather than passed through the gut. Regurgitalites preserve indigestible items such as bones, teeth, scales and shells that a predator ejected from its mouth after consuming prey.

Formation

Many living predators, including owls, hawks, gulls and some reptiles, expel compacted pellets of indigestible matter. Fossil equivalents, recognised by their composition, packing and lack of the digestive etching typical of faecal material, record the same behaviour in extinct animals; examples have been attributed to marine reptiles, pterosaurs, birds and predatory fish.

Scientific value

Because the enclosed remains often represent prey that is otherwise rarely fossilised, regurgitalites are valuable evidence of ancient food webs. They can reveal the diet of the producer, the diversity of small prey animals in an ecosystem, and details of digestive physiology.
