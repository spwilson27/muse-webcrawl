Streptococcus

Streptococcus is a genus of gram-positive coccus (spherical) bacteria belonging to the family Streptococcaceae. The cells divide along a single axis and so grow in chains or pairs, giving the genus its name (from Greek streptos, "twisted chain"). Streptococci are oxidase- and catalase-negative, and many are facultative anaerobes.

Classification and disease

Streptococci are classified by their haemolytic properties on blood agar: alpha-haemolytic (partial, greenish), beta-haemolytic (complete), and gamma (non-haemolytic). They are also grouped by Lancefield antigens. Clinically important species include Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A), which causes strep throat, scarlet fever, and necrotizing fasciitis; S. agalactiae (Group B), a cause of neonatal infections; and S. pneumoniae, a major cause of pneumonia and meningitis.

Other species are harmless commensals or industrially useful: S. thermophilus is used in the production of yogurt and cheese. See also Staphylococcus.
