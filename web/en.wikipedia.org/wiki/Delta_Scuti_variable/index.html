Delta Scuti variable

A Delta Scuti variable is a pulsating variable star that exhibits variations in brightness due to both radial and non-radial pulsations of its surface. They are named after the prototype star Delta Scuti in the constellation Scutum.

Characteristics

Delta Scuti stars are spectral type A to early F stars located in the instability strip on or near the main sequence, with masses of about 1.5 to 2.5 solar masses. Their periods are short, typically between about 18 minutes and 8 hours, and brightness amplitudes range from a few thousandths of a magnitude up to about 0.9 magnitudes in high-amplitude examples. The pulsations are driven by the kappa mechanism acting in the second ionisation zone of helium, with contributions from turbulent pressure in some models.

Subtypes

High-amplitude Delta Scuti stars (HADS) pulsate in one or two radial modes with large amplitudes, while the more common low-amplitude stars pulsate in many simultaneous modes. SX Phoenicis variables are the metal-poor, Population II counterparts of the class.

Significance

Because many modes can be observed simultaneously, Delta Scuti stars are prime targets for asteroseismology, and space missions such as Kepler and TESS have revealed rich frequency spectra used to probe their internal structure and rotation.
