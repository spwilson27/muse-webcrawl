Microstock photography

Microstock libraries host millions of searchable generic images

Microstock photography, also known as micropayment photography, is part of the stock photography industry in which images are sold for very low prices (typically from about US$0.20 to $10 per download) on a royalty-free basis, and contributors are paid small commissions per sale. The model depends on selling a large volume of images through online marketplaces rather than the high-priced, rights-managed licensing of traditional stock agencies.

History

The microstock model was pioneered around 2000 by websites such as iStockphoto, founded by Bruce Livingstone in Calgary, Canada. iStockphoto began as a free image exchange and introduced micropayments in 2001; it was acquired by Getty Images in 2006. Competitors including Shutterstock (founded 2003, notable for its subscription model), Dreamstime, Fotolia and 123RF followed. Shutterstock's 2012 initial public offering marked the industry's maturity.

Business model

Anyone — amateur or professional — can submit photographs (and later illustrations, vectors and video) to a microstock agency. Images pass a quality review, are tagged with keywords, and are then sold repeatedly under royalty-free licences. Contributors typically receive 15–45% of the sale price or a fixed per-download fee. The low prices democratized access to stock imagery for small businesses and bloggers but sharply reduced per-image earnings for professional photographers, prompting ongoing debate within the industry, intensified by the arrival of generative AI imagery.
