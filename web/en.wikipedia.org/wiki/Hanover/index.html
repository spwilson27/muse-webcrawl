Hanover

Hanover (German: Hannover)
Capital of Lower Saxony, Germany
Population: c. 535,000
First mentioned: 1150

Hanover (German: Hannover) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Lower Saxony. Its 535,932 inhabitants (2021) make it the 13th-largest city in Germany as well as the fourth-largest city in northern Germany. The city lies on the River Leine and the Mittelland Canal.

History

Hanover was founded in medieval times on the east bank of the Leine as a small village of ferrymen and fishermen. It became a town in 1241 and a residence of the Welf dukes. From 1714 the electors of Hanover also reigned as kings of Great Britain (beginning with George I), a personal union lasting until 1837. Hanover became a kingdom in 1814 and a Prussian province in 1866. Heavily bombed in the Second World War, the city was rebuilt and became famous for its trade fairs.

Economy and culture

Hanover hosts major trade fairs, including the Hannover Messe industrial fair; it hosted Expo 2000. Major employers include Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles, Continental, and TUI. Sights include the Herrenhausen Gardens, the New Town Hall, and the Maschsee lake.
