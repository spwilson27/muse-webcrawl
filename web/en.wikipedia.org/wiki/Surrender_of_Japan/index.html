Surrender of Japan

The surrender of Japan in World War II was announced by Emperor Hirohito on 15 August 1945 and formally signed on 2 September 1945, bringing the hostilities of World War II to a close. By the end of July 1945, the Imperial Japanese Navy had become incapable of conducting major operations and an Allied invasion of Japan was imminent. Together with the United Kingdom and China, the United States called for the unconditional surrender of the Japanese armed forces in the Potsdam Declaration on 26 July 1945 — the alternative being "prompt and utter destruction".

While publicly stating their intent to fight on to the bitter end, Japan's leaders (the Supreme Council for the Direction of the War) were also making attempts to get the neutral Soviet Union to mediate peace on terms more favourable to the Japanese. While maintaining a sufficient level of diplomatic engagement with the Japanese to give them the impression they might be able to mediate, the Soviets were covertly preparing to attack Japanese forces in Manchuria and Korea, fulfilling a promise secretly made to the United States and the United Kingdom at the Tehran and Yalta Conferences.

Atomic bombings and Soviet entry

On 6 August 1945, at 8:15 am local time, the United States detonated an atomic bomb over the Japanese city of Hiroshima. Sixteen hours later, American President Harry S. Truman called again for Japan's surrender, warning them to "expect a rain of ruin from the air, the like of which has never been seen on this earth." Late in the evening of 8 August, in accordance with the Yalta agreements but in violation of the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact, the Soviet Union declared war on Japan, and shortly after midnight on 9 August invaded the imperial puppet state of Manchukuo. Later that day the United States dropped a second atomic bomb, on Nagasaki.

The Emperor's decision

The combined shock of these events caused Emperor Hirohito to intervene and order the Supreme Council to accept the terms the Allies had set out in the Potsdam Declaration for ending the war. After several days of behind-the-scenes negotiations and a failed coup d'état intended to prevent the announcement, Hirohito gave a recorded radio address to the Empire on 15 August. In the radio address, called the Gyokuon-hōsō ("Jewel Voice Broadcast"), he announced the surrender of Japan to the Allies.

Formal ceremony

On 28 August, the occupation of Japan by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers began. The surrender ceremony was held on 2 September aboard the United States Navy battleship USS Missouri, at which officials from the Government of Japan signed the Japanese Instrument of Surrender, thereby ending the hostilities. American general of the Army Douglas MacArthur, who as Supreme Commander accepted the surrender, oversaw the ceremony.

Aftermath

Following the surrender, Allied forces occupied Japan for seven years, until the Treaty of San Francisco took effect in 1952. The last Japanese soldier to formally surrender was Second Lieutenant Hiroo Onoda, who surrendered in the Philippines in 1974 after hiding in the jungle for nearly thirty years.
