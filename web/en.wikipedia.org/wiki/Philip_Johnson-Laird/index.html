Philip Nicholas Johnson-Laird (born 12 October 1936) is a British psychologist and cognitive scientist. He is best known for developing, together with Ruth M. J. Byrne and others, the theory of mental models as an account of human reasoning, deduction, and comprehension.

Career

Johnson-Laird studied psychology at University College London, where he later became a professor. He subsequently moved to Princeton University, where he was Stuart Professor of Psychology. His early work concerned the psychology of language and thinking; with Peter Wason he co-edited the influential volume Thinking and Reasoning (1968) and conducted classic studies of the Wason selection task.

Mental models theory

In Mental Models (1983), Johnson-Laird proposed that people reason not by applying formal rules of logic but by constructing internal models of possibilities consistent with the premises, and drawing conclusions that hold in those models. The theory explains systematic errors in deduction: reasoners tend to overlook models in which a conclusion fails, especially when problems require multiple models to be held in working memory. With Byrne he extended the account to conditional reasoning and the suppression effect, and later work applied mental models to probabilistic reasoning, counterfactuals, emotion, and creativity.

Honours

Johnson-Laird is a Fellow of the Royal Society and of the British Academy, and a member of the US National Academy of Sciences. He has received numerous awards for his contributions to cognitive psychology.
