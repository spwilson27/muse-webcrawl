Gabriel Barkay

Gabriel Barkay (born 1944) is an Israeli archaeologist specialising in the archaeology of Jerusalem and the biblical period. He is best known for discovering the Ketef Hinnom scrolls, two tiny silver amulets bearing the Priestly Blessing from the Book of Numbers — the oldest surviving texts from the Hebrew Bible.

Barkay has excavated in Jerusalem for over five decades.

Career

Barkay was born in the Budapest ghetto in 1944 and immigrated to Israel as a child. He studied at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and Tel Aviv University, where he completed his doctorate. He has taught at the Hebrew University, Tel Aviv University, and Bar-Ilan University, and has directed or participated in numerous excavations in Jerusalem, including in the City of David, the Jewish Quarter, and around the Temple Mount.

Ketef Hinnom discovery

In 1979, during excavations of Iron Age burial caves at Ketef Hinnom, southwest of Jerusalem's Old City, Barkay's team discovered two small silver scrolls in a repository of grave goods. Carefully unrolled years later at the Israel Museum laboratories, the scrolls proved to be inscribed in paleo-Hebrew script with versions of the Priestly Blessing ("The LORD bless you and keep you..."), dating to the late 7th or early 6th century BCE — roughly 400 years older than the Dead Sea Scrolls.

Temple Mount Sifting Project

Barkay co-founded the Temple Mount Sifting Project with Zachi Dvira, which systematically sifts debris removed from the Temple Mount in 1999, recovering large quantities of artefacts from all periods of the site's history.
