GOG.com (formerly Good Old Games) is a digital distribution platform for video games operated by GOG sp. z o.o., a subsidiary of the Polish company CD Projekt. Launched in 2008, it originally specialised in selling classic PC games updated to run on modern systems, and is best known for its strict policy that all games sold are free of digital rights management (DRM).

GOG sells DRM-free games, downloadable as standalone offline installers.

History

GOG opened in public beta in 2008 with catalogues from publishers such as Interplay and Codemasters, selling older titles for a few dollars with extras like soundtracks and manuals, and guaranteeing compatibility with Windows XP and Vista. A 2010 publicity stunt in which the site appeared to close caused controversy but relaunched the service with a wider catalogue. In 2012 it rebranded from "Good Old Games" to GOG.com and began selling newer and indie releases day-and-date; its own parent company's The Witcher series is a flagship franchise. The optional GOG Galaxy client (2015; version 2.0 in 2019) offers library management, updates and cross-platform friends while keeping DRM-free offline installers available.

Policies and market position

GOG's DRM-free stance, 30-day refund policy (extended in 2020 to allow refunds even after playing), and offline installers distinguish it from the dominant platform Steam. In 2019 GOG ended its "Fair Price Package" regional-pricing credit scheme, citing thinner margins. Though far smaller than Steam, GOG occupies a significant niche among players concerned with game preservation and ownership, and runs a preservation programme to keep classic titles playable on current hardware.
