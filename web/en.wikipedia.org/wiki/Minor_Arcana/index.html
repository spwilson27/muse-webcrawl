Minor Arcana

The Minor Arcana is the set of 56 cards in a tarot deck that resemble the cards of an ordinary playing-card deck: four suits, each containing ten numbered "pip" cards (ace through ten) and four court cards — Page, Knight, Queen, and King. Together with the 22 trump cards of the Major Arcana, they make up the standard 78-card tarot pack.

Suits

In the Latin-suited tarot tradition inherited from Italian and French decks, the suits are Swords, Cups, Coins (or Pentacles), and Batons (or Wands/Staves). Occult writers of the 18th and 19th centuries, including Court de Gébelin and Eliphas Lévi, associated the suits with the four elements and with divisions of society. In cartomancy, the Minor Arcana are generally read as concerning everyday events, people, and situations, in contrast to the weightier spiritual or archetypal themes of the trumps.

Cards and games

The Rider–Waite tarot of 1909 famously gave pictorial scenes to all the pip cards, an innovation by artist Pamela Colman Smith that shaped modern divinatory tarot. Historically, however, tarot was created for trick-taking games in 15th-century Italy, and the Minor Arcana functioned simply as suit cards; in French tarot games the court cards (king, queen, cavalier, jack) carry most of the point value.
