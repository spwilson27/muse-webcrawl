Stephenson's Rocket is an early steam locomotive of 0-2-2 wheel arrangement. It was built for and won the Rainhill Trials of the Liverpool and Manchester Railway, held in October 1829 to choose locomotive designs for the new line.

Built by Robert Stephenson at his company's Forth Street works in Newcastle upon Tyne, Rocket combined several innovations: a multi-tubular boiler, a blast pipe to intensify the fire, and cylinders set at an angle driving the wheels directly. Though not the first steam locomotive, it brought together features that became the template for steam traction for the next 150 years.

Rainhill Trials

Five locomotives competed at Rainhill in October 1829. Rocket was the only entrant to complete all trials successfully, reaching speeds of around 24–30 mph (39–48 km/h) and hauling loads well above the stipulated weights. During the trials the MP William Huskisson was fatally struck by Rocket at the railway's opening the following year — an accident widely reported as the first railway passenger fatality.

Later life and preservation

Rocket worked on the Liverpool and Manchester Railway and later in Cumberland before being altered substantially in service. Donated to the Patent Office Museum in 1862, it is now in the collection of the Science Museum, London. A working replica was built in 1979 for the 150th anniversary, and the original was displayed at the National Railway Museum in York in 2018–19.
