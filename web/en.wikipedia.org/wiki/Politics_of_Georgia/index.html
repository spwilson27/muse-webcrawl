Politics of Georgia

The politics of Georgia involve a parliamentary representative democratic republic with a multi-party system. The President of Georgia is the ceremonial head of state, and the Prime Minister of Georgia is the head of government, exercising executive power. Legislative power is vested in the Parliament of Georgia, a unicameral legislature of 150 members.

Following constitutional amendments adopted in 2017, Georgia completed a transition from a semi-presidential to a parliamentary system. The President is elected by an electoral college rather than by direct popular vote, and executive authority is concentrated in the government headed by the Prime Minister.

History

Georgia declared independence from the Soviet Union in 1991. The 1990s were marked by civil unrest, economic collapse, and conflicts in Abkhazia and South Ossetia. The Rose Revolution of 2003 brought Mikheil Saakashvili to power and a period of rapid reform. In 2012, Georgia experienced its first democratic transfer of power through elections, when the Georgian Dream coalition defeated Saakashvili's United National Movement.

Foreign relations

Georgia has pursued Euro-Atlantic integration, seeking membership in the European Union and NATO, a course complicated by the 2008 Russo-Georgian War and the Russian occupation of Abkhazia and South Ossetia, which Georgia and most of the international community regard as Georgian territory.
