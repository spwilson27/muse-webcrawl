Grand Duchess Tatiana Nikolaevna of Russia

Grand Duchess Tatiana Nikolaevna of Russia (10 June 1897 – 17 July 1918) was the second daughter of Tsar Nicholas II of Russia, the last monarch of Russia, and Tsarina Alexandra. She was murdered with her family in Yekaterinburg in 1918.

Life

Tatiana was regarded as the most elegant of the four sisters and the most like her mother in temperament; she was efficient and reserved, and was often called "the Governess" by her siblings for her habit of organizing the others. During the First World War she trained and worked as a Red Cross nurse alongside her elder sister Olga and their mother, treating wounded soldiers at a hospital in Tsarskoye Selo. She also chaired a committee aiding refugees.

Death and legacy

Following the abdication of Nicholas II in 1917, Tatiana lived with her family under house arrest and in exile, before the family was killed by Bolshevik guards in the Ipatiev House in Yekaterinburg on 17 July 1918. Her remains were recovered from a mass grave near Yekaterinburg in 1991 and identified by DNA analysis. She was canonized with her family by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000.
