The Detective (novel)

The Detective is a 1966 novel by American author Roderick Thorp. It follows Joe Leland, a private investigator and former New York City police detective, who is hired to investigate a murder case in which the apparent killer has already been convicted and executed, and whose enquiries uncover a web of corruption linking the case to a prominent figure's past.

Adaptations and sequels

The novel was adapted into the 1968 film The Detective, starring Frank Sinatra as Joe Leland. Thorp wrote a sequel, Nothing Lasts Forever (1979), in which Leland, visiting his daughter in a Los Angeles skyscraper, battles terrorists who seize the building. Although the sequel was not filmed with Sinatra, it became the basis of the 1988 action film Die Hard, with the character renamed John McClane and played by Bruce Willis. Because of this chain, The Detective is sometimes regarded as the indirect first instalment of the Die Hard franchise.
