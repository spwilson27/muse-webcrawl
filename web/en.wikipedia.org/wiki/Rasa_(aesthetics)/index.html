Rasa (aesthetics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rasa (Sanskrit: रस, literally "juice", "essence" or "taste") is a concept in Indian arts denoting the aesthetic flavour or emotional essence evoked in the audience by a work of art, particularly in theatre, dance, music and poetry. The concept originates in the Natya Shastra, the ancient Sanskrit treatise on the performing arts attributed to Bharata Muni, and was developed extensively by the Kashmiri philosopher Abhinavagupta (c. 950–1016).

Theory

According to the theory, a performance presents bhavas (emotional states) through the combination of determinants (vibhava), consequents (anubhava) and transitory states (vyabhicaribhava). When these are skilfully combined, a durable emotional state (sthayibhava) is transformed in the sensitive spectator (the rasika or sahridaya) into rasa: an aesthetic relish that is distinguished from ordinary emotion because it is universalised, detached from personal circumstance and savoured for its own sake.

The rasas

Bharata enumerated eight rasas; a ninth, shanta, was added by later theorists including Abhinavagupta.

In dance, music and Bhakti

Rasa theory remains a foundation of Indian classical dance forms such as Bharatanatyam and Kathakali, whose performers train in the precise expression of bhava through gesture (mudra) and facial movement (abhinaya). In Hindustani music, ragas are traditionally associated with particular rasas and moods. In devotional (bhakti) traditions, especially Gaudiya Vaishnavism, rasa theory was adapted to describe the modes of loving devotion toward Krishna, culminating in madhurya (sweetness).
