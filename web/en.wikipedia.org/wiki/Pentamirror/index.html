Pentamirror


Type: Optical component
Used in: Single-lens reflex cameras

A pentamirror is an optical device used in the viewfinder systems of some single-lens reflex (SLR) cameras. Like the more expensive pentaprism, it reflects the image from the camera's focusing screen so that the photographer sees an upright, correctly oriented view through the eyepiece; unlike a pentaprism, which is a solid block of glass, a pentamirror is a hollow assembly of mirrors—typically three mirror surfaces arranged in the characteristic roof shape.

Characteristics

Because it is hollow, a pentamirror is considerably lighter and cheaper to manufacture than an equivalent glass pentaprism, making it common in entry-level DSLRs. Its disadvantages are a somewhat dimmer viewfinder image, since mirror reflections lose more light than the total internal reflection inside a prism, and greater susceptibility to misalignment if the camera is knocked. Typical viewfinder magnification and coverage are also slightly reduced compared with pentaprism bodies.

Applications

Pentamirrors appeared widely in consumer film SLRs from the 1980s onwards and were carried into entry-level digital SLRs from manufacturers such as Canon and Nikon, while professional models retained pentaprisms. Mirrorless cameras, which use electronic viewfinders, have made the device largely obsolete.
