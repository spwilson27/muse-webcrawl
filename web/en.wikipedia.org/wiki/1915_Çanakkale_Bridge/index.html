The 1915 Çanakkale Bridge (Turkish: 1915 Çanakkale Köprüsü) is a suspension bridge across the Dardanelles strait in northwestern Turkey, connecting the European side near Gelibolu (Gallipoli) with the Asian side near Lapseki in Çanakkale Province. On completion it became the longest suspension bridge in the world by main span, at 2,023 metres.

Name and symbolism

The name commemorates the Turkish naval victory of 18 March 1915 during the Gallipoli campaign of the First World War. The main span of 2,023 metres symbolises the year 2023, the centenary of the Republic of Turkey, and the bridge's distinctive red-and-white towers echo the Turkish flag; tower details reference the Seyit Onbaşı memorial and artillery shells of the Gallipoli campaign.

Construction

Construction began with a groundbreaking ceremony on 18 March 2017, carried out by a Turkish–South Korean consortium including Limak, Yapı Merkezi, SK ecoplant, and DL E&C under a build–operate–transfer scheme. The bridge was opened to traffic by President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan on 18 March 2022, replacing ferry crossings of the strait and forming part of the Kınalı–Tekirdağ–Çanakkale–Balıkesir motorway corridor.

Engineering

The steel deck carries three lanes of motorway in each direction. When it opened, its 2,023-metre main span surpassed Japan's Akashi Kaikyō Bridge (1,991 metres) as the longest suspension span in the world, and its total length including approach viaducts exceeds 4.6 kilometres.
