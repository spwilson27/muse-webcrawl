Jasna Góra Monastery

The Jasna Góra Monastery in Częstochowa, Poland, is a shrine dedicated to the Virgin Mary and one of the country's most important pilgrimage sites. Founded in 1382 by Pauline monks who came from Hungary, it houses the revered icon of the Black Madonna of Częstochowa.

History

The monastery famously withstood a Swedish siege during the Deluge in 1655, an event that became a symbol of Polish national resistance. King John II Casimir proclaimed the Virgin Mary Queen of Poland in 1656, cementing the shrine's national significance.

Pilgrimage

Millions of pilgrims visit annually, many travelling on foot in organized pilgrimages in August for the Feast of the Assumption. The site includes the basilica, the Knights' Hall, a museum, and the 106-metre bell tower.
