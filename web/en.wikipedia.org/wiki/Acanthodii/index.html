Acanthodii

Acanthodii, the acanthodians or 'spiny sharks', are an extinct class (or grade) of gnathostome fishes that lived from the early Silurian to the Permian, about 439 to 252 million years ago.


Temporal range: Silurian – Permian
Common name: spiny sharks
Kingdom: Animalia; Phylum: Chordata

Description

Acanthodians combined shark-like bodies with bony, diamond-shaped scales and prominent spines supporting most fins. Most were small, under 20 cm, with large eyes suggesting mid-water feeding on plankton and small prey. Despite the nickname they were not true sharks; modern phylogenies place them as stem chondrichthyans — relatives on the lineage leading to sharks and cartilaginous fishes — or a paraphyletic grade near the split of bony and cartilaginous fishes.

History

Named by Louis Agassiz in the 19th century from isolated spines and scales, they flourished in Devonian seas and fresh waters, declined in the Carboniferous, and the last forms disappeared in the Permian.
