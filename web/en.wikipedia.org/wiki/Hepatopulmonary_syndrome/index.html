Hepatopulmonary syndrome

SpecialtyHepatology, pulmonology
SymptomsShortness of breath (worse upright — platypnea), low blood oxygen, clubbing, spider nevi
CausesLiver disease / portal hypertension causing dilatation of lung blood vessels
TreatmentOxygen therapy; liver transplantation is the only definitive cure

Hepatopulmonary syndrome (HPS) is a syndrome defined by a triad of (1) liver disease, (2) abnormal gas exchange causing low arterial oxygen (hypoxemia), and (3) intrapulmonary vascular dilatations. It occurs in a substantial minority of patients with cirrhosis and portal hypertension and significantly worsens prognosis.

Pathophysiology

In liver failure, the pulmonary circulation is exposed to vasodilators — most importantly nitric oxide, whose production is increased by endothelin-1 acting on endothelial receptors, and by bacterial translocation from the gut. Precapillary and capillary vessels in the lung dilate from their normal ~8 micrometres to 15–500 micrometres. Blood then flows through these wide vessels too quickly and too far from alveolar oxygen for full equilibration, producing a ventilation–perfusion mismatch and diffusion limitation, effectively a right-to-left shunt.

Signs and symptoms

Breathlessness is the cardinal symptom. Classically patients report platypnea — dyspnea worse when upright and relieved lying flat — with corresponding orthodeoxia, a measurable fall in oxygen saturation on standing, because the dilated vessels predominate in the lung bases. Digital clubbing, cyanosis, and spider nevi are common.

Diagnosis

HPS is suspected in a patient with liver disease whose pulse oximetry shows oxygen saturation below about 96%. Arterial blood gas analysis shows an increased alveolar–arterial oxygen gradient. Contrast-enhanced echocardiography with agitated saline demonstrates delayed appearance of bubbles in the left heart (after 3–6 cardiac cycles), confirming intrapulmonary shunting; macroaggregated albumin scanning can quantify the shunt.

Treatment

No drug therapy is reliably effective; supplemental oxygen relieves symptoms. Liver transplantation is the only definitive treatment and usually reverses the syndrome over months, so HPS is an accepted indication for transplant priority (MELD exception points) in many systems.
