Sanlu Group

Sanlu Group was a Chinese state-owned dairy products company headquartered in Shijiazhuang, Hebei. It was one of China's largest dairy producers, known for inexpensive infant formula, before collapsing in the 2008 Chinese milk scandal.

History

Founded in 1956 as a dairy cooperative, Sanlu grew into a major national brand. New Zealand dairy cooperative Fonterra acquired a 43% stake in 2005. Sanlu's low-cost infant formula was widely used by poorer and rural families across China.

2008 melamine scandal

In 2008 it emerged that Sanlu's milk powder had been adulterated with melamine, a chemical added to falsely inflate protein readings. Contaminated formula caused kidney stones and kidney failure in infants; around 300,000 babies were affected and at least six died. The scandal triggered a global food-safety crisis, recalls of Chinese dairy products worldwide, and prosecutions. Sanlu's chairwoman Tian Wenhua was sentenced to life imprisonment, and several others received death sentences.

Bankruptcy

Sanlu was declared bankrupt in early 2009; its assets were sold, largely to the Beijing-based Sanyuan Group. The scandal prompted major reforms of Chinese food-safety law and regulation.
