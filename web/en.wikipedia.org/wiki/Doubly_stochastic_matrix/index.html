Doubly stochastic matrix

In mathematics, especially in probability and combinatorics, a doubly stochastic matrix (also called a bistochastic matrix) is a square matrix of nonnegative real numbers, each of whose rows and columns sums to 1. Thus a doubly stochastic matrix is both left stochastic and right stochastic: it is a stochastic matrix whose transpose is also stochastic.

Properties

The set of all n × n doubly stochastic matrices forms a convex polytope known as the Birkhoff polytope. The Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem states that every doubly stochastic matrix is a convex combination of permutation matrices, and that the permutation matrices are exactly the extreme points (vertices) of this polytope. Consequently, doubly stochastic matrices arise naturally as relaxations of assignment problems: the linear assignment problem can be solved over the Birkhoff polytope.

Applications

Doubly stochastic matrices appear in Markov chain theory (a chain with such a transition matrix has the uniform distribution as a stationary distribution), in the theory of majorization, in quantum information (bistochastic channels), and in the Sinkhorn–Knopp algorithm, which scales a positive matrix to doubly stochastic form and underlies applications from optimal transport to image registration.

Generalizations

The concept generalises to doubly stochastic operators on infinite-dimensional spaces and to substochastic matrices, whose row and column sums are at most 1.
