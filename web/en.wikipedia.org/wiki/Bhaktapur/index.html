Bhaktapur (Nepali: भक्तपुर), literally "city of devotees", also known as Bhadgaon, is an ancient city in the Kathmandu Valley of Nepal, about 13 km east of Kathmandu. It is renowned for its well-preserved medieval architecture, temples and Newar culture, and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of the Kathmandu Valley.

History

Bhaktapur was the capital of the Malla kingdom of Nepal from the 12th to the 15th century and remained a wealthy, independent city-state until the unification of Nepal under Prithvi Narayan Shah in the 18th century. Its Durbar Square contains the 55-window palace of King Bhupatindra Malla, the Nyatapola Temple — Nepal's tallest pagoda temple, built in 1702 — and numerous shrines, courtyards and carved wooden windows.

Culture

The city is a centre of traditional pottery, woodcarving and weaving, and is famous for festivals such as Bisket Jatra (the Newar New Year) and for juju dhau, a rich buffalo-milk yogurt. Bhaktapur was badly damaged by the April 2015 Nepal earthquake, after which many of its monuments were restored.
