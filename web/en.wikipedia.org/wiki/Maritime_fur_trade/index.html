Maritime fur trade

The maritime fur trade was a ship-based fur trade system that focused on acquiring furs of sea otters and other animals from the Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest Coast and exchanging them at ports in China for tea, silks, porcelain, and other Chinese goods, which were then sold in Europe and the United States. The trade was pioneered by the Russians working in Alaska, then by British and especially American traders, from the late 18th century into the mid-19th century.

Origins

The trade began following the voyages of Vitus Bering (1741) and James Cook (1778). Bering's expedition brought sea otter pelts to Russia, where they fetched high prices at the Chinese border market of Kiakhta. Cook's crew, after his death in Hawaii, sold sea otter skins obtained at Nootka Sound for striking profits in Canton (Guangzhou), and the publication of Cook's voyage in 1784 alerted merchants to the opportunity.

The triangular trade

A classic voyage was triangular: ships left Boston or British ports carrying trade goods (metal, cloth, firearms, beads) around Cape Horn to the Northwest Coast; spent one or more seasons trading with Indigenous nations — the Haida, Nuu-chah-nulth, Tlingit, Chinook and others — for sea otter pelts; then sailed via Hawaii to Canton, where the furs were exchanged for Chinese goods for the home market. American traders, nicknamed "Boston Men" by Indigenous peoples, came to dominate the trade due to the restrictions the British East India Company's monopoly placed on British merchants.

Impact

The trade transformed the Pacific Northwest. Indigenous groups became skilled and demanding trading partners, and wealth and status competition contributed to the elaboration of the potlatch system; at the same time, the trade spread devastating diseases and intensified conflict. The sea otter was hunted nearly to extinction by the 1830s–1840s, and the trade declined, giving way to land-based fur brigades of the Hudson's Bay Company. The maritime fur trade also spurred exploration, mapping, and the geopolitical rivalries of the Nootka Crisis (1789–1794) between Britain and Spain.
