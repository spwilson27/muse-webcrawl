Healthcare

Healthcare is the provision of health services to individuals and communities by health professionals and allied health fields.

Healthcare is the improvement or maintenance of health via the prevention, diagnosis, treatment, amelioration or cure of disease, illness, injury, and other physical and mental impairments in people. Healthcare is provided by health professionals and allied health fields, including medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, nursing, psychology, and many others.

Levels of care

Healthcare is conventionally divided into primary care (the first point of contact — general practitioners, community nurses, pharmacists), secondary care (specialist care provided in hospitals and clinics, usually on referral), and tertiary care (highly specialised treatment in teaching hospitals and specialised centres). Public health operates at population level through sanitation, vaccination, and health promotion. Access, quality, and cost are the three classic dimensions by which health systems are judged.

Financing and systems

Health systems are financed through general taxation, social insurance, private insurance, and out-of-pocket payment, in varying mixes. Total global spending on health exceeds 10% of world GDP. Systems are often grouped into the Beveridge model (tax-funded public provision, as in the UK's National Health Service), the Bismarck model (social insurance funds, as in Germany), and market-based models (as in the United States). The World Health Organization promotes universal health coverage as a global goal.

Workforce and challenges

The WHO estimates a global shortfall of millions of health workers, concentrated in low-income countries. Contemporary challenges include ageing populations and chronic disease, antimicrobial resistance, medical error and patient safety, rising costs of new technologies and drugs, and the integration of digital health records and telemedicine.
