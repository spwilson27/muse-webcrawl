Rookwood Pottery

Rookwood Pottery Company
Founded: 1880, Cincinnati, Ohio
Founder: Maria Longworth Nichols Storer
Industry: Art pottery

The Rookwood Pottery Company is an American ceramics company founded in Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1880 by Maria Longworth Nichols Storer. It was the first large American art pottery and became internationally famous for its fine art pottery, winning a gold medal at the Paris Exposition of 1889 and top honours at subsequent world's fairs.

Glazes and artists

Rookwood developed celebrated glaze lines including Standard Glaze (amber-toned underglaze slip decoration, often with naturalistic flowers), Iris Glaze, Sea Green, and the matte Vellum glaze. Painters and modellers employed by the firm included Kataro Shirayamadani, Artus Van Briggle (before he founded his own pottery), Sara Sax, and Harriet Wilcox. Each piece was marked with the company's reverse-RP flame mark and usually the artist's cipher.

Later history

The company struggled during the Great Depression and closed in 1967. Its trademarks and moulds passed through several owners; in the 21st century the Rookwood brand was revived in Cincinnati, producing architectural tile and art pottery in the historic Over-the-Rhine district.
