The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) is an international organisation working in the field of nature conservation and the sustainable use of natural resources. Founded in 1948 at Fontainebleau, France, as the International Union for the Protection of Nature, it is the world's oldest and largest global environmental network, bringing together government agencies and civil-society organisations — more than 1,400 member organisations — with a network of some 18,000 volunteer experts organised in commissions. Its headquarters are in Gland, Switzerland.

Red List

The IUCN is best known for the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, founded in 1964, the world's most comprehensive inventory of the global conservation status of plant, animal and fungi species. Species are assessed against quantitative criteria and assigned to categories from Least Concern through Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered to Extinct in the Wild and Extinct. More than 150,000 species have been assessed, of which over 40,000 are classified as threatened with extinction. The Red List is widely used to guide conservation policy, protected-area planning and funding.

Work and influence

IUCN's work spans species survival, protected areas, ecosystem management, environmental law, and the interface of conservation with climate change and livelihoods. It maintains a classification of protected areas (categories I–VI) used worldwide, publishes the World Heritage evaluations of natural sites for UNESCO, and helped develop foundational environmental instruments and concepts, including the World Conservation Strategy (1980) and contributions to the Convention on Biological Diversity and the Ramsar Convention on wetlands. Its World Conservation Congress, held every four years, sets the organisation's policies and has launched global conservation initiatives.

Structure

IUCN is unusual among international organisations in having both states and non-governmental organisations as members. Its six expert commissions include the Species Survival Commission and the World Commission on Protected Areas. The organisation has attracted occasional criticism — for instance, over its engagement with business and industry — but remains the principal scientific authority on species extinction risk and a key adviser to governments and United Nations bodies.
