Balanced audio

Balanced audio is a method of interconnecting audio equipment using balanced lines. Type of connection used commonly on professional audio equipment, it uses two signal conductors carrying the same signal in opposite polarity, plus a ground, so that interference picked up along the cable is rejected by common-mode rejection at the receiving end.

[ Image: XLR connector ]
Three-pin XLR connectors are standard for balanced audio

Operation

A balanced line driver sends the signal on two conductors with equal impedance to ground: one carries the signal with its original polarity ("hot") and the other an inverted copy ("cold"). Any noise induced along the cable run appears identically on both conductors. The differential receiver amplifies only the difference between the two, cancelling the common noise — a figure of merit called common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR).

Connectors

Balanced connections most commonly use three-pin XLR connectors for microphones and ¼-inch TRS (tip-ring-sleeve) phone connectors for line-level equipment. Phantom power (+48 V) for condenser microphones is delivered over the same pair by applying equal voltage to both signal lines relative to ground.

Balanced vs. unbalanced

Consumer equipment usually uses unbalanced lines (e.g. RCA connectors), where the signal travels on a single conductor referenced to the shield. Unbalanced connections are cheaper but more susceptible to hum and radio-frequency interference over long runs, so professional studios, live sound and broadcast use balanced wiring for long cable runs and low-level microphone signals.
