Survival rate

Survival rate is a part of survival analysis. It is the proportion of people in a specified group (for example, patients diagnosed with a disease) who are still alive after a given period of time. It is often expressed as a percentage surviving after one, five, or ten years. Survival rates are used in medicine to describe prognosis, compare treatments, and evaluate the effectiveness of screening and therapy over time.

Definitions

Overall survival counts survival from diagnosis regardless of cause of death. Relative survival compares the observed survival of patients with the expected survival of a comparable disease-free population, thereby isolating mortality attributable to the disease. Disease-free survival and progression-free survival measure the time patients live without recurrence or progression and are common endpoints in clinical trials.

Methods

Survival data are typically analysed with the Kaplan–Meier estimator, which accounts for censored observations—patients whose outcome is unknown because they were lost to follow-up or the study ended. The Cox proportional hazards model is used to compare survival between groups while adjusting for other variables.

Limitations

Survival rates are averages for groups and cannot predict an individual's outcome precisely. They may lag behind improvements in treatment, and increases in measured survival can reflect earlier diagnosis (lead-time bias) rather than extended life.
