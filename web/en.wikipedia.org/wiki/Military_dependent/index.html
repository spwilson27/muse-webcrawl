Military dependent

A military dependent is a person, normally a spouse or child, who depends on a member of the armed forces for support and who is recognized by the military for the purposes of benefits, housing, healthcare, and relocation. In the United States, dependents are registered in the Defense Enrollment Eligibility Reporting System (DEERS) and issued identification cards that give them access to installations and services such as TRICARE healthcare, commissaries, and exchanges.

Military children

Children of service members — colloquially known, sometimes self-applied, as "military brats" — typically experience a distinctive upbringing marked by frequent moves, attendance at many different schools, parental absence during deployments, and upbringing on or near military installations, sometimes overseas. Studies and advocacy organizations note both resilience and stress associated with this lifestyle; schools on U.S. installations abroad are operated by the Department of Defense Education Activity (DoDEA).

Support and benefits

Benefits commonly extended to dependents include healthcare coverage, family housing or housing allowances, family support centers, relocation assistance, and survivor benefits. During deployments, family readiness groups and equivalent organizations in other countries' forces provide information and mutual support to spouses and families.

Legal status overseas

When stationed abroad, dependents are usually covered by the host country's status of forces agreement with the sending state, which governs matters such as legal jurisdiction, employment, and use of military facilities. Dependents accompanying members to overseas postings are described as "command-sponsored"; those who relocate at their own expense without sponsorship may have limited access to services.
