Country: China

Capital: Shenyang

Area: 148,000 km²

Liaoning (Chinese: 辽宁; pinyin: Liáoníng) is a coastal province in Northeast China that is also the smallest, southernmost, and most populous province in the region. With its capital at Shenyang, it is located on the northern shore of the Yellow Sea, and is the northernmost coastal province of the People's Republic of China.

Historically a gateway between China proper and Manchuria, the modern Liaoning province was established in 1907 as Fengtian or Fengtien province and was renamed Liaoning in 1929. It was also known at that time as Mukden province for the Manchu name of Shenyang, then its capital. Under the Japanese-puppet Manchukuo regime, the province reverted to its 1907 name, but the name Liaoning was restored in 1945 and again in 1954.

Geography

Liaoning is bounded by the Yellow Sea (Korea Bay) and Bohai Sea in the south, North Korea's North Pyongan and Chagang provinces in the southeast, Jilin to the northeast, Hebei to the southwest, and Inner Mongolia to the northwest. The Yalu River marks much of its border with North Korea. The province's terrain can be roughly divided into three regions: the western highlands, the central plains around the Liao River, and the eastern hills of the Liaodong Peninsula.

Economy

Liaoning is one of China's most industrialised provinces and forms part of the country's traditional heavy-industry base, sometimes called its "rust belt". Major industries include petrochemicals, metallurgy, machinery manufacturing, and shipbuilding. The port city of Dalian is a major centre for shipping, finance, and tourism. Agriculture remains important, with production of maize, rice, and fruit, and the province is also known for its fossils — the Jehol Biota of western Liaoning has yielded spectacular feathered-dinosaur specimens, including finds from the Yixian Formation.

Administrative divisions

Liaoning is divided into fourteen prefecture-level divisions, all prefecture-level cities, including the sub-provincial cities of Shenyang and Dalian, as well as Anshan, Fushun, Benxi, Dandong, Jinzhou, Yingkou, Fuxin, Liaoyang, Panjin, Tieling, Chaoyang, and Huludao.
