Between Pacific Tides

AuthorEdward F. Ricketts and Jack Calvin
Published1939 (Stanford University Press)
SubjectIntertidal ecology, marine invertebrates

Between Pacific Tides is a pioneering work of marine ecology by Edward F. Ricketts and Jack Calvin, first published in 1939 by Stanford University Press. It describes the invertebrate life of the Pacific coast of North America, organised not taxonomically but by habitat — rocky shore, sandy beach, mud flats, wharf pilings — an ecological arrangement that was innovative for its time.

Contents and influence

The book describes hundreds of species of the intertidal zone from Alaska to Mexico, with attention to how each animal feeds, reproduces, and withstands exposure at low tide. Rather than a systematic catalogue, it offered readers a guide to observing living communities. It has been repeatedly revised and reissued, with later editions updated by Joel W. Hedgpeth and others, and remains a classic of shore natural history.

Ricketts was the model for "Doc" in John Steinbeck's novel Cannery Row, and Between Pacific Tides reflects the holistic ecological outlook the two friends shared, later expressed in their joint work Sea of Cortez (1941).
