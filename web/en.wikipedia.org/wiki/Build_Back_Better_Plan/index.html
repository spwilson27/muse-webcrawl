Build Back Better Plan

The Build Back Better Plan was a legislative framework proposed by U.S. President Joe Biden between 2020 and 2021, intended to revitalize the American economy after the COVID-19 pandemic. It comprised three parts: the American Rescue Plan (a COVID-19 relief bill enacted in March 2021), the American Jobs Plan (an infrastructure proposal), and the American Families Plan (a social policy proposal).

Components

American Rescue Plan

The $1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan Act was signed into law on March 11, 2021. It funded direct payments, expanded unemployment benefits, vaccine distribution, and an expanded child tax credit.

American Jobs Plan

Proposed at roughly $2 trillion over eight years, this plan targeted roads, bridges, water systems, broadband, and clean energy. A scaled-down bipartisan version became the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, signed in November 2021.

American Families Plan

This roughly $1.8 trillion proposal covered universal pre-kindergarten, free community college, paid family leave, and childcare subsidies. Most of its elements were folded into the Build Back Better Act.

Build Back Better Act

The Build Back Better Act, a reconciliation bill of about $2.2 trillion (later reduced), passed the House of Representatives in November 2021 but stalled in the Senate after opposition from Senator Joe Manchin. In August 2022, a narrower successor, the Inflation Reduction Act, enacted many of its climate, healthcare, and tax provisions.
