Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Alliance member brands

The Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance is a French-Japanese strategic alliance between the automobile manufacturers Renault (based in Boulogne-Billancourt, France), Nissan (based in Yokohama, Japan) and Mitsubishi Motors (based in Tokyo, Japan), which together sell more than 1 in 9 vehicles worldwide. Originally known as the Renault–Nissan Alliance, the partnership was founded on 27 March 1999 and restructured from a cross-shareholding arrangement into an equal partnership in 2023.

The alliance is a unique form of partnership in the automotive industry: the companies remain legally independent but share platforms, powertrains, purchasing and technology development. Mitsubishi Motors joined in 2016 after Nissan acquired a controlling 34% stake in the company.

History

In 1999, Renault took a 36.8% stake in the financially troubled Nissan, and Carlos Ghosn led Nissan's dramatic turnaround under the Nissan Revival Plan. The alliance became one of the world's largest automotive groups by sales. Ghosn's arrest in Japan in November 2018 on charges of financial misconduct threw the alliance into crisis and strained Franco-Japanese relations. In 2023 the partners restructured their relationship: Renault reduced its stake in Nissan to 15%, equal to Nissan's stake in Renault, and the companies refocused cooperation on specific projects in electric vehicles, software and regional markets.

Cooperation

The alliance partners jointly develop vehicle platforms (such as the CMF family), share electric vehicle technology through ventures such as Renault's Ampere, and coordinate purchasing through a joint organization. Combined, the alliance was for a time among the world leaders in electric vehicle sales, driven by the Nissan Leaf and Renault Zoe.
