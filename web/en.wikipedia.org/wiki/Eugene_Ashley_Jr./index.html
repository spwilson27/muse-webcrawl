Eugene Ashley Jr.

Eugene Ashley Jr. (12 October 1930 – 7 February 1968) was a United States Army soldier who received the Medal of Honor, America's highest military decoration, for his actions during the Vietnam War. A sergeant first class in the 5th Special Forces Group, he was killed while leading repeated assaults to rescue a trapped Special Forces camp at Lang Vei.

Biography

Ashley was born in Wilmington, North Carolina, and enlisted in the Army in 1950. He served in the Korean War before becoming a Green Beret. By February 1968 he was stationed with Detachment A-101 at Lang Vei, near Khe Sanh in South Vietnam.

Medal of Honor action

On 6–7 February 1968, North Vietnamese forces, supported by PT-76 tanks, overran the Lang Vei camp. Ashley organised a small force of South Vietnamese irregulars and led five separate assaults against enemy positions to open a corridor for the camp's surviving defenders, directing air strikes dangerously close to his own position and exposing himself to heavy fire throughout. During the fifth assault he was mortally wounded by artillery fire. His actions enabled the rescue force to reach and evacuate trapped survivors. President Richard Nixon presented the Medal of Honor to Ashley's family in December 1969.

Legacy

Ashley is buried in Rockfish Memorial Park in Fayetteville, North Carolina. Ashley High School in Wilmington, North Carolina, is named in his memory.
