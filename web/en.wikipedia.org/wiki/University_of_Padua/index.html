University of Padua

The University of Padua (Italian: Università degli Studi di Padova, UNIPD) is a public research university in Padua, Italy. Founded in 1222 by a group of students and teachers seceding from the University of Bologna, it is the second-oldest university in Italy and among the oldest in the world still in operation. Its motto is Universa Universis Patavina Libertas — "Paduan freedom for all, everywhere".

History

Protected by the city and later by the Republic of Venice, Padua became a leading centre of Renaissance learning, especially in medicine, astronomy, and law. Galileo Galilei taught mathematics there from 1592 to 1610, calling the period the happiest of his life. The university houses the world's oldest permanent anatomical theatre (1595) and its botanical garden, founded in 1545, is the oldest academic botanical garden still on its original site and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In 1678 Elena Cornaro Piscopia became the first woman in the world to receive a university degree, a Doctor of Philosophy from Padua.

Today

The university has around 70,000 students across 32 departments and eight schools, and alumni include Nicolaus Copernicus, William Harvey, and physician Giovanni Battista Morgagni, founder of anatomical pathology.
