Chinese water deer

The Chinese water deer (Hydropotes inermis) is a small deer native to China and Korea, notable for lacking antlers and instead bearing prominent downward-pointing canine tusks, especially in males.

Description

Water deer stand about 50 cm at the shoulder and have a coarse yellowish-brown coat. Males sport tusks up to about 8 cm long that protrude from the upper jaw. They are strong swimmers and inhabit reed beds, riverbanks and marshy grassland.

Distribution

The species is native to the lower Yangtze basin and the Korean Peninsula. Introduced populations thrive in England, where escapees from collections such as Woburn Abbey established a feral population, and in parts of France.

Behaviour

Largely solitary and territorial, water deer are unusual among deer in giving birth to large litters of up to seven fawns.
