Billboard Hot 100

The standard music single chart in the United States, published weekly by Billboard magazine.

The Billboard Hot 100 is the music industry standard record chart in the United States for songs, published weekly by Billboard magazine. Chart rankings are based on sales (physical and digital), radio play, and online streaming in the United States.

History

The Hot 100 was officially launched on August 4, 1958, combining previously separate Billboard charts for best sellers in stores, most played by disc jockeys and most played in jukeboxes. The first number-one song of the Hot 100 era was Ricky Nelson's "Poor Little Fool". Over the decades the methodology evolved: Nielsen SoundScan point-of-sale data was adopted in 1991, digital downloads were incorporated in 2005, streaming data in 2007 (expanded substantially in 2013 to include YouTube views), and radio airplay remains a component throughout.

Compilation

The weekly tracking period for sales and streaming runs Friday to Thursday; radio airplay is tracked Monday to Sunday. The new chart is compiled and officially published by Billboard on Tuesday, dated the following Saturday. A "recurrent" rule removes songs that have spent many weeks on the chart once they fall below certain positions, making room for newer releases.

Records

Among the chart's notable records: Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" (2019) spent a record 19 weeks at number one; the Beatles hold the record for the most number-one singles by an artist (20), followed by Mariah Carey; and "Blinding Lights" by the Weeknd was named the greatest Hot 100 song of all time in Billboard's 2021 retrospective ranking.
