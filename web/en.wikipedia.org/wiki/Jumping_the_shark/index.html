Jumping the shark

OriginHappy Days episode, 1977
MeaningThe point at which a creative work begins an irreversible decline in quality
Popularized byJon Hein, Sean Connolly (1980s); jumptheshark.com (1997)

The idiom "jumping the shark" denotes the moment when a television series or other creative work, in an attempt to maintain interest, introduces an implausible or gimmicky event that signals the beginning of a decline in quality from which it never recovers.

Origin

The phrase derives from a 1977 episode of the sitcom Happy Days, "Hollywood: Part 3", in which the character Fonzie, wearing swim trunks and his trademark leather jacket, water-skis over a confined shark. Radio personality Jon Hein and his college roommate Sean Connolly coined the term in the mid-1980s to describe such turning points.

Popularization and usage

Hein's website JumpTheShark.com, launched in 1997, catalogued the moments when hundreds of shows were judged to have declined, and sold to TV Guide in 2006. The expression has since broadened beyond television to describe any brand, institution or phenomenon perceived to have passed its peak through desperate novelty. Happy Days creator Garry Marshall and actor Henry Winkler have both defended the episode, noting the show remained popular for years afterward.
