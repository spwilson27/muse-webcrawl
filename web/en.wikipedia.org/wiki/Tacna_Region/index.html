Tacna Region

Country: Peru

Capital: Tacna

Provinces: 4 (Tacna, Candarave, Jorge Basadre, Tarata)

Area: 16,076 km²

Population: approx. 330,000

The Tacna Region is one of the twenty-five regions of Peru, located in the far south of the country. It borders the Pacific Ocean to the southwest, the Moquegua Region to the north, Puno Region to the northeast, Bolivia to the east, and Chile's Arica y Parinacota Region to the south. Its capital is the city of Tacna.

Geography

The region spans coastal desert, fertile river valleys (Caplina, Sama, and Locumba), and high Andean plateau rising to volcanic peaks over 5,000 m. The climate on the coast is arid and mild, while the highlands are cold and dry. Principal geographic features include the volcanoes Tutupaca and Yucamane and Lake Suches on the Bolivian frontier.

History

Tacna was occupied by Chile during and after the War of the Pacific (1880–1929), and returned to Peru under the Treaty of Lima of 1929. The region was officially reconstituted within Peru thereafter, and its people are known for strong patriotic traditions, including the annual Paseo de la Bandera (Flag Procession) in Tacna city.

Political division

The region is divided into four provinces — Tacna, Candarave, Jorge Basadre, and Tarata — which are subdivided into 28 districts. Tacna province, containing the capital, holds the large majority of the population.

Economy

Agriculture (olives, grapes, oregano, and other valley crops), mining, fishing, and commerce dominate the economy. The ZOFRATACNA special economic zone promotes trade, and proximity to Chile supports extensive cross-border shopping and services.
