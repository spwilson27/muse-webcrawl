Ferroelectric RAM

Ferroelectric RAM (FeRAM or FRAM) is a random-access memory technology that uses a ferroelectric layer — commonly lead zirconate titanate (PZT) — in place of the dielectric in a DRAM-like capacitor to achieve non-volatility. Despite its name, FeRAM is not based on iron.

Operation

Applying an electric field moves the central atom within the ferroelectric crystal between two stable positions, storing a binary state that persists without power. Reading is destructive in classic designs, requiring a write-back. FeRAM offers much lower power consumption and far higher write endurance than flash memory or EEPROM, at the cost of lower storage density and higher price per bit.

Applications

FeRAM is used in smart cards, metering, automotive data logging, industrial controls, and medical devices, and was notably used for save memory in some video game hardware. Manufacturers have included Ramtron (later Cypress, then Infineon), Texas Instruments, and Fujitsu.
