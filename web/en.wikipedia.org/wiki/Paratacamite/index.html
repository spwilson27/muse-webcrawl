Paratacamite is a mineral in the halide minerals category. Its chemical formula is Cu3(Cu,Zn)(OH)6Cl2, a hydrated copper chloride hydroxide in which zinc commonly substitutes for part of the copper. Its name is derived from its close relationship to atacamite, of which it was originally considered a polymorph.

Occurrence

Paratacamite forms as a secondary mineral in the oxidation zones of copper deposits, especially in arid climates, and also as a corrosion product on bronze and copper artefacts and in volcanic fumarole deposits. It was first described in 1906 from the Atacama region of Chile. Notable localities include the copper mines of Chuquicamata and other deposits in the Atacama Desert, Broken Hill in Australia, and localities in Italy, including Mount Vesuvius.

Relation to atacamite and herbertsmithite

Paratacamite is closely related to atacamite and botallackite, which have the same basic chemistry but different crystal structures. When zinc dominates the substituted site the mineral is herbertsmithite, a mineral of interest in condensed-matter physics as a candidate quantum spin liquid because of its kagome lattice of copper ions.

Bronze disease

Copper chloride hydroxides of this group are responsible for "bronze disease", the destructive corrosion cycle that attacks archaeological bronze objects when chlorides react with moisture and oxygen; paratacamite and atacamite are among the powdery green products formed.
