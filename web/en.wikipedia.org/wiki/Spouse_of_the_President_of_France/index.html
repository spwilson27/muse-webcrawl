Spouse of the President of France

The spouse of the President of France is the wife or husband of the sitting president of the French Republic. Unlike in the United States, the role carries no official title—such as "First Lady" (Première dame)—in French law or protocol, though the term is widely used in the media. The president's spouse traditionally resides with the president at the Élysée Palace in Paris, supports charitable and cultural causes, and accompanies the president on state visits and official ceremonies.

Prominent occupants of the role during the Fifth Republic include Yvonne de Gaulle, Claude Pompidou—founder of a major foundation for the elderly and disabled—Anne-Aymone Giscard d'Estaing, Danielle Mitterrand, Bernadette Chirac, Cécilia Sarkozy, Carla Bruni-Sarkozy, Valérie Trierweiler and, since 2017, Brigitte Macron. Some presidential spouses maintained a very low public profile; others were politically active in their own right.

Status debate

The undefined status of the presidential spouse has periodically prompted debate in France. In 2017, President Emmanuel Macron proposed creating an official status for the role, but a public petition against giving it a formal title and budget gathered hundreds of thousands of signatures, and the plan was replaced by a "transparency charter" setting out the spouse's support activities, staff and costs without creating a legal office. The spouse's activities are funded from the Élysée's budget, and the Court of Accounts reviews the associated expenditure.
