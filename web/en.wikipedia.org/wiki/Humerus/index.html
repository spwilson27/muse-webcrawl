Humerus


Anterior view of the left humerus.

The humerus (/ˈhjuːmərəs/; pl.: humeri) is a long bone in the arm that runs from the shoulder to the elbow. It connects the scapula and the two bones of the lower arm, the radius and ulna, and consists of three sections: the upper extremity (including the rounded head, which articulates with the glenoid cavity of the scapula), the body (shaft), and the lower extremity, which articulates with the radius and ulna at the elbow.

Structure

The humerus is the longest and largest bone of the upper extremity. Notable features include the greater and lesser tubercles, the deltoid tuberosity (attachment for the deltoid muscle), the bicipital groove, and the trochlea and capitulum at the distal end. The radial nerve runs in the radial groove along the posterior shaft and is vulnerable in mid-shaft fractures.

Clinical significance

Humeral fractures are classified as proximal, mid-shaft, or distal. The surgical neck is a common fracture site in older adults, while mid-shaft fractures may endanger the radial nerve, causing wrist drop. The medial epicondyle, behind which runs the ulnar nerve, is the structure struck in the sensation colloquially called hitting the "funny bone".
