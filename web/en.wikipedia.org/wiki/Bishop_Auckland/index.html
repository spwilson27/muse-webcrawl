Bishop Auckland

Bishop Auckland is a market town and civil parish in County Durham, England. The town lies at the confluence of the River Wear and its tributary the River Gaunless, about 12 miles (19 km) southwest of Durham. Its name derives from the town's association with the Bishops of Durham, whose residence, Auckland Castle, stands here. The population was recorded at about 25,000 in the 2021 census.

History

Auckland Castle has been a seat of the Bishops of Durham since the 12th century, when Bishop Hugh de Puiset built a manor house on the site. The town grew around the castle and its market. In the 19th century Bishop Auckland became a centre of the Durham coalfield and a railway town on the Stockton and Darlington Railway corridor.

Auckland Project

In the 21st century the Auckland Project, founded by financier Jonathan Ruffer, has led a major regeneration of the town centre around the castle. Attractions include Auckland Castle, reopened in 2019 after restoration; the Spanish Gallery; the Faith Museum; and Kynren, an open-air historical pageant held nearby. The town is also known for Bishop Auckland F.C., one of the most successful amateur football clubs of the 20th century.
