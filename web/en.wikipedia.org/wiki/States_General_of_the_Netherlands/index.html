The States General of the Netherlands (Dutch: Staten-Generaal, abbreviated SG) is the supreme bicameral legislature of the Netherlands, consisting of the Senate (Eerste Kamer) and the House of Representatives (Tweede Kamer). The legislature meets at the Binnenhof in The Hague.

History

The States General originated in the mid-15th century as an assembly of representatives of the provincial States of the Habsburg Netherlands; the first meeting is conventionally dated to 9 January 1464 in Bruges. During the Dutch Republic (1588–1795), the States General was the sovereign governing body of the confederation of seven provinces, composed of delegates from each province. The modern bicameral parliament dates from the constitution of 1815 and the revision of 1848, which made ministers responsible to parliament.

Composition and functions

The House of Representatives, with 150 members elected directly by proportional representation every four years, is the principal legislative chamber: it can initiate and amend legislation and questions ministers. The Senate, with 75 members elected indirectly by the provincial councils, reviews legislation passed by the House; it can approve or reject bills but not amend them.

The two chambers meet in joint session (the Verenigde Vergadering) on special occasions, such as the annual delivery of the Speech from the Throne on Prinsjesdag, and for decisions on the royal succession.
