Jesus, King of the Jews

"Jesus, King of the Jews" is the wording of the inscription (Latin: titulus crucis) that, according to the four canonical Gospels, Pontius Pilate had placed on the cross above Jesus at his crucifixion. The Gospel of John gives it in three languages — Hebrew (or Aramaic), Latin and Greek — as "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews".

The inscription

The Latin form, Iesus Nazarenus, Rex Iudaeorum, gives the acronym INRI, ubiquitous in Christian art. The synoptic Gospels give shorter forms: Mark has "The King of the Jews", Matthew "This is Jesus, the King of the Jews", and Luke "This is the King of the Jews". John reports that the chief priests objected to the wording and asked Pilate to change it to "This man said, I am King of the Jews"; Pilate refused, answering, "What I have written, I have written".

Significance

The title functioned as the formal charge (the causa poenae) against Jesus — a claim to kingship amounting to sedition against Rome — while in Christian interpretation it is read as an unwitting proclamation of Jesus's true identity. A relic in the Basilica of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme in Rome is venerated as a fragment of the original titulus, though its authenticity is disputed.

References
