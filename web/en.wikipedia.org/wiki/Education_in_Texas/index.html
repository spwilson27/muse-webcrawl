Education in Texas

Education in Texas is overseen by the Texas Education Agency (TEA) and administered locally by more than 1,000 independent school districts. The state constitution requires the legislature to support and maintain an efficient system of public free schools. Texas educates over five million public school students, one of the largest school populations in the United States.

K–12 education

Public schools follow the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS) curriculum standards, and students take the State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness (STAAR). The Permanent School Fund, endowed with public lands, is one of the largest educational endowments in the world and helps finance school operations. School finance in Texas has repeatedly been litigated over equity between property-rich and property-poor districts.

Higher education

Major public university systems include the University of Texas System, headed by the University of Texas at Austin, and the Texas A&M University System. Other prominent institutions include Rice University (private), Texas Tech University, the University of Houston and Texas State University. The state's "Top Ten Percent" rule historically guaranteed admission to public universities for students graduating in the top decile of their Texas high school class.
