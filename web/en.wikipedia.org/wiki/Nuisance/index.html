Nuisance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nuisance is an area of tort law concerning indirect interferences with the use and enjoyment of land (private nuisance), with the public's rights (public nuisance), and, in some jurisdictions, a statutory wrong. Private nuisance covers unreasonable interference such as noise, smoke, smells, vibration and encroaching roots or branches, judged by the character of the neighbourhood, the duration and intensity of the interference, and the social utility of the defendant's conduct — “live and let live” standards of ordinary toleration (St Helen's Smelting v Tipping, 1865, distinguished material property damage from personal discomfort judged by locality).

Remedies are damages and injunctions, the latter discretionary since Coventry v Lawrence (2014). Coming to the nuisance is not a defence, though a claimant's abnormal sensitivity may defeat a claim. Public nuisance — obstructing highways, polluting rivers — is both a crime and a tort actionable by individuals who suffer special damage. Statutory nuisance regimes cover noise, smoke and other environmental harms enforced by local authorities.
