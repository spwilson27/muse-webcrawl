Eurosystem

Eurosystem
MembersEuropean Central Bank and the national central banks of euro area member states
CurrencyEuro
FunctionMonetary authority of the euro area

The Eurosystem is the monetary authority of the euro area, the group of member states of the European Union that have adopted the euro as their common currency. It comprises the European Central Bank (ECB) and the national central banks (NCBs) of the euro area countries. The Eurosystem is distinct from the European System of Central Banks (ESCB), which includes the central banks of all EU member states, including those that have not adopted the euro.

Mandate

The primary objective of the Eurosystem, as set out in the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Statute of the ESCB, is to maintain price stability in the euro area. Without prejudice to that objective, it supports the general economic policies in the Union. Its basic tasks are to define and implement the monetary policy of the euro area, conduct foreign exchange operations, hold and manage the official foreign reserves of the member states, and promote the smooth operation of payment systems. The Eurosystem also contributes to financial stability and, since 2014, is involved in banking supervision through the Single Supervisory Mechanism, led by the ECB.

Decision-making and implementation

Monetary policy decisions are taken by the Governing Council of the ECB, which consists of the six members of the ECB's Executive Board and the governors of the euro area national central banks. Policy is implemented in a decentralised manner: the national central banks carry out most operations, such as providing liquidity to banks through refinancing operations, and they put euro banknotes into circulation. The Eurosystem operates the TARGET2 (now T2) real-time gross settlement system for large-value payments in euro.

Banknotes and reserves

The ECB has the exclusive right to authorise the issue of euro banknotes, which are issued by the ECB and the NCBs. The Eurosystem's foreign reserve assets are held by the ECB and the NCBs and managed according to objectives of liquidity, security, and return, subject to the requirements of monetary and exchange rate policy.
