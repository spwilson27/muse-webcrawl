Black Power is a political slogan and philosophy whose proponents sought to achieve self-determination for people of African descent, emphasising racial pride, the creation of independent Black political and cultural institutions, and — for some — self-defence against racist violence, rather than reliance on integration into white-dominated institutions.

Origins

The slogan was popularised in June 1966 when Stokely Carmichael, chairman of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC), declared "We want Black Power!" during the March Against Fear in Mississippi. The phrase captured frustration with the pace of the mainstream civil rights movement and the influence of earlier figures such as Malcolm X. Intellectual roots also ran through Richard Wright's 1954 book Black Power and the movements for African decolonisation.

The movement

The Black Panther Party, founded in Oakland in October 1966 by Huey Newton and Bobby Seale, became the slogan's most famous bearer, combining a Ten-Point Program with community "survival programs" such as Free Breakfast for Children. Black Power transformed American culture: it fuelled Black studies departments, the "Black is beautiful" movement, and iconic gestures such as Tommie Smith and John Carlos's raised-fist salute at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics. The FBI's COINTELPRO program targeted Black Power organisations for surveillance and disruption.

Global influence

Black Power inspired movements beyond the United States, including the Black Consciousness Movement of Steve Biko in apartheid South Africa, Aboriginal activism in Australia, and Dalit Panthers in India.
