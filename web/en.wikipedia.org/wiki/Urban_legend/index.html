Urban legend

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"The Hook" is a classic American urban legend told to generations of teenagers.

An urban legend (also modern legend, urban myth or contemporary legend) is a genre of folklore comprising fictitious stories, presented as true, that circulate in modern culture with macabre, humorous or cautionary themes. Such tales are typically attributed to a "friend of a friend" — the FOAF — which lends them plausibility while keeping them unverifiable.

Characteristics

Urban legends are set in the recent past and in familiar places; they change in the telling, acquiring local details. Many embody anxieties about technology, crime, contamination and strangers: poisoned or tampered Halloween candy, alligators in the sewers of New York, kidney thieves in foreign hotels, killers hiding in the back seat of a car, and gang "initiations" signalled by flashing headlights. Despite repeated debunking, the legends persist because they are memorable stories carrying a moral or warning.

Study and dissemination

Folklorist Jan Harold Brunvand popularised the study of the genre in books beginning with The Vanishing Hitchhiker (1981), named after one of the best-known tales. Chain letters and later email and social media accelerated the spread of legends — including legends about the internet itself, such as "creepypasta" horror stories — and websites such as Snopes were founded largely to investigate them. Scholars note that calling a story an "urban legend" says nothing about whether any given version is false; some are based on genuine events, distorted by retelling.
