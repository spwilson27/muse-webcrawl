Princess Margaret, Countess of Snowdon

Princess Margaret, younger sister of Queen Elizabeth II.

Princess Margaret, Countess of Snowdon (Margaret Rose; 21 August 1930 – 9 February 2002) was the younger daughter of King George VI and Queen Elizabeth (later the Queen Mother) and the younger sister and only sibling of Queen Elizabeth II.

Born21 August 1930, Glamis Castle, Angus, Scotland
Died9 February 2002 (aged 71), London
SpouseAntony Armstrong-Jones, 1st Earl of Snowdon (m. 1960; div. 1978)
ChildrenDavid Armstrong-Jones, 2nd Earl of Snowdon; Lady Sarah Chatto
FatherGeorge VI
MotherElizabeth Bowes-Lyon

Early life

Margaret was born at Glamis Castle in Scotland, the first royal baby born in Scotland since 1600. Her childhood changed when her uncle Edward VIII abdicated in 1936, making her father king and Margaret second in line to the throne. She was educated at home and at court with her sister, and served in the Girl Guides; during the Second World War the princesses remained at Windsor Castle.

The Townsend affair

In the 1950s Margaret fell in love with Group Captain Peter Townsend, a divorced equerry to her father. The proposed marriage provoked a constitutional and religious controversy — the Church of England opposed remarriage after divorce — and in October 1955 Margaret announced that she would not marry him, "mindful of the Church's teachings" and her duty to the Commonwealth.

Marriage and family

On 6 May 1960 she married the photographer Antony Armstrong-Jones at Westminster Abbey — the first royal wedding broadcast on television; he was created Earl of Snowdon. They had two children, David (1961) and Sarah (1964). The marriage, strained by both parties' affairs, ended in divorce in 1978, the first for a senior royal since 1901.

Public role and later life

Margaret was known for her wit, glamour, and devotion to the arts and charities, with a lively social life centred in part on her house on Mustique. Failing health — she was a heavy smoker, underwent lung surgery in 1985, and suffered a series of strokes from 1998 — curtailed her duties. She died at King Edward VII's Hospital, London, in February 2002, weeks before her mother's death at 101.
