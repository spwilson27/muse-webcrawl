Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977

The Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (c 50) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that regulates contracts by limiting the operation and legality of certain contract terms, particularly exclusion and limitation clauses and, at enactment, clauses in consumer and standard-form contracts.

Citation1977 c 50
Royal assent26 October 1977
JurisdictionUnited Kingdom (with variations)

Provisions

Section 2 makes terms excluding liability for death or personal injury resulting from negligence void, and subjects exclusions for other loss to a reasonableness test (section 11 and Schedule 2 guidelines). Sections 6–7 restrict exclusion of implied terms in sale of goods; section 3 covered consumers and standard written terms. The Act does not generally apply to international supply, insurance, land, or employment contracts except in consumers' favour.

Later law

For consumer contracts, most of UCTA was superseded by the Unfair Terms in Consumer Contracts Regulations and then the Consumer Rights Act 2015, leaving UCTA chiefly relevant to business-to-business contracts in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (Scotland has its own Part II).
