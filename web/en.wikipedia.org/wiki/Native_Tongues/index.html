Native Tongues

The Native Tongues (also known as the Native Tongue Posse) was a collective of American hip hop artists who emerged in the late 1980s and early 1990s, known for Afrocentric lyrics, eclectic jazz- and soul-based sampling, and a playful, positive alternative to the gangsta rap then dominating the mainstream. The name derives from Le Tigre's "Watching the Eclipse" — actually from a lyric idea popularised within the group referencing "native tongues" as a metaphor for honest self-expression rooted in African heritage.

Formation and members

The collective formed around three acts signed to New York's burgeoning scene: the Jungle Brothers, whose 1988 album Straight Out the Jungle pioneered jazz rap; De La Soul, whose 1989 debut 3 Feet High and Rising was a landmark of sample collage; and A Tribe Called Quest, led by Q-Tip and Phife Dawg. The groups publicly united on landmark posse cuts — the Jungle Brothers' "Doin' Our Own Dang" (1990) and De La Soul's "Buddy" remix — and on Tribe's "Scenario" (1991), which also introduced Busta Rhymes of Leaders of the New School. Other members and affiliates included Queen Latifah, Black Sheep, Monie Love, Chi-Ali, the Beatnuts, Common, Mos Def, Talib Kweli, and Da Bush Babees.

Style and legacy

Native Tongues artists favoured bohemian dress, Afrocentric imagery, humour, and philosophical lyricism — De La Soul's "D.A.I.S.Y. Age" ("Dinner And Inner Soul Y'all") — over conspicuous machismo. Their albums of 1988–1993 are now canonical, and the collective's influence runs through later alternative hip hop, from the Soulquarians (with whom Q-Tip and others later worked) to artists such as Kanye West and Kendrick Lamar. Although internal tensions and label disputes fragmented the movement by the mid-1990s, members have periodically reunited and continue to be celebrated as founders of conscious and alternative rap.
