Flushing is a neighborhood in the north-central portion of the New York City borough of Queens. It was founded in 1645 under Dutch rule as one of the first European settlements on Long Island and named after the city of Vlissingen in the Netherlands. Downtown Flushing, centered on Main Street, is one of the busiest commercial districts in New York City and home to one of the largest Chinatowns in the United States.

History

In 1657, Flushing residents issued the Flushing Remonstrance, a petition defending religious liberty against Governor Peter Stuyvesant's persecution of Quakers; it is regarded as a precursor to the freedom of religion provisions of the United States Bill of Rights. The Quaker Meeting House (1694) still stands on Northern Boulevard. Flushing was also a center of commercial nurseries in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Demographics

Since the 1970s, Flushing has become a major destination for immigrants, particularly from China, Taiwan, and Korea. Downtown Flushing's Asian population and business community now rival or exceed Manhattan's Chinatown, with extensive Chinese- and Korean-language retail, restaurants, and services. The neighborhood also has significant South Asian and Latino communities.

Landmarks and transportation

Nearby Flushing Meadows–Corona Park was the site of the 1939 and 1964 New York World's Fairs and contains the Unisphere, Citi Field (home of the New York Mets), and the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, host of the US Open. Flushing is served by the Long Island Rail Road's Port Washington Branch and the New York City Subway's 7 train at the Flushing–Main Street station.
