Leucoagaricus gongylophorus

Leucoagaricus gongylophorus is a species of fungus in the family Agaricaceae. It is cultivated by many species of leafcutter ants and fungus-growing ants of the tribe Attini, in a mutualistic symbiosis.

The ants cut pieces of leaves and carry them to underground chambers, where they use them as substrate for the fungus. The fungus produces specialised swollen hyphal tips called gongylidia, which are rich in nutrients and are harvested and eaten by the ants. The ants in turn protect the fungus gardens from parasites and competitors and disperse the fungus when founding new colonies.

Ecology

The symbiosis between attine ants and L. gongylophorus is estimated to have evolved tens of millions of years ago in South America. The fungus is largely unable to survive independently of the ants, reproducing mainly clonally via fragments carried by founding queens. Parasitic fungi such as Escovopsis threaten the gardens and are controlled by the ants and by antibiotic-producing bacteria they carry.

Taxonomy

The species was previously known under names including Attamyces bromatificus for its anamorph form. Molecular studies have confirmed its placement in Leucoagaricus.
