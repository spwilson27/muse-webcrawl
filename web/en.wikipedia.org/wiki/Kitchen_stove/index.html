Kitchen stove

A kitchen stove, or cooker, is a kitchen appliance designed for cooking food, combining a cooktop with burners or heating elements and usually an enclosed oven. Stoves may be fuelled by gas, electricity, wood or other fuels.

History

Cooking fires moved indoors through hearths and chimney fireplaces before enclosed cast-iron wood stoves spread in the 18th and 19th centuries; Benjamin Franklin's 1742 stove and Count Rumford's designs improved efficiency. Gas stoves appeared in the 1820s–1850s, and electric stoves followed in the late 19th century, becoming common with household electrification.

Types

Modern stoves include gas ranges, electric resistance and smooth-top models, and induction cooktops, which heat ferrous pans directly by electromagnetic induction and are noted for speed and efficiency.
