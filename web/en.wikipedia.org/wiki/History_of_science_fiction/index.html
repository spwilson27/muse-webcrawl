History of science fiction

The history of science fiction spans from early fantastic voyages and utopias to the modern genre that emerged in the pulp magazines of the early twentieth century. Science fiction is commonly understood as fiction dealing with imaginative and futuristic concepts such as advanced science and technology, space exploration, time travel, and extraterrestrial life.

Precursors

Early works with science-fictional elements include Lucian's A True Story (2nd century CE), which satirised travellers' tales with a voyage to the Moon, and Johannes Kepler's Somnium (1634). Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) is frequently cited as the first true science fiction novel, using a scientific premise to explore creation and responsibility.

The founders: Verne and Wells

In the later nineteenth century Jules Verne popularised the scientific romance with works such as Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas and From the Earth to the Moon, emphasising plausible technology. H. G. Wells took a more sociological approach in The Time Machine, The War of the Worlds and The Invisible Man, using speculative ideas to comment on class, evolution and empire.

The pulp era and the Golden Age

Hugo Gernsback founded Amazing Stories in 1926 and coined the term "science fiction". Cheap pulp magazines nurtured a dedicated fandom and writers such as E. E. "Doc" Smith, who pioneered space opera. Under editor John W. Campbell, Astounding Science Fiction launched the so-called Golden Age of the late 1930s and 1940s, featuring Isaac Asimov, Robert A. Heinlein and Arthur C. Clarke.

The New Wave and beyond

In the 1960s the New Wave, associated with Michael Moorcock's New Worlds magazine and writers such as J. G. Ballard, Ursula K. Le Guin and Samuel R. Delany, brought literary experimentation and social themes to the fore. Feminist science fiction flourished in the 1970s; cyberpunk emerged in the 1980s with William Gibson's Neuromancer. Since then the genre has diversified globally and across media, dominating popular film and television franchises.
