Touch (also known as touch rugby or touch football) is a non-contact sport derived from rugby league in which players replace tackles with touches. One of the key features of touch is its simplicity: it requires minimal equipment, can be played by participants of varying ages and abilities, and is frequently played in mixed-gender teams. The sport is governed internationally by the Federation of International Touch (FIT) and is played in dozens of countries, with particular strength in Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa.

History

Touch developed in Sydney, Australia, in the 1960s, when it was used as a training technique for rugby league teams and as a social game. The first formal competition is generally dated to 1968, organised by the South Sydney Touch Football Association, and the sport spread through Australia in the 1970s as a recreational alternative to the contact codes. National bodies were established, and the Federation of International Touch was founded in 1985. The first Touch World Cup was held on the Gold Coast, Australia, in 1988.

Rules

Under FIT rules, two teams of six players compete on a field measuring 70 by 50 metres. The aim is to score touchdowns by grounding the ball in the opposing team's touchdown zone, while preventing the opposition from doing the same. A "touch" by a defender on the ball carrier counts in place of a tackle; after six touches, possession changes to the other team. The ball may be passed, knocked, or handed between players but must not be passed forward. Games typically consist of two halves of 20 minutes. After being touched, the ball carrier must perform a "rollball" — placing the ball on the ground and stepping over it — while the defensive team retreats the required distance, and a designated half picks up the ball to restart play.

Competitions

The Touch World Cup is held every four years and features divisions in men's, women's, and mixed categories across multiple age grades. Australia and New Zealand have historically dominated the open divisions. Regional championships and national leagues operate in many countries, and the sport has a large schools and social participation base.

Related variants

Touch is closely related to tag rugby, in which a tackle is simulated by removing a tag from the ball carrier's belt, and to various informal "touch football" games in American and Australian rules football. It is distinct from the North American touch football variants derived from gridiron.
