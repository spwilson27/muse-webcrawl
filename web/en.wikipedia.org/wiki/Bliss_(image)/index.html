Bliss is the name of a stock photograph of a green hill and blue sky with clouds, taken by American photographer Charles O'Rear in Sonoma County, California, in January 1996. It became the default desktop wallpaper of Microsoft's Windows XP operating system (released 2001) and is often described as the most viewed photograph in history, having appeared on an estimated billion computer screens.

History

O'Rear, a former National Geographic photographer, took the picture on medium-format film while driving to visit his girlfriend in Marin County. The hill — normally covered with grapevines — was temporarily bare and green because the vines had been removed after a phylloxera infestation, and a storm had just passed. The image was sold through the stock agency Westlight, acquired by Corbis, and Microsoft purchased all rights to it as the signature image of Windows XP, reportedly for one of the largest fees then paid for a single photograph. O'Rear has said the image was not digitally manipulated.

The site today

The hillside has since been replanted with vineyards, and the exact view is a popular pilgrimage spot for technology nostalgists. In 2017 O'Rear produced follow-up landscape images for a smartphone wallpaper campaign, and the Bliss hill continues to appear in exhibitions about digital culture.
