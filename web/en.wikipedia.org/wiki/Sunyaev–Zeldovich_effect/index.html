Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect

The Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect (named after Rashid Sunyaev and Yakov B. Zeldovich and often abbreviated as the SZ effect, SZE, or SZ) is the spectral distortion of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) through inverse Compton scattering by high-energy electrons in hot intracluster gas. The effect, predicted in 1969–1972, produces a decrement in CMB brightness below about 218 GHz and an increment above it, and allows astronomers to detect galaxy clusters almost independently of their redshift.

Thermal and kinematic effects

In the thermal SZ effect, CMB photons scatter off electrons with temperatures of millions of degrees in the intracluster medium, gaining energy on average; the resulting spectral shift is proportional to the Compton y-parameter, the line-of-sight integral of electron pressure, making the effect a direct probe of cluster thermal energy and gas mass. In the kinematic SZ effect, scattering off electrons moving with the cluster's bulk peculiar velocity produces a smaller, frequency-independent temperature shift proportional to the optical depth and radial velocity.

Observations and uses

Because the effect is a distortion of the CMB itself, its surface brightness does not dim with distance, so surveys by the Planck satellite, the Atacama Cosmology Telescope, and the South Pole Telescope have catalogued thousands of clusters, including some of the most distant known. Combined with X-ray observations, the effect yields cluster distances and estimates of the Hubble constant; polarization and relativistic corrections give further information on gas temperature and structure.
