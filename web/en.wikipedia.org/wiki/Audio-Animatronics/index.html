Audio-Animatronics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An Audio-Animatronic pirate figure

Audio-Animatronics (sometimes shortened to Animatronics) is the registered trademark of a form of robotics animation created by Walt Disney Imagineering for shows and attractions at Disney theme parks, and subsequently expanded on and used by other companies. The robots move and often synchronise with audio by the assistance of an external sound system on the stage (generally a recorded speech or song).

History

Walt Disney became fascinated with mechanical animation after purchasing a mechanical bird in a cage during a trip to New Orleans. The first Audio-Animatronics figures were unveiled at Disneyland in 1963 in Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room, where more than 150 birds, flowers, and tiki figures performed in synchronisation with a musical score. The technology advanced dramatically with the 1964 New York World's Fair, for which Disney created four attractions, including Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln, featuring a life-size, lifelike figure of Abraham Lincoln that could stand, speak, and gesture.

Technology

Early figures were driven by pneumatic and hydraulic systems controlled by reel-to-reel tape systems that encoded movements as tones. Later generations used computer-controlled servo systems, allowing far more fluid and complex motion. The skin of the figures is typically made of flexible materials such as vinyl, silicone, or proprietary blends stretched over a mechanical framework. Programming an Audio-Animatronic figure historically involved animators manually posing the figure through its range of motion; modern systems use computer animation software translated into physical movement.

Notable examples

  • Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room (1963) – the first Audio-Animatronics attraction.
  • Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln (1964) – the first human Audio-Animatronic figure.
  • Pirates of the Caribbean (1967) – populated with dozens of human and animal figures.
  • The Hall of Presidents (1971) – figures of every U.S. president.
  • Na'vi River Journey (2017) – featuring the Shaman of Songs, regarded as one of the most advanced figures.
  • Star Wars: Rise of the Resistance (2019) – large-scale figures including Kylo Ren and AT-AT walkers.

Use outside Disney

Although "Audio-Animatronics" is a Disney trademark, similar robotic figures—generically called animatronics—have been widely used in other theme parks, films, and entertainment venues. Companies such as Sally Corporation and Garner Holt Productions produce animatronic figures for parks worldwide, and the technology has been adapted for film special effects, most famously in creature work predating widespread computer-generated imagery.
