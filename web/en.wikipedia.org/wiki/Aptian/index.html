The Aptian is an age in the geologic timescale and a stage in the stratigraphic column. It is a subdivision of the Early or Lower Cretaceous Epoch/Series and lies between the Barremian and the Albian. The Aptian spans roughly from 125 to 113 million years ago. It is named after the town of Apt in the Vaucluse department of Provence, France, where the French palaeontologist Alcide d'Orbigny introduced it in 1840.

Events

The Aptian saw widespread deposition of black shales during Oceanic Anoxic Event 1a (the Selli Event, about 120 million years ago), when warm climates, high sea levels, and sluggish ocean circulation led to oxygen-poor deep waters and burial of large quantities of organic carbon. Extensive volcanism associated with the Ontong Java Plateau large igneous province has been linked to this event. Marine life included abundant ammonites, such as Deshayesites, used to subdivide the stage, and early planktonic foraminifera that help correlate Aptian rocks worldwide.

Definition

The base of the Aptian is defined by the magnetic anomaly M0r and the first appearance of certain ammonites; its top is marked by the first occurrence of the planktonic foraminifer Microhedbergella renilaevis. See also Cretaceous and Albian.
