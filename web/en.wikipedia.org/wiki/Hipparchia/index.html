Hipparchia

Hipparchia of Maroneia
Born: c. 350 BC, Maroneia, Thrace
Died: c. 280 BC
School: Cynicism
Spouse: Crates of Thebes

Hipparchia of Maroneia (Greek: Ἱππαρχία; c. 350 – c. 280 BC) was a Cynic philosopher and one of the few women philosophers of ancient Greece whose life is recorded in any detail. She was the wife of Crates of Thebes and, unusually for the period, lived the Cynic life publicly alongside him, wearing the same cloak, carrying the same staff and wallet, and attending symposia.

Life

Our main source, Diogenes Laërtius's Lives of the Eminent Philosophers, relates that Hipparchia, from a wealthy family of Maroneia in Thrace, fell in love with Crates's teaching and way of life and threatened to kill herself if her parents would not let her marry him. Crates, at her parents' request, tried to dissuade her by standing before her, throwing off his clothes and displaying his poverty — "this is the bridegroom, this is his property" — but she chose him anyway. The marriage, in which the couple lived openly together in the Cynic manner, was jokingly called the "dog marriage" (kynogamia), after the Cynics' nickname of "dogs".

Philosophy and reputation

Hipparchia is reported to have bested the dialectician Theodorus the Atheist in argument, answering his sophism about what was permissible for Theodorus being permissible for her. Letters and treatises were attributed to her in antiquity, though none survive. Her life became a standing proof-text in ancient debates about the education and capacities of women: the Cynics held that virtue is the same for men and women, and Hipparchia's rejection of wealth, spinning and seclusion for philosophy embodied that claim.
