Luce's choice axiom

Luce's choice axiom is a principle in probability theory and the psychology of choice, formulated by R. Duncan Luce in his 1959 book Individual Choice Behavior. It characterises how the probability of choosing one item from a set depends on the other items available, and it leads to the softmax (multinomial logit) model of choice.

Statement

The axiom says, roughly, that the probability of selecting a particular item over others is unaffected by the presence or absence of other items in the choice set, provided the relative odds between any two alternatives remain fixed. Formally, for alternatives with positive response strengths v(x), the probability of choosing x from a set S is

PS(x) = v(x) / Σy∈S v(y).

A consequence is that the ratio of choice probabilities for two alternatives is the same in every set containing both — an "independence from irrelevant alternatives" (IIA) property at the level of choice probabilities.

Related models

Luce's axiom gives rise to the Bradley–Terry–Luce model for paired comparisons and underlies the multinomial logit model used widely in econometrics and marketing. In machine learning and experimental psychology, the same functional form appears as the softmax function. The axiom has been criticised for empirical failures of IIA, such as the red bus/blue bus problem, which motivated more flexible nested and mixed logit models.
