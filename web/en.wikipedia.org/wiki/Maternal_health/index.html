Maternal health

Maternal health is the health of women during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period. It encompasses the health care dimensions of family planning, preconception, prenatal, and postnatal care in order to ensure a positive and fulfilling experience in most cases and reduce maternal morbidity and mortality in other cases.

Maternal mortality

Maternal death is defined by the World Health Organization as the death of a woman while pregnant or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy from any cause related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management. Global maternal deaths have declined substantially since 1990, but hundreds of thousands of women still die each year from largely preventable causes, with the great majority of deaths occurring in low- and middle-income countries, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.

The leading direct causes of maternal death include severe bleeding (postpartum hemorrhage), infections (usually after childbirth), high blood pressure during pregnancy (pre-eclampsia and eclampsia), complications from delivery, and unsafe abortion. Indirect causes include malaria, HIV/AIDS, and pre-existing conditions aggravated by pregnancy.

Care and interventions

Key interventions include skilled attendance at birth, access to emergency obstetric care (including caesarean section and blood transfusion), antenatal care visits, contraception to prevent unintended pregnancies, and treatment of complications such as eclampsia with magnesium sulfate. The "three delays" model — delays in deciding to seek care, in reaching a facility, and in receiving adequate care — is widely used to analyze barriers to maternal survival.

Global goals

Reducing maternal mortality was Millennium Development Goal 5, and it is now Target 3.1 of the Sustainable Development Goals, which aims to reduce the global maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030.
