Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus

Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus (10 April 1651 – 11 October 1708) was a German mathematician, physicist, physician and philosopher. He is remembered in mathematics for the Tschirnhaus transformation, in physics for his work on burning mirrors and lenses, and in technology for his central role in the European rediscovery of how to make hard-paste porcelain.

Life

Tschirnhaus was born at Kieslingswalde in Upper Lusatia and studied at Leiden, where he absorbed Cartesian philosophy and the new mathematics. He travelled widely in the Netherlands, England, France and Italy, meeting Huygens, Newton and Leibniz, with whom he corresponded for decades on the calculus and philosophy. He settled on his estates in Saxony, pursuing experiments with large burning glasses and founding glassworks.

Mathematics

In a paper of 1683 in the Acta Eruditorum, Tschirnhaus proposed a transformation that removes intermediate terms from a polynomial equation, hoping to solve equations of any degree by radicals. The method succeeded in simplifying cubic and quartic equations but failed for the quintic — as later work, culminating in the Abel–Ruffini theorem, showed it must. The Tschirnhaus transformation is still used in algebra; Bring and Jerrard later used it to reduce the general quintic to a simple normal form.

Porcelain

Working with the young alchemist Johann Friedrich Böttger under the patronage of Augustus the Strong of Saxony, Tschirnhaus directed systematic experiments with clays and high-temperature furnaces. By 1708 the group had produced true hard-paste porcelain, the secret that led to the founding of the Meissen manufactory in 1710. Tschirnhaus died in Dresden in October 1708, before production began, and his contribution was long overshadowed by Böttger's.
