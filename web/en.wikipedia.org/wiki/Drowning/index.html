Drowning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Prompt rescue and resuscitation greatly improve survival after drowning

Drowning is a type of suffocation induced by the submersion of the mouth and nose in a liquid. The World Health Organization defines drowning as "the process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion in liquid"; outcomes are classified as death, morbidity, or no morbidity. Most instances occur in fresh water, and young children are at particular risk.

Drowning is among the leading causes of unintentional injury death worldwide, with an estimated 236,000 deaths in 2019. The great majority occur in low- and middle-income countries, and many incidents go unreported. Contrary to popular portrayal, a drowning person often does not shout or wave: the instinctive drowning response involves pressing the arms laterally, an inability to call for help, and rapid exhaustion.

Pathophysiology

When water enters the airway, it triggers laryngospasm and aspiration. Aspiration of even small volumes washes out pulmonary surfactant and damages the alveolar–capillary membrane, impairing gas exchange; hypoxaemia leads to loss of consciousness and, if uninterrupted, cardiac arrest. Cold water can additionally trigger cold shock and, occasionally, prolonged survival through the mammalian diving reflex, especially in children.

Prevention and treatment

Effective prevention includes barriers around pools, supervision of children, life jackets, swimming and water-safety instruction, and regulation of alcohol use around water. Treatment begins with safe rescue (ideally without the rescuer entering the water), followed by rescue breaths and cardiopulmonary resuscitation; automated external defibrillation and hospital care addressing hypoxia, hypothermia and aspiration-related lung injury may follow. Outcomes after resuscitation range from full recovery to severe neurological injury or death.

Terminology

Older terms such as "near-drowning", "dry drowning" and "secondary drowning" are discouraged by medical bodies in favour of the single term drowning with an outcome qualifier, because the older vocabulary lacked agreed definitions.
