2016 Brussels bombings

On 22 March 2016, three coordinated suicide bombings occurred in Belgium: two at Brussels Airport in Zaventem, and one at Maelbeek metro station in central Brussels, near the institutions of the European Union. Thirty-two civilians and three perpetrators were killed, and more than 300 people were injured. The Islamic State (IS) claimed responsibility for the attacks.

Deaths: 35 (including 3 perpetrators)
Injured: 340+
Perpetrators: Islamic State cell

Attacks

At approximately 07:58, two explosions occurred in the departures hall of Brussels Airport. The bombers were identified as brothers Khalid and Ibrahim el-Bakraoui and Najim Laachraoui. Around an hour later, at 09:11, a third bomb exploded in a train leaving Maelbeek station; Khalid el-Bakraoui was identified as that attacker. A fourth bomb, left at the airport, was later destroyed in a controlled explosion; its owner, Mohamed Abrini, fled and was arrested weeks later.

Investigation

The attacks were linked to the same Brussels-based terrorist cell responsible for the November 2015 Paris attacks. The bombings came four days after the arrest in Brussels of Salah Abdeslam, a key Paris attacks suspect. Belgium raised its threat level to the maximum, Brussels was placed in lockdown, and the airport remained closed for nearly two weeks.

Reactions

Belgium declared three days of national mourning. The attacks prompted debate over Belgian security policy and integration, and led to resignations offers from interior and justice ministers, which the prime minister declined.
