Politics of Portugal

The politics of Portugal operate within a semi-presidential republic under the Constitution of 1976, adopted after the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974 ended the authoritarian Estado Novo regime. Legislative power rests with the unicameral Assembly of the Republic (230 deputies elected by proportional representation using the D'Hondt method).

Institutions

The President of the Republic, elected for five-year terms, appoints the Prime Minister, may dissolve the Assembly and call early elections, and can veto legislation (subject to override). The Prime Minister heads the government. Portugal is a unitary state with two autonomous regions, the Azores and Madeira, and was significantly decentralised to municipalities.

Party system

Since democratisation, government has alternated mainly between the centre-left Socialist Party (PS) and the centre-right Social Democratic Party (PSD, despite its name a liberal-conservative party). Smaller parties include the Left Bloc (BE), the Portuguese Communist Party (PCP) and its electoral alliance, the CDS–People's Party, PAN, the Liberal Initiative and the far-right Chega, which grew rapidly after 2019. A notable arrangement, the 2015–2019 "geringonça" ("contraption"), saw a PS minority government supported in parliament by the parties of the left.
