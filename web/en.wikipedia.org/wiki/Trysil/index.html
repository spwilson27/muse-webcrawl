Trysil

Trysil is a municipality in Innlandet county, Norway, in the traditional district of Østerdalen. The administrative centre is the village of Innbygda. With an area of about 3,014 square kilometres, Trysil is Norway's largest forestry municipality, and it borders Sweden to the east.

History and name

The name Trysil derives from a local river name, probably related to an old word for a quiet stretch of water. Forestry has long dominated the economy, with timber floated down the Trysilelva river system — the waterway, which becomes the Klarälven in Sweden, carried logs for centuries, a tradition maintained into the modern era as one of the last log-driving routes in Norway.

Trysilfjellet ski resort

Trysil is best known for the ski resort on Trysilfjellet, Norway's largest ski area, with dozens of lifts and around 70 kilometres of prepared slopes on three sides of the mountain. Alpine skiing in Trysil dates to 1861, and the area claims one of the world's first organized ski races (in 1855) and an early role in modern ski technique. The resort draws hundreds of thousands of visitors each winter, mostly from Norway, Sweden and Denmark, and has developed extensive summer mountain-biking facilities.

Nature

The municipality includes Fulufjellet National Park, shared with Sweden, and supports populations of bear, lynx, wolverine and moose. The author and poet Einar Skjæraasen, a noted son of Trysil, is commemorated locally.
