Ironman World Championship

The Ironman World Championship is an annual long-distance triathlon held since 1978 over the Ironman distance: a 2.4-mile (3.86 km) swim, a 112-mile (180.25 km) bicycle ride and a marathon 26.22-mile (42.2 km) run, raced in that order. It is widely regarded as the most prestigious event in long-course triathlon.

History

The first Iron Man race was held on Oahu, Hawaii, in 1978, combining three existing endurance events at the suggestion of US Navy commander John Collins. In 1981 the race moved to Kailua-Kona on the island of Hawaii, where it became famous for heat, humidity and crosswinds. In 2023 the championship adopted a split format, with the men's and women's professional races alternating between Kona and Nice, France.

Qualification and records

Entry is mainly by qualification at Ironman races worldwide, supplemented by legacy and lottery programmes. Notable champions include Dave Scott and Mark Allen (six titles each), Paula Newby-Fraser (eight women's titles), Jan Frodeno and Daniela Ryf.
