Virginia House of Delegates

The Virginia House of Delegates is the lower house of the Virginia General Assembly, the legislature of the Commonwealth of Virginia. It has 100 members, each elected for a two-year term from single-member districts of roughly equal population, and is presided over by the Speaker of the House. Together with the 40-member Senate of Virginia, it meets at the Virginia State Capitol in Richmond.

History

The House traces its origin to the House of Burgesses, convened at Jamestown in 1619 — the first elected legislative assembly in the English colonies of the Americas. After independence the Burgesses became the House of Delegates under the Virginia Constitution of 1776. For much of the 20th century the House was dominated by Democrats and the conservative Byrd Organization; party control became competitive from the 1990s onward, and the House has changed hands several times in the 21st century.

Composition and procedure

Delegates are elected in odd-numbered years. Sessions begin in January and last 60 days in even years and 45 days (often extended) in odd years, when a two-year budget is considered. The House originates revenue bills, elects judges and state officials jointly with the Senate, and can impeach state officers. Its day-to-day business is led by the Speaker, majority and minority leaders, and the Clerk of the House.
