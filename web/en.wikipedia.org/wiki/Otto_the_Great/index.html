Otto the Great

Otto I
Reign as King of East Francia: 936–973
Emperor: 2 February 962 – 7 May 973
Born: 23 November 912, Wallhausen
Died: 7 May 973, Memleben
Spouse: Eadgyth of Wessex; Adelaide of Italy
Dynasty: Ottonian

Otto I (23 November 912 – 7 May 973), known as Otto the Great (German: Otto der Große), was East Frankish (German) king from 936 and Holy Roman Emperor from 962 until his death in 973. He was the eldest son of Henry I the Fowler and Matilda of Ringelheim. Otto inherited the Duchy of Saxony and the kingship of the Germans upon his father's death in 936, and his reign is generally considered to mark the founding of the Holy Roman Empire.

Consolidation of the kingdom

Otto faced early revolts from his younger brother Thankmar, his half-brother, and later from his own brother Henry and son Liudolf, which he overcame by 955. He strengthened royal authority by appointing relatives to the great stem duchies, and — most consequentially — by endowing bishops and abbots with lands and regalian rights, building the "Ottonian church system" in which the imperial church served as a counterweight to the hereditary aristocracy. His court patronised the cultural flowering known as the Ottonian Renaissance.

Victory over the Magyars

In 955 Otto crushed the Magyar (Hungarian) invaders at the Battle of Lechfeld near Augsburg, ending the Magyar incursions into Western Europe for good and earning him acclamation as saviour of Christendom. The victory consolidated his prestige and enabled the establishment of marches along the eastern frontier.

Imperial coronation and Italy

Otto intervened in Italy in 951, marrying Adelaide, the widowed queen of Italy, and assuming the Italian crown. Returning in 961 to aid Pope John XII against Berengar II, he was crowned Emperor in St Peter's Basilica on 2 February 962 — the event traditionally seen as the founding of the Holy Roman Empire. The following year he deposed John XII, asserting imperial authority over papal elections through the Privilegium Ottonianum. In 972 his son Otto II married the Byzantine princess Theophanu, sealing recognition of his imperial title by Constantinople.

Death and legacy

Otto died at Memleben in 973 and was buried in Magdeburg Cathedral beside his first wife, Eadgyth. He transformed East Francia into the dominant power in Latin Christendom and founded a dynasty — the Ottonians — that ruled until 1024.
