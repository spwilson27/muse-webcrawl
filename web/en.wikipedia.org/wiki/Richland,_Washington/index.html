Richland is a city in Benton County, Washington, United States. It is located at the confluence of the Columbia and Yakima rivers in southeastern Washington, in the shrub-steppe of the Columbia Basin. Together with Kennewick and Pasco it forms the Tri-Cities metropolitan area, whose population exceeds 300,000. Richland's modern existence is owed to the Manhattan Project: in 1943 the U.S. government acquired the farming village and built the Hanford Site nearby, transforming Richland into a government-planned city for the workers producing plutonium for the atomic bomb.

History

The area was long inhabited by Wanapum and Yakama peoples. Settlers founded the town in the early 1900s, naming it for Nelson Rich, a land developer; it was incorporated in 1910 with a population of a few hundred. In March 1943 the federal government condemned the town and surrounding land for the Hanford Engineer Works, part of the Manhattan Project. The Army and the DuPont company built a construction camp and a planned "village" — designed by Spokane architect G. Albin Pehrson — of prefabricated houses and dormitories that housed over 11,000 residents by 1945. Hanford's B Reactor produced the plutonium used in the Trinity test and the Nagasaki bomb. Richland remained a closed government town until 1957–58, when residents were allowed to buy their homes and the city reincorporated under civilian control. The architect-designed "alphabet houses" of the wartime village remain a distinctive part of the city's housing stock.

Economy

Richland's economy remains closely tied to Hanford, where a decades-long environmental cleanup is now the main mission, managed with support from the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (one of the U.S. Department of Energy's national laboratories, located in Richland and operated by Battelle). Major employers include the Department of Energy, its cleanup contractors, the laboratory, Kadlec Regional Medical Center, and the Columbia Generating Station — Washington's only commercial nuclear power plant, near Hanford. The surrounding region is also noted for irrigated agriculture and wine production.

Geography and climate

Richland has a cold semi-arid climate with hot, dry summers and cold winters, receiving only about 180 mm (7 in) of precipitation a year in the rain shadow of the Cascade Range. Badger Mountain, a popular hiking landmark, rises within the city. The Columbia Point area, where the Yakima meets the Columbia, is a centre for parks, marinas and recreation.

Education and culture

The city is served by the Richland School District; the Hanford High School Falcons and Richland High School Bombers — whose mascot and mushroom-cloud logo reflect the city's atomic heritage — are local institutions. Washington State University Tri-Cities and Columbia Basin College serve higher education. The REACH Museum interprets the region's natural and Hanford history.
