Epimer

In stereochemistry, an epimer is one of a pair of diastereomers that differ in configuration at only one stereogenic center, while having the same configuration at all other centers. Epimers are common among the sugars: for example, D-glucose and D-galactose are epimers differing at carbon 4, while D-glucose and D-mannose are epimers differing at carbon 2.

Epimerization

Epimerization is the interconversion of one epimer into another. In biochemistry it is catalyzed by enzymes called epimerases; in laboratory chemistry, epimerization at a carbon adjacent to a carbonyl group can occur under acidic or basic conditions via an enol or enolate intermediate. Heating epigallocatechin gallate in tea, for instance, epimerizes it to gallocatechin gallate.

Examples

  • The steroids epi-testosterone and testosterone differ only in the orientation of the hydroxyl group at C17.
  • Doxorubicin and epirubicin are epimers; epirubicin was developed as a less cardiotoxic analogue.
  • Methylphenidate's dexmethylphenidate is an epimer-purified form of the drug.
