Detective

A detective is an investigator, usually a member of a law enforcement agency, whose work involves gathering information and evidence to solve crimes and identify offenders. Detectives typically work in plain clothes rather than uniform, and their duties include examining crime scenes, interviewing witnesses and suspects, conducting surveillance, analysing records, and preparing cases for prosecution. In many agencies, "detective" is both a rank and a specialisation, attained after service as a uniformed officer and further training.

Organisation

Police detectives are commonly assigned to specialised units — homicide, robbery, burglary, fraud, narcotics, vice, cybercrime, or cold cases — reflecting the very different skills each requires. In the United Kingdom, detectives serve in the Criminal Investigation Department (CID) and hold ranks prefixed "Detective", such as detective constable and detective inspector, equivalent in seniority to their uniformed counterparts. In the United States, ranks and grades vary widely by department.

Private detectives

A private investigator (private detective) is a civilian licensed to perform investigative work for private clients — for example, locating missing persons, investigating insurance claims, conducting background checks, or gathering evidence for legal proceedings. Private investigators generally have no police powers beyond those of any citizen.

In fiction

The detective is one of the most durable figures in popular culture, anchoring the genre of detective fiction from Edgar Allan Poe's C. Auguste Dupin and Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes through Agatha Christie's Hercule Poirot and Miss Marple, hard-boiled figures such as Sam Spade and Philip Marlowe, and the modern police procedural in literature and television.
