Battle of Maiwand

The Battle of Maiwand was fought on 27 July 1880 near the village of Maiwand, west of Kandahar in Afghanistan, during the Second Anglo-Afghan War. An Afghan army under Ayub Khan destroyed a British–Indian brigade under Brigadier-General George Burrows. It was one of the worst defeats suffered by British arms in Asia, and is remembered for the last stand of the 66th (Berkshire) Regiment and for the devotion of the regimental dog Bobbie.

Background

Ayub Khan, the Afghan governor of Herat and a claimant to the throne, marched on Kandahar in the summer of 1880. A brigade under Burrows, with a contingent of the Wali of Kandahar's unreliable troops, advanced from Kandahar to intercept him near Maiwand. On the morning of 27 July the Wali's infantry deserted to the enemy and the British brigade found itself facing Ayub's entire army — regular regiments with modern artillery plus thousands of ghazi tribal volunteers — on an open plain in ferocious heat.

The battle

The British line — Bombay Grenadiers, Jacob's Rifles, the 66th Foot, cavalry and smoothbore and rifled guns — initially held its own, but Afghan artillery, served with unexpected skill, progressively out-ranged and overwhelmed the British guns, while masses of ghazis worked round both flanks. After several hours the Indian infantry gave way and the line crumbled. The survivors of the 66th retired fighting through the village gardens of Khig; their last eleven men — the "Last Eleven" — formed a small square around the colours and fought back-to-back until all were killed. The remnants of the brigade retreated 45 miles to Kandahar under harassing attack, aided by a famous ride to carry the news. Of the brigade, 969 were killed.

Aftermath

Ayub Khan besieged Kandahar, but was crushed by General Frederick Roberts's relief force, fresh from the celebrated Kabul-to-Kandahar march, at the Battle of Kandahar on 1 September 1880. Maiwand entered British regimental legend: the Maiwand Lion in Forbury Gardens, Reading, commemorates the 328 men of the Berkshire Regiment who died, and Dr. Watson in Arthur Conan Doyle's A Study in Scarlet is a veteran wounded at Maiwand. In Afghanistan the battle is celebrated, and the Afghan heroine Malalai, who tradition says rallied the Afghan line with her veil as a banner, remains a national symbol.
