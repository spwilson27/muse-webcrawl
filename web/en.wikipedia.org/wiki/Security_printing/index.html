Security printing

Security printing is the field of the printing industry that deals with the printing of items such as banknotes, cheques, passports, tamper-evident labels, security tapes, product authentication, stock certificates, postage stamps, and identity cards.

The main goal of security printing is to prevent forgery, tampering, or counterfeiting. In recent years many of the techniques used to protect these high-value documents have become more available to commercial printers, whether they are using the more traditional offset and flexographic presses or the newer digital platforms.

Techniques

Security printing employs special substrates and features that are hard to reproduce: watermarks in mould-made paper or polymer substrates, intaglio printing with its raised ink, fine guilloché engraving, microprinting, optically variable and rainbow inks, holograms, security threads, ultraviolet-fluorescent features, and copy-evident designs. Access to materials such as banknote paper is tightly controlled.

Applications

National banknote printers and specialist firms (such as De La Rue and Giesecke+Devrient) produce currency and identity documents for governments, while commercial security printers produce cheques, certificates, tax stamps, and brand-protection labels.
