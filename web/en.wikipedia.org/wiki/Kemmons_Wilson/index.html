Kemmons Wilson

Placeholder image: Kemmons Wilson, founder of Holiday Inn.

Charles Kemmons Wilson (January 5, 1913 – February 12, 2003) was an American businessman from Memphis, Tennessee, best known as the founder of the Holiday Inn hotel chain. Wilson is widely credited with revolutionising roadside lodging in the United States by standardising motel quality and amenities.

Founding of Holiday Inn

Wilson conceived the idea for Holiday Inn after a 1951 family road trip to Washington, D.C., during which he was frustrated by inconsistent rooms and extra charges for children. He opened the first Holiday Inn in Memphis in August 1952; the name was a joke reference to the Bing Crosby film Holiday Inn, suggested by his architect. The chain grew rapidly through franchising, and its "Great Sign" became an icon of American highway culture.

Business philosophy

Wilson insisted on standardised rooms, air conditioning, free parking, ice machines, and children staying free — features that reshaped traveller expectations. By the time he retired as chairman in 1979, Holiday Inn was one of the world's largest lodging chains.

Later life

After leaving Holiday Inn, Wilson developed real estate and resort projects, including Orange Lake Country Club in Florida. He died in Memphis in 2003 at age 90.
