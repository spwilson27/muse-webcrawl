Judicial Committee of the Privy Council

The Judicial Committee of the Privy Council (JCPC) is the highest court of appeal for the Crown Dependencies, British Overseas Territories, some Commonwealth realms and a few independent Commonwealth countries. Established by the Judicial Committee Act 1833, it sits in the Middlesex Guildhall in London, sharing premises and judges with the UK Supreme Court.

Jurisdiction

The JCPC hears appeals from jurisdictions including Jamaica, the Bahamas, Trinidad and Tobago (partially), Mauritius, Brunei civil cases, Jersey, Guernsey, the Isle of Man and overseas territories. Several Caribbean states have replaced it with the Caribbean Court of Justice. Canada abolished Privy Council appeals in 1949, Australia in stages to 1986, and New Zealand in 2004.

Procedure

Formally the Committee advises the monarch, delivering a single judgment as an Order in Council, though dissents have been permitted since 1966. Its decisions are binding in the appealing jurisdiction and highly persuasive elsewhere in the common-law world.
