Aarti

Aarti (Hindi: आरती, romanised ārtī) is a Hindu religious ritual of worship in which light — usually from one or more lamps burning ghee or camphor — is offered to one or more deities. The word also refers to the songs sung in praise of the deity during the ritual. Aarti is performed one to five times daily in temples and homes, most commonly at dawn and dusk, as part of puja.

The ritual

The aarti plate (thali) typically holds a burning lamp or camphor, flowers, incense, water, and sometimes a bell. The plate is waved in a circular motion before the deity while devotees sing the aarti hymn, accompanied by bells and conch shells. The flame is understood to become imbued with the deity's presence; afterwards, devotees cup their hands over the flame and touch their eyes and heads to receive the blessing. Prasad, sanctified food, is often distributed after the aarti.

Variations

Well-known forms include the Ganga Aarti performed nightly on the ghats of Varanasi, Haridwar and Rishikesh, in which priests offer fire to the river goddess; aarti ceremonies at major temples such as Tirupati, Jagannath Puri and the Golden Temple tradition adapted in Sikh practice; and aartis in honour of specific deities, including "Om Jai Jagdish Hare" for Vishnu, aartis for Ganesh, Lakshmi, Durga and Shiva, and the Marathi aarti "Sukhkarta Dukhharta" popularised in Maharashtra. Aarti is also performed to welcome honoured guests, brides, and newborns, and in the rakhi and bhai dooj festivals.

Etymology

The word derives from the Sanskrit ārārtika, connected with rātri (night), and is interpreted devotionally as the removal of darkness — "that which dispels the darkness of ignorance".
