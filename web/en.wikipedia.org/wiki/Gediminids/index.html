The Gediminids (Lithuanian: Gediminaičiai) were a dynasty of monarchs in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania that reigned from the 14th to the 16th century. Descended from Grand Duke Gediminas (reigned 1316–1341), the dynasty expanded Lithuania into one of the largest states in Europe, stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea.

The Columns of Gediminas, the dynastic emblem of the Gediminids.

Origins and rise

Gediminas, whose origins are obscure, succeeded his brother Vytenis as Grand Duke around 1316. Through diplomacy — including his famous letters inviting German merchants and craftsmen to settle in Vilnius — and military campaigns, he consolidated Lithuanian rule over much of modern Belarus and Ukraine. His sons divided spheres of influence: Algirdas took the east, Kęstutis the west.

The Jagiellonian branch

The most famous descendant of the dynasty was Jogaila, grandson of Gediminas, who in 1386 married Queen Jadwiga of Poland, converted to Christianity, and became King Władysław II Jagiełło of Poland. His descendants, the Jagiellonian dynasty, ruled Poland and Lithuania, and at various times Bohemia and Hungary, until 1572. Other Gediminid lines survived as princely families in the Russian Empire, among them the Golitsyn, Kurakin, Khovansky, and Trubetskoy houses, all claiming descent from Gediminas's sons.

Legacy

The Gediminids are remembered as the founding dynasty of the Lithuanian state. Their emblem, the Columns of Gediminas, remains a national symbol of Lithuania, appearing on its coat of arms' historical forms, military insignia, and the Gediminas Tower in Vilnius, named for the dynasty's founder.
