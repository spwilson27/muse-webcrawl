FSO (automobile)

Full nameFabryka Samochodów Osobowych
Founded1948 / 1951 (production)
HeadquartersŻerań, Warsaw, Poland
IndustryAutomotive

FSO (Fabryka Samochodów Osobowych, "Passenger Automobile Factory") was a Polish automobile manufacturer based in the Żerań district of Warsaw. Founded after the Second World War, it produced cars under its own name for six decades.

History

Production began in 1951 with the Warszawa, a licence-built version of the Soviet GAZ-M20 Pobeda. FSO went on to build the Syrena, the Polski Fiat 125p under licence from Fiat, and from 1978 the Polonez, a Polish-designed hatchback that became its best-known model. After 1989 the plant passed through partnerships with Daewoo and General Motors, producing models such as the Lanos and Aveo, before car production wound down around 2011.

Models

FSO's notable products include the Warszawa, Syrena, Polski Fiat 125p and 126p-related production, the Polonez family (including the Caro and Atu variants), and licence-built Daewoo and Chevrolet models.
