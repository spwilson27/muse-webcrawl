John Russell, 1st Earl of Bedford (c. 1485 – 14/15 March 1555) was an English royal minister in the Tudor era. A courtier of Henry VIII, he served as a soldier, diplomat and administrator, was rewarded with vast former monastic estates at the Dissolution of the Monasteries, and founded the fortunes of the Russell family, which later produced the Dukes of Bedford.

Early career

Russell entered royal service after coming to the attention of Henry VII, traditionally said to have begun when he attended the Archduke Philip of Castile during a forced landing in Dorset in 1506. Under Henry VIII he campaigned in France and was knighted after the Battle of Morlaix in 1522, losing an eye to an arrow. He undertook diplomatic missions to Rome and to the Emperor Charles V, including during the crisis over the King's divorce from Catherine of Aragon.

Rise under Henry VIII and Edward VI

Russell was created Baron Russell in 1539 and received enormous grants of confiscated monastic land, including the abbeys of Tavistock and Woburn and property in Exeter and London (notably the Covent Garden area). As Lord High Admiral and later Lord Privy Seal he was one of the kingdom's senior officers of state. During the Prayer Book Rebellion of 1549 he commanded royal forces in the West Country and relieved Exeter. In 1550 Edward VI created him Earl of Bedford.

Later years and legacy

Russell retained favour under Mary I, serving on diplomatic business to Philip II of Spain. He died in London in March 1555. His descendants, the Earls and later Dukes of Bedford, built their power on the estates he accumulated, including Woburn Abbey and the Bedford estate in Bloomsbury and Covent Garden, London.
