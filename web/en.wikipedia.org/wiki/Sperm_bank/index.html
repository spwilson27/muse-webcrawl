Sperm bank

A sperm bank, semen bank, or cryobank is a facility or enterprise which purchases, stores and sells human semen. The semen is produced and sold by males who are known as sperm donors. The sperm is purchased by or for other persons for the purpose of achieving a pregnancy or pregnancies other than by a sexual partner. Sperm banks also store semen for men wishing to preserve fertility, for example before vasectomy or cancer treatment.

Donation and screening

Donors are typically screened for infectious diseases such as HIV, hepatitis, and syphilis, and for genetic conditions, and their semen quality is assessed. In many jurisdictions donation may be anonymous or identity-release, with differing rules on whether donor-conceived people may learn the donor's identity as adults. Donors are usually compensated, and limits may be placed on the number of families or children per donor to reduce inadvertent consanguinity.

Storage and use

Semen is frozen in liquid nitrogen at −196 °C in straws or vials, a process called cryopreservation, and can be stored for decades. Samples are thawed for use in artificial insemination (intrauterine insemination) or in vitro fertilisation. Recipients include heterosexual couples facing male infertility, single women, and lesbian couples.

Regulation

Regulation varies widely: the United States regulates sperm banks largely through FDA tissue rules; the United Kingdom licenses them through the HFEA and abolished donor anonymity in 2005; some countries prohibit payment or import/export. The first successful human pregnancy using frozen sperm was reported in 1953, and commercial sperm banking expanded from the 1970s.
