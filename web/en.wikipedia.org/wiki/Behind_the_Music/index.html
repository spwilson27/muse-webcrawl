Behind the Music is a documentary television series produced by VH1. Each episode profiles a musician or musical group, documenting their rise to fame, their struggles — often including addiction, financial trouble and band conflict — and their comeback or later career. The series premiered on 17 August 1997 with an episode on Milli Vanilli, and became one of VH1's signature programmes.

Format

Episodes, narrated for most of the run by Jim Forbes, interweave interviews with the artists, family, bandmates, managers and journalists with archival performance and news footage. The formula typically follows a dramatic arc — humble beginnings, breakthrough success, excess and downfall, and redemption — a structure so recognisable it was parodied on The Simpsons ("Behind the Laughter", 2000) and elsewhere.

History

The original run produced more than 200 episodes profiling acts including Metallica, Madonna, Aerosmith, Fleetwood Mac, Lynyrd Skynyrd, TLC, Pantera and Shania Twain. A short-form spin-off, Behind the Music Remastered, updated earlier episodes from 2010. The series ended its VH1 run in 2014 and was revived in 2021 on the streaming service Paramount+, with new episodes on artists including Duran Duran, New Kids on the Block and Bret Michaels.

Impact

Behind the Music helped define the modern music-documentary genre and the popular narrative template of "sex, drugs and rock 'n' roll" rise-and-fall storytelling. It won several awards and critical praise for reviving interest in many profiled artists' catalogues.
