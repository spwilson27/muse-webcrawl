HMS Belfast (C35)

HMS Belfast is a Town-class light cruiser of the Royal Navy, launched in 1938 and now preserved as a museum ship moored in the River Thames in London, administered by the Imperial War Museum. She was the first Royal Navy ship named after the capital of Northern Ireland and one of the largest and most powerful light cruisers built for the navy, with a main armament of twelve 6-inch guns.

Launched17 March 1938
Commissioned5 August 1939
Displacement11,553 tons (full load)

Service history

Belfast was badly damaged by a German magnetic mine in the Firth of Forth in November 1939, breaking her keel, and spent nearly three years under repair and modernisation. Returning to service, she escorted Arctic convoys to the Soviet Union and took part in the Battle of North Cape in December 1943, in which the German battleship Scharnhorst was sunk. On D-Day, 6 June 1944, she bombarded German positions in support of the Normandy landings. She later served in the Far East and in the Korean War, remaining in service until 1963.

Museum ship

Saved from scrapping by a preservation trust, Belfast opened to the public in October 1971. She is moored between London Bridge and Tower Bridge and is one of the most visited museum ships in the world; in 2010 her masts were restored with support from Russian sponsors, reflecting her Arctic convoy heritage.
