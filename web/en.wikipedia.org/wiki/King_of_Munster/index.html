King of Munster

The King of Munster (Irish: Rí Mumhan) was the title of the ruler of the Kingdom of Munster in early medieval and medieval Ireland, one of the five great provincial kingdoms. The kingship was centred on the Rock of Cashel, and the holder was styled in the annals as king of Cashel (rí Caissil).

History

From the fifth century the kingship was dominated by the Eóganachta dynasty, whose branches rotated the title among themselves for several centuries. The Eóganachta combined royal and ecclesiastical authority: Feidlimid mac Cremthanin (died 847) was both king and bishop-abbot, and Cormac mac Cuilennáin (died 908) is remembered as a scholar-bishop killed in battle at Bellaghmoon.

Rival dynasties

In the tenth century the upstart Dál gCais under Mathgamain mac Cennétig and then his brother Brian Boru seized the kingship, ending Eóganachta supremacy. Brian went on to become High King of Ireland before dying at the Battle of Clontarf in 1014. Thereafter the kingship was contested chiefly by the Uí Briain (O'Brien) and the Mac Cárthaigh (MacCarthy) dynasties, and the province was effectively divided into Thomond (north Munster) and Desmond (south Munster).

End of the kingship

Following the Treaty of Windsor (1175) and the Anglo-Norman invasion, the provincial kingship lost real power. Muirchertach Ua Briain (died 1119) was the last king to rule a substantially united Munster; the title lingered in the annals into the twelfth century as the great families were reduced to lordships.
