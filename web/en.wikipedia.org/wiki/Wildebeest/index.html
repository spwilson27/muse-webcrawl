Wildebeest

Genus: Connochaetes

Family: Bovidae

Species: Blue and black wildebeest

The wildebeest (also called the gnu) is an antelope of the genus Connochaetes, native to Eastern and Southern Africa. It belongs to the family Bovidae, which includes true antelopes, cattle, goats, sheep, and other even-toed horned ungulates. There are two species: the black wildebeest (C. gnou) and the blue wildebeest (C. taurinus).

Wildebeest are large, heavily built antelopes standing 115–140 cm (45–55 in) at the shoulder and weighing 126–275 kg (278–606 lb). Both sexes bear large, curved horns. They feed mainly on short grasses of the savanna grasslands and plains.

Migration

The blue wildebeest is famous for its annual long-distance migration, one of the most spectacular wildlife events on Earth. Around 1.5 million wildebeest, accompanied by zebras and gazelles, move in a great loop between the Serengeti of Tanzania and the Maasai Mara of Kenya in search of fresh grazing, crossing crocodile-infested rivers such as the Mara and Grumeti.

Ecology and conservation

Wildebeest are a keystone prey species for lions, hyenas, and other large predators, and their grazing and dung shape grassland ecology. Both species are listed as Least Concern, though populations depend on the protection of migration routes; fencing and development are ongoing threats.
