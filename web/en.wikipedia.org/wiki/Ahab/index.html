Ahab was the seventh king of Israel, the son and successor of King Omri and the husband of Jezebel of Sidon. According to the Hebrew Bible, Ahab reigned for 22 years and did "more to provoke the Lord, the God of Israel, to anger than all the kings of Israel who were before him". He is a central figure in the biblical narratives of the prophets Elijah and Elisha.

Reign

Ahab continued his father's policies of alliance with Phoenicia, cemented by his marriage to Jezebel, daughter of Ethbaal king of Sidon. The biblical account condemns Ahab for promoting the worship of Baal and Asherah in Israel, building a temple and altar to Baal in Samaria. The story of Naboth's vineyard, in which Jezebel arranged the death of Naboth so Ahab could seize his land, is presented as the emblem of his injustice, drawing Elijah's prophecy of doom on the dynasty.

Wars and archaeology

Ahab fought wars against Aram-Damascus under Ben-Hadad II, defeating him at Samaria and at Aphek, but was eventually killed at the battle of Ramoth-Gilead, where an arrow struck him despite his disguise. Outside the Bible, Ahab appears in the Kurkh Monolith of the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III, which credits him with contributing 2,000 chariots and 10,000 soldiers to the coalition that faced Assyria at the Battle of Qarqar in 853 BC — the largest chariot force in the alliance, indicating that Israel under Ahab was a significant military power.

Death

The Book of Kings relates that Ahab's blood was washed from his chariot in the pool of Samaria, where dogs licked it up, fulfilling Elijah's prophecy. He was succeeded by his son Ahaziah.
