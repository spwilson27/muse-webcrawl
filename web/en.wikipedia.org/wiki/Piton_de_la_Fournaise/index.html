Piton de la Fournaise ("Peak of the Furnace") is a shield volcano on the eastern end of Réunion island in the Indian Ocean. It is currently one of the most active volcanoes in the world, along with Kīlauea in the Hawaiian Islands, Stromboli and Etna in Italy, and Mount Erebus in Antarctica.

Geology

The volcano sits within the Enclos Fouqué, a large caldera about 8 kilometres (5.0 mi) wide, open to the sea on its eastern side. It is the younger and still-active product of the Réunion hotspot, which also built the older, now-dormant Piton des Neiges massif to the northwest. Most eruptions occur within the caldera, so lava flows rarely threaten inhabited areas, though flows occasionally cross the island's coastal road and reach the sea.

Eruptive history

Eruptions typically occur several times a year, producing fluid basaltic lava fountains and flows. A major eruption in April 2007 caused the Dolomieu crater floor to collapse by more than 300 metres and sent lava into the ocean near the village of Le Tremblet, forcing evacuations. The volcano is monitored by the Piton de la Fournaise Volcano Observatory (OVPF).

Access

A road climbs from the Plaine des Cafres to the Pas de Bellecombe viewpoint on the caldera rim, from which marked trails descend into the Enclos and up to the summit craters. The area forms part of Réunion National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
