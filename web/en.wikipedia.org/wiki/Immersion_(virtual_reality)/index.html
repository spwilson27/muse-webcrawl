Immersion (virtual reality)

Immersion
FieldVirtual reality, human–computer interaction
Related conceptPresence (telepresence)

Immersion into virtual reality (VR) is a perception of being physically present in a non-physical world. In the technical usage developed by researchers such as Mel Slater, immersion is an objective property of a system: the extent to which it surrounds the user with sensory information, shuts out the physical world, tracks the user's viewpoint accurately and updates the display in real time. The subjective feeling that immersion produces is called presence.

Types of immersion

Following Ernest Adams and other theorists, writers on game design often distinguish tactical immersion (absorption in moment-to-moment skilled action), strategic immersion (absorption in planning and problem-solving) and narrative immersion (absorption in a story). Staffan Björk and Jussi Holopainen describe sensory-motoric, cognitive and emotional immersion, while spatial immersion occurs when a simulated world feels perceptually convincing and explorable.

Factors contributing to immersion

  • Field of view and resolution of the head-mounted display or surround screens.
  • Tracking: low-latency positional and rotational tracking of head and hands.
  • Stereoscopy and spatial audio delivering depth cues consistent with the real world.
  • Interaction fidelity: natural mapping between the user's actions and events in the virtual world.
  • Consistency: a coherent simulated world whose rules do not contradict the user's expectations.

Presence and break in presence

When immersion is high, users respond to virtual events as if they were real — flinching at virtual heights, for example. Glitches, latency or reminders of the physical environment cause a "break in presence". Designers distinguish immersion from the older, looser notion of flow, though the two are related.
