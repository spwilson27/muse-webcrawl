Placental-site trophoblastic tumor

Specialty: Oncology, gynecology

Placental-site trophoblastic tumor (PSTT) is a rare form of gestational trophoblastic disease arising from the intermediate trophoblast of the placental implantation site. Unlike choriocarcinoma, it produces relatively little human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) and grows more slowly, but it can metastasize and is comparatively resistant to chemotherapy.

Signs and symptoms

PSTT usually presents months to years after a pregnancy — which may have been a normal term delivery, a miscarriage, or a molar pregnancy — with abnormal uterine bleeding or amenorrhea and an enlarged uterus. Serum hCG is typically only modestly elevated relative to the tumor burden.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is made by histological examination, typically after curettage or hysterectomy, showing mononucleate intermediate trophoblastic cells infiltrating the myometrium. Because PSTT responds poorly to the chemotherapy regimens used for other trophoblastic tumors, surgical removal — usually hysterectomy — is the mainstay of treatment for disease confined to the uterus; chemotherapy is used for metastatic disease.
