Double-decker bus

A double-decker bus is a bus that has two storeys or decks. They are in use predominantly around the United Kingdom, Ireland and Hong Kong, and are found elsewhere mainly in the form of open-top buses used for sightseeing tours. The classic red London double-decker — particularly the Routemaster — has become an international symbol of the city.

History

Double-decker vehicles trace their origins to two-storey horse-drawn omnibuses and trams of the 19th century. The first motorised double-deckers appeared in the early 20th century; by the 1920s and 1930s they were dominating urban fleets in Britain because a double-decker can carry nearly twice the passengers of a single-decker within the same road footprint.

The AEC Routemaster, introduced by London Transport in 1956, became the archetype: half-cab design, open rear boarding platform and a separate conductor. Routemasters remained in regular service until 2005. In 2012 the New Routemaster, designed by Thomas Heatherwick, entered service with a hybrid drivetrain and a hop-on platform.

Design

The upper deck offers passengers elevated views, which makes double-deckers popular for sightseeing. Modern designs have low floors, wheelchair access at the front entrance, and a staircase usually located behind the driver or in the middle of the vehicle. Typical capacity is 60–90 passengers. Height restrictions imposed by bridges limit overall height to roughly 4.4 metres (14 ft 6 in) in the UK.

Around the world

Hong Kong's franchised bus companies operate one of the world's largest double-decker fleets, with high-capacity three-axle buses. Double-deckers are also common in Ireland (notably Dublin Bus), in several British cities, and in sightseeing fleets in cities such as New York, Paris and Cape Town. Sydney and other Australian cities have adopted double-deckers on high-demand routes.
