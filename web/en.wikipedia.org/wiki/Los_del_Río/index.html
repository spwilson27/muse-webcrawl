Los del Río

Los del Río ("Those from the River") is a Spanish musical duo from Dos Hermanas, Seville, consisting of Antonio Romero Monge (born 1948) and Rafael Ruiz Perdigones (born 1947). Formed in 1962, they performed Andalusian folk and rumba flamenca for decades before achieving worldwide fame in the 1990s with the dance hit "Macarena".

Career

Romero and Ruiz began performing as teenagers and became regulars at Andalusian fairs and festivals, recording numerous albums of sevillanas and rumbas and achieving success in Spain and Latin America. Their song "Macarena", first released in 1993 and named after a neighbourhood of Seville and a woman in the lyrics, became a global phenomenon after a remix by the Bayside Boys in 1995–96, whose English-language version topped the US Billboard Hot 100 for 14 weeks and sparked a worldwide dance craze. The song was performed at the 1996 Democratic National Convention and remains one of the best-selling singles of all time.

Later work

The duo continued recording and touring in the Spanish-speaking world, releasing albums of sevillanas and festive songs, and received numerous honours in Andalusia. "Macarena" has been repeatedly revived, including in a 2019 version featuring their collaboration on "Macarena" remixes and in viral dance revivals.
