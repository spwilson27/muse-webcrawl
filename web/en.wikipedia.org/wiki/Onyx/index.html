Onyx is a banded variety of the silicate mineral chalcedony. The colours of its bands range from black to almost every colour. Commonly, specimens of onyx contain bands of black and/or white. Onyx, as a descriptive term, has also been applied to parallel-banded varieties of alabaster, marble, calcite, obsidian, and opal, and misleadingly to materials with contorted banding, such as "cave onyx" and "Mexican onyx".

The name comes from the Latin onyx, itself from the Greek ὄνυξ, meaning "claw" or "fingernail", because onyx with flesh-toned bands was said to resemble a fingernail.

Varieties

Onyx is formed of bands of chalcedony in alternating colours. It is cryptocrystalline, consisting of fine intergrowths of the silica minerals quartz and moganite. Its bands are parallel to one another, unlike the more chaotic banding of agate, which is curved. Sardonyx is a variant in which the coloured bands are sard (shades of red) rather than black. Black onyx is perhaps the most famous variety, but is less common than onyx with coloured bands. Artificial treatments have been used since ancient times to produce both the black colour in "black onyx" and the reds and yellows in sardonyx; much onyx on the market is artificially coloured.

History and uses

Onyx was used in Egypt as early as the Second Dynasty for bowls and other pottery items. In ancient Rome and Greece it was carved into cameos and intaglios, exploiting its layered structure so that a design in one colour stands out against a background of another. Onyx has traditionally been one of the stones used in jewellery, seals, and hardstone carving, and it is mentioned in the Bible as one of the stones in the breastplate of the High Priest and among the foundation stones of the New Jerusalem.

In modern usage, onyx is popular for beads, cabochons, and carved ornaments; black onyx is a traditional material for rosary beads. Banded calcite sold as "onyx marble" is widely used for vases, lamps, and tabletops, although it is geologically a travertine rather than true chalcedony onyx.
