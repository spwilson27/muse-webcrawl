Insects as feed

Insects as feed are insect species used as animal feed, either for livestock, including aquaculture, or as pet food. Insects have been raised for centuries as a source of food for animals, and industrial-scale insect farming for feed has grown rapidly since the early 21st century.

Nutritional value

Insects are rich in protein, fats, minerals and amino acids. Black soldier fly larvae, mealworms and crickets are among the most commonly farmed species. Their amino acid profiles are comparable to fishmeal and soybean meal, traditional protein sources in animal feed.

Environmental benefits

Insect farming typically requires less land, water and feed than conventional livestock production, and produces lower greenhouse gas emissions. Many farmed insects can be reared on organic side streams and food waste, contributing to a circular economy.

Regulation

The use of insect protein in livestock feed is regulated differently around the world. The European Union authorised processed animal protein from insects in aquaculture feed in 2017 and extended the authorisation to poultry and pig feed in 2021.
