Chiba Prefecture

Chiba Prefecture (千葉県, Chiba-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kantō region of Honshu. Its population is about 6.3 million, making it Japan's sixth-most populous prefecture, and its capital and largest city is Chiba. Much of the prefecture forms the Bōsō Peninsula, which separates Tokyo Bay from the Pacific Ocean.

Geography and economy

The northern part of the prefecture lies within the Tokyo metropolitan area and includes commuter cities such as Funabashi, Matsudo and Kashiwa, while the rural south of the Bōsō Peninsula is known for agriculture — Chiba leads Japan in peanut production — fishing ports, and surf beaches around Kujukuri and Onjuku. The Keiyō Industrial Zone along Tokyo Bay hosts steelworks, oil refineries and power stations, and Narita International Airport, Japan's principal international gateway, is located in the prefecture, as is Tokyo Disney Resort at Urayasu.

History

Chiba Prefecture was created in 1873 from the former provinces of Kazusa, Shimōsa and Awa. It was badly affected by the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, which caused liquefaction damage in bay-side cities and flooding on the Pacific coast.
