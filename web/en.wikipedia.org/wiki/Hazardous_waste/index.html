Hazardous waste

Hazardous waste is waste that poses substantial or potential threats to public health or the environment. In the United States, the treatment, storage, and disposal of hazardous waste is regulated under the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) of 1976. Hazardous wastes are defined under RCRA in 40 CFR 261 and are divided into listed wastes and characteristic wastes exhibiting ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, or toxicity.

Types and sources

Hazardous waste is generated by chemical manufacturing, petroleum refining, metal production, dry cleaning, hospitals (medical waste), agriculture (pesticides), and households, which produce paints, solvents, batteries and electronic waste. Radioactive waste and mixed waste are regulated under separate regimes, such as the Atomic Energy Act.

Management and disposal

Management follows a "cradle-to-grave" manifest system tracking waste from generator through transporter to a permitted treatment, storage and disposal facility. Treatment methods include incineration, chemical neutralisation, stabilisation, and secure landfilling in lined cells with leachate collection. The Basel Convention of 1989 regulates the transboundary movement of hazardous wastes, following scandals over dumping in developing countries, such as the Khian Sea ash incident and the 2006 Probo Koala dumping in Ivory Coast.

Cleanup

Abandoned hazardous waste sites in the United States are remediated under the Superfund programme (CERCLA, 1980), created after the Love Canal disaster. The cost of cleanup falls, where possible, on the parties responsible for the contamination.
