Edward Gough Whitlam (11 July 1916 – 21 October 2014) was an Australian politician and lawyer who served as the 21st prime minister of Australia from 1972 to 1975. He led the Australian Labor Party (ALP) to government at the 1972 election, ending 23 years of Liberal–Country coalition rule.

Reforms

The Whitlam Government implemented an extensive program of reform: it established the universal health insurance scheme Medibank, abolished university tuition fees, introduced federal funding for state schools, withdrew Australian troops from Vietnam, formally recognised the People's Republic of China, lowered the voting age to 18, and supported land rights for Aboriginal Australians. It also introduced no-fault divorce and anti-discrimination legislation.

The Dismissal

Whitlam's government was plagued by scandal and economic difficulties, and in October 1975 the opposition-controlled Senate blocked supply. On 11 November 1975, Governor-General Sir John Kerr dismissed Whitlam and commissioned Liberal leader Malcolm Fraser as caretaker prime minister — the most dramatic constitutional crisis in Australian history. Fraser's coalition won the ensuing election in a landslide.

Later life

Whitlam led Labor to defeat again in 1977 and retired from parliament in 1978. He later served as ambassador to UNESCO and remained a prominent public figure and elder statesman of the Labor Party until his death in 2014, aged 98.
