Kwaidan

Kwaidan: Stories and Studies of Strange Things
AuthorLafcadio Hearn
Published1904
PublisherHoughton, Mifflin and Co.
GenreFolktale collection, supernatural fiction

Kwaidan: Stories and Studies of Strange Things is a 1904 book by Lafcadio Hearn containing retellings of Japanese ghost stories and folktales, together with a series of brief essays on insects. The word kwaidan is an old-fashioned rendering of the Japanese kaidan (怪談), meaning "strange tales" or ghost stories. The book was one of the last works published by Hearn, who had lived in Japan from 1890 and became a Japanese citizen under the name Koizumi Yakumo.

Contents

The stories include celebrated tales such as "The Story of Mimi-nashi Hōichi" (Hōichi the Earless), in which a blind biwa player is protected from ghosts by having the Heart Sutra inscribed over his body — except his ears; "Hoichi the Earless", "The Story of O-Tei", "Jikininki" (a corpse-eating goblin priest), "Mujina", "Rokuro-kubi", "A Dead Secret", "Yuki-Onna" (the snow woman), and "The Story of Aoyama". The volume concludes with three "Insect-Studies": "Butterflies", "Mosquitoes" and "Ants", blending folklore with philosophical reflection.

Adaptations

Four of the stories were adapted in Masaki Kobayashi's 1964 anthology film Kwaidan, which won the Special Jury Prize at the 1965 Cannes Film Festival and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. The book remains one of the most influential English-language introductions to Japanese folklore and the Japanese ghost-story tradition.
