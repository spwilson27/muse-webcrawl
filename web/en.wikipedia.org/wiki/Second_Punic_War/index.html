The Second Punic War (218–201 BC) was the second of three wars between Rome and Carthage, and one of the defining conflicts of the ancient world. It is famous for Hannibal's crossing of the Alps and his string of victories in Italy.

Course

After the siege of Saguntum, Hannibal marched from Iberia over the Alps into Italy, winning at the Trebia (218), Lake Trasimene (217), and catastrophically for Rome at Cannae (216). Rome, following Fabius Maximus's delaying strategy, avoided pitched battle, retook Capua and Syracuse, and campaigned in Iberia under the Scipios. Scipio Africanus captured New Carthage (209) and won at Ilipa (206), then invaded Africa. Hannibal was recalled and defeated at Zama in 202 BC.

Peace and significance

Carthage lost Iberia, its fleet, and paid a huge indemnity, forbidden to wage war without Roman permission. Rome emerged as the dominant western Mediterranean power. Ancient sources include Polybius and Livy. The war's milieu later shaped the Scipionic Circle.
