The Dilleniales are an order of flowering plants, potentially containing one family, Dilleniaceae. The order is named after the German botanist Johann Jacob Dillenius. The best-known genus in the family is Hibbertia, which contains many species grown as ornamental shrubs in Australian gardens; other genera include Dillenia, whose species include the elephant apple (Dillenia indica) of Asia, and Tetracera.

Classification

The taxonomic position of the Dilleniaceae was long debated. In the Cronquist system the family was placed in the order Dilleniales within the subclass Dilleniidae. The APG II system (2003) left the family unplaced as to order, and APG III (2009) similarly listed it among the core eudicots without an ordinal assignment. Molecular phylogenetic studies subsequently placed Dilleniaceae as sister to the superrosids; on that basis the APG IV system (2016) recognised the order Dilleniales as a monotypic order containing only Dilleniaceae, positioned at the base of the superrosids.

Description

Members of the Dilleniaceae are trees, shrubs, lianas and occasional herbs, mostly tropical and subtropical, with centres of diversity in Australia and Southeast Asia. The flowers typically have five sepals and five petals, numerous spirally arranged stamens, and carpels that are free or partly united. Leaves are simple and often have prominently parallel secondary veins.
