Lavra

A lavra or laura (Greek: Λαύρα; Ukrainian and Russian: Лавра) is a type of monastery in Eastern Orthodox tradition consisting of a cluster of cells or caves for hermits, with a church and sometimes a refectory at the centre. The term originally described early monastic settlements in Palestine, such as the Great Lavra of St Sabbas (Mar Saba, founded 483) in the Judean desert.

In Slavic Orthodoxy

In the Russian and Ukrainian traditions, "lavra" became the highest rank of monastery, reserved for a few great houses directly under the patriarch. The most famous are:

  • Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (Kyiv Monastery of the Caves, founded 1051) — a UNESCO World Heritage site.
  • Trinity Lavra of St Sergius at Sergiyev Posad (founded 1337) — spiritual centre of the Russian Church.
  • Alexander Nevsky Lavra in St Petersburg (founded by Peter the Great).
  • Pochayiv Lavra and Sviatohirsk Lavra in Ukraine.

Mount Athos

The Great Lavra (Megisti Lavra, founded 963 by Athanasius the Athonite) is the oldest and first-ranking monastery on Mount Athos, from which many Athonite houses descend.
