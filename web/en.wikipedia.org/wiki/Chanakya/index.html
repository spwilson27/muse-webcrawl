Chanakya

Chanakya (Sanskrit: चाणक्य, Cāṇakya; traditionally dated c. 375–283 BCE) was an ancient Indian teacher, philosopher, economist, and royal adviser. He is traditionally credited as the author of the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, economics, and military strategy, and as the chief architect of the rise of Chandragupta Maurya to power, which led to the founding of the Mauryan Empire. Chanakya is also known by the names Kautilya and Vishnugupta.

Life and legends

Few firm historical facts about Chanakya survive; most accounts come from later literary and legendary traditions, including the Sanskrit play Mudrarakshasa by Vishakhadatta, Jain texts such as Hemachandra's Parishishtaparvan, Buddhist chronicles, and Kashmiri works such as the Kathasaritsagara. According to the standard legend, Chanakya was a Brahmin teacher from Taxila who was insulted at the court of the Nanda king of Magadha; vowing revenge, he took the young Chandragupta under his wing, raised an army, and overthrew the Nanda dynasty (c. 321 BCE), installing Chandragupta on the throne at Pataliputra. As chief minister, Chanakya is said to have secured the new empire against the defeated minister Rakshasa — whom he won over as an ally — and against the successors of Alexander the Great in the northwest.

The Arthashastra

The Arthashastra, rediscovered in 1905 when a manuscript was identified by R. Shamasastry, is a systematic treatise on governance: the duties of the king, administration, law, taxation, diplomacy (including the famous mandala theory of interstate relations), espionage, and war. Its pragmatic, sometimes ruthless counsel on power has led to Chanakya being compared to Machiavelli, though the text predates The Prince by well over a millennium. Modern scholarship holds that the text is a layered work compiled over several centuries, built around a core attributed to Kautilya.

Another work, the Chanakya Niti (or Chanakya Neeti-shastra), is a later compilation of aphorisms on conduct and practical wisdom attributed to him.

Legacy

Chanakya remains a potent figure in Indian public life: diplomatic enclaves in New Delhi (Chanakyapuri) are named after him, and his name is invoked in political and strategic discourse as a symbol of shrewd statecraft. His life and the Mauryan founding have been dramatised in novels, plays, films, and television series in India.
