Lee Alexander McQueen (17 March 1969 – 11 February 2010) was a British fashion designer known for his mastery of tailoring, theatrical runway shows and provocative, emotionally charged designs. He founded his own label, Alexander McQueen, in 1992 and served as chief designer of the French house Givenchy from 1996 to 2001.

Career

Trained as an apprentice tailor on Savile Row, McQueen studied at Central Saint Martins, where his 1992 graduate collection was bought in its entirety by stylist Isabella Blow. His early collections, including "Highland Rape" and the famous "bumster" trousers, earned him both controversy and acclaim. He was named British Designer of the Year four times and appointed CBE in 2003. His label became known for combining sharp tailoring with romantic and often dark imagery — skull motifs, feathers and dramatic silhouettes — and for spectacular shows such as the "Plato's Atlantis" collection of 2010, which introduced the Armadillo boot.

Death and legacy

McQueen died by suicide at his London home in February 2010, shortly after the death of his mother. His brand continued under creative director Sarah Burton. The 2011 Metropolitan Museum of Art exhibition "Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty" became one of the museum's most visited exhibitions, cementing his reputation as one of the most influential designers of his generation.
