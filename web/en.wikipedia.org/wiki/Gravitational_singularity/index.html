Gravitational singularity

A gravitational singularity, spacetime singularity or simply singularity, is a condition in which the gravitational field of a celestial body is predicted to become infinite, or spacetime curvature becomes infinite, so the laws of physics as described by general relativity break down. Singularities are found inside black holes and at the Big Bang.

TypesPoint-like, ring (Kerr), initial (Big Bang)
Key theoremsPenrose–Hawking singularity theorems
RelatedEvent horizon, cosmic censorship

Black holes

In a non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole the singularity is a point; in a rotating Kerr black hole it is a ring. The Penrose–Hawking theorems (1960s) showed singularities are a generic feature of gravitational collapse, not an artefact of symmetry.

Interpretation

Most physicists regard singularities as signalling that general relativity must be replaced by a quantum theory of gravity (e.g. string theory or loop quantum gravity) at Planck scales. Roger Penrose's cosmic censorship hypothesis proposes singularities are always hidden behind event horizons. See Black hole.
