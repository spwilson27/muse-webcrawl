Virgin Mary

The Virgin Mary is a title given to Mary, the mother of Jesus, reflecting the Christian doctrine of the virgin birth—that she conceived Jesus miraculously by the Holy Spirit while remaining a virgin. The title (Greek: ἡ Παρθένος, hē Parthenos; Latin: Virgo Maria) is used especially in Catholic, Orthodox, and some Anglican and Lutheran traditions, and Mary is venerated as the foremost of the saints.

The Virgin and Child, one of the most depicted subjects in Western art.

Doctrine

The Gospels of Matthew and Luke describe the Annunciation, in which the angel Gabriel announces to Mary that she will conceive a son by the power of the Holy Spirit; her consent ("be it unto me according to thy word") is central to devotional reflection. The Council of Ephesus (431) affirmed the title Theotokos ("God-bearer"). Catholic doctrine additionally teaches her perpetual virginity, the Immaculate Conception (defined 1854), and the Assumption into heaven (defined 1950); Eastern Orthodoxy venerates her as ever-virgin and celebrates her Dormition. Most Protestant traditions accept the virgin birth but reject the later dogmas and devotional practices.

Devotion and apparitions

Marian devotion includes prayers such as the Hail Mary and the Rosary, feasts throughout the liturgical year, and pilgrimage to shrines associated with reported apparitions—Guadalupe, Lourdes, and Fátima among the most famous. Mary, known in Islam as Maryam, is also the only woman named in the Qur'an, which devotes a chapter (Surah Maryam) to her and affirms the virgin birth of Jesus (Isa).

In art

The Madonna and Child became one of the most frequent subjects in Western art, from Byzantine icons—where Mary is shown as Theotokos on a throne—to Renaissance masters and modern works. She is conventionally shown in blue, a color associated with her in medieval and Renaissance painting.
