Overview

The 2022 FIFA World Cup was the 22nd FIFA World Cup, contested by 32 men's national teams in Qatar from 20 November to 18 December 2022 — the first World Cup held in the Arab world and the Middle East, and the first played in November–December (to avoid Qatar's summer heat), disrupting club seasons worldwide. It was the last World Cup with 32 teams before expansion to 48 in 2026.

Tournament

Argentina, led by Lionel Messi, won the tournament, beating France in a final widely called the greatest ever: Argentina led 2–0, Kylian Mbappé's two goals in 97 seconds forced extra time, Messi and Mbappé scored again (Mbappé completing a hat-trick), and Argentina won the shootout 4–2. Morocco became the first African and first Arab team to reach a semi-final, finishing fourth; Croatia placed third. Messi won the Golden Ball, Mbappé the Golden Boot (8 goals), and the tournament set an attendance record for its compact scale.

Controversies

Qatar's selection (awarded 2010) was shadowed by corruption investigations (which led to no reversal), by the treatment and deaths of migrant workers who built the infrastructure — figures were fiercely disputed between officials, unions and journalists — and by Qatar's laws on homosexuality, alcohol (banned in stadiums at short notice) and protest symbols. The legacy debate continues alongside celebration of an operationally smooth, highly attended tournament.
