Sforza

The House of Sforza was a ruling family of Renaissance Italy, based in Milan. Founded by the condottiero Muzio Attendolo, nicknamed "Sforza" ("to force") for his strength, the family rose from peasant origins in Romagna to rule the Duchy of Milan from 1450 to 1535 and to marry into the royal houses of Europe.

Rise to power

Muzio's son Francesco Sforza, one of the most successful mercenary captains of his age, married Bianca Maria Visconti, last heir of the Visconti dukes of Milan. When the Ambrosian Republic that succeeded the Visconti collapsed, Francesco conquered Milan in 1450 and was proclaimed duke. His court made Milan a centre of Renaissance art and diplomacy.

Dukes of Milan

Francesco was succeeded by his son Galeazzo Maria (r. 1466–1476), whose extravagance and cruelty ended in his assassination; by the boy Gian Galeazzo; and effectively by Gian Galeazzo's uncle Ludovico "il Moro", who usurped the duchy. Ludovico patronised Leonardo da Vinci and Bramante, but his invitation to Charles VIII of France to invade Italy in 1494 opened the Italian Wars, and he died a French prisoner in 1508. His sons Massimiliano and Francesco II were briefly restored during the wars; on Francesco II's death in 1535 Milan passed to Habsburg Spain.

Other branches

Other Sforza lines ruled Pesaro and Santa Fiora; Caterina Sforza, countess of Forlì, became famous for her defiance of Cesare Borgia. The family name survives in the 20th-century statesman Count Carlo Sforza, an Italian foreign minister.
