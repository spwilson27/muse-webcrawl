Numa Pompilius

Numa Pompilius (c. 753–673 BC; reigned 715–673 BC) was the legendary second king of Rome, succeeding Romulus. A Sabine from the town of Cures, famed for his wisdom and piety, Numa is credited in Roman tradition with establishing most of Rome's religious institutions, its priesthoods, and its calendar — the peaceful counterpart to the warlike founder Romulus.

Reign

According to Livy and Plutarch, the Roman senate chose Numa after an interregnum; he at first refused the crown and accepted only after omens proved favourable. He is said to have founded the priesthoods of the pontifices, flamines, Vestal Virgins, and the Salii, to have built the Temple of Janus (whose doors stood open in war and closed in peace — under Numa they remained closed), and to have divided the year into twelve lunar months, adding January and February to Romulus's ten-month calendar.

Legend

Tradition held that Numa received his religious laws from the nymph Egeria, whom he met in a sacred grove, and that he was a pupil or contemporary of the Greek philosopher Pythagoras — a chronological impossibility that Roman authors themselves debated. After his death, his religious books were said to have been buried with him; when they were unearthed centuries later, the Senate ordered the "Pythagorean" volumes burned as dangerous to established religion, a story reported by Livy.
