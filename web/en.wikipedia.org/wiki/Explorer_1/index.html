Explorer 1

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Explorer 1 is celebrated after its launch, February 1958.

Explorer 1 was the first satellite launched by the United States, and the third artificial satellite overall, following Sputnik 1 and 2. It was launched on 1 February 1958 (UTC) from Cape Canaveral by a Juno I rocket developed by Wernher von Braun's Army team, in America's answer to the Sputnik shock.

The pencil-shaped, 14 kg satellite carried a cosmic-ray detector designed by James Van Allen. Its readings revealed zones where counts dropped unexpectedly — evidence, once understood, of intense radiation trapped in Earth's magnetic field: the Van Allen radiation belts, the first major scientific discovery of the space age. Explorer 1 transmitted for about four months and stayed in orbit until 1970. The success led directly to the creation of NASA later in 1958.
