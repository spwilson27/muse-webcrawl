Rules of baseball

The rules of baseball differ slightly from league to league, but in general share the same basic game play. The official rules are codified in the Official Baseball Rules published by Major League Baseball, from which most other rulebooks derive.

Gameplay

A baseball game is played between two teams of nine players each on a diamond-shaped field. The batting team attempts to score runs by hitting a ball thrown by the pitcher and circling a series of four bases: first, second, third, and home plate. The fielding team tries to record three outs per half-inning by striking out the batter, catching a batted ball in the air, tagging a runner, or forcing a runner out at a base. A game normally lasts nine innings; the team with more runs after both teams have batted an equal number of times wins.

The field

The infield is a square with bases 90 feet apart in professional play. The pitcher's mound sits 60 feet 6 inches from home plate. Beyond the infield lies the outfield, enclosed by a fence whose distance varies by ballpark.

Key rules

A pitch crossing the strike zone that is not hit, or swung at and missed, is a strike; three strikes retire the batter. A pitch outside the zone not swung at is a ball; four balls award the batter first base (a walk). Foul balls count as strikes except that a batter cannot strike out on a foul ball unless it is a bunt or caught on a tip.
