Compressor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A reciprocating air compressor with receiver tank

A compressor is a mechanical device that increases the pressure of a gas by reducing its volume. An air compressor, specifically, converts power (using an electric motor, diesel or gasoline engine, etc.) into potential energy stored in pressurized air (i.e., compressed air). Compressors are used throughout industry to supply compressed air for tools and processes, to move gases through pipelines, in refrigeration and air-conditioning systems, and in the compressor stages of gas turbine engines.

Types

Positive displacement

  • Reciprocating compressors — pistons driven by a crankshaft compress air in cylinders; common from small workshop units to large industrial machines.
  • Rotary screw compressors — two meshing helical rotors trap and compress air continuously; widely used for industrial air supply.
  • Rotary vane compressors — an eccentric rotor with sliding vanes.
  • Scroll compressors — two interleaved spiral scrolls; common in air conditioning.
  • Diaphragm compressors — a flexible membrane isolates the gas, used for high-purity or hazardous gases.

Dynamic (turbocompressors)

  • Centrifugal compressors — a rotating impeller accelerates gas radially outward into a diffuser.
  • Axial compressors — rows of aerofoil blades accelerate gas along the axis; used in jet engines and large industrial service.

Thermodynamics

Compressing a gas raises its temperature; real machines are compared against the ideal limits of isothermal compression (slow, with cooling) and adiabatic (isentropic) compression (no heat transfer). Multi-stage compressors split the pressure rise across stages, often with intercoolers between them, to approach isothermal behaviour and improve efficiency. Compressors are rated by discharge pressure, free air delivery (flow), and power input.

Applications

Compressed air drives pneumatic tools, spray painting, and factory automation; compressors charge breathing-air and scuba cylinders; refrigeration compressors circulate refrigerant in fridges, freezers, and heat pumps; large compressors boost natural gas in pipelines; and turbochargers and superchargers are compressors applied to internal combustion engines.
