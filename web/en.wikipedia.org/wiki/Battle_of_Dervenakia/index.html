Battle of Dervenakia

The Battle of Dervenakia (Greek: Μάχη των Δερβενακίων) was fought on 26–28 July 1822 (Old Style) during the Greek War of Independence. Greek forces under Theodoros Kolokotronis ambushed and destroyed the Ottoman army of Mahmud Dramali Pasha in the passes of Dervenakia, near Nemea in the Peloponnese. It was one of the most decisive Greek victories of the revolution.

Background

In the summer of 1822 the Ottoman commander Dramali Pasha marched south from Larissa with a large army — often estimated at 20,000–30,000 men — to crush the Greek revolt. He occupied Corinth and advanced through the Argolid, burning Argos, but failed to take the citadel of Larissa of Argos or to secure his supplies. Lacking provisions and harassed by scorched-earth tactics, Dramali decided to retreat to Corinth through the Dervenakia pass.

The battle

Kolokotronis had anticipated the move and placed Greek irregulars on the heights along the narrow defile, at Dervenakia and the neighbouring pass of Ayios Sostis. As the Ottoman columns entered the pass on 26 July, Greek fighters opened fire from the slopes and rolled rocks onto the trapped troops. Further attacks on 28 July completed the destruction; much of Dramali's army, baggage, artillery and treasury were lost. Dramali escaped to Corinth, where he died a few months later.

Aftermath

The victory preserved the Greek revolution at a critical moment and made Kolokotronis its most celebrated commander. The booty — including thousands of weapons and the Ottoman war chest — significantly strengthened the Greek cause, although civil conflict among the Greeks soon followed.
