Starfish Prime

Starfish Prime was a high-altitude nuclear test conducted by the United States on July 9, 1962, as part of Operation Dominic and its sub-series Operation Fishbowl. A Thor rocket launched from Johnston Island in the Pacific carried a W49 thermonuclear warhead to an altitude of about 400 kilometres (250 miles), where it detonated with a yield of approximately 1.4 megatons. It remains one of the largest nuclear explosions in outer space.

Effects

The detonation produced a brilliant flash visible across the Pacific and an artificial aurora that lingered for minutes. The resulting electromagnetic pulse (EMP), far stronger than expected, damaged electrical equipment in Hawaii, some 1,445 kilometres (900 miles) away — streetlights failed, burglar alarms were triggered, and a telephone company's microwave link was knocked out. High-energy electrons from the burst were trapped by the Earth's magnetic field, forming an artificial radiation belt that persisted for years and contributed to the failure of several satellites, including the British Ariel 1 and the American Telstar 1.

Context and aftermath

Starfish Prime followed the earlier Argus tests of 1958, which had explored the idea of artificial radiation belts (the "Christofilos effect"). The test, together with Soviet high-altitude tests later in 1962, heightened concern about nuclear explosions in space and contributed to the momentum behind the Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty of 1963, which prohibited nuclear detonations in the atmosphere, outer space, and underwater.
