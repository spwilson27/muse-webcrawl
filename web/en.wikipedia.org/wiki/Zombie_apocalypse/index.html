Zombie apocalypse

Zombie apocalypse is a genre of fiction in which society collapses due to some kind of "zombie" pandemic. Zombies in this genre are typically slow, lumbering and instinct-driven. They usually arise due to some kind of scientific accident or contamination in which primarily humans are transformed or killed and reanimated as zombies.

Genre conventions

Typically, a zombie outbreak is spread through bites or bodily fluids, and survivors must band together, scavenge supplies, and defend themselves both against the undead and against hostile human groups. The collapse of government, law enforcement, and infrastructure is a central theme, as is the moral breakdown of society under pressure.

Notable works

The modern genre was shaped by George A. Romero's film Night of the Living Dead (1968) and its sequels. Later landmarks include the comic and television series The Walking Dead, the novel World War Z by Max Brooks, the film 28 Days Later, and video game series such as Resident Evil and The Last of Us.

Preparedness

The scenario is also used as a metaphor in public preparedness campaigns; in 2011 the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention published a tongue-in-cheek "Zombie Preparedness" guide to promote general emergency readiness.
