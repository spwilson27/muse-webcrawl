Glenn Edward "Bo" Schembechler (1929–2006) was an American college football player and coach. He served as head coach at Miami University (1963–1968) and, most famously, at the University of Michigan (1969–1989), where he became the winningest coach in school history and a defining figure of the Michigan–Ohio State rivalry.

Michigan career

Schembechler, a protégé of Woody Hayes, arrived at Michigan in 1969 and in his first season upset Hayes's top-ranked Ohio State team 24–12, launching the "Ten Year War" between the two programmes. His Wolverines emphasised a physical running game, strong defence, and his mantra that "those who stay will be champions". Michigan won or shared 13 Big Ten championships under him and appeared in ten Rose Bowls, though bowl victories proved elusive (2–8 in bowl games) and he never won a national championship.

Later life and legacy

He also served as Michigan's athletic director (1988–1990) and briefly as president of the Detroit Tigers. Schembechler died on 17 November 2006, the day before a much-anticipated No. 1 vs No. 2 meeting between Ohio State and Michigan. Michigan's football building, Schembechler Hall, bears his name. His legacy was later complicated by revelations that a university doctor abused players during his tenure and that complaints reached the coaching staff.
