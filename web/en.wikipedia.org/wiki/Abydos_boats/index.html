The Abydos boats are a group of ancient Egyptian vessels buried in boat graves at Abydos, dated to around 3000 BC. They are among the oldest surviving planked boats in the world and were interred beside a royal funerary enclosure, apparently to serve the king in the afterlife.

Discovery and excavation

The boat graves were identified in 1991 during excavations by the Pennsylvania–Yale–NYU expedition, with one boat excavated in detail in 2000 under the direction of David O'Connor. Fourteen mudbrick-lined graves, each shaped like a boat, were found arranged in a row. The wooden hulls had largely decayed, leaving impressions and fragments of planking preserved in the sand.

Construction

The vessels were built of planks joined edge to edge with mortise-and-tenon-like fastenings and lashings — a "sewn" construction without a keel in the modern sense. The best-studied hull was about 23 metres (75 feet) long and made of planks roughly 7.5 cm thick, coated inside and out with a yellow pigment and caulked with reeds and papyrus.

Significance

The boats demonstrate that Egyptian shipwrights had mastered planked hull construction a century or more before the better-known Khufu ship at Giza. Their burial beside a mortuary enclosure links them to the long tradition of solar and funerary barques in Egyptian religion.
