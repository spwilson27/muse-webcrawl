Seiwa Genji

The Seiwa Genji (清和源氏) was the most successful and powerful line of the Japanese Minamoto clan (Genji), descended from Emperor Seiwa (reigned 858–876). Many of the most famous Minamoto warriors, including Minamoto no Yoritomo, founder of the Kamakura shogunate, belonged to this line.

Origins

Emperor Seiwa's descendants were granted the surname Minamoto and removed from the imperial succession, a common practice for surplus imperial princes. The line began with Seiwa's grandson Minamoto no Tsunemoto (894–961), who received the name from Emperor Daigo. Tsunemoto's descendants settled in Settsu Province and established themselves as military aristocrats serving the court.

Rise to power

The Seiwa Genji built their reputation suppressing rebellions in the north of Honshū. Minamoto no Yoriyoshi fought in the Earlier Nine Years' War (1051–1062), and his son Minamoto no Yoshiie became the archetypal samurai hero. Later generations, based in the Kantō, intervened in the succession struggles of the twelfth century: the Hōgen (1156) and Heiji (1160) rebellions, and finally the Genpei War (1180–1185), in which Minamoto no Yoritomo destroyed the rival Taira clan.

Shogunal lineages

Because Yoritomo founded the first shogunate, descent from the Seiwa Genji became the customary qualification for the office of shōgun. The Ashikaga clan, which founded the Muromachi shogunate, and the Tokugawa clan, which founded the Edo shogunate (claiming descent through the Nitta branch), both traced their ancestry to the Seiwa Genji.
