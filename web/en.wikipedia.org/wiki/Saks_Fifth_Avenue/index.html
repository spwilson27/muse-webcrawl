Saks Fifth Avenue is an American chain of luxury department stores, founded by Andrew Saks in 1867 in Washington, D.C. Its flagship store, opened in 1924, occupies a landmark building at 611 Fifth Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, across from Rockefeller Center and St. Patrick's Cathedral. The chain is known for high-end designer fashion, cosmetics and accessories, and for its annual Christmas window displays and light show projected on the flagship's facade.

History

Andrew Saks opened his first store in Washington, D.C. in 1867 and expanded to New York in 1902 with a store in Herald Square. After Saks merged with the Gimbels department store group, the Fifth Avenue flagship was built and opened in 1924 under president Horace Saks. Trophy locations followed in cities such as Beverly Hills and Chicago. Saks expanded as a national luxury chain through most of the 20th century, later experimenting with the off-price Saks Off 5th format, launched in 1990.

The company passed through several owners, including Investcorp and the private equity firm NRDC. In 2013, Hudson's Bay Company (HBC) acquired Saks for approximately US$2.9 billion. In 2021 HBC spun off the e-commerce business as a separate company, Saks.com. In 2024, HBC announced the acquisition of Neiman Marcus Group, combining Saks, Bergdorf Goodman and Neiman Marcus under a new entity, Saks Global.

Flagship store

The ten-storey flagship, designed in a restrained classical style, is a designated New York City landmark. Its holiday light show, introduced in the 2000s, became a major seasonal attraction on Fifth Avenue; the show was discontinued in 2024 amid the corporate restructuring.
