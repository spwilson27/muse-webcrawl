Correspondence chess

Correspondence chess is chess played by various forms of long-distance correspondence, traditionally through the postal system but today usually via email or dedicated correspondence chess servers. Players have days to consider each move, and games can last months or years. The international governing body is the International Correspondence Chess Federation (ICCF).

Overview

Governing body: ICCF

Time per move: Typically days

Media: Post, email, web servers

History

Games between cities are recorded from the nineteenth century, and postal chess grew into organised national and world championships. The ICCF, founded in 1951, awards its own grandmaster titles and runs the Correspondence Chess World Championship. Famous correspondence players include the world champion Jonathan Penrose's contemporaries and, in the server era, grandmasters such as Joop van Oosterom.

Computer assistance

Unlike over-the-board chess, most correspondence organisations permit the use of chess engines, which has pushed elite correspondence play toward extremely high draw rates and made opening research and endgame tablebases central to success.
