NASA Deep Space Network

Operator: Jet Propulsion Laboratory for NASA
Stations: Goldstone (California), Madrid (Spain), Canberra (Australia)
Established: 1963
Largest antenna: 70 m dish at each complex

The NASA Deep Space Network (DSN) is a worldwide network of large antennas and communication facilities that supports interplanetary spacecraft missions, radio and radar astronomy observations, and selected Earth-orbiting missions. It is operated by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

Stations

The DSN consists of three deep-space communication complexes placed approximately 120 degrees apart around the globe — at Goldstone in California's Mojave Desert, near Madrid in Spain, and near Canberra in Australia — so that a spacecraft is always in view of at least one station as Earth rotates. Each complex has one 70-metre antenna and several 34-metre antennas.

Operations

The network communicates with dozens of spacecraft, including the Voyager probes at the edge of interstellar space, whose signals arrive at less than a billionth of a trillionth of a watt. The DSN also performs radio science, planetary radar of near-Earth asteroids, and precise spacecraft navigation tracking. Growing demand from lunar and Mars missions has led to upgrades including new 34-metre antennas and optical (laser) communications experiments.
