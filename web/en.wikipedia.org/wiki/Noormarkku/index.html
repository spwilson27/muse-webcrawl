Noormarkku (Swedish: Norrmark) is a former municipality of Finland, located in the Satakunta region in the southwest of the country. It was consolidated with the city of Pori on 1 January 2010. Before the merger the municipality had a population of roughly 6,000 and covered an area of about 332 square kilometres, much of it forest and agricultural land.

History and industry

Noormarkku is best known as the home of the Ahlström industrial family and the A. Ahlström company. The Noormarkku ironworks area (ruukki), founded in the 19th century, grew around sawmilling, iron production, and later the forest industry, and the works community created by the Ahlströms includes notable architecture.

Architecture

The area is famous for Villa Mairea (1938–39), designed by Alvar Aalto for Maire and Harry Gullichsen and considered one of the masterpieces of modernist residential architecture. The Ahlström company's head office and guest facilities, including the Noormarkku Club, remain in the historic works area, which also hosts exhibitions and cultural events.
