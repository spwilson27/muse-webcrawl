The Swiss National Bank (SNB; German: Schweizerische Nationalbank) is the central bank of Switzerland, responsible for the nation's monetary policy and the sole issuer of Swiss franc banknotes. Unusually for a central bank, it is a publicly listed joint-stock company, with about 55 per cent of its shares held by cantons and cantonal banks.

History

The SNB was founded under the National Bank Act and began operations in 1907, taking over note issuance from dozens of cantonal banks. It maintains historic offices in both Bern and Zurich.

Monetary policy

The Bank's mandate is to ensure price stability while taking economic developments into account. From September 2011 to January 2015 it enforced a minimum exchange rate of CHF 1.20 per euro to counter franc appreciation during the European debt crisis; the abrupt abandonment of the floor on 15 January 2015 caused sharp market turbulence. The SNB also held its policy rate in negative territory (down to −0.75 per cent) from 2015 to 2022.

Reserves and profit

The SNB holds one of the world's largest balance sheets relative to GDP, including large foreign currency investments and about 1,040 tonnes of gold. Its profits, split between the Confederation and the cantons, can be very large or deeply negative depending on market movements.
