Wernigerode

Wernigerode is a town in the district of Harz, Saxony-Anhalt, Germany, situated on the northern slopes of the Harz mountains. Nicknamed the "colourful town in the Harz" (die bunte Stadt am Harz), it is noted for its remarkably preserved half-timbered old town and its castle, and has a population of roughly 32,000.

History

First mentioned in 1121, Wernigerode received town rights in 1229. It was the seat of the counts of Wernigerode and later of the counts of Stolberg-Wernigerode. The medieval town hall, begun in 1277 and adorned with carved wooden figures, is among the finest in Germany. From 1949 to 1990 the town lay in East Germany, close to the inner German border.

Sights and tourism

Wernigerode Castle, rebuilt in neo-Gothic style in the 19th century, overlooks the town and houses a museum. The town is the eastern terminus of the Harz Narrow Gauge Railways, whose steam trains climb to the summit of the Brocken, the highest peak in northern Germany. Streets such as the Breite Straße preserve rows of richly decorated timber-framed houses dating from the 15th to 19th centuries.
