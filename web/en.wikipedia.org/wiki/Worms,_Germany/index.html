Worms (German pronunciation: [vɔʁms]) is a city in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, situated on the Upper Rhine about 60 kilometres south-southwest of Frankfurt am Main. It is one of the oldest cities in northern Europe, founded by the Celts as Borbetomagus and later a Roman garrison town. Worms was an important city in the Holy Roman Empire and is famous as the setting of the medieval epic the Nibelungenlied.

History

Worms was the capital of the Kingdom of the Burgundians in the early 5th century, an episode reflected in the Nibelungen saga. The Romanesque Worms Cathedral (Wormser Dom), consecrated in 1110 and completed in the 12th–13th centuries, is one of the great imperial cathedrals of the Rhine. In 1122 the Concordat of Worms ended the Investiture Controversy between pope and emperor.

The city is best known abroad for the Diet of Worms of 1521, at which Martin Luther refused to recant his writings before Emperor Charles V and was declared an outlaw by the Edict of Worms, a defining moment of the Protestant Reformation. Worms also preserves one of Europe's oldest Jewish cemeteries, the Heiliger Sand, and the 11th-century Rashi synagogue; together with Speyer and Mainz it forms the ShUM cities recognised by UNESCO in 2021 for their medieval Jewish heritage.

Culture

The city celebrates the annual Backfischfest wine festival and stages the Nibelungen Festival, an open-air theatre production in front of the cathedral. The Liebfrauenkirche gave its name to the Liebfraumilch style of white wine.
