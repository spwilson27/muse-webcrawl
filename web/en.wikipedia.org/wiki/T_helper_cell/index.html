The T helper cells (TH cells), also known as CD4+ cells, are a type of T cell that play an important role in the adaptive immune system. They aid the activity of other immune cells by releasing cytokines. Virtually all other effector arms of adaptive immunity depend on helper T cell input: they help activate B cells to secrete antibodies, recruit and activate macrophages, and assist in the activation of cytotoxic T cells.

Helper T cells express the CD4 glycoprotein on their surface, which binds MHC class II molecules on antigen-presenting cells. They are the target cells of HIV; the progressive loss of CD4+ cells is the hallmark of untreated HIV infection and AIDS.

Activation

A naïve helper T cell is activated when its T-cell receptor recognises a peptide antigen displayed by MHC class II on a professional antigen-presenting cell (a dendritic cell, macrophage or B cell), together with co-stimulation through CD28. The activated cell secretes interleukin-2, proliferates, and differentiates into one of several effector subsets, with the local cytokine environment determining which.

Effector subsets

Th1 cells

Driven by interleukin-12 and the transcription factor T-bet, Th1 cells secrete interferon-γ, which activates macrophages and supports immunity against intracellular bacteria and viruses. Excessive Th1 activity is implicated in several organ-specific autoimmune diseases.

Th2 cells

Th2 cells, directed by the transcription factor GATA3, produce IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13. They promote IgE production, eosinophil recruitment and expulsion of helminth parasites, and they drive allergic responses and asthma when overactive.

Th17 cells

Th17 cells produce IL-17 and IL-22, recruiting neutrophils and defending mucosal surfaces against extracellular bacteria and fungi. They are strongly implicated in autoimmune conditions such as psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis and multiple sclerosis.

T follicular helper cells

Tfh cells migrate into B-cell follicles of lymphoid organs and, through CD40 ligand and IL-21, drive germinal centre reactions, affinity maturation and the generation of high-affinity antibodies and memory B cells.

Role in disease

HIV infects and destroys CD4+ T cells; a CD4 count below 200 cells/μL defines AIDS. Conversely, overactive helper responses contribute to allergy, asthma and autoimmunity. Because of their coordinating role, helper T cells are central targets and tools in vaccine design.
