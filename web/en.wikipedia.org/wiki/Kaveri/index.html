Kaveri

Kaveri (Cauvery)
Length: about 800 km
Source: Talakaveri, Brahmagiri Hills, Karnataka
Mouth: Bay of Bengal, Tamil Nadu
Basin: about 81,000 km²

The Kaveri (also spelled Cauvery) is a major river of southern India, revered as sacred in Hinduism and personified as the goddess Kaveri Amma. It rises at Talakaveri in the Brahmagiri Hills of the Western Ghats in Karnataka, flows generally south and east for about 800 km across Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and empties into the Bay of Bengal.

Course

From Kodagu district the river passes Srirangapatna and the Shivanasamudra Falls, forms part of the Karnataka–Tamil Nadu border, and descends at Hogenakkal Falls into the plains. At Tiruchirappalli it divides around Srirangam island, and its distributaries spread across a fertile delta — long called the "garden of the south" — before reaching the sea near Poompuhar.

Use and disputes

The Kaveri supports extensive irrigation, the reservoirs at Krishnarajasagara and Mettur, and hydroelectric power. Sharing its waters between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu (with Kerala and Puducherry also parties) has been one of India's most contentious inter-state disputes, addressed by a tribunal award in 2007, a Supreme Court judgment in 2018, and the Cauvery Water Management Authority.
