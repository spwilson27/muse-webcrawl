Kakinomoto no Hitomaro

Bornc. 653/655
Diedc. 707–710
OccupationCourt poet (waka)
EraAsuka period, Japan

Kakinomoto no Hitomaro (柿本人麻呂, c. 653–655 – c. 707–710) was a Japanese waka poet of the late Asuka period and the most prominent poet preserved in the Man'yōshū, the oldest and largest anthology of Japanese poetry, with some 80+ long and short poems attributed to him.

Life

Little is known of his biography. He served as a court poet under Empress Jitō and Emperor Monmu, composing formal elegies and praise poems for imperial princes — including laments for Prince Takechi and Prince Kusakabe — as well as travel poems from postings or journeys to provinces such as Iwami, where tradition holds he died. Details of his family and rank remain obscure.

Poetry and reputation

Hitomaro excelled in the chōka (long poem) form, marked by grand public rhetoric, intricate parallelism, and pillow words, alongside deeply personal tanka on separation from his wife. In the Heian period he was venerated as a saint of poetry (uta no hijiri) and one of the Thirty-six Poetry Immortals; Ki no Tsurayuki's preface to the Kokinshū pairs him with Yamabe no Akahito as the supreme poets of the age.
