Kiss of the Spider Woman (Portuguese: O Beijo da Mulher Aranha) is a 1985 drama film directed by the Argentine-Brazilian filmmaker Héctor Babenco, adapted by Leonard Schrader from Manuel Puig's 1976 novel of the same name. It stars William Hurt, Raul Julia and Sônia Braga.

Plot

In a Brazilian prison during the military dictatorship, two very different men share a cell: Luis Molina, a gay window dresser imprisoned for "corrupting a minor", and Valentín Arregui, a revolutionary journalist tortured by the regime. To pass the time, Molina recounts the plots of old romantic films, including a wartime melodrama about a chanteuse who is secretly a Nazi spy — the "spider woman" who kills with her kiss. Gradually the prisoners' mutual suspicion gives way to intimacy and love. Molina, who has been pressed by the authorities to spy on Valentín, is released on parole and agrees to carry a message for the revolutionaries, with tragic consequences.

Reception and awards

The film premiered in competition at the 1985 Cannes Film Festival, where William Hurt won the Best Actor award. Hurt went on to win the Academy Award for Best Actor, becoming the first actor to win an Oscar for playing an openly gay character. The film was also nominated for Best Picture, Best Director and Best Adapted Screenplay. It was a landmark independent production and is frequently cited among the finest films of the 1980s.

Legacy

The story was later adapted into a Broadway musical (1993) by John Kander, Fred Ebb and Terrence McNally, which won the Tony Award for Best Musical; a film adaptation of the musical, directed by Bill Condon and starring Jennifer Lopez, was released in 2025.
