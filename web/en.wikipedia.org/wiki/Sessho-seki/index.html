The Sessho-seki (殺生石, "Killing Stone") is a stone in the volcanic mountains of Nasu, Tochigi Prefecture, Japan, an area known for its sulfurous hot springs. In Japanese mythology the stone is said to kill anyone who comes into contact with it, and to embody the transformed corpse of Tamamo-no-Mae, a beautiful courtesan who was revealed to be a nine-tailed fox (kitsune) plotting against the Emperor.

Legend

According to the legend, popularised in the Muromachi-period tale Tamamo-no-Mae and later in noh and kabuki, Tamamo-no-Mae was unmasked and slain in the plains of Nasu; her body became the Sessho-seki, exuding poison that killed birds and beasts that approached. The stone was said to have been pacified (or split) by the Buddhist monk Gennō in the 14th century, an episode dramatised in the noh play Sessho-seki.

Modern attention

In March 2022 the stone was found split in two, apparently through natural weathering of a crack noted years earlier. The break attracted widespread attention in Japan and abroad, with local tourism officials noting the coincidence with the folkloric idea that breaking the stone releases the fox spirit; a ritual was later held to pacify it. The surrounding area's volcanic gases are genuinely hazardous, a likely basis for the legend.
