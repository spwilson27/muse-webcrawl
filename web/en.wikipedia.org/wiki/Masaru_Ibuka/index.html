Masaru Ibuka (Japanese: 井深 大; 11 April 1908 – 19 December 1997) was a Japanese electronics engineer and businessman. He was the co-founder, with Akio Morita, of the Sony Corporation, and served as its first president.

Early life and career

Ibuka graduated from Waseda University in 1933 and worked at a film-processing company and then in naval research during the Second World War, developing infrared and electronics systems. In 1946 he and Akio Morita founded the Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo (Tokyo Telecommunications Engineering Corporation) in war-devastated Tokyo, initially repairing radios and building shortwave adapters.

Sony

As the company's chief engineer and visionary, Ibuka drove its defining innovations: Japan's first magnetic tape and tape recorder, the licensing of the transistor from Western Electric, Japan's first transistor radio (1955), and the Trinitron colour television picture tube. The company renamed itself Sony in 1958. Ibuka served as president from 1950 to 1971 and as chairman until 1976.

Ibuka was also deeply interested in early childhood education; his 1971 book Kindergarten Is Too Late! argued that the greatest learning occurs before the age of three, and he helped found an organisation for early development research. He received numerous honours, including the IEEE Founders Medal and Japan's Order of Culture.
