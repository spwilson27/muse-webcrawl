Sorus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A sorus (plural sori) is a cluster of sporangia — structures producing and containing spores — found on the undersides of the fronds of ferns and some other plants. Sori are a defining feature of the leptosporangiate ferns, comprising the great majority of fern species.

Structure

In many species the young sorus is protected by a flap of tissue called an indusium, which shrivels as the sporangia mature. When mature, the sporangia rupture and release spores, which are dispersed by wind. Sori may be round, elongated, or continuous, and their arrangement along veins, margins, or the leaf surface is an important character used in fern identification and taxonomy.

Development

A sorus begins as a receptacle — a raised area of the leaf on which sporangia develop, often in a coordinated sequence from the centre outward (gradate) or all at once (simultaneous). In some ferns, such as the bracken Pteridium, sori form continuous lines along the frond margins protected by the rolled leaf edge, a "false indusium". Other groups, like the tree ferns, bear large round sori on the undersides of their fronds.

Terminology

A cluster of fused or merging sori is called a synsorus, and elongated fused sori along a vein may be termed a coenosorus. The sorus should not be confused with the fruit dots of fungi or the soredia of lichens.
