Carl Linnaeus the Younger

Carl Linnaeus the Younger (Carl von Linné den yngre; 20 January 1741 – 1 November 1783) was a Swedish naturalist, the son of the famous botanist Carl Linnaeus. He succeeded his father as professor of botany at Uppsala University and published the Supplementum Plantarum.

Carl Linnaeus the Younger
Born: 1741, Falun, Sweden
Died: 1783, Uppsala
Field: Botany
Author abbreviation: L.f.

Life

Educated at Uppsala, Linnaeus the Younger was appointed as a demonstrator in the botanical garden while still a teenager and succeeded his father in the chair of botany in 1777. He travelled in Europe in 1781–1783, visiting botanists and collections in England, France, and the Netherlands, but his health failed and he died shortly after returning to Uppsala.

Work

His main work, Supplementum Plantarum Systematis Vegetabilium (1782), described many new plant species using his father's binomial system; his author abbreviation is "L.f." After his death without children, his father's collections were sold to James Edward Smith and taken to London.
