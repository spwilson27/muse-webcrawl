Lê dynasty

The Lê dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Lê) is the name of two Vietnamese dynasties: the Early Lê dynasty (980–1009), founded by Lê Hoàn, and the Later Lê dynasty (1428–1789), the longest-ruling dynasty in Vietnamese history, founded by Lê Lợi.

Early Lê dynasty (980–1009)

The Early Lê dynasty ruled Đại Cồ Việt under three emperors: Lê Đại Hành (Lê Hoàn), Lê Trung Tông and Lê Long Đĩnh. It repelled a Song dynasty invasion and expanded southward at the expense of Champa before being replaced by the Lý dynasty in 1009.

Later Lê dynasty (1428–1789)

Lê Lợi led the Lam Sơn uprising that expelled Ming dynasty occupation forces, founding the Later Lê dynasty in 1428. The dynasty reached its height under Lê Thánh Tông (1460–1497), a major reformer who promulgated the Hồng Đức code and annexed Champa's capital in 1471. In later centuries real power shifted to the rival Trịnh and Nguyễn lords while Lê emperors reigned as figureheads, until the dynasty was ended by the Tây Sơn rebellion in 1789.
