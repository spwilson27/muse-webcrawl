Software Freedom Conservancy

Software Freedom Conservancy, Inc.
Founded: April 7, 2006
Type: 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization
Focus: Free and open-source software
Location: New York City, United States
Key people: Bradley M. Kuhn, Karen Sandler

Software Freedom Conservancy, Inc. is an American non-profit organization founded in 2006 that provides a fiscal and organizational home for free and open-source software (FLOSS) projects. As a fiscal sponsor, the Conservancy holds project assets, accepts donations on their behalf, handles accounting and legal matters, and helps enforce the licenses — especially the GNU General Public License — under which member projects are distributed.

Member projects

Member projects have included BusyBox, Git, Inkscape, Samba, Selenium, Wine, phpMyAdmin, the Outreachy internship program, and dozens of others. The Conservancy hosted the LibrePlanet-adjacent community and, with the Free Software Foundation, published principles for community-oriented GPL enforcement emphasizing education and compliance over litigation.

GPL enforcement

The Conservancy is known for copyleft compliance work. It coordinated enforcement on behalf of BusyBox developers (handled through the Software Freedom Law Center in earlier cases) and, in 2021, funded and supported a consumer-rights lawsuit against Vizio over GPL compliance for its SmartCast TVs — notable for pursuing the case as a third-party beneficiary contract claim on behalf of purchasers. In 2015, the Conservancy supported Christoph Hellwig's GPL suit against VMware in Germany, which ended in 2019 after VMware announced removal of the disputed code. The organization also ran a long campaign regarding the Linux kernel and the "GPL cooperation commitment."

Funding controversies

In 2015 the Linux Foundation ended its funding of the Conservancy after the organization began enforcing the GPL against a Foundation-paid Linux contributor; the episode drew attention to tensions between corporate-sponsored foundations and enforcement-focused charities. The Conservancy relies primarily on individual donations and supporter subscriptions.
