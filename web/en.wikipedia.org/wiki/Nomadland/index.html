Nomadland is a 2020 American drama film written, produced, directed, and edited by Chloé Zhao. It is based on the 2017 non-fiction book by Jessica Bruder about older Americans who, after the Great Recession, adopt transient lifestyles travelling the country in vans and recreational vehicles in search of seasonal work. Frances McDormand stars as Fern, a widow from the collapsed company town of Empire, Nevada, who takes to the road in her van, "Vanguard".

Plot

After the US Gypsum plant in Empire closes and the town is effectively dissolved, Fern sells most of her belongings and buys a van to live in. She works seasonal jobs — at an Amazon fulfilment centre, as a camp host, in beet harvesting and in restaurant kitchens — and joins a community of fellow nomads led by real-life vandweller Bob Wells. Along the way she forms bonds with nomads played largely by non-professional actors portraying versions of themselves (including Linda May, Charlene Swankie, and Bob Wells). Fern repeatedly refuses offers of a settled home, including from a fellow nomad, Dave, choosing instead to return to the road; the film ends with her revisiting the abandoned Empire factory and the storage unit holding her old life.

Production

The film was shot over four months in 2018 across seven states, with a small crew living in vans alongside the cast. McDormand herself worked several of the jobs depicted. Zhao's approach blended scripted narrative with documentary observation of the nomad community.

Reception and awards

Nomadland premiered at the Venice Film Festival in September 2020, winning the Golden Lion. At the 93rd Academy Awards it won Best Picture, Best Director for Zhao, and Best Actress for McDormand. Zhao became the second woman, and the first woman of colour, to win the Academy Award for Best Director. The film was widely praised for its empathy, naturalism, and cinematography of the American West.
