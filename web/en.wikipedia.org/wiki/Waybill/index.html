Waybill

Waybill
TypeTransport document
Issued byCarrier or its agent
RelatedBill of lading, consignment note

A waybill is a document issued by a carrier or its agent giving details and instructions relating to the shipment of a consignment of goods. It typically lists the names of the consignor and consignee, the point of origin and destination, the route, and a description of the freight — its kind, quantity, weight, and dimensions — together with the carriage charges. Unlike a bill of lading, a waybill is not a document of title: the goods are delivered to the named consignee on proof of identity, and the consignment cannot be transferred by endorsing the waybill.

Types

The air waybill (AWB) is used for air freight under the Warsaw and Montreal Conventions; it serves as a receipt of goods, evidence of the contract of carriage, and a customs document. In rail transport, the consignment note under the CIM convention performs a similar role, while road haulage in Europe uses the CMR consignment note. The sea waybill is a non-negotiable alternative to the ocean bill of lading that speeds release of cargo at destination.

House and master waybills

Freight forwarders commonly consolidate shipments: the forwarder issues a house waybill to each individual shipper, while the carrier issues a single master waybill covering the consolidated consignment, naming the forwarder as shipper.

Electronic waybills

Paper waybills are increasingly replaced by electronic equivalents — such as IATA's e-AWB for air cargo — which streamline handling, reduce errors, and allow real-time shipment tracking via the waybill number.
