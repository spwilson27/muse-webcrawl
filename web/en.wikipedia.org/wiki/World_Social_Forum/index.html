World Social Forum

The World Social Forum (WSF; Portuguese: Fórum Social Mundial) is an annual meeting of civil society organisations, social movements, trade unions, and activists who campaign for alternatives to neoliberal globalisation. Its slogan is "Another world is possible" (Um outro mundo é possível). The first forum was held in Porto Alegre, Brazil, in January 2001, deliberately timed to coincide with the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, which its organisers saw as representing the interests of global capital.

History

The forum grew out of the alter-globalisation movement and the protests against the World Trade Organization at Seattle in 1999. Brazilian organisations, notably the Brazilian Association of Non-Governmental Organizations (ABONG) and activists such as Oded Grajew and Chico Whitaker, proposed a counterpart to Davos. The first forum in 2001 drew about 12,000 registered participants; by 2003 the Porto Alegre forum attracted around 100,000 people and coincided with huge demonstrations against the impending invasion of Iraq.

Subsequent world forums were held in Mumbai, India (2004); Caracas, Venezuela (2006, as a polycentric forum also held in Bamako and Karachi); Nairobi, Kenya (2007); Belém, Brazil (2009); Dakar, Senegal (2011); Tunis, Tunisia (2013 and 2015); Montreal, Canada (2016 — the first in the Global North); Salvador, Brazil (2018); Mexico City (2022, largely virtual); and Kathmandu, Nepal (2024). Alongside the global event, regional, national, and thematic social forums are held worldwide.

Charter of Principles

The WSF is governed by a Charter of Principles adopted in 2001. The forum is defined as an open meeting place for reflective thinking and democratic debate, not a deliberative body: no participant may speak in the name of all participants, and the forum does not adopt binding resolutions or manifestos. Political party representatives and armed movements are excluded from participating as such, although government leaders identified with the forum's goals may attend in a personal capacity.

Criticism

The forum has been criticised from several directions: some participants complain that its non-decision-making structure limits its political impact, while others argue that large NGOs dominate its agenda at the expense of grassroots movements. The 2007 Nairobi forum was controversial for high admission fees and corporate sponsorship, which many felt excluded poor Kenyans.
