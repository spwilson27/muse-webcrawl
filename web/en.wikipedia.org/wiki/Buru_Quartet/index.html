The Buru Quartet is a series of four historical novels by the Indonesian author Pramoedya Ananta Toer: This Earth of Mankind, Child of All Nations, Footsteps, and House of Glass. Composed while Pramoedya was a political prisoner on the island of Buru, the novels trace the birth of Indonesian nationalism in the Dutch East Indies at the turn of the 20th century through the life of Minke, a Javanese student and journalist.

Composition

Denied writing materials for much of his imprisonment, Pramoedya first narrated the story orally to fellow prisoners; when paper and typewriters were later permitted, he wrote the novels down. The books were published in Indonesia beginning in 1980 but were soon banned by the Suharto government, remaining prohibited there until after 1998.

Themes

The quartet explores colonial injustice, racial hierarchy, education, journalism, and the awakening of a national consciousness. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest works of Indonesian literature, and translations — notably Max Lane's English versions — brought Pramoedya international recognition.
