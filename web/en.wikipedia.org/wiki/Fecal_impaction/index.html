Fecal impaction

A fecal impaction is a large, hardened mass of stool lodged in the rectum or colon.

A fecal impaction is a solid, immobile bulk of feces that can develop in the rectum as a result of chronic constipation. It is particularly common in elderly people, those who are bedridden, and patients with neuropsychiatric disorders. A fecal impaction is a type and form of constipation in which a hard mass of stool becomes stuck and cannot be expelled.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include chronic constipation, abdominal pain and bloating, a palpable abdominal mass, and paradoxical watery diarrhoea as liquid stool leaks around the obstruction (overflow incontinence). Complications can include stercoral ulceration of the bowel wall, intestinal obstruction, urinary retention, and in severe cases bowel perforation.

Causes

Risk factors include immobility, dehydration, low-fibre diet, neurological conditions such as Parkinson's disease and dementia, and constipating medications including opioids and anticholinergics. Ignoring the urge to defecate and chronic laxative misuse may also contribute.

Treatment

Treatment involves softening and removing the impacted mass, using oral or rectal laxatives, enemas, suppositories, and — where these fail — careful manual disimpaction by a clinician. Long-term management addresses the underlying constipation with hydration, dietary fibre, mobility, and regular laxative regimens, and reviews constipating medication.

Prevention

In care homes and hospitals, bowel charts, scheduled toileting, adequate fluids, and prompt treatment of constipation are used to prevent impaction in at-risk patients.
