Westminster Standards

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Westminster Standards is the collective name for the doctrinal and liturgical documents produced by the Westminster Assembly in the 1640s, which together form the subordinate standards of faith for many Presbyterian and Reformed churches.

Documents

The Standards comprise the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646), the Westminster Larger Catechism and the Westminster Shorter Catechism (1647), the Directory for Public Worship (1645), the Form of Presbyterial Church-Government, and related documents such as the Directory for Family-Worship and the Sum of Saving Knowledge.

The Church of Scotland adopted the Confession in 1647 as its confession of faith, replacing the Scots Confession of 1560. In England and Ireland the standards shaped Presbyterian identity, and through missionary activity they became normative in Presbyterian churches in North America, Korea, Australia and elsewhere. Some denominations have since adopted revised or additional confessional documents, while others continue to require subscription to the Westminster Standards in whole or in part.
