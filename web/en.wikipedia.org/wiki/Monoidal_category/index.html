Monoidal category

In mathematics, a monoidal category (or tensor category) is a category C equipped with a bifunctor ⊗ : C × C → C, called the tensor product or monoidal product, an object I called the unit object or identity object, and three natural isomorphisms: the associator, the left unitor and the right unitor. These isomorphisms are subject to coherence conditions, including Mac Lane's pentagon identity and triangle identity, which guarantee that all diagrams built from them commute.

Examples

Familiar examples include the category of vector spaces over a field with the tensor product of vector spaces and the one-dimensional space as unit; the category of sets with the cartesian product; the category of endofunctors on a category with functor composition; and any cartesian category. The category of modules over a commutative ring is monoidal under tensor product.

Variants

A braided monoidal category adds a natural isomorphism swapping the order of tensor factors, and a symmetric monoidal category is braided with the braiding equal to its own inverse, as in the category of sets. A monoidal category in which the associator and unitors are identities is called strict; Mac Lane's coherence theorem states that every monoidal category is monoidally equivalent to a strict one. Closed monoidal categories have internal hom functors, and compact closed categories play an important role in categorical quantum mechanics.

Applications

Monoidal categories provide the algebraic framework for tensor products throughout mathematics and physics, underpin string diagrams in category theory, and appear in topological quantum field theory, logic (through linear logic) and the semantics of programming languages.
