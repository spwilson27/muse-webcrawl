Place (United States Census Bureau)

In the geography of the United States Census Bureau, a place is a concentration of population with a name, defined boundaries, and a degree of local recognition. The Census Bureau distinguishes two kinds: incorporated places and census-designated places (CDPs).

Incorporated places

An incorporated place is a community legally chartered by its state as a city, town, village, borough, or similar municipal corporation, providing local government services. Incorporated places have legally defined boundaries fixed by state law.

Census-designated places

A census-designated place is a settled, named community that lacks its own municipal government. The Census Bureau delineates CDP boundaries, in cooperation with local officials, purely for statistical purposes, so that data can be published for recognisable communities such as resort towns, unincorporated suburbs, and retirement communities. CDP boundaries may change between censuses and have no legal status.

Statistics

The Census Bureau publishes decennial census and American Community Survey data for all places, and tracks them with FIPS place codes.
