Poldark

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Poldark is a series of historical novels by Winston Graham, published from 1945 to 2002. The series comprises twelve novels: the first seven are set in the 18th century, concluding in Christmas 1799; the remaining five are concerned with the early years of the 19th century and the lives of the descendants of the previous novels' main characters. The protagonist, Ross Poldark, is a Cornish landowner and former British Army officer who returns from the American War of Independence to find his father dead, his estate in ruins and his sweetheart Elizabeth engaged to his cousin Francis.

Plot and themes

Ross Poldark rescues a young urchin, Demelza Carne, from a beating at a fair and takes her into his household as a kitchen maid; they later marry, and Demelza becomes the emotional centre of the series. The novels follow Ross's struggles to revive his family's tin and copper mines, his rivalry with the unscrupulous banker George Warleggan, the hardships of the Cornish mining community, and the wider backdrop of the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars. Graham drew extensively on Cornish history, mining practice and smuggling lore; the fictional parish of Sawle and the Poldark estate of Nampara are set on the north Cornish coast.

Adaptations

The BBC adapted the novels twice. The 1975–1977 television series starred Robin Ellis as Ross and Angharad Rees as Demelza and was a major success. A second adaptation, written by Debbie Horsfield, ran on BBC One from 2015 to 2019, starring Aidan Turner as Ross Poldark and Eleanor Tomlinson as Demelza, and was credited with a significant boost to Cornish tourism — the so-called "Poldark effect".
