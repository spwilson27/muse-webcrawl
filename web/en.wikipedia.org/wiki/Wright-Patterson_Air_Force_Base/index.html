Wright-Patterson Air Force Base (WPAFB) is a United States Air Force base and census-designated place near Dayton, Ohio, in Greene and Montgomery counties. It is the headquarters of Air Force Materiel Command and one of the largest and most organisationally complex bases in the Air Force, employing tens of thousands of military, civilian and contractor personnel.

History

The base combines two historic airfields: Wright Field, named for Wilbur and Orville Wright, and Patterson Field, named for Lieutenant Frank Stuart Patterson, a test pilot killed in 1918. The Wright brothers had earlier conducted flying experiments at nearby Huffman Prairie, now within the base perimeter, after their first flights at Kitty Hawk. The two fields were administratively merged in 1945 and formally united as Wright-Patterson AFB in 1948.

Units and attractions

WPAFB hosts the Air Force Research Laboratory, the National Air and Space Intelligence Center, the 88th Air Base Wing and Air Force Materiel Command headquarters. It is also home to the National Museum of the United States Air Force, the world's largest and oldest military aviation museum, which is free and open to the public.
