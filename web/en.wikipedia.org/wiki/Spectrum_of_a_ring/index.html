Spectrum of a ring

In commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, the spectrum of a commutative ring R, denoted Spec(R), is the set of all prime ideals of R. It is equipped with a topology, the Zariski topology, and a sheaf of rings, making it a locally ringed space; such spaces are the affine schemes, the basic building blocks of modern algebraic geometry as developed by Alexander Grothendieck.

Zariski topology

For an ideal I of R, let V(I) be the set of all prime ideals containing I. The closed sets of the Zariski topology on Spec(R) are exactly the sets V(I). Basic open sets are the distinguished opens D(f) = Spec(R) ∖ V(f) for elements f ∈ R. The topology is quasi-compact but almost never Hausdorff: a point corresponding to a prime ideal 𝔭 has closure equal to V(𝔭), so closed points are precisely the maximal ideals, while non-maximal prime ideals are generic points of the closed subsets they define.

Structure sheaf

The structure sheaf 𝒪 assigns to each distinguished open D(f) the localisation Rf. The stalk of the sheaf at a point 𝔭 is the local ring R𝔭, and the global sections recover the ring itself: Γ(Spec(R), 𝒪) = R. Elements of R can thus be regarded as functions on Spec(R), taking values in the residue fields at each point — the essential geometric viewpoint of scheme theory.

Examples

  • Spec(Z) consists of the zero ideal, a generic point whose closure is the whole space, together with one closed point for each prime number p, corresponding to the ideal (p).
  • Spec(k[x]) for a field k is the affine line over k: its closed points correspond to monic irreducible polynomials, plus the generic point (0).
  • Spec(k) for a field is a single point.

Functoriality

A ring homomorphism R → S induces a continuous map Spec(S) → Spec(R) by taking contractions of prime ideals, making Spec a contravariant functor from commutative rings to locally ringed spaces. Its image is exactly the category of affine schemes, establishing an equivalence between the algebra of rings and the geometry of affine schemes.
