Emperor Kazan (花山天皇, Kazan Tennō, 29 November 968 – 17 March 1008) was the 65th emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. He reigned briefly, from 984 to 986, during the Heian period, before abdicating and taking Buddhist vows — one of the most famous imperial retirements into religious life in Japanese history.

Life and reign

Kazan was the eldest son of Emperor Reizei and was made crown prince after the accession of his uncle, Emperor En'yū. He ascended the throne at about sixteen on En'yū's abdication in 984. His short reign was dominated by the Fujiwara regents; the powerful courtier Fujiwara no Kaneie manoeuvred to place his own grandson — the future Emperor Ichijō — on the throne.

Abdication

In 986, grieving the death of his favourite consort, Kazan was persuaded by the monk Kannen — and, according to tradition, manipulated by Fujiwara no Michikane — to renounce the world. He secretly left the palace at night and took the tonsure at Gangyō-ji temple, becoming a Buddhist monk. Michikane, who had promised to join him, slipped away, leaving the former emperor committed to the religious life; the anecdote became famous through the Ōkagami and other court tales. The child Ichijō succeeded him, with Kaneie as regent, strengthening Fujiwara dominance.

Later life

As the monk Nyokai, Kazan devoted himself to pilgrimage. He is traditionally credited with reviving the Saigoku Kannon Pilgrimage, the circuit of thirty-three temples dedicated to Kannon in the Kansai region, modelled on a pilgrimage he is said to have undertaken after a vision of the bodhisattva. He died in 1008 at the age of 39. His tomb is known as Kamiya no hotori no misasagi in Kyoto.
