Language policy

Language policy is the set of laws, regulations and practices by which states and institutions govern which languages are used in education, government, media and public life, and in what forms.

FieldSociolinguistics, public policy
ComponentsStatus, corpus and acquisition planning

Types of planning

Analysts distinguish status planning (the functions assigned to a language, such as official-language designation), corpus planning (standardising script, spelling and terminology, as by the Académie française) and acquisition planning (decisions about teaching languages in schools). Policies may promote multilingualism, as in Switzerland, India or South Africa, or assimilation to a single national language.

Debates

Language policy intersects with minority rights, decolonisation and language revitalisation. Controversial cases include Quebec's Charter of the French Language, Catalonia's immersion schooling, and post-Soviet states' promotion of titular languages. Critics note that officially neutral policies can entrench dominant-language privilege, while supporters emphasise cohesion and access to services.
