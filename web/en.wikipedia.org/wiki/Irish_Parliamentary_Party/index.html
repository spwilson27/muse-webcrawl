Irish Parliamentary Party

Founded1882 (from Home Rule League)
Dissolved1921
LeadersParnell, John Redmond, John Dillon
IdeologyIrish nationalism, Home Rule

The Irish Parliamentary Party (IPP) was formed in 1882 by Charles Stewart Parnell from the Home Rule League, as the parliamentary party of Irish nationalism at Westminster. For four decades it was the dominant voice of nationalist Ireland and the engine of the Home Rule movement.

Under Parnell

Parnell imposed strict discipline — members pledged to vote as a bloc — and used obstruction and balance-of-power politics to force Ireland onto the agenda. The party won around 85 seats in 1885 and allied with Gladstone. The 1890 O'Shea divorce scandal split the party into Parnellite and anti-Parnellite factions; Parnell died in 1891.

Reunification and Redmond

The factions reunited in 1900 under John Redmond. Holding the balance of power after the 1910 elections, the IPP secured the Parliament Act 1911 and the Third Home Rule Bill, enacted in 1914 but suspended for the First World War. Redmond's support for Irish recruitment proved costly.

Decline

The Easter Rising, the 1918 conscription crisis and the rise of Sinn Féin destroyed the party: in the December 1918 election it fell from 73 seats to six. Its last leader was John Dillon. In the south its constitutional nationalist tradition later fed into parties of the Irish Free State.
