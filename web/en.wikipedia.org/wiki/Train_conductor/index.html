Train conductor

A conductor (in North America) or guard (in the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries) is a train crew member responsible for the safe and efficient operation of the train and for the safety of its passengers and cargo. On North American railroads, the conductor is in charge of the train and its crew, while the engineer (driver) operates the locomotive under the conductor's direction.

Duties

The conductor's duties include ensuring the train operates according to the timetable and operating rules, supervising switching and the making up of trains, inspecting equipment, handling documentation such as waybills, communicating with dispatchers, and coordinating with the engineer. On passenger trains, conductors also collect or check tickets, make announcements, operate doors, and assist passengers. On freight trains, the conductor rides in the locomotive or (historically) the caboose, monitors the train en route, and directs ground work at industries and yards.

History

In the early days of railroading, the conductor was the senior figure on the train, often hiring and supervising the rest of the crew, while the engineer was a subordinate technician. The conductor's authority over the train remains codified in North American operating rulebooks, even as crew sizes have shrunk; the traditional two-person crew of conductor and engineer is standard on most freight railroads, while some passenger services employ additional assistant conductors.
