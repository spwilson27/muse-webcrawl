The Indian Forest Service (IFS) is one of the three All India Services of the Government of India, alongside the Indian Administrative Service and the Indian Police Service. Its officers manage India's forests, wildlife, and environment at both the central and state levels, implementing the National Forest Policy and the country's environmental and wildlife protection laws.

History

Scientific forest management in India began under the British with the creation of the Imperial Forest Department in 1864 under Dietrich Brandis and the Imperial Forest Service in 1867. After independence, the service was reconstituted as an All India Service in 1966 under the All India Services Act, 1951.

Recruitment and roles

Officers are recruited through a competitive examination conducted by the Union Public Service Commission and trained at the Indira Gandhi National Forest Academy in Dehradun. They serve as Divisional Forest Officers, Conservators, and Principal Chief Conservators of Forests, heading state forest forces, and on deputation staff the environment ministry, wildlife institutes, and bodies such as the Forest Survey of India. Their work spans forest conservation, afforestation, wildlife protection, biodiversity, and climate change mitigation.
