Cat People is a 1942 American horror film directed by Jacques Tourneur and produced by Val Lewton for RKO Radio Pictures. It stars Simone Simon as Irena Dubrovna, a Serbian-born fashion designer in New York who believes she is descended from a cursed race of people who transform into black panthers when emotionally or sexually aroused.

Plot

At the Central Park Zoo, Irena meets naval construction engineer Oliver Reed (Kent Smith); they marry, but Irena refuses to consummate the marriage, fearing the curse. As Oliver grows close to his co-worker Alice (Jane Randolph), Irena's jealousy mounts. In the film's most famous sequences — a late-night walk in which Alice senses she is being stalked, ended by the sudden hiss of a bus's air brakes (the original "Lewton bus" jump scare), and a swimming-pool scene lit by rippling shadows — the threat is suggested rather than shown. Irena is eventually killed by a panther she releases at the zoo, pronouncing that she never lied.

Production and legacy

Made for roughly $134,000, the film earned several million dollars and rescued RKO from the financial fallout of Citizen Kane and The Magnificent Ambersons. Its reliance on shadow, sound, and suggestion over explicit spectacle defined the Lewton unit's style and proved hugely influential on the horror genre. A sequel, The Curse of the Cat People (1944), and a remake, Cat People (1982), directed by Paul Schrader, followed. In 1993 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
