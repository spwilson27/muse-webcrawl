Order (decoration)

Insignia of an order: breast star, sash, and badge

An order is a visible honour awarded by a sovereign state, monarch, dynastic house, or organisation to an individual, usually for merit. It typically takes the form of insignia — a badge, star, sash, collar, or medal — worn by the recipient. Orders are the senior category within the broader field of orders, decorations, and medals studied by phaleristics.

History

Modern orders descend from the medieval military-religious orders of knighthood founded during the Crusades, such as the Knights Hospitaller and the Knights Templar, whose members wore distinctive crosses. In the later Middle Ages, monarchical orders of chivalry — the Order of the Garter (1348) in England and the Order of the Golden Fleece (1430) in Burgundy — admitted members as an honour rather than as a religious vocation. Napoleon founded the Légion d'honneur in 1802 as a merit order open regardless of birth, a model widely copied by modern states.

Structure

Many orders are divided into classes or grades, commonly including Grand Cross, Grand Officer, Commander, Officer, and Knight (or Member). Membership may be limited in number, and insignia often vary by grade: grand crosses wear a sash and breast star, commanders a neck badge, and knights a badge on a ribbon. The sovereign or head of state is typically the order's grand master.

Examples

  • Order of the Garter (United Kingdom)
  • Légion d'honneur (France)
  • Order of Merit (United Kingdom and Commonwealth realms)
  • Order of the Rising Sun (Japan)
  • Order of Lenin (former Soviet Union)
