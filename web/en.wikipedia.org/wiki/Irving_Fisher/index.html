Irving Fisher (27 February 1867 – 29 April 1947) was an American economist at Yale, among the first American neoclassical economists and a pioneer of mathematical economics and econometrics.

Contributions

Fisher gave the quantity theory of money its canonical equation of exchange, MV = PT, underpinning later Monetarism; distinguished nominal and real interest rates (the Fisher effect: nominal rates rise with expected Inflation); founded index-number theory; and, after losing his fortune in the 1929 crash — days after declaring stocks had reached a "permanently high plateau" — developed the debt-deflation theory of the Great Depression (1933), in which falling prices raise real debts and deepen slump. He was also a prominent, and controversial, campaigner for eugenics, prohibition and health reform, and built a fortune from his visible-card index system (an ancestor of Rolodex).
