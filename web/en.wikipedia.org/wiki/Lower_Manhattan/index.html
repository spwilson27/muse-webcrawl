Lower Manhattan, also known as Downtown Manhattan or simply Downtown, is the southernmost part of Manhattan, the central borough for business in New York City. It is the historical birthplace of the city, where the Dutch founded New Amsterdam in the 1620s, and it is the seat of the city's government.

The area is defined most commonly as Manhattan south of 14th Street, though definitions vary; the Financial District lies at its southern tip. Lower Manhattan contains City Hall, the Municipal Building, One Police Plaza, the courthouses of the New York County Supreme Court, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation's New York field office.

History

The Lenape people originally inhabited the island. Dutch settlers established a trading post and fort at its tip in 1624–1626; the settlement's protective wall stood along present-day Wall Street. After the English took the colony in 1664, Lower Manhattan grew as a port and commercial center. Federal Hall on Wall Street hosted the inauguration of George Washington as the first U.S. president in 1789.

Economy

Lower Manhattan is the third-largest central business district in the United States, after Midtown Manhattan and the Chicago Loop. It is anchored by Wall Street and the New York Stock Exchange. The September 11, 2001 attacks destroyed the World Trade Center complex; the site was redeveloped with the National September 11 Memorial & Museum and One World Trade Center, completed in 2014 and the tallest building in the Western Hemisphere. In the decades since, the district has also become increasingly residential, with a growing permanent population.

Neighborhoods

Neighborhoods of Lower Manhattan include the Financial District, Tribeca, Battery Park City, the Lower East Side, SoHo, Chinatown, Greenwich Village, the East Village, and Chelsea (south of 14th Street boundary definitions vary).
