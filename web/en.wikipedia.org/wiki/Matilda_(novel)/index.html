Matilda is a children's novel by the British writer Roald Dahl, illustrated by Quentin Blake and published in 1988. It tells the story of Matilda Wormwood, a precocious young girl with neglectful parents who discovers she has telekinetic powers, which she uses to outwit the adults around her, including the tyrannical headmistress Miss Trunchbull.

Plot

Matilda, a brilliant child who taught herself to read and works her way through the adult classics of the public library, receives no encouragement from her dishonest car-dealer father and her bingo-playing mother. At Crunchem Hall Primary School she befriends her gentle teacher, Miss Honey, but clashes with the brutal headmistress Miss Trunchbull, a former Olympic shot-putter who delights in punishing children. After being falsely accused of a prank, Matilda discovers she can move objects with her mind. She uses this power to expose the Trunchbull's role in the death of Miss Honey's father and the theft of her inheritance, driving the headmistress away. When the Wormwoods flee to Spain to escape the police, Matilda stays behind and is adopted by Miss Honey.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a 1996 film directed by Danny DeVito, a stage musical by the Royal Shakespeare Company with music and lyrics by Tim Minchin, which premiered in 2010 and transferred to the West End and Broadway, and a 2022 film adaptation of the musical.

Reception and legacy

Matilda has sold tens of millions of copies worldwide and is regularly voted among the best-loved children's books. In 2018 a statue of Matilda confronting Donald Trump was installed near Dahl's home in Great Missenden to mark the book's 30th anniversary.
