Business analytics

Business analytics (BA) refers to the skills, technologies, and practices for continuous iterative exploration and investigation of past business performance to gain insight and drive business planning. Business analytics focuses on developing new insights and understanding of business performance based on data and statistical methods.

Types

Analytic methods are commonly grouped into four ascending levels of difficulty and value:

  • Descriptive analytics — what happened? Summarizing historical data with reports, dashboards, and visualization.
  • Diagnostic analytics — why did it happen? Drill-down, correlation, and root-cause analysis.
  • Predictive analytics — what will happen? Statistical models such as regression, forecasting, and machine learning.
  • Prescriptive analytics — what should we do? Optimization and simulation that recommend actions.

Relation to business intelligence

Business analytics is often contrasted with business intelligence (BI): BI is usually described as focusing on reporting and querying current and historical data, whereas BA emphasizes statistical analysis, predictive modelling, and optimization to anticipate future events. In practice the terms overlap heavily, and analytics software suites typically combine both.

Applications

Common application areas include marketing analytics and customer segmentation, risk scoring in banking and insurance, supply-chain optimization, human-resources analytics, and fraud detection. The field depends on data quality, data warehousing, and governance, and its adoption accelerated with the growth of big data platforms in the 2000s and 2010s.
