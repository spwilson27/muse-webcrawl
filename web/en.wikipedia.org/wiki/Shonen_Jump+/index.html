Shonen Jump+ (Japanese: 少年ジャンプ+, Shōnen Janpu Purasu) is a digital manga magazine and mobile application published by Shueisha, launched in September 2014 as a companion platform to the print magazine Weekly Shōnen Jump. It serialises original manga, spin-offs of Jump series, and reprints, and allows readers to buy digital editions of the print magazine. It is free to use with some paid content.

History and model

Shueisha created Jump+ to develop a born-digital catalogue alongside its declining print circulations, offering editors and authors more freedom in scheduling (series may appear weekly, fortnightly or monthly) and genre. Its business model combines free first and latest chapters with sales of collected volumes and in-app purchases. In 2019 Shueisha launched the international counterpart service Manga Plus, which publishes many Jump+ series simultaneously in English and other languages.

Notable series

Jump+ produced several of the biggest manga hits of the streaming era, including Spy × Family by Tatsuya Endo, Kaiju No. 8 by Naoya Matsumoto, Chainsaw Man Part 2 by Tatsuki Fujimoto, Dandadan by Yukinobu Tatsu, and Hell's Paradise: Jigokuraku by Yuji Kaku. Spy × Family in particular demonstrated that a digital-first, fortnightly series could rival print flagship titles in sales and anime adaptation success. The platform also hosts one-shots by new authors and an amateur submission service, Jump Rookie, used as a scouting pipeline.

Impact

Jump+ is widely credited with reshaping manga publishing economics — building very large online readerships (its app reported tens of millions of downloads), developing hits without print magazine slots, and competing with rival publisher platforms such as Magazine Pocket and Sunday Webry.
