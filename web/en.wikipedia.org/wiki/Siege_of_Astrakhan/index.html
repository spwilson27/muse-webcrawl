Siege of Astrakhan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Astrakhan took place in 1569, when an Ottoman expeditionary force, supported by the Crimean Khanate, attempted to capture the fortress city of Astrakhan from the Tsardom of Russia. The campaign was part of the Russo-Turkish War of 1568–1570 and of Ottoman efforts to secure the Volga region and open a route toward Persia and Central Asia.

Background

Russia under Ivan IV had conquered the khanates of Kazan (1552) and Astrakhan (1556), gaining control of the whole Volga river. The Ottoman sultan Selim II, advised by Grand Vizier Sokollu Mehmed Pasha, planned to expel the Russians, restore Muslim khanates, and dig a canal between the Don and the Volga that would link the Black Sea to the Caspian.

The siege

In the summer of 1569 a force of Ottoman janissaries and Crimean Tatar cavalry under Devlet I Giray and Kasim Pasha marched on Astrakhan. Work began on the Don–Volga canal, but the project proved impractical and was abandoned. The army proceeded to Astrakhan and besieged the Russian garrison, commanded by Prince Pyotr Serebriany. The garrison resisted, harassment by Cossacks and Russian forces disrupted Ottoman supply lines, and a storm destroyed much of the Ottoman fleet returning via the Black Sea. With winter approaching, the Ottomans lifted the siege and withdrew.

Aftermath

The failure marked the limit of Ottoman expansion toward the Caspian steppe and confirmed Russian control of the Volga. The peace of 1570 left Astrakhan in Russian hands, and the two empires avoided major war in the region for a century.
