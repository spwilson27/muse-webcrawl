Barclays

Barclays plc is a British multinational universal bank, headquartered in London, England. It traces its origins to a goldsmith banking business established in the City of London in 1690. Barclays operates two principal divisions — Barclays UK, the ring-fenced retail and business bank, and Barclays International, comprising its corporate and investment bank, Barclays Investment Bank, and the Barclaycard credit card business. It is one of the "Big Four" clearing banks of the United Kingdom and is designated a systemically important financial institution.

History

The business descends from John Freame and Thomas Gould, goldsmith bankers in Lombard Street from 1690; the Barclay name entered the partnership in 1736 through James Barclay. In 1896 twenty banks merged as Barclay and Company Limited, creating a nationwide branch network; the enlarged bank acquired many rivals through the 20th century. Barclays pioneered the world's first ATM (at Enfield in 1967) and Britain's first credit card, Barclaycard (1966).

The bank expanded its investment banking arm as Barclays de Zoete Wedd and Barclays Global Investors (sold to BlackRock in 2009), and in 2008 acquired the North American operations of the collapsed Lehman Brothers. Barclays was heavily fined for its role in the Libor manipulation scandal in 2012, prompting the resignation of chief executive Bob Diamond; the bank did not take direct UK government equity bailouts during the 2008 crisis, instead raising capital from Gulf investors. UK ring-fencing reforms took effect in 2018–2019.

Operations

Barclays serves tens of millions of customers across the UK, the United States and other markets, sponsors the Premier League (as title sponsor 2001–2016 and continuing banking partner), and lends its name to venues such as the Barclays Center in Brooklyn.
