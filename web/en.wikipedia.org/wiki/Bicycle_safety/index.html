Bicycle safety

Bicycle safety is the use of road traffic safety practices to reduce risk associated with cycling. Risk can be reduced by following safe riding practices, wearing protective equipment such as helmets, improving bicycle maintenance, and designing roads and vehicles to reduce the frequency and severity of collisions.

[Image: A cyclist wearing a helmet riding on a protected cycle track]

Risks

Most serious cyclist injuries result from collisions with motor vehicles, often at intersections; common crash types include motorists turning across a cyclist's path, "dooring" by parked-car occupants, and overtaking collisions. Single-vehicle falls and collisions with fixed objects account for many lesser injuries. Per kilometre travelled, cycling is generally riskier than driving in car-oriented countries, though regular cyclists gain substantial health benefits that studies find outweigh the risk.

Safety in numbers

Researcher Peter Jacobsen documented the "safety in numbers" effect: where and when cycling is more common, the risk per cyclist tends to fall, plausibly because motorists expect and watch for cyclists and because cycling populations gain political support for safer infrastructure.

Countermeasures

Helmets

Bicycle helmets reduce the risk of head injury in a crash; case-control studies and meta-analyses consistently find protective effects, though the size of the benefit and the merits of mandatory helmet laws remain debated (see bicycle law).

Infrastructure

Protected cycle tracks, bicycle lanes, traffic calming, and intersection treatments such as advanced stop lines and separate signal phases reduce conflicts. Lighting, reflectors, and high-visibility clothing improve conspicuity at night.

Education and vehicle design

Rider training (such as "vehicular cycling" curricula) teaches predictable road positioning. Vehicle measures include blind-spot mirrors, side guards on trucks, and automated emergency braking that detects cyclists.

References: Jacobsen, P.L. "Safety in numbers," Injury Prevention (2003); Olivier, J. & Creighton, P. "Bicycle injuries and helmet use: a systematic review and meta-analysis," International Journal of Epidemiology (2017).
