Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales, formerly the National Museum of Wales (Welsh: Amgueddfa Genedlaethol Cymru), is a Welsh Government sponsored body that comprises a network of national museums in Wales. It holds collections of archaeology, art, natural history, geology and social and industrial history, and entry to its museums is free.

History

The institution was founded by royal charter in 1907 following a campaign for a national museum for Wales. Its principal building, the National Museum Cardiff in Cathays Park, opened to the public in 1927. Over the twentieth century the museum expanded to include open-air and industrial sites across the country. In 2022 the body adopted the name Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales.

Museums in the network

  • National Museum Cardiff — art, natural history and archaeology, including major Impressionist and Welsh art collections.
  • St Fagans National Museum of History — an open-air museum of historic buildings moved from across Wales.
  • Big Pit National Coal Museum, Blaenavon — a working coal mine turned museum.
  • National Wool Museum, Dre-fach Felindre.
  • National Slate Museum, Llanberis.
  • National Roman Legion Museum, Caerleon.
  • National Waterfront Museum, Swansea.

Collections

The collections include the art bequests of Gwendoline and Margaret Davies, among the finest collections of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist art in Britain; important early medieval archaeology such as the Mold gold cape (held jointly with the British Museum); and extensive natural science collections underpinning research on Welsh biodiversity.
