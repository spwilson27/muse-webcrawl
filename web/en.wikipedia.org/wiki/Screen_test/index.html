Screen test

A screen test is a method of determining the suitability of an actor or actress for performing on film or in a particular role. In a screen test, the performer is filmed performing a scene or delivering lines so that casting directors, producers and the director can evaluate how they appear and sound on camera — qualities that cannot always be judged from a stage performance, photograph or interview.

Process

Screen tests are usually shot on a studio soundstage with professional lighting, makeup and costume approximating those of the intended production. The performer may read opposite another actor, or opposite a reader off-camera. Tests may also be used to evaluate combinations of actors for chemistry, or to assess makeup, hairstyling and costume designs on camera.

History

During the Hollywood studio era, screen tests were a standard gateway to a studio contract: studios filmed tests of promising theatre actors and models to see whether they were "photogenic". Famous screen tests — for example those made for Gone with the Wind during the search for Scarlett O'Hara — have themselves become celebrated artefacts. Today, tests are more often made for a specific major role in a film or television series after earlier audition rounds.

Related concepts

A screen test differs from an audition, which is typically an earlier, less elaborate evaluation, and from a chemistry read, which focuses on the interaction between two performers being considered as an on-screen pair.
