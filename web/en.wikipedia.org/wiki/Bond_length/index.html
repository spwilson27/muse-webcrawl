Bond length

In molecular geometry, bond length (or bond distance) is the average distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule. It is a transferable property that remains roughly constant between similar bonds in comparable chemical environments, and it is closely related to bond order: the higher the bond order, the shorter and stronger the bond.

Typical values

Bond lengths are measured in picometres (pm) or ångströms (1 Å = 100 pm). Typical values include C–C ≈ 154 pm, C=C ≈ 134 pm, C≡C ≈ 120 pm, C–H ≈ 109 pm, and O–H ≈ 96 pm. Lengths grow down the periodic table as atomic radii increase.

Measurement

Bond lengths are determined experimentally by X-ray and neutron diffraction in solids and by electron diffraction and rotational (microwave) spectroscopy in the gas phase, and can be computed with quantum chemical methods.
