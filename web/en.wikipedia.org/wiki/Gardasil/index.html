Gardasil

Gardasil is a vaccine for use in the prevention of certain strains of human papillomavirus (HPV). It was developed by Merck & Co. High-risk human papillomavirus (hr-HPV) genital infection is the most common sexually transmitted infection among women. The HPV strains that Gardasil protects against are sexually transmitted, specifically HPV types 6, 11, 16 and 18. HPV types 16 and 18 cause an estimated 70% of cervical cancers, and are responsible for most HPV-induced anal, vulvar, vaginal, and penile cancers. HPV types 6 and 11 cause an estimated 90% of genital warts cases. While treatment of the disease does not occur, Gardasil reduces the incidence of HPV-induced disease.

Gardasil was approved in the United States in June 2006. A nine-valent version, Gardasil 9, protects against five additional HPV types and was approved in 2014; it has since become the standard formulation distributed in the United States.

Medical uses

Gardasil is a prophylactic HPV vaccine, meaning it is designed to prevent infections from HPV types 16, 18, 6, and 11 (and, for Gardasil 9, types 31, 33, 45, 52, and 58 as well). For maximum effect, it is recommended that girls receive the vaccine before they become sexually active. The vaccine is recommended for both females and males, generally beginning at age 11 or 12, and is given in two or three doses depending on age.

Impact

Since its introduction, studies in several countries have documented large reductions in HPV infections, genital warts, and precancerous cervical lesions among vaccinated populations, as well as herd effects among unvaccinated individuals.
