Endomorphism

In mathematics, an endomorphism is a morphism of a mathematical object to itself. An endomorphism that is also an isomorphism is an automorphism. For example, an endomorphism of a vector space V is a linear map f: V → V. An endomorphism of a group G is a group homomorphism f: G → G. In general, one can speak of endomorphisms in any category.

Structure

Given any object X in a category, two endomorphisms of X can be composed to yield another endomorphism of X. It follows that the set of all endomorphisms of X, denoted End(X), forms a monoid under composition, with the identity map as its identity element. Invertible endomorphisms of X form a group under composition, the automorphism group Aut(X).

Endomorphism rings

In the category of abelian groups, endomorphisms can be added pointwise as well as composed, so the endomorphisms of an abelian group form a ring, called the endomorphism ring. The same construction applies to modules: the endomorphism ring of a module plays a central role in representation theory and homological algebra. Any ring can be realised as the endomorphism ring of some abelian group.

Examples

  • The Frobenius endomorphism on a commutative ring of characteristic p, sending each element to its p-th power.
  • Endomorphisms of a vector space given by square matrices after a basis is chosen.
  • Idempotent endomorphisms, those satisfying f ∘ f = f, which correspond to projections onto direct summands or retracts.
