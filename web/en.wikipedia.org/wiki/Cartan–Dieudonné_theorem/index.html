Cartan–Dieudonné theorem

In mathematics, the Cartan–Dieudonné theorem, named after Élie Cartan and Jean Dieudonné, establishes that every orthogonal transformation in an n-dimensional symmetric bilinear space can be described as the composition of at most n reflections.

Statement

Let (V, b) be an n-dimensional vector space over a field with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form b (for example, a Euclidean or Minkowski space). Then every orthogonal transformation of V is a product of at most n reflections across hyperplanes through the origin.

A reflection across the hyperplane orthogonal to a non-null vector v sends v to −v and fixes every vector orthogonal to v. The theorem says the orthogonal group is generated by these reflections, with a uniform bound on the number needed.

Context and generalisations

The result extends the familiar fact that every rotation of the plane is a product of two reflections and every isometry of three-dimensional Euclidean space is a product of at most three reflections (four if translations are included). For Euclidean space this is closely related to the classification of motions and to the theorem of Leonardo da Vinci on composition of reflections. Analogues hold for unitary groups (reflections replaced by unitary reflections) and for indefinite forms, with care needed in characteristic 2 and for singular vectors. The theorem is fundamental in the theory of quadratic forms and in the study of spin groups and Clifford algebras, where it underlies the definition of the Pin and Spin groups via products of unit vectors.
