Great Blasket Island

Great Blasket (Irish: An Blascaod Mór) is the principal island of the Blasket Islands, lying off the coast of the Dingle Peninsula in County Kerry, Ireland. The island is about 6 kilometres long and rises to 346 metres at An Cró Mór. The last residents were evacuated in 1953, and the island is now uninhabited, though it can be visited by ferry from Dún Chaoin and Ventry.

History and literature

The island community, which peaked at about 175 people around 1916, was noted for its strong Irish-language storytelling tradition. Scholars such as Robin Flower encouraged islanders to write their life stories, producing a remarkable body of literature that includes Tomás Ó Criomhthain's The Islandman (1929), Peig Sayers' autobiography Peig (1936), and Muiris Ó Súilleabháin's Twenty Years a-Growing (1933). These works are classics of Irish-language literature.

Declining population, the difficulty of reaching medical help in storms, and emigration to America reduced the community until the remaining 22 islanders were resettled on the mainland in November 1953. The ruined village still stands on the island's sheltered north-eastern slope. The Blasket Centre museum on the mainland commemorates the island's heritage.
