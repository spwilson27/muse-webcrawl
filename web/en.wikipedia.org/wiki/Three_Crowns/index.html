Three Crowns (Tre Kronor)
TypeHeraldic charge / national emblem
CountrySweden
ArrangementTwo crowns above one

The Three Crowns (Swedish: tre kronor) is a national emblem of Sweden, composed of three yellow or golden coronets ordered two above and one below on a blue background. The emblem is a prominent part of the Swedish coat of arms and appears on the uniforms of the Sweden men's national ice hockey team, which is widely known by the name "Tre Kronor".

History

The origin of the three crowns motif is debated. It has been used as a Swedish royal symbol since at least the 14th century, appearing on the seal of King Magnus Eriksson and in the coinage and arms associated with Albert of Mecklenburg. One theory connects the symbol with the earlier arms of Cologne, which bore three crowns in honour of the Three Wise Men (Magi). Another links it to the three crowns associated with the legendary King Arthur in medieval heraldry.

In Sweden, the emblem has also been interpreted as representing the three traditional lands of the Swedish realm or, in older interpretations, the kingdoms of the Swedes, the Geats, and the Goths.

Uses

The three crowns appear on the lesser coat of arms of Sweden, on Swedish government buildings, aircraft of the Swedish Air Force (as a roundel element), and the Parliament House in Stockholm is topped by a sculpture of the three crowns. The former royal castle in Stockholm, destroyed by fire in 1697, was itself named Tre Kronor.
