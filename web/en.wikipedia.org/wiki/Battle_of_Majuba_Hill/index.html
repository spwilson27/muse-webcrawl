Battle of Majuba Hill

The Battle of Majuba Hill, fought on 27 February 1881, was the decisive engagement of the First Boer War. Boer commandos under Piet Joubert stormed the summit of Majuba Hill in the Natal Drakensberg foothills, held by British troops under Major-General Sir George Pomeroy Colley, inflicting a humiliating defeat that ended the war and restored the independence of the South African Republic (Transvaal).

Background

Britain had annexed the Transvaal in 1877; the Boers rose in December 1880 and defeated British columns at Bronkhorstspruit, Laing's Nek and Ingogo. Colley, the British commander in Natal, resolved to seize Majuba Hill, which overlooked the Boer position at Laing's Nek. On the night of 26–27 February, some 550 British soldiers occupied the flat summit — but failed to dig adequate defences, brought no artillery, and were given little water.

The battle

At dawn the Boers discovered the occupation and Joubert organised storming parties. Using dead ground and providing covering fire with deadly accuracy, Boer marksmen worked their way up the slopes while others pinned the defenders. The British on the rim, silhouetted against the skyline and firing high, were gradually driven from the edges; in the early afternoon the Boer parties rushed the summit plateau. The British position collapsed, soldiers fleeing or surrendering down the hillside. British losses were 92 killed — including Colley, shot dead late in the action — about 134 wounded and 59 captured; Boer casualties were just one or two killed and a handful wounded.

Aftermath

Majuba was a profound shock to British prestige. Gladstone's government, unwilling to escalate, concluded the Pretoria Convention (1881), restoring Transvaal self-government under nominal British suzerainty. "Remember Majuba" became a Boer rallying cry and a British grievance that festered until the Second Boer War of 1899–1902.
