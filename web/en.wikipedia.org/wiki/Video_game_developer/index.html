Video game developer

A video game developer is a software developer specialising in video game development — the process and related disciplines of creating video games. A developer may range from a single individual working alone to a large studio of hundreds, and may be a first-party developer owned by a console maker, a second-party or third-party studio contracted to a publisher, or an independent ("indie") developer self-publishing its work.

Types of developers

First-party developers are owned by a platform holder (for example Nintendo EPD, PlayStation Studios, Xbox Game Studios) and make games for that owner's systems. Second-party studios are independent but work exclusively with one platform holder. Third-party developers publish through, or are contracted by, external video game publishers, or release games on multiple platforms. Independent developers fund and publish themselves, often distributing digitally via Steam, itch.io or console storefronts.

Development process

Development proceeds through pre-production (concept, design documents, prototyping), production (programming, art, animation, audio, level design), testing and quality assurance, and post-launch support with patches and downloadable content. Roles include game designers, programmers, artists, animators, composers, writers, producers and QA testers. Teams use game engines such as Unity and Unreal Engine, version control and agile methods. The final phase before a deadline is notorious for "crunch" — extended overtime that has drawn sustained criticism and unionisation efforts.

History

In the 1970s and early 1980s games were typically made by one person; the video game crash of 1983 reshaped the industry. Budgets and team sizes grew through the 1990s and 2000s until flagship "AAA" productions employed hundreds and cost hundreds of millions of dollars, while digital distribution in the 2000s–2010s revived small-team independent development, producing hits such as Minecraft and Stardew Valley from tiny teams.
