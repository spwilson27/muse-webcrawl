North Gyeongsang Province (Korean: 경상북도, Gyeongsangbuk-do) is a province in eastern South Korea, and with an area of 19,030 km² it is the largest province in the country. It was formed in 1896 from the northern part of the former Gyeongsang Province. From 1981 to 2016 the provincial capital was the city of Daegu, which since 1981 has been a separate metropolitan city; the capital moved to Andong in 2016.

Geography

The province borders the Sea of Japan (East Sea) to the east and is drained by the Nakdong River. It includes the island of Ulleungdo and the Liancourt Rocks (Dokdo/Takeshima). The Taebaek and Sobaek mountain ranges frame its east and north.

Cities and culture

Principal cities include Pohang, a major steel-producing port home to POSCO; Gyeongju, the ancient capital of the Silla kingdom, rich in UNESCO-listed sites such as Bulguksa and Seokguram; Gumi, an electronics manufacturing centre; and Andong, noted for the Hahoe Folk Village and its traditional Confucian heritage. The province is known for apples, especially from Cheongsong and Daegu's surrounding districts.
