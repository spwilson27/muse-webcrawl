Numbness

Numbness is a loss or reduction of sensation in a part of the body, often accompanied by tingling or a "pins and needles" feeling (paraesthesia). It results from dysfunction anywhere along the sensory pathway, from peripheral nerves to the spinal cord and brain.

Causes

Temporary numbness commonly follows sustained pressure on a nerve, as when a limb "falls asleep". Persistent numbness may indicate peripheral neuropathy (frequently from diabetes), nerve entrapment such as carpal tunnel syndrome, vitamin B12 deficiency, stroke, multiple sclerosis, herniated discs compressing nerve roots, or vascular insufficiency.

Evaluation and treatment

Assessment focuses on the distribution of sensory loss — for example a stocking-glove pattern in polyneuropathy or a dermatomal pattern in radiculopathy — together with reflex and strength testing, blood tests and sometimes nerve conduction studies. Treatment targets the underlying cause; sudden numbness with weakness or speech disturbance is a medical emergency suggestive of stroke.
