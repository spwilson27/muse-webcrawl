The Khwarazmian Empire (Persian: خوارزمشاهیان), also called the Khwarezm-Shah dynasty or Anushteginid dynasty, was a Persianate Sunni Muslim empire of Turkic mamluk origin that ruled large parts of present-day Central Asia, Afghanistan, and Iran from approximately 1077 to 1231. Founded by Anushtegin Gharchai, a former Turkic slave commander, the Khwarazmshahs began as vassals of the Seljuks and the Kara-Khitans before becoming independent.

Rise

The dynasty's seat was Khwarazm, the region around the lower Amu Darya (Oxus) delta south of the Aral Sea, with its capital at Gurganj. Under Atsiz (r. 1127–1156) and Il-Arslan the shahs asserted independence from the Seljuks. Tekish (r. 1172–1200) defeated the last Seljuk sultan of Persia, Toghrul III, in 1194 and took over western Iran, while also contesting Khurasan with the Ghurids.

Peak under Muhammad II

Ala al-Din Muhammad II (r. 1200–1220) destroyed the Ghurid empire's power, conquered Transoxiana from the Kara-Khitans and Qarakhanids by 1215, and extended his rule from the Syr Darya to the Persian Gulf, ruling cities such as Samarkand, Bukhara, Herat, and Nishapur. His vast empire was nevertheless fragile: it rested on Kipchak Turkish military commanders favoured by his mother Terken Khatun, whose court at Gurganj rivalled his own, and relations with the Abbasid caliph al-Nasir in Baghdad were hostile.

Mongol conquest

In 1218 a Mongol caravan was seized at Otrar and its merchants executed with the shah's approval; when Genghis Khan's envoys were killed or humiliated, the Mongols invaded in 1219. The empire's forces, dispersed in garrisons and divided by internal rivalries, were destroyed piecemeal. Bukhara and Samarkand fell in 1220; Gurganj was annihilated after a long siege; Muhammad II fled west and died on an island in the Caspian Sea in late 1220. His son Jalal al-Din Mangburni rallied resistance, defeated a Mongol force at Parwan (1221) but was crushed at the Battle of the Indus, fleeing to India. After years as a freebooting ruler in western Iran and the Caucasus, he was killed in 1231, ending the dynasty. The conquest devastated Central Asia's cities and irrigation systems.

Legacy

Khwarazmian rule was a high point of Persianate culture — the scholar al-Biruni had earlier worked in Khwarazm, and the region's astronomers and mathematicians gave their name to the words "algorithm" (from al-Khwarizmi) and "algebra." Surviving monuments include the mausoleums and minaret of Konye-Urgench in Turkmenistan.
