Droplet

A droplet is a small column of liquid, bounded completely or almost completely by free surfaces. A droplet may form when liquid accumulates at the lower end of a tube or other surface boundary, and may also be produced by condensation from a vapour or by atomisation of a larger mass of liquid.

Surface tension

Droplets form because a liquid has surface tension, which causes it to behave as though covered by an elastic membrane. Small droplets are nearly spherical because surface tension minimizes surface area for a given volume; larger drops are flattened by gravity. On surfaces, a droplet's shape is governed by the contact angle, which measures how much the liquid wets the solid.

Applications

Droplet physics underlies inkjet printing, spray coating, microfluidics ("digital microfluidics" manipulates discrete droplets), pesticide application, and the study of respiratory droplets in disease transmission. A drop falling through air does not keep the classic teardrop shape; small drops are nearly spherical, and larger ones flatten like a hamburger bun.

Rain

Raindrops typically range from about 0.5 mm to several millimetres across. Drops larger than about 5 mm break apart in falling air. Cloud droplets are far smaller, on the order of 10–20 micrometres, and remain suspended until they coalesce.
