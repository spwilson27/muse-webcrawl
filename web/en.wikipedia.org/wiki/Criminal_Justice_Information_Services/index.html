Criminal Justice Information Services

The FBI's CJIS Division in Clarksburg, West Virginia.

The Criminal Justice Information Services Division (CJIS) is a division of the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) located in Clarksburg, West Virginia. Established in 1992, it is the FBI's largest division and serves as the central repository for criminal justice information services in the United States, providing identification and data services to federal, state, tribal and local law-enforcement agencies, as well as to certain authorised private organisations.

Services

CJIS operates several major systems. The Next Generation Identification (NGI) system, successor to IAFIS, holds fingerprint and biometric records. The National Crime Information Center (NCIC) provides real-time data on wanted persons, stolen property and protection orders. The National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) supports firearms purchase checks. The Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) programme, including its successor NIBRS, compiles national crime statistics, and the Interstate Identification Index (III) shares criminal history records between states.

Security policy

Access to CJIS data is governed by the CJIS Security Policy, which sets requirements for encryption, personnel screening, auditing and physical security for the thousands of agencies and vendors that connect to its systems. The division's complex in Clarksburg employs several thousand staff and contractors and houses some of the largest biometric databases in the world.
