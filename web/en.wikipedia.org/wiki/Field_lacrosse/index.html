Field lacrosse

Field lacrosse
Players: 10 per side (men)
Field: 110 × 60 yards
Governing body: World Lacrosse
First codified: 1867 (W. George Beers)

Field lacrosse is a full-field team sport played with a lacrosse stick (crosse) and a hard rubber ball, and is one of the two principal outdoor forms of lacrosse, the other being box lacrosse. It descends from baggataway, a game played by Indigenous peoples of North America for centuries. In the men's game, ten players per side — three attackmen, three midfielders, three defensemen, and a goalie — compete on a field 110 yards long, catching, carrying, and passing the ball in the pocket of the stick to shoot on a 6-by-6-foot goal.

History

Montreal dentist William George Beers codified the rules in 1860 and published a standard set in 1867, replacing the mass games of hundreds of players with organised teams; lacrosse was named Canada's national game. Field lacrosse spread to the United States, England, and Australia, and appeared twice at the Olympic Games (1904 and 1908). Today it is played at professional level in the Premier Lacrosse League and Major League Lacrosse's successor competitions, and widely in American colleges under NCAA rules.

Equipment and play

Players wear helmets, gloves, and shoulder and arm pads; defensemen carry long poles up to six feet, while attackmen and midfielders use short sticks. The women's game (12 per side) uses different rules with limited body contact and modified equipment. Games combine the continuous flow of soccer with the physical checking of hockey, and midfielders must play both offence and defence. Face-offs begin play after each goal, and the "crease" around the goal is the goalie's protected area.

International competition

The World Lacrosse Championship, held every four years since 1967, has been dominated by the United States and Canada, with the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Nationals — representing the game's Indigenous creators — a perennial contender. Lacrosse in the sixes format will return to the Olympic programme at Los Angeles 2028.
