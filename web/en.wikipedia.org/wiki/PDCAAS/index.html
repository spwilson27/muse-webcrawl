Protein Digestibility Corrected Amino Acid Score

The Protein Digestibility Corrected Amino Acid Score (PDCAAS) is a method of evaluating the quality of a protein based on both the amino acid requirements of humans and their ability to digest it. The PDCAAS rating was adopted by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations/World Health Organization (FAO/WHO) in 1993 as the preferred method for protein quality measurement.

Methodology

The PDCAAS is calculated by comparing the amount of the limiting essential amino acid in a test protein with a reference "scoring pattern" of essential amino acids, and correcting that amino acid score for the true fecal digestibility of the protein:

PDCAAS = (mg of limiting amino acid in 1 g of test protein / mg of same amino acid in 1 g of reference protein) × fecal true digestibility

Values are truncated to a maximum of 1.00, even when the calculated score exceeds that value. This truncation is one of the method's most criticized features, since proteins with scores above 1.0 cannot be distinguished from one another.

Limitations and successor

Critics note that PDCAAS uses fecal rather than ileal digestibility, which can overestimate quality because colonic bacteria metabolize some amino acids; that truncation at 1.0 masks differences among high-quality proteins; and that anti-nutritional factors are not fully accounted for. In 2013 the FAO recommended replacing PDCAAS with the Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score (DIAAS), which measures ileal digestibility of each indispensable amino acid and does not truncate values.
