Second Chechen War

Date26 August 1999 – 31 May 2000 (main phase); insurgency to April 2009
LocationChechnya and the North Caucasus, Russia
BelligerentsRussian Federation vs. Chechen Republic of Ichkeria, later Islamist insurgents
ResultRussian victory; federal control restored

The Second Chechen War (Russian: Вторая чеченская война) was an armed conflict between the Russian Federation and the separatist Chechen Republic of Ichkeria, fought from 1999. The large-scale conventional phase ended in mid-2000 with the fall of Grozny, but a guerrilla insurgency continued across the North Caucasus until Russia formally declared its counter-terrorism operation in Chechnya over in April 2009.

Origins

The First Chechen War (1994–1996) had ended with the Khasavyurt Accord and de facto Chechen independence under President Aslan Maskhadov, but the unrecognised republic descended into warlordism, kidnapping, and a growing Islamist challenge led by Shamil Basayev and the Arab commander Ibn al-Khattab. In August 1999 Basayev and Khattab led an incursion into neighbouring Dagestan to support a separatist enclave there. Beginning in September 1999, a series of apartment bombings in Russian cities killed more than 300 people; the Russian government blamed Chechen terrorists, though the bombings' authorship has remained disputed. Prime Minister Vladimir Putin, who directed the response, saw his popularity soar.

Course of the war

Russian forces entered Chechnya on 1 October 1999, advancing methodically behind heavy artillery and air bombardment in deliberate contrast to the hasty assaults of 1994. Grozny was encircled and subjected to devastating bombardment; the United Nations described it in 2003 as the most destroyed city on Earth. Federal troops took the capital in February 2000 after the defending fighters withdrew through a minefield with heavy losses. A direct presidential rule administration was installed, and from 2003 Moscow pursued "Chechenisation", transferring power to the pro-Moscow administration of Akhmad Kadyrov and, after his assassination in 2004, his son Ramzan Kadyrov.

The insurgency continued with terrorist attacks far beyond Chechnya, including the Moscow theatre siege (2002) and the Beslan school siege (2004), in which more than 330 hostages, many of them children, died. Insurgent leaders Maskhadov (2005), Abdul-Halim Sadulayev (2006), and Basayev (2006) were killed in succession, and in 2007 the remnants of the movement re-formed as the Caucasus Emirate.

Human cost and legacy

Estimates of civilian deaths in the second war range from about 25,000 to 50,000 or more, with thousands of enforced disappearances documented by human-rights organisations such as Memorial. Russian military deaths are officially placed in the several thousands. Grozny has since been extensively rebuilt under Ramzan Kadyrov, whose authoritarian rule, backed by Moscow, keeps the republic within the Russian Federation.
