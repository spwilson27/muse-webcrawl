Clovis culture

The Clovis culture is a prehistoric Paleoamerican culture named after the town of Clovis, New Mexico, where its distinctive fluted stone spear points were first recognised in the 1930s. Dating to roughly 13,050–12,750 years ago, it was long considered the earliest widespread culture in the Americas.

Characteristics

Clovis points are bifacial, lanceolate and fluted — a channel flake removed from the base to aid hafting. Associated finds include mammoth and mastodon kill sites, bone and ivory tools, and caches of bifaces such as the Anzick burial in Montana, whose infant genome showed ancestry shared with modern Native Americans.

Clovis first — and its fall

The 'Clovis first' model held that big-game hunters crossed Beringia and spread rapidly south. Sites such as Monte Verde in Chile and Page–Ladson in Florida, securely dated centuries earlier, have overturned it: people reached the Americas before Clovis, though Clovis remains the first continent-wide archaeological horizon.
