Australopithecus anamensis

Temporal rangePliocene, 4.2–3.8 million years ago
First fossilsKanapoi, Kenya (1965; named 1995)

Australopithecus anamensis is a hominin species that lived approximately 4.2 to 3.8 million years ago in eastern Africa. It is one of the earliest known australopithecines and is widely considered ancestral to, or closely related to, Australopithecus afarensis, the species of “Lucy.”

Discovery

First fossils were found at Kanapoi near Lake Turkana, Kenya, in 1965; the species was named in 1995 by Meave Leakey and colleagues — anam means “lake” in Turkana. Fossils also come from Allia Bay (Kenya) and Asa Issie and Woranso-Mille (Ethiopia), where a nearly complete cranium (MRD) was announced in 2019.

Anatomy

A. anamensis combines ape-like features — a projecting face and parallel tooth rows — with human-like bipedal adaptations seen in its tibia, indicating upright walking, and thick tooth enamel suggesting a diet of hard foods.
