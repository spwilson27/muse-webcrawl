Alexander Island, also known as Alexander I Island, is the largest island of Antarctica. It lies in the Bellingshausen Sea west of Palmer Land, Antarctic Peninsula, from which it is separated by Marguerite Bay and George VI Sound. It is about 390 km (240 mi) long and 80 km (50 mi) wide, with an area of roughly 49,070 km², making it the second-largest uninhabited island in the world after Devon Island.

History

The island was discovered on 28 January 1821 by the Russian expedition of Fabian Gottlieb von Bellingshausen, who named it for Tsar Alexander I. It was long thought to be part of the Antarctic mainland and was only proved to be an island in 1940 by Finn Ronne's expedition, which found the sea channel (George VI Sound) separating it from the peninsula beneath an ice shelf.

Features

Alexander Island is mountainous and heavily glaciated; its highest point is Mount Stephenson (2,987 m). The island hosts Fossil Bluff, a British Antarctic Survey refuelling station, and is notable for the subglacial Hodgson Lake. Its territory is claimed by Argentina, Chile and the United Kingdom, claims held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty.
