Firth

Firth is a word in the English and Scots languages used to denote various coastal waters in the United Kingdom, predominantly within Scotland. In the Northern Isles it more usually refers to a smaller inlet, but the word is generally used for long, narrow sea inlets — often the estuary of a river — comparable to a fjord. The word derives from Old Norse fjǫrðr (fjord).

Usage in Scotland

Many of Scotland's major estuaries are firths, including the Firth of Forth (the estuary of the River Forth, crossed by the Forth Bridges near Edinburgh), the Firth of Tay, the Moray Firth (the largest firth in Scotland, near Inverness), the Firth of Clyde on the west coast, the Solway Firth forming part of the border with England, Cromarty Firth, and the Pentland Firth, the strait between mainland Scotland and Orkney. In Orkney and Shetland, smaller inlets such as those around the islands are also called firths.

Usage elsewhere

Outside Scotland, the term is rare in the British Isles, though firths occur as place-names in New Zealand — where they survive from Scottish settlement — notably the Firth of Thames near Auckland. The geology of firths varies: some, like the Firth of Clyde and the firths of the Northern Isles, are drowned glacial valleys akin to fjords, while the great east-coast firths are broad river estuaries.
