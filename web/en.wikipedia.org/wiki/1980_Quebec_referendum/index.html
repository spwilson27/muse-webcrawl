The 1980 Quebec referendum, held on May 20, 1980, was the first referendum on Quebec sovereignty. The provincial government of Premier René Lévesque and the Parti Québécois asked voters for a mandate to negotiate "sovereignty-association"—political independence for Quebec combined with an economic association with Canada. The proposal was defeated, with 59.56 percent voting No and 40.44 percent voting Yes.

Background

The Parti Québécois, founded by Lévesque in 1968, won the 1976 provincial election on a promise to hold a referendum before declaring independence. Its 1979 white paper proposed that Quebec become a sovereign state while retaining the Canadian dollar and a customs and monetary association with Canada. Any final agreement would itself have been submitted to a second referendum.

Campaign

The No campaign, the Comité des Québécois pour le Non, was led provincially by Liberal leader Claude Ryan and powerfully supported by federal Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau, who in a major speech at the Paul Sauvé Arena promised that a No vote would lead to renewed federalism and constitutional change. The Yes side campaigned on the slogan themes of dignity and self-determination. Federalist strength among anglophone and allophone voters, and caution among francophones, underpinned the No victory—though a majority of francophone voters are generally estimated to have supported Yes.

Aftermath

Lévesque accepted the result with the famous line "À la prochaine fois" ("Until next time"). Trudeau proceeded with the patriation of the Canadian Constitution in 1982, which Quebec's National Assembly refused to endorse, sowing grievances that led to the failed Meech Lake and Charlottetown accords and the second sovereignty referendum of 1995.
