Falak-ol-Aflak Castle

Falak-ol-Aflak
LocationKhorramabad, Lorestan, Iran
BuiltSasanian era (224–651 CE)
MaterialsMud-brick, brick, stone, mortar

Falak-ol-Aflak Castle (Persian: فلک الافلاک, "the sky of skies") is a massive fortress on a hill overlooking the city of Khorramabad in Lorestan Province, western Iran. The foundations date to the Sasanian Empire, and the castle was built on the ruins of an earlier stronghold near the Shapurkhast river installations.

Architecture

The fortress covers roughly 5,300 square metres and rises some 40 metres above the surrounding ground at its highest point. Its walls, built of mud-brick, brick, and stone with moisture-resistant layering suited to the humid climate, were originally ringed with twelve towers, of which eight survive. Inside are wells — one about 40 metres deep — courtyards, halls, and a dehumidifying system of channels designed to protect the structure from damp.

History

Known earlier as Shapur-Khast castle and later as the Atabakan citadel, the fort served the Buyid, Seljuk, and later dynasties and was used as a treasury and prison under the Qajars and Pahlavis. Today it houses the Lorestan Museum of anthropology and archaeology, displaying Bronze Age Luristan bronzes. The castle is a national heritage site and was inscribed on UNESCO's tentative list.
