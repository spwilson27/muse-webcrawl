Herengracht

The Herengracht ("Gentlemen's Canal") is one of the three principal canals of Amsterdam's canal belt, the grachtengordel, dug in the early 17th century during the Dutch Golden Age. It is generally considered the most prestigious of the canals, lined with the grand houses of regents, bankers, and merchants.

History and architecture

Work began in 1612 as part of the city's great expansion plan. The stretch between Leidsestraat and Vijzelstraat, where plots were largest and the houses grandest, is known as the Golden Bend (Gouden Bocht). Many façades date from the 17th and 18th centuries in Dutch Baroque and Louis XIV styles. Notable buildings include the Museum Geelvinck-Hinlopen House, the Kattenkabinet cat museum, and the mayor's official residence at Herengracht 502.

Today

The canal belt, including the Herengracht, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010. The Herengracht remains a sought-after residential and office address, and its houses feature in walking tours of historic Amsterdam.
