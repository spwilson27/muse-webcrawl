Ostia Antica

Ostia Antica is an ancient Roman city and harbour town, located about 25 km (15.5 mi) southwest of Rome near the mouth (Latin ostia, "mouths") of the river Tiber. Once Rome's principal seaport, Ostia is now a large archaeological site noted for the excellent preservation of its ancient buildings, magnificent frescoes and impressive mosaics. Because the Tiber has silted up over the centuries, the ruins now lie about 3 km from the sea.

History

Roman tradition dated the founding of Ostia to the fourth king of Rome, Ancus Marcius, around 620 BC, who was said to have established it to exploit the salt pans at the river mouth. Archaeologically, the earliest settlement is a fortified castrum of the 4th century BC. During the Republic, Ostia became the main port of Rome and a naval base; in the Punic Wars it served as a supply base. Under the Empire its population grew to perhaps 50,000–100,000, housed in multi-storey apartment blocks (insulae) of brick-faced concrete, with warehouses (horrea), temples, baths, a theatre, bakeries, taverns and guild halls lining the streets.

The emperors Claudius and Trajan constructed new artificial harbour basins (Portus) to the north, which gradually took over much of Ostia's commercial role. Ostia remained a significant residential and administrative centre into late antiquity, but declined with the fall of the Western Empire and was largely abandoned by the 9th century, its marble later quarried for buildings in Rome, Pisa and elsewhere.

Archaeology

Systematic excavations took place between the late 19th century and the mid-20th, especially under Benito Mussolini in preparation for the planned 1942 Rome World's Fair, when roughly two-thirds of the city was exposed. Notable remains include the theatre (seating about 3,500), the Piazzale delle Corporazioni with its mosaic advertisements of shipping guilds, the Capitolium temple, the Baths of Neptune with their famous black-and-white mosaics, the House of Diana, the Thermopolium (a Roman fast-food bar), and an early synagogue, among the oldest known in Europe.

The modern site

The excavations are open to the public as the Parco Archeologico di Ostia Antica, adjacent to the modern village of Ostia Antica in the municipality of Rome. The site is frequently compared with Pompeii for the insight it gives into everyday Roman urban life, though Ostia was a working commercial city rather than a resort town.
