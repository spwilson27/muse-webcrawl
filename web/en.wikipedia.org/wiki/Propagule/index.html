Propagule

In biology, a propagule is any material that functions in propagating an organism to the next stage in its life cycle, such as by dispersal. Propagules are produced by plants (in the form of seeds or spores), fungi (in the form of spores), and bacteria (for example endospores or microbial cysts). The term is also used in invasion biology and ecology for the units in which organisms are introduced to new environments.

Types

Plant propagules include seeds, spores, bulbs, tubers, and specialized structures such as the pencil-like seedlings of mangroves, which float until they strand and take root. Vegetative propagules arise from asexual reproduction and are genetically identical to the parent, while seeds formed sexually combine genetic material from two parents.

Propagule pressure

In invasion ecology, propagule pressure—the number of individuals introduced and the number of introduction events—is one of the strongest predictors of whether a non-native species becomes established. The concept informs biosecurity and the management of biological invasions, since reducing propagule pressure lowers establishment risk. See also seed dispersal and vegetative reproduction.
