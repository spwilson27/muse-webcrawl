Marcel François Marie Joseph Lefebvre (29 November 1905 – 25 March 1991) was a French Catholic archbishop who became a leading figure of Catholic traditionalism. After serving as a missionary and archbishop in French West Africa and as superior general of the Holy Ghost Fathers, he opposed the reforms of the Second Vatican Council and in 1970 founded the Society of Saint Pius X (SSPX). In 1988 he consecrated four bishops without papal mandate, an act for which the Holy See declared that he had incurred automatic excommunication.

Early life and ministry

Born in Tourcoing to a devout industrial family, Lefebvre studied at the French Seminary in Rome and was ordained in 1929. He joined the Holy Ghost Fathers and served as a missionary in Gabon, where his brother was also a missionary. In 1947 he was appointed apostolic vicar of Dakar and in 1955 became its first archbishop; he also served as apostolic delegate for French-speaking Africa.

Vatican II and traditionalism

Lefebvre attended the Second Vatican Council (1962–1965) and was a leading voice of the conservative Coetus Internationalis Patrum, objecting in particular to religious liberty, ecumenism, and collegiality as framed in the council documents. After the introduction of the revised Mass of Paul VI, he founded a traditionalist seminary at Écône, Switzerland, and the SSPX to train priests for the Tridentine Mass.

Écône consecrations

On 30 June 1988, Lefebvre consecrated four SSPX priests as bishops without the required papal mandate. The Vatican declared the act schismatic and the excommunication of those involved; Lefebvre maintained he had acted out of necessity amid a crisis in the Church. He died in 1991. In 2009 Pope Benedict XVI remitted the excommunications of the four surviving bishops.
