Grandfather clock

Longcase clock
Other namesGrandfather clock, tall-case clock, floor clock
OriginEngland, c. 1670
MechanismWeight-driven pendulum (anchor escapement)
Typical height1.8–2.3 m (6–7.5 ft)

A longcase clock, also known as a grandfather clock (or tall-case or floor clock), is a tall, freestanding, weight-driven pendulum clock, with the pendulum held inside the tower or waist of the case. Clocks of this style became common in England after the invention of the anchor escapement around 1670, which allowed a long seconds pendulum to swing through a narrow arc within a tall case, greatly improving accuracy over earlier clocks.

Design

The case has three main sections: the hood, housing the dial and movement; the waist (trunk), enclosing the pendulum and hanging weights; and the base. Early dials were of brass with silvered chapter rings; painted dials became common after about 1770. Many longcase clocks strike the hours on a bell or gong, and better examples show the date, phases of the moon, or tidal information.

Name

The name "grandfather clock" was popularised by the 1876 song "My Grandfather's Clock" by Henry Clay Work, inspired by a clock at the George Hotel in Piercebridge, Yorkshire, which reportedly stopped when its owner died.
