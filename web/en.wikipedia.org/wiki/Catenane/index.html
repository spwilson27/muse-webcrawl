Catenane

A catenane is a mechanically interlocked molecular architecture consisting of two or more macrocyclic rings linked together like links in a chain, without any covalent bonds between the rings. The name comes from the Latin catena, meaning "chain".

TypeMechanically interlocked molecule
First synthesis1960 (Wasserman, statistical); templated routes followed
RelatedRotaxanes, molecular knots, Borromean rings

Synthesis

Edel Wasserman reported the first catenane in 1960 using a statistical approach with very low yield. Modern syntheses use templating — metal ions, hydrogen bonds, donor–acceptor stacking or hydrophobic effects hold components in crossing geometries before ring-closing reactions lock the links permanently. Jean-Pierre Sauvage's copper-templated catenanes of the 1980s were a landmark.

Significance

Catenanes and their cousins the rotaxanes are prototypes of molecular machines: the rings can rotate or shuttle in response to stimuli. Work on such interlocked molecules contributed to the 2016 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, awarded to Sauvage, J. Fraser Stoddart and Ben Feringa. See also supramolecular chemistry.
