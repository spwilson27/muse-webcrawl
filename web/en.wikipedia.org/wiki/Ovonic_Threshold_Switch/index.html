Ovonic Threshold Switch

An Ovonic Threshold Switch (OTS) is a type of electronic switch made from amorphous chalcogenide glass. It switches from a high-resistance state to a low-resistance state when the applied voltage exceeds a threshold, and returns to the high-resistance state when the current falls below a holding value.

The effect was reported by Stanford R. Ovshinsky in 1968 and formed the basis of his company Energy Conversion Devices and the field he called "ovonics". The switching is volatile — the material does not crystallise, unlike in phase-change memory, where a related chalcogenide is used for non-volatile storage.

OTS devices are used as selector elements in cross-point memory arrays, notably in 3D XPoint memory, where each OTS in series with a phase-change memory cell allows individual cells to be addressed without transistors.
