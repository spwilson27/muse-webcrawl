Konzerthaus Berlin

The Konzerthaus on the Gendarmenmarkt, Berlin

The Konzerthaus Berlin is a concert hall in Berlin, the home of the Konzerthausorchester Berlin. Situated on the Gendarmenmarkt square in the central Mitte district, it was designed by Karl Friedrich Schinkel and built from 1818 to 1821 as a theatre, the Schauspielhaus. Its Great Hall (Großer Saal), seating nearly 1,400, is celebrated for its acoustics and neoclassical interior.

History

The building stands on the site of the earlier Nationaltheater, which burned down in 1817. Schinkel's new Schauspielhaus opened on 26 May 1821 with Goethe's Iphigenie in Tauris and the premiere of Weber's opera Der Freischütz followed weeks later in the same house. The theatre's steps were a focal point of the March Revolution of 1848. Badly damaged by bombing and fire in the Second World War, the building was restored by the East German government and reopened in 1984 as a concert hall for the 750th anniversary of Berlin in 1987. After German reunification it took its present name.

Architecture

Schinkel's design is a landmark of Prussian classicism: a broad staircase leads to an Ionic portico, flanked by two wings, crowned by a pediment with Apollo and the Muses. A quadriga statue above the portico echoes the architect's other Berlin works. The interior contains several performance spaces, including the Great Hall, the Small Hall (Kleiner Saal) and the Werner-Otto-Saal.

Konzerthausorchester

The resident orchestra, founded in 1952 as the Berliner Sinfonie-Orchester, has been led by chief conductors including Kurt Sanderling, Eliahu Inbal, Iván Fischer and, from 2023, Joana Mallwitz. The orchestra performs the bulk of the hall's roughly 550 annual events.
