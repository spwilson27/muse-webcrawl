Motor scooter

A Vespa-style motor scooter

A motor scooter (often simply scooter) is a motorcycle with an underbone or step-through frame, a seat, and a platform for the rider's feet, emphasising comfort and ease of use. Scooters typically have smaller wheels than conventional motorcycles, an enclosed bodywork protecting the rider from the engine and road spray, and automatic or twist-and-go transmission.

History

Early motor scooters appeared in the 1910s and 1920s, but the type became a worldwide phenomenon after the Second World War with the launch of the Italian Vespa by Piaggio in 1946 and the rival Lambretta in 1947. Designed as inexpensive transport for a war-ravaged Europe, they became cultural icons, associated in Britain with the Mod subculture of the 1960s.

Modern scooters

Today scooters range from 50 cc mopeds to large "maxi-scooters" of 850 cc capable of motorway touring. Manufacturers such as Honda, Yamaha, Suzuki, Piaggio and Kymco produce them in large numbers, and they dominate urban transport in much of Asia. Electric scooters, including battery-swap models, have grown rapidly since the 2010s.
