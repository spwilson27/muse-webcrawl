Frobenius manifold

In mathematics, a Frobenius manifold is a flat Riemannian manifold with a compatible structure of a commutative, associative algebra equipped with a nondegenerate invariant bilinear form on each tangent space, varying in a prescribed way. Frobenius manifolds were introduced by Boris Dubrovin in the early 1990s as a geometric axiomatisation of two-dimensional topological field theory.

Definition

Informally, a Frobenius manifold consists of a manifold whose tangent spaces carry the structure of a Frobenius algebra: a commutative associative multiplication with a unit, together with a flat metric that is invariant under the multiplication. The multiplication is encoded in a potential function (or prepotential) whose third derivatives give the structure constants of the algebra; associativity of the multiplication is then equivalent to a system of nonlinear partial differential equations for the potential, the Witten–Dijkgraaf–Verlinde–Verlinde (WDVV) equations, also called the associativity equations.

Examples

The motivating example comes from quantum cohomology: the (small or big) quantum cohomology ring of a smooth projective variety carries the structure of a Frobenius algebra, and its deformation space — the space of cohomology classes used as parameters — is a Frobenius manifold, with the potential given by the generating function of Gromov–Witten invariants. Other fundamental examples arise from singularity theory: the base of a miniversal deformation of an isolated hypersurface singularity carries a Frobenius manifold structure (the Saito–Dubrovin structure), tying the theory to reflection groups and integrable systems.

Related structures

Frobenius manifolds are intimately connected with integrable hierarchies of PDEs of KdV type, isomonodromic deformations, mirror symmetry and the theory of Gromov–Witten invariants. Generalisations include F-manifolds, which drop the metric, and bi-flat structures arising in the theory of integrable systems.
