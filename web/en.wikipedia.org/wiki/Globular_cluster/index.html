Globular cluster

A globular cluster is a spheroidal conglomeration of stars gravitationally bound together, with a higher concentration of stars towards its centre. Globular clusters contain some of the oldest stars in a galaxy, typically 10–13 billion years old.

Globular cluster
Stars: 10,000 to several million
Diameter: ~10–300 light-years
Milky Way clusters: ~150 known

Properties

The Milky Way hosts about 150 globular clusters orbiting in its halo, including Omega Centauri and 47 Tucanae, bright enough to see with the naked eye. Their stars are metal-poor Population II stars. Harlow Shapley used their distribution in 1918 to estimate the Sun's distance from the Galactic Center.

Formation

Clusters are thought to form in intense starbursts, possibly in dwarf galaxies later accreted. Unlike open clusters they remain bound for billions of years. See also Georges Lemaître for cosmology context.
