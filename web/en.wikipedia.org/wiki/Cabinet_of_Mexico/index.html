The Cabinet of Mexico (Spanish: Gabinete de México) is the group of senior officials who head the secretariats of state and other principal bodies of the federal public administration of Mexico. Its members are appointed and may be freely removed by the President of Mexico, the head of the executive branch.

The National Palace in Mexico City, seat of the federal executive.

Composition

The cabinet comprises the Secretaries of the Interior (Gobernación); Foreign Affairs; National Defense; Navy; Security and Citizen Protection; Finance and Public Credit; Welfare; Environment and Natural Resources; Energy; Economy; Agriculture and Rural Development; Infrastructure, Communications and Transportation; Public Education; Health; Labor and Social Welfare; Tourism; Agrarian, Territorial and Urban Development; Culture; and Public Administration, along with the Legal Counsel of the Executive and the head of the Office of the Presidency. The "legal cabinet" of secretaries is complemented by an "expanded cabinet" of heads of decentralized agencies such as Pemex, the Federal Electricity Commission, IMSS, and ISSSTE.

Role

Under Mexico's presidential system, cabinet secretaries are individually responsible to the president rather than collectively responsible to Congress, though the Chamber of Deputies may summon them to testify. Mexico's single six-year presidential term (the sexenio) means a new cabinet takes office every six years, with the president-elect announcing appointments before the December (now October) inauguration.
