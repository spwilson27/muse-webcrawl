Battle of the Bulge

The Battle of the Bulge (16 December 1944 – 25 January 1945), also called the Ardennes Offensive, was Nazi Germany's last major offensive on the Western Front of World War II. Surprise attack through the Ardennes created a deep "bulge" in the Allied line, but the offensive failed to reach Antwerp and consumed Germany's remaining reserves.

Background

Hitler planned to split the Allied armies, cross the Meuse and retake Antwerp, hoping to force a negotiated peace in the west. Some 400,000 men and over 1,400 tanks were secretly assembled opposite a thinly held American sector, achieving complete surprise in bad weather that grounded Allied air power.

Battle

German spearheads — including Kampfgruppe Peiper, whose men murdered American prisoners at Malmedy — advanced toward the Meuse but were blocked at key road hubs. At Bastogne the encircled 101st Airborne held out; asked to surrender, Brig. Gen. Anthony McAuliffe replied "NUTS!" Patton's Third Army pivoted and relieved Bastogne on 26 December as skies cleared and Allied aircraft returned. German spearheads stalled short of the Meuse near Dinant, out of fuel.

Aftermath

By late January the bulge was erased. Germany had lost irreplaceable men, tanks and aircraft (including in the disastrous Operation Bodenplatte), leaving the Rhine defences weakened. It was the largest battle ever fought by the US Army.
