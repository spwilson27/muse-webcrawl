Tábor is a town in the South Bohemian Region of the Czech Republic, situated on the Lužnice River. It is famous for its role in the Hussite Wars: the town was founded in 1420 by radical Hussites and named after Mount Tabor in Galilee, a name laden with biblical significance for the movement.

History

The Hussite settlers organized Tábor as a fortified, egalitarian community in which property was initially held in common — newcomers deposited their possessions in communal chests in the square. The town became the centre of the Taborites, the most radical wing of the Hussite movement, whose armies under commanders such as Jan Žižka (who died in 1424) and Prokop the Great repeatedly defeated crusading forces sent against them. A labyrinth of tunnels and cellars was dug beneath the town, serving as storage and shelter.

Sights

The historic old town preserves a maze of narrow streets around Žižka Square, dominated by the late-Gothic Church of the Transfiguration and a statue of Jan Žižka. The Hussite Museum, housed in the old town hall, documents the movement's history, and visitors can tour portions of the underground tunnels. The Kotnov Castle tower overlooks the river valley.
