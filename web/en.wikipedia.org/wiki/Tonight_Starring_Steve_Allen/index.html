Tonight Starring Steve Allen was an American talk show hosted by Steve Allen that aired on NBC from September 27, 1954, to January 25, 1957. It was the first incarnation of the long-running Tonight Show franchise and is widely credited with inventing the format of the late-night television talk show.

History

The program originated as a local late-night show on NBC's New York station before network president Sylvester "Pat" Weaver moved it to the full network as a late-night companion to the morning show Today. Allen, a pianist, comedian, and composer, brought a loose, improvisational style to the desk, mixing celebrity interviews with comedy sketches, audience participation, and musical performances.

Many of the conventions now standard in late-night television were pioneered on the show: the opening monologue, the house band, comic sidekicks and recurring characters, remote broadcasts, man-on-the-street interviews, and stunts performed outside the studio. Allen's regulars included announcer Gene Rayburn and bandleader Skitch Henderson.

Legacy

Allen left the program in 1957 to concentrate on a prime-time variety series. He was succeeded briefly by a magazine-format version of the show before Jack Paar took over and relaunched the franchise as The Tonight Show Starring Jack Paar. Johnny Carson, Jay Leno, Conan O'Brien, and Jimmy Fallon followed in later decades, but the basic template Allen established — a desk, a couch, a band, and a host who joked about the day's events — remains the template of American late-night television.
