Warner Bros. Discovery, Inc. (WBD) is an American multinational mass media and entertainment conglomerate headquartered in New York City. It was formed on April 8, 2022, through the merger of AT&T's WarnerMedia with Discovery, Inc., in a Reverse Morris Trust transaction valued at about $43 billion.

The Warner Bros. water tower in Burbank, emblem of the studio business.

History

AT&T, which had acquired Time Warner in 2018 and renamed it WarnerMedia, announced in May 2021 that it would spin off the division and merge it with Discovery, led by David Zaslav. The deal closed in April 2022, making Zaslav chief executive of the combined company. WBD moved quickly to cut costs and reduce the roughly $55 billion debt load inherited from the merger, cancelling projects, restructuring its streaming services, and in 2023 combining HBO Max and Discovery+ content into a service branded Max in the United States (renamed back to HBO Max in 2025). In 2025 the company announced plans to split into two publicly traded companies — one holding its studios and streaming businesses, the other its global cable networks.

Operations

The company's businesses include the Warner Bros. film and television studios; premium network HBO; news channel CNN; the former Discovery factual networks (Discovery Channel, HGTV, Food Network, TLC, Animal Planet); Turner entertainment networks TNT and TBS; DC Comics and DC Studios; and sports rights including, historically, a share of March Madness and NHL coverage in the U.S., plus Eurosport internationally.
