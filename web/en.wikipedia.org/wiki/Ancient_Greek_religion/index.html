Ancient Greek religion

RegionAncient Greece and the wider Hellenic world
Principal deitiesThe twelve Olympians
PracticesSacrifice, prayer, festivals, oracles, mystery cults

Ancient Greek religion encompasses the beliefs and ritual practices of the ancient Greeks, centred on the worship of the Olympian gods — Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Aphrodite, Ares, Hephaestus, Demeter, Hermes and Dionysus or Hestia — along with numerous local deities, heroes and nature spirits.

Beliefs and mythology

Greek religion had no creed or central scripture; its mythology, preserved in works such as Hesiod's Theogony and the Homeric epics, told how the gods governed the world and interacted with mortals. Fate stood above even the gods. The afterlife was centred on the shadowy realm of Hades, with the mystery cults, such as the Eleusinian Mysteries, offering initiates hope of a better lot after death.

Practices

Worship took place at sanctuaries and temples, on outdoor altars, and in the home. The central act was sacrifice — libations, burnt offerings and animal sacrifice — accompanied by prayer and processions. City-states held great festivals such as the Panathenaea at Athens and the Olympic Games in honour of Zeus. Oracles, most famously Apollo's at Delphi, were consulted on public and private matters.

Decline

Greek religion gradually lost ground in late antiquity as Christianity spread through the Roman Empire; pagan public worship was suppressed under Christian emperors in the fourth century AD, although elements of the tradition survived in folklore and decisively shaped later European art and literature.
