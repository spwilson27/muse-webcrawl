Hōnen (法然, 1133–1212), born Seishimaru, was a Japanese Buddhist priest and the founder of the Jōdo-shū school of Pure Land Buddhism. He taught that rebirth in Amida Buddha's Pure Land could be attained simply by reciting the nembutsu — "Namu Amida Butsu" ("I take refuge in Amida Buddha") — making salvation available to ordinary people rather than only to monastic elites.

Life

Hōnen was born in Mimasaka Province, the son of a provincial official who was assassinated when Hōnen was a child; the dying father's plea that his son not seek revenge turned the boy toward Buddhism. He was ordained at Mount Hiei, the great Tendai monastery, where he studied widely but became dissatisfied with scholastic practice. Inspired especially by the Chinese master Shandao, he concluded in 1175 that the exclusive practice of the nembutsu was the path suited to the degenerate age of mappō, and began teaching publicly in Kyoto.

Teaching

In his Senchaku Hongan Nembutsushū (1198), Hōnen argued that in the age of mappō all "self-power" practices are beyond people's capacity and that one must rely wholly on Amida's "other-power" through the nembutsu. His teaching drew followers from all classes, including courtiers, warriors, fishermen and prostitutes, provoking hostility from the established temples.

Persecution and legacy

In 1207 the court, under pressure from Mount Hiei and Kōfuku-ji, suppressed the movement: several disciples were executed and Hōnen, aged 74, was exiled to Tosa on Shikoku (later pardoned and settled in Kyoto, where he died in 1212). His disciple Shinran founded Jōdo Shinshū, which became the largest school of Japanese Buddhism. Hōnen's tomb is at Chion-in in Kyoto, the head temple of Jōdo-shū.
