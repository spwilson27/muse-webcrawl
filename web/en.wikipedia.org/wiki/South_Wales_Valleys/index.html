South Wales Valleys

The South Wales Valleys, known locally simply as the Valleys, are a group of industrialised valleys in South Wales, running roughly north–south from the Brecon Beacons toward Cardiff and Newport. They include the Rhondda (Fach and Fawr), Cynon, Taff, Rhymney, Sirhowy, Ebbw, and Garw valleys, among others. The settlements grew around coal mining and ironworking in the 19th century into tightly knit linear communities of terraced housing.

History

Industrialisation transformed thinly populated rural valleys into some of the most densely populated parts of Wales. Mining culture produced strong institutions — trade unions, working men's institutes, male voice choirs, and rugby clubs — but the collapse of deep coal mining after the 1984–85 strike caused lasting economic deprivation, and the Valleys remain among the poorer parts of the United Kingdom, with regeneration programmes continuing.

Geography and transport

Each valley is a narrow, steep-sided trough with a single main road and rail line running its length, converging on Cardiff. The Valleys Metro proposals seek to improve rail services as part of the Cardiff Capital Region.
