The British Nationality Act 1981 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom concerning British nationality since 1 January 1983, when it came into force. It replaced the single status of Citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies, created by the British Nationality Act 1948, with three principal categories of citizenship, and for the first time aligned the status of British citizen with the right of abode in the United Kingdom.

Background

The 1948 Act had created a common status embracing the United Kingdom and its colonies; subsequent Commonwealth Immigrants Acts (1962 and 1968) and the Immigration Act 1971 restricted settlement, producing a mismatch between citizenship and the right to enter and live in Britain. A 1977 Green Paper under the Labour government, and the Conservative government's promise in opposition, led to a comprehensive redefinition. The new Act was shaped by the principle that citizenship should reflect a close connection with the United Kingdom itself rather than with the shrinking empire.

Main provisions

The Act created three categories:

  • British citizenship, for persons closely connected with the United Kingdom, the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man, carrying the right of abode;
  • British Dependent Territories citizenship (renamed British Overseas Territories citizenship by the British Overseas Territories Act 2002), for persons connected with a dependent territory;
  • British Overseas citizenship, a residual category for Citizens of the United Kingdom and Colonies who qualified for neither of the above, generally carrying no right of abode anywhere.

It also retained the statuses of British subject and British protected person in residual form. The automatic acquisition of citizenship by birth in the United Kingdom (jus soli) was abolished: a child born in the UK after commencement is British only if a parent is a British citizen or "settled" in the UK. Citizenship by descent is broadly limited to the first generation born abroad. The Act also removed the earlier provisions under which women could lose or be denied citizenship on marriage, and equalised the rights of mothers and fathers to pass on citizenship.

Subsequent developments

The Act remains the foundation of modern British nationality law but has been amended many times, including by the Nationality, Immigration and Asylum Act 2002, the Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006, and the Nationality and Borders Act 2022. Special provision was made for Hong Kong: the status of British National (Overseas) was created for Hong Kong residents before the 1997 handover, and in 2021 a visa route was opened for BN(O) status holders to settle in the UK.
