Broadcast Music, Inc.

Broadcast Music, Inc. (BMI) is a performance rights organisation in the United States. It collects blanket licence fees from businesses that use music, and distributes the money as royalties to its affiliated songwriters, composers and music publishers. Together with ASCAP and SESAC, it is one of the major American performing rights organisations, representing more than a million songwriters and tens of millions of musical works.

History

BMI was founded in 1939 by American broadcasters as an alternative to ASCAP, whose high licence fees and dominance by established Tin Pan Alley and Broadway writers had caused conflict with radio stations. In 1940–41, when ASCAP's demands led broadcasters to boycott its catalogue, BMI filled the airwaves with music from genres ASCAP had neglected — including rhythm and blues, country, jazz and Latin music — giving many Black, rural and immigrant writers their first route to royalties.

This openness proved decisive in the 1950s: BMI signed many of the writers who created rock and roll, and its catalogue came to dominate popular music. BMI and ASCAP both operate under consent decrees with the US Department of Justice dating from 1941, which regulate their licensing practices.

Operations

BMI issues blanket licences to radio and television stations, streaming services, venues, bars and other businesses, allowing them to perform any work in its repertoire. Performances are tracked through census and sample surveys, and royalties are distributed quarterly. BMI reported annual collections exceeding $1 billion for the first time in 2017 and has since surpassed $1.5 billion in annual revenue.

Awards

BMI presents annual awards honouring the most-performed songs in genres including pop, country, Latin, Christian and film and television music.
