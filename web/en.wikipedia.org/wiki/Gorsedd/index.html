Gorsedd

A gorsedd (Welsh: gorsedd, plural gorseddau) is a community or meeting of modern druids and others who maintain the Welsh cultural tradition, most prominently the Gorsedd of the Bards of the Isle of Britain (Gorsedd Beirdd Ynys Prydain), which conducts the ceremonies of the National Eisteddfod of Wales. The word comes from the Welsh for "throne" or "mound" and originally denoted a bardic assembly.

History

The modern Gorsedd was founded in 1792 by the Welsh antiquarian and poet Iolo Morganwg, who held the first meeting on Primrose Hill in London and claimed — largely on the basis of manuscripts he himself fabricated — that the institution descended from ancient druidic gatherings. Despite the invented antiquity, the Gorsedd became permanently attached to the eisteddfod movement and, from 1819, to the provincial and later National Eisteddfod, where its pageantry frames the major literary prizes.

Structure and ceremony

Members are admitted in three grades distinguished by robe colour: Ovates (green), Bards (blue), and Druids (white). Membership is conferred on poets, writers, musicians, scholars, and others who have made a distinguished contribution to Welsh culture; some are admitted by examination in the Welsh language. The Archdruid, elected for a term of three years, presides over the Gorsedd circles — stone circles erected at eisteddfod sites — at the Chairing and Crowning ceremonies. Related gorseddau exist in Cornwall (Gorsedh Kernow, founded 1928) and Brittany (Goursez Breizh).
