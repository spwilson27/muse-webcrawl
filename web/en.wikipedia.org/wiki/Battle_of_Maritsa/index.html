The Battle of Maritsa, also known as the Battle of Chernomen, was fought on 26 September 1371 between Ottoman forces and an army led by the Serbian brothers Vukašin Mrnjavčević, king of the Serbs, and his brother Despot Jovan Uglješa. It was one of the most consequential battles in the Ottoman conquest of the Balkans.

Background

By the 1360s the Ottomans had established themselves in Thrace, taking Adrianople (Edirne). Uglješa, who ruled lands around Serres, recognised the growing threat and, with his brother Vukašin, raised a large army to drive the Ottomans back while Sultan Murad I was occupied in Anatolia. The Serbian host marched east toward Adrianople, while the Ottoman defence of Thrace rested with the frontier commander Lala Şahin Pasha.

The battle

The Serbian army encamped near the village of Chernomen on the Maritsa river, close to Adrianople. According to the chronicles, the Serbs failed to post adequate guards, and during the night of 25–26 September the Ottoman force launched a surprise attack on the camp. The Serbian army was routed in the darkness; thousands were killed or drowned in the Maritsa as they fled. Both Vukašin and Uglješa were killed. The river was said to have run red with blood, giving rise to the name by which the site was remembered.

Aftermath

The disaster at the Maritsa opened Macedonia and much of the southern Balkans to Ottoman expansion. Many Serbian and Bulgarian lords became Ottoman vassals, and the road was cleared toward the later climactic confrontation at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389. The battle is remembered in Serbian epic tradition as a catastrophe brought on by disunity among the Christian lords.
