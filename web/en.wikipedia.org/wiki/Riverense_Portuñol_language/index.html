Riverense Portuñol (Spanish: portuñol riverense) is a contact language spoken in the border region between Uruguay and Brazil, particularly around the twin cities of Rivera (Uruguay) and Santana do Livramento (Brazil). It arose from centuries of contact between Spanish and Portuguese in a region whose sovereignty shifted repeatedly between the Spanish and Portuguese empires and their successor states. Locally it is also called fronterizo ("border speech"), brasilero or simply portuñol.

Characteristics

Riverense is not a fixed mixture but a continuum of varieties blending Portuguese phonology and vocabulary with both Portuguese and Spanish grammatical structures. Speakers shift along the continuum depending on context, audience and formality. Because Portuguese historically dominated daily life in northern Uruguay while Spanish was the language of the state and schooling, the variety carries Portuguese features embedded in a largely Iberian-Romance grammar shared by both languages. Pronunciation, verb forms and vocabulary may change from sentence to sentence, and speakers typically also command standard Spanish (and often Portuguese) to some degree.

Status and culture

For much of the 20th century Uruguayan language policy sought to hispanicise the north, and Portuñol was stigmatised as a "corrupt" mixture. Since the 1990s and 2000s, academic research and cultural production have increasingly recognised it as a legitimate linguistic heritage: it features in local literature, music and a notable body of poetry (the poet Fabián Severo is its best-known literary exponent). Uruguayan education authorities have acknowledged the dialect in mother-tongue teaching programmes in border schools. Related Portuñol varieties are spoken all along the Brazil–Uruguay border, including in Artigas, Cerro Largo and Rocha departments.
