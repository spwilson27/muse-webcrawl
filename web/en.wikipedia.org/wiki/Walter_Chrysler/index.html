Walter Chrysler

Walter Percy Chrysler (April 2, 1875 – August 18, 1940) was an American automotive industrialist, the founder of the Chrysler Corporation (now part of Stellantis). He began his career in the railroad industry, rising from machinist's apprentice to executive, before moving into automobiles.

Automotive career

Chrysler turned around the struggling Buick Motor Company for General Motors and became a GM vice-president, but left after disputes with William C. Durant. He then rescued the Willys-Overland and Maxwell Motor companies, reorganizing Maxwell into the Chrysler Corporation in 1925. The first Chrysler car, the Chrysler Six of 1924, offered advanced features such as hydraulic brakes and a high-compression engine at a moderate price.

In 1928 Chrysler launched the Plymouth and DeSoto brands and acquired Dodge Brothers, making his company one of the "Big Three" American automakers. The same year he financed the Chrysler Building in New York City, briefly the tallest building in the world, and was named Time magazine's Man of the Year for 1928.
