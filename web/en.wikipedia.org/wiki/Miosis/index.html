Miosis

Miosis is the excessive constriction of the pupil of the eye, the opposite of mydriasis (dilation). Pupil size is controlled by two muscles of the iris: the sphincter pupillae, driven by the parasympathetic nervous system, constricts the pupil, while the dilator pupillae, driven by the sympathetic system, widens it. Miosis results from increased parasympathetic activity or loss of sympathetic tone.

Causes

Physiological miosis occurs in bright light and during near focusing (accommodation). Pathological and pharmacological causes include opioid drugs — pinpoint pupils are a classic sign of opioid overdose — organophosphate and nerve-agent poisoning, and cholinergic eye drops such as pilocarpine used to treat glaucoma. Horner's syndrome, caused by interruption of the sympathetic pathway, produces miosis along with ptosis and reduced sweating. Brainstem (pontine) haemorrhage can also cause pinpoint pupils.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause; opioid-induced miosis accompanied by respiratory depression is treated with naloxone. Agents that dilate the pupil are called mydriatics.
