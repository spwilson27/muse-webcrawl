Thomas Howard, 3rd Duke of Norfolk

Born: 1473
Died: 25 August 1554 (aged 80–81), Kenninghall, Norfolk
Title: 3rd Duke of Norfolk (from 1524)
Offices: Earl Marshal, Lord High Admiral, Lord High Treasurer
Spouses: Anne of York; Elizabeth Stafford

Norfolk, portrait by Holbein

Thomas Howard, 3rd Duke of Norfolk, KG, PC (1473 – 25 August 1554) was an English politician and nobleman of the Tudor era. A soldier, courtier, and the leading conservative magnate of Henry VIII's reign, he was uncle to two of the king's wives — Anne Boleyn and Catherine Howard — and narrowly escaped execution at the end of the reign.

Military career

Howard served as Lord High Admiral and campaigned in Scotland and France. At the Battle of Flodden (1513), where he commanded under his father, the English destroyed the invading Scottish army and killed James IV. He succeeded his father as Duke of Norfolk in 1524 and became the kingdom's premier nobleman, serving as Earl Marshal and Lord High Treasurer.

Politics and religion

Norfolk maneuvered through the factional struggles of the court: he helped engineer the fall of Cardinal Wolsey, presided over the trial of his niece Anne Boleyn in 1536, and crushed the Pilgrimage of Grace, the great northern rebellion against the Dissolution of the Monasteries, with ruthless reprisals in 1537. A religious conservative, he opposed Thomas Cromwell's evangelical reforms and secured the conservative Act of Six Articles (1539).

Downfall and restoration

In December 1546 Norfolk and his son, the Earl of Surrey, were arrested for treason. Surrey was executed in January 1547; Norfolk, condemned by attainder, was saved only because Henry VIII died the night before the scheduled execution. He remained in the Tower under Edward VI, was released and restored by Mary I in 1553, and died at Kenninghall in 1554.
