Organozinc chemistry

Organozinc chemistry is the study of organozinc compounds, chemical compounds containing carbon–zinc bonds. Organozinc reagents were among the first organometallic compounds made — Edward Frankland prepared diethylzinc in 1848 — and they remain valuable in organic synthesis because they are milder and more functional-group-tolerant than organolithium or Grignard reagents.

Classes and preparation

Important classes include diorganozincs (R2Zn), organozinc halides (RZnX), and zincates. They are prepared by oxidative addition of zinc metal to organic halides, by transmetalation, or by halogen–zinc exchange.

Reactions

Signature transformations include the Reformatsky reaction, the Simmons–Smith cyclopropanation, and the Negishi cross-coupling, in which organozinc reagents couple with organic halides under palladium or nickel catalysis — work recognised by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
