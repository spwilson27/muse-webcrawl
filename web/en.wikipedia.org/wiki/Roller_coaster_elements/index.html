Roller coaster elements

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Roller coaster elements are the individual parts of roller coaster design and operation, such as a track, hill, loop, or turn. Variations in normal track movement that add thrill or excitement to the ride are often termed "thrill elements".

Basic elements

The lift hill raises the train to gain potential energy, traditionally by chain lift, though modern coasters may use launch systems (hydraulic, LSM, or LIM) instead. Bunny hills and camelbacks produce airtime — the sensation of weightlessness from negative g-forces. Banked turns, over-banked turns, and horseshoe turns redirect the train laterally, while helices sustain positive g-forces. A brake run slows or stops the train, and block sections keep multiple trains safely separated.

Inversions

Inverting elements turn riders upside down. The classic vertical loop uses a clothoid shape to moderate g-forces. Other inversions include the corkscrew, Immelmann, dive loop, cobra roll, zero-g roll, pretzel loop, and heartline roll. Steel coasters hold the record for most inversions, with designs exceeding fourteen. Wooden coasters historically lacked inversions, though modern hybrid designs incorporate them.

Other features

Tunnels, headchoppers, water elements such as splashdowns, and on-ride theming add variety. Designers measure performance in terms of positive and negative g-forces, laterals, pacing, and airtime, balancing intensity with rider comfort and safety standards.
