Light cone

In special and general relativity, a light cone (or "null cone") is the path that a flash of light, emanating from a single event (localized to a single point in space and a single moment in time) and traveling in all directions, would take through spacetime.

Construction

If the light is imagined in two dimensions, the flash spreads out in an expanding circle, and a graph plotting time against the spatial dimensions traces a cone. In three spatial dimensions the expanding sphere gives a four-dimensional hypercone. Conventionally, the vertical axis of the diagram represents time and the horizontal axes represent space, with the apex of the cone at the event where the flash is emitted.

Future and past light cones

The cone divides into two halves. The future light cone contains all events that can be reached by a signal traveling at or below the speed of light from the apex event — everything that the event can possibly influence. The past light cone contains all events from which a light signal could have reached the apex — everything that could have influenced it. Events outside the cone (in the region sometimes called "elsewhere") are spacelike separated from the apex: no signal can connect them to it, and their time ordering relative to the apex is frame-dependent.

Mathematical description

In Minkowski spacetime the light cone of the origin is the set of points satisfying x² + y² + z² − c²t² = 0, the locus of null intervals. In general relativity, light cones are defined locally by the metric tensor and can tilt and distort near massive bodies, a picture that underlies the causal structure of spacetime.
