Xalisco (Spanish pronunciation: [xaˈlisko]) is a city and municipality in the Mexican state of Nayarit. It lies immediately south of the state capital, Tepic, and forms part of the Tepic metropolitan area. The name derives from the Nahuatl xalli ("sand") and ixco ("on the surface" or "face"), meaning roughly "place on the sandy surface."

History

The area was inhabited in pre-Hispanic times by indigenous peoples of the region. After the Spanish conquest of the region in the 1530s, Xalisco was established as a colonial settlement; a Franciscan convent was founded in the 1540s. During the colonial period it was part of the province of Nueva Galicia.

Geography and economy

The municipality occupies terrain at the foot of the San Juan volcanic hills near Tepic. Agriculture, including sugarcane, coffee, and fruit production, has traditionally been important to the local economy, and the city has grown as a residential and commercial extension of the state capital. Xalisco is known locally for its coffee production and for the annual fair held in August.
