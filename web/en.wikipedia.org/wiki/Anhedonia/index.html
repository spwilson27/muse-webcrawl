Anhedonia

Anhedonia
Loss of the capacity to experience pleasure
FieldPsychiatry, clinical psychology
Associated withMajor depression, schizophrenia, substance use disorders
Term coinedThéodule-Armand Ribot (1896)

Anhedonia is a diverse array of deficits in hedonic function, including reduced motivation or ability to experience pleasure. It is a core symptom of major depressive disorder and a negative symptom of schizophrenia, and also occurs in substance use disorders, Parkinson's disease and eating disorders.

Types

Clinicians commonly distinguish social anhedonia (reduced pleasure from interpersonal contact) from physical anhedonia (reduced pleasure from food, touch and other sensations). Research further separates anticipatory pleasure (wanting and motivation, "desire"), consummatory pleasure (enjoyment in the moment, "liking") and impairments in reward learning; these components can dissociate.

Mechanisms

Anhedonia is associated with dysfunction of the brain's reward circuitry, particularly dopaminergic signalling in the mesolimbic pathway and the ventral striatum/nucleus accumbens. It predicts poorer treatment outcomes and is linked to suicide risk independently of depressed mood severity.

Assessment and treatment

Common measures include the Snaith–Hamilton Pleasure Scale (SHAPS) and the Chapman scales for social and physical anhedonia. There is no specific approved drug treatment; management targets the underlying condition, and behavioural activation, exercise and some dopaminergic agents and psychotherapies show promise in research.
