The Abbey of Our Lady of Gethsemani is a Catholic monastery near Bardstown, Kentucky, belonging to the Order of Cistercians of the Strict Observance (Trappists). Founded in 1848 by monks from Melleray Abbey in France under Dom Eutropius Proust, it is the oldest Trappist monastery in the United States still in operation and the mother house of several American foundations.

History

Forty-four monks from Melleray arrived in Kentucky in December 1848, first settling at a Sisters of Loretto property before establishing the present site. The abbey survived the Civil War — the surrounding county saw fighting at Bardstown — and grew through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, founding daughter houses including monasteries in Georgia (Conyers), South Carolina (Mepkin), Utah and California. Its church, a simple white basilica, and its enclosure walls are landmarks of Nelson County.

Thomas Merton

Gethsemani is best known as the home of the writer and contemplative Thomas Merton, who entered in 1941 and wrote his bestselling autobiography The Seven Storey Mountain (1948) there under the name Father Louis. Merton's grave in the abbey cemetery remains a place of pilgrimage. In 1956 the Dalai Lama's later friend and interlocutor spent his final years partly at a hermitage on the abbey grounds; he died in Bangkok in 1968. In 1993 the Dalai Lama visited Gethsemani for an interreligious dialogue, part of the abbey's role in Christian–Buddhist encounter.

Life and economy

The community follows the Rule of St Benedict with Trappist austerity: silence, common prayer of the Liturgy of the Hours beginning in the early morning, and manual labour. The monks support themselves by making cheese, fruitcake and bourbon fudge sold by mail order, by farming, and by hosting retreatants in a guesthouse open to visitors of all faiths.
