Ubuntu Touch

Ubuntu Touch is a mobile version of the Ubuntu operating system, designed around a gesture-based interface intended to converge phone, tablet and desktop computing. It was developed by Canonical beginning in 2011 and announced in 2013.

History

Ubuntu Touch shipped on a small number of commercial devices, including phones from BQ and Meizu, but failed to gain a significant market share against Android and iOS. In April 2017 Canonical ended development as part of a wider refocusing on cloud and server products. The community project UBports took over development and continues to maintain and port the system to a range of devices.

Features

The interface uses edge swipes rather than buttons to navigate, and its Lomiri shell (formerly Unity 8) aims to provide a convergent experience: connected to a monitor, a phone can present a desktop-style interface. Applications are distributed through the OpenStore.
