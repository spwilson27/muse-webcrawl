Rump Parliament

Period1648–1653, restored 1659–1660
Preceded byLong Parliament
EventPride's Purge (6 December 1648)

The Rump Parliament was the English Parliament after Colonel Thomas Pride purged the Long Parliament on 6 December 1648 of those members hostile to the New Model Army's intention to try Charles I for high treason.

History

The remaining "Rump" — about 200 of 470 members, with far fewer attending — established the High Court that tried and executed Charles I in January 1649, abolished the monarchy and House of Lords, and declared the Commonwealth of England. It governed through the Council of State, fought the Anglo-Dutch War, and legislated on trade (Navigation Act 1651).

Dissolution

Oliver Cromwell, frustrated by its self-perpetuation and corruption, famously dissolved it on 20 April 1653 ("In the name of God, go!"). The Rump was briefly restored in 1659–60 before General Monck readmitted excluded members, paving the way for the Restoration. See Rotten borough, Robert Walpole.
