The Pensacola class was a class of two heavy cruisers of the United States Navy, the first American "treaty cruisers" designed under the limits of the Washington Naval Treaty of 1922.

Design

To mount ten 8-inch guns — in twin and triple turrets in an unusual superfiring arrangement — within the 10,000-ton treaty limit, the ships were lightly armoured and initially top-heavy, rolling badly until modified with additional ballast and altered bilge keels. Originally classified as light cruisers because of their thin armour, they were redesignated heavy cruisers in 1931 because of their gun calibre.

Service

Pensacola and Salt Lake City were commissioned in 1929 and 1930 and served throughout the Second World War in the Pacific, fighting at Midway, Guadalcanal, the Aleutians and the great carrier battles of 1944–45. Both survived the war, were used as target ships in the Bikini atomic tests of 1946, and were sunk as targets in 1948.
