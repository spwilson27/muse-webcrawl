Hand cannon

TypeEarly firearm
Place of originChina
In use13th century – 16th century

The hand cannon (Chinese: 手銃, shǒuchòng), also known as the hand gonne in its early European forms, is the earliest known type of true firearm: a simple metal barrel, closed at the breech except for a small touch hole, mounted on a wooden stock or held with a socket over a pole. It appeared in China by the 13th century and spread across Eurasia, remaining in use into the 1500s before being superseded by the arquebus with its mechanical lock.

History

Gunpowder weapons developed in Song and Jin dynasty China from fire lances into metal-barrelled guns during the 12th and 13th centuries. The oldest surviving hand cannon, the Heilongjiang hand cannon, is dated to around 1288, and the bronze Xanadu gun bears an inscription dating it to 1298. A bronze cannon excavated in Inner Mongolia has been dated by some scholars to the Western Xia period, possibly as early as 1227.

The technology travelled west through the Mongol world. Hand cannons appear in the Middle East and Europe by the early 14th century; an illustration in Walter de Milemete's manuscript of 1326 shows a vase-shaped cannon firing a large arrow, and small bronze hand gonnes were used in sieges and by shipboard and garrison troops across Europe.

Design and use

A hand cannon consists of a barrel of cast bronze or wrought iron with a touch hole near the breech. Early examples were socketed onto a wooden haft, which the gunner tucked under the arm, braced against the chest, or rested on a wall or forked support. The charge of powder and projectile — a stone, iron ball, or cluster of shot — was loaded from the muzzle. To fire, the gunner applied a hot iron wire, ember, or length of slow match to the touch hole. Accuracy and rate of fire were poor by later standards, but the weapon's noise, smoke, and ability to penetrate armour at short range made it a valued auxiliary weapon, especially in sieges and naval fighting.

Legacy

The hand cannon established the basic form of all later firearms: a closed tube in which expanding powder gas propels a projectile. Adding a stock shaped for the shoulder, a serpentine lever to hold the match, and eventually flint and percussion locks turned it into the arquebus, musket, and rifle.
