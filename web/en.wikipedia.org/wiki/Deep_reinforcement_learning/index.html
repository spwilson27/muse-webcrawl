Deep reinforcement learning

Deep reinforcement learning (deep RL) is a subfield of machine learning that combines reinforcement learning (RL) and deep learning. RL considers the problem of a computational agent learning to make decisions by trial and error. Deep RL incorporates deep learning into the solution, allowing agents to make decisions from unstructured input data — such as raw pixels — without manual engineering of the state space.

History

Early successes in combining neural networks with RL included Gerald Tesauro's TD-Gammon (1992), which learned backgammon at a strong level through self-play. The field took off in 2013–2015 when DeepMind introduced the Deep Q-Network (DQN), which learned to play dozens of Atari 2600 games directly from screen pixels, reaching human-level performance on many of them. Subsequent milestones included AlphaGo and AlphaZero in board games, OpenAI Five in Dota 2, and AlphaStar in StarCraft II. Deep RL methods, particularly proximal policy optimization (PPO), later became central to fine-tuning large language models via reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

Methods

Value-based methods

Value-based algorithms estimate the expected return of taking an action in a state. DQN approximates the optimal action-value function with a neural network, stabilizing training with an experience replay buffer and a separate target network. Improvements include Double DQN, dueling network architectures, prioritized replay, and distributional RL (such as C51 and Rainbow).

Policy-based and actor–critic methods

Policy-gradient methods optimize a parameterized policy directly. Actor–critic algorithms — including A3C/A2C, DDPG, TD3, SAC, and PPO — combine a learned value function (critic) with a policy (actor) to reduce variance and improve stability. These methods are widely used in continuous-control tasks such as robotics.

Model-based methods

Model-based deep RL learns or uses a model of the environment's dynamics to plan, as in MuZero, improving sample efficiency at the cost of greater complexity.

Challenges

Deep RL is notoriously sample-inefficient and sensitive to hyperparameters and random seeds; results can be difficult to reproduce. Other open problems include safe exploration, credit assignment over long horizons, generalization to new environments, and reward specification — the difficulty of designing reward functions that capture what designers actually want, which leads to "reward hacking" behaviors.
