Challenger 3

The Challenger 3 is a British main battle tank being developed as a major upgrade of the Challenger 2 for the British Army. Announced under the Challenger 2 Life Extension Programme (LEP), the conversion replaces the Challenger 2's rifled 120 mm gun and turret with an entirely new turret built by Rheinmetall mounting the smoothbore 120 mm L55A1 gun — the same class of weapon used by the Leopard 2 — along with new sights, digital architecture, and enhanced protection. The programme covers 148 tanks.

Background

The Challenger 2 entered service in 1998 armed with the L30A1 rifled gun, which used ammunition unique to the British Army. By the 2010s the fleet faced obsolescence in firepower, sights, and electronics. After studies of options including outright replacement, the Ministry of Defence selected an upgrade path. In May 2021 a contract worth about £800 million was awarded to Rheinmetall BAE Systems Land (RBSL) to convert 148 Challenger 2 hulls to the Challenger 3 standard at Telford, England.

Design changes

  • New turret and gun: a fully digitised turret with the 120 mm L55A1 smoothbore gun, allowing Challenger 3 to fire standard NATO smoothbore ammunition, including programmable high-explosive rounds and modern kinetic-energy penetrators.
  • Sights: new primary and secondary sights with automatic target detection and tracking for commander and gunner.
  • Protection: provision for the new modular EPSOM and FARNHAM armour packages and for fitting an active protection system; improved under-body and roof protection.
  • Digital architecture: an open electronic architecture, new day/night all-weather cameras, and enhanced power generation; a digital ("glass") backbone shared with other armoured vehicles.
  • Mobility: upgraded suspension (third-generation hydrogas), improved cooling, and an upgraded engine filtration system; the CV12 engine is retained with enhancements.

Programme status

The first pre-production Challenger 3 was unveiled in early 2024, and live-fire trials of the new turret and gun began in Germany in 2024. Challenger 3 is planned to reach initial operating capability around 2027, with full operating capability in the early 2030s, serving with the Royal Tank Regiment and the Queen's Royal Hussars and forming, with the Ajax reconnaissance family and Boxer mechanised infantry vehicles, the core of the Army's armoured brigades. Its planned out-of-service date is around 2040.
