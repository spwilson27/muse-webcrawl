Alkaptonuria

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

GeneHGD (homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase)
InheritanceAutosomal recessive
PrevalenceAbout 1 in 250,000–1,000,000 worldwide; higher in Slovakia
SymptomsDark urine, ochronosis, joint disease

Alkaptonuria is a rare inherited disorder in which the body cannot properly break down the amino acids tyrosine and phenylalanine. Deficiency of the enzyme homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase, encoded by the HGD gene, causes homogentisic acid to accumulate and be excreted in urine, which turns dark on standing — giving the condition its popular name, "black urine disease".

Signs and symptoms

The darkening of urine is often the only sign in infancy, detectable as black staining of nappies. Over decades, homogentisic acid polymerises into a dark pigment that deposits in connective tissues — a process called ochronosis. Pigmentation appears as grey-blue discolouration of the ears and the whites of the eyes, and deposition in cartilage causes a destructive arthritis of the spine and large joints, typically from the fourth decade. Kidney and prostate stones and heart-valve disease also occur.

History

The biochemist Archibald Garrod studied alkaptonuria in the early 1900s and in 1902 made it the first human disease recognised as following Mendelian recessive inheritance — his concept of the "inborn error of metabolism". The HGD gene was identified as the cause in 1996.

Treatment

There is no cure. Nitisinone, a drug that blocks the upstream production of homogentisic acid, markedly lowers its levels and has been approved for alkaptonuria in the European Union; it was earlier used for hereditary tyrosinemia type I. Dietary restriction of tyrosine and phenylalanine, vitamin C, physiotherapy and joint replacement surgery are supportive measures.
