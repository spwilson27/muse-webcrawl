The Korea Football Association (KFA; Korean: 대한축구협회) is the governing body of football in South Korea. It organizes the South Korea national football teams, including the men's and women's senior sides, and oversees the domestic game in cooperation with the professional K League and the Korea Football League system.

History

Football was introduced to Korea in the late nineteenth century, and an early governing body was formed in 1928, followed by the establishment of the Joseon Football Association in 1933. After liberation from Japanese rule, the association was reorganized and joined FIFA in 1948, the year South Korea fielded its first national team at the London Olympics. The KFA was a founding member of the Asian Football Confederation in 1954.

National teams

The men's national team, nicknamed the Red Devils for its supporters, has qualified for eleven FIFA World Cups, reaching the semi-finals on home soil in 2002 — the best finish by an Asian team. The team has won the AFC Asian Cup twice, in 1956 and 1960. The women's national team has appeared at multiple Women's World Cups, and Korean youth sides have achieved strong results at FIFA youth tournaments.

Domestic competitions

The KFA runs the Korea Cup (formerly the FA Cup) knockout competition and governs the semi-professional and amateur league pyramid below the K League 1 and K League 2, which are run by the Korea Professional Football League. It also administers futsal and beach soccer in South Korea.
