Robert B. Silvers

Born: December 31, 1929, Mineola, New York
Died: March 20, 2017, New York City
Occupation: Editor
Known for: Co-founder and editor of The New York Review of Books

Robert Benjamin Silvers (December 31, 1929 – March 20, 2017) was an American editor who co-founded The New York Review of Books in 1963 and edited it for more than fifty years, shaping it into one of the most influential literary and political journals in the English-speaking world.

Early life and career

Silvers was born on Long Island and educated at Yale, graduating in 1947 at seventeen. He studied law at Yale without taking a degree, served as press secretary to Connecticut governor Chester Bowles, and worked in Paris for NATO before joining The Paris Review as its Paris editor. He later became an associate editor of Harper's Magazine.

The New York Review of Books

During the 1963 New York newspaper strike, Silvers, Barbara Epstein, Jason Epstein, and Elizabeth Hardwick founded The New York Review of Books, with publisher A. Whitney Ellsworth. Silvers and Barbara Epstein co-edited the paper until her death in 2006; Silvers then edited it alone until his own death in 2017. Under his famously meticulous editorship—the stuff of publishing legend—the Review published long, argumentative essays by writers such as Susan Sontag, Gore Vidal, Joan Didion, Oliver Sacks, and Václav Havel, combining literary criticism with reporting and political commentary, notably opposition to the Vietnam and Iraq wars. He also edited numerous anthologies drawn from the magazine.

Honors

Silvers received the National Humanities Medal in 2013 and was made a Chevalier of the French Legion of Honour. His papers and the inner workings of his editorship were documented in the 2014 film The 50 Year Argument.
