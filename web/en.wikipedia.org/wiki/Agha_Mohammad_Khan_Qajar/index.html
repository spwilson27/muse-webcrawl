Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar (Persian: آقا محمد خان قاجار; 14 March 1742 – 17 June 1797) was the founder of the Qajar dynasty of Iran, reigning from 1789 until his assassination in 1797. After decades of civil war following the fall of the Zand dynasty, he reunified Iran by brutal but effective campaigning, made Tehran his capital, and reasserted Persian control over parts of the Caucasus.

Early life

A son of the chief of the Qoyunlu branch of the Qajar tribe, he was castrated as a young child — reportedly on the orders of the Afsharid ruler Adel Shah — and thus bore the title Agha, used for court eunuchs. He spent years as a hostage at the Zand court in Shiraz, escaping in 1779 on Karim Khan Zand's death to begin his bid for power from his base at Astarabad.

Reunification of Iran

Through relentless campaigns he defeated the Zands — capturing Lotf Ali Khan in 1794 and ordering him blinded and executed — subdued rival Qajar factions, and took Kerman, where the population was massacred or blinded in reprisal. In 1795 he invaded Georgia, a Russian protectorate, and sacked Tbilisi, carrying thousands into slavery. He was crowned Shah in 1796 on the Mughan plain. His cruelty was notorious, but his campaigns recreated a unified Iranian state roughly within its earlier borders.

Assassination and succession

In June 1797, while campaigning again in the Caucasus, he was assassinated at Shusha by two servants he had sentenced to death the previous day and unwisely left at liberty overnight. He was succeeded by his nephew Fath-Ali Shah.
