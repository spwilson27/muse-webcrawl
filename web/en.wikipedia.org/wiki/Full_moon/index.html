A full moon is the lunar phase that occurs when the Moon appears fully illuminated from Earth's perspective. This happens when Earth is situated between the Sun and the Moon, so that the Moon's sunlit hemisphere faces Earth. Full moons occur roughly once a month, every 29.53 days (one synodic month), and the Moon rises at about sunset and sets at about sunrise.

Characteristics

At full moon the near side of the Moon is almost entirely sunlit, which washes out surface shading and makes craters hard to see except for bright ray systems such as Tycho's. Because the lunar orbit is tilted relative to the ecliptic, a lunar eclipse occurs only when a full moon coincides with the Moon's crossing of a node. When the full moon occurs near perigee it appears slightly larger and brighter, a phenomenon popularly called a "supermoon".

In calendars and folklore

Many traditional calendars are lunar or lunisolar, and festivals are often keyed to the full moon: examples include Passover and Easter (set by the first full moon after the spring equinox in the ecclesiastical computus), Vesak, and the Mid-Autumn Festival. Traditional English names for the full moons of the year include Wolf Moon, Harvest Moon and Hunter's Moon. Folklore has long linked the full moon with madness (hence "lunatic") and with werewolves, although scientific studies find little evidence for behavioural effects.
