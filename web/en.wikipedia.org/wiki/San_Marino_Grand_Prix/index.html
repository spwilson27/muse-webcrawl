The San Marino Grand Prix (Italian: Gran Premio di San Marino) was a Formula One championship race held at the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari in Imola, Italy, from 1981 to 2006. Although named for the nearby microstate of San Marino, the race took place entirely on Italian soil; the title allowed Italy to host a second Grand Prix alongside the Italian Grand Prix at Monza.

History

The first Formula One race at Imola was the non-championship Dino Ferrari Grand Prix in 1979, followed by the 1980 Italian Grand Prix. From 1981 the circuit hosted the San Marino Grand Prix annually. The event is most somberly remembered for the 1994 race weekend, among the darkest in Formula One history: Austrian rookie Roland Ratzenberger was killed in qualifying on 30 April, and three-time world champion Ayrton Senna died in a crash at the Tamburello corner during the race on 1 May 1994. The deaths prompted sweeping safety reforms in the sport and a redesign of the Imola circuit.

Michael Schumacher won the race a record seven times, including the final edition in 2006. After that race the event was dropped from the calendar amid circuit contract disputes; Formula One returned to Imola in 2020 under the new title of the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix.

Winners

Repeat winners include Michael Schumacher (7), Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna (3 each), and Nigel Mansell and Damon Hill (2 each). Ferrari and Williams share the constructors' record with eight victories apiece.
