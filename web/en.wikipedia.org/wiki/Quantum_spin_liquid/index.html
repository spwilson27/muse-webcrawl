Quantum spin liquid

In condensed matter physics, a quantum spin liquid is a phase of matter that can be formed by interacting quantum spins in certain magnetic materials. Unlike a conventional magnet, which freezes into an ordered pattern of spins at low temperature, a quantum spin liquid remains dynamically disordered — "liquid-like" — even at absolute zero, because strong quantum fluctuations prevent the spins from ordering. The state is characterised by long-range quantum entanglement and emergent fractionalised excitations.

History and theory

The concept originates with Philip Anderson's 1973 proposal of a "resonating valence bond" (RVB) state for the triangular-lattice Heisenberg antiferromagnet, in which spins pair into singlet bonds that resonate among many configurations. Anderson revived the idea in 1987 as a candidate theory for high-temperature superconductivity. Later theory classified spin liquids by their emergent gauge structure (for example Z2 and U(1) spin liquids) and topological order, and Alexei Kitaev's exactly solvable honeycomb model (2006) demonstrated a spin liquid hosting Majorana fermions and anyons, excitations with fractional quantum numbers.

Signatures

Experimentally, a candidate spin liquid shows:

  • No magnetic ordering or spin freezing down to temperatures far below the interaction scale, verified by muon spin spectroscopy and neutron scattering
  • A broad continuum of magnetic excitations in inelastic neutron scattering, instead of sharp spin waves, indicating fractionalisation into spinons
  • Heat transport or specific heat inconsistent with conventional quasiparticles
  • Strong geometric frustration, often from triangular or kagome lattices of antiferromagnetically coupled spins

Candidate materials

Promising candidates include the organic salts κ-(BEDT-TTF)2Cu2(CN)3 and EtMe3Sb[Pd(dmit)2]2, the kagome mineral herbertsmithite (ZnCu3(OH)6Cl2), and the honeycomb material α-RuCl3, which approaches the Kitaev spin liquid under applied magnetic fields. Because their entangled excitations may be robust against local noise, quantum spin liquids are also of interest for topological quantum computation.
