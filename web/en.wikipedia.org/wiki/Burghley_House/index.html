Burghley House

Burghley House is a grand sixteenth-century English country house near Stamford, Lincolnshire, on the border with Cambridgeshire. It is one of the principal surviving examples of Elizabethan prodigy house architecture, built between 1555 and 1587 by Sir William Cecil, Lord Burghley, the chief adviser and Lord High Treasurer of Queen Elizabeth I. The house remains the home of the Cecil family, Earls (later Marquesses) of Exeter.

Architecture

The exterior, built in a French-influenced classicism around a courtyard, bristles with obelisks, turrets and strapwork and presents a largely unaltered Elizabethan silhouette — "an image of the founder's pride", as it has been called. Inside, the interiors were substantially remodelled in the late 17th century by John Cecil, 5th Earl of Exeter, whose state rooms contain celebrated painted ceilings and walls by Antonio Verrio (including the Heaven Room) and wood carving attributed to Grinling Gibbons. The chapel, great hall and the George Rooms among others display one of Britain's finest private collections of paintings, including works by Van Dyck, Gainsborough and Reynolds, together with outstanding ceramics and furniture.

Parkland and events

The park was landscaped by Lancelot "Capability" Brown in the 18th century and contains a deer park, a lake, and — added in the 21st century — the Garden of Surprises and a sculpture garden. Since 1961 the park has hosted the Burghley Horse Trials, one of the world's leading three-day eventing competitions and a CCI5* event. The house has appeared frequently on screen, including in the film Pride & Prejudice (2005) as Rosings Park and in The Crown.
