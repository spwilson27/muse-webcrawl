Leading

In typography, leading (pronounced "ledding") is the space between adjacent lines of type; the exact definition varies. In the days of metal type, leading was measured by the thin strips of lead inserted between lines of type in a composing stick to increase the vertical distance between them. In modern digital typesetting it is generally understood as the distance from one baseline of text to the next, and is controlled in CSS by the line-height property.

Terminology and measurement

Leading is usually expressed together with the type size, as in "10 on 12" or "10/12", meaning 10-point type set with 12 points from baseline to baseline. The difference between the leading and the type size is sometimes called the line gap or interline spacing. Type set with no extra space is described as "set solid".

Effect on readability

Appropriate leading makes text easier to read: lines that are too close collide visually and tire the reader, while lines too far apart break the text into disconnected strips and make it hard to find the start of the next line. As a rule of thumb, body text is often set with leading of about 120% of the type size, with longer line lengths generally needing more leading. Headlines, by contrast, are frequently set solid or even with negative relative leading.

In digital systems

Page-layout and web software inherited the metal-type vocabulary. In CSS, line-height may be given as a multiple of the font size, a length, or a percentage; fonts also carry built-in line-gap metrics that differ between operating systems, which is why identical stylesheets can produce slightly different line spacing across platforms.
