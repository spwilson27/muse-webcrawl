Khuresh

Khuresh (Tuvan: Хүреш) is the traditional form of wrestling practised by the Tuvan people of Tuva in southern Siberia, closely related to Mongolian wrestling (bökh). It is one of the "three games of men" contested at festivals alongside horse racing and archery.

Rules and customs

Wrestlers wear traditional costume including the shuudak, a cropped jacket with long sleeves and an open chest — said, in legend, to prove that no competitor is a woman. Matches take place outdoors on grass or sand; a wrestler loses when any part of the body other than the soles of the feet touches the ground. Before and after bouts, wrestlers perform an eagle-like dance (devis) mimicking the flight of a bird, led by a herald (zasuul) who sings praises of the wrestlers. Competitions are typically single-elimination, and champions receive animal-based titles.
