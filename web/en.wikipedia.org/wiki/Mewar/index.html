Mewar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The white marble palaces of Udaipur seen across Lake Pichola at dusk
The white marble palaces of Udaipur seen across Lake Pichola at dusk

Mewar is a region in south-central Rajasthan, western India, historically a Rajput kingdom ruled by the Sisodia dynasty from capitals at Nagda, Chittorgarh and finally Udaipur (founded 1559, capital from the 1560s). The Ranas of Mewar styled themselves regents (diwan) of the god Eklingji and were famed among Rajput states for refusing matrimonial submission to the Mughals; Maharana Pratap's resistance after the fall of Chittorgarh (1568) and the Battle of Haldighati (1576) made Mewar the emblem of Rajput honour.

History

Founded by Bappa Rawal in the eighth century (dynastic legend), Mewar's fortress-capital Chittorgarh was sacked three times — by Alauddin Khalji (1303), Bahadur Shah of Gujarat (1535) and Akbar (1568) — each remembered with sieges and jauhar. Udai Singh II founded Udaipur in the Aravalli hills; Pratap fought on from the hills until his death (1597), and his son Amar Singh made peace with Jahangir (1615) on face-saving terms.

Later

Mewar accepted British paramountcy in 1818, joined Rajasthan in 1948–49, and its royal family (the Maharanas, lately of the House now headed by Vishvaraj Singh after a contested succession) remains central to Udaipur's identity and tourism. The region covers today's Udaipur, Chittorgarh, Rajsamand and Bhilwara districts.
