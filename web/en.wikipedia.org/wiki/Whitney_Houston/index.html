Whitney Houston

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Whitney Houston singing the national anthem at Super Bowl XXV in 1991, in a white tracksuit — the performance that became the standard]

Whitney Elizabeth Houston (9 August 1963 – 11 February 2012) was an American singer and actress, nicknamed 'the Voice', among the best-selling and most awarded recording artists of all time. Discovered in her mother's gospel shadow and signed by Clive Davis to Arista in 1983, her debut album (1985) and Whitney (1987) produced a record seven consecutive US No. 1 singles, and her recording of 'I Will Always Love You' for the film The Bodyguard (1992) became the best-selling single by a female artist in history.

Houston starred in The Bodyguard (the best-selling soundtrack album ever), Waiting to Exhale (1995) and The Preacher's Wife (1996). Her rendition of the national anthem at Super Bowl XXV in 1991, during the Gulf War, is regarded as the standard for the ritual.

Rise

Born in Newark, New Jersey, to gospel singer Cissy Houston (her cousins included Dionne Warwick; Aretha Franklin was an honorary aunt), Whitney sang in church and modelled as a teenager before Davis signed her on the strength of club showcases. The debut's ballads ('Saving All My Love for You', 'Greatest Love of All') and the second album's dance-pop established a voice of power, clarity and melisma that a generation of singers — the 'Whitney school' — imitated wholesale.

Decline and death

Her marriage to Bobby Brown (1992–2007) and escalating drug use unravelled the voice and the career through the 1990s–2000s, punctuated by comebacks and the candid 2002 Diane Sawyer interview. She died by accidental drowning, with heart disease and cocaine as contributing factors, in a Beverly Hills hotel bathtub on Grammy weekend 2012, aged 48. Posthumous documentaries (Whitney, 2018) and a biopic (I Wanna Dance with Somebody, 2022) reassessed the machinery that consumed her.
