The Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia (German: Protektorat Böhmen und Mähren; Czech: Protektorát Čechy a Morava) was a partially-annexed territory of Nazi Germany that was established on 16 March 1939 after the German occupation of the Czech lands. The protectorate's population was mostly ethnic Czechs.

After the Munich Agreement of September 1938, Nazi Germany had already annexed the German-speaking Sudetenland. On 15 March 1939, German troops occupied the remaining Czech territories, and Adolf Hitler proclaimed the protectorate from Prague Castle the following day. The Slovak lands became the separate, nominally independent Slovak Republic.

Administration

The protectorate retained a nominal Czech government under State President Emil Hácha, but real power lay with the Reich Protector, the German official who supervised the Czech administration. Konstantin von Neurath served as the first Reich Protector; in September 1941 he was effectively replaced by SS-Obergruppenführer Reinhard Heydrich, whose harsh rule — a mixture of repression and concessions to Czech industrial workers — earned him the nickname "the Hangman of Prague".

Assassination of Heydrich and reprisals

Heydrich was mortally wounded in Prague on 27 May 1942 by Czechoslovak soldiers Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš, parachuted in from Britain in Operation Anthropoid, and died on 4 June. The Germans responded with savage reprisals: the village of Lidice was destroyed and its male inhabitants murdered, and the village of Ležáky was likewise obliterated. Thousands of Czechs were executed during the ensuing state of civil emergency.

End of the protectorate

The protectorate ended with Germany's surrender in May 1945, following the Prague uprising and the arrival of Soviet and American forces. Czechoslovakia was restored as an independent state.
