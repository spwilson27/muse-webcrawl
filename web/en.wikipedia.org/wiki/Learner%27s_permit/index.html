Learner's permit

A learner's permit, also known as a learner's license, provisional license, or instruction permit, is a restricted license issued to a person who is learning to drive but has not yet satisfied the requirements for a full driver's license. It allows the holder to operate a motor vehicle under supervision, usually with a fully licensed adult in the vehicle, so that they can gain practical driving experience.

General requirements

Applicants for a learner's permit typically must meet a minimum age, pass a written knowledge test on traffic laws and road signs, and pass a vision screening. In many jurisdictions the permit holder must complete a set number of supervised driving hours, including night driving, before becoming eligible for a road test and a full or intermediate license. This staged approach is known as graduated driver licensing.

Restrictions

Common restrictions on learner's permits include:

  • Driving only while accompanied by a licensed adult supervisor
  • Prohibitions on driving during certain late-night hours (in some jurisdictions)
  • Limits on the number or age of passengers
  • A zero-tolerance rule for alcohol
  • Display of learner plates (such as L-plates) on the vehicle in some countries

By country

United States

In the United States, licensing is handled by the states. Most states allow a learner's permit at age 15 or 16, requiring a written test; after a holding period and logged supervised practice, the driver may test for an intermediate license and later a full license. Details such as minimum ages, supervised hours, and passenger limits vary by state.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, a provisional driving licence may be obtained from age 15 years and 9 months, and a holder may drive a car from age 17 under the supervision of a driver over 21 who has held a full licence for at least three years. Learners must display L plates and may not drive on motorways unless accompanied by an approved driving instructor in a dual-control car.

Australia

Australian states issue learner permits (L plates) followed by provisional licences in stages (red and green P plates in several states), with logbook requirements for supervised hours and probationary restrictions.
