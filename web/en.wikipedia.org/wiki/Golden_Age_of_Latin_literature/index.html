Golden Age of Latin literature

The Golden Age of Latin literature (in Latin often called aurea aetas) is the period of classical Latin writing conventionally dated from about 70 BC to AD 18 — from the first surviving speeches of Cicero, through the age of Augustus, to the death of Ovid. It is regarded as the high point of Latin prose and poetry, when the language attained the polished form that later ages took as the standard of "classical" Latin. It is followed by the Silver Age.

Prose

The dominant figure is Cicero (106–43 BC), whose speeches, rhetorical treatises and philosophical works set the norm for Latin prose style; his contemporary Julius Caesar wrote the lean, admired commentaries on the Gallic and Civil Wars. The historian Sallust chronicled the Catilinarian conspiracy and the Jugurthine War in an archaic, epigrammatic style, and Livy composed his monumental history of Rome, Ab Urbe Condita. Cornelius Nepos wrote biography, and Varro — the most learned Roman of his age — produced works on language and agriculture, mostly now lost.

Poetry

Poetry reached maturity with Lucretius, whose De Rerum Natura expounded Epicurean philosophy, and Catullus, whose short poems range from tender love lyrics to invective. Under Augustus and his minister Maecenas, patron of the arts, flourished the greatest Roman poets: Virgil, author of the Eclogues, Georgics and the national epic the Aeneid; Horace, master of the ode, satire and literary epistle; and the elegists Tibullus and Propertius. Ovid, with the Metamorphoses and his love poetry, closes the period; his exile by Augustus in AD 8 and death in AD 17/18 mark its conventional end.

Characteristics

Golden Age Latin is marked by the codification of grammar and rhetoric, a conscious classicism modelled on Greek literature, and a tension between the ornate "Asianic" and plain "Attic" styles that Cicero reconciled. After the establishment of the Principate, literature increasingly centred on the imperial court, a shift reflected in the more mannered style of the succeeding Silver Age (from about AD 18 to AD 130).
