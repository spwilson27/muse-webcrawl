ʻAbdu'l-Bahá

ʻAbdu'l-Bahá (Persian: عبد البهاء; 23 May 1844 – 28 November 1921), born ʻAbbás Effendi, was the eldest son of Baháʼu'lláh, founder of the Baháʼí Faith, and served as its leader from 1892 until his death in 1921.

Life

Born in Tehran, he shared his father's exiles to Baghdad, Istanbul and finally Akka in Ottoman Palestine. In Baháʼu'lláh's will, the Kitáb-i-ʻAhd, ʻAbdu'l-Bahá was appointed interpreter of his father's writings and head of the Faith. After his release from Ottoman confinement in 1908, he travelled widely through Egypt, Europe and North America (1911–1913), giving talks promoting peace, racial unity and the harmony of science and religion.

Works and legacy

His writings and recorded talks, including Some Answered Questions, Paris Talks and The Secret of Divine Civilization, are central texts of Baháʼí literature. He is buried in Haifa, Israel, where the Shrine of ʻAbdu'l-Bahá is under construction near the Baháʼí World Centre.
