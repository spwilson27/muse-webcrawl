Personal health record

A personal health record (PHR) is a health record in which health data and information related to the care of a patient are maintained by the patient themselves. This stands in contrast to the electronic health record (EHR), which is maintained by clinicians and institutions. PHRs may include diagnoses, medications, allergies, immunisations, laboratory results, and data from wearable devices, as well as notes kept by the individual.

Types

PHRs may be standalone — paper files, spreadsheets, or apps the patient maintains independently; tethered — patient portals connected to a specific provider's EHR system, through which patients view results and message clinicians; or interconnected, aggregating data from multiple providers, pharmacies, insurers, and devices. Standards such as HL7 FHIR and initiatives like Apple Health Records and national patient-access rules have made interconnected PHRs more practical.

Benefits and challenges

Advocates argue PHRs improve patient engagement, care coordination (especially for chronic conditions and patients seeing multiple providers), and the completeness of information available in emergencies. Challenges include data accuracy, health literacy, privacy and security risks, low adoption among some populations, and the burden of keeping records current. Discontinued services such as Google Health (2008–2012) and Microsoft HealthVault (2007–2019) illustrate the difficulty of sustaining standalone platforms.
