SETI@home

SETI@home was a volunteer distributed computing project run by the University of California, Berkeley, launched in 1999. It used internet-connected computers to analyse radio signals from space for signs of extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI).

LaunchedMay 17, 1999
HibernationMarch 31, 2020
PlatformBOINC
OperatorUC Berkeley SETI Research Center

How it worked

Data recorded by radio telescopes such as Arecibo were split into small work units and sent to volunteers' computers, which searched for narrow-band signals, spikes, Gaussians, pulses and triplets that might indicate an artificial source. Results were returned and cross-checked. The software ran as a screensaver or in the background.

Scale and legacy

Millions of participants in over 200 countries contributed, making it one of the largest distributed computing projects ever and popularising volunteer computing. It moved to the BOINC platform in 2004. No confirmed extraterrestrial signal was found; the famous Wow! signal predated the project. In 2020 the project entered hibernation to analyse accumulated results, while inspiring projects such as Folding@home and Einstein@Home.
