Gloss (annotation)

A gloss is a brief notation, especially a marginal or interlinear one, of the meaning of a word or wording in a text. It may be in the language of the text or in the reader's language if that is different. Collections of glosses form a glossary; a large collection arranged as a dictionary is called a lexicon.

History

Glossing was a central activity of medieval scholarship. Scribes and students annotated manuscripts of the Bible, Roman law and classical authors with explanatory notes — interlinear glosses between the lines and marginal glosses at the edges. Famous examples include the interlinear Old English gloss added to the Lindisfarne Gospels in the 10th century, and the Glossa Ordinaria, the standard medieval commentary on the Bible. Over time, glosses were sometimes copied into the text itself, complicating textual criticism.

Modern usage

In linguistics, interlinear glossing is used to give a word-by-word or morpheme-by-morpheme analysis of a text in another language, often following conventions such as the Leipzig Glossing Rules.
