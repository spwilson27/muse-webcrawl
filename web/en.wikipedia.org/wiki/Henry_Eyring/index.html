Henry Eyring

Henry Eyring (February 20, 1901 – December 26, 1981) was a Mexican-born American theoretical chemist whose principal contribution was the theory of absolute reaction rates (transition state theory). Over a career at Princeton University and the University of Utah he published more than 600 papers and numerous books.

Work

In 1935 Eyring, with Michael Polanyi and Meredith Gwynne Evans, developed the theory of the activated complex, giving the Eyring equation, which expresses reaction rates in terms of thermodynamic quantities of the transition state. He applied statistical mechanics broadly — to reaction kinetics, viscosity, diffusion, molecular biology, and the structure of liquids (his "significant structure" theory). With his students he performed one of the first quantum-mechanical calculations of a chemical reaction (H + H₂), with Walter Heitler-era methods adapted in work at Berlin and Princeton.

Life

Born in Colonia Juárez, Chihuahua, Mexico, to a Latter-day Saint family, he fled to the United States during the Mexican Revolution. He earned his PhD at UC Berkeley. A devout member of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, he wrote on the compatibility of science and religion. Despite widespread expectation, he never received the Nobel Prize, an omission often cited as one of the award's notable oversights.
