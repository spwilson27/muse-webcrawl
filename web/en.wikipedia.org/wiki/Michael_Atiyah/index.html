Sir Michael Francis Atiyah (22 April 1929 – 11 January 2019) was a British-Lebanese mathematician specialising in geometry. One of the most influential mathematicians of the 20th century, he is best known for the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, proved with Isadore Singer, which linked analysis, geometry and topology and later found applications in theoretical physics.

Life and career

Atiyah was born in London to a Lebanese writer father, Edward Atiyah, and a Scottish mother, and was educated in Sudan and Egypt before attending Manchester Grammar School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he took his doctorate under W. V. D. Hodge. He held posts at Cambridge, Oxford (as Savilian Professor of Geometry), the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, and finally the University of Edinburgh. He was president of the Royal Society (1990–1995), Master of Trinity College, Cambridge, and the first director of the Isaac Newton Institute.

Work

With Friedrich Hirzebruch he developed topological K-theory; with Singer he proved the index theorem in 1963, work recognised by the Fields Medal (1966) and jointly by the Abel Prize (2004). His later research on gauge theory, monopoles and the geometry of Yang–Mills equations connected deep mathematics with quantum field theory and string theory. In 2018, near the end of his life, he attracted wide attention for a claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis that was not accepted by the mathematical community.
