The Nissan Leaf (Japanese: 日産・リーフ) is a battery electric compact car manufactured by Nissan. It was introduced in Japan and the United States in December 2010 and is currently in its second generation, launched in 2017. The name "Leaf" is a backronym for "Leading, Environmentally friendly, Affordable, Family car". The Leaf was the world's best-selling plug-in electric car for much of the 2010s and became the first electric vehicle to surpass 500,000 global sales, in 2020.

First generation (ZE0; 2010)

The first-generation Leaf used a 24 kWh lithium-ion battery (later upgraded to 30 kWh) driving an 80 kW (107 hp) synchronous electric motor, with an initial EPA-rated range of 73 miles (117 km), later improved to 84 miles and then 107 miles for the 30 kWh version. It won the 2011 European Car of the Year, the 2011 World Car of the Year, and the 2011–2012 Japan Car of the Year.

Second generation (ZE1; 2017)

The second-generation Leaf, unveiled in September 2017, adopted a more conventional hatchback design, a 40 kWh battery with a 110 kW (147 hp) motor, and an EPA range of 151 miles (243 km). A Leaf Plus (e+) variant with a 62 kWh battery and 160 kW motor followed in 2019, raising the EPA range to up to 226 miles (364 km). The model introduced Nissan's ProPilot semi-autonomous driver assistance and the e-Pedal one-pedal driving system.

Sales and significance

By December 2019, the Leaf was the world's best-selling plug-in electric car in history, with more than 450,000 units sold; cumulative sales passed 500,000 in 2020 and 650,000 by 2023. It was a pioneer of the modern mass-market electric vehicle, preceding and helping to normalize the segment later dominated by models such as the Tesla Model 3.
