Laying on of hands

The laying on of hands is a religious practice in which a person places their hands on another person with a spiritual purpose — blessing, healing, ordination, or the imparting of the Holy Spirit. It appears in both the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament and remains part of the ritual life of many Christian traditions.

Laying on of hands at an ordination.

Biblical background

In the Old Testament, hands are laid on sacrificial animals, and Moses lays hands on Joshua to commission him. In the Gospels, Jesus lays hands on the sick and blesses children; in the Acts of the Apostles the practice accompanies healing, baptism in the Spirit, and the appointment of the first deacons and missionaries.

Uses in Christianity

The Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Anglican traditions employ the laying on of hands in ordination, confirmation/chrismation, and the anointing of the sick. Pentecostal and charismatic churches emphasise it in prayers for healing and for the baptism in the Holy Spirit, and it also appears in Latter-day Saint ordinances.
