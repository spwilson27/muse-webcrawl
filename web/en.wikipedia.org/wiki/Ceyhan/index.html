Ceyhan

Ceyhan is a city and district in Adana Province in southern Turkey, with a population of roughly 160,000. It lies on the Ceyhan River (the ancient Pyramus) in the fertile Çukurova plain, about 43 kilometres east of the city of Adana, and is an important agricultural, commercial, and energy hub.

Economy and energy

The district's economy is based on the agriculture of the Çukurova plain — cotton, wheat, maize, citrus, and soybeans — together with related processing industries. Ceyhan gained strategic importance as an energy terminal: the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan (BTC) oil pipeline, which carries crude oil from the Caspian Sea across Azerbaijan and Georgia, terminates at the Ceyhan marine terminal near Yumurtalık on the Mediterranean coast, as does the Kirkuk–Ceyhan oil pipeline from northern Iraq. Storage facilities, refineries in planning, and a designated energy-specialized industrial zone have made the area a focus of Turkish energy policy.

History

The area has been settled since antiquity, lying near ancient Mopsuestia (modern Yakapınar) on the Pyramus, a river of Cilicia mentioned by classical authors. The modern town developed in the 19th century, partly through the settlement of Nogai refugees from the Caucasus in the 1860s, and grew with the railway and the cotton trade of Çukurova. Notable sites in the district include the medieval castle of Yılanlı Kale ("Snake Castle"), associated with the Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia, overlooking the river valley.

Geography

The Ceyhan River, about 509 kilometres long, rises in the Anti-Taurus Mountains near Elbistan and flows southwest across the plain to the Gulf of İskenderun. The district has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters.
