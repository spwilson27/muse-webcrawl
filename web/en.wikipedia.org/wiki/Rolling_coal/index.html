Rolling coal

Rolling coal is the practice of modifying a diesel engine to increase the amount of fuel entering the engine in order to produce large plumes of black or grey diesel exhaust emissions. Legally, such vehicles must have a diesel particulate filter to reduce pollution; removing or disabling it is a violation of the United States Clean Air Act.

Practice

Rolling coal is sometimes practiced intentionally as a form of anti-environmental protest or to engulf pedestrians, cyclists, or other vehicles in soot, but it is more commonly done as a form of showing off or entertainment. Practitioners often modify pickup trucks, frequently at a cost of several thousand dollars, by installing smoke switches or removing emissions-control equipment.

Legality

Several U.S. states, including Colorado, New Jersey, Maryland, Utah, and Connecticut, have enacted laws specifically prohibiting rolling coal, and the Environmental Protection Agency considers removal of emissions-control devices illegal under federal law. The practice has drawn criticism for its environmental and health effects and for its use as a form of harassment.
