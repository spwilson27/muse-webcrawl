Window

A window is a space in a wall or door that allows the passage of light and may also allow ventilation, usually glazed with glass or another transparent or translucent material and held in a frame. Windows provide daylight and views while excluding weather and reducing heat loss and noise.

History

Early buildings had small unglazed openings covered with shutters, cloth, animal horn or oiled paper. The Romans made early use of glass windows, and glass windows became common in churches and wealthy homes in the medieval period. In Britain window size was long affected by the window tax (1696–1851). Sheet-glass manufacturing (cylinder, then float glass after the 1950s) made large, cheap panes possible, enabling the modern glazed curtain wall.

Types

Common window types include:

  • Fixed windows — non-opening picture windows;
  • Casement windows — hinged at the side;
  • Sash windows — vertically sliding glazed panels;
  • Awning and hopper windows — hinged at top or bottom;
  • Bay and bow windows — projecting from the wall;
  • Clerestory, skylight and dormer windows — in roofs or high walls;
  • Stained-glass windows — coloured glass set in lead cames, typical of Gothic churches.

Construction and performance

A typical window consists of a frame (of timber, aluminium, uPVC or composites), one or more sashes, and glazing. Modern units use double or triple glazing — two or three panes separated by sealed gaps filled with air or argon, often with low-emissivity coatings — to improve insulation, measured by the U-value. Window coverings such as blinds, curtains and shutters control light, privacy and solar gain.

Related meanings

In computing, a window is a rectangular area of a graphical user interface displaying a program or document. The word also gives us figurative terms such as "window of opportunity" and "launch window".
