Rimutaka Incline

Location: Remutaka Range, New Zealand
Opened: 1878
Closed: 1955 (replaced by Rimutaka Tunnel)
Gradient: 1 in 15 (6.67%)
System: Fell mountain railway

The Rimutaka Incline was a 3-mile (5 km) section of the Wairarapa Line in New Zealand that climbed over the Rimutaka Range (now Remutaka Range) between Summit and Cross Creek. Opened in 1878, it used the Fell mountain railway system, in which locomotives gripped a raised centre rail with horizontal driving wheels to climb the 1 in 15 gradient.

Operation

Special Fell locomotives of the H class worked the incline for 77 years; descending trains used Fell brake vans gripping the centre rail. The incline was notorious for wind — in 1880 a train was blown off the line at Siberia Curve, killing four people, after which wind shelters were erected.

Closure and trail

The incline closed on 29 October 1955 when the Rimutaka Tunnel opened. The formation is now the Remutaka Rail Trail, a popular walking and cycling route, and one H class locomotive survives at the Fell Locomotive Museum in Featherston.
