The Province of Massachusetts Bay was a colony in British America which became one of the thirteen original states of the United States. It was chartered on 7 October 1691 by William III and Mary II, the monarchs of the Kingdoms of England and Scotland, and was created by merging the former Massachusetts Bay Colony with Plymouth Colony and other territories, including Maine and Nova Scotia (the latter soon detached).

Government

Under the 1691 charter the governor was appointed by the Crown, replacing the elected Puritan magistracy of the old colony; the General Court (assembly) remained elected by property holders, and town meetings flourished. The charter granted religious toleration to all Protestants, ending the Congregational monopoly. The capital at Boston made the province one of the most populous and commercially vigorous in British America.

Road to revolution

Friction grew over parliamentary taxation and customs enforcement after the French and Indian War: the Stamp Act riots, the Boston Massacre (1770), the Boston Tea Party (1773) and the punitive Massachusetts Government Act led the colonists to form a Provincial Congress in 1774. Fighting at Lexington and Concord in April 1775 opened the American Revolutionary War; royal government collapsed, and in 1780 Massachusetts adopted its own constitution — the oldest still-functioning written constitution in the world.
