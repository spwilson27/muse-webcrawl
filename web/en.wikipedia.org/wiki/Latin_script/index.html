Latin script

Type: Alphabet
Languages: Latin and most European languages; widely used worldwide
Parent: Greek alphabet via Old Italic scripts

The Latin script, also known as the Roman script, is a writing system based on the letters of the classical Latin alphabet, itself derived from a form of the Greek alphabet via Etruscan. It is the most widely used alphabetic writing system in the world, the standard script of English and most European languages and adopted by many languages in Africa, the Americas, Oceania and Asia, including Vietnamese and Malay/Indonesian, and the official script of Turkish since 1928.

History

The classical Latin alphabet had 23 letters; J, U and W were added in medieval and early modern times, giving the 26-letter ISO basic Latin alphabet. Roman square capitals and uncial, Carolingian minuscule and blackletter hands shaped European handwriting; printing standardised letterforms.

Adaptations

Languages extend the basic letters with diacritics (é, ñ, ç, ø), digraphs (ch, ll, ij) and additional letters (ß, æ, þ in Icelandic). Vietnamese uses stacked tone and vowel marks, among the most elaborate adaptations.

Romanisation

Latin script is the usual target for transliteration of other scripts, e.g., pinyin for Chinese and Hepburn for Japanese.
