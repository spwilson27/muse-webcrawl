Sir Julius Chan (29 August 1939 – 30 January 2025) was a Papua New Guinean politician who served twice as Prime Minister of Papua New Guinea, from 1980 to 1982 and again from 1994 to 1997.

Early life

Chan was born on the Tanga Islands in what is now New Ireland Province, the son of a Chinese trader from Guangdong and a local mother. Educated in Brisbane, Australia, he entered the public service and co-founded the People's Progress Party in 1968, entering the pre-independence House of Assembly the same year.

Prime minister

As finance minister in the government of Michael Somare, Chan was a principal architect of Papua New Guinea's independence settlement in 1975. He succeeded Somare as prime minister on 11 March 1980, serving until the 1982 election returned Somare to power.

Chan returned as prime minister in 1994. His second government became embroiled in the Sandline affair of 1997, in which his government contracted the mercenary company Sandline International to intervene in the Bougainville conflict; the resulting military revolt and public protests forced him to step aside and contributed to his defeat at the 1997 election.

Later career

In 2007 Chan was elected Governor of New Ireland Province, an office he held until his death. He advocated greater provincial autonomy and resource revenue sharing for his province. He died on 30 January 2025, and was widely mourned as one of the founding fathers of independent Papua New Guinea — often described as the country's "last man standing" among its independence-era leaders.
