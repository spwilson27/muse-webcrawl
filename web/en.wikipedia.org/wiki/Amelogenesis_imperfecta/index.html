Amelogenesis imperfecta
Type: Inherited disorder of tooth enamel formation
Genes involved: AMELX, ENAM, MMP20, KLK4, FAM83H and others

Amelogenesis imperfecta (AI) is a congenital disorder which presents with a rare abnormal formation of the enamel or external layer of the crown of teeth, unrelated to any systemic or generalised conditions. Enamel is composed mostly of mineral, formed and regulated by the proteins in it; in AI these proteins malfunction, producing enamel that is too thin, too soft, improperly mineralised, or discoloured.

Types

AI is traditionally grouped into hypoplastic (thin but mineralised enamel), hypocalcified (soft, poorly mineralised enamel that wears away), and hypomaturation (enamel of normal thickness but mottled and insufficiently hard) types, with mixed forms also described. Inheritance may be autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, or X-linked, depending on the causative gene.

Clinical features and management

Affected teeth are yellow-brown or discoloured, sensitive, prone to rapid wear and breakage, and both primary and permanent dentitions are affected. There is no cure; dental management is restorative and preventive, often involving crowns, bonding, and careful oral hygiene to preserve function and appearance, frequently beginning in childhood.
