Cuckoo clock

A cuckoo clock is a pendulum-regulated clock that strikes the hours with a sound like a cuckoo's call and has an automated bird that emerges to accompany the striking. It is closely associated with the Black Forest region of Germany, where the craft developed in the eighteenth century.

Mechanism

Two small bellows and pipes inside the clock produce the two notes of the cuckoo call. At the hour, a wire releases the carved wooden bird, which tips forward as the bellows sound. Traditional movements are weight-driven, with carved pine-cone weights, and run for one day or eight days between windings; modern versions may be quartz powered.

Styles

The best-known designs are the chalet style, depicting an Alpine house, and the older carved style, featuring leaves, birds and hunting motifs in carved linden wood. Antique Black Forest clocks are widely collected, and the region's clock-making is celebrated in museums such as the German Clock Museum in Furtwangen.
