Jim Brown

James Nathaniel Brown (February 17, 1936 – May 18, 2023) was an American professional football player, civil rights activist and actor. A fullback for the Cleveland Browns of the National Football League from 1957 to 1965, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest running backs — and one of the greatest football players — of all time.

Brown in action for the Cleveland Browns.

Football career

Brown starred at Syracuse University in football, lacrosse, basketball and track before being drafted by Cleveland in 1957. In nine NFL seasons he led the league in rushing eight times, was named to the Pro Bowl every year, won three NFL Most Valuable Player awards, and helped the Browns win the 1964 NFL championship. He retired abruptly in 1966, while filming The Dirty Dozen, holding the league records for career rushing yards (12,312) and touchdowns (126), despite playing only nine seasons of 12–14 games.

Acting and activism

After football Brown pursued an acting career, with roles in The Dirty Dozen (1967), Ice Station Zebra (1968), 100 Rifles (1969) and later films. He was also a prominent civil rights figure: in 1967 he convened the Cleveland Summit of leading Black athletes in support of Muhammad Ali, and in 1988 he founded the Amer-I-Can programme to help gang members and prisoners change their lives.

Legacy

Brown was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1971 and the Lacrosse Hall of Fame; many consider him the greatest lacrosse player of his era as well. The NFL named its rushing title the Jim Brown Award. He died at his home in Los Angeles in 2023.
