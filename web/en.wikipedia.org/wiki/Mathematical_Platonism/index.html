Mathematical Platonism

Mathematical Platonism is the view in the philosophy of mathematics that mathematical entities — such as numbers, sets, and geometric figures — exist independently of human minds, language, and physical reality, and that mathematical statements describe this abstract realm and are objectively true or false.

Arguments

Supporters cite the apparent objectivity and necessity of mathematics, its indispensability to science (the Quine–Putnam indispensability argument), and theorems such as Gödel's incompleteness results, which Gödel himself took to support Platonism. Kurt Gödel and Roger Penrose are notable advocates.

Criticisms

Paul Benacerraf's epistemological challenge asks how humans can know about causally inert abstract objects. Alternatives include nominalism, formalism, structuralism, and fictionalism.
