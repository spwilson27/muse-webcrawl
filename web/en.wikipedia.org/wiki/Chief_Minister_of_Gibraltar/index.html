The Chief Minister of Gibraltar is the head of His Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, the devolved government of the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar. The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor of Gibraltar, acting on behalf of the British monarch, and is conventionally the leader of the largest party or coalition in the Gibraltar Parliament following a general election. The office is based at No. 6 Convent Place in Gibraltar.

History

The office was created in 1964, when Joshua Hassan of the Association for the Advancement of Civil Rights became the first Chief Minister following the introduction of a new constitution granting Gibraltar a measure of internal self-government. Hassan dominated Gibraltarian politics for much of the second half of the 20th century, serving two long spells in office (1964–1969 and 1972–1987) and leading the territory through the period when Spain closed the land border in 1969.

Subsequent Chief Ministers have included Adolfo Canepa, Joe Bossano (1988–1996), Peter Caruana (1996–2011), and Fabian Picardo, who has held the office since December 2011 as leader of the Gibraltar Socialist Labour Party in alliance with the Liberal Party.

Role

The Chief Minister leads the Council of Ministers, sets government policy on matters devolved to Gibraltar — including finance, taxation, health, education, and economic development — and represents the territory internationally, including in negotiations with Spain and the European Union concerning Gibraltar's status. Defence, foreign affairs, and internal security formally remain the responsibility of the Governor.
