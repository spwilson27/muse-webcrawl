Decapodiformes

A squid, showing the eight arms and two longer tentacles characteristic of the group.
Scientific classification
PhylumMollusca
ClassCephalopoda
SubclassColeoidea
SuperorderDecapodiformes

Decapodiformes is a superorder of cephalopod molluscs that includes all species with ten limbs: eight short arms and two longer feeding tentacles. The group comprises the squids and cuttlefish, in contrast to the octopuses of the Octopodiformes, which have only eight arms and no tentacles. Decapodiforms possess an internal shell that is reduced to varying degrees — the pen or gladius of squids and the cuttlebone of cuttlefish.

Classification

The superorder is generally divided into several orders, including:

  • Teuthida — the squids, the largest and most diverse group, from coastal species to the giant squid of the deep ocean
  • Sepiida — the cuttlefish, broad-bodied bottom-dwellers with a gas-filled cuttlebone used for buoyancy
  • Sepiolida — the bobtail squids, small rounded forms that often bury themselves in sediment
  • Spirulida — represented by the single living species Spirula spirula, with a coiled internal shell
  • Idiosepiida — the pygmy squids, among the smallest cephalopods
  • Bathyteuthida and Oegopsida in classifications that split the squid orders further

Characteristics

Decapodiforms have streamlined mantles with fins, highly developed eyes, and arms bearing suckers that are often armed with hooks or horny rings. The two retractile tentacles end in clubs used to snare prey. Like other coleoids, they can change colour and texture using chromatophores, eject ink, and swim by jet propulsion. Many species are commercially important as food, and global squid fisheries land millions of tonnes annually.
