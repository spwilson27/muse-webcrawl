James Wilkinson (24 March 1757 – 28 December 1825) was an American soldier, statesman and — as archival evidence later revealed — a paid agent of the Spanish Empire, designated "Agent 13". He served twice as the senior officer of the United States Army, was the first governor of the Louisiana Territory, and was deeply involved in Aaron Burr's western conspiracy, which he ultimately betrayed. Historian Robert Leckie called him "a general who never won a battle or lost a court-martial".

Revolutionary War

Wilkinson studied medicine before joining the Continental Army in 1775. He served at the siege of Boston and in the invasion of Canada, became an aide to Horatio Gates, and was brevetted brigadier general for carrying the news of the victory at Saratoga to Congress — though he embarrassed himself by repeating confidential material, and was implicated in the Conway Cabal against George Washington. He later served as clothier general and sat in the Pennsylvania assembly.

The Spanish pensioner

Moving to Kentucky in the 1780s, Wilkinson promoted its trade down the Mississippi and, in 1787, swore an oath of allegiance to Spain and began receiving a Spanish pension in exchange for intelligence and for advancing Spanish interests in the American West. In the U.S. Army of the 1790s he served under Anthony Wayne in the Northwest Indian War and succeeded Wayne as senior officer in 1796. As governor of the Louisiana Territory after its purchase, he collaborated with Aaron Burr in exploring a western venture, then denounced Burr to President Jefferson when the scheme became dangerous, testifying against him at his treason trial — while protecting himself.

War of 1812 and later life

In the War of 1812 Wilkinson, by then a major general, commanded the unsuccessful St. Lawrence campaign of 1813, marked by defeats at Chrysler's Farm and Lacolle Mills and mutual recrimination with subordinates. He was relieved of command and cleared by courts of inquiry. After the war he published his memoirs, sought a land grant in Texas, and died in Mexico City in 1825 while lobbying the Mexican government. His Spanish agency was conclusively documented from Spanish archives in the 20th century.
