Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (born 26 February 1954) is a Turkish politician who has been the president of Turkey since 2014. He previously served as prime minister from 2003 to 2014 and as mayor of Istanbul from 1994 to 1998. He co-founded the Justice and Development Party (AK Party) in 2001 and has led it to repeated national election victories, becoming the dominant figure in Turkish politics in the early 21st century.

Early life and rise

Erdoğan was born in the Kasımpaşa district of Istanbul to a family originally from Rize Province. He attended an imam hatip religious school and studied business administration. A semi-professional footballer in his youth, he rose through the Islamist Welfare Party of Necmettin Erbakan. Elected mayor of Istanbul in 1994, he won praise for tackling the city's water, pollution and traffic problems. In 1998 he was convicted of inciting religious hatred for reciting a poem at a rally, served several months in prison, and was banned from office, ending his mayoralty.

Prime minister

After the AK Party won the 2002 general election, Erdoğan became prime minister in March 2003. His early governments pursued EU accession reforms, oversaw strong economic growth and expanded infrastructure and healthcare. Relations with the military establishment and judiciary, historically guardians of Turkish secularism, grew increasingly confrontational. His government's response to the 2013 Gezi Park protests drew international criticism, and corruption investigations that year triggered a bitter split with the Gülen movement.

Presidency

Erdoğan won the 2014 presidential election, Turkey's first by direct popular vote. Following a failed coup attempt in July 2016, which he blamed on the Gülen movement, the government conducted sweeping purges of the military, judiciary, civil service and media. A 2017 referendum narrowly approved a transition to an executive presidential system, greatly expanding the presidency's powers. He was re-elected in 2018 and again in 2023, defeating Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu in a runoff.

His presidency has featured an assertive foreign policy — including military operations in Syria, involvement in Libya and support for Azerbaijan — alongside periods of severe economic difficulty, including a currency crisis and very high inflation in the early 2020s. Critics accuse him of authoritarian drift and eroding judicial independence and press freedom; supporters credit him with economic development, infrastructure expansion and restoring religious conservatives' place in public life.
