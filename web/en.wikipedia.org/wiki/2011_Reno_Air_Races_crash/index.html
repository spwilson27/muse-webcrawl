2011 Reno Air Races crash

Date: 16 September 2011

Site: Reno Stead Airport, Reno, Nevada, United States

Aircraft: North American P-51D Mustang "The Galloping Ghost"

Pilot: Jimmy Leeward (74)

Deaths: 11 (including the pilot)

Injuries: 69

On 16 September 2011, The Galloping Ghost, a highly modified North American P-51D Mustang racing aircraft flown by Jimmy Leeward, crashed into spectators at the Reno Air Races at Reno Stead Airport in Nevada. Leeward and ten spectators were killed, and 69 people were injured. It was the third-deadliest airshow disaster in United States history, after accidents in 1951 and 1972.

The aircraft

The Galloping Ghost had a long racing history dating back to the 1940s and had been extensively modified for speed, including a clipped wing and a boiling-water cooling system replacing the Mustang's ventral radiator. Leeward, an experienced racing pilot and Hollywood stunt flyer, had owned the aircraft since 1983 and returned it to racing in 2010.

The crash

During the Unlimited Gold Heat, the aircraft suddenly pitched up, rolled, and dove into the box-seat area in front of the grandstand at high speed. Photographs taken moments before impact showed that a trim tab on the left elevator had partially separated. The abrupt pitch-up subjected Leeward to forces estimated at over 17 g, almost certainly incapacitating him.

Investigation

The National Transportation Safety Board determined that the probable cause was deteriorated and improperly secured locknut screws on the elevator trim tab system, which allowed the tab to flutter and fail at racing speed. The NTSB also cited the aircraft's extensive undocumented modifications. The accident led to sweeping safety changes for the Reno Air Races and for air racing generally, including increased distances between race courses and spectators.
