Herod Antipas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Coins of Herod Antipas showing a palm branch, minted at Tiberias
Coins of Herod Antipas showing a palm branch, minted at Tiberias

Herod Antipas (before 20 BC – after AD 39) was a son of Herod the Great who ruled as tetrarch of Galilee and Perea from 4 BC to AD 39. In the New Testament he is the ruler who imprisoned and executed John the Baptist (after the famous dance of Salome) and who examined Jesus before the crucifixion ('that fox', Luke 13:32). His marriage to Herodias, his brother's wife, and his building of Tiberias define his reign.

Contents1. Reign
2. Downfall

Reign

Antipas inherited the most Jewish part of his father's kingdom and ruled it capably for 43 years — the longest of the Herodian tetrarchs — founding Sepphoris's rebuilding and the new capital Tiberias (c. AD 19, on a graveyard, Jews said) on the Sea of Galilee. His divorce of Aretas IV's daughter for Herodias provoked John's denunciation and a border war (36) in which Aretas routed him — read by contemporaries (Josephus says) as divine punishment for John's death.

Downfall

Agrippa I's rise undid him: accused (by Agrippa's letter) of stockpiling arms and conspiring with Parthia, Antipas was deposed by Caligula in 39 and exiled to Gaul; Herodias chose to share his exile. His tetrarchy passed to Agrippa — the last Herodian king of a reunited Jewish kingdom.
