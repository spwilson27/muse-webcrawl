The World Heritage List is maintained by the World Heritage Committee of the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) under the World Heritage Convention, adopted in 1972. The list contains properties forming part of the cultural and natural heritage which the Committee considers as having outstanding universal value.

Background

The Convention Concerning the Protection of the World's Cultural and Natural Heritage was adopted by UNESCO's General Conference on 16 November 1972, prompted in part by the international campaign to save the monuments of Nubia from flooding by the Aswan High Dam. In 1978 the first twelve properties were inscribed, including Yellowstone National Park, the Galápagos Islands, and the historic centres of Kraków and Quito.

Nomination and criteria

To be included, sites must be nominated by their country (a State Party to the Convention) and meet at least one of ten selection criteria — six cultural and four natural — demonstrating "outstanding universal value", and must satisfy conditions of integrity and/or authenticity and have adequate protection and management. Sites are classed as cultural, natural, or mixed properties.

Distribution

Italy and China have the most inscribed properties, each with around sixty sites. Sites considered to be in danger are placed on the separate List of World Heritage in Danger. Inscription brings international recognition and can help mobilise funding for conservation, but listing also imposes monitoring and reporting obligations on the host state.
