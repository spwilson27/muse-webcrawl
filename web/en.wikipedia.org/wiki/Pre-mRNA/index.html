Precursor mRNA

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Precursor mRNA (pre-mRNA) is an immature single strand of messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA). Pre-mRNA is synthesized from a DNA template in the cell nucleus by transcription. It comprises much of the RNA class termed heterogeneous nuclear RNA (hnRNA). Once pre-mRNA has been completely processed, it is termed mature mRNA, or simply mRNA.

Processing

In eukaryotes, pre-mRNA must be processed before it can be translated. The steps of processing include:

  • 5′ capping — the addition of a 7-methylguanosine cap to the 5′ end of the transcript, protecting it from degradation and aiding export and translation.
  • Splicing — the removal of introns by the spliceosome and the joining of exons, often in alternative combinations (alternative splicing) so that one gene can yield several protein isoforms.
  • 3′ polyadenylation — cleavage of the transcript and addition of a poly(A) tail of roughly 100–250 adenine residues, which stabilizes the molecule.

Editing, such as adenosine-to-inosine deamination, can also modify individual nucleotides in some transcripts.

Export and translation

Mature mRNA is exported through nuclear pore complexes to the cytoplasm, where ribosomes translate it into protein. Quality-control systems, including nonsense-mediated decay, degrade aberrantly processed transcripts.
