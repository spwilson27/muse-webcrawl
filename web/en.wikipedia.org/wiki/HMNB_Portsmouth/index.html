HMNB Portsmouth, home of the Royal Navy's aircraft carriers

His Majesty's Naval Base, Portsmouth (HMNB Portsmouth) is one of three operating bases in the United Kingdom for the Royal Navy (the others being HMNB Clyde and HMNB Devonport). Portsmouth has been a significant naval port since the Middle Ages: Richard I ordered the first dock built in 1194, and Henry VII established the world's first dry dock there in 1496, making it one of the oldest and most historically important naval bases in the world.

History

The dockyard expanded through the age of sail — the surviving Georgian storehouses, ropewalks and dry docks now form Portsmouth Historic Dockyard — and through the industrial era, when Portsmouth built HMS Warrior-era ironclads and Dreadnought battleships. It was a principal embarkation and repair port in both world wars and the base from which much of the Falklands task force sailed in 1982.

Today

The base is home port to both Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers, HMS Queen Elizabeth and HMS Prince of Wales, for which the harbour was extensively dredged, as well as Type 45 destroyers and Type 23 frigates. Support services are provided under contract by BAE Systems Maritime Services. The naval base commander also oversees the King's Harbour Master of Portsmouth.

Historic ships

Within and beside the base, the Historic Dockyard preserves HMS Victory (Nelson's flagship at Trafalgar, still in commission), the remains of the Tudor warship Mary Rose, HMS Warrior (1860) and the monitor HMS M33.
