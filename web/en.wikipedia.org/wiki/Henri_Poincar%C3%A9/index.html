Jules Henri Poincaré (29 April 1854 – 17 July 1912) was a French mathematician, theoretical physicist and philosopher of science, often called a polymath and 'The Last Universalist'.

Work

Poincaré founded algebraic topology, developed the theory of automorphic functions and analytic functions of several variables, and in celestial mechanics his work on the three-body problem laid foundations for chaos theory, including the discovery of homoclinic tangles and sensitive dependence.

Physics

He formulated Lorentz transformations, discussed synchronisation and the principle of relativity (1904–05) contemporaneously with Einstein, and gave the Poincaré group its name. In philosophy he defended conventionalism about geometry.

Poincaré conjecture

His 1904 conjecture — that every simply connected closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere — became a Millennium Prize Problem, proved by Grigori Perelman in 2002–03.
