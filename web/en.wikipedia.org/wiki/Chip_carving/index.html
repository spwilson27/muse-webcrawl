Chip carving

Chip carving is a decorative woodworking technique in which knives are used to remove small chips of wood from a flat surface, creating geometric patterns, lettering and stylised motifs. It is one of the oldest and simplest forms of wood carving.

Tools and technique

Chip carving typically uses just two knives: a cutting knife and a stab knife. Designs are drawn or transferred onto basswood, butternut or pine blanks, and chips are removed with two angled cuts meeting at a point, producing crisp triangular and curved facets.

Traditions

The craft has deep roots in European folk art, particularly in Scandinavia, Germany and Eastern Europe, and remains popular with hobbyists through guilds and pattern books.
