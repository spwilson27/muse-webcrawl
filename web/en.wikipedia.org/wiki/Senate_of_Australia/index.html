Senate of Australia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Senate of Australia — 76 senators: 12 for each state and 2 for each mainland territory. Upper house of the Parliament of Australia.

The Senate is the upper house of the bicameral Parliament of Australia, the lower house being the House of Representatives. Established by the Constitution of Australia at federation in 1901, the Senate was designed on the model of the United States Senate to represent the states equally: each of the six states elects twelve senators regardless of population, while the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory each elect two, for a total of 76.

Elections

Senators are directly elected by single transferable vote proportional representation, state-wide. State senators serve six-year terms, with half the state seats contested at each ordinary election; territory senators serve terms tied to the House of Representatives. The Senate's proportional system means governments rarely hold a majority in it, making the chamber a genuine house of review in which minor parties and independents often hold the balance of power.

Powers

Under section 53 of the Constitution, the Senate has nearly equal legislative power with the House: it may not originate or amend money bills, but it may reject them outright and may request amendments — a power whose use in blocking supply contributed to the 1975 constitutional crisis. Most government legislation is amended in the Senate, and its committee system, including estimates hearings, is a powerful instrument of scrutiny. Deadlocks between the houses can be resolved by a double dissolution followed, if necessary, by a joint sitting under section 57.
