X-10 Graphite Reactor

The X-10 Graphite Reactor is a decommissioned nuclear reactor at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in Tennessee. Built as part of the Manhattan Project, it was the world's second artificial nuclear reactor (after Enrico Fermi's Chicago Pile-1) and the first designed and built for continuous operation. It was originally known as the Clinton Pile, after Clinton Engineer Works.

History

The reactor was intended as a pilot plant for the much larger plutonium production reactors at Hanford, Washington. Construction began in 1943 and the X-10 went critical on 4 November 1943. It was a graphite-moderated, air-cooled reactor using natural uranium fuel slugs; by the end of 1944 it had produced the first significant quantities of plutonium for study at Los Alamos.

Post-war use

After the war the reactor was used for research and for producing radioisotopes — in August 1946 it supplied the first nuclear-produced radioisotope for a civilian use, carbon-14 shipped to a cancer hospital. It was shut down in 1963 and designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965. The reactor's control room and graphite face are open to visitors on museum tours.
