AlliedSignal Inc. was an American aerospace, automotive and engineering conglomerate formed in 1985 by the merger of the Allied Corporation (formerly Allied Chemical) and the Signal Companies, which included the Garrett Corporation, a major maker of aircraft engines and turbochargers. Headquartered in Morristown, New Jersey, the company streamlined its name from "Allied-Signal" to "AlliedSignal" in 1993.

Business

AlliedSignal's principal divisions produced auxiliary power units and small gas turbines for aircraft, avionics, turbochargers for cars and trucks, automotive safety products and filters (including the Fram, Autolite and Bendix brands), and speciality chemicals and fibres such as nylon and refrigerants. Under chairman Lawrence Bossidy, who joined from General Electric in 1991, the company pursued aggressive margin improvement and Six Sigma quality programmes, sharply raising its market value during the 1990s.

Merger with Honeywell

In 1999 AlliedSignal acquired the smaller Honeywell Inc. for about $14.8 billion and adopted the better-known Honeywell name for the combined company, creating one of the world's largest aerospace and industrial controls groups. The merged firm retained AlliedSignal's Morristown headquarters. An attempted subsequent merger between Honeywell and General Electric was blocked by the European Commission in 2001.
