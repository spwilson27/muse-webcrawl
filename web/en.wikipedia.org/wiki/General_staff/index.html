General staff

A general staff is a group of officers, enlisted personnel and civilian staff who serve the commander of a division or larger military formation, coordinating operations, intelligence, logistics, planning and administration. The modern general staff system was pioneered by Prussia in the early 19th century and widely imitated.

History

After Prussia's defeats by Napoleon, reformers such as Gerhard von Scharnhorst and August von Gneisenau created a professional, merit-selected staff corps trained at the Kriegsakademie. Under Helmuth von Moltke the Elder, the Great General Staff made the Prussian — and later German — army's war planning famously systematic, an approach copied by most major powers.

Organization

Staff sections are conventionally numbered: personnel (1), intelligence (2), operations (3), logistics (4), plans (5), signals/communications (6), training (7), finance (8) and civil-military cooperation (9). Prefixes denote the level: G- for army-level staff, S- for brigade and below, J- for joint staffs, and C- for combined (multinational) headquarters.

Staff officers

The chief of staff coordinates the work of all sections on behalf of the commander. Staff officers are expected to prepare plans, draft orders and anticipate problems rather than command troops directly, though staff service is a normal prerequisite for high command.
