Overview

The Continental Divide Trail (CDT) is a United States National Scenic Trail running roughly 3,100 miles (5,000 km) between the US borders with Canada (at Glacier National Park, Montana) and Mexico (in New Mexico), following the Continental Divide of the Americas as closely as practicable. It is the highest and most remote of the "Triple Crown" long trails, which also includes the Appalachian Trail and the Pacific Crest Trail.

Character

The trail crosses five states and peaks in Colorado, where it reaches its highest point at Grays Peak (14,278 ft) via a summit route. Designated in 1978, it remains the least complete and least crowded of the three crown trails; most thru-hikers travel southbound from June. The Continental Divide Trail Coalition supports its completion and protection.
