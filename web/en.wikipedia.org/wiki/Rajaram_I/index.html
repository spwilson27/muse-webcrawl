Rajaram Bhonsle I (14 February 1670 – 3 March 1700) was the second son of Shivaji, the founder of the Maratha Empire, and the younger half-brother of Sambhaji, whom he succeeded as Chhatrapati in 1689 after Sambhaji's capture and execution by the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb.

Reign

Crowned at Raigad in March 1689, Rajaram fled as Mughal forces overran the Maratha heartland and escaped, disguised, to the fortress of Jinji (Gingee) in Tamil country. From Jinji he directed a war of resistance for nearly a decade while commanders such as Santaji Ghorpade and Dhanaji Jadhav waged guerrilla campaigns in Maharashtra that bled the Mughal armies. The Mughals besieged Jinji for years before finally taking it in 1698, by which time Rajaram had escaped back to Maharashtra.

Death and legacy

Rajaram died of illness at Sinhagad Fort in March 1700, aged 30. After his death his widow Tarabai ruled as regent for their young son Shivaji II and continued the war until Aurangzeb's death in 1707. Rajaram's reign, fought from exile under desperate conditions, is credited with preserving the Maratha state through its gravest crisis.
