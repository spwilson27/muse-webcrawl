Trout

Trout is the common name for a number of species of carnivorous freshwater fish in the family Salmonidae, closely related to salmon. The word "trout" is also used as part of the name of some non-salmonid fish, such as the spotted seatrout. Trout species fall into three genera: Oncorhynchus (the Pacific trout and salmon), Salmo (the Atlantic trout, including the brown trout), and Salvelinus (the chars, some of which are called trout).

Species

Well-known trout include the rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), the brown trout (Salmo trutta), the brook trout (Salvelinus fontinalis), the lake trout (Salvelinus namaycush), the cutthroat trout (Oncorhynchus clarkii), the golden trout, the Apache trout, and the bull trout. Many trout are anadromous or potamodromous: sea-run forms of rainbow trout are called steelhead, and sea-run brown trout are called sea trout.

Habitat

Trout are typically found in cool, clear streams and lakes. Most species require well-oxygenated water and clean gravel beds for spawning. Although many species have been introduced around the world for sport fishing, native trout populations are sensitive indicators of water quality and are threatened by habitat degradation, warming waters, and competition from introduced species.

As food and sport

Trout have long been prized as food fish and as quarry for fly fishing. They are farmed commercially in many countries, and rainbow trout are among the most widely cultivated freshwater fish. Trout flesh is generally mild and varies in colour from white to deep orange-red depending on diet, particularly the consumption of carotenoid-rich crustaceans.
