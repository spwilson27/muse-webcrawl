Refresh rate


Successive frames drawn on a raster display.

The refresh rate, also known as the vertical refresh rate or vertical scan rate, is the number of times per second that a raster-based display device displays a new image. It is measured in hertz (Hz). A refresh rate of 60 Hz means the image is redrawn 60 times per second.

History

For cathode-ray tube (CRT) displays, refresh rate was determined by the rate at which the electron beam rescanned the phosphor screen; rates below about 60–75 Hz produced visible flicker. Early television standards locked refresh to the mains frequency (60 Hz in NTSC countries, 50 Hz in PAL countries) to avoid interference. Modern LCD and OLED panels do not flicker in the same way, but refresh rate still governs motion smoothness and input latency.

Higher refresh rates

Gaming and professional monitors offer 120 Hz, 144 Hz, 240 Hz and beyond; higher refresh rates reduce motion blur and make fast movement appear more fluid. Variable refresh rate technologies (such as NVIDIA G-Sync and AMD FreeSync) synchronise the display's refresh with the graphics card's frame output to eliminate screen tearing.

Refresh rate vs frame rate

Refresh rate is a property of the display; frame rate is the rate at which a source (game, video) produces new images. Displaying 30 fps content on a 60 Hz screen requires each frame to be shown twice.
