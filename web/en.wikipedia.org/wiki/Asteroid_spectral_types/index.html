Asteroid spectral types

Asteroids are classified by the reflectance spectra of their surfaces

Asteroid spectral types are classifications of asteroids based on the shape and features of their reflectance spectra, along with albedo, which together indicate surface composition. The main systems are the Tholen classification and the later SMASS classification.

Major classes

The three broadest groupings are: C-type (carbonaceous, dark, the most common in the outer main belt), S-type (silicaceous/stony, common in the inner belt), and M-type (metallic, probably exposed iron–nickel cores). SMASS refines these into classes such as A, B, D, E, K, L, Q, R, T, V (basaltic, linked to Vesta) and X, with subclasses denoted by lowercase letters.

Interpretation

Spectral matching with meteorite types connects most ordinary chondrites to S-type and Q-type asteroids and carbonaceous chondrites to C-complex asteroids, informing models of planetesimal formation in the early Solar System.
