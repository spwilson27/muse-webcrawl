Chicago Loop

The Loop is the central business district of Chicago, Illinois. It is one of the city's 77 community areas, and its name derives from the circuit of elevated railway that forms a loop around its core. The Loop is the seat of Chicago's government, its financial district, and home to many of its theatres, retailers and landmark buildings. It is bordered by the Chicago River to the north and west, Lake Michigan to the east, and Roosevelt Road to the south.

History

The area that became the Loop was the original site of Chicago's settlement and, after the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, the centre of its rebuilding. In the 1880s and 1890s the district became the birthplace of the skyscraper, including the Home Insurance Building (1885), often considered the first modern skyscraper. The elevated Loop circuit was completed in 1897, unifying several elevated lines and giving the district its name.

Architecture

The Loop contains landmark works by Louis Sullivan, Daniel Burnham, Ludwig Mies van der Rohe and others, including the Carson, Pirie, Scott Building, the Rookery, the Monadnock Building and the Willis (Sears) Tower just west of the district boundary. Chicago City Hall, the Art Institute of Chicago on its eastern edge, and Grant Park define its civic spaces.

Economy and transport

The Loop is a major financial and commercial centre, housing the Chicago Board of Trade, the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago and numerous corporate headquarters. It is the hub of the Chicago "L" rapid transit system, whose elevated lines circle the district, and is served by several Metra commuter rail terminals including Union Station and Ogilvie Transportation Center.
