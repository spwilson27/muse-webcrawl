David Brent is a fictional character played by Ricky Gervais in the British mockumentary sitcom The Office (2001–2003) and its spin-off film David Brent: Life on the Road (2016). He is the general manager of the Slough branch of the fictional Wernham Hogg paper company, and one of the most celebrated comic creations in British television.

[ Hero image: David Brent performing his infamous improvised dance at the office party ]

Character

Brent desperately wants to be liked and admired, and regards himself as both a brilliant boss — "a friend first, and a boss second, probably an entertainer third" — and a gifted musician and philosopher. In reality he is a deluded, needy middle manager whose jokes misfire, whose attempts at sensitivity descend into crassness, and whose self-image is continually undermined by his staff's indifference and by his superior, Neil Godwin. Memorably, he marks a staff training day by producing a guitar and performing his own songs, including "Free Love Freeway", and he attempts to motivate staff with an improvised dance at the office party that became the show's most famous scene.

Story arc

Over the series Brent is made redundant, fails in a career as a motivational speaker, and appears briefly in the documentary's aftermath as a minor celebrity. In the 2016 film he works as a travelling salesman for a cleaning-products company while cashing in his pension to fund a self-indulgent tour with his band, Foregone Conclusion, who barely tolerate him. The character nevertheless ends on a note of pathos: beneath the monstrous ego, the show consistently reveals a lonely man terrified of insignificance.

Legacy

Brent served as the model for Michael Scott, the central character of the American version of The Office. He was voted among the greatest comedy characters in British television history, and Gervais's performance won him two BAFTA awards.
