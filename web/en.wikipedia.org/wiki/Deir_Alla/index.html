Deir Alla

Location: Balqa Governorate, Jordan

Region: Jordan Valley

Type: Tell (archaeological mound)

Deir Alla (Arabic: دير علا) is a town and archaeological site in the Balqa Governorate of Jordan, in the Jordan Valley near the confluence of the Zarqa River (the biblical Jabbok) with the Jordan River. The ancient tell at Deir Alla has been identified by many scholars, though not conclusively, with the biblical city of Succoth, and also proposed as Penuel.

Archaeology

The mound was excavated by a Dutch expedition under H. J. Franken beginning in 1960. Occupation spans the Bronze and Iron Ages, including a Late Bronze Age sanctuary. The site's most famous find came in 1967, when excavators recovered hundreds of fragments of a plaster text written in ink — the Deir Alla inscription — from a room destroyed by earthquake in the Iron Age, around the ninth or eighth century BC.

The inscription

The text, written in a local Canaanite–Aramaic-related dialect, records visions of a seer named Balaam son of Beor, the same figure who appears in the Book of Numbers in the Hebrew Bible, making the find of major significance for the study of ancient Israelite religion and West Semitic languages.

Modern town

The present-day town is an agricultural centre of the Jordan Valley and hosts an agricultural research station; an archaeological museum displays material from the excavations and nearby sites.
