Empress Myeongseong

Empress Myeongseong

Born: 19 October 1851, Yeoju, Joseon

Died: 8 October 1895, Gyeongbok Palace, Seoul (assassinated)

Spouse: King (later Emperor) Gojong

Known as: Queen Min

Posthumous title: Empress Myeongseong (1897)

Empress Myeongseong (명성황후; 19 October 1851 – 8 October 1895), commonly known as Queen Min, was the queen consort of King Gojong of Joseon Korea and one of the most powerful political figures of the late dynasty. A skilled politician who sought to counter growing Japanese influence by drawing closer to Russia, she was assassinated by Japanese agents in 1895 in the incident known as the Eulmi Incident.

Early life and marriage

Born into the Yeoheung Min clan in Yeoju, Gyeonggi Province, she was orphaned young and raised in modest circumstances. In 1866 she was selected as queen for the young King Gojong, in part because her family lacked powerful connections that might threaten the regent, Gojong's father the Heungseon Daewongun. She proved far more formidable than expected: an avid reader of history and statecraft, she built her own faction at court and by the early 1870s had helped push the Daewongun from power.

Political career

Queen Min led a cautiously modernising but conservative faction, resisting both her father-in-law's isolationism and radical reformers. She survived an attempt on her life during the Imo Incident of 1882, fleeing the palace in disguise. After the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) placed Korea under Japanese dominance, she manoeuvred to increase Russian influence at court to dilute Japanese control.

Assassination

On 8 October 1895, Japanese agents and soldiers, acting with the involvement of the Japanese minister Miura Gorō, stormed Gyeongbok Palace and murdered the queen in the Okhoru pavilion; her body was burned. The killing, known as the Eulmi Incident, provoked international outrage and a wave of anti-Japanese righteous-army uprisings in Korea. Gojong took refuge in the Russian legation the following year. In 1897, having proclaimed the Korean Empire, he granted his late wife the posthumous title Empress Myeongseong.

Legacy

In South Korea she is remembered as a national heroine who resisted imperial encroachment; her life has been dramatised in television series, film, and the long-running musical The Last Empress.
