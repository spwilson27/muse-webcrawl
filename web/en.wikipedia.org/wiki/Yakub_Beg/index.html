Yakub Beg

Born: 1820, Pskent, Khanate of Kokand
Died: 29 May 1877, Korla, Xinjiang
Known for: Ruling Kashgaria (Yettishar), 1865–1877

Muhammad Yaqub Beg (Uzbek: Yoqub Bek; 1820 – 29 May 1877), commonly known as Yakub Beg, was an adventurer and military commander from the Khanate of Kokand who seized control of Kashgar and most of the Tarim Basin in Xinjiang between 1865 and 1877, ruling an Islamic state known as Kashgaria or Yettishar ("Seven Cities").

Rise to power

Yaqub Beg rose as a general in Kokand's service amid the chaos of the Dungan Revolt and Khoja uprisings that shattered Qing authority in Xinjiang in the 1860s. Sent to Kashgar in 1865 escorting a Khoja claimant, he rapidly displaced his nominal master and, by 1870, controlled the southern oases and had taken Turpan and Ürümqi in the north.

Rule and foreign relations

Styling himself Atalik Ghazi, Yaqub Beg built an autocratic administration, reformed taxation and customs, and courted foreign recognition as a bulwark in the Anglo-Russian "Great Game." Both Britain and Russia sent missions to Kashgar and concluded commercial treaties with him; the Ottoman sultan granted him the title of emir. His rule, however, was financially burdensome and harsh for the local population.

Fall

The Qing general Zuo Zongtang's methodical reconquest of Xinjiang beginning in 1876 stripped away Yaqub Beg's northern possessions. Yaqub Beg died at Korla in May 1877 — reportedly by suicide, poison, or illness — as Qing forces advanced, and his state quickly collapsed; Kashgar fell by the end of 1877.
