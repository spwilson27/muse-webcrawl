Troitse-Sergiyeva Lavra

The Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius (Russian: Троице-Сергиева лавра, Troitse-Sergiyeva Lavra) is the most important Russian monastery and the spiritual centre of the Russian Orthodox Church. It is situated in the town of Sergiyev Posad, about 70 kilometres northeast of Moscow by road, on the historic route to Yaroslavl.

History

The monastery was founded in 1337 by St. Sergius of Radonezh, one of Russia's most venerated saints, who built a wooden church in honour of the Holy Trinity deep in the forest. The community grew into a major landowner and a fortress of Russian national identity; in 1408 it was burned by the Tatars and rebuilt in stone. During the Time of Troubles, the lavra famously withstood a 16-month Polish–Lithuanian siege (1608–1610). In 1744 Empress Elizabeth conferred on it the honorific title of lavra, the highest rank for an Orthodox monastery.

Architecture

The ensemble includes more than fifty buildings, among them the Trinity Cathedral (1422–23), which houses the relics of St. Sergius and icons by Andrei Rublev, who painted his famous Trinity for its iconostasis; the Assumption Cathedral (1559–1585), modelled on its Kremlin namesake; and an elegant five-tier bell tower 88 metres high. In 1993 the complex was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

The monastery today

Closed by the Soviet authorities in 1920, the lavra was returned to the Church in the 1940s and again became the residence of the Patriarch for a period. It now houses several hundred monks and the Moscow Theological Academy and Seminary, and receives a constant stream of pilgrims.
