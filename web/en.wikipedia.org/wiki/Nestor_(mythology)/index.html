Nestor (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Nestor (Ancient Greek: Νέστωρ) of Gerenia was a legendary king of Pylos, the son of Neleus and Chloris. He is a central figure in Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, where he appears as the elderly, wise counsellor of the Achaean army at Troy, famed for his eloquence, memory, and long experience.

Mythology

Nestor was the youngest son of Neleus, king of Pylos. When Heracles slew Neleus and all of Nestor's eleven brothers, Nestor alone survived because he was being raised away from home in Gerenia. Apollo granted him a life spanning three generations of men, which explains his great age in the Trojan War, in which he nonetheless still fought as a charioteer and adviser.

In the Iliad

In the Iliad, Nestor repeatedly tries to reconcile Agamemnon and Achilles, urges the Achaeans to build a defensive wall, and recounts long stories of his youthful exploits against the Epeians and in the battle of the Lapiths and Centaurs. His son Antilochus is killed by Memnon. Nestor's great cup, described in Book 11, inspired a famous inscribed Geometric-era vessel from Pithekoussai known as "Nestor's Cup".

In the Odyssey

Unlike many Greek heroes, Nestor returned home safely and quickly after the sack of Troy. In Book 3 of the Odyssey, Telemachus visits him at Pylos seeking news of his father Odysseus; Nestor receives him hospitably and sends his son Peisistratus to accompany him to Sparta.

Legacy

Because of his legendary longevity and wisdom, "a Nestor" became a proverbial term for a wise elder statesman or the oldest and most venerable member of a group. The Bronze Age palace excavated at Ano Englianos near Pylos is popularly called the Palace of Nestor.
