The Lake of Sainte-Croix (French: Lac de Sainte-Croix) is a reservoir in southeastern France, created on the river Verdon between the departments of Var and Alpes-de-Haute-Provence. It is the third-largest lake in France by surface area and is known for its striking turquoise-coloured water.

History

The lake was formed by the construction of the Sainte-Croix dam, built by Électricité de France between 1971 and 1974 and put into service in 1973. Filling the reservoir drowned the valley floor and submerged the village of Les Salles-sur-Verdon, which was rebuilt on higher ground, as well as two road bridges and a large spring, the Fontaine-l'Évêque, which once fed the Verdon. The project also required expropriating land from several communes around the lake, including Sainte-Croix-du-Verdon, Bauduen and Les Salles.

Geography

The reservoir extends upstream to the entrance of the Gorges du Verdon, one of Europe's most famous river canyons. The river flows into the lake beneath the Galetas bridge, a popular viewpoint where visitors can rent boats and pedal craft to enter the lower gorge. The lake covers about 22 km², is up to 93 metres deep, and holds roughly 760 million cubic metres of water. Its hydroelectric installation is complemented by a smaller plant at Quinson downstream.

Tourism

The lake is a major summer resort area, with beaches and waterside villages at Les Salles-sur-Verdon, Sainte-Croix-du-Verdon and Bauduen. Swimming, sailing, windsurfing and electric boating are popular; internal-combustion engines are restricted to protect the water quality, since the reservoir also supplies drinking water and irrigation to the region.
