Dirichlet series

In mathematics, a Dirichlet series is any series of the form

∑n=1∞ an / ns,

where s is complex and an is a complex sequence. It is a special case of a general Dirichlet series. Dirichlet series play an important role in analytic number theory. The most famous Dirichlet series is the Riemann zeta function, which has the Dirichlet character of the trivial character.

Convergence

A Dirichlet series converges absolutely in some right half-plane if the coefficients do not grow too fast. The boundary between convergence and divergence is characterized by the abscissa of convergence and the abscissa of absolute convergence; unlike power series, these can differ, and the region of convergence is a half-plane rather than a disc. By analytic continuation, the function defined by a Dirichlet series may extend to a meromorphic function on a much larger domain, as happens with the zeta function.

Euler products

When the coefficient sequence is multiplicative, the Dirichlet series factors as an Euler product over the prime numbers, expressing the connection between the additive structure of the integers and their prime factorization. This identity is the analytic engine behind Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, proved using the L-functions built from Dirichlet characters.

Examples

  • The Riemann zeta function ζ(s), with all coefficients equal to 1.
  • Dirichlet L-functions, whose coefficients are periodic Dirichlet characters.
  • The Dirichlet eta function, an alternating analogue of ζ(s).
  • Series attached to arithmetic functions such as the Möbius function (giving 1/ζ(s)) and Euler's totient function.
