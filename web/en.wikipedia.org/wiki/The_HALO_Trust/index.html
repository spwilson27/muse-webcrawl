The HALO Trust (Hazardous Area Life-support Organization) is a non-political and non-religious registered British charity and American non-profit organisation that removes debris left behind by war, in particular land mines. Founded in 1988 by former British Army colonel Colin Campbell Mitchell, his wife Sue Mitchell, and Guy Willoughby, it began work in Afghanistan in the wake of the Soviet war there.

Operations

HALO recruits and trains local deminers and operates in dozens of countries and territories, including Cambodia, Angola, Mozambique, Sri Lanka, Kosovo, Ukraine, and Afghanistan. It has cleared hundreds of thousands of landmines and millions of items of unexploded ordnance, and also conducts mine-risk education, survey, and weapons and ammunition management. It is the world's largest humanitarian mine-action organisation, employing roughly ten thousand people, most of them nationals of affected countries.

Public profile

Diana, Princess of Wales, famously walked through a HALO-cleared lane in an active minefield near Huambo, Angola, in January 1997 wearing a HALO flak jacket and visor, bringing global attention to the campaign for a landmine ban, which culminated in the Ottawa Treaty later that year. Her son Prince Harry has continued the association, walking a minefield in Angola in 2019.
