Linguistic discrimination

Linguistic discrimination (also called glottophobia or linguicism) is the unfair treatment of individuals based on their use of language, including their native language, dialect, accent, or vocabulary. It can operate through laws and institutional policies, as well as through everyday social judgements in employment, education, housing and the legal system.

Forms

Forms of linguistic discrimination include official-language policies that disadvantage minority-language speakers, discrimination against speakers of stigmatised dialects or creoles in schools and workplaces, and accent-based bias in hiring. In the United States, disputes have arisen over "English-only" workplace rules; in other countries, speakers of regional languages have historically been punished in schools for using their mother tongue.

Scholarship and law

The concept of "linguicism" was developed by the linguist Tove Skutnabb-Kangas to parallel other forms of discrimination. Many constitutions and human-rights instruments protect language rights to some degree, though protections for accent and dialect are usually weaker than those for named languages. Courts have sometimes treated accent discrimination as a form of national-origin discrimination.
