Aloha ʻOe

"Aloha ʻOe" ("Farewell to Thee") is the best-known song composed by Liliʻuokalani, the last monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, who wrote it in 1878. A lament of parting between lovers, it became an enduring symbol of Hawaiian culture and of farewell generally.

Composition and spread

According to tradition, Liliʻuokalani was inspired by a tender parting embrace she witnessed at Maunawili ranch on Oahu, adapting the melody from the earlier song "The Lone Rock by the Sea". The Royal Hawaiian Band under Henry Berger helped popularise it, and it was performed for departing visitors at Honolulu's piers for decades, accompanied by flower leis.

In popular culture

The song has been recorded by countless artists, quoted in films and cartoons, and used at graduations, funerals and closing ceremonies throughout Hawaii. Elvis Presley included a version in the film Blue Hawaii (1961). Its chorus — "Aloha ʻoe, aloha ʻoe" — is recognised worldwide as a Hawaiian farewell.
