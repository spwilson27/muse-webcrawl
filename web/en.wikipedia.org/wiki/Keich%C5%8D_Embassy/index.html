Keichō Embassy

The Keichō Embassy (Japanese: 慶長遣欧使節, Keichō Ken'ō Shisetsu) was a Japanese diplomatic mission to Europe in the early 17th century, sent by the daimyō Date Masamune of Sendai in 1613. It was the first Japanese embassy to visit the Americas and crossed the Pacific and Atlantic oceans.

Voyage

Led by Hasekura Tsunenaga and accompanied by the Franciscan friar Luis Sotelo, the embassy sailed aboard the Japanese-built galleon San Juan Bautista from Tsukinoura in October 1613, reaching Mexico in 1614. The party then crossed the Atlantic to Spain, where they were received by King Philip III, and continued to Rome, where Hasekura was received by Pope Paul V in 1615.

Return and legacy

The embassy returned to Japan in 1620, by which time the Tokugawa shogunate had begun its policy of isolation and persecution of Christians, nullifying the mission's goals of trade and religious exchange. The voyage remains a celebrated episode in Japanese diplomatic history, and Hasekura is honoured as the first Japanese ambassador to the Americas and Europe.
