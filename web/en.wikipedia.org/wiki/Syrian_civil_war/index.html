Syrian civil war
Date: 2011 – 2024 (major phase)
Location: Syria
Parties: Assad government, opposition, Kurdish-led SDF, Islamic State, foreign powers

The Syrian civil war is a multi-sided conflict in Syria that began in 2011 with pro-democracy protests during the Arab Spring and a violent government crackdown under President Bashar al-Assad.

Course

Armed rebellion formed the Free Syrian Army; jihadist groups including al-Nusra Front and the Islamic State (IS) seized territory, prompting US-led intervention against IS from 2014 and Russian intervention supporting Assad from 2015. Turkey intervened in the north; Kurdish-led forces (SDF) controlled the northeast. Key battles included Aleppo (2012–16), Raqqa (2017) and sieges of Ghouta. Chemical weapons attacks, notably Ghouta (2013) and Khan Shaykhun (2017), drew international condemnation.

Outcome

In late November 2024 a Hayat Tahrir al-Sham-led offensive seized Aleppo, Hama, Homs and, on 8 December 2024, Damascus; Assad fled to Russia, ending over five decades of Assad family rule, and a transitional government formed. The war killed an estimated 500,000–600,000+ people and displaced over half the pre-war population, with millions of refugees abroad.
