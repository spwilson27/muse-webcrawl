Bristol is a city and unitary authority in South West England, straddling the River Avon. It is the most populous city in the region, with about 470,000 inhabitants in the city proper and over a million in the wider urban area. Bristol received a royal charter in 1155 and was granted county status in 1373, one of the first places in England so honoured.

History

Bristol grew wealthy as a port trading in wool, wine and, later, tobacco and enslaved people: from the late 17th century its merchants played a central role in the transatlantic slave trade, a legacy the city continues to confront — most visibly in June 2020, when protesters toppled the statue of slave trader Edward Colston into the harbour. In the 19th century Isambard Kingdom Brunel transformed the city, building the ss Great Western, the ss Great Britain and the Clifton Suspension Bridge across the Avon Gorge. The city was heavily bombed in the Bristol Blitz of 1940–41.

Economy and culture

Bristol has major aerospace (Airbus, Rolls-Royce), creative media and financial sectors, and two universities, the University of Bristol and the University of the West of England. It is known for its music scene — birthplace of trip hop acts Massive Attack and Portishead — its street art, including works by Banksy, and the annual Bristol International Balloon Fiesta.
