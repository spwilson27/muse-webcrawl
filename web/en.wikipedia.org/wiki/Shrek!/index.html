Shrek! is a 1990 American picture book written and illustrated by the cartoonist William Steig (1907–2003). The book tells the story of Shrek, a hideous green ogre who leaves his parents' swamp to find a hideous princess, guided by a donkey and by a witch's prophecy. The name "Shrek" derives from the Yiddish word shrek, meaning "fright" or "terror", cognate with the German Schreck.

Plot

Shrek's parents, deciding their son is old enough to go out into the world, literally kick him out of their swamp. Along the way he meets a witch, who tells his fortune: he will be carried by a donkey, meet a knight, and marry a princess even uglier than he is. Shrek delights in his own hideousness and in frightening peasants, animals, and even thunder and lightning. After defeating the knight and entering the castle, he encounters the princess, and the two immediately fall in love, appalled and delighted by each other's monstrous appearance. They marry and live "horribly ever after," scaring the socks off all who fell within range of their 99-decibel voices.

Reception and legacy

The book was praised for Steig's distinctive prose, filled with wordplay and mock-medieval flavour, and for subverting fairy-tale conventions: beauty is ugliness, and the monsters find happiness in being monstrous. In 2001, DreamWorks Animation adapted the book into the animated film Shrek, which became a major critical and commercial success, winning the first Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. The film radically expanded Steig's story and launched a long-running franchise of sequels, spin-offs, and a stage musical.

Author

William Steig was a celebrated New Yorker cartoonist who began writing children's books in his sixties, including Sylvester and the Magic Pebble (1969), which won the Caldecott Medal. Shrek!, published when Steig was 82, became his best-known book through the success of the film adaptation.
