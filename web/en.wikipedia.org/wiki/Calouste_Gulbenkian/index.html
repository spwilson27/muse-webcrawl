Calouste Sarkis Gulbenkian (23 March 1869 – 20 July 1955) was an Armenian businessman, art collector and philanthropist. He played a major role in making the petroleum reserves of the Middle East available to Western development and is credited with being the first person to exploit Iraqi oil. He acquired the nickname "Mr Five Per Cent" for the share of oil concessions he habitually retained for himself.

Oil career

Born in Constantinople to an Armenian merchant family and educated in London, Gulbenkian brokered the interests that became the Turkish Petroleum Company (later the Iraq Petroleum Company), in which the 1928 "Red Line Agreement" gave him a 5% interest across most of the former Ottoman territories. His holdings and intermediation between governments and companies created one of the largest private fortunes of his era.

Art collection and legacy

Gulbenkian amassed one of the greatest private art collections of the 20th century — some 6,000 objects spanning Egyptian antiquities, Islamic art, European painting (including works by Rembrandt, Rubens and Monet), Lalique jewellery and decorative arts — much of it assembled from sales by the Soviet Hermitage in the 1920s–30s. He settled in Lisbon during the Second World War and left his fortune and collection to the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation, established in 1956, which maintains the Calouste Gulbenkian Museum in Lisbon (opened 1969) and supports arts, science, education and charity, particularly in Portugal and the Armenian diaspora.
