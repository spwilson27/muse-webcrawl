Glutamic acid

Glutamic acid
FormulaC₅H₉NO₄
Typeα-amino acid, acidic

Glutamic acid (symbol Glu or E) is an α-amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It is non-essential in humans, meaning the body can synthesise it. Its side chain carboxylate is glutamate.

Function

Glutamate is the most abundant excitatory neurotransmitter in the vertebrate nervous system and a precursor for GABA, glutamine and glutathione. In food, glutamate produces the savoury umami taste.

History and food use

Glutamic acid was isolated from wheat gluten in 1866. Kikunae Ikeda identified glutamate as the source of umami in kombu broth in 1908, leading to monosodium glutamate (MSG) production. See umami.
