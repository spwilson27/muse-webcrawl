Tadalafil

Trade names: Cialis, Adcirca, others

Class: PDE5 inhibitor

Uses: Erectile dysfunction, benign prostatic hyperplasia, pulmonary arterial hypertension

Elimination half-life: 17.5 hours

Formula: C22H19N3O4

Tadalafil, sold under the brand name Cialis among others, is a medication used to treat erectile dysfunction, benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), and pulmonary arterial hypertension. It is taken by mouth. Onset is typically within half an hour, and the duration is up to 36 hours — much longer than comparable drugs — which earned it the nickname "the weekend pill".

Medical uses

Tadalafil was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2003 for erectile dysfunction, and later for the signs and symptoms of BPH (2011) and, under the brand name Adcirca, for pulmonary arterial hypertension (2009). It is available in on-demand doses (10–20 mg) and a low daily dose (2.5–5 mg), the latter also approved for BPH.

Mechanism of action

Tadalafil is a selective inhibitor of phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5), the enzyme that degrades cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) in smooth muscle. Inhibiting PDE5 increases cGMP levels, promoting smooth-muscle relaxation and increased blood flow in the corpus cavernosum of the penis and in the pulmonary vasculature. It differs chemically from sildenafil and vardenafil, being a β-carboline derivative, and has greater selectivity for PDE5 over PDE6 but some activity at PDE11.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, indigestion, back pain, muscle aches, flushing, and nasal congestion. Serious but rare effects include priapism, sudden vision or hearing loss, and dangerously low blood pressure when combined with nitrates or certain alpha blockers; co-administration with organic nitrates is contraindicated.

History

Tadalafil was discovered by Glaxo Wellcome (now GSK) in partnership with ICOS Corporation and developed and marketed by Eli Lilly and ICOS as Cialis. Following patent expiry, generic versions became widely available in many countries from the late 2010s.
