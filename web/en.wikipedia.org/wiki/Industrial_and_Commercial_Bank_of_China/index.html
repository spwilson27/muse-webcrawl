The Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC; Chinese: 中国工商银行) is a Chinese multinational banking company. Founded on 1 January 1984, it is commonly cited as the largest bank in the world by total assets and by tier 1 capital, and is one of China's "Big Four" state-owned commercial banks.

ICBC is the world's largest bank by assets.

History

ICBC was created in 1984 when the People's Bank of China shed its commercial banking operations to concentrate on central banking. It grew alongside China's economic expansion, and in October 2006 it completed what was then the world's largest initial public offering, raising about US$21.9 billion with a simultaneous dual listing in Hong Kong and Shanghai. The bank has consistently ranked at or near the top of global league tables by assets, deposits, loans and net profit.

Operations

ICBC provides corporate and personal banking, treasury operations, investment banking, asset management, and insurance and financial leasing through subsidiaries. It serves hundreds of millions of personal customers and several million corporate customers in China, and operates branches and subsidiaries across Asia, Europe, the Americas, the Middle East, Africa and Oceania. It is designated a global systemically important bank by the Financial Stability Board.
