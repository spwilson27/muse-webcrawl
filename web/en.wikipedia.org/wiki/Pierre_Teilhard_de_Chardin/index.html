Born1 May 1881, Orcines, France
Died10 April 1955, New York City, United States
OccupationJesuit priest, palaeontologist, philosopher

Pierre Teilhard de Chardin SJ (1 May 1881 – 10 April 1955) was a French Jesuit priest, scientist, palaeontologist and philosopher who developed a distinctive synthesis of evolutionary science and Christian theology, centred on the idea that the universe is evolving towards an ultimate point of maximum complexity and consciousness, which he called the Omega Point.

Life

Born at the Château of Sarcenat near Clermont-Ferrand, Teilhard entered the Society of Jesus in 1899 and studied geology and palaeontology in Paris. He served as a stretcher-bearer in the First World War, an experience that shaped his mystical writings. From 1923 he worked in China, participating in expeditions in Central Asia and in the research at Zhoukoudian associated with the discovery of Peking Man. His theological writings — above all Le Milieu Divin and The Phenomenon of Man (written 1938–40) — could not be published in his lifetime because of opposition from the Holy Office, and he spent his final years in New York, dying there on Easter Sunday 1955.

Thought

Teilhard described successive envelopes of the Earth: the geosphere, the biosphere, and the noosphere — the sphere of human thought, a term he helped popularise. Evolution, in his view, has an intrinsic direction ("orthogenesis") toward greater "complexification" and consciousness, converging on the Omega Point, which he identified theologically with Christ. His works were published posthumously and became hugely influential, though a 1962 monitum (warning) from the Holy Office cautioned against his theological positions, even as later popes and theologians have praised aspects of his vision.
