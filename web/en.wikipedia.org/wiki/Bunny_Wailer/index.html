Bunny Wailer

Neville O'Riley Livingston OM (10 April 1947 – 2 March 2021), known professionally as Bunny Wailer, was a Jamaican singer-songwriter and percussionist. He was an original member of the reggae group The Wailers along with Bob Marley and Peter Tosh. A three-time Grammy Award winner, he is considered one of the longtime standard-bearers of reggae music. He was also known as Jah B.

Early life

Livingston was born in Nine Miles, Jamaica, where he spent his earliest years. His father, Thaddeus Livingston, ran a grocery store. As a young child, Livingston befriended Bob Marley, and the two later became stepbrothers when Marley's mother Cedella Booker married Livingston's father. The boys were raised together in Nine Miles and later in the Trenchtown area of Kingston.

The Wailers

In 1963, Livingston co-founded the vocal group that became the Wailers with Bob Marley, Peter Tosh, Junior Braithwaite, Beverley Kelso, and Cherry Smith. They recorded first in the ska style for producer Coxsone Dodd at Studio One, scoring a Jamaican hit with "Simmer Down" in 1964. Livingston handled harmony vocals and percussion and occasionally sang lead, as on "Who Feels It Knows It" and "Dreamland". After serving a prison sentence for cannabis possession in 1967–68, he rejoined the group for its early reggae recordings with Lee "Scratch" Perry.

Solo career

Livingston left the Wailers in 1973, reluctant to tour internationally. His 1976 solo debut, Blackheart Man, is widely regarded as a roots reggae classic. Through his own Solomonic label he released albums including Protest (1977), Struggle (1978), and Rock 'n' Groove (1981). He won the Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album three times: for Time Will Tell: A Tribute to Bob Marley (1990), Hall of Fame: A Tribute to Bob Marley's 50th Anniversary (1995), and Communication (1999).

Death and legacy

Bunny Wailer died on 2 March 2021 at a hospital in Kingston, Jamaica, aged 73. He was the last surviving founding member of the Wailers. In 2012 he received the Order of Jamaica, and in 2017 he was awarded the Order of Merit, one of Jamaica's highest honours. He is remembered as the most overtly spiritual and roots-oriented member of the Wailers.
