Godrevy (Cornish: Godrevi) is an area on the north coast of Cornwall, England, at the eastern end of St Ives Bay, near the town of Hayle. It comprises Godrevy Head, a headland largely owned by the National Trust, the long sandy Godrevy beach — the northern section of the three-mile beach stretching from Hayle — and offshore Godrevy Island with its lighthouse.

Godrevy Lighthouse

Godrevy Lighthouse, a 26-metre white octagonal tower built in 1858–59 on Godrevy Island, was erected after the wreck of the steamer SS Nile on the treacherous Stones reef in 1854 with the loss of all aboard. The light was automated in 1939 and the station later converted to solar power. The lighthouse is widely believed to have inspired Virginia Woolf's novel To the Lighthouse (1927): Woolf visited St Ives as a child, and the lighthouse in the novel is modelled on Godrevy, though set in the Hebrides.

Nature and recreation

The headland and cliffs are noted for wildflowers, breeding seabirds and grey seals hauled out below Mutton Cove, and the beach is a popular surfing spot. The South West Coast Path crosses the headland.
