Goldeneye (estate)

Goldeneye is the original name of James Bond novelist Ian Fleming's estate on Oracabessa Bay on the northern coastline of Jamaica. He purchased the land in 1946 and built his house there in 1947, naming it after Operation Goldeneye, a wartime plan he had worked on while serving in British Naval Intelligence.

Ian Fleming

Fleming wintered at Goldeneye every year from 1947 until his death in 1964. It was there, beginning in January 1952, that he wrote all fourteen of his James Bond books, starting with Casino Royale. The house was deliberately plain — Fleming said he wanted no distractions from writing — with a sunken garden, a small private beach and a reef offshore in the lagoon. Guests over the years included Noël Coward, whose own Jamaican home, Firefly, was nearby, as well as actors, politicians and writers.

The name later provided the title of the 1995 James Bond film GoldenEye, the first to star Pierce Brosnan.

Later history

In 1976 the estate was acquired by Chris Blackwell, founder of Island Records, whose mother Blanche Blackwell had been a close friend of Fleming. Blackwell expanded the property, adding villas, cottages and the Fleming Villa itself as accommodation, and developed it into a resort operated by Island Outpost. The estate covers about 52 acres and includes the original house, where visiting writers in residence continue the property's literary association.
