Shatapatha Brahmana

The Shatapatha Brahmana (Sanskrit: "Brahmana of the hundred paths") is a prose text describing Vedic rituals, attached to the Shukla Yajurveda. It is one of the largest and most important of the Brahmana texts, composed roughly in the 8th to 6th centuries BCE.

Contents

Surviving in two recensions, Madhyandina (14 books) and Kanva (17 books), the text gives detailed instructions for major soma sacrifices, the building of fire altars (agnicayana), and royal consecrations. It also preserves myths and legends, including an early version of the flood story of Manu, and speculations that fed into later Upanishadic thought.

Authorship and study

Tradition associates parts of the text with the sage Yajnavalkya. The Madhyandina recension was translated into English by Julius Eggeling in the Sacred Books of the East series (1882-1900), making it a key source for the study of ancient Indian religion.
