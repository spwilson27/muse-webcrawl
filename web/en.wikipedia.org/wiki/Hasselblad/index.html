Hasselblad

Victor Hasselblad AB is a Swedish manufacturer of medium format cameras and photographic equipment, headquartered in Gothenburg, Sweden. The company is best known for the classic medium format camera system used by professional photographers for decades, and for supplying the cameras used by NASA astronauts on the Moon during the Apollo program.

History

The firm began as a trading company founded in 1841 by Fritz Wiktor Hasselblad. His great-grandson Victor Hasselblad developed an aerial reconnaissance camera for the Swedish Air Force during the Second World War, and in 1948 the company launched the 1600F, its first civilian medium format camera. The 500C of 1957 established the modular V System — interchangeable lenses, film backs and viewfinders — that became a studio standard.

Space photography

Modified Hasselblad cameras were flown on Mercury, Gemini and Apollo missions. The Hasselblad 500EL Data Camera carried by Apollo 11 produced the iconic photographs of the first Moon landing in July 1969; a dozen cameras were left on the lunar surface, with only their film magazines returned to Earth.

Digital era

Hasselblad moved into digital medium format with the H System and later the mirrorless X1D (2016), one of the first compact medium format mirrorless cameras. In 2017 the Chinese drone maker DJI acquired a majority stake in the company, and Hasselblad cameras have since been integrated into DJI's Mavic drones.
