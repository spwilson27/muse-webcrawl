Eannatum

Eannatum (Sumerian: É.AN.NA-tum2) was a Sumerian king (ensi) of Lagash who reigned around 2454–2425 BCE (middle chronology). He was the grandson of Ur-Nanshe, founder of the First Dynasty of Lagash, and the son of Akurgal. Eannatum established one of the first verifiable empires in history, conquering much of Sumer and campaigning beyond it.

Conquests

Eannatum subdued the cities of Ur, Uruk, Kish, Akshak, Larsa, and Nippur, and defeated Elam and Subartu to the east. His most famous conflict was a border war with the neighbouring city-state of Umma over the fertile Gu-Edin plain. His victory over Umma, whose ruler Ush was killed, is commemorated on the Stele of the Vultures, one of the earliest known historical monuments depicting warfare, which shows the king leading his phalanx and vultures carrying off the heads of the slain. Eannatum also imposed a boundary treaty and erected monuments at the border, recorded in his inscriptions.

Legacy

Despite his power, Lagash's dominance waned after his death; his brother Enannatum I succeeded him, and Umma soon renewed the conflict. Eannatum's inscriptions and the Stele of the Vultures (now largely in the Louvre) are key sources for Early Dynastic III Sumer.
