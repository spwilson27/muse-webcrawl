Diagnosis

Diagnosis (pl.: diagnoses) is the identification of the nature and cause of a certain phenomenon. Diagnosis is used in many different disciplines, with variations in the use of logic, analytics, and experience, to determine "cause and effect". In medicine, a diagnosis is the process of determining which disease or condition explains a person's symptoms and signs; the information required is typically collected from a history and physical examination, supplemented by laboratory tests and medical imaging.

Medical diagnosis

The diagnostic process begins with the patient's medical history, followed by examination and the formation of a differential diagnosis — a list of candidate conditions ranked by likelihood. Further investigations narrow the possibilities, and a working diagnosis guides treatment. Diagnosis can be complicated by non-specific symptoms, co-existing conditions, and rare diseases; diagnostic error remains a significant cause of patient harm, and much research is devoted to reducing it.

Other uses

The term is also used in fields such as computer troubleshooting (diagnostics), organisational analysis, education, and nursing, where nursing diagnosis identifies human responses to health conditions rather than diseases themselves.
