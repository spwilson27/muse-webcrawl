Norfolk (/ˈnɔːrfək/) is an independent city in the Commonwealth of Virginia in the United States. At the 2020 census, the population was 238,005, making it the third-most populous city in Virginia after neighboring Virginia Beach and Chesapeake.

Norfolk is located at the core of the Hampton Roads metropolitan area, named for the large natural harbor of the same name located at the mouth of Chesapeake Bay. It is one of nine cities and seven counties that constitute the Hampton Roads metro area, officially known as the Virginia Beach–Norfolk–Newport News, VA–NC MSA.

History

Norfolk was established in 1682 and incorporated in 1736. The city was burned by retreating British forces and local loyalists early in the American Revolutionary War in January 1776. During the American Civil War, the harbor was the site of the Battle of Hampton Roads in March 1862, the first battle between ironclad warships, the USS Monitor and the CSS Virginia.

Naval presence

Norfolk is home to Naval Station Norfolk, the world's largest naval station by supported population, home port to aircraft carriers and other vessels of the United States Atlantic Fleet. The city also hosts NATO's Allied Command Transformation headquarters. The maritime industry, defense contracting, and port cargo operations through the Port of Virginia are central to the local economy.

Geography and climate

The city is bordered by multiple bodies of water: the Elizabeth River, Willoughby Bay, and Chesapeake Bay. Much of Norfolk sits at low elevation and is vulnerable to sea level rise and recurrent tidal flooding. Norfolk has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild winters.

Culture

Norfolk is home to the Chrysler Museum of Art, Nauticus (including the battleship USS Wisconsin), the Virginia Opera, the Virginia Stage Company, and Old Dominion University and Norfolk State University. The city is sometimes symbolized by its mermaid statues scattered throughout downtown and Ocean View.
