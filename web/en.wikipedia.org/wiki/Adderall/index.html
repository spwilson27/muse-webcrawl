Adderall
Combination of amphetamine salts
Clinical data: prescription stimulant
Legal status: Schedule II (US)

Adderall and Mydayis are trade names for a combination drug containing four salts of amphetamine, used in the treatment of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and narcolepsy.

Medical uses

The mixture of dextroamphetamine and levoamphetamine salts increases dopamine and norepinephrine activity in the brain, improving attention and impulse control in many people with ADHD. Formulations include immediate-release and extended-release (Adderall XR) versions.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include appetite loss, insomnia, dry mouth, anxiety and increased heart rate and blood pressure. The drug has a high potential for misuse and dependence and is tightly regulated.

History

Adderall was introduced in 1996 by Richwood Pharmaceuticals, reusing an earlier formulation marketed as Obetrol.
