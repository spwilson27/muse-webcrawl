Gaza envelope

Hebrewעוטף עזה (Otef Aza)
CountryIsrael
DistrictSouthern District
DefinitionCommunities within approx. 7 km of the Gaza border

The Gaza envelope (Hebrew: עוטף עזה, Otef Aza) is the term used in Israel for the populated areas in the Southern District adjacent to the Gaza Strip, generally defined as the communities lying within about seven kilometres of the Gaza border fence. The zone includes the city of Sderot, towns such as Netivot and Ofakim at its edge, and dozens of kibbutzim and moshavim, including Kfar Aza, Be'eri, Nahal Oz, Nir Oz, Re'im, Magen, Kerem Shalom and Sufa.

Background

Most of the communities were founded in the 1940s and 1950s as agricultural settlements, some as part of the "Eleven Points in the Negev" of 1946. After Israel's 2005 disengagement from Gaza and Hamas's takeover in 2007, the envelope became the principal target of rocket and mortar fire, incendiary balloons and cross-border attacks from Gaza, prompting Israeli government programmes for fortified rooms, subsidised housing and tax benefits to sustain the population.

October 2023 attack and aftermath

On 7 October 2023, Hamas-led militants breached the border and attacked many envelope communities, killing residents and taking hostages, with the Nova music festival near Re'im also attacked. The region was evacuated en masse — over 100,000 residents were displaced to hotels and temporary housing across Israel. Reconstruction and the return of residents, coordinated by the Tkuma Directorate established in 2023, proceeded gradually from 2024, with several kibbutzim planning multi-year rebuilding.
