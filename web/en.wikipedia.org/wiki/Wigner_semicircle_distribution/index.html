The Wigner semicircle distribution, named after physicist Eugene Wigner, is the probability distribution supported on the interval [−R, R] whose probability density function is a semicircle of radius R centred at the origin and suitably normalised. It is famous as the limiting distribution of the eigenvalues of many random symmetric matrices — the content of Wigner's semicircle law.

Semicircle law

In random matrix theory, consider an n × n symmetric matrix whose entries above the diagonal are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1 (for example, a Wigner matrix, of which the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble is a special case). As n grows, the empirical distribution of the eigenvalues, scaled by 1/√n, converges to the semicircle distribution with radius R = 2. Wigner proved this in the 1950s in work motivated by the statistics of energy levels of heavy atomic nuclei, using the method of moments: the moments of the semicircle law are the Catalan numbers, which count the pairings that survive in the large-n limit.

Properties

The distribution has mean 0 and variance R²/4. Its odd moments vanish by symmetry, and its even moments for R = 2 are the Catalan numbers 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, ... The semicircle law is the free-probability analogue of the central limit theorem: in free probability, the semicircle distribution plays the role that the normal distribution plays classically, appearing as the limit of sums of freely independent random variables. Its Cauchy (Stieltjes) transform satisfies a simple quadratic equation, a fact exploited in modern proofs and in applications to wireless communications, statistics (covariance estimation) and theoretical physics.
