Vocal music

Vocal music is a type of singing performed by one or more singers, either with instrumental accompaniment or a cappella, in which singing (vocal production) provides the main focus of the piece. It is among the oldest forms of music: because it requires no instrument beyond the human body, vocal traditions exist in every known culture, and the voice was likely humanity's first musical instrument.

Forms

Vocal music spans an enormous range of genres and structures. In Western classical music they include the art song and Lied, the aria and other components of opera and oratorio, the Mass and motet in sacred music, the madrigal and partsong for ensemble, and large choral works with orchestra. Outside the classical canon, vocal music encompasses folk song, jazz singing, pop and rock vocals, rap, throat singing, yodelling and countless ritual and ceremonial traditions.

Voice types and ensembles

Singers are classified by vocal range and timbre into types such as soprano, mezzo-soprano, contralto, countertenor, tenor, baritone and bass. Vocal ensembles range from duets and chamber choirs to massed choirs of hundreds. Choral singing combines voices in parts, and harmonic techniques such as barbershop close harmony or Bulgarian folk dissonance are specific to vocal media. Extended vocal techniques — overtone singing, growling, belting, vocal fry and beatboxing — expand the palette of the voice beyond conventional pitch production.

Text and wordlessness

Most vocal music sets words, and the relation between text and music — prosody, word-painting, declamation — is a central compositional concern. But the voice is also used wordlessly: in vocalise exercises and works, in scat singing in jazz, in the syllabic chants of many traditions, and as a pure timbral instrument in film scores and contemporary composition.

History

Plainchant in medieval Europe, polyphonic writing of the Renaissance, the rise of opera around 1600, the Romantic Lied and the mass choral societies of the 19th century trace one arc of vocal music's development; parallel arcs run through the classical traditions of India and the Arab world, Chinese opera, African communal singing and innumerable local forms. Recording technology and the microphone transformed vocal style in the 20th century, enabling intimate crooning and making the singer the dominant figure of popular music.
