Absence of Malice is a 1981 American drama film directed by Sydney Pollack and written by Kurt Luedtke. It stars Paul Newman as Michael Gallagher, a Miami liquor distributor who becomes the target of a federal investigation leaked to a newspaper reporter played by Sally Field.

Plot

When a union leader disappears, federal prosecutor Elliot Rosen leaks Gallagher's FBI file to reporter Megan Carter, hoping pressure will force Gallagher to cooperate. Carter's story, though legally protected because it lacks "malice", ruins Gallagher's business and reputation and leads to tragedy when a source's alibi is exposed. Gallagher engineers a scheme that exposes the misconduct of the investigators and the newspaper, culminating in an inquiry led by a Department of Justice official played by Wilford Brimley.

Reception

The film was a commercial success and received three Academy Award nominations: Best Actor (Newman), Best Supporting Actress (Melinda Dillon) and Best Original Screenplay. It is frequently shown in journalism schools as a study of press ethics and the actual malice standard in libel law.
