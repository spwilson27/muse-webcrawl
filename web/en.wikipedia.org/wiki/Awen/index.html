Awen

LanguageWelsh, Cornish, Breton
MeaningPoetic inspiration, muse

Awen is a Welsh, Cornish and Breton word meaning (poetic) inspiration or muse. In Welsh tradition it is the flowing inspiration of the bard, associated with figures such as Taliesin and with the goddesses and mythology of medieval Welsh literature; it appears in the work of poets including Hywel ab Owain Gwynedd.

Symbol

The modern Awen symbol — three rays descending from three points, sometimes within circles — was devised by the 18th–19th-century Welsh antiquarian Iolo Morganwg. It is widely used in modern Druidry (notably the Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids) and Welsh cultural life, representing truth, light, or the balance of elements.

Modern use

Awen is invoked at the National Eisteddfod of Wales ceremonies and used as a name for organisations, schools and publishing ventures in Wales.
