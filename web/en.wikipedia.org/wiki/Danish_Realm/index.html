Overview

The Danish Realm, officially the Kingdom of Denmark, is the polity consisting of Denmark proper and its two autonomous territories in the North Atlantic: the Faroe Islands (home rule since 1948) and Greenland (home rule 1979, self-government 2009). The Danish term Rigsfællesskabet, "the Community of the Realm", captures its character as a composite state under one monarch and one constitution, with foreign, defence and monetary policy shared.

Greenland and the Faroes

Greenland, the world's largest island, with about 56,000 people — mostly Inuit — controls most domestic affairs under the 2009 Self-Government Act, which recognises Greenlanders as a people with a right to independence by referendum; its status has become a live geopolitical question amid Arctic great-power interest. The Faroes (about 54,000 people) run nearly all domestic policy and famously stay outside the EU (as does Greenland since 1985), while Denmark proper is an EU member.
