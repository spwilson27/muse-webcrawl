Designated marksman

A designated marksman (DM) is a military marksman role in an infantry squad. The DM's role is to supply rapid accurate fire on enemy targets at ranges up to 800 metres (870 yd) with a rifle often called a designated marksman rifle equipped with a telescopic sight. The DM operates as an integral member of the infantry squad, providing a capability between that of the standard rifleman and a dedicated sniper.

Role and doctrine

Unlike a sniper, who operates independently, specialises in camouflage and infiltration, and engages targets at long range, the designated marksman remains with the squad and fights in the open alongside other riflemen. The DM extends the squad's effective range, engages fleeting or partially exposed targets, counters enemy snipers, and provides overwatch.

History

The concept has roots in the Soviet Dragunov SVD issued at platoon level from the 1960s. Western armies formalised the role during the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, where engagements in open terrain exposed the limited reach of standard 5.56 mm carbines.

Weapons

  • United States: M38 SDMR, Mk 12 SPR, M110A1 SDMR, SR-25 pattern rifles.
  • United Kingdom: L129A1 sharpshooter rifle.
  • Germany: G28 designated marksman rifle.
  • Russia and former Soviet states: SVD Dragunov.

Training

Designated marksmen are selected from experienced riflemen and receive additional marksmanship training in range estimation, wind reading, ballistics and observation, though typically not the full fieldcraft curriculum of sniper school.
