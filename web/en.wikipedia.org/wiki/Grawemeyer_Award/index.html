Grawemeyer Award

The Grawemeyer Awards are five annual prizes given in the fields of music composition, religion, education, psychology and ideas improving world order. The awards were founded in 1984 by industrialist and University of Louisville alumnus H. Charles Grawemeyer, who wished to recognize powerful ideas in the arts, humanities and sciences rather than lifetime achievement; the Louisville Grawemeyer Awards are presented by the university and its partners, including the Louisville Presbyterian Theological Seminary for the religion award.

Fields and laureates

The Award for Music Composition, first presented in 1985 to Witold Lutosławski, is among the most prestigious in classical music; subsequent winners include György Ligeti, Krzysztof Penderecki, Thomas Adès and Kaija Saariaho. The Award for Ideas Improving World Order, whose first recipient was Mikhail Gorbachev (for the nuclear arms reduction initiative), has honored figures such as Kofi Annan and Muhammad Yunus. Each award carries a substantial cash prize, historically US$200,000–$1 million in the prize's early, larger form.
