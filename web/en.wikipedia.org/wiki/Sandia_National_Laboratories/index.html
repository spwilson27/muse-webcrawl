Sandia National Laboratories (SNL), also known as Sandia, is one of three research and development laboratories of the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) of the United States Department of Energy. Its primary site is in Albuquerque, New Mexico, on Kirtland Air Force Base, with a second major site in Livermore, California, alongside smaller facilities elsewhere, including the Tonopah Test Range and a test site on Kauai, Hawaii.

Sandia's primary mission is the development, engineering and testing of the non-nuclear components of nuclear weapons, together with a broad portfolio of work in energy, computing, materials science, pulsed power and national security.

History

Sandia traces its origins to the Manhattan Project. In 1945, the project's Z Division was established at Sandia Base near Albuquerque to handle weapons assembly and engineering; an April 1948 letter from President Harry S. Truman asked AT&T to take over management of the site, citing a wish for the operation to be run on an industrial rather than an academic basis. The laboratory became Sandia Laboratory in 1948–49, operated by Sandia Corporation, a wholly owned subsidiary of Western Electric and later AT&T.

During the Cold War Sandia engineered weapon components such as arming, fuzing and firing systems, parachute retarders, and permissive action links. In 1979 Congress redesignated it a national laboratory. After the Cold War its mission broadened into stockpile stewardship, nonproliferation, energy systems and homeland security. Lockheed Martin operated the lab from 1993 to 2017, when management passed to National Technology and Engineering Solutions of Sandia (NTESS), a subsidiary of Honeywell International.

Research areas

  • Nuclear weapons engineering — design and qualification of non-nuclear components, stockpile surveillance, and life-extension programmes.
  • Pulsed power — the Z Pulsed Power Facility ("Z machine"), the world's most powerful laboratory radiation source, used for inertial confinement fusion and materials research.
  • Computing — high-performance computing, including the ASCI Red supercomputer (1997), the first machine to exceed one teraflop in sustained performance.
  • Energy and climate — solar and wind energy, grid modernisation, energy storage, and the Combustion Research Facility in Livermore.
  • Materials and engineering sciences, microelectronics (the Microsystems and Engineering Sciences Applications complex, MESA), and robotics.
  • Nonproliferation and defence systems, including satellite payloads and verification technology.

Facilities

The Albuquerque site occupies large areas of Kirtland AFB and includes the Z machine, the MESA microelectronics complex, and extensive environmental test facilities for shock, vibration, thermal and centrifuge testing. The California site in Livermore, established in 1956, hosts the Combustion Research Facility. Sandia also operates the Tonopah Test Range in Nevada and the Kauai Test Facility for rocket launches.
