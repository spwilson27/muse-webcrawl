Bootstrapping

Bootstrapping is a self-starting process that proceeds without external input. The term derives from the expression 'to pull oneself up by one's bootstraps'. It is used in many fields.

Computing

In computing, booting loads an operating system; a bootstrap loader starts the system, and a bootstrapping compiler can compile itself. Bootstrap protocols configure devices automatically.

Statistics

The bootstrap is a resampling method introduced by Bradley Efron in 1979 to estimate sampling distributions by resampling with replacement from observed data.

Business and finance

Bootstrapping a business means starting with personal savings and revenue without external investors. In finance it also denotes building a yield curve (see Bootstrapping (finance)).

Other uses

In linguistics, bootstrapping describes how children leverage known information to learn language; in biology, bootstrap support measures phylogenetic tree reliability.
