Osman Nuri Pasha

Osman Nuri Pasha (1832–1900), also known as Gazi Osman Pasha, was an Ottoman field marshal and hero of the Siege of Plevna (1877) during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78. Born in Tokat, he graduated from the Ottoman military academy and rose through the ranks serving in the Crimean War and various campaigns.

At Plevna in modern-day Bulgaria, Osman Pasha's forces resisted repeated Russian and Romanian assaults for several months, inflicting heavy casualties before being forced to surrender in December 1877 after a failed breakout attempt in which he was wounded. His gallant defence earned him the honorific "Gazi" (the Victorious) and international admiration; his sword was returned to him by the Russian command as a mark of respect.

Osman Pasha later served as Ottoman Minister of War and as a palace marshal under Sultan Abdul Hamid II. He died in Istanbul in 1900. The "Plevna March" (Plevne Marşı) in Turkish music commemorates his defence.
