Sơn Đoòng Cave

LocationPhong Nha-Kẻ Bàng National Park, Quảng Bình, Vietnam
Lengthapprox. 9 km
Depthup to 200 m
Discovered1991 (Hồ Khanh); surveyed 2009
GeologyLimestone, formed 2–5 million years ago

Hang Sơn Đoòng ("Mountain River Cave") is a cave in Phong Nha-Kẻ Bàng National Park, Quảng Bình Province, Vietnam, and is regarded as the largest known cave passage in the world by volume. Its main chamber is more than 5 km long, up to 200 m high and 150 m wide — large enough to contain a city block of skyscrapers.

Discovery and exploration

The cave's entrance was found in 1991 by a local man, Hồ Khanh, who had heard wind and water inside but could not relocate the site for years. In 2009 a team of the British Cave Research Association, guided by Hồ Khanh, surveyed the cave and announced its record dimensions, surpassing Deer Cave in Malaysia. The cave was formed in Carboniferous/Permian limestone by the Rao Thuong River some 2–5 million years ago.

Features

Two enormous dolines — collapsed ceiling sections — admit sunlight and rain, allowing a jungle ecosystem with trees, ferns and monkeys to flourish inside the cave. The first doline is known as Watch Out for Dinosaurs; the second, the Garden of Edam. The cave contains giant stalagmites up to 80 m tall (among them the "Hand of Dog"), rimstone pools, cave pearls the size of baseballs, and a calcite wall nicknamed the Great Wall of Vietnam that blocked the first expedition's progress.

Tourism

Public expeditions began in 2013, operated by Oxalis Adventure under strict quotas of a few hundred permits per year. The multi-day trek requires porters and cave experts and is frequently booked out well in advance. Proposals for a cable car into the cave have drawn strong opposition from conservationists.
