DMARC

DMARC
Full name: Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance
Standard: RFC 7489 (2015)
Builds on: SPF, DKIM

DMARC is an email authentication protocol designed to let domain owners protect their domains from unauthorised use, including phishing and email spoofing. It was first published in 2012 and standardised as RFC 7489 in 2015.

How it works

DMARC builds on SPF and DKIM by requiring "alignment" between those checks and the visible From domain. The domain owner publishes a DNS policy — none, quarantine, or reject — telling receivers how to handle messages that fail authentication, and receives aggregate and forensic reports.

Adoption

Major mailbox providers support DMARC, and in 2024 Google and Yahoo began requiring senders of bulk email to publish DMARC records, making it effectively mandatory for legitimate high-volume mail.
