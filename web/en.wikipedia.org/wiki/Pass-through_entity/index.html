Pass-through entity

A pass-through entity (also called a flow-through entity) is a business structure in United States tax law whose income, deductions, and credits pass through to its owners and are reported on their individual tax returns, rather than being taxed at the entity level. This avoids the double taxation that applies to regular C corporations.

Types

Common pass-through entities include S corporations, partnerships, limited liability companies (LLCs) taxed as partnerships or S corporations, and sole proprietorships. Owners receive a Schedule K-1 reporting their share of the entity's items.

Tax treatment

The entity generally files an informational return but pays no federal income tax itself. The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 created a deduction under Section 199A for qualified business income from pass-through entities, subject to limitations. State treatment varies, and many states have enacted elective pass-through entity taxes as a workaround to the federal cap on state and local tax deductions.
