Priorat is a comarca (county) in the province of Tarragona, in Catalonia, Spain. Its capital is the town of Falset. The region takes its name from a priory (priorat in Catalan) of Carthusian monks established at Scala Dei in the 12th century, whose lands formed the historic core of the area.

Wine

Priorat is internationally renowned for its wines. The Priorat Denominació d'Origen Qualificada (DOQ) is one of only two Spanish wine regions, along with Rioja, to hold the highest DOQ classification. The wines are typically powerful reds made mainly from Garnacha (Grenache) and Cariñena (Carignan) grapes grown on steep terraces.

The distinctive feature of the region is its soil, known locally as llicorella — a dark slate and quartzite soil that stresses the vines, restricts yields and gives the wines their characteristic minerality and concentration. Vineyards are often planted on slopes so steep that they must be worked by hand or with mules.

History

Winemaking in the area dates back to the Carthusian monks of the Charterhouse of Scala Dei, founded in 1194. The region's wines declined in the late 19th and 20th centuries after the phylloxera epidemic destroyed vineyards and many residents emigrated. A revival began in the 1980s and 1990s, when a small group of producers — including René Barbier, Álvaro Palacios, Daphne Glorian, Carles Pastrana and José Luis Pérez — began making modern wines from old vines, bringing Priorat to international attention.

The neighbouring Montsant DO surrounds the Priorat DOQ and produces wines in a related style. The landscape of the comarca is dominated by the Montsant mountain range, a popular area for hiking and climbing.
