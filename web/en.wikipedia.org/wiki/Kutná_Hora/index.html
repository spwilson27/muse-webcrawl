Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora (German: Kuttenberg) is a town in the Central Bohemian Region of the Czech Republic. In the Middle Ages it was one of the most important royal mining towns in Bohemia, its wealth derived from silver mines, and it rivalled Prague in economic and political significance. Its historic centre, including the Cathedral of St Barbara and the Church of the Assumption of Our Lady at Sedlec, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1995.

History

Silver mining began in the area in the 13th century; in 1300 King Wenceslaus II issued the Ius regale montanorum mining code and established the royal mint, which produced the famous Prague groschen. The Decree of Kutná Hora (1409), issued by Wenceslaus IV, gave the Bohemian nation a majority of votes at Charles University and indirectly precipitated the founding of the University of Leipzig. Mining declined after the 16th century as the richest ores were exhausted.

Landmarks

The Cathedral of St Barbara, patron saint of miners, is a masterpiece of late Gothic architecture begun in 1388, with contributions by the Parler workshop and Benedikt Rejt. The nearby Sedlec Ossuary, a chapel decorated with the bones of tens of thousands of people, is one of the town's most visited sites. Other landmarks include the Italian Court (the former royal mint and residence) and the Gothic Stone House.
