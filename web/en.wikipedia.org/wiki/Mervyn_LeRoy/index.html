Mervyn LeRoy (October 15, 1900 – September 13, 1987) was an American film director and producer. One of the most prolific and versatile filmmakers of Hollywood's Golden Age, he helped shape the output of Warner Bros. and MGM during the 1930s and 1940s.

Career

LeRoy began in vaudeville and silent films before becoming a director at Warner Bros., where he made the landmark gangster film Little Caesar (1931) and the prison drama I Am a Fugitive from a Chain Gang (1932). Moving to MGM as a producer, he oversaw The Wizard of Oz (1939). His later directorial credits include Random Harvest (1942), Madame Curie (1943), Thirty Seconds Over Tokyo (1944), Little Women (1949), Quo Vadis (1951), Mister Roberts (1955), and Gypsy (1962).

Honours

LeRoy received an Honorary Academy Award in 1946 for the short film The House I Live In, and the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award in 1976. He received eight Academy Award nominations in total.
