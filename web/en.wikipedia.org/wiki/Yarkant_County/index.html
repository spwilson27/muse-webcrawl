Yarkant County, also known as Shache County (Chinese: 莎车县), is a county in the Kashgar Prefecture of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in northwestern China. Its seat, the oasis town of Yarkant (Yeken), lies on the Yarkand River at the western edge of the Taklamakan Desert on the southern route of the historic Silk Road. It is one of the most populous counties in China, with a population exceeding 850,000, the large majority Uyghur.

History

Yarkant was the centre of the Yarkent Khanate (1514–1705), a Chagatai Mongol successor state that at its height ruled much of the Tarim Basin. The khanate's rulers patronised Islamic learning and music; the royal necropolis at Yarkant preserves the tombs of its sultans, and the mausoleum of Amannisa Khan (1526–1560), the queen credited with preserving the Twelve Muqam musical suites, stands nearby.

Earlier, the oasis was tributary to successive powers controlling the southern Silk Road, including the Han and Tang empires, and was described by Marco Polo, who noted its cotton and gardens. In the 19th century Yarkant was a centre of the Dungan Revolt-era state of Yakub Beg, and British and Russian missions visited it during the "Great Game". Robert Shaw and George Hayward were among the travellers who reached it in 1868–1870.

Geography and economy

The county occupies an alluvial plain watered by the Yarkand River, which rises in the Karakoram. Agriculture — cotton, wheat, maize, melons, walnuts, and other fruit — is the mainstay of the economy, supported by irrigation from the river and canals. The town is connected by the Kashgar–Hotan railway and by highway to Kashgar, about 190 km to the northwest.
