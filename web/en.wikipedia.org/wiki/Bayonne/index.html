Bayonne

Country: France
Region: Nouvelle-Aquitaine
Department: Pyrénées-Atlantiques
Population: ~52,000 (city); metropolitan area ~300,000

Bayonne (Basque: Baiona) is a city in south-western France at the confluence of the Nive and Adour rivers, a few kilometres from the Bay of Biscay. It is a subprefecture of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department and the cultural capital of the northern (French) Basque Country. Together with Anglet and Biarritz it forms a continuous urban area.

History

The site was a Roman castrum known as Lapurdum. In the Middle Ages Bayonne was a flourishing port under, successively, the dukes of Aquitaine, the kings of England (from 1152 to 1451), and the French crown. Its medieval Jewish and later Portuguese-Jewish communities were renowned, and Bayonne is credited with introducing chocolate-making to France in the 17th century. The city gave its name to the bayonet, said to have been first made there. During the Peninsular War, the Battle of Bayonne was fought nearby in 1814.

Culture

Bayonne is famous for its cured ham (jambon de Bayonne), its chocolate, Basque culture and language, and the Fêtes de Bayonne, a five-day summer festival begun in 1932 that is among the largest in France, during which participants wear white with red scarves. The Gothic Sainte-Marie Cathedral, with its cloister, dominates the old town of Grand Bayonne, Petit Bayonne across the Nive, and the quarter of Saint-Esprit across the Adour.

Economy and transport

The Port of Bayonne handles metallurgical and chemical traffic. The city is a stop on the railway from Paris to the Spanish frontier at Hendaye–Irún, and high-speed services connect it with Bordeaux and Paris.
