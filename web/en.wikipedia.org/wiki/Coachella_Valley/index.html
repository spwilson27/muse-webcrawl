The Coachella Valley is an arid rift valley in the Colorado Desert of Southern California, extending about 72 km (45 mi) southeast from the San Bernardino Mountains into Riverside County to the northern shore of the Salton Sea. The valley is bounded by the San Jacinto and Santa Rosa Mountains to the west and the Little San Bernardino Mountains to the east, and lies largely along the San Andreas Fault.

Communities and economy

The valley's nine cities include the resort city of Palm Springs, Palm Desert, Rancho Mirage and Indio. The economy rests on tourism — the valley is famous for golf courses, mid-century modern architecture, and the Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival and Stagecoach Festival held at Indio — and on agriculture irrigated by the Coachella Canal, notably table grapes and the great majority of the United States' date crop.

Geography and climate

Parts of the valley floor lie below sea level. Summers are extremely hot, with temperatures frequently above 43 °C (110 °F), while winters are mild and sunny, drawing seasonal "snowbird" residents. The indigenous Cahuilla people have inhabited the valley for centuries.
