Air time (rides)

In the context of amusement rides, air time is the sensation of weightlessness — or of being lifted out of one's seat — experienced when a roller coaster or similar ride produces negative g-forces. It occurs when the ride vehicle crests a hill or descends faster than gravity alone would pull the rider downward.

Types

Enthusiasts distinguish "floater air time", a gentle sensation of floating sustained over parabolic hills, from "ejector air time", a sharper force that pushes the rider forcefully against the restraint. Air time is generated by elements such as bunny hills, camelbacks, and steep first drops, and is measured in seconds when comparing coasters; rides noted for abundant air time include wooden coasters with sequences of small hills and modern steel designs with sustained negative-g segments.

Safety

Restraint systems — lap bars, seat belts, and over-the-shoulder harnesses — are designed to keep riders secure through negative-g portions of the ride, and permitted air time is regulated by design standards such as those of ASTM International. The sensation is one of the most prized qualities in coaster enthusiast rankings.
