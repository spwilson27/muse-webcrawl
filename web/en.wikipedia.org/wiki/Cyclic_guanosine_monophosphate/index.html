Cyclic guanosine monophosphate

AbbreviationcGMP
Chemical formulaC10H12N5O7P
Molar mass345.21 g/mol
Synthesized byGuanylate cyclase

Cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) is a cyclic nucleotide derived from guanosine triphosphate (GTP). It acts as a second messenger, much like cyclic AMP (cAMP), transmitting signals inside cells in response to hormones, neurotransmitters, and physical stimuli such as light.

Synthesis and degradation

cGMP is synthesized from GTP by the enzyme guanylate cyclase. Two forms exist: a soluble form activated by nitric oxide (NO), and membrane-bound forms activated by peptide hormones such as atrial natriuretic peptide. cGMP is broken down by phosphodiesterases; phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) is the principal cGMP-specific enzyme in vascular smooth muscle.

Functions

In blood vessels, NO released from endothelial cells diffuses into smooth muscle, activates soluble guanylate cyclase, and the resulting rise in cGMP activates protein kinase G, which lowers intracellular calcium and relaxes the muscle — producing vasodilation. In the retina, cGMP keeps sodium channels of photoreceptor cells open in the dark; light activates a phosphodiesterase that hydrolyses cGMP, closing the channels and generating the visual signal. cGMP also regulates platelet aggregation, intestinal fluid secretion, and cell growth.

Pharmacology

PDE5 inhibitors such as sildenafil (Viagra) raise cGMP levels by blocking its breakdown and are used to treat erectile dysfunction and pulmonary arterial hypertension. Nitrate drugs such as nitroglycerin act upstream by donating nitric oxide.
