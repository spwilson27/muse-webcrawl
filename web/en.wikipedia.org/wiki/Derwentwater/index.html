Derwentwater, or Derwent Water, is a lake in the Lake District National Park in Cumbria, England. It lies immediately south of the town of Keswick and is fed and drained by the River Derwent. The lake occupies a broad, flat-floored valley carved by glaciers, surrounded by fells including Catbells on its western shore and Friar's Crag, a famous viewpoint, on the east.

Geography

Derwentwater is about 4.6 km (nearly 3 miles) long and up to 1.9 km wide, and is comparatively shallow, with a maximum depth of about 22 metres. It contains several islands, including Derwent Island (occupied by a National Trust house), Lord's Island, St Herbert's Island — named after the 7th-century hermit St Herbert, a friend of St Cuthbert — and Rampsholme Island. The lake is considered one of the most scenic in England and is sometimes called the "Queen of the Lakes".

Recreation

The lake is used for sailing, kayaking, paddleboarding and wild swimming, and passenger launches have circumnavigated it since Victorian times, calling at jetties around the shore. The Keswick Launch connects with popular walking routes, including the ascent of Catbells and the path around the lake's perimeter. The Borrowdale valley at the lake's southern end is famed for its Atlantic oak woodlands and for the Lodore Falls.

Name

The name derives from the River Derwent, whose name is of Celtic origin, probably meaning "valley thick with oaks".
