Honda Motor Co., Ltd. (Japanese: 本田技研工業株式会社, Honda Giken Kōgyō Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Minato, Tokyo. It is the world's largest manufacturer of motorcycles and one of the largest automobile manufacturers, and also produces power equipment, marine engines, personal watercraft, garden equipment, aircraft and robots.

History

The company was founded by engineer Soichiro Honda, who began by fitting small engines to bicycles after the Second World War, and incorporated Honda Motor in 1948 with Takeo Fujisawa. The Honda Cub (Super Cub), launched in 1958, became the best-selling motor vehicle in history, with production passing 100 million units. Honda entered the United States motorcycle market in 1959 and began automobile production in 1963 with the T360 mini truck and S500 sports car. The Civic (1972) and Accord (1976) established Honda in the American car market, and in 1986 it launched Acura, the first Japanese luxury car brand.

Products and technology

Honda is known for engineering innovations including the CVCC low-emission engine (1973) and the VTEC variable valve timing system. Its car range includes the Civic, Accord, CR-V and Jazz/Fit; its motorcycle range runs from the Super Cub to the Gold Wing tourer and CBR sports bikes. Subsidiaries and divisions include Honda Aircraft Company, maker of the HondaJet business jet, and Honda Power Products. The humanoid robot ASIMO, demonstrated from 2000, became an emblem of the company's research.

Motorsport

Honda has a long motorsport history in motorcycle Grand Prix racing and Formula One, where it has competed as a constructor (1964–1968, 2006–2008) and as an engine supplier, powering championship-winning cars for McLaren in the late 1980s and early 1990s and for Red Bull Racing in the 2020s. Honda also supplies engines to the IndyCar Series.
