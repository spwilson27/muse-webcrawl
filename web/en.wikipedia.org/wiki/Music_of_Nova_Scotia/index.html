Music of Nova Scotia

The music of Nova Scotia is a component of the culture of Nova Scotia, Canada. The province is especially celebrated for the Celtic musical culture of Cape Breton Island, whose fiddle tradition draws on its Scottish Gaelic heritage, and for producing internationally known performers in folk, country, rock and pop.

Cape Breton fiddling

Cape Breton fiddle music, preserved by Scottish settlers and their descendants, is characterised by driving rhythms, piano accompaniment and close ties to step dancing. Celebrated Cape Breton fiddlers include Buddy MacMaster, Natalie MacMaster and Ashley MacIsaac. The island also sustains a Gaelic song tradition and community ceilidhs.

Notable artists

Nova Scotia performers who have reached national and international audiences include country singer Hank Snow, folk legend Stan Rogers (closely associated with the province), Anne Murray, the Rankin Family, Sloan, Sarah McLachlan, and the folk musician Stompin' Tom Connors, who was closely tied to the region. Halifax developed a notable indie rock scene in the 1990s, and the province's music is promoted by the East Coast Music Awards.
