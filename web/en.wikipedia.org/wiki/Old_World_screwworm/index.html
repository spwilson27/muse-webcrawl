Old World screwworm

Chrysomya bezziana, the Old World screwworm fly, is a species of blowfly and an obligate parasite of mammals. It is distributed across tropical Africa, the Middle East, the Indian subcontinent, Southeast Asia and parts of Indonesia and Papua New Guinea. Its larvae feed on the living tissue of warm-blooded animals, causing a condition known as myiasis.

Life cycle and harm

Adult females lay batches of eggs at the edges of wounds, scratches or body orifices of livestock, wildlife and occasionally humans. The hatching larvae burrow into living flesh, feeding in a characteristic screw-like manner that gives screwworms their name. Untreated infestations enlarge rapidly, attract further egg-laying, and can kill the host. The species is a serious pest of cattle, sheep, goats and other livestock, and a notifiable pest in many countries where it does not occur, such as Australia.

Control

Control relies on wound treatment, insecticides, surveillance and quarantine. The sterile insect technique, used successfully against the related New World screwworm, has been considered and used locally for C. bezziana. A serious incursion of the species into Iraq in the 1990s prompted an international eradication response.
