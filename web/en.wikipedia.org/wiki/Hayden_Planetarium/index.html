The Hayden Planetarium is a public planetarium operated as part of the American Museum of Natural History in New York City. Since 2000 its centrepiece has been the Hayden Sphere within the Rose Center for Earth and Space, housing the Space Theater, one of the most powerful virtual-reality simulators in the world. Astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson has served as its Frederick P. Rose Director since 1996.

History

The original planetarium opened on October 3, 1935, funded largely by a gift and loan arranged through banker and philanthropist Charles Hayden; the museum's Hayden Planetarium used a Zeiss Mark II projector to reproduce the night sky on a dome roughly 23 metres across. It quickly became a major educational attraction and a centre for public astronomy in the United States.

The original building was demolished in 1997 and replaced by the Rose Center for Earth and Space, which opened on February 19, 2000. The new Hayden Planetarium occupies the top half of an 87-foot glass-cube-enclosed sphere; its Space Theater uses a custom Zeiss Mark IX star projector and a digital dome projection system driven by a visualisation supercomputer to render datasets of the observable universe.

Exhibitions and programmes

Beneath the sphere, the Heilbrunn Cosmic Pathway spirals through 13 billion years of cosmic history, and surrounding halls present the sizes and scales of the universe, Earth science and meteorites. The planetarium produces immersive space shows narrated by well-known actors and scientists, and Tyson's reclassification of Pluto as a dwarf planet in the Rose Center's exhibits in 2000 anticipated the 2006 IAU decision and drew widespread public attention.
