The Imperial Japanese Navy (IJN; Japanese: 大日本帝國海軍, Dai-Nippon Teikoku Kaigun) was the navy of the Empire of Japan from 1868 to 1945, when it was dissolved following Japan's defeat in the Second World War. Built from near-nothing in the Meiji era on British and French models, it became by the 1920s the world's third-largest navy and arguably its most innovative in naval aviation and torpedo warfare.

Origins and rise

Founded in the aftermath of the Meiji Restoration, the navy won its spurs in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) at the Battle of the Yalu River, and astonished the world in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05), when Admiral Tōgō's fleet annihilated the Russian Baltic Fleet at Tsushima. Japan fought alongside the Allies in the First World War, and the Washington Naval Treaty of 1922 fixed its capital-ship tonnage at a 5:5:3 ratio relative to Britain and the United States.

Innovation and expansion

Constrained by treaty ratios, the IJN pursued qualitative superiority: the world's most powerful battleships (Yamato class), the exceptional Long Lance oxygen torpedo, elite night-fighting doctrine, and a pioneering carrier force — by 1941 the Kidō Butai concentrated six fleet carriers, more than any other navy. Its naval air arm was widely regarded as the best in the world at the opening of the Pacific War.

Second World War

The IJN opened the Pacific War with the attack on Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941) and a rapid conquest of Southeast Asia and the western Pacific. The tide turned at Midway (June 1942), where four fleet carriers were lost. Thereafter American industrial output, codebreaking, and submarine blockade overwhelmed Japan; the great battles of the Philippine Sea (1944) and Leyte Gulf (1944) destroyed the IJN as an effective force, and its surviving ships were sunk at anchor or expended in operations such as the one-way sortie of Yamato in April 1945.

Legacy

The navy was formally dissolved in 1945. In 1954 Japan established the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force as its successor naval service.
