Gujarat is a state in western India, situated along the country's western coast with the longest mainland coastline of any Indian state, about 1,600 km (990 mi). It borders Rajasthan to the northeast, Madhya Pradesh to the east, Maharashtra to the southeast, and the Arabian Sea to the west, as well as the Pakistani province of Sindh to the northwest. Its capital is Gandhinagar, while its largest city is Ahmedabad. The Gujarati-speaking population of the state is about 60 million, and Gujarati is its official language.

Gujarat is known for its entrepreneurial culture, a long coastline dotted with ports, and the ancient cities of the Indus Valley Civilisation such as Lothal and Dholavira. It is the birthplace of Mahatma Gandhi, born in Porbandar in 1869, and of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, whose colossal statue, the Statue of Unity, is the world's tallest statue.

History

The region's history stretches back to the Indus Valley Civilisation; Lothal was one of the world's earliest known dockyards. In the centuries that followed, Gujarat came under the Mauryan, Gupta, Maitraka, and Solanki dynasties, growing rich on maritime trade with Arabia, Persia, and Europe. The port of Bharuch was famous in the ancient world. Under the Gujarat Sultanate and later the Mughal Empire, Surat became one of the busiest ports of Asia. During British rule the territory was divided between princely states and the Bombay Presidency.

On 1 May 1960, the bilingual Bombay State was split along linguistic lines, creating the states of Gujarat and Maharashtra.

Geography

Gujarat has a varied terrain: the arid Rann of Kutch in the northwest, a vast salt marsh famous for its white desert and the wild ass sanctuary; the Kathiawar (Saurashtra) peninsula; and the fertile plains of the Sabarmati, Mahi, Narmada and Tapi rivers in the east. The Gir Forest is the only wild home of the Asiatic lion. The state experiences dry conditions in the north and a monsoon climate elsewhere.

Economy

Gujarat is among India's most industrialised states, a leader in chemicals, petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, textiles, and diamond cutting and polishing — a large share of the world's diamonds are processed in Surat. The port of Mundra is among India's largest private ports. The state is also a major producer of cotton, groundnuts, milk and dairy products; the Amul cooperative movement began here. In recent decades it has invested heavily in solar and wind energy.

Demographics

Gujaratis are the predominant ethnic group. Hinduism is the majority religion, with significant Jain, Muslim, Christian and Sikh minorities. Cities include Ahmedabad, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot, Bhavnagar and Jamnagar. The state is famous for the Navratri garba dance festivals, Gujarati cuisine, and the stepwells (vavs) exemplified by Rani ki Vav at Patan, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
