Otto Ludwig Hölder (22 December 1859 – 29 August 1937) was a German mathematician whose name attaches to several fundamental results, most famously Hölder's inequality in analysis and the Jordan–Hölder theorem in group theory.

Life

Hölder was born in Stuttgart and first studied engineering at the Stuttgart Polytechnikum before turning to mathematics at Tübingen and Berlin, where he was influenced by Weierstrass, Kronecker, and Kummer. He took his doctorate at Tübingen in 1882, habilitated at Göttingen, and held chairs at Tübingen from 1889 and at Leipzig from 1899 until his retirement in 1929. He served as president of the German Mathematical Society in 1918.

Work

In his 1889 paper on the means of function values, Hölder proved the inequality that now bears his name, a generalisation of Cauchy's inequality to sums and integrals in Lp spaces; versions of it had been anticipated by Leonard James Rogers, and it is sometimes called the Rogers–Hölder inequality. Hölder also introduced the Hölder condition — a strengthening of continuity defined by a power-law bound — and the class of Hölder spaces fundamental in the theory of partial differential equations.

In group theory, the Jordan–Hölder theorem, completed by Hölder in work of 1889, asserts that any two composition series of a finite group have the same length and the same factors up to order and isomorphism, a cornerstone of the subject. Hölder additionally proved that a finite simple group whose order involves restricted prime factors must be of prime order or a group of prime-power order in classical cases, and he classified finite simple groups of small order. His late work turned to the philosophy and foundations of arithmetic and geometry, including studies of the axioms of quantity.
