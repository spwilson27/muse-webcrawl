GPT

GPT may refer to several things. Most prominently, it denotes generative pre-trained transformer, a family of large language models developed by OpenAI beginning in 2018, and more broadly the class of autoregressive transformer models trained to predict the next token in text.

Generative pre-trained transformers

OpenAI introduced the first Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT-1) in June 2018, demonstrating that a transformer language model pre-trained on a large text corpus and then fine-tuned could achieve strong results on many natural-language tasks. GPT-2 (2019) scaled the approach to 1.5 billion parameters and surprised researchers with coherent generated text. GPT-3 (2020), with 175 billion parameters, showed strong few-shot learning. GPT-4 (2023) added multimodal input and substantially improved reasoning performance; models in the family power the ChatGPT chatbot launched in November 2022.

The architecture is a decoder-only transformer trained with next-token prediction. "Pre-training" on broad data, followed by instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback, turned the base model into a general-purpose assistant and helped ignite the generative AI boom of the 2020s, with similar GPT-style models developed by many other laboratories.

Other uses

GPT is also an abbreviation for the GUID Partition Table disk-partitioning standard, for glutamate–pyruvate transaminase (an enzyme also called alanine transaminase), and for various airport and organisation codes.
