Metsamor (Armenian: Մեծամոր) is a town in Armavir Province of Armenia, founded in 1969 to house workers of the Armenian Nuclear Power Plant, which is also commonly called Metsamor after the town. It lies in the Ararat plain west of Yerevan, near the border with Turkey. The town's name derives from the Metsamor River; the name is also shared with the nearby Bronze and Iron Age archaeological site of Metsamor (Taronik), one of the most important prehistoric settlements excavated in Armenia.

Nuclear power plant

The Armenian Nuclear Power Plant, commissioned in the 1970s with two Soviet VVER-440 reactors, supplies a large share of Armenia's electricity. It was shut down after the 1988 Spitak earthquake; one unit (Unit 2) was restarted in 1995 amid an energy crisis and has continued operating with safety upgrades and licence extensions, while its long-term future — and a long-discussed replacement plant — remain subjects of policy debate. The plant's proximity to the Turkish border and its older reactor design have made it a recurring focus of regional safety discussion.

Archaeological site

The ancient Metsamor site, occupied from the Bronze Age into the medieval period, was a fortified settlement and metallurgical centre with a citadel, temple complex, and extensive necropolis; excavations since 1965 have produced rich finds now held in the Metsamor Historical-Archaeological Museum.
