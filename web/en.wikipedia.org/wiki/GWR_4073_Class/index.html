The 4073 Class or Castle Class are 4-6-0 steam locomotives of the Great Western Railway, built between 1923 and 1950. Designed by Charles B. Collett for working the company's express passenger trains, they were a development of George Jackson Churchward's Star Class, with a much larger boiler. They proved to be reliable, powerful and economical, and were one of the most successful locomotive designs of the British "Big Four" era; the GWR publicised them as "the most powerful express passenger locomotive in Britain".

History

The first locomotive, No. 4073 Caerphilly Castle, emerged from Swindon Works in August 1923 and was displayed at the British Empire Exhibition at Wembley, where in famous trials the Castles outperformed the larger LNER Pacifics. Production continued under the GWR and into British Railways ownership, ending with No. 7037 Swindon in 1950. All were named after castles, mostly in the GWR's territory.

Performance

Castles hauled the Cheltenham Flyer — for a time the world's fastest scheduled steam train — and in 1925 No. 4079 Pendennis Castle famously outperformed the LNER's newer Pacifics during exchange trials. In preservation, members of the class have continued to run on the main line.

Preservation

Eight Castles survive, including No. 4073 Caerphilly Castle in the Museum of the Great Western Railway, Swindon; No. 4079 Pendennis Castle; No. 5029 Nunney Castle; No. 5043 Earl of Mount Edgcumbe; No. 5051 Earl Bathurst; No. 5080 Defiant; No. 7027 Thornbury Castle; and No. 7029 Clun Castle.
