Despicable Me is an American animated media franchise created by Sergio Pablos and produced by Illumination for Universal Pictures. The franchise centres on Felonious Gru, a supervillain-turned-secret-agent and father, and his army of small yellow henchmen, the Minions. Beginning with the film Despicable Me in 2010, it has grown into one of the highest-grossing animated franchises of all time, with its films grossing more than $5 billion worldwide.

Characters

The series stars Steve Carell as Gru, whose thick accent and gradual transformation from villain to devoted father anchor the story. Kristen Wiig voices Lucy Wilde; the adopted daughters Margo, Edith, and Agnes are voiced by Miranda Cosgrove, Dana Gaier, and Elsie Fisher (later Madison Polan). The Minions — voiced principally by co-director Pierre Coffin — became breakout stars, spawning their own spin-off films, shorts, and theme-park attractions.

Reception and impact

The franchise is among the most commercially successful in animation, and the Minions became a global merchandising phenomenon and internet meme. Attractions based on the series operate at Universal theme parks, including Despicable Me Minion Mayhem. Pharrell Williams's song "Happy", written for Despicable Me 2, became a worldwide hit and won a Grammy Award.
