Antonia Minor (36 BC – AD 37), also known as Antonia the Younger, was a Roman noblewoman, the younger daughter of Mark Antony and Octavia Minor, and thus a niece of Augustus. She married the general Nero Claudius Drusus and was the mother of Germanicus and the emperor Claudius, and the paternal grandmother of Caligula. Revered for her fidelity — she never remarried after Drusus's early death — she was one of the most influential women of the early Roman Empire.

Life

Antonia was born in Athens in 36 BC while her mother was travelling to join Mark Antony; after Antony's defeat and death she was raised in Rome by Octavia and, briefly, by Antony himself in the brief period of contact. In 16 BC she married Drusus, Augustus's stepson, with whom she had several children, of whom three survived infancy: Germanicus, Livilla, and Claudius. Drusus died in Germany in 9 BC, and Antonia, according to tradition at Augustus's urging against remarriage, remained a widow for the rest of her life.

Influence and death

Antonia managed extensive estates, maintained a cosmopolitan household that included future client kings, and corresponded with Herod Agrippa, whom she helped financially. Under Tiberius her standing was high; she is credited with informing the emperor of Sejanus's conspiracy in AD 31, helping to bring about his fall. She died on 1 May AD 37, shortly after Caligula's accession; hostile sources claim her grandson treated her so harshly that she took her own life, though illness is also reported. Claudius honoured her memory after becoming emperor, giving her the title Augusta posthumously.
