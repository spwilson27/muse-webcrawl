Substantia nigra

The substantia nigra (Latin for "black substance") is a basal ganglia structure located in the midbrain that plays an important role in reward and movement. The substantia nigra is the largest nucleus in the midbrain, lying posterior to the cerebral peduncles. It is distinguished by its dark color, which results from high levels of neuromelanin in dopaminergic neurons. The loss of these neurons is a pathological hallmark of Parkinson's disease.

The substantia nigra appears dark because of neuromelanin pigment in its dopaminergic neurons.

Structure

The substantia nigra is divided into two principal parts: the pars compacta (SNc), which contains densely packed dopaminergic neurons whose neuromelanin gives the structure its dark appearance, and the pars reticulata (SNr), a more diffuse region of predominantly GABAergic neurons. A smaller pars lateralis is sometimes distinguished. The pars compacta projects dopamine to the striatum via the nigrostriatal pathway, modulating movement and reward-related learning.

Function

Dopaminergic neurons of the pars compacta modulate the activity of the striatum, gating motor programs and signaling reward prediction errors. The pars reticulata serves as one of the main output nuclei of the basal ganglia, tonically inhibiting the thalamus and superior colliculus and participating in the control of eye movements and posture.

Clinical significance

Degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the pars compacta causes Parkinson's disease, producing tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability. Damage to the substantia nigra is also implicated in other parkinsonian syndromes. Conversely, excessive dopaminergic signaling in related circuits has been linked to schizophrenia and addiction.

History

The substantia nigra was first described in the eighteenth century and named by Samuel Thomas von Sömmerring in 1788. Its association with Parkinson's disease was established in the twentieth century, and in 1960 researchers demonstrated the severe depletion of striatal dopamine in Parkinsonian brains, paving the way for levodopa therapy.
