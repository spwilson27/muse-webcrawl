Cyclohexanol is the organic compound with the formula C6H11OH. The molecule is related to cyclohexane by replacement of one hydrogen atom with a hydroxyl group. This compound exists as a deliquescent colorless solid with a camphor-like odor, which, when very pure, melts near room temperature. Billions of kilograms are produced annually, mainly as a precursor to nylon.

Production

Cyclohexanol is produced by the oxidation of cyclohexane in air, typically using a cobalt catalyst. This process forms a mixture of cyclohexanol and cyclohexanone, often called "KA oil" (ketone-alcohol oil). Alternatively, cyclohexanol can be produced by the hydrogenation of phenol.

Uses

As noted above, cyclohexanol is an important feedstock in the polymer industry. It is a precursor to adipic acid, which is used in the manufacture of nylon 6,6. It is also a precursor to cyclohexanone, used to make caprolactam, the precursor to nylon 6. In addition, cyclohexanol is used as an industrial solvent and as a precursor to plasticizers and other compounds.

Basic reactions

Cyclohexanol undergoes the reactions expected of a secondary alcohol. Oxidation gives cyclohexanone, and further oxidation with nitric acid gives adipic acid. Dehydration gives cyclohexene.

Safety

Cyclohexanol is mildly toxic, with an LD50 of about 2 g/kg in rats when administered orally. It is a modest irritant to skin and eyes and can cause central nervous system depression at high doses.
