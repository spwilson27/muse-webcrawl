Lysander

Lysander (died 395 BCE) was a Spartan military and political commander who led Sparta to victory in the Peloponnesian War against Athens. As navarch (admiral), he built a strong fleet with Persian financial support obtained through his alliance with Cyrus the Younger.

Career

Lysander won the Battle of Notium (406 BCE) and decisively destroyed the Athenian fleet at Aegospotami in 405 BCE, after which Athens, starved by blockade, surrendered in 404 BCE. He installed oligarchic governments — including the Thirty Tyrants in Athens — and "decarchies" of his supporters in former Athenian subject cities, amassing great personal influence and wealth unusual for a Spartan. His power later waned as King Agesilaus II rose to prominence. Lysander was killed at the Battle of Haliartus in 395 BCE, at the start of the Corinthian War.

Legacy

Ancient writers such as Plutarch (whose Life of Lysander survives) portrayed him as a brilliant, ambitious, and unscrupulous figure whose personal networks abroad undermined Sparta's traditional discipline. He received heroic honours after death in some cities and was among the first Greeks to be worshipped as a god during his lifetime, at Samos.
