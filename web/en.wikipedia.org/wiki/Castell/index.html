Castell

A castell is a human tower built traditionally at festivals in Catalonia and neighbouring regions of Spain. Teams called colles compete to build and dismantle towers of up to ten levels, crowned by a small child, the enxaneta, who raises a hand in salute at the summit.

OriginCatalonia, Spain
UNESCO statusIntangible Cultural Heritage (2010)
BasePinya (crowd base)

Structure

A castell rests on the pinya, a dense base of hundreds of people that cushions falls and supports the trunk (tronc) of stacked levels, each usually of two to five people standing on the shoulders below. The top levels — the pom de dalt — are formed by small, agile children wearing helmets in modern practice.

Towers are named by their structure, for example a 3 de 8 is three people wide and eight levels high. A castell is complete (descarregat) only when it is dismantled in an orderly way after the enxaneta crosses the top.

History and culture

The tradition grew in the late 18th and 19th centuries around Valls and Tarragona, evolving from the earlier Ball de Valencians. Castells were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2010. The motto of the castellers is Força, equilibri, valor i seny — strength, balance, courage and good sense.
