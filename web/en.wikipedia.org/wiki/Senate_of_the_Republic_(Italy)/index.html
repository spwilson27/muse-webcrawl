Senate of the Republic (Italy)

The Senate of the Republic (Italian: Senato della Repubblica), or simply the Senate (Senato), is the upper house of the bicameral Italian Parliament, the lower house being the Chamber of Deputies. The two houses together form a perfect bicameral system, meaning they perform identical functions with identical powers; the Senate differs mainly in the composition and election of its members.

Composition

Following the constitutional reform of 2020, which reduced the number of parliamentarians, the Senate has 200 elected senators, down from 315. Of these, 196 are elected in Italy and 4 by Italians residing abroad. In addition, former presidents of the Republic are senators for life by right, and the president may appoint up to five citizens who have honoured the nation through high achievements as senators for life. Senators must be at least 40 years old, and voters must be at least 18 (the voting age for the Senate was lowered from 25 by a 2021 constitutional amendment).

Seat

The Senate meets at Palazzo Madama in Rome, a former Medici residence named after Madama Margherita of Austria. The building has hosted the Senate since 1871, first of the Kingdom of Italy and then of the Republic.

President of the Senate

The President of the Senate presides over the chamber and is second in the order of precedence of the Italian Republic, acting as substitute for the President of the Republic when the latter is unable to serve. The president is elected by the senators at the beginning of each legislature.

History

The Republican Senate succeeded the Senate of the Kingdom (Senato del Regno), a body of royally appointed members established under the Statuto Albertino of 1848. The current Senate was first elected in 1948 under the Constitution of the Italian Republic.
