Magan (civilization)

Magan (also written Makkan) was an ancient civilisation referred to in Sumerian cuneiform texts of around 2300 BCE as a major trading partner and source of copper and diorite for Mesopotamia. It is generally identified with the region of present-day Oman and the United Arab Emirates, at the south-eastern corner of the Arabian Peninsula, together with parts of the opposite Iranian coast in some interpretations.

Trade

Sumerian and Akkadian texts describe ships of Magan arriving at ports such as Ur carrying copper, diorite stone for statues, ivory, carnelian and other goods. Magan lay at one end of a Bronze Age maritime trading network linking Mesopotamia with Dilmun (Bahrain) and Meluhha, the Indus Valley civilisation. Copper from the mines of the Al Hajar Mountains in Oman was one of the mainstays of this trade and was smelted and cast near the mining sites before export.

Archaeology

Within Oman and the Emirates, Magan corresponds to the cultures known archaeologically as the Hafit period (c. 3200–2500 BCE), famous for its beehive-shaped stone tombs, and the Umm an-Nar culture (c. 2600–2000 BCE), characterised by circular tower tombs, round fortified towers and settlements such as Bat, Hili and Umm an-Nar island. Sumerian mythology also associated the region with the god Enki and the deity Ninkhursag through the Dilmun cycle.
