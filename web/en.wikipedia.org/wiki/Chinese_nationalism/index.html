Chinese nationalism

Key episodesMay Fourth Movement (1919); Second Sino-Japanese War; patriotic education campaign
ConceptsCentury of humiliation; Zhonghua minzu (Chinese nation)

Chinese nationalism is a form of nationalism that asserts the Chinese people are a nation and promotes the cultural and political unity of China. It emerged in the late Qing dynasty in response to foreign imperialism and national crisis.

History

Reformers such as Liang Qichao and revolutionaries such as Sun Yat-sen developed modern nationalist ideas as the Qing state weakened; defeat by Japan in 1895 and the humiliations of the treaty-port era fueled movements such as the May Fourth Movement of 1919. Nationalism was central to both the Nationalist and Communist movements, and the narrative of a "century of humiliation" ending with the founding of the People's Republic in 1949 remains prominent.

Contemporary forms

Since the 1990s, a patriotic education campaign has emphasized national unity, territorial integrity — including Taiwan, Tibet and Xinjiang — and China's historical achievements. Popular nationalism also appears in consumer boycotts, online activism and public responses to international disputes.
