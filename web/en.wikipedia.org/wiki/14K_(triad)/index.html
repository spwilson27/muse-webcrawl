14K (triad)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 14K (十四K) is a triad group based in Hong Kong and one of the most numerous Chinese organized-crime societies in the world, with a membership commonly estimated in the tens of thousands across some thirty subgroups. Rivalling the Sun Yee On as Hong Kong's largest triad, it is active internationally in drug trafficking, extortion, gambling, money laundering, and other rackets.

History

The 14K was founded in Guangzhou (Canton) in 1945 by Kot Siu-wong and associates, reportedly linked to Kuomintang loyalists organising against the communists; the "14" is often connected to the address of its founding headquarters and the "K" to karat gold or to Kot. As the communists won the Chinese Civil War, members fled to Hong Kong, where the society grew rapidly in the postwar decades. A major police crackdown followed the 1956 riots, and many senior figures were deported to Taiwan, but the organisation regrouped.

Structure and activities

Unlike tightly centralised gangs, 14K factions operate semi-independently under the shared name and ritual tradition inherited from the Tiandihui-derived triad system, with ranks encoded in traditional numerology. Factions have clashed violently over casino VIP-room rackets in Macau and over drug routes abroad; notorious figures associated with it include Wan Kuok-koi ("Broken Tooth Koi"), imprisoned in Macau from 1999 to 2012. The society has branches reported in North America, Europe, Southeast Asia, and Australia.
