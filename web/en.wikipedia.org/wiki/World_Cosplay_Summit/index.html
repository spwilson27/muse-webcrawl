The World Cosplay Summit (WCS; Japanese: 世界コスプレサミット, Sekai Kosupure Samitto) is an annual international cosplay event held in Nagoya, Japan, promoting the international exchange of Japanese popular culture, especially anime and manga. Its centrepiece is the World Cosplay Championship, in which pairs of cosplayers representing their countries perform short stage skits in hand-made costumes.

History

The first summit was held in 2003 by TV Aichi, growing out of the growing international popularity of cosplay at events such as the World Cosplay Championship's predecessor gatherings. Early summits involved only a handful of countries, including France, Germany and Italy. In 2005 the event moved to Oasis 21 in Sakae, Nagoya, and introduced the championship format. Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs became a supporter in 2006, recognising cosplay as a tool of cultural diplomacy, and the event has since expanded to include more than 40 participating countries and regions, with preliminary contests held worldwide.

Championship

Each country selects a team of two cosplayers through national preliminary competitions. In Nagoya the teams present a performance of about two and a half minutes based on a Japanese source work, wearing costumes they have made themselves. Judging weighs costume accuracy and quality, performance, and fidelity to the source material. The summit also features the Osu Cosplay Parade, in which thousands of cosplayers march through the Osu shopping district, and appearances at locations such as the Aichi Prefectural Government offices.

Cultural role

The WCS is one of the most prominent examples of Japan's "Cool Japan" cultural outreach, and Nagoya has embraced the event as a fixture of its summer calendar, with cosplayers welcomed at city landmarks and by local officials.
