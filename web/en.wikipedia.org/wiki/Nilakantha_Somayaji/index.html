Nilakantha Somayaji

Keļallur Nilakantha Somayaji (1444–1544), also referred to as Keļallur Comatiri, was a major Indian mathematician and astronomer of the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics. One of his most influential works was the comprehensive astronomical treatise Tantrasamgraha, completed in 1501.

Biography

Nilakantha was born in Trikkandiyur, near modern Tirur in Kerala, into a Nambudiri Brahmin family. He studied astronomy and Vedanta under Ravi, and was a disciple of Damodara, son of Paramesvara, the founder-figure of the Kerala school's lineage stretching back to Madhava of Sangamagrama.

Works

His Tantrasamgraha revised the computational system of Indian astronomy in eight chapters. He also wrote the Aryabhatiya Bhasya, a commentary on Aryabhata's work, and several shorter treatises on eclipses and planetary theory.

Contributions

Nilakantha presented a partially heliocentric planetary model in which Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn orbit the Sun while the Sun orbits the Earth — a geo-heliocentric arrangement comparable to the later Tychonic system. He also refined Madhava's series results and gave improved equations for the centre of Mercury and Venus.
