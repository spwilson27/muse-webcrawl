Stilicho

Flavius Stilicho (c. 359 – 22 August 408) was a military commander (magister militum) of the Western Roman Empire and, for thirteen years, its effective ruler as guardian of the emperor Honorius. Of Vandal descent on his father's side, he rose to dominate the West during the crisis years in which Alaric and the Visigoths invaded Italy.

Rise to power

Stilicho's father was a Vandal cavalry officer in Roman service; his mother was a Roman citizen. He joined the army, rose quickly, and in 384 was sent on an embassy to Persia. He married Serena, the niece and adopted daughter of the emperor Theodosius I, and was promoted to command the armies of the East and then of the West. On his deathbed in 395, Theodosius entrusted Stilicho with the guardianship of his young sons, Honorius in the West and Arcadius in the East — a claim the eastern court refused to recognise.

Defender of Italy

Stilicho campaigned against Alaric in Greece (395–397), invaded Africa to suppress the revolt of Gildo, and defeated the great invasion of the Gothic king Radagaisus at Fiesole in 406. His most famous victories were over Alaric at Pollentia (402) and Verona (403), which saved Italy. To meet these threats he stripped the Rhine frontier of troops, a decision blamed when Vandals, Alans and Suebi crossed the frozen Rhine on the last night of 406 and overran Gaul.

Downfall

Court rivals, led by the official Olympius, convinced Honorius that Stilicho plotted to make his own son Eucherius emperor. Stilicho refused to let his troops resist the imperial order — a restraint that sealed his fate: he took sanctuary in a church at Ravenna, was lured out on a promise of safety, and was beheaded on 22 August 408. His fall was followed by a massacre of the families of Rome's Germanic soldiers, driving thousands of them over to Alaric — who sacked Rome two years later.
