Physician Payments Sunshine Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Open Payments database created by the Act.

The Physician Payments Sunshine Act is a 2010 United States federal law, enacted as section 6002 of the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act, that requires manufacturers of drugs, medical devices, and biological and medical supplies covered by Medicare, Medicaid, or CHIP to report payments and other transfers of value to physicians and teaching hospitals to the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). The data are published annually in a public, searchable database known as Open Payments.

Provisions

Applicable manufacturers must report payments for consulting, speaking, travel, meals, research, gifts, and ownership or investment interests, with details of the recipient, amount, date, and nature of payment. Certain items are excluded, such as product samples intended for patients and payments under a de minimis threshold (originally $10 per payment / $100 aggregate, adjusted for inflation). Group purchasing organizations must report ownership interests held by physicians. The law was introduced by Senators Chuck Grassley and Herb Kohl following concern that undisclosed industry payments could influence prescribing and research.

Implementation and effects

Data collection began in August 2013 and the first Open Payments data were released in September 2014. The database has been used in numerous studies associating industry payments with prescribing patterns, and is consulted by patients and journalists. The SUPPORT Act of 2018 expanded the definition of covered recipients to include physician assistants, nurse practitioners, and other advanced practice clinicians, effective for data collected from 2021.
