Tteokguk

TypeSoup
Place of originKorea
Main ingredientsTteok (rice cakes), broth (beef, anchovy or seafood), egg, gim
Similar dishesTteokmanduguk

Tteokguk (떡국) is a traditional Korean soup made with sliced rice cakes (tteok) in a clear broth. It is traditionally eaten during the celebration of the Korean New Year, Seollal, and its consumption is associated with good fortune, prosperity and the gaining of a year of age.

Preparation

The dish is made by simmering garae-tteok — long, cylinder-shaped white rice cakes — sliced diagonally into thin ovals, in a broth that may be based on beef, dried anchovies, chicken or seafood. The soup is garnished with julienned egg yolks and whites fried as thin omelettes (jidan), shredded seasoned beef, and crumbled roasted seaweed (gim). A common variation, tteokmanduguk, adds dumplings (mandu) to the soup.

Cultural significance

Eating a bowl of tteokguk on New Year's morning is one of the central customs of Seollal. The white colour of the rice cakes symbolises purity and a fresh start, while their coin-like oval shape is said to represent wealth. In traditional Korean age reckoning, everyone gained a year of age at the New Year, and a common joking question asked of children was "how many bowls of tteokguk have you eaten?", meaning how old they were. The dish is also served at other celebrations and as a warming winter meal.

History

Tteokguk is documented in 19th-century Joseon dynasty records of seasonal customs, including the Dongguksesigi, which describes it as a New Year soup made by slicing white rice cakes into a broth with pheasant or beef. Regional variations exist across the Korean peninsula, differing in broth base and garnishes.
