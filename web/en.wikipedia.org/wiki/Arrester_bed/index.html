Arrester bed

An arrester bed, also called an escape ramp, runaway truck ramp or truck arrester bed, is a traffic device that enables vehicles which are having braking problems to stop safely. It is typically a long, sand- or gravel-filled lane connected to a steep downhill grade of a main road, designed to accommodate large trucks that cannot slow down, most often because of brake failure on mountain descents.


An arrester bed on a mountain highway

Design

Arrester beds use deep, loose aggregate — commonly rounded river gravel — whose rolling resistance rapidly decelerates a vehicle that enters it. Other designs include gravity escape ramps that use an uphill grade, sand-pile ramps, and mechanical systems using nets or cables. Beds are typically placed before a sharp curve or built-up area where an out-of-control vehicle would cause the greatest harm, and are signed in advance so drivers know the facility is available.

Use

A driver experiencing brake fade or failure steers onto the ramp; the vehicle ploughs into the gravel and usually stops within the length of the bed. Recovery then requires a tow vehicle to pull the truck out backwards. After each use, the bed must be re-graded, as the displaced aggregate leaves ruts that reduce effectiveness.

Related systems

At airports, the analogous problem of runway overruns is addressed by engineered materials arrestor systems (EMAS), beds of crushable concrete blocks at the end of runways. On aircraft carriers, arresting gear of steel cables stops landing aircraft.
