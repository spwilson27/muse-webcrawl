Basque people

The Basques (Basque: euskaldunak; Spanish: vascos; French: Basques) are an indigenous ethnic group native to the Basque Country, a region spanning the western Pyrenees in north-central Spain and south-western France, straddling the modern border along the Bay of Biscay. They are known for their language, Basque (euskara), a language isolate unrelated to any other living language, and for a distinctive cultural identity maintained through centuries of surrounding Roman, Visigothic, Frankish, Spanish and French rule.

History

The Basques are widely regarded as one of the oldest ethnic groups in Europe. Genetic and archaeological studies suggest substantial continuity between the present-day Basque population and the pre-Indo-European inhabitants of the western Pyrenean region. During the Roman era the Basques' ancestors — peoples such as the Vascones — occupied territory on both sides of the Pyrenees. In the Middle Ages, much of the Basque-speaking area came under the Kingdom of Navarre and later the crowns of Castile and France, while retaining distinctive legal privileges known as the fueros.

In the 19th and 20th centuries the Basque Country industrialised rapidly, especially around Bilbao, and Basque nationalism emerged as a major political force, led from 1895 by Sabino Arana, founder of the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV). During the Spanish Civil War the autonomous Basque government sided with the Republic; the bombing of Guernica in 1937 became an international symbol of the war. Under the Franco regime the Basque language was suppressed in public life. Since Spain's 1978 constitution, the Basque Autonomous Community has enjoyed extensive self-government, and Basque has co-official status in Spain and recognition in parts of France.

Language

Basque is a language isolate with several dialects; a standardised form, euskara batua, was developed in the 20th century. It is an ergative language with agglutinative morphology. Roughly 750,000 people speak Basque, most of them in Spain.

Culture

Basque culture is noted for its rural sports (herri kirolak) such as stone lifting and wood chopping, the improvised sung poetry of bertsolaritza, the ball game pelota, distinctive cuisine including pintxos, and a strong tradition of gastronomic societies. The red beret (txapela) and the ikurriña flag are widely recognised Basque symbols.

Diaspora

Significant Basque diasporas exist in the Americas, particularly in Argentina, Chile, Mexico, Cuba and the western United States, where Basque shepherds settled in Idaho, Nevada and California.
