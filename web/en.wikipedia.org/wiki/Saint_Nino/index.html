Saint Nino

Bornc. 280, Cappadocia
Diedc. 332, Bodbe, Georgia
Venerated inGeorgian Orthodox Church, Eastern Orthodox Church, Roman Catholic Church
Feast14 January (27 January N.S.)
AttributesGrapevine cross

Saint Nino (Georgian: წმინდა ნინო, ts'minda nino; c. 280 – c. 332) was a woman from Cappadocia who preached Christianity in the early 4th century in the ancient Georgian kingdom of Iberia (Kartli), leading to its Christianization. She is honoured as the enlightener of the Georgians and is regarded in the Georgian Orthodox Church as equal to the apostles.

Life

According to Georgian tradition, Nino was born in Cappadocia to a Roman family; her father Zabulon is said to have been a relative of Saint George. Raised in Jerusalem under the care of a relative of the Patriarch, she heard accounts of the Seamless Robe of Christ, believed to have been taken to Mtskheta in Iberia, and resolved to journey there. Travelling through Armenia, she escaped the persecution in which the virgins Hripsime and Gayane were martyred, and reached Mtskheta around 320.

Mission in Iberia

In Mtskheta, Nino preached, healed the sick and made a cross from grapevine branches bound with her own hair — the distinctive grapevine cross that remains a symbol of the Georgian Church. She is credited with healing Queen Nana and converting King Mirian III, who proclaimed Christianity the state religion of Iberia (traditionally dated to 326 or 337), making Georgia one of the earliest Christian kingdoms. Nino later withdrew to Bodbe in Kakheti, where she died and was buried; the Bodbe Monastery stands over her tomb.

Veneration

Saint Nino is one of Georgia's most beloved saints. Her feast, Ninooba, is celebrated on 14 January in the Georgian church calendar, and a second feast on 1 June marks her arrival in Georgia. Countless Georgian churches are dedicated to her, and Nino remains a popular female name in Georgia.
