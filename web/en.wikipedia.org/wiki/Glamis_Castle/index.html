Glamis Castle is situated beside the village of Glamis in Angus, Scotland. It is the home of the Earl of Strathmore and Kinghorne, and was the childhood home of Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother and the birthplace of Princess Margaret.

History

The site has been occupied since at least the eleventh century; Shakespeare set the murder of King Duncan in Macbeth at Glamis, though the historical Duncan I died in battle near Elgin. In 1372 Robert II granted the thanage of Glamis to Sir John Lyon, whose family — later Bowes-Lyon — has held it ever since. The great L-plan tower house was largely rebuilt in the seventeenth century, with further remodelling in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries producing the castle's turreted silhouette.

Legends

Glamis is often called Scotland's most haunted castle. Its legends include the "Monster of Glamis", said to be a hideously deformed heir kept hidden in a secret room, and the ghost of Lady Janet Douglas, burned as a witch in 1537. A secret chamber is also said to house Earl Beardie, condemned to play cards with the Devil until Doomsday.

Today

The castle and its gardens are open to the public, while remaining the family seat of Simon Bowes-Lyon, 19th Earl of Strathmore and Kinghorne.
