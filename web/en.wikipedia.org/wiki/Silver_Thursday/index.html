Silver Thursday
Date27 March 1980
LocationCOMEX, New York, United States
Key figuresNelson Bunker Hunt; William Herbert Hunt
OutcomeSilver price collapsed from nearly $50 to under $11 per troy ounce

Silver Thursday was an event in the United States silver commodity markets on Thursday, 27 March 1980, when the price of silver collapsed following the attempt of Texas oil heir brothers Nelson Bunker Hunt and William Herbert Hunt to corner the world silver market.

Background

During the 1970s the Hunt brothers, fearing inflation and the devaluation of paper currencies, accumulated enormous positions in physical silver and silver futures on the COMEX exchange in New York and the Chicago Board of Trade. By early 1980 they and their partners controlled a large share of the world's deliverable silver supply, and the price rose from about $6 per troy ounce in early 1979 to a record $49.45 on 18 January 1980.

Collapse

In response, the exchanges introduced restrictive rules — notably COMEX's "Silver Rule 7," which sharply limited leveraged futures positions. Unable to meet margin calls as prices fell, the Hunts' position unravelled; on 27 March 1980 the price plunged to below $11 per ounce, and the brothers faced losses estimated at over a billion dollars. Fears that the failure of the Hunts could destabilise brokerage firms prompted the Federal Reserve to encourage a bank rescue loan.

Aftermath

The Hunts were later convicted of conspiring to manipulate the silver market, fined, and declared bankruptcy in 1988. Silver Thursday led to tighter regulation of commodity futures markets.
