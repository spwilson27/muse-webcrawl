Member of Parliament

A member of parliament (MP) is a person elected or appointed to a parliament, usually to represent the voters of a constituency in a national legislature. The title is used in many countries with parliamentary systems derived from, or influenced by, the British Westminster system, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, India, New Zealand and numerous Commonwealth and European states.

Role

MPs scrutinise and vote on legislation, debate public policy, hold the executive to account through questions and committees, approve taxation and public spending, and represent the interests and grievances of their constituents ("casework"). Many also serve as ministers in government or as opposition spokespersons while remaining MPs.

By country

United Kingdom

In the UK, MPs are members of the House of Commons, elected from 650 single-member constituencies by first-past-the-post. Members of the devolved legislatures are styled differently — MSPs in Scotland, MSs in Wales and MLAs in Northern Ireland — while members of the upper house, the House of Lords, are not MPs.

Canada and Australia

Canadian MPs sit in the House of Commons in Ottawa, elected from single-member ridings. In Australia, "MP" strictly denotes members of the federal House of Representatives, with senators distinguished by title; state parliaments also use MP (and MLC for legislative councils).

India

Indian MPs sit in either the Lok Sabha (House of the People), directly elected, or the Rajya Sabha (Council of States), largely elected by state assemblies.

Election and tenure

MPs are normally elected at general elections for fixed or maximum terms (commonly four or five years), though the term can end early through dissolution. By-elections fill vacancies arising between general elections. In some systems a proportion of MPs is elected from party lists under proportional representation, sometimes without a specific geographic constituency.

Privileges

Most parliaments grant members certain privileges, notably parliamentary immunity or privilege protecting freedom of speech in debate from legal action, to enable them to perform their duties without interference.
