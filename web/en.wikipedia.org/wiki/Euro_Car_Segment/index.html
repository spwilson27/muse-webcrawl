Euro Car Segment

The Euro car segments are a classification of passenger cars used in Europe, defined in European Commission legislation and widely used by the automotive industry, motoring press, and market analysts to group vehicles by size and market position. The segments run from A (smallest) through F (largest), with additional categories for SUVs and crossovers (J-segment), multi-purpose vehicles (M-segment), and sports cars (S-segment).

The Euro segments rank passenger cars from A (city cars) to F (luxury cars).

Usage

The classification originated in European Commission documents on competition in the car market and is used for market analysis, emissions regulation context, and consumer guidance. It is distinct from the size classifications used in the United States (such as the EPA classes) and from Euro NCAP crash-test groupings, although the boundaries roughly correspond to vehicle length and price. Because segments are not precisely defined by measurements, individual models near a boundary are sometimes assigned differently by different sources.

In recent decades the J-segment has grown dramatically as crossovers and SUVs have displaced conventional hatchbacks, saloons, and estates across all size classes, and manufacturers commonly describe SUVs using the letter of their equivalent hatchback segment (for example, a "C-segment SUV").
