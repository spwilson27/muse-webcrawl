Koi

Koi (鯉, "carp"), more specifically nishikigoi (錦鯉, "brocaded carp"), are coloured varieties of the common carp (Cyprinus carpio) kept for decorative purposes in outdoor ponds and water gardens.

History and varieties

Breeding of ornamental koi began in Niigata, Japan, in the early 19th century, when rice farmers noticed and bred carp with bright colour mutations. Varieties are distinguished by colouration, pattern and scalation: major classes include Kōhaku (white with red markings), Sanke, Shōwa, Ogon and Butterfly koi. Prize fish can sell for very large sums.

Keeping

Koi are hardy, social fish that can live for decades — exceptional individuals are claimed to exceed a century — and grow to 60–90 cm. They require large, well-filtered ponds. Released koi can become invasive pests in the wild.
