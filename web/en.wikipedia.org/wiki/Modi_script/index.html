Modi script

Modi (Marathi: मोडी, Moḍī) is a script used to write the Marathi language, which is the primary language spoken in the Indian state of Maharashtra. It is a cursive variant of the Devanagari script, designed for fast writing, in which most letters are written without lifting the pen and the headline stroke of Devanagari is replaced by a continuous flow through the word.

History

The Modi script is believed to have developed during the Yadava period and was used extensively from the medieval period onward. It became the standard administrative and commercial script of the Maratha Empire and remained widely used for government records, account books and personal correspondence through the 19th and early 20th centuries. Tradition attributes its creation or systematisation to Hemadpant (Hemadri Pandit), a minister of the Yadava dynasty in the 13th century, though some accounts trace it to the Maurya era or to scribes under the Bahmani Sultanate.

Use

Modi was the script in which most Maratha-era documents, including the famous bakhar chronicles, were written. During British rule it was taught in schools alongside Balbodh Devanagari, but its use declined in the 20th century as printed Marathi standardised on Devanagari. In 1964 the Government of Maharashtra discontinued the use of Modi for official purposes, although a large body of historical documents in Modi survives in archives, and efforts continue to teach and digitise the script. Modi was added to the Unicode Standard in June 2014 with the release of version 7.0.

Characteristics

Modi letters are generally more rounded and abbreviated than their Devanagari equivalents. Consonants are written with a cursive flow, vowel signs are simplified, and the script traditionally lacks a shirorekha (headline). Several distinct styles of Modi existed, including Chitnisi, Bilavalkari, Mahadevapanti and Ranadi.
