Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson

Horatio Nelson
Born: 29 September 1758, Burnham Thorpe, Norfolk, England
Died: 21 October 1805 (aged 47), Cape Trafalgar, Spain
Allegiance: Great Britain / United Kingdom
Service: Royal Navy
Rank: Vice-Admiral
Battles: American Revolutionary War, French Revolutionary Wars, Napoleonic Wars — Nile, Copenhagen, Trafalgar

Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson, 1st Duke of Bronte (29 September 1758 – 21 October 1805) was a British flag officer in the Royal Navy. His inspirational leadership, grasp of strategy, and unconventional tactics brought about a number of decisive British naval victories during the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars.

Nelson was born into a moderately prosperous Norfolk family and joined the navy through the influence of his uncle, Maurice Suckling. He rose rapidly but suffered periods of ill health and unemployment. He saw service in the West Indies, the Mediterranean, and at home.

Major victories

At the Battle of the Nile (1798) his fleet destroyed the French fleet supporting Napoleon's Egyptian expedition. At Copenhagen (1801) he famously disregarded a signal to withdraw by raising his telescope to his blind eye — he had lost sight in one eye at Calvi in 1794, and an arm at Tenerife in 1797. At the Battle of Trafalgar (21 October 1805), his fleet defeated the combined Franco-Spanish fleet without losing a ship, but Nelson was shot by a French musketeer and died that afternoon, having flown the signal "England expects that every man will do his duty."

Personal life and legacy

His affair with Emma, Lady Hamilton, was a public scandal. Nelson's Column in London's Trafalgar Square and monuments across Britain commemorate him; he remains one of Britain's most revered naval heroes.
