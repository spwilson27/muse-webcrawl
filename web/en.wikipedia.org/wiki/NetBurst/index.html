NetBurst

The NetBurst microarchitecture, called P68 inside Intel, was the successor to the P6 microarchitecture in the x86 CPU line made by Intel. It was introduced with the Pentium 4 in November 2000 and was used in Pentium 4, Pentium D and some Xeon and Celeron processors until its discontinuation in the mid-2000s, when Intel returned to P6-derived designs with the Core microarchitecture.

Design

NetBurst pursued performance through very high clock speeds, enabled by an unusually long instruction pipeline — 20 stages in the original Willamette core and 31 stages in the later Prescott core. Features included a rapid execution engine running the arithmetic logic units at double the core clock, a trace cache storing decoded micro-operations, quad-pumped front-side bus and, in later versions, Hyper-Threading simultaneous multithreading.

Legacy

The deep pipeline suffered large penalties on branch mispredictions and the design's power consumption and heat grew prohibitive; the planned 4 GHz Tejas core was cancelled in 2004. NetBurst's scaling strategy was abandoned in favour of wider, more energy-efficient cores.
