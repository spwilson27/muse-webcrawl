Lon Chaney

Lon Chaney
Born: Leonidas Frank Chaney, 1 April 1883, Colorado Springs, Colorado
Died: 26 August 1930 (aged 47), Los Angeles, California
Occupation: Actor, director, makeup artist
Nickname: "The Man of a Thousand Faces"

Leonidas Frank "Lon" Chaney (April 1, 1883 – August 26, 1930) was an American actor and makeup artist. He is regarded as one of the most versatile and powerful actors of early cinema, renowned for his transformative performances and pioneering, often painful, makeup artistry, which earned him the nickname "The Man of a Thousand Faces".

Chaney designed and endured his own elaborate makeups.

Early life

Chaney was born in Colorado Springs to deaf parents, an upbringing that taught him expressive pantomime from childhood. He worked as a stage actor, tour guide at Pikes Peak, and in vaudeville before entering films in 1912, first at Universal.

Career

Chaney's breakthrough came with The Miracle Man (1919), in which he contorted his body to play a cripple. His most famous roles are Quasimodo in The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1923) — for which he wore a heavy rubber hump and harness — and the Phantom in The Phantom of the Opera (1925), whose skull-like makeup remained secret until the film's release. Other notable films include He Who Gets Slapped (1924), The Unholy Three (1925, remade as his only sound film in 1930), The Unknown (1927) and London After Midnight (1927), now the most famous of all lost films.

Death and legacy

Chaney died suddenly of a throat haemorrhage in 1930, just as sound films began, aged 47. His son, Creighton Chaney, became the horror star Lon Chaney Jr. Chaney's innovations in character makeup and his intense, sympathetic portrayals of outsiders and grotesques made him a foundational figure of screen horror.
