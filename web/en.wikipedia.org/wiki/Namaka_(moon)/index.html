Namaka (moon)

Discovery30 June 2005
Discovered byMichael E. Brown and team
OrbitsHaumea
Mean diameter~170 km
Orbital period~18 days
Named afterNāmaka, Hawaiian sea goddess

Namaka is the smaller, inner moon of the trans-Neptunian dwarf planet Haumea. It was discovered on 30 June 2005 by the team of Michael E. Brown at the W. M. Keck Observatory and was announced later that year, together with the recognition of Haumea's other moon, Hiʻiaka.

Name

The moon is named after Nāmaka, the Hawaiian goddess of the sea and sister of the fire goddess Pele. In keeping with the naming of the Haumea system, the names draw on Hawaiian mythology connected with the island of Hawaiʻi, where the discovery observations were made. Before receiving its name, the moon carried the provisional designation S/2005 (2003 EL61) 2 and was nicknamed "Blitzen" by the discovery team.

Orbit and properties

Namaka orbits Haumea in roughly 18 days at a distance of about 25,600 km, on an orbit inclined relative to that of Hiʻiaka. Its orbit is perturbed strongly by resonances with Hiʻiaka and by Haumea's elongated, rapidly rotating body, so its orbital elements change measurably over periods of years; these perturbations have been used to estimate the moons' masses. Namaka is roughly 170 km across and notably fainter than Hiʻiaka, with a surface rich in water ice like the rest of the Haumea collisional family.
