The Eridanus Supervoid is a large void — a vast, nearly empty region of space containing far fewer galaxies than average — proposed to lie in the direction of the constellation Eridanus. It has been put forward as a possible explanation for the CMB cold spot, an unusually cold region in the cosmic microwave background (CMB) first detected by the WMAP satellite.

The cold spot connection

The CMB cold spot is a region roughly 10 degrees across that is significantly colder than the surrounding microwave sky. If a huge void lies between us and the surface of last scattering, light passing through it would lose energy through the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect as the void expands and its gravitational potential decays, producing an extra cooling in that direction.

Evidence and debate

Surveys of radio galaxies and later galaxy redshift surveys in the cold-spot direction reported an underdensity consistent with a supervoid several hundred megaparsecs across, centred around redshift 0.2. However, other analyses have questioned whether the observed underdensity is large or deep enough to explain the cold spot's full temperature decrement, and whether such an extreme structure is compatible with the standard ΛCDM model of cosmology. The supervoid remains one of the largest candidate structures in the observable universe, and the explanation of the cold spot is still debated.
