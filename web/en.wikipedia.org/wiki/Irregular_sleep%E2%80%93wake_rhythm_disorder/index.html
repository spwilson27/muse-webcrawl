Irregular sleep–wake rhythm disorder

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Actigraphy showing fragmented sleep across the 24-hour day

Irregular sleep–wake rhythm disorder (ISWRD) is a circadian rhythm sleep disorder characterised by the absence of a clear circadian pattern of sleep and wakefulness. Sleep is fragmented into at least three episodes across the 24-hour day, with the total sleep time roughly normal for age, but patients experience chronic insomnia at night and excessive daytime sleepiness with frequent napping.

Causes

The disorder is most often seen in association with neurodegenerative disease, particularly Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, and in people with intellectual disability or brain injury—conditions that may damage the suprachiasmatic nucleus or reduce exposure to synchronising cues (zeitgebers) such as light and activity.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on sleep diaries and actigraphy recorded over at least one to two weeks, showing an irregular pattern. Treatment focuses on strengthening circadian cues: bright-light therapy, structured daytime activity, good sleep hygiene and sometimes melatonin at night, together with management of the underlying condition.
