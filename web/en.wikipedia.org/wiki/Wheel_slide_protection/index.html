Wheel slide protection

Wheel slide protection and wheel slip protection are railway terms used to describe automatic systems used to detect and prevent wheel slide during braking or wheel slip during acceleration. The concept is analogous to anti-lock braking systems (ABS) and traction control systems used in road vehicles.

Operation

When a train brakes, the available adhesion between wheel and rail can be exceeded, particularly in conditions of contamination such as leaf mulch, grease, rain, or frost on the rail head. If the braking force exceeds adhesion, the wheelset stops rotating and slides along the rail. Sliding reduces braking efficiency (because sliding friction is lower than the coefficient of adhesion that can be exploited by a rotating wheel), lengthening stopping distances, and quickly wears flat spots on the wheel tread, which damage both wheel and rail.

A wheel slide protection system continuously monitors the rotational speed of each axle (or groups of axles) using speed sensors. When it detects that an axle is decelerating much faster than the train itself — indicating a slide — it momentarily reduces the brake cylinder pressure on that axle by opening dump valves, allowing the wheel to rotate again and restore adhesion, before reapplying the brake. This cycling repeats rapidly, keeping the wheel near the optimum creep rate for maximum braking.

Slip protection

During traction, the same sensors detect a wheelset accelerating faster than the train, indicating wheel slip. The system responds by reducing traction power to that axle and, in some designs, automatically applying sand to increase adhesion.

Sanding

Many systems are integrated with sanding equipment, which deposits sand in front of the wheels to improve adhesion during both braking and acceleration, especially in low-adhesion conditions associated with autumn leaf fall.
