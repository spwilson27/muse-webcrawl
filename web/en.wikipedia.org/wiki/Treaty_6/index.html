Treaty 6

Treaty 6
Signed23 and 28 August, 9 September 1876
LocationFort Carlton and Fort Pitt, Saskatchewan
PartiesThe Crown; Cree, Assiniboine and other First Nations
Area~121,000 sq mi (central Alberta and Saskatchewan)

Treaty 6 is one of the Numbered Treaties between the Crown in Canada and First Nations. It was signed in August and September 1876 at Fort Carlton and Fort Pitt in what is now Saskatchewan, with adhesions by further bands in later years. The treaty covers a large area of central Alberta and Saskatchewan, including the modern cities of Edmonton and Saskatoon. The First Nations signatories were principally Plains and Woods Cree, along with Assiniboine and other peoples.

Terms

Under the written terms, the First Nations ceded title to some 121,000 square miles of land in exchange for reserves, annuities, agricultural implements, and annual provisions. Signatory chiefs received medals, flags and suits of clothing, and every family was promised a yearly supply of ammunition and twine. Treaty 6 is distinctive among the Numbered Treaties for two clauses: a promise of relief in the event of pestilence or general famine, and the "medicine chest clause", by which a medicine chest was to be kept at the house of the Indian agent — a provision later argued to ground a right to health care.

Negotiations

The Crown negotiators were led by Alexander Morris, lieutenant governor of Manitoba and the North-West Territories. First Nations leaders such as Mistahimaskwa (Big Bear) initially refused to sign, fearing the loss of their way of life; Big Bear adhered only in 1882 under pressure of starvation. The near-extinction of the buffalo and outbreaks of smallpox formed the grim backdrop to the negotiations, and oral traditions of the signatory nations hold that the treaty was understood as an agreement to share the land rather than surrender it outright.

Legacy

Treaty 6 territory is home to dozens of First Nations today, and the treaty remains central to legal and political debates in Alberta and Saskatchewan over land, resources, and the interpretation of treaty rights. The Confederacy of Treaty Six First Nations represents signatory nations in Alberta.
