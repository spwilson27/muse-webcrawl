Crossbench

A crossbencher is an independent or minor-party member of some legislatures, such as the British House of Lords and the Parliament of Australia. The name comes from the crossbenches, between the government and opposition benches, where such members sit.

United Kingdom

Crossbench peers in the House of Lords are non-party-political and include many life peers appointed for expertise, former civil servants, judges and academics. They elect a Convenor of the Crossbench Peers. As of recent years they number roughly 180 members, about a quarter of the House.

Australia

In the Australian Senate, the crossbench comprises senators from minor parties and independents who are neither government nor opposition; governments often need their votes to pass legislation.

Other uses

The term is also used in New Zealand and other Westminster-style parliaments.
