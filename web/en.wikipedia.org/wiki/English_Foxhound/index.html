English Foxhound

English Foxhound
Origin: England
Group: Hound
Height: 58–64 cm (23–25 in)
Weight: 27–34 kg (60–75 lb)
Coat: Short, dense, weatherproof
Colour: Tricolour, bicolour, or any hound colour

The English Foxhound is a breed of scent hound developed over some two hundred years for hunting foxes in packs on horseback in England. It is the archetypal pack hound, bred not as an individual pet but as a member of a working pack kept by a hunt, with pedigrees recorded by Masters of Foxhounds since the eighteenth century.

History

The breed arose as fox hunting superseded stag hunting in the eighteenth century, creating demand for a faster, more enduring hound than the old Southern Hound. Breeders crossed heavier hounds with lighter, speedier stock — some accounts suggest greyhound and even bulldog influence — to produce a dog able to run for hours over open country. Famous kennels such as the Brocklesby, Belvoir and Meynell packs shaped the breed, and the Masters of Foxhounds Association, founded in 1881, maintains the stud book.

Characteristics

English Foxhounds are strongly built, with straight bone, a deep chest, and a level topline. They are bred for stamina, a good nose, and a distinctive pack voice ("cry"). Temperament in kennels is generally equable, but the breed's energy, size and pack instinct make it rarely kept as a household dog.

Influence

The English Foxhound is the foundation of the American Foxhound, developed in colonial Virginia from imported English stock, and influenced foxhound breeds elsewhere in the world. Its meticulous pedigree records are among the oldest continuous stud books of any dog breed.
