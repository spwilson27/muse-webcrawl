Occupational lung disease

Occupational lung diseases comprise a group of conditions caused by the inhalation of dusts, chemicals, fumes or other agents in the workplace, often over long periods. They include the pneumoconioses (such as coal workers' pneumoconiosis, silicosis and asbestosis), occupational asthma, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease attributable to workplace exposures, and occupational lung infections and cancers.

Major types

Pneumoconioses are fibrotic lung diseases caused by inhaled mineral dusts: coal mine dust (black lung), crystalline silica (silicosis), and asbestos (asbestosis, together with pleural disease and mesothelioma). Occupational asthma is asthma caused or worsened by workplace sensitizers such as flour dust, isocyanates and laboratory animal allergens. Hypersensitivity pneumonitis results from immune responses to inhaled organic antigens, as in farmer's lung and bird fancier's lung. Byssinosis ("brown lung") affects textile workers exposed to cotton dust.

Prevention

Control follows the hierarchy of controls: eliminating or substituting the hazardous agent, engineering controls such as ventilation and wet methods, administrative controls, and respiratory protective equipment as a last resort. Health surveillance with spirometry and radiography, exposure limits set by bodies such as NIOSH and OSHA, and workers' compensation schemes are key elements of management.
