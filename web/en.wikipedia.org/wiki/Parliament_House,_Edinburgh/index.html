Parliament House, Edinburgh

Parliament House
Location: Parliament Square, Royal Mile, Edinburgh
Built: 1632–1639
Original use: Parliament of Scotland
Current use: Court of Session (Scotland's supreme civil court)

Parliament House in Edinburgh, at the foot of the Royal Mile behind St Giles' Cathedral, was purpose-built in the 1630s to house the Parliament of Scotland and the Court of Session. Commissioned by Charles I, it was constructed between 1632 and 1639 to designs overseen by the royal master mason. Its central feature, Parliament Hall, has a magnificent hammerbeam oak roof of the 1630s and remains one of the finest secular interiors in Scotland.

History

Before Parliament House, the Scottish Parliament met in the tolbooth and other locations. The new building gave Scotland one of the earliest purpose-built parliament houses in Europe. Parliament sat there until the Acts of Union 1707 dissolved the Parliament of Scotland; the building then became exclusively the home of the courts. The advocate and author Sir Walter Scott served as a Clerk of Session there.

Architecture

The complex was heavily remodelled in the early 19th century: the classical facade on Parliament Square, designed by Robert Reid and built in 1803–1807, conceals the older structure, of which Parliament Hall and the Laigh Hall beneath it survive. Statues of Scottish legal figures line the Hall, and a full-length statue of Lord Melville stands in the square outside. The complex was extended along George IV Bridge in the 19th century with further court buildings.

Present day

Parliament House remains the seat of the Court of Session and the High Court of Justiciary. Since 1999 the modern Scottish Parliament has met in its own building at Holyrood, opened in 2004.
