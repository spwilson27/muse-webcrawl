The Zodiac Killer is the pseudonym of an unidentified serial killer who murdered five known victims in Northern California in 1968 and 1969. The killer taunted police and newspapers with a series of letters and cryptograms sent to the San Francisco Chronicle, the San Francisco Examiner and the Vallejo Times-Herald, signing them with a crossed-circle symbol resembling a gunsight or the logo of Zodiac watches. Despite extensive investigation and numerous suspects, the case remains unsolved and the killer's identity unknown.

The crossed-circle signature used by the Zodiac Killer in his letters and at crime scenes.

Confirmed attacks

The first confirmed murders occurred on 20 December 1968, when high-school students Betty Lou Jensen and David Faraday were shot on Lake Herman Road near Benicia. On 4 July 1969 Michael Mageau and Darlene Ferrin were shot in a car park in Vallejo; Mageau survived. On 27 September 1969 Bryan Hartnell and Cecelia Shepard were stabbed at Lake Berryessa; Hartnell survived and reported that the attacker wore a hooded costume bearing the crossed-circle symbol. On 11 October 1969 taxi driver Paul Stine was shot in San Francisco, and the killer mailed a piece of Stine's bloodied shirt to the Chronicle as proof.

Letters and ciphers

Beginning in August 1969, the killer sent a three-part cipher (known as the Z408) to three newspapers, demanding publication on pain of further murders. Schoolteacher Donald Harden and his wife Bettye cracked it within days; its message taunted readers and said the killer believed his victims would become his slaves in the afterlife. A later 340-character cipher (the Z340), mailed in November 1969, resisted solution for 51 years until an international team of codebreakers announced its decryption in December 2020, with the solution confirmed by the FBI. It contained no name. Two other ciphers, the Z13 and Z32, remain unsolved.

Suspects and legacy

The most publicised suspect was Arthur Leigh Allen, a Vallejo schoolteacher investigated repeatedly by police, though handwriting, fingerprint and DNA comparisons never conclusively matched him; he died in 1992. Many other suspects have been proposed by authors and amateur detectives. The case became a fixture of American popular culture, inspiring the films Dirty Harry (1971) and David Fincher's Zodiac (2007), and the killer's letters are regarded as early examples of a murderer cultivating a media persona.
