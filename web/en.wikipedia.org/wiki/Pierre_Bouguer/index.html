Pierre Bouguer

Pierre Bouguer (16 February 1698 – 15 August 1758) was a French mathematician, geophysicist, geodesist, and astronomer. He is remembered as the "father of naval architecture" and for his foundational work in photometry and geodesy.

Life and work

Born in Le Croisic, Brittany, Bouguer succeeded his father as royal professor of hydrography. In 1735 he joined the French Geodesic Mission to Peru, led by Charles Marie de La Condamine, to measure a degree of latitude near the equator and determine the shape of the Earth; the expedition lasted nearly a decade and confirmed Newton's prediction of an oblate Earth.

Bouguer's Traité du navire (1746) founded modern naval architecture, introducing the metacentre. His Essai d'optique sur la gradation de la lumière (1729) was the first work on photometry and stated what is now known as the Beer–Lambert–Bouguer law for the absorption of light. In geodesy, the Bouguer anomaly and Bouguer gravity reduction are named after him. He was a member of the French Academy of Sciences, and his name is one of the 72 inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.
