Spatial disorientation

Spatial disorientation is a condition in which a person is unable correctly to perceive the position, attitude, or motion of their body or vehicle in relation to the Earth's surface. In aviation it is a major cause of accidents, occurring when pilots lose visual references — in cloud, darkness, or over featureless terrain — and must rely on the vestibular and proprioceptive senses, which are unreliable in flight.

Mechanisms

The semicircular canals detect angular acceleration but cannot register a constant-rate turn, and the otolith organs cannot distinguish gravity from sustained acceleration. This produces powerful illusions: the leans (a false sensation of bank), the graveyard spiral (an unnoticed tightening spiral dive), the Coriolis illusion (tumbling sensations after head movement in a turn), and the somatogravic illusion (a false pitch-up sensation during acceleration, implicated in several fatal accidents).

Prevention

Pilot training emphasizes trusting flight instruments over bodily sensations, maintaining instrument proficiency, and using autopilots in instrument meteorological conditions. Disorientation has been identified as a factor in numerous crashes, including the 1999 accident that killed John F. Kennedy Jr. and the 2020 helicopter crash that killed Kobe Bryant.
