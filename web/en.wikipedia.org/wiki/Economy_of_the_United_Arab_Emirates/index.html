Economy of the United Arab Emirates

CurrencyUAE dirham (AED)
Trade organisationsWTO, OPEC, GCC
GDP (nominal)Approx. $500 billion (2023 est.)
Main industriesPetroleum and petrochemicals, aluminium, trade and logistics, tourism, financial services
Oil reservesAmong the ten largest in the world, mostly in Abu Dhabi

The economy of the United Arab Emirates is one of the most diversified in the Gulf region. Long dependent on oil and natural gas revenues — the federation holds some of the world's largest proven reserves, concentrated in Abu Dhabi — the UAE has invested heavily in trade, logistics, tourism, aviation, real estate, and financial services, with Dubai in particular developing as a global commercial hub with a largely non-oil economy.

Structure

Hydrocarbons still account for a substantial share of government revenue and GDP, but the non-oil sector now contributes the majority of output. Free trade zones such as Jebel Ali, a major port and industrial area operated by DP World, allow full foreign ownership and have attracted thousands of companies. Emirates and Etihad Airways anchor a large aviation sector, and tourism — centred on Dubai's hotels, shopping, and landmarks such as the Burj Khalifa — employs a significant workforce.

Labour and policy

The population is majority expatriate, and foreign workers, mainly from South Asia, make up most of the labour force. Recent policy changes include the introduction of a 5% value-added tax in 2018 and a 9% federal corporate tax in 2023, long-term "golden visa" residency for investors and professionals, and continued diversification under strategies promoting technology, renewable energy, and manufacturing.
