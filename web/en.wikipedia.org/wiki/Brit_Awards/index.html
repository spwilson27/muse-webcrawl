The Brit Awards (stylised as the BRITs) are the British Phonographic Industry's annual popular music awards, first held in 1977 and staged annually since 1982. They are the most high-profile music awards ceremony in the United Kingdom, broadly equivalent to the American Grammy Awards, and honour both British and international artists.

History

The awards began in 1977 as part of the Queen's Silver Jubilee celebrations, honouring the previous 25 years of music. After a gap, they were revived in 1982 and televised, growing into a major live event held for many years at Earls Court and later at The O2 Arena in London. The ceremony is famous for memorable performances and incidents, from Jarvis Cocker's stage invasion during Michael Jackson's 1996 performance to Adele's interrupted 2012 acceptance speech. The statuette, depicting Britannia, has been redesigned by leading British artists and designers including Vivienne Westwood, Philip Treacy, Zaha Hadid and Es Devlin.

Categories and winners

Principal categories include British Album of the Year, British Single of the Year, British Artist of the Year, Best Group, International Artist, and the Rising Star award (formerly Critics' Choice). Robbie Williams holds the record for the most Brit Awards overall, while Adele, Coldplay, Arctic Monkeys, One Direction and Dua Lipa are among the most honoured acts of their eras. In 2022 the formerly gendered solo categories were merged into gender-neutral Artist of the Year awards.

The BRIT School

Profits from the awards support the BRIT Trust, which funds music education including the BRIT School in Croydon, whose alumni include Adele, Amy Winehouse and Loyle Carner.
