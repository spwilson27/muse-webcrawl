White Tara

White Tara (Sanskrit: Sita Tārā; Tibetan: sgrol dkar) is a female bodhisattva in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, one of the principal forms of the goddess Tara. She embodies compassion, serenity, and above all long life, and her practice is especially associated with healing and the removal of obstacles to longevity.

Iconography

White Tara is depicted as a beautiful young woman with white skin, seated in full lotus posture on a lotus and moon disc. She has seven eyes — the usual two, one in her forehead, and one in each palm and sole — symbolizing her vigilant, all-seeing compassion. Her right hand rests in the gift-granting (varada) mudra, her left holds the stem of a lotus (utpala) flower. Together with Amitayus and Ushnishavijaya, she forms the triad of long-life deities in Tibetan Buddhism.

Traditions

According to legend, Tara was born from a tear of the bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara, shed in compassion for suffering beings; the white form is connected with his white aspect. In Tibetan tradition, the Chinese princess Wencheng and the Nepalese princess Bhrikuti, wives of King Songtsen Gampo, were venerated as emanations of White and Green Tara respectively. The Seventh Dalai Lama and many other teachers promoted her long-life initiation (wang) for the public.

Mantra

Her long-life mantra is Oṃ tāre tuttāre ture mama āyuḥ-puṇya-jñāna-puṣṭiṃ kuru svāhā — the Green Tara mantra with an insertion invoking increase of life, merit, and wisdom. Practice of White Tara is widespread in Mongolia, Tibet, Nepal, and Bhutan, where her images adorn temples and household shrines.
