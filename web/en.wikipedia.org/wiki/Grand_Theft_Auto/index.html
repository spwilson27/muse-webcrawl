Grand Theft Auto (often abbreviated GTA) is a series of action-adventure games created by David Jones and Mike Dailly, later developed principally by Rockstar North and published by Rockstar Games. The series is named after the American crime of motor vehicle theft. It is one of the best-selling video game franchises of all time, with over 400 million units sold.

Gameplay

The games are set in fictional, satirical versions of American cities—such as Liberty City (New York), Vice City (Miami) and San Andreas (California/Nevada)—and follow criminals completing missions within an open world that players may freely explore. Gameplay mixes driving, shooting and role-playing elements, and notoriously allows players to commit crimes that attract escalating police attention, measured by a "wanted" star system.

History

The original Grand Theft Auto (1997) used a top-down 2D perspective. The series made the transition to 3D with Grand Theft Auto III (2001), a landmark title that defined the modern open-world genre, followed by Vice City (2002) and San Andreas (2004). Grand Theft Auto IV followed in 2008, and Grand Theft Auto V (2013) became one of the most commercially successful entertainment products ever released, supported by the enduring online mode GTA Online. A sixth main entry, Grand Theft Auto VI, set in Vice City, has been announced.

Controversy

The series has been persistently controversial for its violence, criminal themes and adult content, attracting litigation, political criticism and moral campaigns, while also receiving widespread critical acclaim for its design, storytelling and cultural satire.
