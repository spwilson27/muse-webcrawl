Topa Inca Yupanqui

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Inca stonework at Ollantaytambo in the Sacred Valley, an estate associated with Topa Inca's era]

Topa Inca Yupanqui (after 1440 – 1493), also spelled Túpac or Tupaq Inka Yupanki, was the tenth Sapa Inca of the Inca Empire, reigning 1471–1493, and its greatest conqueror. Succeeding his father Pachacuti, under whom he had already commanded the northern campaigns, he extended Tawantinsuyu from Quito southward through Chile to the Maule River — its southernmost limit, where the Mapuche checked Inca arms — absorbed the wealthy Chimor kingdom on Peru's north coast, and pushed east into the Amazonian fringe (Antisuyu). His conquests roughly doubled the empire his father had founded, making it the largest state in the Americas; his son Huayna Capac, who succeeded him, completed its expansion to the empire Pizarro found.

The conqueror

Topa Inca's campaigns (as reconstructed from the Spanish chronicles' Inca informants) took Chincha and the south coast, the Colla kingdoms again, then the great prize: Chimor, whose lord Minchancaman was carried to Cuzco and whose goldsmiths were transplanted to enrich the capital — the imperial technique of absorbing skills with peoples. In the south his armies crossed the Atacama and reached the Maule (c. 1485), where Mapuche resistance — a three-day battle in the chronicles — fixed the frontier; in the north his son Huayna Capac's cradle was Quito, the empire's second capital that Topa Inca developed.

Death and succession

Topa Inca died in 1493 (poison by a wife, Chuqui Ocllo, is alleged in the chronicles; the alleged succession revision from Capac Huari to Huayna Capac foreshadows the fatal custom of contested heirs). His mummy, estate and panaca (royal lineage corporation) were among Cuzco's richest until Polo de Ondegardo burned the royal mummies for the Spanish in 1559. With Pachacuti he shares the empire's creation: Pachacuti designed it, Topa Inca filled its map.
