Orion's Belt

Orion's Belt is a prominent asterism in the constellation Orion, formed by three bright stars — Alnitak, Alnilam and Mintaka — that appear in a nearly straight line across the Hunter's waist. All three are massive blue supergiants lying roughly 1,200–2,000 light-years from Earth. The Belt is one of the most recognisable patterns in the night sky and has featured in the mythologies of many cultures.

The three stars

Alnilam, the middle star, is a B-type supergiant about 2,000 light-years away. Alnitak, at the eastern end, is a triple star system whose primary is a hot blue supergiant. Mintaka, at the western end, is a multiple system and one of the brightest eclipsing binaries. Just south of Alnitak lie the Flame Nebula and the famous Horsehead Nebula.

In culture

The three stars have been known as the Three Kings, the Three Sisters, Jacob's Staff and by many other names. A fringe "Orion correlation theory" claims the layout of the Giza pyramids mirrors the Belt, a view rejected by mainstream Egyptology.

Navigation

The Belt points roughly toward Sirius in one direction and Aldebaran in the other, and its rising can be used to find east.
