Seattle Center

The Seattle Center is a 74-acre (30 ha) arts, educational, tourism, and entertainment park in the Lower Queen Anne neighborhood of Seattle, Washington, United States. It was built for the Century 21 Exposition, the 1962 World's Fair, and remains the city's principal fairground and festival site. Its best-known landmark is the Space Needle, the 605-foot (184 m) observation tower completed for the fair.

History

The site had a civic history before the fair: the Seattle Civic Auditorium, Civic Ice Arena, and Civic Field were built there in the 1920s with a bequest from saloon owner James Osborne. For the 1962 exposition the grounds were extensively remodelled to designs led by architect Paul Thiry. After the fair closed, the site was retained as a public park and cultural campus — an unusually successful conversion of a world's fair site — and hosted anniversary celebrations in 2012 for the fair's fiftieth anniversary.

Attractions and institutions

Seattle Center is home to the Space Needle; the Pacific Science Center, with its distinctive arches designed by Minoru Yamasaki; the Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP), housed in a flowing Frank Gehry building opened in 2000 as the Experience Music Project; the Chihuly Garden and Glass exhibition; and the Seattle Children's Museum. The Seattle Center Monorail, built by Alweg for the fair, still runs between the grounds and Westlake Center downtown. Climate Pledge Arena, redeveloped from the fair's Washington State Coliseum/KeyArena, hosts the NHL Seattle Kraken and the WNBA Seattle Storm.

Performing-arts tenants include Seattle Opera and Pacific Northwest Ballet at McCaw Hall, the Seattle Repertory Theatre, and the Seattle Children's Theatre. The Mural Amphitheatre, the International Fountain (rebuilt in the 1990s), and the Exhibition Hall and Armory food hall host the campus's outdoor events.

Festivals

The grounds host Seattle's largest recurring festivals, among them the Northwest Folklife Festival (Memorial Day weekend), the Bite of Seattle, Bumbershoot (the city's long-running music and arts festival, held over Labor Day weekend), and the cultural Festál series celebrating ethnic communities through the year. Winterfest brings seasonal programming around the holidays.
