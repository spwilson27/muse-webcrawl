American paddlefish

American paddlefish
Scientific name: Polyodon spathula
Family: Polyodontidae
Order: Acipenseriformes
Conservation status: Vulnerable (IUCN)
Native range: Mississippi River basin

The American paddlefish (Polyodon spathula), also known as the Mississippi paddlefish or spoonbill, is a species of ray-finned fish native to the Mississippi River basin in North America. It is the only living species in the paddlefish family Polyodontidae following the extinction of the Chinese paddlefish, which was declared extinct in 2022.

Description

The paddlefish is distinguished by its greatly elongated, paddle-shaped rostrum, which can account for roughly a third of the animal's total length. Adults commonly reach 1.5 metres (5 ft) in length and can weigh 27 kilograms (60 lb) or more, though historically much larger individuals were reported. The skin is smooth and scaleless, and the skeleton is composed largely of cartilage, a primitive feature shared with its relatives the sturgeons.

Feeding and senses

Paddlefish are filter feeders that swim with their large mouths open, straining zooplankton from the water with fine gill rakers. The rostrum is covered with tens of thousands of electroreceptors, which the fish uses to detect the weak electrical fields given off by swarms of plankton such as Daphnia.

Conservation

Populations declined through the twentieth century because of overfishing, including harvest for their eggs, which are processed into caviar, and because dams block their spawning migrations. The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN and is protected or regulated throughout much of its range.
