John Herman Sengstacke (November 25, 1912 – May 28, 1997) was an American newspaper publisher. The nephew of Chicago Defender founder Robert Sengstacke Abbott, he took over the Defender after his uncle's death in 1940 and built it into the centre of a major African-American publishing enterprise.

Career

Educated at Hampton Institute, Sengstacke worked his way through every department of the family newspaper business. Under his leadership the Defender, one of the most influential Black newspapers in America, converted from a weekly to a daily in 1956. He also acquired or founded other papers, including the Michigan Chronicle and the Tri-State Defender in Memphis.

Civil rights and public life

Sengstacke was a prominent figure in civil rights politics, co-founding the National Newspaper Publishers Association, the trade organisation of the Black press. He served on presidential commissions under several administrations and used his papers to campaign against segregation and for Black political representation. He remained publisher of the Defender until late in life, and died in Chicago in 1997.
