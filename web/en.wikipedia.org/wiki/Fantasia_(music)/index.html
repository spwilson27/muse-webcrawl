Fantasia (music)

A fantasia (also fantasy, fantasie, or fancy) is a musical composition with a free form and often an improvisatory style. Fantasias are not bound to the conventions of strict forms such as the sonata or fugue, though they may incorporate fugal or sectional writing within a fluid overall structure.

History

The term appeared in the Renaissance for instrumental pieces by composers such as Luis de Milán and John Dowland, and in England the "fancy" flourished in viol consort music by Byrd, Gibbons, and Purcell. In the Baroque era, Bach's Chromatic Fantasia and Fugue exemplified the genre's dramatic freedom, while keyboard "fantasies" prefaced fugues and suites.

Classical and Romantic eras

Mozart and Beethoven wrote celebrated piano fantasias, and the Romantic period produced large-scale examples by Schubert (the Wanderer Fantasy), Chopin (Fantaisie in F minor), and Liszt. In the 20th century Ralph Vaughan Williams's Fantasia on a Theme by Thomas Tallis became one of the best-known orchestral fantasias.
