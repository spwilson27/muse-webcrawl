Corn maze

A corn maze or maize maze is a maze cut out of a corn field. Corn mazes are popular agritourism attractions in North America and increasingly elsewhere, typically opening in late summer and autumn, often alongside pumpkin patches, hayrides, and Halloween events. Paths are cut through standing maize so that the design — frequently a picture or theme visible only from the air — becomes a walkable puzzle with checkpoints or interactive games.

Corn maze designs are planned to be read from the air.

History

The first recognisably modern corn maze is generally credited to Lebanon Valley College in Annville, Pennsylvania (1993), created as a fundraiser in the shape of a dinosaur, with an influential early commercial maze following in 1996. The concept drew on much older hedge-maze and turf-maze traditions. Design and cutting were transformed by GPS guidance: today specialist firms design mazes on computer, and paths are cut with GPS-steered mowers either before planting or when the crop is young, after which the corn grows up around the paths.

Records and notable mazes

Guinness World Records has recognised several temporary corn mazes as the largest of their kind, including a 2014 maze at Cool Patch Pumpkins in Dixon, California, of about 60 acres (24 ha). Large recurring mazes operate across the US Corn Belt and in Britain, where "maize maze" attractions appear each summer. Because the attraction is temporary, mazes are harvested at season's end and redesigned annually.

Operation

Operators mitigate the risk of visitors becoming genuinely lost with bridges overlooking the maze, flagged checkpoints, staff lookouts, map-and-clue games, and emergency exits; some offer flashlight nights and haunted-maze events in October. "Flashlight mazes" and mini-mazes in straw bales cater to younger children.
