Wing Chun (Chinese: 詠春; also romanised as Ving Tsun or Wing Tsun) is a traditional Southern Chinese kung fu style and a form of self-defence that requires quick arm movements and strong legs to defeat opponents. Based on the principle that the shortest distance between two points is a straight line, it emphasises centreline control, simultaneous attack and defence, economy of motion and sensitivity drills rather than brute strength — making it reputedly suitable for smaller practitioners.

Legend attributes its creation to the Buddhist nun Ng Mui, one of the Five Elders of the Shaolin Temple, who taught the art to her student Yim Wing-chun, after whom it is named. The style was developed in Guangdong, spread by teachers such as Leung Jan of Foshan, and brought to worldwide attention by Ip Man, who taught in Hong Kong from 1949; his most famous student was Bruce Lee.

History and legend

Documented history begins in the 19th century with practitioners among the Red Boat opera companies of Guangdong. Leung Jan, a Foshan herbalist, systematised the art and taught Chan Wah-shun, who in turn taught a young Ip Man. Ip Man's sons Ip Chun and Ip Ching, and students including Wong Shun Leung, preserved and propagated the Hong Kong lineage.

Principles

Core concepts include the centreline (the vertical plane through the body's midline, defended and attacked along), simultaneous block-and-strike, trapping hands (lop sao), low kicks delivered below the waist while the hands work above, and relaxation to generate short-range "inch" power. Training in chi sao ("sticking hands") develops tactile reflexes for controlling an opponent's arms at close range.

Curriculum

The three empty-hand forms are Siu Nim Tao ("little idea"), Chum Kiu ("seeking the bridge") and Biu Jee ("thrusting fingers"). The wooden dummy (muk jong) form trains positioning and conditioning; weapons comprise the six-and-a-half-point pole (luk dim boon gwun) and the eight-chopping butterfly swords (baat jaam dou).

Lineages

Major branches include the Ip Man lineage (dominant worldwide), the mainland Foshan and Guangzhou styles, Pan Nam Wing Chun, and the Vietnamese descendant Vĩnh Xuân. Spellings vary by lineage organisation.

In popular culture

The Ip Man film series (from 2008, starring Donnie Yen) drove a global revival of interest in the style, and Wing Chun concepts underpin the Jeet Kune Do developed by Bruce Lee.
