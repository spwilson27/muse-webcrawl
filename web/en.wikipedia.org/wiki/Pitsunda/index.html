Pitsunda (Georgian: ბიჭვინთა, Bichvinta; Abkhaz: Пиҵунда) is a resort town on the Black Sea coast in the Gagra District of Abkhazia, a territory recognised internationally as part of Georgia but administered de facto independently. It is known for its pine groves, beaches, and its medieval cathedral.

History

The site was the ancient Greek colony of Pityus (Pityount), founded by Milesians, and later a Roman and Byzantine fortress town. In the Middle Ages Bichvinta was an important port and church centre of the Kingdom of Abkhazia; the Patriarchal Cathedral of St Andrew, built at the end of the 10th century under Bagrat III, still stands in the town and contains 14th–16th century frescoes as well as a large modern pipe organ used for concerts.

Soviet era and after

In the Soviet period Pitsunda became a fashionable state resort: a complex of high-rise hotels was built in the 1960s, and a state dacha there was used by Nikita Khrushchev, who was vacationing at Pitsunda when he was removed from power in 1964. The pine reserve around the cape is protected for its relic Pitsunda pines.

Geography

The town sits on a flat cape projecting into the Black Sea, about 25 km south of Gagra. Relict pine forests, a freshwater lake (Inkit) and a mild subtropical climate made it a popular health and holiday destination.
