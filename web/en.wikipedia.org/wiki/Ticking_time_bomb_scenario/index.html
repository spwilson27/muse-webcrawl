Ticking time bomb scenario

The ticking time bomb scenario is a thought experiment that has been used in the ethics debate over whether torture can ever be justified. It is usually stated as follows: a bomb has been planted that will soon explode and kill many people; a suspect in custody knows where it is; torturing the suspect is the only way to obtain the information in time to defuse the bomb. If torture would be acceptable in this case, the argument runs, then an absolute prohibition is mistaken.

History and criticism

The scenario appeared in Jean Lartéguy's 1960 novel Les Centurions and was later discussed by philosophers including Michael Walzer and popularised in debates following the September 11 attacks, including arguments by Alan Dershowitz for regulated "torture warrants". Critics argue the scenario rests on unrealistic certainty — that the right person is held, that torture works quickly, and that there are no alternatives — and that accepting the exception would legitimise institutionalised abuse.
