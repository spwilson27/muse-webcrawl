Southern (train operating company)

Southern is a train operating company in England, owned by Govia Thameslink Railway, which operates the Southern brand of the Thameslink, Southern and Great Northern (TSGN) franchise. It provides commuter and regional services in South London and the counties of East Sussex, West Sussex, Surrey, Kent, and Hampshire, and into Buckinghamshire and Hertfordshire on some routes via the West London line.

History

The Southern brand was launched in 2001 when Govia's subsidiary South Central took over the Network SouthCentral franchise from Connex South Central, reviving the name of the pre-nationalisation Southern Railway. The company rebranded as Southern in 2004, introducing a green livery reminiscent of the old Southern Railway. In 2009 its franchise was extended and absorbed the Gatwick Express service, which it continues to run as a distinct brand.

In July 2015, Southern was incorporated into the large Thameslink, Southern and Great Northern franchise operated by Govia Thameslink Railway, a joint venture between Go-Ahead Group (65%) and Keolis (35%). The period from 2016 saw severe disruption caused by industrial action over the introduction of driver-only operation on Southern services, which led to widespread cancellations and a high-profile dispute with the RMT union. Performance problems during this era drew parliamentary scrutiny and calls for the franchise to be stripped.

Services

Southern operates intensive metro services in South London, stopping services to suburbs such as Sutton, Epsom, and Caterham, and main-line services to Brighton, Eastbourne, Hastings, Portsmouth, Southampton, and Uckfield. Its trains are principally Class 377 Electrostar electric multiple units, supplemented by Class 387s; diesel services on the non-electrified Marshlink and Uckfield lines used Class 171 Turbostars until their transfer away in the 2020s.
