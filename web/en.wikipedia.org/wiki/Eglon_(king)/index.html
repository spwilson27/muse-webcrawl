Eglon (king)

Eglon

Title: King of Moab

Source: Book of Judges, chapter 3

Oppressed: The Israelites, for 18 years

Killed by: Ehud son of Gera

Eglon (Hebrew: עֶגְלוֹן, ʿEglōn) was a king of Moab in the biblical Book of Judges. According to Judges 3, God "strengthened Eglon the king of Moab against Israel" because of Israel's sins; Eglon allied with the Ammonites and Amalekites, defeated Israel, and took possession of the "City of Palms" (Jericho). The Israelites served Eglon for eighteen years.

Assassination

The Israelites sent tribute to Eglon by the hand of Ehud, a left-handed Benjamite. Ehud made himself a double-edged dagger, which he concealed on his right thigh, and after delivering the tribute returned, claiming to have a secret message from God for the king. Eglon, described in the narrative as "a very fat man", dismissed his attendants and rose to receive the message, whereupon Ehud drew the dagger with his left hand and thrust it into the king's belly so deeply that the fat closed over the blade.

Ehud locked the doors of the king's cool roof chamber and escaped. The servants, finding the doors locked and assuming the king was relieving himself, waited "till they were ashamed", giving Ehud time to reach Seirah in the hill country of Ephraim. There he blew the trumpet and led the Israelites down to seize the fords of the Jordan, cutting off the Moabites' retreat; the narrative reports about 10,000 Moabites slain and the land resting for eighty years.

Interpretation

The story is noted for its earthy humour and detail, including the pun on Eglon's name, which resembles the Hebrew word for a (fatted) calf, and its portrait of Ehud's left-handedness as an advantage — his weapon on the unexpected side passed the guards' inspection.
