David (Verrocchio)

The David is a bronze statue by the Italian Renaissance sculptor Andrea del Verrocchio, dating to the 1470s (c. 1473–1475). Commissioned by the Medici, it depicts the young biblical hero David in a confident, almost jaunty pose after his victory over Goliath, whose severed head lies at his feet. It is now in the Museo Nazionale del Bargello in Florence.

Description

Unlike Donatello's nude David of a generation earlier, Verrocchio's hero is clothed in a short, decorated leather breastplate and skirt, standing in an elegant contrapposto with one hand on his hip and the other holding his sword downward. The youthful model's features have traditionally been linked to the young Leonardo da Vinci, who was an apprentice in Verrocchio's workshop at the time — Vasari records that Leonardo modelled for the figure.

History

The statue was sold by the Medici to the Signoria of Florence in 1476 and placed in the Palazzo Vecchio, where David served as a symbol of Florentine republican virtue against tyranny. It passed to the Uffizi and then to the Bargello in the 19th century.
