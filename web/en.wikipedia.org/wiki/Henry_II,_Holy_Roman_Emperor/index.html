Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor

Saint Henry II (6 May 973 – 13 July 1024), also known as Henry the Saint, was Holy Roman Emperor from 1014 until his death, the last ruler of the Ottonian dynasty. He was crowned King of Germany in 1002 after the death of his cousin Otto III, and King of Italy in 1004.

Reign

Henry's reign was dominated by wars on the empire's frontiers: campaigns against Bolesław I of Poland ended in the Peace of Bautzen (1018), which left Poland in possession of Lusatia and Milsko as imperial fiefs; he also campaigned in Italy against Arduin of Ivrea and Byzantine forces in the south, and intervened in Burgundy, which he arranged would pass to the Empire on the death of Rudolph III. At home, Henry worked closely with the Church, endowing bishoprics — most notably the new diocese of Bamberg (1007), where he founded the cathedral and where he is buried — and supporting monastic reform in the tradition of Cluny and Gorze.

Canonisation

Henry was canonised by Pope Eugene III in 1146; his wife Cunigunde was also canonised. His feast day is 13 July. He is remembered as a pious ruler whose childless death ended the Ottonian line, succeeded by the Salian Conrad II.
