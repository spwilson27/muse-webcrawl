Sauron (pronounced /ˈsaʊrɒn/) is the title character and the primary antagonist, through the forging of the One Ring, of J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings (1954–55), where he rules the land of Mordor and has the ambition of ruling the whole of Middle-earth. By the end of the Third Age, he is little more than a vast, lidless Eye wreathed in flame, a symbol of his searching will.

Character history

Sauron was originally Mairon, a Maia of Aulë the Smith, before being corrupted by Melkor (Morgoth), the first Dark Lord, whom he served as chief lieutenant through the First Age. After Morgoth's defeat he repented insincerely and fled judgment. In the Second Age he appeared in fair form as Annatar, "Lord of Gifts", and taught the Elven-smiths of Eregion ring-craft, then forged the One Ring in Mount Doom to dominate the bearers of the other Rings of Power. Unmasked, he made war on the Elves, was captured by the Númenóreans, and corrupted their king Ar-Pharazôn, bringing about Númenor's destruction — in which Sauron lost his ability to assume a fair form.

The War of the Ring

Defeated by the Last Alliance of Elves and Men and deprived of the Ring by Isildur, Sauron slowly regained strength, first as the Necromancer of Dol Guldur and then openly in Mordor, behind the fortress of Barad-dûr. In The Lord of the Rings he seeks the Ring, now carried by the hobbit Frodo Baggins, while Aragorn draws his attention away from Mordor. When the Ring is destroyed in the fires of Mount Doom (destroyed by Gollum's fall with it), Sauron's power collapses and he is reduced to a powerless spirit of malice.

Analysis

Scholars have read Sauron as a Satanic figure of corrupted intellect and will, an embodiment of industrial tyranny and surveillance, and a study in the evil of domination for its own sake — a servant turned master whose fall mirrors Morgoth's.
