Totila, original name Baduila (died 1 July 552), was the penultimate king of the Ostrogoths, reigning from 541 to 552. A skilled military and political leader, Totila reversed the tide of the Gothic War, recovering most of Italy from the Byzantine Empire before his defeat and death at the Battle of Taginae.

Reign

Totila was a nephew of the Ostrogothic king Ildibad and was chosen as king after the brief reign of Eraric. At the time of his accession the Ostrogoths had been reduced to a remnant north of the Po river following the conquests of the Byzantine general Belisarius.

Military campaigns

Totila won victories at Verona and Faenza in 542, recaptured Naples in 543, and took Rome twice — in 546 and again in 550 — although he repopulated and restored the city after his first capture rather than destroying it, contrary to some later accounts. He built a fleet that raided Sicily, Corsica, Sardinia and the Greek coast. His social policies, including freeing slaves and redistributing land revenue to peasants, won him support among the Italian population.

Death

In 552 the Byzantine general Narses invaded Italy with a large army. At the Battle of Taginae (also called Busta Gallorum) in July 552, Totila was mortally wounded and died shortly afterwards. His successor Teia was killed at the Battle of Mons Lactarius the following year, effectively ending organised Ostrogothic resistance.
