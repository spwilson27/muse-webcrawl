The Last Picture Show is a 1971 American coming-of-age drama film directed by Peter Bogdanovich, adapted from Larry McMurtry's 1966 novel. Shot in luminous black and white in Archer City, Texas, it portrays the restless lives of teenagers and the dying of their small town in the early 1950s.

Plot

In the fictional town of Anarene, high-school seniors Sonny Crawford and Duane Jackson drift through football, the picture show, and affairs — Duane with the beautiful, calculating Jacy Farrow, Sonny with his coach's lonely wife, Ruth Popper. Around them the town fades: Sam the Lion, owner of the cinema, café and pool hall, dies; the movie house closes with a final screening of Red River; Duane ships out for Korea and Sonny remains behind in the empty streets.

Reception

The film was nominated for eight Academy Awards, winning Best Supporting Actor (Ben Johnson) and Best Supporting Actress (Cloris Leachman), and launched the careers of Jeff Bridges and Cybill Shepherd. It is preserved in the National Film Registry and is considered a masterpiece of the New Hollywood movement. A sequel, Texasville (1990), adapted McMurtry's follow-up novel.
