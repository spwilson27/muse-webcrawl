Geraint son of Erbin

Geraint son of Erbin (Welsh: Geraint fab Erbin) is a legendary figure in Welsh tradition, a king of Dumnonia and a hero associated with the court of King Arthur. He is the protagonist of the Welsh romance Geraint and Enid, one of the Three Welsh Romances associated with the Mabinogion, and of the earlier poem Geraint son of Erbin preserved in the Black Book of Carmarthen.

The poem

The Black Book of Carmarthen poem is a series of englynion stanzas describing Geraint's exploits, including the battle of Llongborth, where "Geraint's brave men" fell. The poem associates the battle with Arthur, mentioning "Arthur's men" and describing Arthur as "the steadfast emperor, director of the toil of battle". The site of Llongborth is often identified with Langport in Somerset or Portsmouth.

The romance

In Geraint and Enid, Geraint, a knight of Arthur's court, marries Enid after avenging an insult to Queen Gwenhwyfar. Suspecting his wife's fidelity after she laments his reputation for softness, he subjects her to a gruelling silent journey through dangers that prove her loyalty; the couple are reconciled, and Geraint eventually inherits his father's kingdom in Cornwall.

Historical basis

Geraint may be based on a historical ruler of Dumnonia in the late 5th or 6th century. A Geraint, king of Dumnonia, received a surviving letter from Aldhelm, abbot of Malmesbury, written around 705, concerning the Celtic church's dating of Easter — though this may be a later namesake.
