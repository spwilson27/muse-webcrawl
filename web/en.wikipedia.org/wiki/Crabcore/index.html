Crabcore

Crabcore is a tongue-in-cheek internet term describing a style of stage performance associated with certain metalcore and post-hardcore bands in the late 2000s and early 2010s. The name refers to the low, wide-legged squatting stance adopted by guitarists — knees bent deeply with legs spread apart — which resembles the posture of a crab, often accompanied by synchronised headbanging and side-to-side swaying.

Origin

The term went viral in 2009 following the release of the music video for "Stick Stickly" by the American metalcore band Attack Attack!, in which the members performed breakdowns in the characteristic deep crouch. Commenters on blogs and video-sharing sites seized on the crab-like poses, and "crabcore" quickly became a mocking but affectionate label. Members of the band and others in the scene responded with varying mixtures of humour and irritation as the meme spread.

Associated style

Beyond the stance, crabcore became shorthand for a broader package of scene aesthetics of the period: heavily produced metalcore mixing screamed verses with Auto-Tuned clean choruses, electronic and trance-influenced interludes, deep-V guitars held low, straightened fringes, and breakdown-centred songwriting. Bands frequently associated with the tag in the media included Attack Attack!, Asking Alexandria, and other Warped Tour acts of the era, though few embraced the label themselves.

Legacy

Although coined derisively, crabcore has been retrospectively treated with some nostalgia as a marker of a particular moment in internet-era heavy music, and the term survives in retrospectives on metalcore's commercial peak. Attack Attack! themselves later referenced the meme in reunion-era material and performances.
