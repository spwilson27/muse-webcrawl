Bảo Đại (22 October 1913 – 30 July 1997), born Nguyễn Phúc Vĩnh Thụy, was the 13th and final emperor of the Nguyễn dynasty of Vietnam, reigning from 1926 until his abdication in 1945. He later served as chief of state of the French-backed State of Vietnam from 1949 to 1955, when he was deposed in a referendum by his prime minister, Ngô Đình Diệm, who proclaimed the Republic of Vietnam.

Early life and reign

Educated in France at the Lycée Condorcet and in political science, Vĩnh Thụy succeeded his father, Khải Định, at age twelve but continued his studies in France until 1932, governing through regents. On returning to Huế he announced reforms, abolished some outdated court practices, and married Nam Phương, who was raised to empress. Real power, however, remained with the French protectorate administration, and the young emperor was largely a figurehead.

Abdication and later roles

In March 1945, after the Japanese coup against the French administration, Bảo Đại proclaimed Vietnamese independence and headed an empire government under Japanese sponsorship. Following Japan's collapse, he abdicated on 25 August 1945 in favour of Hồ Chí Minh's Democratic Republic of Vietnam, famously declaring he would rather be a citizen of a free country than king of an enslaved one. He briefly served as "supreme advisor" to the new government before going into exile. In 1949 the French persuaded him to return as chief of state of the anti-communist State of Vietnam — the so-called "Bảo Đại solution" — but he spent long periods in France, and his authority eroded. The rigged 1955 referendum orchestrated by Diệm removed him, and he lived the rest of his life in exile in France, dying in Paris in 1997. He was the last monarch of Vietnam.
