Judah (son of Jacob)

Judah (Hebrew: יְהוּדָה, Yehudah) was, according to the Book of Genesis, the fourth son of Jacob and Leah and the founder of the Israelite Tribe of Judah. By extension, his name gives rise to "Judah" (the kingdom), "Judea," and "Jew."

Genesis narrative

Judah appears prominently in the Joseph story: it was Judah who proposed selling Joseph to Ishmaelite traders rather than killing him. The intervening chapter Genesis 38 recounts Judah's relationship with his daughter-in-law Tamar, who bore him Perez and Zerah. Later, when the brothers travel to Egypt for grain, Judah eloquently offers himself as a slave in place of Benjamin, moving Joseph to reveal himself — a scene often read as Judah's moral transformation.

Jacob's blessing

In Genesis 49, the dying Jacob blesses Judah as a "lion's cub" and promises that the sceptre shall not depart from Judah, a passage traditionally interpreted messianically. Judah's descendants include David and the kings of Judah.

Tribe of Judah

The tribe bearing his name settled the southern hill country of Canaan, including Jerusalem and Hebron, and formed the core of the later Kingdom of Judah after the schism of the united monarchy.
