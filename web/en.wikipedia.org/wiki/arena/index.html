Arena

An arena is a large enclosed platform, often circular or oval-shaped, designed to showcase theatre, musical performances, or sporting events. It is composed of a large open space surrounded on most or all sides by tiered seating for spectators, and may be covered by a roof. The key feature of an arena is that the event space is the lowest point, allowing maximum visibility. The term comes from the Latin harena, "sand", referring to the sand-covered floor of Roman venues such as the Colosseum, where the sand absorbed blood from gladiatorial combat.

History

The archetypal arena is the Roman amphitheatre, built for gladiatorial contests, public spectacles, and dramas. The Colosseum in Rome, completed in AD 80, seated around 50,000 spectators around an elliptical arena. In Spain and parts of Latin America, bullrings continue the tradition of a circular sanded arena for bullfighting.

Modern arenas

Modern indoor arenas are multipurpose venues hosting ice hockey, basketball, indoor soccer, wrestling, boxing, mixed martial arts, and concerts; retractable or convertible surfaces allow rapid changeovers between ice, court, and stage configurations. Typical capacities range from a few thousand to over 20,000 seats. Arena football and indoor athletics use adapted versions of the format. Many arenas are named for corporate sponsors under naming-rights agreements.

Notable examples

Famous arenas include Madison Square Garden in New York, the O2 Arena in London, the Accor Arena in Paris, and the Saitama Super Arena in Japan, one of the largest indoor arenas in the world. Historically significant amphitheatres survive at Rome, Verona, Nîmes, and Pula.
