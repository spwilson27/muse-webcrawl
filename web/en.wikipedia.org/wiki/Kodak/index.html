The Eastman Kodak Company, referred to simply as Kodak (/ˈkoʊdæk/), is an American public company that produces various products related to its historic basis in film photography. The company is headquartered in Rochester, New York, and is incorporated in New Jersey. It is best known for photographic products, including film, cameras, and printers, and it was for most of the 20th century the dominant company in photographic film.

History

Kodak was founded by George Eastman and Henry A. Strong on May 23, 1892. Eastman had earlier marketed a roll-film system and the Kodak camera of 1888 under the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest," which brought photography to a mass consumer market. The company's ubiquitous "Kodak moment" slogan and its dominance of the film market made it one of the most valuable brands in the world; by the mid-1970s Kodak accounted for the overwhelming share of film and camera sales in the United States.

Decline and bankruptcy

Although Kodak engineer Steven Sasson invented the first self-contained digital camera in 1975, the company was slow to transition to digital photography, fearing damage to its lucrative film business. As consumer film demand collapsed in the 2000s, Kodak's revenues fell sharply. The company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in January 2012 and emerged in September 2013, having shed its legacy liabilities and exited several consumer businesses, including selling many patents. The post-bankruptcy Kodak focuses on commercial printing, advanced materials and chemicals, and licensing its brand.

Products

Kodak continues to manufacture motion picture and still photography film, which has seen a modest revival among filmmakers and enthusiasts. Its business segments include print systems, enterprise inkjet systems, and advanced materials and chemicals. The Kodak name is also licensed to third-party manufacturers for products ranging from cameras to batteries.
