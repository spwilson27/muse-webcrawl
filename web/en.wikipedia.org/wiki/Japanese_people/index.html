Japanese people

Image placeholder: Japanese people

Japanese people (Japanese: 日本人, Hepburn: Nihonjin) are an East Asian ethnic group native to the Japanese archipelago. Japanese people constitute approximately 97.6% of the population of Japan. Worldwide, approximately 125 million people are of Japanese descent, making them one of the largest ethnic groups. Approximately 122 million Japanese people are residents of Japan, and there are approximately 4 million members of the Japanese diaspora.

History

Archaeological evidence indicates that Stone Age people lived in the Japanese archipelago during the Paleolithic period between 39,000 and 21,000 years ago. The Jōmon people, hunter-gatherers who inhabited Japan from about 14,000 BCE, were followed by the Yayoi people, who arrived from continental Asia from about 1000 BCE bringing wet-rice farming and metalworking. Modern Japanese descend largely from a mixture of Jōmon and Yayoi ancestry, with later contributions from subsequent migrations.

Genetic studies, including the tripartite model proposed in 2021, suggest the modern Japanese population derives ancestry from three sources: Jōmon hunter-gatherers, Yayoi-period migrants from Northeast Asia, and Kofun-period migrants from East Asia.

Language

The Japanese language is the primary language of the Japanese people, spoken by nearly the entire population. It belongs to the Japonic language family, whose only other surviving members are the Ryukyuan languages spoken in the Ryukyu Islands.

Culture and society

Japanese culture has been shaped by Shinto and Buddhism, the dominant religions, alongside strong influences from Chinese civilization and, since the Meiji Restoration, from the West. The Japanese diaspora, beginning with emigration to Hawaii and the Americas in the late 19th century, has produced significant communities in Brazil, the United States, and Peru, whose members are known as Nikkeijin.
