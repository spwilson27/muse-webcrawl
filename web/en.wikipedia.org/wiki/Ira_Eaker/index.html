Ira Eaker

Ira Clarence Eaker (April 13, 1896 – August 6, 1987) was a general of the United States Army Air Forces during World War II. Eaker, together with Carl "Tooey" Spaatz, was the architect of the daylight strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany, and he commanded the U.S. Eighth Air Force in England during its build-up and first year of operations.

Early career

Born in Field Creek, Texas, Eaker was commissioned in the infantry in 1917 before transferring to the Air Service. He became known as an accomplished pilot and airpower thinker: in 1929 he was one of the crew of the Question Mark, which set an endurance record using aerial refuelling, and in 1930 he made the first transcontinental flight flown solely on instruments. With Henry "Hap" Arnold he wrote the books This Flying Game, Winged Warfare, and Army Flyer.

World War II

Eaker arrived in England in February 1942 to organise the American bomber command, and he personally led the first Eighth Air Force heavy-bomber mission against Rouen in August 1942. A firm believer in precision daylight bombing — summarised in his famous briefing line that "we won't do much talking until we've done more fighting" — he pressed the Casablanca directive for a combined bomber offensive, memorably lobbying Winston Churchill at Casablanca in January 1943 not to force the Americans into night area bombing. In late 1943 he was moved to command the Allied air forces in the Mediterranean, while the Eighth passed to Jimmy Doolittle. He ended the war in Washington as deputy commanding general of the Army Air Forces.

Later life

Eaker retired as a lieutenant general in 1947 and became an aerospace industry executive and syndicated columnist on military affairs. Congress promoted him to full general on the retired list in 1985. He died in 1987 and was buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
