Concert band

A concert band in performance.

A concert band, also called a wind ensemble, wind band, or symphonic band, is a performing ensemble consisting of members of the woodwind, brass, and percussion families of instruments, usually with a double bass, and sometimes piano or harp. Unlike a marching band, a concert band performs seated indoors. The repertoire includes original works for band, transcriptions of orchestral music, light music, and marches.

Instrumentation

Typical sections include flutes (with piccolo), oboes, bassoons, clarinets of many sizes (with the clarinet choir serving a role analogous to strings in an orchestra), saxophones, cornets/trumpets, French horns, trombones, euphoniums, tubas, and a varied percussion section. The term "wind ensemble" often denotes a smaller group with one player per part, following the model Frederick Fennell established at the Eastman School of Music in 1952.

History

The modern concert band descends from military bands, whose instrumentation stabilized in the nineteenth century thanks to instrument makers such as Adolphe Sax. In the United States, professional touring bands—above all John Philip Sousa's—popularized band music around 1900. School and university bands then became widespread, and from the mid-twentieth century composers wrote increasingly substantial original works for the medium, expanding its artistic standing.
