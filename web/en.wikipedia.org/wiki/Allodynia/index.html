Allodynia is a condition in which pain is caused by a stimulus that does not normally elicit pain. For example, mild touch, light pressure from clothing, or gentle temperature changes may be perceived as intensely painful. The term derives from the Ancient Greek állos ("other") and odýnē ("pain"), and it is a hallmark feature of neuropathic pain.

Types

Allodynia is usually classified according to the modality of the provoking stimulus. Mechanical allodynia is pain evoked by touch or pressure; it may be static (from sustained gentle pressure) or dynamic (from light brushing of the skin). Thermal allodynia is pain produced by mildly warm or cool temperatures, and movement allodynia is pain triggered by movement of a joint or muscle.

Causes

Allodynia occurs in a wide variety of conditions that injure or sensitize the nervous system, including peripheral nerve injury, diabetic neuropathy, postherpetic neuralgia (following shingles), fibromyalgia, migraine, complex regional pain syndrome, and spinal cord injury. It is thought to arise when damaged or sensitized nociceptive neurons lower their activation threshold, or when central sensitization in the spinal cord and brain causes normally innocuous sensory input to be routed into pain pathways.

Mechanism

Following nerve injury, low-threshold mechanoreceptor fibers (Aβ fibers) may gain access to pain-processing circuits in the dorsal horn of the spinal cord, so that light touch is interpreted as pain. Ectopic discharges from damaged nerves, loss of inhibitory interneuron function, and long-lasting changes in synaptic strength ("wind-up") all contribute to the phenomenon.

Treatment

Management is directed at the underlying cause and at reducing neuropathic pain. Medications commonly used include certain antidepressants (such as duloxetine and tricyclic agents), anticonvulsants such as gabapentin and pregabalin, topical lidocaine or capsaicin, and in some cases opioids or NMDA-receptor antagonists. Desensitization therapy and other non-pharmacological approaches may also be used.
