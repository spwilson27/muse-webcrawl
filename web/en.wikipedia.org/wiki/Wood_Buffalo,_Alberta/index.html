Wood Buffalo, Alberta

The Regional Municipality of Wood Buffalo is a specialised municipality in northeastern Alberta, Canada. It is the largest municipality in Canada by area and includes the urban centre of Fort McMurray and the heart of the Athabasca oil sands.

Geography

Wood Buffalo covers about 61,000 square kilometres of boreal forest, muskeg and river valleys along the Athabasca and Clearwater Rivers, bordering Saskatchewan and the Northwest Territories. Wood Buffalo National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, lies partly within and north of the region.

Communities

The largest community is Fort McMurray. Other communities include Anzac, Conklin, Draper, Fort Chipewyan, Fort Fitzgerald, Fort MacKay, Gregoire Lake Estates, Janvier and Saprae Creek. Several First Nations and Métis communities are located in the region.

Economy

The economy is dominated by oil sands extraction and processing, with major operations by Suncor, Syncrude and Canadian Natural Resources north of Fort McMurray. Population and employment fluctuate with energy prices and project construction.

History

The municipality was formed in 1995 by amalgamation of the City of Fort McMurray and Improvement District No. 18. In May 2016 the Horse River wildfire forced the evacuation of nearly 90,000 people, the largest wildfire evacuation in Canadian history, destroying about 2,400 homes and buildings.

Demographics

The population was about 72,000 in the 2021 census, with a large additional shadow population of temporary workers living in work camps.
