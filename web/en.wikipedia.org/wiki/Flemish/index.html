Flemish

Flemish may refer to the people, language, and culture of Flanders, the northern, Dutch-speaking part of Belgium. In its most common uses, "Flemish" denotes the Flemish people (Dutch-speaking Belgians) and the varieties of the Dutch language spoken in Flanders, collectively called Flemish Dutch.

Language

The official language of Flanders is Dutch, and the standard language used in education, government, and media is essentially the same Standard Dutch as in the Netherlands, with differences in pronunciation, vocabulary, and idiom comparable to those between national varieties of other pluricentric languages. Beneath the standard, Flanders has a rich dialect landscape — West Flemish, East Flemish, Brabantic, and Limburgish — alongside an informal intermediate register known as tussentaal ("in-between language"). About 6.5 million people speak Dutch in Belgium.

People and region

The Flemish Region and the Flemish Community are two of Belgium's three federal regions and communities. Their capital is Brussels, shared with the French Community. Historically, Flanders centered on the County of Flanders, a medieval principality whose cities — Bruges, Ghent, Ypres — grew rich on the cloth trade. The modern Flemish Movement arose in the 19th century to defend Dutch language rights within the Belgian state, leading to Belgium's federal structure.

Other uses

"Flemish" also appears in names such as Flemish painting (the art of the Southern Netherlands, including the Flemish Primitives and artists like Rubens), the Flemish bond in bricklaying, and the Flemish Giant rabbit breed.
