Weathering hypothesis

The weathering hypothesis, proposed by public health researcher Arline Geronimus in 1992, holds that the health of women and people of colour deteriorates earlier in adulthood as a consequence of the cumulative physiological effects of living in a socially and economically marginalised environment. Chronic exposure to stressors — discrimination, poverty, and environmental hazards — produces "weathering", an accelerated biological ageing analogous to the allostatic load concept.

Evidence

Studies consistent with the hypothesis find that Black women in the United States show earlier onset of chronic disease and higher rates of adverse birth outcomes with advancing age than white women, a pattern Geronimus argued could not be explained by individual behaviour alone. Telomere-length and epigenetic-clock studies have been used to test the hypothesis at the molecular level.

Reception

The hypothesis has been influential in health-disparities research, though debated: critics note difficulties in separating weathering from other socioeconomic and healthcare-access factors.
