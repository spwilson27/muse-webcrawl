Solar power by country

Many countries and territories have installed significant solar power capacity into their electrical grids to supplement or substitute for conventional energy sources. Solar power consists of photovoltaics (PV) and concentrated solar power (CSP). Global installed PV capacity passed 1 terawatt in 2022 and roughly 1.6 TW by the end of 2023, making solar the fastest-growing source of new electricity generation worldwide.

Overview

China is by far the largest producer of solar power, with installed PV capacity exceeding 600 GW by the end of 2023 — more than the next several countries combined — and it dominates global manufacturing of modules, cells, wafers, and polysilicon. The United States ranks second, followed by Japan, Germany (the early market leader under its pioneering feed-in tariff), and India. Australia has the highest per-capita PV capacity, driven by very high rooftop solar uptake.

Solar share of electricity

In several countries solar meets a striking share of demand on sunny days: Australia, Spain, Greece, the Netherlands, and Chile have all seen solar generate well over 10% of annual electricity, and instantaneous shares far higher, pushing grid operators to manage the "duck curve" of midday surplus and evening ramp. National policies — feed-in tariffs, auctions, net metering, tax credits such as the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act incentives, and China's provincial targets — have strongly shaped each market's trajectory.
