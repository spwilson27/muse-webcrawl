First Army (United Kingdom)

The First Army was a formation of the British Army that existed during the First and Second World Wars. The First Army in the First World War was formed in France in 1914 and fought on the Western Front until the Armistice. In the Second World War it was re-formed in 1942 to command the British and Allied land forces in the Operation Torch landings in North Africa and the subsequent Tunisia campaign.


First Army infantry in Tunisia, spring 1943.

First World War

The original First Army was formed on 26 December 1914 when the British Expeditionary Force's corps were split into two armies. Its first commander was Lieutenant-General Sir Douglas Haig, who led it until he became Commander-in-Chief of the BEF in December 1915. It fought at Neuve Chapelle (March 1915), Aubers Ridge, Festubert, and Loos (1915). Subsequent commanders included Sir Henry Rawlinson, Sir Charles Monro, and finally Sir Henry Horne, who commanded it from September 1916 until the end of the war, including the Hundred Days Offensive of 1918.

Second World War

The Second World War First Army was activated in 1942 under Lieutenant-General Kenneth Anderson. It landed in French North Africa on 8 November 1942 as the eastern task force of Operation Torch, landing around Algiers. Its principal formations were V Corps and IX Corps, and it also oversaw the British element of the Allied advance into Tunisia, working alongside the US II Corps and the French XIX Corps.

The race for Tunis in November–December 1942 failed in the face of rapid Axis reinforcement, mud, and logistical strain, settling into a winter stalemate. In February 1943 the Army came under the 18th Army Group, and after the Axis counterstrokes at Kasserine it regrouped. In April–May 1943 the First Army delivered the main blow of Operation Vulcan and Operation Strike, breaking through at Medjez el Bab and capturing Tunis on 7 May 1943, days before the final Axis surrender in Africa. The Army was disbanded shortly afterwards, its headquarters cadre forming the nucleus for later formations in Italy.
