Channel Dash

The Channel Dash (German: Unternehmen Zerberus, Operation Cerberus) was a German naval operation during the Second World War. From 11 to 13 February 1942, a Kriegsmarine squadron comprising the battleships Scharnhorst and Gneisenau, the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen, and their escorts sailed from Brest in Brittany through the English Channel to German home ports.

The operation

The dash was planned by Vice Admiral Otto Ciliax and supported by a massive Luftwaffe air umbrella (Operation Donnerkeil). Despite being detected, British responses were hampered by poor weather, radar jamming, and delayed reactions; attacks by the Royal Navy and RAF failed to stop the squadron, though both battleships struck mines. Six Swordfish torpedo bombers of 825 Naval Air Squadron, led by Lieutenant Commander Eugene Esmonde, were all shot down in a celebrated attack.

The operation was a tactical success for Germany but a strategic withdrawal: the ships escaped the threat of RAF bombing at Brest but were bottled up in German ports, and Gneisenau was disabled by bombing shortly afterward.
