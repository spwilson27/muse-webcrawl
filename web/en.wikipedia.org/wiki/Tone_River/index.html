Tone River

The Tone River (利根川, Tone-gawa) is a river in the Kantō region of Japan on the island of Honshu. It is 322 kilometres (200 mi) long — the second-longest river in Japan after the Shinano River — and has the largest drainage basin in the country, covering about 16,840 square kilometres.

The Tone rises from Mount Ōminakami on the border of Gunma and Niigata prefectures and flows southeast across the Kantō Plain to the Pacific Ocean at Chōshi, Chiba. Historically the river flowed into Tokyo Bay and flooded Edo (modern Tokyo) repeatedly; in the Edo period its course was diverted eastward to the Pacific in a vast engineering project, which protected the capital and made the river's basin a major rice-growing region.

Use

The Tone is a vital water source for the Tokyo metropolitan area, supplying drinking water, irrigation, and hydroelectric power through a network of dams including Yamba Dam. Its banks and floodplains are also used for parks, cycling, and recreation.
