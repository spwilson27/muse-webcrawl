Carmentalia

The Carmentalia was an ancient Roman festival in honour of the goddess Carmenta, celebrated on 11 and 15 January. Carmenta was a goddess of childbirth and prophecy, regarded as the mother of Evander, the legendary founder of Pallantium on the site of Rome.

Observance

The festival was observed chiefly by women, who prayed to Carmenta under her two aspects, Porrima (or Postverta) and Antevorta — goddesses who were said to know the past and the future, and to oversee head-first and breech presentations in childbirth. Offerings were made at her shrine near the Porta Carmentalis, at the foot of the Capitoline Hill.

Customs

It was forbidden to bring leather or skins — materials from dead animals — into Carmenta's shrine, as their presence was considered inauspicious for a festival of birth. The Roman poet Ovid describes the festival in the first book of the Fasti, connecting it with the myth of Carmenta's prophecy of the future greatness of Rome and of the deified Hercules.
