Federal Reserve Bank of New York

The Federal Reserve Bank of New York is one of the twelve Federal Reserve Banks of the United States and the largest and most influential by assets and activity. Located at 33 Liberty Street in Manhattan, it is responsible for the Second District of the Federal Reserve System, covering New York State, northern New Jersey, Fairfield County, Connecticut, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

Role

The New York Fed implements monetary policy: its Open Market Trading Desk conducts the open market operations directed by the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), buying and selling U.S. Treasury and agency securities. Its president is a permanent voting member and vice chair of the FOMC — the only Reserve Bank president with a permanent vote — reflecting New York's role as the centre of U.S. financial markets. The bank also executes foreign-exchange interventions for the Treasury and the Fed, provides services to foreign central banks, and supervises large financial institutions in its district.

Gold vault

Beneath the Liberty Street building, eighty feet below street level and resting on Manhattan bedrock, is one of the world's largest gold vaults, holding gold owned by foreign governments, central banks and international organisations; the Federal Reserve itself owns none of it. At its mid-twentieth-century peak it held a substantial share of the world's monetary gold reserves.

History

Benjamin Strong, the bank's first president (1914–1928), made it the dominant force in the early Federal Reserve System and pioneered open market operations. The bank played central roles in responses to the 1987 stock market crash, the 1998 Long-Term Capital Management rescue, and the 2007–08 financial crisis, including the bailouts of Bear Stearns and AIG, which drew lasting political scrutiny.
