Swedbank AB is a Nordic-Baltic banking group based in Stockholm, Sweden, and one of the largest banks in Sweden. The bank serves around seven million private customers and several hundred thousand corporate clients, with its principal markets in Sweden and the Baltic states of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania.

History

Swedbank traces its roots to 1820, when Sweden's first savings bank, Göteborgs Sparbank, was founded in Gothenburg. In 1992, a group of savings banks merged to form Sparbanken Sverige, and in 1997 it combined with Föreningsbanken to create FöreningsSparbanken. The bank adopted the Swedbank name in 2006. During the financial crisis of 2008–2009, its heavy exposure to the Baltic economies led to large credit losses, though the bank weathered the crisis without a state bailout.

Operations

The group provides retail banking, corporate banking, asset management and insurance. Swedbank is the dominant bank in Estonia and a major player in Latvia and Lithuania, where it works alongside the locally managed savings banks in Sweden. In 2019 the bank faced a serious money-laundering scandal over suspicious transactions in its Baltic operations, which led to regulatory fines and a change of leadership.
