Karl Dönitz (16 September 1891 – 24 December 1980) was a German naval commander who led the U-boat arm of the Kriegsmarine during the Second World War and, in the war's final days, succeeded Adolf Hitler as head of state of Nazi Germany.

Career

Dönitz commanded a U-boat in the First World War and between the wars rebuilt Germany's submarine force, developing the Rudeltaktik ("wolfpack") tactic of group attacks on convoys. As Befehlshaber der U-Boote he directed the Battle of the Atlantic, which came close to severing Britain's supply lines before turning decisively in the Allies' favour in 1943. That year he replaced Erich Raeder as Commander-in-Chief of the Navy.

Succeeding Hitler and trial

Named in Hitler's testament as his successor, Dönitz became Reich President on 30 April 1945 and headed the short-lived Flensburg Government, which oversaw Germany's surrender before being dissolved and arrested by the Allies on 23 May. At the Nuremberg trials he was convicted of war crimes and crimes against peace and sentenced to ten years in Spandau Prison. Released in 1956, he wrote memoirs and lived quietly near Hamburg until his death.
