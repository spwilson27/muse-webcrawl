Liège Revolution

The Liège Revolution (Walloon: Revolucion lidjwesse), sometimes called the Happy Revolution, was a 1789 uprising in the Prince-Bishopric of Liège, in present-day Belgium, that overthrew Prince-Bishop César-Constantin-François de Hoensbroeck and established the Republic of Liège.

Background and course

Inspired by Enlightenment ideas and by the French Revolution then beginning, citizens of Liège — encouraged by conflict between the prince-bishop and the city's Third Estate over gambling revenues at Spa — rose in August 1789. The prince-bishop fled, and a republic was proclaimed, promptly adopting a Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of Franchimont modelled on the French one.

End

The experiment was short-lived. Austrian troops restored the prince-bishop in 1791, and the territory was annexed by France in 1795. The revolution is remembered as a precursor of Belgian independence and a landmark in Walloon history.
