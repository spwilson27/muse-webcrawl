Battle of Blue Waters

The Battle of Blue Waters (Lithuanian: Mėlynųjų Vandenų mūšis; Ukrainian: Битва на Синіх Водах) was fought in the autumn of 1362 (some sources give 1363) at the Syni Vody ("Blue Waters"), a tributary of the Southern Bug in present-day Ukraine. The army of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, led by Grand Duke Algirdas, decisively defeated the forces of three Tatar beys of the Golden Horde — Kutlubuga, Khadji-bey and Demetrius — opening the way for Lithuanian expansion into the Pontic steppe and the incorporation of Kyiv and Podolia.

Background

In the mid-14th century the Golden Horde was weakened by succession struggles known as the "Great Troubles". Grand Duke Algirdas of Lithuania, pursuing a policy of gathering the lands of the Rus', campaigned south into territories loosely controlled by Tatar uluses in Podolia and the Kyiv region, which had already fallen under Lithuanian influence after the Battle of Irpin River a generation earlier.

Battle

Details come from late chronicle accounts (notably the Bychowiec Chronicle) and must be treated with caution. The Lithuanian-Rus' army, organized in several regiments, met the combined forces of the three beys near the Blue Waters. According to the chronicles, the Tatars opened with archery and feigned assaults, but the Lithuanian flanking regiments struck the Tatar wings, breaking their formation; the Tatar beys were killed and their forces routed, fleeing toward the Black Sea coast.

Significance

The victory brought Kyiv, Podolia and vast steppe territories firmly under the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, making it one of the largest states in Europe and pushing its frontier toward the Black Sea. It is regarded in Lithuanian and Ukrainian historiography as one of the most important battles of the 14th century, a precursor to the more famous victory at Grunwald in 1410.
