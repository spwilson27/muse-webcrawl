Blair House

Location: 1651 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, D.C.
Built: 1824
Function: Official guest house of the President of the United States

Blair House is the official guest residence for heads of state and other distinguished visitors to Washington, D.C., located across Pennsylvania Avenue from the White House. It has served this role since 1942.

History

The house was built in 1824 for Joseph Lovell, the first Surgeon General of the U.S. Army, and later belonged to newspaper publisher Francis Preston Blair, after whom it is named. The complex now comprises four linked townhouses with more floor space than the White House itself.

Notable events

On 1 November 1950, while President Harry S. Truman was staying at Blair House during White House renovations, two Puerto Rican nationalists attempted to assassinate him on its front steps; a White House police officer, Leslie Coffelt, was killed in the exchange.
