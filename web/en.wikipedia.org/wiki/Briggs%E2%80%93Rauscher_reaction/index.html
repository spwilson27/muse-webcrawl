Briggs–Rauscher reaction

The Briggs–Rauscher reaction is one of the best-known oscillating chemical reactions, a popular chemistry demonstration in which an aqueous solution cycles repeatedly between colourless, amber, and deep blue-black. It was developed in 1972 by two high-school teachers, Thomas Briggs and Warren Rauscher.

Chemistry

The reaction mixes hydrogen peroxide, potassium iodate, manganese(II) sulfate, sulfuric acid, and starch. Two competing pathways alternately consume and produce iodine; starch forms a blue complex with iodine when its concentration rises, producing the striking colour oscillation, which continues for several minutes until the reagents are exhausted.

Demonstration

Because the colour changes are vivid and self-sustaining, the reaction is a staple of lecture demonstrations illustrating non-equilibrium thermodynamics and chemical oscillation.
