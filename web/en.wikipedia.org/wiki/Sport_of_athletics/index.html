Sport of athletics


Sprinting is a core discipline of athletics.

The sport of athletics is a collection of sporting events that involve competitive running, jumping, throwing, and walking. Known in North America as track and field (with road running and racewalking included under the wider umbrella), it is one of the oldest organised sports, with roots in the ancient Olympic Games, and forms the centrepiece of the modern Summer Olympics.

Disciplines

Track events range from the 100 metres sprint through middle- and long-distance races to hurdles, steeplechase, and relays. Field events comprise jumps (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault) and throws (shot put, discus, hammer, javelin). Combined events — the decathlon and heptathlon — test all-round ability. Road events include the marathon, and racewalking is contested on both road and track.

Organisation

The sport is governed internationally by World Athletics (founded 1912 as the IAAF). Its premier events are the Olympic athletics programme and the World Athletics Championships, held biennially since 1983. The one-day Diamond League circuit hosts elite meetings around the world.

History

Competitive running and jumping featured in ancient festivals, most famously at Olympia from 776 BC. The modern sport took shape in nineteenth-century England, where amateur clubs and universities codified rules and standardised distances.
