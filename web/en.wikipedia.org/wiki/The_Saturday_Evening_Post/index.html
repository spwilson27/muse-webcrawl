Overview

The Saturday Evening Post is an American magazine, founded in 1821 in Philadelphia, that became one of the most widely circulated and influential magazines in the United States during the first half of the twentieth century. Published weekly for most of its history by the Curtis Publishing Company, it combined fiction, nonfiction, cartoons, and features aimed at a middle-class readership.

Under editor George Horace Lorimer (1899-1936), the Post published work by many of America's leading writers and became especially famous for its cover illustrations, above all the more than 300 covers painted by Norman Rockwell between 1916 and 1963, which became iconic images of American life.

Decline and survival

Rising television competition and changing advertising markets eroded the magazine's fortunes after World War II. Weekly publication ceased in 1969, but the Post was revived in 1971 by the Saturday Evening Post Society under Beurt SerVaas, shifting to health and nostalgia content. It continues to publish on a bimonthly schedule and maintains the Norman Rockwell cover archive as a central part of its identity.
