Golestan National Park

Golestan National Park (Persian: پارک ملی گلستان), also known as the Golestan Forest (Jangal-e Golestan), is an Iranian national park in Golestan Province, in north-eastern Iran, extending into the neighbouring provinces of North Khorasan and Semnan. It is the oldest and one of the largest national parks in Iran and has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 1976.

Geography and ecology

The park lies in the eastern Alborz mountains, between the humid Caspian region and the dry Iranian plateau, giving it an exceptional range of habitats: Hyrcanian temperate forests, juniper woodland, mountain steppe, and river valleys, with elevations from about 400 to over 2,400 metres. It harbours roughly 1,350 plant species — a large fraction of Iran's flora.

Wildlife

Mammals include the Persian leopard, brown bear, wolf, goitered gazelle, urial sheep, wild boar, maral (red deer), and roe deer. The park is one of the last refuges in Iran for several of these species and is an important site for raptors and other birds.

Threats

The park is bisected by the Tehran–Mashhad highway, a source of wildlife roadkill, and faces pressure from poaching, livestock grazing, and floods — notably a major flood in 2001. Conservation proposals have included rerouting the road around the park.
