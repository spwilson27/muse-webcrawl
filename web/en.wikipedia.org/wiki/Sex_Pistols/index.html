Sex Pistols

Sex Pistols
Origin: London, England
Years active: 1975–1978, reunions
Genre: Punk rock
Members: Johnny Rotten, Steve Jones, Paul Cook, Glen Matlock, Sid Vicious

The Sex Pistols were an English punk rock band formed in London in 1975, managed by Malcolm McLaren. Though their initial career lasted only two and a half years, they are regarded as one of the most influential acts in popular music, initiating the British punk movement.

History

Signed and dropped by EMI and A&M amid notoriety — including a profanity-laden live TV interview in 1976 — they released their sole studio album Never Mind the Bollocks, Here's the Sex Pistols (1977) on Virgin, featuring "Anarchy in the U.K." and "God Save the Queen". Bassist Glen Matlock was replaced by Sid Vicious. The band collapsed on a chaotic US tour in January 1978; Vicious died of a heroin overdose in 1979.

Legacy

They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2006, refusing to attend.
