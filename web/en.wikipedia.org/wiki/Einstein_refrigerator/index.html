Einstein refrigerator

Einstein–Szilárd refrigerator
InventorsAlbert Einstein, Leó Szilárd
Patented1930 (US 1,781,541)
TypeAbsorption refrigerator, no moving parts

The Einstein refrigerator (or Einstein–Szilárd refrigerator) is an absorption refrigerator invented in 1926 by physicists Albert Einstein and his former student Leó Szilárd. Patented in the United States on November 11, 1930, it has no moving parts, operates at constant pressure, and requires only a heat source.

History

Einstein was moved by a news report of a Berlin family killed when a refrigerator seal leaked toxic gases. He and Szilárd designed a refrigerator using safer working fluids — ammonia, butane, and water — driven by heat such as a gas flame. Electrolux acquired some patents, but the design was overtaken by Freon-based compressors and changing economics.

Operation

The cycle uses partial pressure differences: butane evaporates in the presence of ammonia gas, absorbing heat; subsequent condensation and separation steps are driven by the heat input, with an electromagnetic pump variant in some designs using liquid metal.

Legacy

Researchers at Oxford and elsewhere revived the design in the 2000s as a potential low-cost, electricity-free cooler for off-grid and developing-world use.
