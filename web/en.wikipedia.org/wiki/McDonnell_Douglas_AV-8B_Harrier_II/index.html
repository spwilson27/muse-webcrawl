McDonnell Douglas AV-8B Harrier II

AV-8B Harrier II
Role: V/STOL ground-attack aircraft
Manufacturer: McDonnell Douglas / Boeing; BAE Systems
First flight: 9 November 1978 (YAV-8B)
Introduction: January 1985
Primary users: United States Marine Corps; Spanish Navy; Italian Navy
Developed from: Hawker Siddeley Harrier

The McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) AV-8B Harrier II is a single-seat, single-engine ground-attack aircraft that constitutes the second generation of the Harrier family. Capable of vertical or short take-off and landing (V/STOL), it can operate from small amphibious assault ships and forward operating bases. It is operated primarily by the United States Marine Corps, and in export form by the Spanish and Italian navies.

Design and development

The AV-8B was a joint American–British development of the first-generation Harrier. Although it retains the same basic configuration — a Rolls-Royce Pegasus turbofan whose thrust is directed through four rotating nozzles — it is essentially a new aircraft: it has a larger composite wing with supercritical airfoil, leading-edge root extensions for improved manoeuvrability, an enlarged cockpit, and roughly double the range and payload of the earlier AV-8A. A substantial proportion of the airframe is built of carbon-fibre composites.

The two-seat trainer variant is the TAV-8B. The radar-equipped AV-8B+ variant, fitted with the APG-65 radar and capable of firing the AIM-120 AMRAAM, serves with Spain and Italy as well as the USMC.

Operational history

Marine Corps Harriers flew extensively in the 1991 Gulf War, the Iraq War and the war in Afghanistan, operating from amphibious ships and expeditionary airfields in close air support and armed reconnaissance roles. The type is scheduled to be replaced in USMC service by the F-35B Lightning II, with retirement planned in the late 2020s.
