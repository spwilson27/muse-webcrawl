Fire retardant

A fire retardant is a substance that is used to slow down or stop the spread of fire or reduce its intensity. This is commonly accomplished by chemical reactions that reduce the flammability of fuels or delay their combustion. Fire retardants may also cool the fuel through physical action or endothermic chemical reactions. They are available in the form of powder mixed with water, foams, gels, coatings and sprays.

Aerial firefighting

Long-term retardants dropped from air tankers ahead of wildfires are typically mixtures of water, fertiliser salts such as ammonium phosphates and ammonium sulfates, thickeners, and iron oxide pigment that colours them red so pilots and ground crews can see where they have been applied. Unlike water, the retardant continues to work after the water evaporates: the salts alter the way vegetation burns, reducing fire intensity and rate of spread so ground crews can build control lines. Well-known products include the Phos-Chek family of retardants.

Mechanisms of action

  • Chemical: gas-phase radical quenching (as in halogenated flame retardants); condensed-phase char formation promoted by intumescent and phosphorus compounds; endothermic decomposition releasing inert gases.
  • Physical: cooling the fuel, coating it to exclude oxygen, and diluting flammable gases.

Materials applications

Flame-retardant chemicals are added to plastics, textiles, foams and electronic housings to meet flammability standards. Classes include brominated flame retardants, organophosphorus compounds, aluminium trihydroxide, antimony trioxide synergists and newer polymeric and bio-based retardants. Some legacy retardants, particularly certain brominated compounds, have been restricted because of persistence, bioaccumulation and toxicity concerns.

Environmental concerns

The fertiliser salts in wildland retardants can harm aquatic life if dropped directly into waterways, so agencies maintain avoidance buffers around lakes and streams. Research continues into the ecological effects of repeated applications and into alternative formulations.
