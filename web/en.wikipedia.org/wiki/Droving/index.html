Droving

Droving is the practice of moving livestock over long distances by walking them "on the hoof". Historically, drovers moved cattle, sheep, and other animals from farms to markets, ports, or better pastures, often over hundreds of miles along designated drove roads.

History

In Britain, droving was a major trade from the Middle Ages until the coming of the railways, with Welsh and Scottish drovers walking cattle to English markets. In Australia, drovers became iconic figures of the outback, moving huge herds along stock routes such as the Canning Stock Route; the most famous was Harry Redford, who drove stolen cattle over unexplored country in 1870. In the United States, cattle drives along trails such as the Chisholm Trail shaped the figure of the cowboy.

Modern practice

Although motor transport displaced long-distance droving, it continues on a smaller scale in Australia and elsewhere, both for practical grazing along stock routes (the "long paddock") and for recreation and heritage events.
