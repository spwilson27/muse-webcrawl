The Zulu Social Aid & Pleasure Club is an African American parading krewe and benevolent organisation in New Orleans, Louisiana, best known for its parade on Mardi Gras morning and for its decorated coconut throws. Founded in its modern form in 1909, Zulu is one of the oldest African American Carnival organisations in the city and a central institution of Black New Orleans culture.

History

Zulu's roots lie in a group of working-class Black labourers, the "Tramps", who marched in 1901. In 1909 the group saw a vaudeville skit, "There Never Was and Never Will Be a King Like Me", about the Zulu people of southern Africa, and adopted the Zulu theme; William Story became the first King Zulu in 1909. The club was formally incorporated in 1916. Louis Armstrong, born in New Orleans, reigned as King Zulu in 1949, an honour he described as one of the proudest moments of his life.

The coconut throw

Zulu's signature throw is the hand-painted, decorated coconut. Because early coconuts were thrown with their rough husks, city concerns about injuries led to periodic bans; in 1987–88 Louisiana passed the "coconut law" shielding the club from liability, and coconuts are now typically handed to spectators rather than thrown.

Parade and characters

The Zulu parade rolls on Mardi Gras morning before the Rex parade, proceeding from its headquarters through historic Black neighbourhoods to Canal Street. Its court includes characters such as the Big Shot, the Witch Doctor, the Ambassador, Mr. Big Stuff, and the Governor, alongside King Zulu and the elected queen.
