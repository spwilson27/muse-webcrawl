Shadow education

Shadow education is a term used in comparative education for private supplementary tutoring that mirrors or "shadows" mainstream schooling. It includes fee-charging lessons outside school hours in academic subjects, delivered by cram schools, tutoring companies and individual tutors.

Scope

Shadow education is especially extensive in East and Southeast Asia, where competitive examinations drive demand: examples include juku in Japan, hagwons in South Korea and large tutoring sectors in China and India. It is also growing in Europe, North America and Africa. The term was popularised by researcher Mark Bray, who documented its scale for UNESCO.

Debates

Supporters argue that tutoring helps students keep up and enriches learning. Critics point to household costs, student stress and increased educational inequality, since wealthier families can buy more and better tutoring. Several governments have attempted regulation, most notably China's 2021 "double reduction" policy restricting for-profit tutoring in core school subjects.
