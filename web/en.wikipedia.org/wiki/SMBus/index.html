SMBus

The System Management Bus (SMBus or SMB) is a single-ended two-wire computer bus used for simple communication, most notably between a computer motherboard and low-speed peripherals such as power-related chips, temperature sensors, fan controllers, and battery management systems. It was defined by Intel and Duracell in 1994 and is derived from I²C.

SMBus is most readily found in "smart battery" subsystems, where it carries information about battery charge state, and in desktop and laptop motherboards, where it connects thermal sensors and the serial presence detect (SPD) EEPROMs on memory modules.

Relationship to I²C

SMBus is derived from I²C and is largely a subset of it, so many devices operate on both. The main differences are electrical (SMBus uses lower current and different voltage thresholds), timing (SMBus adds a minimum clock speed and a 35 ms clock-low timeout), and protocol (SMBus defines specific transaction formats such as Read Byte, Write Word, and Block Read, plus an optional Packet Error Checking byte). The SMBus specification is maintained by the SBS Forum, sponsored by Intel.

Versions

Version 1.0 was released in 1995; version 1.1 in 1998; version 2.0 in 2000 added the Host Notify protocol and other features; version 3.0 (2014) raised maximum speeds, and version 3.1 (2018) made further refinements supporting clock speeds up to 1 MHz.

Applications

Beyond motherboards, SMBus is used in smart battery chargers under the Smart Battery System (SBS) specification, in Power Management Bus (PMBus) devices for digital power management, and as the physical basis for the IPMI platform management interfaces in servers.
