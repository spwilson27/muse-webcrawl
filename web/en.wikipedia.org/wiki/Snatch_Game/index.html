Snatch Game

The Snatch Game is a recurring challenge on the reality competition series RuPaul's Drag Race and its international franchises, in which contestants impersonate celebrities in a parody of the television game show Match Game. Introduced in the show's second season (2010), it has become the programme's best-known and most anticipated challenge, and is widely credited with reviving the art of drag celebrity impersonation.

Format

Queens sit at a two-tiered panel in costume as their chosen celebrity (or occasionally a fictional or historical character), while host RuPaul, playing the Gene Rayburn role, asks fill-in-the-blank questions; two guest "contestants" give answers, and the queens must "match" them with funny written responses. Success depends on accuracy of impersonation, speed of wit, and interaction with fellow panellists. The challenge is usually followed by a runway presentation, and performance in it strongly influences elimination.

Notable performances

Critics and fans frequently cite standout impersonations including Chad Michaels as Cher, Jinkx Monsoon as Edith Bouvier Beale ("Little Edie"), Bianca Del Rio as Judge Judy, Alaska as Mae West, Adore Delano as Anna Nicole Smith, BenDeLaCreme as Paul Lynde and as Julie Andrews, and Trinity the Tuck as Caitlyn Jenner. In later seasons and spin-offs, variations such as "Snatch Game of Love" and "Snatch Game: All Stars" have been staged; a celebrated international version appeared on RuPaul's Drag Race: UK vs the World.

Reception and influence

The challenge has been praised by television critics for testing improvisation, character acting and comic timing simultaneously, and episodes featuring it are often among the highest-rated of each season. It is credited with popularising impersonation-based formats elsewhere in reality TV, and "Snatch Game" has become shorthand within drag culture for a performer's celebrity-impersonation skill. Contestants prepare intensively for it, and poor impersonation choices — deliberately obscure celebrities or ones the queen cannot sustain — are a frequent narrative device.
