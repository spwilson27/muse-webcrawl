The São Paulo Art Biennial (Portuguese: Bienal de São Paulo) is a major international contemporary art exhibition held every two years in São Paulo, Brazil. Founded in 1951 by industrialist Ciccillo Matarazzo and modelled on the Venice Biennale, it is the second-oldest art biennial in the world and the largest in the Southern Hemisphere.

History

The first edition in 1951, held at the Museu de Arte Moderna, introduced many Brazilians to modern European and American art; Max Bill's Tripartite Unity won the international sculpture prize, a landmark for Brazilian concrete art. From 1957 the exhibition has occupied the pavilion designed by Oscar Niemeyer and Hélio Uchôa in Ibirapuera Park, now called the Ciccillo Matarazzo Pavilion. During Brazil's military dictatorship the Bienal faced an international boycott (1969 onwards) and censorship; in 1970 many artists withdrew in protest. The 1985 edition, curated by Sheila Leirner, replaced national pavilions with large open-plan "streets" of art, and the 24th edition (1998), directed by Paulo Herkenhoff around the concept of anthropophagy, is widely regarded as one of the most influential biennials ever staged.

Organisation

The Bienal is organised by the Fundação Bienal de São Paulo, which also represents Brazil at the Venice Biennale. Since 2010 admission has been free; recent editions (such as the 34th, "Though it's dark, still I sing", 2021, and the 35th, "Choreographies of the Impossible", 2023) have each drawn hundreds of thousands of visitors.
