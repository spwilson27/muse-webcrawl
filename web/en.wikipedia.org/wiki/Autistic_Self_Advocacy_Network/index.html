The Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN) is an American nonprofit organisation run by and for autistic people. Founded in 2006, ASAN advocates for the inclusion of autistic people in decisions that affect them, following the disability rights principle of "nothing about us without us". It is a leading voice of the autism rights movement and promotes a social model of disability, neurodiversity and acceptance rather than cure-oriented approaches to autism.

History

ASAN was founded in November 2006 by Ari Ne'eman, then a college student, together with other autistic advocates. Ne'eman served as its first president. In 2009 he was nominated by President Barack Obama to the National Council on Disability, becoming the first openly autistic person to hold such a position; he was confirmed in 2010 after a controversial delay. ASAN grew from a small volunteer group into a national organisation with staff, policy programs and regional chapters, and has published extensively on health care, employment, education and civil rights.

Positions and activities

The organisation advocates against aversive interventions and restraint and seclusion in schools, opposes subminimum wages for disabled workers, and supports supported decision-making as an alternative to guardianship. ASAN campaigns for greater representation of autistic people on the boards of autism charities and criticised campaigns it viewed as stigmatising, such as Autism Speaks' 2009 "I Am Autism" video, against which it organised joint protests with other disability groups. ASAN co-founded the annual Disability Day of Mourning vigils, honouring disabled people killed by caregivers.

ASAN produces resources including the Welcome to the Autistic Community handbook, plain-language policy toolkits and the "Autism Acceptance Month" initiative, promoting April as a month of acceptance rather than only awareness. It also maintains the Autistic Scholars Fellowship and participates in coalitions such as the Consortium for Citizens with Disabilities.
