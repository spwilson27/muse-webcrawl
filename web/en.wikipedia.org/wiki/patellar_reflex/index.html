Patellar reflex

TypeMonosynaptic stretch reflex
Spinal levelL2–L4
Elicited byTapping the patellar tendon

The patellar reflex, also called the knee-jerk reflex, is a stretch reflex which tests the L2, L3, and L4 segments of the spinal cord. Striking the patellar tendon with a reflex hammer stretches the quadriceps muscle, triggering a contraction that extends the lower leg.

Mechanism

The tap stretches muscle spindles in the quadriceps femoris. Ia afferent fibers carry the signal to the spinal cord, where they synapse directly on alpha motor neurons — a monosynaptic arc — causing the quadriceps to contract. Inhibitory interneurons simultaneously relax the hamstrings.

Clinical significance

The reflex is part of the standard neurological examination. An absent or diminished response may indicate peripheral nerve or lower motor neuron lesions, whereas an exaggerated response suggests an upper motor neuron lesion. See also the reflex arc.
