Spatial frequency

In mathematics, physics and engineering, spatial frequency is a characteristic of any structure that is periodic across position in space. It is a measure of how often sinusoidal components (as determined by the Fourier transform) of the structure repeat per unit of distance, analogous to how ordinary (temporal) frequency measures repetitions per unit of time. The SI unit is cycles per metre; in imaging, cycles per millimetre or line pairs per millimetre are common.

Definition

For a wave varying in one spatial dimension, the spatial frequency is the reciprocal of the wavelength: the number of complete cycles that fit into one unit of length. In wave equations it is often expressed as the wavenumber, the spatial frequency multiplied by 2π, measured in radians per unit distance. Structures in two or three dimensions have a spatial frequency for each dimension of periodicity, together with a direction of variation.

Vision

In the study of vision, spatial frequency is usually expressed in cycles per degree of visual angle. The human visual system decomposes scenes into sinusoidal components through channels tuned to different spatial frequencies: high spatial frequencies convey fine detail and edges, low frequencies convey coarse shape, shading and overall layout. Sensitivity to sinusoidal gratings across frequencies is summarised by the contrast sensitivity function, which peaks at a few cycles per degree and falls off at high frequencies — the basis of the eye's spatial resolution limit.

Applications

Spatial frequency underlies image processing (filtering, compression, resolution measurement), optics (the modulation transfer function), medical imaging such as MRI (where data are acquired directly in spatial-frequency "k-space"), and texture analysis. JPEG compression, for instance, discards high-spatial-frequency detail to which the eye is less sensitive.
