Carl Icahn

Carl Celian Icahn (born February 16, 1936) is an American businessman and investor. He is the founder and controlling shareholder of Icahn Enterprises, a diversified conglomerate holding company based in Sunny Isles Beach, Florida. Icahn is regarded as a pioneer of shareholder activism and one of the most prominent "corporate raiders" of the 1980s.

Early life and career

Raised in Queens, New York, Icahn studied philosophy at Princeton University and briefly attended medical school before serving in the army. He joined Wall Street in 1961 and founded Icahn & Co. in 1968, focusing on options trading and arbitrage.

Activist investing

Icahn built his reputation by taking large stakes in companies he viewed as undervalued and pressing for management changes, asset sales, or share buybacks — most famously in his 1985 hostile takeover of Trans World Airlines. Later campaigns targeted companies including Texaco, RJR Nabisco, Time Warner, Yahoo!, Netflix, and Apple. His pronouncements on companies sometimes move their share prices, an effect known as the "Icahn lift".

Public service

In 2017 Icahn briefly served as a special adviser on regulatory reform to President Donald Trump, resigning after several months.
