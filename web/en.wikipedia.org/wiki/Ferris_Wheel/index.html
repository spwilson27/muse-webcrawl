A Ferris wheel (also called a Giant Wheel or an observation wheel) is an amusement ride consisting of a rotating upright wheel with multiple passenger-carrying components (gondolas or cabins) attached to the rim, kept upright by gravity as the wheel turns. The original Ferris Wheel was designed and constructed by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr. as the centrepiece of the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago.

History

Smaller hand-turned "pleasure wheels" existed in Europe from the 17th century, and a large wooden wheel was described at the 1853 New York World's Fair. Ferris, a Pittsburgh bridge engineer, set out to rival the Eiffel Tower with a steel wheel 80.4 metres tall, carrying 36 cars each seating 60 people. It was a triumph of the Chicago fair and was later re-erected at the 1904 St. Louis exposition before being demolished in 1906.

Observation wheels

Modern giant wheels emphasise sightseeing over thrill: enclosed, air-conditioned capsules rotate slowly, often completing a single revolution in 20–30 minutes. Landmark examples include the London Eye (135 m, opened 2000), the Singapore Flyer (165 m, 2008), the High Roller in Las Vegas (168 m, 2014) and Ain Dubai (250 m, opened 2021), the tallest in the world. "Ferris wheel" remains a generic term in American English, despite being originally a proper name.

Design

Wheels may be supported on both sides or cantilevered from one side, driven by rim-mounted motors or friction wheels, with cabins either gravity-hung or mechanically levelled. Smaller travelling versions appear at fairs and carnivals worldwide.
