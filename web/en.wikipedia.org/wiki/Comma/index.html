Comma

The comma punctuation mark

The comma , is a punctuation mark that appears in several variants in different languages. It has the same shape as an apostrophe or a single closing quotation mark in many typefaces, but it differs from them in that it is placed on the baseline of the text. Some other graphemes are identical in form to the comma but are placed differently or used for different purposes.

History

Punctuation was systematised in the 3rd century BCE by Aristophanes of Byzantium, whose system of dots at different heights marked pauses; the term komma in Greek referred to a short clause. The modern comma shape developed in medieval and Renaissance Italy — Aldus Manutius and his circle standardised the low, curved mark in printed books around 1500.

Uses in English

The comma is used to separate items in a list (including the debated serial or "Oxford comma" before the final conjunction), to set off introductory and parenthetical elements, to separate independent clauses joined by a conjunction, to mark quotations and dates, and to clarify meaning by indicating pauses. In numbers, commas (or points, depending on locale) separate groups of three digits; in many European languages the comma instead serves as the decimal marker.

Other uses

In music theory, a comma is a very small interval (such as the syntonic comma of about 21.5 cents) arising in tuning systems. Commas also appear in mathematics and computing — as separators in lists, function arguments, and thousands grouping — and as a diacritic in Romanian and Latvian orthography, where a comma below a letter differs subtly from a cedilla.
