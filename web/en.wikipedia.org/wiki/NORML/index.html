The National Organization for the Reform of Marijuana Laws (NORML) is a social welfare organization based in Washington, D.C., that advocates for the reform of marijuana laws in the United States regarding both medical and responsible recreational use. NORML represents the interests of the tens of millions of Americans who use marijuana responsibly.

According to its mission statement, "NORML's mission is to move public opinion sufficiently to legalize the responsible use of marijuana by adults, and to serve as an advocate for consumers to assure they have access to high-quality marijuana that is safe, convenient and affordable."

History

NORML was founded in 1970 by Keith Stroup, funded by a $5,000 grant from the Playboy Foundation. Stroup had previously worked as a consumer advocate with Ralph Nader's Public Citizen organization, and modeled NORML in part on consumer-advocacy approaches to drug policy.

During the 1970s, NORML played a central role in the decriminalization movement, contributing to the decriminalization of small amounts of marijuana in eleven states during the decade. In 1978, the controversies surrounding Peter Bourne, President Jimmy Carter's drug adviser, marked a turning point in the organization.

In the 1980s, Stroup departed and Jon Gettman led the organization, followed by Richard Cowan in the early 1990s. In 1994, Allen St. Pierre joined NORML and later became its executive director, serving until 2016, when Erik Altieri succeeded him.

Activities

NORML supports the removal of all criminal penalties for the private possession and responsible use of marijuana by adults, including the cultivation for personal use and casual nonprofit transfers of small amounts. It also supports the development of a legally controlled market for cannabis. The organization operates a network of state and local chapters in the United States and has affiliated chapters internationally.

NORML publishes information on state and federal marijuana laws, sponsors legal seminars, and maintains the NORML Foundation, a 501(c)(3) educational organization. Alongside groups such as the Marijuana Policy Project and the Drug Policy Alliance, NORML is one of the longest-standing organizations in the American drug policy reform movement.

Media

NORML has received notable media coverage for advertising campaigns, including a 2009 advertisement featuring then-President Barack Obama and Olympic swimmer Michael Phelps, and pro-legalization billboards timed to coincide with major sporting events.
