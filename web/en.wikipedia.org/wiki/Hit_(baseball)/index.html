Hit (baseball)
A hit occurs when a batter strikes the baseball into fair territory and reaches base without errors or fielder's choice.

In baseball statistics, a hit (denoted by H) is credited to a batter when he hits the ball into fair territory and reaches base safely without the benefit of an error or a fielder's choice. Hits are classified as singles, doubles, triples, and home runs.

Types of hits

A single takes the batter to first base, a double to second, and a triple to third. A home run, in which the batter circles all bases, is also a hit. An infield hit is a ground ball on which the batter beats the throw to first.

Records

Pete Rose holds the Major League Baseball career record with 4,256 hits, followed by Ty Cobb. Ichiro Suzuki recorded 262 hits in 2004, the single-season record. A batter's hit total is a key component of batting average and on-base plus slugging.

No-hitters

A pitcher who completes a game without allowing a hit throws a no-hitter; if no batter reaches base at all, it is a perfect game. See also Batting average.
