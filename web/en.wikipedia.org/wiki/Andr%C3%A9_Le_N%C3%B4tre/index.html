André Le Nôtre

Born12 March 1613, Paris, France
Died15 September 1700 (aged 87), Paris
OccupationLandscape architect
Known forGardens of Versailles; French formal garden

André Le Nôtre (1613–1700) was a French landscape architect and the principal gardener of King Louis XIV of France. He designed the gardens of the Palace of Versailles, the most celebrated example of the French formal garden (jardin à la française), and his work defined formal garden design across Europe for a century.

Career

Le Nôtre came from a family of royal gardeners and trained in painting and architecture as well as horticulture. His design for the gardens of Vaux-le-Vicomte (1656–1661), created for Nicolas Fouquet with architect Louis Le Vau and painter Charles Le Brun, so impressed Louis XIV that after Fouquet's fall the king took the whole team to Versailles. There Le Nôtre laid out the great axis, the Grand Canal, parterres, bosquets and avenues on an unprecedented scale. He also redesigned the Tuileries Garden in Paris, extending its axis toward what became the Champs-Élysées, and worked at Chantilly, Saint-Germain, Fontainebleau and, abroad, at Greenwich for Charles II of England.

Style and legacy

Le Nôtre's style imposes geometric order on nature: long vistas, symmetric parterres, clipped hedges, still water in canals and basins, and carefully staged surprises in wooded groves. His gardens expressed the absolutist ideology of Louis XIV — nature ordered by reason and the crown — and were imitated from Caserta to Peterhof.
