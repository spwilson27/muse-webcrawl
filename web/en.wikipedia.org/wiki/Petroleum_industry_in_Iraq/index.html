Petroleum industry in Iraq

The petroleum industry in Iraq is the dominant sector of the Iraqi economy, accounting for the overwhelming majority of government revenue and export earnings. Iraq holds the world's fifth-largest proven crude oil reserves — roughly 145 billion barrels — concentrated in supergiant fields in the south around Basra (Rumaila, West Qurna, Zubair, Majnoon) and in the north around Kirkuk. Production in recent years has been around 4 million barrels per day, making Iraq OPEC's second-largest producer after Saudi Arabia.

History

Oil seepages at Baba Gurgur near Kirkuk were known since antiquity. Commercial development began under the Turkish Petroleum Company, whose successor, the Iraq Petroleum Company (IPC), struck oil at Kirkuk in 1927. The industry was nationalized in 1972 under the Ba'athist government, ending IPC control. Production peaked near 3.5 million barrels per day in 1979, then suffered through the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), the Gulf War (1991), and UN sanctions in the 1990s, when exports flowed only under the Oil-for-Food Programme.[1]

After 2003

Following the 2003 invasion, production collapsed amid sabotage and decay, then recovered as Iraq held licensing rounds in 2009–2010 awarding technical service contracts to international firms including BP, ExxonMobil, Shell, CNPC, and Lukoil to develop the southern supergiants. Exports leave mainly through the Basra oil terminal in the Persian Gulf; the northern pipeline to Ceyhan, Turkey, has been repeatedly disrupted, and its closure in 2023 halted Kurdistan Region exports. Oil dependence — over 90% of government revenue — leaves the Iraqi budget highly exposed to price swings, and associated gas is still widely flared.
