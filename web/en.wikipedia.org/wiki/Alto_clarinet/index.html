Alto clarinet

Classification: Wind, woodwind, single-reed
Key: E♭ (occasionally F)
Range: Written E below middle C upward
Related: Clarinet, bass clarinet, basset horn

The alto clarinet is a woodwind instrument of the clarinet family, pitched in E♭ between the soprano clarinet and the bass clarinet. It is about a third larger than the standard B♭ soprano clarinet and sounds a major third lower. The alto clarinet usually has an upturned bell and a curved or straight neck, giving it a superficial resemblance to a small saxophone.

History

Alto clarinets were developed in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, with makers such as Heinrich Grenser producing instruments in F and E♭. A claim for its invention around 1809 is associated with Iwan Müller. The instrument was intended to fill the alto voice in clarinet choirs and military bands, much as the viola does in the orchestra, but it never achieved a secure place in the symphony orchestra.

Use

Today the alto clarinet is found mainly in concert bands and clarinet choirs, where it strengthens the middle register. Its orchestral appearances are rare; its part, when written, is often reassigned to the basset horn or an additional clarinet. Several composers of band music, and clarinet choir arrangers, value its mellow timbre, which blends the soprano clarinets with the bass clarinet section.

Construction

Like other clarinets, the alto is usually made of grenadilla or other dense wood, or of plastic in student models, with a cylindrical bore and a single-reed mouthpiece. Most instruments extend down to written E♭, giving the instrument a compass beginning at concert G♭. Because demand is low, few manufacturers offer professional alto clarinets, and the instrument's intonation has historically been criticised.
