Dog training

Dog training is a type of animal training, the application of behavior analysis which uses the environmental events of antecedents (trigger for a behavior) and consequences to modify the dog behavior, either for it to assist in specific activities or undertake particular tasks, or for it to participate effectively in contemporary domestic life. While training dogs for specific roles dates back to Roman times at least, the training of dogs to be compatible household pets developed with suburbanization in the 1950s.

A dog learns from interactions it has with its environment. This can be through classical conditioning, where it forms an association between two stimuli; non-associative learning, where its behavior is modified through habituation or sensitisation; and operant conditioning, where it forms an association between an antecedent and its consequence.

History

Dogs have been trained for work since antiquity: herding, guarding, hunting and warfare. Formalised training methods developed in the 19th and 20th centuries, including Konrad Most's work in Germany and the popularisation of clicker training following Karen Pryor's Don't Shoot the Dog! (1984). The late 20th century saw a shift away from punishment-based methods toward reward-based positive reinforcement.

Methods

Most working breeds are trained using the guidance of the four quadrants of operant conditioning: positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement, positive punishment and negative punishment. Modern professional bodies, such as the Association of Professional Dog Trainers, generally advocate reward-based methods. Common tools include treats, toys, praise, leashes, collars and, in clicker training, a conditioned reinforcer that marks the desired behaviour.

Types of training

Training may be for basic obedience (sit, stay, recall, loose-leash walking), behaviour modification (reactivity, separation anxiety), or specialised work: assistance dogs, search and rescue, detection, herding, guarding, and competitive dog sports such as agility, obedience trials and Schutzhund/IPO.
