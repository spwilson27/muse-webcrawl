Calumet Farm is a Thoroughbred breeding and training farm in Lexington, Kentucky, United States. Founded in 1924 by William Monroe Wright, heir to the Calumet Baking Powder fortune, it became the most successful racing stable in American history under his son Warren Wright.

Golden era

Under trainer Ben A. Jones and later his son Jimmy Jones, Calumet won the Kentucky Derby a record eight times between 1941 and 1968. Its champions included Whirlaway (1941) and Citation (1948), both winners of the American Triple Crown, as well as Pensive, Ponder, Hill Gail, Iron Liege, Tim Tam and Forward Pass. Calumet horses raced in the farm's famous devil's red and blue silks, and the farm led the North American earnings list for breeders and owners many times.

Decline and revival

After the death of owner Lucille Wright Markey in 1982, the farm passed to heirs and became mired in debt and litigation; its star stallion Alydar died in 1990 in circumstances that led to fraud convictions. The farm was sold at auction in 1992 to Henryk de Kwiatkowski, who restored it, and in 2012 it was purchased by Brad Kelley's Calumet Investment Group, under which it returned to breeding and racing prominence.
