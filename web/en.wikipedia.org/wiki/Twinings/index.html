Twinings is an English marketer of tea and other beverages, including coffee, hot chocolate, and malt drinks, based in Andover, Hampshire. The brand was founded in 1706 by Thomas Twining, who opened Tom's Coffee House on London's Strand; in 1717 he expanded into the adjoining premises at 216 Strand, which remains Twinings' flagship shop and is among London's oldest continuously operating businesses. The company's lion logo, adopted in 1787, is one of the world's oldest in continuous use.

History

Thomas Twining built the business selling tea — then a luxury — rather than coffee, and by the 1730s Twinings was exporting to America. His grandson Richard Twining lobbied Prime Minister William Pitt the Younger to reduce the heavy tea tax, helping bring about the Commutation Act of 1784, which slashed the duty and undercut smugglers. Twinings supplied tea to Queen Victoria and holds a royal warrant. The firm long claimed credit for creating the Earl Grey blend.

Modern era

Twinings was acquired by Associated British Foods in 1964. It sells over a hundred blends in more than 100 countries, including English Breakfast, Lady Grey (created in the 1990s), and a wide range of green and herbal infusions. Its main production facility is in Andover, with further plants abroad. Twinings also markets Ovaltine in some territories and runs an ethical sourcing programme for its supply gardens.
