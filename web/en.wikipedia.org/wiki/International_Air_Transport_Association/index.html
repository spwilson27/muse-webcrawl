The International Air Transport Association (IATA) is a trade association of the world's airlines founded in 1945. Its member airlines account for about 82–83% of total available seat kilometres of air traffic. IATA supports airline activity and helps formulate industry policy and standards.

IATA's head office is in Montreal, with executive offices in Geneva.

History

IATA was formed in Havana, Cuba, in April 1945 as the successor to the International Air Traffic Association, founded in The Hague in 1919. At its founding it had 57 members from 31 nations, mostly in Europe and North America. Early work included setting technical standards and, under the Chicago Convention framework, coordinating international fares through traffic conferences—a role that diminished with deregulation from the late 1970s onward.

Activities

  • IATA airport and airline codes — the familiar two-letter airline designators (e.g. BA, LH) and three-letter airport codes (e.g. LHR, JFK).
  • Safety — the IATA Operational Safety Audit (IOSA) is a condition of membership; IATA publishes annual safety reports.
  • Financial services — the Billing and Settlement Plan (BSP) and CASS systems process hundreds of billions of dollars annually between airlines, agents and cargo providers.
  • Standards — bar-coded and electronic ticketing, baggage rules, dangerous goods regulations, and the New Distribution Capability (NDC) XML standard.
  • Advocacy — on taxation, regulation, infrastructure charges, and sustainability, including the industry's commitment to net-zero carbon emissions by 2050.
