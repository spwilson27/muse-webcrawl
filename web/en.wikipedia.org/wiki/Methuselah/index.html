Methuselah

Methuselah (US: /məˈθuːzˌlɑː/; Hebrew: מְתוּשֶׁלַח Məṯūšélaḥ, "Man of the dart/spear", or "his death shall bring") was a biblical patriarch and a figure in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. He had the longest lifespan of all those given in the Bible, having died at the age of 969. According to the Book of Genesis, Methuselah was the son of Enoch, the father of Lamech, and the grandfather of Noah. Elsewhere in the Bible, Methuselah is mentioned in genealogies in 1 Chronicles and the Gospel of Luke.

In the Bible

Genesis 5 gives Methuselah's age at the birth of Lamech as 187 years and states that he lived a further 782 years, dying at 969. Calculations from the Masoretic chronology place his death in the year of the Flood, which has prompted much commentary on whether he perished in it. His name has become proverbial for longevity: a "Methuselah" is anything extremely old, and the name is given to a 6-litre wine bottle, to the Methuselah tree — a Great Basin bristlecone pine in California's White Mountains, roughly 4,850 years old — and to the substellar "Methuselah star" HD 140283.

Interpretations

Biblical scholars have proposed many explanations for the extraordinary ages of the Genesis patriarchs: mistranslation of months for years, symbolic or honorific numbers, dynastic rather than personal names, and the conventions of ancient king lists such as the Sumerian King List, whose rulers reign for tens of thousands of years. The Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch give slightly different figures for Methuselah's age, differences that matter for biblical chronologies of the Flood.

In other texts

Methuselah appears in the apocryphal Book of Enoch as the recipient of Enoch's revelations, and in the Book of Jubilees. In the Latter Day Saint tradition, the Book of Moses expands his story, presenting him as a prophet who remained on earth so that the line of patriarchs would not be cut off.
