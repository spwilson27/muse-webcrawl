Auxerre

Auxerre is a commune in north-central France, the prefecture (capital) of the Yonne department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region. It lies on the River Yonne, about 147 kilometres (91 miles) southeast of Paris. The town is known for its historic centre, its Gothic cathedral, and the wines produced in the surrounding countryside, including nearby Chablis.

History

Auxerre was a flourishing Gallo-Roman centre called Autissiodorum, lying on the main road from Lyon to Boulogne-sur-Mer. It became a bishop's seat in the 3rd century, and its bishop Saint Germain (Germain l'Auxerrois) gave the town lasting fame in the 5th century. In the Middle Ages it was the seat of a county that passed to the dukes of Burgundy, and then to the French crown. Auxerre is also associated with the composer and music theorist of the Notre Dame school, and later with figures such as the mathematician Joseph Fourier, who was born there in 1768.

Sights

The Cathedral of Saint-Étienne, built mainly in the 13th century, is a notable example of Burgundian Gothic architecture, famous for its stained glass and its crypt with early medieval frescoes. The Abbey of Saint-Germain preserves some of the oldest frescoes in France, dating to the 9th century. The old town contains many timber-framed houses, the 15th-century Tour de l'Horloge clock tower, and quays along the Yonne.

Sport

Auxerre is home to the football club AJ Auxerre, long guided by coach Guy Roux, which won the French league title in 1996 and became well known for developing young players.
