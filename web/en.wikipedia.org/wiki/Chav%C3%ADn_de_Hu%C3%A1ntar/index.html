Chavín de Huántar

LocationAncash Region, Peru
CultureChavín culture
Periodc. 1200–500 BCE
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1985)

Chavín de Huántar is an archaeological site in the Peruvian Andes containing ruins and artefacts constructed by the Chavín culture, a pre-Inca Andean civilisation. Located at about 3,180 metres above sea level at the confluence of the Huacheksa and Mosna rivers, it was a major ceremonial and pilgrimage centre of the ancient Andean world.

The temple complex

The site consists of massive stone temple platforms — the Old Temple and the New Temple (Castillo) — honeycombed with interior galleries. In a cruciform chamber deep in the Old Temple stands the Lanzón, a 4.5-metre granite monolith carved with a principal deity figure, still in its original position.

Significance

Chavín art and religion spread widely across the central Andes during the Early Horizon period, influencing later cultures. The site was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.

1996–97 hostage crisis

The name also became associated with "Operación Chavín de Huántar", the Peruvian military operation that ended the 1997 Japanese embassy hostage crisis in Lima.
