Blue Note Records

Blue Note Records is an American jazz record label founded in New York City in 1939 by Alfred Lion, a German-Jewish immigrant. Synonymous with hard bop and modern jazz, the label documented many of the most important recordings of the genre during the 1950s and 1960s, distinguished by Rudy Van Gelder's engineering, Francis Wolff's photography and Reid Miles's iconic sleeve designs.

History

Lion began by recording boogie-woogie and traditional jazz (Meade Lux Lewis, Albert Ammons, Sidney Bechet), then championed bebop — Thelonious Monk's early sessions for Blue Note in 1947–52 helped establish his reputation. Photographer Francis Wolff, Lion's childhood friend, joined the company in 1941 and documented sessions in black-and-white photographs that became central to the label's identity.

The classic era

From the mid-1950s Blue Note recorded the core of hard bop: Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers, Horace Silver, Clifford Brown, Sonny Rollins, John Coltrane's Blue Train (1957), Cannonball Adderley, Lee Morgan, Herbie Hancock, Freddie Hubbard, Wayne Shorter, McCoy Tyner and Ornette Coleman. Sessions were recorded by Rudy Van Gelder at his studios in Hackensack and Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, and Lion famously paid musicians for rehearsals to ensure polished performances.

Later years

Liberty Records acquired Blue Note in 1966; Lion retired in 1967 and output declined in the 1970s. EMI revived the label in 1985 with reissues and new signings, and Blue Note later broadened beyond jazz with artists such as Norah Jones, whose Come Away with Me (2002) sold tens of millions of copies. Under producer Don Was, president since 2012, the label continues to release new jazz and an extensive reissue programme, including the Tone Poet vinyl series.
