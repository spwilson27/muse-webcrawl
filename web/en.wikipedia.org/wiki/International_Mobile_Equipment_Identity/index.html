International Mobile Equipment Identity

The International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI) is a number, usually unique, to identify 3GPP and iDEN mobile phones, as well as some satellite phones. It is usually found printed inside the battery compartment of the phone but can also be displayed on-screen by dialing *#06#.

Structure

The IMEI has 15 digits: an eight-digit Type Allocation Code (TAC) identifying the model and origin, a six-digit serial number, and a final check digit computed with the Luhn algorithm. Phones with two SIM slots have two IMEI numbers.

Use

GSM networks use the IMEI to identify valid devices and can block a stolen phone from the network via the Central Equipment Identity Register, even if a different SIM card is inserted. Many countries maintain shared blacklists to discourage phone theft, although IMEI numbers can sometimes be illegally altered.

Distinction from IMSI and MSISDN

The IMEI identifies the equipment, whereas the IMSI identifies the subscriber (stored on the SIM) and the MSISDN is the telephone number. A network can log the IMEI independently of the SIM used.
