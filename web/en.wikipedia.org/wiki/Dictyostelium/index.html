Dictyostelium

Dictyostelium
Domain: Eukaryota
Phylum: Amoebozoa
Class: Dictyostelia
Genus: Dictyostelium
Type species: Dictyostelium mucoroides

Dictyostelium is a genus of single- and multi-celled eukaryotic, phagotrophic bacterivores. Though they are Protista, they are unrelated to fungi; they are commonly called slime molds or social amoebae, and are classified in the Amoebozoa.

Life cycle

In the feeding stage, individual amoebae live in soil and leaf litter, consuming bacteria. When food is exhausted, tens of thousands of starving cells stream together, responding to pulses of cyclic AMP, to form a multicellular aggregate. The aggregate transforms into a migrating slug (pseudoplasmodium) and finally into a fruiting body: a ball of spore cells held aloft on a stalk of dead cells. Spores are dispersed by the wind and germinate into new amoebae when they land in favourable conditions.

Model organism

The species Dictyostelium discoideum is a widely used model organism in molecular and developmental biology, because it is easy to culture, genetically tractable, and bridges unicellular and multicellular life. Its genome was sequenced in 2005. Research using D. discoideum has illuminated chemotaxis, cell differentiation, social evolution and altruism, phagocytosis and aspects of human disease.
