Jefferson-Hemings controversy

The Jefferson-Hemings controversy is the long-running historical dispute over whether Thomas Jefferson had a sexual relationship with his enslaved servant Sally Hemings and fathered her children. First alleged in print by James T. Callender in 1802, the claim was denied by Jefferson's family and many historians for nearly two centuries.

Key developments

Fawn Brodie's 1974 biography revived scholarly interest, and Madison Hemings's 1873 memoir provided a descendant's account. The decisive turn came in 1998, when DNA testing linked a Hemings descendant to the Jefferson male line.

Outcome

The Thomas Jefferson Foundation concluded in 2000 that Jefferson most likely fathered all six of Hemings's children, a view now held by the mainstream of historians, though a minority of scholars continues to dissent.
