Architecture for Control Networks

Architecture for Control Networks (ACN) protocol stack overview.

Architecture for Control Networks (ACN) is a suite of network protocols for controlling entertainment technology equipment, such as theatrical lighting, over Ethernet and other IP networks. It is standardised as ANSI E1.17 by the Entertainment Services and Technology Association (ESTA) and maintained by PLASA. ACN was designed as a flexible, extensible successor to device-specific control protocols, providing a common framework in which controllers and devices from different manufacturers can interoperate.

Components

The suite includes the Session Data Transport (SDT) protocol for reliable and multicast delivery, the Device Management Protocol (DMP) for getting and setting device properties, and the Device Description Language (DDL), an XML-based language through which devices describe their own properties and structure to controllers. ACN is closely associated with the streaming protocol ANSI E1.31 (sACN), which uses ACN's root layer framing to carry DMX512 data over IP networks and is widely deployed for large lighting installations.

Design goals

ACN addresses devices abstractly as collections of properties rather than as channels of control data, allowing the same framework to address lighting dimmers, moving lights, media servers, rigging motors and other show-control equipment. Because implementations are complex compared with DMX512 and sACN, full ACN adoption has been slower, but its description and discovery concepts have influenced later entertainment networking standards.
