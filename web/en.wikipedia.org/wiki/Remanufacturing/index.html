Remanufacturing

Remanufacturing is the rebuilding of a product to the specifications of the original manufactured product using a combination of reused, repaired and new parts. It involves the complete disassembly, cleaning, inspection, repair and testing of a used product (the "core") so that it performs at least as well as a new one, typically with a warranty to match.

Process and industries

Unlike simple repair or refurbishment, remanufacturing is an industrial process carried out to a standardised specification. It is well established in the automotive parts sector (engines, alternators, starter motors), as well as in aerospace, heavy machinery, photocopiers, toner cartridges and medical equipment.

Benefits

Remanufacturing conserves raw materials and energy — studies commonly cite savings of up to 80–90 per cent of the energy of new manufacture — reduces waste and emissions, and typically allows products to be sold at 40–60 per cent of the price of new equivalents. It is a key strategy within the circular economy.
