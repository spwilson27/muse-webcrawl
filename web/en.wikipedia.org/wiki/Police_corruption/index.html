Police corruption

Silhouette of a police officer

Police corruption is a form of police misconduct in which law enforcement officers improperly use their authority for personal or organisational gain. It is a specific form of corruption and can range from minor abuses to organised criminal activity.

Forms

Common forms include accepting bribes in exchange for overlooking offences; extortion; protecting illegal gambling, prostitution, or narcotics operations; theft of seized money or drugs; planting or fabricating evidence; perjury ("testilying"); and selling confidential police information. Scholars distinguish "grass-eaters", officers who passively accept small gratuities, from "meat-eaters", who aggressively solicit major bribes — terminology popularised by the Knapp Commission, which investigated corruption in the New York Police Department in the 1970s. Later inquiries, such as the Mollen Commission of the 1990s, examined drug-related corruption.

Causes and countermeasures

Contributing factors identified by researchers include low pay, wide discretionary power, close contact with criminals, organisational culture, and weak oversight. Countermeasures include internal affairs units, civilian review boards, integrity testing, body-worn cameras, rotation of assignments, and independent commissions of inquiry. Police corruption undermines public trust and the rule of law, and its prevalence varies greatly between countries and forces.
