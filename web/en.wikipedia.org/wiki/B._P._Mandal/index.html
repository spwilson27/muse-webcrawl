Bindheshwari Prasad Mandal (1918–1982) was an Indian politician from Bihar, best known as chairman of the Second Backward Classes Commission (1979), universally called the Mandal Commission, whose 1980 report reshaped India's politics of caste and reservation.

Political career

Mandal was born into a landowning Yadav family in Murho village, Madhepura district. He served as a member of the Bihar Legislative Assembly and of the Lok Sabha, and in 1968 became Chief Minister of Bihar for a brief period (about a month), heading a coalition government. He was associated over his career with the Congress, the Shoshit Samaj Dal and later the Janata Party.

Mandal Commission

In 1979 the Janata government of Morarji Desai appointed Mandal to chair the commission charged with identifying the socially and educationally backward classes of India. Its report, submitted in December 1980, identified 3,743 castes as Other Backward Classes (OBCs), estimated at 52% of the population, and recommended 27% reservation for OBCs in central government jobs and public-sector undertakings, in addition to the existing 22.5% for Scheduled Castes and Tribes. Shelved for a decade, the report was implemented in 1990 by Prime Minister V. P. Singh, triggering nationwide protests, the anti-Mandal agitation and a lasting realignment of Indian politics often called the "Mandal revolution". The Supreme Court upheld OBC reservation in Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992), subject to a 50% ceiling and exclusion of the "creamy layer".
