"Mrs. Robinson" is a song by American music duo Simon & Garfunkel from their fourth studio album, Bookends (1968). Written by Paul Simon, the song was famously featured in the 1967 film The Graduate, whose title character refers to the seductive older woman played by Anne Bancroft. The single, released in April 1968, became the duo's second chart-topper on the Billboard Hot 100 and won the Grammy Award for Record of the Year in 1969.

Background

Director Mike Nichols, a fan of Simon & Garfunkel, commissioned songs for The Graduate; while editing, he used their existing tracks as placeholders. Simon worked on a song then called "Mrs. Roosevelt" — an elegy for a fading America that name-checked Eleanor Roosevelt and baseball star Joe DiMaggio — and Nichols persuaded him to adapt it as "Mrs. Robinson," although only fragments appeared in the film itself. The completed version was recorded for Bookends. The famous "coo-coo-ca-choo" refrain and the line "Where have you gone, Joe DiMaggio?" lament the loss of heroes in American life; DiMaggio was reportedly puzzled or displeased at first, since he had not "gone" anywhere, but warmed to the song after Simon explained its meaning.

Legacy

The song is a staple of oldies radio and one of Simon & Garfunkel's signature recordings. It has been covered by many artists, including Frank Sinatra (who altered the lyrics), the Lemonheads, and Bon Jovi. Mickey Mantle, a friend of Simon, joked about not making the lyric; Simon later sang the song at DiMaggio's memorial events and performed it at Yankee Stadium in 1999 in DiMaggio's honour.
