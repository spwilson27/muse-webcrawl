Naval Group is a French industrial group specialised in naval defence design, development and construction. Until 2017 it was known as DCNS (previously DCN, Direction des Constructions Navales). The French state is the majority shareholder, with Thales holding a significant stake. The company employs roughly 16,000 people and is Europe's largest naval shipbuilder.

History

Naval Group traces its origins to the state naval dockyards founded under Cardinal Richelieu in 1631 and developed over centuries at Brest, Toulon, Cherbourg and Lorient. The arsenal system was reorganised as DCN in the 20th century; in 2003 DCN became a state-owned company, and in 2007 it took over Thales's naval activities in France, becoming DCNS. The shorter name Naval Group was adopted in 2017 to support export growth.

Products

The group's products include France's nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines of the Triomphant class and their SNLE 3G successors, the Barracuda-class nuclear attack submarines, the aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle and the planned PANG carrier, FREMM and FDI frigates, Gowind corvettes, and the conventionally powered Scorpène-class submarines exported to several navies. It also provides through-life support and naval services.

International affairs

Naval Group won the Australian SEA 1000 competition in 2016 to build twelve Attack-class submarines, but the programme was cancelled in 2021 when Australia announced the AUKUS partnership — a loss that caused a diplomatic rift between France and Australia. The company continues major export programmes in India, Egypt, Greece, Belgium and the Netherlands, among others.
