Settee (sail)

The settee is a lateen sail with the front (leading) lower corner cut off, giving the sail a four-sided shape that combines features of the triangular lateen and the quadrilateral lug sail. On a settee, the short head of the sail is laced to a long yard that crosses the mast at an angle, but the yard stops short of extending far forward of the mast at deck level, so the sail has a short luff rather than the lateen's long forward-pointing tack.

Use

The settee rig was widely used in the Mediterranean, the Red Sea and the western Indian Ocean on craft such as the sambuk and other dhows, and on Mediterranean xebecs and settee-rigged coasting vessels. Compared with the true lateen, the settee is easier to handle when tacking because the yard does not need to be hauled around the mast to the same extent, and it avoids the lateen's problem of the yard pressing against the mast on one tack ("bad tack"), at the cost of some aerodynamic efficiency.
