James "Midge" Ure OBE (born 10 October 1953) is a Scottish musician, singer-songwriter and record producer. He first achieved commercial success in the 1970s with the teen pop band Slik and the punk/new wave group Rich Kids, before co-founding the electronic project Visage and joining Ultravox as frontman in 1979, steering the band to international success with songs such as "Vienna" (1981). With Bob Geldof he co-wrote the 1984 charity single "Do They Know It's Christmas?" and co-organised Band Aid, Live Aid and Live 8.

Early career

Ure grew up in Cambuslang near Glasgow. After working as an engineer, he joined the band Salvation, which became Slik; the group scored a UK number one in 1976 with "Forever and Ever". Following Slik's decline he formed Rich Kids with former Sex Pistol Glen Matlock. In 1978 he briefly played guitar with Thin Lizzy on tour, and in 1979 co-founded Visage with Steve Strange and Rusty Egan, scoring a hit with "Fade to Grey".

Ultravox

Ure replaced John Foxx as Ultravox's lead singer and guitarist in 1979. The band's album Vienna (1980) and its title track, which reached No. 2 in the UK in 1981 and later topped an Official Charts poll of the nation's favourite number-two singles, defined the British synth-pop era. Further successes included Rage in Eden (1981), Quartet (1982) and the single "Dancing with Tears in My Eyes" (1984). Ure also had a solo UK number one with "If I Was" in 1985.

Band Aid and charity work

Prompted by television reports of the Ethiopian famine, Ure and Bob Geldof wrote "Do They Know It's Christmas?" in 1984; recorded by the supergroup Band Aid, it became one of the best-selling singles of all time and led to the Live Aid concerts of 13 July 1985, which Ure helped organise. He was appointed OBE for services to music and charity.
