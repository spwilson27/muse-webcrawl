Winemaking

Winemaking (also wine making or vinification) is the production of wine, starting with the selection of fruit, its fermentation into alcohol, and the bottling of the finished liquid. The history of wine-making stretches over thousands of years. The science of wine and winemaking is known as oenology. A winemaker may also be called a vintner.

Process

After harvest, grapes are crushed — traditionally by foot, now usually by mechanical crusher-destemmers — to release the juice (must). For white wines the juice is generally separated from the skins before fermentation; for reds, the must ferments in contact with the skins, extracting colour, tannin and flavour. Yeasts, either ambient or cultured strains of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, convert sugars into ethanol and carbon dioxide. A secondary malolactic fermentation, by lactic acid bacteria, softens acidity in most red wines and some whites.

Following fermentation the wine is clarified by settling, racking, fining and filtration, then matured in stainless steel tanks or oak barrels, which can add flavours of vanilla, spice and toast. Finally the wine is bottled, sometimes with additional bottle aging before release. Sparkling wines undergo a second fermentation in the bottle (the traditional method) or in a tank (the Charmat method).

Factors affecting the wine

Grape variety, climate, soil and vintage weather — collectively expressed as terroir — along with harvest timing and cellar decisions, determine the character of the finished wine. Modern winemaking also manages oxygen exposure, temperature, sulfur dioxide additions and microbial stability.
