Moduli space

In mathematics, in particular algebraic geometry, a moduli space is a geometric space (usually a scheme or an algebraic stack) whose points represent algebro-geometric objects of some fixed kind, or isomorphism classes of such objects. Such spaces frequently arise as solutions to classification problems: if one can show that a collection of interesting objects (e.g. the smooth algebraic curves of a fixed genus) can be given the structure of a geometric space, then one can parametrize such objects by introducing coordinates on the resulting space. In this context, the term "modulus" is used synonymously with "parameter"; moduli were first used in this way by Riemann in the nineteenth century.

A moduli space parametrizes isomorphism classes of geometric objects.

Moduli problems

Moduli theory grew out of the problem of classifying families of objects such as algebraic curves. Riemann observed that a smooth projective curve of genus g depends on 3g − 3 complex parameters (for g ≥ 2), the moduli of the curve. These parameters count captures the dimension of the moduli space Mg of genus-g curves, which is a central object of study.

Fine and coarse moduli spaces

A fine moduli space is one that carries a universal family over it, so that families over any base correspond exactly to maps into the moduli space. Many natural problems do not admit fine moduli spaces because the objects have nontrivial automorphisms; one then settles for a coarse moduli space, whose points are still in bijection with isomorphism classes but which does not carry a universal family. A modern remedy is to work with moduli stacks, which keep track of automorphisms and recover the fine behaviour in a generalized sense.

Examples

Important examples include the moduli spaces of elliptic curves and of smooth curves of a given genus; moduli of abelian varieties; moduli of stable vector bundles on a fixed curve or surface; moduli of stable maps, central to Gromov–Witten theory and enumerative geometry; and character varieties in representation theory. Hilbert schemes and Quot schemes are universal constructions from which many moduli spaces are obtained as quotients by group actions, via geometric invariant theory.
