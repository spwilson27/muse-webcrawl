Saw Lu (Burmese: စောလူး) was king of the Pagan dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from about 1077/78 to about 1084. He was the son and successor of Anawrahta, the founder of the Pagan Empire.

Reign

According to the Burmese chronicles, Saw Lu was an ineffectual ruler compared with his father. He entrusted much of the administration to his foster brother and childhood companion, Nga Yamankan, a Mon official from Thaton. The chronicles describe Saw Lu as preferring leisure at court while Yamankan consolidated power.

Rebellion and death

Yamankan eventually rebelled and seized the king. Saw Lu was rescued briefly by his half-brother Kyansittha, the celebrated general of Anawrahta's armies, but declined to escape when offered the chance, and was killed by Yamankan's forces around 1084. Kyansittha defeated the rebellion and succeeded to the throne, becoming one of the most celebrated kings of the dynasty.

Historiography

Modern historians caution that the chronicle portrait of Saw Lu as a weak king may be exaggerated, in part because inscriptions suggest administrative continuity with Anawrahta's reign. His reign length in the chronicles also varies between sources.
