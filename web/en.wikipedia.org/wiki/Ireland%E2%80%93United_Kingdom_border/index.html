Ireland–United Kingdom border

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The border between Ireland and Northern Ireland at Killeen

The Ireland–United Kingdom border, also known as the Irish border, is the international border between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland (part of the United Kingdom) on the island of Ireland. It runs for 499 km (310 miles) from Lough Foyle in the northwest to Carlingford Lough in the northeast, separating the Irish counties of Donegal, Leitrim, Cavan, Monaghan and Louth from the Northern Irish counties of Derry/Londonderry, Tyrone, Fermanagh, Armagh and Down.

History

The border was created by the Government of Ireland Act 1920, which partitioned Ireland, and became an international frontier when the Irish Free State left the United Kingdom in 1922. During the Troubles it was heavily militarised, with customs posts and military checkpoints on major crossings; these were dismantled following the 1998 Good Friday Agreement.

After Brexit

Both states were long members of the European Union and its single market, making the border effectively invisible. The United Kingdom's withdrawal from the EU created the risk of a "hard border"; the Northern Ireland Protocol (later the Windsor Framework) avoided customs checks on the island by keeping Northern Ireland aligned with EU goods rules, introducing checks in the Irish Sea instead.
