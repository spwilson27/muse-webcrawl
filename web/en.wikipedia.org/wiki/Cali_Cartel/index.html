Cali Cartel

The Cali Cartel was based in Cali and the Valle del Cauca department of southern Colombia.

The Cali Cartel (Spanish: Cartel de Cali) was a drug cartel based in southern Colombia, around the city of Cali and the Valle del Cauca Department. Its founders were the brothers Gilberto and Miguel Rodríguez Orejuela, José Santacruz Londoño, and Hélmer Herrera. At its height in the early to mid-1990s, the Cali Cartel was estimated to control some 80 percent of the world's cocaine market and was, after the death of Pablo Escobar in 1993, the most powerful drug trafficking organisation in the world.

Operations

In contrast to the violent, flamboyant style of the rival Medellín Cartel, the Cali leaders cultivated an image of legitimate businessmen, investing in pharmacies, radio stations and other legal enterprises, and preferring bribery and infiltration of political and business elites to open warfare. The cartel ran sophisticated intelligence networks, including surveillance of officials and rival traffickers, and pioneered methods such as concealing cocaine in concrete posts, frozen vegetables and other export cargo.

Downfall

In the summer of 1995, Colombian police captured six of the cartel's seven top leaders, including Gilberto Rodríguez Orejuela (June 1995) and Miguel Rodríguez Orejuela (August 1995). José Santacruz Londoño escaped custody in 1996 but was killed by rival traffickers the same year; Hélmer Herrera was murdered in prison in 1998. The Rodríguez Orejuela brothers were later extradited to the United States (2004–2005) and pleaded guilty in 2006 to cocaine trafficking charges. With its leadership removed, the cartel fragmented, and its territories were contested by successor groups such as the Norte del Valle Cartel.
