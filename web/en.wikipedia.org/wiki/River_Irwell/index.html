The River Irwell is a tributary of the River Mersey in north-west England. It rises at Irwell Springs on Deerplay Moor, near Bacup in Lancashire, and flows 39 miles (63 km) southwards through Rawtenstall, Ramsbottom, Bury, Radcliffe, and the cities of Salford and Manchester, before joining the Mersey near Irlam.

Course

From its source high on the Rossendale moors, the Irwell descends through the Irwell Valley, once lined with textile mills, quarrying and mining. At Bury it is joined by the River Roch; at Radcliffe by the River Croal. Through Salford and Manchester the river forms the historic boundary between the two cities, passing the University of Manchester, Castlefield, and Salford Quays' headwaters before meeting the Manchester Ship Canal near its junction with the Mersey.

History

During the Industrial Revolution the Irwell became one of the most polluted rivers in Britain, receiving effluent from hundreds of mills, dye works, and chemical plants, and was described as little more than an open sewer. The Mersey and Irwell Navigation, and later the Manchester Ship Canal (1894), used parts of its course for navigation. From the 1970s onwards, water quality improved dramatically following investment in sewage treatment, and salmon were recorded in the river in the 21st century for the first time in over a century.

Flooding

The lower Irwell flooded Salford severely on several occasions, including in 1866, 1946, 1954, and on Boxing Day 2015. Flood alleviation works in Salford, including a storage basin at Castle Irwell, have since been completed.
