Politics of Georgia

Polity type Unitary semi-presidential republic (parliamentary republic since 2018 constitutional changes)
Constitution Constitution of Georgia (1995, as amended)
Legislature Parliament of Georgia (unicameral, 150 members)
Capital Tbilisi (legislative seat Kutaisi 2012–2019)

The politics of Georgia take place in a framework of a semi-presidential (since the 2017–2018 constitutional amendments, effectively parliamentary) representative democratic republic, whereby the Prime Minister is the head of government and the President is the largely ceremonial head of state, elected since 2024 by an electoral college rather than by direct popular vote. Executive power is exercised by the government; legislative power is vested in the Parliament of Georgia. The judiciary is formally independent.

Independence and early years

Georgia declared independence from the Soviet Union on 9 April 1991. Its first president, Zviad Gamsakhurdia, was ousted in a violent coup in early 1992 and succeeded by former Soviet foreign minister Eduard Shevardnadze. The early 1990s were marked by civil war and separatist conflicts in Abkhazia and South Ossetia, whose de facto separation — supported by Russia — remains unresolved and is the central issue of Georgia's foreign and security policy.

Rose Revolution and after

The bloodless Rose Revolution of November 2003 forced Shevardnadze's resignation after disputed parliamentary elections, bringing Mikheil Saakashvili and the United National Movement to power on a program of anti-corruption, market reform, and integration with NATO and the European Union. In August 2008 Georgia fought and lost a five-day war with Russia over South Ossetia and Abkhazia, after which Russia recognized both territories as independent states.

In the 2012 parliamentary election the Georgian Dream coalition, led by billionaire Bidzina Ivanishvili, defeated Saakashvili's party in the country's first peaceful electoral transfer of power. Georgian Dream has remained in government since, while relations with the West and with Russia, the pace of democratic reform, and Georgia's EU candidacy (granted in December 2023) have remained recurring points of domestic and international contention, including large protests over "foreign agents" legislation in 2023 and 2024 and disputed parliamentary elections in October 2024.

Institutions

The Parliament of Georgia is a unicameral body of 150 members, elected under a fully proportional system since 2024. The Government is headed by the Prime Minister, nominated by the majority. Georgia is divided administratively into regions (mkhare), municipalities, and the autonomous republics of Adjara and Abkhazia. The Constitutional Court sits in Batumi.
