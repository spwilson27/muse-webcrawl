The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) is a component of the United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) responsible for cybersecurity and infrastructure protection across all levels of government, and for coordinating cybersecurity programs with states and the private sector. It is the successor to the National Protection and Programs Directorate.

CISA was established when President Donald Trump signed the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency Act of 2018, and Christopher Krebs, who had led the predecessor directorate, became its first director. Jen Easterly served as director from 2021 to 2025.

Mission

The agency defends federal civilian networks, issues alerts and binding operational directives on vulnerabilities, coordinates election security, assesses risk to the nation's critical infrastructure sectors, and runs public awareness efforts such as the "Shields Up" campaign during heightened threat periods. Its Joint Cyber Defense Collaborative brings together government and major technology and security companies for operational planning against cyber threats.
