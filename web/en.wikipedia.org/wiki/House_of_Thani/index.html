House of Thani

The House of Thani (Arabic: آل ثاني, Āl Thānī) is the ruling family of Qatar. The family descends from the Banu Tamim tribe and has governed the Qatari peninsula since the mid-19th century, when Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani emerged as its ruler.

History

Mohammed bin Thani (c. 1788–1878) moved his family to Doha and became the first ruler of Qatar in the modern sense. His son Jassim bin Mohammed Al Thani is regarded as the founder of the State of Qatar, having unified its tribes and defended Qatari autonomy against Ottoman and Bahraini influence, notably at the Battle of Al Wajbah in 1893.

Modern rule

Qatar became a British protectorate in 1916 and gained independence in 1971 under Sheikh Ahmad bin Ali Al Thani. Subsequent emirs include Khalifa bin Hamad Al Thani, Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, who modernised the country and founded Al Jazeera, and the current emir, Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, who succeeded on his father's abdication in 2013.

The family remains central to Qatari public life, holding the offices of emir, prime minister, and key ministerial and sovereign-wealth posts.
