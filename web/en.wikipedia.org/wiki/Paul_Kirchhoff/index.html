Paul Kirchhoff

Paul Kirchhoff (17 August 1900 – 9 December 1972) was a German-Mexican anthropologist known for coining the term "Mesoamerica" and defining it as a cultural area.

Born17 August 1900, Hörste, Germany
Died9 December 1972, Mexico City, Mexico
Known forDefining Mesoamerica

Career

Kirchhoff studied ethnology in Germany and emigrated to Mexico in 1936 after the rise of Nazism, becoming a Mexican citizen. He taught at the National School of Anthropology and History and helped found Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH).

Mesoamerica

In his 1943 paper "Mesoamérica: sus límites geográficos, composición étnica y caracteres culturales", Kirchhoff defined Mesoamerica as a region whose peoples shared traits such as maize agriculture, the ritual ballgame, stepped pyramids, a 260-day calendar and human sacrifice — a framework still fundamental to the archaeology of Mexico and Central America.
