The National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) is a semi-autonomous agency within the United States Department of Energy. Established by Congress in 2000, it is responsible for the military application of nuclear science: maintaining the safety, security, and effectiveness of the U.S. nuclear weapons stockpile without explosive testing; supporting naval nuclear propulsion; and reducing the global danger from weapons of mass destruction.

History

The NNSA was created in response to security scandals at the national laboratories in the late 1990s, including the Wen Ho Lee espionage investigation at Los Alamos, which exposed serious weaknesses in the Department of Energy's security management. Congress established the agency as a separately organised unit within the department, with its own administrator — the Under Secretary of Energy for Nuclear Security — to give nuclear security a clearer chain of command.

Missions

  • Stockpile stewardship — certifying the reliability of nuclear weapons through science-based simulation and experiments rather than underground testing, and running life-extension programmes for warheads.
  • Naval reactors — providing nuclear propulsion plants for the U.S. Navy's aircraft carriers and submarines.
  • Nonproliferation — securing vulnerable nuclear materials worldwide, converting research reactors from highly enriched uranium fuel, and supporting international safeguards.
  • Emergency response — operating specialised teams that respond to nuclear and radiological incidents.

The nuclear security enterprise

The NNSA oversees a complex of laboratories and production sites, including Los Alamos National Laboratory, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Sandia National Laboratories, the Pantex Plant in Texas, the Y-12 National Security Complex in Tennessee, the Savannah River Site, the Nevada National Security Site, and the Kansas City National Security Campus. Most day-to-day work is performed by management-and-operating contractors.
