Michael I Rangabe (Greek: Μιχαὴλ Ῥαγγαβέ; c. 770 – 11 January 844) was Byzantine emperor from 811 to 813. Son-in-law of Emperor Nikephoros I, he came to power after Nikephoros was killed and his son Staurakios wounded at the catastrophic Battle of Pliska against the Bulgars under Khan Krum.

Rise to power

Michael was a courtier of aristocratic family who had served as kouropalates (master of the palace) under Nikephoros I, whose daughter Prokopia he married. After the disaster of Pliska in July 811, the wounded Staurakios abdicated, and, with the support of the Patriarch Nikephoros and the court, Michael was acclaimed emperor on 2 October 811.

Reign

Michael reversed his father-in-law's austere fiscal policies, distributing largesse and reconciling the Church — he was notably devout and generous to monasteries, and leaned toward the iconophile party, recalling the exiled monk Theodore the Studite. Against Krum he achieved little: the Bulgars seized Mesembria in 812 and defeated the Byzantines at the Battle of Versinikia on 22 June 813. Michael's hesitation at the battle — troops fled, and the emperor abandoned the field — destroyed his prestige.

Deposition and death

Leo the Armenian, a general, was proclaimed emperor by the army; Michael abdicated on 11 July 813, was tonsured a monk under the name Athanasios, and retired with his family to the island of Prote in the Sea of Marmara, where he lived for over thirty years, dying in 844. His son Theophylaktos, co-emperor during the reign, was castrated and exiled; another son, Niketas, later became Patriarch Ignatios of Constantinople.
