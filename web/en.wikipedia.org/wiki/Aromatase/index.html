Aromatase
EC number1.14.14.14
GeneCYP19A1 (chromosome 15)
Protein familyCytochrome P450
ReactionAndrogens → estrogens (aromatisation)

Aromatase, also called estrogen synthetase or estrogen synthase, is an enzyme responsible for a key step in the biosynthesis of estrogens. It is a member of the cytochrome P450 superfamily (EC 1.14.14.14), encoded in humans by the CYP19A1 gene. Aromatase catalyses the aromatisation of androgens — converting androstenedione to estrone and testosterone to estradiol — the final and rate-limiting step in estrogen production.

Function and distribution

The enzyme is located in the endoplasmic reticulum of estrogen-producing cells. It is expressed in the gonads (ovarian granulosa cells and testicular Leydig cells), placenta, brain, adipose tissue, bone, skin and blood vessels. In postmenopausal women, aromatisation in adipose tissue becomes the principal source of circulating estrogen. Aromatase activity in the brain also plays a role in sexual differentiation and behaviour in many vertebrates.

Clinical significance

Excess aromatase activity is implicated in estrogen-dependent conditions such as some breast and endometrial cancers, endometriosis and gynecomastia. Conversely, aromatase deficiency — a rare genetic disorder of CYP19A1 — causes virilisation of female infants and mothers during pregnancy and, in males, tall stature with unfused epiphyses, osteoporosis and metabolic abnormalities, showing that estrogens are essential for skeletal maturation in both sexes.

Aromatase inhibitors

Aromatase inhibitors such as anastrozole, letrozole and exemestane block estrogen synthesis and are mainstays of treatment for hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women. They are also used off-label and illicitly in bodybuilding to counter estrogenic effects of anabolic steroids.
