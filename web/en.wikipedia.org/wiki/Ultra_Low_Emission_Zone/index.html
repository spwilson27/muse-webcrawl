Ultra Low Emission Zone

The Ultra Low Emission Zone (ULEZ) is an area in London, England, where an emissions standard based charge is applied to drivers of the most polluting vehicles. Plans were announced by the Mayor of London, Boris Johnson, in 2015; the scheme was implemented by his successor, Sadiq Khan, on 8 April 2019 in central London, replacing the earlier T-Charge. It was expanded to the inner London area within the North and South Circular roads in October 2021, and to the whole of Greater London on 29 August 2023.

Location: Greater London
Launched: 8 April 2019
Charge: £12.50/day for non-compliant cars, vans and motorcycles
Operator: Transport for London

Standards and operation

To drive within the zone without charge, petrol vehicles must generally meet Euro 4 standards, diesel vehicles Euro 6, and motorcycles Euro 3. Non-compliant vehicles pay a daily charge (with heavier vehicles covered by the direct-vision and LEZ schemes); enforcement uses automatic number-plate recognition cameras. Unlike the Congestion Charge, ULEZ operates 24 hours a day, every day except Christmas Day.

Impact and controversy

Transport for London reported rapid improvements: roadside nitrogen dioxide concentrations in central London fell markedly after 2019, and the proportion of compliant vehicles rose above 95% following the 2023 expansion. The London-wide expansion was nonetheless politically contentious — challenged in the courts (a legal challenge by five councils failed in July 2023) and blamed as a factor in the narrow Conservative hold of the Uxbridge and South Ruislip by-election in July 2023. Scrappage schemes were funded to help residents and businesses replace non-compliant vehicles. Analogous clean air zones have since been introduced in other UK cities.
