Hotline Miami 2: Wrong Number is a top-down shooter video game developed by Dennaton Games and published by Devolver Digital. Released on March 10, 2015, it is the sequel to Hotline Miami (2012) and serves as both a prequel and a continuation of its story.

Gameplay

Like its predecessor, the game presents brutal, fast-paced combat from a top-down perspective: the player clears rooms of enemies with melee weapons and firearms, dying from a single hit and restarting instantly. Animal masks granting special abilities return, though many chapters restrict the player to fixed characters — including a group of copycat vigilantes called the Fans, a mobster's son, a detective, and a soldier in a fictional 1985 war in Hawaii.

Plot

The narrative unfolds non-linearly across 1985–1991, exploring the aftermath of Jacket's rampage in the first game, the organisation "50 Blessings", and an escalating conflict between the United States and Russia that culminates in a nuclear strike on Miami.

Reception

Reviews praised the game's synthwave soundtrack — featuring artists such as Perturbator and Carpenter Brut — stylish presentation, and expanded story, while finding its larger levels and difficulty spikes less satisfying than the original's. The game was refused classification in Australia because of a controversial scene in its opening, a decision that drew wide attention.
