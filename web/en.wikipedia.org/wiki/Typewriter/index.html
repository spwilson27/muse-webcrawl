Typewriter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A typewriter is a mechanical or electromechanical machine for typing characters. Typically, a typewriter has an array of keys, and each one causes a different single character to be produced on paper by striking an inked ribbon selectively against the paper with a type element.

History

Numerous inventors developed typing machines in the 19th century. The first commercially successful typewriter was patented by Christopher Latham Sholes and marketed by E. Remington and Sons from 1873. Sholes and his collaborators devised the QWERTY keyboard layout, which remains standard today. By the early 20th century, manufacturers such as Underwood, Royal, Olivetti and Smith Corona dominated the market.

Design

Standard features include a keyboard, a platen (roller) that holds the paper, a movable carriage, a ribbon mechanism, and a shift key allowing upper- and lower-case letters. Later electric typewriters, notably the IBM Selectric (1961), replaced typebars with a rotating "golf ball" element.

Legacy

Typewriters transformed office work, journalism and literature, and created new employment for typists. From the 1980s they were displaced by word processors and personal computers, though they retain a niche following among writers and collectors.
