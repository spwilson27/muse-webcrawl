Broadhead

A broadhead is a large cutting point assembly mounted on an arrow shaft for hunting and warfare. Unlike narrow field points used for target practice, broadheads are designed with two or more sharp blades that cut a wide wound channel, causing rapid blood loss in game animals. In modern bowhunting, the choice and legality of broadheads are regulated in many jurisdictions.

History

Broad cutting arrowheads are among the oldest projectile points: leaf-shaped and barbed heads of stone, bronze and iron were used across Europe, Asia and the Americas. In medieval England, the broadhead was a military arrowhead with wide cutting edges effective against unarmoured men and horses, contrasted with the narrow bodkin point intended to pierce mail and plate armour. Japanese yajiri and Chinese broadheads similarly developed elaborate blade shapes.

Modern types

  • Fixed-blade broadheads: one-piece or replaceable-blade heads, typically with two, three or four blades, which must be tuned to fly accurately.
  • Mechanical (expandable) broadheads: blades fold against the ferrule in flight and deploy on impact, allowing a large cutting diameter with field-point-like aerodynamics.
  • Cut-on-contact heads: the tip itself is part of the cutting edge, favoured for low-poundage and traditional bows.

Typical weights range from 75 to 200 grains (about 5–13 grams), with 100 and 125 grains most common. Many regions require a minimum cutting diameter and prohibit barbed designs so that a head can be withdrawn from an animal if necessary.
