Generic term

A generic term is a word or name that is the common name for a class of goods or services. In trademark law, a term that becomes generic for the product itself loses trademark protection — a process called genericide.

Examples

Former trademarks that became generic include aspirin, escalator, thermos, heroin and zipper. Other marks such as Kleenex, Band-Aid and Hoover are often used generically in everyday speech but remain legally protected trademarks whose owners actively police usage to avoid genericide.
