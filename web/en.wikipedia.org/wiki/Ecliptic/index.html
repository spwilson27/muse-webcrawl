Ecliptic

The ecliptic or ecliptic plane is the orbital plane of Earth around the Sun. From the perspective of an observer on Earth, it is the apparent path of the Sun across the celestial sphere over the course of a year.

Ecliptic
Plane: Earth's orbit around Sun
Inclination to celestial equator: ~23.4°
Constellations: Zodiac

Sun's apparent motion

Because Earth takes one year to orbit the Sun, the Sun appears to move along the ecliptic against the background stars, passing through the twelve constellations of the zodiac. Eclipses of the Sun and Moon occur only when the Moon is near the ecliptic, hence the name.

Relation to celestial equator

The ecliptic is inclined at about 23.44 degrees to the celestial equator, due to Earth's axial tilt. The two intersections are the equinoxes — vernal (March) and autumnal (September) — while the extreme points are the solstices.

Ecliptic coordinates

Astronomers use ecliptic longitude and latitude to locate Solar System objects, since planets orbit roughly in this plane. The obliquity varies slowly over tens of thousands of years between about 22.1° and 24.5°.

Planets and zodiac

All major planets lie within a few degrees of the ecliptic. Astrology historically divided it into twelve signs of 30° each, starting at the vernal equinox.
