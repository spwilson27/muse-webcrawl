Heilongjiang


Heilongjiang Province
Capital: Harbin
Area: approx. 454,800 km²
Population: approx. 31.9 million (2020)

Heilongjiang (Chinese: 黑龙江; pinyin: Hēilóngjiāng) is a province in northeast China. It is the northernmost and easternmost province of the country and contains China's northernmost point (in Mohe City) and easternmost point (at the junction of the Amur and Ussuri rivers). The provincial capital and largest city is Harbin. The province's name derives from the Chinese name for the Amur River, the "Black Dragon River".

Geography

Heilongjiang borders Jilin to the south and Inner Mongolia to the west; it also borders Russia (Amur Oblast, Jewish Autonomous Oblast, Khabarovsk Krai, Primorsky Krai and Zabaykalsky Krai) to the north and east. The Amur River marks much of the border with Russia. The province includes the Greater Khingan and Lesser Khingan mountain ranges and the Songnen and Sanjiang plains. It has a harsh subarctic and humid continental climate with long, severely cold winters.

History

The region was historically inhabited by peoples such as the Sushen, Mohe, and Jurchen, the latter ancestors of the Manchus who founded the Qing dynasty. After the Treaty of Aigun (1858) and the Convention of Peking (1860), the Qing ceded lands north of the Amur and east of the Ussuri to the Russian Empire. In the early 20th century the Chinese Eastern Railway, centred on Harbin, brought large-scale settlement; from 1932 the province formed part of the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo. Heilongjiang became part of the People's Republic of China in 1949.

Economy

Heilongjiang is a major agricultural region, producing soybeans, maize, wheat and other grains on its fertile black soils. The Daqing oil field, discovered in 1959, long made the province a centre of China's petroleum industry; coal mining, forestry and machinery manufacturing are also important.

Administrative divisions

The province administers 12 prefecture-level cities and the Daxing'anling Prefecture, including Harbin, Qiqihar, Daqing, Mudanjiang, Jiamusi and Mohe.
