Carlos Humberto Santana Barragán (born 20 July 1947) is a Mexican and American guitarist who rose to fame in the late 1960s and early 1970s with his band Santana, which pioneered a fusion of rock and roll with Latin American jazz, Afro-Cuban rhythms, and blues. His melodic, sustain-rich guitar tone is among the most recognizable in rock music. Rolling Stone has repeatedly ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time.

Early life and breakthrough

Santana was born in Autlán, Mexico, where his father was a mariachi violinist. The family moved to Tijuana and then to San Francisco, where Carlos formed the Santana Blues Band in 1966. Managed by Bill Graham, the band played a celebrated set at the Woodstock festival in August 1969—before their debut album was even released—which propelled them to stardom.

Career

The albums Santana (1969), Abraxas (1970), and Santana III (1971) produced classics including "Evil Ways," "Black Magic Woman," "Oye Cómo Va," and "Samba Pa Ti." In the 1970s Santana embraced jazz fusion and the spiritual teachings of guru Sri Chinmoy, recording albums such as Caravanserai (1972) and collaborating with John McLaughlin and Alice Coltrane. After a period of declining commercial fortunes, he returned spectacularly with Supernatural (1999), a collaboration with younger artists including Rob Thomas ("Smooth"), which won nine Grammy Awards, including Album of the Year, and became one of the best-selling albums worldwide. Santana was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1998.
