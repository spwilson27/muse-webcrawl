Rosoboronexport

Founded4 November 2000
HeadquartersMoscow, Russia
TypeState-owned enterprise
ParentRostec
RoleSole state intermediary for Russian arms exports and imports

Rosoboronexport (Russian: Рособоронэкспорт) is Russia's state-owned company responsible for the export and import of military and dual-use products, technologies and services. Created by decree of President Vladimir Putin on 4 November 2000 through the merger of the earlier state arms-trading firms Rosvooruzhenie and Promexport, it holds a near-monopoly position as the sole state intermediary agency for Russia's military exports. Since 2007 it has formed part of the state corporation Rostec.

Activities

Rosoboronexport markets the output of hundreds of Russian defence enterprises, including combat aircraft such as Sukhoi and MiG fighters, helicopters, air-defence systems such as the S-400, tanks and armoured vehicles, naval vessels and submarines, and Kalashnikov small arms. It negotiates contracts with foreign governments, arranges licensed production and offset deals, and provides after-sales support, spare parts and training. The company is a fixture at international air shows and arms exhibitions such as MAKS in Russia and exhibitions in the Middle East, Asia and Latin America.

Market position

Russia has historically been the world's second-largest arms exporter after the United States, and Rosoboronexport has reported an export order book valued in the tens of billions of US dollars, with major customers including India, China, Algeria, Vietnam and countries in the Middle East and Africa. India has traditionally been its largest single market, buying fighters, tanks, frigates and air-defence systems.

Sanctions

The company and its leadership have been subject to international sanctions. The United States sanctioned Rosoboronexport in the 2000s over alleged transfers to Iran, and imposed further measures under the Countering America's Adversaries Through Sanctions Act (CAATSA) of 2017. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, extensive US, EU and allied sanctions targeted the company and the wider Russian defence sector, complicating deliveries, payments and insurance for its export contracts.

Leadership

Its long-serving director general since 2007 has been Anatoly Isaykin, who previously worked in the KGB and in the predecessor export organisations. The company is headquartered in Moscow, on Stromynka Street, and maintains representative offices in dozens of countries.
