Propulsive efficiency

In aerospace engineering, propulsive efficiency is a measure of how effectively a propulsion system converts the energy it imparts to the propellant stream into useful propulsive power — the rate at which thrust does work on the vehicle. It is one factor of overall engine efficiency, alongside thermal (or cycle) efficiency, and for rockets it is related to how closely the exhaust velocity matches the vehicle speed.

Propulsive efficiency peaks when exhaust velocity is close to vehicle velocity

Definition

For a jet or rocket producing thrust F on a vehicle travelling at speed V, with exhaust velocity Ve relative to the vehicle, propulsive efficiency is ηp = 2V / (V + Ve) when the intake and exhaust pressures are matched. Propulsive efficiency reaches its theoretical maximum of 100% when Ve = V — the exhaust is left stationary in the reference frame and no kinetic energy is wasted in the jet. However, thrust vanishes in that limit, so practical designs trade efficiency against thrust per unit mass flow.

Implications

  • Propellers and high-bypass turbofans accelerate a large mass of air by a small amount, giving exhaust velocities close to flight speed and hence high propulsive efficiency at subsonic speeds — but they cannot operate in vacuum or at very high speed.
  • Pure jets and rockets accelerate a small mass to very high velocity, suiting high flight speeds at the cost of lower propulsive efficiency when the vehicle is slow.
  • A rocket starting from rest initially has zero propulsive efficiency because the vehicle gains no power while stationary; efficiency rises as the vehicle accelerates toward the exhaust velocity, a relationship captured in discussions of the Oberth effect and overall mission energy budgets.

Propulsive efficiency, thermal efficiency, and the rocket equation together explain the design choices of aircraft and launch vehicles: for example, why airliners use large high-bypass fans, and why rockets are relatively inefficient in the first moments of flight but indispensable beyond the atmosphere.
