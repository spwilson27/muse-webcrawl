Crime thriller

The crime thriller is a film, television and literary genre that combines elements of crime fiction with the suspense and tension of the thriller. Crime thrillers centre on criminal activity — murder, robbery, organized crime or investigation — but drive the narrative through escalating jeopardy, twists and the psychology of criminals, victims and investigators, rather than the puzzle-solving emphasis of the classic detective story.

Characteristics

Crime thrillers typically feature high stakes, fast pacing, morally ambiguous protagonists — such as compromised police officers, criminals planning a heist, or ordinary people drawn into violence — and plots structured around chases, deadlines and reversals. Subgenres include the police procedural thriller, the heist film, the serial-killer thriller, the legal thriller and neo-noir.

Examples

Notable crime thrillers in cinema include The French Connection (1971), Se7en (1995), Heat (1995), The Silence of the Lambs (1991), Memories of Murder (2003) and Prisoners (2013). In literature, authors such as Patricia Highsmith, Gillian Flynn and Scandinavian noir writers including Jo Nesbø and Stieg Larsson have defined the modern psychological crime thriller.
