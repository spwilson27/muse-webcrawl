Schoenflies notation

Schoenflies symbols describe the point groups of molecules and crystals

The Schoenflies notation, named after the German mathematician Arthur Moritz Schoenflies, is a notation primarily used to specify point groups in three dimensions. Point groups in three dimensions are widely used in chemistry to classify the symmetry of molecules and in crystallography; Schoenflies notation is the preferred system in spectroscopy and molecular chemistry, while the Hermann–Mauguin (international) notation dominates crystallography.

Elements of the notation

The symbol is built from letters denoting the principal symmetry structure, with subscripts giving the order of rotation axes:

SymbolMeaning
CnCyclic group: one n-fold rotation axis
CnvWith n vertical mirror planes (containing the axis)
CnhWith a horizontal mirror plane (perpendicular to the axis)
DnDihedral group: n-fold axis plus n two-fold axes perpendicular to it
Dnh, DndDihedral with horizontal mirror, or with diagonal mirrors
SnImproper rotation (rotation–reflection) group
Cs, CiOnly a mirror plane (σ), or only an inversion centre (i)
T, O, ITetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral groups
Td, Th, Oh, IhFull polyhedral groups with mirror/inversion symmetry
C∞v, D∞hPoint groups of linear molecules without / with an inversion centre

Examples

Water (bent) belongs to C2v; ammonia (trigonal pyramidal) to C3v; benzene to D6h; methane to Td; carbon dioxide to D∞h; and buckminsterfullerene (C60) to Ih, the highest finite point-group symmetry. Determining a molecule's Schoenflies symbol is the first step in applying group theory to molecular orbitals, vibrational spectroscopy (IR and Raman activity), and selection rules.

Relation to other systems

Schoenflies symbols correspond systematically to Hermann–Mauguin symbols (e.g. C2v ↔ mm2; D6h ↔ 6/mmm). They are also used, with extensions, for the 32 crystallographic point groups and, historically, in Schoenflies's own enumeration of the 230 space groups.
