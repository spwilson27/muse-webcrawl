Oldest member of the House of Commons

The oldest member of the House of Commons is the longest-serving or eldest sitting member of the lower house of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. The title is distinct from that of Father of the House, which is conferred on the member with the longest continuous service; the two may be held by different people. Where the oldest member is also the Father of the House, the roles are sometimes conflated in popular usage.

Role

The position carries no formal parliamentary powers, but by convention the oldest member — like the Father of the House — may preside over the election of a new Speaker when the Father is unavailable or is himself a candidate. Records of the oldest members extend back centuries; notable holders have included members who first entered Parliament in their youth and remained into their nineties. In the 20th century, Winston Churchill was both the oldest member and Father of the House near the end of his Commons career.

Lists

Lists of oldest members are maintained by Parliament, distinguishing between the oldest sitting member by age at any given time and the oldest ever to sit. The ages of early members are often uncertain because of incomplete birth records.
