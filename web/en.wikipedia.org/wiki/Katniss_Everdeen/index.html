Katniss Everdeen is the fictional protagonist and narrator of Suzanne Collins's The Hunger Games trilogy of novels and their film adaptations. A skilled archer and huntress from the impoverished coal-mining District 12 of the dystopian nation of Panem, she volunteers to take her younger sister's place in the Hunger Games, a televised fight to the death imposed by the Capitol.

Characterisation

Katniss is sixteen years old at the start of The Hunger Games. Since her father's death in a mine explosion, she has supported her mother and her sister Prim by illegally hunting in the woods beyond the district fence with her friend Gale Hawthorne. Collins has described Katniss as a survivalist shaped by deprivation; her name is taken from the katniss plant, an edible aquatic plant also known as arrowhead.

Role in the novels

In The Hunger Games (2008), Katniss volunteers as tribute in place of Prim, whose name is drawn at the Reaping. Together with District 12's male tribute, Peeta Mellark, she is styled as "the girl on fire" and marketed as one of a pair of star-crossed lovers. In the arena she allies with the young tribute Rue and, with Peeta, threatens a double suicide with poisonous nightlock berries, forcing the Capitol to declare them joint victors. In Catching Fire (2009) the act of defiance sparks unrest across the districts, and Katniss is thrown back into the arena for the 75th Hunger Games before being rescued by rebels. In Mockingjay (2010) she becomes the symbolic "Mockingjay" of the rebellion against President Snow, though she is increasingly traumatised by the war; she ultimately assassinates President Alma Coin, the rebel leader, recognising her own authoritarian ambitions.

Portrayal and reception

Katniss was portrayed by Jennifer Lawrence in the four film adaptations (2012–2015), a performance widely praised by critics and credited with anchoring one of the most commercially successful female-led action franchises in cinema history. The character is frequently discussed as a landmark heroine for young readers, notable for her pragmatism, emotional guardedness, and moral ambivalence rather than conventional heroic warmth.
