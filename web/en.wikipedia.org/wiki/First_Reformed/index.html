First Reformed is a 2017 American drama film written and directed by Paul Schrader. It stars Ethan Hawke as Ernst Toller, a Protestant minister of a small historic church in upstate New York who begins to question his faith as he becomes entangled with a pregnant parishioner, played by Amanda Seyfried, and her radical environmentalist husband.

Plot

Ernst Toller, a former military chaplain whose son died in the Iraq War, serves as pastor of First Reformed Church, a 250-year-old Dutch Reformed parish now operating largely as a tourist attraction. Suffering from poor health and keeping a journal of his spiritual struggles, Toller meets Mary, a pregnant parishioner whose husband Michael is a despairing environmental activist. After Michael dies by suicide, Toller increasingly takes up his cause, confronting the church's wealthy corporate sponsor over pollution and drifting toward a desperate plan of his own. The film ends ambiguously as Mary interrupts Toller's preparations on the day of the church's reconsecration ceremony.

Production

Schrader has described the film as a return to the austere, transcendental style of filmmakers such as Robert Bresson, Yasujirō Ozu and Carl Theodor Dreyer, a style he analyzed in his book Transcendental Style in Film. The film was shot in a nearly square 1.37:1 aspect ratio to heighten its sense of confinement.

Reception

The film premiered at the 74th Venice International Film Festival and was widely acclaimed; Hawke's performance and Schrader's screenplay were singled out for praise. Schrader was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, his first Oscar nomination.
