Mbombela

Mbombela, formerly known as Nelspruit, is a city in north-eastern South Africa and the capital of Mpumalanga province. It lies on the Crocodile River in the fertile De Kaap valley, about 330 kilometres east of Johannesburg and close to the southern entrance of Kruger National Park. The name Mbombela is a siSwati word meaning "a lot of people together in a small space"; it was officially adopted in 2009, replacing Nelspruit (founded in 1905 and named after the Nel brothers, local farmers).

Economy and transport

Mbombela is a commercial hub for a subtropical farming region producing citrus, mangoes, bananas, avocados, macadamia nuts and timber. It stands on the Maputo Corridor (N4 highway and railway) linking Gauteng to the port of Maputo in Mozambique, and is served by Kruger Mpumalanga International Airport. Tourism to the Kruger National Park, the Panorama Route and the Blyde River Canyon is a major industry.

2010 World Cup

The 46,000-seat Mbombela Stadium, noted for its giraffe-shaped roof supports and zebra-striped seats, was built for the 2010 FIFA World Cup and hosted four first-round matches. The city is also home to the Lowveld National Botanical Garden, set among granite outcrops at the confluence of the Crocodile and Nels rivers, and to Sudwala Caves, among the oldest known cave systems in the world.
