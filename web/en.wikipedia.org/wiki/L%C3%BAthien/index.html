Lúthien

Lúthien Tinúviel is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, an Elven princess of Doriath in the First Age of Middle-earth. Her story is told in The Silmarillion, in the long poem The Lay of Beren and Lúthien, and in the standalone volume Beren and Lúthien (2017).

Story

Lúthien, daughter of King Thingol and the Maia Melian, fell in love with the mortal man Beren. Her father demanded a Silmaril from Morgoth's crown as bride-price; together Beren and Lúthien infiltrated Angband, and Lúthien enchanted Morgoth with song and dance so Beren could cut a Silmaril from the crown. After Beren's death, Lúthien's grief moved Mandos, and she chose mortality so that she and Beren might live a second life together. She is considered the most beautiful of all the Children of Ilúvatar, and Arwen Undómiel is her descendant.
