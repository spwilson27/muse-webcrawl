Archduke Albert of Austria (13 November 1559 – 13 July 1621) was, jointly with his wife Isabella Clara Eugenia, sovereign of the Habsburg (Spanish) Netherlands from 1598 until his death, and one of the most capable Habsburg commanders and governors of his generation. A younger son of Emperor Maximilian II, he was the brother of Emperors Rudolf II and Matthias.

Early life and church career

Raised at the court of his uncle Philip II of Spain, Albert was originally destined for the Church: he became a cardinal in 1577, at eighteen, and served as Viceroy of Portugal (1583–93) and Archbishop of Toledo (1594). Released from his vows, he was appointed Governor-General of the Spanish Netherlands in 1595.

Sovereign of the Netherlands

In 1598 Philip II ceded the Netherlands to his daughter Isabella and Albert, whom she married in 1599, on condition that the provinces would revert to Spain if the couple had no surviving children. As commander, Albert captured Calais and Hulst but suffered a severe defeat at the Battle of Nieuwpoort (1600) at the hands of Maurice of Nassau. After Ambrogio Spinola's campaigns recovered momentum, Albert and Isabella negotiated the Twelve Years' Truce with the Dutch Republic in 1609 — a de facto recognition of Dutch independence.

Their court at Brussels was a brilliant centre of Counter-Reformation culture, patronising Peter Paul Rubens and the painters of the Flemish Baroque. The couple founded churches and convents, issued the Perpetual Edict of 1611, and are remembered as the "Archdukes" par excellence in Belgian history. Albert died in 1621, childless; the Netherlands reverted to the Spanish crown, with Isabella continuing as governess until 1633.
