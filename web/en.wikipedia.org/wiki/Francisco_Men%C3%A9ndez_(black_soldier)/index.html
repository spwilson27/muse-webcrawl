Francisco Menéndez (black soldier)

Diedafter 1748
Known forLeader of Fort Mose, Florida

Francisco Menéndez (c. 1704 – after 1748) was a free Black militiaman who became the leader of Fort Mose, the first legally sanctioned free African settlement in what is now the United States.

Life

Born in Senegambia, Menéndez was enslaved and taken to South Carolina, from where he escaped south to Spanish Florida during the Yamasee War period. In St. Augustine he won his freedom through service in the militia and baptism into the Catholic Church.

Fort Mose

In 1738 the Spanish governor established Gracia Real de Santa Teresa de Mose (Fort Mose), with Menéndez as its captain and civil leader. He defended the settlement against the British attack of 1740 and later was captured by English privateers before regaining his freedom and returning to Florida. Fort Mose is today recognised as a landmark of African American history.
