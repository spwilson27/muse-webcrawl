Swiss Federal Railways (German: Schweizerische Bundesbahnen, SBB; French: Chemins de fer fédéraux suisses, CFF; Italian: Ferrovie federali svizzere, FFS) is the national railway company of Switzerland. Headquartered in Bern, it operates most of the country's standard-gauge network, carrying hundreds of millions of passengers a year and large volumes of freight, and is regarded as one of the most punctual and intensively used railways in the world.

History

In the second half of the 19th century, Swiss railways were built by private companies, many foreign-financed. A referendum in 1898 approved nationalization, and the SBB was founded on 1 January 1902, absorbing the major private lines. The network was electrified early, largely in response to coal shortages during the First World War, using the 15 kV AC system that remains standard. In 1999 the SBB was converted into a special stock corporation wholly owned by the Swiss Confederation.

Services

SBB operates long-distance InterCity and EuroCity trains, the regional S-Bahn networks of major cities, and freight services through its subsidiary SBB Cargo. Swiss timetables are built around a nationwide integrated clock-face schedule (Taktfahrplan) with coordinated connections at hub stations. Famous routes served include those through the Gotthard Base Tunnel, the world's longest railway tunnel, opened in 2016.
