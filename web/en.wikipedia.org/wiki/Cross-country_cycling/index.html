Cross-country cycling

Cross-country cycling (XC) is a discipline of mountain biking held on terrain of moderate technical difficulty — typically loops combining climbs, descents, forest roads and singletrack. It is the only mountain biking discipline in the Olympic Games.

Racing formats

The classic format, cross-country Olympic (XCO), is a mass-start multi-lap race of about 80–100 minutes. Related formats include cross-country marathon (XCM) over much longer distances, short-track (XCC) races of about 20 minutes, and cross-country eliminator sprints. Cross-country became an Olympic event at the Atlanta Games of 1996.

Bikes

XC bikes emphasise low weight and pedalling efficiency: hardtail or short-travel full-suspension frames, 29-inch wheels, and lightweight components. Races are governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale, with a World Cup series held each season.
