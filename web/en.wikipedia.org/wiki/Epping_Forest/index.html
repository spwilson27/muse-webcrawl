Epping Forest

LocationEssex and Greater London, England
AreaAbout 2,400 hectares (5,900 acres)
Managed byCity of London Corporation

Epping Forest is a 2,400-hectare (5,900-acre) area of ancient woodland in southeast England, straddling the border between Greater London and Essex. It stretches from Forest Gate in south London to just north of Epping in Essex, following a ridge between the valleys of the rivers Lea and Roding.

History

The forest was historically a royal hunting forest. The Epping Forest Act 1878 ended enclosure and preserved the forest for public recreation, with Queen Victoria dedicating it "to the use and enjoyment of my people for all time" in 1882. It is managed by the City of London Corporation.

Ecology

The forest is a Site of Special Scientific Interest, noted for its ancient pollarded beech, oak, and hornbeam trees, its ponds, and its populations of deer and rare invertebrates.
