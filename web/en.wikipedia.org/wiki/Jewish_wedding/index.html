Jewish wedding

A wedding ceremony under the chuppah.

A Jewish wedding is a wedding ceremony that follows Jewish laws and traditions. Its distinctive features include the signing of the ketubah (marriage contract), the ceremony beneath the chuppah (wedding canopy), the giving of a ring by the groom to the bride under the chuppah, and the breaking of a glass at the conclusion of the ceremony.

Before the ceremony

Traditionally, the bride and groom fast on the wedding day and do not see each other for a period beforehand. The ceremony may be preceded by a bedeken, in which the groom places a veil over the bride's face. The ketubah, an ornate marriage contract outlining the husband's obligations, is signed by two witnesses; in more egalitarian ceremonies both partners may sign.

The ceremony

The ceremony takes place under the chuppah, a canopy symbolizing the home the couple will build together. It includes the betrothal blessings (kiddushin), the exchange of a ring accompanied by the declaration "Behold, you are consecrated to me according to the law of Moses and Israel," the reading of the ketubah, and the seven blessings (sheva brachot). The ceremony traditionally concludes with the groom breaking a glass underfoot — interpreted variously as a reminder of the destruction of the Temple in Jerusalem and of the fragility of relationships — followed by the shout of "Mazel tov!"

Celebration

After the ceremony the couple traditionally spends a short time alone together (yichud), followed by a festive meal and dancing, which may include the hora, in which the bride and groom are lifted on chairs. The seven blessings are repeated after the grace after meals during the week of celebration.
