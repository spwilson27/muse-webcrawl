Baskin-Robbins

Baskin-Robbins is an American multinational chain of ice cream and cake shops founded in 1945 in Glendale, California, by brothers-in-law Burt Baskin and Irv Robbins. It is famous for its "31 flavors" slogan — one for every day of the month — and for offering pink sample spoons. Long part of the Dunkin' Brands group, it was acquired by Inspire Brands in 2020.

History

Baskin, who had made ice cream for troops during the Second World War, and Robbins, who grew up working in his father's ice cream shop, merged their separate stores in the late 1940s. They were early pioneers of food franchising, selling their stores to managers so the company could expand rapidly while keeping quality local. By the 1960s the chain had hundreds of shops across the United States.

Flavours and marketing

The company claims to have introduced more than 1,300 flavours, including innovations such as Pralines 'n Cream and Jamoca. Famous flavours were developed for cultural moments — for example, a flavour marked the Beatles' arrival in America, and "Lunar Cheesecake" celebrated the 1969 Moon landing. The number "31" is hidden in the modern "BR" logo. The chain operates thousands of locations in dozens of countries, with especially large presences in Japan, South Korea, and the Middle East.
