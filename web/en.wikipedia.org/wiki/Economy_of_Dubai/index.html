Economy of Dubai

The economy of Dubai is one of the most diversified in the Gulf region. Although oil was discovered in Dubai in 1966, petroleum and natural gas now account for only a small share of the emirate's revenue; growth is driven instead by trade, logistics, tourism, real estate, financial services and aviation. Dubai's GDP exceeds US$100 billion.

Trade and logistics

Dubai developed as a trading port on the Gulf, and re-export trade remains central. The Jebel Ali Port, operated by DP World, is among the world's busiest ports, and the adjacent Jebel Ali Free Zone hosts thousands of companies. Numerous specialised free zones — for media, technology, finance, gold and commodities — allow full foreign ownership and have attracted multinational firms. The Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC) operates under its own English-language common-law framework.

Tourism and aviation

Dubai International Airport is one of the world's busiest for international passengers, and the flag carrier Emirates links the city to destinations on six continents. Tourism, retail festivals such as the Dubai Shopping Festival, and attractions including the Burj Khalifa, the Palm Islands and large shopping malls contribute a significant share of GDP.

Real estate and finance

Government-linked developers such as Emaar and Nakheel transformed the skyline with large-scale projects. The property market suffered a sharp correction in 2009 during the global financial crisis, when Dubai World's request for a debt standstill shook markets and Abu Dhabi provided support; the market later recovered and has boomed again. The emirate's population of over three million is overwhelmingly expatriate, and its workforce depends heavily on migrant labour, mainly from South Asia.
