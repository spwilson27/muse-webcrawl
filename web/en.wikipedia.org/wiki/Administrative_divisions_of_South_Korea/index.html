South Korea is divided administratively into provinces and province-level cities, and below them into cities, counties and districts. Local autonomy, suspended under the authoritarian governments, was restored with direct elections of local councils in 1991 and of local executives in 1995.

[Image: Map of South Korea's first-level administrative divisions]

First-level divisions

At the top level are: Seoul, a special city (teukbyeol-si) and the capital; six metropolitan cities (gwangyeok-si) — Busan, Daegu, Incheon, Gwangju, Daejeon and Ulsan; Sejong, a special self-governing city created in 2012 as a new administrative capital; six provinces (do) — Gyeonggi, Chungcheongbuk, Chungcheongnam, Jeollanam, Gyeongsangbuk and Gyeongsangnam; and three special self-governing provinces — Gangwon, Jeollabuk and Jeju — which enjoy wider autonomy. Gyeonggi Province, surrounding Seoul and Incheon, is the most populous, with over thirteen million residents.

Lower levels

Below the provinces are cities (si, population generally over 50,000), counties (gun, more rural) and, within the large cities, autonomous districts (gu). Cities above 500,000 may have non-autonomous districts. The third level comprises towns (eup), townships (myeon) and urban neighbourhoods (dong), and the lowest formal unit is the village (ri). Big cities with populations over one million, such as Suwon and Changwon, are designated "special-case cities" (teungnyesi) with expanded administrative powers.
