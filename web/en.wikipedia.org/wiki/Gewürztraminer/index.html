Gewürztraminer

Species: Vitis vinifera
Origin: Tramin (Termeno), South Tyrol, Italy/Germany region
Notable regions: Alsace, Germany, Alto Adige, Austria, New World
Signature aromas: Lychee, rose petal, ginger, exotic spice

Gewürztraminer (German: [ɡəˈvʏʁtstʁaˌmiːnɐ]) is an aromatic white wine grape variety used in white wines that performs best in cooler climates. In English it is sometimes colloquially called Gewürz (and often shortened to "Gewürtz" or "Gewerz"). The variety has a pink to red skin colour, which makes it a "white wine grape" with unusually dark skin. The name literally means "spice Traminer" — Gewürz meaning spice or perfume, and Traminer after the village of Tramin in South Tyrol.

Characteristics

Gewürztraminer wines are among the most distinctive and easily recognised of all white wines, famous for their flamboyant bouquet of lychees, rose petals, passion fruit, and spices such as ginger and cinnamon. The grape naturally accumulates high sugar and perfume compounds (terpenes) but low acidity, so wines can be full-bodied and slightly oily in texture, and are made in styles ranging from bone-dry to lusciously sweet late-harvest versions. The lychee aroma so closely mimics the fruit that the wine is often used as a textbook example of varietal character.

Regions

The variety reaches its classic expression in Alsace, France, where it is one of the four noble grapes permitted in Alsace Grand Cru wines and produces rich, spicy, sometimes off-dry wines, including vendange tardive and Sélection de Grains Nobles dessert styles. It is also widely grown in Germany (see German wine classification), Austria, Italy's Alto Adige, Eastern Europe, and in cooler New World regions such as New Zealand, Oregon, British Columbia, and Ontario.

Food pairing

Because of its aromatic intensity and low acidity, Gewürztraminer is a classic match for spicy Asian dishes — Thai, Sichuan, and Indian cuisines — as well as for strongly flavoured cheeses such as Munster, smoked foods, and foie gras.
