Xebec

A xebec (also spelled zebec or chebec) was a Mediterranean sailing ship used mainly for trading from the 16th to the 19th centuries. Xebecs combined a long, narrow, fast hull influenced by galley design with lateen sails on two or three masts, and many could also be rowed. They were especially associated with the Barbary corsairs of North Africa, who used them for raiding commerce, and with Spanish, French and other Mediterranean navies that countered them with xebecs of their own.

Characteristics

A typical naval xebec of the 18th century was three-masted, lateen-rigged (square sails could be set on the foremast in following winds), carried between 16 and 24 guns, and had a crew of up to 350 men for corsair operations. Their shallow draft, speed and weatherliness made them effective in the variable winds of the Mediterranean. The Royal Navy captured and purchased several, sometimes rating them as sloops or small frigates.

Decline

With the suppression of Barbary piracy in the early 19th century and the transition to steam, xebecs declined; the last were built in the mid-19th century, though lateen-rigged trading descendants survived locally.
