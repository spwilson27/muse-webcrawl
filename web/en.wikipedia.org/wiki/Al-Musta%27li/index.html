Al-Musta'li

Fatimid Cairo, seat of al-Musta'li's caliphate

Abū al-Qāsim Aḥmad ibn al-Mustanṣir (16 September 1074 – 12 December 1101), better known by his regnal name al-Mustaʿlī biʾllāh ("he who is raised up by God"), was the ninth Fatimid caliph and the nineteenth imam of Musta'li Ismaili Shia Islam, reigning in Cairo from 1094 until his death in 1101. His accession, engineered by the vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah, was disputed by his elder brother Nizar, provoking the Nizari–Musta'li schism that split Ismailism permanently.

Accession and schism

On the death of al-Mustansir — the longest-reigning Fatimid caliph — al-Afdal, who had succeeded his father Badr al-Jamali as vizier and commander of the armies, installed the young al-Musta'li, his own brother-in-law, bypassing the elder son Nizar. Nizar fled to Alexandria and raised a revolt; defeated and captured, he was executed (tradition says immured). His supporters, led by Hassan-i Sabbah in Persia, rejected al-Musta'li's imamate, forming the Nizari branch, while the Fatimid heartlands recognised al-Musta'li — the origin of the Musta'li community that survives today, principally among the Bohras of India.

Reign

Real power rested with al-Afdal, and al-Musta'li's reign was dominated by warfare in Syria and Palestine: conflict with the Seljuk Turks, and from 1097 the arrival of the First Crusade, which captured Jerusalem from Fatimid hands in 1099 after al-Afdal's army was defeated at Ascalon. Al-Musta'li died in December 1101 and was succeeded by his five-year-old son, al-Amir.
