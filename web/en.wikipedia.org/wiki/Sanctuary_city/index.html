Sanctuary city

Related: Sanctuary movement; immigration enforcement

Countries: United States, Canada, United Kingdom (City of Sanctuary)

A sanctuary city is a municipality whose local policies limit cooperation with national immigration enforcement. There is no single legal definition: practices typically include declining to hold people solely on immigration detainers, restricting police inquiries into immigration status, and limiting information-sharing with federal immigration authorities. Supporters argue such policies build trust between immigrant communities and local police; critics argue they obstruct enforcement of immigration law.

History

The term derives from the 1980s Sanctuary movement, in which churches sheltered Central American refugees. Cities such as San Francisco adopted "City of Refuge" ordinances beginning in 1985, and the concept expanded as hundreds of counties and cities adopted non-cooperation policies in the 2000s and 2010s.

Policy debates

Sanctuary policies have been the subject of repeated legal and political conflict in the United States, including federal attempts to withhold funding from sanctuary jurisdictions, which courts have often blocked, and state laws either protecting or pre-empting local sanctuary policies. Research on their effects on crime is contested; several studies find no increase in crime in sanctuary jurisdictions.

Elsewhere

In the United Kingdom, the City of Sanctuary movement encourages towns and cities to welcome asylum seekers and refugees, while several Canadian cities describe themselves as sanctuary or "access without fear" cities.
