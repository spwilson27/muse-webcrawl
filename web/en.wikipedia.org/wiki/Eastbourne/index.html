Eastbourne

Eastbourne is a town on the south coast of England, in the county of East Sussex, approximately 19 miles (31 km) east of Brighton and 84 km (52 mi) south of London. It sits at the eastern end of the South Downs, close to the chalk cliffs of Beachy Head and the Seven Sisters. At the 2021 census the town had a population of around 99,000.

History

The area has been settled since the Stone Age, and there is evidence of Roman occupation. The name derives from the Old English burna (stream) and the "East" prefix distinguishing it from a neighbouring settlement. Eastbourne grew rapidly as a fashionable Victorian seaside resort after the arrival of the railway in 1849, largely through the efforts of the 7th Duke of Devonshire and local landowners, who laid out wide tree-lined streets, parks and a grand seafront.

Geography and landmarks

The town is sheltered by the South Downs and is known for a comparatively sunny, mild climate. Landmarks include Eastbourne Pier (opened 1870), the bandstand on the seafront, the Sovereign Harbour marina development, and the Towner Art Gallery. To the west rises Beachy Head, at 162 metres one of the highest chalk sea cliffs in Britain.

Economy and culture

Tourism, conferences and services dominate the local economy. The town hosts the Eastbourne International, a grass-court tennis tournament held at Devonshire Park in the run-up to Wimbledon, and the annual Airbourne airshow. Eastbourne has a reputation as a retirement destination, though its population has become younger in recent decades.

Transport

Eastbourne railway station provides services to London Victoria, Brighton and Ashford International. The A22 and A27 roads connect the town with the wider region.
