Aladdin Sane

Aladdin Sane — studio album by David Bowie
Released: 13 April 1973 (UK)
Recorded: October 1972 – January 1973, Trident Studios, London; RCA Studios, New York and Nashville
Genre: Glam rock, hard rock
Label: RCA
Producer: Ken Scott, David Bowie
Preceded by: The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars (1972)
Followed by: Pin Ups (1973)

Aladdin Sane is the sixth studio album by the English musician David Bowie, released in the United Kingdom on 13 April 1973 through RCA Records. Co-produced by Bowie and Ken Scott, it was recorded with his backing band the Spiders from Mars — comprising Mick Ronson, Trevor Bolder and Mick Woodmansey — plus pianist Mike Garson, during sessions in London and the United States between Bowie's 1972–73 Ziggy Stardust Tour legs. Much of the material was written on the road in America, and many tracks are labelled with the cities that inspired them.

Music and persona

Bowie described the album as "Ziggy goes to America", and its title character — a pun on "a lad insane" — a new, darker iteration of Ziggy Stardust reflecting on fame, transience and schizophrenia (his half-brother Terry Burns suffered from the condition, an influence on songs such as the schizophrenic title track). Musically the record is harder and more avant-garde than its predecessor, with Garson's jazz- and avant-garde-inflected piano central to "Aladdin Sane (1913–1938–197?)", "Time", and "Lady Grinning Soul". Singles included "The Jean Genie" (UK No. 2) and "Drive-In Saturday"; the album also features "Panic in Detroit", "Cracked Actor", "Watch That Man", and a cover of the Rolling Stones' "Let's Spend the Night Together".

Cover and reception

The cover photograph by Brian Duffy — Bowie bare-chested with a red-and-blue lightning bolt across his face — became one of the most famous images in popular music, and the album was Bowie's first UK number one. Contemporary reviews were mixed-to-positive, with some critics finding it rushed beside Ziggy Stardust, but retrospectives consistently rank it among his finest works; it has sold millions of copies and appears on numerous best-album lists. Bowie retired the Ziggy/Aladdin persona on stage at the Hammersmith Odeon on 3 July 1973.

Track listing

All tracks written by David Bowie except "Let's Spend the Night Together" (Jagger–Richards): "Watch That Man", "Aladdin Sane (1913–1938–197?)", "Drive-In Saturday", "Panic in Detroit", "Cracked Actor", "Time", "The Prettiest Star", "Let's Spend the Night Together", "The Jean Genie", "Lady Grinning Soul".
