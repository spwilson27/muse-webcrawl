Paradise Garage

Location84 King Street, New York City
Opened1977 (fully opened 1978)
Closed1987
Resident DJLarry Levan
OwnerMichael Brody

The Paradise Garage was a legendary New York City nightclub at 84 King Street in Hudson Square, Manhattan, operating from 1977 to 1987. Housed in a former parking garage — the source of its name and of the traffic-sign logo — it became one of the most influential venues in the history of dance music, the spiritual home of resident DJ Larry Levan and the birthplace of the genre later known as "garage house."

The club

Founded by Michael Brody, the Garage was a private, members-only club with a predominantly Black and Latino gay clientele. It served no alcohol, opened late, and often ran well into the following afternoon; the focus was entirely on dancing and the music. Its sound system, designed with Richard Long and Associates, was famed for its power and clarity and set a benchmark for club audio.

Larry Levan and garage music

Levan's marathon sets blended disco, soul, funk, rock, dub, gospel, and early electronic music, often manipulated live with re-edits and effects. His eclectic, spiritually charged style profoundly shaped house music — the Chicago sound took direct inspiration, and New York/New Jersey house became known as "garage" in honour of the club. Artists including Grace Jones, Chaka Khan, and Gwen Guthrie (whose "Ain't Nothin' Goin' On but the Rent" was dubbed the Garage's national anthem) performed or premiered records there.

Legacy

The Paradise Garage closed in September 1987; Brody died of AIDS-related illness the same year, and Levan in 1992. Its influence on DJ culture, remixing, and club sound-system design has been enormous and is documented in books and films, and the building at 84 King Street remained a site of pilgrimage for dance-music fans until its demolition in 2018.
