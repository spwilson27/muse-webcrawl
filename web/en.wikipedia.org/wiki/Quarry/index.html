Quarry

A quarry is a type of open-pit mine from which rock, stone, gravel, sand or other construction materials are extracted. Unlike underground mines, quarries work at the surface, cutting the ground in a series of level benches. The products — collectively called aggregates, plus dimension stone such as marble, granite, limestone and slate — underpin construction worldwide.

Methods

Extraction begins by stripping topsoil and overburden. Dimension stone is sawn or split into blocks with minimal blasting, while aggregate rock is drilled and blasted, then crushed, screened and washed into graded sizes. Stone may also be won by wire sawing, jet piercing or hammering along natural beds. Quarrying is ancient: Egyptians quarried limestone and granite for the pyramids, and Roman quarries supplied marble across the empire.

Environmental issues and after-use

Quarries generate dust, noise, vibration and heavy traffic, and can alter groundwater and habitats; modern permits therefore require progressive restoration. Worked-out quarries often flood, forming deep, steep-sided lakes that are attractive but hazardous to swimmers; others become nature reserves, landfills, amphitheatres or climbing venues. Famous examples include the Eden Project in a Cornish china-clay pit and the marble quarries of Carrara, Italy.
