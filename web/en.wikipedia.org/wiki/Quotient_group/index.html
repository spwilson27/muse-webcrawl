Quotient group

A quotient group or factor group is a mathematical group obtained by aggregating similar elements of a larger group using an equivalence relation that preserves the group structure. Formally, given a group G and a normal subgroup N of G, the quotient group, written G/N and read "G modulo N", has as its elements the cosets of N in G, with the group operation defined by (aN)(bN) = (ab)N.

Definition and motivation

The construction requires N to be a normal subgroup—invariant under conjugation—for the multiplication of cosets to be well-defined. The quotient group captures what remains of G after "collapsing" N to the identity. The canonical example is the integers modulo n: the quotient Z/nZ is the cyclic group of order n familiar from modular arithmetic. Similarly, the quotient of the real numbers by the integers, R/Z, is the circle group of rotations.

Properties

  • The order of G/N equals the index [G : N] = |G| / |N| when these are finite.
  • If G is abelian, then so is every quotient group; the same holds for nilpotent and solvable groups.
  • There is always a natural projection homomorphism from G onto G/N with kernel N.
  • By the first isomorphism theorem, every quotient of G is isomorphic to the image of a homomorphism out of G, and conversely.

Examples

The parity of permutations gives the quotient of the symmetric group Sn by the alternating group An, which has order 2. Quotient groups are fundamental throughout algebra and topology, where, for example, fundamental groups of quotient spaces and homology groups are built from quotients of abelian groups.
