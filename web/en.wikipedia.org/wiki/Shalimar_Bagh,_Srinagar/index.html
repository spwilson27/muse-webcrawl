Shalimar Bagh, Srinagar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shalimar Bagh is a Mughal garden in Srinagar, in the Indian-administered union territory of Jammu and Kashmir, linked through a channel to the northeast shore of Dal Lake. It was built in 1619 by the Mughal emperor Jahangir for his wife Nur Jahan, and is the largest of the three great Mughal gardens of the Kashmir valley, alongside Nishat Bagh and Chashme Shahi. The name Shalimar is popularly rendered as "abode of love".

Layout

The garden covers about 12.4 hectares (31 acres) and follows the Persian charbagh tradition adapted to the sloping terrain, arranged in three descending terraces fed by the Gopi spring. The lowest terrace was the public garden, the second the emperor's private garden, and the third, the zenana (harem) garden, centred on a black marble pavilion surrounded by fountains and shaded by chinar trees. A celebrated inscription in the garden reproduces the Persian couplet attributed to Amir Khusrau: "If there is a paradise on earth, it is this, it is this, it is this."

History

The site had an older garden dating to the sixth century. Jahangir's garden was extended by his son Shah Jahan in 1630. Under Sikh and later Dogra rule the garden was maintained as a royal pleasure ground; the Dogra maharajas installed electricity in the pavilions, among the earliest such installations in Kashmir. Today Shalimar Bagh is a public park and one of Srinagar's principal tourist attractions, famous for its spring blossom and autumn chinar foliage.
