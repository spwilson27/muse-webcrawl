Ariovistus was a leader of the Suebi and other Germanic peoples in the second quarter of the 1st century BC. He and his followers took part in the war of the Arverni and the Sequani against the Aedui in Gaul, assisting the latter. The Sequani invited Ariovistus into Gaul around 71 BC to fight the Aedui; after victory at the Battle of Magetobriga (c. 63 BC), the Suebi settled in the lands of the Sequani in what is now Alsace and Franche-Comté, and Ariovistus came to dominate the region.

Conflict with Caesar

Ariovistus had been recognised by the Roman Senate as "king and friend of the Roman people" in 59 BC, during Caesar's consulship. However, after Gallic tribes appealed to Rome against his rule, Julius Caesar confronted him in 58 BC, the first campaign of the Gallic Wars. An exchange of embassies and a famous personal conference failed; Caesar's account in De Bello Gallico presents Ariovistus as arrogant and treacherous.

At the Battle of Vosges in September 58 BC, Caesar's legions defeated the Germanic coalition. Ariovistus escaped across the Rhine with a remnant of his forces; his two wives and one of his daughters died, and another daughter was captured, according to Caesar. He died soon afterwards, reportedly of his wounds or of grief; the Suebi thereafter withdrew east of the Rhine.

Legacy

The encounter between Caesar and Ariovistus is a set-piece of the Commentarii de Bello Gallico and a foundational episode in the Roman-Germanic frontier narrative. Ariovistus is regarded as an early example of a Germanic leader building a cross-tribal kingship.
