Berlin Crisis of 1961

Berlin Crisis of 1961
Date4 June – 9 November 1961
LocationBerlin, Germany
OutcomeConstruction of the Berlin Wall; status quo maintained

The Berlin Crisis of 1961 was the last major European political-military incident of the Cold War about the occupational status of the German capital and of Berlin as such.

Background

Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev demanded the Western powers withdraw from West Berlin and renewed an ultimatum at his Vienna summit with US President John F. Kennedy in June 1961. Meanwhile, thousands of East Germans were fleeing west through Berlin, draining the GDR of workers.

The Wall and standoff

On 13 August 1961 East Germany, with Soviet backing, sealed the border and began building the Berlin Wall. In October, American and Soviet tanks confronted each other at Checkpoint Charlie for some 16 hours over Allied access rights, raising fears of escalation before both sides withdrew.

Aftermath

The crisis ended with the divided status quo entrenched: the Wall stood until 1989, and the Western presence in West Berlin continued.
