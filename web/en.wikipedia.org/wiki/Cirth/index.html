Cirth

The Cirth (Sindarin pronunciation roughly [ˈkirθ], meaning "runes", singular certh) is a semi-artificial script invented by J. R. R. Tolkien for the languages of Middle-earth. In Tolkien's fictional history, the original Certhas was created by the Elf Daeron, the minstrel and loremaster of King Thingol of Doriath, and was later expanded into the Angerthas. The Dwarves adopted and adapted the script, using it for inscriptions carved in stone and metal.

The Angerthas, the expanded Cirth rune-row.

History in the fiction

According to Tolkien's legendarium, Daeron devised the Cirth in the First Age for inscribing names and brief memorials on wood, stone or metal. The Elves of Beleriand used the script relatively little, preferring the Tengwar of Fëanor for writing with brush or pen. The Dwarves of Khazad-dûm learned the Cirth from the Elves, modified the values of some runes to suit the sounds of Khuzdul, and spread the script eastward as they migrated. The Men of the East and other peoples further adapted the runes; a simplified version came to be used in Eriador.

Structure

The Cirth are angular characters resembling historical Germanic runes, designed to be carved rather than written. The original system had 19 characters; the Dwarvish Angerthas Daeron and Angerthas Moria expanded the inventory to around 60 runes, including separate forms for sounds not distinguished in earlier versions. Tolkien presents the systems in Appendix E of The Lord of the Rings, where a table gives the runes and their values in the Angerthas of Erebor and of Moria.

Uses in the works

Cirth inscriptions appear in several places in Tolkien's published work: the title-page inscriptions of The Hobbit use a simple English rune mapping; Balin's tomb in Moria bears an inscription in Angerthas Moria reading "Balin Fundinul uzbad Khazaddûmu" ("Balin son of Fundin, Lord of Moria"); and the Book of Mazarbul is depicted as partly written in Cirth.

Encoding

The Cirth are not part of the Unicode standard as a separate block, though proposals have been made and the script is encoded in the unofficial ConScript Unicode Registry in the Private Use Area.
