Music of Suriname

The music of Suriname reflects the country's exceptionally diverse population, which includes people of African, East Indian, Javanese, Chinese, Indigenous and European descent. Suriname is well known for kaseko music and for its Indo-Caribbean traditions.

Kaseko

Kaseko is a fusion of numerous popular and folk styles derived from Africa, Europe and the Americas. The term derives from the French casser le corps ("break the body"), a dance of enslaved people on plantations. Kaseko is rhythmically complex, using instruments including skratji (a large drum), snare drum, congas, and an ensemble of saxophone, trumpet and trombone, sometimes interrupted by solo vocals in Sranan Tongo. It developed out of bigi poku ("big drum") music in the early 20th century and was popularised from the 1970s by bandleaders such as Lieve Hugo, dubbed the "King of Kaseko".

Kawina

Kawina originated as devotional and entertainment music among Afro-Surinamese communities, performed with drums, shak-shak (shakers) and call-and-response singing, often associated with the Winti religion. In the 1980s it gave rise to kawina-derived popular styles in the Netherlands and Suriname.

Other traditions

Hindustani Surinamese maintain traditions such as baithak gana, brought from India and also found in Guyana and Trinidad. Javanese Surinamese communities perform gamelan and kroncong. Indigenous peoples have their own ceremonial music, while the Maroon peoples of the interior preserve rich drumming and song traditions such as awasa and aleke — aleke itself becoming a modern popular genre. Surinamese musicians have also been influential in the Dutch music scene through genres such as bubbling, a fast dancehall-derived style created in The Hague by DJ Moortje.
