Piano duet

A piano duet is a piece of music written for two pianists playing at a single piano, conventionally described as music for "piano four hands" (French: piano à quatre mains). The two parts are called primo (the upper part) and secondo (the lower part). It is distinguished from a piano duo, in which two pianists play on two separate pianos.

History and repertoire

Piano duets became popular in the late 18th and 19th centuries as domestic music-making, allowing symphonies, operas, and chamber works to be heard at home in transcription before recordings existed. Mozart and Schubert composed original duets; Schubert's Fantasia in F minor, D.940, is among the most celebrated. Brahms, Dvořák (Slavonic Dances), Fauré (Dolly Suite), Debussy, and Ravel (Ma mère l'Oye) all wrote significant four-hand works.

Notation and performance

Duets are usually printed with primo and secondo on facing pages or in score. The players sit side by side, the primo player generally taking responsibility for pedalling. Four-hand playing demands close coordination of timing, balance, and page turns.
