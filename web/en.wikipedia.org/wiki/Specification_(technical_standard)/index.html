Specification (technical standard)

Specification
PurposeStates requirements a product, material, system, or service must satisfy
Related conceptsTechnical standard, data sheet, requirements specification

A specification often refers to a set of documented requirements to be satisfied by a material, design, product, or service. It is a common early part of the engineering design process in many fields. A specification is a type of technical standard that is established by an enterprise, a regulator, or a standards organization, and it is usually applied in engineering, manufacturing, construction, software, and procurement.

Types

Specifications may be classified by what they prescribe. A performance specification states the results to be achieved without dictating how; a design (or prescriptive) specification specifies exact characteristics, materials, and methods. Functional specifications in software describe what a system should do, while requirements specifications capture needs for engineering projects. Product specifications (data sheets) list the measurable attributes of an item offered for sale.

Use

In construction and manufacturing, specifications form part of the contract documents, complementing drawings: drawings show what is to be built, specifications state the quality and properties of materials and workmanship. Conformance is verified through testing, inspection, and certification. In procurement law, specifications must be written so as not to improperly exclude competitors, often by citing recognised standards (ISO, ASTM, EN, BS, DIN, JIS).

Specifications and standards

A specification differs from a general standard in that it applies to a particular product, project, or contract, though specifications commonly reference public standards. Where a specification is used repeatedly and gains wide adoption it may become a de facto standard, and standards bodies may adopt it as a formal standard.
