Huguette Marcelle Clark (9 June 1906 – 24 May 2011) was an American heiress and philanthropist, the youngest daughter of copper magnate and United States Senator William A. Clark. She became widely known late in life for her extreme reclusiveness: although she owned grand residences in California, Connecticut and New York, she spent the last two decades of her life living in hospital rooms in New York City.

Life

Clark briefly married William MacDonald Gower in 1928. She was an accomplished painter and musician and a devoted collector of Japanese art and antique dolls. Her 104-year lifespan spanned most of the twentieth century.

Estate

On her death she left an estate estimated at more than $300 million. Two conflicting wills led to a highly publicised legal dispute between her distant relatives and the beneficiaries of her later will, including her nurse and the hospital where she lived; the case was settled in 2013. Her life is chronicled in the bestselling book Empty Mansions by Bill Dedman and Paul Clark Newell Jr.
