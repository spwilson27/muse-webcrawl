Komusō

The komusō (虚無僧, "priest of nothingness") were a group of Japanese monks of the Fuke school of Zen Buddhism who flourished during the Edo period (1603–1868). They are characterised by the straw basket (a tengai) worn completely over the head, manifesting the absence of a specific ego, and by playing the shakuhachi flute as a form of meditation.

Practice

Komusō practised suizen — "blowing Zen" — playing solo pieces called honkyoku while begging for alms. Many were former samurai (rōnin), and the privilege of travelling freely granted to them by the Tokugawa shogunate made the disguise attractive to spies and fugitives, leading to abuses and eventual scrutiny.

Dissolution

The Fuke sect was abolished in 1871 early in the Meiji period, though the shakuhachi tradition was preserved through secular transmission.
