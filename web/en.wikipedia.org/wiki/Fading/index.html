Fading

In wireless communications, fading is the variation of signal attenuation over time or distance, caused by changes in the transmission channel, weather, or multipath propagation. Fading can severely affect the performance of radio links and is a central topic in the design of wireless systems.

A received signal undergoing multipath fading.

Causes

Multipath fading occurs when the transmitted signal reaches the receiver by several paths with different lengths; the copies interfere constructively or destructively as the geometry changes. Large-scale fading, including shadowing by obstacles, affects the average signal level over longer distances, while small-scale fading describes rapid fluctuations over short distances or times. Rain fade affects higher-frequency satellite and terrestrial links.

Models and mitigation

Common statistical models include Rayleigh fading (no line of sight) and Rician fading (a dominant line-of-sight component). Engineers combat fading with diversity techniques — using multiple antennas, frequencies, or time slots — together with error-correcting codes, interleaving, and adaptive modulation.
