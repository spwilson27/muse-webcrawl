Ingrid Daubechies

Born: 17 August 1954, Houthalen, Belgium
Known for: Daubechies wavelets
Fields: Mathematics, physics
Institutions: Duke University, Princeton University

Ingrid Daubechies (born 17 August 1954) is a Belgian-American physicist and mathematician, best known for her work with wavelets in image compression. The Daubechies wavelets, which she developed in the late 1980s, form the mathematical basis of the JPEG 2000 image compression standard and are widely used in signal processing.

Career

Daubechies studied physics at the Vrije Universiteit Brussel and completed her doctorate there in 1980. After research positions in Belgium and at Bell Laboratories in the United States, she became a professor at Princeton University, later moving to Duke University. In 2011 she became the first woman president of the International Mathematical Union.

Daubechies wavelets

Her construction of orthonormal wavelet bases with compact support and a maximal number of vanishing moments for a given support width was a breakthrough, making wavelet transforms practical for applications ranging from the FBI's fingerprint database to medical imaging and digital cinema.

Honours

She has received numerous awards, including a MacArthur Fellowship, the Steele Prize, the Nemmers Prize in Mathematics, and in 2023 the Wolf Prize in Mathematics.
