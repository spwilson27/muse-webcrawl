Rendaku

Rendaku (連濁, lit. "sequential voicing") is a phenomenon in Japanese morphophonology where the initial consonant of the second component of a compound word becomes voiced. For example, hito + hito becomes hitobito ("people"), and hon + hana may remain separate while ikebana shows the change of hana to bana ("flower arranging"). The voicing is marked in Japanese writing systems by the dakuten diacritic.

Constraints

Rendaku is unpredictable and governed by several tendencies rather than strict rules. Lyman's Law states that rendaku does not occur if the second element already contains a voiced obstruent. Rendaku is also less common in compounds of Sino-Japanese origin and in words of foreign origin, and tends to be blocked when the compound has a coordinative rather than subordinating structure.
