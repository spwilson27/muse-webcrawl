Ismail al-Jazari

Badīʿ az-Zaman Abu l-ʿIzz ibn Ismāʿīl ibn ar-Razāz al-Jazarī (1136–1206), commonly known as al-Jazari, was a Muslim polymath: a scholar, inventor, mechanical engineer, artisan and artist from Jazira in Mesopotamia (in present-day Turkey). He served the Artuqid dynasty at Diyarbakır and is best known for writing The Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices in 1206.

The Book of Ingenious Devices

His book describes fifty mechanical devices in six categories, including water clocks, automata, fountains and water-raising machines, with detailed instructions for their construction. Famous designs include the elephant clock, the castle clock, and musical robot bands. Al-Jazari is credited with early descriptions of the crankshaft combined with a connecting rod, and the segmental gear. His work built on earlier inventors such as the Banū Mūsā brothers and influenced later European mechanical engineering.
