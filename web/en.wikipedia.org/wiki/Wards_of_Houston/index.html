Wards of Houston

The wards of Houston are a historic system of political subdivisions of Houston, Texas, United States. When Houston was incorporated in 1837, it was divided into four wards, each electing aldermen to the city council. A fifth ward was added in 1840 and a sixth in 1876.

History

The wards were laid out from the centre of the city at Main Street and Congress Avenue. The First Ward lay to the northwest, the Second Ward to the northeast, the Third Ward to the southeast, the Fourth Ward to the southwest, the Fifth Ward north of Buffalo Bayou's junction, and the Sixth Ward northwest along the bayou. Each ward developed a distinct character: the Fourth Ward became known as Freedmen's Town, founded by formerly enslaved people after emancipation, while the Third Ward became a centre of Houston's African American community and home to Texas Southern University.

Legacy

The ward system of government was abolished in 1915 when Houston moved to an at-large council, but the ward names persist as neighbourhood identities and in institutions such as churches, civic clubs and the historic Sixth Ward district, one of the city's oldest residential neighbourhoods.
