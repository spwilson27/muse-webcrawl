Grand Duchy of Moscow

The Grand Duchy of Moscow (Russian: Великое княжество Московское), also known as Muscovy, was a medieval Russian principality centred on Moscow that grew from a minor appanage of Vladimir-Suzdal in the late 13th century into the dominant power of the Rus' lands. Its rulers, of the Rurik dynasty, gathered neighbouring principalities by purchase, inheritance and conquest, threw off the suzerainty of the Golden Horde, and in 1547 transformed the state into the Tsardom of Russia when Ivan IV was crowned Tsar.

Rise under the Daniilovichi

Moscow is first mentioned in 1147. Its appanage prince from about 1263 was Daniel, youngest son of Alexander Nevsky. His descendants expanded steadily: Ivan I "Kalita" ("Moneybag") obtained from the Mongol khan the yarlyk (patent) for the grand principality of Vladimir and the right to collect tribute from the Rus' lands, enriching Moscow. Dmitry Donskoy defeated the Mongols at the Battle of Kulikovo in 1380, the first major Russian victory over the Horde, though suzerainty continued for another century.

Consolidation and independence

After a succession war under Vasily II, Ivan III "the Great" (r. 1462–1505) annexed Novgorod (1478) and Tver (1485), adopted the title "Sovereign of all Rus'", married Sophia Palaiologina, niece of the last Byzantine emperor, and in 1480 faced the Great Stand on the Ugra River, after which Mongol suzerainty ended without a pitched battle. Ivan's law code of 1497 and the rebuilding of the Kremlin by Italian architects marked Moscow's emergence as a centralised state; the monk Philotheus of Pskov soon proclaimed Moscow the "Third Rome".

Territory and society

Vasily III added Pskov, Smolensk and Ryazan, rounding out the westward expansion. The state's service nobility held land (pomestie) conditionally on military service, while peasants were progressively bound to the land — a process culminating in full serfdom in the following century. In January 1547 the young Ivan IV was crowned "Tsar and Grand Prince of all Rus'", converting the grand duchy into a tsardom.
