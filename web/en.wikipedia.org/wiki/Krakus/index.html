Krakus

Krakus, Krak or Grakch was a legendary Polish prince, the founder of Kraków, and a ruler of the Lechitic tribe of Vistulans. According to the earliest recorded account, by the chronicler Wincenty Kadłubek (writing around the turn of the 13th century), Krakus lived in the 8th century and was a wise and just ruler chosen by his people.

Legend

Kadłubek's chronicle relates that Krakus defeated an army of Gauls, founded the city that bears his name on Wawel Hill above the Vistula river, and ruled wisely. Later versions of the legend, elaborated by Jan Długosz in the 15th century, added the story of the Wawel Dragon: a monstrous dragon lived in a cave beneath Wawel Hill and terrorised the countryside; in Długosz's account it was slain by Krakus's sons, while in a popular later variant a clever cobbler named Skuba (or Krakus himself) fed the beast a sheep stuffed with sulfur, which it ate and then drank so much river water that it burst.

After Krakus's death, he was succeeded by his sons and then by his daughter Wanda, who, according to legend, drowned herself in the Vistula rather than marry a German prince. The Krakus Mound (Kopiec Krakusa) in the Podgórze district of Kraków, a prehistoric tumulus traditionally regarded as his burial place, is one of the oldest surviving structures associated with the city. A second mound, the Wanda Mound, commemorates his daughter.

Historicity

No historical evidence confirms Krakus's existence; the name may derive from the pre-Slavic or Celtic word for a stronghold, or from kruk, Polish for raven. Nevertheless, the legend is deeply embedded in Polish national tradition, and the name Kraków itself is popularly explained as "Krak's town".
