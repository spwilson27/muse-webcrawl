Round dance

A round dance is a type of folk dance in which dancers form a circle, often holding hands, and perform steps together while moving around the ring. Round dances are among the oldest known dance forms and are found in cultures worldwide. The term also names a modern ballroom category and the Native American Round Dance ceremony.

Folk traditions

Circle dances include the Balkan kolo and horo, the Greek syrtos, the Romanian hora (also central to Jewish celebration dancing), the Catalan sardana, Breton an-dro, and Scandinavian ring dances. Such dances typically accompany weddings, feast days and seasonal rituals, with steps ranging from simple walking patterns to fast, intricate footwork led by a caller or lead dancer.

Native American Round Dance

Among Plains Indigenous peoples of North America, the Round Dance is a social and ceremonial gathering in which participants move in a circle around singers and drummers, side-stepping while holding hands. Originating, in Cree tradition, as a healing and memorial ceremony given by a widow, it is now also a widespread social dance held in winter, symbolising friendship, community and the circle of life.

Modern ballroom

In modern ballroom dancing, "round dance" refers to cued ballroom dancing — choreographed sequences in rhythms such as waltz, foxtrot, cha-cha and rumba, prompted by a cuer — popularised internationally by organisations such as Roundalab.
