DBv

dBV is a unit of measurement for the amplitude of a signal in electronics and telecommunications, expressed in decibels (dB) relative to a reference voltage of one volt (V), regardless of the impedance of the circuit. It is widely used to specify input and output levels of audio equipment and other signal-processing devices.

Definition

For a voltage V, the level in dBV is defined as

LdBV = 20 log10(V / 1 V).

Thus 1 volt corresponds to 0 dBV, 2 volts to approximately +6.02 dBV, and 0.1 volts to −20 dBV. Because the reference is a voltage, the ratio uses the factor 20 (rather than the factor 10 used for power ratios), consistent with power being proportional to the square of voltage.

Use

In professional and consumer audio, line levels are commonly specified in dBV or in the related unit dBu (referenced to 0.7746 volts, independent of load impedance). Consumer line level is nominally −10 dBV, whereas professional line level is +4 dBu, about 1.78 volts — a difference of roughly 12 dB between the two conventions. Other related voltage-referenced decibel units include dBm, which in telecommunications historically references the voltage delivering 1 milliwatt into 600 ohms.
