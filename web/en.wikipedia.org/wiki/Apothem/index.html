Apothem

The apothem (from Greek apóthema, "put down") of a regular polygon is a line segment from the centre to the midpoint of one of its sides. Equivalently, it is the line drawn from the centre of the polygon that is perpendicular to one of its sides. The word is also used for the radius of the inscribed circle (incircle) of the polygon, and for the slant height components of regular pyramids.

Formulas

For a regular polygon with side length s, n sides and circumradius R, the apothem a is given by a = s / (2 tan(π/n)) = R cos(π/n). Because the polygon divides into n congruent triangles of base s and height a, its area is simply A = ½ × perimeter × apothem = ½nsa.

Examples

For a square of side s, the apothem is s/2. For an equilateral triangle it is s/(2√3); for a regular hexagon it is (√3/2)s, equal to the height of the equilateral triangles that compose it. In a regular pyramid, the apothem is the slant height of a triangular face; in a cone it corresponds to the slant height.
