Bluff (poker)

[Image: A poker player pushing chips forward with a weak hand]

In the card game of poker, a bluff is a bet or raise made with a hand which is not thought to be the best hand. To bluff is to make such a bet. The objective of a bluff is to induce a fold by at least one opponent who holds a better hand. The size and frequency of a bluff determines its profitability to the bluffer. By extension, the phrase "calling somebody's bluff" is often used outside the context of poker to describe situations where one person demands that another prove a claim, or prove that they are not bluffing.

Types

A pure bluff is made with a hand that has little or no chance of improving at showdown. A semi-bluff is a bet with a drawing hand that is not currently the best hand but has a reasonable chance of improving, giving the bet two ways to win: the opponent folds, or the draw completes. A bluff made on an early betting round and continued on later rounds is called a multi-street bluff.

Optimal bluffing frequency

Game theory prescribes bluffing with some frequency so that opponents cannot profitably fold all their hands to a bet or call all of them. In simplified models, the bluff-to-value-bet ratio is set so that the opponent is indifferent between calling and folding.

Bluffing in other games

Bluffing is central to many other games, including bridge bidding, liar's dice, and negotiation in general, and is modelled in game theory by signalling and cheap-talk games.
