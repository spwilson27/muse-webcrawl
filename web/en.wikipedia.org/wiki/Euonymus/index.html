Euonymus is a genus of flowering plants in the staff vine family, Celastraceae, commonly called spindle or spindle tree. It comprises about 130 species of deciduous and evergreen shrubs, small trees and lianas, found mostly in East Asia, including the Himalayas, and also in Europe, Australasia, North America and Madagascar.

Description

The inconspicuous flowers occur in small groups and may be green, yellow, pink or dark purple. The fruit is a pink or white four- or five-valved pod-like berry, which splits open to reveal the fleshy-coated orange or red seeds. The seeds are eaten by frugivorous birds, which digest the fleshy seed coat and disperse the seeds in their droppings.

Cultivation and uses

Many species are grown as ornamental plants for their autumn colour and bright fruit. Euonymus alatus (burning bush) is widely planted but is considered invasive in parts of North America. The hard, fine-grained wood of the European spindle (Euonymus europaeus) was traditionally used to make spindles for spinning wool, as well as toothpicks, skewers and artists' charcoal.

Toxicity

All parts of the plant are poisonous if ingested, containing alkaloids and cardenolides. Symptoms of poisoning include abdominal pain, diarrhoea and, in severe cases, convulsions.
