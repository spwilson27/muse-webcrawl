GIUK gap

The GIUK gap (sometimes written G-I-UK gap) is an area in the northern Atlantic Ocean that forms a naval chokepoint. Its name is an acronym for Greenland, Iceland, and the United Kingdom, the two lines of which are the two naval gaps needed to be closed to create a blockade against Soviet/Russian naval forces seeking to enter the open Atlantic during the Cold War and after.

Strategic significance

The gap comprises the Greenland–Iceland gap and the Iceland–UK gap. During the Cold War, NATO monitored Soviet Northern Fleet submarines transiting the gap using the SOSUS underwater listening network, patrol aircraft such as the P-3 Orion and Nimrod, and attack submarines, because Soviet ballistic missile and attack submarines needed to pass through it to threaten transatlantic shipping and the American eastern seaboard. After the Cold War the monitoring effort declined, but renewed Russian submarine activity and the strategic importance of transatlantic undersea cables have revived attention on the gap since the 2010s.
