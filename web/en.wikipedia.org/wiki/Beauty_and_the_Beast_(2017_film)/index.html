Beauty and the Beast is a 2017 American musical romantic fantasy film directed by Bill Condon from a screenplay by Stephen Chbosky and Evan Spiliotopoulos. Co-produced by Walt Disney Pictures and Mandeville Films, it is a live-action remake of Disney's 1991 animated film of the same name, itself an adaptation of Jeanne-Marie Leprince de Beaumont's 1756 version of the fairy tale.

The film stars Emma Watson as Belle and Dan Stevens as the Beast, with Luke Evans, Kevin Kline, Josh Gad, Ewan McGregor, Stanley Tucci, Audra McDonald, Ian McKellen and Emma Thompson in supporting roles. It follows the story of a young prince cursed into a beast, who must learn to love and be loved before the last petal of an enchanted rose falls.

Plot

In Rococo-era France, a selfish prince refuses shelter to an enchantress during a storm; she curses him into a Beast, his servants into household objects, and erases the castle from the memories of the nearby village of Villeneuve. The spell can only be broken if the Beast learns to love and is loved in return before the last petal of an enchanted rose falls.

Belle, an inventive young woman in Villeneuve, seeks more than her provincial life and repeatedly rejects the vain hunter Gaston. When her widowed father Maurice is imprisoned by the Beast for taking a rose, Belle takes his place. Over time, aided by the servants Lumière, Cogsworth, Mrs. Potts, Chip and the wardrobe Madame de Garderobe, Belle and the Beast bond over books and shared loneliness. The remake expands the backstory: Belle's mother died of plague in Paris, and the Beast's cruelty was shaped by his abusive father.

After a romantic ballroom dance, the Beast frees Belle to rescue Maurice from a mob led by Gaston, who has falsely accused Maurice of insanity. Belle reveals the Beast's existence to the villagers, provoking Gaston to lead an attack on the castle. Gaston shoots the Beast and falls to his death; as the rose's last petal falls, Belle confesses her love, breaking the curse and restoring the prince and his servants to human form.

Production

Disney announced the remake in April 2014 as part of its series of live-action reimaginings of animated classics. Principal photography took place at Shepperton Studios in England from May to August 2015. The production combined live action with extensive motion capture and CGI; Dan Stevens performed the Beast on stilts in a motion-capture suit. Alan Menken returned to score the film, re-recording the original songs by himself and Howard Ashman and composing three new songs with lyricist Tim Rice, including "Evermore".

Music

The soundtrack includes new recordings of the 1991 songs — "Belle", "Be Our Guest", "Gaston", "Something There" and "Beauty and the Beast" — plus the new songs "Aria", "How Does a Moment Last Forever", "Evermore" and "Days in the Sun". Ariana Grande and John Legend performed the title song for the end credits, and Celine Dion performed "How Does a Moment Last Forever".

Release and reception

The film premiered in London on February 23, 2017, and was released in the United States on March 17, 2017, in standard, Disney Digital 3-D, RealD 3D and IMAX formats. It grossed over $1.2 billion worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing live-action musical film of all time at that point and the second-highest-grossing film of 2017. Reviews were generally positive, praising the performances, faithfulness to the original and production design, with some criticism of its length and close adherence to the 1991 film. It received two Academy Award nominations, for Best Production Design and Best Costume Design.

Controversy

Before release, director Bill Condon described a brief moment involving LeFou as an "exclusively gay moment", which drew both praise and boycott calls; the film was briefly restricted in Russia and temporarily withdrawn in Malaysia over a dispute about cutting the scene.
