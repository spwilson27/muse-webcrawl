Dag Hjalmar Agne Carl Hammarskjöld (29 July 1905 – 18 September 1961) was a Swedish economist and diplomat who served as the second Secretary-General of the United Nations from April 1953 until his death in a plane crash in 1961. He remains the youngest person to have held the post, aged 47 at his appointment.

Early career

Hammarskjöld was the son of Hjalmar Hammarskjöld, a former prime minister of Sweden. He studied at Uppsala University and Stockholm University, earning degrees in literature, philosophy, economics and law. He worked in the Swedish Ministry of Finance and the Bank of Sweden, and served as a cabinet minister in the Swedish government in the early 1950s.

Secretary-General

Appointed after Trygve Lie's resignation, Hammarskjöld reshaped the office into an active instrument of preventive diplomacy. He organized the first UN peacekeeping force during the Suez Crisis of 1956 (UNEF), negotiated the release of captured American airmen in China, and managed interventions during the Lebanon crisis of 1958 and the Congo Crisis of 1960–61.

Death and legacy

Hammarskjöld died when his aircraft crashed near Ndola, Northern Rhodesia (now Zambia), while en route to ceasefire negotiations in the Congo. The circumstances of the crash have been the subject of investigations and continuing controversy. He was posthumously awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1961. His spiritual journal, Markings, was published after his death.
