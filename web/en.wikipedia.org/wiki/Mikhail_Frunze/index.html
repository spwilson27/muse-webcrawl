Mikhail Frunze

Mikhail Vasilyevich Frunze (1885–1925) was a Bolshevik revolutionary and Red Army commander during the Russian Civil War, afterwards People's Commissar for Military and Naval Affairs of the Soviet Union. Born in Pishpek (now Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan) to a Moldovan peasant father and Russian mother, he joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1904 and sided with the Bolsheviks.

Civil War

Frunze became one of the most successful Red commanders. In 1919 he led the counter-offensive on the Eastern Front that defeated Admiral Kolchak's forces, and in 1920 he commanded the Southern Front against General Wrangel in Crimea, culminating in the storming of the Perekop isthmus. He also directed operations against the Basmachi in Central Asia and against Nestor Makhno's forces in Ukraine.

Reforms and death

Appointed chief of staff and then, in January 1925, head of the Red Army in succession to Trotsky, Frunze drove the military reform of 1924–25: a mixed cadre-and-militia system, unified doctrine and "one-man command". He died on 31 October 1925 during surgery for a stomach ulcer — an operation the Politburo had urged on him despite warnings, prompting enduring speculation, echoed in Boris Pilnyak's story "The Tale of the Unextinguished Moon", that his death was politically arranged.

Legacy

His birthplace Pishpek was renamed Frunze in 1926 (it reverted to Bishkek in 1991). The Frunze Military Academy in Moscow bore his name, as did streets, ships and the Frunze district pattern across the USSR.
