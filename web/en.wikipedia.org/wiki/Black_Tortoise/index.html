The Black Tortoise is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations. Known in Chinese as Xuánwǔ (玄武, "Dark Warrior"), it represents the north and the winter season, and is associated with the element of water. It is usually depicted as a tortoise entwined with a snake.

Symbolism

The Four Symbols divide the sky into four palaces, each containing seven lunar mansions. The Black Tortoise's seven mansions occupy the northern portion of the ecliptic. In Chinese cosmology the tortoise symbolised longevity and endurance, while the creature's dark colour linked it to water and the north. In Daoism, Xuanwu was elevated into a major deity, the Perfected Emperor of the North, widely worshipped from the Song dynasty onward, with his principal temple on Mount Wudang.

In other cultures

The Black Tortoise spread with Chinese astronomy and mythology to neighbouring cultures: it is Genbu in Japan, Hyeonmu in Korea, and Huyền Vũ in Vietnam. In these traditions it likewise guards the northern direction and appears in art, heraldry, and folklore. In South Korea, depictions of Hyeonmu appear in Goguryeo tomb murals.

The Four Symbols

  • Azure Dragon — east, spring, wood
  • Vermilion Bird — south, summer, fire
  • White Tiger — west, autumn, metal
  • Black Tortoise — north, winter, water
