Erythropoietin

Erythropoietin (EPO) is a glycoprotein cytokine secreted mainly by the kidneys in response to low blood oxygen (hypoxia). It stimulates production of red blood cells (erythropoiesis) in the bone marrow. Recombinant human EPO is a major medicine for anaemia — and a notorious performance-enhancing drug in endurance sport.

Physiology

When oxygen delivery falls, hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs) increase EPO transcription. EPO binds its receptor on erythroid progenitor cells, preventing apoptosis and driving maturation; red cell mass rises over days to weeks, improving oxygen carriage. Low EPO explains the anaemia of chronic kidney disease.

Medical use

Epoetin alfa and related erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, produced recombinantly since 1989, transformed treatment of renal anaemia, reducing transfusions. Longer-acting versions include darbepoetin alfa and methoxy polyethylene glycol-epoetin beta. Overcorrection raises risks of thrombosis and cardiovascular events, so dosing targets are conservative.

Doping

From the early 1990s, illicit EPO use in cycling and distance running boosted haematocrit — sometimes fatally — until a urine test was introduced in 2000 and the Athlete Biological Passport later made detection more robust.
