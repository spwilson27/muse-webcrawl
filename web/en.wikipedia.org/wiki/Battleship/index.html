Battleship

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Second World War battleship firing a broadside, smoke and spray around it]
USS Iowa firing her 16-inch guns.

A battleship is a large armoured warship with a main battery of heavy guns. Battleships dominated naval warfare from the 1880s until the Second World War: Britain\'s HMS Dreadnought (1906) reset the type with all-big-gun armament and steam turbines, sparking a naval arms race. Giants such as Japan\'s Yamato — the heaviest battleship ever at over 71,000 tons — were eclipsed by aircraft carriers, and the last battleships, the US Iowa class, were retired by the 1990s–2000s.

From ironclads to dreadnoughts

Steam and shell guns produced ironclad warships from the 1860s; \'pre-dreadnoughts\' mixed gun calibres. Dreadnought\'s uniform main battery and turbine speed made all earlier battleships obsolete overnight, lending her name to the type and its successors \'super-dreadnoughts\'.

World wars

The Battle of Jutland (1916) was the only full clash of battle fleets in the First World War. In the Second, carriers and aircraft outranged guns — Bismarck, Yamato and Musashi were all sunk by air attack — though battleships remained valuable for shore bombardment, as at Normandy.

Final decades

The four Iowa-class ships were repeatedly reactivated (Korea, Vietnam, and the 1980s with Tomahawk missiles and a famous 1991 Gulf War role) before becoming museum ships. No battleship remains in active service anywhere.
