Vanpool

A vanpool is a form of shared-ride public or employer-sponsored transport in which a group of commuters — typically five to fifteen people — travel together in a van provided by a transit agency, employer, or commercial operator. One participant usually drives, sometimes with a backup driver, and riders split the operating costs, which are often subsidised through employer benefits or pre-tax commuter programmes. Vanpooling is a form of ridesharing suited to long commutes and corridors poorly served by fixed-route transit.

Operation

Programmes are administered by regional transit agencies, transportation management associations, employers, or private vendors. The operator supplies the vehicle, insurance, maintenance and fuel arrangements; the group sets its route and schedule, collecting members at homes or park-and-ride lots. In the United States, vanpools may qualify as "transit" for commuter tax benefits and high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lane access, and larger programmes — such as those in the Puget Sound region and Houston — rank among the biggest public transport services by passenger trips in their areas.

Benefits and limits

Vanpooling reduces single-occupancy vehicle trips, emissions and parking demand at relatively low public cost per trip, and can be created or dissolved quickly as demand changes. Its limitations include the need for a critical mass of commuters with similar origins, destinations and schedules, and the coordination burden on volunteer drivers.
