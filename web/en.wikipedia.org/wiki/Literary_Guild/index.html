Literary Guild

The Literary Guild of America is an American book club founded in 1927 by Samuel Craig and Harold K. Guinzburg. It was created partly as a competitor to the Book of the Month Club, which had been founded the previous year. Members received a monthly magazine, Wings, containing reviews and a featured selection chosen by an editorial board, and could buy editions at a discount.

History

The club's first selection was The Story of Mankind by Hendrik Willem van Loon-related offerings aside, early picks included Richard Halliburton's The Royal Road to Romance (the actual first selection, 1927). The Guild pioneered the "negative option" model refined by book clubs generally: the featured book was shipped automatically unless the member declined it. Club editions were manufactured more cheaply than trade editions, a practice sometimes criticised by bibliophiles, but the clubs greatly widened the market for serious fiction and nonfiction in small-town America.

Over the following decades the Literary Guild changed owners several times, passing through Doubleday and later Bertelsmann's book club operations, and in time became a brand within a consolidated direct-mail and online bookselling business alongside the Book-of-the-Month Club.

Selected notable selections

  • The Royal Road to Romance by Richard Halliburton (first selection)
  • Works by Pearl S. Buck, including The Good Earth-era bestsellers
  • Main selections and alternates spanning literary fiction, biography and history through the 20th century
