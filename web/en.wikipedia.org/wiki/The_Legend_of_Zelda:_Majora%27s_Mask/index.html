The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask

The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask is a 2000 action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo 64. The sixth main instalment in The Legend of Zelda series, it is the direct sequel to Ocarina of Time and is noted for its darker tone and three-day time cycle.

DeveloperNintendo EAD
PublisherNintendo
DirectorEiji Aonuma
Release27 April 2000 (Japan)

Gameplay

Link has three days — endlessly repeated using the Song of Time — to stop the Moon from crashing into Termina. Transformation masks turn Link into a Deku, Goron or Zora, and a schedule-driven world lets him follow dozens of characters through side quests recorded in the Bombers' Notebook.

Reception and legacy

Developed in about a year reusing Ocarina of Time's engine, the game sold about 3.36 million copies and was initially divisive, but has since been reappraised as one of the series' most distinctive and acclaimed entries. A remake, Majora's Mask 3D, appeared on Nintendo 3DS in 2015.
