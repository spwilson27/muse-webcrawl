The Sandinista National Liberation Front (Spanish: Frente Sandinista de Liberación Nacional, FSLN) is a Nicaraguan political party and former revolutionary movement. Founded in 1961, it overthrew the Somoza dictatorship in 1979, governed Nicaragua through the 1980s, returned to power in 2007 under Daniel Ortega, and has ruled since.

Origins and revolution

The FSLN was founded by Carlos Fonseca, Tomás Borge and Silvio Mayorga, among others, taking its name and inspiration from Augusto César Sandino, who had resisted the U.S. occupation of Nicaragua in the 1920s–30s. After years of guerrilla struggle marked by setbacks — Fonseca was killed in 1976 — the movement unified its factions and, amid nationwide insurrection, toppled President Anastasio Somoza Debayle in July 1979, ending the Somoza family's four-decade rule.

In government, 1979–1990

The Sandinista government (first a junta, then under President Daniel Ortega after the 1984 elections) launched literacy and health campaigns, land reform and nationalisations. Its rule was dominated by the Contra War: the United States, under President Ronald Reagan, funded and armed the Contra insurgency, contributing to severe economic hardship, hyperinflation and a military draft. The conflict underlay the Iran–Contra scandal in the United States. In the 1990 elections the FSLN unexpectedly lost to Violeta Chamorro's UNO coalition and handed over power peacefully.

Return to power

Ortega returned to the presidency after winning the 2006 election, and the FSLN has since consolidated control over state institutions, winning subsequent elections amid growing international criticism. Mass protests in April 2018 over social-security reforms were met with a violent crackdown that left hundreds dead, prompting sanctions and widespread condemnation; the government describes the events as a failed coup attempt. In 2021 Ortega won a fourth consecutive term after leading opposition candidates were jailed. In 2025 constitutional changes made Ortega and his wife, Vice President Rosario Murillo, "co-presidents".
