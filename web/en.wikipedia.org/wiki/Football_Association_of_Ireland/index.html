The Football Association of Ireland (FAI; Irish: Cumann Peile na hÉireann) is the governing body for association football in the Republic of Ireland. It organises the League of Ireland, the FAI Cup and the Republic of Ireland men's and women's national teams, and oversees the development of the game at all levels.

History

The FAI was founded in Dublin in 1921, during the Irish War of Independence, after clubs in the south broke away from the Belfast-based Irish Football Association (IFA). The split left two governing bodies on the island of Ireland, a situation that persists today, with the IFA governing Northern Ireland. The Free State team played its first international at the 1924 Paris Olympics, and the association joined FIFA in 1923. The FAI was a founding member of UEFA in 1954.

The association's profile rose with the national team's qualification for Euro 1988 and the 1990 World Cup under Jack Charlton. The Aviva Stadium in Dublin, opened in 2010 on the site of Lansdowne Road, is owned jointly by the FAI and the Irish Rugby Football Union and hosts the national teams' home matches. In 2019–2020 the association underwent major governance reform and financial restructuring after a governance and funding crisis, with support from the Irish government and UEFA.

Competitions

  • League of Ireland (Premier Division and First Division)
  • FAI Cup and League of Ireland Cup (formerly)
  • Women's National League
  • FAI Intermediate and Junior Cups
