Bánh mì

Bánh mì (from Vietnamese bánh mì, literally "bread") is a Vietnamese sandwich that combines a short baguette with a thin, crisp crust and a soft, airy interior, filled with a fusion of French-derived ingredients (pâté, mayonnaise, cold cuts) and Vietnamese ingredients (pickled carrots and daikon, cucumber, coriander, chilli, and seasoned meats). It is one of the most internationally recognised dishes of Vietnamese cuisine.

History

The baguette was introduced to Vietnam during French colonial rule in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Vietnamese bakers adapted it, using rice flour in part and a different baking process to produce a lighter, crispier loaf. The modern sandwich developed in Saigon in the 1950s — the Hòa Mã shop (opened 1958) is often credited with popularising the filled bánh mì — and spread worldwide after 1975 with Vietnamese emigration. In 2011 the Oxford English Dictionary added "banh mi" as an entry.

Ingredients

A classic bánh mì thập cẩm ("mixed") includes pâté, Vietnamese sausage (chả lụa), sliced pork or head cheese, butter or mayonnaise, pickled carrot and daikon (đồ chua), cucumber spears, coriander, and Maggi seasoning or soy sauce, with optional fresh chilli and onions. Popular variations include bánh mì thịt nướng (grilled pork), bánh mì xíu mại (meatballs), bánh mì trứng (fried egg), and bánh mì chay (vegetarian, with tofu).

Global popularity

Bánh mì has become a fixture of street food and restaurants across the United States, Australia, France, and elsewhere, praised for its combination of crisp bread, rich meats, and fresh, acidic vegetables. It is frequently cited among the world's best sandwiches.
