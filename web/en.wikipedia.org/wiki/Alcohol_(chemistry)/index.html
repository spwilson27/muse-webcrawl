Alcohol (chemistry)

In chemistry, an alcohol is a type of organic compound that carries at least one hydroxyl (−OH) functional group bound to a saturated carbon atom. The term was originally applied to ethanol, the alcohol found in alcoholic drinks, but now denotes the whole class. Alcohols range from the simple methanol and ethanol to sugars, steroids such as cholesterol, and complex natural products.

Classification

Alcohols are classified by the number of carbon substituents on the carbon bearing the hydroxyl group: primary (1°), secondary (2°) and tertiary (3°) alcohols. Compounds with more than one hydroxyl group are called polyols — diols (such as ethylene glycol), triols (such as glycerol) and so on. Phenols, in which the hydroxyl group is attached to an aromatic ring, are often treated as a separate class because of their distinct acidity.

Nomenclature

In the IUPAC system, alcohols are named by adding the suffix -ol to the parent hydrocarbon name (methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol), with locants indicating the position of the hydroxyl group. Common names combine the alkyl group name with "alcohol," as in ethyl alcohol or isopropyl alcohol.

Properties

The hydroxyl group makes alcohols polar and capable of hydrogen bonding, giving them higher boiling points and greater water solubility than hydrocarbons of similar mass; solubility decreases as the carbon chain lengthens. Alcohols are weakly amphoteric: they can act as very weak acids (pKa around 16–18) or as bases at the oxygen atom.

Reactions and uses

Characteristic reactions include oxidation (primary alcohols to aldehydes and carboxylic acids, secondary alcohols to ketones), dehydration to alkenes or ethers, and esterification with carboxylic acids. Alcohols are used as solvents, fuels and fuel additives (ethanol, methanol), disinfectants (ethanol, isopropanol), antifreeze (ethylene glycol) and as intermediates in the manufacture of plastics, detergents and pharmaceuticals. Ethanol is produced industrially by hydration of ethylene and by fermentation of sugars.

Toxicity

Ethanol is the principal psychoactive alcohol consumed in beverages. Methanol is highly toxic, causing blindness and death through its metabolite formic acid; ethylene glycol is similarly poisonous. In the body, alcohols are metabolized chiefly by the enzymes alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase.
