Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young

Crosby, Stills & Nash (CSN) is a folk-rock supergroup formed in 1968 by David Crosby (formerly of the Byrds), Stephen Stills (Buffalo Springfield) and Graham Nash (the Hollies), known for tight vocal harmonies. With the addition of Neil Young in 1969, they performed as Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young (CSNY).

Career

Their debut album Crosby, Stills & Nash (1969) included "Suite: Judy Blue Eyes" and "Marrakesh Express". Their second public performance was at Woodstock. With Young, Déjà Vu (1970) reached number one in the US, featuring "Teach Your Children", "Our House" and "Ohio", written in response to the Kent State shootings.

Legacy

The members' solo careers and shifting combinations continued for decades amid celebrated reunions and well-publicised feuds. Crosby died in 2023, effectively ending the group.
