Toulouse (Occitan: Tolosa) is the capital of the French region of Occitanie and the prefecture of the Haute-Garonne department, in southwestern France. It lies on the banks of the River Garonne, midway between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea. The fourth-largest city in France, it is nicknamed la Ville rose ("the Pink City") for the terracotta bricks used in many of its buildings.

History

Toulouse was the Roman city of Tolosa and later the capital of the Visigothic kingdom in the 5th century. In the Middle Ages the counts of Toulouse ruled one of the great principalities of southern France; the city was a centre of the Cathar movement and was besieged during the Albigensian Crusade, passing to the French crown in the 13th century. Under the Ancien Régime it prospered on the trade in woad (pastel) dye, which financed many of its Renaissance mansions. The University of Toulouse, founded in 1229, is one of the oldest in Europe.

Economy

Toulouse is the centre of the European aerospace industry: it hosts the headquarters and main assembly lines of Airbus, the French space agency CNES and its Toulouse Space Centre, and a dense cluster of aerospace suppliers and research institutes. It is also a major centre for electronics, biotechnology and higher education, with one of France's largest student populations.

Landmarks and culture

  • The Capitole, the neoclassical city hall facing the Place du Capitole.
  • The Basilica of Saint-Sernin, the largest remaining Romanesque church in Europe, on the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela.
  • The Jacobins convent, a masterpiece of Southern Gothic architecture, housing the relics of Thomas Aquinas.
  • The Canal du Midi, a UNESCO-listed 17th-century canal linking the Garonne to the Mediterranean.
  • The Cité de l'espace, a space museum and theme park.

The city's rugby union club, Stade Toulousain, is among the most successful in Europe. Local specialities include cassoulet, Toulouse sausage, and violet-flavoured confectionery.

Transport

Toulouse is served by Toulouse-Blagnac Airport, a metro system (inaugurated 1993), tramways, and high-speed rail links toward Bordeaux and Paris. The Garonne and the Canal du Midi remain defining features of its urban landscape.
