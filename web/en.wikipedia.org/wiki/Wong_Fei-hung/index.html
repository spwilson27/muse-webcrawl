Wong Fei-hung

Born: 9 July 1847, Foshan, Guangdong, Qing Empire

Died: 25 March 1925 (aged 77), Guangzhou, Republic of China

Style: Hung Ga (Hung拳)

Teacher: Wong Kei-ying

Occupation: Martial artist, physician

Wong Fei-hung (born Wong Sek-cheung; 1847–1925) was a Chinese martial artist, physician, and folk hero of the late Qing dynasty and early Republic of China. A master of the Hung Ga style of kung fu, he is regarded as one of the most famous Cantonese martial artists in history and has been the subject of numerous films, television series, and novels.

Life

Wong was born in Foshan, Guangdong, the son of Wong Kei-ying, himself one of the famed Ten Tigers of Canton. He learned Hung Ga from his father and, according to tradition, aspects of his craft from the wandering master Lam Sai-wing's teachers and others. As a young man he demonstrated martial arts in the street alongside his father and worked as an instructor to militia units and later to the Black Flag Army of Liu Yongfu.

Wong practised medicine at his clinic, Po Chi Lam (寶芝林), in Canton (Guangzhou), where he treated patients regardless of their ability to pay. He was celebrated for his skill with acupuncture and herbal medicine. His famous techniques in legend include the "No-Shadow Kick" (mou ying geuk) and the Tiger Crane form of Hung Ga. He died in 1925; much of the Po Chi Lam collection burned after his death.

Legacy

Wong Fei-hung's life has been adapted to film more often than almost any other historical figure. Actor Kwan Tak-hing played him in a series of some 77 black-and-white Cantonese films beginning in 1949, fixing the image of Wong as a grave, Confucian folk hero. Later portrayals include Gordon Liu in Challenge of the Masters (1976), Jackie Chan in Drunken Master (1978), and Jet Li in Tsui Hark's Once Upon a Time in China series (1991–1997). The melody "A Man Should Better Himself", adapted from the traditional tune "General's Mandate", has become inseparably associated with the character.
