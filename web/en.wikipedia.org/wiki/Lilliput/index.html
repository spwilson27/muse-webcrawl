Lilliput is a fictional island nation that appears in the first part of Jonathan Swift's 1726 satirical novel Gulliver's Travels. Its inhabitants, the Lilliputians, stand about six inches (15 cm) tall — one twelfth the height of a normal human — and everything on the island is built to the same miniature scale.

Description

Ship's surgeon Lemuel Gulliver is washed ashore in Lilliput after a wreck and wakes to find himself tied down by the tiny inhabitants. Swift locates Lilliput (with its rival neighbour Blefuscu) in the Indian Ocean, northwest of Van Diemen's Land (Tasmania). The island's capital is Mildendo. Lilliputian government, law, and customs are described in elaborate mock-ethnographic detail: officials are chosen by rope-dancing and leaping over sticks, funerals bury the dead head-downwards, and fraud is punished more severely than theft.

Satire

Lilliput is a satire of the court and politics of Georgian Britain. The bitter war between Lilliput and Blefuscu over which end of a boiled egg should be broken — "Big-Endians" versus "Little-Endians" — parodies religious and dynastic conflicts between Britain and France and between Protestant denominations. The word Lilliputian has entered English as an adjective for the extremely small and, by extension, for the trivial and petty.
