Food delivery

Food delivery is a courier service in which a restaurant, store, or independent food-delivery company delivers food to a customer. An order is typically made either through a restaurant or grocer's website or mobile app, or through a food ordering company. The delivered items can include entrées, sides, drinks, desserts, or groceries and are typically delivered in boxes or bags. The delivery person will normally drive a car, but may use bicycles or motor scooters in cities.

History

The first food delivery services were for military personnel; the Romans set up facilities along their road network to supply travelling soldiers. Modern restaurant delivery grew with pizza chains in the post-war United States, which built their business models around telephone orders and rapid delivery. The first online food ordering service, Pizza Hut's PizzaNet, launched in 1994, and during the 2010s smartphone-based platforms such as Uber Eats, DoorDash, Deliveroo, and Meituan greatly expanded the market, connecting customers to many restaurants from a single app.

Market and business models

Ordering platforms generally charge restaurants a commission on each order and may charge customers delivery and service fees. Couriers are commonly engaged as independent contractors, a model that has generated debate about workers' rights and employment status in many countries. The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated adoption of food delivery, as lockdowns closed dining rooms and consumers shifted to takeaway and delivery.
