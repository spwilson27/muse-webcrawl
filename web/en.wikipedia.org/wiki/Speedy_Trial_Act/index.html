Speedy Trial Act

The Speedy Trial Act of 1974 (18 U.S.C. §§ 3161–3174) establishes time limits for completing the various stages of a federal criminal prosecution in the United States. It implements the Sixth Amendment right to a speedy trial.

Speedy Trial Act of 1974
Enacted: 1974
Codified: 18 U.S.C. §§ 3161–3174
Purpose: Time limits for federal criminal trials

Requirements

Information or indictment must generally be filed within 30 days of arrest, and trial must commence within 70 days of indictment or first appearance, whichever is later. Certain delays are excluded, for example delays resulting from continuances granted in the ends of justice, pre-trial motions, and the unavailability of the defendant or an essential witness.

Sanctions

If the limits are not met, the indictment must be dismissed on motion of the defendant, either with or without prejudice, considering the seriousness of the offence, the facts leading to dismissal, and the impact of reprosecution.

Case law

Important decisions include Zedner v. United States (2006), holding a defendant may not prospectively waive the Act, and Bloate v. United States (2010) on excluded time for preparing pre-trial motions. The constitutional test remains that of Barker v. Wingo.
