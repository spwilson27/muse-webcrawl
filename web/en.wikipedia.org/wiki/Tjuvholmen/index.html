Tjuvholmen is a neighbourhood and small peninsula in the Frogner borough of Oslo, Norway, located just west of Aker Brygge on the Oslofjord. Its name, meaning "Thieves' Islet", derives from its history in the 18th century as a place where thieves were hanged.

History and redevelopment

For much of the 20th century the area was used by the port and industry, including shipyard facilities of Akers Mekaniske Verksted. As part of the Fjord City redevelopment of Oslo's waterfront, Tjuvholmen was transformed in the 2000s and 2010s into a mixed-use district of offices, apartments, hotels, restaurants, and cultural institutions, connected to Aker Brygge by bridges and organised around canals and public spaces.

Astrup Fearnley Museum

The district's landmark is the Astrup Fearnley Museum of Modern Art, which moved to Tjuvholmen in 2012 in a building designed by Renzo Piano, distinguished by its sweeping sail-like glass roofs. The surrounding sculpture park, designed with the museum, includes works by artists such as Anish Kapoor and Louise Bourgeois. The area also features a public bathing area and viewing tower.
