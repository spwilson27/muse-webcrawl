Herpesviridae

Herpesviridae is a large family of DNA viruses that cause infections and certain diseases in animals, including humans. The members of this family are also known as herpesviruses. A common feature is the ability to establish lifelong latent infection in the host, with periodic reactivation. More than 130 herpesviruses are known, nine of which primarily infect humans.

Structure and replication

Herpesviruses are large, enveloped, double-stranded DNA viruses with an icosahedral capsid surrounded by a protein tegument layer. Their genomes (roughly 125–290 kbp) encode up to several hundred proteins. Replication occurs in the cell nucleus; after primary infection the virus retreats to a latent state — in nerve ganglia for the alphaherpesviruses, in lymphocytes for the gamma- and betaherpesviruses.

Human herpesviruses

The nine human types include herpes simplex viruses 1 and 2 (cold sores and genital herpes), varicella-zoster virus (chickenpox and shingles), Epstein–Barr virus (infectious mononucleosis, associated with several cancers), cytomegalovirus, human herpesviruses 6 and 7 (roseola), and Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (HHV-8).

Taxonomy

The family is divided into three subfamilies: Alphaherpesvirinae (fast-growing, neurotropic), Betaherpesvirinae (slow-growing, including cytomegalovirus) and Gammaherpesvirinae (lymphotropic, including Epstein–Barr virus). Herpesviruses infect virtually all vertebrate groups and some invertebrates.
