Mission-type tactics

Mission-type tactics (German: Auftragstaktik) is a method of military leadership in which the commander gives subordinates a clearly defined mission, the forces and time available, and freedom to decide how to accomplish it. It contrasts with detailed command (Befehlstaktik) specifying exactly how tasks are to be carried out.

Origins

The concept developed in the Prussian and later German armies in the 19th century, associated with Helmuth von Moltke the Elder, who held that no plan survives contact with the enemy and that subordinate initiative was essential. It became central to German operational practice and to Blitzkrieg.

Modern use

Mission-type tactics underlie the modern doctrines of mission command adopted by the United States, British, and other NATO armies, emphasising commander's intent, decentralised execution, and trust.
