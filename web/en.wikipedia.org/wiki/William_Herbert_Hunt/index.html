William Herbert Hunt (1862 – 20 November 1951) was an Australian architect active principally in Western Australia during the gold-boom era of the 1890s and early twentieth century. He is remembered for a series of substantial commercial and hotel buildings in Perth and Fremantle.

Career

Hunt trained as an architect in South Australia and worked in Sydney before moving to Western Australia during the gold rushes, when the rapid growth of Perth created strong demand for new commercial architecture. His best-known work is the Palace Hotel on St Georges Terrace, Perth (1897), a grand three-storey Federation Free Classical hotel built for the American entrepreneur John De Baun. He also designed warehouses, offices and hotels in Fremantle and Kalgoorlie as the boom spread.

Style and legacy

Hunt's work is characterised by rendered masonry, classical detailing and generous verandah and tower elements suited to the Western Australian climate. Several of his buildings are heritage listed. He practised in Perth for decades and died there in 1951, aged 89.
