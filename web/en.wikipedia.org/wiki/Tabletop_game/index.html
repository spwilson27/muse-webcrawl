Tabletop game

Typical components of tabletop games: boards, cards, dice and tokens

A tabletop game is a game that is normally played on a table or other flat surface. The term is used to distinguish physical, face-to-face games from video games and sports, and encompasses several major categories.

Categories

Board games involve the movement or placement of pieces on a pre-marked board, from ancient games such as chess and Go to modern designs such as Catan (1995), which helped launch the modern hobby board game boom. Card games use playing cards as the primary component, ranging from traditional games like poker and bridge to collectible card games such as Magic: The Gathering. Dice games centre on dice as the main randomising element. Tabletop role-playing games, pioneered by Dungeons & Dragons (1974), involve players narrating the actions of characters guided by a gamemaster and rule system. Miniature wargames simulate battles using model figures and terrain.

History

Tabletop games are among the oldest forms of play: the Royal Game of Ur and the Egyptian game Senet date back several thousand years. Chess evolved in India and Persia, and playing cards spread from China through Europe. The 20th century saw mass-market titles such as Monopoly (1935) and Scrabble, followed by a renaissance of designer ("Eurogame") board games from the 1990s onward, with a large and growing hobby market, conventions such as Spiel in Essen, and crowdfunding expanding the industry further.
