The Ice Bucket Challenge is a viral activity in which a person dumps a bucket of ice water over their head — or has it dumped for them — films the act, posts the video on social media, and nominates others to do the same within 24 hours or make a donation to charity. In the summer of 2014 the challenge became a global phenomenon in support of research into amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, also known as Lou Gehrig's disease or motor neurone disease), raising unprecedented sums and public awareness for the condition.

History

Challenges involving cold water and charitable donations circulated in various forms in 2013 and early 2014. The ALS connection was cemented in July 2014 when golfer Chris Kennedy nominated his wife's cousin, whose husband had ALS, and — decisively — when former Boston College baseball player Pete Frates, who had ALS, and fellow patient Pat Quinn promoted the challenge through their Boston networks. From there it spread explosively through August 2014. Participants included Mark Zuckerberg, Bill Gates (who built a Rube Goldberg-style rig for the purpose), Oprah Winfrey, and heads of government; President Barack Obama declined the dousing and donated instead.

Impact

The ALS Association reported receiving about $115 million in donations during the 2014 campaign period, compared with a few million in a comparable normal period, and worldwide ALS organisations raised an estimated $220 million. The money substantially increased ALS research funding; the Association credited challenge-funded Project MinE with contributing to the identification of the ALS-associated gene NEK1 in 2016. The challenge also permanently enlarged the donor base and research community for a previously obscure disease.

Criticism

Commentators criticised the challenge for "slacktivism", for wasting water during droughts (particularly in California), and for the share of participants who doused themselves without donating or mentioning ALS. A small number of injuries and one death were linked to imitations and stunts. The ALS Association responded by encouraging water conservation and by publishing detailed accounts of how donations were spent. The challenge has recurred in smaller anniversary campaigns since 2014.
