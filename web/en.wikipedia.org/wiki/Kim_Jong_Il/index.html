Kim Jong Il

Kim Jong Il (Korean: 김정일; 16 February 1941/1942 – 17 December 2011) was a North Korean politician who ruled the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea) from 1994 until his death in 2011. The second of the Kim dynasty's supreme leaders, he succeeded his father, state founder Kim Il Sung, and was succeeded by his son Kim Jong Un. He held the posts of General Secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea, Chairman of the National Defence Commission and Supreme Commander of the Korean People's Army, governing through the "military-first" (Songun) policy amid famine and international confrontation over North Korea's nuclear weapons programme.

Early life and rise

Official North Korean biography places Kim's birth on Mount Paektu in 1942, heralded by a double rainbow; Soviet records indicate he was born in 1941 in the village of Vyatskoye near Khabarovsk, where his father commanded a Red Army battalion of Korean exiles. Educated in Pyongyang (with periods in China and Malta), he joined the Workers' Party apparatus in the 1960s, working in propaganda and the arts — he is credited with treatises on cinema and opera — and was gradually groomed as successor, being publicly confirmed at the 6th Party Congress in 1980.

Rule

Kim assumed power on his father's death in July 1994, after a three-year mourning period, taking the top party and military posts. His rule coincided with the "Arduous March" famine of the mid-1990s, in which hundreds of thousands — by some estimates over a million — died, and with economic contraction and dependence on China. He elevated the military through Songun politics, maintained the regime's vast system of political prison camps (kwanliso), and presided over North Korea's first nuclear tests in 2006 and 2009 and long-range missile development, interspersed with negotiations such as the Six-Party Talks. In June 2000 he held the first inter-Korean summit with South Korea's Kim Dae-jung in Pyongyang.

Personality cult and death

Kim was the object of an intense personality cult as the "Dear Leader", portrayed as a polymath and military genius; foreign accounts emphasised his private film collection, fear of flying (he travelled by armoured train), and reclusive diplomacy. After a reported stroke in 2008, his youngest son Kim Jong Un was elevated; Kim died of a heart attack on 17 December 2011, reportedly aboard his train, and was succeeded smoothly. His body lies in state beside his father's at the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun.
