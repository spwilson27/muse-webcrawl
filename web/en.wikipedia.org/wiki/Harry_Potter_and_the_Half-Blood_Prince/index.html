Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince

Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince is a fantasy novel written by British author J. K. Rowling and the sixth and penultimate novel in the Harry Potter series. Set during Harry Potter's sixth year at Hogwarts, the novel explores the past of the boy wizard's nemesis, Lord Voldemort, and Harry's preparations for the final battle against him.

Author: J. K. Rowling

Published: 16 July 2005

Publisher: Bloomsbury (UK); Scholastic (US)

Pages: 607 (UK edition)

Preceded by: Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix

Followed by: Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows

Plot

As Voldemort's forces wage open war on both the wizarding and Muggle worlds, Harry begins private lessons with headmaster Albus Dumbledore, viewing memories in the Pensieve that trace Voldemort's origins as Tom Riddle and revealing his creation of Horcruxes — objects containing fragments of his soul that render him effectively immortal. At school, Harry excels in Potions using an old textbook annotated by the mysterious "Half-Blood Prince", who proves to be a young Severus Snape. Harry dates Ginny Weasley, while Ron and Hermione's friendship strains over Ron's relationship with Lavender Brown.

Draco Malfoy, tasked by Voldemort with killing Dumbledore, smuggles Death Eaters into Hogwarts through a pair of Vanishing Cabinets. After Harry and a weakened Dumbledore retrieve a locket Horcrux from a seaside cave, they return to find the Dark Mark over the school; atop the Astronomy Tower, Dumbledore is disarmed by Draco but killed by Snape with the Killing Curse. Harry discovers the locket is a fake, left with a note by "R.A.B.", and resolves not to return to Hogwarts, instead hunting the remaining Horcruxes with Ron and Hermione.

Publication and reception

The book was released on 16 July 2005 and sold a record-breaking nine million copies in its first 24 hours in the United States and United Kingdom combined. It won the 2006 British Book of the Year award. Reviewers praised its darker tone and emotional depth, though the killing of Dumbledore provoked strong reactions among readers.

Film adaptation

The film adaptation, directed by David Yates and starring Daniel Radcliffe, Rupert Grint, and Emma Watson, was released in July 2009 and grossed over $934 million worldwide.
