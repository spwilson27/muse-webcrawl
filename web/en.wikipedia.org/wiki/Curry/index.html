Curry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A bowl of curry with rice and naan bread]

Curry is a dish with a sauce seasoned with spices, mainly associated with the cuisines of the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia. The English word derives from the Tamil kari; British rule carried the concept home, where curry became a national favourite, and Japanese curry — introduced via the Royal Navy — became a distinct sweet-thick style served over rice and as kare pan and curry udon.

Curries vary from fiery vindaloo and creamy korma to Thai green curry with coconut milk, Japanese curry roux, and the dry spice-rubbed dishes also called curries. Core aromatics include turmeric, cumin, coriander, chillies, ginger, garlic and regional blends such as garam masala and curry powder — the latter a British invention.

Contents
1. History

History

Spiced stews are ancient in India; chillies arrived from the Americas after 1500 and transformed them. The British East India Company era standardised "curry" for export, and the first London curry houses (from 1809–10) served officials returning from India. Post-1945 South Asian migration made curry houses a British high-street fixture, producing hybrid dishes such as chicken tikka masala.
