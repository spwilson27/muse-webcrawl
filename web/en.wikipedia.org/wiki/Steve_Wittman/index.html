Steve Wittman

Born: April 5, 1904
Died: April 27, 1995 (aged 91)
Occupation: Air racer, aircraft designer
Known for: Wittman Tailwind, Wittman W-10

Sylvester Joseph "Steve" Wittman (April 5, 1904 – April 27, 1995) was an American air racer and aircraft designer, regarded as one of the most influential figures in homebuilt aviation.

Career

Wittman began flying in the 1920s and competed in air races for more than five decades, flying aircraft such as Bonzo and Chief Oshkosh. His small, fast racing designs set numerous records and influenced generations of builders.

His most famous homebuilt design, the Wittman Tailwind (1953), is a high-wing two-seat monoplane widely credited with helping launch the modern homebuilding movement. The later Wittman W-10 Tailwind derivative remained popular for decades.

Legacy

The terminal at Wittman Regional Airport in Oshkosh, Wisconsin — home of EAA AirVenture — is named for him. Wittman was inducted into the National Aviation Hall of Fame in 1984.
