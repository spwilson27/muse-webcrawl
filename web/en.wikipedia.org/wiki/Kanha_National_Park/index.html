Kanha National Park

Kanha National Park, also known as Kanha–Kisli National Park, is one of the largest national parks of India, located in the Mandla and Balaghat districts of Madhya Pradesh. It was created in 1955 and became a tiger reserve in 1973 under Project Tiger. The park covers a core area of about 940 km² in the Satpura hill ranges.

Flora and fauna

Kanha's landscape of sal and bamboo forests, grassy meadows and ravines supports a notable population of Bengal tigers, Indian leopards, sloth bears, dholes and Indian bison (gaur). The park is famed for saving the hardground barasingha (swamp deer), whose population recovered from near extinction — about 66 animals in 1970 — through dedicated conservation centred on the Kanha meadow.

In culture

The forests of the region are widely believed to have inspired Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book (1894), although Kipling himself never visited Kanha and drew on other accounts and nearby areas such as Seoni. Kanha remains one of India's best-known and best-managed tiger reserves and a major wildlife tourism destination.
