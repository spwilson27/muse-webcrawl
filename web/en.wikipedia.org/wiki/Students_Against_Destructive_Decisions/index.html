Students Against Destructive Decisions (SADD), originally Students Against Driving Drunk and later Students Against Destructive Decisions, is a non-profit organisation focused on peer-to-peer education about making safe decisions, particularly among adolescents in the United States. Its chapters are based mainly in middle schools, high schools and colleges.

History

SADD was founded in 1981 by Robert Anastas, a health teacher and hockey coach at Wayland High School in Massachusetts, after two students from the school were killed in separate alcohol-related car crashes. With his students, Anastas developed the "Contract for Life", in which teenagers and parents agreed that the student could call home for a safe ride at any time, and parents deferred discussion until later. The model spread rapidly through schools and was endorsed by federal road-safety agencies.

Expanded mission

In 1997 the organisation changed its name from Students Against Driving Drunk to Students Against Destructive Decisions, broadening its remit beyond impaired driving to underage drinking, drug use, risky driving behaviours such as texting while driving and failure to use seat belts, as well as teen suicide prevention and personal safety. SADD's approach emphasises students as the primary messengers to their peers, supported by adult advisors and national programmes and conferences.
