The 1952 Winter Olympics, officially the VI Olympic Winter Games, were a winter multi-sport event held in Oslo, Norway, from 14 to 25 February 1952. They were the first Winter Olympics held in a large capital city and in Scandinavia, the birthplace of modern skiing, and were widely praised for their organisation. The Olympic flame was lit in Morgedal, the home of ski pioneer Sondre Norheim, and carried to Oslo by relay on skis.

Highlights

Norwegian speed skater Hjalmar Andersen was the star of the Games, winning three gold medals (1500 m, 5000 m, and 10,000 m) before enormous home crowds at Bislett Stadium. Norway topped the medal table with 7 golds and 16 medals overall. American figure skater Dick Button successfully defended his title and performed the first triple jump in competition. Alpine skiing saw the debut of the giant slalom, and women's cross-country skiing appeared for the first time, with Finland's Lydia Wideman winning the inaugural 10 km race. Bandy was held as a demonstration sport. Canada won its last Olympic ice hockey gold for fifty years, represented by the Edmonton Mercurys.

Participating nations

Thirty nations competed, including debut appearances by New Zealand and Portugal. Germany and Japan, excluded in 1948, returned; the Soviet Union did not yet participate in the Winter Games, though it sent observers.

Venues and legacy

Events were held at Bislett Stadium (speed skating and figure skating), the Holmenkollen ski jump (whose rebuilt concrete bowl dates from these Games), Jordal Amfi (ice hockey), and alpine courses at Norefjell and Rødkleiva. The Oslo Games set a template for compact, city-centred Winter Olympics and remain a landmark in Norwegian sporting history.
