Fifth Dynasty of Egypt

Fifth Dynasty
Period: Old Kingdom, c. 2494–2345 BC
Capital: Memphis
First pharaoh: Userkaf
Last pharaoh: Unas
Notable monuments: Abusir pyramids, sun temples

The Fifth Dynasty of ancient Egypt ruled for roughly 150 years, from about 2494 to 2345 BC, during the Old Kingdom period. Its nine kings — Userkaf, Sahure, Neferirkare Kakai, Shepseskare, Neferefre, Nyuserre Ini, Menkauhor Kaiu, Djedkare Isesi, and Unas — are best known for building pyramids at Abusir and Saqqara and, distinctively, a series of temples dedicated to the sun god Ra.

Religion and the sun temples

The dynasty's rise was steeped in the cult of Ra of Heliopolis; later folk tradition, recorded in the Westcar Papyrus, told of the first three kings being triplets born to a priest's wife by the god Ra himself. The first king, Userkaf, built the first sun temple at Abu Gorab, and most of his successors followed: six sun temples are known from texts, though only those of Userkaf and Nyuserre have been located. These temples featured a squat obelisk on a pedestal as the cult focus, approached by a causeway from a valley temple.

Achievements

The reign of Sahure is famous for detailed reliefs depicting a trading expedition to Punt and a fleet returning with exotic goods, incense trees, and animals. The pyramids of the dynasty were smaller and more cheaply built than those of the Fourth Dynasty, but their mortuary temples were decorated with exceptionally fine relief work, and the Abusir Papyri — administrative archives from the temples — give a uniquely detailed picture of Old Kingdom temple economy. Under Djedkare Isesi, who reigned some four decades, administration was reorganised and the power of provincial officials grew.

End of the dynasty

The last king, Unas, is famous for the Pyramid Texts inscribed inside his pyramid at Saqqara — the earliest known corpus of Egyptian religious texts. With his death around 2345 BC the dynasty ended and the throne passed to Teti, founder of the Sixth Dynasty.
