Intelligence analysis

Intelligence analysis is the application of individual and collective cognitive methods to weigh data and test hypotheses within a secret socio-cultural context. Analysts evaluate information from many sources—human intelligence (HUMINT), signals intelligence (SIGINT), imagery (IMINT), and open sources (OSINT)—to produce assessments that inform policymakers and commanders.

The intelligence cycle

Analysis sits within the intelligence cycle: direction (defining requirements), collection, processing, analysis and production, and dissemination. In practice the cycle is iterative, with analysts tasking collectors for additional reporting.

Structured analytic techniques

To reduce cognitive biases such as mirror imaging, confirmation bias, and groupthink—causes examined in analyses of failures such as Pearl Harbor and the 2003 Iraq WMD assessments—agencies teach structured techniques, including Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH), key assumptions checks, devil's advocacy, red teaming, and indicators and warning methodologies pioneered by writers such as Sherman Kent and Richards J. Heuer Jr.

Estimative language

Analytic products express uncertainty with standardized probability phrases ("likely", "almost certainly") and confidence levels. In the United States, Intelligence Community Directive 203 sets analytic tradecraft standards.

Types

  • Current intelligence – day-to-day reporting
  • Estimative intelligence – longer-term forecasting
  • Warning intelligence – alerting decision-makers to imminent threats
  • Scientific and technical intelligence
