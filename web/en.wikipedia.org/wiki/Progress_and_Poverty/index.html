Progress and Poverty

Progress and Poverty: An Inquiry into the Cause of Industrial Depressions and of Increase of Want with Increase of Wealth: The Remedy is a book by social theorist and economist Henry George, published in 1879. The book seeks to explain why poverty accompanies economic and technological progress, and proposes a remedy: a tax on the value of land — the single tax — replacing all other taxes. It was George's first book, and it sold several million copies, exceeding all other books sold in the United States in the 1890s except the Bible.

Content

George observes that technological progress and rising productivity increase total wealth, yet wages remain depressed and poverty persists. Drawing on the Ricardian law of rent, he argues that as population and production advance, the rent of land rises and absorbs the gains of progress: landowners capture the increase in land values created by society as a whole, while labour and capital are squeezed. Land speculation further aggravates the cycle, contributing, he argues, to industrial depressions.

The remedy

George proposes to abolish all taxes except a single tax on the unimproved value of land — a land value tax. Since land is fixed in supply, taxing its rental value would not discourage production; it would, he argued, discourage speculation, make land available to users, and provide public revenue sufficient for government, since "we must make land common property" in effect by collecting rent for public purposes while leaving titles undisturbed.

Reception and influence

The book launched the Georgist / single-tax movement, inspired land reform campaigns in the United States, Britain, Australia and elsewhere, and influenced figures including Leo Tolstoy, Sun Yat-sen and Winston Churchill. Economists from Alfred Marshall to Milton Friedman praised the analysis of land taxation, Friedman calling the land value tax "the least-bad tax". Critics, including Karl Marx (who dismissed the book in correspondence), disputed George's theory of depressions and his exclusive focus on land.
