Russo-Ukrainian War

[Image: a map of Ukraine showing areas of control / a destroyed building after shelling]

The Russo-Ukrainian War began in 2014 with Russia's annexation of Crimea and support for separatists in the Donbas, and escalated dramatically on 24 February 2022, when Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

Background

Relations between Russia and Ukraine deteriorated after Ukraine's 2014 Revolution of Dignity ousted a pro-Russian president. Russia annexed Crimea — a move not recognised internationally — and fighting in eastern Ukraine continued for eight years despite ceasefire agreements reached in Minsk.

Full-scale invasion

Russian forces attacked from the north, east and south in February 2022, aiming initially at Kyiv, but withdrew from the capital region after Ukrainian resistance. Fighting concentrated thereafter in the east and south, including the long sieges and battles for Mariupol and Bakhmut, Ukrainian counteroffensives that retook Kharkiv region and Kherson city in 2022, and a front line that has since moved comparatively slowly amid heavy casualties and destruction. Ukraine has received extensive Western military and financial aid, and Western states have imposed wide sanctions on Russia.

Consequences

The war has killed and injured very large numbers of soldiers and civilians — exact figures are disputed and continue to rise — displaced millions of Ukrainians, disrupted global food and energy supplies, and led to war-crimes investigations and an International Criminal Court arrest warrant for Russia's president relating to the deportation of Ukrainian children.
