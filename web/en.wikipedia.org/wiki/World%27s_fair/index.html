World's fair
First: Great Exhibition, London, 1851
Governing body: Bureau International des Expositions (since 1928)

A world's fair, also called a world's exposition or universal exhibition, is a large international exhibition designed to showcase the achievements of nations in industry, technology, science, art and culture.

History

The tradition began with the Great Exhibition of 1851 in the Crystal Palace in London, followed by celebrated Paris expositions (1889, for which the Eiffel Tower was built), the World's Columbian Exposition at Chicago (1893), the 1939 and 1964 New York World's Fairs, Expo 67 in Montreal, and Expo 2010 in Shanghai, the largest by attendance. Since 1928 the Bureau International des Expositions has regulated the events, distinguishing World Expos (every five years) from specialised expos.

Legacy

Fairs have debuted technologies from the telephone to television and left lasting landmarks and urban districts in host cities. See also Great Exhibition.
