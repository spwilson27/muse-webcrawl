Siege of Budapest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Budapest (29 December 1944 – 13 February 1945) was the encirclement and capture of the Hungarian capital by Soviet and Romanian forces during the closing months of the Second World War. It was one of the longest and bloodiest urban sieges of the war, devastating the city and causing enormous civilian suffering.

Background

Following the failed attempt by Regent Miklós Horthy to withdraw Hungary from the war in October 1944, Germany installed the Arrow Cross regime of Ferenc Szálasi. Adolf Hitler declared Budapest a fortress city (Festung Budapest) to be defended to the last. Soviet forces of the 2nd and 3rd Ukrainian Fronts under Rodion Malinovsky and Fyodor Tolbukhin completed the encirclement of the city on 29 December 1944. Inside the pocket were roughly 79,000 German and Hungarian troops under SS general Karl Pfeffer-Wildenbruch.

The siege

Soviet summons to surrender were rejected — an incident in which Soviet envoys were killed remains controversial. Fighting then proceeded street by street. Pest, on the flat eastern bank of the Danube, fell by 18 January 1945; the defenders withdrew across the river, blowing up all the Danube bridges, and made their last stand in the hills of Buda and the Castle District. Three German relief attempts (Operations Konrad I–III) failed to break the encirclement. A final breakout attempt on 11 February 1945 ended in disaster, with only a few hundred men reaching German lines.

Aftermath

On 13 February the remaining garrison surrendered. The siege killed an estimated 38,000 civilians, through combat, starvation, disease and atrocities committed by all sides, and left some 80 percent of the city's buildings damaged or destroyed. Military losses were heavy on both sides.
