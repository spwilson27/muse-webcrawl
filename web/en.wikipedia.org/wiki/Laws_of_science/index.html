Scientific law

Scientific laws or laws of science are statements, based on repeated experiments or observations, that describe or predict a range of natural phenomena. A law typically summarises a regularity of nature in a concise verbal or mathematical form, without necessarily explaining the mechanism behind it — explanation being the role of a scientific theory.

Characteristics

Laws are developed from empirical evidence and are accepted as universally applicable within their domains. Well-known examples include Newton's laws of motion, the laws of thermodynamics, the law of conservation of energy, and the ideal gas law. Unlike theories, laws do not "become" theories over time; the two serve different functions, and a law may be refined or superseded when its limits are discovered, as Newtonian mechanics was subsumed by relativity and quantum mechanics.

Philosophy of science

Philosophers debate whether laws of nature are true regularities in the world, descriptions of human reasoning about it, or something in between. In mathematics and logic, similar terms ("laws of thought") refer to fundamental principles of valid reasoning.
