Keelboat

A keelboat is a riverine cargo-capable working boat, or a smaller mid-sized recreational sailing yacht. Historically, keelboats were long, narrow, cigar-shaped riverboats used for transporting cargo and passengers on the rivers of the early United States; in modern usage the term usually denotes a small-to-mid-sized sailboat fitted with a fixed keel instead of a centreboard or daggerboard.

Historical river keelboats

On American rivers such as the Mississippi, Ohio, and Missouri, keelboats carried freight in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Downstream they floated with the current; upstream they were poled, rowed, or hauled by cordelle (tow lines) by their crews. The Lewis and Clark expedition used a keelboat to ascend the Missouri River in 1804.

Modern sailing keelboats

Recreational keelboats are typically between 5 and 10 metres long. The fixed ballasted keel provides stability and resistance to leeway, making the boats more seaworthy than dinghies but generally too large to be easily trailered or beached.
