Social class in the United Kingdom

The social structure of the United Kingdom has historically been highly influenced by the concept of social class — a system of stratification based on occupation, wealth, education, accent and inherited status. Although class boundaries have blurred since the mid-20th century, class remains a prominent theme in British culture and politics.

Traditional classes

The traditional tripartite model divided society into the upper class (landed aristocracy and gentry), the middle class (professionals, managers and business owners, itself split into upper-, middle- and lower-middle), and the working class (manual workers, further divided into skilled, semi-skilled and unskilled). Accents, schools (notably the public schools), and distinctions such as the "U and non-U" usage famously catalogued in the 1950s served as class markers.

Modern measurement

Official statistics use the NS-SEC occupational classification. The Great British Class Survey (2011–2013), applying Pierre Bourdieu's model of economic, social and cultural capital, proposed seven classes, from an "elite" through established and technical middle classes to a "precariat" at the bottom — reflecting how class increasingly involves cultural and social resources, not income alone.
