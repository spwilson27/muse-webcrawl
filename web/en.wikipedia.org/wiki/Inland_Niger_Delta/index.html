Inner Niger Delta

Inner Niger Delta
Floodplain in Mali, West Africa
Formed by the Niger and Bani rivers
Seasonally flooded area up to about 30,000 km²

The Inner Niger Delta, also known as the Macina or Masina, is a large area of lakes, floodplains and wetlands in the middle course of the Niger River in Mali, where the Niger and its tributary the Bani spread out and divide into a maze of channels before rejoining. It is the largest wetland in West Africa and one of the largest inland deltas in the world.

Hydrology and ecology

Floodwaters arrive from the Guinean highlands between July and September and slowly inundate the delta, the flooded area reaching up to roughly 30,000 square kilometres in wet years before receding. The annual flood pulse supports exceptionally productive fisheries, flooded rice cultivation and grazing on the rich floodplain pastures (bourgou) as the waters fall. The delta hosts large numbers of waterbirds and is designated a Ramsar site of international importance.

People

Bozo and Somono fishers, Fulani pastoralists and Bambara and Marka farmers share the delta's resources under long-standing customary arrangements, and trading towns such as Djenné and Mopti grew up at its edges.
