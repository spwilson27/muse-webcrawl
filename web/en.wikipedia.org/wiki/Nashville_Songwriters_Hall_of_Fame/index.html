Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame

Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame
Founded: 1970
Location: Music City Center, Nashville, Tennessee
Operated by: Nashville Songwriters Foundation
Inductees: More than 200

The Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame is an American hall of fame honoring outstanding songwriters, principally those associated with Nashville, Tennessee, and country music, although its inductees span many genres. It was established in 1970 by the Nashville Songwriters Foundation.

History and induction

The Hall of Fame was created to recognize the contributions of the songwriters behind Nashville's music industry. Inductees are chosen by a committee of peers and music-industry professionals, and new members are honored each year at an induction ceremony and gala in Nashville. Inductees are recognized in categories that have included contemporary songwriter, contemporary songwriter/artist, veteran songwriter, veteran songwriter/artist, and a legacy category for historical figures.

Among the first inductees in 1970 were Gene Autry, Johnny Cash, Jimmie Davis, Felice and Boudleaux Bryant, and Cindy Walker. Later inductees include Hank Williams, Kris Kristofferson, Dolly Parton, Willie Nelson, Harlan Howard, Loretta Lynn, Bob Dylan, Merle Haggard, Tom T. Hall, and many others whose songs defined American popular music.

Physical hall

For most of its history the Hall of Fame existed without a permanent physical home. In 2013, a gallery opened inside Nashville's Music City Center convention complex, displaying biographies, photographs, and memorabilia of the inductees along with interactive exhibits. The Nashville Songwriters Foundation also maintains the "Songwriters' Seat" markers and related exhibits celebrating the craft of songwriting.
