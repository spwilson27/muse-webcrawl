The Dobro is a brand of resonator guitar, originally produced by the Dobro Manufacturing Company and later by Gibson. The name derives from the Dopyera brothers ("Do" + "bro"), Slovak-American luthiers who developed the instrument in the late 1920s, and it also echoes the Slovak word dobrý, meaning "good".

History

John Dopyera and his brothers founded the National String Instrument Corporation in 1927, producing metal-bodied resonator guitars. After disputes with National, the Dopyeras left in 1929 to form the Dobro Manufacturing Company, introducing wooden-bodied resonator guitars using a single inverted cone resonator, in contrast to National's biscuit-bridge design. Dobro merged with National in 1932 to form the National-Dobro Corporation. Production continued under various owners until Gibson acquired the brand; Dobro instruments are now produced in Nashville.

Design and sound

A Dobro guitar amplifies its sound acoustically through a spun aluminium resonator cone set into the body, often covered by a decorative metal coverplate. Most Dobros are played lap-style with a steel bar and are strung with a square neck, and the instrument became central to bluegrass and country music, notably through players such as Josh Graves and Jerry Douglas.

Trivia

"Dobro" has become a generic term for any resonator guitar, though it remains a registered trademark in some jurisdictions.
