The County Court of Victoria is an intermediate trial court in the Australian state of Victoria. It sits between the Magistrates' Court of Victoria (below) and the Supreme Court of Victoria (above) in the state's court hierarchy and is the principal trial court for most serious criminal offences and for civil claims of substantial value.

Jurisdiction

In its criminal jurisdiction, the County Court hears indictable offences other than the most serious categories reserved to the Supreme Court (such as murder and treason), and hears appeals from the Magistrates' Court. In its civil jurisdiction it has, since reforms in 2007, unlimited monetary jurisdiction in most civil matters, including personal injury, commercial disputes and damages claims, and it also has appellate jurisdiction in some matters. The court operates specialist lists, including a commercial division, a damages and compensation division, and the Koori Court division, established under the County Court Act for sentencing of Aboriginal offenders in certain circumstances.

Organisation

The Court is led by a Chief Judge and is staffed by several dozen judges, judicial registrars and associate judges. It sits in Melbourne and on circuit in regional centres across Victoria. The court's principal building is at 250 William Street, Melbourne, in the city's legal precinct. The current governing legislation is the County Court Act 1958 (Vic), although the court traces its origins to the county courts established in 1852 following examples in England.
