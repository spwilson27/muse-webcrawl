Jeffrey Tambor

Born: July 8, 1944, San Francisco, California, U.S.
Occupation: Actor
Years active: 1972–present
Known for: The Larry Sanders Show, Arrested Development, Transparent

Jeffrey Michael Tambor (born July 8, 1944) is an American actor. He is known for his television roles as Hank Kingsley on The Larry Sanders Show (1992–1998), George Bluth Sr. and Oscar Bluth on Arrested Development (2003–2019), and Maura Pfefferman on the Amazon series Transparent (2014–2017), for which he won two Primetime Emmy Awards and a Golden Globe Award.

Early life

Tambor was born in San Francisco, California, to a Conservative Jewish family of Hungarian and Ukrainian descent. He studied acting at San Francisco State University and earned a master's degree at Wayne State University in Detroit. He began his career in repertory theater and on Broadway.

Career

Tambor appeared in films such as ...And Justice for All (1979), There's Something About Mary (1998), How the Grinch Stole Christmas (2000), Hellboy (2004), The Hangover (2009), and The Death of Stalin (2017), and provided voices for animated works including Monsters vs. Aliens. His television breakthrough came as the pompous sidekick Hank Kingsley on Garry Shandling's The Larry Sanders Show, earning six Emmy nominations. On Arrested Development he played the imprisoned patriarch George Bluth Sr. and his twin brother Oscar.

His portrayal of Maura Pfefferman, a transgender woman coming out late in life, on Transparent won widespread acclaim. In 2018 he departed the series following allegations of misconduct, which he denied in part; the show concluded with a musical finale in 2019.

Personal life

Tambor published a memoir, Are You Anybody?, in 2017. He has been married to Kasia Ostlun since 2001 and has four children with her, in addition to an adult daughter from a previous relationship.
