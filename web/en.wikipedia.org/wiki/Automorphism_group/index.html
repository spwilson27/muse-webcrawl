Automorphism group

In mathematics, the automorphism group of an object is the group consisting of all automorphisms of the object — that is, all isomorphisms from the object to itself — under composition of morphisms. It is a way of describing the symmetries of the object, and it is a fundamental construction in group theory and category theory. The automorphism group of an object X is commonly denoted Aut(X).

Definition

Let X be an object in some category, such as a group, a graph, a vector space or a topological space. An automorphism is an isomorphism f : X → X. The set of all automorphisms, equipped with composition, forms a group: composition is associative, the identity map is an automorphism, and every isomorphism has an inverse which is also an automorphism.

Examples

The automorphism group of a finite set of n elements is the symmetric group Sn. The automorphism group of a vector space V is the general linear group GL(V) of invertible linear maps. For a graph, Aut(X) is the group of permutations of the vertices that preserve adjacency; the Petersen graph, for instance, has automorphism group isomorphic to S5. The automorphism group of the rational numbers under addition is the group of multiplications by non-zero rationals.

Inner and outer automorphisms

For a group G, conjugation by an element g — the map x ↦ gxg−1 — is an automorphism called an inner automorphism. The inner automorphisms form a normal subgroup Inn(G) of Aut(G), isomorphic to G/Z(G) where Z(G) is the centre. The quotient group Out(G) = Aut(G)/Inn(G) is the outer automorphism group, measuring symmetries not given by conjugation.

Importance

Automorphism groups encode the symmetry of algebraic structures and appear across mathematics: Galois groups are automorphism groups of field extensions, and Klein's Erlangen programme characterises geometries by their automorphism (transformation) groups. Cayley's theorem — every group is isomorphic to a subgroup of a symmetric group — can be viewed as embedding a group in the automorphism group of a set.
