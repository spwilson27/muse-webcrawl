Zymogen

A zymogen, or proenzyme, is an inactive precursor of an enzyme. A zymogen requires a biochemical change — usually the hydrolysis of one or more peptide bonds — for it to become an active enzyme.

Function

Organisms produce enzymes as zymogens to prevent the enzymes from digesting the cells that make them, and to control precisely when and where enzyme activity is unleashed. Digestive proteases are the textbook case: the pancreas secretes trypsinogen and chymotrypsinogen into the small intestine, where enteropeptidase and trypsin activate them safely away from pancreatic tissue. Premature activation inside the pancreas causes pancreatitis.

Examples

Other well-known zymogens include pepsinogen (activated to pepsin by stomach acid), prothrombin (converted to thrombin in blood clotting), proinsulin (processed to insulin), and the procaspases that drive apoptosis.
