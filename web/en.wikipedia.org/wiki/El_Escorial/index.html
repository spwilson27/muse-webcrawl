El Escorial

The Royal Site of San Lorenzo de El Escorial is a vast historical residence of the kings of Spain in the town of San Lorenzo de El Escorial, about 45 kilometres northwest of Madrid, in the foothills of the Sierra de Guadarrama. Built between 1563 and 1584 under King Philip II, it combines a monastery, basilica, royal palace, pantheon, library, college and seminary in a single immense granite complex, and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

History

Philip II commissioned the building to commemorate the Spanish victory over France at the Battle of St. Quentin on St. Lawrence's Day, 10 August 1557, and to serve as a mausoleum for his father, the Emperor Charles V, and the Spanish Habsburg dynasty. The architects were Juan Bautista de Toledo and, after his death, Juan de Herrera, whose austere classical style — the estilo herreriano — the building exemplifies. Tradition holds that its grid-like plan evokes the gridiron on which St. Lawrence was martyred.

Description

The complex is roughly 207 by 161 metres and contains some 4,000 rooms in popular accounts, 16 courtyards and long corridors covering many kilometres. The basilica, crowned by a great dome, stands at its centre. The Royal Pantheon beneath the high altar holds the marble tombs of Spanish kings and queens from Charles V onward. The library, with a frescoed barrel vault by Pellegrino Tibaldi, preserves tens of thousands of volumes and manuscripts, including a famous collection of Arabic manuscripts.

Collections

The monastery houses important paintings by Titian, El Greco, Velázquez, Tintoretto and Hieronymus Bosch, many collected by Philip II, and the frescoes of the Hall of Battles depict Spanish military victories. The Augustinian monks still maintain the monastery, school and basilica.
