Facial motion capture

Facial motion capture (also called performance capture when combined with body capture) is the process of electronically converting the movements of a person's face into a digital character model, used in film, video games, and virtual production.

Methods

Marker-based systems track reflective or coloured dots applied to the actor's face with specialised cameras and solve the marker motion onto a facial rig. Markerless systems use one or more video cameras — often head-mounted cameras (HMCs) — plus computer vision and machine learning to reconstruct expression. Depth-sensing and photogrammetry approaches, and modern phone-based systems such as Apple's ARKit blendshapes and Live Link Face, have made facial capture widely accessible.

Workflow

Captured motion is typically expressed as blendshape weights or action-unit parameters derived from the Facial Action Coding System (FACS), cleaned by animators, and retargeted to characters with very different proportions. Notable uses include Gollum in The Lord of the Rings, Davy Jones in Pirates of the Caribbean, the Na'vi in Avatar, and digital performances in games such as L.A. Noire (which used the distinct MotionScan technology) and Hellblade: Senua's Sacrifice.

Challenges

Eye gaze, lip contact, tongue, and subtle skin deformation remain difficult, and accuracy in the uncanny valley region demands high-fidelity solving combined with hand animation.
