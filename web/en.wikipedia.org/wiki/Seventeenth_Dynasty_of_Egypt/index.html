Seventeenth Dynasty of Egypt

The Seventeenth Dynasty of ancient Egypt (c. 1580–1550 BC) was a dynasty of pharaohs based in Thebes in Upper Egypt during the late Second Intermediate Period. While the Hyksos Fifteenth Dynasty ruled the Nile Delta in the north, and the Sixteenth Dynasty is usually seen as a line of Theban vassals, the kings of the Seventeenth Dynasty eventually broke Hyksos power and reunified the country, inaugurating the New Kingdom.

History

The dynasty's rulers began as local Theban kings. Over time their territory expanded northward as Hyksos power waned. King Seqenenre Tao is remembered for opening open warfare against the Hyksos king Apepi; his mummy bears severe head wounds, suggesting he died in battle. His successor Kamose launched campaigns reaching the edges of Hyksos territory, as recorded on the Kamose stelae. Kamose's brother Ahmose I completed the conquest, capturing the Hyksos capital Avaris and pursuing them to Sharuhen in the southern Levant, and is conventionally counted as the founder of the Eighteenth Dynasty and the New Kingdom, though he was the last king in the Seventeenth Dynasty line of descent.

Rulers

Known pharaohs of the dynasty include:

  • Rahotep
  • Sobekemsaf I
  • Sobekemsaf II
  • Intef V, Intef VI and Intef VII (the Nubkheperre, Sekhemre-Wepmaat and Sekhemre-Heruhirmaat Intefs)
  • Senakhtenre Ahmose
  • Seqenenre Tao
  • Kamose

The dynasty's royal women, such as Tetisheri, Ahhotep I and Ahmose-Nefertari, were unusually prominent and were venerated long after their deaths.

Tombs

The kings of the Seventeenth Dynasty were buried at Dra Abu el-Naga on the Theban west bank, in relatively modest tomb complexes that included small brick pyramids. Several of their mummies were later found reburied in the Deir el-Bahari cache (DB320).
