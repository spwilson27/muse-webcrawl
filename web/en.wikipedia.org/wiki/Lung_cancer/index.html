Lung cancer, also known as lung carcinoma, is a malignant tumour characterised by uncontrolled cell growth in tissues of the lung. It is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide (around 1.8 million deaths a year, approximate), with two main types: non-small-cell lung cancer (about 85% of cases, including adenocarcinoma, squamous-cell and large-cell carcinoma) and small-cell lung cancer, which grows faster.

By far the dominant cause is tobacco smoking, responsible for the large majority of cases; other causes include radon gas, asbestos, air pollution and second-hand smoke. Symptoms — persistent cough, coughing blood, breathlessness, chest pain, weight loss — often appear late, so many cases are diagnosed at advanced stage; five-year survival has historically been low but is improving with targeted therapies and immunotherapy for molecular subtypes (EGFR, ALK and others). Screening with low-dose CT reduces mortality in high-risk smokers.
