The Advanced Research Projects Agency–Energy (ARPA-E) is a United States government agency within the Department of Energy tasked with promoting and funding research and development of advanced energy technologies. It was modeled on DARPA, the defense research agency, on the theory that a small, agile, risk-tolerant agency could accelerate high-impact innovation that conventional programs and private capital might neglect.

History

ARPA-E was authorized by the America COMPETES Act of 2007, following a recommendation in the National Academies report Rising Above the Gathering Storm. It received its first appropriation—$400 million—under the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009, and Arun Majumdar, a Berkeley professor and laboratory director, became its first director.

Model and programs

The agency employs program directors on limited terms who design focused programs, set aggressive technical milestones, and actively manage teams of universities, startups, and companies. Projects typically run two to four years and must show a path to market adoption. Programs have addressed grid-scale energy storage, power electronics, advanced batteries, electrofuels, methane leak detection, fusion energy, and carbon capture. ARPA-E also convenes an annual Energy Innovation Summit.

Impact

By the early 2020s ARPA-E had supported more than a thousand projects; agency reports credit its portfolio with attracting several billion dollars in follow-on private investment and spawning over a hundred new companies, though assessments of its additionality remain a subject of policy debate.
