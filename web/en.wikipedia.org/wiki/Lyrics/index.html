Lyrics

Lyrics are words that make up a song, usually consisting of verses and choruses. The writer of lyrics is a lyricist; where the lyricist also composes the music, they are a singer-songwriter. The word derives from the lyre: in ancient Greece, lyric poetry was verse sung to the accompaniment of the lyre, and "lyrics" retains the connection between poetry and song.

Structure and craft

Popular song lyrics typically combine verse, chorus, and often a bridge, using rhyme, metre, and repetition to fit and reinforce the melody. Lyrics range from literal narrative to abstract imagery, and their craft — prosody (matching word stress to musical stress), rhyme schemes, and hooks — is a studied discipline. In opera, the text is the libretto; in musical theatre and art song, lyrics are judged together with the score.

Copyright and study

Lyrics are protected by copyright separately from the musical composition in many jurisdictions. Scholars analyse lyrics as literature, and some lyricists — such as Bob Dylan, who received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2016 — have been recognised as poets in their own right.
