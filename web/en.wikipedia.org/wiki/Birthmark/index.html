Birthmark
TypesVascular, pigmented
OnsetPresent at birth or appearing in early infancy
TreatmentUsually none; laser therapy in some cases

A birthmark is a congenital, benign irregularity on the skin that is present at birth or appears in the first few months of life. Birthmarks are caused by overgrowths of blood vessels, pigment cells, smooth muscle, fat, or keratinocytes.

Types

Vascular birthmarks arise from abnormal blood vessels. They include infantile hemangiomas ("strawberry marks"), port-wine stains (capillary malformations that persist into adulthood), and salmon patches, which are very common in newborns and usually fade. Pigmented birthmarks include café-au-lait spots, Mongolian spots (congenital dermal melanocytosis), and congenital melanocytic nevi (moles present at birth).

Prognosis and treatment

Most birthmarks are harmless and require no treatment, though some — such as large congenital nevi — carry a small increased risk of melanoma, and certain marks can signal underlying syndromes (for example, a facial port-wine stain may be associated with Sturge–Weber syndrome). Pulsed dye laser therapy can lighten many vascular marks, and oral propranolol is used for problematic infantile hemangiomas.
