Cortes of Cádiz

The Cortes of Cádiz was a revival of the traditional Cortes (parliament) of Spain, convened during the Peninsular War after the French occupation of most of the country. Meeting on the Isla de León (now San Fernando) and then in Cádiz from 1810 to 1813, it proclaimed national sovereignty and promulgated the Spanish Constitution of 1812.

Overview

Convened: 24 September 1810

Location: Cádiz, Spain

Major act: Constitution of 1812 ("La Pepa")

Proceedings

Delegates came from mainland Spain, the Americas and the Philippines, although American representation was limited and contentious. The Cortes abolished the Inquisition and seigneurial jurisdictions, enacted freedom of the press, and drafted a liberal constitution establishing a constitutional monarchy with a unicameral legislature. King Ferdinand VII annulled its work on his restoration in 1814, but the Cortes of Cádiz became a founding reference point for Spanish and Latin American liberalism.
