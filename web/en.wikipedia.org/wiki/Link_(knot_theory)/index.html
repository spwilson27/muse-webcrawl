Link (knot theory)

In mathematical knot theory, a link is a collection of knots which do not intersect, but which may be linked (interlocked) together. A knot is a link with one component. Two links are considered equivalent if they are ambient isotopic — that is, one can be deformed into the other without cutting.

Examples

The simplest non-trivial link is the Hopf link, two circles linked once, with linking number ±1. The Whitehead link has linking number zero but is still linked. The Borromean rings consist of three components such that removing any one ring unlinks the other two, a property called Brunnian when it holds for a link of any number of components.

Invariants and descriptions

Links are described by link diagrams with crossing information, and studied through invariants such as the linking number, the Alexander and Jones polynomials generalised to several components, and link groups. The components of a link form a disjoint union of circles embedded in three-dimensional space (or the 3-sphere). A link with components arranged as separate unknots is called the unlink.
