Armadillidium vulgare

Armadillidium vulgare, the common pill woodlouse

Armadillidium vulgare, the common pill woodlouse, pill isopod, or roly-poly, is a European species of woodlouse in the family Armadillidiidae. Through human activity it has become one of the most widely distributed terrestrial isopods in the world, and it is among the most extensively studied land isopods.

Description

Adults typically reach about 12–18 mm in length. The body is oval, strongly convex above and protected by a heavy, smooth, segmented calcified exoskeleton, usually dark grey, though yellow-spotted forms also occur. Like other members of its family, A. vulgare can roll into a near-perfect ball (conglobation) when threatened, leaving no appendages exposed — a defence that also reduces water loss.

Distribution and habitat

Native to the Mediterranean region and Europe, the species has been introduced throughout the Americas, Asia, Australia and elsewhere. It is particularly tolerant of dry conditions compared with many woodlice, thanks to its thick cuticle and ball-rolling behaviour, and lives under stones, bark and leaf litter, in gardens, grassland and disturbed ground.

Ecology

A. vulgare feeds mainly on decaying plant material, leaf litter, fungi and algae, playing a role in decomposition and nutrient cycling. Females carry eggs and young in a fluid-filled brood pouch (marsupium). The species can live for several years. Populations may be affected by the feminising bacterium Wolbachia, and heavy infestations can damage seedlings, so the species is sometimes regarded as a minor garden pest.
