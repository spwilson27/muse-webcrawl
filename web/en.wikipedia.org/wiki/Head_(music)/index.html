Head (music)

In music, the term head refers to the main melody or theme of a composition, most commonly in the context of jazz and other improvised forms. The head is the composed portion of a tune — typically played at the beginning and end of a performance — that frames the improvised solos in between.

Usage in jazz

In a typical jazz performance, the ensemble plays the head once (or sometimes twice) at the start, stating the theme of the piece. Individual musicians then take improvised solos over the chord changes of the tune, after which the band returns to the head to close the performance. This structure is often called "head–solos–head" form.

A head arrangement is an arrangement that is worked out collectively by the musicians during rehearsal or performance rather than written down. Head arrangements were especially characteristic of early jazz and of territory bands in the swing era, where musicians would devise riffs and harmonized passages by ear and commit them to memory. The band of Count Basie was particularly famous for its head arrangements, which gave its performances a loose, spontaneous character.

Related concepts

The chord progression of the head often serves as the basis for contrafacts — new melodies written over existing changes. The head also contrasts with the bridge, the contrasting middle section of a standard song form.
