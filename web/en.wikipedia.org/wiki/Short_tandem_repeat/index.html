Short tandem repeat

A short tandem repeat (STR), also called a microsatellite, is a region of DNA in which a short motif — typically two to six base pairs — is repeated in tandem, for example (CA)n or (GATA)n. STR loci vary greatly in repeat number between individuals because replication slippage adds or removes repeat units at a relatively high rate.

Forensic and genetic use

Because STR allele lengths are easily measured by PCR and capillary electrophoresis and differ between people, STR profiling is the standard method of DNA fingerprinting in forensic science. National databases such as the FBI's CODIS system are built on panels of core STR loci (expanded to 20 core loci in 2017). STRs are also used in paternity testing, population genetics, genetic genealogy and linkage mapping.

Disease

Expansion of trinucleotide repeats beyond a threshold causes a class of genetic disorders — the repeat expansion diseases — including Huntington's disease (CAG), fragile X syndrome (CGG) and myotonic dystrophy (CTG), in which the repeat grows across generations (anticipation).

Distinction from VNTRs

STRs (microsatellites) have shorter repeat units than minisatellites/variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs), whose motifs are typically 10–60 base pairs and which formed the basis of the earliest DNA profiling methods.
