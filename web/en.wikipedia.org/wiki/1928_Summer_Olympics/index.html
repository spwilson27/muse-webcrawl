1928 Summer Olympics

The 1928 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the IX Olympiad, were held from 28 July to 12 August 1928 in Amsterdam, Netherlands. Forty-six nations sent 2,883 athletes, including 277 women, to compete in 109 events in 14 sports. The Games are remembered for the return of Germany after its post-war exclusion, the first participation of women in Olympic athletics and gymnastics, and several enduring innovations.

Highlights

The Amsterdam Games introduced traditions still in use: the Olympic flame was lit in a tower at the stadium for the first time, and the parade of nations began with Greece and ended with the host nation. The Olympic Stadium, designed by Jan Wils, won the gold medal in the art competitions for architecture.

Finland's Paavo Nurmi won his ninth and final Olympic gold medal, in the 10,000 metres. American swimmer Johnny Weissmuller added two gold medals, while Canada's Percy Williams won the sprint double. Women's athletics, included for the first time, concluded with controversy when several runners collapsed after the 800 metres final — an image used by officials to exclude women's middle-distance races until 1960. Uruguay defended its Olympic football title, defeating Argentina in a replayed final. India won the first of eight consecutive gold medals in field hockey. The United States topped the medal table with 22 golds.

Participating nations

Germany returned to the Olympics for the first time since the First World War. A total of 46 National Olympic Committees sent athletes, and the Games also featured the debut of the Olympic rings on official posters and the standardisation of the running track at 400 metres.
