Dresdner Bank

Dresdner Bank AG was one of Germany's largest banks. Founded in Dresden in 1872, it grew into the country's second- or third-largest bank for much of the 20th century. It was acquired by the insurer Allianz in 2001 and sold to Commerzbank in 2008–09, after which the Dresdner Bank brand was discontinued in 2010.

History

The bank was founded by the conversion of the private banking house of Michael Kaskel in Dresden. It expanded rapidly, opened a Berlin office in 1884 and moved its registered office there in 1950 after its Saxon business was expropriated in the Soviet occupation zone. Re-established in the West, it regained its position among Germany's "big three" banks alongside Deutsche Bank and Commerzbank.

Third Reich and postwar period

During the Nazi era Dresdner Bank grew through the "Aryanisation" of Jewish-owned banks and business in occupied Europe, a role documented by historians after 2000. After the war the bank rebuilt its branch network and in the 1990s expanded internationally, acquiring the London merchant bank Kleinwort Benson in 1995 to form its investment banking arm, Dresdner Kleinwort.

Takeovers

Allianz bought Dresdner Bank in 2001 for about €24 billion, aiming to create a financial conglomerate; the combination underperformed, and heavy losses in the 2008 financial crisis forced a sale to Commerzbank, completed in May 2009. Branches were rebranded as Commerzbank in 2010.
