Lexicon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lexicon (from Greek lexikon, "of or for words") is the vocabulary of a language — the complete stock of meaningful units (words, idioms and other lexical items) that speakers of the language know and use. In linguistics, the lexicon is one component of a speaker's mental grammar alongside phonology, morphology and syntax; in lexicography, the term also denotes the word-stock recorded in a dictionary or glossary, especially of an ancient language.

The mental lexicon

Psycholinguistics studies how words are stored and retrieved in the mind: entries in the mental lexicon are thought to encode a word's sound, spelling, grammatical behaviour and meaning, and to be organised in networks of association. Native speakers are estimated to know tens of thousands of word families, acquired largely without instruction.

Lexicon and grammar

The boundary between the lexicon and the grammar is a central theoretical question. Phenomena such as irregular plurals, idioms and derivational morphology must be at least partly listed, whereas regular syntactic combination is rule-governed. Various frameworks handle this differently: some place much structure inside lexical entries, others minimise the lexicon. The size and structure of a language's lexicon also change historically through borrowing, coinage, semantic shift and obsolescence.
