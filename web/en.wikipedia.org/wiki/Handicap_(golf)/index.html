Handicap (golf)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A handicap in golf is a numerical measure of a golfer's potential that enables players of varying abilities to compete against one another. Better players are those with the lowest handicaps. A player with a handicap of 0 is called a "scratch golfer", and one with a handicap of approximately 18 relative to par is sometimes called a "bogey golfer".

World Handicap System

Since 2020, most of the world has used the World Handicap System (WHS), developed by The R&A and the USGA, which replaced six different regional systems. Under the WHS, a Handicap Index is calculated from the best 8 of the player's most recent 20 score differentials, where a differential combines the adjusted gross score with the Course Rating and Slope Rating of the tees played. The maximum Handicap Index is 54.0.

Course Handicap and playing handicap

For a particular round, the Handicap Index is converted into a Course Handicap using the Slope Rating of the course and tees, and competitions may apply a handicap allowance (a percentage of the Course Handicap) depending on the format, producing the playing handicap. The resulting strokes are subtracted — or, for "plus" handicaps, added — to the gross score to give a net score.

History

Handicapping developed in Scotland and England in the 19th century as three-score averaging systems, and was formalised differently in Britain (CONGU), the United States (USGA), Australia, South Africa, Argentina and continental Europe (EGA). The WHS unified these approaches with a common formula while retaining local administration by national associations and clubs.
