River Lagan

The River Lagan (from Irish Abhainn an Lagáin, "river of the low-lying district") is a major river in Northern Ireland which runs 53.5 miles (86 km) from Slieve Croob in County Down to Belfast, where it enters Belfast Lough on the Irish Sea. The river forms the boundary between County Antrim and County Down for much of its lower course.

Course

Rising at Slieve Croob, the Lagan flows through Dromara, Donaghcloney and Magheralin before reaching Lisburn, once the centre of the Irish linen industry, which grew up along its banks. In Belfast it passes the Waterfront Hall and the Titanic Quarter; the Lagan Weir, completed in 1994, maintains the river at a constant level through the city, ending the era of exposed mudflats at low tide and anchoring the regeneration of the riverside.

The towpath and navigation

The Lagan Navigation once linked Belfast with Lough Neagh via a canal running beside the river. Its towpath is now a popular walking and cycling route from Belfast to Lisburn, passing through the Lagan Valley Regional Park, an area of landscaped parkland and woodland threaded along the river corridor.
