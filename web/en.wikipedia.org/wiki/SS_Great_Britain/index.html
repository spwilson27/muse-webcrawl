SS Great Britain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: SS Great Britain preserved in dry dock in Bristol with masts and funnel]
SS Great Britain in Bristol.

SS Great Britain is a museum ship in Bristol, England, and the most important historic steamship of the 19th century. Designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel and launched in 1843, she was the first iron-hulled, screw-propelled ocean liner and, at 98 metres, the largest ship in the world — the ancestor of all modern shipping. After carrying emigrants to Australia and serving as a warehouse and hulk in the Falkland Islands, she was salvaged in 1970 and returned to the Bristol dry dock where she was built.

Design and career

Brunel lengthened the original paddle design for a propeller (influenced by Archimedes\' trials) and an iron hull strong enough for a 3,400-ton ship. Launched by Prince Albert in 1843, she crossed to New York in 1845 but ran aground in Dundrum Bay in 1846; refitted with new owners, she carried thousands of emigrants to Melbourne from 1852.

Decline and rescue

Converted to sail in 1882 after engine removal, she was scuttled at Sparrow Cove, Falklands (1937) after storm damage. In 1970 she was patched, refloated onto a pontoon and towed 13,000 km home to Bristol — watched by huge crowds — for restoration in the Great Western Dockyard.

Museum

Conserved in a dry dock with a \'glass sea\' at her waterline and a dehumidified hull environment, SS Great Britain opened as a museum with recreated Victorian interiors and the Brunel Institute; she is the top-rated visitor attraction of her kind in Britain.
