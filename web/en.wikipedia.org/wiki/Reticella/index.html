Reticella

Reticella (also reticello or in French reticelle) is a needle lace dating from the 15th century and remaining popular into the first quarter of the 17th century. Originally, reticella was a form of cutwork in which threads were drawn out from linen fabric to make a grid on which patterning was worked, but in its developed form it was made entirely of thread and was the earliest true needle lace.

Development

Reticella developed from drawn-thread work and cutwork embroidery. The ground fabric was progressively eliminated: first threads were cut away and the remaining threads were overcast and ornamented, then the design was worked on a temporary foundation of parchment or green baize. The characteristic style is geometric, with squares, circles, triangles, and star patterns filled with buttonhole stitch.

Pattern books

Designs for reticella appeared in the many lace pattern books published in Italy, Germany, and France in the late 16th and early 17th centuries, including those of Federico Vinciolo (1587) and Cesare Vecellio. Reticella in turn gave rise to the freer, more curvilinear needle laces such as punto in aria and the Venetian gros point.
