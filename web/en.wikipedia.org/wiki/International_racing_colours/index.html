International racing colours

International racing colours refers to the standardised national colour schemes once required on racing cars in international competition. The scheme, codified by the forerunners of the FIA in the early twentieth century, assigned each nation a body colour and in many cases distinguishing chassis, bonnet or stripe colours.

Table of colours

France raced in blue, Germany in white and later bare silver, Italy in red, the United Kingdom in green, the United States in white with blue longitudinal stripes, Belgium in yellow, Japan in ivory white with a red disc, and the Netherlands in orange. The colours were allocated when the Gordon Bennett Cup introduced national teams to motor racing in 1900.

End of the scheme

When the FIA permitted sponsorship liveries in 1968 the mandatory national colours lapsed, though some teams — most famously Ferrari with its rosso corsa — retained their traditional colour as part of their identity.
