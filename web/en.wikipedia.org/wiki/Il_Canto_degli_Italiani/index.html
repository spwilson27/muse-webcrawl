"Il Canto degli Italiani" ("The Song of the Italians") is the national anthem of Italy. Popularly known as "Fratelli d'Italia" ("Brothers of Italy") from its opening line, or the "Inno di Mameli" ("Mameli's Hymn"), its lyrics were written by the young Genoese poet Goffredo Mameli in 1847 and set to music by Michele Novaro the same year.

History

Written during the Italian unification movement (Risorgimento), the song became a patriotic rallying hymn. Mameli himself died in 1849 defending the Roman Republic, aged 21. After the Second World War the anthem was adopted provisionally on 12 October 1946, when the Italian Republic replaced the monarchy, and it remained in use by custom for decades before being formally established in law in 2017.

Lyrics and themes

The anthem calls Italians to unity and struggle, invoking figures such as Scipio, the medieval Lombard League, and Francesco Ferruccio. Only the first verse and chorus are normally performed at official occasions.

Debates

There have been recurring debates about whether to replace the anthem or to perform the full text; proposals have included Verdi's chorus "Va, pensiero". Nevertheless, Mameli's hymn remains firmly established.
