The Motion Picture Association (MPA) is an American trade association representing the major film studios of the United States, along with the streaming service Netflix. Founded in 1922 as the Motion Picture Producers and Distributors of America (MPPDA), and long known as the Motion Picture Association of America (MPAA) from 1945 to 2019, the organization advocates for the film and television industry in matters of public policy, fights copyright infringement, and administers the voluntary film rating system used in the United States.

History

The association was founded in 1922 in the wake of scandals in Hollywood, with former Postmaster General Will H. Hays as its first president; for decades it was known simply as the "Hays Office." Under Hays, the studios adopted the Motion Picture Production Code (the "Hays Code") in 1930, enforcing it strictly from 1934 to 1968 to regulate moral content in films. The organization was renamed the Motion Picture Association of America in 1945. Under president Jack Valenti, who served from 1966 to 2004, the Code was replaced in 1968 by the film rating system, a voluntary scheme in which the association's Classification and Rating Administration assigns ratings — G, PG, PG-13 (added in 1984), R, and NC-17 (which replaced X in 1990) — to guide parents and exhibitors. Subsequent presidents have included former congressman Dan Glickman, former senator Chris Dodd, and (from 2017) former ambassador Charles Rivkin. In September 2019 the association dropped "of America" from its name to reflect its global role, unifying its branding worldwide as the MPA. Netflix became a member in 2019, the first streaming company to join.

Activities

The MPA lobbies governments on behalf of its members on issues including copyright protection, tax incentives for production, trade, and market access abroad. It is a prominent anti-piracy organization, coordinating enforcement actions, maintaining lists of notorious markets, and supporting site-blocking and litigation worldwide through partner groups such as the Alliance for Creativity and Entertainment, founded in 2017. It also publishes annual reports on the economic contribution and audience of the film and television industry.

Criticism

The ratings system has been criticized for secrecy — raters are anonymous — and for treating sexual content more harshly than violence, for inconsistency, and for the commercial stigma attached to the NC-17 rating, themes explored in the 2006 documentary This Film Is Not Yet Rated. The association's anti-piracy lobbying, including support for the proposed SOPA and PIPA legislation in 2011–2012, has also drawn criticism from civil-liberties and technology groups.
