Lurs

The Lurs (also Lur or Lor, Persian: لر) are an Iranian people living mainly in the mountainous west and southwest of Iran, in the Zagros range. Their territory, Luristan, roughly corresponds to the modern provinces of Lorestan, Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad, Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari, and parts of Ilam, Khuzestan and Fars. The Luri population is commonly estimated at around four to five million people.

Language

Luri is a group of closely related Southwestern Iranian dialects, forming a continuum between Persian and Kurdish. It is usually divided into Northern Luri (spoken around Khorramabad), Bakhtiari, and Southern Luri (including Kohgiluyeh and Mamasani varieties). Some classifications treat Bakhtiari as a separate dialect cluster within the Luri group.

History

The Lurs descend in part from the ancient peoples of the Zagros, in a region once dominated by Elam and later by Kassites and Medes. In the medieval Islamic period, Luristan was divided into Greater Lur (Lur-e Bozorg) and Lesser Lur (Lur-e Kuchek), each ruled by local dynasties such as the Hazaraspids and the Khorshidi atabegs. The region long maintained substantial autonomy under tribal khans, and its formidable terrain made it difficult for central governments to control. Under Reza Shah in the 1920s and 1930s, Lur tribes were forcibly disarmed and many nomads were settled, though pastoral nomadism between summer and winter pastures survives to a reduced degree.

Culture

Traditionally, many Lurs were transhumant pastoralists herding sheep and goats, organised in tribal groups (tayefeh) under khans. Lur culture is noted for its music, played on instruments such as the kamancheh and sorna, for distinctive mourning and wedding dances, and for finely woven textiles and black goat-hair tents. The majority of Lurs are Twelver Shia Muslims, with a minority historically adhering to the syncretic Ahl-e Haqq (Yarsan) faith in some northern districts.
