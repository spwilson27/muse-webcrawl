War in Iraq (2013–2017)

The War in Iraq (2013–2017) was fought between Iraq and its allies against the Islamic State (ISIL).

Date: 2013 – 9 December 2017
Location: Iraq
Result: Iraqi government victory

The War in Iraq (2013–2017) was an armed conflict between the government of Iraq, backed by Kurdish forces, Shia militias and a US-led international coalition, and the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL). It began with ISIL's escalating insurgency in western Iraq and its dramatic offensives of 2014, and ended with Iraq's declaration of victory in December 2017 after the liberation of all ISIL-held territory.

Background

After the withdrawal of US forces in 2011, Sunni grievances against the government of Nouri al-Maliki fuelled unrest, and ISIL — growing out of al-Qaeda in Iraq and the Syrian civil war — seized Fallujah and Ramadi in Anbar Province in late 2013 and early 2014.

Course of the war

In June 2014 ISIL captured Mosul, Iraq's second city, and advanced toward Baghdad, proclaiming a "caliphate". The United States and allies launched airstrikes under Operation Inherent Resolve, while Iran-backed Popular Mobilization Forces and Kurdish Peshmerga fought on the ground. Iraqi forces gradually retook Tikrit (2015), Ramadi (2015–16), Fallujah (2016) and finally Mosul after a grinding nine-month battle ending in July 2017. On 9 December 2017, Prime Minister Haider al-Abadi declared final victory over ISIL in Iraq.

Consequences

The war killed tens of thousands of civilians, displaced millions, and devastated cities such as Mosul and Ramadi. ISIL's occupation was marked by mass atrocities, including the genocide of the Yazidis. Reconstruction needs were estimated in the tens of billions of dollars.
