Adenomyosis

Adenomyosis is a medical condition characterised by the growth of endometrial tissue — the tissue that normally lines the uterus — into the muscular wall of the uterus (the myometrium). The displaced tissue continues to respond to hormonal cycles, thickening and bleeding with each menstrual period, which can cause the uterus to enlarge and can lead to heavy, painful periods and chronic pelvic pain. The condition is benign but can significantly affect quality of life and fertility.

[Image: MRI of the pelvis showing a thickened junctional zone in adenomyosis]

Signs and symptoms

Many people with adenomyosis have no symptoms. When symptoms occur they include heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia), severe menstrual cramps (dysmenorrhoea), chronic pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, and a tender, enlarged, "boggy" uterus on examination. The condition is most common in people in their 40s and early 50s who have had children, though improved imaging has led to more frequent diagnosis in younger patients.

Cause

The cause is not fully understood. The leading theories propose that endometrial tissue invades the myometrium after disruption of the boundary between them (for example after pregnancy, caesarean section or uterine surgery), or that the deposits arise from stem cells or from embryonic (Müllerian) rests. Adenomyosis frequently coexists with endometriosis and uterine fibroids.

Diagnosis and treatment

Transvaginal ultrasound and MRI can visualise characteristic features, including a thickened junctional zone, myometrial cysts and striations; historically the diagnosis could only be confirmed by examining the uterus after hysterectomy. Management ranges from analgesics and hormonal treatments (combined contraceptives, progestins, the levonorgestrel intrauterine system, GnRH agonists) to uterine artery embolisation and conservative surgery; hysterectomy is the definitive cure.
