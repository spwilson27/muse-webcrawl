Tennessee Ernie Ford

Ernest Jennings Ford (February 13, 1919 – October 17, 1991), known professionally as Tennessee Ernie Ford, was an American singer and television host. His bass-baritone voice and down-home manner made him a star of country, pop and gospel music; his 1955 recording of "Sixteen Tons" spent ten weeks at number one on the country chart and eight weeks atop the pop chart.

Career

Born in Bristol, Tennessee, Ford worked in radio before serving as a bombardier in the Second World War. Signed to Capitol Records in 1949, he scored country hits such as "Mule Train" and "Shotgun Boogie". On television he starred in NBC's The Ford Show (1956–1961), famous for closing each episode with a hymn — a practice that led to a string of best-selling gospel albums including Hymns (1956).

Legacy

Ford recorded about 50 albums, received a Grammy Award for Great Gospel Songs (1964), a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and posthumous induction into the Country Music Hall of Fame (1990) and Gospel Music Hall of Fame. He died in 1991, aged 72, shortly after a White House state dinner.
