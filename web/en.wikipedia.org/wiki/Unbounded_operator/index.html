Unbounded operator

In mathematics, more specifically functional analysis and operator theory, the notion of an unbounded operator provides an abstract framework for dealing with differential operators, unbounded observables in quantum mechanics, and other applications. Unlike bounded (continuous) linear operators, an unbounded operator may not be defined on the whole space; instead it is defined on a dense subspace, its domain, which becomes an essential part of the data.

Definition

Let X and Y be Banach spaces. An unbounded operator is a linear map T : D(T) → Y, where the domain D(T) is a linear subspace of X. The operator is called closed if its graph is closed in X × Y, and closable if it has a closed extension. Adjoint operators are defined through duality pairings, generalizing the Hilbert-space adjoint, and are central to the theory of self-adjoint operators.

Examples and applications

The differentiation operator d/dx on a space of functions is the prototypical unbounded operator; in quantum mechanics, position and momentum observables are unbounded self-adjoint operators, a fact formalized by the Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem, which states that an everywhere-defined symmetric operator on a Hilbert space must be bounded. The spectral theory of unbounded self-adjoint operators underlies much of mathematical physics, and the Cayley transform relates them to bounded unitary operators.
