The Basel Program was the first Zionist platform, adopted on 31 August 1897 at the First Zionist Congress in Basel, Switzerland. Drafted principally by Max Nordau under the direction of Theodor Herzl, it set out the aims of the Zionist movement: the establishment of a legally assured home for the Jewish people in Palestine.

Text and aims

The program stated: "Zionism seeks to establish a home for the Jewish people in Palestine secured under public law." To this end it proposed four means: the promotion of the settlement of Palestine by Jewish agricultural and industrial workers; the organisation and binding together of Jewry through local and international institutions; the strengthening of Jewish national sentiment and consciousness; and preparatory steps toward obtaining government consent for the attainment of the movement's aims.

Significance

The Basel Program remained the formal platform of the Zionist Organization for decades. Herzl famously wrote in his diary after the congress that in Basel he had "founded the Jewish state", predicting it would become reality within fifty years — a prophecy fulfilled with the establishment of Israel in 1948.
