Chimichurri

Type: Sauce / marinade

Place of origin: Argentina and Uruguay

Main ingredients: Parsley, garlic, olive oil, oregano, red wine vinegar

Chimichurri is an uncooked sauce used both as a marinade and as a table condiment for grilled meat. It originated in Argentina and is also popular in Uruguay and throughout Latin America. The sauce is traditionally served with asado (barbecued meats), especially beef.

Preparation

Chimichurri is made from finely chopped parsley, minced garlic, olive oil, oregano and red wine vinegar. Additional seasonings may include salt, red pepper flakes, cumin, lemon and bay leaf. Two main varieties exist: green chimichurri (chimichurri verde), and red chimichurri (chimichurri rojo), which adds tomato and red bell pepper. The ingredients are whisked together and allowed to rest so the flavours blend before serving.

Etymology

The origin of the name is uncertain. Popular accounts link it to Basque settlers in Argentina — possibly from the Basque word tximitxurri, loosely meaning "a mixture of several things in no particular order" — or to a corruption of English phrases such as "Jimmy's curry" or "give me the curry" (che-mi-curry) associated with British and Irish settlers. None of these explanations is firmly documented.

Use

Chimichurri is spooned over grilled steaks, chorizo and other meats at the table, and is also brushed onto meat during cooking or used as a marinade. It is served with choripán, the chorizo sandwich emblematic of Argentine street food.
