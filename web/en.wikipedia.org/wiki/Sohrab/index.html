Sohrab

Sohrab (Persian: سهراب, Sohrāb) is a legendary warrior from the Persian epic Shahnameh by Ferdowsi. He is the son of the great hero Rostam and the princess Tahmina of Samangan. His tragic story — in which father and son meet in single combat without knowing each other, and Rostam kills Sohrab — is one of the most famous episodes of Persian literature.

The tale

Rostam, on a hunting trip near Samangan, marries Princess Tahmina and leaves her an onyx armband as a token for their child. Sohrab grows up in Turan and, hoping to find his father and make him king of Iran, joins the Turanian army of Afrasiab, who conceals the warriors' identities from each other. In their duel, after Sohrab twice spares the older man, Rostam mortally wounds him; only then does Sohrab reveal the token. Rostam's grief, and the Shah's refusal to send the healing elixir in time, complete the tragedy.

Legacy

The story has been retold across Persian, Turkish, and world literature, notably in Matthew Arnold's poem "Sohrab and Rustum" (1853). Sohrab remains a popular male given name in Iran and the Persian-speaking world, and the tale is a standard subject of Persian miniature painting and traditional storytelling (naqqali).
