A secondary school is an institution that provides secondary education, the stage of formal education following primary education and preceding tertiary education. In most jurisdictions secondary education is part of compulsory schooling and typically covers students aged roughly 11 to 18. In the UNESCO ISCED framework it comprises level 2 (lower secondary) and level 3 (upper secondary).

Organisation by country

Terminology and structure vary widely. In the United States and Canada, secondary education is generally delivered in middle schools (or junior high schools) followed by high schools, ending at grade 12. In England and Wales, pupils transfer at age 11 to secondary schools and sit GCSE examinations at 16, after which they may continue to sixth form, a sixth-form college, or further education. Many European systems divide pupils into academic and vocational tracks — such as the German Gymnasium, Realschule and Hauptschule, or the French collège followed by the lycée. In Australia and New Zealand, secondary schools (often called high schools or colleges) run from about year 7 or 8 to year 12 or 13.

Curriculum

Secondary curricula broaden and deepen primary study: mathematics, science, the national language and literature, foreign languages, history, geography, civics, the arts, physical education, and increasingly computing. Upper secondary education commonly allows specialisation and culminates in school-leaving examinations — such as the A-level, the International Baccalaureate, the French baccalauréat, or the German Abitur — that qualify students for university admission or employment. Many systems also offer vocational and technical pathways alongside academic ones.

Purpose

Secondary education aims to complete basic education, develop abstract and critical thinking, prepare students for work or further study, and support social and personal development during adolescence. Universal secondary education is a target of the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goal 4.
