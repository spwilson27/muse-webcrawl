Independent voter

An independent voter, often called an unaffiliated voter in the United States, is a voter who does not align with a political party. Definitions vary: registration without a party, self-identification in surveys, or actual split-ticket behaviour.

In the United States

Independents form a plurality in many polls, though political scientists note most "lean" toward a party and vote consistently with that lean, leaving true swing independents a smaller share. Registration rules matter: in closed-primary states, unaffiliated voters may be barred from primaries, while open-primary states let them choose a ballot.

Elsewhere

The term also describes voters unattached to parties in other democracies, and candidates running without party affiliation. Independent candidates occasionally win office, especially locally.

Characteristics

Research finds independents on average pay less attention to politics and hold more mixed ideological views, though the group is heterogeneous — spanning libertarians, centrists and disaffected partisans.
