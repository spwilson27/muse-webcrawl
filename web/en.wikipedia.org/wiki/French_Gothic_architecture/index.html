French Gothic architecture is an architectural style which emerged in France in 1140 and was dominant until the mid-16th century. It originated in the Île-de-France and Picardy regions of northern France and gave birth to the Gothic style that spread across Europe. Its defining features include the pointed arch, the rib vault, the flying buttress, and large windows filled with stained glass and tracery.

Origins

The style began with Abbot Suger's rebuilding of the Basilica of Saint-Denis (consecrated 1144), where a luminous chevet combined rib vaults, pointed arches and stained glass. Early Gothic cathedrals followed at Sens, Noyon, Laon and Paris (Notre-Dame, begun 1163), progressively increasing height and window area while retaining massive walls.

High Gothic and Rayonnant

The rebuilding of Chartres Cathedral after the fire of 1194 set the High Gothic pattern: a three-level elevation of arcade, triforium and clerestory, flying buttresses, and vast stained-glass windows. Reims (begun 1211) and Amiens (begun 1220) pushed vault heights still higher; Beauvais, begun in 1225, reached 48 metres before its choir vaults collapsed in 1284. From the 1240s the Rayonnant style, exemplified by Louis IX's Sainte-Chapelle in Paris (1248), dissolved walls into screens of glass and delicate bar tracery, with radiating rose windows giving the phase its name.

Flamboyant Gothic

In the late 14th and 15th centuries the Flamboyant style introduced flame-like, flowing tracery, complex vault patterns and elaborate surface ornament, seen in the west front of Rouen Cathedral, the church of Saint-Maclou in Rouen, and the abbey of Mont Saint-Michel's choir. Civil buildings — town halls, palaces such as the Palais Jacques-Cœur in Bourges — adopted the style as well. It gradually gave way to Renaissance forms in the early sixteenth century.

Features

  • Pointed arch — channels loads more efficiently and permits vaults of varied plan.
  • Rib vault — concentrates thrust on piers, freeing the wall between them.
  • Flying buttress — external half-arch transferring vault thrust to outer piers.
  • Stained glass and rose windows — theological light (lux) as a central design aim.
