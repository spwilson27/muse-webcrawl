Taenia saginata

Taenia saginata (synonym Taeniarhynchus saginatus), commonly known as the beef tapeworm, is a zoonotic tapeworm belonging to the order Cyclophyllidea and genus Taenia. It is an intestinal parasite in humans, which uses cattle as its intermediate host.

Morphology and life cycle

Adult worms live in the human small intestine and may reach 4 to 12 metres in length, with some specimens reported much longer. The body consists of a scolex bearing four suckers but no hooks (unlike the armed scolex of Taenia solium), followed by a chain of proglottids. Gravid proglottids detach and leave the body, releasing eggs on pasture. Cattle ingest the eggs; the larvae encyst in muscle as cysticerci. Humans become infected by eating raw or undercooked beef containing cysticerci.

Symptoms and treatment

Most infections are asymptomatic or cause mild abdominal discomfort, though the passage of motile proglottids can be distressing. Unlike T. solium, T. saginata does not cause human cysticercosis. Treatment is usually a single dose of praziquantel or niclosamide. Prevention relies on cooking beef thoroughly and on meat inspection.
