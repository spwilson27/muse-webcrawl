Muhammad's wives

The wives of Muhammad, the prophet of Islam, are known in Islamic tradition as the "Mothers of the Believers" (Ummahāt al-Muʾminīn). Muhammad is traditionally reported to have married eleven women over the course of his life, beginning with Khadija bint Khuwaylid.

Khadija

Khadija, a wealthy merchant of Mecca, was Muhammad's first wife and his employer; they married when he was about 25 and remained monogamously married for some 25 years until her death around 619 CE. She was the first believer in his prophetic mission and bore him several children, including Fatima.

Later marriages

After Khadija's death, Muhammad married Sawda bint Zamʿa and subsequently Aisha bint Abi Bakr, daughter of his close companion Abu Bakr. Other wives included Hafsa bint Umar; Zaynab bint Khuzayma; Umm Salama; Zaynab bint Jahsh; Juwayriya bint al-Harith; Umm Habiba; Safiyya bint Huyayy; and Maymunah bint al-Harith. Many of these marriages are understood in tradition as forming political and tribal alliances or providing for widows. Maria al-Qibtiyya, an Egyptian Copt, is also associated with his household, though her exact status is debated.

Significance

The wives, particularly Aisha and Umm Salama, are important transmitters of hadith — accounts of Muhammad's sayings and practices — and remain revered figures in Islam.
