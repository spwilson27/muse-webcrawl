Arancini

Place of origin: Italy (Sicily)

Type: Fried rice balls

Main ingredients: Rice, ragù, mozzarella, breadcrumbs

Arancini (Italian: [aranˈtʃiːni]; Sicilian: arancini or arancine) are Italian rice balls that are stuffed, coated with breadcrumbs and deep fried. They are a staple of Sicilian cuisine. The most common fillings are ragù (meat sauce), mozzarella and ham, or mushrooms. The name derives from their shape and golden colour, which resembles an orange (arancia in Italian).

History

Arancini are thought to have originated in 10th-century Sicily under Arab rule, when rice and saffron were introduced to the island and dishes of rice seasoned with saffron and meat were common. The practice of breading and frying the rice is said to have developed later, partly for portability — the balls were a convenient food for travellers and are associated with the feast of Saint Lucy (Santa Lucia) on 13 December, when they are traditionally eaten in Palermo.

Regional variations

In eastern Sicily, particularly Catania, arancini are often conical in shape, said to evoke Mount Etna, while in Palermo they are typically round. A long-running debate concerns the gender of the name: arancine (feminine) is traditional in the west of the island and arancini (masculine) in the east. Fillings have multiplied in modern rosticcerie to include pistachio, swordfish, 'nduja, and sweet versions with chocolate or ricotta.

Preparation

Cooled saffron risotto is formed around the filling, rolled in flour, egg, and breadcrumbs, then deep fried until golden. In Sicily they are sold in bars and rosticcerie and eaten as street food or a light meal.
