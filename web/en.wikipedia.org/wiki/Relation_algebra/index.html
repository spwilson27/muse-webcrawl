Relation algebra

In mathematics, an algebra of binary relations, usually called a relation algebra, is an algebraic structure whose elements are binary relations and whose operations include Boolean operations on relations, composition of relations, and conversion (taking the converse relation).

History

The calculus of relations originated with Augustus De Morgan and Charles Sanders Peirce in the 19th century, and was developed into an equational theory by Ernst Schröder. Alfred Tarski and his students gave the modern axiomatic treatment of relation algebras in the 1940s.

Definition

A relation algebra is a Boolean algebra equipped with an associative binary operation of composition having an identity element (the identity relation), and a unary involution of conversion satisfying certain additional equations connecting the three operations. Not every abstract relation algebra can be represented as an algebra of genuine binary relations; the question of representability is a central topic of the theory.

Applications

Relation algebras provide an algebraic foundation for the relational algebra of database theory and for reasoning about programs and specifications in computer science.
