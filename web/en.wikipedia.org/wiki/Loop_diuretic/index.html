Loop diuretic

ClassDiuretics
Site of actionThick ascending limb of the loop of Henle
TargetNa+–K+–2Cl− co-transporter (NKCC2)
ExamplesFurosemide, bumetanide, torasemide, ethacrynic acid

Loop diuretics are a class of diuretic drugs that act on the Na+–K+–2Cl− co-transporter (NKCC2) in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle in the kidney's nephrons. Because this segment normally reabsorbs about a quarter of the filtered sodium load, blocking it produces the most powerful diuresis of any diuretic class; loop diuretics are therefore called "high-ceiling" diuretics.

Medical uses

The principal agents are furosemide, bumetanide, torasemide (torsemide) and ethacrynic acid. They are used principally for oedema associated with heart failure, liver cirrhosis and kidney disease, for acute pulmonary oedema (where intravenous furosemide acts rapidly), and as adjuncts in hypertension; they are also used to treat hypercalcaemia, since blocking the ascending limb increases calcium excretion.

Adverse effects

Common side effects follow from electrolyte loss: hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, dehydration and metabolic alkalosis, together with raised uric acid (provoking gout) and, at high doses or with rapid intravenous injection, ototoxicity. Ethacrynic acid, a non-sulfonamide, is an alternative for patients with sulfonamide allergy.
