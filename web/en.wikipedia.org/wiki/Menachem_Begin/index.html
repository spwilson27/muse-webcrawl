Menachem Begin (Hebrew: מְנַחֵם בֵּגִין; 16 August 1913 – 9 March 1992) was an Israeli politician who served as the sixth Prime Minister of Israel from 1977 to 1983. A former commander of the Irgun paramilitary organisation in the pre-state underground, he founded the Herut party and led the Israeli right in opposition for nearly three decades before his Likud bloc won the 1977 election — the "upheaval" (Mahapakh) that ended Labour's dominance of Israeli politics. In office he negotiated the Camp David Accords (1978) and the Egypt–Israel peace treaty (1979) with Anwar Sadat, for which both men received the Nobel Peace Prize.

Early life and Irgun

Begin was born in Brest-Litovsk and studied law at the University of Warsaw, becoming a leader of Betar, the Revisionist Zionist youth movement founded by Ze'ev Jabotinsky. After the Soviet invasion of Poland he was arrested by the NKVD and sent to the Gulag; released under the Sikorski–Mayski agreement, he reached Mandatory Palestine with the Polish Anders Army in 1942. In December 1943 he became commander of the Irgun, leading its revolt against the British, which included the King David Hotel bombing of 1946. The Irgun's role at Deir Yassin (1948) remains controversial. In June 1948 Begin narrowly avoided civil war during the Altalena affair, when David Ben-Gurion ordered the shelling of the Irgun arms ship.

Opposition years

Begin transformed the Irgun into the Herut political party and sat in the Knesset from 1949. He vehemently opposed reparations from West Germany in 1952, and led Herut into the Likud alliance in 1973. His support among Mizrahi Jews and his populist rhetoric broadened the right's appeal until the electoral breakthrough of 1977.

Prime minister

Begin's government liberalised the economy, launched Project Renewal for distressed neighbourhoods, expanded West Bank settlements, and strengthened ties with the United States. Following Sadat's visit to Jerusalem in November 1977, the Camp David Accords led to the 1979 peace treaty with Egypt — Israel's first with an Arab state — and full withdrawal from Sinai. In June 1981 Begin ordered the bombing of Iraq's Osirak nuclear reactor. In June 1982 Israel invaded Lebanon to expel the PLO; the war and the Sabra and Shatila massacre damaged Begin's standing, and, depressed after his wife Aliza's death, he resigned in 1983 ("I cannot go on") and withdrew from public life.
