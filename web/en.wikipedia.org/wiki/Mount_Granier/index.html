Mount Granier
Elevation1,933 m (6,342 ft)
LocationSavoie and Isère, France
RangeChartreuse Mountains

Mount Granier (French: Mont Granier) is a 1,933-metre limestone mountain in the Chartreuse range of the French Prealps, between Chambéry in Savoie and Grenoble in Isère. Its sheer northern face, towering some 900 metres above the vineyards of the Combe de Savoie, makes it one of the most recognisable peaks of the region.

The landslide of 1248

On the night of 24–25 November 1248, a massive landslide from Mount Granier buried the town of Saint-André and several villages, killing an estimated 1,000 to 5,000 people — one of the largest recorded landslides in European history. The debris field created the lakes and vineyard landscape of Les Abymes ("the abysses"). Smaller rockfalls, notably in 2016, have closed climbing routes on the face.

Climbing and access

The normal route ascends from the Col du Granier via the Pas des Barres, with ladders and fixed cables, while the north face offers long rock climbs. The summit rewards hikers with views over Mont Blanc, the Belledonne range and Lake Bourget.
