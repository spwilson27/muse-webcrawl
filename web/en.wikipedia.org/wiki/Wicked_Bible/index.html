Wicked Bible

The Wicked Bible, also called the Adulterous Bible or Sinners' Bible, is an edition of the King James Bible published in 1631 by the royal printers Robert Barker and Martin Lucas in London. It is famous for a typographical error in the Ten Commandments: the word "not" was omitted from Exodus 20:14, so the text read "Thou shalt commit adultery."

Consequences

When the error was discovered about a year later, King Charles I was outraged. Barker and Lucas were fined £300 (a very large sum, roughly a lifetime's wages), had their printing licence revoked, and the edition was recalled and destroyed. Only a small number of copies — around a dozen or fewer — survive today, and they are valuable collectors' items.

Speculation

Theories about the error range from simple compositor's mistake to sabotage by a rival printer, Bonham Norton, though no proof of deliberate tampering has been established.
