Lauberhorn ski races

The Lauberhorn course beneath the Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau

The Lauberhorn ski races (German: Lauberhornrennen) are among the world's most famous alpine ski races, held annually in mid-January near Wengen in the Bernese Oberland of Switzerland. First run in 1930, the event is a fixture of the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup and is renowned for its downhill course, the longest in World Cup racing at about 4.27 kilometres (2.65 miles).

The downhill course

The Lauberhorn downhill starts at 2,315 metres on the shoulder of the Lauberhorn mountain and descends more than 1,000 vertical metres to Wengen. Racers reach speeds of up to about 160 km/h (100 mph), the fastest on the World Cup circuit, on the Hanegg section. Famous features include the Hundschopf jump, the Minsch-Kante, the Wasserstation tunnel beneath the railway line, and the Österreichisches Loch ("Austrian hole"). Winning times are around two and a half minutes, an unusually long duration that tests endurance as well as technique.

Other events

The race weekend traditionally includes a slalom and, in earlier formats, an alpine combined. The slalom is held on a separate, steep piste above Wengen. The event draws tens of thousands of spectators to the car-free resort, which is reachable only by the Wengernalp railway.

History

The races were founded in 1930 by the Ski Club Wengen and Ernst Gertsch. Along with the Hahnenkamm races in Kitzbühel, Austria, the Lauberhorn forms part of the classic core of downhill racing. The event has occasionally been cancelled or shortened because of weather, including heavy snowfall or high winds.
