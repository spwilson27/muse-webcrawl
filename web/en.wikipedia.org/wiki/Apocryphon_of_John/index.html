The Apocryphon of John, also called the Secret Book of John, is a 2nd-century Gnostic text (in the Sethian tradition) that presents itself as a secret revelation given by the risen Jesus to the apostle John. It is one of the most important sources for Gnostic mythology and cosmology. The church father Irenaeus summarised and attacked a version of it in Against Heresies (c. 180 AD).

Contents

The text describes the ineffable supreme God, the Monad, and the emanation of the divine realm (the Pleroma), including the figure of Barbelo and the self-generated Christ. It recounts the fall of Sophia (Wisdom), whose unauthorised act produces the ignorant creator god Yaldabaoth — identified with the God of the Old Testament — who fashions the material world and humanity with his archons. A long middle section retells Genesis 1–7 from a Gnostic perspective, followed by a dialogue on the fate of souls and salvation through revealed knowledge (gnosis).

Manuscripts

No Greek copy survives; the work is preserved in four Coptic manuscripts — three among the Nag Hammadi library (codices II, III and IV) and one in the Berlin Gnostic Codex — in long and short recensions. Its multiple copies attest to its central place in Sethian Gnostic communities.
