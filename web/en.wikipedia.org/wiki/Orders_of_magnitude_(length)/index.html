Orders of magnitude (length)

Length scales

Unit: Metre (SI)

Range: 10−35 m to 1026 m and beyond

An order of magnitude is a factor of ten. This article compares lengths across the full range of scales known in physics, from the smallest meaningful length to the diameter of the observable universe, a span of more than sixty orders of magnitude.

Subatomic and atomic scales

LengthExample
1.6 × 10−35 mPlanck length, the scale at which quantum gravity is expected to dominate
~10−18 mSensitivity limit of the Large Hadron Collider for particle sizes
0.84 fm (10−15 m)Approximate radius of a proton
53 pmBohr radius of a hydrogen atom
100 pm – 1 nmTypical atomic diameters and the wavelength of X-rays

Astronomical scales

LengthExample
1 AU (1.496 × 1011 m)Mean distance from the Earth to the Sun
1 light-year (9.46 × 1015 m)Distance light travels in one Julian year
~100,000 light-yearsDiameter of the Milky Way galaxy
93 billion light-yearsApproximate diameter of the observable universe

Because the scales involved differ so enormously, lengths are commonly grouped in powers of ten, each group constituting one order of magnitude. The metre was redefined in 1983 — and again within the revised SI of 2019 — as the distance light travels in vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second, fixing the speed of light exactly.
