August Vincent Theodore Spies (December 10, 1855 – November 11, 1887) was an American upholsterer, radical labour activist, and newspaper editor. Spies is remembered as one of the anarchists in Chicago who were found guilty of conspiracy to commit murder following a bomb attack on police in Chicago's Haymarket Square in 1886, in a controversial trial. Spies was hanged the following year along with three others; he is considered a martyr of the labour movement.

August Spies, editor of the Arbeiter-Zeitung.

Biography

Born in Friedewald, Germany, Spies emigrated to the United States in 1872 and settled in Chicago, where he worked as an upholsterer and became involved in socialist politics and the trade union movement. He became editor of the German-language anarchist newspaper Arbeiter-Zeitung in 1880.

On May 3, 1886, Spies spoke at a rally near the McCormick Harvesting Machine Company works where strikers clashed with police; two workers were killed. The following evening Spies addressed the rally in Haymarket Square that ended when a bomb was thrown at advancing police. Although no evidence linked Spies to the bomb itself, prosecutors argued his speeches and writings incited the act, and he was convicted of conspiracy.

On the scaffold on November 11, 1887, Spies reportedly declared: "The time will come when our silence will be more powerful than the voices you strangle today." In 1893, Illinois governor John Peter Altgeld pardoned the surviving defendants and condemned the trial as unjust.
