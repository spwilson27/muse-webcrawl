Piraeus (Greek: Πειραιάς) is a port city within the Athens metropolitan area, in the Attica region of Greece. It lies about 12 kilometres southwest of Athens city centre, on the eastern coast of the Saronic Gulf. Piraeus is Greece's largest port and one of the largest passenger ports in Europe, serving as the main gateway to the Greek islands. According to the 2021 census, the municipality had a population of about 163,000, making it the fifth-largest municipality in Greece.

History

Piraeus was chosen as Athens' port in the early 5th century BCE, when Themistocles persuaded the Athenians to fortify its three natural harbours — Kantharos, Zea and Munichia — instead of the open roadstead at Phaleron. Laid out on a grid plan attributed to Hippodamus of Miletus, it was joined to Athens by the Long Walls, making the two cities a single fortress. Its naval dockyards housed the triremes that underpinned Athenian sea power; the loss of Piraeus at the end of the Peloponnesian War (404 BCE) marked Athens' defeat. The port declined under Roman and Byzantine rule and was a small settlement by the Ottoman period.

After Greek independence Piraeus was revived as the new kingdom's chief port, growing explosively with industry, shipping and the arrival of refugees from Asia Minor in 1922, who left a deep mark on its culture and music (rebetiko). Heavily bombed in the Second World War, it was rebuilt as a modern shipping and ferry hub.

The modern port

The Port of Piraeus handles most of Greece's ferry traffic to the Aegean islands and Crete and is a major Mediterranean container port. In 2016 China's COSCO Shipping acquired a majority stake in the Piraeus Port Authority, investing heavily in the container terminals and making Piraeus a principal European entry point in China's Belt and Road Initiative. The city is also home to much of Greece's shipping industry, the Hellenic Naval Academy, and the football club Olympiacos, based at the Karaiskakis Stadium.
