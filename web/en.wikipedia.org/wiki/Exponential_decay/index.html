Exponential decay
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

Exponential decay describes a quantity decreasing at a rate proportional to its current value: N(t) = N₀e^(−λt), where λ is the decay constant. Its signature feature is a constant half-life — the time to halve — regardless of how much remains.

The process governs radioactive decay, drug elimination from the body, cooling (Newton's law), capacitor discharge and the damping of oscillations. The inverse, exponential growth, governs early-stage epidemics and compound interest; both arise because the change per unit time scales with the amount present.
