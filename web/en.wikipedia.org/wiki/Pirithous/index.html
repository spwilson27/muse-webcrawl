Pirithous

In Greek mythology, Pirithous (also Pirithoos or Peirithoos) was the son of Ixion (or, in some accounts, of Zeus) and Dia, a king of the Lapiths of Thessaly, and the close friend and companion of the hero Theseus.

Friendship with Theseus

According to legend, Pirithous tested Theseus by driving off his cattle from Marathon; when Theseus pursued him, the two were so impressed by each other's courage and appearance that they swore an oath of friendship instead of fighting. Pirithous fought alongside the Lapiths at the battle against the Centaurs that broke out at his wedding feast to Hippodamia, when the drunken centaur Eurytion attempted to carry off the bride — the Centauromachy, a favourite subject of Greek art.

Descent to the underworld

After the deaths of their wives, Theseus and Pirithous resolved to marry daughters of Zeus. They abducted Helen for Theseus, and then descended into Hades to seize Persephone as a bride for Pirithous. Hades trapped them, seating them on the Chair of Forgetfulness. Heracles later rescued Theseus during his twelfth labour, but when he tried to free Pirithous, the earth shook, and Pirithous was left in the underworld forever.
