Bulk cargo

Bulk cargo
Types: Dry bulk, liquid bulk
Carried by: Bulk carriers, tankers
Examples: Grain, coal, iron ore, crude oil

Bulk cargo is commodity cargo that is transported unpackaged in large quantities, loaded directly into the hold of a ship rather than in individual containers or packages. It is distinguished from break bulk cargo, which is loaded in separate units such as bags, boxes, or barrels.

Categories

Dry bulk includes the major bulks — iron ore, coal, and grain — and minor bulks such as steel products, cement, sugar, salt, and aggregates. Liquid bulk includes crude oil and petroleum products, liquefied natural gas and LPG, chemicals, and edible oils, carried in tankers. Cargoes are loaded and unloaded by conveyors, grabs, pumps, and pneumatic systems at specialized bulk terminals.

Trade significance

Bulk shipping underpins the world economy: iron ore and coal feed steel production, grain shipments feed populations, and crude oil moves energy across oceans. Dry bulk vessels range from small coastal ships to very large ore carriers exceeding 400,000 deadweight tonnes, and freight rates are tracked by indices such as the Baltic Dry Index.

Safety

Bulk cargoes present special hazards, including cargo shifting, liquefaction of cargoes such as nickel ore and bauxite, dust explosions in grain, and structural stresses during loading, regulated under conventions including the IMO's IMSBC Code.
