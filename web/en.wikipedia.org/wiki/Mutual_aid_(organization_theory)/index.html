Mutual aid (organization theory)

Mutual aid groups coordinate neighbours to meet material needs directly.

Mutual aid, in organization theory and political thought, is a principle of organisation in which people cooperate voluntarily and reciprocally to meet one another's needs — food, shelter, care, disaster relief — without a central authority distributing charity from above. The term is most associated with the anarchist Peter Kropotkin, whose 1902 book Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution argued that cooperation, as much as competition, drives evolution in animal and human societies.

Concept

Unlike charity, which is typically a one-way transfer from donor to recipient, mutual aid is horizontal and reciprocal: members both give and receive, and the recipient of help today may be its provider tomorrow. Kropotkin pointed to medieval guilds, village communes and friendly societies as historical examples. In organisation theory, mutual aid describes self-organised networks that rely on shared norms, trust and decentralised coordination rather than hierarchy or market exchange.

History and modern examples

Friendly societies and fraternal organisations in 19th-century Britain and North America pooled members' contributions to provide sickness benefits, burial funds and support for widows — a function later partly assumed by welfare states and commercial insurance. Mutual aid revived in activist contexts: the Black Panther Party's "survival programs" such as Free Breakfast for Children in the 1970s, disaster relief networks after Hurricane Katrina (such as the Common Ground Collective), Occupy Sandy after Hurricane Sandy in 2012, and thousands of neighbourhood mutual aid groups formed during the COVID-19 pandemic from 2020 onwards, which organised grocery deliveries, prescription collection and financial solidarity funds. Critics note such groups can struggle with scale, burnout and informal exclusion; supporters argue they respond faster and with more dignity than bureaucratic relief. This article concerns mutual aid as an organisational principle; for the legally constituted form of organisation owned by its members, see Mutual organization.
