Sanremo Music Festival

Festival di Sanremo
Location: Teatro Ariston, Sanremo, Italy
First held: 1951
Broadcaster: RAI 1
Role: Selects Italy's Eurovision entry (usually)

The Sanremo Music Festival (Italian: Festival della canzone italiana di Sanremo) is the most popular Italian song contest and awards ceremony, held annually in the city of Sanremo, Liguria, since 1951. It is broadcast live on Rai 1 over five evenings, usually in February, and has been the inspiration for the Eurovision Song Contest.

History

The first festival took place in 1951 at the Sanremo Casino; since 1977 it has been staged at the Teatro Ariston. It launched the careers of many Italian stars, including Domenico Modugno, whose winning "Nel blu, dipinto di blu (Volare)" (1958) became an international hit, as well as Adriano Celentano, Mina, Eros Ramazzotti, Laura Pausini, and Andrea Bocelli.

Format and significance

Competing artists — divided between established "Big" artists and newcomers — perform original, previously unreleased Italian songs. Voting combines juries and public televoting. Since 2015 the winner of the Big Artists category has generally been offered the right to represent Italy at the Eurovision Song Contest. The festival remains a major national media event, routinely attracting television audiences of more than ten million viewers per night.
