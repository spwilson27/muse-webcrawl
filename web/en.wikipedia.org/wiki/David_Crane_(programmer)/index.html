David Crane (programmer)

Born1955, Nappanee, Indiana, U.S.
OccupationVideo game designer, programmer
Known forCo-founding Activision; Pitfall!

David Crane (born 1955) is an American video game designer and programmer. In 1979 he co-founded Activision, the first third-party video game developer, together with Alan Miller, Bob Whitehead, Larry Kaplan and Jim Levy, after leaving Atari over its refusal to credit designers.

Career

At Atari, Crane designed games including Outlaw, Canyon Bomber and slot-machine simulations for the Atari 2600. At Activision he created Dragster, Fishing Derby, Laser Blast, Freeway and Grand Prix, and above all Pitfall! (1982), one of the best-selling Atari 2600 games ever, which sold over four million copies and founded the platform-game genre conventions of running, jumping and swinging across a scrolling jungle. He also developed the Display Processor Chip used in later 2600 cartridges.

Later work

Crane left Activision in the late 1980s and co-founded Absolute Entertainment, producing titles such as A Boy and His Blob (1989), for which he won design awards, and Amazing Tennis. He later worked at Skyworks Technologies on advergames and casual games, and remains active in retro-gaming projects and speaking on early game development.
