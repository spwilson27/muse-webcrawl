The Mediterranean and Middle East theatre was a major theatre of operations during the Second World War, fought between June 1940 and May 1945 across the Mediterranean Sea, North and East Africa, the Horn of Africa, Greece and the Balkans, the Middle East, and Italy.

Overview

Fighting began when Italy declared war on Britain and France on 10 June 1940. Italy attacked Egypt from Libya and British possessions in East Africa. British Commonwealth forces counterattacked in Operation Compass, and Germany intervened with Erwin Rommel's Afrika Korps in 1941. The desert war swung back and forth until the Second Battle of El Alamein (October–November 1942) and the Anglo-American landings in Morocco and Algeria (Operation Torch) doomed the Axis position in Africa; Axis forces in Tunisia surrendered in May 1943.

Italy and the Balkans

The Allies invaded Sicily in July 1943, prompting the fall of Mussolini; mainland Italy was invaded in September 1943, and Italy switched sides, though German forces occupied the north and fought a long defensive campaign up the peninsula until May 1945. Earlier, Germany and Italy had invaded Yugoslavia and Greece in 1941, and Britain fought campaigns in Iraq, Syria, and Iran. The naval war for control of the Mediterranean — the siege of Malta, convoy battles, and the attack on Taranto — ran throughout.

Outcome

The theatre tied down substantial Axis forces, gave the Western Allies their first victories over Germany, and secured Middle Eastern oil and the Suez Canal route. Fighting ended with the surrender of German forces in Italy on 2 May 1945.
