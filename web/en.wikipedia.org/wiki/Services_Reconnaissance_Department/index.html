Services Reconnaissance Department

The Services Reconnaissance Department (SRD), also known as Z Special Unit after its best-known operational arm, was a specialist Allied special operations unit of the Second World War that conducted reconnaissance, raiding, and covert operations behind Japanese lines in Southeast Asia and the Southwest Pacific. Based in Australia, it reported through the Allied Intelligence Bureau.

Formation

The SRD was established in 1942 following the creation of the Allied Intelligence Bureau under General MacArthur's headquarters. It absorbed the role of the British Special Operations Australia and was given the cover name Services Reconnaissance Department. Its operating section became known as "Z Special Unit", while its administrative and training apparatus worked under other cover names. Training took place at sites including Fraser Island (Z Experimental Station) and Cairns in Queensland.

Operations

Z Special Unit's most famous mission was Operation Jaywick in September 1943, in which a party led by Ivan Lyon sailed the captured Japanese fishing vessel Krait to Singapore Harbour and sank or damaged some 39,000 tons of shipping with limpet mines. Its follow-up, Operation Rimau (1944), ended in disaster, with all 23 participants killed or captured and executed. SRD teams also operated extensively in Borneo — supporting the Semut operations and the 1945 landings — as well as in Timor, New Guinea, and the Netherlands East Indies, gathering intelligence, rescuing airmen, and working with local peoples.

Legacy

The SRD was disbanded after the war, though elements of its experience informed Australia's post-war special forces. The vessel Krait is preserved at the Australian National Maritime Museum, and Jaywick remains one of the most celebrated special operations of the war.
