The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria (often called the Global Fund) is an international financing organisation based in Geneva, Switzerland, created in 2002 to raise and disburse resources to prevent and treat HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, and malaria.

History and work

The Fund was established following calls at the 2001 UN General Assembly Special Session on HIV/AIDS and the G8 summit for a new financing mechanism. It operates as a partnership between governments, civil society, and the private sector, channelling grants to more than 100 countries through performance-based funding. It does not implement programmes itself but finances national and community programmes, and it raises money in multi-year "replenishment" rounds from donor governments and private contributors such as the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation.

Impact

Programmes supported by the Global Fund are credited with saving tens of millions of lives since 2002, contributing to sharp declines in deaths from AIDS, tuberculosis, and malaria in low- and middle-income countries.
