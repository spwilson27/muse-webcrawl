Fathers and Sons (novel)

Fathers and Sons (Russian: Ottsy i deti, literally "Fathers and Children") is an 1862 novel by Russian writer Ivan Turgenev, published in The Russian Messenger. It is regarded as one of the great works of 19th-century fiction and popularised the term nihilism.

AuthorIvan Turgenev
LanguageRussian
Published1862
GenreRealist novel

Plot

Arkady Kirsanov returns from university to his father's estate Marino with his friend Yevgeny Bazarov, a medical student and self-proclaimed nihilist who rejects authority, tradition and romantic sentiment. Bazarov clashes with Arkady's uncle Pavel, a refined liberal of the older generation. At a provincial town Bazarov meets the widow Anna Sergeyevna Odintsova; falling in love undermines his doctrines. After a duel with Pavel and a visit to his humble parents, Bazarov contracts typhus while treating peasants and dies, tended by his grieving parents. Arkady marries Odintsova's sister Katya and settles into estate life.

Themes

The novel dramatises the generational conflict between 1840s liberals and 1860s radicals, science versus art, and the emotional limits of nihilism. Bazarov became an archetype debated fiercely by contemporary critics — some saw him as satire, others as a hero.

Reception and legacy

Publication provoked controversy in Russia and made Turgenev's reputation in Europe and America, where Henry James admired it. The term "nihilist" entered wide usage through Bazarov.
