Smoke

Smoke is an aerosol (a suspension or dispersion of solid particles in a gas) of unburned particles, most commonly carbon and ash, which are the result of incomplete combustion of carbonaceous materials such as wood, coal, tobacco, oil and other fuels. Smoke may also contain liquid droplets, gases such as carbon monoxide, and thousands of other chemicals.

Composition

Most of the particles in smoke are fine carbonaceous solids, often with adsorbed organic compounds. Wood smoke contains gases and vapours including carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and particulate matter (much of it PM2.5, small enough to penetrate deep into the lungs). Tobacco smoke additionally contains nicotine and numerous carcinogens.

Health hazards

Smoke inhalation is the primary cause of death in victims of indoor fires. The smoke kills by a combination of thermal damage, poisoning and pulmonary irritation caused by carbon monoxide, hydrogen cyanide and other combustion products. Wildfire smoke can degrade air quality over entire regions, and occupational and household smoke exposure is a major global health burden.

Uses

Smoke has been used by humans since antiquity: to preserve and flavour food (smoking), to signal over distances (smoke signals), in religious and ceremonial practices (incense), for pest control, and in warfare as an obscurant (smoke screens and smoke grenades).
