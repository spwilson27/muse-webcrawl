The American Physical Society (APS) is a nonprofit membership organisation of physicists, founded in 1899 at Columbia University. It is the world's second-largest organisation of physicists (after the German Physical Society) and publishes a family of leading peer-reviewed journals, organises major scientific meetings, and advocates for physics research and education.

Publications

The APS publishes the Physical Review journals — Physical Review Letters, Physical Review A through E, Physical Review X, Physical Review Applied, Physical Review Fluids, Physical Review Materials, and others — and Reviews of Modern Physics. Its magazine Physics Today is published by the American Institute of Physics on behalf of the society's members.

Meetings and activities

The annual March Meeting is one of the largest physics conferences in the world, drawing more than 10,000 attendees; the April Meeting focuses on astrophysics, particle physics, and gravitation. The society awards numerous prizes and medals, runs education and outreach programs, and issues statements on science policy. It is a member society of the American Institute of Physics.
