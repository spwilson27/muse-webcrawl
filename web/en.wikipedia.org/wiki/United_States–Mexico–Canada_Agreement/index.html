United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement

The United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA; known as CUSMA in Canada and T-MEC in Mexico) is a free trade agreement between Canada, Mexico, and the United States that replaced the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA). It was signed on November 30, 2018, revised in December 2019, and entered into force on July 1, 2020.

Negotiation

Renegotiation of NAFTA was a campaign priority of US President Donald Trump, who described NAFTA as a bad deal for American workers. Talks began in August 2017. A bilateral understanding between the US and Mexico in August 2018 was followed by Canada's agreement to join at the end of September 2018, shortly before a US-imposed deadline.

Provisions

  • Automobiles: 75% of a vehicle's content must be produced in North America to qualify for tariff-free treatment, and 40–45% must be made by workers earning at least US$16 per hour.
  • Dairy: Canada opened a larger share of its dairy market to US producers.
  • Labour and environment: strengthened enforcement provisions, including a rapid-response mechanism for facility-specific labour complaints in Mexico.
  • Digital trade: prohibitions on customs duties for digital products and protections for cross-border data flows.
  • Sunset clause: the agreement runs for 16 years, with a joint review after six years.
