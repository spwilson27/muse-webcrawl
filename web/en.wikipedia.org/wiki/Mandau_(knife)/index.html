Mandau (knife)

The mandau is the traditional weapon of the Dayak people of Borneo, used both as a working blade and as a ceremonial headhunting sword. It is closely related to the smaller jimpul and is carried in a decorated wooden scabbard, often together with a small companion knife.

Description

The single-edged blade, typically 50 to 70 cm long, is convex on one side and slightly concave on the other, often inlaid near the tip with brass or silver dots and geometric patterns. The hilt is carved from horn or wood into stylised animal and spirit motifs, wrapped with rattan, and sometimes tufted with hair. Scabbards are carved and painted and may carry amulets and carvings recording the owner's journeys.

Cultural role

Among groups such as the Iban, Kayan, and Kenyah, the mandau was central to headhunting tradition; blades associated with taken heads were considered especially potent. Today mandaus are produced mainly as heirlooms, ceremonial objects, and art pieces.
