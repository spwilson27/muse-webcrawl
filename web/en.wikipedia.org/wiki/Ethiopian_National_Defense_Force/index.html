The Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF) is the military of Ethiopia, comprising the Ethiopian Ground Force and the Ethiopian Air Force; Ethiopia has been landlocked since Eritrean independence in 1993 and maintains no navy of consequence, though a naval arm was re-established in 2019. The ENDF is among the largest armed forces in Africa and contributes heavily to African Union and United Nations peacekeeping missions.

History

The modern force descends from the armies of the Ethiopian Empire, reorganised under Emperor Haile Selassie with foreign advisers and tested in the Second Italo-Ethiopian War (1935–36) and the Korean War, where the Kagnew Battalion fought with distinction. Under the Derg (1974–1991) the military expanded massively with Soviet support to become one of sub-Saharan Africa's largest, fighting Eritrean separatists and Somalia in the Ogaden War (1977–78). After the Derg's fall in 1991, the EPRDF rebuilt the armed forces around Tigrayan-led command structures.

Recent conflicts

Ethiopia fought Eritrea in the border war of 1998–2000 and intervened in Somalia from 2006 against Islamist militants, later contributing troops to AMISOM. From November 2020 to November 2022 the ENDF fought the Tigray People's Liberation Front and allied regional forces in the Tigray War, a devastating internal conflict ended by the Pretoria Agreement. Operations against insurgencies in Oromia and Amhara have continued since.

Structure

The force is organised into regional commands (Northern, Eastern, Western, Southern and Central), special forces, and the Republican Guard protecting the federal leadership. Ethiopia's defence industry, coordinated by the Metals and Engineering Corporation (METEC) and successor bodies, produces small arms, vehicles and ammunition.
