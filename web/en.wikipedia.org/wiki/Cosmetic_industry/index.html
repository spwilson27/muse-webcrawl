Cosmetic industry

The cosmetic industry manufactures and distributes cosmetics — products applied to the body for cleansing, beautifying or altering appearance, including make-up, skincare, haircare, fragrance and personal-care products. It is a global business worth several hundred billion US dollars annually.

History

Cosmetics have been used since antiquity — kohl in ancient Egypt, rouge in China and Greece — but the modern industry arose in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with entrepreneurs such as Helena Rubinstein, Elizabeth Arden, Max Factor and Eugène Schueller, founder of L'Oréal. Mass production, department stores and advertising made cosmetics a mass-market good after the Second World War.

Market

Skincare is the largest product category, followed by haircare and make-up. The Asia-Pacific region is the largest market. Large multinational groups dominate, alongside fast-growing independent ("indie") and celebrity brands sold through social media and e-commerce.

Regulation and issues

Regulation varies: the European Union bans animal testing for cosmetics and requires pre-market safety assessment, while the United States Food and Drug Administration historically had limited pre-market authority, expanded by the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022. Ongoing debates concern ingredient safety, microplastics, sustainability, marketing claims and the industry's effect on body image.
