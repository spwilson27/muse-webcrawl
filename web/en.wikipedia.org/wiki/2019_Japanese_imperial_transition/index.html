The 2019 Japanese imperial transition was the change of the Chrysanthemum Throne from Emperor Akihito to his son Emperor Naruhito. Akihito abdicated on 30 April 2019 — the first abdication by a Japanese emperor in more than 200 years — and Naruhito acceded the following day, ending the Heisei era and beginning the Reiwa era. The transition, planned for over two years under special legislation, unfolded through a carefully choreographed series of ceremonies through the autumn of 2019.

Background

In August 2016, Akihito delivered a rare televised address hinting at his wish to abdicate, citing age and declining health and his concern that he could no longer fulfil the emperor's demanding constitutional and ritual duties. Because the Imperial House Law made no provision for abdication, the Diet in June 2017 enacted a special law permitting Akihito alone to step down. The government later set the date, arranging an extended Golden Week holiday around the transition.

Abdication and accession

On 30 April 2019 Akihito formally relinquished the throne in the Taijō no Gi ceremony at the Imperial Palace, thanking the Japanese people in a final address as emperor; he assumed the title Emperor Emeritus (Jōkō). At midnight the era changed to Reiwa — a name drawn from the Man'yōshū poetry anthology, announced a month earlier — and on 1 May Naruhito succeeded in the Kenji-tō Shōkei no Gi, inheriting the imperial regalia and seals, before delivering his first address as emperor.

Enthronement

The formal enthronement ceremony, Sokui Rei Seiden no Gi, was held on 22 October 2019, attended by royalty and dignitaries from some 180 countries; Naruhito, enthroned on the Takamikura throne, pledged to fulfil his role as symbol of the state and of the unity of the people. A celebratory parade through Tokyo followed on 10 November. The cycle concluded with the Daijōsai, the sacred first-fruits offering by the new emperor, in November 2019.

Significance

The transition was the first abdication since Emperor Kōkaku in 1817 and the first accession under the post-war constitution to proceed from a living predecessor. It prompted continuing national discussion about the imperial succession, including the shrinking size of the Imperial Family and the male-only succession rules.
