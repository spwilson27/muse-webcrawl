The Daijō-daijin (太政大臣), usually translated as Chancellor of the Realm, was the highest office in the Daijō-kan, the imperial Council of State of Japan under the ritsuryō system established in the late 7th and early 8th centuries. The office was modelled on Tang Chinese institutions.

Role

The Daijō-daijin stood at the apex of the court bureaucracy, presiding over the Council of State and acting as the emperor's chief adviser and the model of administration. Because the post demanded exceptional ability and prestige, it was often left vacant; the accompanying officials, the Sadaijin (Minister of the Left) and Udaijin (Minister of the Right), conducted much of the day-to-day government.

History

Prominent holders included Fujiwara clan regents who dominated Heian-period politics. During the medieval period the office became largely honorific, conferred on powerful figures such as Taira no Kiyomori (1167) and later on Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Briefly revived in the early Meiji period, the title was last held by Sanjō Sanetomi until the Daijō-kan was replaced by the modern cabinet system in 1885.
