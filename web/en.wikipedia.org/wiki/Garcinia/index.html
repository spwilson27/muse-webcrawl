Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Malpighiales
Family: Clusiaceae
Genus: Garcinia L.

Garcinia is a genus of flowering plants in the family Clusiaceae native to the tropics of Asia, Australia, tropical and southern Africa, and Polynesia. The genus includes several hundred species of evergreen trees and shrubs, most of them dioecious, with glossy opposite leaves and a milky or yellowish latex. The genus is named after the French botanist Laurent Garcin (1683–1752).

Notable species

The best-known species is the purple mangosteen (Garcinia mangostana), widely cultivated in Southeast Asia for its sweet, juicy fruit, often called the "queen of fruits". Other economically important species include Garcinia indica (kokum), whose sour rind is used in Indian cooking and traditional medicine; Garcinia gummi-gutta (Malabar tamarind), whose rind is used as a souring agent in fish curries and has been marketed in weight-loss supplements; and Garcinia kola (bitter kola) of West Africa. The yellow resin gamboge, used historically as a pigment and purgative, is obtained from several species including Garcinia hanburyi.

Description and ecology

Garcinia species are typically small to medium-sized rainforest understorey trees. The flowers are usually unisexual, and plants are frequently apomictic, producing seeds without fertilization, so that seedlings are clones of the mother tree. Fruits are berries, often with a thick rind rich in xanthones and other polyphenolic compounds that have attracted pharmacological interest.

Uses

Beyond fruit and flavouring, Garcinia species provide timber, resins, dyes, and traditional remedies. Hydroxycitric acid from G. gummi-gutta rind has been studied for effects on appetite and fat metabolism, though evidence for efficacy in weight loss is weak and contested.
