Hipólito Yrigoyen

Juan Hipólito del Sagrado Corazón de Jesús Yrigoyen (12 July 1852 – 3 July 1933) was an Argentine politician of the Radical Civic Union (UCR) who served twice as President of Argentina, from 1916 to 1922 and from 1928 until he was overthrown in 1930. He was the first president elected under the Sáenz Peña Law's secret, compulsory male suffrage.

Early career

Born in Buenos Aires to a family of Basque origin, Yrigoyen worked as a teacher, police commissioner, and provincial deputy, and became wealthy as a landowner. He co-founded the Civic Union and then the UCR with his uncle Leandro N. Alem, and led the Radicals' strategy of revolutionary uprisings and electoral abstention against the conservative oligarchy until the electoral reform of 1912 opened the way to power.

Presidencies

Yrigoyen's first administration maintained Argentine neutrality in the First World War, promoted university reform (sparked by the Córdoba reform of 1918), expanded public works, and created the state oil company YPF in 1922 under his successor Marcelo Torcuato de Alvear. His governments also faced labor conflict, including the repression of the Semana Trágica (1919) and the Patagonia strikes. Elected again in 1928 with a landslide, his second term was overwhelmed by the Great Depression, political polarization, and his own declining health; on 6 September 1930 General José Félix Uriburu overthrew him in Argentina's first modern military coup, beginning the "Infamous Decade".

Legacy

Known as "el Peludo" for his reclusive style, Yrigoyen remains a foundational figure of Argentine democracy and of the Radical party, symbolizing the entry of the middle classes into national politics.
