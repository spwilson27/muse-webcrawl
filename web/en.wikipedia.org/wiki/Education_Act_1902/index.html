Education Act 1902

The Education Act 1902, also known as the Balfour Education Act, was an Act of Parliament of the United Kingdom that made major changes in the administration of state education in England and Wales. It abolished the school boards created by the Elementary Education Act 1870 and transferred their functions to county and county borough councils as local education authorities.

Provisions

The Act brought voluntary (mainly church) schools into the publicly funded system, allowing them to receive funding from local rates, and empowered local authorities to provide secondary and technical education. It created a coordinated national system of primary and secondary schooling.

Controversy

Funding of Anglican and Catholic schools from the rates provoked fierce opposition from Nonconformists, led by figures such as John Clifford, some of whom refused to pay the education rate. The Act remained a foundation of English education until the Education Act 1944.
