Enkutatash

Enkutatash (Ge'ez: እንቁጣጣሽ) is the first day of the New Year in Ethiopia and Eritrea. It occurs on Meskerem 1 on the Ethiopian calendar, which is 11 September (or, during a leap year, 12 September) according to the Gregorian calendar.

Observed byEthiopians and Eritreans
TypeNational holiday
DateMeskerem 1 (11/12 September)
SignificanceFirst day of the Ethiopian calendar year

Observance

Large celebrations are held at home and in churches. Families gather, and young girls dressed in traditional clothes gather bouquets of daisies — the yellow adey abeba flowers that bloom at the end of the rainy season — and sing New Year songs, receiving small gifts of money or bread in return.

The Ethiopian tourism commission gives the origin of the celebration as the return of the Queen of Sheba from her visit to King Solomon in Jerusalem. Her chiefs welcomed her back by replenishing her treasury with jewels, known as enku; the name Enkutatash, "gift of jewels," commemorates this.

Calendar

The Ethiopian calendar is roughly seven to eight years behind the Gregorian calendar, because it calculates the Annunciation of Jesus differently. Ethiopia accordingly entered the year 2017 on 11 September 2024. The calendar has 13 months: 12 months of 30 days and a 13th month of 5 or 6 days.
