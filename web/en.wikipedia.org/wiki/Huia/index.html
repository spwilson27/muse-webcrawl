Huia

Scientific nameHeteralocha acutirostris
FamilyCallaeidae
StatusExtinct (last confirmed sighting 1907)
RangeNorth Island, New Zealand

The huia (Heteralocha acutirostris) was a species of wattlebird endemic to the North Island of New Zealand. It is one of the most famous extinct New Zealand birds, distinguished by the striking difference in bill shape between the sexes: the male had a short, sturdy bill used for chiselling into wood, while the female had a long, slender, decurved bill for probing crevices.

Extinction

The huia was hunted for its highly prized tail feathers, which were worn by Māori chiefs as a symbol of rank and later became fashionable among Europeans. Habitat destruction and introduced predators compounded the pressure. The last confirmed sighting was in 1907, and the species is generally considered extinct.

In culture

Huia feathers remain potent symbols in Māori culture, and the bird is commemorated in New Zealand place names, literature and conservation campaigns.
