Greyhound Lines, Inc., usually shortened to Greyhound, is an American intercity bus company serving more than 1,600 destinations in the United States, Canada and Mexico. It is the largest provider of intercity bus transportation in North America, and its running-dog logo is one of the most widely recognised in American travel.

History

The company traces its origins to 1914, when Swedish immigrant Carl Eric Wickman began carrying miners between Hibbing and Alice, Minnesota, in a Hupmobile for 15 cents a ride. The enterprise expanded through the 1920s through mergers of regional bus lines; the name Greyhound — inspired by the sleek grey buses of one acquired line — was adopted by the end of the decade, and the running greyhound logo followed in the 1930s. Charles Lindbergh is sometimes credited with suggesting the dog be made to appear in motion.

Greyhound grew with the federal highway system, popularised long-distance bus travel with slogans such as "Leave the driving to us" (1956), and built landmark Streamline Moderne terminals across the country. The company diversified into food services, car rental and even the Dial soap business, before slimming back to bus operations. After decades of declining ridership, bankruptcy in 1990 and ownership by the British transport group FirstGroup from 2007, Greyhound was sold in 2021 to FlixMobility, the German operator of FlixBus.

Operations

Greyhound operates a hub-and-spoke intercity network with major terminals in cities including New York, Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles and Atlanta, offering services such as e-ticketing, onboard Wi-Fi and reserved seating. Its BoltBus and other sub-brands were folded into the main network in the FlixBus era.

In popular culture

The Greyhound bus is a fixture of American song, film and literature, symbolising long journeys, fresh starts and the open road; it appears in works ranging from blues songs to Midnight Cowboy and in the name of the 2020 film Greyhound (about a warship named for speed, not the bus company).
