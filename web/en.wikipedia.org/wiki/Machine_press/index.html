Machine press

A machine press, also called a stamping press or power press, is a machine tool used to shape or cut material by deformation with a die. The workpiece, usually sheet metal, is placed between two halves of a tool and a ram drives one half against the other with great force, forming operations such as punching, blanking, bending, embossing and coining.

Types

Presses are classified by their drive mechanism. Mechanical presses use a motor and flywheel, storing energy that is delivered through a crank or eccentric mechanism. Hydraulic presses use fluid pressure acting on a cylinder, giving full force throughout the stroke. Servo presses use programmable servo motors to control the ram profile precisely. Presses are also described by frame style, such as gap-frame (C-frame) and straight-side designs, and by tonnage.

Operation

In a typical press line, coil stock or blanks are fed between the punch and die; the ram descends, the tool cuts or forms the part, and the finished piece is ejected. Progressive dies perform several operations in sequence as the strip advances through the press. Because of the forces involved, presses are fitted with guards, light curtains and two-hand controls for operator safety.

Applications

Machine presses are central to mass production in the automotive, appliance, electronics and coin-minting industries.
