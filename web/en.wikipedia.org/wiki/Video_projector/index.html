Video projector

A video projector is an image projector that receives a video signal and projects the corresponding image onto a projection screen using a lens system. Video projectors use a very bright ultra-high-performance lamp, a xenon arc lamp, metal halide lamp, LED, or solid-state blue, RB, RGB or remote fibre-optic RGB lasers to provide the illumination required to project the image.

A modern digital video projector

Overview

All video projectors use a very bright light source to project the image, and most modern ones can correct any curves, blurriness and other inconsistencies through manual settings. Video projectors are widely used for applications such as conference room presentations, classroom training, home cinema, movie theatres and concerts. In schools and other educational settings, they are sometimes connected to an interactive whiteboard.

Technologies

Several technologies are used to form the image: LCD projectors pass light through liquid-crystal panels; DLP (digital light processing) projectors reflect light off a chip covered with microscopic mirrors (a digital micromirror device); LCoS (liquid crystal on silicon) combines aspects of both; and laser projectors use lasers as the light source, offering longer life and consistent brightness. Early video projection relied on CRT projectors, which used three cathode-ray tubes and were heavy and difficult to align.

Resolution and brightness

Projectors are characterised by their native resolution (commonly SVGA, XGA, 720p, 1080p and 4K UHD) and their brightness output, measured in lumens. Throw ratio describes the relationship between projection distance and image width; short-throw and ultra-short-throw models can project large images from very close to the screen.
