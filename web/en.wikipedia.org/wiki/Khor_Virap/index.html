Khor Virap

Khor Virap (Armenian: Խոր Վիրապ, "deep dungeon") is an Armenian monastery in the Ararat plain of Armenia, near the closed border with Turkey and in view of Mount Ararat. It is one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in Armenia, famous as the place where Gregory the Illuminator was imprisoned for about thirteen years by the pagan king Tiridates III before converting the king and, with him, Armenia — the first nation to adopt Christianity as a state religion, in 301 AD.

History

The site, near the ancient capital Artashat, was a royal prison. Gregory, a Christian missionary, was cast into a deep pit here around 288 AD and miraculously survived; when Tiridates was stricken with madness, Gregory was released, cured the king, and baptised him and his court. A chapel was built over the pit in the 7th century by Nerses III, and the present Church of the Holy Mother of God (Surp Astvatsatsin) dates from 1662. In the Middle Ages the monastery housed a seminary and scriptorium.

Visitors can still descend by ladder into the pit where Gregory was held. On feast days white doves are released at the monastery, and the view of Ararat across the Araxes valley makes Khor Virap one of the iconic images of Armenia.
