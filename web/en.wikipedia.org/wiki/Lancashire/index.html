Lancashire is a ceremonial county in North West England. It borders Cumbria to the north, North Yorkshire and West Yorkshire to the east, Greater Manchester and Merseyside to the south, and the Irish Sea to the west. The county town is Preston, the seat of Lancashire County Council, while the historic county town is Lancaster, which gives the county its name. Its emblem is the Red Rose of Lancaster, famous from the Wars of the Roses against the white rose of York.

History

The county palatine of Lancaster emerged in the 12th century from lands between the Ribble and the Mersey and north of them. Lancashire remained largely rural until the Industrial Revolution transformed it into the world centre of the cotton textile industry: the damp climate, fast streams and later coal measures powered mills in Manchester, Bolton, Blackburn, Burnley, Preston and dozens of other towns, and Liverpool grew into a great port shipping Lancashire cloth worldwide. The world's first inter-city railway, the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830), opened here. The Lancashire cotton famine of 1861–65, caused by the American Civil War cutting off raw cotton supplies, brought mass hardship. In 1974 local government reorganisation transferred Manchester, Liverpool and their districts to the new counties of Greater Manchester and Merseyside, and the Furness peninsula to Cumbria, leaving the modern ceremonial county much smaller than the historic one.

Geography

The west of the county is a low coastal plain (the Fylde and West Lancashire plain) facing Morecambe Bay and the Irish Sea, with the resort of Blackpool. Inland rise the moorlands of the Forest of Bowland and the West Pennine Moors and Rossendale hills. Principal rivers are the Ribble, Lune, Wyre, Calder and Darwen. Major settlements include Preston, Blackburn, Blackpool, Burnley, Lancaster, Chorley, Accrington and Nelson. The M6 motorway runs the length of the county.

Culture

Lancashire is associated with hotpot, black pudding, Eccles cakes and Lancashire cheese; with brass bands, clog dancing and the dialect celebrated by performers such as George Formby and Gracie Fields; and with Blackpool's illuminations and tower. County cricket is played by Lancashire CCC at Old Trafford (now in Greater Manchester), and the Roses rivalry with Yorkshire remains fierce in sport and popular identity.
