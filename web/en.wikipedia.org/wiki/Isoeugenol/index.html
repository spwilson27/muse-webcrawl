Isoeugenol

Isoeugenol

IUPAC name: 2-methoxy-4-(prop-1-en-1-yl)phenol

Formula: C10H12O2

Molar mass: 164.20 g/mol

Appearance: pale yellow oily liquid

CAS number: 97-54-1

Isoeugenol is a phenylpropanoid, an aromatic organic compound with the molecular formula C10H12O2. It is an isomer of eugenol, differing in the position of the double bond in the propenyl side chain: in eugenol the double bond is terminal (an allyl group), while in isoeugenol it is conjugated with the benzene ring, making isoeugenol a propenylbenzene derivative. It occurs as two geometric isomers, cis and trans, of which the trans form predominates in most preparations.

Occurrence and production

Isoeugenol occurs naturally in the essential oils of plants such as ylang-ylang and nutmeg and is a minor component of clove oil. Industrially it is produced by isomerising eugenol (obtained from clove oil) with base, typically potassium hydroxide at elevated temperature. Like eugenol, it has a spicy, clove-like, somewhat floral odour.

Uses

Isoeugenol is used in perfumery and flavouring, and historically was an important intermediate in the manufacture of vanillin by oxidation. It is one of the fragrance allergens that must be declared on cosmetic labels in the European Union, as it is a recognised contact sensitiser; its use in fragrance products is restricted by IFRA standards and EU cosmetics regulation. It also shows antimicrobial and antioxidant activity in laboratory studies and is the biosynthetic precursor of some lignans and other phenylpropanoids in plants.
