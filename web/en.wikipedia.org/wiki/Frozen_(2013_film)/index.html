Frozen (2013 film)

Frozen is a 2013 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Animation Studios. Loosely inspired by Hans Christian Andersen's fairy tale "The Snow Queen", it tells the story of Princess Anna, who journeys with ice salesman Kristoff, his reindeer Sven, and the snowman Olaf to find her estranged sister Elsa, whose ice powers have trapped their kingdom of Arendelle in eternal winter. It became the highest-grossing animated film of its time and won the Academy Awards for Best Animated Feature and Best Original Song ("Let It Go").

Plot

Princess Elsa of Arendelle possesses magical ice powers she struggles to control; after accidentally injuring her younger sister Anna, she is isolated for years. At Elsa's coronation, an argument over Anna's hasty engagement to Prince Hans causes Elsa to reveal her powers, and she flees, unwittingly plunging Arendelle into winter. Anna pursues her to the North Mountain, where Elsa builds an ice palace and sings "Let It Go". When Anna confronts her, Elsa accidentally freezes Anna's heart; only "an act of true love" can save her. As Hans reveals his villainy and moves to kill Elsa, the freezing Anna throws herself between them — her self-sacrifice is itself the act of true love, thawing her heart. Elsa learns that love controls her powers, restores summer, and reconciles with Anna.

Production and music

Disney had attempted a Snow Queen adaptation since the 1930s; the project was revived in 2011 under directors Chris Buck and Jennifer Lee, the first woman to direct a Disney animated feature. Songs by Kristen Anderson-Lopez and Robert Lopez — including "Let It Go", "Do You Want to Build a Snowman?", and "For the First Time in Forever" — drive the story; the transformation of Elsa from villain to sympathetic heroine was prompted by "Let It Go". The setting draws on Norwegian landscapes, architecture, and Sami culture.

Reception and legacy

Frozen grossed over $1.28 billion worldwide, then an all-time record for an animated film, and was praised for its animation, songs, and portrayal of sisterly love. It spawned an enormous merchandising phenomenon, a Broadway musical (2018), the shorts Frozen Fever (2015) and Olaf's Frozen Adventure (2017), and the sequel Frozen II (2019), which grossed even more.
