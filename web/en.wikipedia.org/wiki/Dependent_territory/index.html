Dependent territory

Dependent territory
DefinitionTerritory not possessing full political sovereignty, administered by a state
ExamplesGibraltar, Puerto Rico, French Polynesia, Greenland, Guam
SynonymsDependency, overseas territory, external territory
[Image: World map highlighting dependent territories and their administering states]

A dependent territory, dependent area, or dependency is a territory that does not possess full political independence or sovereignty as a sovereign state, yet remains politically outside the administering state's integral area. A dependency is commonly distinguished from a subnational entity in that it is not fully integrated into the administering state, even though it may be a first-level subdivision for some purposes.

Characteristics

Dependent territories typically have their own local government and varying degrees of self-rule, while the administering state retains responsibility for defence and foreign affairs. Their inhabitants may or may not hold full citizenship of the administering state, and the territories may not be represented in its national legislature on the same basis as integral regions.

Examples

The United Kingdom administers 14 British Overseas Territories, including Gibraltar, Bermuda and the Falkland Islands, and there are three Crown Dependencies (Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man). The United States administers unincorporated territories such as Puerto Rico, Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands. France, Denmark (Greenland and the Faroe Islands), the Netherlands, Australia, New Zealand and Norway also administer dependent or external territories. Many present-day dependencies are remnants of colonial empires; the United Nations maintains a list of non-self-governing territories whose peoples have yet to attain a full measure of self-government.
