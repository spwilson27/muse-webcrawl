The Marikana massacre was the killing of 34 striking miners by the South African Police Service on 16 August 2012 at the Lonmin platinum mine near Marikana in North West province — the deadliest use of force by South African security forces against civilians since the end of apartheid.

Background

Rock drill operators, demanding a wage increase to R12,500 per month, had struck from 10 August, bypassing the National Union of Mineworkers in favour of the rival AMCU. Clashes in the preceding days killed ten people, including two police officers and two security guards.

The shooting

On 16 August police attempted to disperse and disarm thousands of strikers gathered on a koppie (hill). Police opened fire with automatic weapons, killing 17 men at the main scene and 17 more at a second site some 500 metres away, where many were shot while fleeing or surrendering; 78 more were wounded.

Aftermath

The Farlam Commission of Inquiry criticised the police operation and Lonmin's conduct. The massacre badly damaged the ANC government's standing, fuelled debate about inequality and police militarisation, and remains commemorated annually by miners and unions.
