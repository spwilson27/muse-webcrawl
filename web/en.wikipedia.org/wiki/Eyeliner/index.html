Eyeliner

Eyeliner is a cosmetic used to define the eyes. It is applied around the contours of the eye(s) to create a variety of aesthetic effects, most commonly a line along the upper and/or lower lash line that makes the eyes appear larger and the lashes thicker. Eyeliner is a staple of modern makeup and is worn by people of all genders, including in stage, film, and fashion contexts.

History

Eye lining is one of the oldest cosmetic practices. In ancient Egypt, both men and women lined their eyes with kohl — a preparation of ground galena (lead sulfide) and other minerals — for beauty, sun protection, and believed protective and medicinal purposes; depictions date back to around 3100 BC and earlier. Kohl use spread across the Middle East, North Africa, and South Asia, where it is also known as surma or kajal. In the West, eyeliner fell out of common fashion for centuries before being revived in the early twentieth century, influenced by the discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb (1922) and by cinema; it became prominent in the 1960s with bold graphic styles, in punk and goth subcultures in the late 1970s and 1980s ("guyliner" worn by male musicians), and again through social-media-driven trends in the 2010s and 2020s.

Types

  • Pencil eyeliner — a wood or twist-up pencil, easy to apply and smudge for softer looks.
  • Gel eyeliner — a waxy gel applied with a brush; long-wearing and precise.
  • Liquid eyeliner — applied with a fine brush or felt tip for sharp lines, including the "winged" or "cat-eye" flick.
  • Kohl and kajal — soft, dark preparations applied especially to the waterline.
  • Powder or eyeshadow liner — applied wet or dry with an angled brush for a diffused line.
  • Cake eyeliner — a water-activated solid used historically and in theatrical makeup.

Application and styles

Eyeliner may be applied to the upper lash line, lower lash line, both, or tightly between the lashes ("tightlining"). The waterline — the inner rim of the eyelid — can also be lined. Styles range from a subtle, natural line to extended wings, double wings, graphic floating lines, and smoky, smudged looks. Colors are traditionally black or brown, but eyeliners are manufactured in a wide range of shades, including white (used to make eyes appear larger) and metallics.

Health and safety

Because eyeliner is used close to the eye, regulators treat it as a cosmetic requiring safe ingredients; traditional kohl made with lead compounds has been flagged by health authorities as a source of lead exposure, particularly for children. Sharing eyeliner and using expired eye cosmetics increase the risk of infection.
