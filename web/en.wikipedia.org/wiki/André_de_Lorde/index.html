André de Lorde (11 July 1869 – 6 September 1942) was a French playwright, remembered as the leading writer for the Théâtre du Grand-Guignol in Paris and nicknamed the "Prince of Terror".

Career

De Lorde worked by day as a librarian, but from the late 1890s supplied the Grand Guignol with a stream of short horror plays — he wrote over a hundred of them — featuring madness, mutilation, medical horror and revenge. His famous pieces include The Laboratory of Hallucinations, The System of Doctor Tarr and Professor Fether (adapted from Edgar Allan Poe) and The Old Women.

He frequently collaborated with the psychologist Alfred Binet, director of the psychology laboratory at the Sorbonne, whose case studies of insanity and suggestion gave the plays their clinical detail. De Lorde also wrote novels and plays for other theatres, but it is the Grand Guignol work that defined him.

Legacy

The Grand Guignol's blend of naturalism and gore, much of it written by de Lorde, is a recognised ancestor of modern horror theatre and cinema, including the splatter film and the psychological thriller.
