Doan's Crossing, a historic ford of the Red River used by cattle drives.

Doan's Crossing was a ford of the Red River on the border between Texas and present-day Oklahoma, and one of the best-known crossing points on the Great Western Cattle Trail (the Western Trail). In 1878 the trader Jonathan Doan established a store and trading post near the crossing, on the Texas side in Wilbarger County, supplying the cowboys who drove herds of longhorn cattle north from Texas to the railheads and ranges of Kansas and beyond.

History

During the peak years of the cattle drives in the late 1870s and 1880s, hundreds of thousands of cattle passed over the trail, and Doan's store became a landmark settlement — at one time the northernmost outpost of civilisation on the route, with a hotel, saloon and corral. The settlement, known simply as Doans, declined after the trail era ended and railroads reached the region; the crossing was superseded by bridges. The community of Doans survives as a small unincorporated place, and Texas pioneers held annual picnics at the site from the 1880s onward, commemorated by a monument erected in 1931.
