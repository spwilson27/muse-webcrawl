Female gaze

The female gaze is a term popularized by feminist film theorist Laura Mulvey in her 1975 essay "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema". It represents the perspective of a woman viewer or filmmaker, in contrast to the male gaze, which Mulvey argued dominates mainstream cinema by presenting women as passive objects of male desire.

Concept

Whereas the male gaze depicts the world from a masculine, heterosexual perspective, the female gaze aims to represent characters — especially women — as subjects with interiority and agency, rather than objects to be looked at. The concept has since been applied beyond film to literature, photography, advertising, and video games, and is debated among scholars: some use it simply to mean works created by women, while others insist it describes a particular way of framing empathy, emotion, and point of view.

Criticism and development

Writers such as bell hooks, with her notion of the "oppositional gaze", extended and critiqued gaze theory by centering race and spectatorship. Contemporary filmmakers frequently cited in discussions of the female gaze include Céline Sciamma, Greta Gerwig, and Sofia Coppola.
