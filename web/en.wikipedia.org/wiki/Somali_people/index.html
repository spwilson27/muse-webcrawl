Somali people

The Somali people (Somali: Soomaalida) are a Cushitic ethnic group native to the Horn of Africa, who share a common ancestry, culture, and history. The lowland Somali-speaking region, Greater Somalia, spans present-day Somalia, Djibouti, the Somali Region of Ethiopia, and northeastern Kenya. Ethnic Somalis number an estimated 28–30 million worldwide, including a large global diaspora formed since the outbreak of the Somali civil war in 1991.

Language and culture

Somalis speak Somali, a Lowland East Cushitic language of the Afroasiatic family, written in an official Latin-based script since 1972. Somali society is traditionally organised around patrilineal clan families — including the Darod, Dir, Hawiye, Isaaq, and Rahanweyn — which remain central to social and political life. Poetry holds an honoured place in Somali culture, which has been called a "nation of poets", and oral tradition long carried law (xeer), history, and genealogy.

Religion and history

Virtually all Somalis are Sunni Muslims, Islam having reached the coast by the early medieval period through trade across the Red Sea and Indian Ocean. Historic Somali states and sultanates — among them Ifat, Adal, Ajuran, and the Geledi Sultanate — participated in regional and maritime commerce. Camel pastoralism, supplemented by agro-pastoralism in the riverine south, underlies the traditional economy.

Diaspora

Substantial Somali communities now live in Europe (especially the United Kingdom and Scandinavia), North America (notably Minnesota and Toronto), and the Middle East, maintaining strong remittance ties to the Horn.
