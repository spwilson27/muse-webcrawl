Cagsawa Ruins

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cagsawa Ruins (also spelled Kagsawa or Cagsaua) are the remnants of a 16th-century Franciscan church in Barangay Busay, Daraga, Albay, in the Philippines. The site is famous for its surviving belfry, which stands before the perfect cone of Mayon Volcano and is one of the most photographed landmarks in the country.

History

The church was originally built in 1587 by Franciscan friars and was destroyed by Dutch pirates in 1636. Rebuilt in 1724, it was destroyed again on 1 February 1814, when the most violent eruption of Mayon in recorded history buried the town of Cagsawa in volcanic debris. Hundreds of residents who had sought refuge inside the church were killed. The façade survived until it was further damaged by an earthquake and erosion, leaving the belfry as the principal standing structure.

Today

The ruins are protected as the Cagsawa Ruins Park, maintained by the municipal government of Daraga and declared a national cultural treasure. The site hosts souvenir shops, the Cagsawa National Museum (a branch of the National Museum of the Philippines), and an annual festival. The image of the belfry framed by Mayon has become a symbol of both the region's beauty and the volcano's destructive power.
