Cambrian Mountains

Cambrian Mountains
Location: Wales, United Kingdom
Highest point: Plynlimon, 752 m
Nickname: the "Green Desert of Wales"

The Cambrian Mountains (Welsh: Mynyddoedd Cambria) are a series of uplands in mid Wales, forming the sparsely populated watershed spine of the country between Snowdonia in the north and the Brecon Beacons in the south. Plynlimon, their highest summit at 752 metres, is the source of both the River Severn and the River Wye, the two longest rivers in Britain.

Geography and land use

The range consists largely of rolling moorland, blanket bog, and conifer plantations, with sheep farming dominant. Historically the area was mined for lead, silver, and zinc. Its remoteness earned it the nickname "the Green Desert of Wales." The Elan Valley reservoirs in the east supply water to Birmingham.

Designation debates

The Cambrian Mountains lack the national park status of Snowdonia and the Brecon Beacons; a national park proposal in the 1970s was abandoned. Campaigners have long sought stronger landscape protection, and part of the range lies within the Cambrian Mountains Initiative area and a UNESCO-recognised biosphere region around the Dyfi valley.
