House of Fürstenberg (Swabia)

The House of Fürstenberg of Swabia is a German princely family originating in the Baar region of southwestern Germany, named after Fürstenberg Castle above the Danube near Donaueschingen. The Swabian line descends from the counts of Urach and became one of the most prominent noble houses of the Holy Roman Empire.

History

The family first appears in the 11th century with the counts of Urach. Around 1218, Count Egino V of Urach inherited the possessions of the dukes of Zähringen in the Baar, and his descendants adopted the name Fürstenberg after the castle of that name. Through inheritance and partition, the counts of Fürstenberg acquired extensive territories in the Black Forest, along the upper Danube, and later in Austria and Bohemia.

In 1664 the counts were raised to princely rank, and the Fürstenberg lands briefly formed a principality within the Empire. After the mediatisation of 1806, the principality was divided between Baden, Württemberg, and Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, though the family retained its vast estates, including the princely brewery in Donaueschingen.

Branches

The house divided into several lines, including Fürstenberg-Messkirch, Fürstenberg-Stühlingen, and Fürstenberg-Blumberg. The surviving princely line is based at Donaueschingen, while a Bohemian branch held lands around Kruschowitz (Křušovice).
