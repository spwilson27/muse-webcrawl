Bassenthwaite Lake

Bassenthwaite Lake is a lake in the Lake District in the county of Cumbria, England. It is the fourth largest of the lakes in the Lake District by area, at roughly 5.3 km², and lies at the foot of Skiddaw, north of the town of Keswick. The lake is fed by, and drains into, the River Derwent. It is the only body of water in the Lake District to use the word "lake" in its name — the others are "meres", "waters" or "tarns" — although the similarly named waters are generally larger.

Wildlife

The lake and its surroundings are designated a National Nature Reserve and a Site of Special Scientific Interest. Bassenthwaite became famous in 2001 when ospreys nested beside the lake — the first ospreys known to have bred in the Lake District for around 150 years — and a public viewpoint at Dodd Wood has operated since. The lake supports a population of vendace, one of Britain's rarest freshwater fish, as well as pike, perch and roach. Otters and red squirrels are found in the surrounding woods.

Geography and recreation

The A66 road runs along the western shore, while the eastern shore is quieter and largely wooded. The village of Bassenthwaite lies at the northern end. Mirehouse, a historic house on the eastern shore, is open to the public. The Lakes Distillery is located near the northern end of the lake. Sailing and kayaking take place on the lake, which hosts the Bassenthwaite Sailing Club, notable for its annual regatta week.
