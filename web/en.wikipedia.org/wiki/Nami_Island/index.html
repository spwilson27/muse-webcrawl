Nami Island

Nami Island (Korean: 남이섬, Namiseom) is a half-moon-shaped river island located in Chuncheon, Gangwon Province, South Korea, formed as the land around it was inundated by the rising water of the Bukhan River when the Cheongpyeong Dam was built in 1944. The island is named after General Nami, a notable figure in Korean history who died at the age of 28 after being falsely accused of treason during the reign of King Sejo; his (unconfirmed) tomb is said to lie on the island.

Tourism

Nami Island is one of South Korea's most popular tourist destinations, famous for its tree-lined roads — including lanes of metasequoia, ginkgo, pine and Korean white pine — and for its emphasis on nature and culture rather than vehicles (cars are restricted on much of the island). It draws millions of visitors a year, many of them international tourists.

The island's fame across Asia surged after it was used as a filming location for the 2002 KBS television drama Winter Sonata, a landmark of the Korean Wave (Hallyu); statues of the drama's protagonists stand on the island.

Naminara Republic

In 2006 the island's operators declared it the Naminara Republic, a tongue-in-cheek "micronation" with its own passport-style entry tickets, stamps, currency and flag, marketing itself as a cultural and environmental tourism brand. Admission is framed as an immigration procedure, and visitors' "visas" support the island's cultural programs.
