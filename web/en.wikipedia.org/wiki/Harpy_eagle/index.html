Harpy eagle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The harpy eagle (Harpia harpyja) is a large, powerful Neotropical species of eagle, and among the largest and most powerful raptors in the world. It inhabits tropical lowland rainforests from southern Mexico through Central and South America, especially the Amazon basin.

Description

Females can weigh up to 9–10 kg with a wingspan exceeding 2 metres; males are considerably smaller. The bird is grey and white with a black breast band, a striking double crest, and massive tarsi and talons — the rear talon can be as long as a grizzly bear's claw. Its short, broad wings allow maneuverable flight through the forest canopy.

Ecology and conservation

Harpy eagles hunt mainly arboreal mammals, particularly sloths and monkeys, snatching them from the canopy. Pairs nest in emergent trees such as the kapok and raise a single eaglet every two to three years, making populations slow to recover from losses. Habitat destruction and shooting have greatly reduced the species, especially in Central America; it is classified as Vulnerable by the IUCN and is the national bird of Panama.
