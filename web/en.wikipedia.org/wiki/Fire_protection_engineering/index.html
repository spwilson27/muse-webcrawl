Fire protection engineering

Fire protection engineering Also called: fire safety engineering
Scope: fire dynamics, detection, suppression, egress, smoke control, structural fire resistance
Key bodies: SFPE, NFPA
Professional credential: PE in fire protection (United States)

Fire protection engineering is the application of science and engineering principles to protect people, property, and the environment from the harmful and destructive effects of fire and smoke. It includes the analysis of fire hazards and the design of systems for detection, alarm, suppression, and containment, as well as safe egress and the fire behaviour of structures and materials.

Scope

Fire protection engineers analyse how fires start and spread, how smoke moves through buildings, and how occupants respond. They design automatic sprinkler systems, fire alarms, smoke-control systems, and passive protection such as fire-rated walls and doors. Work spans buildings, industrial facilities, transport, tunnels, and offshore installations.

Approaches

Historically, design followed prescriptive building codes specifying exactly what to build. Modern practice increasingly uses performance-based design, in which engineers demonstrate by modelling and calculation—such as computational fluid dynamics fire models—that a proposed solution meets defined safety objectives, allowing more flexible architecture.

Profession

The discipline developed during the twentieth century in response to major urban fires and is represented internationally by the Society of Fire Protection Engineers (SFPE), founded in 1950. In the United States, professional licensure as a Professional Engineer in fire protection has been available since the 1980s, and university programmes offer dedicated degrees.
