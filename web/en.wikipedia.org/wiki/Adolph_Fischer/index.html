Adolph Fischer

Adolph Fischer (1858 – November 11, 1887) was a German-American anarchist and labor activist convicted and executed in connection with the 1886 Haymarket affair in Chicago. A typographer by trade, Fischer edited the German-language anarchist newspaper Arbeiter-Zeitung and was one of eight defendants tried for conspiracy in the bombing that killed police officers at a labor rally in Haymarket Square. He maintained his innocence of the bombing itself and refused to ask for clemency.

Life and trial

Fischer emigrated to the United States as a teenager and became active in the typographers' union and the German-language socialist movement before embracing anarchism. He edited the Arbeiter-Zeitung and helped produce the "Revenge Circular" distributed after police killed striking workers at the McCormick Reaper Works on May 3, 1886. After the bomb was thrown at Haymarket the following evening — Fischer was not present at the square at the time — he was arrested at the newspaper office. The prosecution's case rested on his writings and speeches rather than on any direct link to the unknown bomb-thrower. Convicted of conspiracy to murder, he was hanged on November 11, 1887, together with August Spies, Albert Parsons and George Engel. In 1893 Governor John Peter Altgeld pardoned the three surviving defendants and condemned the trial as unjust; Fischer and his co-defendants were later widely regarded as martyrs of the labour movement.
