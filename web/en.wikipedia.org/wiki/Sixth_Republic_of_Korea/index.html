Sixth Republic of Korea

The Sixth Republic of South Korea is the current government of the Republic of Korea, established by the Constitution of 1987. It followed the Fifth Republic after the June Democratic Uprising of 1987 forced the authoritarian government to accept direct presidential elections and sweeping democratic reforms.

History

Mass protests in June 1987, triggered by the death of a student activist under police torture and by the regime's refusal to allow direct elections, led Roh Tae-woo, the ruling party's presidential candidate, to issue the June 29 Declaration conceding the opposition's demands. A new constitution, approved by referendum in October 1987, restored direct presidential elections, strengthened the National Assembly and Constitutional Court, and guaranteed civil liberties.

Political development

Roh won the first election of the new era in December 1987, aided by a split opposition. His successor Kim Young-sam (1993–1998) pursued reform of the military's political role and anti-corruption measures. The peaceful transfer of power to opposition candidate Kim Dae-jung in 1998 marked the consolidation of democracy; Kim's presidency oversaw recovery from the Asian financial crisis and the historic 2000 inter-Korean summit. Subsequent presidents — Roh Moo-hyun, Lee Myung-bak, Park Geun-hye (impeached and removed in 2017), Moon Jae-in and Yoon Suk-yeol — continued the pattern of competitive elections and institutional contestation characteristic of the Sixth Republic.

Government

South Korea is a presidential republic: the president is directly elected for a single five-year term, the unicameral National Assembly is elected for four years, and an independent Constitutional Court reviews legislation and impeachment.
