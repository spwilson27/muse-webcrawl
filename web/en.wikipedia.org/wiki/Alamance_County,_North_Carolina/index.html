Alamance County, North Carolina

Alamance County is a county in the Piedmont region of North Carolina, United States. Its county seat is Graham, and its largest city is Burlington. The county was formed in 1849 from Orange County and named for Great Alamance Creek, near which the Battle of Alamance — the culminating clash of the War of the Regulation — was fought in 1771, an event sometimes described as a precursor skirmish of the American Revolution. The 2020 census recorded a population of about 171,000.

Burlington, the largest city in Alamance County.

History and economy

Alamance County became a centre of the North Carolina textile industry in the nineteenth century: Edwin M. Holt's Alamance Plaids mill, established on the creek in 1837, pioneered coloured cotton cloth in the South, and the rail junction that grew into Burlington took its name from the railroad shops ("company shops") built there in the 1850s. Burlington Industries, founded in 1923, grew into one of the world's largest textile firms. In May 1771 Governor William Tryon's militia defeated the Regulator movement at Alamance; the battlefield is preserved as a state historic site. The county is also home to Elon University and the Alamance Battleground. See also Burlington, Battle of Alamance and Graham.
