Chelation therapy

FieldMedicine, toxicology
Approved usesHeavy metal poisoning (lead, iron overload, etc.)

Chelation therapy is a medical treatment using chelating agents - molecules that bind metal ions - to remove toxic metals from the body.

Established uses

It is standard care for lead poisoning, acute iron poisoning and iron overload, Wilson's disease (penicillamine), and some other metal toxicities, where agents such as calcium-disodium EDTA, DMSA or deferoxamine increase urinary excretion.

Alternative claims

From the 1950s some practitioners promoted EDTA chelation for atherosclerosis and autism. Evidence has not established benefit for heart disease; a large NIH-funded trial (TACT) reported modest, debated results in some diabetic patients after heart attack. Regulators warn against over-the-counter chelation products.

Risks

Improper use can cause kidney damage, dangerous drops in calcium and other minerals, and death.
