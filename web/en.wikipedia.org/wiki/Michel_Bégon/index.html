Michel Bégon

Born: Michel Bégon de la Picardière, 25 December 1638, Blois, France

Died: 14 March 1710 (aged 71), Rochefort, France

Occupation: Naval administrator, colonial intendant

Known for: The begonia (named in his honor)

Michel Bégon de la Picardière (25 December 1638 – 14 March 1710) was a French colonial and naval administrator. He served as the first intendant of the French Antilles and as intendant of the naval port of Rochefort, and is remembered today chiefly because the flowering plant genus Begonia was named after him.

Career

Born into a family of officeholders at Blois, Bégon entered royal administrative service and was appointed in 1682 as intendant of justice, police, and finance for the French colonies in the Caribbean, based at Martinique and then Saint-Domingue (present-day Haiti), where he worked until 1685. In that role he encouraged agriculture and settlement and clashed with planters over his attempts to regulate the colonies and protect their revenues.

Returning to France, Bégon became intendant of the navy at Rochefort in 1688, a post he held until his death. At Rochefort he oversaw the arsenal — one of France's principal naval bases — administering shipbuilding, supplies, and personnel, and he was an important patron and organizer in the port city. His son, Michel Bégon de la Picardière (1667–1747), later served as intendant of New France (Canada) from 1725 to 1748.

Botanical legacy

Bégon was an enthusiastic amateur naturalist and patron of botany. At Rochefort he received plants and specimens collected in the Americas, and he corresponded with and supported the botanist Charles Plumier, a Franciscan friar who undertook expeditions to the West Indies. Plumier named the genus Begonia — a large and now very popular genus of flowering plants with over 2,000 species — in honor of his patron. The name was later adopted by Linnaeus. Bégon died at Rochefort on 14 March 1710.
