Lydney is a town in Gloucestershire, England, on the west bank of the River Severn near the edge of the Forest of Dean. It lies on the small River Lyd, from which it takes its name, and has a long history as a port and industrial settlement.

History

At Lydney Park there are remains of a Romano-British temple complex dedicated to the god Nodens, excavated in the twentieth century with finds including curse tablets and a mosaic. The town later developed around coal, iron, and tinplate industries served by its harbour and by canal and tramroad links to the Forest of Dean coalfield. The novelist and archaeologist Tessa Wheeler and her husband Mortimer Wheeler excavated at Lydney in the 1920s.

Transport and amenities

Lydney railway station, on the Gloucester–Newport line, also serves as the southern terminus of the Dean Forest Railway heritage line. The harbour, once busy with coal exports, is now used mainly for leisure boating, and the town has a marina on the Severn.

Notable connections

J. R. R. Tolkien visited the Lydney excavations, and the site's ring and curse tablets are sometimes discussed among the background influences on his fiction, a connection Tolkien himself treated cautiously.
