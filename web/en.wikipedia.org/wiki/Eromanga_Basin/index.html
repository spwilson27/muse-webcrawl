Eromanga Basin

Extent of the Eromanga Basin (image placeholder)

The Eromanga Basin is a large Mesozoic sedimentary basin in north-eastern Australia, covering parts of Queensland, New South Wales, South Australia and the Northern Territory. It forms the largest of the subdivisions of the Great Artesian Basin, one of the largest artesian groundwater basins in the world, and covers roughly 1,000,000 square kilometres.

Geology

The basin accumulated thick sequences of Jurassic and Cretaceous fluvial and lacustrine sediments, followed by a major marine incursion during the Early Cretaceous (Aptian–Albian), when much of inland Australia was covered by an epicontinental sea. Sediments include the Cadna-owie Formation, the Bulldog Shale, the Toolebuc Formation and the Winton Formation, the latter famous for its dinosaur fossils, including those found near Winton in Queensland.

Resources

The Eromanga Basin hosts significant petroleum resources: major oil and gas fields around Jackson and Moomba (in the underlying Cooper Basin region), with oil accumulations in Jurassic and Cretaceous reservoirs such as the Hutton Sandstone and Murta Formation. It also supplies coal seam gas and artesian water essential to pastoral industries. The basin is known for opal fields such as Coober Pedy in its southwest, which lie in weathered Cretaceous Bulldog Shale.
