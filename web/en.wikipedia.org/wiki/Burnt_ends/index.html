Burnt ends

Burnt ends are flavorful pieces of meat cut from the "point" half of a smoked beef brisket. A signature of Kansas City-style barbecue, they are produced by returning the cubed point to the smoker after the brisket's initial cook, rendering more fat and creating deeply caramelized, crispy-edged bark with a tender interior.

Preparation

A whole packer brisket consists of two muscles: the lean "flat" and the fatty "point." After smoking, the point is separated, cubed, seasoned again (often with rub and sometimes sauce), and returned to the pit for several more hours. The extended cooking renders the point's abundant fat and concentrates flavor in the crunchy exterior.

History

For decades, Kansas City pitmasters treated the crisp brisket trimmings as scraps, handing them out free to waiting customers. Arthur Bryant's restaurant is credited with popularizing them; a 1972 article by Calvin Trillin in Playboy praised the free burnt edges at Bryant's and helped transform them into a sought-after menu item sold by the pound.

Variations

"Poor man's burnt ends" use chuck roast in place of brisket, and pork belly burnt ends — cubed, rubbed, smoked, and glazed — have become a widespread modern variant, along with versions made from hot dogs and other meats in home-smoking culture.
