The Odeon of Herodes Atticus (Greek: ᾨδεῖον Ἡρῴδου τοῦ Ἀττικοῦ; also called Herodeion or Herodion) is a stone Roman theatre structure located on the southwest slope of the Acropolis of Athens, Greece. The building was completed in AD 169 and renovated in 1950.

It was built by Herodes Atticus, a wealthy Greek aristocrat and Roman senator, in memory of his wife Aspasia Annia Regilla. The steep-sloped theatre, with a three-story stone front wall and a wooden roof made of cedar of Lebanon timber, seated about 5,000 people. It was destroyed and left in ruins by the Heruli in AD 267.

Modern use

The audience seats and stage were restored with Pentelic marble in the 1950s. Since then it has been the main venue of the Athens Festival, held from May to October each year, hosting performances by artists ranging from Maria Callas to contemporary musicians and theatre companies.
