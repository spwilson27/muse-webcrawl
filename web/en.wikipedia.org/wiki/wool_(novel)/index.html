Wool is a series of science fiction novellas and novels by American author Hugh Howey. The first story, a short novella also titled Wool, was self-published in July 2011 through Amazon's Kindle Direct Publishing. It was later combined with four sequel novellas into a novel, and followed by Shift and Dust, forming the Silo trilogy.

Plot

The story is set in a post-apocalyptic future in which the remnants of humanity live in a vast underground silo, forbidden to go outside because the surface world is toxic. The silo's inhabitants can view the ruined landscape through sensors, which are periodically cleaned by condemned exiles in protective suits — a ritual that always ends with the cleaner's death. The narrative begins with Sheriff Holston, who, grieving his wife, asks to go outside.

Reception and adaptation

Wool became a bestseller and a landmark of self-publishing; Howey retained digital rights while selling print rights to Simon & Schuster. The series was adapted into the Apple TV+ television series Silo (2023). See also dystopian fiction.
