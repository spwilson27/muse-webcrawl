The Bradley Fighting Vehicle (BFV) is a family of American tracked armoured fighting vehicles manufactured by BAE Systems Land & Armaments (formerly United Defense and FMC). It is named for U.S. General Omar Bradley. The family consists principally of the M2 Bradley infantry fighting vehicle, which carries a squad of infantry into battle, and the M3 Bradley cavalry fighting vehicle, which performs reconnaissance and scouting missions with a smaller dismount team and more ammunition.

Development

The Bradley was developed in response to the Soviet BMP-1 and to replace the unarmoured, open-topped M113 armoured personnel carrier. Development in the 1970s was long and controversial: requirements shifted between troop transport, anti-tank capability and reconnaissance, and the programme was criticised for cost growth and for the vehicle's vulnerability — a saga satirised in the book and film The Pentagon Wars. The M2 entered service in 1981. Successive upgrades produced the M2A1 with TOW II missiles, the M2A2 with improved armour, and the M2A3 with digital electronics and improved sights.

Design

The Bradley is armed with a 25 mm M242 Bushmaster chain gun firing armour-piercing and high-explosive ammunition, a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun, and a twin-tube launcher for BGM-71 TOW anti-tank guided missiles. Its aluminium and steel armour protects against small arms and shell splinters, with explosive reactive armour kits added for Iraq service. A 600-horsepower Cummins diesel gives a road speed of about 56 km/h (35 mph); early versions were amphibious with a water barrier erected by the crew. The crew is three — commander, gunner and driver — with room for six or seven infantrymen in the M2.

Service history

Bradleys fought in the 1991 Gulf War, where they destroyed more Iraqi armoured vehicles than the Abrams tank, and in the 2003 invasion of Iraq and subsequent occupation, where improvised explosive devices and rocket-propelled grenades inflicted significant losses and drove further armour upgrades. Bradleys have also served in the Balkans, Afghanistan, and were supplied to Ukraine from 2023, seeing heavy combat there, and to Saudi Arabia and Lebanon. The U.S. Army has repeatedly sought a replacement under the Optionally Manned Fighting Vehicle programme.
