Ground granulated blast-furnace slag

AbbreviationGGBS or GGBFS
SourceBy-product of iron blast furnaces
Main compositionSilicates and aluminosilicates of calcium
UseSupplementary cementitious material in concrete

Ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS or GGBFS) is obtained by quenching molten iron slag (a by-product of iron and steel-making) from a blast furnace in water or steam, to produce a glassy, granular product that is then dried and ground into a fine powder. It is used as a supplementary cementitious material in concrete and cement.

Production and properties

Rapid quenching prevents crystallisation, leaving a glassy calcium aluminosilicate that reacts with water and calcium hydroxide (released by Portland cement hydration) to form additional cementitious compounds. Typical GGBS contains roughly 40% CaO, 35% SiO₂, and 10–15% Al₂O₃.

Use in concrete

GGBS commonly replaces 30–70% of the Portland cement in a mix. Benefits include improved long-term strength and durability, lower heat of hydration (reducing thermal cracking in mass concrete), greater resistance to chloride ingress and sulfate attack, and lighter colour. Setting is slower, especially in cold weather, which can lengthen construction schedules.

Environmental aspects

Substituting GGBS for Portland clinker substantially reduces the carbon footprint of concrete, since cement manufacture is a major source of industrial CO₂ emissions.
