CA Brive

Full name: Club Athlétique Brive Corrèze Limousin
Founded: 1910
Location: Brive-la-Gaillarde, France
Ground: Stade Amédée-Domenech
League: Pro D2

Club Athlétique Brive Corrèze Limousin, known as CA Brive, is a French rugby union team founded in 1910 and based in Brive-la-Gaillarde in the Corrèze departement of the Limousin region. They wear black and white and play in the Stade Amédée-Domenech.

History

The club was founded on 15 March 1910. Brive took part in the first French championship final in 1920 but did not win a major title until much later. The club's golden era came in the mid-1990s under coach Didier Casadéi and president Patrick Sébastien. In 1997, Brive won the Heineken Cup, the European club championship, defeating Leicester Tigers 28–9 in the final at Cardiff Arms Park. They returned to the final the following year but lost to Bath.

Brive has spent much of its recent history alternating between the Top 14 and the second-tier Pro D2. The club is known for its passionate supporters and its identity as a working-class team from a small city, punching above its weight in French rugby.

Honours

  • Heineken Cup: Champions 1996–97; Runners-up 1997–98
  • Challenge Yves du Manoir: Champions 1995–96
