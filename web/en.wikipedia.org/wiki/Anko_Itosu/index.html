Ankō Itosu (糸洲 安恒, 1831 – 1915) was an Okinawan martial artist regarded as the father of modern karate. He brought the formerly secret art of ti (hand) into the public school system and shaped the kata practice from which most modern styles descend.

Life

Itosu was born in Shuri, then capital of the Ryukyu Kingdom, and trained under the great Shuri-te master Sōkon Matsumura. Small and introverted as a youth, he developed legendary strength — he was famed for his grip and for crushing bamboo — and served as a secretary to the last Ryukyu king before the kingdom's abolition.

Reforms

In 1901 Itosu succeeded in having karate introduced into Okinawan elementary schools as physical education. To make it suitable for children and group teaching, he simplified the dangerous techniques of the older kata and around 1905 created the five Pinan (Heian) kata, derived from older forms such as Kūsankū. In 1908 he wrote his famous "Ten Precepts of Karate" (Tode Jukun), a letter to the authorities presenting karate as a means of health and character development rather than fighting.

Legacy

Itosu's students — including Gichin Funakoshi, founder of Shotokan, and Kenwa Mabuni, founder of Shitō-ryū — carried his version of karate to mainland Japan and the world. His emphasis on kata, safety and moral education remains central to traditional karate.
