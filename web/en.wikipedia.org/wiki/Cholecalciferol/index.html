Cholecalciferol

Cholecalciferol
Vitamin D3
Formula: C27H44O
Molar mass: 384.64 g/mol
Source: skin synthesis, oily fish, supplements

Cholecalciferol, also known as vitamin D3, is a type of vitamin D that is made by the skin when exposed to sunlight. It is also found in some foods and can be taken as a dietary supplement. Cholecalciferol is used to treat and prevent vitamin D deficiency and associated diseases, including rickets and osteomalacia.

Production

In the skin, 7-dehydrocholesterol reacts with ultraviolet light at wavelengths between 290 and 315 nm present in sunlight, as well as in ultraviolet lamps and tanning beds, to form previtamin D3, which spontaneously isomerizes to vitamin D3. The compound is made from cholesterol in wool grease (lanolin) for use in supplements and food fortification.

Metabolism

Cholecalciferol is converted in the liver to calcifediol (25-hydroxycholecalciferol), which is then converted in the kidneys to calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol), the hormonally active metabolite of vitamin D. Calcitriol increases the absorption of calcium and phosphate from the intestine and regulates bone mineralization.

Medical use

Cholecalciferol is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. It is widely used to prevent deficiency in people with limited sun exposure, malabsorption syndromes, or chronic kidney disease (in its active forms). Excessive intake can cause hypercalcemia.
