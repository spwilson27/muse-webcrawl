DVB-S

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

DVB-S (Digital Video Broadcasting – Satellite) is the original standard of the DVB Project for satellite television, developed in the early 1990s and first published as EN 300 421. It defines the framing structure, channel coding and modulation for digital multi-programme television transmitted by satellite, and it became the dominant worldwide standard for direct-to-home (DTH) satellite broadcasting.

History

DVB-S was developed between 1993 and 1997 by the EBU/ETSI/CENELEC Joint Technical Committee building on work by the European DVB Project. The first commercial DVB-S services launched in the mid-1990s, and the standard rapidly replaced analogue and earlier proprietary digital systems, enabling operators to carry many more channels per satellite transponder than analogue FM transmission.

Technical characteristics

  • Modulation: QPSK (quadrature phase-shift keying) with root-raised-cosine filtering.
  • Error correction: A concatenated scheme combining an outer Reed–Solomon code with an inner convolutional (Viterbi-decoded) code, with selectable code rates of 1/2, 2/3, 3/4, 5/6 and 7/8.
  • Multiplexing: MPEG-2 transport streams carrying multiple television, radio and data services in a single transponder channel.
  • Data rates: Typically around 38 Mbit/s per 33 MHz transponder at the most commonly used settings.

DVB-S2

In 2003 the DVB Project approved a second-generation standard, DVB-S2, which replaced QPSK-only modulation with a range of schemes (QPSK, 8PSK, 16APSK and 32APSK) and introduced powerful LDPC forward error correction together with adaptive coding and modulation (ACM). DVB-S2 provides roughly 30 percent more capacity than DVB-S under the same conditions and became the standard for HDTV satellite broadcasting and professional contribution links. Its further extension, DVB-S2X, was published in 2014.
