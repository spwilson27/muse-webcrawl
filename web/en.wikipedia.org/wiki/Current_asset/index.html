Current asset

[Image: A sample balance sheet showing current assets listed above non-current assets]

In accounting, a current asset is any asset which is expected to be sold, consumed, used, or exhausted through the normal operations of a business within the current fiscal year or operating cycle, or held primarily for trading. Current assets are presented on the balance sheet in order of liquidity.

Examples

Typical current assets include cash and cash equivalents; short-term investments and marketable securities; accounts receivable (trade receivables); inventories of raw materials, work in progress and finished goods; and prepaid expenses. Assets are classified as non-current — such as property, plant and equipment, long-term investments and intangible assets — if they are expected to benefit the business for more than one year.

Analysis

Current assets are compared with current liabilities to assess short-term financial health. The current ratio (current assets ÷ current liabilities) and the more conservative quick ratio (which excludes inventories) are standard liquidity measures. The difference between current assets and current liabilities is working capital, a key indicator of a company's operational efficiency and ability to meet its obligations.
