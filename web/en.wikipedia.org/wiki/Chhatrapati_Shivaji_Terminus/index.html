Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus

Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus (CST, formerly Victoria Terminus and Bombay VT) is a historic railway station and UNESCO World Heritage Site in Mumbai, Maharashtra, India. Designed by the British architect Frederick William Stevens in the Victorian Gothic Revival style blended with Indian traditional elements, it was built between 1878 and 1887 and serves as the headquarters of India's Central Railway and one of the busiest stations in the country.

History

The station stands on the site of Bori Bunder, from which India's first passenger train ran to Thane in 1853. Stevens's design, with its great stone dome, turrets, pointed arches and ornate Italian Gothic detailing executed by Indian craftsmen, was completed for the Golden Jubilee of Queen Victoria in 1887. The station was renamed Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus in 1996 after the 17th-century Maratha ruler Shivaji, and "Maharaj" was added to the name in 2017.

Operations and events

CST handles long-distance trains and the Central and Harbour lines of the Mumbai Suburban Railway, used by millions of commuters. On 26 November 2008 the station was attacked by two gunmen during the Mumbai terrorist attacks, killing 58 people; a memorial commemorates the victims.
