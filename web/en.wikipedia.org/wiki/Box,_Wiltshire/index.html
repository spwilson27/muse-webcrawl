Box is a large village and civil parish in Wiltshire, England, about 5 miles (8 km) east of Bath and 2 miles (3 km) west of Corsham. The parish includes the villages of Box and the smaller settlements of Ashley, Box Hill, and part of Middlehill. The village lies in the valley of the Bybrook, a tributary of the River Avon, and is known for its building stone, its association with Isambard Kingdom Brunel's Box Tunnel, and a rich Roman and industrial heritage.

History

The area has been occupied since Roman times. In 1897 the remains of a substantial Roman villa, with mosaic floors and a large number of rooms, were uncovered at Box; further discoveries suggest an important Roman settlement connected with the road between Bath (Aquae Sulis) and Silchester. The villa was partly re-excavated in the 20th century and is considered one of the more elaborate Roman rural houses in the region.

The village name is thought to derive from the box tree (Buxus sempervirens), which grows on the local limestone. The parish church of St Thomas à Becket is mostly of the 14th and 15th centuries, though it stands on an earlier foundation.

Stone quarrying

Box lies at the heart of the Bath stone district. The local oolitic limestone, known commercially as Box Ground stone, was quarried extensively from the 18th century onwards, and underground quarries around Box supplied stone for buildings across southern England. Quarrying declined in the 20th century but the extensive mine workings beneath Box Hill remain a prominent feature of the area; some former underground workings were adapted for defence purposes during the Second World War and are associated with the large underground complex at nearby Corsham.

Box Tunnel

The village's most famous engineering landmark is Box Tunnel, designed by Brunel for the Great Western Railway and opened in 1841. At just under 2 miles (3.2 km) long, it was the longest railway tunnel in the world at the time of its construction, and its western portal, dressed in classical style, faces the village. The tunnel passes through Box Hill at a gradient of 1 in 100, and its construction cost the lives of around a hundred workers.

Geography and amenities

Box sits on the A4 road between Bath and Chippenham. The village has a primary school, shops, pubs, and a parish hall, and is surrounded by countryside designated as part of the Cotswolds Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The Bybrook flows through the village, and Hazelbury Manor, a medieval manor house with notable gardens, lies just to the west.

Notable residents

The musician Peter Gabriel founded Real World Studios in a converted mill at Box, attracting recording artists from around the world. The village has also been home to writers and engineers associated with the railway and the quarrying industry.
