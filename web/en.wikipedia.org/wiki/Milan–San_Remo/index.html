Milan–San Remo (Italian: Milano–Sanremo), nicknamed La Classicissima di primavera ("the Spring classic"), is an annual road cycling race between Milan and Sanremo in north-western Italy. With a distance of about 298 kilometres it is the longest professional one-day race of the modern era, and it traditionally opens the "monuments" season as the first of the five Monuments of cycling.

History

The race was first held in 1907, won by Lucien Petit-Breton, and was promoted by the newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport. Eddy Merckx holds the record with seven victories (1966–1976). Famous winners include Fausto Coppi, whose 1946 solo victory by a huge margin relaunched Italian cycling after the war, Gino Bartali, Sean Kelly, Oscar Freire (three wins), Fabian Cancellara, and in recent editions Mathieu van der Poel (2023) and Jasper Philipsen (2024).

Route

The course runs south from Milan across the Lombard plain to the Passo del Turchino, descends to the Ligurian coast at Genoa, and then follows the Mediterranean westward along the Via Aurelia. The race is usually decided in its final 30 kilometres, over the coastal climbs of the Cipressa (introduced in 1982) and the Poggio di San Remo (added in 1960), a short ascent followed by a fast, twisting descent into Sanremo and a finish on the Via Roma. Because the climbs are modest in gradient after nearly 300 kilometres of racing, Milan–San Remo is often called the "sprinters' classic", though solo attackers who crest the Poggio clear can hold off the bunch.

Characteristics

Despite — or because of — its length, the race is known for a long, relatively controlled build-up followed by an explosive finale; editions have been reshaped by weather, from the snow-shortened 2013 race (won by Gerald Ciolek) to strong coastal winds. A women's edition, revived under the name Milano–Sanremo Donne, returned to the calendar in 2025.
