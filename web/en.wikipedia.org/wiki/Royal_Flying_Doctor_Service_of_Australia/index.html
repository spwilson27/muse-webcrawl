Royal Flying Doctor Service of Australia

Royal Flying Doctor Service
Founded: 1928 (as the Australian Inland Mission Aerial Medical Service)
Founder: Reverend John Flynn
Headquarters: Australia
Type: Non-profit aeromedical organisation

The Royal Flying Doctor Service of Australia (RFDS) is one of the largest and most comprehensive aeromedical organisations in the world. It provides extensive primary health care and 24-hour emergency services to people who live, work and travel across the vast and sparsely populated areas of the Australian Outback.

History

The service was founded in 1928 by the Reverend John Flynn, a Presbyterian minister of the Australian Inland Mission. Inspired by the death of a young stockman who bled to death while waiting days for medical help, Flynn combined two new technologies — aviation and the pedal-powered radio developed by Alfred Traeger — to bring doctors to remote communities. The first base opened at Cloncurry, Queensland, and the first flight took place on 17 May 1928.

The organisation was originally known as the Australian Inland Mission Aerial Medical Service, later the Flying Doctor Service. Queen Elizabeth II granted the "Royal" prefix in 1955. During the 1930s and 1940s the service expanded across the continent, establishing bases in Western Australia, South Australia, New South Wales and Victoria.

Services

The RFDS provides aeromedical evacuations, primary health care clinics, telehealth consultations, dental services, and mental health support. Its aircraft carry intensive-care equipment and are staffed by doctors, flight nurses and pilots. The service operates a fleet of more than 75 aircraft from over 20 bases, covering an area of more than 7.6 million square kilometres — roughly the size of Western Europe.

The service is famously non-profit and relies heavily on fundraising and community donations alongside government funding. Its motto and legacy are celebrated in Australian culture; John Flynn appears on the Australian twenty-dollar note.
