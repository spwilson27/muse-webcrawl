NCSY (formerly the National Conference of Synagogue Youth) is an Orthodox Jewish youth movement run by the Orthodox Union. Founded in 1954, it aims to inspire Jewish teenagers — particularly those in public schools with little Jewish education — to connect with Judaism through social, educational, and volunteer programmes.

Programmes

NCSY runs regional chapters across North America and internationally, organising Shabbatons (weekend retreats), summer programmes including trips to Israel such as The Jerusalem Journey (TJJ), and relief missions. Its Jewish Student Union (JSU) clubs operate in hundreds of public high schools, offering Jewish culture and learning clubs for students. NCSY's alumni network and youth leadership programmes feed into gap-year and campus Jewish life.

History

The organisation began in 1954 as part of the Orthodox Union's efforts to engage synagogue youth, and expanded its outreach focus under leaders such as Pinchas Stolper. Over time it shifted toward outreach to non-Orthodox and unaffiliated teens, becoming one of the largest Jewish youth organisations in the world.
