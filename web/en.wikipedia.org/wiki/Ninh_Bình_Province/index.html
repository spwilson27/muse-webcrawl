Ninh Bình is a province in northern Vietnam, located in the southern part of the Red River Delta about 90 km south of Hanoi. Though one of Vietnam's smaller provinces by area, it is historically significant as the site of Hoa Lư, the capital of Vietnam in the 10th and early 11th centuries under the Đinh and Early Lê dynasties, and it is internationally known for its dramatic limestone karst landscapes.

Geography

Ninh Bình borders Hòa Bình and Hà Nam to the north, Nam Định to the east, and Thanh Hóa to the south and west, with a short coastline on the Gulf of Tonkin. Much of the province is low delta plain, but its western and southern districts are dominated by limestone karst mountains pierced by rivers and caves. The Đáy River flows through the province toward the sea.

History

After defeating the Twelve Warlords, Đinh Bộ Lĩnh unified Vietnam and in 968 founded the Đinh dynasty with its capital at Hoa Lư, set amid the defensible karst hills. The capital moved to Hanoi (Thăng Long) in 1010 under Lý Thái Tổ, but Hoa Lư's temples to the Đinh and Lê emperors remain important historical sites. The province in its modern form was re-established in 1991 when Ninh Bình was separated from Hà Sơn Bình province.

Tourism and economy

Tourism centres on the Tràng An Scenic Landscape Complex, inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site in 2014 — Vietnam's first mixed cultural and natural heritage site — together with nearby Tam Cốc-Bích Động, famous for boat rides through rice paddies and caves, the ancient capital of Hoa Lư, Bái Đính Pagoda (among the largest Buddhist temple complexes in Southeast Asia), Phat Diem Cathedral, and Cúc Phương National Park, Vietnam's oldest national park (established 1962), which extends into the province. The local economy also includes agriculture (rice, pineapples), cement and building materials, and handicraft villages; a local specialty is dishes made from mountain goat.
