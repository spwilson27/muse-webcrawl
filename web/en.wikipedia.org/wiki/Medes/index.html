Medes

The Medes were an ancient Iranian people who spoke the Median language and who inhabited an area known as Media between western and northern Iran. Around the 11th century BC, they occupied the mountainous region of northwestern Iran and the northeastern and eastern region of Mesopotamia (in modern Hamadan Province). Their consolidation into a kingdom is believed to have occurred in the 8th century BC.

History

According to Herodotus, the Median tribes were unified by Deioces, who founded the capital Ecbatana (modern Hamadan). His successors Phraortes and Cyaxares expanded Median power; Cyaxares reorganised the army, allied with Babylon, and together with Nabopolassar destroyed Nineveh in 612 BC, ending the Neo-Assyrian Empire. Under Astyages, the last Median king, the empire extended from Cappadocia to Bactria, but in 550 BC Cyrus the Great, himself of Persian and (by tradition) Median descent, revolted and absorbed Media into the Achaemenid Empire, where Medes retained high status — Greek sources routinely paired "the Medes and the Persians".

Culture and religion

Median religion is associated with the Magi, a priestly caste mentioned by Herodotus as one of the Median tribes, influential in later Zoroastrian practice. Little Median literature survives, and their language is known mainly from loanwords and names in Old Persian, Greek, and Assyrian sources. Median art and architecture, including columned halls at sites such as Nush-i Jan and Godin Tepe, influenced early Achaemenid styles.
