History of Mali

The history of Mali covers successive West African empires controlling trans-Saharan trade, French colonisation as French Sudan, independence in 1960, and modern struggles with coups and insurgency.

Empires

The Ghana Empire, then the Mali Empire founded by Sundiata Keita (c.1235), grew rich on gold and salt. Mansa Musa's 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca displayed immense wealth; Timbuktu and Djenné became centres of Islamic scholarship. The Songhai Empire succeeded it in the 15th–16th centuries until Moroccan invasion in 1591.

Colonial and independence

France conquered the region in the 1890s. Mali gained independence under Modibo Keïta (1960) with a socialist path, followed by Moussa Traoré's military rule (1968–1991), multiparty democracy under Alpha Oumar Konaré and Amadou Toumani Touré.

Modern crises

A 2012 coup and Tuareg/jihadist rebellion in the north, French intervention (Operation Serval), MINUSMA peacekeeping, and further coups in 2020–2021 under Assimi Goïta marked recent decades.
