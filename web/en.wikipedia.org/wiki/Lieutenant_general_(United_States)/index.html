Lieutenant general (United States)

Lieutenant general
Country: United States
Rank: Three-star officer
Pay grade: O-9
Above: Major general; below: General

In the United States Armed Forces, a lieutenant general is a three-star general officer in the United States Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force. A lieutenant general ranks above a major general and below a general, and carries the pay grade of O-9.

Role and appointment

Lieutenant generals typically command corps-sized units of 20,000 to 45,000 soldiers, serve as deputy commanders of combatant commands, or hold senior staff posts at the Pentagon. Appointments to the grade are tied to specific positions, are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate, and normally require retirement after 38 years of service or at age 64 unless further appointed.

History

The rank was first conferred on George Washington as "Lieutenant General and Commander in Chief" in 1798, and later awarded sparingly — to Winfield Scott by brevet, and during the Civil War to Ulysses S. Grant, whose promotion revived the grade. It became a regular feature of the rank structure in the twentieth century.
