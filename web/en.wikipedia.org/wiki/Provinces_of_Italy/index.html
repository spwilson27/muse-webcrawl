Provinces of Italy

The provinces of Italy (Italian: province d'Italia) are the second-level administrative divisions of the Italian Republic, below regions and above municipalities (comuni). Italy is divided into about 107 provincial-level bodies, a number that includes ordinary provinces, metropolitan cities, the autonomous provinces of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol, and the regional decentralisation bodies of Friuli-Venezia Giulia and Sicily.

History

The provincial system descends from the administrative organisation of the Kingdom of Sardinia and was extended across Italy at unification in 1861. Originally the province was headed by a prefect representing central government; elected provincial councils were introduced later. A 2014 reform (the Delrio law) downgraded most provinces to indirectly elected second-level bodies and replaced the largest with metropolitan cities, such as Rome Capital, Milan, Naples, Turin and Bologna.

Functions

Provinces are responsible for such services as provincial roads, upper-secondary school buildings, environmental protection and territorial planning, although many powers have been transferred to the regions. Each province is identified by a two-letter code used in postal addresses and vehicle registration plates.

Special cases

The Autonomous Province of Trento and the Autonomous Province of Bolzano (South Tyrol) exercise most of the powers of a region. In Sardinia and Sicily, regional reforms have repeatedly reorganised or abolished ordinary provinces in favour of metropolitan cities and free municipal consortia.
