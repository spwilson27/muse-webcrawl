Junket (dessert)

Junket
TypeDessert
Place of originEngland
Main ingredientsMilk, sugar, rennet, vanilla

Junket is a traditional English milk-based dessert made from sweetened milk curdled with rennet, the enzyme rennin. It is somewhat similar to panna cotta and blancmange, but is set with rennet rather than with gelatine or starch.

History

The name derives from the Norman French jonquette, a rush basket in which the curds were drained; medieval dishes of curds and cream, sometimes called junket or curds and cream, were delicacies of the English court. In the early modern period "junket" came to mean any sweetmeat or feast, and later a banquet generally.

Preparation

Whole milk is sweetened, flavoured (traditionally with vanilla, rosewater or brandy), warmed to blood heat, and rennet is stirred in. The milk is left undisturbed to set into a delicate curd, then chilled and served, often grated with nutmeg or cinnamon. Once cut, the curd releases whey, so junket is eaten soon after setting.

Commercial preparations

Rennet tablets and powders — long sold under the "Junket" brand in North America by the Danish-founded Junket company (Christian Hansen's laboratory) — made the dessert a popular home preparation in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, particularly as a light food for invalids and children because of its digestibility.
