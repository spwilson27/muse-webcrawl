Freaks is a 1932 American pre-Code horror film produced and directed by Tod Browning for Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer. Set in a traveling circus, it stars real carnival sideshow performers with physical disabilities alongside conventional actors. The film is based in part on Tod Robbins's short story "Spurs."

Plot

The trapeze artist Cleopatra marries the little-person ringmaster Hans for his money, planning with her lover, the strongman Hercules, to poison him. When the other performers discover the plot at the wedding feast — chanting "one of us, one of us" as they accept Cleopatra into their community — she drunkenly mocks them. In a stormy climax the performers take revenge, and Cleopatra is transformed into a grotesque "human duck."

Controversy and legacy

Freaks was met with revulsion on release; audiences walked out, MGM withdrew the film, and it was banned in the United Kingdom for over thirty years. Browning's career never recovered. Roughly half an hour was cut and lost after disastrous previews. Rediscovered at the Venice Film Festival in 1962 and championed by the 1960s counterculture, the film is now regarded as a classic — notable for portraying its disabled performers as sympathetic and ordinary while the able-bodied villains are monstrous. In 1994 it was selected for the United States National Film Registry.
