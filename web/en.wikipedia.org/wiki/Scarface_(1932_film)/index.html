Scarface (also released as Scarface: The Shame of the Nation) is a 1932 American gangster film directed by Howard Hawks and produced by Howard Hughes. Loosely based on the life of Al Capone, it stars Paul Muni as Italian immigrant gangster Antonio "Tony" Camonte, whose violent rise through Chicago's bootlegging underworld ends in his destruction.

Production and censorship

The film was delayed for over a year by battles with the Hays Office over its violence and perceived glorification of crime; Hughes was forced to add a moralising subtitle, a condemnatory prologue, and an altered ending in some versions before release. Despite, or because of, the controversy, it became a landmark of the early gangster cycle alongside Little Caesar and The Public Enemy.

Legacy

Scarface introduced George Raft's coin-flipping gangster mannerism and is noted for its visual motif of an "X" foreshadowing each murder. Brian De Palma's 1983 remake, starring Al Pacino, transplanted the story to the Miami cocaine trade. In 1994 the original was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
