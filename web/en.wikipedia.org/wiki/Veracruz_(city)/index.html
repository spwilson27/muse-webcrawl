Veracruz, also known as Heroica Veracruz, is a major port city on the Gulf of Mexico in the Mexican state of Veracruz. Founded by the Spanish explorer Hernán Cortés in 1519 as Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz, it was the first Spanish settlement in mainland Mexico and for three centuries the principal gateway between New Spain and Europe. The city should not be confused with the surrounding state of the same name, for which it is not the capital (the capital is Xalapa).

History

Cortés landed near the present city on Good Friday 1519 and founded the settlement before marching inland to conquer the Aztec Empire. Veracruz became the port from which the treasures of New Spain were shipped to Spain and through which European goods and settlers arrived; the fortress of San Juan de Ulúa, built on an island in the harbour, guarded it against pirates.

The city earned the title "Four Times Heroic" for its resistance to foreign invasions: it was bombarded and occupied during the Pastry War with France (1838), captured by United States forces in the Mexican-American War (1847), occupied again during the French intervention (1860s), and in 1914 United States forces occupied the port during the Mexican Revolution. The 1914 defence by naval cadets is especially commemorated.

Geography and economy

Veracruz lies on a low, sandy coastal plain and has a hot, humid tropical climate, subject in winter to strong northerly winds known as nortes. The port is among Mexico's oldest and most important, handling automobiles, containers, grain and petroleum products; a major expansion into the bay north of the city opened in 2019. Tourism centres on the historic centre, the malecón promenade, the aquarium, nearby beaches at Boca del Río, and the famous Carnival, one of Mexico's largest.

Culture

The city is known for its Afro-Caribbean-influenced music and dance, including danzón in the zócalo and the jarocho tradition of son jarocho, whose best-known song is "La Bamba". Veracruz cuisine features Gulf seafood, such as huachinango a la veracruzana (red snapper Veracruz-style), and strong coffee served in the historic Gran Café de la Parroquia.
