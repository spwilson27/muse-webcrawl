Overdubbing

Overdubbing (also known as layering) is a technique used in audio recording in which audio tracks that have been pre-recorded are then played back and monitored, while simultaneously recording new, doubled, or augmented tracks onto one or more available tracks of a digital audio workstation or tape machine.

Pioneered by Les Paul and developed with multitrack recording in the 1950s and 1960s, overdubbing allows a single performer to play multiple parts and is fundamental to modern popular music production.
