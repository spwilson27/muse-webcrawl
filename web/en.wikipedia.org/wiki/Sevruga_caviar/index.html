Sevruga caviar

Sevruga caviar is one of the three classic varieties of caviar, made from the roe of the sevruga sturgeon (Acipenser stellatus), also called the stellate sturgeon or starry sturgeon. Along with beluga and osetra, it is traditionally ranked among the highest grades of caviar harvested from the Caspian Sea and Black Sea basins.

Characteristics

Sevruga eggs are the smallest of the three classic caviars, typically grey to dark grey in colour, with a delicate, fine grain and a pronounced, complex flavour often described as buttery with a distinctive briny intensity. Because the sevruga sturgeon matures earlier and reproduces more frequently than the beluga or osetra, its caviar has historically been somewhat less expensive, though still a luxury product.

The fish

The sevruga (Acipenser stellatus) is an anadromous sturgeon native to the Caspian, Black, Azov, and Aegean seas, reaching about 2.2 metres in length. Like other sturgeons it is critically endangered from overfishing and habitat loss, and international trade in its caviar is regulated under CITES. Wild Caspian caviar exports have been subject to periodic bans and quotas, and farmed sevruga-type caviar has become increasingly common.

Serving

Like other fine caviars, sevruga is served chilled, traditionally on crushed ice with a mother-of-pearl or horn spoon (metal being said to taint the flavour), accompanied by blini, toast points, crème fraîche, or simply eaten on its own.
