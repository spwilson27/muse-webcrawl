The John Rylands Library is a late-Victorian neo-Gothic building on Deansgate in Manchester, England. It was founded by Enriqueta Augustina Rylands in memory of her husband, the textile magnate John Rylands, and opened to the public on 1 January 1900. Designed by architect Basil Champneys, its ornate stone interior resembles a cathedral, with a celebrated reading room lit by traceried windows.

Collections

The library houses one of the world's richest collections of rare books and manuscripts, including the Rylands Library Papyri — among them the St John's Gospel fragment (P52), regarded as the earliest surviving fragment of the New Testament — Gutenberg Bibles, a first folio of Shakespeare, and the Spencer collection of Aldine and other early printed books assembled by Earl Spencer. Its archives include the papers of Elizabeth Gaskell and the Manchester Guardian archive.

University library

In 1972 the library merged with the library of the Victoria University of Manchester to form the John Rylands University Library of Manchester, now the University of Manchester Library. The Deansgate building operates as the John Rylands Research Institute and Library, a special-collections centre open to researchers and visitors. It is a Grade I listed building.
