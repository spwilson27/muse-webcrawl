Asparuh (Bulgarian: Аспарух; c. 640–701) was a Bulgar khan who led a group of Bulgars south of the Danube in the late 7th century and founded the First Bulgarian Empire in 681, an event traditionally regarded as the birth of the Bulgarian state. He was the third son of Kubrat, the founder of Old Great Bulgaria in the Pontic steppe.

Migration and settlement

After Kubrat's death and the Khazar destruction of Old Great Bulgaria around 668, Asparuh led his followers westward, settling in the Ongal area at the Danube delta. From there the Bulgars raided and then settled the Byzantine provinces of Moesia and Scythia Minor, entering into alliance with local Slavic tribes — the Seven Slavic tribes and the Severians.

War of 680–681 and recognition

Emperor Constantine IV campaigned against the Bulgars in 680 but withdrew owing to illness, and his army was routed at the Battle of Ongal. In 681 Byzantium signed a peace treaty recognising Asparuh's state and agreeing to pay an annual tribute. Asparuh established his capital at Pliska and built a polity uniting Bulgar military aristocracy with the Slavic population. He died in 701, traditionally in battle against the Khazars, and was succeeded by his son Tervel.

Legacy

Asparuh is celebrated in Bulgaria as the founder of the nation; the town of Isperih and Asparuh Peak in Antarctica bear versions of his name, and he placed at the head of Bulgarian national polls of great historical figures.
