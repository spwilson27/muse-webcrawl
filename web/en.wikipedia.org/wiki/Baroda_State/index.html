Baroda State was a princely state in present-day Gujarat, western India, ruled by the Gaekwad dynasty of the Marathas. Founded after the Maratha commander Pilaji Rao Gaekwad conquered Songadh in 1721 and centred on the city of Baroda (Vadodara), it became one of the largest and richest princely states, entitled to a 21-gun salute under British paramountcy.

Progressive rule

Under Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III (r. 1875–1939), Baroda became a model of reform: it pioneered free and compulsory primary education in India, built libraries across the state, founded the Bank of Baroda, and patronised figures including B. R. Ambedkar, whom the state sponsored for study abroad. The Laxmi Vilas Palace, completed in 1890, remains one of India's grandest royal residences.

Accession

The last ruling maharaja, Pratap Singh Rao Gaekwad, signed the instrument of accession to the Dominion of India; Baroda was merged into Bombay State on 1 May 1949, ending more than two centuries of Gaekwad rule.
