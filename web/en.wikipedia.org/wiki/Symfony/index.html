Symfony is a free and open-source PHP web application framework and a set of reusable PHP components, released under the MIT License. It was originally developed by Fabien Potencier of the French web agency SensioLabs and first published in 2005.

Overview

Symfony follows the model–view–controller pattern and emphasises reusable, decoupled components — including HttpKernel, DependencyInjection, Routing, Form, Validator, and Templating — that can be used independently in other projects. Its components underpin many other PHP projects, most notably the Laravel framework and the Drupal content management system. The framework provides features such as the Twig templating engine (developed alongside it), the Doctrine ORM integration, a debug toolbar and profiler, and a command-line console.

Releases

Symfony publishes on a regular time-based schedule, with minor releases every six months and long-term support (LTS) versions maintained for several years. Major versions include Symfony 2 (2011), a ground-up rewrite; Symfony 4 (2017), which slimmed the standard edition around Flex and bundles; and subsequent 5.x, 6.x, and 7.x series aligned with modern PHP versions.

Community

The project is supported by a large international community, the SymfonyCon and SymfonyLive conferences, and the SymfonyCasts tutorial platform. It is sponsored and coordinated by the Symfony company founded by Potencier.
