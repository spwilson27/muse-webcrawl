Time of Troubles

The Time of Troubles (Russian: Смутное время, Smutnoye vremya), also known as the Smuta, was a period of political crisis during the Tsardom of Russia that began in 1598 with the death of Feodor I, the last of the House of Rurik, and ended in 1613 with the accession of Michael I of the House of Romanov. It was a time of lawlessness and anarchy following the death of Fyodor I, a weak and possibly intellectually disabled ruler who died without an heir; his death ended the Rurik dynasty, leading to a succession crisis, multiple pretenders to the throne (the False Dmitrys), intervention by the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and Sweden, famine, and widespread civil war.

Course of events

Boris Godunov, brother-in-law and regent of Feodor, was elected tsar by a Zemsky Sobor in 1598, but his rule was undermined by the terrible famine of 1601–1603, worsened by the global cooling effects of the 1600 Huaynaputina eruption. In 1604 a pretender claiming to be Dmitry Ivanovich, the youngest son of Ivan the Terrible who had died in 1591, invaded with Polish support; Godunov died in 1605 and False Dmitry I was crowned in Moscow, only to be murdered in a 1606 uprising that installed Vasily Shuisky. A second False Dmitry, supported by Polish magnates, set up a rival court at Tushino, while Sweden and the Commonwealth intervened openly: Polish forces occupied Moscow in 1610 and the boyars offered the crown to the Polish prince Władysław. The occupation provoked a national uprising; in 1612 a volunteer army raised by the Nizhny Novgorod merchant Kuzma Minin and commanded by Prince Dmitry Pozharsky liberated Moscow. In February 1613 a Zemsky Sobor elected 16-year-old Michael Romanov as tsar, founding a dynasty that ruled until 1917.

Legacy

The Time of Troubles left deep demographic and economic devastation but became a foundational national myth of Russia saved by popular unity; the Minin and Pozharsky monument stands on Red Square, and 4 November is celebrated as Unity Day. The period inspired major works of Russian culture, including Pushkin's drama Boris Godunov and Mussorgsky's opera of the same name.
