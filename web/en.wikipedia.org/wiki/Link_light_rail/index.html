Link light rail
Locale: Seattle, Washington, US
Operator: Sound Transit (service by King County Metro)
Opened: July 18, 2009
Lines: 1 Line, 2 Line, T Line

Link light rail is a light rail rapid transit system serving the Seattle metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Washington. It is managed by Sound Transit in partnership with local transit providers, and operated and maintained under contract by King County Metro.

Services and expansion

The system opened in 2009 with the Central Link segment between downtown Seattle and Tukwila, extended the same year to Seattle–Tacoma International Airport. The 1 Line now runs from Lynnwood through downtown Seattle and the Rainier Valley to Angle Lake, following major extensions to the University of Washington (2016), Northgate (2021) and Lynnwood (2024). The 2 Line serves the Eastside, including Bellevue and Redmond, and crosses Lake Washington on the Homer M. Hadley Memorial Bridge; the T Line serves Tacoma. Sound Transit 2 and Sound Transit 3 ballot measures fund continuing expansion, ultimately planned to connect Everett, Tacoma, West Seattle and Ballard.

Operations

Trains use low-floor light rail vehicles from Kinkisharyo and Siemens, run mostly at grade in the Rainier Valley and elevated or in tunnels elsewhere, and share the Downtown Seattle Transit Tunnel with buses until 2019. Fares are distance-based via the ORCA card system, and ridership has grown steadily with each extension.
