Defile (geography)

In geography and military terminology, a defile is a narrow pass or gorge through mountains or other difficult terrain, through which troops, travellers, or a road or river must pass in a restricted column. The word comes from the French défilé, from défiler, "to march in a file". Because a defile forces movement into single file or a narrow front, it is a classic site for ambushes and for the defence of a route by a small force.

Military significance

Defiles have shaped military history: an army strung out in a defile cannot deploy its full strength, cannot easily retreat, and is vulnerable to attack from the heights above. Famous battles fought in or at defiles include the Battle of Thermopylae (480 BC) in a coastal pass in Greece, the ambush of Roman legions travelling through forest and marsh in the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest (AD 9), medieval and early modern actions in Alpine passes, and numerous engagements in the Khyber Pass between Afghanistan and Pakistan. Military engineers speak of a defile also for any constriction in an advance, such as a bridge, a causeway through marsh, or a gap in an obstacle.

Examples

  • Thermopylae, Greece
  • Khyber Pass, Afghanistan/Pakistan
  • Iron Gates of the Danube, Serbia/Romania
  • Brenner Pass, Austria/Italy
  • Roncevaux Pass, Pyrenees

Related terms

A defile is closely related to a mountain pass, a gorge, and a strait in naval contexts. In marching terminology, "to defile" is to pass through such a constriction in file. The term is also used by extension in geomorphology for narrow, steep-sided valleys and in some regions as a place-name element.
