Samsung Group is a South Korean multinational manufacturing conglomerate headquartered in Samsung Town, Seoul. It is the largest of South Korea's family-controlled chaebol, founded in 1938 by Lee Byung-chul as a trading company dealing in dried fish, groceries and noodles. Its flagship subsidiary, Samsung Electronics, is one of the world's largest technology companies — the leading maker of smartphones (the Galaxy line), memory chips and televisions — and sells its products through samsung.com. Samsung's businesses account for a substantial share of South Korea's exports and GDP, giving the group an outsized influence on the country's economy, politics and culture.

History

After starting in food trading and textiles, Samsung entered electronics in the late 1960s and semiconductors in the 1970s and 1980s, a bet that made Samsung Electronics a global leader in DRAM and NAND flash memory. Under chairman Lee Kun-hee, who took over in 1987 and famously declared in 1993 that employees should "change everything except your wife and children", the group pushed quality and design; by the 2010s its Galaxy smartphones were the main rival to Apple's iPhone.

The group was shaken by the 2016 Galaxy Note 7 battery-fire recall and by a corruption scandal: de facto leader Lee Jae-yong was convicted in 2017 of bribing associates of President Park Geun-hye, served prison time, and was pardoned in 2022. Lee Kun-hee died in 2020 and Lee Jae-yong became executive chairman in 2022. In semiconductors Samsung competes with TSMC in foundry manufacturing and with SK Hynix and Micron in memory, and supplies components to rivals including Apple.

Operations

Major affiliates include Samsung Electronics (semiconductors, phones, displays, appliances), Samsung SDI (batteries), Samsung Heavy Industries (shipbuilding), Samsung C&T (construction and trading — builder of the Burj Khalifa), Samsung Life Insurance, and the theme park operator Samsung Everland. Samsung Electronics is the group's largest company by revenue and a top holder of patents worldwide.

Influence

Samsung is a major sponsor of sport, including a long-standing worldwide Olympic partnership, and its brand is consistently ranked among the most valuable in the world. Critics focus on the chaebol governance model, cross-shareholdings that let the founding family control the group with a small stake, and workplace safety in its chip plants.
