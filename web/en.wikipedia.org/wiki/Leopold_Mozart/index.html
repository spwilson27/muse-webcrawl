Leopold Mozart

Johann Georg Leopold Mozart (14 November 1719 – 28 May 1787) was a German composer, violinist, and music teacher from Augsburg. He is best known as the father and teacher of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and Maria Anna "Nannerl" Mozart, whose childhood careers he managed, and as the author of an influential treatise on violin playing.

Life and career

Born in Augsburg, Leopold studied philosophy and law at Salzburg University but left without a degree, entering the service of the Prince-Archbishop of Salzburg as a musician. He rose to become deputy Kapellmeister at the Salzburg court in 1763. In 1756 — the year of Wolfgang's birth — he published his Versuch einer gründlichen Violinschule (A Treatise on the Fundamental Principles of Violin Playing), a landmark pedagogical work that was widely translated and reprinted.

The tours

Recognising his children's extraordinary gifts, Leopold took Wolfgang and Nannerl on a series of tours across Europe from 1762 onwards, including the "Grand Tour" of 1763–66 to Paris and London, and three journeys to Italy. The trips built Wolfgang's fame and experience but consumed Leopold's own composing career. His surviving works include symphonies, concertos, divertimenti, and the piece long attributed to him (and sometimes to his son) known as the "Toy Symphony".

Later years

Relations between Leopold and his adult son became strained, notably over Wolfgang's move to Vienna and marriage to Constanze Weber, whom Leopold distrusted. Leopold's extensive letters are a primary source for Wolfgang's biography. He died in Salzburg in 1787.
