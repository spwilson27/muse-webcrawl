Visuddhimagga

The Visuddhimagga ("The Path of Purification") is the great treatise on Theravada Buddhist doctrine and meditation written by the monk-scholar Buddhaghosa in the 5th century CE in Sri Lanka. Composed in Pali at the Mahavihara monastery in Anuradhapura, it is a systematic manual summarising and interpreting the teachings of the Pali Canon according to the Mahavihara tradition, and it remains the most influential non-canonical text in Theravada Buddhism.

Structure

The work is built around the threefold training and the seven stages of purification (satta-visuddhi) drawn from the Rathavinita Sutta:

  • Virtue (sīla) — purification of conduct: the monastic precepts and ascetic practices;
  • Concentration (samādhi) — purification of mind: a detailed catalogue of forty meditation subjects (kammatthana), the kasinas, recollections, and foulness meditations, leading through the absorptions (jhāna);
  • Understanding (paññā) — purification of view and the succeeding insight knowledges: analysis of mind and matter, dependent origination, the aggregates, and the progressive stages of insight (vipassanā-ñāṇa) culminating in nibbāna.

Influence

Written as an introduction to Buddhaghosa's great commentaries on the Canon (tradition holds it was composed as a test of his competence before he was permitted to translate the Sinhala commentaries into Pali), the Visuddhimagga became the standard reference for Theravada meditation theory. Its scheme of insight stages underlies the modern Burmese vipassana movement, and Ñāṇamoli's English translation, The Path of Purification (1956), made it a foundational text for Western students of Theravada.
