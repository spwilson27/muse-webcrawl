Serie D is Italy's top amateur division, organised into nine regional groups.

Serie D is the top division of the Italian non-professional football association, the Lega Nazionale Dilettanti, and the fourth tier of the Italian football league system. It sits below the professional Serie A, Serie B, and Serie C, and above the regional Eccellenza leagues. Founded in its earliest form in 1948 and renamed Serie D in 1959, it is one of the largest national-level leagues in the world by number of clubs.

Format

Serie D comprises around 160 clubs divided into nine groups (gironi), organised on a geographical basis and labelled A through I. Each group champion is promoted directly to Serie C. Additional promotion places are contested through play-offs, while the lowest-placed clubs in each group are relegated to Eccellenza, with further relegation decided by play-outs. Although classified as amateur, many Serie D clubs are historic names—former Serie A sides recovering from financial collapse often restart from Serie D, and champions receive the traditional Scudetto Dilettanti.

History

The championship's origins trace to the post-war Promozione league of 1948. It became the Quarta Serie and then Serie D in 1959. For many years it was a semi-professional "Serie D" sandwiched between Serie C2 and the regional leagues; following the 2014 reform that unified Lega Pro, Serie D became firmly the fourth tier and the first amateur level of the pyramid. A Coppa Italia Serie D, contested by Serie D clubs, has been played since 1999.
