Majorca, or Mallorca, is the largest island of the Balearic Islands, which are part of Spain, and the seventh largest island in the Mediterranean Sea. The capital of the island, Palma, is also the capital of the autonomous community of the Balearic Islands.

History

Majorca has been inhabited since the Palaeolithic era; its Talaiotic culture left megalithic remains. Phoenicians, Romans (who founded Palma and Pollentia in 123 BC), Vandals, Byzantines and Moors followed. In 1229 James I of Aragon conquered the island, and it became part of the Crown of Aragon and later of Spain. The Serra de Tramuntana in the north-west is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its historic agricultural landscape.

Geography and climate

The island has two mountainous regions — the Serra de Tramuntana and the Serres de Llevant — with a fertile central plain, Es Pla, between them. Its Mediterranean climate has hot, dry summers and mild winters. The coastline is indented with coves (cales) and sandy bays.

Tourism

Tourism is the dominant industry: Majorca receives well over ten million visitors a year, drawn by beach resorts such as Magaluf, Alcúdia and Cala Millor, as well as by hiking and cycling in the Tramuntana. Palma airport is among Spain's busiest. In recent years the island has debated the environmental and housing pressures of mass tourism.

Culture

Frédéric Chopin and George Sand famously wintered at the charterhouse of Valldemossa in 1838–39. The island is the birthplace of the tennis player Rafael Nadal, and its cuisine includes sobrassada, ensaïmada pastries and pa amb oli.
