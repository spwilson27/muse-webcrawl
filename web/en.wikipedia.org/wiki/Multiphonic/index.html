Multiphonic

A multiphonic is a sound produced on a wind instrument in which two or more pitches are heard simultaneously, contrary to the instrument's normal single-note behaviour. Multiphonics are produced with special fingerings, often combined with adjusted embouchure and air pressure, that set up multiple vibration modes in the air column at once.

History and use

Though occasionally noticed earlier, multiphonics were systematically explored from the 1960s onward as composers of contemporary classical music sought new sonorities. Bruno Bartolozzi's New Sounds for Woodwind (1967) catalogued fingerings for oboe, clarinet, bassoon, and flute multiphonics. They are now a standard extended technique on woodwinds and, less commonly, brass instruments, whose players may combine a played note with a sung pitch.

Characteristics

Multiphonic sonorities are often unstable and rich in difference tones, giving them a dense, sometimes harsh quality suited to avant-garde textures. Precise fingering charts are published for each instrument because results vary between makers and players.
