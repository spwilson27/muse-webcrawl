Mikhail Kalashnikov

Mikhail Timofeyevich Kalashnikov (10 November 1919 – 23 December 2013) was a Soviet and Russian lieutenant general and small-arms designer, best known for creating the AK-47 assault rifle, the most widely produced firearm design in history.

Life

Born to a peasant family in Kurya, Altai Krai, Kalashnikov was deported with his family to Siberia during collectivisation. He worked as a mechanic and was conscripted into the Red Army in 1938, serving as a tank commander. Wounded at the Battle of Bryansk in 1941, he began designing weapons during his convalescence, producing submachine gun and carbine prototypes before his 1947 design won the Soviet assault rifle competition.

The AK-47 and later work

The Avtomat Kalashnikova, adopted in 1949, became famous for reliability, simplicity and ease of mass production; derivatives include the AKM, AK-74, the RPK light machine gun and the PK machine gun (the latter a separate design). Kalashnikov worked at the Izhevsk machine plant for the rest of his career and received numerous state honours, including Hero of Socialist Labour and, post-Soviet, Hero of the Russian Federation.

Reflections

Late in life he expressed ambivalence about his invention's toll: in a 2013 letter to the Patriarch of the Russian Orthodox Church, published after his death, he wrote of his "spiritual pain" at the deaths caused by his rifles, while maintaining that he had designed them to defend his homeland. He died in Izhevsk aged 94.
