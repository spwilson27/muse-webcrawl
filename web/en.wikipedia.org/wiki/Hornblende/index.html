Hornblende

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hornblende crystals

Hornblende is a complex inosilicate series of minerals within the amphibole group. It is not a recognised mineral species in its own right under modern International Mineralogical Association nomenclature, but the name is retained as a general field and petrographic term for dark amphiboles whose composition falls within the calcic amphibole range, with the general formula (Ca,Na)2–3(Mg,Fe,Al)5(Si,Al)8O22(OH)2.

Properties

Hornblende is typically dark green, brown, or black, with a vitreous to dull lustre, a hardness of 5–6 on the Mohs scale, and a specific gravity of about 2.9–3.4. It crystallises in the monoclinic system, forming prismatic crystals with the characteristic amphibole cleavage at angles near 56° and 124°. Composition varies widely through coupled substitutions of sodium, calcium, magnesium, iron, and aluminium, grading toward related amphiboles such as edenite, pargasite, and tschermakite.

Occurrence

Hornblende is a very common rock-forming mineral in igneous rocks such as granite, syenite, diorite, gabbro, and andesite, and it is the principal mineral of the metamorphic rock amphibolite. It forms under moderate to high temperatures in the presence of water, and its argon content is used in 40Ar/39Ar geochronology to date cooling through roughly 500 °C.
