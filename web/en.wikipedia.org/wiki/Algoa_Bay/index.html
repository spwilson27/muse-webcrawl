Algoa Bay is a wide inlet on the Indian Ocean coast of the Eastern Cape province of South Africa, lying between Cape Recife and Cape Padrone. The city of Gqeberha (formerly Port Elizabeth) is situated on its western shore. The name Algoa is thought to derive from the Portuguese para Algoa, "to Goa", reflecting its place on the sea route to India.

History

The Portuguese navigator Bartolomeu Dias rounded the bay in 1488, and Vasco da Gama passed it in 1497. British settlers of 1820 landed at Algoa Bay, leading to the founding of Port Elizabeth. The bay became the Eastern Cape's principal port; the modern Port of Ngqura, a deep-water transshipment harbour east of the city, opened in 2009.

Wildlife

Algoa Bay is an important marine ecosystem. Its island groups — including St Croix Island and Bird Island — host the world's largest colonies of African penguins and large breeding colonies of Cape gannets. The bay is home to bottlenose dolphins (it is sometimes promoted as the "bottlenose dolphin capital of the world"), southern right and humpback whales in season, and great white sharks. Much of the bay falls within the Algoa Bay Hope Spot and adjoins the Addo Elephant National Park marine protected area.
