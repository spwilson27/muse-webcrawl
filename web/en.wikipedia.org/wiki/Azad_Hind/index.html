Azad Hind

The Provisional Government of Free India (Hindi: Ārzī Hukūmat-e-Āzād Hind), commonly known as Azad Hind, was a provisional government established in Japanese-occupied Singapore during World War II. It was created in October 1943 and was supported by the Empire of Japan.

History

Azad Hind was proclaimed by Subhas Chandra Bose on 21 October 1943 in Singapore. Bose became its head of state and head of government, and formed a cabinet that included A. C. Chatterji, S. A. Ayer, and Lakshmi Sahgal, who commanded the Rani of Jhansi Regiment, an all-women unit of the Indian National Army (INA).

The government claimed jurisdiction over Indian territory in exile and was recognized by nine Axis-aligned states, including Japan, Germany, and Italy. It declared war on the United Kingdom and the United States, and its armed force, the INA, fought alongside the Japanese Imperial Army in the campaigns in Burma and at Imphal and Kohima in northeastern India.

Territory

In December 1943, Japan handed over the nominal administration of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands to the provisional government; Bose renamed them Shaheed (Martyr) and Swaraj (Self-rule) Islands and hoisted the Azad Hind flag at Port Blair. In practice, Japanese forces retained effective control.

Dissolution

Following Japan's surrender and Bose's reported death in a plane crash in Taipei on 18 August 1945, Azad Hind ceased to exist. The subsequent Red Fort trials of INA officers in 1945–46 became a major catalyst for the final phase of the Indian independence movement.
