Cash conversion cycle

[Image: Cash conversion cycle diagram]
The cycle: inventory → receivables → payables
FieldCorporate finance / working capital management
FormulaCCC = DIO + DSO − DPO
Measured inDays

The cash conversion cycle (CCC), also known as the net operating cycle, measures how long a firm is deprived of cash when it increases its investment in inventory and other resources in order to increase sales. It expresses, in days, the time between a company's paying for its inventory and its receiving cash from selling that inventory.

Formula

CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO, where DIO (days inventory outstanding) is the average number of days inventory is held, DSO (days sales outstanding) is the average time taken to collect receivables, and DPO (days payable outstanding) is the average time the company takes to pay its suppliers. DIO and DSO lengthen the cycle; DPO shortens it, since the firm holds onto cash longer before paying suppliers.

Interpretation

A shorter cycle generally indicates more efficient working capital management and better liquidity. Some powerful retailers achieve a negative cash conversion cycle, collecting from customers before paying suppliers, effectively financing operations with supplier credit. Because the CCC ties up (or releases) cash, it is a key input to cash flow analysis and a focus of operational improvement efforts.
