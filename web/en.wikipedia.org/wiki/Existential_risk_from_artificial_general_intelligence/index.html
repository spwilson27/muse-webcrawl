Existential risk from artificial general intelligence

Existential risk from artificial general intelligence refers to the hypothetical risk that progress in artificial general intelligence (AGI) could lead to human extinction or another irreversible global catastrophe. Arguments in this area were popularised by philosopher Nick Bostrom and computer scientist Stuart Russell, among others.

Commonly discussed mechanisms include the alignment problem — the difficulty of ensuring that a highly capable AI system pursues goals compatible with human values — instrumental convergence, in which self-preservation and resource acquisition arise as sub-goals for many possible final goals, and the possibility of a rapid "intelligence explosion" outpacing human control.

The topic is debated: surveys show a range of expert opinion, and critics argue the risks are speculative or distract from nearer-term harms of AI systems. Organisations such as the Machine Intelligence Research Institute, the Future of Humanity Institute, and various AI safety research groups have studied the problem, and it has influenced policy discussions and voluntary safety commitments by AI laboratories.
