Burgers' equation

Burgers' equation is a fundamental nonlinear partial differential equation occurring in fluid mechanics, gas dynamics and traffic flow. In its one-dimensional viscous form it reads ∂u/∂t + u ∂u/∂x = ν ∂²u/∂x², where u is the fluid velocity and ν the kinematic viscosity. It is named after the Dutch physicist Johannes Martinus Burgers, who used it from 1939 onward as a simplified model of turbulence.

Behaviour

With ν = 0 (the inviscid equation) smooth initial data steepen and break down in finite time, forming a discontinuity — a shock wave — after which the solution must be understood as a weak solution satisfying conservation of the flux u²/2 and an entropy condition. With positive viscosity the shock is smoothed into a travelling transition layer whose thickness is of order ν/c, where c is the jump speed. Burgers' equation thus captures the essential competition between nonlinear steepening and diffusive smoothing.

Cole–Hopf transformation

The viscous equation is one of the few nonlinear PDEs that can be linearised exactly: the Cole–Hopf transformation u = −2ν (∂φ/∂x)/φ converts it into the linear heat equation for φ, giving explicit solutions such as the N-wave and Taylor shock profiles.

Applications

It serves as a model problem for the theory of hyperbolic conservation laws, a test bed for numerical schemes for the Navier–Stokes equations, and a model in traffic flow where density waves form behind bottlenecks, and in cosmology for the adhesion model of large-scale structure.
