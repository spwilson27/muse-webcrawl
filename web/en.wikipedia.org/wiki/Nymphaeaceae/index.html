Nymphaeaceae

Family: Nymphaeaceae
Order: Nymphaeales
Common name: Water lilies

Nymphaeaceae is a family of flowering plants, commonly called water lilies. They live as rooted aquatic herbs in temperate and tropical climates around the world, growing in freshwater ponds, lakes, and slow-moving streams. The family contains about five genera with roughly 70 known species.

Description

Water lilies grow from rhizomes anchored in mud, sending long stalks to the surface where their round, waxy floating leaves (lily pads) spread out. The flowers, which float or rise above the water, are often large and showy, in colours including white, pink, yellow, and blue. The genus Victoria of South America produces enormous leaves up to 3 metres across, while Nymphaea is the most widespread and familiar genus.

Ecology and uses

Nymphaeaceae form part of one of the earliest-diverging lineages of flowering plants. They provide shelter for fish and invertebrates and shade that suppresses algae. Many species and hybrids are grown as ornamentals in garden ponds, and the flowers of the blue lotus (Nymphaea caerulea) held religious significance in ancient Egypt.
