River Tyburn

SourceHampstead, London
MouthRiver Thames at Pimlico
StatusLargely subterranean (lost river)

The River Tyburn is a river in London, now largely culverted and running underground. It rose in Hampstead, flowed south through Swiss Cottage, Regent's Park and Mayfair, crossed what is now Buckingham Palace's gardens, and entered the River Thames near Pimlico. It should not be confused with Tyburn Brook, a tributary further west.

Course and history

The Tyburn gave its name to the village of Tyburn near modern Marble Arch, notorious for the Tyburn gallows where public executions were held for centuries. In the Middle Ages the river supplied water to the City of London via conduits from Tyburn springs. As London grew, the river was progressively covered over; today its course passes beneath Marylebone and Mayfair, and a section can be glimpsed through an antiques arcade in Davies Street, where goldfish are kept in its water.

In popular culture

The Tyburn features in literature about London's "lost rivers" and appears as a goddess character in Ben Aaronovitch's Rivers of London series.
