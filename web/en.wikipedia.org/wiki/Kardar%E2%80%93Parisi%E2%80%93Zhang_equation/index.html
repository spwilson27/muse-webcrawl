The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) equation is a non-linear stochastic partial differential equation introduced in 1986 by Mehran Kardar, Giorgio Parisi, and Yi-Cheng Zhang. It describes the time evolution of a growing interface height field h(x, t):

∂h/∂t = ν ∇²h + (λ/2)(∇h)² + η(x, t)

where ν is a surface tension (smoothing) coefficient, λ measures the strength of lateral growth, and η is a Gaussian white noise term representing random deposition fluctuations.

Physical motivation

The equation models processes in which a surface grows through random deposition, such as bacterial colony expansion, burning paper fronts, crystal growth, and traffic flow analogues. The non-linear (∇h)² term arises because growth occurs normal to the local surface: a tilted region grows sideways as well as upward.

Universality class

The KPZ equation defines a universality class of non-equilibrium growth phenomena. In one spatial dimension the scaling exponents are exactly known — roughness exponent α = 1/2 and growth exponent β = 1/3 — and fluctuation distributions are given by the Tracy–Widom distributions, a result that links KPZ growth to random matrix theory. In higher dimensions the exponents are known only approximately or numerically, and the strong-coupling regime of the equation remains an active problem in statistical physics and probability theory.

Related models

Through the Cole–Hopf transformation, the one-dimensional KPZ equation maps onto the stochastic heat equation and, in turn, to directed polymers in random media. Discrete models in the KPZ class include the Eden model, ballistic deposition, and the asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP).
