Monofin

A monofin is a type of swimfin, typically used in finswimming and free-diving, that consists of a single, wide blade attached to foot pockets for both feet. The swimmer moves using a dolphin kick — an undulating motion of the whole body — rather than the flutter kick used with a pair of conventional fins.

History

Monofins appeared in competitive finswimming in the Soviet Union in the early 1970s; the first notable design is credited to 1972. The large single blade offered greater propulsion than two fins, and the design spread rapidly through European finswimming competitions governed by CMAS.

Construction

Modern monofins are made of fibreglass or carbon fibre, sometimes with rubber or neoprene foot pockets. Blades vary in stiffness and shape for different disciplines: a relatively flexible, broad blade for finswimming sprints and distance events, and very stiff, wide blades for deep freediving disciplines such as constant weight. Some designs incorporate a slight angle between the foot pocket and blade to match ankle extension during the kick cycle.

Use

Monofins are used in finswimming pool events, open-water finswimming, freediving depth and dynamic apnea disciplines, and, in softer recreational forms, in "mermaiding". Because both legs are bound together, technique relies on core and hip undulation; training without supervision carries the same shallow-water blackout risks as other breath-hold swimming.
