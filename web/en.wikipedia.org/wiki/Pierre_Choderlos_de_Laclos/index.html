Pierre Ambroise François Choderlos de Laclos (18 October 1741 – 5 September 1803) was a French novelist, official, Freemason and army general, best known as the author of the epistolary novel Les Liaisons dangereuses (Dangerous Liaisons), published in 1782 and considered one of the masterpieces of 18th-century literature.

Life and career

Born in Amiens into a recently ennobled family, Laclos pursued a career as an artillery officer, serving in garrisons in France and briefly in the colonies; he also contributed to military engineering, working on fortifications at Île-d'Aix. Literary ambition led him to poetry, a comic opera (Ernestine), and eventually to prose fiction. During long, dull garrison postings he composed Les Liaisons dangereuses, in which the Marquise de Merteuil and the Vicomte de Valmont conduct a ruthless game of seduction and revenge through 175 letters. The novel was an immediate scandal and success; Laclos framed it as a moral warning, though readers prized its psychological acuity and elegant cynicism.

Laclos later became secretary to the Duke of Orléans, was involved in the early politics of the French Revolution, was imprisoned during the Terror, and survived to be rehabilitated under Napoleon, who made him a general of artillery. He died of dysentery at Taranto in 1803 while serving in Italy. Les Liaisons dangereuses has been repeatedly adapted for stage and screen, notably Stephen Frears's 1988 film Dangerous Liaisons.
