Danse macabre (Saint-Saëns)

Danse macabre, Op. 40
ComposerCamille Saint-Saëns
Year1874 (premiered 1875)
GenreTone poem
Based onPoem by Henri Cazalis

Danse macabre, Op. 40, is a tone poem for orchestra composed by Camille Saint-Saëns in 1874 and premiered in Paris on 24 January 1875. It began in 1872 as a song for voice and piano setting a poem by Henri Cazalis, based on the medieval allegory of the Dance of Death.

Music and programme

According to the legend evoked by the poem, Death appears at midnight on Halloween and summons the dead from their graves to dance to his fiddle. The piece opens with a harp playing the twelve strokes of midnight (the note D repeated twelve times) in 3/4 time. A solo violin, representing Death, plays a theme using the tritone — the "diabolus in musica" — with the instrument's E string tuned down to E-flat (scordatura) to make the interval prominent.

Skeletons dance to a waltz until a cockcrow (represented by the oboe) signals dawn, when the dead return to their graves. The work famously uses a xylophone to imitate rattling bones. Early audiences were unsettled by its grotesque atmosphere; it is now one of Saint-Saëns's most popular works and is frequently used in film, television and Halloween programming.

Arrangements

Saint-Saëns and others arranged the piece for piano and other forces; Franz Liszt made a piano transcription.
