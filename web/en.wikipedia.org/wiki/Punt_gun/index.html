Punt gun

A punt gun is an extremely large shotgun used in the 19th and early 20th centuries for commercial hunting of waterfowl. Mounted in a low hunting boat called a punt, the gun could fire over a pound of shot at once and kill dozens of birds on the water with a single discharge.

Description

Punt guns could exceed 3 metres in length with bore diameters around 5 cm (2 inches). Too heavy to shoulder, they were fixed to the boat, which was aimed at the flock; recoil could drive the punt backwards through the water.

Decline

Market hunting with punt guns devastated waterfowl populations and was outlawed in most US states during the 1860s–1900s and curtailed by federal law including the Migratory Bird Treaty Act of 1918. Small numbers survive in museums and in limited traditional use in the United Kingdom.
