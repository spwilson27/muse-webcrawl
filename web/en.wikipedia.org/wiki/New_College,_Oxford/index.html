New College is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in England. Founded in 1379 by William of Wykeham, Bishop of Winchester, under the full name of the College of St Mary of Winchester in Oxford, it was the first Oxford college to be founded on the now-standard pattern of a large self-contained quadrangle combining chapel, hall, library, and accommodation, and it was paired with Wykeham's school, Winchester College, founded in 1382 as its feeder.

History and buildings

Despite its name, New College is among Oxford's older foundations. Its Great Quad, completed in the 1380s, set the template for collegiate architecture. The chapel is noted for its medieval stained glass, its antechapel reredos, and Jacob Epstein's sculpture of Lazarus; the college also preserves a section of the medieval city wall of Oxford, which it is obliged by agreement with the city to maintain and which is inspected by the Lord Mayor every three years in a ceremonial perambulation. The cloisters and bell tower were used as a filming location for Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire.

Life and reputation

The college is one of Oxford's largest and wealthiest, with roughly 430 undergraduates and 360 graduates. Its choir, directed by the organist, is internationally renowned and has made many recordings of Renaissance and contemporary sacred music. The college's motto is "Manners Makyth Man", Wykeham's own. Notable alumni include the physicist and broadcaster, politicians, judges, and the writer and actor Hugh Grant. Academically New College consistently ranks near the top of the Norrington Table of Oxford colleges.
