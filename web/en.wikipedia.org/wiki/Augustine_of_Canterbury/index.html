Augustine of Canterbury

Saint Augustine of Canterbury
Died: 26 May 604 (604/605), Canterbury
Title: First Archbishop of Canterbury (597–604)
Sent by: Pope Gregory the Great
Feast day: 26 May (Roman Catholic/Anglican)
Venerated in: Roman Catholic, Anglican, Eastern Orthodox

Augustine of Canterbury (died 26 May 604) was a Benedictine monk who became the first Archbishop of Canterbury in 597. Sent by Pope Gregory the Great, he led the Gregorian mission that began the conversion of Anglo-Saxon England to Christianity, and is called the "Apostle to the English".

The Gregorian mission

Augustine, prior of St Andrew's monastery in Rome, set out in 596 with about forty companions. Landing in Kent in 597, he was received by King Æthelberht, whose wife Bertha was already a Christian. Æthelberht converted and was baptised, and gave Augustine a seat in Canterbury, where he founded the church that became Canterbury Cathedral and the abbey of St Peter and St Paul (later St Augustine's Abbey).

Archbishop

Consecrated archbishop, Augustine established sees at Rochester and London (St Paul's) in 604 and attempted — largely unsuccessfully — to bring the existing British (Celtic) churches under Roman authority, a failure recorded by Bede. Gregory's letters guided Augustine's pastoral approach, advising gradual adaptation of pagan temples and festivals.

Death and legacy

Augustine died in 604 and was buried at his abbey in Canterbury; his shrine was later moved within the abbey church. Bede's Ecclesiastical History is the main source for his mission. His feast is kept on 26 May.
