Vuk Karadžić

Vuk Stefanović Karadžić
Born: 6 November 1787, Tršić
Died: 7 February 1864, Vienna
Known for: Serbian language reform

Vuk Stefanović Karadžić (6 November 1787 – 7 February 1864) was a Serbian philologist, anthropologist and linguist. He was one of the most important reformers of the modern Serbian language, and is considered the father of the study of Serbian folklore.

Language reform

Karadžić reformed the Serbian literary language and standardised the Serbian Cyrillic alphabet, following the principle "write as you speak" (piši kao što govoriš). Working in Vienna with the Slovene linguist Jernej Kopitar, he published a Serbian grammar (1814) and the first Serbian Dictionary (1818), which contained over 26,000 words collected from the spoken language. His reformed alphabet has 30 letters, one for each phoneme. The reform was fiercely opposed by the church and conservative writers who favoured the hybrid Slavo-Serbian literary language, but it was officially adopted by Serbia in 1868, four years after his death.

Folklore

Karadžić collected Serbian folk songs, tales and customs, publishing volumes of lyric and epic poetry — including the Kosovo cycle and songs about Prince Marko — that won the admiration of European literary figures such as Jacob Grimm, Goethe and Ranke. He also translated the New Testament into the reformed Serbian vernacular (published 1847).

Legacy

Karadžić's portrait appears on the Serbian 10-dinar banknote, and the Vuk Foundation and numerous schools bear his name. His work underpins the modern standard Serbian, Bosnian, Croatian and Montenegrin languages' shared phonemic spelling principle.
