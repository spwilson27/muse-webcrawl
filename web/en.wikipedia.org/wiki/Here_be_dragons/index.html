Here be dragons

"Here be dragons" (Latin: hic sunt dracones) is a phrase popularly associated with old maps, said to mark dangerous or unexplored territories. In fact, the Latin wording appears on only one surviving early map — the Hunt-Lenox Globe, a small copper globe of about 1503–1511, where it is written near the coast of Southeast Asia.

Cartographic practice

Medieval and early modern mapmakers more often filled unknown regions with drawings of sea monsters, wild beasts, and mythical peoples than with written warnings. Such imagery reflected both genuine uncertainty about distant lands and decorative convention. The popular phrase entered common usage only in the 20th century, as a shorthand for any uncharted or hazardous domain.

Modern usage

Today "here be dragons" is used metaphorically in computing, exploration, and business for parts of a system or territory that are undocumented and best approached with caution.

References: Van Duzer, C., "Sea Monsters on Medieval and Renaissance Maps" (2013); Library of Congress, Hunt-Lenox Globe description.
