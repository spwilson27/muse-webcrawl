Cuban Americans (Spanish: cubanoamericanos) are Americans of Cuban ancestry, including immigrants from Cuba and their descendants. At roughly 2.4 million people, they are one of the largest Hispanic groups in the United States. The largest concentration is in South Florida, particularly Miami and Hialeah, where Cuban culture strongly shapes the region; significant communities also exist in Union City and West New York, New Jersey.

Migration waves

Large-scale migration followed the Cuban Revolution of 1959. The first wave (1959–1962) brought much of Cuba's professional and business class; later movements included the Freedom Flights (1965–1973), the Mariel boatlift of 1980, and the balsero (rafter) crisis of 1994. Under the Cuban Adjustment Act of 1966 and the subsequent "wet foot, dry foot" policy (1995–2017), Cubans who reached US soil received a fast track to permanent residency. A further large wave arrived in the early 2020s amid Cuba's economic crisis.

Culture and politics

Cuban Americans have been prominent in business, medicine, and politics, and the community has historically leaned Republican owing to anti-communist sentiment, though voting patterns have shifted across generations. Miami's Little Havana is the cultural heart of the diaspora.
