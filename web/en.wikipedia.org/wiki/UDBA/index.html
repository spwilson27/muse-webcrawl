UDBA

The UDBA (Uprava državne bezbednosti, "State Security Administration") was the secret police organisation of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia from 1946 to 1991, succeeding OZNA and later reorganised as the SDB. It answered to the federal interior ministry.

Formed1946
Dissolved1991–92
PredecessorOZNA
JurisdictionSFR Yugoslavia

Activities

The UDBA monitored dissidents at home and Yugoslav émigré communities abroad, particularly Croatian, Serbian, Albanian and Macedonian nationalist exiles. It is widely alleged to have carried out assassinations of émigrés in Western Europe, including Croatian nationalists in West Germany — killings later litigated in German courts after Yugoslavia's breakup, notably the 1983 murder of Stjepan Đureković.

Reorganisation

After the 1966 fall of security chief Aleksandar Ranković, accused of abusing power and bugging Tito's residence, the service was decentralised to the republics and renamed the State Security Service (SDB). It dissolved with Yugoslavia in the early 1990s, its republic branches becoming the security agencies of the successor states.
