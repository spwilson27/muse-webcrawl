Gambling in Hong Kong

Gambling in Hong Kong is mostly restricted and heavily regulated by the Government of Hong Kong. Since 1977, the government has issued a limited number of licences to conduct gambling activities. The most common forms of legal gambling are horse racing (regarded as a form of entertainment for most people in Hong Kong), organised by the Hong Kong Jockey Club, and lotteries. Many Hong Kongers also participate in social gambling such as mahjong, which is not regulated as long as it is not organised as a business.

Legal status

The Gambling Ordinance (Cap. 148), enacted in 1977, prohibits all gambling except that which is authorised by the government or licensed under the ordinance. Illegal gambling includes bookmaking with unauthorised bookmakers and operating unlicensed gambling establishments. Gambling debts arising from illegal gambling are not enforceable in Hong Kong.

Hong Kong Jockey Club

The Hong Kong Jockey Club, founded in 1884, holds a monopoly on horse racing, football betting, and lotteries in Hong Kong. It operates the Mark Six lottery and accepts bets on horse races held at the Happy Valley and Sha Tin racecourses. Football betting was legalised in 2003. The Jockey Club is one of the largest taxpayers in Hong Kong and a major donor to charitable causes.

Casinos

There are no legal casinos in Hong Kong. Many residents travel to nearby Macau, where casino gambling is legal, or take part in gambling cruises that sail into international waters.
