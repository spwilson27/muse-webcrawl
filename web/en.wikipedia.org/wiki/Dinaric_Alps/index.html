Dinaric Alps

The Dinaric Alps, also called the Dinarides, are a mountain range in Southern and Southeastern Europe, stretching some 645 kilometres along the eastern coast of the Adriatic Sea from the Julian Alps in the northwest down through Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, and Albania, where they merge with the Šar and Pindus ranges.

The range is named after Mount Dinara (1,831 m) on the border of Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina; its highest peak is Maja Jezercë (2,694 m) in the Albanian Alps (the Accursed Mountains). Geologically the Dinarides are dominated by karst limestone — indeed the scientific term karst derives from the Kras plateau in this region — with dramatic features such as the Postojna and Škocjan caves, deep river canyons, poljes, and disappearing rivers.

The mountains form the watershed between the Adriatic and the Black Sea drainage basins. Their rugged terrain sheltered distinctive cultures and served historically as a frontier between coastal city-states, the Ottoman Empire, and the Habsburg lands. Large areas are protected in national parks such as Durmitor, Paklenica, and the Plitvice Lakes.
