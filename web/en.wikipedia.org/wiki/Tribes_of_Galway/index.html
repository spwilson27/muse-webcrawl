Tribes of Galway

The Tribes of Galway (Irish: Treibheanna na Gaillimhe) were fourteen merchant families who dominated the political, commercial, and social life of the city of Galway in western Ireland between the mid-13th and late 17th centuries.

The families

The fourteen families were: Athy, Blake, Bodkin, Browne, D'Arcy, Deane, Font, French, Joyce, Kirwan, Lynch, Martin, Morris, and Skerrett. They were largely of Norman, Welsh, and English origin, intermarried extensively, and controlled the city's mayorship — the Lynch family alone supplied Galway with dozens of mayors.

History

The label "tribes" was applied by Oliver Cromwell's forces as a term of contempt, which the families defiantly adopted as a badge of honour. Their power declined after the sieges of Galway in 1651–52 and 1691 during the Cromwellian and Williamite wars. The coats of arms of the fourteen tribes are displayed on flags in Eyre Square in Galway. See Galway.
