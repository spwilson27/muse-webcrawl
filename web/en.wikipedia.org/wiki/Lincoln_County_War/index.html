Lincoln County War

The Lincoln County War was an Old West conflict between rival factions in Lincoln County, New Mexico Territory, in 1878. The feud began as a business rivalry between the established mercantile monopoly of Lawrence Murphy and James Dolan and the newer enterprise of John Tunstall and Alexander McSween, and it made Billy the Kid famous.

Background

Murphy and Dolan's firm, known as "the House", controlled trade, banking, and government contracts in the county, with the support of Sheriff William Brady. Tunstall, an English rancher and merchant backed by cattleman John Chisum, opened a competing store and bank in 1876–77.

The war

The conflict turned violent when Tunstall was murdered in February 1878 by a sheriff's posse. His cowboys, deputised as the Regulators and including Billy the Kid, sought revenge, killing Sheriff Brady and others. The fighting culminated in the five-day Battle of Lincoln in July 1878, after which McSween was killed and his house burned. Federal troops intervened, and territorial governor Lew Wallace later issued an amnesty proclamation.

Legacy

The war became a staple of Western legend and has been depicted in numerous books and films, including Young Guns (1988).
