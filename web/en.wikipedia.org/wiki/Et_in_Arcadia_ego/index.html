Et in Arcadia ego

Et in Arcadia ego (also known as Les Bergers d'Arcadie, "The Arcadian Shepherds") is a 1637–38 painting by Nicolas Poussin (1594–1665), the leading painter of the classical French Baroque style. It depicts a pastoral scene with idealized shepherds from classical antiquity clustering around an austere tomb in Arcadia.

ArtistNicolas Poussin
Year1637–1638
MediumOil on canvas
LocationLouvre, Paris

Interpretation

The Latin phrase is a memento mori: "Even in Arcadia, there [am] I", as if spoken by Death personified — even in the paradise of Arcadia, death is present. An earlier version was painted by Guercino around 1618–22, showing a skull. Erwin Panofsky's influential essay examined the shifting meaning of the phrase and the tomb inscription tradition.

Legacy

The painting and phrase have inspired literature, including Evelyn Waugh's Brideshead Revisited, where it gives a chapter its title, and numerous later artistic and philosophical discussions of mortality in an idyllic setting.
