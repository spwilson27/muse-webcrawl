The Academy of St Martin in the Fields (ASMF) is an English chamber orchestra, based in London. Founded in 1958 by the violinist Neville Marriner, it took its name from the church of St Martin-in-the-Fields on Trafalgar Square, where the ensemble gave its first concerts. It is one of the most recorded orchestras in the world, with a discography of more than 500 albums.

History

Marriner assembled the original Academy as a small group of leading London string players performing without a conductor, specialising in Baroque and Classical repertoire played with crisp, modern precision. The orchestra began recording for labels including Argo, L'Oiseau-Lyre, Philips and EMI, and its 1960s and 1970s recordings helped popularise composers such as Vivaldi, Handel and Corelli for a mass audience. Over time the ensemble expanded to symphonic size when required, and Marriner increasingly conducted from the podium; he remained its guiding figure and president until his death in 2016.

The violinist Iona Brown served as artistic director from 1974, and the pianist Murray Perahia became principal guest conductor. In 2011 the American violinist Joshua Bell succeeded Marriner as music director — the second in the orchestra's history — leading the group playing from the concertmaster's chair in the Academy's founding tradition.

Recordings and reputation

The Academy's prolific catalogue includes the best-selling soundtrack to the film Amadeus (1984), which won a Grammy Award, and recordings with soloists from Jacqueline du Pré to Hilary Hahn. Renowned for its polished, bright sound, the orchestra tours extensively worldwide and maintains a partnership with its namesake church for regular London performances, alongside education and community programmes.
