Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays is a compendium of information on mathematical games, written by Elwyn Berlekamp, John Conway, and Richard Guy. It was first published in 1982 in two volumes, and a second edition was published in four volumes from 2001 to 2004. The book is widely regarded as the foundational text of combinatorial game theory, developing the theory of partizan games and introducing concepts such as surreal numbers to a broad mathematical audience.

Contents

The work covers a vast range of games, including Hackenbush, Domineering, Treblecross, Sprouts, Snort, Dawson's Kayles, Green Hackenbush, and many others, analyzing them through the lens of Conway's theory of combinatorial games. In this theory, a game is defined recursively as a pair of sets of games — the options available to the two players, Left and Right — and numbers arise as a special class of games. The book develops the arithmetic of games, introduces the notion of the "value" of a position, and explains mean value theory, atomic weights, and the analysis of sums of games.

The second edition is divided into four volumes: "Winning Ways" proper, and volumes devoted to specific families of games, including "dozens of new games" added since the first edition. Volume topics range over numbers and games, playing with numbers, games ancient and modern, pursuit games, and puzzles.

Background and influence

The book grew out of Conway's On Numbers and Games (1976), which presented the underlying mathematics, and Berlekamp and Guy's interest in applying the theory to concrete games. It is known for its playful style, rich illustrations, and depth of analysis. Winning Ways laid the groundwork for the modern study of combinatorial game theory and inspired subsequent works such as Aaron Siegel's Combinatorial Game Theory and the "Games of No Chance" conference volumes.
