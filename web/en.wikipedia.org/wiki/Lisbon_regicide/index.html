Lisbon regicide

The Lisbon regicide or Lisbon Regicide (Portuguese: Regicídio de Lisboa) was the assassination of King Carlos I of Portugal and his heir-apparent, Luís Filipe, Prince Royal of Portugal, in the Terreiro do Paço in Lisbon on 1 February 1908. The assassins, members of the Portuguese Carbonária (a revolutionary secret society linked to the Portuguese Republican Party), were Manuel Buíça and Alfredo Luís da Costa, who were shot dead at the scene by members of the royal bodyguard.

Background

By 1908 the Portuguese monarchy faced mounting opposition. The republican movement had grown in strength, fuelled by the humiliation of the British Ultimatum of 1890, financial instability, and resentment of the dictatorial government of Prime Minister João Franco, whom King Carlos had appointed in 1906 with powers to govern without parliament. An attempted republican coup in Lisbon on 28 January 1908 had been suppressed only days before the assassination.

The attack

On 1 February 1908 the royal family returned to Lisbon from the Ducal Palace of Vila Viçosa. After disembarking, King Carlos, Queen Amélie, and their two sons travelled by open landau across the Terreiro do Paço. As the carriage crossed the square, shots rang out. Manuel Buíça, a former soldier armed with a Winchester carbine, and Alfredo Costa opened fire on the carriage.

King Carlos was killed instantly. The Prince Royal, Luís Filipe, was mortally wounded and died minutes later; it is often said that for about twenty minutes he was, technically, King of Portugal. The younger prince, Manuel, was wounded in the arm, while Queen Amélie famously defended her sons with a bouquet of flowers. Both assassins were killed at the scene by the royal escort and police.

Aftermath

Manuel II, the younger son, succeeded to the throne. The regicide did not immediately bring about a republic, but it severely undermined the monarchy's legitimacy. Just over two years later, on 5 October 1910, a republican revolution overthrew Manuel II and proclaimed the Portuguese First Republic.
