The Ram Mandir, officially the Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Mandir, is a Hindu temple complex in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India. It is located at the site of Ram Janmabhoomi, held by Hindus to be the birthplace of Rama, the principal deity of Hinduism in his aspect as the hero of the Ramayana. The temple was inaugurated on 22 January 2024 with the consecration (Pran Pratishtha) of the idol of Ram Lalla, attended by Prime Minister Narendra Modi and a large gathering of religious and public figures.

Background

The site was occupied for centuries by the Babri Masjid, a mosque built in 1528–29. Many Hindus believed it stood on the site of an earlier temple marking Rama's birthplace. The dispute over the site fueled one of modern India's most contentious religious and political movements; in December 1992 a mob of Hindu activists demolished the mosque, triggering nationwide riots. After decades of litigation, the Supreme Court of India in 2019 awarded the disputed land for the construction of a temple and directed that alternative land be given for a mosque. The Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Teerth Kshetra trust was formed to oversee construction, and the foundation stone was laid in August 2020.

Architecture

The temple was designed by the Sompura family of temple architects, in the Nagara style of North India, built principally of pink sandstone from Rajasthan with no steel or iron used in the main structure. The complex has three storeys, five pavilions (mandapas), and a main spire (shikhara) about 49 metres high. The principal idol, carved from dark stone by sculptor Arun Yogiraj, depicts Rama as a five-year-old child.

Significance

The temple's opening marked the culmination of a central campaign of the Bharatiya Janata Party and Hindu nationalist movement, and drew tens of millions of visitors in its first months. It also spurred large-scale redevelopment of Ayodhya as a pilgrimage city.
