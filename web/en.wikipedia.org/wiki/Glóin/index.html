Glóin is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. A Dwarf of the line of Durin, he is one of the thirteen Dwarves of Thorin Oakenshield's company in The Hobbit (1937), and the father of Gimli, a member of the Fellowship of the Ring in The Lord of the Rings.

Appearances

In The Hobbit, Glóin joins Thorin's quest to reclaim the Lonely Mountain (Erebor) from the dragon Smaug. He is initially sceptical of Bilbo Baggins as the company's burglar. Like his companions he is captured by trolls, goblins, spiders and Elves in turn, fights in the Battle of the Five Armies, and afterwards settles in Erebor with his share of the treasure.

In The Fellowship of the Ring, Glóin travels to Rivendell with his son Gimli as an emissary of King Dáin II Ironfoot, and reports to the Council of Elrond on ominous messengers from Mordor seeking news of Bilbo and the Ring, and on the war already afflicting the Dwarves and the Men of Dale. Glóin joins the feast at Rivendell and reminisces with Frodo. Tolkien took the name from Glói, a dwarf listed in the Old Norse Dvergatal ("Catalogue of Dwarves") in the Poetic Edda, the source of most of the company's names.
