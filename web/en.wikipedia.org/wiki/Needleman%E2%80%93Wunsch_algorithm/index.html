Needleman–Wunsch algorithm

Needleman–Wunsch algorithm
FieldBioinformatics, computer science
TechniqueDynamic programming
Published1970
AuthorsSaul B. Needleman, Christian D. Wunsch

The Needleman–Wunsch algorithm is an algorithm used in bioinformatics to align protein or nucleotide sequences. Published by Saul B. Needleman and Christian D. Wunsch in 1970, it was one of the first applications of dynamic programming to biological sequence comparison and produces a globally optimal alignment of two sequences under a chosen scoring scheme.

Method

The algorithm constructs a scoring matrix in which each cell represents the best alignment score of the prefixes of the two sequences ending at that position. Cells are filled using a recurrence that considers three possibilities — aligning the two current residues (match or mismatch), or introducing a gap in either sequence — adding the corresponding score from a substitution matrix (such as BLOSUM or PAM) or a gap penalty. After the matrix is complete, an optimal alignment is recovered by tracing back from the bottom-right cell to the top-left.

Applications and variants

Needleman–Wunsch is used for pairwise global alignment of DNA, RNA, and protein sequences. Its running time and memory grow with the product of the sequence lengths, so heuristic methods such as BLAST are preferred for database searches. The Smith–Waterman algorithm adapts the same dynamic-programming framework to find optimal local alignments.
