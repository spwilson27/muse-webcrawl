Kol Yisrael (Hebrew: קול ישראל, "Voice of Israel") was Israel's public domestic and international radio service, operated by the Israel Broadcasting Authority (IBA) from 1948 until the IBA was shut down on 10 May 2017. The name had earlier been used by the underground radio of the Haganah during the Mandate period and the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Kol Yisrael's stations included the news and talk network Reshet Bet, the classical and cultural station Kol HaMusika, the Hebrew music station Reshet Gimel, Arabic-language programming, and broadcasts for immigrants in many languages, as well as external services aimed at listeners abroad, including a shortwave service to the Jewish diaspora and, historically, a Persian service beamed at Iran.

History

Radio in Mandatory Palestine began with the Palestine Broadcasting Service in 1936, broadcasting in English, Arabic and Hebrew (the Hebrew service called Kol Yerushalayim). The Haganah ran clandestine broadcasts under the name Kol Yisrael from 1940 and during the 1948 war. With independence, the service became the state radio under the Prime Minister's Office, and in 1965 the newly created Israel Broadcasting Authority took it over. For decades Kol Yisrael was Israel's dominant source of broadcast news; its hourly news bulletins and the newsreel Yoman HaShavua were fixtures of national life, and the distinctive opening beeps of its news broadcasts became a national signal.

Stations and services

  • Reshet Aleph — general, cultural and educational programming, with Arabic and other minority-language broadcasts.
  • Reshet Bet — news, current affairs and talk; the flagship network.
  • Reshet Gimel — Israeli and Hebrew popular music.
  • Kol HaMusika — classical music and jazz.
  • Reshet Hei / Kol Yisrael International — broadcasts for immigrants and overseas listeners in languages including English, French, Russian, Amharic, Spanish and Persian.
  • 88 FM and Reshet Moreshet — eclectic music and religious-traditional programming respectively.

Its internet and shortwave services were known to overseas listeners as Israel Radio International.

Closure and replacement

After long-running debate over reform of the IBA, the Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation (Kan) was established by law in 2014. Following delays, the IBA was closed abruptly on 10 May 2017 — its final television broadcast ended with staff singing the national anthem — and Kan began broadcasting on 15 May 2017, taking over Kol Yisrael's radio frequencies and several of its stations under new branding, while retaining popular programmes and many of the same journalists and presenters.
