Noam Chomsky

Avram Noam Chomsky (born 7 December 1928) is an American linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist and political activist, sometimes called 'the father of modern linguistics'. His 1957 book Syntactic Structures launched the generative programme: the theory that human language is an innate biological capacity governed by a universal grammar, against the behaviourist view that language is learned habit.

Chomsky's hierarchy of formal grammars underpins theoretical computer science; his later Minimalist Program asks how simple the grammar's design can be. In politics, Chomsky (with Edward Herman) developed the 'propaganda model' of media (Manufacturing Consent, 1988) and has been a prominent critic of US foreign policy since the Vietnam War. Based at MIT from 1955 (and later the University of Arizona), he is among the most cited scholars alive and a lightning rod in both his fields.
