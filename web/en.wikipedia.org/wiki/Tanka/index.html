Tanka

Tanka (Japanese: 短歌, "short poem") is a classical Japanese poetic form consisting of five lines with a total of 31 on (sound units) in a 5-7-5-7-7 pattern. It is the principal form of waka, traditional Japanese court poetry, and enjoyed a continuous history of more than 1,300 years.

History

Tanka appear in Japan's earliest anthologies: the Man'yōshū (c. 759) contains some 4,200 of them. The form dominated the Heian-period imperial anthologies, beginning with the Kokin Wakashū (c. 905), whose preface by Ki no Tsurayuki defined its poetics. Court poets such as Ono no Komachi and later masters including Saigyō and Fujiwara no Teika refined the form. In the Meiji era, poets such as Masaoka Shiki and Yosano Akiko modernised tanka's diction and subject matter; Shiki also promoted the shorter haiku, which had developed from the opening verse of linked poetry.

Structure and practice

The first three lines (5-7-5, the kami-no-ku) typically present an image; the final two (7-7, the shimo-no-ku) respond to or reflect on it. Unlike haiku, tanka freely accommodates personal emotion, love, and everyday life. The form remains widely practised: Japan's Imperial Court still holds the annual Utakai Hajime poetry reading, newspapers run daily tanka columns, and tanka is composed in many languages worldwide.
