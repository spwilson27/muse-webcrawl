Russian invasion of Ukraine

This article is about the invasion that began in February 2022. For the broader conflict since 2014, see Russo-Ukrainian War.

On 24 February 2022, Russia invaded Ukraine in a major escalation of the Russo-Ukrainian War, which had begun in 2014. The invasion, the largest conventional military attack on a European country since the Second World War, has caused tens of thousands of deaths, displaced millions of people, and triggered the largest refugee crisis in Europe since 1945.

Background

Following the Euromaidan protests and the Revolution of Dignity in 2014, Russia annexed Crimea and backed separatist forces in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine. Through 2021, Russia built up a large military presence along Ukraine's borders while demanding that Ukraine never be admitted to NATO and that the alliance roll back its presence in Eastern Europe. Western governments rejected these demands and warned of severe sanctions in response to an invasion.

In a televised address on 21 February 2022, Russian president Vladimir Putin recognised the independence of the self-proclaimed Donetsk and Luhansk people's republics. Three days later he announced a "special military operation" to "demilitarise and denazify" Ukraine, claims widely rejected internationally as baseless pretexts.

Course of the war

Russian forces attacked from the north (toward Kyiv), the northeast (toward Kharkiv), the east (the Donbas), and the south (from Crimea toward Kherson and Mariupol). The initial attempt to seize Kyiv failed amid fierce Ukrainian resistance and logistical problems, and Russia withdrew from northern Ukraine in April 2022, after which evidence of mass killings of civilians emerged in towns such as Bucha.

Russia subsequently concentrated on the east and south, capturing Mariupol after a prolonged siege and annexing, following widely condemned referendums, four Ukrainian oblasts in September 2022. Ukraine launched major counteroffensives that liberated Kharkiv Oblast in September 2022 and Kherson in November 2022. In 2023–2024 the front settled into attritional warfare, with Russia making slow advances in Donetsk Oblast. In August 2024 Ukrainian forces launched a surprise incursion into Russia's Kursk Oblast, and North Korean troops were subsequently deployed to assist Russia there.

International reaction

The invasion was condemned by the United Nations General Assembly and most governments worldwide. Western countries imposed sweeping sanctions on Russia and supplied Ukraine with extensive military, financial, and humanitarian aid. The International Criminal Court issued an arrest warrant for Putin in March 2023 over the alleged unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children. Finland and Sweden abandoned decades of neutrality to join NATO in 2023 and 2024 respectively.

Humanitarian impact

Millions of Ukrainians fled abroad, and millions more were internally displaced. Civilian casualties numbered in the tens of thousands, with total military casualties estimated in the hundreds of thousands on both sides. Attacks on energy infrastructure, the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam in June 2023, and fighting around the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant raised continuing humanitarian and safety concerns.
