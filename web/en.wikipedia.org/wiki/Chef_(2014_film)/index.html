Chef is a 2014 American comedy-drama film written, directed by and starring Jon Favreau. Favreau plays Carl Casper, a celebrated Los Angeles chef who, after a humiliating public clash with a food critic and his restaurant's owner, quits his job and rediscovers his love of cooking — and his family — by running a Cuban food truck.

Plot

Carl Casper, head chef at a Los Angeles restaurant, is stifled by owner Riva (Dustin Hoffman), who insists on the same safe menu. When influential critic Ramsey Michel (Oliver Platt) savages the food, Casper's ill-advised response on Twitter — sent publicly because he does not understand the platform — goes viral, and a meltdown video ends his career. Casper's ex-wife Inez (Sofía Vergara) persuades him to come to Miami, where her first ex-husband (Robert Downey Jr.) gives him a battered food truck. With his young son Percy managing social media and his friend Martin (John Leguizamo) cooking beside him, Casper refits the truck as "El Jefe", selling Cuban sandwiches. Driving back to Los Angeles through New Orleans and Austin, the truck draws crowds wherever Percy's posts announce it. Casper reconciles with his son and, ultimately, with Michel, who invests in a new restaurant for him.

Production

Favreau, who had directed the blockbuster Iron Man films, made Chef as a small, personal independent production; the story is often read as a metaphor for his return to intimate filmmaking. Chef Roy Choi, co-founder of the Kogi food-truck movement, co-produced and trained Favreau in professional cooking technique; all the food prepared on screen was real and was eaten by the cast and crew. The film's soundtrack blends Cuban music, New Orleans brass and Austin blues.

Reception

The film premiered at South by Southwest in March 2014 and grossed over $45 million worldwide on a modest budget. Critics praised its warmth, performances and food photography; some found the third act sentimental. It holds a high approval rating on review aggregators and is frequently credited with popularising food-truck culture and "food porn" cinematography. Favreau later extended the concept in the Netflix series The Chef Show (2019), again with Roy Choi.
