The Metropolitan line, branded as the Met, is a London Underground line serving the City of London end of the line from Aldgate and Baker Street out to the London suburbs of Uxbridge, Watford, Amersham and Chesham in the north-west. It is coloured purple on the Tube map.

History

The line descends from the Metropolitan Railway, which opened on 10 January 1863 between Paddington and Farringdon as the world's first underground passenger railway. It was extended repeatedly into Middlesex, Hertfordshire and Buckinghamshire, driving the growth of "Metro-land" suburbs. In 1933 the railway became part of the London Passenger Transport Board; the far extension to Brill and Verney Junction was later closed.

Services

Metropolitan line trains are the fastest on the Underground, with non-stop running between Finchley Road and Wembley Park on fast services to Amersham and Chesham (the latter a single-track branch served by a 4-car shuttle for many years before through trains were introduced in 2010). Watford branch services terminate at Baker Street. The line shares track with the Circle and Hammersmith & City lines between Aldgate and Baker Street, and with Chiltern Railways between Harrow-on-the-Hill and Amersham.

Rolling stock

The line is operated by S8 Stock trains, introduced from 2010 to replace A Stock, which had served since 1961. S Stock was the first Underground fleet with air-conditioning and walk-through gangways.
