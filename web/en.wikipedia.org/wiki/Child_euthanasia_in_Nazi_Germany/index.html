Overview

Child euthanasia in Nazi Germany was the systematic murder of children with physical or mental disabilities under the Nazi regime. It was organised from 1939 by the 'Reich Committee for the Scientific Registering of Serious Hereditary and Congenital Illnesses', authorised by a secret decree of Adolf Hitler, and is regarded as a precursor and testing ground for the wider 'Aktion T4' programme and the Holocaust. This article describes the history in factual, non-graphic terms.

History

Beginning in 1939, midwives and doctors were required to report newborns and infants with specified disabilities to the Reich Committee. A panel of medical experts decided cases from questionnaires, without examination. Selected children were transferred to special paediatric wards (Kinderfachabteilungen) in clinics across Germany and Austria, where they died through deliberately administered overdoses of sedatives or through starvation. At least 5,000 children were killed; the programme expanded during the war to older children and adolescents, including some in occupied Poland.

Aftermath and remembrance

After 1945, some participating physicians were tried — notably in proceedings connected with the Hadamar and Kaufbeuren institutions and against staff of the Am Spiegelgrund clinic in Vienna — though many perpetrators escaped prosecution or resumed medical careers. The history was for decades little discussed; memorial and research initiatives, including at former ward sites, now document the victims' names and stories, and the programme is a standard subject in historical scholarship on Nazi racial hygiene.
