Weird Tales is an American fantasy and horror fiction pulp magazine founded by J. C. Henneberger and J. M. Lansinger in late 1922; the first issue, dated March 1923, appeared on February 1, 1923. Billed as "The Unique Magazine", it became the most important venue of its era for fantasy and horror short fiction.

History

After early financial difficulties, the magazine came under editor Farnsworth Wright in 1924 and flourished. It launched or sustained the careers of many of the genre's defining writers. H. P. Lovecraft published many of his Cthulhu Mythos stories there; Robert E. Howard created Conan the Barbarian in its pages; and Robert Bloch, Clark Ashton Smith, Seabury Quinn and Tennessee Williams all appeared. The magazine's striking covers, many by Margaret Brundage, became pulp icons.

The original run ended in 1954 after 279 issues. Weird Tales has been revived several times since, and a modern incarnation continues to publish new weird fiction.

Influence

The magazine is credited with shaping the modern genres of horror and sword-and-sorcery fantasy, and its title gave the wider field the term "weird fiction".
