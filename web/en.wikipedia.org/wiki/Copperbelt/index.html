Overview

The Copperbelt is a natural region in Central Africa straddling northern Zambia and the southern Democratic Republic of the Congo, one of the world's richest copper-producing districts, and a major source of cobalt. Its mines — around Zambian towns such as Kitwe, Ndola, Chingola and Mufulira, and Congolese Lubumbashi, Kolwezi and Likasi — lie in a belt of mineralised sedimentary rock (the Central African Copperbelt) several hundred kilometres long.

History

Industrial mining began in the colonial 1920s–30s and drew migrant labour from across the region, creating distinctive mining towns and unions that became politically powerful at independence. Nationalisations in Zambia (ZCCM) and Congo (Gécamines) were followed by privatisations from the 1990s; Chinese investment has expanded strongly since the 2000s, and the belt's cobalt is strategic for batteries.
