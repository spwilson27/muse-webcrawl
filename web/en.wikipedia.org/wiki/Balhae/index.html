Balhae (Korean: 발해; Chinese: 渤海, Bóhǎi) was a multi-ethnic kingdom established in 698 by Dae Joyeong, a former general of Goguryeo, after the fall of that kingdom to the alliance of Tang China and Silla. At its height it covered southern Manchuria, the northern part of the Korean Peninsula, and the present-day Russian Maritime Province (Primorsky Krai).

History

Dae Joyeong, of Goguryeo (or, in some accounts, Sumo Mohe) origin, founded the state — initially called Zhen (Jin) — after defeating a Tang army at the Battle of Tianmenling in 698. His son and successor Dae Muye expanded Balhae's territory and in 732 its navy raided the Tang port of Dengzhou. Under King Seon in the early 9th century, Balhae reached its greatest extent and prosperity, organising its territory into 5 capitals, 15 prefectures and 62 counties on the Tang model, and trading and exchanging embassies with Tang China and Japan.

Balhae's population included descendants of Goguryeo people and a large Mohe (Tungusic) component. Its culture blended Goguryeo, Tang Chinese and indigenous elements; it was praised in Chinese sources as a flourishing "prosperous land east of the sea". Its historiographical identity — Korean, Chinese or Manchurian — has long been disputed between Korean, Chinese and Russian scholarship.

Fall

Weakened by internal decline, Balhae was conquered in 926 by the Khitan under Abaoji, founder of the Liao dynasty. Many Balhae refugees, including its last crown prince Dae Gwang-hyeon, fled south to Goryeo, which absorbed them. Revival attempts such as Later Balhae failed to restore the state.
