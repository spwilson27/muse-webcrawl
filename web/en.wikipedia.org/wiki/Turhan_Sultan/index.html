Turhan Hatice Sultan (Ottoman Turkish: ترخان سلطان, c. 1627 – 4 August 1683) was the Haseki Sultan of the Ottoman Sultan Ibrahim I and the Valide Sultan and regent of the Ottoman Empire as the mother of Mehmed IV. Turhan was prominent for the regency she held during her son's youth, standing as one of the two women in Ottoman history to be officially recognised as regents, alongside her mother-in-law and rival Kösem Sultan. She was a prominent figure during the era known as the Sultanate of Women.

Early life

Turhan, whose original name is unknown, was of Ruthenian origin. She was captured during one of the raids by the Tatars and sold into slavery. When she was about twelve years old she was sent to the Topkapı Palace as a gift to Kösem Sultan, the mother of Sultan Ibrahim. She was trained in the harem and became one of Ibrahim's concubines, giving birth to the future Mehmed IV on 2 January 1642.

Regency

When Ibrahim was deposed in 1648, the six-year-old Mehmed ascended the throne, and effective power passed to his grandmother Kösem Sultan. Kösem's reported plan to depose Mehmed in favour of his half-brother Süleyman led Turhan to act first; Kösem was murdered in September 1651, and Turhan became Valide Sultan and regent of the empire.

As regent, Turhan exercised wide powers, attending meetings of the divan from behind a screen. Faced with fiscal crisis, war with Venice over Crete and the blockade of the Dardanelles, she took the fateful step in 1656 of appointing Köprülü Mehmed Pasha as Grand Vizier, granting him extraordinary authority — an appointment widely seen as ending the period of harem regency and beginning the Köprülü era of reform.

Building works

Turhan completed the Yeni Mosque (New Mosque) in Eminönü, Istanbul, whose construction had been begun by Safiye Sultan decades earlier and left unfinished; it was completed in 1665. She also built two fortresses, Seddülbahir and Kumkale, at the entrance to the Dardanelles to protect the capital against the Venetian fleet, as well as numerous fountains, schools and charitable foundations.

Death

Turhan died on 4 August 1683 in Edirne and was buried in the mausoleum she had built at the Yeni Mosque in Istanbul. She is remembered as the last of the powerful valide sultans of the Sultanate of Women.
