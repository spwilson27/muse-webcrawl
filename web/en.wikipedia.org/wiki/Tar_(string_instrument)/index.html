Tar (string instrument)

The tar (Persian: تار) is a long-necked, waisted lute found throughout Iran, Azerbaijan, Armenia, Georgia, and parts of Central Asia. Its name means "string" in Persian. The tar features a distinctive double-bowl sound box carved from mulberry wood, a membrane (traditionally skin) soundboard, a long fretted neck with tied gut frets, and metal strings plucked with a small brass or horn plectrum (mezrab).

Forms

The Iranian tar usually has five strings (two single and paired courses plus a bass), later six. In the 19th century the Azerbaijani musician Sadigjan redesigned the instrument — enlarging the body, curving the neck, raising the strings, and increasing them to eleven — creating the Azerbaijani tar, central to mugham performance. The craftsmanship and performance art of the Azerbaijani tar was inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2012.

Role in music

The tar is a principal solo and accompaniment instrument in the Persian dastgah and Azerbaijani mugham systems: its tied movable frets allow the quarter-tone intervals of those modal systems, and its powerful, bright tone suits both courtly and folk settings. Noted masters include Mirza Abdollah, Darvish Khan, and (in Azerbaijan) players within the mugham trio format alongside kamancheh and daf/gaval.
