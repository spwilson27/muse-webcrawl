Vanessa Bell (1879–1961)

Vanessa Bell (née Stephen; 30 May 1879 – 7 April 1961) was an English painter and interior designer, a member of the Bloomsbury Group and sister of Virginia Woolf. She is considered one of the major contributors to British portrait painting and landscape art in the 20th century.

Early life

Vanessa Stephen was the eldest daughter of Sir Leslie Stephen and Julia Prinsep Duckworth. She was educated at home and studied art first under Ebenezer Cook, then at Arthur Stockdale Cope's art school, and from 1901 at the Royal Academy Schools. After her father's death in 1904 she moved with her siblings to 46 Gordon Square, Bloomsbury, where the circle of writers, artists and critics that became the Bloomsbury Group began to meet.

Career

In 1907 she married the art critic Clive Bell; with him she had two sons, Julian and Quentin. She founded the Friday Club in 1905 to provide an exhibiting society for younger artists, and with Roger Fry she helped organize the landmark Post-Impressionist exhibitions of 1910 and 1912, which introduced British audiences to Cézanne, Van Gogh, Picasso and Matisse. Her own work of this period, including Studland Beach (c. 1912) and the portrait Iris Tree, moved toward the simplification and abstraction of Post-Impressionism.

In 1913 she co-founded the Omega Workshops with Fry and Duncan Grant, designing textiles, furniture and interiors; she also designed book jackets for all of Virginia Woolf's books published by the Hogarth Press. From 1916 she lived much of the year at Charleston farmhouse in Sussex with Duncan Grant and David Garnett; Charleston, with its rooms painted by Bell and Grant, became the country home of the Bloomsbury circle.

Later life and legacy

Bell continued to paint portraits, still lifes and landscapes until her death at Charleston in 1961. Although her reputation was for many years overshadowed by that of her sister, exhibitions and scholarship from the 1970s onward re-established her as a central figure of British modernism.
