Disinformation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Disinformation is false information deliberately spread to deceive people. It is sometimes confused with misinformation, which is false information spread unintentionally; disinformation, by definition, involves intent to mislead. The word derives from the Russian dezinformatsiya, associated with Soviet intelligence practice, and entered English in the 1950s.

History

Deception in statecraft is ancient, but disinformation as a named, organised practice is associated with the Soviet Union, where the KGB maintained a dedicated department for "active measures" (aktivnyye meropriyatiya) — forged documents, planted stories, and front organisations intended to influence foreign politics. Notorious campaigns included Operation INFEKTION, which spread the false claim that the United States had created the HIV/AIDS virus. Western intelligence services also conducted deception and covert influence campaigns during the Cold War.

Techniques

  • Forgery and fabrication — invented documents, quotes, statistics or events.
  • Amplification — using bot networks, troll farms and sympathetic media to magnify a narrative.
  • Astroturfing — faking grassroots support or outrage.
  • Selective truth and misleading context — real material presented so as to imply a false conclusion ("malinformation" when genuine information is used to harm).
  • Firehose of falsehood — high-volume, multi-channel, rapid and repetitive messaging with little regard for consistency.

Online disinformation

Social media platforms have dramatically lowered the cost of producing and distributing disinformation, including around elections, public health (such as vaccine falsehoods) and armed conflicts. Countermeasures discussed by researchers and policymakers include fact-checking, prebunking and media-literacy education, platform labelling and removal policies, and transparency rules for political advertising. Critics note tensions with free-expression rights and the risk that "disinformation" labelling is itself abused.
