Provinces of Austria

This article is about the historical provinces of Austria. For the modern federal states, see States of Austria.

The provinces of Austria historically referred to the hereditary lands (Erblande) of the House of Habsburg and, more generally, to the crown lands that composed the Austrian half of the Habsburg Monarchy. Over the centuries the term has covered territories both within and far beyond the borders of the present Republic of Austria.

The hereditary lands

The core Habsburg possessions included the Archduchy of Austria (divided into Upper and Lower Austria), the duchies of Styria, Carinthia and Carniola, the Princely County of Tyrol, the County of Gorizia and Gradisca, the city of Trieste, and the Vorarlberg. Together with the Bohemian crown lands (Bohemia, Moravia, Austrian Silesia) and the lands of the Hungarian crown, these formed the Habsburg realm.

Crown lands of Cisleithania

After the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, the Austrian Empire (Cisleithania) was organised into crown lands, each with its own diet and governor: Lower and Upper Austria, Salzburg, Styria, Carinthia, Carniola, Tyrol, Vorarlberg, the Austrian Littoral, Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia, Galicia and Lodomeria, Bukovina and Dalmatia.

Modern usage

Today Austria is a federation of nine states (Bundesländer): Burgenland, Carinthia, Lower Austria, Salzburg, Styria, Tyrol, Upper Austria, Vienna and Vorarlberg. Most of these perpetuate the boundaries of the old crown lands, while Burgenland was created from territory ceded by Hungary in 1921.
