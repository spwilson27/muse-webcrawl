High Wycombe

High Wycombe, historically called Chipping Wycombe or simply Wycombe, is a market town in Buckinghamshire, England. The largest town in the county, it had a population of about 125,000 in the wider built-up area in the early 2020s. It lies about 29 miles (47 km) west-northwest of central London, in the valley of the River Wye at the edge of the Chiltern Hills.

History

Wycombe was long famous for furniture making, especially Windsor chairs, earning it the nickname "the furniture town"; chair-making declined in the later 20th century. The town retains an historic annual ceremony in which the mayor and councillors are publicly weighed—a tradition said to shame officials who grew fat at public expense.

Transport and local features

High Wycombe railway station lies on the Chiltern Main Line to London Marylebone, and the town is served by the M40 motorway. Nearby attractions include West Wycombe Park, the Hellfire Caves, and Hughenden Manor, home of Benjamin Disraeli.
