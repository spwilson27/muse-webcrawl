Epworth Sleepiness Scale

The Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) is a self-administered questionnaire used to measure a person's general level of daytime sleepiness. It was introduced in 1991 by Dr Murray Johns of the Epworth Hospital in Melbourne, Australia, and is one of the most widely used subjective sleepiness scales in clinical practice and research.

Method

Respondents rate, on a scale of 0 (would never doze) to 3 (high chance of dozing), their likelihood of falling asleep in eight everyday situations, such as sitting and reading, watching TV, sitting in a parked car, or lying down in the afternoon. The scores are summed to give a total from 0 to 24. Scores of 10 or below are generally considered normal; 11–14 indicates mild, 15–17 moderate, and 18 or above severe excessive daytime sleepiness, warranting investigation.

Uses and limitations

The ESS is used in screening for sleep disorders such as obstructive sleep apnoea, narcolepsy, and idiopathic hypersomnia, and for monitoring treatment response. Because it is subjective, it may disagree with objective measures such as the Multiple Sleep Latency Test, and scores can be influenced by the respondent's insight and honesty.
