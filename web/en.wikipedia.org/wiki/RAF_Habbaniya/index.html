RAF Habbaniya

RAF Habbaniya
Location:Near Habbaniyah, Al Anbar, Iraq
In use:1936–1959
Operator:Royal Air Force

RAF Habbaniya was a Royal Air Force station in Iraq, built on a plateau between the Euphrates river and Lake Habbaniyah, west of Baghdad. Opened in 1936, it replaced RAF Hinaidi as the main British air base in the country under the Anglo-Iraqi Treaty of 1930, and grew into a large, self-contained base with its own schools, hospital, cinema, and yacht club on the lake.

Anglo-Iraqi War 1941

Habbaniya is best known for the siege of May 1941 during the Anglo-Iraqi War. After Rashid Ali's pro-Axis government seized power, Iraqi forces occupied the plateau above the airfield and demanded the cessation of flying. The base's commander, Air Vice-Marshal H. G. Smart, refused, and on 2 May RAF aircraft — largely obsolete trainers and bombers flown in part by instructors and pupils of No. 4 Flying Training School under Group Captain (later Air Marshal) Savile — attacked the Iraqi positions. Reinforced by the relief column Habforce advancing from Transjordan, the garrison defeated the besiegers and secured the base, a pivotal first step in the British reoccupation of Iraq.

Post-war and closure

After the war, Habbaniya remained the principal RAF station in Iraq, hosting transport and maritime squadrons and serving as a staging post on routes to the Far East. Under a new treaty in 1955 the base was formally transferred to the Iraqi Air Force as part of the Baghdad Pact arrangements, but RAF units remained as tenants until after the 1958 Iraqi revolution; the last British personnel left in 1959. The site subsequently served as an Iraqi military airbase (al-Taqaddum).
