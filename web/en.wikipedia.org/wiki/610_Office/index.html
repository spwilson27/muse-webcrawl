610 Office

The 610 Office was an agency of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the government of the People's Republic of China, established on 10 June 1999 — the date from which it took its name — to coordinate the campaign against the spiritual movement Falun Gong.

Background

In April 1999, some ten thousand Falun Gong practitioners gathered near the Zhongnanhai leadership compound in Beijing to appeal against perceived persecution. On 10 June 1999, the CCP leadership established a special leading group and office to handle the Falun Gong issue, headed at its founding by senior official Li Lanqing. On 20 July 1999 the government formally banned Falun Gong.

Structure and role

The central 610 Office sat above a network of provincial, municipal, and local offices reaching down to workplaces and neighbourhoods. It coordinated public security organs, courts, propaganda departments and other agencies in surveillance, "re-education", and prosecution of practitioners. Because it operated largely outside the normal legal framework, critics and human-rights organisations described it as an extra-legal or secret-police body, and linked it to reports of arbitrary detention, torture, and deaths in custody — allegations the Chinese government denied.

Dissolution

As part of Xi Jinping's 2018 institutional reforms of party and state organs, the central 610 Office was dissolved and its functions absorbed into the Central Political and Legal Affairs Commission and the Ministry of Public Security.
