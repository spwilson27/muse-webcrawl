Mixtape

A mixtape (alternatively mix-tape, mix tape or mixed tape) is a compilation of music, typically from multiple sources, recorded onto a medium. With origins in the 1980s home taping culture, the term later came to describe any compilation of songs recorded onto any audio format, and in hip hop culture it came to denote an independently released collection of songs, often distributed for free as promotion.

History

The emergence of affordable cassette recorders in the 1970s allowed listeners to assemble personal compilations for the first time. Early home taping drew the ire of the recording industry, which ran campaigns such as the British Phonographic Industry's "Home Taping Is Killing Music" slogan of the early 1980s. Despite this, cassette culture flourished: mixtapes were exchanged as gifts, used by suitors as declarations of affection, and traded by mail among fans of underground music.

In hip hop, DJs such as DJ Kool Herc's successors and New York figures including DJ Clue? and Tony Touch sold or gave away tapes of exclusive freestyles and blends. By the 2000s, rappers such as 50 Cent and Lil Wayne built audiences largely through mixtape releases before signing major-label deals. The format migrated to CD and then to digital distribution through sites such as DatPiff, becoming a standard promotional vehicle.

Home taping and personal mixtapes

The homemade mixtape was typically recorded in real time, dubbing songs from records, radio or other tapes in a chosen sequence. Sequencing was widely regarded as an art in itself: the order of songs could build an emotional arc, and the roughly 45-minute sides of a C90 cassette imposed a structure of two "acts." Handwritten or typed track listings and decorated inlay cards were part of the craft, a culture memorably depicted in Nick Hornby's 1995 novel High Fidelity.

Hip hop mixtapes

In hip hop usage, a mixtape is a collection of songs by an artist, often featuring freestyles over other artists' beats, remixes and unreleased material. Because such tapes frequently used uncleared samples, they were traditionally given away free or sold informally, outside the commercial album market. The 2009 release of Drake's So Far Gone and later commercially released "mixtapes" such as If You're Reading This It's Too Late (2015) blurred the line between mixtape and album.
