Succinate dehydrogenase

Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), also known as succinate-coenzyme Q reductase or mitochondrial Complex II, is an enzyme complex of the inner mitochondrial membrane that participates in both the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain. It catalyses the oxidation of succinate to fumarate, reducing FAD to FADH2 and passing electrons to coenzyme Q.

EC number1.3.5.1
SubunitsSDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD
LocationInner mitochondrial membrane
ReactionSuccinate + ubiquinone → fumarate + ubiquinol

Structure

The complex has four subunits: a flavoprotein (SDHA) containing covalently bound FAD, an iron–sulfur protein (SDHB), and two hydrophobic membrane-anchor subunits (SDHC and SDHD) that bind haem b and the ubiquinone site. Uniquely among electron-transport complexes, all of its subunits are encoded in the nuclear genome.

Clinical significance

Inherited mutations in SDH subunit genes cause hereditary paraganglioma and phaeochromocytoma syndromes, as the complex acts as a tumour suppressor; severe deficiency causes Leigh syndrome. SDH inhibition by malonate is a classic tool in biochemical research.
