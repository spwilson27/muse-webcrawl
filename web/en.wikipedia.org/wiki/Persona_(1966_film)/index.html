Persona is a 1966 Swedish psychological drama film written and directed by Ingmar Bergman and starring Bibi Andersson and Liv Ullmann. It is considered one of the greatest films ever made, regularly appearing near the top of critic polls, including the Sight and Sound Greatest Films of All Time list.

Plot

Elisabet Vogler, a celebrated stage actress, suddenly stops speaking during a performance of Electra. At a remote summer house by the sea she is cared for by Alma, a talkative young nurse, who fills the silence with confessions — about an erotic encounter on a beach, an abortion, her fiancé — while Elisabet listens without answering. As the women's identities begin to blur and merge — in one famous shot their faces are fused into one — roles of speaker and listener, patient and tormentor, reverse: Alma forces Elisabet to repeat a single sentence, and an episode of threatened cruelty with boiling water breaks the actress's silence. The film opens and closes with self-reflexive images of the film projector itself, framing the story as an artefact of cinema.

Themes and production

Bergman conceived Persona while recovering from pneumonia; he wrote it in nine weeks and shot it on Fårö and in Stockholm with cinematographer Sven Nykvist. Its themes — identity, doubling, silence, motherhood, art and performance, and the horror of contemporary violence glimpsed on television news — have generated an enormous critical literature. Susan Sontag's 1967 essay on the film helped establish its reputation in the English-speaking world.

Legacy

Persona has influenced filmmakers from David Lynch (Mulholland Drive) to Robert Altman (3 Women). Bergman himself ranked it among his most important works, saying that in Persona — and later Cries and Whispers — he had gone as far as he could go.
