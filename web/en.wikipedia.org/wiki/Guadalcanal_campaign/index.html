The Guadalcanal campaign, also known as the Battle of Guadalcanal and codenamed Operation Watchtower by the United States, was a military campaign fought between 7 August 1942 and 9 February 1943 on and around the island of Guadalcanal in the Pacific theater of World War II. It was the first major land offensive by Allied forces against the Empire of Japan.

On 7 August 1942, Allied forces, predominantly United States Marines, landed on Guadalcanal, Tulagi, and Gavutu in the Solomon Islands, intending to deny the Japanese use of the islands to threaten supply routes between the United States, Australia, and New Zealand, and to secure an airfield then under construction. The Japanese defenders were outnumbered and the landing achieved strategic surprise.

The struggle for Henderson Field

The Allies captured the nearly complete airfield, which they named Henderson Field, and aircraft based there — the so-called "Cactus Air Force" — controlled the skies around the island by day. Japan responded with repeated naval bombardments, night resupply runs known as the "Tokyo Express", and a series of major ground offensives at the Tenaru, Edson's Ridge, and the Matanikau. Control of the surrounding waters seesawed in a string of brutal naval battles, including Savo Island, the Eastern Solomons, Cape Esperance, the Santa Cruz Islands, and the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal.

Outcome

The decisive Allied victory at the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal in November 1942 shifted the initiative permanently. Unable to reinforce or resupply, Japan evacuated its remaining ground forces by early February 1943. Along with the Battle of Midway, Guadalcanal is widely regarded as a turning point of the Pacific War: it marked the transition by the Allies from defensive operations to the strategic offensive that would carry through island-hopping campaigns to Japan itself.

Casualties

Japanese forces lost roughly 19,200 dead on land, along with some 38 warships including two battleships and heavy cruiser and destroyer losses, and around 683 aircraft. Allied losses included about 7,100 dead, 29 ships sunk in the surrounding waters, and some 615 aircraft.
