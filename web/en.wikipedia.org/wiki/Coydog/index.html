Coydog

A coydog is a canid hybrid resulting from a mating between a coyote and a domestic dog. The term is also sometimes applied loosely to the eastern coyote, but true coydogs are the offspring of a coyote and a dog and may be bred in captivity or occur occasionally in the wild where the two populations overlap.

Characteristics

Coydogs generally resemble medium-sized dogs with coyote-like features, including a bushy, downward-carried tail, pointed ears and a narrow snout. Their behaviour is often described as a mix of the two parents: they may inherit the coyote's shyness and wariness along with the dog's greater tolerance of humans. Breeding cycles can be a management problem, because male coydogs do not follow the same seasonal reproductive pattern as male coyotes, and hybrid females have a shifted oestrus cycle, which can complicate reproduction in the wild.

In the wild

Hybridisation between dogs and coyotes in the wild is considered relatively uncommon, partly because coyotes are strongly territorial and often aggressive toward dogs, and partly because their breeding seasons do not fully align. Genetic studies of the eastern coyote show it carries dog and wolf ancestry from past hybridisation events, but these populations are distinct from first-generation coydogs. Coydogs kept as pets are controversial and are regulated or prohibited in some jurisdictions because of concerns about unpredictability and rabies control.
