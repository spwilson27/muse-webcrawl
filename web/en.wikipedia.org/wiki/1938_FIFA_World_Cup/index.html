The 1938 FIFA World Cup was the third edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in France from 4 to 19 June 1938. Italy successfully defended the title they had won in 1934, defeating Hungary 4–2 in the final at the Stade Olympique Yves-du-Manoir in Colombes, Paris. Italy thus became the first country to retain the World Cup, and manager Vittorio Pozzo remains the only coach to win two World Cups.

Background

FIFA's decision to stage consecutive tournaments in Europe angered South America: Uruguay boycotted again and Argentina withdrew after their bid to host was rejected, leaving Brazil as the only South American entrant. Austria qualified but withdrew after the Anschluss annexed the country into Germany in March 1938; some Austrian players joined the German squad, which was surprisingly eliminated by Switzerland in the first round after a replay. The tournament retained the straight knockout format of 1934, and the Dutch East Indies (now Indonesia) became the first Asian team to appear at a World Cup.

Tournament summary

In the quarter-finals, Brazil beat Czechoslovakia 2–1 in a replay of the violent "Battle of Bordeaux", in which three players were sent off and several injured. Italy defeated hosts France 3–1, Hungary comfortably beat Switzerland 2–0 in their replay, and Sweden thrashed Cuba 8–0. Italy then beat Brazil 2–1 in the semi-final in Marseille — Brazil controversially rested their star striker Leônidas for the final — while Hungary overwhelmed Sweden 5–1.

Final

Italy led 3–1 at half-time through two goals by Gino Colaussi and one by Silvio Piola, with Pál Titkos replying for Hungary. György Sárosi pulled a goal back in the 70th minute, but Piola scored again to complete a 4–2 win. Italian captain Giuseppe Meazza received the trophy. The next World Cup was not held until 1950 because of the Second World War; Italy remained world champions for twelve years.
