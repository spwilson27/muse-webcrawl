Overview

Sargon of Akkad (Akkadian: Šarru-ukīn, "legitimate king") was the first ruler of the Akkadian Empire, reigning for 56 years by tradition around 2334–2279 BC. Rising — in later legend from a cupbearer's post at the court of Ur-Zababa of Kish — he conquered the Sumerian city-states of Mesopotamia (defeating Lugal-zage-si of Umma) and forged history's first durable multi-ethnic empire, reaching toward the Mediterranean, Anatolia and Elam.

Legacy

Sargon installed his daughter Enheduanna as high priestess at Ur — she is the first named author in history. Akkadian became the diplomatic lingua franca of the Near East for a millennium, and Sargon's legend (including a birth story later echoed in the Moses narrative) made him the archetypal empire-builder for Mesopotamian kings, down to the Neo-Assyrian Sargon II who took his name two millennia later. His capital, Akkad, has never been located.
