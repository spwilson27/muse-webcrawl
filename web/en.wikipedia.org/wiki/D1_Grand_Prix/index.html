D1 Grand Prix

CategoryDrifting (motorsport)
CountryJapan
Founded2000 (first events); series from 2001

The D1 Grand Prix is a professional drifting competition series in Japan, widely credited with transforming drifting from an underground street pastime into a recognised motorsport. It was created by Daijiro Inada, founder of the tuning magazine Option, with the "Drift King" Keiichi Tsuchiya serving as chief judge. After demonstration events in 2000, the championship began in 2001.

Format

Events consist of solo qualifying runs followed by tsuiso—tandem battles in which two cars drift side by side, with judges scoring line, angle, speed, and style. A machine-assisted judging system (the D1 Original Scoring System, using GPS and sensor data) was introduced in the 2010s to supplement human judging. Competitions are held at circuits such as Ebisu Minami, Odaiba, and Fuji Speedway, favouring rear-wheel-drive Japanese performance cars like the Nissan Silvia, Toyota AE86 and Chaser, and Mazda RX-7.

Influence

The D1 Grand Prix inspired professional drifting series worldwide, including Formula D in the United States and competitions in Europe, and helped popularise drifting culture through video games and media. Its exhibitions abroad in the early 2000s, including events at Irwindale Speedway in California, were landmark moments in the sport's international growth.
