The California State Assembly is the lower house of the California State Legislature, the upper house being the California State Senate. The Assembly has 80 members, each representing a district of roughly equal population — approximately 490,000 residents per district, making them the largest-population state legislative districts in the United States. Members are elected every two years.

Composition and terms

Assembly members serve two-year terms. Since the passage of Proposition 28 in 2012, legislators may serve a maximum of 12 years in total in the state legislature, in either house or a combination of both. The Assembly is presided over by the Speaker, one of the most powerful offices in California politics, who controls committee assignments and the flow of legislation. The chamber's motto, displayed in the Assembly, is Legislatorum est justas leges condere ("It is the duty of legislators to pass just laws").

History

The Assembly first met in 1849–1850 at San Jose, before the capital moved permanently to Sacramento. The chamber has had 80 members since 1862. In 1990 voters imposed term limits (Proposition 140), later modified by Proposition 28. The Assembly chamber in the Capitol is decorated in green, in the tradition of the British House of Commons.

Functions

Along with the Senate, the Assembly passes bills, adopts the state budget, and may propose constitutional amendments and override gubernatorial vetoes by a two-thirds vote. Revenue bills are traditionally introduced in the Assembly.
