Soberanía National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Soberanía) is a national park in Panama located along the eastern bank of the Panama Canal, in the provinces of Panamá and Colón. Extending roughly 25 kilometres north of Panama City, it protects one of the most accessible areas of tropical rainforest in the country and forms part of the corridor of protected lands guarding the Canal watershed.

History

The park was created by executive decree on May 27, 1980. Much of its forest had previously been protected as part of the former Panama Canal Zone, where restrictions on settlement and logging preserved large tracts of lowland tropical forest close to the canal. After the Torrijos–Carter Treaties, the lands were transferred to Panama, and the park system was established to maintain forest cover essential to the canal's water supply.

Ecology

Soberanía protects lowland semi-deciduous tropical forest drained by tributaries of the Chagres River and Gatun Lake. It is renowned among ornithologists: more than 500 bird species have been recorded within the park, a remarkable total that has made it one of the most celebrated birding sites in the Americas. The famous Pipeline Road (Camino del Oleoducto), a maintenance track built during the Second World War, runs some 17 kilometres through the forest and is considered one of the finest birdwatching trails in the world — a single morning's count along it may exceed 300 species.

Mammals include mantled howler monkeys, white-faced capuchins, tamarins, three-toed sloths, coatis, agoutis, ocelots and, rarely, jaguars. The park also harbours harpy eagles, the national bird of Panama, along with hundreds of butterfly species and a rich amphibian fauna.

Access and trails

Besides the Pipeline Road, popular routes include the Plantation Road trail, following an old cocoa plantation route, the Camino de Cruces, a surviving stretch of the colonial Spanish cobblestone road that once crossed the isthmus, and the Panama Rainforest Discovery Center, which operates a canopy observation tower near Pipeline Road. The park lies within the broader complex of protected areas that includes Chagres National Park and Camino de Cruces National Park.
