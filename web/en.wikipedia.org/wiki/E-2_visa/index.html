E-2 visa

E-2 visa
TypeNonimmigrant visa
PurposeTreaty investment in a U.S. enterprise
EligibilityNationals of treaty countries who invest a substantial amount of capital in a U.S. business
Period of stayUp to two years initially; renewable in two-year increments
AuthorityImmigration and Nationality Act §101(a)(15)(E)

The E-2 visa is a nonimmigrant visa classification in the United States that allows a national of a country with which the United States maintains a treaty of commerce and navigation to be admitted to the United States when investing a substantial amount of capital in a U.S. business. Certain employees of such a person or of a qualifying organization may also be eligible for the classification.

The E-2 category derives from section 101(a)(15)(E)(ii) of the Immigration and Nationality Act. It is one of two "E" treaty-based classifications, the other being the E-1 treaty trader visa.

Eligibility requirements

To qualify for E-2 classification, the treaty investor must:

  • Be a national of a country with which the United States maintains a treaty of commerce and navigation;
  • Have invested, or be actively in the process of investing, a substantial amount of capital in a bona fide enterprise in the United States; and
  • Be seeking to enter the United States solely to develop and direct the investment enterprise, shown by owning at least 50 percent of the enterprise or possessing operational control through a managerial position or other corporate device.

Substantial investment

There is no fixed minimum dollar amount for the investment. Adjudicators apply a proportionality test, weighing the amount invested against the total cost of establishing or purchasing the enterprise: the lower the cost of the enterprise, the higher, proportionately, the investment must be. The invested funds must be "at risk" in the commercial sense and irrevocably committed to the enterprise. The enterprise must also be more than marginal — it must have a significant economic impact, generate enough income to support more than just the investor and the investor's family, or make a significant economic contribution.

Period of stay and renewals

Qualified treaty investors and employees are allowed a maximum initial stay of two years. Requests for extension of stay may be granted in increments of up to two years each, and there is no limit to the number of extensions an E-2 nonimmigrant may be granted, provided the investor continues to meet the requirements. All E-2 nonimmigrants, however, must maintain an intention to depart the United States when their status expires or is terminated.

Dependents

Spouses and unmarried children under 21 years of age may accompany or follow to join E-2 visa holders in E-2 dependent status. Spouses of E-2 workers are considered employment-authorized incident to their status.

Treaty countries

The United States maintains qualifying treaties with dozens of countries, including the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Japan, South Korea, Australia (whose nationals use the related E-3 visa for specialty occupations), Canada, and Mexico. The list of treaty countries is maintained by the U.S. Department of State and changes from time to time as treaties enter into force or are terminated.
