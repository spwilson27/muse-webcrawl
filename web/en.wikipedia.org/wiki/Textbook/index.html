Textbook

A textbook is a book containing a comprehensive compilation of content in a branch of study, produced to meet the needs of teachers and learners in schools and universities. Textbooks are usually published by specialist publishers and are distinguished from trade books by their structured pedagogy, exercises, and alignment with curricula and examinations.

History

Standard instructional texts date back to antiquity — Euclid's Elements served as a geometry textbook for two millennia. The printing press enabled mass-produced schoolbooks such as spellers, primers, and readers; in the United States the McGuffey Readers sold over 100 million copies in the 19th century. Modern K-12 textbook adoption in large U.S. states such as Texas and California has historically influenced content nationwide.

Costs and open textbooks

College textbook prices have risen much faster than general inflation, prompting criticism of frequent new editions and bundled access codes, and encouraging used-book markets, rentals, and open educational resources. Open textbooks — freely licensed texts from projects such as OpenStax — have been widely adopted as a lower-cost alternative.

Digital formats

Textbooks are increasingly delivered as e-books and interactive digital platforms with embedded quizzes, videos, and adaptive learning features, changing how publishers sell and how students access course materials.
