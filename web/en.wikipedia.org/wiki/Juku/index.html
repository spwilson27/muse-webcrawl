Juku

CountryJapan
TypePrivate cram schools
PurposeSupplemental education, examination preparation

Juku (Japanese: 学習塾, gakušū juku) are private, fee-paying cram schools in Japan that offer supplementary classes outside regular school hours. They are a major part of the Japanese education landscape, attended by a large share of elementary and secondary students.

Overview

Juku range from small neighborhood operations run by a single teacher to large national chains with hundreds of branches. Classes typically focus on preparing students for competitive entrance examinations for junior high schools, high schools and universities, though many also provide remedial help or enrichment in subjects such as mathematics, English and Japanese.

History and scale

The juku industry expanded rapidly in the postwar decades as competition for places at prestigious schools intensified. At its peak, a majority of urban middle school students attended some form of juku, and the sector came to represent a significant household expense.

Criticism

Critics argue that juku widen educational inequality, since wealthier families can afford more tutoring, and contribute to long study hours for children. Supporters counter that they compensate for shortcomings of public schooling and offer individualized instruction.
