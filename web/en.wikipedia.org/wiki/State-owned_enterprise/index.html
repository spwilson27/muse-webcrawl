State-owned enterprise

[Image: a state-owned power station with cooling towers]

A state-owned enterprise (SOE) is a business owned wholly or largely by a government, created to pursue commercial and public objectives together. SOEs dominate strategic sectors in many economies — energy, railways, defence, telecoms, banking — and are the main corporate form in state-led systems such as China's, where firms like State Grid, Sinopec and the big state banks are among the world's largest companies, supervised by SASAC under the authority of the Communist Party of China.

Economics and reform

Arguments for state ownership include natural Monopoly, national security, industrial strategy and providing services markets won't; arguments against include soft budget constraints (losses absorbed by the state — Kornai's phrase), weak incentives, political interference and crowding out private firms. Waves of privatisation (Britain in the 1980s, post-communist transitions in the 1990s) shrank the sector in the West, where remaining SOEs (rail infrastructure, postal services, sovereign wealth funds' holdings) are often corporatised at arm's length. Their governance — boards, listing minority stakes, OECD guidelines — is a major applied topic in Public finance and corporate governance.
