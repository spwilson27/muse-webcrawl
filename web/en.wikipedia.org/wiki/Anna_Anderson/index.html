Anna Anderson

Anna Anderson (16 December 1896 – 12 February 1984) was the best known of the many impostors who claimed to be Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna of Russia, the youngest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II, who was murdered with her family by the Bolsheviks in Ekaterinburg in 1918. Anderson's claim attracted international attention for over sixty years and generated lawsuits, books, and the 1956 film Anastasia.

The claim

In February 1920 an unidentified young woman was pulled from a Berlin canal after a suicide attempt and confined to a mental hospital as "Fräulein Unbekannt" ("Miss Unknown"). From 1922 she claimed to be Anastasia, saying she had survived the execution and escaped with the help of a guard. She displayed scars, spoke of details of the imperial family's life, and convinced some émigré acquaintances and relatives, though the surviving Romanov family and Anastasia's tutors rejected her. A German legal case over her identity, begun in 1938, ended in 1970 with the court ruling her claim "neither established nor refuted".

Later life and identification

Anderson spent much of her life in Germany and, from 1968, in Charlottesville, Virginia, where she married historian Jack Manahan and lived in eccentricity as "Anastasia Manahan". She died in 1984. In 1994, DNA testing of preserved tissue samples showed her mitochondrial DNA did not match that of the Romanovs' maternal relatives but did match a great-nephew of Franziska Schanzkowska, a Polish factory worker with a history of mental illness who had disappeared in 1920—confirming that Anderson was Schanzkowska. The 2007 discovery and 2009 DNA confirmation of the remains of all seven Romanov family members closed the question of Anastasia's survival.
