Narkomfin Building

The Narkomfin Building (Russian: Дом Наркомфина) is a block of flats in Moscow, Russia, designed by Moisei Ginzburg and Ignaty Milinis and built between 1928 and 1930 for employees of the People's Commissariat of Finance (Narkomfin). It is a celebrated monument of Soviet Constructivist architecture and of the experimental communal housing programme of the 1920s.

Design

Raised on pilotis, the long residential block contains "living cells" — compact split-level duplex apartments, some of the types F and K developed by Ginzburg's OSA group research section — connected by wide access galleries intended as social condensers. Private kitchens and bathrooms were minimised in favour of communal facilities in a linked annexe: a canteen, gymnasium, library, laundry and kindergarten, topped by a roof garden and solarium. The scheme embodied the ideology of the "transitional" house-commune, designed to transform everyday life (byt) toward collectivism.

Later history

Under Stalin, communal housing fell from favour; the building was subdivided and altered, its communal block repurposed, and it decayed over decades despite listing as a cultural monument. After long neglect, a major restoration led by Alexei Ginzburg, the architect's grandson, was completed around 2020, returning the block largely to its original appearance as apartments and a hotel.
