Pullback (differential geometry)

In mathematics, particularly in differential geometry, the pullback is a natural operation that transports geometric objects defined on one manifold back to another along a smooth map. If φ : M → N is a smooth map between smooth manifolds, the pullback by φ converts functions, differential forms, and tensors on N into corresponding objects on M. It is the contravariant counterpart of the pushforward.

Pullback of functions

For a smooth function f : N → R, the pullback is simply the composition:

φ*f = f ∘ φ : M → R.

Pullback of differential forms

For a differential form ω on N, the pullback φ*ω is the form on M defined pointwise by

(φ*ω)p(v1, …, vk) = ωφ(p)(dφpv1, …, dφpvk),

where dφ is the differential (pushforward) of φ. The pullback has three fundamental properties: it is linear, it respects the wedge product (φ*(ω ∧ η) = φ*ω ∧ φ*η), and it commutes with the exterior derivative (φ*(dω) = d(φ*ω)). The last property makes the pullback a chain map between de Rham complexes, inducing maps on de Rham cohomology.

Pullback of vector bundles

Given a vector bundle E → N and a map φ : M → N, the pullback bundle φ*E → M has fiber over p equal to the fiber of E over φ(p). The pullback of the tangent bundle along an immersion recovers, in the case of the inclusion of a submanifold, the ambient tangent vectors restricted to the submanifold.

Functoriality

Pullbacks reverse the direction of composition: (ψ ∘ φ)* = φ* ∘ ψ*. Together with the identity pullback id* = id, this says that pullback is a functor from the category of manifolds (with a fixed target) to vector spaces or bundles, a fact used throughout topology and geometry.
