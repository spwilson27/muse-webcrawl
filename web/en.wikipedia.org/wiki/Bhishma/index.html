Bhishma (born Devavrata) is one of the most powerful and revered characters of the Indian epic Mahabharata. The son of King Shantanu and the river goddess Ganga, he is the grand-uncle and patriarch of both the Pandavas and the Kauravas.

The terrible vow

To enable his father to marry the fisherwoman Satyavati, Devavrata vowed lifelong celibacy and renounced his claim to the throne — a "terrible vow" (bhishma pratijna) from which he took his name. Blessed by his father with the boon of choosing the time of his own death, he served Hastinapura as regent and counsellor for generations.

Kurukshetra War

Bound by duty to the throne, Bhishma commanded the Kaurava army for the first ten days of the war, though his sympathies were divided. He fell when Arjuna, shielded by Shikhandi (whom Bhishma would not fight), pierced him with arrows; lying on a bed of arrows, he discoursed on dharma in the Shanti Parva and died after the solstice, at a time of his choosing.
