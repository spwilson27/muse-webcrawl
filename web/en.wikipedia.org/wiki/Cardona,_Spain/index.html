Cardona, Spain

Cardona is a town and municipality in the comarca of Bages, in the province of Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain. It is situated on a hill almost surrounded by the river Cardener, a tributary of the Llobregat, about 90 km northwest of Barcelona.

History

Cardona is best known for its imposing castle, the Castell de Cardona, built from 886 by Wilfred the Hairy and home to the immensely powerful Dukes of Cardona, known as the "kings without crowns" for their wealth and influence in the Crown of Aragon. Nearby lies a great salt mountain, the Muntanya de Sal, exploited since Roman times; the salt deposits made Cardona's lords rich and the town an important trading centre. The collegiate church of Sant Vicenç, within the castle precinct, is a fine example of Catalan Romanesque architecture.

The castle

The fortress was the last in Catalonia to surrender to Bourbon troops in the War of the Spanish Succession, holding out until 18 September 1714, a week after Barcelona itself had fallen. Today part of the castle operates as a parador (state-run hotel).
