Walter Felsenstein

Walter Felsenstein (30 May 1901 – 8 October 1975) was an Austrian theatre and opera director. He was intendant of the Komische Oper Berlin from its founding in 1947 until his death, where he revolutionised the performance of opera by insisting that productions serve the dramatic text and music equally, a concept that came to be known as "musical theatre" realism.

Career

Felsenstein began as an actor and director in Lübeck, Mannheim, and other German theatres, and worked in Frankfurt and Zurich during the Nazi period. Invited to East Berlin in 1947, he founded the Komische Oper in the former Metropol-Theater. His productions — including The Bartered Bride, Madama Butterfly, and The Cunning Little Vixen — were performed in German translation with meticulously rehearsed ensemble acting.

Legacy

Felsenstein's demands that singers act truthfully and that every stage action be justified by the score profoundly influenced opera directors worldwide, including his successors Harry Kupfer and, indirectly, modern directors' theatre.
