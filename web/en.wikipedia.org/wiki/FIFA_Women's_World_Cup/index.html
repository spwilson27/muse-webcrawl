The FIFA Women's World Cup is an international association football competition contested by the senior women's national teams of FIFA's member associations. It has been held every four years since the inaugural tournament in China in 1991, one year after the corresponding men's edition, and is the most important tournament in women's football.

The United States won the first tournament and remains the most successful team with four titles; its 1999 home triumph — Brandi Chastain's winning penalty before 90,185 at the Rose Bowl — was a landmark for women's sport. Germany (2), Norway, Japan and Spain have also won. The 2023 tournament in Australia and New Zealand expanded to 32 teams, drew record crowds of nearly two million, and was won by Spain, who beat England 1–0 in the Sydney final through Olga Carmona's goal. Brazil will host in 2027.
