IAS 39

Former International Accounting Standard on financial instruments

IAS 39 measurement categories

IAS 39: Financial Instruments: Recognition and Measurement was an International Accounting Standard issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) setting out requirements for recognising and measuring financial assets, financial liabilities and some contracts to buy or sell non-financial items. It was largely replaced by IFRS 9 for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018, though its hedge-accounting provisions could continue to be applied in certain cases.

Key features

IAS 39 classified financial assets into categories including held-to-maturity investments, loans and receivables, available-for-sale assets, and assets at fair value through profit or loss, with different measurement bases for each. It also contained detailed rules on derivatives, impairment (using an incurred-loss model) and hedge accounting, including fair value hedges, cash flow hedges and hedges of net investments in foreign operations.

Criticism and replacement

The standard was frequently criticised as complex and rules-based, and its incurred-loss impairment model was blamed during the 2007–08 financial crisis for delaying the recognition of credit losses ("too little, too late"). IFRS 9 replaced it with a classification based on business model and cash-flow characteristics and an expected-credit-loss impairment model.
