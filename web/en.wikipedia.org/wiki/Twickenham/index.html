Twickenham is a suburban district of south-west London, England, on the River Thames, about 15 km (9.3 miles) west-southwest of Charing Cross. Historically part of Middlesex, it is now the administrative centre of the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames. Twickenham is world-famous as the home of Twickenham Stadium, the largest rugby union stadium in the world and the headquarters of English rugby.

History

The name probably derives from "Twicca's homestead" or a bend ("ham") between two rivers. In the eighteenth century Twickenham became a fashionable riverside retreat for aristocrats and men of letters. Alexander Pope built his celebrated villa and grotto here, and Horace Walpole created Strawberry Hill, the pioneering Gothic Revival house that launched a fashion in architecture. Other great houses include Marble Hill House, York House and Orleans House.

Twickenham Stadium

The Rugby Football Union bought the site — then a market garden nicknamed "the Cabbage Patch" — in 1907, and the first international was played there in 1910. Rebuilt repeatedly, the stadium now holds 82,000 spectators, hosts England's home matches in the Six Nations Championship, and staged the finals of the 1991 and 2015 Rugby World Cups. It also houses the World Rugby Museum and is a major concert venue.

Features

The riverside at Twickenham features Eel Pie Island, famous in the 1960s for its rhythm-and-blues hotel venue, reached by a footbridge from the Embankment. Twickenham railway station provides services to London Waterloo, and the town is the home of St Mary's University. Local areas include St Margarets, Whitton and Strawberry Hill.
