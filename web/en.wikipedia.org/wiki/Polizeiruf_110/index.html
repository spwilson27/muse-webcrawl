Polizeiruf 110 ("Police Call 110") is a German television crime drama series. First broadcast in the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) in 1971, it was originally intended as a counterpart and competitor to the West German series Tatort. After German reunification, the series survived the dissolution of East German television and was taken over by ARD, where it continues to the present day alongside Tatort.

History

The first episode, "Der Fall Lisa Murnau", aired on 27 June 1971 on Deutscher Fernsehfunk (DFF). The title refers to 110, the police emergency telephone number in East Germany (retained in unified Germany). Unlike Western crime series of the period, East German Polizeiruf episodes often devoted considerable attention to the social background of crimes and the lives of offenders, reflecting the officially encouraged view that crime in a socialist society arose from residual social causes.

Format

Like Tatort, the modern series is produced by regional ARD broadcasters, each with its own team of investigators and setting. Well-known teams have been based in cities including Munich, Berlin, Rostock, Halle, Potsdam, Magdeburg and Wrocław (a German-Polish team). Episodes run about 90 minutes and are shown on Sunday evenings on Das Erste.

Notable detectives

In the GDR era, popular investigators included Oberleutnant Peter Fuchs, played by Peter Borgelt, who appeared in dozens of episodes. In the unified series, long-serving teams have included Schmücke and Schneider in Halle, played by Jaecki Schwarz and Wolfgang Winkler, and Bukow and König in Rostock, played by Charly Hübner and Anneke Kim Sarnau.
