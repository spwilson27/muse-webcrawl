Clarice M. Starling is a fictional character created by American novelist Thomas Harris. She is the protagonist of the novels The Silence of the Lambs (1988) and Hannibal (1999), and the films adapted from them. An FBI trainee and later special agent, Starling is best known for her interviews with the imprisoned serial killer Dr. Hannibal Lecter, whose psychological insights help her track the killer "Buffalo Bill." The American Film Institute ranked her, as portrayed by Jodie Foster, the sixth-greatest heroine in American cinema history.

Character biography

Starling was raised in West Virginia, the daughter of a town marshal who was killed in the line of duty when she was a child. Orphaned, she was sent to live with relatives on a Montana ranch, from which she ran away after witnessing the slaughter of spring lambs — an experience that haunts her and gives The Silence of the Lambs its title. She graduated with honors from the University of Virginia with degrees in psychology and criminology, then joined the FBI Academy at Quantico.

In The Silence of the Lambs

Assigned by Behavioral Science chief Jack Crawford to interview Hannibal Lecter about the Buffalo Bill murders, Starling trades personal disclosures for Lecter's cryptic clues — his famous quid pro quo. Her investigation leads her to the killer's house in Belvedere, Ohio, where she kills Buffalo Bill and rescues his captive, Catherine Martin. The case makes her a celebrated new agent.

In Hannibal and beyond

In Hannibal, set seven years later, Starling's career has stalled after a botched drug raid. Drawn back into Lecter's orbit by the vengeful Mason Verger, she pursues Lecter to Florence and back to the United States. The novel's controversial ending, in which she becomes Lecter's companion, divided readers; the 2001 film altered it. Starling returned as the lead of the 2021 CBS television series Clarice, portrayed by Rebecca Breeds and set a year after the events of The Silence of the Lambs.
