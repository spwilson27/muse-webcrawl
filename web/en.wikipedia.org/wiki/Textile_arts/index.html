Textile arts

A woven tapestry, one of the historic textile arts (placeholder)

The textile arts are arts and crafts that use plant, animal, or synthetic fibres to construct practical or decorative objects. Textiles are among the oldest human technologies — fragments of woven flax date back tens of thousands of years — and textile work has been central to clothing, shelter, trade, and artistic expression in nearly every culture.

Techniques

Major techniques include weaving (interlacing warp and weft on a loom, producing tapestries, carpets, and cloth), knitting and crochet (building fabric from interlocking loops of yarn), embroidery (decorating fabric with needle and thread), quilting and appliqué, felting (matting fibres without weaving, likely the earliest textile method), braiding and macramé, lacemaking, and surface design such as dyeing (including tie-dye, batik, and ikat), printing, and painting on cloth. Materials range from wool, linen, cotton, and silk to modern synthetics and recycled media.

History and significance

Textiles were luxury goods and stores of value — from medieval Flemish tapestries and Persian carpets to West African kente cloth and Andean weavings, which encoded status and cosmology. The mechanisation of spinning and weaving was the leading edge of the Industrial Revolution. In the 20th and 21st centuries, fibre art emerged as a fine-art movement, and textile traditions are studied for what they reveal about trade, gender, and technology; because fibres decay, surviving ancient textiles are rare and precious to archaeology.
