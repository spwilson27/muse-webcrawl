Semper Paratus (march)

"Semper Paratus" ("Always Ready") is the official march of the United States Coast Guard. The words and music were written in 1927 by Captain Francis Saltus Van Boskerck, USCG. It shares its title with the Coast Guard's motto, Semper Paratus.

History

Van Boskerck, a Coast Guard officer known as the service's musical chronicler, wrote the first verse and chorus in 1922 while stationed in the Aleutian Islands aboard the cutter Yamacraw's district; he composed the melody on a piano in a public health station in Unalaska. The remaining verses were written in 1927 in Savannah, Georgia, where he commanded the cutter Yamacraw. Van Boskerck presented the song to the Commandant in Washington, and it was enthusiastically adopted; the Coast Guard Band adopted it as the service's official song, and the sheet music was arranged and published for band.

Lyrics

The chorus proclaims: "We're always ready for the call, we place our trust in Thee. / Through surf and storm and howling gale, high shall our purpose be. / 'Semper Paratus' is our guide, our fame, our glory too. / To fight to save or fight and die! Aye! Coast Guard, we are for you." The verses recall the service's history, including the Revenue Cutter Service and the surfmen of the Life-Saving Service.

Usage

The march is performed by the United States Coast Guard Band at official ceremonies, graduations at the Coast Guard Academy, and changes of command. It remains one of the best-known American service songs alongside "The Marines' Hymn" and "The U.S. Air Force".
