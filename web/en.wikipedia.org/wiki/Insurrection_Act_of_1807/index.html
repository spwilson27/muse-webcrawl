Insurrection Act of 1807

The Insurrection Act of 1807 is a United States federal law (10 U.S.C. §§ 251–255) that empowers the President of the United States to deploy U.S. military and federalized National Guard troops within the United States in particular circumstances, such as to suppress civil disorder, insurrection, or rebellion.

The Act is a major exception to the Posse Comitatus Act of 1878, which generally limits the use of the military for domestic law enforcement. It has been invoked on numerous occasions, including during the civil rights era and the 1992 Los Angeles riots.

History

Enacted in 1807, the law consolidated and expanded earlier militia acts of the 1790s. Its provisions allow deployment at the request of a state legislature or governor, or unilaterally by the President when unlawful obstructions make enforcement of federal law impracticable.
