Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) are a group of growth factors also known as cytokines and as metabologens. Originally discovered by their ability to induce the formation of bone and cartilage, BMPs are now considered to constitute a group of pivotal morphogenetic signals that orchestrate tissue architecture throughout the body. They belong to the transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) superfamily.

Discovery

In 1965, Marshall R. Urist published experiments in Science showing that demineralized, lyophilized sections of bone implanted into muscle pouches of rodents induced new bone formation. Urist proposed the name "bone morphogenetic protein" in 1971. The proteins themselves were purified and cloned in the 1980s by John Wozney and colleagues.

Types

More than twenty BMPs have been identified in humans (BMP1–BMP20 and related growth differentiation factors, GDFs). BMP2 and BMP7 are among the best studied. BMP1 is an exception: it is a metalloprotease (procollagen C-proteinase) rather than a TGF-β family signaling molecule.

Function

BMPs signal through serine/threonine kinase receptors (type I and type II), activating receptor-regulated SMADs (SMAD1/5/8), which complex with SMAD4 and enter the nucleus to regulate target genes such as Runx2 and Osterix in osteoblast differentiation. Beyond bone, BMPs regulate embryonic dorsal–ventral patterning, kidney and eye development, iron metabolism (via hepcidin), and vascular biology. Their activity is modulated by extracellular antagonists including noggin, chordin, and gremlin.

Clinical uses

Recombinant human BMP-2 (rhBMP-2, marketed as Infuse) and BMP-7 (OP-1) have been approved for certain spinal fusions, long-bone non-unions, and oral/maxillofacial procedures. Their use has been controversial because of cost, complications such as ectopic bone formation and swelling, and debate over efficacy data. Rare mutations in BMP signaling components cause diseases such as fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (via ACVR1, the BMP type I receptor) and hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia.
