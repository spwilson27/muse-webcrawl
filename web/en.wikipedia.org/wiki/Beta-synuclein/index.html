Beta-synuclein

GeneSNCB
FamilySynucleins
LocationPresynaptic terminals, brain

Beta-synuclein is a protein that in humans is encoded by the SNCB gene. It is a member of the synuclein family of small, soluble proteins — together with alpha-synuclein and gamma-synuclein — that are found mainly in neural tissue and are concentrated in presynaptic terminals.

Structure and function

Beta-synuclein is a 134-amino-acid protein. Like other synucleins it is natively unfolded in solution and can adopt helical structure on binding membranes. Unlike alpha-synuclein, beta-synuclein lacks part of the central hydrophobic NAC region that drives aggregation, and it is correspondingly much less prone to form fibrils. Indeed, beta-synuclein has been shown in vitro and in transgenic models to inhibit the aggregation of alpha-synuclein, and it has been investigated as a possible neuroprotective agent in Parkinson's disease.

Clinical significance

Although alpha-synuclein is the principal component of Lewy bodies, beta-synuclein has been reported in association with neurodegenerative conditions, and rare SNCB variants have been linked to dementia with Lewy bodies. Beta-synuclein immunoreactivity in cerebrospinal fluid and blood has been studied as a biomarker of synaptic degeneration in Alzheimer's disease.
