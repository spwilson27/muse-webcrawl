The Morrison government was the federal executive government of Australia led by Prime Minister Scott Morrison of the Liberal Party, in coalition with the National Party. It succeeded the Turnbull government in August 2018 after a Liberal leadership spill, won the 2019 federal election against expectations, and was defeated at the 2022 federal election by the Labor Party under Anthony Albanese.

First term and the 2019 election

Morrison, previously Treasurer, became Liberal leader and prime minister on 24 August 2018 after Malcolm Turnbull lost the confidence of his party room. With Josh Frydenberg as Treasurer, the government campaigned in 2019 on tax cuts and economic management against Labor's ambitious tax and climate platform, and unexpectedly retained office with a small majority — a result Morrison called a "miracle".

Bushfires and COVID-19

The 2019–20 "Black Summer" bushfires damaged the government's standing, in part because of Morrison's holiday in Hawaii during the crisis. From early 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic dominated: the government closed Australia's borders, governed through a new National Cabinet with state and territory leaders, and introduced large economic support programs including the JobKeeper wage subsidy. Australia recorded comparatively low infection and mortality rates through 2020–21, though the vaccine rollout of 2021 was criticised as slow.

Other events

The period saw the AUKUS security pact with the United States and United Kingdom (September 2021), attended by friction with France over a cancelled submarine contract; continued tensions with China; the 2021 parliament-house culture controversies; and the "secret ministries" affair, revealed after the government's defeat, in which Morrison had himself appointed to administer five additional departments without public disclosure — a practice later found by the Governor-General's inquiry and a High Court-backed report to have undermined responsible government conventions.

2022 election

On 21 May 2022 the Coalition lost office, losing seats to Labor, the Greens, and independent "teal" candidates campaigning on climate change and integrity. Morrison resigned as Liberal leader and was succeeded by Peter Dutton.
