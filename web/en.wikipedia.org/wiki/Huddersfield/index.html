Huddersfield is a market town in the Metropolitan Borough of Kirklees in West Yorkshire, England. It is the administrative centre and largest settlement in the borough. The town is 14 miles (23 km) south-west of Leeds and 24 miles (39 km) north-east of Manchester.

Huddersfield was a prominent mill town in the Industrial Revolution, producing woollen textiles exported worldwide, and it remains known for its Victorian architecture, its railway station — described by John Betjeman as "a stately home with trains in it" — and the University of Huddersfield.

History

There has been a market town at Huddersfield since Anglo-Saxon times; the placename is recorded in the Domesday Book as Oderesfelte. The Ramsden family bought the manor in 1599 and developed the town: the Ramsden estate built the Cloth Hall (1766), the Huddersfield Broad Canal (1774–80) and much of the Georgian and Victorian town centre. During the Industrial Revolution the area became a centre of the Luddite unrest of 1811–12, when workers destroyed cropping machines in local mills; the town also saw Chartist agitation. In 1969 the town centre's architectural heritage was highlighted by the construction of the Queensgate Market and the university quarter.

Landmarks and culture

Landmarks include St George's Square with the station façade and the statue of Huddersfield-born prime minister Harold Wilson, Victoria Tower on Castle Hill (1899), the Lawrence Batley Theatre, and the Galpharm (John Smith's) Stadium. Huddersfield is the birthplace of rugby league: in 1895, 22 northern clubs met at the George Hotel and formed the Northern Union, the breakaway that became the Rugby Football League. The town's club, Huddersfield Giants, and football club Huddersfield Town A.F.C. (FA Cup winners in 1922 and league champions three seasons running in the 1920s) both play at the stadium.

The Huddersfield Contemporary Music Festival is a major international new-music event, and the town has a strong tradition of choral singing, including the Huddersfield Choral Society (founded 1836).

Governance and demography

Since 1974 Huddersfield has been part of Kirklees Council. The town has significant South Asian and Afro-Caribbean communities and hosts a well-known Caribbean carnival. It is served by the M62 motorway and by TransPennine rail routes between Leeds, Manchester and Liverpool.
