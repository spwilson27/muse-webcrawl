Châtelet–Les Halles is a major interchange station on the RER commuter rail network of Paris, France. Opened in 1977 beneath the Forum des Halles in the 1st arrondissement, it is one of the largest underground stations in the world and a central hub of the Paris transport network.

Description

The station is served by three RER lines: the east–west RER A, the north–south RER B, and the RER D. Long underground corridors connect it with the Châtelet and Les Halles stations of the Paris Métro (lines 1, 4, 7, 11 and 14), forming a single vast interchange complex.

History

The station was built as part of the redevelopment of the Les Halles district after the demolition of the old central food markets, and as the centrepiece of the new RER express network. It opened on 9 December 1977, when RER A and RER B were joined at the site; the RER D followed in the 1990s. The complex has been repeatedly renovated, most recently in connection with the rebuilding of the Forum des Halles and the construction of the Canopée above it in the 2010s.

Usage

Châtelet–Les Halles handles several hundred thousand passengers a day and tens of millions a year, making it one of the busiest rail stations in Europe. The long transfer corridors, some several hundred metres, are a well-known feature of commuting in Paris.
