The Royal 22nd Regiment (French: Royal 22e Régiment) is an infantry regiment of the Canadian Army. It is the most senior francophone regiment in Canada and one of only three regular-force infantry regiments. Its members are universally known in English as the "Van Doos", a phonetic corruption of the French vingt-deux ("twenty-two"). The regiment consists of three regular battalions and two reserve battalions, with regimental headquarters at the Citadelle of Quebec, where its soldiers also mount the guard in summer.

History

The regiment was raised in October 1914 as the 22nd (French Canadian) Battalion, Canadian Expeditionary Force, so that French-speaking Canadians could serve in their own language during the First World War. It fought at Ypres, the Somme, Vimy Ridge, Passchendaele, Amiens, and Mons, earning a formidable reputation. Made a permanent regiment in 1920 and granted the title "Royal" by King George V in 1928, it is unique in having kept its number in English usage rather than a translated name.

In the Second World War the regiment landed in Sicily in July 1943 and fought up the Italian peninsula — including the bitter fighting at Ortona and the breaking of the Hitler Line — before moving to North-West Europe in 1945. It served in Korea in 1951–53, contributed extensively to peacekeeping missions from Cyprus to the Balkans, and deployed battle groups to Afghanistan, particularly Kandahar province, where its soldiers won numerous decorations for valour.

Traditions

The regimental mascot is Batisse, a white goat — a tradition dating to a goat presented during the First World War era — who lives at the Citadelle and holds an honorary rank. The beaver on the regimental badge, the scarlet tunic, and the motto Je me souviens (also Quebec's provincial motto) express its French-Canadian identity. The regiment's colonel-in-chief is the sovereign of Canada.
