Uncle Tupelo was an American alternative country band formed in Belleville, Illinois, in 1987 by Jay Farrar, Jeff Tweedy, and Mike Heidorn. Combining punk rock energy with country, folk, and traditional American music influences, the band became the flagship act of the alt-country movement; its debut album No Depression lent its name to the genre's defining magazine and website. After the band broke up in 1994, Farrar formed Son Volt while Tweedy and the remaining members formed Wilco.

Uncle Tupelo's blend of punk and country made them pioneers of alternative country.

History

Farrar and Tweedy had played together since high school in the Primitives before forming Uncle Tupelo, taking the name from a cartoon. Their debut No Depression (1990), including a hardcore-inflected cover of the Carter Family's "No Depression in Heaven", attracted a devoted following. The largely acoustic March 16–20, 1992, produced by Peter Buck of R.E.M., was a critical success. Signing to Sire Records, the band released Anodyne (1993) with an expanded lineup, but tensions between Farrar and Tweedy led Farrar to quit in early 1994; the band played its final shows that spring.

Legacy

Though commercially modest, Uncle Tupelo is widely credited with founding alternative country, bridging punk and Americana influences for later acts. Both successor bands, Wilco and Son Volt, went on to major critical acclaim, and the two camps' rivalry became a staple of roots-rock lore.
