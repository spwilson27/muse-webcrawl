Structural stability

In mathematics, structural stability is a fundamental property of a dynamical system which means that the qualitative behaviour of the trajectories is unaffected by small perturbations (small in a suitable C1 topology). Examples of such qualitative properties are the numbers of fixed points and periodic orbits, and the topology of the phase portrait.

The concept was introduced by Aleksandr Andronov and Lev Pontryagin in 1937 under the name "systèmes grossiers", or rough systems. They announced a criterion for structural stability in two dimensions. The theory was developed further by Stephen Smale and others in the 1960s, during which it became clear that structural stability is a surprisingly delicate property in higher dimensions.

Definition

A vector field (or diffeomorphism) is structurally stable if every sufficiently small perturbation of it is topologically equivalent to it — that is, there is a homeomorphism of the state space mapping trajectories of the original system onto trajectories of the perturbed system.

Main results

On compact two-dimensional manifolds, the Peixoto theorem characterises structurally stable flows: they are precisely the Morse–Smale flows having finitely many fixed points and periodic orbits, all hyperbolic, with no saddle connections; such flows are dense among all flows on orientable surfaces. In higher dimensions, structurally stable systems are not dense: Smale showed that systems with horseshoe dynamics and infinitely many periodic orbits arise robustly, while other robust phenomena, such as Newhouse's homoclinic tangencies, preclude structural stability on open sets of systems.

Hyperbolicity

Structural stability is closely tied to hyperbolicity: Anosov diffeomorphisms and, more generally, Axiom A systems satisfying the strong transversality condition are structurally stable. Whether structural stability is equivalent to Axiom A plus strong transversality (the stability conjecture) was a central problem resolved affirmatively through work of Robbin, de Melo, Robinson, Mañé and others.
