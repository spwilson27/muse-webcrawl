Downloadable content

Downloadable content (DLC) is additional content created for an already released video game, distributed through the Internet by the game's publisher. It can range from cosmetic items such as character outfits to substantial new story campaigns, maps, levels and game modes, and it may be free or sold as a form of monetization.

History

Precursors of DLC included expansion packs sold on disc and, earlier still, mail-order and downloadable add-ons for early home computers. The practice became standard with sixth- and seventh-generation consoles: Microsoft's Xbox Live and Sony's PlayStation Network let publishers sell content directly to players. One of the first widely publicised items of paid DLC, the horse armour pack for The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion (2006), became a lasting symbol of controversies over pricing of small cosmetic additions.

Forms

DLC includes expansion packs, map packs, new playable characters, cosmetic skins, season passes (bundles of future DLC sold in advance) and "games as a service" content updates. On PC, storefronts such as Steam manage DLC as entitlements attached to the base game. A distinction is often drawn between genuinely new post-release development and "on-disc DLC" — content created before release but locked behind a paywall — which many players criticise.

Industry impact

DLC extends the commercial life of games and smooths publisher revenues between major releases, but has raised debates over microtransactions, loot boxes, fragmented content, game preservation and whether base games are intentionally left incomplete. Regulators in several countries have examined paid random-item mechanics sold as downloadable content.
