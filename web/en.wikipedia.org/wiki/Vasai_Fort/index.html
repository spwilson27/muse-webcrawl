Vasai Fort

Vasai Fort (also known as Bassein Fort) is a large ruined fortress in Vasai, in the Palghar district north of Mumbai, Maharashtra, India. Built and expanded by the Portuguese, who knew it as Fortaleza de São Sebastião de Baçaim, it was the headquarters of Portuguese power in the northern Konkan in the 16th and 17th centuries before falling to the Marathas in 1739.

History

The site was fortified under the Gujarat Sultanate and ceded to the Portuguese by the Treaty of Bassein (1534). The Portuguese rebuilt it into a walled city containing churches, convents, a cathedral, hospitals, and administrative buildings, guarding the trade of the northern coast. After a celebrated campaign, Chimaji Appa captured the fort for the Maratha Empire in May 1739 following a long siege; the Treaty of Bassein (1802), signed elsewhere in the region's history by Baji Rao II, later helped trigger the Second Anglo-Maratha War.

The ruins today

Within the walls stand the roofless remains of several churches, including those of St. Anthony and the Jesuit church, along with watchtowers, cisterns, and gateways such as the land and sea gates. Protected by the Archaeological Survey of India, the fort is a popular filming location and day-trip destination from Mumbai.
