Pusey House, Oxford

Pusey House is an Anglican religious institution of the University of Oxford, located on St Giles' in central Oxford, England. Founded in 1884 as a memorial to Edward Bouverie Pusey, Regius Professor of Hebrew and a leader of the Oxford Movement, it serves as a centre of Anglo-Catholic worship, teaching, and pastoral care within the university.

History

Pusey died in 1882, and his friends and admirers purchased his extensive theological library and raised funds to found a house where it could be kept and where priests could serve the university in his tradition. The first Principal was Charles Gore, later Bishop of Oxford. The present buildings, designed principally by Temple Moore and completed in stages between 1914 and 1926, include a noted Gothic Revival chapel.

Library and activities

The Pusey House library is one of the finest Anglo-Catholic collections in Britain, with around 75,000 volumes covering theology, liturgy, patristics, and church history, built around Pusey's own books and papers. The House maintains a daily round of prayer and Mass in the Anglo-Catholic tradition, hosts lectures and study groups, and provides chaplaincy to Oxford students regardless of their college. It stands adjacent to St John's College and opposite the site of the former St Giles' House.
