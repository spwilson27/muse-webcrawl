Merkel nerve endings are mechanoreceptors found in the basal epidermis and hair follicles. They consist of a Merkel cell in close apposition with an enlarged sensory nerve ending — the Merkel cell–neurite complex — and are slowly adapting type I (SA1) receptors. They are named after German anatomist Friedrich Merkel, who described the cells in 1875.

Function

Merkel nerve endings provide information on mechanical pressure, position, and deep static touch features, such as shapes and edges. Their small, well-defined receptive fields and sustained firing during a maintained stimulus make them ideally suited for the perception of form and texture and for reading Braille. They are most concentrated in highly sensitive areas such as the fingertips and lips.

Mechanism

The mechanosensitive ion channel Piezo2 mediates the transduction of touch both in Merkel cells and in the associated afferent fibres, as shown by genetic studies in mice and humans.

Clinical significance

The malignant counterpart of the Merkel cell is implicated in Merkel cell carcinoma, a rare and aggressive skin cancer. Loss of Merkel endings with age and in neuropathies contributes to declining tactile acuity.
