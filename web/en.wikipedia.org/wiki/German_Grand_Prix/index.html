German Grand Prix

Formula One cars racing at the Hockenheimring.

The German Grand Prix (German: Großer Preis von Deutschland) is a motor race that has been held for most years since 1926 and was a round of the Formula One World Championship from 1951 for much of the championship's history. It is one of the oldest national Grands Prix, though financial difficulties have kept it off the modern calendar since 2019.

Venues and history

The first German Grand Prix was held in 1926 at the AVUS circuit in Berlin. From 1927 the race moved to the Nürburgring, whose fearsome 22.8-kilometre Nordschleife hosted it for decades and witnessed classics such as Juan Manuel Fangio's legendary comeback victory in 1957. In the 1930s the race was dominated by the state-backed Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union "Silver Arrows" and drivers like Rudolf Caracciola and Bernd Rosemeyer.

After the Second World War the event resumed under Formula One rules. Niki Lauda's near-fatal crash at the Nürburgring in 1976 ended the Nordschleife's F1 tenure; from 1977 the race was held at the Hockenheimring (with one return to the shortened Nürburgring Grand Prix circuit in 1985). From 2007 the two venues alternated, and the race was held under the Eifel Grand Prix name at the Nürburgring in 2020. Michael Schumacher is the most successful German driver in the event, with four wins; overall, Rudolf Caracciola won the pre-championship race six times, and Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton share the Formula One-era record.

Absence

Falling attendances and the loss of promoter support led to the race's cancellation in 2015 and its absence after the 2019 edition, despite Germany's major automotive industry and heritage in the sport.
