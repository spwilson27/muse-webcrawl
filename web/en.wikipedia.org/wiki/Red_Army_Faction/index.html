The Red Army Faction (German: Rote Armee Fraktion, RAF), also known as the Baader–Meinhof Group, was a West German far-left militant organization founded in 1970. Its members described themselves as an urban guerrilla group fighting what they saw as the fascist character of the West German state and American imperialism.

History

The group was founded by Andreas Baader, Gudrun Ensslin, Horst Mahler, and Ulrike Meinhof. Over nearly three decades it carried out bombings, assassinations, kidnappings, and bank robberies. The crisis of the "German Autumn" of 1977 — the kidnapping and murder of Hanns Martin Schleyer and the hijacking of Lufthansa Flight 181 — marked its peak. The deaths of Baader, Ensslin, and Jan-Carl Raspe in Stammheim Prison in October 1977 remain controversial.

Dissolution

The RAF formally announced its dissolution in a communiqué in 1998. Its attacks were responsible for more than 30 deaths.
