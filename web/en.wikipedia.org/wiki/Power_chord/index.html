Power chord

A power chord is a colloquial name for a chord in guitar music, commonly played on electric guitar with amplification and distortion. It consists of the root note and the perfect fifth above it, often with the root doubled at the octave; lacking a third, it is neither major nor minor.

Structure and notation

Power chords are notated with the root letter followed by the number 5, as in E5 or G5. Because the third is omitted, the same shape serves in both major and minor keys. On a guitar in standard tuning, the basic two- or three-finger shape can be moved along the neck to play any root, making power chords easy to learn and fast to shift.

Sound

The simplicity of the interval is well suited to distortion: overdriven amplifiers emphasise the harmonics of the root and fifth, producing a thick, aggressive sound, whereas fuller chords can turn muddy. Intermodulation between the root and fifth also generates a subharmonic that reinforces the root.

Usage

Power chords underpin much of rock, punk and heavy metal, from early recordings such as The Kinks' "You Really Got Me" (1964) and the work of Link Wray, through Black Sabbath and AC/DC to punk bands such as the Ramones and metal rhythm playing generally. They are also used, usually clean, in folk and blues guitar styles.
