A sacbe (plural sacbeob), from Yucatec Maya sak beh, "white road", is a raised paved road built by the ancient Maya of Mesoamerica. Sacbeob were constructed of limestone rubble faced with cut stone and surfaced with lime plaster (sascab), which gave them their bright white appearance.

A sacbe at Dzibilchaltún, Yucatán.

Construction and use

Sacbeob range from short ceremonial avenues linking plaza groups within a city to long-distance causeways connecting separate polities. They are typically between 2 and 10 metres wide and raised up to a few metres above the surrounding terrain in low-lying areas. The longest known, between Cobá and Yaxuná in the northern Yucatán, runs about 100 kilometres and has been studied by archaeologists since the 1930s.

Function

Scholars debate whether sacbeob were primarily economic (facilitating trade and tribute), political (displaying the power of the building polity and integrating its territory), or religious (serving as processional routes). In Maya cosmology roads could also have symbolic dimensions, linking settlements to sacred places and to the Milky Way, itself sometimes called a "white road".
