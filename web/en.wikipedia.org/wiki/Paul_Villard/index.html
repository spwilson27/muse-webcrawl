Paul Villard

Paul Ulrich Villard (28 September 1860 – 13 January 1934) was a French chemist and physicist. He is best known for discovering gamma rays in 1900 while studying the radiation emitted by radium.

Discovery of gamma rays

Working at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris, Villard investigated the radiation from radium salts. In 1900 he reported that part of the radiation was not deflected by a magnetic field and was much more penetrating than the alpha and beta components — passing through thick layers of lead. Ernest Rutherford recognised this as a third, distinct type of radiation and in 1903 named it gamma radiation. Credit for the discovery was slow to attach to Villard, whose name remained comparatively little known outside physics.

Other work

Villard also studied cathode rays, the chemistry of hydrates under pressure, and electrical circuits. The Villard circuit — a voltage-multiplier arrangement using a capacitor and diode — bears his name, as does the related Villard cascade. He proposed an early ionisation chamber method for measuring radioactivity. Villard was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1908.
