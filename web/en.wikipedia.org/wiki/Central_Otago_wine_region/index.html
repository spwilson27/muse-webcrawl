Central Otago wine region

The Central Otago wine region is a geographical indication in New Zealand's Otago region and the world's southernmost commercial wine region, with vineyards lying around the 45th parallel south. It is renowned internationally for intensely flavoured, fruit-driven Pinot noir, which accounts for the large majority of plantings.

Climate and terroir

Unlike New Zealand's other maritime wine regions, Central Otago has a continental climate: hot, dry summers, cold winters, large diurnal temperature variation, and low rainfall and humidity. Vineyards sit at elevations of roughly 200–400 metres in sheltered river valleys and on terraces with schist-derived, free-draining soils. Spring and autumn frosts are a significant hazard, managed with wind machines, helicopters, and water sprinklers.

Subregions and history

Subregions include Gibbston, Bannockburn, Cromwell Basin, Alexandra, Bendigo, and Wānaka. Vines were first planted in the area in the 19th century — the Frenchman Jean Désiré Féraud won awards for wines near Alexandra in the 1880s — but the modern industry dates from plantings in the late 1970s and 1980s at places such as Rippon in Wānaka and Gibbston Valley, followed by rapid growth in the 1990s and 2000s as the region's Pinot noir gained international acclaim.
