Judah ha-Nasi

Judah ha-Nasi (יהודה הנשיא, "Judah the Prince"), also known simply as Rabbi or Rabbeinu HaQadosh ("our holy teacher"), was a second-century rabbi and the redactor and editor of the Mishnah, the foundational work of Rabbinic Judaism. He lived approximately 135–217 CE.

Life

Judah was born in Roman Palestine, traditionally on the day Rabbi Akiva died as a martyr. He was the son of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel II and became nasi (patriarch) of the Sanhedrin, headquartered first at Beit She'arim and later Sepphoris in Galilee. He maintained a close, if partly legendary, relationship with a Roman emperor often identified as Antoninus Pius or Marcus Aurelius.

The Mishnah

Around 200 CE, fearing that the oral Torah would be lost through persecution and dispersion, Judah compiled the Mishnah: six orders of legal teachings organized by subject, from agricultural laws to festivals, family law, damages, holy things, and ritual purity. This act of codification shaped all subsequent Jewish law and study.

Legacy

Judah's humility and learning made him the paradigmatic sage; in rabbinic literature he is called simply "Rabbi." He is buried at Beit She'arim, now a national park and UNESCO World Heritage Site.
