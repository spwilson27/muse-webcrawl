Quatrain

A quatrain is a type of stanza, or a complete poem, consisting of four lines. It is one of the most common stanza forms in European poetry, appearing in ballads, hymns, sonnets and epigrams. Quatrains are classified by their rhyme scheme: AABB, ABAB, ABBA, ABCB, AAAA and non-rhyming forms.

Types

  • Elegiac stanza / heroic quatrain — ABAB in iambic pentameter, used by Gray's Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard;
  • Ballad stanza — usually ABCB or ABAB, alternating four and three stresses, the staple of folk ballads and hymns;
  • Envelope rhyme — ABBA, used in Tennyson's In Memoriam;
  • Rubaiyat stanza — AABA, associated with Omar Khayyam's Rubaiyat;
  • Monorhyme quatrain — AAAA, four lines on one rhyme.

Two quatrains plus a sestet make up the Petrarchan sonnet; two make the English ballad's common measure. The quatrain also figures in Chinese jueju poetry and in the four-line stanzas of Arabic and Persian forms.
