Supe Valley

The Supe Valley is a river valley on the north-central coast of Peru, in the Lima Region, formed by the Supe River as it flows from the Andes to the Pacific Ocean. It is internationally significant as the location of Caral, one of the oldest known cities in the Americas and the centre of the Caral–Supe (Norte Chico) civilization.

Archaeology

The valley contains numerous Late Archaic (3000–1800 BCE) monumental sites, including Caral, Áspero, Miraya, Lurihuasi, and Chupacigarro. These centres feature platform mounds, sunken circular plazas, and residential complexes, built by a society that subsisted on marine resources from the rich Humboldt Current fisheries combined with irrigated agriculture, particularly of cotton used for fishing nets. The Sacred City of Caral–Supe was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2009.

Geography

The Supe River is a short, seasonal coastal river. The valley floor supports agriculture — including asparagus, beans, and fruit — under irrigation, set between barren desert hills typical of the Peruvian coast.
