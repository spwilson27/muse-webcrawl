Lake Lanier, officially Lake Sidney Lanier, is a reservoir in the northern portion of the U.S. state of Georgia. It was created by the completion of Buford Dam on the Chattahoochee River in 1956, and is also fed by the waters of the Chestatee River. The lake encompasses 38,000 acres (150 km²) of water with over 690 miles (1,110 km) of shoreline, and lies across parts of Hall, Forsyth, Dawson, Gwinnett and Lumpkin counties.

The lake is named for the poet Sidney Lanier and was built and is operated by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for flood control, water supply, hydroelectric power and recreation. It is the primary water source for metropolitan Atlanta.

History

Construction of Buford Dam began in 1950; the flooded valley had been home to farming communities, and cemeteries, businesses and the town of Oscarville lay in the impoundment area. The lake filled by 1956. During droughts, falling water levels have exposed old roadbeds, building foundations and other remnants of the pre-lake landscape.

Recreation and use

Lake Lanier is one of the most-visited Corps of Engineers lakes in the United States, with millions of visitors annually for boating, fishing, swimming and camping. The Lake Lanier Islands resort area includes beaches, a water park and golf. The lake hosted the rowing and sprint canoeing events of the 1996 Summer Olympics at the Lake Lanier Olympic Park near Gainesville.

The lake has a reputation in local folklore for drownings and boating accidents, and for stories about the flooded town of Oscarville — a Black community driven out by racial violence in 1912, decades before the lake was built — which figures prominently in accounts of the lake's haunted reputation.

Water disputes

Lanier's releases downstream affect Georgia, Alabama and Florida, and the lake has been central to the long-running "tri-state water dispute" over the Apalachicola–Chattahoochee–Flint River Basin, involving Atlanta's water supply, downstream navigation, agriculture and the Apalachicola Bay oyster fishery.
