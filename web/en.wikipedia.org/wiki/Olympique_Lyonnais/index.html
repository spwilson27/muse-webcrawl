Olympique Lyonnais (commonly referred to as Lyon or OL) is a French professional football club based in Lyon, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes. Founded in 1950, the club plays in Ligue 1, the top flight of French football, and is one of the most popular clubs in France.

Lyon won seven consecutive Ligue 1 championships between 2002 and 2008, a national record at the time, and reached the UEFA Champions League semi-finals in 2010 and 2020. The club's women's team, Olympique Lyonnais Féminin, is among the most successful in Europe, with a record number of UEFA Women's Champions League titles.

History

The club was formed in 1950 from the multi-sport club Lyon Olympique Universitaire. Under long-serving president Jean-Michel Aulas (from 1987), Lyon rose from the second division to dominance in the 2000s, built on a renowned academy and an astute transfer model. Stars of the era included Juninho Pernambucano, famous for his free kicks, Karim Benzema and Michael Essien.

Stadium

In 2016 Lyon moved from the Stade de Gerland to the new 59,186-seat Parc Olympique Lyonnais (Groupama Stadium) in Décines-Charpieu, which hosted matches at UEFA Euro 2016 and the 2019 FIFA Women's World Cup final.

Women's team

The women's section, acquired in 2004, has dominated French and European football, winning numerous Division 1 Féminine titles and a record eight Women's Champions League titles, including five consecutively (2016–2020).

Honours
