Disciple (Christianity)

In Christianity, a disciple is a dedicated follower of Jesus. This term is found in the New Testament only in the Gospels and Acts. In the ancient world, a disciple was a follower or adherent of a teacher — the Greek word mathētēs means a learner or pupil.

Disciple
Greek: mathētēs (learner)
Related: Apostle, the Twelve

The Twelve

Jesus chose twelve disciples, also called apostles, including Simon Peter, Andrew, James and John (sons of Zebedee), Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him and was replaced by Matthias.

Beyond the Twelve

The Gospels also speak of a wider circle — Luke mentions seventy (or seventy-two) sent out on mission, and women disciples including Mary Magdalene, Joanna and Susanna. In Acts, all believers in the early church are called disciples.

Discipleship

Christian discipleship involves learning from and imitating Jesus, taking up one's cross, obedience to his teachings, love of neighbour, and participation in the Great Commission to "make disciples of all nations" (Matthew 28:19). Movements from monasticism to modern discipleship training emphasise practices such as prayer, study, service and evangelism.
