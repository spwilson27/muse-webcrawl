Working cow horse

DisciplineWestern riding / stock horse
PhasesReining ("dry work"), cow work ("fence work")
Governing body (U.S.)National Reined Cow Horse Association (NRCHA)
Common breedsAmerican Quarter Horse, Paint, Appaloosa

Working cow horse is a western riding competition for stock horses in which horse and rider work cattle. Rooted in the traditions of California's vaqueros and the ranch work of the American West, the event tests a horse's athleticism, responsiveness and "cow sense" in two phases: a reining pattern and work with a live cow.

Competition format

In the reined work ("dry work"), the horse performs a prescribed reining pattern including circles at speed, flying lead changes, spins, sliding stops and rollbacks. In the cow work, a single cow is released into the arena. The horse must first hold ("box") the cow at one end of the arena, then run it down the fence and turn it at least once in each direction ("fence work"), and finally drive the cow into the center and circle it in both directions. Judges score each phase, typically on a 60–80 scale with 70 as average.

Training traditions

Classical reined cow horse training follows the California bridle-horse progression: the horse starts in a snaffle bit, advances to the hackamore (bosal), then to the "two-rein" stage combining a bosal and a bridle, and finally works "straight up in the bridle" with a spade bit. The process traditionally takes several years. Major events include the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity for three-year-olds, held since 1970.

Related events

Working cow horse is related to cutting, reining, ranch versatility and working ranch horse classes. A variant without the fence work is sometimes offered as "working cow horse boxing" for novice riders.
