Southend Pier is a major landmark in Southend-on-Sea, Essex, England. Extending 1.34 miles (2.16 km) into the Thames Estuary, it is the longest pleasure pier in the world. The length is a consequence of the shallow estuary shore: at low tide the sea recedes far from the town, and the pier was needed so that steamers could land visitors regardless of the tide.

History

The original wooden pier opened in 1830 and was progressively extended. The present iron pier, designed by James Brunlees, opened in 1889, and its railway — first horse-drawn, then electric — has carried passengers to the pier head since 1890. The pier served as a naval control point (HMS Leigh) in the Second World War. It has survived several fires, including one in 1976 that destroyed the pier head, a collision with the coaster Kingsabbey in 1986 that severed the pier, and a further fire in 2005. A modern cultural centre at the pier head opened in 2012.

The pier railway

The Southend Pier Railway runs the full length of the pier with two platforms at each end, using diesel trains (and previously historic electric stock). The pier head houses a lifeboat station of the RNLI, one of the few accessible at the end of a pier railway.
