The San Diegan was a passenger train service between Los Angeles and San Diego, California, operated by the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway from 1938 and by Amtrak from 1971 until the name was retired in 2000.

History

Launched in March 1938 with streamlined equipment, the San Diegan ran along the scenic "Surf Line" beside the Pacific coast and became one of the busiest intercity rail corridors in the United States. Under Amtrak the service expanded to as many as a dozen daily round trips; some trains briefly extended to Santa Barbara. In June 2000 the route was rebranded as the Pacific Surfliner, which continues the service today.
