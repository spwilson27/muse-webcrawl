Neighbourhood plan

A neighbourhood plan is a statutory planning document in England, introduced by the Localism Act 2011, which allows local communities — parish and town councils or designated neighbourhood forums — to shape the development of their area. Once a plan has passed an independent examination and a local referendum, it is "made" by the local planning authority and forms part of the development plan used in deciding planning applications.

Process

Preparing a neighbourhood plan involves designating a neighbourhood area, gathering evidence and consulting residents, drafting policies (for example on housing allocations, design, green spaces and local facilities), and submitting the plan for examination. The examiner checks that the plan meets the "basic conditions", including general conformity with the strategic policies of the local plan. If more than half of those voting in the referendum support it, the plan comes into force.

Scope and limits

Neighbourhood plans can allocate sites for development and set detailed local policies, but they cannot be used to block development required by higher-level plans; they must plan positively to support local development needs. By the early 2020s, hundreds of neighbourhood plans had been made across England, and the concept influenced wider debates about community involvement in planning.
