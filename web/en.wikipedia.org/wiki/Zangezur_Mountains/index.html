Zangezur Mountains

Zangezur Mountains
Countries: Armenia, Azerbaijan (Nakhchivan)
Highest peak: Mount Kaputjugh
Elevation: 3,905 m
Range: Lesser Caucasus

The Zangezur Mountains (Armenian: Զանգեզուրի լեռնաշղթա) form a mountain range in the Lesser Caucasus that runs along the border between Armenia's Syunik Province and the Nakhchivan exclave of Azerbaijan. The range forms part of the watershed between the basins of the Aras and Vorotan rivers.

Geography

The range extends roughly north-south for about 130 kilometres. Its highest summit is Mount Kaputjugh (3,905 m), one of the highest peaks of the Armenian Highlands. Other notable summits include Sisian and Khustup. Steep forested slopes on the Armenian side give way to alpine meadows near the crest.

Environment

The Zangezur range is an important area for biodiversity in the Caucasus, with oak and juniper woodlands at lower elevations and habitats for species such as the bezoar goat, Armenian mouflon and Caucasian leopard. Parts of the range lie within protected areas including the Zangezur Biosphere Complex and Arevik National Park in Armenia.

Infrastructure

The mountains have historically been a barrier to east-west travel between Armenia and Nakhchivan. Roads crossing the range include routes through the Vorotan pass. The region has been discussed in connection with proposed transport corridors across the South Caucasus.
