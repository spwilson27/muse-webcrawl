Nicholas Culpeper (1616–1654).

Nicholas Culpeper (18 October 1616 – 10 January 1654) was an English botanist, herbalist, physician and astrologer. His book The English Physitian (1652), later enlarged and known as the Complete Herbal, is one of the most enduring works of popular medicine in the English language and has remained in print almost continuously since the seventeenth century.

Life

Culpeper was born at Ockley, Surrey, the day after his father's death. He studied at Cambridge University but abandoned a intended career in the church, was apprenticed to an apothecary in London, and set up practice in Spitalfields, then outside the jurisdiction of the City of London and its College of Physicians. During the English Civil War he fought on the Parliamentarian side and was wounded at the First Battle of Newbury in 1643. He died of consumption in 1654, aged 37.

Works and ideas

Culpeper sought to wrest medicine from the monopoly of Latin-reading physicians. In 1649 he published an unauthorised English translation of the College of Physicians' Pharmacopoeia Londinensis as A Physicall Directory, an act of professional rebellion that made official medicines accessible to ordinary readers. His own herbal combined Galenic humoral medicine with astrological correspondences: each plant and disease was assigned to a planet, and treatments were framed within that system. His emphasis on inexpensive, locally available English herbs rather than costly imported drugs made his work a handbook of self-help medicine for the poor.

Legacy

Culpeper's Complete Herbal has never been out of print for long and shaped popular herbal practice in Britain and America for three centuries. Though its astrology is now historical, many of its plant descriptions remain valuable records of seventeenth-century practice.
