Henry's law

Henry's law is a gas law stating that, at constant temperature, the amount of a given gas dissolved in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of that gas above the liquid. It was formulated by the English chemist William Henry in 1803.

Formulation

One common form is p = kH·c, where p is the partial pressure of the solute gas, c is its concentration in solution, and kH is the Henry's law constant, which depends on the gas, solvent, and temperature. Equivalently, solubility increases with pressure and usually decreases as temperature rises.

Applications

Henry's law explains the fizz of carbonated drinks (carbon dioxide escapes when pressure is released), the behaviour of gases in the blood of divers (relevant to decompression sickness), and the exchange of gases such as oxygen, carbon dioxide, and pollutants between the atmosphere and oceans and lakes. It holds only for dilute solutions and gases that do not react chemically with the solvent.
