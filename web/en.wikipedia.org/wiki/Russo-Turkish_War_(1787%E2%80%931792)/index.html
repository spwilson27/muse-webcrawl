Russo-Turkish War (1787–1792)
Date1787–1792
LocationBlack Sea region, Balkans, Caucasus
ResultRussian victory; Treaty of Jassy

The Russo-Turkish War of 1787–1792 was an unsuccessful attempt by the Ottoman Empire to regain lands lost to the Russian Empire in the previous Russo-Turkish War (1768–1774), including Crimea, which Russia had annexed in 1783. Russia fought in alliance with Austria, which joined the war in 1788.

Campaigns

Russian armies under Grigory Potemkin and Alexander Suvorov captured the key fortresses of Ochakov (1788) and Izmail (1790) after famously costly assaults, while Ushakov's Black Sea fleet won victories at Fidonisi, Kerch, Tendra, and Cape Kaliakra. Austria made separate peace at Sistova in 1791, but Russia fought on until Ottoman defeats made negotiation unavoidable.

Treaty of Jassy

The Treaty of Jassy, signed in January 1792, confirmed Russia's annexation of Crimea and ceded the Yedisan region between the Bug and Dniester rivers, making the Dniester the Russo-Turkish frontier in Europe. The war consolidated Russian power on the northern Black Sea coast, where Odessa was founded shortly afterwards in 1794.
