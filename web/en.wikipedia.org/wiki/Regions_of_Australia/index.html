Regions of Australia

The regions of Australia are the broad geographical areas into which the continent is commonly divided. The most basic division is by the states and territories — New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania, the Northern Territory and the Australian Capital Territory — but many other regional schemes exist for statistical, meteorological, biogeographical and administrative purposes.

Biogeographic regions

The Interim Biogeographic Regionalisation for Australia (IBRA) divides the continent into 89 bioregions, such as the Simpson Strzelecki Dunefields, the Wet Tropics of Queensland and the Jarrah Forest, based on climate, geology, landform, native vegetation and species information.

Wine regions

Australian wine regions are protected as Geographical Indications; notable ones include the Barossa Valley, Hunter Valley, Margaret River, Yarra Valley and McLaren Vale.

Statistical regions

The Australian Bureau of Statistics maintains a hierarchy of statistical areas (SA1–SA4) grouped into Greater Capital City Statistical Areas and regions of the states.
