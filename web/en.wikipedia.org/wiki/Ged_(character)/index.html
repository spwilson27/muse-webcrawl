Ged, also known by his use-name Sparrowhawk, is the central character of Ursula K. Le Guin's Earthsea fantasy series. He first appears in A Wizard of Earthsea (1968) and features prominently in The Tombs of Atuan (1971), The Farthest Shore (1972), Tehanu (1990), and The Other Wind (2001).

Ged, called Sparrowhawk, Archmage of Roke.

Character overview

Ged is born in the village of Ten Alders on the island of Gont, the son of a bronze-smith. As a boy he shows a natural gift for magic, and after defending his village from Kargish raiders with a fog-spell he is taken as an apprentice by the mage Ogion the Silent. Impatient with Ogion's quiet teaching, Ged goes to the school for wizards on the island of Roke, where his pride and rivalry with a fellow student lead him to loose a shadow upon the world — a creature that hunts him across the archipelago. The quest to confront and name the shadow forms the plot of A Wizard of Earthsea, a classic coming-of-age narrative.

Later life

In later books Ged becomes Archmage of Roke, the head of the school of wizardry. In The Farthest Shore he journeys with the young prince Arren to discover why magic is draining out of Earthsea, and spends his power in closing the breach between the living and the dead. Stripped of his magic, he retires to Gont, where the events of Tehanu and The Other Wind find him living a quiet life as a farmer and goat-herd.

Names in Earthsea

In Le Guin's world, every person has a true name in the Old Speech, known only to themselves and to those they trust, and a use-name for everyday life. Ged's use-name, Sparrowhawk, comes from his youthful habit of summoning sparrowhawks. The theme of naming — and of balance, shadow, and self-knowledge — runs through all his appearances.
