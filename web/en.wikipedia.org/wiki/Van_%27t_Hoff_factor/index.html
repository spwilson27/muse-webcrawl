Van 't Hoff factor

Symboli
Named afterJacobus Henricus van 't Hoff
Used inColligative properties of solutions

The van 't Hoff factor (symbol i) is a measure of the effect of a solute on the colligative properties of a solution, such as osmotic pressure, freezing-point depression, boiling-point elevation, and vapour-pressure lowering. It is defined as the ratio of the actual concentration of particles produced when a substance dissolves to the concentration the substance would have if it did not dissociate.

Values

For non-electrolytes such as glucose or urea, i ≈ 1. For strong electrolytes that dissociate completely, i approaches the number of ions per formula unit: about 2 for NaCl, 3 for CaCl2, and so on. Measured values are usually somewhat lower because of ion pairing and other non-ideal interactions, especially at higher concentrations. Substances that associate in solution, such as carboxylic acids in benzene, can show i < 1.

Application

The factor enters the equations for colligative properties: for example, osmotic pressure Π = MRT, and freezing-point depression ΔTf = Kfm. It is named for the Dutch physical chemist Jacobus Henricus van 't Hoff, the first winner of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
