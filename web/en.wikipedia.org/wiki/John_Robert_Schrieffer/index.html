John Robert Schrieffer

BornMay 31, 1931, Oak Park, Illinois, U.S.
DiedJuly 27, 2019 (aged 88), Tallahassee, Florida, U.S.
Alma materMIT; University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign
Known forBCS theory of superconductivity
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1972)

John Robert Schrieffer (May 31, 1931 – July 27, 2019) was an American theoretical physicist who shared the 1972 Nobel Prize in Physics with John Bardeen and Leon Cooper for the BCS theory of Superconductivity.

The BCS wavefunction

As Bardeen's graduate student at the University of Illinois, Schrieffer made the decisive step of writing down the many-body wavefunction of the superconducting ground state — a coherent superposition of occupied and empty pair states — reportedly while riding the New York City subway in early 1957. The "BCS wavefunction" allowed the computation of the theory's thermodynamic predictions and remains a canonical example of a variational ground state.

Later career

Schrieffer held professorships at the University of Pennsylvania, UC Santa Barbara (where he directed the Institute for Theoretical Physics, now the Kavli Institute), and Florida State University, where he worked on High-temperature superconductivity. He also wrote the standard textbook Theory of Superconductivity (1964).
