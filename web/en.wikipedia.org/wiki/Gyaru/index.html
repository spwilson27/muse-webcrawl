Gyaru

Gyaru (Japanese: ギャル) is a Japanese fashion subculture that emerged in the 1970s and peaked in popularity in the 1990s and 2000s. The name is a transliteration of the English slang word "gal". Gyaru style is characterised by heavily bleached or dyed hair, dramatic makeup with false eyelashes and dark eyeliner, decorated nails, and counter-cultural attitudes toward Japanese norms of beauty and behaviour.

History and styles

The subculture rose with the kogal (high-school girl) style of the mid-1990s — loose socks, shortened skirts and deep tans — centred on Tokyo's Shibuya district and the Shibuya 109 department store. The extreme ganguro style pushed tanning and white makeup further, while later offshoots included hime gyaru (princess-like, with big curled hair and lace), agejo, and male counterparts, gyaruo. Magazines such as egg and Popteen codified the look.

Decline and legacy

Gyaru declined after about 2010 as mainstream fashion shifted, its flagship magazines folded, and adherents aged. The style remains an iconic symbol of Heisei-era youth culture and continues to influence Japanese street fashion retrospectives and internet aesthetics.
