If Beale Street Could Talk (film)

If Beale Street Could Talk is a 2018 American romantic drama film written and directed by Barry Jenkins, based on James Baldwin's 1974 novel of the same name. Set in Harlem in the early 1970s, it tells the story of Clementine "Tish" Rivers and Alonzo "Fonny" Hunt, whose plans to marry are shattered when Fonny is falsely accused of rape and imprisoned while Tish is pregnant with their child. The film stars KiKi Layne and Stephan James, with Regina King in an Academy Award–winning supporting role.

Plot

Tish and Fonny, childhood friends turned lovers, search for an apartment in New York City while facing racial discrimination from landlords. After Fonny is arrested on a false identification and sent to jail awaiting trial, Tish learns she is pregnant. Her family rallies around her, while her mother, Sharon, travels to Puerto Rico to plead with the accuser, Victoria Rogers, who suffers a breakdown and miscarries, leaving the case unresolved. Fonny ultimately accepts a plea deal for a lesser charge. Years later, Tish and their young son visit Fonny in prison, sharing a meal and hope for his eventual release.

Production

Jenkins wrote the screenplay in 2013, the same summer he wrote Moonlight, and adapted Baldwin's novel with the blessing of the Baldwin estate — the first English-language feature film adaptation of Baldwin's fiction. Cinematographer James Laxton shot on large-format digital cameras, and Nicholas Britell composed the acclaimed jazz- and soul-inflected score.

Reception

The film premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2018 and received widespread critical acclaim for its performances, cinematography, and faithful, lyrical rendering of Baldwin's prose. It received three Academy Award nominations — Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Original Score, and Best Supporting Actress — with Regina King winning for her portrayal of Sharon Rivers. It also won the Independent Spirit Award for Best Feature.
