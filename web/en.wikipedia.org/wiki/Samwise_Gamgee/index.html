Samwise Gamgee, usually called Sam, is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's fantasy novel The Lord of the Rings. A hobbit of the Shire, he is the gardener of Bag End and the devoted companion of Frodo Baggins, accompanying him on the quest to destroy the One Ring in the fires of Mount Doom. Tolkien described Sam as "the chief hero" of the story, and critics read him as an emblem of the ordinary English soldier and servant class whose loyalty carries the epic.

Role in the story

Sam, son of Hamfast "the Gaffer" Gamgee, is caught eavesdropping on Frodo and Gandalf and is ordered to accompany Frodo into exile. As a member of the Fellowship he serves as Frodo's cook, pack-bearer, and conscience; after the Fellowship breaks, he alone follows Frodo and Gollum into Mordor. He wounds the great spider Shelob with the Elvish blade Sting and the Phial of Galadriel, briefly bears the Ring himself after believing Frodo dead — resisting its temptation to make him a mighty gardener-lord — and rescues Frodo from the Tower of Cirith Ungol. On the final slopes of Mount Doom, when Frodo can walk no further, Sam carries him: "Come, Mr. Frodo! I can't carry it for you, but I can carry you."

Home again, Sam restores the Shire with Galadriel's gift of earth from Lothlórien, marries Rosie Cotton, and is elected Mayor of the Shire seven times. After Rosie's death he is said, as the last of the Ring-bearers, to have sailed West from the Grey Havens. Within the fiction he is also one of the authors of the Red Book of Westmarch.

Creation and reception

Tolkien modelled Sam partly on the batmen (officers' soldier-servants) he had known in the First World War, writing that his Sam was "a reflexion of the English soldier, of the privates and batmen I knew in the 1914 war, and recognised as so far superior to myself." The character has been portrayed on screen by Sean Astin in Peter Jackson's film trilogy (2001–2003), a performance widely praised for the films' emotional centre.
