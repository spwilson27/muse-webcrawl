National Union of Mineworkers (South Africa)

The National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) is a trade union in South Africa, founded on 4 December 1982 by Cyril Ramaphosa, who became its first general secretary. It organises workers in the mining, energy and construction sectors and is one of the largest affiliates of the Congress of South African Trade Unions (COSATU).

History

Black mineworkers had been excluded from recognised union rights under apartheid. The NUM grew rapidly in the 1980s, organising migrant labourers on the gold and coal mines, and led the great mineworkers' strike of 1987, one of the largest strikes in South African history. It played a central role in the anti-apartheid struggle and in the transition to democracy.

In the 2010s the NUM lost many members in the platinum belt to the rival Association of Mineworkers and Construction Union (AMCU), a rivalry that formed part of the background to the Marikana massacre of August 2012. The union remains affiliated to the ANC-led tripartite alliance.
