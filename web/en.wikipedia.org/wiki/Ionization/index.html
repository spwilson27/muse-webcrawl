Ionization

Ionization (or ionisation) is the process by which an atom or molecule acquires a negative or positive charge by gaining or losing electrons, often in conjunction with other chemical changes. The resulting electrically charged atom or molecule is called an ion.

Ionization energy

The energy required to remove an electron from a neutral atom in the gas phase is called the ionization energy. Successive ionization energies increase as more electrons are removed; ionization energy generally rises across a period of the periodic table and falls down a group, reflecting atomic structure.

Mechanisms

Ionization can occur through collisions with energetic particles, absorption of electromagnetic radiation (photoionization), strong electric fields, or chemical reactions such as the dissociation of acids in solution. When radiation removes electrons from molecules in living tissue, it is termed ionizing radiation, distinguished from non-ionizing radiation by its higher energy.

Applications

Ionization underlies mass spectrometry source techniques (such as electrospray and MALDI), gas discharge lamps, ion thrusters, smoke detectors using americium-241, and the formation of plasma.
