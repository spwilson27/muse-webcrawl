Buda Castle (Hungarian: Budavári Palota) is the historical castle and palace complex of the Hungarian kings in Budapest. It stands on the southern tip of Castle Hill, in the Castle District of Buda, overlooking the Danube. The complex is part of the Budapest UNESCO World Heritage Site, declared in 1987.

History

The first royal residence on the site was built under Béla IV in the 13th century after the Mongol invasion. Sigismund of Luxembourg and Matthias Corvinus transformed it into one of the great Gothic and Renaissance palaces of Europe. The castle was ruined during the Ottoman occupation and the siege of 1686 that retook Buda, then rebuilt in Baroque style under the Habsburgs; Empress Maria Theresa's palace was completed in the 18th century and greatly expanded in neo-Baroque form by architects Miklós Ybl and Alajos Hauszmann in the late 19th century. It was devastated again in the Siege of Budapest in 1944–45 and rebuilt in simplified form during the Communist era.

Today

The palace now houses the Hungarian National Gallery, the Budapest History Museum and the National Széchényi Library. The Castle District around it contains Matthias Church, the Fisherman's Bastion and medieval burghers' houses. A funicular, the Budavári Sikló, links the palace to the Chain Bridge below.
