Gendarmerie Nationale

The Gendarmerie Nationale (French pronunciation: [ʒɑ̃daʁməʁi nɑsjɔnal]) is one of two national law enforcement forces of France, along with the National Police. It is a branch of the French Armed Forces placed under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of the Interior — with additional duties to the Ministry of Defense — for its military operations. The gendarmerie's missions include policing rural and suburban areas, border protection, counter-terrorism support, and military police duties.

History

The gendarmerie traces its origins to the Maréchaussée of the Ancien Régime, the military police force of early modern France. In 1791 the revolutionary government renamed it the Gendarmerie Nationale, and Napoleon Bonaparte expanded its role and prestige. The force became the model for gendarmeries in many other countries.

Organization

The Gendarmerie is organized into the departmental gendarmerie (gendarmes départementaux), responsible for everyday policing in small towns and rural areas, and the mobile gendarmerie (gendarmerie mobile), which handles crowd control, riot duty, and reinforcement. Specialized units include the GIGN intervention force, the Republican Guard, the Maritime Gendarmerie, the Air Transport Gendarmerie, and the National Gendarmerie Intervention Group. Although administratively attached to the Ministry of the Interior since 2009, gendarmes retain military status.

Duties

The Gendarmerie polices about 95% of French territory and roughly half the population, covering rural zones, suburbs, and small towns, while the National Police covers large cities. Its duties span criminal investigation, traffic enforcement, search and rescue (including mountain rescue by the PGHM), protection of official residences, and overseas military police missions.
