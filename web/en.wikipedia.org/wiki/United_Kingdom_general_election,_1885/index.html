The 1885 United Kingdom general election was held from 24 November to 18 December 1885. It was the first election fought after the Third Reform Act (the Representation of the People Act 1884) had extended the vote to most male householders, roughly doubling the electorate, and after the Redistribution of Seats Act 1885 had redrawn the constituency map, mostly into single-member seats.

Campaign and results

William Ewart Gladstone's Liberals won 319 seats against 247 for Lord Salisbury's Conservatives, but fell short of an overall majority. The balance of power was held by Charles Stewart Parnell's Irish Parliamentary Party, which swept 85 of the 103 Irish seats. Joseph Chamberlain had campaigned on an "Unauthorised Programme" of social reform, including free education and smallholdings for agricultural labourers ("three acres and a cow").

Aftermath

The result precipitated the Irish Home Rule crisis. Gladstone's conversion to Home Rule — revealed in the "Hawarden Kite" episode — split the Liberal Party, with Chamberlain and the Liberal Unionists breaking away. After Salisbury's brief caretaker ministry, Gladstone formed a government in February 1886 and introduced the first Home Rule Bill, whose defeat triggered the 1886 general election and two decades of mostly Unionist government.
