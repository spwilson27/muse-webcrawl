The Central District (Hebrew: מחוז המרכז, Meḥoz HaMerkaz) is one of the six administrative districts of Israel. It surrounds the Tel Aviv District and covers much of the fertile coastal plain (the Sharon and the Shephelah fringe), with its capital at Ramla and its largest city Rishon LeZion. With over two million inhabitants, it is Israel's second most populous district.

Geography and settlements

The district is divided into four subdistricts: Sharon, Petah Tikva, Ramla, and Rehovot. Major cities include Rishon LeZion, Petah Tikva, Rehovot, Kfar Saba, Ra'anana, Herzliya (partly), Hod HaSharon, Netanya, Modi'in-Maccabim-Re'ut, Ramla, and Lod. The population includes both Jewish and Arab citizens; Arab localities such as Tayibe, Tira, and Qalansawe lie in the "Triangle" area in the district's east, alongside the mixed cities of Ramla and Lod. The district is also home to older moshavim and kibbutzim from the early Zionist settlement of the coastal plain, extensive citrus groves (historically), and Ben Gurion Airport, Israel's main international gateway, near Lod.

Economy

The Central District forms part of the greater Tel Aviv metropolitan area (Gush Dan and its outer ring). High-tech and science-based industry is concentrated in places such as the Weizmann Institute of Science and the science parks of Rehovot and Ness Ziona, the high-tech zones of Ra'anana and Herzliya Pituach, and logistics facilities around the airport. Agriculture, especially citrus, remains significant in the Sharon plain.
