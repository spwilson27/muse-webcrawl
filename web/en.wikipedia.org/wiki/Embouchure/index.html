Embouchure

[Hero image placeholder: brass player's embouchure]

Embouchure (from French bouche, "mouth") is the use of the lips, facial muscles, tongue, and teeth in the playing of a wind instrument. This includes shaping the lips to the mouthpiece of a brass instrument or the placing of the lips against the edge or reed of a woodwind instrument. Correct embouchure technique is essential to tone quality, range, intonation, endurance, and articulation, and it varies considerably between instrument families.

Brass embouchure

For brass instruments, the player presses the lips against a cup-shaped mouthpiece and buzzes them by blowing, much like blowing a raspberry; the vibrating lips act as the reed. The aperture, lip tension, and mouthpiece placement (traditionally about two-thirds upper lip for trumpet) determine pitch in combination with the air column. Different schools of brass pedagogy emphasize different setups; excessive mouthpiece pressure and major embouchure changes are common sources of playing problems, and focal dystonia affecting the embouchure is a recognized occupational hazard.

Woodwind embouchures

  • Single-reed instruments (clarinet, saxophone): the mouthpiece rests on the lower lip cushioned over the teeth; the upper teeth rest on top of the mouthpiece, and the corners of the mouth seal around it.
  • Double-reed instruments (oboe, bassoon): both lips are rolled over the teeth and the reed is taken between them; the embouchure is compact and exerts fine control over the small reed.
  • Flute: there is no mouthpiece contact of the same kind; the player shapes an aperture in the lips and directs an airstream across the edge of the embouchure hole, splitting the air to excite the tube.

Physiology

The embouchure is formed principally by the orbicularis oris and surrounding facial muscles. Brass players develop considerable labial strength and endurance; woodwind embouchures emphasize fine control of pressure and shape. Teachers stress relaxation, efficient air use, and gradual development to avoid injury.
