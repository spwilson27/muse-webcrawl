Philibert of Savoy (Italian: Filiberto di Savoia; 1622–1652) was a prince of the House of Savoy who served as Spanish viceroy of Sicily from 1622 to 1624. He is generally known as Emanuel Philibert of Savoy (Emanuele Filiberto di Savoia).

Life

Philibert was a younger son of Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy, and of Catherine Michaela of Spain, daughter of Philip II of Spain, which made him a grandson of the Spanish king and a useful instrument of Spanish policy in Italy. As a younger son he was destined for a career in Spanish service. His uncle Philip III of Spain appointed him grand prior of Castille and León in the Order of Saint John, and in 1612 he was made admiral — Capitán General de la Mar — of the Spanish Mediterranean fleet at an unusually young age, a largely honorific command that nonetheless placed him at the centre of Spanish naval policy.

In 1622 Philip IV appointed the twenty-year-old Philibert viceroy and captain-general of Sicily, the strategically vital island that supplied grain and anchored Spanish power in the central Mediterranean. His government in Palermo lasted until 1624, when he was recalled and replaced.

Later life and death

Philibert subsequently lived largely in Sicily, holding extensive property there, and remained a figure at the Spanish viceregal court. He died in Palermo in 1652, aged about thirty, during a period of plague and unrest in the island, and was buried there.

He is not to be confused with his great-grandfather Emmanuel Philibert, Duke of Savoy (1528–1580), the victor of St. Quentin, nor with Emmanuel Philibert of Savoy, Prince of Carignano.
