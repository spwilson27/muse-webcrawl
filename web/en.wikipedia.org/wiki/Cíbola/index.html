Cíbola

Cíbola was one of the legendary Seven Cities of Gold sought by Spanish conquistadors in the American Southwest in the 16th century. Reports of wealthy cities north of New Spain, spread by survivors of the Narváez expedition, prompted Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza to send expeditions in search of them.

The search

In 1539 the Franciscan Marcos de Niza, guided by the Moorish slave Estebanico, reported seeing a rich city from a distance. In 1540 Francisco Vázquez de Coronado led a large expedition north; reaching the Zuni pueblo of Hawikuh, his men found no gold, only adobe towns — the supposed cities of Cíbola. Estebanico had been killed at Hawikuh the previous year. Coronado's army stormed the pueblo and wintered on the Rio Grande before marching as far as present-day Kansas in search of Quivira, another rumoured golden land.

Legacy

The myth of the Seven Cities, sometimes linked to the legend of seven bishops who fled the Muslim conquest of Iberia, persisted in Spanish colonial lore and later in American folklore. Cíbola gave its name to Cibola County, New Mexico, and the Cibola National Forest.
