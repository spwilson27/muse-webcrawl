General Staff of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation

The General Staff building on Znamenka Street, Moscow.

The General Staff of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation is the military staff of the Russian Armed Forces and the central command and control body of the Russian Ministry of Defence. Headed by the Chief of the General Staff—who is also First Deputy Minister of Defence—it plans and directs the operations of the armed forces in peace and war, subordinate to the President of Russia as Supreme Commander-in-Chief and to the Minister of Defence.

Organisation

The General Staff includes the Main Operations Directorate, the Main Intelligence Directorate (GRU), the Main Organisational-Mobilisation Directorate, and directorates for communications, electronic warfare, and military topography, among others. It oversees the military districts/joint strategic commands, the strategic rocket forces' employment planning, mobilisation preparation, and military intelligence. Its headquarters are in Moscow, and the National Defence Management Centre, opened in 2014, acts as its high-technology command hub.

History

The institution descends from the Imperial Russian General Staff, formalised in the 19th century, and from the Soviet General Staff, which was the central planning organ of the Red Army during the Second World War under figures such as Boris Shaposhnikov, Aleksandr Vasilevsky, and Georgy Zhukov (as chief 1941). After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the Russian General Staff was formed from its Soviet predecessor in 1992. Reforms under Defence Minister Anatoliy Serdyukov (2008–2012) reduced its size and shifted some functions to the ministry and services, while later changes restored some of its authority.

Chiefs

Valery Gerasimov has served as Chief of the General Staff since 2012, directing Russia's military operations in Syria and in Ukraine from 2022, when he was additionally appointed overall commander of the Russian forces in Ukraine in January 2023.
