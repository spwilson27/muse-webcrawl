Maryland in the American Civil War

During the American Civil War, Maryland was a slave state that remained in the Union. Its strategic location surrounding Washington, D.C., made its loyalty vital. President Abraham Lincoln suspended habeas corpus and arrested secessionist legislators to prevent Maryland from seceding.

Secession crisis

The Baltimore Riot of April 19, 1861, saw violence between Union troops and Confederate sympathizers. Federal occupation followed, and the state legislature, meeting in Frederick, did not vote for secession.

Wartime experience

Maryland supplied soldiers to both sides, with roughly 60,000 serving the Union and about 25,000 joining the Confederacy. Major battles fought on Maryland soil included Antietam, South Mountain, and Monocacy. The state was crossed repeatedly by Confederate armies during the Maryland and Gettysburg campaigns.

Emancipation

A new state constitution in 1864 abolished slavery in Maryland, months before the Thirteenth Amendment.
