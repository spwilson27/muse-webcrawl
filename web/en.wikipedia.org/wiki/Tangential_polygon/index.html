Tangential polygon

FieldEuclidean geometry
Defining propertyAll sides tangent to a single incircle
Dual conceptCyclic polygon

In Euclidean geometry, a tangential polygon, also known as a circumscribed polygon, is a convex polygon that contains an incircle tangent to every side of the polygon. The dual polygon of a tangential polygon is a cyclic polygon, whose vertices all lie on a circumcircle.

Characterizations

A polygon is tangential if and only if its internal angle bisectors are concurrent at the incenter. For a tangential quadrilateral, the Pitot theorem states that the sums of the lengths of opposite sides are equal, and this condition is also sufficient. Every triangle is tangential, as is every regular polygon and every rhombus.

Properties

If a tangential polygon has an even number of sides, alternating sides sum to the same total. The area of a tangential polygon equals one-half the perimeter multiplied by the inradius, generalizing the triangle formula A = rs.
