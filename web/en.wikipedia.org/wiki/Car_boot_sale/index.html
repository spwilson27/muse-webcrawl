Car boot sale

Image placeholder: A car boot sale in progress.

A car boot sale or boot fair is a form of market in which private individuals gather to sell household and garden goods they no longer need. Originating in the United Kingdom, car boot sales are a way for people to clear out unwanted items and for buyers to find bargains, in a setting somewhat like a giant communal garage sale.

Format

Sellers pay a small fee for a pitch, drive onto a field, car park, or school ground — often early on a weekend morning — and display their goods on tables, blankets, or the ground in front of their cars, traditionally selling straight from the open boot (trunk). Buyers usually pay a modest entrance charge or enter free. Items commonly sold include clothing, toys, books, bric-a-brac, furniture, tools, and plants. Haggling is expected.

History and spread

Car boot sales began appearing in Britain in the 1970s and grew into a mass weekend pastime by the 1980s and 1990s, with large events attracting hundreds of sellers. Similar events occur in Australia (where they may be called "trash and treasure" or swap meets) and elsewhere. Organisers and local authorities have increasingly had to address problems such as the sale of counterfeit and stolen goods at some events.
