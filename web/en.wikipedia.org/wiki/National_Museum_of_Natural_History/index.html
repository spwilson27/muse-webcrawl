The National Museum of Natural History (NMNH) is a natural history museum administered by the Smithsonian Institution, located on the National Mall in Washington, D.C. With free admission, it is one of the most visited natural history museums in the world, welcoming several million visitors each year.

Collections

The museum's collections total over 146 million specimens of plants, animals, fossils, minerals, rocks, meteorites, human remains, and cultural artifacts — the largest natural history collection in the world. Iconic exhibits include the Hope Diamond, the Hall of Fossils, the Sant Ocean Hall, and the Hall of Human Origins.

History

The museum opened in 1910 as the new home of the Smithsonian's natural history collections, which had outgrown the original Smithsonian Institution Building. It was among the first Smithsonian buildings constructed exclusively to hold the national collections and research facilities.

Research

Over 400 scientists work at the museum, conducting research in anthropology, botany, entomology, invertebrate and vertebrate zoology, mineral sciences, and paleobiology. The museum also hosts the National Anthropological Archives.
