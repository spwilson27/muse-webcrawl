Hans von Ohain

Born14 December 1911, Dessau, German Empire
Died13 March 1998 (aged 86), Melbourne, Florida, US
FieldsPhysics, aerospace engineering
Known forCo-inventor of the jet engine; designer of the first operational turbojet

Hans Joachim Pabst von Ohain (14 December 1911 – 13 March 1998) was a German physicist and engineer who was one of the inventors of jet propulsion. Working at the Heinkel aircraft company, he designed the engine that powered the Heinkel He 178, the world's first turbojet aircraft, which flew on 27 August 1939. He and the British engineer Frank Whittle, working independently, are jointly credited with inventing the jet engine, and shared the Charles Stark Draper Prize in 1991.

Early life and the first engine

Ohain earned a doctorate in physics and aerodynamics from the University of Göttingen in 1935. While still a student he conceived a gas turbine engine with a centrifugal compressor and turbine mounted back-to-back on a single shaft, and patented the design in 1936. His professor Robert Pohl introduced him to aircraft manufacturer Ernst Heinkel, who funded development.

The He 178 and wartime work

Ohain's first experimental engine, the HeS 1, ran on hydrogen in March 1937; the gasoline-burning HeS 3 followed, and a developed version powered the purpose-built He 178 on its historic first flight, piloted by Erich Warsitz, days before the outbreak of the Second World War. Ohain went on to lead development of the HeS 8, intended for the Heinkel He 280 fighter, but the engine's difficulties and official preference for rival programmes at Junkers and BMW kept his designs from production; the first operational jet fighter, the Messerschmitt Me 262, flew with Junkers Jumo 004 engines.

Post-war career

In 1947 Ohain was brought to the United States under Operation Paperclip. He worked at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio, becoming chief scientist of the Aero Propulsion Laboratory and publishing research on fluid mechanics, magnetohydrodynamics, and optical energy storage. He met Whittle after the war and the two became friends; joint honours included the Draper Prize of the National Academy of Engineering in 1991. Ohain retired in 1979 and died in Florida in 1998.
