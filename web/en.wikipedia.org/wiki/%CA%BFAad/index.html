ʻAad

For the ancient Arabian tribe described in Islamic tradition, see the people of ʿĀd (Arabic: عَادٌ), the community to whom the prophet Hud was sent. The name is rendered in transliteration with variant capitalisation of the second letter, reflecting the Arabic emphatic consonant and the ʿayn at the start of the word.

Summary

The ʿĀd were said to descend from ʿĀd ibn ʿAws, of the line of Shem son of Noah, and to have dwelt in the winding sands (al-Aḥqāf) of the southern Arabian Peninsula after the time of Thamud — or, in other accounts, before them. Renowned in legend for their stature and their many-pillared city of Iram, they were destroyed, according to the Quran, by a furious wind (rīḥ ṣarṣar) after refusing Hud's preaching. Their fate is cited repeatedly as a warning to later peoples.

Genealogy and lore

Arab genealogists counted the ʿĀd among the "perished Arabs" (al-ʿArab al-bāʾida) together with Thamud, Tasm and Jadis. Pre-Islamic poetry and South Arabian inscriptions have been searched for traces of them, but no certain historical attestation has been established.
