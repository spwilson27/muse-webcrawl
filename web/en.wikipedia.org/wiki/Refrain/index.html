Refrain


A repeated refrain in sheet music.

A refrain (from Vulgar Latin refringere, "to repeat", via Old French refrain) is the line or lines that are repeated in music or in poetry, usually after every stanza. In popular music the refrain is often called the chorus, and is contrasted with the verse.

In music

The refrain is the most memorable part of a song: a fixed block of text and melody that returns between verses. In the standard verse–chorus form of popular song, the chorus carries the song's title and central idea. Related repetition devices include the ritornello of Baroque music and the burden of medieval song.

In poetry

Refrains are characteristic of ballads and of fixed forms such as the villanelle, triolet, and rondeau, where whole lines recur at set intervals. Dylan Thomas's "Do not go gentle into that good night" uses its two refrain lines to structure the entire villanelle.

Other uses

By extension, "refrain" denotes any often-repeated phrase or complaint in speech or writing—"a familiar refrain."
