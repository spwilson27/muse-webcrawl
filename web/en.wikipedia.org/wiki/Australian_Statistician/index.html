Australian Statistician

The Australian Statistician is the head of the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), Australia's national statistical agency. The Statistician is the Commonwealth's chief statistician, responsible for the Census of Population and Housing, the production of official demographic, economic, social and environmental statistics, and the independence and integrity of the Australian statistical system. The officeholder is appointed by the Governor-General, normally for a term of up to five years, under the Australian Bureau of Statistics Act 1975.

History

The position descends from the Commonwealth Statistician, created when the Commonwealth Bureau of Census and Statistics was established under the Census and Statistics Act 1905. George Handley Knibbs, the first Commonwealth Statistician (1906–1921), built the federal statistical service and ran the first national census in 1911. Notable successors include Roland Wilson and William McLennan, the first to hold the modern title of Australian Statistician after the ABS Act 1975 made the Bureau a statutory authority independent of ministerial direction on statistical matters.

Role and independence

The Statistician determines — by law, independently of the responsible minister — the procedures and content of statistical collections, and decides when and how statistics are released. This statutory independence, including the Statistician's sole authority to authorise the release of data collected under the Census and Statistics Act and strict secrecy provisions, is regarded as foundational to public trust in official statistics. The Statistician also serves as Australia's representative in international statistical forums, including the United Nations Statistical Commission.
