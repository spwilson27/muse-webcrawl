Catalan's conjecture (aliquot sequences)

Catalan's conjecture in the theory of aliquot sequences (not to be confused with Catalan's conjecture on perfect powers, now Mihăilescu's theorem) states that every aliquot sequence eventually terminates, either by reaching a prime number followed by 1, by reaching a perfect number or amicable or sociable cycle, and never grows without bound.

Background

An aliquot sequence is generated by repeatedly replacing a positive integer n by the sum of its proper divisors, s(n). A sequence terminates if it reaches 1; it enters a cycle if it reaches a perfect number (cycle of length 1), an amicable pair (length 2) or a sociable cycle (length > 2). Eugène Charles Catalan conjectured in the nineteenth century that no sequence can increase indefinitely.

Status

The conjecture remains unproven. The smallest starting number whose fate is unknown is 276, whose sequence has been computed to thousands of digits without terminating or cycling. The related Cunningham-style searches have verified that all sequences starting below large bounds terminate, except for a small set of unresolved "Lehmer" numbers including 276, 552, 564, 660 and 966.
