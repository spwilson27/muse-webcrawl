Lanarkshire

Lanarkshire, also called the County of Lanark (Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Lannraig), is a historic county, registration county and lieutenancy area in Scotland. It lies in the central Lowlands and is bounded by Renfrewshire, Dunbartonshire, Stirlingshire, Linlithgowshire, Midlothian, Peeblesshire, Dumfriesshire and Ayrshire.

History

Lanarkshire was historically divided into three wards: the upper, middle and nether wards, corresponding roughly to the valleys of the River Clyde. The county town was Lanark, although the seat of government later moved to Hamilton. The county council was based at Lanark County Buildings in Hamilton from 1914. The county was abolished for local government purposes in 1975 and replaced by districts within the Strathclyde region.

Geography

The county is dominated by the valley of the River Clyde, which flows from the Southern Uplands in the south-east to the Firth of Clyde at Glasgow. The largest settlement is Glasgow, Scotland's biggest city, which historically grew as a separate county of a city. Other towns include Motherwell, Hamilton, Coatbridge, Airdrie, Wishaw, East Kilbride and Rutherglen.

Economy

During the Industrial Revolution Lanarkshire became a centre of coal mining, iron and steel making, centred on the Lanarkshire coalfield and towns such as Motherwell. Textiles, shipbuilding on the Clyde and heavy engineering were also major employers before deindustrialisation in the later twentieth century.
