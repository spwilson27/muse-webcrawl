Master keying
MechanismPin tumbler with master wafers
ApplicationsHotels, schools, offices, housing

Master keying is a system of lock keying in which a group of locks, each operated by its own individual key, can also all be opened by a single master key. It is commonly used in hotels, schools, offices, and apartment buildings to balance individual security with managerial access.

How it works

In pin-tumbler locks, master keying adds extra pin segments (master wafers) creating two valid shear lines in each lock: one for the change key and one shared by the master key. Hierarchies can extend to grand-master and great-grand-master keys controlling ever larger groups.

Trade-offs

Master-keyed systems sacrifice some security—the more keys that open a lock, the more vulnerable it is to picking and to unauthorized key derivation—so they are planned carefully with key-control records.
