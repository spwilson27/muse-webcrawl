Colfax County War

The Maxwell Land Grant country in north-eastern New Mexico, scene of the Colfax County War.

The Colfax County War was a land dispute and series of violent clashes in Colfax County, New Mexico Territory, between roughly 1873 and 1888. It pitted settlers and squatters living on the vast Maxwell Land Grant against the grant's new corporate owners, the Maxwell Land Grant and Railway Company, which sought to evict them.

Background

The Maxwell Land Grant, confirmed at some 1.7 million acres, was sold in 1870 to a syndicate with British and Dutch backing. Many resident farmers and miners, who believed they held rights from the original Mexican grant or by settlement, resisted eviction notices. A local leader of the settlers' cause was the Reverend Franklin J. Tolby, whose murder in September 1875 ignited open violence.

Violence

The conflict involved assassinations, lynchings, courthouse raids (including the scattering of county records), and gunfights involving figures such as Clay Allison, a gunman who sided with the settlers. Estimates of the death toll vary widely, from about 20 to as many as 200 over the course of the troubles. Federal troops were at times deployed, and the territorial governor Samuel Axtell was removed in part over the affair's entanglement with the Santa Fe Ring.

Resolution

The company's title was confirmed by the U.S. Supreme Court in 1887, and the grant's ejection suits were largely upheld in subsequent litigation, though many settlers eventually purchased their farms or left. The war remains a classic case study of western land-grant conflicts.
