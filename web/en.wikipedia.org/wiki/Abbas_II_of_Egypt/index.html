Abbas Helmy II (14 July 1874 – 19 December 1944) was the last Khedive of Egypt and the Sudan, reigning from 1892 to 1914. His reign coincided with the British occupation of Egypt, and his attempts to assert independence from British control defined his rule.

Early life and accession

The eldest son of Khedive Tewfik, Abbas was educated in Lausanne and at the Theresianum in Vienna. He succeeded his father while still a teenager, inheriting a country effectively administered by the British agent and consul-general, Lord Cromer.

Conflict with Britain

Young and nationalistic, Abbas clashed with Cromer over appointments and the army. The 1894 "Frontier Incident", in which he criticised British-officered Egyptian troops, forced a humiliating retraction. He quietly funded the emerging nationalist movement of Mustafa Kamil and maintained contacts with the Ottoman sultan, Egypt's nominal suzerain.

Later reign and deposition

After Cromer's departure in 1907, relations with Britain eased but nationalist pressure grew. When the First World War began in 1914, Abbas was in Constantinople recovering from an assassination attempt. Britain declared a protectorate over Egypt, deposed him on 19 December 1914, and installed his uncle Hussein Kamel as Sultan.

Exile and death

Abbas lived in exile, chiefly in Switzerland, intriguing for restoration until formally renouncing his claims. He died in Geneva in 1944, the last surviving ruler of the Muhammad Ali dynasty to have governed Egypt in the khedivial line.
