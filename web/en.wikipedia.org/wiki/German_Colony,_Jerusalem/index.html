German Colony, Jerusalem

Location South-central Jerusalem, south of the Old City
Founded 1873
Founded by German Templers
Main street Emek Refaim Street

The German Colony (Hebrew: המושבה הגרמנית, HaMoshava HaGermanit) is a historic neighborhood in Jerusalem, established in 1873 by members of the German Templar Society (Temple Society), a pietistic Protestant movement from Württemberg that settled in Ottoman Palestine in the 19th century. Today it is an affluent residential and commercial district, known for its distinctive stone architecture and its main street, Emek Refaim, lined with cafés, restaurants, and boutiques.

History

The Templers bought land in the Refaim Valley southwest of the Old City in 1873, after first settling near Jaffa and Haifa (where they founded the German Colony of Haifa in 1868). They built one- and two-storey stone houses in a style blending Swabian village architecture with local elements — red-tiled roofs, thick stone walls, shutters, and biblical inscriptions over doorways — along what became Emek Refaim Street. The community established a school, workshops, and farms, and benefited from the Jerusalem–Jaffa railway (1892), whose station stood nearby.

During the Second World War, the British Mandate authorities, treating the German residents as enemy aliens, deported many to Australia (where some were interned at Tatura) and expropriated Templer property. After 1948 the neighborhood in West Jerusalem was settled by Jewish residents, including veterans and immigrants; the Templers' remaining property claims were addressed in later agreements between Israel and West Germany.

Today

The German Colony is among Jerusalem's most desirable neighborhoods. Many original Templer houses survive, protected by preservation rules, alongside later Bauhaus and modern buildings. Emek Refaim is a major leisure and dining street; landmarks include the International Christian Embassy building, the former Templer community hall, the old railway station complex (First Station) just to the north, and the nearby Liberty Bell Park and Khan Theatre. Together with adjacent Baka, Greek Colony, and Katamon, it forms a belt of garden suburbs built outside the Old City walls in the late Ottoman period.
