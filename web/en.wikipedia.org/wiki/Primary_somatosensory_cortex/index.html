Primary somatosensory cortex

The primary somatosensory cortex (S1) is a region of the cerebral cortex located in the postcentral gyrus of the parietal lobe. It is the main sensory receptive area for the sense of touch, receiving thalamic projections conveying information about touch, proprioception, pain, and temperature from the contralateral side of the body.

Anatomy

S1 comprises Brodmann areas 3a, 3b, 1, and 2, arranged from anterior to posterior across the postcentral gyrus. Area 3b is considered the primary area proper, receiving dense input from the ventroposterior nucleus of the thalamus. Area 3a, in the depth of the central sulcus, responds mainly to proprioceptive input, while areas 1 and 2 process progressively more complex cutaneous and proprioceptive features.

Somatotopy

Neurons in S1 are organized somatotopically: adjacent body parts are represented in adjacent cortical territory, forming the distorted map known as the sensory homunculus described by Wilder Penfield. Body parts with fine tactile discrimination, such as the fingertips and lips, occupy disproportionately large cortical areas compared with the trunk or limbs.

Function and clinical relevance

S1 is essential for perceiving texture, shape, size, and vibration and for sensorimotor integration. Lesions of the postcentral gyrus produce contralateral deficits in tactile discrimination, stereognosis, and proprioception, while sparing crude touch to a degree. Phantom limb phenomena and cortical reorganization after amputation have been extensively studied in relation to S1 plasticity.
