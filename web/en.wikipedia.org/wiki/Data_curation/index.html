Data curation

Data curation is the organization and integration of data collected from various sources. It involves annotation, publication, and presentation of the data such that its value is maintained over time and the data remains available for reuse and preservation. Data curators enable data discovery and retrieval, maintain data quality, add value, and provide support for reuse by researchers and organizations.

Activities

Data curation encompasses the active and ongoing management of data through its lifecycle: appraisal and selection, ingest, metadata creation, quality assurance, transformation and enrichment, access management, and long-term preservation. The Digital Curation Centre's lifecycle model describes stages from creation and appraisal through storage and reuse. The FAIR principles — Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable — published in 2016, provide widely adopted guidelines for good data curation practice.

Context

The term emerged from the sciences, where large datasets from instruments and simulations required professional stewardship, and is now used across libraries, archives, museums, and industry. In machine learning, data curation also refers to the careful selection, cleaning, and labelling of training datasets, which strongly influence model quality.
