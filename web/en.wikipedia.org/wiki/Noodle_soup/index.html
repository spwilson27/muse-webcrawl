Noodle soup

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A bowl of noodle soup served with chopsticks.

Noodle soup refers to a variety of soups in which noodles are a principal ingredient, served in a flavoured broth, often with meat and vegetables. Noodle soups are staples of many cuisines, particularly in East, Southeast and Central Asia, but versions also exist in Europe and the Americas. Common features include a hot broth — clear or rich, spiced or mild — and noodles made from wheat, rice, buckwheat, egg or starch.

Varieties

East Asia

  • Ramen (Japan): wheat noodles in a rich broth — shoyu, miso, shio or tonkotsu — topped with chashu pork, nori, scallions and seasoned egg.
  • Udon and soba: thick wheat and buckwheat noodles respectively, served in a dashi-based broth.
  • Lamian (China): hand-pulled wheat noodles; Lanzhou beef noodle soup, with clear spiced broth, beef, white radish and chilli oil, is among the most famous.
  • Wonton noodles: thin egg noodles with shrimp or pork dumplings, especially associated with Cantonese cuisine and Hong Kong.

Southeast Asia

  • Phở (Vietnam): flat rice noodles in a star-anise-spiced beef or chicken broth with herbs, bean sprouts and lime.
  • Laksa (Malaysia/Singapore): noodles in a spicy coconut-curry broth with seafood or chicken.
  • Khao soi (northern Thailand): egg noodles in a coconut curry broth, topped with crispy fried noodles.

Elsewhere

  • Chicken noodle soup (North America/Europe): egg noodles in chicken broth with vegetables, a classic comfort food and cold remedy.
  • Lagman (Central Asia): hand-pulled noodles in a meat and vegetable soup or sauce, of Uyghur origin.
  • Soto mie, thukpa (Tibet/Nepal) and Korean kal-guksu are further regional examples.

Instant noodle soup

The invention of instant noodles by Momofuku Ando in 1958 — flash-fried noodles sold with powdered soup, first as Chicken Ramen — turned noodle soup into a global convenience food, with tens of billions of servings consumed annually worldwide.
