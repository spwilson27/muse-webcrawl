Final Fantasy XII

Publisher: Square Enix
Director: Hiroyuki Ito, Hiroshi Minagawa

Platforms: PlayStation 2, PlayStation 4, Windows, Nintendo Switch, Xbox One
Release: March 16, 2006 (JP)
Genre: Role-playing
Series: Final Fantasy

Final Fantasy XII is a role-playing video game developed and published by Square Enix for the PlayStation 2. Released in 2006, it is the twelfth main instalment of the Final Fantasy series. The game introduced several innovations to the franchise, including an open world, a controllable camera, and the "Active Dimension Battle" system, which replaced random encounters with seamless real-time combat on the field map.

Gameplay

Players control a party of up to three characters in battle, with additional guest characters joining at times. The Gambit system allows players to program party members' actions in response to specified conditions, such as attacking the nearest enemy or healing an ally below a set health threshold. Character growth is governed by the License Board, a grid of licenses that unlock abilities, equipment, and stat boosts, purchased with License Points earned in battle.

Plot

The game takes place in the world of Ivalice, where the kingdoms of Archadia and Rozarria are waging an extended war. Caught between them is the small kingdom of Dalmasca. The story follows Vaan, a young orphan of Rabanastre, and Princess Ashe, heir to the Dalmascan throne, as they join a resistance movement against the Archadian Empire and confront the ambitions of Vayne Solidor and the mysterious beings known as the Occuria.

Development and release

Development was led by Yasumi Matsuno until he left the project partway through due to health concerns; Hiroyuki Ito and Hiroshi Minagawa took over as directors. A remastered version, Final Fantasy XII: The Zodiac Age, based on the Japan-only International Zodiac Job System release, was published in 2017 for PlayStation 4 and later ported to other platforms.

Reception

The game received critical acclaim for its graphics, world design, and battle system, and sold more than six million copies on PlayStation 2. It won several Game of the Year awards and is frequently cited among the best entries in the series.
