Texture (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In music, texture is how the melodic, rhythmic, and harmonic materials are combined in a composition, determining the overall quality of the sound in a piece. It is often described in terms of density — how many layers of sound are heard at once — and the relationships among those layers.

Types of texture

The standard categories are: monophony, a single melodic line without accompaniment (e.g., plainchant); homophony, one dominant melody supported by chords, including its rhythmic variant homorhythm; polyphony (counterpoint), two or more independent melodic lines of roughly equal importance; heterophony, simultaneous variations of the same melody; and biphony, a drone combined with a melody. Texture may change within a work and is a central analytical consideration in describing style from medieval organum to contemporary composition.

Description

Texture is commonly characterized with adjectives such as thick or thin, dense or transparent, and analyzed by identifying the number and roles of voices or parts, their ranges, and how active each layer is.
