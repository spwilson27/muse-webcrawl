Nastaliq

Nastaʿlīq (نستعلیق) is one of the main calligraphic hands used in writing the Perso-Arabic script, and traditionally the predominant style in Persian calligraphy. It developed in Iran in the 14th and 15th centuries by combining the naskh and taʿliq scripts; its invention is traditionally attributed to the calligrapher Mir Ali Tabrizi. The name is a contraction of naskh and taʿlīq ("hanging").

Characteristics

Nastaliq is famous for its fluid, cascading appearance: words descend diagonally from upper right to lower left, letters vary in vertical depth, and each word is written as an unbroken flowing unit. These features give it great beauty but make it exceptionally difficult for movable type and digital typesetting; Urdu printing long relied on lithography and hand-lettered newspapers, and computer fonts for Nastaliq (such as Noto Nastaliq Urdu) required sophisticated shaping engines.

Usage

Nastaliq is the standard script for Urdu and is widely used for Persian literary and artistic calligraphy, as well as for Kashmiri, Punjabi (Shahmukhi) and Ottoman Turkish poetry. In Iran itself, ordinary Persian print uses naskh-derived typefaces, with Nastaliq reserved for poetry and display.
