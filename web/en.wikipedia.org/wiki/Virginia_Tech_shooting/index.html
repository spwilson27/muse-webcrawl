The Virginia Tech shooting was a mass shooting that occurred on April 16, 2007, at Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University in Blacksburg, Virginia, United States. Seung-Hui Cho, a 23-year-old undergraduate student at the university, killed 32 people and wounded 17 others in two separate attacks before taking his own life. It was the deadliest school shooting in United States history at the time.

Attacks

The first attack took place shortly after 7:00 a.m. in West Ambler Johnston Hall, a co-educational residence hall, where Cho shot two students. Roughly two hours later — during which Cho returned to his room, changed clothes, and mailed a package of writings and video recordings to NBC News — he entered Norris Hall, an engineering building, chained the main doors shut, and moved through classrooms shooting students and faculty. Among the dead was Holocaust survivor and professor Liviu Librescu, who barricaded his classroom door to allow his students to escape through windows. The attack ended when Cho shot himself as police breached the building.

Perpetrator

Cho was a senior English major who had emigrated from South Korea as a child. He had a documented history of mental health difficulties, including selective mutism and depression, and had been declared mentally ill by a Virginia court in 2005 and ordered to seek outpatient treatment. His creative writing had disturbed classmates and teachers. The fact that he nevertheless purchased firearms after passing background checks exposed a gap in the reporting of mental-health adjudications to the federal database.

Aftermath and legacy

The university held a convocation the following day at which President George W. Bush spoke, and poet Nikki Giovanni delivered a widely remembered address. An official review panel appointed by Virginia governor Tim Kaine criticised university officials' handling of the interval between the two attacks and the mental-health system's failures. The shooting led to changes in Virginia law on background checks and campus safety, heightened national debate over gun control and campus security, and the creation of the April 16 memorial of 32 stones on the Drillfield. The massacre remained the deadliest mass shooting in modern U.S. history until the 2016 Pulse nightclub shooting and the 2017 Las Vegas shooting surpassed it.
