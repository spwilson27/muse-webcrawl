Moustiers-Sainte-Marie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Moustiers-Sainte-Marie and the star suspended between its cliffs.

Moustiers-Sainte-Marie (Occitan: Mostiers Santa Maria) is a commune in the Alpes-de-Haute-Provence department in southeastern France, near the entrance of the Verdon Gorge. It is a member of the Les Plus Beaux Villages de France ("The most beautiful villages of France") association. The village, home to roughly 700 inhabitants, sits between two towering limestone cliffs through which a stream and waterfall run.

The star

Above the village, a gold-painted star hangs on a chain some 225 metres long stretched between the cliffs. According to legend recounted by Provençal poet Frédéric Mistral, the chain and star were placed there in the 10th century by the knight Bozon de Blacas, who had vowed, while captured during the Crusades, to hang a star in honour of St Mary if he returned home. The star and chain have been replaced several times; the present star dates from 1957 and the chain from 1882.

Faience

Moustiers became famous in the 17th and 18th centuries for fine tin-glazed earthenware — faïence de Moustiers — produced by workshops such as those of the Clérissy family, decorated in blue, and later polychrome, with lace-like dentelle motifs, grotesques, and hunting scenes. The craft declined in the 19th century but was revived in the 20th; workshops and a museum of faience operate in the village today. The Romanesque parish church of Notre-Dame-de-l'Assomption, with its Lombard-style bell tower, stands at the village centre.
