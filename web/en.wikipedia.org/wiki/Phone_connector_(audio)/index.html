Phone connector (audio)


A phone connector is a family of cylindrical electrical connectors originally used in telephone switchboards, now ubiquitous for audio signals.

A phone connector is a common type of electrical connector used for analog audio signals. It takes the form of a cylindrical plug divided into conductive sections separated by insulating bands; the letters T, R and S in designations such as TS and TRS denote the tip, ring and sleeve contacts.

History

The design dates to the manual telephone switchboards of the late 19th century — a version was patented in 1878 in connection with switchboards in Boston. The original size, 1⁄4 inch (6.35 mm), remains standard for electric guitars, amplifiers, professional audio equipment and patch bays.

Sizes and configurations

The 3.5 mm "miniature" version became the standard headphone connector for portable audio players, phones and computers; a 2.5 mm "sub-miniature" size was used on some mobile phone headsets and cameras. A two-conductor TS plug carries unbalanced mono audio; a three-conductor TRS plug carries stereo or balanced mono audio; TRRS plugs add a fourth contact, used for a microphone channel and remote-control signalling on headsets. Contact assignments, especially for TRRS, vary between the OMTP and CTIA conventions.

The connector persists despite competition from USB-C and wireless audio, and remains the standard on musical instruments and professional equipment.
