Jane Jacobs

Jane Jacobs (née Butzner; 4 May 1916 – 25 April 2006) was an American-born Canadian author and activist whose 1961 book The Death and Life of Great American Cities revolutionised urban planning thought, advocating dense, mixed-use, walkable neighbourhoods and criticising mid-century urban renewal.

Urban ideas

Jacobs championed "eyes on the street" — natural surveillance by residents — and short blocks, mixed primary uses, and building diversity as generators of city life. She led opposition to Robert Moses's proposed Lower Manhattan Expressway, which would have demolished her Greenwich Village neighbourhood.

Toronto years

She moved to Toronto in 1968 and helped defeat the Spadina Expressway, becoming an influential voice in Canadian urban policy. Her later works include The Economy of Cities (1969) and Dark Age Ahead (2004). Her birthday, 4 May, is marked by Jane's Walk events worldwide.
