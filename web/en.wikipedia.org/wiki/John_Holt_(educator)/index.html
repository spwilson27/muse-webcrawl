John Caldwell Holt (14 April 1923 – 14 September 1985) was an American author and educator, a leading figure in school reform and the founder of the modern homeschooling movement. His books How Children Fail (1964) and How Children Learn (1967), based on close observation of his own classrooms, argued that children fail not from inability but from fear, boredom and confusion produced by schooling itself. In his later work he concluded that schools could not be adequately reformed and championed learning outside school — the approach he called "unschooling."

Early life

Holt was born in New York City, attended Phillips Exeter Academy, and graduated from Yale in 1943. He served in the U.S. Navy aboard submarines during the Second World War, then worked for the world federalist movement before becoming an elementary school teacher in Colorado and later in Boston. A gifted cellist, he performed throughout his life and wrote Never Too Late (1978) about taking up music seriously in his forties.

How Children Fail

Drawing on a classroom diary kept with his colleague Bill Hull, Holt analysed how bright children adopt strategies — guessing, bluffing, avoiding risk — to get through school without understanding. How Children Fail became a bestseller and a manifesto of 1960s school reform, influencing the open-classroom movement. How Children Learn (1967) described the natural, exploratory learning of very young children before school. Later books include The Underachieving School (1969), Freedom and Beyond (1972) and Escape from Childhood (1974), which argued provocatively for extending adult rights, including the vote, to children.

Homeschooling and unschooling

Convinced by experience that institutional reform was futile, in 1977 Holt founded the newsletter Growing Without Schooling, the first periodical for homeschoolers in the United States, and in Teach Your Own (1981) provided the movement's founding text. He coined the term "unschooling" for child-led learning woven into everyday life rather than delivered as a curriculum. Holt also travelled and lectured widely, supported legal challenges on behalf of homeschooling families, and continued the newsletter until his death from cancer in Boston in 1985. His work remains central to alternative education worldwide.
