Termination of employment

Termination of employment is an employee's departure from a job, ending the employment relationship. It may be voluntary — resignation or retirement — or involuntary, when the employer dismisses the worker (firing, layoff, redundancy) or the contract expires. "Dismissal" strictly refers to employer-initiated termination, often for cause such as misconduct or poor performance.

Forms

Layoffs end jobs for business reasons (downsizing, closure) rather than the worker's fault; redundancy in Commonwealth usage means the role itself is eliminated. Constructive dismissal occurs when an employer's conduct effectively forces resignation. Many jurisdictions require notice periods or pay in lieu, severance payments, and a fair process; wrongful-dismissal and unfair-dismissal law restricts arbitrary firing.

Employment at will

In most of the United States, employment is presumed "at will": either party may end it at any time for any lawful reason, subject to statutory exceptions (discrimination, retaliation) and public-policy limits. Most other OECD countries require just cause and advance notice.
