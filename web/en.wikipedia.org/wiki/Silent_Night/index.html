"Silent Night" (German: "Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht") is one of the world's best-known Christmas carols. The lyrics were written in 1816 by the Austrian priest Joseph Mohr, and the melody was composed in 1818 by Franz Xaver Gruber, a schoolteacher and church organist. The carol was first performed on Christmas Eve 1818 at St Nicholas Church in Oberndorf bei Salzburg, with Mohr and Gruber singing to guitar accompaniment. It has been translated into more than 140 languages and was declared an intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO in 2011.

History

Mohr wrote the poem "Stille Nacht" in 1816 at Mariapfarr. Two years later, as assistant priest at Oberndorf, he asked Gruber to set it to music for two voices, choir and guitar. Later legend attributed the guitar to a broken church organ damaged by mice or flooding; the true reasons are unrecorded. The Strasser and Rainer singing families from the Ziller valley carried the song through the German-speaking lands; by the 1840s it was sung in Berlin and New York, its authorship forgotten and variously ascribed to Haydn, Mozart or Beethoven before research in the 1850s–90s restored Mohr and Gruber.

Text and translations

Mohr's original has six verses; most modern versions sing three (usually the first, sixth and second verses of the original). John Freeman Young's 1859 English translation supplies the familiar "Silent night, holy night / All is calm, all is bright". The carol figured in the Christmas truce of 1914, sung simultaneously in German and English across no man's land, and Bing Crosby's 1935 recording became one of the best-selling singles of all time.

Legacy

The original Oberndorf church was demolished after flood damage; the Silent Night Chapel, built on its site in 1937, and the Silent Night Museum draw pilgrims each Christmas Eve, when the carol is sung there in many languages in the original two-voice-and-guitar arrangement.
