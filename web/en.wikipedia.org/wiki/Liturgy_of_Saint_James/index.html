Liturgy of Saint James

The Liturgy of Saint James is the ancient eucharistic rite of the Church of Jerusalem.

The Divine Liturgy of Saint James is a form of the Christian eucharistic liturgy traditionally attributed to James the Just, the "brother of the Lord" and first bishop of Jerusalem. It is the historic rite of the Church of Jerusalem and the mother liturgy of the Antiochene family of rites. In Greek and Syriac forms it underlies the West Syriac liturgies used by the Syriac Orthodox, Syro-Malankara, and Maronite churches.

History

The liturgy's core is genuinely ancient: its anaphora reflects the worship of fourth-century Jerusalem described by Cyril of Jerusalem in his Mystagogical Catecheses, and parallels in the Apostolic Constitutions. The surviving texts accumulated prayers over centuries, so the present form cannot be James's own composition, but tradition in Jerusalem maintained his name for the rite. In the Byzantine world it was largely displaced by the Liturgies of Saint Basil and Saint John Chrysostom, surviving in occasional use on James's feast day (23 October in the Greek calendar) and in a few places, notably Jerusalem and the island of Zakynthos.

Structure

Notable features include an elaborate preparation of the gifts, the singing of the hymn "Let all mortal flesh keep silence" in place of the Cherubic Hymn during the Great Entrance, an anaphora with an extended epiclesis invoking the Holy Spirit, intercessions for the whole Church, and communion given with the words "The holy Body of our Lord Jesus Christ..." addressed individually. The rite also preserves the practice of the celebrant washing his hands with a public prayer of unworthiness and a distinctive formula before the Lord's Prayer.

Modern use

Interest in the liturgy revived in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries through scholarly editions and translations. It is now celebrated annually by some Eastern Orthodox parishes, by Eastern Catholic churches of the Antiochene tradition, and occasionally in Western contexts of liturgical renewal. Its Syriac descendant, the Liturgy of Saint James of the West Syriac Rite, remains in regular use through its dozens of derived anaphoras.
