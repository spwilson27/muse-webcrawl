Telegraph Creek

Telegraph Creek is a small community in northwestern British Columbia, Canada, located on the Stikine River. It is the only permanent settlement on the Stikine and serves as a gateway to the river's canyon country. The community takes its name from the Collins Overland Telegraph project of the 1860s.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Tahltan First Nation. In the 1860s the Western Union Telegraph Company surveyed a route through the valley for a proposed telegraph line to Europe via Siberia; although the project was abandoned, the name remained. Gold rushes on the Stikine and later the Cassiar district made Telegraph Creek a supply point, and Hudson's Bay Company posts operated nearby.

Geography and access

Telegraph Creek lies at the head of navigation of the Stikine River, roughly 130 kilometres from the Alaska border. A steep gravel road, the Telegraph Creek Road, connects it to Dease Lake on Highway 37. The surrounding Grand Canyon of the Stikine is renowned among whitewater paddlers.
