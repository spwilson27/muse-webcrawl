Manitoba Hydro is the electric power and natural gas utility in the province of Manitoba, Canada. Founded in 1961, it is a provincial Crown corporation, governed by the Manitoba Hydro-Electric Board and the Manitoba Hydro Act. Today the company operates 16 interconnected generating stations, and has more than 527,000 electric customers and more than 263,000 natural gas customers.

Almost all of Manitoba Hydro's electricity is generated from water power: its generating stations are located principally on the Nelson River and the Winnipeg River in northern and eastern Manitoba. The utility's total generating capacity is roughly 6,000 megawatts, and it is one of the largest exporters of electricity in Canada, selling surplus power to neighbouring provinces and to the United States Midwest.

History

Electric service in Manitoba began with private and municipal utilities in Winnipeg in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The Manitoba Hydro-Electric Board was created in 1949 to extend service to rural areas, and in 1961 the board merged with the Winnipeg Electric Company to form Manitoba Hydro, consolidating generation and distribution across the province.

Major developments followed on the Nelson River, including the Kettle, Long Spruce and Limestone generating stations, built to supply growing demand and export markets. More recent projects include the Wuskwatim generating station, developed in partnership with the Nisichawayasihk Cree Nation, and the Keeyask generating station on the lower Nelson River.

Operations

Manitoba Hydro's headquarters, Manitoba Hydro Place in downtown Winnipeg, opened in 2009 and was designed as one of the most energy-efficient office towers in North America. The corporation also distributes natural gas in southern Manitoba through its Centra Gas subsidiary, acquired in 1999.
