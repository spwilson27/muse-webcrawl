Cumans

Cumans
Also: Polovtsy, Kipchaks (western branch)
Region: Pontic–Caspian steppe
Language: Cuman (Turkic)
Period: 10th–14th centuries

The Cumans were a Turkic nomadic people who, with the Kipchaks, formed the Cuman–Kipchak confederation dominating the Eurasian steppe from the Black Sea to Central Asia between the 11th and 13th centuries. In Russian sources they are the Polovtsy.

History

Migrating westward, the Cumans raided and traded with Kievan Rus', Byzantium, Hungary and Bulgaria, and played a decisive role at the Battle of Kalka (1223) against the Mongols — though the confederation was crushed in the Mongol invasions of 1237–1240. Many fled to Hungary, where Cuman settlers received land in the Kunság region, and others to Bulgaria and Anatolia.

Culture and language

The Codex Cumanicus, a 14th-century manual for missionaries and merchants, records their Turkic language. They left carved stone statues (balbals) across the steppe, practised shamanism and later adopted Christianity and Islam. Descendants contributed to the ethnogenesis of Tatars, Kazakhs, Hungarians of Kunság and others; Mamluk sultans of Egypt such as Baybars had Kipchak-Cuman origins.
