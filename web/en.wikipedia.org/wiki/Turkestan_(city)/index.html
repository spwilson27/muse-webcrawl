Turkestan (Kazakh: Түркістан) is a city and the administrative centre of Turkestan Region in southern Kazakhstan, near the Syr Darya river. One of the oldest cities in Kazakhstan, it was historically known as Yasi and became a major centre of Islamic learning and pilgrimage in Central Asia. Since 2018 it has undergone rapid development as a spiritual and cultural capital of the Turkic world.

History

The oasis settlement of Yasi appears in sources from the 5th century. In the 12th century it became associated with the Sufi teacher Khoja Ahmed Yasawi, who settled and died there in 1166, making the city a pilgrimage destination sometimes called the "second Mecca" for Central Asian Muslims. Timur (Tamerlane) commissioned a monumental mausoleum over Yasawi's grave at the end of the 14th century; the unfinished but magnificent structure remains a masterpiece of Timurid architecture and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The city later came under the Kazakh Khanate, for which it served as a capital and the burial place of many khans. It was taken by the Russian Empire in 1864. In 2018, following the renaming of South Kazakhstan Region as Turkestan Region, the city became the regional capital, prompting large-scale construction of cultural, administrative, and tourist facilities.

Landmarks

The Mausoleum of Khoja Ahmed Yasawi dominates the old town, surrounded by the remains of the citadel and other Timurid-era monuments. The newer Keruen Saray complex and the Congress Hall reflect the city's 21st-century expansion.
