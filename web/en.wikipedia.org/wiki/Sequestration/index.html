Sequestration

Sequestration is a term used in several fields for the act of setting something apart, isolating it, or taking it into legal custody. Its precise meaning depends on context, including law, chemistry, medicine, and fiscal policy.

In law

In legal usage, sequestration is a court-ordered seizure of property to prevent its removal or waste, or the confiscation of assets. In Scots law it is also the term for personal bankruptcy proceedings.

In fiscal policy

In United States budget law, sequestration refers to automatic, across-the-board spending cuts that take effect when budget targets are exceeded, a mechanism introduced by the Gramm–Rudman–Hollings Act of 1985 and applied notably in 2013 under the Budget Control Act of 2011.

In science and medicine

In chemistry and environmental science, sequestration means capturing and storing a substance, as in carbon sequestration. In medicine, it can describe the isolation of part of an organ, such as a pulmonary sequestration in the lung, or the pooling of blood cells in the spleen during sickle cell crisis.
