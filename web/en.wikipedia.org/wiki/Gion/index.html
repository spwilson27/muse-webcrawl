Gion

Gion (祇園) is a district of Kyoto, Japan, originating as an entertainment district in the Sengoku period, in front of Yasaka Shrine (Gion Shrine). The district is internationally known for its traditional wooden machiya merchant houses and as the most famous geisha district in Japan, where geiko (Kyoto dialect for geisha) and maiko (apprentice geiko) entertain in ochaya tea houses.

Location: Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto, Japan
Known for: Geiko and maiko, machiya, Gion Matsuri

History

Gion began as lodging and refreshment houses serving pilgrims to Yasaka Shrine, and by the 18th century its tea houses were licensed to offer the services of geisha. The district's buildings were designed to evade sumptuary taxes: narrow frontages of 5–6 metres conceal deep lots extending far back from the street. Parts of Gion, especially Hanamikōji Street and the Shirakawa canal area, are now designated preservation districts.

Gion Matsuri

The district gives its name to the Gion Matsuri, the festival of Yasaka Shrine held throughout July and one of Japan's most famous festivals, noted for the Yamaboko Junko procession of enormous decorated floats. The festival originated in 869 as a rite to appease the gods during an epidemic.

Modern day

Gion remains a working geisha district with two hanamachi (geisha communities), Gion Kōbu and Gion Higashi. Tourism pressure in recent years has led to photography restrictions on private streets and campaigns to protect the privacy of residents and geiko.
