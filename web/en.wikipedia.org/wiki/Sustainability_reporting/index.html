Sustainability reporting

Sustainability reporting is the disclosure and communication of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) goals—as well as a company's progress towards them. It is a type of non-financial reporting that enables companies to convey their sustainability performance and impact, both positive and negative, to their stakeholders.

Frameworks and standards

A number of frameworks guide sustainability reporting. The Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) Standards, first released in 2000, are the most widely used. Other frameworks include the standards of the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB), the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD), and the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB), which issued its first standards in 2023. In the European Union, the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) made reporting mandatory for large companies, applying European Sustainability Reporting Standards.

Motivations and criticism

Companies report on sustainability to meet regulatory requirements, respond to investor and consumer demand for transparency, manage risk, and demonstrate corporate responsibility. Critics argue that voluntary sustainability reports can amount to greenwashing when disclosures are selective or unverified, and have pushed for standardized metrics and third-party assurance.
