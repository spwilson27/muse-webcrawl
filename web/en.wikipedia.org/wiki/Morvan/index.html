Morvan

Highest pointHaut-Folin, 901 m

The Morvan is a mountainous massif in east-central France, lying in the departments of Nièvre, Saône-et-Loire, Yonne, and Côte-d'Or in the region of Bourgogne-Franche-Comté. A granite outcrop forming one of the northern arms of the Massif Central, it is known for its forests, lakes, and sparse population.

Geography

The massif rises to 901 metres at Haut-Folin and is cut by the valleys of the Yonne, Cure, and Arroux rivers. Large reservoirs, including the Lac des Settons and Lac de Pannecière, were built in the 19th and 20th centuries partly to float timber downriver to Paris, a trade that once defined the region's economy.

History and culture

In antiquity the area lay near the oppidum of Bibracte, capital of the Aedui, on Mont Beuvray. The Morvan was a stronghold of the French Resistance during the Second World War. Much of the region has been protected since 1970 as the Morvan Regional Natural Park, one of France's first regional parks. The local Burgundian dialect, Morvandiau, retains distinctive Gallic-influenced features.
