Rubidium–strontium dating

TypeRadiometric dating method
Parent isotopeRubidium-87
Daughter isotopeStrontium-87
Half-lifeApprox. 49 billion years
Used onIgneous and metamorphic rocks and minerals

Rubidium–strontium dating is a radiometric dating method that estimates the age of rocks and minerals from the decay of rubidium-87 (87Rb) to strontium-87 (87Sr), with a half-life of about 49 billion years. It is particularly useful for very old igneous and metamorphic rocks.

Method

Because rubidium and strontium behave differently chemically, different minerals in a rock start with different Rb/Sr ratios but the same initial 87Sr/86Sr ratio. Over time 87Sr accumulates in proportion to the Rb content. Plotting samples on an isochron diagram — 87Sr/86Sr against 87Rb/86Sr — yields a straight line whose slope gives the age and whose intercept gives the initial strontium isotope composition, avoiding the need to assume that initial value.

Applications

The method has dated meteorites and ancient cratonic rocks, helping to establish the age of the Earth and the early history of the Moon. Because metamorphism can reset the system, Rb–Sr dates may record the time of a thermal event rather than original crystallisation.
