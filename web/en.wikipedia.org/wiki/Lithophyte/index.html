Lithophyte

Lithophytes are plants that grow on the surfaces of rocks or within their crevices. They are one of the ecological groups of plants distinguished by their substrate, alongside epiphytes (which grow on other plants) and terrestrial plants. The word derives from the Greek lithos, "stone", and phyton, "plant".

Types

Lithophytes are commonly divided into two groups:

  • Epilithic species grow on the surface of rock, exposed to the weather;
  • Endolithic species grow within cracks, fissures and pores of the rock, sometimes almost entirely concealed.

Some lithophytes are "obligate", occurring only on rock, while others are "facultative", growing on rock where competition or grazing excludes them from soil. Many orchids, bromeliads, ferns, mosses, liverworts and lichens are lithophytic; saxicolous lichens are among the most extreme examples, colonising bare cliff faces and gravestones.

Adaptations

Rock surfaces are harsh habitats: they offer little water storage, few nutrients, large temperature swings and high exposure to sun and wind. Lithophytes show adaptations such as thick, leathery or succulent leaves, water-storage tissues, dense root mats that wedge into fissures, and the ability to obtain nitrogen from rainwater, dust and the droppings of birds. Some secrete organic acids that slowly dissolve the rock surface, helping both to anchor the plant and to begin soil formation.

Ecology

Because they can colonise bare rock, lithophytes are pioneer species in primary succession — among the first organisms to occupy new lava flows, quarried faces and glacially scoured bedrock. Their accumulation of organic debris in crevices creates the first pockets of soil, enabling other plants to establish. Notable lithophytic floras occur on limestone pavements, inselbergs, cliff faces and the tepuis of South America.

Examples

  • Rock orchids such as Dendrobium kingianum of eastern Australia
  • Many Ficus species begin life as lithophytes on cliffs and walls
  • The "living stones" and cliff-dwelling succulents of southern Africa
  • Wall-rue (Asplenium ruta-muraria) and other spleenwort ferns on masonry
