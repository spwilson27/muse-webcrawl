Deal, Kent

Deal is a coastal town in Kent, England, which lies where the North Sea and the English Channel meet, eight miles north-east of Dover and eight miles south of Ramsgate. It is a former fishing, mining and garrison town whose history is closely linked to the anchorage known as the Downs. Nearby are Deal Castle and Walmer Castle, built by Henry VIII.

History

Deal was one of the Cinque Ports and a major naval anchorage; Julius Caesar is traditionally said to have landed nearby in 55 BC. Deal Castle, built 1539–40, is one of the Tudor coastal artillery forts. The town was a notorious centre for smuggling in the eighteenth century, and its boatmen were famous for salvage work on the Goodwin Sands.

The town today

Deal retains a long seafront, a pier opened in 1957, and a conservation area with many Georgian houses. The former Royal Marines barracks closed in the 1990s, and the town is now a quiet seaside resort and commuter town.
