Lactic acid is an organic acid with the formula CH3CH(OH)COOH, formally 2-hydroxypropanoic acid. It was first isolated in 1780 by the Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele from sour milk (hence the name, from Latin lac, "milk"). In solution it loses a proton to form the lactate ion. Lactic acid is chiral, existing as L-(+)-lactic acid, the form produced in animal tissue, and D-(−)-lactic acid.

Biological role

In animals, lactate is constantly produced from pyruvate by the enzyme lactate dehydrogenase during normal metabolism, and its production increases during vigorous exercise when oxygen supply limits oxidative phosphorylation. Contrary to a common belief, lactate itself does not cause the burning sensation or delayed muscle soreness; it is recycled to glucose in the liver via the Cori cycle or oxidised by other tissues. Rising lactate is, however, a marker of stress in the body, and lactic acidosis can occur in severe illness.

Fermentation and uses

Lactic acid bacteria such as Lactobacillus ferment sugars to lactic acid, giving yoghurt, kefir, sourdough, sauerkraut, kimchi and pickles their sour taste and preservation. Industrially, lactic acid is used as food additive E270 (acidity regulator and preservative), in cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, and as the monomer for polylactic acid (PLA), a biodegradable plastic.
