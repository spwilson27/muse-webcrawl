Sauvignon blanc is a green-skinned grape variety originating from western France, in the Loire Valley and Bordeaux regions. It produces a crisp, dry and aromatic white wine, and is one of the most widely planted wine grapes in the world. The name derives from the French sauvage ("wild") and blanc ("white").

History

Sauvignon blanc has been grown in the Loire and Bordeaux for centuries; in Bordeaux it is traditionally blended with Sémillon for both dry whites and the sweet wines of Sauternes. A natural crossing of Sauvignon blanc with Cabernet Franc in the 17th century gave rise to Cabernet Sauvignon. In the 1880s cuttings reached California, where Robert Mondavi's oak-aged "Fumé Blanc" style popularised the grape in the United States.

Styles

The grape's character depends strongly on climate. Cool-climate examples — Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé in the Loire, and Marlborough in New Zealand, whose pungent tropical style became a global phenomenon from the 1980s — show grassy, gooseberry and passionfruit aromas driven by methoxypyrazines and thiols. Warmer regions give riper melon and peach notes. Most Sauvignon blanc is fermented in stainless steel and drunk young, though Bordeaux examples may be barrel-aged.

Food pairing

Its high acidity makes it a classic match for goat's cheese, shellfish, sushi and herb-heavy dishes, and it is one of the few wines that pairs well with asparagus.
