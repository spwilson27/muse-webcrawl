Super Bowl LVII was the championship game of the 2022 NFL season, played on 12 February 2023 at State Farm Stadium in Glendale, Arizona. The Kansas City Chiefs defeated the Philadelphia Eagles 38–35, winning the franchise's third Super Bowl title.

The game

Both teams entered with 14–3 records as their conferences' top seeds, and both scored on their opening drives. Philadelphia, behind quarterback Jalen Hurts — who rushed for three touchdowns, threw for another, and ran in a two-point conversion in an outstanding performance — led 24–14 at halftime. Kansas City quarterback Patrick Mahomes, playing on a high ankle sprain aggravated during the game, led the Chiefs to score on every second-half possession: touchdown passes to Kadarius Toney and Skyy Moore, a Toney punt return of 65 yards (a Super Bowl record) setting up one score, and an Isiah Pacheco rushing touchdown. With the game tied 35–35, a controversial defensive holding call against Philadelphia cornerback James Bradberry on third down allowed the Chiefs to run down the clock, and Harrison Butker kicked the winning 27-yard field goal with eight seconds remaining.

Significance

Mahomes was named Super Bowl MVP for the second time, having also won the regular-season MVP — the first player to win both in the same season since Kurt Warner in 1999. The game was the first Super Bowl with two Black starting quarterbacks (Mahomes and Hurts) and featured the first brother-versus-brother matchup, between Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce and Eagles centre Jason Kelce, dubbed the "Kelce Bowl". Rihanna's halftime performance, during which she revealed her second pregnancy, drew enormous attention, and the Fox telecast averaged about 113 million viewers, then the second-most-watched programme in U.S. television history. The playing surface, a specially grown natural grass field, was widely criticised by players for being slippery.
