Doris (Greece)

Doris (Greek: Δωρίς) is a small mountainous district in central Greece, bounded by Aetolia, southern Thessaly, Ozolian Locris, and Phocis. It was the original homeland of the Dorian Greeks, who were believed in antiquity to have migrated from this region to conquer the Peloponnese. Doris lay in the valley of the upper Cephissus and the Pindus foothills, and was sometimes called Tetrapolis after its four towns: Boium, Cytinium, Erineus, and Pindus (also called Acyphas).

History

Despite its small size, Doris held religious significance as the metropolis of the Dorians, and it was a member of the Delphic Amphictyony. The district was repeatedly invaded because of its strategic position on routes between northern and southern Greece; it was ravaged during the Phocian occupation in the Third Sacred War and again in later conflicts. In the classical period the Dorian tetrapolis remained a minor but symbolically important state.
