Execution of Charles I

The execution of Charles I took place on Tuesday 30 January 1649 outside the Banqueting House in Whitehall, London. After being tried and convicted of high treason by a High Court of Justice set up by the Rump Parliament, Charles I was beheaded — an unprecedented public execution of a reigning English monarch, following the English Civil War.

Execution of Charles I
Date: 30 January 1649
Location: Whitehall, London
Charge: High treason

Trial

Charles refused to plead, denying the court's legitimacy. Fifty-nine commissioners signed his death warrant; they became known as the regicides.

Execution and legacy

Charles wore two shirts so he would not shiver and appear afraid, gave his final speech asserting he died a martyr of the people, and was executed with a single blow. The monarchy was abolished and England became the Commonwealth under Oliver Cromwell. After the Restoration, surviving regicides were tried and executed.
