Santeria

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Santeria was the name adopted by several musical acts, including American rock bands.

Santeria is the name — an unaccented rendering of the Spanish word Santería — borne by several musical acts and works of popular culture, distinct from the Afro-Cuban religion Santería.

Music

Santeria (American rock band). A rock band formed in San Diego, California, in the early 2010s, built around the brothers Brandon and Matt De La Cruz, released indie rock recordings and toured the southwestern United States before members moved on to other projects.

"Santeria" (song). "Santeria" is a well-known song by the American ska-punk band Sublime, released as a single in 1997 from their self-titled third album. Written by Bradley Nowell and drawing on reggae and dub influences, the song describes jealousy and revenge in a troubled love affair, using the title — after the religion — as a metaphor for a curse placed on a rival. It became one of Sublime's signature tracks and a staple of alternative radio.

The name also appears on albums and recordings by independent artists in the metal, hip-hop and electronic scenes, typically invoking the religion's aura of Afro-Caribbean ritual and magic.

Other uses

In publishing and film databases, "Santeria" without the accent is the standard ASCII filing for titles referring to the religion, and several horror and thriller works — including the novel Santeria and related screen treatments — use the spelling for stories built around Afro-Cuban ritual and crime.
