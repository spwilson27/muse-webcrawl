British Army Regimental System

Regimental system
Country: United Kingdom
Service: British Army
Origins: Cardwell Reforms, 1870s–1881

The regimental system is the method by which the British Army organises its infantry and some other arms. Under the system, soldiers are recruited into a regiment — typically associated with a county or region — and serve within its battalions for most of their careers, fostering strong unit identity and traditions.

History

The system took its modern form under the Cardwell and Childers Reforms of the 1870s and 1881, which paired regular battalions with militia and volunteer units in county regiments with a single depot and badge. Successive amalgamations, especially after 1957 and again in the 2000s, merged historic regiments into larger formations while preserving their names and customs within battalion identities.

Structure

A regiment may consist of several battalions, regular and reserve, administered by a regimental headquarters. The system emphasises esprit de corps, local ties and continuity of tradition, and has been widely imitated in Commonwealth armies.
