Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598)

The Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598), known in Korea as the Imjin War (임진왜란), were two invasions of Joseon Korea launched by Toyotomi Hideyoshi with the aim of conquering Ming China. The wars devastated Korea but ended in Japanese withdrawal after Hideyoshi's death.

First invasion (1592)

Japanese forces landed at Busan in April 1592 and rapidly advanced to Seoul and Pyongyang, aided by arquebus-equipped infantry. Korean admiral Yi Sun-sin, commanding armoured "turtle ships", destroyed Japanese supply fleets at sea; Ming Chinese intervention in 1593 helped drive the Japanese south to a coastal enclave near Busan.

Second invasion (1597–1598)

After failed peace talks, Japan invaded again in 1597. Korean and Ming forces checked the advance; Yi Sun-sin's victory at the Battle of Myeongnyang (1597) became legendary. Hideyoshi's death in September 1598 prompted withdrawal; Yi was killed at the final Battle of Noryang.

Consequences

The war caused massive civilian deaths, destruction of Korean cultural heritage and land records, and the transfer of Korean potters and printing technology to Japan. It weakened both Ming China and the Toyotomi regime, facilitating the rise of the Tokugawa shogunate.
