Bernard Thévenet (born 10 January 1948) is a French former professional road racing cyclist. He won the Tour de France twice, in 1975 and 1977, and is best remembered for ending the reign of Eddy Merckx, who had dominated the race for half a decade.

Career

Thévenet turned professional in 1970 with the Peugeot team. He announced himself at the Tour de France that year, winning a mountain stage at La Mongie. Riding for Peugeot throughout his career, he developed into one of the finest climbers of his generation.

In the 1975 Tour, on the climb to Pra-Loup in the Alps, Thévenet attacked Merckx and rode away to take the yellow jersey, which he held to Paris. The victory made him a national hero in France. Two years later he won the 1977 Tour de France, holding off Hennie Kuiper in a race dominated by time trials and mountain stages.

His career was interrupted by health problems in 1978–79; he later admitted to having taken cortisone during his career, an admission unusual for its time. He retired in 1981 and afterwards worked as a television commentator and race organiser.

Major results

  • Tour de France: general classification 1975, 1977; nine stage wins
  • Critérium du Dauphiné Libéré: 1975, 1976
  • French national road race champion: 1973
