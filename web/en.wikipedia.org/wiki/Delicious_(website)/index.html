Delicious (stylised as del.icio.us) was a social bookmarking web service for storing, sharing, and discovering web bookmarks. The site was founded by Joshua Schachter and Peter Gadjokov in 2003 and acquired by Yahoo in 2005. By the end of 2008, the service had more than 5.3 million users and 180 million unique bookmarked URLs. Yahoo sold Delicious to AVOS Systems in April 2011, and the site relaunched in September 2011. It was later acquired by Science Inc. and then by Pinboard, its long-time competitor, in 2017, after which it was placed into read-only mode.

History

Schachter built the site out of his own bookmark file, extending a system he had run as "Memepool". Delicious pioneered the widespread use of free-form tagging to organise bookmarks, allowing users to assign arbitrary words to saved links and to browse the aggregated tags of the whole community. This style of collaborative classification became known as a folksonomy and was a defining feature of the Web 2.0 era.

Under Yahoo, the service was largely neglected, and in December 2010 a leaked internal slide listing Delicious among products to be "sunsetted" caused an exodus of users, many to Pinboard. AVOS Systems, founded by YouTube creators Chad Hurley and Steve Chen, bought the service in 2011 and repeatedly redesigned it, changes that were generally unpopular with long-time users.

Features

Delicious let users save bookmarks from any browser, tag them, write short descriptions, and mark them private or public. Public bookmarks appeared in reverse-chronological feeds per user, per tag, and across the whole site; the "popular" page surfaced links being saved by many users, making Delicious an early tool for discovering trending content on the web. Browser extensions and a simple API made it easy to integrate with other tools.

Legacy

Delicious is remembered as one of the pioneering social web services: it demonstrated tagging at scale, influenced the design of later bookmarking and read-it-later products, and its eventual decline is frequently cited in discussions about the fragility of centralised web services and the importance of data export.
