The Araceae are a family of monocotyledonous flowering plants known as the arum family, in which the flowers are borne on a type of inflorescence called a spadix, usually partially enclosed by a leaf-like bract called a spathe. The family comprises roughly 140 genera and more than 4,000 species, mostly tropical, and is especially diverse in the rainforests of the Americas and Southeast Asia.

Characteristics

Members range from tiny floating duckweeds (now included in the subfamily Lemnoideae) to the giant titan arum (Amorphophallus titanum), whose unbranched inflorescence is the largest known. Many species contain calcium oxalate crystals (raphides) that make their tissues irritating or toxic unless specially prepared. Some genera, such as the corpse flowers, generate heat and carrion-like odours to attract pollinating flies and beetles.

Economic importance

The family includes important food crops — taro (Colocasia esculenta), cocoyam (Xanthosoma), and konjac — and a large share of popular houseplants, including Philodendron, Monstera, peace lilies (Spathiphyllum), pothos, and calla lilies (which, despite the name, are not true lilies).
