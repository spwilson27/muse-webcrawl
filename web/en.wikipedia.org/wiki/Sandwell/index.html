The Metropolitan Borough of Sandwell is a metropolitan borough in the West Midlands county of England, forming part of the Black Country and the West Midlands conurbation. Its name derives from Sandwell Priory and the Sand Well spring in Sandwell Valley. The borough council is based in Oldbury.

History

Sandwell was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972 by merging the former county boroughs of Warley (Smethwick and Rowley Regis) and West Bromwich, together with parts of surrounding districts. The area was a heartland of the Industrial Revolution, known for coal mining, iron and steel, glass (especially at Smethwick, where Chance Brothers made lighthouse glass), engineering and metal-bashing trades. The decline of heavy industry in the late twentieth century brought high unemployment, followed by regeneration programmes.

Towns and places

The borough comprises six main towns: West Bromwich (the largest, home to West Bromwich Albion F.C. at The Hawthorns), Oldbury, Smethwick, Tipton, Rowley Regis and Wednesbury. Sandwell Valley Country Park, between West Bromwich and Birmingham, preserves the site of Sandwell Priory (founded in the twelfth century) and offers a large green space with the RSPB Sandwell Valley reserve.

Transport and governance

The M5 and M6 motorways cross the borough; rail services run on the Birmingham–Wolverhampton line and the West Midlands Metro tram links Wednesbury and West Bromwich with Birmingham and Wolverhampton. Sandwell Council is the local authority; the borough forms part of the West Midlands Combined Authority, whose elected mayor serves the wider region.
