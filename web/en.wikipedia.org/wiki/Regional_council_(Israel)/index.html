A regional council (Hebrew: מועצה אזורית, Mo'atza Azorit) is a type of local government in Israel that provides municipal services to a group of rural communities, most of them kibbutzim and moshavim, spread over a relatively large geographic area. Regional councils are one of three forms of local government in Israel, alongside municipalities (cities) and local councils (towns).

Each regional council is made up of several small communities, usually between three and fifty-four settlements, each of which is too small to constitute a municipality on its own. The council provides shared services such as education, culture, welfare, environmental protection, road maintenance, water and public order for all of its member communities.

History

The first regional council, the Har Hebron and earlier Gezer and Emek Hefer frameworks, date to the British Mandate period; Emek Hefer Regional Council, established in 1940, is generally considered the first. After the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, the framework was formalized under the Municipalities Ordinance and related legislation, and the number of regional councils grew as rural settlement expanded.

Structure and governance

A regional council is governed by a council composed of representatives of the member communities; representation is generally proportional to each community's population, though small communities are guaranteed at least one representative. The head of the council is elected directly by all residents of the council's area in municipal elections held every five years. The council elects committees and employs a professional staff headed by a director-general.

Responsibilities

Regional councils are responsible for primary and secondary education within their areas, cultural and sporting activities, social welfare services, agricultural development support, environmental quality, sanitation, local roads, public transportation coordination, and regional planning and building committees. Because their member settlements often include agricultural land, regional councils control a large share of Israel's open land area — together they cover the great majority of the country's territory outside the cities.

List of regional councils

Notable regional councils include Hof HaSharon, Emek Hefer, Mateh Yehuda (the largest by number of settlements), Sha'ar HaNegev, Eshkol, Ramat HaNegev (the largest by area), Megiddo, Golan, and Upper Galilee. Many border-area councils, such as those surrounding the Gaza Strip, have played a prominent role in national news.
