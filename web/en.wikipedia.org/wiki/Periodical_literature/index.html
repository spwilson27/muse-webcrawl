Periodical literature

Periodical literature (also called periodicals, serials, or journals in some contexts) is literature published in successive parts, usually at regular intervals, under a common title. The category includes newspapers, magazines, academic journals, newsletters, yearbooks, and other publications issued daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, or at other recurring intervals, each part being distinguished by a date and/or numbering.

Characteristics

Unlike books, periodicals are intended to continue indefinitely, and each issue typically contains contributions by multiple authors. Libraries catalogue periodicals by title rather than by individual article, and cite them by volume and issue number. Periodicals are usually assigned an International Standard Serial Number (ISSN), the serial counterpart to the book's ISBN.

History

Periodical publishing began in the early 17th century with the first newspapers and learned journals — the Journal des sçavans and the Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society, both founded in 1665, are among the earliest scholarly periodicals. The 19th century saw an explosion of cheap magazines and newspapers; the 20th century added professional and trade journals; and from the 1990s many periodicals moved partially or wholly online, including open-access journals.

Types

  • Newspapers — general or specialised news, usually daily or weekly.
  • Popular magazines — aimed at general readers, on subjects from fashion to science.
  • Trade and professional publications — aimed at practitioners of a specific industry.
  • Academic journals — peer-reviewed research literature.
