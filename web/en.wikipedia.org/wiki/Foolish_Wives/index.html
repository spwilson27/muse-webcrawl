Foolish Wives

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Foolish Wives is a 1922 American silent drama film written and directed by Erich von Stroheim, who also stars. Produced by Universal Pictures and marketed as the first "million-dollar movie", it is a story of swindling, seduction and blackmail among expatriates and aristocrats in Monte Carlo after the First World War.

Plot

Count Wladislaw Sergius Karamzin, a Russian con man posing as a nobleman and former officer, lives in a rented Monte Carlo villa with two women posing as his cousins. The trio survives by passing counterfeit money and fleecing wealthy visitors. Karamzin targets the wife of the American special envoy, attempting seduction and extortion, while also preying on the villa's maid and on a counterfeiter's mentally disabled daughter. His schemes unravel in a storm and fire climax, and he is murdered by the counterfeiter whose daughter he has ruined; his body is dumped in a sewer.

Production

Von Stroheim was notorious for extravagance and exactitude: he built a vast full-scale replica of Monte Carlo's Casino Square at Universal and shot for some eleven months, amassing hundreds of thousands of feet of film. His original cut reportedly ran many hours; Universal took the film away and cut it to about 14 reels for release, and the negative was further trimmed for reissues. The production established von Stroheim's legend as "the man you love to hate" and as a director at war with the studio system.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major box-office success and was praised for its realism and performances. Surviving prints derive from later, shorter versions; restorations, including one by the San Francisco Silent Film Festival era film-preservation community, have reconstructed the film as closely as possible. It is regarded as a landmark of silent cinema and the first great von Stroheim film.
