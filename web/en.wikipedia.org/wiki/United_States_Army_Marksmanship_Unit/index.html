United States Army Marksmanship Unit

The United States Army Marksmanship Unit (USAMU or AMU) is a United States Army organization at Fort Moore, Georgia, dedicated to competitive shooting, marksmanship research and training support. Founded in 1956, it is often called the “Home of Champions” because of the large number of national, world and Olympic medals won by its soldiers.

Mission

The unit fields teams in service rifle, service pistol, action shooting, shotgun and international rifle and pistol disciplines, and supports the Army's small-arms programmes. Its soldiers test techniques and equipment, conduct clinics, and help train deploying troops and other shooters. A custom firearms shop builds and maintains precision rifles, pistols and shotguns used by the teams.

History

President Dwight D. Eisenhower authorized the unit in 1956 to raise the standard of American competitive marksmanship, at a time when Soviet shooters dominated international events. AMU members soon became central to United States Olympic and world championship teams. Competitors associated with the unit have included many of the most successful American marksmen in rifle, pistol and shotgun sports.

Role beyond competition

Competition is a means of developing expertise rather than the unit's only purpose. Lessons from precision shooting are fed into marksmanship doctrine, instructor development, weapons testing and public outreach. The AMU also hosts major matches at Fort Moore, including interservice and national-level competitions.
