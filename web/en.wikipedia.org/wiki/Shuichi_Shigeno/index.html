Shuichi Shigeno (しげの 秀一, Shigeno Shūichi, born March 8, 1958) is a Japanese manga artist famous for his car- and motorcycle-themed manga. His best-known work, Initial D (1995–2013), serialized in Kodansha's Weekly Young Magazine, sold over 55 million copies and spawned a multimedia franchise of anime series, films, and video games (notably Sega's Initial D Arcade Stage), and is credited with popularizing Japanese street racing culture and the Toyota AE86 worldwide.

Career

Shigeno debuted as a professional manga artist in 1981. His breakthrough came with Bari Bari Densetsu (1983–1991), a motorcycle racing manga that won the Kodansha Manga Award in the shōnen category in 1985 and was adapted into an anime OVA. He also created the baseball manga DO-P-KAN and the romance series Sailor Ace.

Initial D and sequels

Initial D follows delivery driver Takumi Fujiwara, who becomes a legendary street racer on the mountain passes (touge) of Gunma Prefecture in his father's aging Toyota Sprinter Trueno AE86. After completing the series in 2013, Shigeno began MF Ghost in 2017, a successor set in a future where self-driving cars dominate and internal-combustion cars race in the MFG competition; it concluded in 2025 and received an anime adaptation beginning in 2023. His next work, Subaru and Subaru, a further sequel following two new protagonists named Subaru, began serialization in 2025.
