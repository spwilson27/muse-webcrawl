Mahikeng

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mahikeng (Tswana for "Place of Rocks"), formerly known as Mafikeng and historically spelled Mafeking, is the capital city of the North West province of South Africa. It lies close to the border with Botswana, about 260 kilometres west of Johannesburg. The name derives from the Tswana words mahika and geng, referring to the rocks of the area.

History

The area was the headquarters of the Barolong Boo Ratshidi people. The town was founded in 1885 during the period of British Bechuanaland. Mahikeng is best known internationally for the Siege of Mafeking during the Second Boer War (1899–1900), in which a British garrison under Colonel Robert Baden-Powell held out for 217 days until relieved; the siege made Baden-Powell a national hero and influenced his founding of the Scout movement. From 1960 to 1980 Mahikeng served, anomalously, as the capital of the Bophuthatswana bantustan while formally a separate enclave, and after 1994 became capital of the new North West province.

Geography and institutions

The town is home to a campus of North-West University and to the Mahikeng Airport. Sol Plaatje, the writer and first secretary-general of the ANC, lived and worked in the town, where his house is a museum.
