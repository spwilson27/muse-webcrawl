Petroleum reservoir

A petroleum reservoir, or oil and gas reservoir, is a subsurface accumulation of hydrocarbons contained in porous or fractured rock formations. The hydrocarbons are trapped by overlying rock formations with lower permeability. The reservoir rock must be both porous, to store the fluids, and permeable, to allow oil and gas to flow to a wellbore.

Formation

Petroleum originates from organic-rich source rock buried and heated until it generates oil and gas, which then migrate upward through permeable strata until they are trapped. Conventional traps include anticlines, fault traps and stratigraphic traps, sealed by impermeable cap rock such as shale or salt. Within the reservoir, fluids separate by density: gas at the top, oil in the middle and water below.

Exploitation

Reservoir engineers estimate reserves and plan production using geological, geophysical and production data. Primary recovery uses the reservoir's natural pressure; secondary recovery injects water or gas to maintain pressure; enhanced oil recovery methods — thermal, chemical or miscible gas injection — may extract a further fraction. Even with these techniques, typically only a portion of the original oil in place is recovered. Unconventional reservoirs, such as shale formations, have very low permeability and are produced by horizontal drilling combined with hydraulic fracturing.
