Mount Aso (阿蘇山, Aso-san) is the largest active volcano in Japan and among the largest calderas in the world. Located in Aso Kujū National Park in Kumamoto Prefecture on the island of Kyushu, it consists of a vast caldera — roughly 25 kilometres north–south by 18 kilometres east–west — formed by four gigantic eruptions between about 270,000 and 90,000 years ago, within which a group of central cones remains active.

Geology and activity

The last of the four caldera-forming eruptions (Aso-4, ~90,000 years ago) was among the largest known on Earth, depositing pyroclastic flows across much of Kyushu and ash as far as Hokkaido. Of the central cones — Takadake (the highest), Nakadake, Nekodake, Kishimadake, and Eboshidake — only Nakadake is currently active, its crater containing an acidic turquoise crater lake and emitting continuous plumes of volcanic gas. Eruptions are frequent; significant recent episodes occurred in 2016 (part of the Kumamoto earthquake sequence period) and a phreatic eruption on 20 October 2021 that prompted evacuations of hikers.

Human landscape

Unusually, the caldera floor is inhabited: towns, farms, roads, and a railway occupy it, and its grasslands have been maintained by controlled burning for a millennium of grazing. The Aso Shrine, one of Japan's oldest, anchors the region's religious life. Visitors reach Nakadake's crater rim by road and ropeway when gas levels permit, making Aso one of the few places where tourists can look directly into an active crater.
