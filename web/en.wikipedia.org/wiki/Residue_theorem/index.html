Residue theorem

The residue theorem, also known as the Cauchy's residue theorem, is a powerful tool to evaluate line integrals of analytic functions over closed curves; it can often be used to compute real integrals and infinite series as well. It is a generalisation of the Cauchy integral theorem and Cauchy's integral formula.

Statement

Let U be a simply connected open subset of the complex plane containing a finite list of points a1, …, an, and let f be a function defined and holomorphic on U except at those points. If γ is a positively oriented, piecewise smooth, closed curve in U not passing through any ak, then

∮γ f(z) dz = 2πi Σk I(γ, ak) Res(f, ak),

where I(γ, ak) is the winding number of γ around ak and Res(f, ak) is the residue of f at that point. For a simple contour traversed once counter-clockwise, all winding numbers equal 1.

Applications

The theorem is widely used to evaluate improper real integrals — trigonometric integrals, integrals over the real line closed by semicircular contours in the upper or lower half-plane (sometimes aided by Jordan's lemma), integrals involving branch cuts (keyhole contours), and sums of infinite series, as well as in physics and engineering applications such as fluid dynamics and signal processing.
