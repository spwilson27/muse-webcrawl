Azoospermia

Azoospermia is the medical condition of a man whose semen contains no sperm. It is associated with male infertility, but many forms are amenable to medical treatment. In humans, azoospermia affects about 1% of the male population and may be seen in up to 20% of male infertility situations in Canada. In a 2013 review, azoospermia was found to affect roughly 3% of infertile men, rising to 12% among men with severe oligozoospermia.

Causes

Azoospermia is classified into three major types: pretesticular, testicular, and post-testicular. Pretesticular azoospermia is characterised by inadequate stimulation of otherwise normal testicles and genital tract, typically because of low levels of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). Testicular azoospermia, also called non-obstructive azoospermia, occurs when the testes themselves are abnormal and unable to produce sperm. Post-testicular, or obstructive, azoospermia occurs when sperm are produced but not ejaculated because of a physical obstruction in the genital tract, for example following a vasectomy or due to congenital absence of the vas deferens associated with cystic fibrosis.

Diagnosis

Azoospermia is usually discovered in the course of an infertility investigation. It is established on the basis of semen analysis in which no sperm are seen in the ejaculate, including after centrifugation of the sample. Further evaluation includes genetic testing, hormone assessment, and sometimes testicular biopsy to determine whether spermatogenesis is occurring.

Treatment

Pre- and post-testicular azoospermia are frequently correctable, while testicular azoospermia is usually permanent except in cases of varicocele-associated azoospermia. Obstruction can be treated surgically, and sperm can often be retrieved directly from the testis or epididymis for use with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). Hormonal deficiencies may respond to gonadotropin therapy.
