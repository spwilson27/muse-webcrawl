Science podcast

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A science podcast is a podcast — an episodic series of downloadable or streamed audio (and sometimes video) programmes — devoted to science, technology, medicine, the natural world or the methods and culture of research. Science podcasts range from professional productions by broadcasters and scientific journals to independent shows made by scientists, teachers, comedians and enthusiasts. They are a significant medium of science communication.

History

Podcasting emerged in 2004–2005, and science was among its earliest genres. Long-running examples include The Naked Scientists (a Cambridge-based radio show distributed as a podcast from 2005), Science Friday (the NPR programme), This Week in Science (broadcast since 1999 and podcast soon after), and the journal-produced shows of Nature and Science, whose podcasts began in the mid-2000s. The 2010s brought a wave of narrative and entertainment-led successes, such as Radiolab, The Infinite Monkey Cage (BBC Radio 4, with Brian Cox and Robin Ince), Science Vs, Ologies with Alie Ward, and The Skeptics' Guide to the Universe, one of the longest-running science podcasts.

Formats

  • News round-ups: weekly summaries of new research, often produced by journals and broadcasters.
  • Interview shows: conversations with researchers about their work or a topic of expertise.
  • Narrative storytelling: edited, documentary-style episodes exploring a question or history.
  • Panel and comedy formats: hosts discuss stories with humour — a format credited with reaching audiences beyond committed science fans.
  • Educational series: lecture-style or curriculum-supporting content for students and teachers.

Audience and impact

Science podcasts are valued for depth and convenience: episodes of 30–90 minutes allow more nuance than broadcast news segments, and on-demand listening fits commuting and exercise. Surveys of listeners suggest they skew educated and already interested in science, but popular shows reach audiences of millions. Podcasts are used in classrooms and informal learning, and they have become a route for scientists to build public profiles. During the COVID-19 pandemic, shows such as This Week in Virology became prominent sources of expert discussion.

Challenges

The medium's openness means quality varies, and health-related podcasts have been criticised for promoting unsupported treatments. Producers also face discoverability and funding problems; successful independent shows typically rely on advertising, crowdfunding memberships, or institutional backing.
