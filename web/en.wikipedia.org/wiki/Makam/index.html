Makam

Makam (Turkish: makam; Arabic: maqām) is the system of melodic modes used in Turkish classical music and other Ottoman-influenced traditions. A makam is far more than a scale: it specifies characteristic melodic progressions, phrases, opening and closing formulas, important notes, and conventions for modulation.

Structure

Makams are built by combining a tetrachord (dörtlü) and a pentachord (beşli) over an octave divided in theory into 53 commas (Holderian commas) — in practice performed with a set of characteristic microtonal intervals. The final note is called the durak (tonic), the strong dominant-like note is the güçlü, and a typical improvised or composed melody follows a characteristic melodic course called seyir, which may begin high and descend, begin low and ascend, or proceed from the middle.

Common makams

Hundreds of makams have been documented, though a smaller core set dominates practice: Hicaz, Hüzzam, Nihavend, Rast, Uşşak, Beyati, Saba, Çargâh, and Kürdi among them. Closely related systems include the Arabic maqām, the Persian dastgāh, and the Byzantine echos; the Ottoman tradition also influenced Greek, Balkan, and Sephardic music.

Performance

Makam governs both composed forms — such as the peşrev, saz semaisi, and şarkı — and improvisation (taksim). Principal instruments include the tanbur, ney, ud (oud), kanun, and kemençe. The theory of Arel-Ezgi-Uzdilek, codified in the 20th century, is the standard reference for the modern Turkish system.
