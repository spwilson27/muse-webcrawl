Siegel modular form

In mathematics, a Siegel modular form is a generalization of an ordinary modular form to several complex variables. Siegel modular forms are holomorphic functions on the Siegel upper half-space — the space of symmetric g × g complex matrices with positive definite imaginary part — that transform in a prescribed way under the action of the symplectic group Sp(2g, Z) (or a subgroup). The integer g is called the degree or genus; for g = 1 the definition recovers the classical theory of elliptic modular forms. The theory was founded by Carl Ludwig Siegel in the 1930s in connection with the analytic theory of quadratic forms.

Definition

Let ℍg denote the Siegel upper half-space of degree g. A Siegel modular form of degree g, weight k, for the full modular group Sp(2g, Z) is a holomorphic function f : ℍg → C satisfying f(γZ) = det(CZ + D)k f(Z) for all γ = (A B; C D) in Sp(2g, Z), with no extra growth condition needed when g ≥ 2 by the Koecher principle. Forms vanishing at the cusp at infinity are called cusp forms. More general definitions involve a level (congruence subgroup), a character, and vector-valued forms, where the automorphy factor is a representation of GL(g, C).

Examples and structure

Examples include Siegel Eisenstein series, theta series attached to positive definite quadratic forms, and (for g = 2) forms constructed from classical modular forms via the Saito–Kurokawa lift. The rings of modular forms for small degree are known: for degree 2, the ring of scalar-valued forms for the full group is generated by the Eisenstein series E4 and E6, the cusp forms χ10 and χ12, and an additional generator in weight 35 (Igusa's theorem). Jun-ichi Igusa determined the structure of the ring for genus 2 and of related rings using the geometry of moduli of abelian varieties.

Applications

Siegel modular forms are the functions on moduli spaces of principally polarized abelian varieties; they encode arithmetic information through their Fourier coefficients and associated L-functions, and play a central role in the Langlands program (e.g., the paramodular conjecture) and in string theory, where genus-2 partition functions are Siegel modular forms.
