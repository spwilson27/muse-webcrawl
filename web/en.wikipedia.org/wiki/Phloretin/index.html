Phloretin

IUPAC name3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)-1-(2,4,6-trihydroxyphenyl)propan-1-one
FormulaC15H14O5
Molar mass274.27 g/mol
AppearancePale yellow solid

Phloretin is a dihydrochalcone, a type of natural phenol and flavonoid-related compound. It is the aglycone of phlorizin (phloretin glucoside), from which it can be obtained by hydrolysis. Phloretin is found in the leaves of apple trees and other members of the rose family, and in the Manchurian apricot.

Biochemistry

Phloretin is best known as an inhibitor of glucose transporters. It blocks the facilitated glucose transporters (GLUT family) and has been widely used as a research tool in studies of membrane transport. In contrast, its glucoside phlorizin inhibits the sodium-glucose cotransporters (SGLT) of the kidney and intestine; phlorizin served historically as the chemical inspiration for the SGLT2 inhibitor ("gliflozin") class of antidiabetic drugs.

Uses and research

Phloretin exhibits antioxidant activity and has been investigated for applications in cosmetics, where it is used in skin formulations (sometimes combined with vitamin C and ferulic acid), and in food science, where it has been studied as a natural preservative and as an inhibitor of the formation of certain advanced glycation end products. It also inhibits some urea transporters and aquaporins in experimental settings.
