The Rings of Power are magical artefacts in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium of Middle-earth. Forged in the Second Age by the Elven-smiths of Eregion under the guidance of Sauron (in his fair guise as Annatar, "Lord of Gifts"), they were designed to preserve and enhance the power of their bearers — and, secretly, to subject them to the domination of the One Ring, which Sauron forged alone in Mount Doom. The story of the Rings frames the history of the later Second Age and the War of the Ring in The Lord of the Rings.

History in the legendarium

Around S.A. 1200, Sauron, seeking to ensnare the Elves, came to Eregion and taught Celebrimbor and the Gwaith-i-Mírdain (the brotherhood of jewel-smiths) the craft of ring-making. Between about S.A. 1500 and 1590 they made the Great Rings: sixteen rings later divided by Sauron into the Seven and the Nine, and finally the Three Rings, which Celebrimbor made alone and unsullied by Sauron's direct touch. In S.A. 1600 Sauron forged the One Ring in Orodruin, pouring into it a great part of his own native power, so that it would rule the others; when he put it on, the Elves perceived his treachery and hid the Three. The ensuing War of the Elves and Sauron devastated Eregion; Sauron recovered the Sixteen and distributed Seven to the Dwarf-lords and Nine to kings and sorcerers of Men. The Men became the Nazgûl (Ringwraiths); the Dwarves proved resistant to domination, though the rings inflamed their greed. The Three — Narya, Nenya, and Vilya — were held in secrecy by Elven lords (eventually Gandalf, Galadriel, and Elrond) and lost their power when the One was destroyed in S.A. 3019 of the Third Age.

Powers and properties

The Great Rings enhanced the natural power of their bearers, preserved things from decay and change (the "embalming" of Lothlórien and Rivendell), and conferred invisibility on mortal wearers by shifting them into the Unseen world — except for the Three, which did not make wearers invisible and were primarily preservative and healing. All the Rings save the One were adorned with gems; their influence was perilous because all save the Three were ultimately subject to the One, and any use of them while Sauron held the One exposed the bearer to his will.

In other media

The forging of the Rings is dramatised in the Amazon television series The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power (2022– ), set in the Second Age, and appears across adaptations and games of Tolkien's work. The Ring Verse — "Three Rings for the Elven-kings under the sky..." — is among the most quoted passages of Tolkien's writing.
