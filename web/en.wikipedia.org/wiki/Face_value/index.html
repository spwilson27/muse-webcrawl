Face value

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The face value, also known as the nominal value, is the value printed or inscribed on an object such as a coin, banknote, stamp, ticket, or financial security. It is the value stated by the issuing authority and is distinct from the object's intrinsic value (for example, the melt value of the metal in a coin) and from its market value, which is what buyers are willing to pay for it.

Coins and currency

For modern fiat currency, face value is simply the denomination: a twenty-dollar bill has a face value of twenty dollars regardless of the cost of the paper and ink. Historically, coins often circulated at face values close to the value of their precious-metal content, but debasement could reduce intrinsic value below face value. Collectible coins frequently trade at market values well above face value, while demonetized currency may retain only numismatic value.

Securities

For a bond, the face value (or par value) is the amount the issuer promises to repay at maturity, and coupon interest payments are usually calculated as a percentage of it. A bond trading "at par" sells for its face value; above face value it trades at a premium, below it at a discount. For shares of stock, par value is an arbitrary accounting figure set in the corporate charter and typically bears little relation to market price.

Other uses

Postage stamps are sold and valid for postage at face value. Insurance policies have a face value (face amount) payable on the insured event. The phrase "at face value" is also used figuratively, meaning to accept something as it appears without deeper scrutiny.
