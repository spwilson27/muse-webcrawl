Mega Test

The Mega Test is a high-range intelligence test created by Ronald K. Hoeflin, first published in Omni magazine in 1985. It was designed to measure IQ at extremely high levels — far beyond the ceiling of standardized tests such as the WAIS — for distinguishing among people in the very top fraction of the population.

AuthorRonald K. Hoeflin
Published1985, in Omni magazine
Items48 problems
PurposeMeasuring very high IQ

Contents

The test consists of 48 problems, divided between verbal analogies and number series and spatial items, to be solved untimed with a book of references but no outside help permitted. Hoeflin set the qualifying score for his Mega Society — an organization for people with extremely high IQs — at a level he estimated corresponded to a one-in-a-million rarity on the test. Because the test was published in a popular magazine, more than 10,000 people attempted it, though only a small fraction submitted answers for scoring.

Background

Hoeflin founded the Mega Society in 1982 and created a sequence of high-range tests (the Mega, Titan, and others) out of dissatisfaction with the ceilings of conventional IQ tests, which cluster scores near the mean and cannot reliably rank individuals many standard deviations above it. High-range tests like the Mega Test remain controversial among psychometricians: they are not normed on representative samples, and their score conversions rest on assumptions about the statistical distribution of ability. Critics also note the security problem — a published test's answers circulate, so results obtained after publication are suspect.
