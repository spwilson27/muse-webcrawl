Fujiwara-kyō

Fujiwara-kyō (藤原京) was the capital of Japan for sixteen years, between 694 and 710. It was located in Yamato Province (present-day Kashihara, Nara Prefecture), having been moved from nearby Asuka.

It was Japan's first city to incorporate the Chinese grid-pattern capital design on a major scale, modelled on Chang'an, the capital of Tang dynasty China. The palace, Fujiwara Palace, stood at the centre of the northern part of the city.

History

Construction began under Empress Jitō, who moved the court there in 694. The capital was used by the reigns of Jitō, Monmu and Genmei. In 710 the court moved to Heijō-kyō at Nara, and Fujiwara-kyō was abandoned; a fire in 711 may have destroyed much of what remained.

Excavations have revealed the layout of the palace and streets, including roof tiles and wooden tablets (mokkan) that illuminate early Japanese administration.
