The Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry ("The Very Rich Hours of the Duke of Berry") is an illuminated book of hours commissioned by John, Duke of Berry, brother of King Charles V of France, and painted principally by the Limbourg brothers between about 1412 and 1416. It is widely regarded as the most famous and finest surviving illuminated manuscript of the 15th century, and the supreme example of International Gothic painting.

Contents

The manuscript contains 206 leaves of high-quality vellum with 131 miniatures. Its most celebrated section is the calendar, in which each month is illustrated with a full-page scene of seasonal labour or courtly life set before one of the Duke's castles: peasants warming themselves by a fire in February, the harvest beneath the Château de Saumur in September, and the Duke himself feasting in January. Above each scene is a semicircular astronomical chart — remarkable early artefacts of courtly science. The religious miniatures include the cycles of prayers, the Psalms, and the famously grotesque Anatomical Man, showing the signs of the zodiac mapped onto the human body.

Creation and provenance

Work ceased in 1416 when both the Duke and the Limbourg brothers died, probably of plague, leaving the book unfinished. It passed through several owners; in the 1480s Jean Colombe completed the illumination for Charles I, Duke of Savoy. The manuscript eventually came to the Château de Chantilly, where the Duc d'Aumale acquired it in the 1850s and bequeathed it with his collection to the Institut de France. Kept at the Musée Condé, the original is too fragile for permanent display and was shown publicly only on rare occasions.
