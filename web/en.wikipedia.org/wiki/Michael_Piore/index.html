Michael Joseph Piore (born 14 August 1940) is an American economist, the David W. Skinner Professor of Political Economy (Emeritus) at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. He is known for developing the theory of dual (segmented) labour markets and for his influential work on flexible production and industrial districts.

Work

In Birds of Passage (1979) and earlier papers with Peter Doeringer in Internal Labor Markets and Manpower Analysis (1971), Piore argued that labour markets are segmented into a "primary" sector of stable, well-paid jobs with internal promotion ladders and a "secondary" sector of low-wage, insecure work, with migration and institutions playing central roles in allocating workers.

With Charles Sabel he wrote The Second Industrial Divide (1984), an influential study arguing that the crisis of mass production opened the possibility of a alternative path based on "flexible specialisation" — networks of small, innovative firms in industrial districts such as those of Emilia-Romagna in Italy. Piore later worked on employment policy, migration, and the role of interpretation and identity in economic organisation, including studies of Mexican immigrant communities and of labour market regulation in the United States.
