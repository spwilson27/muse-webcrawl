Darug

The traditional lands of the Darug people around the Sydney basin.

The Dharug, also spelled Darug, are an Aboriginal Australian people who traditionally lived in the region of what is now Sydney, New South Wales. Darug country extended across much of the Cumberland Plain and the Sydney basin, from the Hawkesbury River in the north-west to the Georges River in the south.

Language and groups

The Darug language was spoken in two main dialects: a coastal dialect around Sydney Harbour and an inland dialect spoken on the Cumberland Plain. The Darug comprised numerous clan groups, including the Burramattagal around Parramatta, the Wallumedegal, and the Cannemegal. Darug people should not be confused with the Eora, a term used for the coastal clans around the harbour, or with the neighbouring Gundungurra and Tharawal peoples.

Contact and colonisation

Darug people experienced some of the earliest and most intense effects of British colonisation after the First Fleet arrived in 1788. The smallpox epidemic of 1789 devastated the population, and land grants progressively displaced communities from their country. Resistance included the guerrilla campaign led by the Bidjigal warrior Pemulwuy in the 1790s.

Today

Darug descendants maintain a strong community presence in western Sydney, and the Darug language has been the subject of revival efforts in schools and community programs. Darug custodianship is acknowledged in land acknowledgements and local council protocols across the Sydney region.
