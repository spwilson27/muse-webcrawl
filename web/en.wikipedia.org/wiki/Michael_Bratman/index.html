Michael E. Bratman (born 25 July 1945) is an American philosopher, the Durfee Professor in the School of Humanities and Sciences at Stanford University. He is known for his "planning theory" of intention and for his influential account of shared cooperative activity and shared intention.

Philosophical work

In Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason (1987), Bratman argued that intentions are not merely desires or beliefs but distinctive plan-laden mental states that structure and constrain an agent's future deliberation, providing stability and coordination over time. The theory became a standard reference point in the philosophy of action and in artificial intelligence, where his collaborative work with colleagues inspired the belief–desire–intention (BDI) model of software agents.

His later work, including Faces of Intention (1999), Structures of Agency (2007) and Shared Agency: A Planning Theory of Acting Together (2014), developed accounts of how two or more people can act together through interlocking intentions and mutual responsiveness — an influential framework in debates about collective intentionality. Bratman's papers in Planning, Time, and Self-Governance (2018) further explore temporally extended agency.

Honours

Bratman is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and has held fellowships including a Guggenheim. His work is a central reference in contemporary philosophy of action.
