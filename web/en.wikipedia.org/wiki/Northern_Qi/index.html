Northern Qi

The Northern Qi (Chinese: 北齊; pinyin: Běi Qí) was one of the Northern dynasties of China during the Northern and Southern dynasties period, ruling the northeast from 550 to 577 CE. It succeeded the Eastern Wei and was founded by Gao Yang, who reigned as Emperor Wenxuan. Its capital was Ye (near modern Linzhang, Hebei), with Taiyuan as a secondary capital.

History

The Gao family, of mixed Han and Xianbei background, had controlled the Eastern Wei court as regents before Gao Yang deposed its last emperor and founded Qi. Wenxuan began energetically—strengthening the army, codifying law, and campaigning against the Göktürks and steppe peoples—but his reign degenerated into erratic and violent behaviour. Later emperors, including the notorious Gao Zhan and Gao Wei, presided over court corruption, factional purges, and the execution of capable generals such as Hulü Guang.

Fall and legacy

Weakened by misrule, Northern Qi was conquered by the rival Northern Zhou in 577; the last Qi emperor fled and was captured. Northern Zhou then reunified the north, paving the way for the Sui dynasty's reunification of China. The dynasty is remembered for spectacular Buddhist patronage—the cave temples at Xiangtangshan and Tianlongshan and a distinctive, sensuous style of sculpture are among the finest of the period.
