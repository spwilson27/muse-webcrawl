Yat-Kha (also written Yat Kha) is a band from Tuva, in southern Siberia, founded by guitarist and vocalist Albert Kuvezin. The group is internationally known for combining traditional Tuvan throat singing (khöömei) and folk instruments with rock, punk, and electronic influences, creating a distinctive fusion sometimes described as "Tuvan rock."

History

Albert Kuvezin, formerly of the Tuvan folk group Huun-Huur-Tu, formed Yat-Kha in the early 1990s. The band's early work paired Kuvezin's extraordinarily deep kargyraa-style throat singing with sparse electronic backing. Later line-ups added drums, bass, and the traditional igil (horse-head fiddle) and doshpuluur, moving toward a heavier folk-rock sound.

Music and reception

Yat-Kha's albums include Yenisei Punk (1995) and Dalai Beldiri (1999). The band won the BBC Radio 3 Award for World Music in the Asia/Pacific category in 2002 and has toured widely in Europe and North America, helping to popularise Tuvan throat singing with rock audiences. Collaborators have included the Finnish electronic musician Ivan Sokolovsky.
