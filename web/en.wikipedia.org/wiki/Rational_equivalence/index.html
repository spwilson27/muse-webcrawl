Rational equivalence

In algebraic geometry, rational equivalence is an equivalence relation on algebraic cycles of a variety. Two cycles are rationally equivalent if one can be deformed into the other through a family of cycles parametrised by the projective line. It is the finest of the standard "adequate" equivalence relations on cycles and gives rise to the Chow group of the variety.

Definition

Let X be a smooth projective variety. A cycle of codimension p is a formal integer linear combination of irreducible subvarieties of codimension p. Given an irreducible subvariety W of X × P1 that dominates P1, the fibres over two points of P1 are cycles on X that are declared rationally equivalent; the relation generated by such elementary equivalences is rational equivalence. The group of codimension-p cycles modulo rational equivalence is the Chow group CHp(X).

Examples and properties

On the projective line every two points are rationally equivalent, so its Chow group of zero-cycles is the integers, counted by degree. On a curve, rational equivalence of divisors coincides with linear equivalence: two divisors are rationally equivalent if and only if their difference is the divisor of a rational function, so the Chow group CH1 of a smooth projective curve is its Picard group.

Rational equivalence is finer than algebraic equivalence, numerical equivalence and homological equivalence. For divisors on a smooth projective variety the intersection pairing with curves factors through rational equivalence, making Chow groups the natural home of intersection theory as developed by Fulton and others.
