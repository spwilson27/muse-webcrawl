Aberystwyth

Aberystwyth (Welsh: [abɛˈrəstwɪθ]) is a university and seaside town in Ceredigion, Wales, situated at the confluence of the rivers Ystwyth and Rheidol on the shore of Cardigan Bay, from which it takes its name ("mouth of the Ystwyth"). Its population is around 13,000, rising substantially in term time with the students of Aberystwyth University.

History

The area shows Iron Age settlement on Pen Dinas hill. The town grew around the castle founded by Edward I in 1277; the ruins still stand on the headland south of the promenade. Aberystwyth received its charter in 1277. In the 19th century it developed as a fashionable Victorian seaside resort, with the arrival of the railway in 1864, a pier (1865), and the grand Castle Hotel — never completed as a hotel — which instead became home in 1872 to the University College of Wales, the first constituent institution of the University of Wales.

Education and culture

Aberystwyth University (founded 1872) is the town's dominant institution. The National Library of Wales, founded in 1907 and housed on Penglais Hill, is one of the UK's legal deposit libraries and holds the largest collection of Welsh and Celtic material in the world, including the Black Book of Carmarthen and the Hengwrt Chaucer manuscript. Other landmarks include the Aberystwyth Arts Centre, the Vale of Rheidol Railway to Devil's Bridge, and the cliff railway up Constitution Hill.

Aberystwyth is strongly Welsh-speaking by English standards, and is a centre of Welsh-language culture and publishing. The town's football club, Aberystwyth Town, plays in the Cymru Premier.
