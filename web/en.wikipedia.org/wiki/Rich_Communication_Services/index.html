Rich Communication Services

Rich Communication Services (RCS) is a communication protocol standard for instant messaging, primarily for mobile phones, developed and defined by the GSMA. It aims to be a replacement of SMS and MMS on cellular networks, adding features familiar from internet messaging apps while remaining carrier-based.

Features

RCS adds group chat, high-resolution photo and video sharing, file transfer, typing indicators, read receipts, location sharing and improved business messaging with verified senders and rich cards. Messages are sent over the data network, falling back to SMS where RCS is unavailable.

History

The initiative began in 2007–2008 under the GSMA, but adoption by carriers was slow and fragmented for a decade. In 2016 the GSMA published the Universal Profile, a single agreed specification intended to make implementations interoperable worldwide. Google became the technology's main champion, building RCS "Chat" features into Android Messages and rolling out its own hosted service where carriers did not. Apple, long the major holdout, added RCS support to the iPhone in iOS 18 in 2024.

Encryption

The base RCS standard does not mandate end-to-end encryption; Google's implementation added end-to-end encryption for one-to-one and group conversations over its service, and work continues on standardising interoperable encryption through the Messaging Layer Security protocol.
