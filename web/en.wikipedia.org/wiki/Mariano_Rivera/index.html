Mariano Rivera

Mariano Rivera (born November 29, 1969) is a Panamanian-American former professional baseball pitcher who played 19 seasons in Major League Baseball for the New York Yankees, from 1995 to 2013. Widely regarded as the greatest relief pitcher of all time, he is Major League Baseball's career leader in saves (652) and games finished (952).

Born in Panama City, Panama, Rivera signed with the Yankees as an amateur free agent in 1990. After beginning as a starting pitcher, he moved to the bullpen, serving as setup man to closer John Wetteland on the 1996 World Series championship team before becoming the Yankees' closer in 1997. Relying almost exclusively on a single pitch—a cut fastball with late, sharp movement—he was a key figure in the Yankees dynasty that won four World Series in five years (1996, 1998–2000) and a fifth in 2009. He was named the 1999 World Series MVP and the 2003 ALCS MVP, and holds postseason records including a 0.70 ERA and 42 saves.

Later career and honors

A thirteen-time All-Star, Rivera was the last active player permitted to wear uniform number 42, retired across baseball in honor of Jackie Robinson; the Yankees retired Rivera's number in 2013. His entrance from the bullpen to Metallica's "Enter Sandman" became one of baseball's iconic rituals. In 2019 he was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame, becoming the first player ever elected unanimously by the Baseball Writers' Association of America.
