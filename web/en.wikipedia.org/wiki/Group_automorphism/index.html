Group automorphism

Field: Abstract algebra

Object: Automorphism group Aut(G)

In abstract algebra, an automorphism of a group G is a bijective group homomorphism from G to itself. The set of all automorphisms of G forms a group under composition, called the automorphism group of G and denoted Aut(G).

Inner and outer automorphisms

For any element g of G, conjugation by g (mapping x to gxg−1) is an automorphism called an inner automorphism. The inner automorphisms form a normal subgroup Inn(G) of Aut(G), and the quotient Aut(G)/Inn(G) is the outer automorphism group Out(G). The map from G to Inn(G) has kernel equal to the center Z(G), so Inn(G) is isomorphic to G/Z(G).

Examples

The automorphism group of a cyclic group of order n is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of integers modulo n, of order φ(n). The symmetric group S6 is exceptional among symmetric groups in having a nontrivial outer automorphism.
