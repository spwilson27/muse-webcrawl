In statistics, the method of moments is a method of estimation of population parameters. The same principle is used to derive many estimators: the method starts by expressing the population moments (i.e., the expected values of powers of the random variable under consideration) in terms of the parameters of interest. Those expressions are then set equal to the sample moments. The number of equations to solve equals the number of parameters to estimate; solving them gives the method-of-moments estimates.

Method

Suppose a distribution has parameters θ1, …, θk. Its first k population moments can be written as functions of these parameters. Replacing the population moments E[Xr] with the sample moments (1/n)Σxir and solving the resulting system yields the estimates. For example, for a gamma distribution the mean and variance give estimates of the shape and scale parameters directly.

Properties

Method-of-moments estimators are usually consistent and asymptotically normal, and are valued for their simplicity and closed forms. However, they are generally not efficient: maximum likelihood estimators usually have smaller variance. Moment estimators can also fall outside the parameter space (e.g., negative variance estimates in some problems) or fail to use all the information in the sample distribution.

Generalised method of moments

Lars Peter Hansen's generalised method of moments (GMM, 1982) extends the idea to more moment conditions than parameters, choosing estimates that minimise a weighted sum of squared deviations from the conditions. GMM is widely used in econometrics, particularly with instrumental variables.
