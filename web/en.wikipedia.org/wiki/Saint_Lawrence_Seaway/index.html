The Saint Lawrence Seaway (French: Voie maritime du Saint-Laurent) is a system of locks, canals and channels in Canada and the United States that permits oceangoing vessels to travel from the Atlantic Ocean to the North American Great Lakes, as far inland as the western end of Lake Superior. The seaway, opened in 1959, is jointly managed by the St. Lawrence Seaway Management Corporation in Canada and the Saint Lawrence Seaway Development Corporation in the United States.

Geography and construction

The seaway proper runs from Montreal to Lake Erie, including the Montreal–Lake Ontario section with seven locks and the Welland Canal (eight locks) by-passing Niagara Falls in Ontario. Together the locks raise ships about 180 m from sea level to Lake Erie. Construction, begun in 1954, was a massive joint undertaking; it flooded a number of Ontario villages (the "Lost Villages") and created Lake St. Lawrence. The seaway was formally opened on 26 June 1959 by Queen Elizabeth II and President Dwight D. Eisenhower.

Shipping

Ships using the seaway are limited by the lock size to about 225 m in length and 24 m beam — vessels known as "Seawaymax". Bulk cargoes such as grain, iron ore, coal and steel dominate traffic, and annual tonnage has ranged between roughly 30 and 50 million tonnes in recent decades. The navigation season runs from about March to December, the river being closed by ice in winter.
