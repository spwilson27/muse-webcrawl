Neoaves

Neoaves is a clade of birds that contains nearly all modern birds (Neornithes) other than the Palaeognathae (ratites and tinamous) and the Galloanserae (ducks, chickens and their relatives). Almost 95% of the roughly 11,000 known living species of birds belong to the Neoaves.

Characteristics

The early diversification of neoavian groups occurred very rapidly around the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event, and the relationships between the major lineages have been notoriously difficult to resolve because of this explosive radiation. Neoaves includes groups as diverse as passerines, parrots, owls, penguins, hummingbirds and seabirds.

Phylogeny

Large-scale genomic studies, including whole-genome sequencing projects published in the 2010s, have clarified many relationships, grouping Neoaves into several major subclades such as Columbaves, Elementaves, Aequornithes and Telluraves. Nevertheless, the exact branching order near the base of the group remains an active area of research.
