Robert Michels

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Robert Michels (9 January 1876 – 3 May 1936) was a German-born Italian sociologist who formulated the iron law of oligarchy in Political Parties: A Sociological Study of the Oligarchical Tendencies of Modern Democracy (1911). A former socialist disappointed by the German Social Democratic Party from within, he studied how revolutionary parties develop conservative leadership castes, and generalised the finding to all complex organisations.

Michels's work, with those of Mosca and Pareto, founded elite theory in political science. His later trajectory is itself a case study of his themes: disillusioned with democracy's organisational fate, he emigrated intellectually and literally to Italy, joined Mussolini's Fascist Party and held a chair at Perugia under the regime — a development his critics read as the iron law's author surrendering to it, and his defenders as consistent pessimism about mass democracy. Modern scholarship treats the “law” as a robust tendency thesis rather than a literal law, and Michels as a founder of the sociology of organisations and parties.
