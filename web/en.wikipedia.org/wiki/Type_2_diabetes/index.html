Type 2 diabetes (T2D), formerly known as adult-onset diabetes, is a form of diabetes mellitus characterized by high blood sugar, insulin resistance, and a relative lack of insulin. Common symptoms include increased thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, increased hunger, fatigue, and blurred vision. Symptoms typically develop slowly. Long-term complications include cardiovascular disease, stroke, diabetic retinopathy (which may lead to blindness), kidney failure, and poor blood flow in the limbs that may lead to amputations.

Cause

Type 2 diabetes develops when the body's cells fail to respond properly to insulin (insulin resistance) and, over time, when the pancreatic beta cells cannot produce enough insulin to compensate. It is primarily caused by a combination of genetic factors and lifestyle factors, most importantly excess body weight and insufficient exercise. A number of dietary factors, including high consumption of sugar-sweetened drinks, also increase risk.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is by blood tests such as fasting plasma glucose (≥7.0 mmol/L or 126 mg/dL), the oral glucose tolerance test (2-hour glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L or 200 mg/dL), or glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c ≥6.5%). Type 2 diabetes differs from diabetes mellitus type 1, in which insulin production is absent due to autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells.

Management

Management begins with lifestyle modification: increased exercise, weight loss, and a healthy diet. If blood sugar remains elevated, the medication metformin is generally recommended first. Other medicines include sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, and insulin itself. Regular screening for complications of the eyes, kidneys, and feet is important.

Epidemiology

Type 2 diabetes accounts for roughly 90% of all diabetes cases. Rates have risen markedly since the 1960s in parallel with obesity. In 2017 about 462 million people were affected worldwide, and prevalence continues to climb, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.
