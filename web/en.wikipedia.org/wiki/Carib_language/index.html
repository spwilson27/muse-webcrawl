Carib language

Carib (endonym Kari'nja or Karìna) is a Cariban language spoken by the Kali'na (Carib) people of the northern coast of South America, in Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, French Guiana and Brazil. It is the namesake and one of the best-documented members of the Cariban family, which at European contact was spread across northern South America and into the Lesser Antilles. The number of fluent speakers is estimated at roughly 8,000–10,000, and the language is considered endangered, though it has official recognition and is taught in schools in parts of Suriname and French Guiana.

Kari'nja is spoken in coastal communities from Venezuela to French Guiana and Brazil.

History

The Cariban languages are thought to have spread from the Guiana highlands and the Orinoco basin. Kari'nja speakers reached the Caribbean islands shortly before the Europeans, giving their name to the Caribbean Sea; the Island Carib language of the Lesser Antilles, however, was largely Arawakan in grammar with Cariban vocabulary, while the Garifuna language of Central America descends from it. European missionaries and colonial officials produced the first grammars and dictionaries of mainland Carib from the 17th century onward, making it one of the earliest documented Indigenous languages of the Americas.

Features

Kari'nja is a polysynthetic, agglutinative language with a relatively small phoneme inventory, including a high central vowel often written y. It distinguishes inclusive and exclusive first-person plurals ("we including you" vs. "we excluding you"), marks evidentiality distinctions in some dialects, and has a rich system of postpositions and verbal person prefixes. Word order is relatively free and pragmatically driven. Several regional dialects — including Venezuelan, Guyanese, Surinamese (with eastern and western varieties) and Brazilian — differ in phonology and vocabulary.

Status and revitalisation

Intergenerational transmission has weakened under pressure from Spanish, English, Dutch, French and Portuguese, and most younger speakers are bilingual. Nevertheless Kari'nja maintains ceremonial and everyday use in villages such as Galibi in French Guiana and Bigi Poika in Suriname, and orthography standardisation, school materials and media programming support its maintenance.
