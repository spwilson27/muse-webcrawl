Early medieval Ireland
Period: c. 400–800 AD
Followed by: Viking Age Ireland

Early medieval Ireland, from about 400 to 800 AD, covers the period from the traditional lifetime of Saint Patrick and the conversion to Christianity to the beginning of the Viking raids. It saw the emergence of Irish monastic culture, the flowering of Insular art, and a society organised into small kingdoms called túatha.

Society

Ireland was politically fragmented among over a hundred local kings, with provincial overkings and an increasingly assertive Uí Néill dynasty claiming the high kingship centred on Tara. Law was maintained by professional jurists (brehons), and status depended on wealth in cattle and clients.

Church and culture

Monasteries such as Armagh, Clonmacnoise, Iona and Bangor became major centres of learning, producing illuminated manuscripts like the Book of Kells and sending missionaries — Columba, Columbanus and others — across Britain and continental Europe. Irish scholars preserved Latin learning and developed distinctive penitentials, computus and vernacular literature, while metalworkers produced masterpieces such as the Tara Brooch and Ardagh Chalice.
