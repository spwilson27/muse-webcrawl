"Sultans of Swing" is a song by the British rock band Dire Straits, written by frontman Mark Knopfler. Released as the band's debut single in May 1978 and included on their self-titled debut album, it became an international hit in early 1979 and launched the band's career. The song is celebrated for Knopfler's clean-toned, virtuosic lead guitar work, especially its extended closing solo, and routinely appears on lists of the greatest guitar songs ever recorded.

Composition and inspiration

Knopfler wrote the song after watching a mediocre Dixieland jazz band playing to a nearly empty pub in South London; the band's leader announced with grand irony that they were "the Sultans of Swing". The lyric paints the scene — a rainy night, a small audience of indifferent teenagers ("the boys fool around in the corner"), and individual musicians, including "Guitar George", who "knows all the chords" but plays rhythm strictly, and young Harry, who works by day but plays honky-tonk at night. Knopfler originally composed it on a steel guitar in open tuning and considered it dull until he reworked it on a Stratocaster; the song is in D minor.

Recording and release

A demo of the song, recorded at Pathway Studios in 1977, won airplay on BBC Radio London through DJ Charlie Gillett's Honky Tonk programme, attracting record company interest. Dire Straits recorded the album version at Basing Street Studios in February 1978 with producer Muff Winwood. After the album took off in the United States, the single was re-released and climbed to No. 4 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 8 in the UK Singles Chart in 1979.

Legacy

"Sultans of Swing" was a staple of Dire Straits' live shows, with celebrated extended versions on the live album Alchemy (1984). Knopfler's fingerstyle playing on the track — performed without a pick, with a distinctive arpeggiated final solo — is considered one of the most influential guitar performances in rock. The song was inducted into discussions of all-time great singles and remains one of the band's signature recordings.
