Severn bore

The Severn bore is a tidal bore seen on the tidal reaches of the River Severn in southwestern England. It forms when the rising tide is funnelled into the increasingly narrow and shallow estuary, creating a wave that travels upstream against the river current. The bore is rated from one to five stars in published guides according to its predicted height and quality.

Formation

The Severn Estuary has the second largest tidal range in the world, about 15 metres (50 ft). On big tides the incoming water is compressed by the estuary's funnel shape into a steep-fronted wave. The bore travels at roughly 8–13 mph and is visible on about 130 mornings a year, with the largest bores occurring in the days after the new and full moons in February–April and August–October. Notable viewing points include Minsterworth, Newnham and Over Bridge near Gloucester.

Surfing

The bore is famous among surfers, who first rode it in 1955 and now gather to ride the wave for miles upstream. In 2006 the British surfer Steve King set a world record by riding the Severn bore for 7.6 miles (12.2 km), the longest ride on a single wave at the time.
