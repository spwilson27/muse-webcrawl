Brussels Airport (IATA: BRU) is an international airport 12 km northeast of Brussels, Belgium, located in the municipality of Zaventem in Flemish Brabant. It is Belgium's largest airport, handling around 25 million passengers a year before the COVID-19 pandemic, and is home port for Brussels Airlines.

History

The airport originated as a German military airfield built during the occupation of 1940–44 on flat land near Steenokkerzeel and Zaventem. After the war it became Belgium's national civil airport, and a new terminal opened for the Expo 58 world's fair. A major expansion in the 1990s added Pier B (1994), and Pier A opened in 2002 for Schengen flights.

2016 attacks

On 22 March 2016 the departures hall was struck by two suicide bombings as part of coordinated attacks in Brussels that also hit the Maelbeek metro station; 32 victims were killed in total across the attacks. The airport closed for nearly two weeks and reopened with reduced capacity and enhanced security.

Facilities

The airport has a single terminal with two piers, two runways in use for commercial traffic, and a railway station beneath the terminal connecting it to Brussels and other Belgian cities. It is a major European cargo hub, particularly for pharmaceuticals.
