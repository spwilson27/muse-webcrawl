Farmall

ManufacturerInternational Harvester
Production1924–1973 (main run)
TypeRow-crop agricultural tractors

Farmall was a model name and later a brand name for tractors manufactured by the American company International Harvester. The original Farmall, introduced in 1924, is widely credited with revolutionizing tractor design by popularizing the row-crop configuration.

History

Before the Farmall, most tractors were heavy standard-tread machines suited mainly to plowing and belt work. The Farmall was designed as a lightweight, general-purpose machine whose narrow front end and high rear clearance allowed it to straddle crop rows for cultivating — work still widely done by horses. Its success forced competitors such as John Deere and Ford to develop rival row-crop tractors.

Models

Famous Farmall models include the original Farmall Regular, the letter series (A, B, C, H, and M) introduced in 1939 with Raymond Loewy's streamlined styling, the Cub (1947) for small farms, and the numbered series of the 1950s and 1960s such as the 460, 560, 806, and 1066. The H and M became among the most popular tractors ever sold in North America. International Harvester phased out the Farmall name in the early 1970s, though Case IH later revived it for a line of compact tractors.

Legacy

More than five million Farmall-branded tractors were built, and restored examples are fixtures of antique tractor shows across the United States.
