James Iovine (born March 11, 1953) is an American entrepreneur, record executive, and former record producer. He co-founded Interscope Records in 1990 and served as chairman of Interscope Geffen A&M. With Dr. Dre he founded Beats Electronics (Beats by Dre) in 2006, which Apple acquired in 2014 for $3 billion, after which Iovine joined Apple and helped launch Apple Music.

Early career

Iovine began as a recording engineer in New York in the 1970s, working at the Record Plant on sessions for John Lennon and Bruce Springsteen, including Born to Run and Darkness on the Edge of Town. As a producer he worked on albums such as Patti Smith's Easter (featuring "Because the Night"), Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers' Damn the Torpedoes, and U2's Rattle and Hum.

Interscope and Beats

Interscope, founded with Ted Field and backed by Atlantic Records, became one of the defining labels of the 1990s and 2000s, releasing records by Tupac Shakur, Dr. Dre, Nine Inch Nails, Eminem, 50 Cent, and Lady Gaga. Beats Electronics, launched with Dr. Dre, transformed headphones into a fashion category and spawned the Beats Music streaming service, folded into Apple Music in 2015.

Other work

Iovine was a mentor on American Idol and, with Dr. Dre, endowed the USC Jimmy Iovine and Andre Young Academy for arts, technology and the business of innovation in 2013. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022 (Ahmet Ertegun Award, with his induction presented alongside Dr. Dre's later induction).
