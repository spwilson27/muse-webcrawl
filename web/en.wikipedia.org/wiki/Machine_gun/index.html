Machine gun

A machine gun (MG) is a fully automatic, rifled auto-loading firearm designed for sustained direct fire with rifle cartridges. Unlike other automatic firearms such as automatic rifles and submachine guns, machine guns are typically belt-fed or fed from large-capacity magazines, mounted on bipods, tripods or vehicles, and optimised for sustained fire in a support role.

History

Early rapid-fire weapons included the hand-cranked Gatling gun of the 1860s and the first true recoil-operated automatic design, the Maxim gun of 1884, invented by Hiram Maxim. Machine guns transformed land warfare in the First World War, where water-cooled guns on fixed mountings dominated defensive positions. The interwar and Second World War periods produced lighter air-cooled designs such as the German MG 34 and MG 42 and the concept of the general-purpose machine gun.

Classification

Machine guns are commonly divided into light machine guns, carried and fired by individual soldiers; medium machine guns, usually crew-served on mounts; heavy machine guns, firing large cartridges such as 12.7 mm; and general-purpose machine guns, which can fill both light and medium roles. Squad automatic weapons are a related category of light support weapons.

Mechanism

Machine guns operate by gas, recoil or blowback mechanisms and typically fire from an open bolt to aid cooling. Sustained fire requires quick-change barrels on many designs, since a fixed barrel would overheat.
