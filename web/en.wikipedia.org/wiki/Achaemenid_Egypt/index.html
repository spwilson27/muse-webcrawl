Achaemenid Egypt refers to the two periods when ancient Egypt was ruled by the Persian Achaemenid Empire: the Twenty-seventh Dynasty (525–404 BC) and the Thirty-first Dynasty (343–332 BC). The Persian kings ruled as pharaohs, administering Egypt through a resident governor, the satrap.

First Persian period

Cambyses II defeated Pharaoh Psamtik III at the Battle of Pelusium in 525 BC and took the pharaonic throne. His successor Darius I completed a canal from the Nile to the Red Sea, codified Egyptian law, and built temples, earning a reputation as a lawgiver. Egypt, a rich province supplying grain and tribute, revolted several times — notably at the death of Darius and during the Inaros revolt of the 460s BC, supported by Athens. Persian rule ended in 404 BC when Egypt regained independence under Amyrtaeus.

Second Persian period

Artaxerxes III reconquered Egypt in 343 BC, defeating Nectanebo II, the last native pharaoh. This second period was short and unpopular; in 332 BC the satrap Mazaces surrendered Egypt without a battle to Alexander the Great.
