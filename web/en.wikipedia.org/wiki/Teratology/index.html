Teratology

Teratology is the study of congenital abnormalities and abnormal formations in organisms — their causes, mechanisms, and patterns. A teratogen is any agent that can disturb the development of an embryo or fetus and cause a birth defect.

History and principles

Although unusual births were recorded from antiquity, modern teratology emerged in the 20th century: Norman Gregg linked rubella in pregnancy to congenital cataracts in 1941, and the thalidomide tragedy of the late 1950s and early 1960s showed that a drug could cause severe limb malformations. James G. Wilson formulated six principles of teratology covering susceptibility by genotype and developmental stage, dose–response relationships, and specific mechanisms of action.

Causes

Teratogenic factors include infections (rubella, Zika virus), drugs and chemicals (thalidomide, alcohol, isotretinoin), ionising radiation, maternal metabolic conditions, and genetic abnormalities.
