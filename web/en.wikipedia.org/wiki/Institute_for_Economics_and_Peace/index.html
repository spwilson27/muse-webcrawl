The Institute for Economics and Peace (IEP) is an independent, non-partisan, non-profit think tank dedicated to measuring peace and assessing its economic benefits. Founded in 2007 by Australian technology entrepreneur Steve Killelea, it has offices in Sydney, New York, Brussels, Mexico City, The Hague, and Harare.

Work

IEP is best known for the Global Peace Index (GPI), an annual ranking of around 163 countries by their level of peacefulness, first published in 2007. The index uses 23 qualitative and quantitative indicators grouped into ongoing conflict, societal safety and security, and militarization. The institute also produces the Global Terrorism Index, the Positive Peace Index, the Ecological Threat Report, and national peace indices for countries including the United States, the United Kingdom, and Mexico. Its research develops the "Positive Peace" framework — the attitudes, institutions, and structures that sustain peaceful societies — and estimates the global economic impact of violence, which it has valued at roughly ten percent of world GDP in purchasing-power terms.

Reception

The Global Peace Index is widely cited by media, governments, and international organizations. Like other composite indices, it has been debated on methodological grounds, including indicator weighting and data availability, which IEP addresses in its annual methodology reports.
