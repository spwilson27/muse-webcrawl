Barrel racing


A competitor rounding the second barrel

Barrel racing is a rodeo event in which a horse and rider attempt to run a cloverleaf pattern around three preset barrels in the fastest time. Although both men and women compete at some levels, it is primarily a women's event in professional rodeo, where it is sanctioned by the Women's Professional Rodeo Association (WPRA) and contested at events of the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA), culminating at the National Finals Rodeo in Las Vegas.

The pattern

Three barrels are set in a triangle. The rider may begin with either the right or left barrel, circle each barrel as tightly as possible, and sprint back across the timing line. Knocking over a barrel incurs a five-second penalty in most rule sets. Winning times at large arenas are typically 13–15 seconds, with the rider's and horse's ability to rate speed into the turns and accelerate out of them being decisive.

History and horses

The event developed in the early 20th century as a novelty event for women at rodeos, originally with figure-eight and cloverleaf variants; it was standardised as the event grew, and the WPRA was founded in 1948. Barrel horses are most often American Quarter Horses valued for agility and short-burst speed; elite futurity and professional horses can command prices in the hundreds of thousands of dollars. Famous competitors include Charmayne James, whose horse Scamper won ten world titles, and Hailey Kinsel with her mare Sister.
