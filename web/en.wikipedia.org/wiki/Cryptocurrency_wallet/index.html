Cryptocurrency wallet

A cryptocurrency wallet is a device, physical medium, program or service that stores the public and private keys used to transact with cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin. The wallet does not literally hold coins — the cryptocurrency exists only as records on a blockchain — but the keys it stores prove ownership and authorise transfers. Lose of the private key means permanent loss of access to the associated funds.

Types

Wallets are commonly divided into "hot" wallets, which are connected to the internet (mobile, desktop and web wallets), and "cold" wallets, which are kept offline, including dedicated hardware wallets and paper wallets. Custodial wallets are operated by a third party such as an exchange, which holds the keys on the user's behalf, while non-custodial wallets leave the user in sole control. Most modern wallets are hierarchical deterministic (HD) wallets, generating all keys from a single seed phrase, typically 12 or 24 words under the BIP39 standard.

Security

Common threats include phishing, malware that steals seed phrases, and SIM-swap attacks against exchange accounts. Best practice includes backing up the recovery phrase offline, verifying addresses before sending, and keeping large holdings in cold storage. See also Bitcoin and Public-key cryptography.
