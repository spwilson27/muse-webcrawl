Usage


A usage guide

Usage is the way in which words and phrases are actually used by speakers and writers of a language — the conventions of vocabulary, grammar, spelling and idiom that are current in a speech community. In linguistics, usage is the empirical reality of language, studied through corpora; in the long-running debate between descriptivism and prescriptivism, descriptivists hold that usage itself defines correctness, while prescriptivists appeal to established rules and authorities.

Usage guides

A long tradition of usage guides attempts to record and adjudicate disputed points — split infinitives, "less" versus "fewer", the dangling modifier, and shifting meanings such as literally or decimate. Landmarks include H. W. Fowler's A Dictionary of Modern English Usage (1926), Strunk and White's The Elements of Style (1959), and modern corpus-based works such as Merriam-Webster's Dictionary of English Usage and Bryan Garner's Modern English Usage, with its "language-change index" grading how far disputed usages have spread.

Other senses

In law, usage can mean a long-established practice with normative force (as in trade usage under commercial law); in other fields it simply means the act or extent of using something — energy usage, data usage.
