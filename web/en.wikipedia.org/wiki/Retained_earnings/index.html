Retained earnings

Retained earnings are the accumulated net income of a corporation that is retained by the corporation at a particular point of time, rather than distributed to shareholders as dividends. They form part of shareholders' equity on the balance sheet and represent the total profits a company has earned since its inception that have been reinvested in the business instead of being paid out.

Calculation

The retained earnings at the end of a period equal the beginning retained earnings plus net income for the period, minus dividends declared. A negative balance is called an accumulated deficit. Because retained earnings accumulate over the whole life of the firm, an established company may carry a very large balance; Apple's retained earnings, for example, have been reduced in recent decades by large share repurchase programmes, which are charged partly against retained earnings.

Uses and restrictions

Retained earnings provide an internal source of finance for expansion, debt repayment and working capital. In some jurisdictions part of the balance may be legally restricted (appropriated retained earnings), for example by loan covenants or statutory reserves, and therefore unavailable for dividends. Retained earnings are not the same as cash: profits may be tied up in receivables, inventory or fixed assets.
