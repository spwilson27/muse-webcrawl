The Austin City Limits Music Festival (often abbreviated ACL Fest or simply ACL) is an annual music festival held in Zilker Park in Austin, Texas, over two consecutive three-day weekends each October. Inspired by the KLRU/PBS music series Austin City Limits, the festival was founded in 2002 and is produced by Austin-based C3 Presents.

History

The festival began in 2002 as a two-day event drawing about 25,000 fans per day. It expanded to three days in 2003 and, in 2013, to its current two-weekend format, with largely identical lineups on each weekend. The 2020 edition was cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic and replaced by a virtual retrospective event. Daily attendance is capped at roughly 75,000, bringing an estimated total attendance of about 450,000 across both weekends.

Festival features

Performances run simultaneously on eight stages, spanning rock, indie, country, folk, blues, gospel, hip hop, and electronic music. Beyond music, the festival includes the ACL Eats food court showcasing Austin restaurants, the family-oriented "Austin Kiddie Limits" area, art installations and markets, and sustainability programmes such as "Rock & Recycle".

Economic and cultural impact

ACL Fest is one of the largest music festivals in the United States and a major contributor to Austin's "Live Music Capital of the World" identity, generating an economic impact estimated in the hundreds of millions of dollars for the city each year, along with substantial funding for Austin parks through the Austin Parks Foundation.
