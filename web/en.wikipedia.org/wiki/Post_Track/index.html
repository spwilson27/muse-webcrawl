Post Track

Type: Historic mail route
Location: Australia
Era: 19th century

Post Track refers to a historic mail and supply route in Australia along which postal services were carried, typically by horse, camel or coach, linking remote settlements and stations before the advent of reliable roads and motor transport. Such tracks were often based on older Aboriginal pathways and stock routes, following water sources across arid country.

History

In the 19th century, the expansion of pastoral settlement into the interior created demand for regular mail delivery to isolated properties. Contractors carried mail on horseback or by camel train along defined tracks, stopping at change stations and waterholes. Routes such as those in South Australia and Queensland became lifelines for remote communities, delivering letters, newspapers and small goods on schedules that could span weeks.

Legacy

With the arrival of motor vehicles and later air mail services such as the Royal Flying Doctor Service's pedal-radio network, the old post tracks fell out of use, though many survive as station tracks, heritage trails and unsealed roads. They are remembered in Australian bush literature and local museums as symbols of the isolation of outback life.
