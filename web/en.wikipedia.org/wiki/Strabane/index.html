Strabane (from Irish An Srath Bán, meaning "the white river-holm") is a town in west County Tyrone, Northern Ireland. It lies on the east bank of the River Mourne, where the Mourne and River Finn meet to form the River Foyle, directly across the border from the twin town of Lifford in County Donegal, Republic of Ireland. Strabane had a population of 13,172 at the 2021 census. It was heavily affected during the Troubles, and was for a time one of the most economically deprived towns in Northern Ireland.

History

The area was settled in the early 17th century during the Plantation of Ulster, when the town was granted to the Earl of Abercorn (James Hamilton) and Scottish settlers built a new town near an older Irish settlement. Strabane grew as a market and later a linen and printing town. The printer John Dunlap, who printed the first copies of the United States Declaration of Independence, was born in Strabane in 1747, as was the writer Flann O'Brien (Brian O'Nolan) in 1911. During the Troubles the town suffered bombings and shootings, and its border location made it a focus of security operations. In August 2011, severe flooding damaged parts of the town.

Geography and transport

Strabane stands at the confluence of the rivers Finn and Mourne. The A5 road runs through the town, linking Derry to the north with Omagh and the A4 to the south. The town lost its railway in 1965 with the closure of the County Donegal Railways and Great Northern Railway lines; the nearest station is now in Derry. A long-planned A5 dual-carriageway upgrade has been the subject of legal challenges and delays.

Culture

Strabane is home to the Alley Theatre and arts centre. The town's musical heritage includes country and folk traditions, and its murals and the large "Tinney's" sculptural figures reflect local history. Gaelic games are popular, with Strabane Sigersons GFC representing the town.
