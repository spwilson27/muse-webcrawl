Miao people

The Miao are an ethnic group native to southern China, recognised by the Chinese government as one of the country's 56 official ethnic groups. Numbering roughly twelve million in China, they live mainly in the mountainous provinces of Guizhou, Hunan, Yunnan, Sichuan, Guangxi, Hainan, Guangdong and Hubei. Related groups known as Hmong live in Southeast Asia and in a worldwide diaspora.

History

Miao groups trace their origins to peoples of the middle Yangtze region who migrated southward and westward over many centuries under pressure from expanding Han Chinese states. A series of Miao rebellions against the Ming and Qing dynasties — notably in the 18th and 19th centuries — reflected resistance to taxation and land encroachment. Many Miao communities settled in high-altitude villages, practising terraced wet-rice farming alongside maize and other dry crops.

Culture

Miao culture is renowned for its embroidery, batik, and silverwork: festival dress can include elaborate horn-shaped silver headdresses and densely embroidered jackets whose patterns encode clan and regional identity. Festivals such as the Miao New Year and the Sisters' Meal Festival feature lusheng (reed-pipe) music, dancing, buffalo fights and courtship songs. Traditional religion combines ancestor veneration with shamanic practice, though some Miao are Christian, a legacy of 19th- and 20th-century missionary activity.

Language

The Miao languages belong to the Hmongic branch of the Hmong–Mien family and comprise several mutually unintelligible varieties, sometimes treated as distinct languages (Hmu, Qo Xiong, Hmong and others). Romanised writing systems were devised in the 20th century, and minority-language education is offered in some autonomous prefectures, such as Qiandongnan Miao and Dong Autonomous Prefecture in Guizhou.
