Ahmad Shah Massoud

Ahmad Shah Massoud (Dari: احمد شاه مسعود; 2 September 1953 – 9 September 2001) was an Afghan military leader and politician. A guerrilla commander during the Soviet–Afghan War, he later served as Minister of Defense and led the Northern Alliance against the Taliban. He was nicknamed the "Lion of Panjshir" for his defence of the Panjshir Valley.

Soviet–Afghan War

An ethnic Tajik from the Panjshir Valley, Massoud studied engineering at Kabul University and joined the Islamist movement of Burhanuddin Rabbani. From 1979 he organised one of the most effective mujahideen resistances against Soviet forces, repelling repeated major offensives into Panjshir; in 1983 he controversially negotiated a temporary truce with the Soviets. His administration of the valley — organising schools, health services and mining of lapis lazuli and emeralds — won him a reputation unusual among the commanders.

Civil war and assassination

After the fall of Najibullah in 1992, Massoud became Minister of Defense but Kabul descended into civil war against Gulbuddin Hekmatyar and later the Taliban, who seized the capital in 1996. Massoud retreated to the northeast and led the United Front (Northern Alliance), holding out against the Taliban and warning Western governments about al-Qaeda. On 9 September 2001 he was assassinated by two al-Qaeda suicide bombers posing as journalists — two days before the September 11 attacks. In 2021 the Afghan parliament posthumously declared him a national hero, and 9 September is observed in his memory.
