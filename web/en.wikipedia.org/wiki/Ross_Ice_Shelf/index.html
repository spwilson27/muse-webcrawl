Ross Ice Shelf

LocationRoss Sea, Antarctica
AreaApprox. 487,000 km² (188,000 sq mi)
ThicknessSeveral hundred metres
Named forSir James Clark Ross

The Ross Ice Shelf is the largest ice shelf of Antarctica, with an area of roughly 487,000 square kilometres (188,000 sq mi) — about the size of France — and a thickness of several hundred metres. It fills a great embayment of the Ross Sea between Ross Island and the Edward VII Peninsula, and its nearly vertical seaward face, known historically as the Ice Barrier, rises 15 to 50 metres above the sea.

Exploration

The shelf was discovered by the expedition of Sir James Clark Ross in January 1841; Ross, unable to sail through it, named the formation the "Victoria Barrier", and it later took his name. The ice front served as the gateway for the heroic-age expeditions to the South Pole: Roald Amundsen sledged onto the shelf at the Bay of Whales in 1911 on his way to the Pole, while Robert Falcon Scott's and Ernest Shackleton's parties used routes from Ross Island at its western edge. Richard E. Byrd established the Little America bases on the shelf beginning in 1928.

Geography and glaciology

The shelf is fed by glaciers and ice streams flowing from both the West Antarctic and East Antarctic ice sheets, including the great West Antarctic ice streams and the Beardmore and other Transantarctic Mountain outlet glaciers. Much of the shelf is afloat; Roosevelt Island is an ice-covered landmass entirely surrounded by the shelf. The front advances seaward at up to several metres per day and periodically calves enormous tabular icebergs — most famously iceberg B-15 in 2000, one of the largest ever recorded.

Because the Ross Ice Shelf buttresses the ice streams behind it, its stability is a major question in projections of sea-level rise from West Antarctica. Scientific programmes, including drilling projects through the shelf into the water cavity and sediments below, study its history and its interaction with the ocean. The shelf's western margin, at McMurdo Sound, is the site of McMurdo Station and Scott Base, the largest research stations in Antarctica.
