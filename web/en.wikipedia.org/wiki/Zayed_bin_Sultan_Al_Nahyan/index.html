Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan

Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan
Born: 6 May 1918, Abu Dhabi
Died: 2 November 2004, Abu Dhabi
Emir of Abu Dhabi: 1966-2004
President of the UAE: 1971-2004

Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan (1918-2004) was the founding father and first President of the United Arab Emirates, serving from the federation's establishment in 1971 until his death in 2004. He was also the ruler (Emir) of Abu Dhabi from 1966. Revered in the Emirates as the "Father of the Nation," he is widely credited with using oil wealth to build modern infrastructure, education and healthcare.

Early life and rule of Abu Dhabi

The youngest of four sons of Sheikh Sultan bin Zayed, Zayed spent years as the ruler's representative in the eastern region at Al Ain, where he gained a reputation for mediating tribal disputes and an interest in falconry, poetry and traditional life. In 1966 he succeeded his elder brother Sheikh Shakhbut as ruler of Abu Dhabi with British support, as oil exports — begun in 1962 — transformed the emirate's revenues.

Federation

After Britain announced its withdrawal from the Trucial States, Zayed worked with Sheikh Rashid of Dubai to unite the emirates. The United Arab Emirates was formed on 2 December 1971, and Zayed was elected its first president, a position to which he was re-elected every five years. He directed oil income into roads, ports, schools, housing and hospitals across all seven emirates, including the poorer northern ones.

Legacy

Zayed promoted tolerance and regional diplomacy, mediated disputes within the Arab world, and supported large-scale greening and afforestation projects in the desert. He died on 2 November 2004 and was succeeded in both his roles by his son, Sheikh Khalifa. His name is borne by the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in Abu Dhabi, where he is buried, as well as by numerous institutions; 2018 was declared the "Year of Zayed" to mark the centenary of his birth.
