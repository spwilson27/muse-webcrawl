Eritrean–Ethiopian War
Date: 6 May 1998 – 18 June 2000
Location: Eritrea–Ethiopia border
Result: Algiers Agreement; border awarded largely to Eritrea by boundary commission
Deaths: estimates 70,000–100,000+

The Eritrean–Ethiopian War, also known as the Badme War, was a conflict between Ethiopia and Eritrea from 1998 to 2000 over disputed border territory, principally the town of Badme. It was one of the largest wars in Africa at the time, fought with hundreds of thousands of troops in trench warfare reminiscent of the First World War.

Course of the war

Fighting began in May 1998 after Eritrean forces entered Badme. After a lull, Ethiopia launched major offensives in 1999 and 2000, ultimately retaking Badme and advancing into Eritrea before a cessation of hostilities was signed in June 2000. The Algiers Agreement of December 2000 formally ended the war and established a boundary commission, whose ruling Ethiopia long refused to implement, leaving a "no war, no peace" stalemate until a reconciliation in 2018.
