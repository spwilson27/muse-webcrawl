Circumstellar disk

A circumstellar disk (or disc) is a torus, pancake or ring-shaped accumulation of matter composed of gas, dust, planetesimals, asteroids or collision fragments in orbit around a star.

Circumstellar disk
Types: Protoplanetary, debris, accretion
Examples: Beta Pictoris, HL Tauri

Types

Around young stars, protoplanetary disks are the birthplaces of planets. Around older stars, debris disks — such as that of Beta Pictoris — consist of dust generated by colliding planetesimals, analogous to the Solar System's Kuiper belt. Accretion disks around compact objects are a related phenomenon.

Observation

Disks are detected by infrared excess and imaged directly by telescopes including Hubble, ALMA and ground-based adaptive-optics instruments; ALMA's 2014 image of HL Tauri revealed ring structures thought to mark forming planets.

Evolution

Protoplanetary disks typically disperse within about 10 million years through accretion, photoevaporation and planet formation.
