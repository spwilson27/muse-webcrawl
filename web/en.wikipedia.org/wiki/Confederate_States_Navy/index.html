Confederate States Navy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


CSS Virginia, the Confederacy's most famous ironclad

The Confederate States Navy (CSN) was the naval branch of the Confederate States armed forces, established by an act of the Confederate Congress on 21 February 1861. It was responsible for Confederate naval operations during the American Civil War against the United States Navy.

Under Secretary of the Navy Stephen Mallory, the CSN, always vastly outnumbered (the Union Navy grew to over 600 ships), pursued a strategy of coastal and river defense, commerce raiding, and technological innovation to offset Union superiority.

Operations and innovation

The Confederacy pioneered the combat use of ironclads: CSS Virginia, built on the hull of the scuttled USS Merrimack, fought USS Monitor at Hampton Roads in March 1862 in the first battle between ironclad warships. Commerce raiders such as CSS Alabama, built in Britain and commanded by Raphael Semmes, sank or captured dozens of Union merchant ships. The CSN also deployed torpedoes (mines) on a large scale, and the submarine CSS H. L. Hunley became the first combat submarine to sink an enemy warship, USS Housatonic, in February 1864.

Organization

The navy never had more than a few dozen effective warships, supplemented by privateers early in the war and by blockade runners operated largely by private interests. Its forces fought in notable engagements at New Orleans, Mobile Bay, and on the James and Mississippi rivers. The service ceased to exist with the collapse of the Confederacy in the spring of 1865.
