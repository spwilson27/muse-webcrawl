Ariq Böke (c. 1219–1266), also spelled Arik Buka or Erik Böge, was the seventh and youngest son of Tolui and grandson of Genghis Khan. After the death of his brother, the Great Khan Möngke, in 1259, Ariq Böke was proclaimed khan by a kurultai in the Mongolian heartland in 1260, while his elder brother Kublai was simultaneously elected by a rival assembly in China.

Toluid civil war

The ensuing Toluid Civil War (1260–1264) split the Mongol Empire. Ariq Böke drew support from the traditionalist steppe aristocracy and from much of the Ögedeid and Chagatai houses, and controlled the capital region around Karakorum, but Kublai controlled the far richer resources of north China. After defeats at Shimultai (1260) and Alemdar (1261), famine in the capital and the defection of key allies such as Alghu of the Chagatai Khanate undermined Ariq Böke's position. In 1264 he surrendered to Kublai, who pardoned him after a public reckoning with his followers; he died in captivity in 1266, in circumstances some sources regard as suspicious.

Legacy

Although he lost, Ariq Böke's bid confirmed the permanent fragmentation of the unified Mongol Empire into effectively independent khanates. His descendants later ruled in the Yuan dynasty's steppe domains, and his name was invoked by later Mongol traditionalists resisting the sinicisation associated with Kublai's line.
