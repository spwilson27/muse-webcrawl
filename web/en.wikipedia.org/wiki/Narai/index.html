Narai

Narai the Great
Reign: 26 October 1656 – 11 July 1688
Kingdom: Ayutthaya
Predecessor: Prasat Thong (dynasty founder)
Successor: Phetracha

Narai (Thai: สมเด็จพระนารายณ์มหาราช; 1633 – 11 July 1688), known as Narai the Great, was the king of the Ayutthaya kingdom from 1656 to 1688 and arguably the most famous king of the Prasat Thong dynasty. His reign was the most prosperous and cosmopolitan period of Ayutthaya's history, distinguished by extensive diplomatic and commercial contacts with European powers, Persia and Japan.

Reign

Narai moved his principal residence to Lopburi, where he built a palace combining Thai and European architectural elements. He promoted foreign trade, granting concessions to the Dutch and English East India Companies and exchanging embassies with the France of Louis XIV: Siamese ambassadors were received at Versailles in 1686, and French envoys including the Chevalier de Chaumont and, later, a French garrison under the terms negotiated by Narai's Greek-born minister Constantine Phaulkon, reached Ayutthaya.

Fall

The growing French military presence and Phaulkon's influence provoked a xenophobic reaction led by the general Phetracha. During Narai's final illness in 1688, Phetracha seized power in the Siamese revolution of 1688, executed Phetracha's rivals including Phaulkon, expelled the French, and took the throne on Narai's death.
