Ocean Terminal, Hong Kong

Ocean Terminal is a cruise terminal and shopping centre located on Canton Road in Tsim Sha Tsui, Kowloon, Hong Kong. Opened on 3 March 1966, it was the first cruise terminal in Asia and, at the time, one of the largest retail developments in the territory. The terminal forms part of the Harbour City complex developed by The Wharf (Holdings) Limited.

History

The pier occupies the site of the former Kowloon wharves of Hong Kong and Kowloon Wharf and Godown Company. When Ocean Terminal opened in 1966, Hong Kong still received a large share of its passengers by ocean liner, and the terminal provided deep-water berthing for the largest passenger ships of the day alongside multi-storey retail space — a pioneering combination of transport hub and shopping mall. Through the 1970s and 1980s, the adjacent development grew into Harbour City, Hong Kong's largest shopping complex, and Ocean Terminal was integrated into it.

Facilities

The terminal building extends into Victoria Harbour with berthing for large cruise vessels, including ships that cannot be accommodated at the newer Kai Tak Cruise Terminal. Its retail floors contain numerous shops and restaurants, and the Ocean Terminal Deck, a rooftop observatory opened in 2017, offers panoramic views across the harbour toward Hong Kong Island. The complex is within walking distance of the Star Ferry pier, the Tsim Sha Tsui MTR station, and the Hong Kong Cultural Centre.

Significance

Ocean Terminal remains a landmark of Tsim Sha Tsui and a symbol of Hong Kong's maritime heritage. It is a popular vantage point for harbour events such as the New Year fireworks and the Symphony of Lights.
