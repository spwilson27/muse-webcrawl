Gino Bartali

Gino Bartali (Italian pronunciation: [ˈdʒiːno ˈbartali]; 18 July 1914 – 5 May 2000) was an Italian road racing cyclist, one of the most successful riders of the pre- and post-Second World War era. He won the Tour de France twice (1938 and 1948) and the Giro d'Italia three times (1936, 1937, 1946), along with numerous classics including Milan–San Remo four times and the Giro di Lombardia three times.

Career

Born in Ponte a Ema, Florence, Bartali turned professional in 1935 and quickly became Italy's leading rider. His rivalry with Fausto Coppi — five years younger — divided Italian cycling fans into bartaliani and coppiani and mirrored the country's postwar social tensions: Bartali the devout, traditional Catholic from Tuscany, Coppi the modern northern innovator. His 1948 Tour de France victory, ten years after his first and achieved with a famous mountain comeback after he had trailed by over 20 minutes, is credited in Italian popular memory with helping to calm the nation after the attempted assassination of Communist leader Palmiro Togliatti in July 1948 — a story Bartali himself and historians treat with some nuance. He retired in 1954.

War record

After his death it emerged that during the German occupation Bartali had used his training rides as cover for a courier network organised with Cardinal Elia Dalla Costa and Rabbi Nathan Cassuto, carrying forged identity documents hidden in his bicycle frame to help Jews escape persecution; he also sheltered a Jewish family in a cellar. In 2013 Yad Vashem recognised him as Righteous Among the Nations. It is estimated his network helped save hundreds of people.
