Arch of Titus

The Arch of Titus (Italian: Arco di Tito; Latin: Arcus Titi) is a 1st-century AD honorific arch, located on the Via Sacra, Rome, just to the south-east of the Roman Forum.

Arch of Titus
LocationVia Sacra, Rome, Italy
Builtc. AD 81–82
Built by/forDomitian, honouring Titus
TypeTriumphal arch

History

It was constructed in c. AD 81–82 by Emperor Domitian shortly after the death of his older brother Titus to commemorate Titus's official deification and the victory of Titus together with their father, Vespasian, over the Jewish rebellion in Judaea.

Reliefs

The south panel depicts spoils from the Siege of Jerusalem (AD 70), including the Menorah and trumpets carried in triumph. The north panel shows Titus in a quadriga. These reliefs provided important evidence for the appearance of Second Temple implements.

Influence

The arch has provided the general model for many triumphal arches erected since the 16th century, including the Arc de Triomphe in Paris. Its inscription was the model for Latin capitals.
