Per Frans Wahlöö (5 August 1926 – 22 June 1975) was a Swedish author and journalist. With his partner Maj Sjöwall he co-wrote the ten-volume Martin Beck series of police procedurals (1965–1975), which reshaped Scandinavian crime fiction and strongly influenced the later Nordic noir genre.

Life and career

Wahlöö was born in Gothenburg and grew up in Lund. He worked as a crime reporter and journalist, and travelled widely; in 1956 he was expelled from Franco's Spain for his reporting. Politically a Marxist, he brought an explicit social-critical perspective to his fiction. Before meeting Sjöwall he had published several solo novels, including thrillers and the dystopian Murder on the Thirty-First Floor (1964).

The Martin Beck series

Beginning with Roseanna (1965) and ending with The Terrorists (1975), Sjöwall and Wahlöö planned and wrote a ten-novel sequence subtitled "The Story of a Crime", following Stockholm homicide detective Martin Beck and his colleagues. Written in a deliberately flat, documentary style, with chapters drafted alternately by the two authors, the books used the police procedural as a vehicle for a sustained critique of Swedish social democracy, bureaucracy and police institutions. The series won international acclaim; The Laughing Policeman (1968) received the Edgar Award for Best Novel.

Death and legacy

Wahlöö died of pancreatic cancer in Malmö in 1975, shortly after completing the final Beck novel. The Sjöwall–Wahlöö books remain in print worldwide and are credited with inventing the modern Scandinavian crime novel, influencing writers from Henning Mankell to Jo Nesbø.
