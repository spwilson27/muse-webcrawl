Hardanger fiddle

The Hardanger fiddle (Norwegian: hardingfele) is a traditional Norwegian stringed instrument similar to the violin, but distinguished by four or five additional understrings that resonate sympathetically, giving it a distinctive ringing, echoing sound. It is the national instrument of Norway and is used mainly for folk dance music.

Construction

Hardanger fiddles are typically more ornately decorated than ordinary violins, with mother-of-pearl inlay, carved lion's-head scrolls, and black penwork decorations known as rosing. The oldest surviving instrument is dated 1651 and was made by Ole Jonsen Jaastad of Hardanger, the region in western Norway from which the instrument takes its name.

Music and tradition

The instrument is closely associated with Norwegian folk dance traditions such as the springar, gangar and halling, and with legendary players such as Myllarguten (Targeir Augundsson). Composers including Edvard Grieg drew on Hardanger fiddle tunes in classical works.
