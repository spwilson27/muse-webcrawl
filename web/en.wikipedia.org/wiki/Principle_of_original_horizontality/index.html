Principle of original horizontality

Principle of original horizontality
Field: Stratigraphy, geology
Proposed by: Nicolaus Steno (1669)

The principle of original horizontality states that layers of sediment are originally deposited horizontally under the action of gravity. It is one of the fundamental principles of stratigraphy, first articulated by the Danish scientist Nicolaus Steno in 1669.

Application

Because sediments settle out of water or air onto a surface, they accumulate in essentially flat layers. Consequently, sedimentary rock strata that are now tilted, folded, or faulted must have been deformed after deposition — a key inference for reconstructing the geological history of an area.

Qualifications

Some deposits, such as cross-bedded sands and delta foresets, are laid down at slight initial angles, but these remain minor compared with later tectonic tilting. The principle works together with the principle of superposition and the principle of lateral continuity in relative dating of rock layers.
