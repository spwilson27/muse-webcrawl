The Newlander–Nirenberg theorem is a fundamental result in differential geometry. It states that an almost complex structure on a smooth manifold is integrable — that is, it arises from a genuine complex manifold structure — if and only if the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes identically.

Statement

An almost complex structure is an endomorphism J of the tangent bundle with J² = −I. The Nijenhuis tensor NJ(X,Y) = [X,Y] + J[JX,Y] + J[X,JY] − [JX,JY] measures the failure of J to come from holomorphic coordinates. The theorem, proved by August Newlander and Louis Nirenberg in 1957, says NJ = 0 exactly when every point has a neighbourhood with holomorphic coordinates for J.

Significance

The result is the complex analogue of the Frobenius theorem and is considerably harder, because its proof requires solving a system of partial differential equations rather than ordinary differential equations. It underlies the modern theory of complex manifolds.
