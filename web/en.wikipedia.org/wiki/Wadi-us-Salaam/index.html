Wadi-us-Salaam

LocationNajaf, Iraq
TypeIslamic cemetery
SizeApprox. 6 km² (1,485 acres)
GravesEstimated 5–6 million

Wadi-us-Salaam (Arabic: وادي السلام, "Valley of Peace") is an Islamic cemetery located in the holy city of Najaf, Iraq. It is the largest cemetery in the world, covering roughly 600 hectares. The cemetery is situated near the shrine of Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib, and millions of Shia Muslims aspire to be buried there, with bodies transported from across Iraq and abroad.

The vast necropolis stretches for kilometres around Najaf.

History

Burials at the site date back more than a thousand years; tradition associates the valley with the prophets Hud and Salih, whose graves are located within it. The cemetery expanded dramatically during the Iran–Iraq War, the 1991 uprisings, the sectarian violence after 2003 and the war against the Islamic State, when funeral processions for fallen fighters became a daily occurrence.

Burial practices

Burial follows Islamic ritual washing and shrouding, often performed in Najaf. Graves range from simple brick-lined plots to family vaults and multi-storey tombs; burial space has become increasingly expensive as the cemetery grows.
