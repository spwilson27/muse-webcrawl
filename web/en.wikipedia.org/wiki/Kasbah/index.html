Kasbah

A kasbah (Arabic: قصبة, qasba, 'citadel') is a fortified quarter, citadel or fortified residence in North African cities. In Morocco the term also denotes fortified villages of rammed earth in the south, while in Algeria it may refer to the old medina quarter, as in the Casbah of Algiers.

Kasbah
Type: Citadel / fortified quarter
Region: Maghreb
Materials: Stone, rammed earth

Function

Kasbahs housed the local ruler and garrison, served as refuge during attack, and overlooked the medina and its souk. They typically have high walls, corner towers and a single fortified gate.

Examples

The Kasbah of the Udayas in Rabat, Kasbah Tamnougalt, Aït Benhaddou (a ksar often called a kasbah), and the Casbah of Algiers — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — are notable. In Fez, kasbahs such as Kasbah an-Nouar guarded the medina.

Etymology in other uses

The word entered Spanish as alcazaba for Moorish fortresses in Iberia.
