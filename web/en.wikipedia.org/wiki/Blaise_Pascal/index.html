Blaise Pascal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Classical portrait painting of Blaise Pascal in 17th-century dress]

Blaise Pascal (19 June 1623 – 19 August 1662) was a French mathematician, physicist, inventor and philosopher. A child prodigy educated by his father, he made important contributions to projective geometry, probability theory, fluid mechanics and the study of atmospheric pressure, and invented one of the first mechanical calculators, the Pascaline.

Science and mathematics

Pascal's work on fluids established principles still taught today, including Pascal's law of pressure in enclosed fluids; the SI unit of pressure, the pascal, is named for him. His correspondence with Fermat in 1654 laid foundations of probability theory. In mathematics he is also associated with Pascal's triangle, a convenient arrangement of binomial coefficients known in earlier cultures.

Religion and philosophy

After a religious experience in 1654 — the so-called Night of Fire — Pascal turned increasingly to philosophy and theology. His unfinished defence of Christianity was published posthumously as the Pensées, and it contains the famous argument known as Pascal's wager.
