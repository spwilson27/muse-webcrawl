Italian design

[Image: Italian design icons: the Vespa scooter and Olivetti Valentine typewriter]
Post-war Italian design icons.

Italian design refers to the tradition of industrial design, furniture, fashion, automotive and interior design developed in Italy, which became internationally renowned in the twentieth century for combining craftsmanship, technical innovation and expressive, sculptural form. Milan, with its Salone del Mobile fair (founded 1961) and Triennale exhibitions, is its principal centre.

Post-war golden age

After the Second World War, Italian manufacturers such as Piaggio (the Vespa scooter, 1946, designed by Corradino D'Ascanio), Fiat (the 500, 1957) and Olivetti — whose Lettera 22 and Valentine typewriters, designed by Ettore Sottsass, won international awards — embodied a joyful, affordable modernity. Furniture makers like Cassina, Artemide and Kartell collaborated with architect-designers such as Gio Ponti, Achille Castiglioni, Vico Magistretti and Joe Colombo, exploiting new plastics and production methods.

Radical design and Memphis

In the late 1960s and 1970s the "radical design" movement (Archizoom, Superstudio) challenged functionalism; its legacy flowered in Sottsass's Memphis Group (1981), whose playful postmodern furniture and objects became emblematic of 1980s design.

Automotive design

Italian coachbuilders and studios — Pininfarina, Bertone, ItalDesign (Giorgetto Giugiaro) — shaped the look of post-war sports cars for Ferrari, Lamborghini, Alfa Romeo and Maserati, and exported their services worldwide.
