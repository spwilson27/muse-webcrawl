The Oslo Opera House (Norwegian: Operahuset i Oslo) is the home of the Norwegian National Opera and Ballet, and the national opera theatre in Norway. The building is situated in the Bjørvika neighbourhood of central Oslo, at the head of the Oslofjord. It is the largest cultural building constructed in Norway since Nidaros Cathedral was completed around 1300.

History

The decision to build a new opera house formed part of the redevelopment of Oslo's Bjørvika waterfront. An architectural competition held in 2000 attracted more than 200 entries and was won by the Norwegian firm Snøhetta. Construction began in 2003, with Statsbygg as the developer for the Ministry of Culture. The building was completed ahead of schedule and opened officially on 12 April 2008, in a gala attended by King Harald V.

Architecture

The design is dominated by angular planes of white Carrara marble and granite that rise directly out of the harbour water, an effect the architects compared to an iceberg. The sloping roof plane is paved and fully accessible: visitors can walk from the water's edge to the top of the building, making the roof a public plaza and viewpoint over the fjord. The glazed foyer, framed by oak, offers views across the harbour, while the main auditorium is lined in warm oak in deliberate contrast to the cool exterior.

The main auditorium seats 1,364 people in a classical horseshoe form; two smaller stages, the Second Stage and the rehearsal stage, seat roughly 440 and 200. The building contains more than 1,100 rooms across some 49,000 square metres, including workshops for scenery, costumes, and wigs.

Reception and awards

The opera house won the culture award at the World Architecture Festival in Barcelona in 2008 and the European Union Prize for Contemporary Architecture, the Mies van der Rohe Award, in 2009. It quickly became one of Oslo's most visited landmarks and a symbol of the Fjord City waterfront regeneration.
