Ambient music

Ambient music
Stylistic origins: Minimalism, electronic, musique concrète, furniture music, Krautrock, dub
Cultural origins: 1960s–1970s, United Kingdom and Germany
Key figure: Brian Eno
Subgenres: Dark ambient, ambient dub, chill-out, drone, lowercase

Ambient music is a genre of music that emphasises tone and atmosphere over traditional musical structure or rhythm. It may have a "visual" or unobtrusive quality. According to Brian Eno, who popularised the term with his 1978 album Ambient 1: Music for Airports, ambient music "must be as ignorable as it is interesting."

Origins

Precursors include Erik Satie's "furniture music" (musique d'ameublement), the drones of La Monte Young and American minimalism, musique concrète, Miles Davis's atmospheric recordings, German kosmische music (Tangerine Dream, Klaus Schulze, Popol Vuh) and Jamaican dub. Eno developed his approach while recovering from an accident, listening to harp music at low volume merging with room sounds; he also explored tape-loop systems on Discreet Music (1975) and with Robert Fripp.

Development

In the late 1980s and 1990s ambient merged with house and techno in "chill-out" rooms at raves; artists such as The Orb, Aphex Twin (Selected Ambient Works), Boards of Canada and Biosphere brought it to electronic audiences. Subgenres include dark ambient, ambient dub, drone music and lowercase, and the genre has flourished in streaming-era functional listening, film scores and video-game soundtracks.

Characteristics

Typical features are slow or absent beats, sustained pads and drones, field recordings, repetition with gradual change, and extensive reverb and delay, designed to induce calm and a sense of space.
