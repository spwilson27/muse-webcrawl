Prime Minister of Pakistan

Office: Head of government of Pakistan
Residence: Prime Minister's House, Islamabad
Appointer: President of Pakistan, on election by the National Assembly
First holder: Liaquat Ali Khan (1947)

The Prime Minister of Pakistan (Urdu: وزیر اعظم پاکستان) is the head of government of Pakistan and chief executive of the federal government. The prime minister leads the executive branch, oversees the cabinet, and is responsible for the administration of the country, while the President of Pakistan serves as head of state.

History

The office was created when Pakistan gained independence in 1947, with Liaquat Ali Khan as its first holder. Pakistan's political history has alternated between parliamentary and presidential systems; the 1973 Constitution firmly established a parliamentary system in which the prime minister holds most executive power. Periods of martial law repeatedly interrupted civilian rule, and no elected prime minister completed a full five-year term until the 2010s-era governments of the democratic transition period.

Powers and duties

The prime minister is elected by the National Assembly, advises the president on appointments including judges, service chiefs and governors, chairs the cabinet, and represents Pakistan internationally. The prime minister can be removed through a vote of no confidence, as happened in April 2022.

Notable holders

Prominent prime ministers include Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, Benazir Bhutto — the first woman to head the government of a Muslim-majority country — Nawaz Sharif, who served three non-consecutive terms, and Imran Khan.
