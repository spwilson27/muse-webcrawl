Nathaniel Kleitman
Born: April 26, 1895, Chișinău, Bessarabia, Russian Empire
Died: August 13, 1999 (aged 104), Los Angeles, California
Fields: Physiology, sleep research
Institutions: University of Chicago
Known for: Discovery of REM sleep; founding modern sleep research

Nathaniel Kleitman (April 26, 1895 – August 13, 1999) was an American physiologist at the University of Chicago who is widely regarded as the father of modern sleep research. He founded the world's first sleep laboratory and, with his student Eugene Aserinsky, discovered rapid eye movement (REM) sleep.

Career

Born in Chișinău in the Russian Empire (now Moldova), Kleitman emigrated to the United States in 1915. He joined the University of Chicago faculty in 1925 and established a dedicated sleep laboratory, the first of its kind. His landmark textbook Sleep and Wakefulness (1939) summarized the field. In 1938, Kleitman and Bruce Richardson spent 32 days isolated in Mammoth Cave, Kentucky, attempting to adapt to an artificial 28-hour day—an experiment that drew wide public attention and provided early evidence on circadian rhythms.

Discovery of REM sleep

In the early 1950s Kleitman and graduate student Eugene Aserinsky observed periods of rapid, jerky eye movements during sleep in infants and adults, which they reported in 1953. With another student, William Dement, Kleitman showed that these REM periods were associated with dreaming and occurred in a regular cycle of roughly 90 minutes alternating with non-REM sleep, transforming scientific understanding of sleep architecture. He also proposed the concept of the basic rest–activity cycle (BRAC).

Later life

Kleitman retired from Chicago in 1960 and moved to California. He lived to the age of 104, long enough to see sleep medicine become an established discipline with thousands of accredited sleep centers. Dement, his student, founded the first sleep disorders clinic at Stanford and popularized Kleitman's legacy.
