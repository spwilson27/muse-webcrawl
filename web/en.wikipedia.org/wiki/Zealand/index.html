Zealand

Zealand (Danish: Sjælland)
Country: Denmark
Area: 7,031 km²
Population: about 2.3 million
Largest city: Copenhagen

Zealand (Danish: Sjælland) is the largest and most populous island of Denmark, situated in the Baltic Sea between the Jutland peninsula and the Swedish province of Scania. The Danish capital, Copenhagen, lies partly on Zealand's eastern coast and partly on the neighbouring island of Amager.

Geography

Zealand covers about 7,031 square kilometres. Its landscape is low and gently rolling, shaped by Ice Age glaciation, with fjord-like inlets such as Roskilde Fjord and Isefjord in the north. The island is connected to Funen by the Great Belt Fixed Link, to Sweden by the Øresund Bridge from Copenhagen, and by bridges to the southern islands of Falster and Møn.

History and sights

The island was the core of the medieval Danish kingdom; Roskilde, its former capital, hosts the cathedral where Danish monarchs are buried and a museum of Viking ships. Other major towns include Helsingør, home to Kronborg Castle — the Elsinore of Shakespeare's Hamlet — and the university city of Roskilde. The chalk cliffs of Stevns Klint, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, mark the island's southeast.

Administration

Most of Zealand forms the Region Zealand (Region Sjælland), while the northeastern part, including Copenhagen, belongs to the Capital Region of Denmark.
