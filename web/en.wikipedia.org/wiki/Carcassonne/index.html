Carcassonne

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Carcassonne is a French fortified city in the department of Aude, in the region of Occitanie. It is the prefecture of the department and had a population of roughly 46,000. The city is famous for the Cité de Carcassonne, a medieval fortress restored by the theorist and architect Eugène Viollet-le-Duc in 1853 and added to the UNESCO list of World Heritage Sites in 1997, as was the Canal du Midi, which passes through the lower town.

History

The hill of Carcassonne was occupied from the Neolithic period and became a Roman town, Carcaso, on the route between the Mediterranean and the Atlantic. Visigoths, then Saracens, then Franks held it in turn. In the medieval period it was the seat of the Trencavel viscounts and a stronghold of Cathar sympathies; it surrendered to Simon de Montfort's crusaders in 1209 during the Albigensian Crusade and was annexed to the French crown in 1247. The double ring of walls — with 53 towers and barbicans — was built up under Philip III and Philip IV.

Made obsolete as a frontier fortress after the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659) shifted the border with Spain, the Cité decayed until nineteenth-century antiquarian pressure led to its great restoration under Viollet-le-Duc, whose work — including the pointed slate roofs of the towers — remains debated but saved the site. Across the Aude lies the lower town, the Bastide Saint-Louis, founded in 1247.

Economy and culture

Tourism dominates the modern economy: the Cité receives several million visitors a year. The city gives its name to the tile-laying board game Carcassonne (2000) by Klaus-Jürgen Wrede, which won the Spiel des Jahres and spread the city's image worldwide. Carcassonne is also on the route of the Canal du Midi and serves as a gateway to the Cathar castles of the Corbières.
