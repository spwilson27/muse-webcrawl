Binationalism

Binationalism is a political concept concerning the governance of a territory shared by two national, ethnic or cultural groups under a single state. It is most often discussed in relation to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict as a form of one-state solution, but also in contexts such as Belgium, Canada, Cyprus and Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Concept

Binational arrangements may take the form of federation, confederation, consociational power-sharing, or a unitary state with collective rights for both nations. Proponents argue it can recognise two peoples' national rights without partition; critics argue it can entrench deadlock or fail to satisfy self-determination.

Israel and Palestine

In Mandatory Palestine, groups such as Brit Shalom in the 1920s–30s advocated a binational state for Jews and Arabs. The idea has been revived periodically by intellectuals and activists as an alternative to the two-state solution, with debate over democracy, demography and national identity.

Other examples

Consociational and federal systems in Belgium, Canada's bilingual federalism, and post-war Bosnia are sometimes described as binational or multinational.
