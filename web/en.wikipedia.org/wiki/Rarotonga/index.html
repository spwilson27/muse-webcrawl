Rarotonga

Rarotonga
Country: Cook Islands
Area: 67.4 km²
Highest point: Te Manga, 658 m
Population: approx. 10,000–13,000
Capital: Avarua (on the island)

Rarotonga is the largest and most populous of the Cook Islands, a volcanic island in the South Pacific Ocean. The capital of the Cook Islands, Avarua, is on its north coast. The island is surrounded by a coral reef and lagoon, and its interior is mountainous and covered in tropical forest.

Geography

Rarotonga is roughly circular, about 11 km across, with a 32 km coastal road encircling it. The highest peak, Te Manga, rises to 658 m. A cross-island track crosses the interior via Te Rua Manga ("the Needle"), a prominent rock pinnacle.

History

The island was settled by Polynesian voyagers, and Māori tradition holds that canoes departed from Rarotonga for New Zealand. European contact began in the late 18th century, and London Missionary Society missionaries such as John Williams arrived in the 1820s. Rarotonga became a British protectorate in 1888 and was annexed to New Zealand in 1901; the Cook Islands became self-governing in free association with New Zealand in 1965.

Tourism

Tourism is the island's main industry, centred on resorts around Muri Beach and the lagoon. Rarotonga International Airport links the island to New Zealand and other Pacific destinations.
