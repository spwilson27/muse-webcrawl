Hovhannes Vorotnetsi

Hovhannes Vorotnetsi (Armenian: Հովհաննես Որոտնեցի; 1315–1386) was an Armenian philosopher, theologian, and educator, regarded as one of the most important figures of the medieval Armenian intellectual revival. He founded the school of Tatev Monastery in Syunik.

Life

Born in the village of Vorot in the province of Syunik, Hovhannes studied at the Gladzor University under Esayi Nchetsi. After years of study and teaching, he established a school at Tatev Monastery around 1340, which became the leading centre of Armenian learning in the late 14th century.

Works and thought

Vorotnetsi wrote commentaries on Aristotle's Categories and On Interpretation, on Porphyry's Isagoge, and works defending Armenian Christian doctrine against Latin (Catholic) theological influence, including his treatise Against the Latins and the Book of Questions tradition he helped shape. He introduced elements of Latin scholastic method into Armenian philosophy while defending the doctrinal positions of the Armenian Church.

Legacy

His most famous pupil, Grigor Tatevatsi, succeeded him at Tatev and systematised his teaching. Vorotnetsi's school preserved Armenian manuscript culture during a period of political turmoil and foreign invasion.
