Rickenbacker International Corporation is a string instrument manufacturer based in Santa Ana, California. The company is credited with producing the world's first commercially successful electric guitar: the lap steel nicknamed the "Frying Pan", developed in 1931 by Adolph Rickenbacker and George Beauchamp. Rickenbacker guitars and basses — prized for their bright, chiming "jangle" — are closely associated with musicians including John Lennon, George Harrison, Pete Townshend, Chris Squire, Lemmy Kilmister and R.E.M.'s Peter Buck.

History

Swiss-born toolmaker Adolph Rickenbacker (born Rickenbacher) supplied metal bodies for National resonator guitars; with musician-inventor George Beauchamp and Paul Barth he developed the horseshoe-magnet electric pickup. Their Ro-Pat-In Corporation — renamed Electro String — began selling the aluminium A-22 lap steel, the "Frying Pan", in 1932, followed by bakelite and wooden electric Spanish guitars. After the Second World War, F. C. Hall bought the company (1953) and modernised the line: designer Roger Rossmeisl created the Combo series, the 300 series with its distinctive crescent soundhole, and the solid-body designs.

The British Invasion and after

Rickenbacker's fortunes were transformed in 1964 when the Beatles appeared in America: John Lennon played a 325 Capri, and George Harrison received one of the first 360/12 twelve-string models, whose ringing sound became a signature of the era and later of the Byrds' Roger McGuinn and Tom Petty. The 4000-series basses, especially the neck-through 4001 and 4003, became icons in the hands of Paul McCartney, Chris Squire of Yes, and Motörhead's Lemmy. Unlike most rivals, Rickenbacker has remained a family-owned, US-made manufacturer, producing instruments in relatively small numbers at Santa Ana and fiercely protecting its designs and trademarks.

Design characteristics

Rickenbackers are noted for single-coil "toaster" and high-gain pickups, stereo "Rick-O-Sound" output on deluxe models, the slanted frets of some early models, triangular crushed-pearl inlays, and finishes such as Fireglo (sunburst red) and Jetglo (black). The twelve-string models place the octave strings after the main strings — the reverse of most makers — a key part of their distinctive tone.
