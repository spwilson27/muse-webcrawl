Max Horkheimer (14 February 1895 – 7 July 1973) was a German philosopher and sociologist, a leading figure of the Frankfurt School of critical theory. As director of the Institute for Social Research in Frankfurt from 1930, he reshaped it into a centre for interdisciplinary Marxist social theory.

Life

Horkheimer was born in Stuttgart to a wealthy Jewish textile manufacturer. After leaving school to work in the family firm, he studied philosophy and psychology at Munich, Freiburg and Frankfurt, completing his doctorate in 1922. He became director of the Institute for Social Research in 1930 and founded its journal, the Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung. Fleeing the Nazis in 1933, he moved the Institute first to Geneva and then to Columbia University in New York. He returned to Frankfurt after the war, re-founding the Institute in 1951 and serving as rector of Goethe University Frankfurt.

Thought

In essays such as "Traditional and Critical Theory" (1937), Horkheimer defined critical theory against positivist social science, arguing that theory should be self-reflective, historically situated, and aimed at emancipation from domination. With Theodor W. Adorno he wrote Dialectic of Enlightenment (1944/1947), arguing that Enlightenment rationality had collapsed into instrumental reason and the administered world, producing the "culture industry" and enabling fascism and mass deception. Other works include Eclipse of Reason (1947) and Critique of Instrumental Reason.
