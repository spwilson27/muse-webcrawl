The Royal Marechaussee (Dutch: Koninklijke Marechaussee, KMar) is the national gendarmerie force of the Netherlands and one of the four armed services of the Dutch military. Founded by King William I on 26 October 1814 on the model of the French gendarmerie, it combines military status with police duties.

Tasks

The Marechaussee guards the Dutch borders and performs border policing at airports (notably Amsterdam Airport Schiphol) and seaports, including immigration checks and combating human smuggling. It protects the royal household and provides security for the central bank's money transports, acts as military police for the Netherlands Armed Forces, supplies close-protection and special security units (BSB), and supports the civilian police in public order and counter-terrorism roles. Since the reorganisation of the Dutch police, the Marechaussee has taken on an expanded role in guarding objects and persons of national importance.

Organisation

Personnel hold military rank and wear a dark-blue uniform; districts are organised into brigades across the country, with the national headquarters (LTC) in The Hague. The Marechaussee museum is in Buren, in the former Royal Orphanage.
