Transgender rights movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The transgender rights movement is a movement to promote the legal status of transgender people and to eliminate discrimination and violence against transgender people regarding housing, employment, public accommodations, education, and health care. A major goal of transgender activism is to allow changes to identification documents to conform with a person's current gender identity without the need for gender-affirming surgery or any medical requirements, which is known as gender self-identification.

History

Early organizing included Magnus Hirschfeld's Institut für Sexualwissenschaft in Berlin, founded in 1919, which advocated for people today described as transgender before it was destroyed by the Nazis in 1933. In the United States, transgender people participated in the Compton's Cafeteria riot of 1966 in San Francisco and the Stonewall uprising of 1969 in New York City, events often regarded as milestones in LGBT activism. Organizations such as the Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries (STAR), founded by Sylvia Rivera and Marsha P. Johnson in 1970, provided housing and support to homeless transgender youth.

Issues

Legal recognition

Activists campaign for legal gender recognition procedures, ranging from requirements of medical intervention to self-determination models adopted by countries such as Argentina (2012), Ireland (2015) and Spain (2023). Recognition on identity documents affects access to employment, travel, marriage and services.

Healthcare

Access to gender-affirming healthcare, including hormone therapy and surgeries, and its coverage by insurers and public health systems, is a central issue. Activists also advocate depathologization; the World Health Organization's ICD-11, adopted in 2019, moved "gender incongruence" out of the chapter on mental disorders.

Discrimination and violence

The movement campaigns for anti-discrimination protections and against violence targeting transgender people, commemorated annually on the Transgender Day of Remembrance (November 20). It also addresses disputes over participation in sports, access to sex-segregated facilities, military service, and the rights of transgender prisoners and minors.

Opposition and backlash

The movement faces organized opposition, including gender-critical feminists and conservative political movements, which dispute aspects of gender identity law, youth medical care and sports eligibility. These disagreements have driven legislative battles in many countries during the 2010s and 2020s.
