The royal coat of arms of Scotland is the official coat of arms borne by the King of Scots from the twelfth century until the Union of the Crowns in 1603 and the political union with England in 1707. Its central element — a red lion rampant on a gold field within a double royal tressure — remains one of the most recognisable symbols of Scotland, appearing on the Royal Banner of Scotland (the "Lion Rampant") and, in differenced form, in the Scottish version of the royal arms of the United Kingdom.

Blazon and design

The shield is blazoned: Or, a lion rampant Gules armed and langued Azure within a double tressure flory-counter-flory of the second. The double tressure, decorated with fleurs-de-lis, is traditionally said to commemorate the Auld Alliance with France, though its origins likely lie in a simpler border added during the reign of Alexander III. The achievement includes the crown of Scotland, a crest of a crowned red lion seated facing forward holding the sword and sceptre of state, and two unicorn supporters — the unicorn being Scotland's national animal — crowned and chained, the dexter supporter bearing the banner of St Andrew and the sinister the royal banner.

Mottoes

Above the crest appears the motto In defens, a contraction of the Scots "In my defens God me defend". Below the shield is the motto of the Order of the Thistle, Nemo me impune lacessit ("No one provokes me with impunity"), on a compartment strewn with thistles.

History

The lion rampant is associated with William I of Scotland ("William the Lion") in the late twelfth century and was used by his successors, becoming hereditary under Alexander II and Alexander III. After 1603, James VI and I quartered the Scottish arms with those of England and Ireland; in the Scottish version the Scottish arms took precedence in the first and fourth quarters. Following the Acts of Union 1707, the combined arms were used throughout Great Britain, but the Scottish quartering and unicorn supporters continue to be used within Scotland today, including by the monarch, the Scottish Government's predecessors, courts, and on Scottish variants of official emblems.
