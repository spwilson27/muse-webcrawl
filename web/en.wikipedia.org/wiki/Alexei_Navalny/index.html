Alexei Anatolyevich Navalny (Russian: Алексей Анатольевич Навальный; 4 June 1976 – 16 February 2024) was a Russian opposition leader, lawyer, and anti-corruption activist. He gained international recognition through his investigations into corruption in Russia, which he published on his blog and in documentary films, and he organised large-scale anti-government demonstrations. He founded the Anti-Corruption Foundation (FBK) in 2011.

Career

Navalny trained as a lawyer and began his political activity in the liberal party Yabloko before being expelled in 2007. Through his blog he campaigned against state-owned companies' abuses and popularised the description of the ruling United Russia party as the "party of crooks and thieves", a phrase that entered common usage. After the disputed 2011 parliamentary elections he became a leading figure in the ensuing protest movement. In 2013 he ran for mayor of Moscow, winning 27% of the vote, and he later built a national network of campaign offices while attempting to run for president in 2018, from which he was barred.

Poisoning and imprisonment

In August 2020 Navalny was poisoned with a Novichok nerve agent while on a flight in Siberia; he was evacuated to Berlin, where he recovered, and investigations by Bellingcat and media partners implicated Russia's Federal Security Service. He returned to Moscow in January 2021 and was immediately arrested. His suspended sentence was converted into imprisonment, and further trials added long sentences on fraud and "extremism" charges that he and international observers denounced as politically motivated. Amnesty International recognised him as a prisoner of conscience.

Death

Navalny died on 16 February 2024 at the Polar Wolf penal colony in Kharp, in the Russian Arctic, where he was serving a 19-year sentence. Russian authorities said he collapsed after a walk. His death prompted widespread international condemnation and tributes; he was buried in Moscow on 1 March 2024, with large crowds attending despite official restrictions. He received the Sakharov Prize of the European Parliament in 2021.
