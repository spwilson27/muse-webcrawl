Hiʻiaka

Hiʻiaka (full name Hiʻiaka-i-ka-poli-o-Pele, "Hiʻiaka held in the bosom of Pele") is a Hawaiian goddess, the youngest and favourite sister of the volcano goddess Pele, and the patroness of the hula. In Hawaiian mythology she is born from an egg that Pele carries under her arm and nurtures, and she grows into a powerful goddess of the forest, healing and dance.

The journey for Lohiʻau

The epic of Hiʻiaka (Ka Moʻolelo o Hiʻiaka), one of the longest works of Hawaiian literature, tells how Pele sent Hiʻiaka from Kīlauea to Kauaʻi to fetch Pele's lover, the chief Lohiʻau. Travelling with her companions (including the fern goddess Pāʻū-o-Palaʻe and the attendant Wahineʻōmaʻo), Hiʻiaka fought moʻo (lizard spirits), restored the dead to life, and overcame many obstacles. When her journey took too long, the jealous Pele destroyed Hiʻiaka's beloved lehua forest and her friend Hopoe; the sisters were eventually reconciled. The saga, preserved in Hawaiian-language newspapers in the early 20th century, runs to more than a thousand pages in translation.

Hula and worship

Hiʻiaka is revered as the originator of the hula, which she is said to have first performed for Pele. Hula hālau traditionally maintain altars to her and to the goddess Laka, and her emblematic plants include the native forests and ferns of Puna, Hawaiʻi.
