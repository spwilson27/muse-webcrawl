Bridge (dentistry)

A bridge is a fixed dental restoration used to replace one or more missing teeth by joining an artificial tooth (a pontic) definitively to adjacent teeth or dental implants. The supporting teeth, called abutments, are typically fitted with crowns that are joined to the pontic, so the prosthesis "bridges" the gap left by the missing tooth.

Dental bridge
TypeFixed prosthodontic restoration
PurposeReplace missing teeth
ComponentsPontic(s), retainers, abutment teeth or implants
MaterialsPorcelain-fused-to-metal, zirconia, all-ceramic, gold alloys

Types

Conventional bridges use full crowns on abutment teeth on both sides of the gap. Cantilever bridges are supported on one side only. Resin-bonded (Maryland) bridges use metal or ceramic wings bonded to the backs of adjacent teeth with minimal preparation, and are common for single front teeth. Implant-supported bridges anchor to implants rather than natural teeth.

Procedure and care

Treatment usually requires reshaping the abutment teeth, taking impressions or digital scans, fitting a temporary bridge, and cementing the definitive restoration fabricated in a dental laboratory. Bridges typically last ten to fifteen years or more with good oral hygiene; cleaning beneath the pontic with floss threaders or interdental brushes is essential, since the abutment teeth remain susceptible to decay and periodontal disease, the most common causes of bridge failure.
