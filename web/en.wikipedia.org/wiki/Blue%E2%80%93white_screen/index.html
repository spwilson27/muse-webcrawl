Blue–white screen

The blue–white screen is a screening technique in molecular biology that allows rapid detection of recombinant bacteria in cloning experiments. It is based on the α-complementation of the enzyme β-galactosidase encoded by the lacZ gene.

Principle

Bacteria are grown on agar containing the chromogenic substrate X-gal and the inducer IPTG. If the plasmid's cloning site is uninterrupted, functional β-galactosidase cleaves X-gal to produce blue colonies. Insertion of foreign DNA into the cloning site disrupts the gene, so colonies carrying recombinant plasmids remain white.

Use

The method lets researchers pick likely recombinants at a glance before confirming them by sequencing or restriction analysis. It is a standard feature of common cloning vectors such as the pUC series.
