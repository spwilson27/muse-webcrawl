Solar furnace


Type: Concentrated solar facility
Example: Odeillo solar furnace, France
Power: 1 MW (Odeillo)
Max temperature: Over 3,500 °C

A solar furnace is a structure that uses concentrated solar power to produce high temperatures, usually for industry or research. It works by focusing sunlight from a large array of mirrors (heliostats) onto a small focal area, achieving temperatures of thousands of degrees Celsius without fuel or contamination.

Notable furnaces

The largest solar furnace is at Odeillo in the French Pyrenees, opened in 1968, with a parabolic concentrator nearly 40 metres high fed by 63 heliostats; its thermal power is about one megawatt and focal temperatures exceed 3,500 °C. Smaller research furnaces operate in Germany (DLR, Jülich/Cologne), Switzerland (Paul Scherrer Institute), Uzbekistan (Parkent), and the United States.

Applications

Solar furnaces are used for materials testing under extreme heat (such as spacecraft thermal protection), high-temperature chemistry, production of solar fuels by thermochemical splitting of water or CO2, purification of materials, and calibration of instruments. Because the heat is clean and precisely controllable, they are valuable research tools.
