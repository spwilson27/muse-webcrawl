Józef Haller von Hallenburg (13 August 1873 – 4 June 1960) was a Polish lieutenant general and legionary in the Polish Legions during the First World War. He was a harcmistrz (scoutmaster), the president of the Polish Scouting and Guiding Association, and a political and social activist who commanded the Polish Army in France — the "Blue Army" — and led Polish forces in the taking of Pomerelia and the symbolic "Poland's Wedding to the Sea" in 1920.

Early career

Haller was born in Jurczyce near Kraków, in Austrian Galicia, into a landowning family. He studied at the military academy in Vienna and served as an artillery officer in the Austro-Hungarian Army, retiring in 1910 to devote himself to the cooperative and scouting movements in Galicia. He helped organise Polish rifle associations and scouting before 1914.

First World War

During the First World War Haller commanded the 2nd Brigade of the Polish Legions fighting alongside Austria-Hungary against Russia; in February 1918, protesting the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, which ceded Chełm Land to Ukraine, he broke through the front at Rarańcza with his troops — the only Polish formation to do so. After internment and escape, he travelled to France, where in 1918 he took command of the Polish Army being formed under the Polish National Committee. His troops, named the Blue Army after their French horizon-blue uniforms, numbered some 70,000 men, including many Polish volunteers from the United States, and fought on the Western Front in the war's final months.

Independent Poland and later life

In 1919 the Blue Army was transferred by train to Poland, where it fought in the Polish–Ukrainian and Polish–Soviet wars. In February 1920 Haller commanded the Polish takeover of Pomerelia and performed the ceremony of "Poland's Wedding to the Sea" at Puck, throwing a ring into the Baltic. He served as an army inspector and briefly as a deputy, clashing with Józef Piłsudski; after the May Coup of 1926 he retired and became a leading figure of the opposition Front Morges. During the Second World War he was Minister of Education in the Polish government-in-exile in London, where he died in 1960. His ashes were returned to Poland in 1993.
