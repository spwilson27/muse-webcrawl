FEBO

FEBO is a Dutch chain of fast-food restaurants and snackbars, famous for its automatiek — walls of coin- or card-operated heated vending compartments from which customers serve themselves hot snacks. The chain was founded in 1941 by baker Johan I. de Borst, whose bakery "Maison Febo" stood on the Ferdinand Bolstraat in Amsterdam; the name FEBO derives from the street's initials.

Concept

The classic FEBO offering consists of Dutch snack staples such as the kroket (croquette), frikandel, kaassoufflé, bami and nasi discs, and kipcorn, kept warm behind small glass doors. Customers pay at the compartment or at the counter, where fresh fries and drinks are also served. Most outlets prepare snacks on site, continuing the founder's emphasis on freshly made croquettes rather than factory products.

Present day

FEBO remains a family business with several dozen outlets, concentrated in Amsterdam and the wider Netherlands; an outlet at Amsterdam Airport Schiphol introduces many visitors to the automat format, a Dutch institution that FEBO helped to popularize.
