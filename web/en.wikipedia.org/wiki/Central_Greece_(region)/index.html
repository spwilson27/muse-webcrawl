Central Greece (Greek: Στερεά Ελλάδα, Stereá Elláda, "Continental Greece") is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece. It occupies the eastern part of the traditional region of Central Greece north of Attica, comprising the regional units of Boeotia, Euboea (including the large island of Euboea and Skyros), Evrytania, Phocis, and Phthiotis. Its capital is Lamia, and its largest city is Chalcis on Euboea.

Geography

Central Greece is the second-largest Greek region by area and one of the most mountainous: the Pindus range continues into Evrytania, and Mount Parnassus (2,457 m) rises in Phocis, home to the ancient sanctuary of Delphi and a major ski centre. The Boeotian plain around Thebes and Livadeia is an important agricultural area, drained by Lake Copais (drained in the nineteenth century). The region borders Thessaly to the north, West Greece to the west, and Attica to the south, and is connected to the Peloponnese and Attica by the Rio–Antirrio and Chalkida bridges and the national motorway network.

History

The region's ancient territories included Boeotia, Phocis, and Locris, and it contains sites of panhellenic importance: Delphi, Thermopylae, Plataea, Chaeronea, and Orchomenus. In the Greek War of Independence it saw major battles, including Alamana and Gravia (1821). The name Sterea Ellada historically denoted all of mainland Greece as opposed to the Peloponnese and the islands.

Economy

Agriculture (cotton, olives, cereals, livestock), aluminium production at Aspra Spitia (Aluminium of Greece), tourism at Delphi and the Parnassus resorts, and industry around Chalcis and Thebes anchor the regional economy. Ferry links from Rafina and Agia Marina serve Euboea alongside the bridge at Chalcis.
