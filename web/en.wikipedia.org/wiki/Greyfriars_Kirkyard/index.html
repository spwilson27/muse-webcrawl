Greyfriars Kirkyard

[Image: Greyfriars Kirkyard]

Greyfriars Kirkyard is the graveyard surrounding Greyfriars Kirk in Edinburgh, Scotland. It is located at the southern edge of the Old Town, adjacent to George Heriot's School and the Flodden Wall.

History

The kirkyard was established in 1561–62 on land granted by Mary, Queen of Scots, taking its name from the Franciscan (Greyfriars) friary formerly on the site. In 1638 the National Covenant was signed in the kirk. After the Battle of Bothwell Bridge in 1679, some 1,200 Covenanters were imprisoned in a field within the kirkyard — the Covenanters' Prison — and many died there. "Martyr's Monument" commemorates executed Covenanters.

Famous burials and legends

The kirkyard is famous for Greyfriars Bobby, the Skye Terrier said to have guarded his master's grave for 14 years, and for the Mackenzie Poltergeist associated with the tomb of Sir George Mackenzie. Elaborate mortsafes and iron grilles testify to the era of grave-robbing. The graveyard is also popularly linked to names used in the Harry Potter novels.
