Howard Rheingold

Howard Rheingold (born July 7, 1947) is an American writer, critic and teacher known for his books on the cultural, social and political implications of modern communication media and virtual communities. His best-known works include The Virtual Community (1993) and Smart Mobs (2002).

Biography

Rheingold was born in Phoenix, Arizona, and graduated from Reed College in 1968. His interest in mind-amplifying technology grew out of his research into the history of computing, which led to the book Tools for Thought: The History and Future of Mind-Expanding Technology (1985), profiling figures such as Douglas Engelbart, Alan Kay and the researchers at Xerox PARC.

In 1985 Rheingold joined the WELL (Whole Earth 'Lectronic Link), one of the earliest online communities, and became one of its most prominent participants. His account of life in that community, The Virtual Community: Homesteading on the Electronic Frontier (1993), is regarded as a foundational text in the study of online social life.

Smart mobs and later work

In Smart Mobs: The Next Social Revolution (2002), Rheingold coined the term "smart mob" for groups that use mobile communication and computing devices to organise collective action, from text-messaging protests in the Philippines to flash mobs. The book extended his earlier themes into the era of ubiquitous mobile connectivity. In Net Smart: How to Thrive Online (2012) he turned to the practical literacies — attention, participation, collaboration, critical consumption of information and network awareness — needed to use digital media well.

Rheingold has taught at the University of California, Berkeley's School of Information and at Stanford University, where his courses have covered virtual communities, social media and digital journalism. He was the founding executive editor of HotWired, one of the first commercial web publications, and has written extensively on the "infotention" skills required by networked publics.
