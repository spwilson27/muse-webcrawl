Project ARTICHOKE

Project ARTICHOKE was a Central Intelligence Agency project, approved in 1951, that researched methods of interrogation and behavior modification, including the use of drugs such as LSD, hypnosis, sensory deprivation, and other techniques. It grew out of the earlier Project BLUEBIRD and was a predecessor to the better-known Project MKULTRA.

Scope and aims

ARTICHOKE teams investigated whether individuals could be made to perform acts, including attempted assassinations, against their will, and studied amnesia-inducing techniques for use on agents and defectors. The project established so-called security teams that used interrogation methods abroad. A 1952 memorandum famously asked, "Can we get control of an individual to the point where he will do our bidding against his will and even against fundamental laws of nature, such as self-preservation?"

Legacy

Project ARTICHOKE's functions were absorbed into MKULTRA, authorized in 1953. Details became public through declassified documents, Freedom of Information Act releases, and congressional investigations in the 1970s, contributing to reforms of intelligence oversight.
