Tin(II) fluoride

Tin(II) fluoride, commonly called stannous fluoride, is a chemical compound with the formula SnF2. It is a colourless solid used as an ingredient in toothpaste, where it provides fluoride for the prevention of dental caries and also has antibacterial effects.

Properties

The compound melts at 213 °C and boils at 850 °C. In the solid state it has a polymeric structure with bridging fluoride ions. In aqueous solution it hydrolyses readily and oxidises to tin(IV) species, which complicated its early use in dentifrices; stabilised formulations overcame this problem.

Dental use

Stannous fluoride was introduced in toothpaste in the 1950s (notably in Crest) after research by Joseph Muhler at Indiana University. It converts hydroxyapatite in tooth enamel to the harder fluorapatite and reduces gingivitis through its antimicrobial action, giving it advantages over sodium fluoride for gum health.

Other uses and safety

Beyond dentistry, tin(II) fluoride has been used as a reducing agent and in tin-plating baths. Like other fluoride salts it is toxic in large quantities, but at the concentrations used in toothpaste it is considered safe and effective.
