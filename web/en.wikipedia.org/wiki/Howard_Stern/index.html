Howard Stern

Howard Allan Stern (born January 12, 1954) is an American radio and television personality, best known for his radio show The Howard Stern Show, which gained popularity when it was nationally syndicated on terrestrial radio from 1986 to 2005. He has described himself as the "King of All Media" for his successes outside radio.

Career

Born in Queens, New York, Stern developed an interest in radio as a child and worked at stations in Hartford, Detroit and Washington, D.C., before joining WNBC in New York in 1982. Fired in 1985, he moved to WXRK (K-Rock), where his morning show — mixing interviews, comedy bits and candid talk about his personal life — became the top-rated programme in its market and was syndicated across the United States. His conflicts with the FCC over alleged indecency led to fines totalling millions of dollars for his employers.

Stern's autobiography Private Parts (1993) became a bestseller and was adapted into a 1997 film in which he played himself. In 2006 he moved to Sirius Satellite Radio (later Sirius XM) in a landmark deal, freeing his show from FCC regulation. He later served as a judge on America's Got Talent (2012–2015) and published the interview collection Howard Stern Comes Again in 2019.
