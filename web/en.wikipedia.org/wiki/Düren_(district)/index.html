The District of Düren (German: Kreis Düren) is a district (Kreis) in the west of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, between the cities of Aachen and Cologne. Its capital is the town of Düren. Neighbouring districts include Aachen, Euskirchen, Rhein-Erft-Kreis and Heinsberg.

Geography

The district covers part of the northern Eifel hills in the south and the flat Jülich Börde plain in the north, and is crossed by the river Rur (Roer). The Rur reservoir (Rurtalsperre) in the Eifel portion is one of Germany's largest dams. The northern part of the district is affected by open-cast lignite mining, including the Hambach surface mine, one of the largest in Europe.

Towns and municipalities

The district comprises 15 municipalities, among them the towns of Düren, Jülich, Linnich, Heimbach and Nideggen, and the municipalities of Aldenhoven, Inden, Langerwehe, Merzenich, Niederzier, Nörvenich, Titz, Vettweiß, Kreuzau and Hürtgenwald. The town of Jülich is known for the Forschungszentrum Jülich research centre.

History

The modern district was created in 1972 by merging the former districts of Düren and Jülich as part of the North Rhine-Westphalian municipal reforms. Historically the area was divided among the Duchy of Jülich, the Electorate of Cologne and other territories of the Holy Roman Empire.
