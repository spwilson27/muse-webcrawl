Clear script

The term Clear script may refer to the Mongolian Clear Script (todo bichig), an alphabet created in 1648 by Zaya Pandita for the Oirat language. This page describes the term in its general linguistic sense.

In the study of writing systems, a clear script is any script whose letterforms unambiguously represent the sounds of the language it writes — that is, a script with a close, consistent correspondence between graphemes and phonemes, so that a reader who knows the letters can pronounce a written word correctly and a writer can spell a heard word correctly.

Characteristics

Scripts described as "clear" or transparent typically exhibit:

  • a one-to-one or near one-to-one mapping between letters and sounds;
  • distinct letterforms for sounds that are distinct in speech, avoiding the ambiguity found in older scripts where one sign served several sounds;
  • consistent rules for vowel notation, including in positions where earlier scripts omitted or merged vowels.

Examples

The Mongolian Clear Script was so named because its creator, the Oirat monk Zaya Pandita, reformed the traditional Mongolian alphabet in 1648 precisely to remove such ambiguities. Several other writing systems have been praised or designed for similar transparency, including the Finnish and Turkish orthographies, Hangul for Korean, and various phonetic alphabets devised by missionaries and linguists for previously unwritten languages.
