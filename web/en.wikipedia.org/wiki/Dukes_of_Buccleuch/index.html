Duke of Buccleuch

The Duke of Buccleuch (/bəˈkluː/ bə-KLOO), formerly also spelled Buccleugh, is a title in the Peerage of Scotland created twice. The first creation, in 1663, was for James Scott, Duke of Monmouth, the eldest illegitimate son of Charles II, and his wife Anne Scott, who was created Duchess of Buccleuch in her own right. After Monmouth's execution for treason in 1685, the dukedom survived in Anne's line because it had been granted with a special remainder.

History

The Scotts of Buccleuch were a great Border family, elevated first as Lords Scott of Buccleuch (1606) and Earls of Buccleuch (1619). Through marriage in 1743, the Buccleuch inheritance united with that of the Dukes of Queensberry, and the family became one of the largest landowners in Britain. The dukes' principal seats include Drumlanrig Castle in Dumfriesshire, Bowhill House in Selkirkshire, and Boughton House in Northamptonshire, the last inherited through the Montagu line.

The present holder is Richard Scott, 10th Duke of Buccleuch and 12th Duke of Queensberry, who succeeded in 2007. The Buccleuch Estates remain among the largest private landholdings in Scotland, and successive dukes have been notable art collectors; the Buccleuch collection includes works by Rembrandt and Leonardo da Vinci's Madonna of the Yarnwinder.
