A Generation III reactor is a classification of nuclear fission power reactors whose designs evolved from the earlier Generation II reactors, with evolutionary improvements in fuel technology, thermal efficiency, modularised construction, passive and standardised safety systems, and reduced capital costs. The first Generation III reactors entered service in Japan in the mid-1990s.

Overview

Generation III designs are evolutionary developments rather than revolutionary departures: they build directly on the pressurised water reactor (PWR), boiling water reactor (BWR) and pressurised heavy-water reactor (PHWR) lineages. Design goals typically include a longer operating life (commonly 60 years), a core damage frequency at least an order of magnitude lower than Generation II plants, and simplified, standardised construction to reduce costs.

Generation III+ designs

Generation III+ designs incorporate further improvements, most notably passive safety systems that rely on gravity, natural circulation and compressed gases rather than powered pumps, allowing the reactor to be cooled safely for an extended period without operator action or external power. Examples include the Westinghouse AP1000 and the GE Hitachi ESBWR.

List of designs

Major Generation III and III+ designs include the AREVA/Framatome EPR, the Westinghouse AP1000, the Mitsubishi APWR, the GE Hitachi ABWR and ESBWR, the Korean APR-1400, the Chinese Hualong One (HPR1000), and the Russian VVER-1200 (AES-2006).
