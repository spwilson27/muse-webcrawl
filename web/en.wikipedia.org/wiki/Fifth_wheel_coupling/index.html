Fifth wheel coupling

Fifth wheel coupling
Used on: Semi-tractors, large pickup trucks
Trailer fitting: Kingpin (2 in or 3.5 in)
Function: Pivoting hitch carrying vertical load

The fifth wheel coupling provides the link between a semi-trailer and its towing unit — a semi-tractor, a heavy truck, or a suitably equipped pickup. It consists of a kingpin, a vertical steel pin mounted under the front of the trailer, and a horseshoe-shaped coupling plate on the tractor, the "fifth wheel" itself, whose locking jaws grip the kingpin. The coupling carries a large share of the trailer's weight and pivots to allow articulation in turns and over uneven ground.

Design and operation

The tractor's fifth wheel is a thick, greased steel plate with a V-shaped throat at the rear leading to the locking mechanism. To couple, the driver reverses the tractor under the trailer's front overhang so the kingpin slides up the plate and snaps into the jaws; air and electrical lines are then connected and the trailer's landing gear raised. Standard kingpins are 2 inches (50.8 mm) in diameter for most road haulage, with 3.5-inch pins used for very heavy loads. Because the coupling sits over or ahead of the tractor's rear axles, the trailer's weight is borne by the tractor rather than dragging behind it, greatly improving stability compared with a drawbar hitch.

Origin of the name

The name derives from horse-drawn wagons and carriages, where a horizontal circular plate — a literal fifth wheel — was mounted above the front axle to allow it to pivot under the body. Early motor trucks adopted the name for their swivelling coupling plates. The expression "fifth wheel" also entered the language as a metaphor for a superfluous person or thing.

Recreational use

Scaled-down fifth wheel hitches mounted in pickup truck beds are used to tow large recreational "fifth-wheel trailers", which offer more interior space and steadier towing than conventional bumper-pull travel trailers.
