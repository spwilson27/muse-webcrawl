Red envelope

A red envelope, red packet, or hongbao (traditional Chinese: 紅包; simplified Chinese: 红包; pinyin: hóngbāo; Cantonese: 利是, lai see) is a gift of money given during holidays or for special occasions such as weddings, graduations, and birthdays in China and other East and Southeast Asian societies. The red colour of the envelope symbolises good luck and prosperity in Chinese culture, and is believed to ward off evil spirits.

Usage

Red envelopes are most commonly given by married couples and elders to children and unmarried younger relatives during Lunar New Year, accompanied by auspicious greetings; traditionally the recipient kneels or offers New Year wishes before accepting, and the envelope is not opened in the giver's presence. At weddings, guests give red envelopes (or white/joss-paper-wrapped equivalents in some contexts) to the couple to defray the banquet cost, with amounts recorded in a gift ledger. Employers may give them to staff as bonuses, and they are also given at the opening of businesses, at funerals in some regions (in different colours), and on the birth of a child.

Origins and legend

According to legend, in ancient times a demon named Sui (祟) terrorised children on New Year's Eve; the noise of coins wrapped in red paper frightened it away, and the coins — yasui qian, "money to suppress Sui" — became New Year gifts strung on red thread. Paper red envelopes became widespread with the development of printing in the Ming and Qing dynasties, replacing threaded coins.

Digital red envelopes

In 2014 the messaging app WeChat launched virtual red envelopes for Lunar New Year, allowing users to send money gifts individually or to groups where random allocations are grabbed competitively; the feature distributed billions of envelopes in its first seasons and became a major driver of WeChat Pay adoption, with rival platforms such as Alipay running competing campaigns. Digital hongbao are now common across China and the diaspora.

Similar customs

Comparable money-gift envelopes include Korean sebaetdon given in silk or paper pouches at Seollal, Vietnamese lì xì at Tết (also red), Japanese otoshidama in decorated white envelopes (pochibukuro), and the green envelopes (sampul hijau) of Malay Eid al-Fitr celebrations in Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia.
