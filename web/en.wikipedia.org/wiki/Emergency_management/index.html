Emergency management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Emergency management, or disaster management, is the discipline of dealing with and avoiding risks from natural and human-caused hazards. It involves the organised coordination of resources and responsibilities for preparing for, responding to, and recovering from emergencies such as floods, earthquakes, hurricanes, industrial accidents, epidemics, and terrorist attacks, with the goals of saving lives, protecting property and the environment, and minimising economic disruption.

The four phases

  • Mitigation — long-term measures to reduce or eliminate risk, such as building codes, land-use planning, levees, and seismic retrofitting.
  • Preparedness — planning, training, exercises, warning systems, stockpiling, and public education before an event.
  • Response — immediate actions during and after an event: search and rescue, firefighting, emergency medical care, evacuation, and sheltering.
  • Recovery — restoring infrastructure, housing, and livelihoods afterwards, from short-term relief to long-term reconstruction.

Organisation

Emergency management follows the principle that response begins locally and escalates: municipal and regional agencies handle most incidents, with national bodies — such as the US Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), created in 1979 — providing support when local capacity is overwhelmed. Command is commonly structured through the Incident Command System (ICS), developed in the 1970s and later incorporated into the US National Incident Management System (NIMS). Internationally, the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) and the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies coordinate large responses, while frameworks such as the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015) set global priorities.

Professional practice

Modern emergency managers use hazard and vulnerability analysis, geographic information systems, and social research on warning response and community resilience. The field has professionalised through academic programmes and credentials such as the Certified Emergency Manager designation, and has expanded toward an "all-hazards", whole-community approach that includes businesses, voluntary organisations, and vulnerable populations in planning.
