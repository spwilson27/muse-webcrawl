Root nodule

Root nodules are swellings found on the roots of certain plants, principally legumes (family Fabaceae), that form a symbiosis with nitrogen-fixing bacteria. Inside the nodules, bacteria — collectively called rhizobia in legumes — convert inert atmospheric nitrogen (N2) into ammonia the plant can use, in exchange for carbohydrates and a protected, low-oxygen environment.

Formation and function

Nodulation begins with chemical signalling: plant roots release flavonoids that attract rhizobia and switch on bacterial nod genes; bacterial Nod factors in turn trigger root hair curling, infection threads and division of cortical cells that builds the nodule. The oxygen-carrying protein leghemoglobin — which gives active nodules their pink colour — buffers oxygen so the oxygen-sensitive enzyme nitrogenase can work. A smaller group of non-legume plants (actinorhizal plants such as alder) form nodules with the actinomycete Frankia.

Agricultural importance

Legume–rhizobium symbiosis is the largest natural source of fixed nitrogen in agriculture; rotations with clover, soybean, peas and alfalfa enrich soils and reduce fertilizer needs. Farmers commonly inoculate seed with selected rhizobial strains.
