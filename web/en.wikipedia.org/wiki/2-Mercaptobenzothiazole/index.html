2-Mercaptobenzothiazole (MBT) is an organosulfur compound with the formula C7H5NS2. The molecule consists of a benzene ring fused to a 2-thione-substituted thiazole ring. It is a pale yellow solid that exists predominantly in the thione tautomeric form and is one of the most widely produced rubber chemicals in the world.

Production

MBT is manufactured industrially by reacting aniline, carbon disulfide and sulfur at high temperature and pressure. Production volumes are very large, reflecting its central role in the rubber industry.

Uses

MBT is used principally as an accelerator for the vulcanization of rubber and as the parent compound of a family of derived accelerators, including MBTS (2,2'-dithiobis(benzothiazole)) and various sulfenamides. It is found in tyres, footwear, gloves and other rubber goods, and is also employed as a corrosion inhibitor, a fungicide component and an analytical reagent for certain metal ions.

Health and environment

MBT is a recognised skin sensitiser and a common cause of allergic contact dermatitis from rubber products. In 2016 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified 2-mercaptobenzothiazole as probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A), based largely on evidence of urinary bladder tumours in exposed workers. Because it is released from tyres and other products, it is a widespread micropollutant in road runoff and wastewater.
