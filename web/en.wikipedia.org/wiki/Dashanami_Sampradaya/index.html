Dashanami Sampradaya

The Dashanami Sampradaya (Sanskrit: दशनामि सम्प्रदाय, "Tradition of Ten Names") is a Hindu monastic tradition of wandering ascetics (sannyasins) traditionally attributed to the philosopher Adi Shankara (8th century CE). Initiates into the order receive one of ten monastic names appended to their own.

The ten names

The ten names are Saraswati, Bharati, Puri, Tirtha, Ashrama, Vana, Aranya, Giri, Parvata and Sagara. Each name is associated with one of the four cardinal monastic seats (mathas) founded, according to tradition, by Shankara at Sringeri (south), Puri (east), Dvaraka (west) and Jyotirmath (north), and with particular gotras, Vedas and other attributes. Members are often known simply as "Swamis", and their titles — such as Saraswati or Giri — indicate their lineage within the order.

Organisation

Dashanami monks belong to the Shaivite tradition and are organised into akharas (regiments) that play a prominent role at the Kumbh Mela. Historically, some orders of Dashanami ascetics were militarised, serving as warrior-monks; the tradition also produced many scholars and teachers of Advaita Vedanta, the philosophy of Shankara.
