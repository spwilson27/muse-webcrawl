Corno Grande (Italian for "Great Horn") is the highest point in the Apennine Mountains, rising to 2,912 metres (9,554 ft) above sea level. It is the culminating summit of the Gran Sasso d'Italia massif in the Abruzzo region of central Italy, within the Gran Sasso e Monti della Laga National Park. The mountain lies in the province of Teramo, near the border with the province of L'Aquila.

Geography

Corno Grande consists of four principal peaks: the Western Peak (Vetta Occidentale, 2,912 m), the highest; the Central Peak (Vetta Centrale, 2,893 m); the Eastern Peak (Vetta Orientale, 2,903 m); and the Torrione Cambi (2,875 m). The massif is composed chiefly of limestone and dolomite of Mesozoic origin. On its northern flank lies the Calderone glacier, long considered the southernmost glacier in Europe, which has retreated dramatically in recent decades and is now often described as a glacieret or divided ice body.

Climbing

The standard route (via normale) ascends from Campo Imperatore, reached by cable car from Fonte Cerreto near Assergi, passing the Duca degli Abruzzi refuge and the Sella del Brecciaio before climbing to the Western Peak. The first recorded ascent was made in 1573 by the Bolognese engineer Francesco De Marchi, accompanied by the chamois hunter Francesco Di Domenico, an exploit remarkable for its era. The mountain is a popular destination for hikers and climbers, though weather can change rapidly and the upper sections involve exposed scrambling.
