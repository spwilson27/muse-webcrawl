Altitude

Altitude is a distance measurement, usually in the vertical or "up" direction, between a reference datum and a point or object. The exact definition and reference datum vary by context — altitude is used in geography, aviation, geometry, astronomy, and sport. In geography, altitude generally refers to elevation above mean sea level. In aviation, altitude is measured with an altimeter referenced to sea-level pressure, while height is measured above ground level.

In geography

The altitude of a land feature is its elevation above mean sea level. The highest point on Earth, the summit of Mount Everest, stands about 8,849 metres above sea level. High-altitude locations — generally above about 2,500 metres — impose physiological stresses on humans because of reduced partial pressure of oxygen, which can cause altitude sickness.

In aviation

Pilots use several related measures: indicated altitude (read from the altimeter), true altitude (above sea level), absolute altitude (above ground), and pressure altitude and density altitude, used for performance calculations. At higher levels aircraft fly by flight levels referenced to the standard pressure setting of 1013.25 hPa.

In astronomy

In the horizontal coordinate system, altitude is the angle between the horizon and a celestial object, measured from 0° at the horizon to 90° at the zenith; together with azimuth it fixes an object's apparent position in the sky.

Effects on organisms

Decreasing atmospheric pressure with altitude reduces available oxygen, affecting humans, animals, and even plant communities; organisms native to high mountains show adaptations such as increased haemoglobin affinity or, in birds, more efficient lungs.
