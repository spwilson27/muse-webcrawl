Foreskin

In human anatomy, the foreskin, also known as the prepuce, is the double-layered fold of smooth muscle tissue, blood vessels, neurons, skin and mucous membrane that covers and protects the glans penis and the urinary meatus when the penis is not erect. It is typically retractable over the glans.

Structure and development

The foreskin is attached to the glans by the frenulum and contains specialised nerve endings. At birth it is usually fused to the glans and separates naturally over childhood; it is non-retractable in most newborns and becomes retractable in most boys by adolescence. The inner layer is a mucous membrane, and the space between foreskin and glans is sometimes called the preputial sac.

Function

The foreskin protects the glans and meatus, maintains moisture of the mucosal surface, and contributes to sensation. Medical opinion on its immunological and protective roles continues to be discussed in the literature.

Health and circumcision

Conditions affecting the foreskin include phimosis (inability to retract), paraphimosis and balanitis (inflammation). Removal of the foreskin by circumcision is one of the most common surgical procedures worldwide, performed for religious, cultural, hygienic and medical reasons, and remains the subject of ethical and medical debate.
