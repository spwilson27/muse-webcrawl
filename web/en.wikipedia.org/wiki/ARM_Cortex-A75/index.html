ARM Cortex-A75

ARM Cortex-A75

Designer: ARM Holdings

Announced: May 2017

Architecture: ARMv8.2-A (AArch64)

Pipeline: Out-of-order, 3-way superscalar

Role: High-performance "big" core for DynamIQ systems

Predecessor: ARM Cortex-A73

Successor: ARM Cortex-A76

The ARM Cortex-A75 is a high-performance central processing unit implementing the ARMv8.2-A 64-bit instruction set, designed by ARM Holdings and announced in May 2017 alongside the Cortex-A55. It succeeds the Cortex-A73 as ARM's flagship mobile CPU core and was the first core designed for ARM's DynamIQ cluster technology, which allows big and little cores to share a single cluster and cache hierarchy.

Design

The Cortex-A75 is a three-wide decode, out-of-order superscalar design. ARM claimed performance gains of around 16–48 percent over the A73 in various workloads, with particular emphasis on improved machine-learning and single-thread performance. It supports the ARMv8.2-A extensions including half-precision floating point and the dot-product instructions (in later revisions) that accelerate neural-network inference. In typical DynamIQ configurations it is paired with power-efficient Cortex-A55 cores.

Implementations

The Cortex-A75 was used in flagship mobile chips including the Qualcomm Snapdragon 845 (as the basis of the semi-custom Kryo 385 Gold cores) and the Samsung Exynos 9810 (as the reference for its partner big cores in some regions), powering many 2018 flagship smartphones.
