Eden Valley wine region

TypeAustralian Geographical Indication (wine zone/region)
LocationBarossa Zone, South Australia
ElevationApprox. 380–630 m (1,250–2,070 ft)

Notable producersHenschke, Pewsey Vale, Yalumba

The Eden Valley wine region is an Australian Geographical Indication in South Australia, located in the hills east of the Barossa Valley. Its higher altitude and cooler climate distinguish it from the warmer Barossa floor, and it is particularly renowned for elegant, age-worthy Riesling as well as refined Shiraz.

Geography and climate

The region sits along the Mount Lofty Ranges at elevations roughly 200 metres above the Barossa Valley, giving cooler nights, a longer growing season, and wines with higher natural acidity. Soils are generally rocky and low in fertility, with weathered schist and quartz. Key towns include Eden Valley and Springton; the sub-area of High Eden, to the south, is noted for premium Riesling.

History and wines

Grapes were planted in the area from the mid-19th century by English and Silesian settlers. The region is home to some of Australia's most famous single vineyards, including Henschke's Hill of Grace, planted around 1860 with Shiraz vines that rank among the oldest in the world. Eden Valley Rieslings are celebrated for lime and floral aromatics and their capacity to develop toasty complexity over decades in bottle.
