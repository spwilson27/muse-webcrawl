Coloratura is an elaborate melody with runs, trills, wide leaps, or similar virtuoso-like material, or a passage of such music. Operatic roles in which such music plays a prominent part, and singers of those roles, are also called coloratura. Its best-known use is in vocal music of the 18th and early 19th centuries, particularly in bel canto opera arias, where the ornamentation was regarded as an essential expressive device rather than mere decoration.

Historical usage

The term derives from the Italian coloratura, meaning "coloring". It was first applied to the florid ornamentation of 16th-century vocal music and to the notation of such music. By the Baroque era, composers such as Handel wrote demanding divisions for both voices and instruments. In the Classical and bel canto periods, composers including Mozart, Rossini, Donizetti and Bellini wrote showcases of agility — Mozart's Queen of the Night in The Magic Flute, with her famous high Fs, is among the most celebrated examples.

Coloratura soprano

A coloratura soprano is a type of operatic soprano voice that specializes in music distinguished by agile runs, leaps and trills. The coloratura soprano may be further divided into lyric and dramatic subcategories. Roles include Lucia in Donizetti's Lucia di Lammermoor (with its celebrated "Mad Scene"), Gilda in Verdi's Rigoletto, and Olympia in Offenbach's The Tales of Hoffmann. Celebrated coloratura sopranos of the recording era include Joan Sutherland, Beverly Sills, Maria Callas (in her early career), Edita Gruberová and Diana Damrau.
