Château Latour

The tower that gives Château Latour its name, at the southern edge of Pauillac.

Château Latour is a wine estate in Pauillac, in the Médoc region of Bordeaux, France, ranked as a Premier Grand Cru Classé (First Growth) in the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855. The estate takes its name from a medieval fortified tower, the Tour de Saint-Maubert, built during the Hundred Years' War to guard the Gironde estuary. Latour is celebrated for producing especially powerful, long-lived Cabernet Sauvignon-dominated wines.

History

Château Latour
Location: Pauillac, Médoc, France
Appellation: Pauillac AOC
Classification: Premier Grand Cru Classé (1855)
Owner: François Pinault (Artémis group), since 1993
Cases per year: about 18,000 (grand vin)

The estate was established in the 18th century by the Ségur family, proprietors of several great Médoc properties, and after the French Revolution passed through the de Beaumont and de Clauzel families and later a group of British owners including the Pearson group and Allied Lyons. In 1993 Latour was purchased by the French businessman François Pinault through his holding company Artémis. From 2013, the estate controversially withdrew from the en primeur futures system, choosing to release its wines only when it judged them ready to drink.

Vineyard and wines

The vineyard totals about 96 hectares, of which the historic core, the Enclos of some 47 hectares surrounding the château, supplies the grand vin. Plantings are roughly 80% Cabernet Sauvignon, 18% Merlot and small amounts of Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot, on deep gravel over clay and marl close to the Gironde, whose moderating influence protects the vines from frost. The grand vin, Château Latour, is known for structure and great ageing potential; the second wine, Les Forts de Latour, is drawn from younger vines and plots outside the Enclos, and a third wine is sold simply as Pauillac.
