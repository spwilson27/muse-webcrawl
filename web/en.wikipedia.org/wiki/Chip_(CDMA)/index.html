Chip (CDMA)

In code-division multiple access (CDMA) communications, a chip is a pulse of a direct-sequence spread spectrum (DSSS) code. Each binary symbol is represented by a sequence of chips, and the rate at which chips are transmitted — the chip rate — is much higher than the symbol rate.

Chip rate

The ratio of the chip rate to the symbol rate is the spreading factor or processing gain. For example, in IS-95 the chip rate is 1.2288 megachips per second. A higher chip rate spreads the signal over a wider bandwidth, providing resistance to interference and multipath fading.

Orthogonality

Chips are generated from spreading codes such as Walsh codes or pseudorandom noise (PN) sequences. Receivers correlate the incoming chip stream with the code to recover the original data bits.
