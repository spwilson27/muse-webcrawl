Crass Records

Crass Records was a British independent record label founded in 1979 by the anarcho-punk band Crass, based at Dial House in Epping, Essex. The label embodied the band's DIY ethic, selling records at prices stamped on the sleeve ("Pay no more than...") and giving a platform to other anarchist and experimental artists.

History and releases

Crass formed the label after difficulties releasing their debut album The Feeding of the 5000 (1978) on another label, whose pressing plant objected to the track "Reality Asylum." As Crass Records, the band released its own records including Stations of the Crass (1979), Penis Envy (1981) and Christ – The Album (1982), alongside the Bullshit Detector compilations of demo tapes sent by unknown bands. Other artists included Poison Girls, Conflict (early releases via the related Corpus Christi imprint), Flux of Pink Indians, Zounds and Annie Anxiety.

Legacy

Crass Records demonstrated that a band could record, package, distribute and price its own music outside the mainstream industry, becoming a template for independent labels and the wider anarcho-punk movement. After Crass disbanded in 1984 the catalogue passed through various reissue programmes, including remastered "Crassical Collection" editions.
