Citation (horse)

Citation (April 11, 1945 – August 8, 1970) was a champion American Thoroughbred racehorse. He is the eighth winner of the American Triple Crown, having won the Kentucky Derby, the Preakness Stakes, and the Belmont Stakes in 1948. Citation was the first Thoroughbred to win more than $1 million in prize money, a landmark in American racing history. He was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1959.

Racing career

Bred and owned by Calumet Farm of Lexington, Kentucky, Citation dominated American racing as a three-year-old in 1948, winning 19 of his 20 starts, including the Triple Crown under jockey Eddie Arcaro. His streak of 16 consecutive victories, achieved in 1950–51, stood as a record for decades and was matched by Cigar in 1996. He retired as the world's leading money-winning Thoroughbred.

Legacy

In The Blood-Horse magazine's ranking of the top 100 American racehorses of the 20th century, Citation was ranked third, behind only Man o' War and Secretariat. He stood at stud at Calumet Farm and sired the champion filly Silver Spoon. The Citation Handicap and other races are named in his honour.
