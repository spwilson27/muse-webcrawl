Lochleven Castle

Lochleven Castle (also spelled Loch Leven Castle) is a ruined castle on an island in Loch Leven, in the Perth and Kinross local authority area of Scotland. Possibly built around 1300, the castle was the site of the imprisonment of Mary, Queen of Scots, in 1567–68, and her forced abdication.

Lochleven Castle
Location: Castle Island, Loch Leven, Perth and Kinross, Scotland
Built: c. 1300 (tower house late 14th/early 15th century)
Owner: Historic Environment Scotland
Famous event: Imprisonment of Mary, Queen of Scots (1567–1568)

History

A castle may have been built on the island by 1257, when the 16-year-old King Alexander III was forcibly brought there by his regents. During the First War of Scottish Independence the castle was held by the English, and was captured by the Scots before 1313, probably by William Wallace. It was visited by David II and by Robert II, who died in its successor period of use as a royal prison.

Mary, Queen of Scots

Mary stayed at Lochleven as a guest in 1563, debating with John Knox, before being imprisoned there on 17 June 1567 following her surrender at Carberry Hill. Held in the Glassin Tower under Sir William Douglas of Lochleven, she miscarried twins, was forced to sign abdication papers in favour of her infant son James VI on 24 July 1567, and — after one failed attempt — escaped on 2 May 1568 disguised as a servant, aided by the young Willie Douglas and George Douglas, rowing ashore during a May Day pageant.

The castle today

The ruins comprise a rectangular courtyard with a five-storey tower house at one corner and the round Glassin Tower opposite. The island is now reached by ferry from Kinross, and the castle is in the care of Historic Environment Scotland.
