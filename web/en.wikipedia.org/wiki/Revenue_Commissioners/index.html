Revenue Commissioners

The Revenue Commissioners (Irish: Na Coimisinéirí Ioncaim), commonly referred to as Revenue, is the Irish Government agency responsible for customs, excise, taxation and related matters. It was established in 1923, shortly after the creation of the Irish Free State, by merging the Irish functions of the British Customs and Excise and Inland Revenue.

Functions

Revenue's core business is the assessment and collection of taxes and duties, including income tax, corporation tax, VAT, excise duties and customs duties, as well as the administration of schemes such as the Local Property Tax. It also plays a role in combating smuggling, drug trafficking and the shadow economy, operating the Customs Service and employing specialist units including detector dog teams. The Office is headed by a board of three Commissioners, one of whom serves as Chairman, appointed by the Government; its head office is at Dublin Castle.
