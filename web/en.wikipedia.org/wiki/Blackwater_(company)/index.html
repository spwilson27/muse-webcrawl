Blackwater was an American private military company founded in 1997 by former Navy SEAL officer Erik Prince. It became the best-known of the private security contractors employed by the United States government during the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, providing armed security for officials, training and support services.

History and contracts

Blackwater began as a training facility in the Great Dismal Swamp of North Carolina. After the 2000 attack on USS Cole and especially after the 2003 invasion of Iraq, it won major State Department contracts to protect American diplomats, including Ambassador Paul Bremer. In March 2004 four of its contractors were ambushed and killed in Fallujah, their mutilated bodies hung from a bridge — an event that precipitated the First Battle of Fallujah.

Nisour Square and fallout

On 16 September 2007, Blackwater guards escorting a diplomatic convoy opened fire in Baghdad's Nisour Square, killing 17 Iraqi civilians. The incident caused international outrage, a temporary Iraqi ban on the company, and U.S. prosecutions; four guards were convicted in 2014 and pardoned in 2020. Amid the reputational damage, the company rebranded as Xe Services in 2009 and Academi in 2011, before merging into Constellis Holdings in 2014.
