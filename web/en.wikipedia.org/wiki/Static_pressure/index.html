Static pressure

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In fluid mechanics the term static pressure has several uses:

  • In the design and operation of aircraft, static pressure is the air pressure in the aircraft's static pressure system.
  • In fluid mechanics, many authors use the term static pressure in preference to just pressure to avoid ambiguity. Often, however, the word "static" may be dropped and in that usage pressure is the same as static pressure in a nominated place in a fluid.
  • In thermodynamics, pressure is usually called static pressure.

Static pressure in fluid flow

The concept of pressure is central to the study of fluids. A pressure can be identified for every point in a body of fluid, independent of whether the fluid is in motion. Pressure can be measured at a point in a fluid using an aneroid, Bourdon tube, mercury column or similar instrument.

In incompressible fluid flow analysis, the total pressure along a streamline is decomposed by Bernoulli's principle into static pressure and dynamic pressure:

p0 = p + q, where q = ½ρv2

Here p0 is the total (stagnation) pressure, p is the static pressure, q is the dynamic pressure, ρ is the fluid density and v is the flow speed. Aircraft pitot-static systems measure static pressure through flush static ports to determine altitude and, combined with pitot pressure, airspeed.

Static pressure in HVAC

In heating, ventilation and air-conditioning, static pressure refers to the resistance to airflow in ductwork and components, measured in inches of water column or pascals. Fans must overcome the total external static pressure of filters, coils, dampers and ducts to deliver the design airflow.
