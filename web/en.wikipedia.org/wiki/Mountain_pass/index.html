Mountain pass

A mountain pass is a route through a mountain range or over a ridge. If following the lowest route through the range, a pass is locally the highest point on that route. Many of the world's mountain ranges have presented barriers to travel, so passes have been important since before recorded history, and have played a key role in trade, war, and migration. A pass may be very short, consisting of a slight dip in the ridge, or may be a valley many kilometres long, whose highest point is identifiable only by surveying.

Terminology

Passes are known by many local names around the world: col in French, joch in German, la in Tibetan (as in Nathu La), ghat in India, puerta and puerto in Spanish, and saddle, gap, notch, and wind gap in English. In Scotland, the term bealach is used. The top of a pass is frequently the only flat ground in the area, and may be a desirable location for buildings, parking, and shrines.

Use and significance

Because passes channel movement, they have long been strategic chokepoints. Armies from Hannibal crossing the Alps to the campaigns along the Khyber Pass between Afghanistan and Pakistan have fought over them. Trade routes depended on them: the Silk Road crossed the Torugart and Irkeshtam passes of Central Asia, and Alpine passes such as the Brenner and Great St Bernard linked northern and southern Europe. Roads and railways still seek out passes, though tunnels increasingly bypass the highest and most weather-exposed crossings; many famous pass roads — the Grossglockner High Alpine Road, the Transfăgărășan, the Stelvio Pass with its hairpin bends — are now tourist attractions and iconic cycling climbs in their own right.

Notable passes

Among the world's highest motorable passes are those of the Ladakh region of India, including Khardung La (about 5,359 m) and the Chang La. Historic examples include the Khyber Pass, the Brenner Pass (1,370 m, the lowest of the main Alpine crossings), Donner Pass in California's Sierra Nevada, and Thorong La (5,416 m) on Nepal's Annapurna Circuit trekking route.
