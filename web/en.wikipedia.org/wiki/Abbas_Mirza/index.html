Abbas Mirza (Persian: عباس میرزا; August 20, 1789 – October 25, 1833) was a Qajar crown prince of Iran and the best-known Iranian military commander of the Russo-Persian Wars. Appointed heir apparent by his father Fath-Ali Shah despite not being the eldest son, he governed Azerbaijan from Tabriz and led Iran's armies against the Russian Empire for two decades.

Wars with Russia

Abbas Mirza commanded Persian forces in the Russo-Persian War of 1804–1813, which ended with the Treaty of Gulistan, by which Iran ceded much of its Caucasian territory. In the war of 1826–1828, after initial successes, his forces were decisively defeated; the Treaty of Turkmenchay cost Iran the Erivan and Nakhichevan khanates, a heavy indemnity, and capitulatory rights for Russia. He also campaigned against the Ottoman Empire (1821–1823) with considerable success.

Reforms

Convinced that Iran's defeats stemmed from military and institutional backwardness, Abbas Mirza pioneered reform: he created European-style regiments (the Nezam-e Jadid, "New Order") trained by British and French officers, established an arms factory and printing press in Tabriz, sent Iranian students to England (from 1811 and 1815) — among the first Iranian students in Europe — and sponsored translation of European works. He is often regarded as a forerunner of Iran's later modernisation movements.

Death and succession

Sent east to reassert control in Khorasan, Abbas Mirza died at Mashhad in 1833, predeceasing his father. The succession passed to his son, who became Mohammad Shah Qajar in 1834. Abbas Mirza should not be confused with Abbas ibn Firnas, the ninth-century Andalusian polymath.
