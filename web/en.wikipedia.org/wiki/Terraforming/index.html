Terraforming

Terraforming is the hypothetical process of deliberately modifying the atmosphere, temperature, surface topography or ecology of a planet or moon to be similar to Earth, making it habitable for humans and other terrestrial life. The term was coined in Jack Williamson's 1942 science-fiction story "Collision Orbit" and popularised scientifically by Carl Sagan, who proposed seeding Venus with algae (1961) and later studied warming Mars.

Mars proposals

Terraforming Mars would require thickening its thin CO₂ atmosphere, warming the surface to release frozen CO₂ and water, and creating breathable air. Proposed methods include importing volatiles from comets, producing super-greenhouse gases (perfluorocarbons) in situ, orbital mirrors, and darkening the polar caps. Studies suggest Mars's accessible CO₂ may be insufficient for full Earth-like pressure with foreseeable technology, and Mars lacks a protective magnetosphere.

Other worlds and ethics

Venus (cooling and CO₂ removal), the Moon, Titan and Europa have all been discussed, as has the more modest "paraterraforming" of enclosed habitats. Ethical debates weigh planetary protection of possible indigenous life against arguments for spreading terrestrial life, and realistic timescales of centuries to millennia.
