New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision

The New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision (NYSDOCCS) is the agency of New York State government responsible for the state's correctional facilities, the incarcerated population, and the supervision of parolees and other released offenders in the community.

History

The department was created on 1 April 2011 by the merger of the former Department of Correctional Services and the Division of Parole. New York's prison system grew dramatically during the late 20th century under the Rockefeller drug laws, peaking at more than 70,000 incarcerated people around 1999, before declining substantially in subsequent decades.

Facilities

DOCCS operates more than forty correctional facilities, classified as maximum, medium, and minimum security, including well-known prisons such as Sing Sing, Attica, Auburn, Clinton, Green Haven, and Elmira. It employs tens of thousands of correction officers and civilian staff.

Functions

Beyond custody, the department provides educational, vocational, substance-abuse, and reentry programs, operates prison industries (Corcraft), and supervises people released on parole, post-release supervision, and other community sanctions.
