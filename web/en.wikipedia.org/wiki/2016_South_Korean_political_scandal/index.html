2016 South Korean political scandal

The 2016 South Korean political scandal, often known as Choi Soon-sil gate, was a political scandal involving President Park Geun-hye and her longtime confidante Choi Soon-sil, who was revealed to have exercised extensive undue influence over the president and government affairs despite holding no official position.

Revelations

In October 2016, media reports — notably by JTBC, which obtained a tablet computer containing presidential documents — showed that Choi had received and edited drafts of presidential speeches, influenced policy and personnel decisions, and used her connections to extort large donations from major conglomerates (chaebol) to foundations she controlled. Choi's daughter's preferential admission to Ewha Womans University added to public outrage.

Protests and impeachment

Mass candlelight protests, the largest in South Korean history, drew millions calling for Park's resignation. The National Assembly voted to impeach Park on 9 December 2016, and on 10 March 2017 the Constitutional Court unanimously upheld the impeachment, removing her from office. An early presidential election in May 2017 was won by Moon Jae-in.

Trials

Park was convicted of abuse of power, bribery and coercion in 2018 and sentenced to a lengthy prison term (she was pardoned in 2021). Choi Soon-sil was also convicted and imprisoned. Samsung executive Lee Jae-yong was convicted of bribery in connection with the scandal.
