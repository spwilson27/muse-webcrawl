Apartment
Also calledFlat (British English); unit (Australian English)
TypeSelf-contained housing unit within a larger building
TenureRental, condominium ownership, cooperative

An apartment (American English), or flat (British English), is a self-contained housing unit — containing its own kitchen, bathroom and living space — that occupies only part of a larger building, generally on a single storey. Apartments range from small studio or efficiency units to multi-bedroom homes, penthouses and loft conversions, and they are the dominant form of housing in dense city centres worldwide.

History

Multi-family dwellings date to antiquity — the Roman insulae housed most urban residents of the empire. Purpose-built apartment blocks appeared in Paris and other European capitals in the 18th and 19th centuries; tenement housing followed in industrial cities such as New York, and the 20th century added modernist tower blocks, garden apartments and suburban complexes.

Tenure and types

Apartments may be rented from a landlord, owned individually as condominiums (strata title in Australia), or held through a housing cooperative in which residents own shares in the building. Common descriptive types include studios, one- and two-bedroom units, duplexes (spanning two floors), lofts, penthouses and serviced apartments.

By country

Terminology and prevalence vary widely: purpose-built flats predominate in much of Europe and Asia (in South Korea, large apateu complexes are the norm), while detached houses dominate many North American suburbs. Legal regimes governing leases, security of tenure and building standards differ accordingly.
