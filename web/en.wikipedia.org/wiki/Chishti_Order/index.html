The Chishti Order (Persian: چشتیہ, Chishtiyya) is a tariqa, a Sufi order within the mystical branch of Islam. It was founded in the small town of Chisht near Herat (in present-day Afghanistan) in the mid-10th century, and became the most influential Sufi order in medieval India.

History in South Asia

The order was brought to India by Moinuddin Chishti (1143–1236), who settled at Ajmer in Rajasthan; his shrine, the Ajmer Sharif Dargah, remains one of the most visited Sufi shrines in the world. His disciples included Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki of Delhi and, in the next generation, Baba Farid of Pakpattan and Nizamuddin Auliya of Delhi, under whom the order reached its height of influence during the Delhi Sultanate.

Practices and principles

The Chishtis emphasised love of God, tolerance, service to humanity, and distance from worldly power and royal patronage. They are known for the practice of sama — listening to devotional music and poetry, including qawwali — as an aid to spiritual ecstasy, a practice not shared by all Sufi orders. Their hospices (khanqahs) were open to all, irrespective of religion.
