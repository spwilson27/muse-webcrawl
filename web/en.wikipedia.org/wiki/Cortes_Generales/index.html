The Cortes Generales (Spanish for "General Courts") are the bicameral legislative chambers of Spain, consisting of the Congress of Deputies (the lower house) and the Senate (the upper house). They meet in Madrid: the Congress in the Palacio de las Cortes and the Senate in the nearby Palacio del Senado.

The Cortes represent the Spanish people and exercise legislative power, approve the budget, and control the actions of the Government, as provided in the Spanish Constitution of 1978. The Congress holds the dominant role: it invests the President of the Government and can override Senate vetoes.

History

The name derives from the medieval Cortes — assemblies of the estates of the kingdoms of Castile, Aragon and León; the Cortes of León of 1188 are sometimes cited among the earliest parliamentary bodies in Europe. The modern bicameral system dates from the 19th century (Estatuto Real, 1834; Constitution of 1845), was restored in unicameral form during the Second Republic, and was re-established in bicameral form by the Constitution of 1978.

Composition

The Congress of Deputies has 350 members elected by proportional representation in provincial constituencies. The Senate combines senators elected in provinces (four per peninsular province) with senators appointed by the parliaments of the autonomous communities, making it in part a chamber of territorial representation.
