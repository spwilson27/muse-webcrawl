Luc Plamondon (born 2 March 1942) is a French-Canadian lyricist and music business figure. He is best known for his work on the rock operas Starmania and Notre-Dame de Paris. Plamondon is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in francophone popular music, with an intense and often controversial personality.

Career

Born in Saint-Raymond, near Quebec City, Plamondon studied languages and art history and travelled in Europe before turning to songwriting in the early 1970s. He wrote hits for most of Quebec's major artists — including Diane Dufresne, Robert Charlebois and Ginette Reno — before collaborating with French composer Michel Berger on Starmania (1978), a dystopian rock opera that became a landmark of French-language musical theatre, with songs such as "Le monde est stone" and "Quand on arrive en ville".

He later wrote for international francophone stars, notably Celine Dion, for whom he wrote the album Dion chante Plamondon (1991), including "L'amour existe encore" and "Je danse dans ma tête". In 1998 his musical Notre-Dame de Paris, composed with Riccardo Cocciante and based on Victor Hugo's novel, premiered in Paris and became a worldwide phenomenon; its song "Belle" was voted France's song of the century in a public poll. An English-language version of the show later played in the West End and Las Vegas.

Honours

Plamondon has received numerous Félix awards and was named a Chevalier of the National Order of Quebec and an Officer of the Order of Canada. He was inducted into the Canadian Songwriters Hall of Fame and has a star on Canada's Walk of Fame in Toronto. An asteroid, 8351 Paley, is not connected to him, but the minor planet 10048 Grönbech is likewise unrelated; more fittingly, he has been honoured at home by the naming of streets and cultural venues in Quebec.
