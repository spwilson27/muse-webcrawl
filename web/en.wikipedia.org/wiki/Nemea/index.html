Nemea

Nemea (Greek: Νεμέα) is an ancient site in the northeastern part of the Peloponnese in Greece, in the region of Corinthia. In Greek mythology it was the home of the Nemean lion, killed by Heracles as the first of his twelve labours, and in antiquity it hosted the Nemean Games, one of the four Panhellenic Games of ancient Greece.

The sanctuary and games

The sanctuary of Zeus at Nemea was administered by the city of Cleonae and later by Argos. The games, held every two years, were founded according to tradition in 573 BC; victors received a wreath of wild celery. The site's stadium, with its vaulted entrance tunnel, and the Temple of Zeus — three of whose columns remained standing and more of which have since been re-erected — are among the principal remains.

Excavations and modern revival

Excavations by the University of California, Berkeley, under Stephen G. Miller from the 1970s uncovered the stadium, baths, hostel and other structures, now presented in an on-site museum. Since 1996 the Society for the Revival of the Nemean Games has organised modern revival games in which participants run barefoot in the ancient stadium.
