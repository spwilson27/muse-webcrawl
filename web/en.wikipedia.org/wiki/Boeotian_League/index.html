Boeotian League
Greek nameτὸ κοινὸν τῶν Βοιωτῶν (to koinon tōn Boiōtōn)
CapitalThebes (with federal sanctuary at Onchestus)
Periodc. 550 BCE – 171 BCE (with interruptions)
Chief magistratesBoeotarchs (7 or 11)

The Boeotian League (Greek: τὸ κοινὸν τῶν Βοιωτῶν) was a federal league (koinon) of the city-states of Boeotia in ancient Greece, normally dominated by Thebes. It is among the earliest and best-documented examples of a federal state in the Greek world, providing proportional representation of its member districts in federal councils.

Constitution

Our fullest description comes from the Hellenica Oxyrhynchia, describing the League as reorganized after 395 BCE. Boeotia was divided into eleven districts (merē), each contributing one boeotarch (federal general and chief magistrate), 60 federal councillors, judges, and fixed contingents of troops—1,000 hoplites and 100 cavalry per district. The federal council of 660 members sat in four sections, each of which prepared business ratified by the others. Membership in both local and federal government was subject to a property qualification.

History

A Boeotian federation existed by the late 6th century BCE, issuing coinage with the distinctive Boeotian shield. The League medized ambiguously at Plataea (479 BCE), was dissolved by Athens during its occupation of Boeotia (457–447 BCE), and again by Sparta under the King's Peace (386 BCE). Restored in 378/7 BCE, it underwrote the Theban hegemony: restructured on a more democratic basis with seven boeotarchs, it fielded the armies that won at Leuctra (371 BCE). After Macedon's victory at Chaeronea (338 BCE) the League became an instrument of Macedonian control, survived in reduced form into the Hellenistic era, and was finally dissolved by Rome in 171 BCE.
