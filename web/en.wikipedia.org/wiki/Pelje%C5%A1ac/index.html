Pelješac is a peninsula in southern Dalmatia, Croatia, the second largest peninsula in the country after Istria. It extends about 65 kilometres into the Adriatic Sea and is administratively part of Dubrovnik-Neretva County.

Geography and history

The peninsula is mountainous and narrow, connected to the mainland at the isthmus of Ston, where the medieval Republic of Ragusa built the great Walls of Ston to protect valuable salt pans that are still worked today. Towns include Ston, Orebić — once a home port of sea captains — Trpanj and Janjina.

Wine and the bridge

Pelješac is famous for its red wines from the Plavac Mali grape, grown on steep sun-baked slopes at Dingač and Postup, among Croatia's most celebrated appellations. Tourism, oyster farming at Mali Ston and fishing are also important. In July 2022 the Pelješac Bridge opened, linking the peninsula — and with it the Dubrovnik exclave — directly to the rest of Croatia, bypassing the short coastal strip of Bosnia and Herzegovina at Neum.
