Seabed

The seabed (also known as the seafloor or ocean floor) is the bottom of the world's oceans. Its topography includes the continental shelf, slope and rise, abyssal plains, mid-ocean ridges, oceanic trenches and seamounts. The seabed is shaped by plate tectonics, sedimentation, currents, volcanism and erosion.

Structure and sediments

Oceanic crust beneath the seabed is basaltic and young by geological standards, continually created at mid-ocean ridges and consumed at subduction zones. It is draped with sediments — terrigenous material near continents, biogenic oozes (calcareous and siliceous) in productive waters, and red clays in the deep ocean.

Habitat and resources

The seabed supports the benthic zone, including cold-water corals, hydrothermal vent communities and abyssal fauna. It is also a source of resources such as offshore oil and gas, sand and gravel, and polymetallic nodules, and is governed by international law including UNCLOS and the International Seabed Authority.
