Susan Calvin

Dr. Susan Calvin is a fictional character in Isaac Asimov's Robot series of science fiction stories. She is the chief robopsychologist at U.S. Robots and Mechanical Men, Inc., the major manufacturer of robots in Asimov's 21st century, and serves as the central human character in many of the stories, diagnosing and resolving problems arising from the robots' positronic brains and their interpretation of the Three Laws of Robotics.

Character

Calvin is portrayed as a cold, sharp, fiercely intelligent woman who often seems more comfortable with robots than with people. Born in 1982, she joined U.S. Robots in 2003 after completing a doctorate and remained with the company for decades, becoming the foremost expert on robot psychology — a discipline she effectively founded. In the framing device of the collection I, Robot (1950), an elderly Calvin recounts her career to a reporter.

Appearances

Calvin appears in stories including "Liar!", in which a telepathic robot's well-meant deceptions cause emotional harm; "Evidence", where she is consulted over whether a politician is a robot; "The Evitable Conflict"; and the later novel Robots and Empire, in which she appears in historical recordings. Harlan Ellison's unproduced screenplay of I, Robot made her the central character, and she was played by Bridget Moynahan in the 2004 film I, Robot, though the film version differs greatly from Asimov's character.
