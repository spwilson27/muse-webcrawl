Ahmed III

Ahmed III (Ottoman Turkish: احمد ثالث; 30 December 1673 – 1 July 1736) was Sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1703 to 1730. His reign, particularly its second half, is known in Turkish history as the Tulip Period, a time of peace, cultural flowering and cautious openness to European influences, which ended with his deposition in the Patrona Halil rebellion.

Reign

Ahmed came to the throne after the Edirne Incident deposed his brother Mustafa II. Early in his reign Charles XII of Sweden, defeated by Russia at Poltava (1709), took refuge in Ottoman territory; the empire briefly warred with Russia, regaining Azov by the Treaty of Pruth (1711), but lost the Morea and much of Serbia and Wallachia to Austria by the Treaty of Passarowitz (1718).

Tulip Period and fall

Under Grand Vizier Nevşehirli Damat Ibrahim Pasha (1718–1730) the court pursued peace, diplomacy and cultural patronage: the first Ottoman Turkish printing press (İbrahim Müteferrika, 1727), embassies to Paris, tulip gardens and pleasure palaces along the Bosphorus such as Sadabad. Extravagance amid economic hardship provoked the Patrona Halil rebellion of 1730; Ahmed surrendered the grand vizier to the mob and abdicated in favour of his nephew Mahmud I, dying in confinement in 1736. A poet and calligrapher himself, Ahmed left notable fountains in Constantinople, including the Fountain of Ahmed III outside Topkapı Palace.
