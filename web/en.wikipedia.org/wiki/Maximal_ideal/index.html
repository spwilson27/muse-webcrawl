Maximal ideal
Field: Ring theory (abstract algebra)

In mathematics, more specifically in ring theory, a maximal ideal is an ideal that is maximal (with respect to set inclusion) amongst all proper ideals. In other words, an ideal I of a ring R is maximal if I is not equal to R itself and there is no other ideal strictly between I and R.

Properties

A central characterization: an ideal I is maximal if and only if the quotient ring R/I is a field. Maximal ideals are always prime ideals. By Krull's theorem, every unital ring with a proper ideal has at least one maximal ideal, a result whose proof uses Zorn's lemma.

Examples

In the ring of integers, the maximal ideals are the principal ideals (p) generated by prime numbers. In a polynomial ring over a field, ideals generated by irreducible polynomials are maximal in one variable; the Nullstellensatz describes maximal ideals of polynomial rings over algebraically closed fields in terms of points.
