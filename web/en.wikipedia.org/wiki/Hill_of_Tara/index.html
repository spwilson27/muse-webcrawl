The Hill of Tara (Irish: Teamhair) is an elongated limestone ridge near the River Boyne in County Meath, Ireland, famous in Irish tradition as the seat of the High Kings of Ireland. The hilltop is covered with archaeological monuments spanning some five thousand years.

Monuments

The oldest monument is the Mound of the Hostages (Duma na nGiall), a Neolithic passage tomb dating to about 3400–2800 BC, which yielded thousands of cremated burials. Later features include the great circular enclosures of Ráth na Ríg (the Fort of the Kings) and the paired earthworks of the Forradh and Tech Cormaic, the Banqueting Hall (a long rectangular earthwork once interpreted as a ceremonial avenue), and the ringfort Ráth Laoghaire. On the summit stands the Lia Fáil (Stone of Destiny), a standing stone said to have roared under the feet of the rightful king.

History and tradition

In early Irish literature Tara was the ritual centre of kingship, associated with the Feast of Tara (Feis Temro) and with Saint Patrick's confrontation with King Lóegaire in the 5th century. Its kingship was ceremonial long after its political importance faded. In 1843 Daniel O'Connell held one of his "monster meetings" for Repeal of the Union on the hill, drawing hundreds of thousands. Controversy in the 2000s over the route of the M3 motorway through the nearby Gabhra valley made Tara a focus of heritage-protection campaigning.
