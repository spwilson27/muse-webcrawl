Mika Häkkinen

Mika Pauli Häkkinen (born 28 September 1968 in Vantaa, Finland) is a Finnish former racing driver who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1998 and 1999 with McLaren-Mercedes. Nicknamed "The Flying Finn", he is regarded as one of Michael Schumacher's greatest rivals; Schumacher himself described Häkkinen as the competitor he most respected.

Career

Häkkinen progressed through karting and junior formulae, winning the British Formula 3 championship in 1990, and joined Lotus in Formula One in 1991. After two points-scoring seasons he moved to McLaren as a test driver in 1993 and was promoted to the race team when Michael Andretti departed late that season, famously outqualifying Ayrton Senna on his debut for the team.

A near-fatal crash in practice for the 1995 Australian Grand Prix left him in a coma; an emergency tracheotomy saved his life, and he recovered to race again. His first win came at the 1997 European Grand Prix at Jerez. With the Adrian Newey-designed McLaren MP4/13, he dominated 1998, winning eight races and the title at Suzuka, and retained the championship in 1999 after a close battle with Eddie Irvine. He finished runner-up to Schumacher in 2000, highlighted by his celebrated overtaking move on Schumacher at Spa. After a sabbatical in 2002, he announced his retirement from Formula One; he later raced in the DTM (2005–2007).

Legacy

Häkkinen won 20 Grands Prix and took 26 pole positions. He remains active as a driver manager, ambassador and commentator, including work with McLaren.
