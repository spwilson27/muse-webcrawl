Yama (Hinduism)

Yama (Sanskrit: यम) is the Hindu god of death and justice, ruler of the underworld realm of the ancestors, and one of the Lokapalas, guardian of the southern direction. In the Vedas he is the first mortal to die, who thereby became king of the departed; in later tradition he judges the dead according to their karma, assisted by his scribe Chitragupta.

Mythology

Yama is the son of the sun god Surya (Vivasvat) and twin brother of the river goddess Yamuna. He rides a buffalo and carries a noose (pāśa) with which he draws the soul from the body, and a mace. His two four-eyed dogs, Sharvara and Shyama, guard the path to his realm, Yamaloka. In the Katha Upanishad, the boy Nachiketa visits Yama and receives from him the teaching on the self and immortality. In the Mahabharata, Yama tests Yudhishthira in the form of a yaksha and, disguised as a dog, follows him to heaven's gate; Yudhishthira's refusal to abandon the dog reveals his virtue.

Judgment and legacy

Yama's epithet Dharmaraja, "king of dharma," stresses his role as impartial judge rather than a figure of evil. His cult and concept spread widely: he appears as Yanluo Wang in Chinese religion, as Emma-ō in Japan, and in Buddhist and Jain traditions as judge of the dead.
