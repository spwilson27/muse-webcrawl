Term life insurance

Term life insurance or term assurance is life insurance that provides coverage at a fixed rate of payments for a limited period of time, the relevant term. After that period expires, coverage at the previous rate of premiums is no longer guaranteed and the client must either forgo coverage or potentially obtain further coverage with different payments or conditions. If the insured dies during the term, the death benefit will be paid to the beneficiary. Term insurance is typically the least expensive way to purchase a substantial death benefit per dollar of premium.

A term life insurance policy

Features

Policies are commonly sold with terms of 10, 20 or 30 years. Most term policies have no savings or investment component, unlike permanent life insurance (such as whole life or universal life), and therefore build no cash value; their sole value is the guaranteed death benefit. Common variations include:

  • Level term — the death benefit and (in level-premium policies) the premium stay the same throughout the term.
  • Annual renewable term — renewed each year with premiums rising with age.
  • Decreasing term — the death benefit declines over the term, often used to cover a mortgage balance.
  • Return of premium — premiums are refunded if the insured survives the term, at considerably higher cost.

Many policies include a conversion privilege allowing the holder to convert to a permanent policy without new medical underwriting.

Use

Because it is inexpensive during the working years, term insurance is widely recommended to cover temporary needs such as income replacement for dependants, mortgage repayment and children's education costs during the years when a family's expenses and debts are highest.
