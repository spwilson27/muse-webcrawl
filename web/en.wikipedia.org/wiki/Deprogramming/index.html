Deprogramming

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Deprogramming refers to measures intended to persuade a person in a controversial new religious movement (often called a cult) to abandon their beliefs and leave the group. The practice emerged in the United States in the 1970s, most closely associated with Ted Patrick, often called the "father of deprogramming". Early deprogramming frequently involved the involuntary abduction and detention of the member, sometimes under court-ordered conservatorships obtained by parents — methods that generated kidnapping prosecutions, civil suits and lasting controversy.

History

As movements such as the Unification Church, the Children of God and the Hare Krishnas recruited young adults in the 1970s, some families hired deprogrammers to remove members by force and subject them to intensive counter-indoctrination, presenting information critical of the group and its leader. Supporters argued that members were victims of "brainwashing" or mind control who could not leave voluntarily; critics, including many sociologists of religion, disputed the brainwashing model and condemned deprogramming as a violation of religious freedom.

Legal issues

Several deprogrammers were convicted of kidnapping or unlawful imprisonment, and civil judgments were awarded against practitioners and organisations such as the Cult Awareness Network — most prominently after the 1991 abduction of Jason Scott, which led to a multimillion-dollar judgment and CAN's bankruptcy in 1996. Courts in the United States generally refused to extend parental conservatorships over adult children for deprogramming purposes.

Exit counselling

Largely because of these legal risks, involuntary deprogramming in the United States was mostly replaced in the 1980s and 1990s by voluntary exit counselling, in which the member agrees to meet counsellors and family, remains free to leave, and evaluates information about the group. Similar forced "deprogramming" practices have been reported in other countries, including cases in Japan involving members of the Unification Church.
