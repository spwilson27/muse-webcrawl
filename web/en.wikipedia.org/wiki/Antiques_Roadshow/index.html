Antiques Roadshow

Antiques Roadshow is a British television programme produced by the BBC in which antiques appraisers travel to various regions of the United Kingdom (and occasionally abroad) to appraise antiques brought in by local people. It has been running since 1979, growing out of a 1977 documentary, The Great Antiques Hunt. The format has been licensed around the world, including a long-running American version on PBS that began in 1997.

Format

Members of the public bring objects — paintings, furniture, ceramics, jewellery, toys, militaria and memorabilia — to a large venue, often a stately home or landmark. Specialist appraisers discuss each object's history and craftsmanship on camera before revealing an estimated auction value, often to the owner's surprise. Pieces of exceptional interest or value feature prominently; the programme has uncovered lost works of art, rare Fabergé items and a £1 million scale model of Antony Gormley's Angel of the North.

Presenters and experts

The British series has been presented by Michael Aspel (2000–2008) and Fiona Bruce (since 2008), among earlier hosts including Arthur Negus and Hugh Scully. Its roster of experts has included figures such as Eric Knowles, Judith Miller, Lars Tharp and the late Henry Sandon.

Impact

The programme is one of the BBC's longest-running factual series and has popularised the valuation-day format, spawning imitations in many countries.
