Whirlwind

A whirlwind is a weather phenomenon in which a vortex of wind — a vertically oriented rotating column of air — forms due to instabilities and turbulence created by heating and flow gradients. Whirlwinds occur all over the world and in any season.

Types

The major types of whirlwind include tornadoes, waterspouts, and landspouts, which descend from convective clouds or arise from intense surface heating. Minor whirlwinds include dust devils, steam devils, and snow devils, which form at the surface under clear skies. Gustnadoes are short-lived whirlwinds associated with gust fronts.

Formation

Minor whirlwinds form when localized pockets of superheated air near the surface rise rapidly through cooler air above, stretching and intensifying background rotation. Major whirlwinds, such as tornadoes, form within large storm systems where wind shear and buoyancy organize rotation on a much larger scale.
