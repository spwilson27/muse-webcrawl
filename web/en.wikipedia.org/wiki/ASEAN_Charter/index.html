ASEAN Charter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Charter of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, commonly known as the ASEAN Charter, is the constitutive document of ASEAN, signed by the heads of government of its ten member states in Singapore on 20 November 2007 and in force from 15 December 2008. It gave ASEAN legal personality, codified its purposes and principles (including the “ASEAN Way” of consensus and non-interference, alongside new language on democracy, rule of law and human rights), and structured its organs: the Summit, Coordinating and Community Councils, a secretary-general and secretariat in Jakarta, and the ASEAN Intergovernmental Commission on Human Rights (AICHR, established 2009).

The Charter was drafted by an Eminent Persons Group and a High Level Task Force in the fortieth-anniversary reform moment, partly to make ASEAN's commitments credible for the ASEAN Community project (launched 2015). Critics note its principles pull against each other — human-rights commitments bounded by non-interference, consensus decision-making that slows responses (as in the South China Sea and Myanmar crises) — while supporters credit it with institutionalising Southeast Asia's central diplomatic forum.
