Southern Foodways Alliance

The Southern Foodways Alliance (SFA) is a non-profit organization based at the University of Mississippi in Oxford, Mississippi, dedicated to documenting, studying and exploring the diverse food cultures of the American South. It was founded in 1999 by a group of chefs, writers and academics including John T. Edge, who served as its director for more than two decades, and John Egerton, whose book Southern Food helped inspire its creation.

Work

The SFA collects oral histories — now numbering well over a thousand — chronicling the lives of pitmasters, farmers, oyster harvesters, bakers, cooks and restaurateurs across the South. It publishes the quarterly journal Gravy and produces the Gravy podcast, films, and books, and its work treats barbecue, fried chicken, tamales in the Mississippi Delta, Vietnamese Gulf Coast fishing communities and many other traditions as serious subjects for cultural scholarship.

Events

The Alliance's flagship event is its annual fall symposium in Oxford, Mississippi, which brings together scholars, chefs and the public for lectures and shared meals, alongside tasting events and field trips in Southern cities. The SFA also presents awards recognising contributions to Southern food culture, including the Ruth Fertel Keeper of the Flame Award and the Craig Claiborne Lifetime Achievement Award.

Mission

The SFA describes its mission as setting a common table where Southern food cultures are documented and celebrated, with attention to questions of race, class, gender and immigration that shape the region's tables. It is affiliated with the Center for the Study of Southern Culture at the University of Mississippi.
