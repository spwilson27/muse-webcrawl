The Walkway over the Hudson (also known as the Poughkeepsie Bridge or the Poughkeepsie Railroad Bridge) is a steel cantilever bridge spanning the Hudson River between Poughkeepsie, New York, on the east bank and Highland, New York, on the west bank. Built as a double-track railroad bridge in 1889, it was the longest bridge in the world at the time and a vital freight link between New England and the rest of the United States.

After a 1974 fire damaged the deck, the bridge fell out of use. It was converted into a pedestrian walkway and opened on October 3, 2009, as the Walkway over the Hudson State Historic Park. At 1.28 miles (2.06 km) long and 212 feet (65 m) above the river, it is the longest pedestrian bridge in the world.

History

The bridge was built by the Manhattan Bridge Building Company for the Poughkeepsie Bridge Company and opened to rail traffic on January 1, 1889. For decades it carried heavy freight trains, including the Maybrook Line of the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad, allowing goods to cross the Hudson south of Albany. The 1974 fire ended rail service, and ownership passed through several hands before the nonprofit Walkway over the Hudson organisation raised funds for the conversion.

The park

The walkway connects to the Hudson Valley Rail Trail on the west and the Dutchess Rail Trail on the east, forming part of the Empire State Trail. It draws hundreds of thousands of visitors annually and hosts events such as the Walkway Marathon and July Fourth fireworks viewings.
