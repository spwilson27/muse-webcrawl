Post-postmodernism

Post-postmodernism is a wide-ranging set of developments in critical theory, philosophy, architecture, art, literature, and culture which are emerging from and reacting to postmodernism.

Overview

Since the 1990s, a number of theorists have argued that the postmodern era has ended or is ending, and that a successor sensibility is taking shape. Proposals for naming this successor include metamodernism, postmillennialism, digimodernism, performativism, and the New Sincerity. Common threads among these accounts are a renewed interest in sincerity, earnestness, authenticity, and meaning, combined with an oscillation between modernist commitment and postmodernist irony rather than a simple return to modernism.

History of the term

The term "post-postmodernism" began appearing in the 1990s. In 1995 the landscape architect and urban planner Tom Turner proposed that the built environment was moving beyond postmodernism toward a synthesis he called "post-postmodernism," welcoming a return to pattern and meaning. Literary critics and cultural theorists subsequently applied the phrase to fiction, film, and digital culture that appeared to move past postmodern irony and deconstruction.

Characteristics

Post-postmodern works are often described as reconstructive: after deconstruction, they attempt to rebuild narrative, emotion, and ethical commitment. Metamodernism, as articulated by Timotheus Vermeulen and Robin van den Akker in 2010, describes a "structure of feeling" that oscillates between modern enthusiasm and postmodern irony. Other accounts emphasize the impact of digital technology, social media, and networked communication on artistic production and reception.

Criticism

Skeptics note that the proliferation of competing labels suggests the successor paradigm has not yet consolidated, and that many works described as post-postmodern still employ postmodern techniques. Some scholars question whether periodizing labels remain useful at all.
