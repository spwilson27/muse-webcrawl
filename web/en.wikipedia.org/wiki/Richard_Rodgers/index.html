Richard Rodgers (1902–1979), composer of more than 900 songs

Richard Charles Rodgers (28 June 1902 – 30 December 1979) was an American composer who worked primarily in musical theatre. With 43 Broadway musicals and over 900 songs to his credit, Rodgers was one of the most significant American composers of the 20th century, and his compositions have had a significant impact on popular music.

Rodgers was the first person to win all of what are considered the top American entertainment awards in theatre, film, recording and television — an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony (an EGOT) — and one of only two (with Marvin Hamlisch) to win a Pulitzer Prize as well.

Rodgers and Hart

Rodgers met lyricist Lorenz Hart in 1919, and over 24 years they created a string of hit musicals including A Connecticut Yankee (1927), On Your Toes (1936), Babes in Arms (1937), The Boys from Syracuse (1938) and Pal Joey (1940). Their songs — including "My Funny Valentine", "The Lady Is a Tramp", "Manhattan", and "Blue Moon" — became standards. The partnership ended with Hart's declining health and death in 1943.

Rodgers and Hammerstein

With Oscar Hammerstein II, Rodgers produced a new, integrated form of musical play beginning with Oklahoma! (1943), followed by Carousel (1945), South Pacific (1949), The King and I (1951), Cinderella for television (1957), and The Sound of Music (1959). Rodgers regarded Carousel as his favourite of their scores. The team also founded a pioneering publishing and production company that reshaped Broadway business practice.

After Hammerstein

After Hammerstein's death in 1960, Rodgers composed both words and music for No Strings (1962) and collaborated with lyricists including Stephen Sondheim (Do I Hear a Waltz?, 1965), Martin Charnin, and Sheldon Harnick. He died in New York City in 1979, aged 77.
