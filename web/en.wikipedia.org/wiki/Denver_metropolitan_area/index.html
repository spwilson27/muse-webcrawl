Denver metropolitan area

The Denver metropolitan area, centred on the City and County of Denver, Colorado, is the most populous metropolitan region of Colorado. The U.S. Office of Management and Budget defines the Denver–Aurora–Lakewood Metropolitan Statistical Area as comprising ten counties along the Front Range and the northeastern edge of the South Platte River valley; it had a population of about 2.97 million at the 2020 census, ranking among the twenty most populous metro areas in the United States. The wider Denver–Aurora combined statistical area, which adds the Boulder and Greeley areas, exceeds 3.6 million.

Geography and communities

The metro area sits just east of the foothills of the Rocky Mountains on the high plains, at elevations around a mile above sea level. Major constituent cities besides Denver include Aurora, Lakewood, Arvada, Westminster, Thornton, Centennial, and Highlands Ranch. Regional cooperation is coordinated by the Denver Regional Council of Governments (DRCOG), and public transport — buses and an expanding light-rail and commuter-rail network — is provided by the Regional Transportation District (RTD).

Economy

The regional economy is anchored by telecommunications, aerospace and defence (with major facilities in the southern suburbs), energy, finance, healthcare, and a large federal presence. Denver International Airport, one of the world's largest airports by area, opened in 1995 northeast of the city and is a principal hub for United, Southwest, and Frontier Airlines.
