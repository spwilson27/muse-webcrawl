Throw (martial arts)

Throw
Focus: Grappling
Used in: Judo, wrestling, jujutsu, aikido, sambo, shuai jiao
Japanese: nage-waza (投げ技)
Related: Takedown, sweep, sacrifice throw

A throw is a grappling technique in martial arts that involves off-balancing or lifting an opponent and throwing them to the ground. Throws are a central component of arts such as judo, wrestling, Japanese jujutsu, aikido, sambo, and Chinese shuai jiao. Unlike a takedown, in which the attacker may also go to the ground, a throw is generally executed so that the thrower remains standing, or lands in a dominant position, while the opponent is projected through the air or driven to the mat.

A hip throw (koshi-waza), among the most widely taught throwing techniques.

Mechanics

Most throws combine three elements, formalised in judo as kuzushi (breaking the opponent's balance), tsukuri (fitting into position), and kake (execution). The thrower uses leverage, rotation, and the opponent's own momentum rather than strength alone: the opponent's centre of mass is moved beyond their base of support and then directed to the ground.

Types of throws

Judo classifies standing throwing techniques (nage-waza) into hand techniques (te-waza), hip techniques (koshi-waza), foot and leg techniques (ashi-waza), and sacrifice techniques (sutemi-waza), in which the thrower falls to the ground to complete the throw. Wrestling recognises throws such as the suplex and hip toss; aikido emphasises projection throws that redirect an attacker's energy; shuai jiao favours fast jacket throws combined with kicking the supporting leg.

Safety

Because throws can be dangerous, practitioners first learn ukemi — breakfall techniques for landing safely — and train on padded mats (tatami). Throws that spike an opponent on the head or neck are prohibited in most rule sets.
