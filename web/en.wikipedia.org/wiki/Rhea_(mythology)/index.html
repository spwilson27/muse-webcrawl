In Greek mythology, Rhea or Rheia (Ancient Greek: Ῥέα) is one of the Titans, daughter of Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth), sister and wife of Cronus, and mother of the first generation of Olympian gods: Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, Poseidon and Zeus. She is a mother goddess figure associated with fertility, motherhood and the mountains of Crete.

Mythology

Cronus, warned that he would be overthrown by his own son, swallowed each of his children at birth. When Rhea bore Zeus, she hid the infant in a cave on Mount Ida in Crete — where he was raised by nymphs and the goat Amalthea — and instead gave Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes, which he swallowed. When Zeus grew to manhood he forced Cronus to disgorge his siblings, and together they overthrew the Titans in the Titanomachy.

Cult and identification

Rhea was worshipped especially in Crete and in Asia Minor, where she merged with the Phrygian mother goddess Cybele; in Roman religion her counterpart was Ops, consort of Saturn. Her name was sometimes connected in antiquity with the Greek word for "flow" (rheo), and Plato suggested an etymology linking Rhea with rivers and springs as sources of generation. A moon of Saturn and the flightless birds called rheas are named after her.
