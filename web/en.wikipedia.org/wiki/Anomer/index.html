Anomer

Anomer
Type: Stereoisomer (epimer)
Applies to: Cyclic sugars and glycosides
Forms: α (alpha) and β (beta)

In carbohydrate chemistry, an anomer is a type of stereoisomer, or epimer, found in the cyclic forms of sugars. Anomers differ in configuration at the anomeric carbon — the carbon atom, derived from the carbonyl group of the open-chain form, that bears the ring oxygen's neighbouring hydroxyl group in the ring structure.

Anomerisation

When a monosaccharide such as glucose cyclises, the new stereocentre at the anomeric carbon can adopt two configurations. In the standard Haworth projection of a D-sugar, the α-anomer has the anomeric hydroxyl group on the opposite side of the ring from the CH2OH substituent, while in the β-anomer it is on the same side. In solution, the ring opens and recloses, interconverting the anomers — a process underlying mutarotation, in which the optical rotation of a freshly prepared sugar solution drifts to an equilibrium value.

The anomeric effect

Contrary to simple steric expectations, electronegative substituents at the anomeric centre often prefer the axial position. This stabilisation, known as the anomeric effect, is attributed to hyperconjugative donation from a ring oxygen lone pair into the antibonding orbital of the axial bond.

Significance

Anomeric configuration strongly affects the chemistry and biology of sugars: the α and β linkages in starch and cellulose, respectively, determine whether the polysaccharide is a digestible energy store or a structural material, because enzymes are specific for one configuration.
