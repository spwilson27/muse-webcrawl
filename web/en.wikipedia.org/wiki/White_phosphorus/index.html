White phosphorus
Formula: P4
Appearance: White, waxy solid
Melting point: 44.15 °C
Hazards: Pyrophoric, highly toxic

White phosphorus is an allotrope of phosphorus consisting of tetrahedral P4 molecules. It is a translucent, waxy solid that glows in the dark (chemiluminescence) and ignites spontaneously in air at about 30 °C, so it is stored under water. It is produced industrially by heating phosphate rock with coke and silica in an electric furnace.

Properties and hazards

White phosphorus is extremely toxic; ingestion can cause fatal liver damage, and chronic occupational exposure historically caused necrosis of the jaw ("phossy jaw") among match workers. Its combustion produces dense white clouds of phosphorus pentoxide, which is used in smoke screening.

Uses

Most white phosphorus is converted to phosphoric acid and other phosphorus compounds. In munitions it is used for smoke screens, illumination, and incendiary effects; its use against personnel is restricted by international humanitarian law conventions on incendiary weapons.
