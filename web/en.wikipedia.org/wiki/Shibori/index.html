Shibori

Shibori (しぼり / 絞り) is a Japanese manual resist dyeing technique that produces patterns on fabric. The word comes from the verb shiboru, "to wring, squeeze or press". Cloth is shaped by binding, stitching, folding, twisting, compressing or capping before dyeing, so that the resisted areas remain undyed or lighter.

Techniques

Recognised families of technique include kanoko (bound resist, similar to tie-dye), miura (looped binding), kumo (pleated and bound, giving spider-web patterns), nui (stitched resist), arashi (pole-wrapping, producing diagonal storm-like patterns), and itajime (shape-resist using clamped blocks).

History

Shibori has been practised in Japan since at least the eighth century; surviving examples are preserved in the Shōsō-in collection at Nara. It is traditionally associated with indigo dyeing on cotton, silk and hemp, and with production centres such as Arimatsu and Narumi. Similar resist techniques exist worldwide, from Indonesian plangi to West African traditions.
