Salim Chishti

Salim Chishti (1478–1572) was a Sufi saint of the Chishti Order in India during the Mughal era. The emperor Akbar honoured him greatly after crediting the saint's blessing with the birth of his heir, who was named Salim — the future emperor Jahangir — after him.

Born1478, Delhi
Died1572, Fatehpur Sikri
Venerated inIslam (Sufism)
Major shrineTomb of Salim Chishti, Fatehpur Sikri

Life

A descendant of the Chishti lineage of Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti of Ajmer, Salim Chishti travelled widely in Arabia and Central Asia before settling at Sikri, west of Agra, where he lived in a cave and attracted disciples.

Akbar and Fatehpur Sikri

Akbar, childless, sought the saint's blessing in 1568; after sons were born, Akbar built a new capital, Fatehpur Sikri, around the saint's hospice, with a grand mosque and, after Salim's death in 1572, a white marble tomb for him in the mosque courtyard — one of the finest examples of Mughal architecture, later famed for its jali screens. Devotees, especially those praying for children, still tie threads to the tomb's screens.
