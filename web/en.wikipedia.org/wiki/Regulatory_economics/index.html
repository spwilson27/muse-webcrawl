Regulatory economics

Regulatory economics is the economics of regulation. It is the application of law by government or regulatory agencies for various purposes, including remedying market failure, protecting the environment, centrally managing an economy, and enriching well-connected firms (rent seeking). Regulation is commonly defined as the imposition of constraints by government upon otherwise voluntary activity.

Theories of regulation

Public interest theory holds that regulation is supplied in response to public demand to correct inefficient or inequitable market practices. Capture theory, associated with George Stigler's 1971 economic theory of regulation, argues that regulation is instead acquired by organised interest groups and designed to benefit them. Incentive regulation — such as price-cap (RPI − X) regulation of utilities — seeks to give regulated firms incentives to reduce costs while protecting consumers.

Fields

Important applications include the regulation of natural monopolies (electricity, water, railways), financial regulation, environmental regulation, and occupational licensing.
