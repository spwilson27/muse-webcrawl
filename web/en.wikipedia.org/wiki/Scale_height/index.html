Scale height

In atmospheric and planetary science, the scale height is the vertical distance over which the pressure of an atmosphere decreases by a factor of e (the base of natural logarithms, approximately 2.718). It measures how "extended" an atmosphere is: a large scale height means a gradually thinning atmosphere, a small one means a compact atmosphere that drops off rapidly with altitude.

Scale height
Symbol: H
Formula: H = kBT / (mg)
Earth value: ≈ 8.5 km
Depends on: Temperature, molecular mass, gravity

Definition and derivation

For an ideal gas in hydrostatic equilibrium at temperature T, pressure varies with height z approximately as P(z) = P0 · e−z/H, where the scale height is H = kBT/(mg): kB is the Boltzmann constant, m the average mass of an air molecule, and g the acceleration due to gravity. Because temperature varies with altitude, real atmospheres have a scale height that changes with height; for Earth its average value is about 8.5 km in the lower atmosphere.

Other planets

Scale heights vary widely across the Solar System. Venus, with its hot, heavy CO2 atmosphere, has a scale height near its surface of about 15 km; Mars, cold and thin, about 11 km; Titan, with a cold nitrogen atmosphere and weak gravity, about 20–50 km, giving it a very extended haze layer. For gas giants such as Jupiter the scale height in the upper atmosphere is roughly 27 km. Scale height is central to interpreting stellar occultations, the transit spectra of exoplanets (a larger scale height produces stronger spectral features), and models of atmospheric escape.
