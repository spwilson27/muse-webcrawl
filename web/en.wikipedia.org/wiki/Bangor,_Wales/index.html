Bangor is a cathedral city and community in Gwynedd, North Wales, near the eastern end of the Menai Strait opposite Anglesey. It is one of the smallest cities in the United Kingdom and one of only six places classed as a city in Wales. The city is dominated by Bangor University, whose students form a large share of the population during term time.

History

The origins of the city date to the founding of a monastic establishment by Saint Deiniol in the 6th century; the name Bangor derives from a Welsh word for a wattled enclosure. Bangor Cathedral stands on the site of Deiniol's monastery and claims the longest continuous use as a place of Christian worship in Britain. The diocese of Bangor is among the oldest in Wales. The city grew in the 19th century with the slate trade of the nearby Penrhyn quarry, shipped through Port Penrhyn, and with tourism along the North Wales coast.

Landmarks

Garth Pier, opened in 1896, is one of the longest piers in Wales. Other landmarks include the cathedral, the university's Main Arts Building, Pontio arts centre, and the Roman camp remains at nearby locations. Bangor railway station lies on the North Wales Coast line.
