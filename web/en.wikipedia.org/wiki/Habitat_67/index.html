Habitat 67

Habitat 67, or simply Habitat, is a housing complex at Cité du Havre, on the Saint Lawrence River in Montreal, Canada, designed by Israeli-Canadian architect Moshe Safdie based on his thesis at McGill University. It was built as a pavilion for Expo 67, the World's Fair held from April to October 1967, and is considered an architectural landmark and one of the most recognisable buildings in Canada.

Design

The complex consists of 354 identical prefabricated concrete modules — "boxes" — arranged in various combinations and connected by steel cables. Originally intended to comprise 158 residences, cost overruns reduced the project; the finished structure contains about 146 units formed from one to four boxes each. Every unit has a private roof terrace, and the arrangement gives each residence the openness and light of a suburban home within a dense urban block.

Legacy

Habitat 67 launched Safdie's career and became an emblem of Expo 67's "Man and His World" theme. It remains a sought-after address and is frequently cited as a pioneering experiment in prefabricated modular housing, though its high construction cost limited direct imitation.
