Avolition

Avolition is a psychological and psychiatric symptom characterised by a severe lack of motivation to initiate and persist in self-directed, purposeful activities. It is one of the core negative symptoms of schizophrenia, and also occurs in major depressive disorder and other conditions. People experiencing avolition may neglect personal hygiene, withdraw from work or school, and show little interest in social or recreational activities, despite often wanting to participate.

Distinguishing features

Avolition differs from simple laziness or fatigue: it reflects a deficit in the motivation system rather than a choice or lack of energy. It is also distinguished from anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure) and from aboulia, a related neurological condition involving loss of will. Neurologically, avolition has been linked to dysfunction in dopaminergic reward circuits, particularly involving the prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum.

Assessment and treatment

Clinicians assess avolition using negative-symptom rating scales such as the PANSS, SANS and BNSS. It is among the negative symptoms least responsive to antipsychotic medication; management emphasises psychosocial rehabilitation, behavioural activation, cognitive remediation, and treatment of co-occurring depression. Avolition is a major predictor of poor functional outcomes in schizophrenia.
