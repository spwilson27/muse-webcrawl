Liu Bannong (Chinese: 劉半農; 13 May 1891 – 14 July 1934), born Liu Fu (劉復), was a Chinese poet, linguist, translator and educator, and a leading figure of the May Fourth and New Culture movements. He taught at Peking University, where he worked alongside Hu Shih, Chen Duxiu and Qian Xuantong, and edited the influential journal New Youth (Xin Qingnian).

Career

Born in Jiangyin, Jiangsu, Liu largely educated himself and began his career in Shanghai journalism and publishing, writing fiction and translating Western literature (he translated works by Turgenev and others, among the first translations of Western fiction into Chinese). In 1917 Chen Duxiu invited him to join the faculty of Peking University and the editorial circle of New Youth, where Liu campaigned for vernacular literature (baihua) against classical Chinese, attacked the conservative "Mandarin Duck and Butterfly" school of popular fiction, and famously staged — with Qian Xuantong — a fabricated correspondence of letters to the editor (the "double act", 1918) to provoke debate.

Liu is widely credited with introducing into modern written Chinese the distinct feminine third-person pronoun 她 (tā, "she"), proposed in his 1920 essay "On the question of the character 'ta'", which became standard usage.

Phonetics

From 1920 to 1925 Liu studied in London and Paris, earning a doctorate in experimental phonetics at the University of Paris. His dissertation on the tones of Chinese was a pioneering application of laboratory phonetics to Chinese dialects. Returning to Peking University, he founded a phonetics laboratory, made early recordings of Chinese dialects and folk songs, and wrote the poetry collection Wasp Waist (揚鞭集, 1926) and the much-loved poem-song "Teach Me How Not to Miss Him" (教我如何不想她, 1926), set to music by Zhao Yuanren.

Death and legacy

Liu died in Beijing on 14 July 1934, aged 43, of an acute illness contracted during a fieldwork trip to record dialects in the northwest. Lu Xun wrote a celebrated memorial essay for him. His contributions to modern Chinese poetry, the vernacular movement, and the scientific study of Chinese phonetics have kept him a central figure in accounts of China's literary modernisation.
