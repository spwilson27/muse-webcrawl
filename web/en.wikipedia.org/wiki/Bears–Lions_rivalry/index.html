Bears–Lions rivalry

The Bears–Lions rivalry is a National Football League (NFL) rivalry between the Chicago Bears and the Detroit Lions. The two franchises, both members of the NFC North division, first met in 1930, when the Lions were based in Ohio as the Portsmouth Spartans, making this one of the oldest continuously played rivalries in the NFL. The teams have met at least twice every season since the Lions relocated to Detroit in 1934.

History

The rivalry began on 21 September 1930, when the Spartans defeated the Bears. In 1932 the two teams met in the first playoff game in NFL history — an indoor championship playoff at Chicago Stadium, arranged after the teams finished tied in the standings; the Bears won 9–0, and the game's success helped prompt the creation of an annual NFL championship game.

The Bears dominated long stretches of the series, particularly during their championship runs of the 1940s and the Mike Ditka era of the 1980s. Detroit's best periods included the 1950s, when the Lions won three NFL championships, and the Barry Sanders years of the 1990s. In 2011, the teams played a notable game in Chicago during which a brawl resulted in multiple ejections.

Thanksgiving tradition

The Lions have hosted a game on Thanksgiving Day since 1934, and the Bears have frequently been the visiting team in that fixture, adding a recurring national stage to the rivalry. Memorable Thanksgiving meetings include the 1997 game in which Barry Sanders rushed for 167 yards. Under coach Dan Campbell, the Lions' resurgence in the 2020s — including division titles — renewed the rivalry's intensity, with both teams competing for the NFC North crown.
