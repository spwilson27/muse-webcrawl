Sesame (Sesamum indicum) is a flowering plant in the genus Sesamum, widely naturalised in tropical regions around the world and cultivated for its edible seeds, which grow in pods. It is one of the oldest oilseed crops known: sesame was domesticated in the Indian subcontinent and Africa well over 3,000 years ago and is highly tolerant of drought.

Cultivation and production

Sesame seeds are small, flat, and rich in oil (about half their weight), with a mild nutty flavour. World production runs to several million tonnes annually, led by Sudan, Myanmar, Tanzania, and India. The seeds are harvested when the pods ripen and split — a property behind the phrase "open sesame", as the pods burst open to release the seeds.

Culinary uses

Sesame is used whole on breads and buns, ground into pastes such as tahini (central to hummus) and into sweets such as halva, and pressed for sesame oil, a staple cooking and flavouring oil in East and South Asian cuisines. Sesame is also a recognised food allergen and is listed among the major allergens in US and EU labelling law.
