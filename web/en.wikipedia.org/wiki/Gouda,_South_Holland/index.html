Gouda, South Holland

Gouda (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈɣʌudaː]) is a city and municipality in the west of the Netherlands, in the province of South Holland. It is famous worldwide for Gouda cheese, its historic city centre, stroopwafels, and its 15th-century city hall.

History

Founded around 1139 near the Gouwe river, Gouda received city rights in 1272 from Count Floris V. It flourished as a trading centre on routes between Amsterdam, Rotterdam and Utrecht. Fires in the 14th and 16th centuries destroyed much of the old town, but the Gothic Stadhuis (1450) on the Markt survived.

Cheese

Gouda cheese, accounting for a large share of Dutch cheese production, was traditionally traded — not necessarily made — in the city. Farmers brought cheese to the Gouda market, where it was weighed in the Waag (weigh house, 1668) and traded by hand-clapping negotiation. The cheese market is re-enacted on Thursday mornings in summer.

Sights

The Sint-Janskerk is renowned for its stained-glass windows (the Gouda Windows, 1530–1603). Other landmarks include the Markt, one of the largest market squares in the Netherlands, and the Museum Gouda. See also Netherlands and South Holland.

Food

Beyond cheese, Gouda is the birthplace of the stroopwafel, a thin waffle with caramel syrup filling, traditionally placed over hot coffee to soften.
