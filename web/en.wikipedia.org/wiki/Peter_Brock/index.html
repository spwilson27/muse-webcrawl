Peter Brock

BornPeter Geoffrey Brock, February 26, 1945, Richmond, Victoria, Australia
DiedSeptember 8, 2006, Gidgegannup, Western Australia
OccupationRacing driver

Peter Geoffrey Brock (February 26, 1945 – September 8, 2006), known as "Peter Perfect", "The King of the Mountain" or simply "Brocky", was an Australian motor racing driver. He is one of Australia's most successful and best-known racing drivers, synonymous with Holden and with the Bathurst 1000 endurance race at Mount Panorama, which he won a record nine times.

Career

Brock made his Bathurst debut in 1969 with the Holden Dealer Team and won the race for the first time in 1972 in a Holden Torana. His record tally of nine Bathurst victories (1972, 1975, 1978, 1979, 1980, 1982, 1983, 1984 and 1987) remains unmatched, and his six pole positions included a famous lap record in 1979 when he won by six laps. He also won the Australian Touring Car Championship three times and the Sandown endurance race nine times.

Later life and death

Brock founded the Peter Brock Foundation to support young people and promoted road safety throughout his retirement. He was killed on September 8, 2006, when his Daytona Coupe crashed into a tree during the Targa West rally near Perth. He received a state memorial service at Sandown Raceway, and the Bathurst 1000 winner's trophy was renamed the Peter Brock Trophy in his honour. He was inducted into the V8 Supercars Hall of Fame and the Australian Sport Hall of Fame.
