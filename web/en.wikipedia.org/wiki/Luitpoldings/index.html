Luitpoldings

The Bavarian stem duchy

The Luitpoldings were a medieval German dynasty that ruled the Duchy of Bavaria from about 907 until 947, and again from 983 to 985. They are named after Luitpold, Margrave of Bavaria, who died in 907 fighting the Hungarians at the Battle of Pressburg.

History

Luitpold's son Arnulf "the Bad" succeeded him and effectively re-established the Bavarian stem duchy, asserting a semi-independent position toward the East Frankish kings: he controlled church appointments, coinage, and foreign policy, and defended Bavaria against Hungarian raids. After Arnulf's death in 937, his descendants clashed with King Otto I, who deposed Arnulf's son Eberhard and installed his own brother-in-law Berthold, the last Luitpolding duke, who ruled from 938 to 947.

A second branch of the family, descended from Arnulf's younger son, briefly recovered the duchy when Henry "the Wrangler" became Duke of Bavaria in 955 and again in 985, though the family's ducal line ended with him and his son Henry II — who, however, became King of Germany and Holy Roman Emperor in 1002. The Luitpoldings also had ties by marriage to the royal houses of Burgundy and Italy.
