Kardashev scale

Proposed byNikolai Kardashev
Year1964
FieldAstrobiology, SETI
MeasuresCivilization by energy consumption

The Kardashev scale is a method of measuring a civilization's level of technological advancement based on the amount of energy it is able to use. The scale was proposed by Soviet astronomer Nikolai Kardashev in 1964 and has since become a standard framework in discussions of extraterrestrial life and the future of human civilization.

The three types

Kardashev defined three categories of civilization based on their energy consumption:

TypeDescriptionPower (approx.)
Type IA planetary civilization able to use and store all of the energy available from its parent star that reaches its home planet.~4 × 1012 watts (original formulation); ~1016–1017 W in modern usage
Type IIA stellar civilization able to harness the total energy output of its star, for example by means of a Dyson sphere.~4 × 1026 watts
Type IIIA galactic civilization able to control energy on the scale of its entire host galaxy.~4 × 1037 watts

Kardashev's original figures were based on the power values of 4 × 1012 watts for Type I, 4 × 1026 watts for Type II, and 4 × 1037 watts for Type III.

Humanity's current status

Human civilization does not yet reach Type I on the scale. Astronomer Carl Sagan proposed interpolating intermediate values by defining a continuous formula, K = (log10P − 6) / 10, where P is the power a civilization uses in watts. On this measure, humanity was rated at about 0.7 in the 1970s and roughly 0.73 in the early 21st century.

Extensions

Various extensions have been proposed, including Type IV civilizations controlling the energy of an entire universe and Type V controlling collections of universes, as well as alternative scales based on information mastery, size, or control over fundamental forces. Critics note that the scale measures energy supply rather than efficiency, knowledge, or sustainability, and that advanced civilizations might in fact reduce their energy signatures.
