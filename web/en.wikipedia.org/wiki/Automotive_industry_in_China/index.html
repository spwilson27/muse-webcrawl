The automotive industry in China has been the world's largest by production volume and by sales since 2009. China produced more than 30 million vehicles in 2023 — roughly a third of global output — and in the same year overtook Japan to become the world's largest vehicle exporter. The industry combines giant state-owned groups, private manufacturers and joint ventures with foreign brands, and China dominates global production of electric vehicles and traction batteries.

History

China's motor industry began with Soviet assistance: the First Automobile Works (FAW) in Changchun produced its first Jiefang truck in 1956, and the Hongqi limousine followed for party leaders. Production remained small and state-directed until reform and opening. In the 1980s Beijing admitted foreign makers only through joint ventures — beginning with American Motors' Beijing Jeep (1983) and Volkswagen's Shanghai venture (1984/85) — in exchange for technology transfer and localisation. After WTO accession in 2001, private firms such as Geely and Great Wall entered, and rising incomes triggered explosive growth in car ownership.

Electric vehicles

From the 2010s the state promoted "new energy vehicles" (battery-electric, plug-in hybrid and fuel-cell) through subsidies, licence-plate privileges and industrial policy under the Made in China 2025 strategy. BYD grew into the world's largest maker of electrified vehicles, CATL became the largest EV battery maker, and start-ups such as NIO, XPeng and Li Auto built premium smart-EV brands. By 2024, new energy vehicles accounted for roughly half of new car sales in China in some months.

Trade tensions

The rapid growth of low-cost Chinese EV exports prompted defensive measures abroad: the United States raised tariffs on Chinese EVs to 100 per cent in 2024, and the European Union imposed additional duties of up to about 35 per cent after an anti-subsidy investigation, while Chinese makers expanded plants in Thailand, Brazil, Hungary and other markets. Domestically, intense price wars from 2023 squeezed margins across the industry.
