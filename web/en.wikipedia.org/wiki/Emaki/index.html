Emaki

Emaki (絵巻), also called emakimono (絵巻物), is a Japanese illustrated narrative system that combines text and pictures, typically painted on a horizontal handscroll. It was developed in Japan between the 11th and 16th centuries, in the Heian and Kamakura periods, and depicts the unfolding of a story as the scroll is unrolled from right to left.

Characteristics

An emakimono is a long paper or silk scroll, typically 30–40 cm high and 9–12 m long. The viewing experience is sequential: the reader unrolls about 60 cm at a time, revealing alternating sections of calligraphic text (kotobagaki) and painting. Techniques unique to the genre include iji-dōzu (depicting the same character multiple times in one scene) and the fukinuki yatai "blown-off-roof" perspective, which shows the interiors of buildings from above.

Notable examples

Famous surviving emaki include the Genji Monogatari Emaki (Illustrated Handscroll of The Tale of Genji, c. 12th century), the Shigisan Engi Emaki, the Ban Dainagon Ekotoba, and the Chōjū-jinbutsu-giga (Scrolls of Frolicking Animals). Many are designated National Treasures of Japan.
