Supra (feast)

A supra (Georgian: სუფრა) is a traditional Georgian feast and a central part of Georgian social culture. The feast is led by a toastmaster called the tamada, who proposes a structured sequence of toasts — to peace, to God, to children, to friendship, to women, to heroes, and to other themes — and keeps order at the table, blending the meal with storytelling and polyphonic singing. In 2017, the traditional supra was inscribed by UNESCO on its list concerning the intangible cultural heritage of Georgia.

Types

There are two principal types of supra. The festive supra (lkhinis supra) celebrates joyful occasions such as weddings and birthdays, while the somber kelekhi accompanies funerals, with a stricter, more restrained order of toasts honoring the deceased. The number of guests can range from a family table to hundreds of participants.

The tamada and toasts

The tamada is chosen for eloquence, wit, and knowledge of tradition. Toasts are drunk — traditionally from a polished horn (kantsi) for special toasts — and guests may elaborate on the tamada's toast in turn, a practice known as alaverdi, passing the theme to another speaker. Songs, including the polyphonic Mravalzhamieri, punctuate the meal.

Food and drink

The table is covered with dishes such as khachapuri, khinkali, mtsvadi (grilled meat), pkhali, and eggplant with walnut sauce, accompanied by Georgian wine, traditionally made in buried clay vessels called qvevri — a winemaking method also recognized by UNESCO. The abundance of the table symbolizes hospitality and prosperity.
