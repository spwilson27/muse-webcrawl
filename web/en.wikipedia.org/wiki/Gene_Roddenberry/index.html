Eugene Wesley Roddenberry Sr. (August 19, 1921 – October 24, 1991) was an American television screenwriter and producer who created the Star Trek franchise. Known as the "Great Bird of the Galaxy", he was one of the first television writers inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame.

Early life and career

Roddenberry flew B-17 bombers with the U.S. Army Air Forces in the Pacific during World War II, then worked as a commercial pilot for Pan Am and as a Los Angeles police officer while breaking into television writing. He wrote for series such as Highway Patrol and Have Gun – Will Travel (winning a Writers Guild Award), and created the military drama The Lieutenant (1963).

Star Trek

Roddenberry pitched Star Trek as a "Wagon Train to the stars": an optimistic future in which a diverse crew explores the galaxy aboard the USS Enterprise. NBC aired the original series from 1966 to 1969; cancelled after three seasons, it became a phenomenon in syndication, spawning an animated series, the 1979 film Star Trek: The Motion Picture and further movies. In 1987 Roddenberry launched Star Trek: The Next Generation, which became one of the most successful syndicated dramas ever made and anchored a franchise that continues today.

His vision — post-scarcity economics, a united Earth, and stories that allegorised contemporary issues of war, race and technology — made Star Trek a lasting cultural institution.

Death and legacy

Roddenberry died in 1991, shortly after a screening of Star Trek VI. A sample of his ashes was flown into orbit aboard the space shuttle Columbia in 1992, and later memorial spaceflights have carried his remains toward deep space. Posthumous series developed from his notes include Earth: Final Conflict and Andromeda.
