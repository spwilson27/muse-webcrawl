Haveli
RegionIndia and Pakistan (especially Rajasthan and Gujarat)
Origin of termArabic/Persian hawali, "partition" or "private space"
FeaturesCentral courtyard, frescoes, jharokha balconies, carved facades

A haveli is a traditional townhouse or mansion in the Indian subcontinent, usually built around an inner courtyard and noted for its ornate decoration. The word derives from the Arabic hawali, meaning "partition" or "private space".

Architecture

Havelis were built by wealthy merchants, noblemen, and landowners, particularly in Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Punjab, from the Mughal era through the nineteenth century. They typically feature one or more courtyards (chowks) around which rooms are arranged, providing light, ventilation, and privacy. Exterior facades are often richly adorned with carved sandstone or wooden balconies (jharokhas), latticework screens (jaalis), and painted frescoes depicting religious scenes, processions, and everyday life.

Famous examples

The painted havelis of the Shekhawati region of Rajasthan — towns such as Mandawa, Nawalgarh, and Fatehpur — are famed as an "open art gallery". Patwon Ji Ki Haveli in Jaisalmer and the havelis of Jodhpur and Bikaner are also celebrated. Many havelis have been converted into heritage hotels or museums, while others remain family homes; in Pakistan, notable examples survive in Lahore's old city.
