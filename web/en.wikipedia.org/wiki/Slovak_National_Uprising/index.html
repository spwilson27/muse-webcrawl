The Slovak National Uprising (Slovak: Slovenské národné povstanie, SNP) was a military uprising organized by the Slovak resistance during World War II. It began on August 29, 1944, in Banská Bystrica in an attempt to overthrow the collaborationist regime of Jozef Tiso and remove Slovakia from the Axis. It is one of the largest anti-Nazi resistance actions in Europe and is commemorated in Slovakia as a national holiday (August 29).

Background

The Slovak State (1939–1945), a German client state under Tiso, contributed troops to the Eastern Front and deported most of its Jewish population in 1942. Resistance coalesced in the Slovak National Council (1943), uniting democratic and communist groups, while dissident officers in the Slovak Army — notably Lt. Col. Ján Golian — planned an uprising in coordination with the advancing Red Army.

Course

The uprising began prematurely after partisans ambushed a German mission at Martin; Germany responded by occupying Slovakia. Insurgents held a liberated zone in central Slovakia around Banská Bystrica, fielding an army of some 60,000 soldiers plus partisans, aided by Soviet and Western airlifts and by Czechoslovak units from the USSR, including paratroopers and an air regiment. However, the planned support from the Red Army through the Dukla Pass was delayed, and German forces suppressed the conventional phase of the uprising with the fall of Banská Bystrica on October 27–28, 1944.

Aftermath

Insurgents withdrew to the mountains and fought as partisans until Slovakia's liberation in spring 1945. The German occupation that followed brought terror and reprisals, the renewed deportation of Jews, and massacres of civilians. The uprising nonetheless placed Slovakia on the side of the victorious Allies and remains a founding symbol of modern Slovak statehood.
