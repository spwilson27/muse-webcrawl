Lathe

A lathe is a machine tool that rotates a workpiece about an axis of rotation to perform various operations such as cutting, sanding, knurling, drilling, deformation, facing, and turning, with tools that are applied to the workpiece to create an object with symmetry about that axis. Lathes are used in woodturning, metalworking, metal spinning, thermal spraying, parts reclamation, and glass-working. They can be used to shape pottery, the best-known design being the potter's wheel. Suitably equipped lathes can also produce screw threads, tapered work, and complex solids of revolution. The lathe is one of the oldest machine tools — its history stretches to ancient Egypt — and it was central to the Industrial Revolution, earning a reputation as the "mother of machine tools" because the early screw-cutting lathes could reproduce their own precision components and make parts for other machines.

Parts

A typical lathe consists of a bed, a headstock containing the spindle that drives the workpiece, a tailstock that supports the far end of the work or holds drills, and a carriage that moves the cutting tool along and across the rotating work. Work is held in a chuck, between centres, or on a faceplate. In an engine lathe, the carriage is driven by a lead screw for thread cutting or a feed rod for ordinary turning; modern CNC lathes replace manual controls with computer-controlled servomotors.

History

Evidence for simple lathes dates to ancient Egypt (around 1300 BC), where a two-person lathe was turned by an assistant pulling a cord. The bow lathe and pole lathe were used through the Middle Ages. The development of the screw-cutting lathe by Henry Maudslay around 1797–1800, with a slide rest and changeable gearing, standardised thread production and laid the foundations of precision engineering. During the nineteenth century, lathes were powered by steam-driven line shafts; the twentieth century brought individual electric motors and, late in the century, computer numerical control (CNC).

Types

  • Engine lathe: the general-purpose metalworking lathe.
  • Wood lathe: lighter machines for turning wood, with tools held by hand on a rest.
  • CNC lathe / turning centre: computer-controlled machines, often with live tooling and bar feeders for production work.
  • Turret and capstan lathes: production lathes with multiple tools mounted on an indexing turret.
  • Watchmaker's lathe: small, precise lathes for horology.
  • Vertical lathe: machines with a vertical spindle for very large, heavy workpieces.
  • Glass lathe: used in scientific glassblowing.
