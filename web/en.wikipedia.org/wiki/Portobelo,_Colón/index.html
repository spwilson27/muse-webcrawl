Portobelo (historically Porto Bello in English) is a historic port city and corregimiento in Portobelo District, Colón Province, Panama. Founded in 1597, it was one of the most important ports of Spanish America during the colonial period, serving as the Atlantic terminus where Peruvian silver and other treasures from the Pacific were transferred to the Spanish treasure fleets bound for Seville and Cádiz.

Christopher Columbus named the bay "Puerto Bello" ("beautiful port") in 1502 during his fourth voyage. The town replaced Nombre de Dios as the Caribbean outlet of the Camino Real after that settlement proved indefensible against pirate attack.

History

During the great trade fairs (ferias) held when the treasure fleet arrived, Portobelo swelled from a small garrison town into one of the busiest commercial centers in the New World, as silver from Potosí — carried by mule train across the isthmus from Panama City — changed hands for European goods. The wealth of the fairs made the town a target. Francis Drake raided it in 1596 and died of dysentery offshore shortly afterward; he was buried at sea in a lead coffin near the town. In 1668 Henry Morgan sacked Portobelo, famously using captured religious figures as human shields to storm the fortresses, and held the town for ransom.

In November 1739, early in the War of Jenkins' Ear, Admiral Edward Vernon captured Portobelo with only six ships of the line — a victory celebrated extravagantly in Britain, where the name "Portobello" was given to streets and districts in London, Edinburgh and elsewhere. Spain retook the port, but the trade system never recovered; the fairs ended, the route shifted around Cape Horn, and Portobelo declined into a quiet fishing and agricultural town.

Fortifications

The Spanish built extensive defenses around the bay, including the forts of San Jerónimo, Santiago de la Gloria and San Fernando. Together with the fortifications at the mouth of the Chagres River at San Lorenzo, they were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980 as the "Fortifications on the Caribbean Side of Panama". The ruins are among the finest surviving examples of Spanish military architecture of the 17th and 18th centuries in the Americas.

Culture

Portobelo is a center of Afro-Panamanian Congo culture, expressed in music, dance and the colorful Diablos y Congos festivals. The church of San Felipe houses the famous Cristo Negro (Black Christ) of Portobelo, a wooden statue venerated by pilgrims from across Panama and the Caribbean, who walk to the town each October 21 for its festival.
