Israel–Hamas war

[Image: smoke rising over buildings in Gaza / aid supplies being distributed]

The Israel–Hamas war — increasingly referred to as the Gaza war — began on 7 October 2023, when the Palestinian militant group Hamas led an attack on southern Israel, killing around 1,200 people and taking roughly 250 hostages. Israel responded with airstrikes and a ground invasion of the Gaza Strip.

Course of the war

Israeli operations devastated much of Gaza's housing, hospitals and infrastructure. Ceasefire and hostage-exchange arrangements were reached at points, including a brief truce in late 2023 and further agreements in 2025, amid repeated negotiations mediated by Qatar, Egypt and the United States. The conflict also widened at times to exchanges with Hezbollah in Lebanon and direct strikes between Israel and Iran.

Humanitarian situation

Gaza's health authorities have reported tens of thousands of Palestinian deaths; Israel disputes some figures while international agencies describe a severe humanitarian crisis, with widespread displacement, hunger and disease. Israel states its aims as destroying Hamas and freeing the hostages; critics, including some international legal bodies, have accused Israel of disproportionate force and worse — accusations Israel rejects. The International Court of Justice has considered a genocide case brought by South Africa, which Israel denies.
