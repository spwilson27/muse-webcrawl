The Sandia Mountains are a mountain range in the US state of New Mexico, immediately east of Albuquerque on the eastern edge of the Rio Grande rift. The name Sandia is Spanish for "watermelon", a reference either to the pinkish-red colour the granite cliffs take on at sunset or to an old belief that wild gourds grew at their foot.

Geography and geology

The range runs roughly 17 miles north–south. Its western face is a steep fault-block escarpment of granite capped in places by Pennsylvanian limestone — a layer of rock famously visible from the city — while the eastern slopes descend more gently through pine and fir forest. The high point is Sandia Crest at 10,678 feet (3,255 m). Most of the range lies within the Cibola National Forest, and the southern portion is protected as the Sandia Mountain Wilderness.

Recreation

The Sandia Peak Tramway, opened in 1966, carries passengers from the northeast edge of Albuquerque to the crest — at 2.7 miles one of the longest aerial tram spans in the world. Hiking trails such as the La Luz Trail climb the west face, and the Sandia Peak Ski Area operates on the eastern slopes in winter. The mountains are also a noted site for rock climbing and hang gliding.

Archaeology

Sandia Cave, in Las Huertas Canyon near the north end of the range, was excavated in the 1930s and was once controversially claimed as evidence of an extremely early "Sandia Man" occupation of the Americas, a claim now largely discounted, though the cave did contain Folsom-era material.
