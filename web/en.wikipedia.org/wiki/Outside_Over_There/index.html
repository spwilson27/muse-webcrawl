Outside Over There

AuthorMaurice Sendak
Published1981
PublisherHarper & Row
HonoursCaldecott Honor Book (1982)

Outside Over There is a picture book written and illustrated by Maurice Sendak, published in 1981. It forms the third part of an informal trilogy with Where the Wild Things Are (1963) and In the Night Kitchen (1970), each book tracing a child's journey through anger, fantasy and return. Outside Over There was named a Caldecott Honor Book in 1982.

Plot

While her father is away at sea and her mother sits distracted in an arbour, young Ida must watch her baby sister. Playing her wonder horn with her back turned, Ida fails to notice goblins climbing in the window, stealing the baby and leaving a changeling made of ice. When the ice baby melts in Ida's arms, she sets out — sailing backwards out of the window in her mother's rain cloak — to "outside over there", confronts the goblins at their wedding feast, and dances them into exhaustion with her horn until they release the children they have stolen. She rescues her real sister and returns home, where a letter from her father asks her to keep watch over Mama and the baby until he returns.

Background and reception

Sendak drew on his childhood memories of the Lindbergh kidnapping, on Mozart's The Magic Flute (Ida's wonder horn and rescue quest echo Tamino's), and on the Romantic imagery of Philipp Otto Runge. Reviewers praised the luminous, unsettling illustrations while noting the book's darkness; it is often considered the most personal and demanding of Sendak's major works. The book was a significant inspiration for the 1986 film Labyrinth.
