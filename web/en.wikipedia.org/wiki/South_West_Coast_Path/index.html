South West Coast Path

The South West Coast Path is England's longest waymarked long-distance footpath and National Trail. Completed in 1978, it stretches for 630 miles (1,014 km), running from Minehead in Somerset, along the coasts of Devon and Cornwall, to Poole Harbour in Dorset.

History

The path originated as a route for coastguards patrolling for smugglers, who needed to see into every cove. It was designated section by section through the 1970s and is maintained by Natural England and local authorities, with diversions as cliffs erode.

Route

The trail passes Exmoor, the Jurassic Coast (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Land's End, the Lizard, Plymouth, Dartmouth and Lulworth Cove. Total ascent, about four times the height of Everest, makes it one of Britain's most demanding trails; most walkers take 7–8 weeks.

Economy

The path generates significant income for coastal communities and is promoted by the South West Coast Path Association.
