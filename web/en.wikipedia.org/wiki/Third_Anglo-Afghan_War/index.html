The Third Anglo-Afghan War (6 May – 8 August 1919) was a brief conflict between the Emirate of Afghanistan and the British Empire. It began when the new Afghan ruler, Amir Amanullah Khan, seeking full independence from British control over Afghanistan's foreign policy, invaded British India across the Durand Line in the aftermath of the First World War. The war ended with the Treaty of Rawalpindi, in which Britain recognised Afghanistan's independence — 19 August is still celebrated as Afghan Independence Day.

Background

Since the Treaty of Gandamak (1879), Britain had controlled Afghanistan's foreign relations in exchange for subsidies and protection. Amanullah, who seized the throne after his father's assassination in February 1919, proclaimed complete independence. With British India unsettled by unrest — the Amritsar massacre had occurred in April — and its army demobilising, Amanullah judged the moment favourable, denounced the old treaties, and ordered his forces across the frontier.

Course of the war

Afghan forces occupied Bagh and threatened the Khyber Pass, while a general call to jihad sought to raise the frontier tribes. British-Indian troops, though stretched, counter-attacked with superior artillery, logistics and — for the first time in the region — aircraft: a single Handley Page bomber raided Kabul on 24 May 1919, a shock that contributed to Amanullah's request for an armistice. In Waziristan and the south, Afghan general Nadir Khan captured the fort at Thal, the war's notable Afghan success. Fighting sputtered out by June, and an armistice preceded the peace conference at Rawalpindi.

Aftermath

The Treaty of Rawalpindi (8 August 1919) ended the British subsidy and arms traffic but, crucially, Britain accepted Afghanistan as a fully independent state in its external relations, confirmed by the Anglo-Afghan Treaty of 1921. Amanullah embarked on an ambitious programme of modernisation. Militarily indecisive, the war was a clear Afghan political victory and closed the series of Anglo-Afghan wars begun in 1839.
