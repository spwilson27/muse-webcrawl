John D. Rockefeller Jr.

John Davison Rockefeller Jr. (January 29, 1874 – May 11, 1960) was an American financier and philanthropist, the fifth child and only son of Standard Oil co-founder John D. Rockefeller. He was involved in the development of the vast office complex in Midtown Manhattan known as Rockefeller Center, making him one of the largest real-estate holders in the city. Towards the end of his life he was famous primarily for his philanthropy, donating over US$537 million to myriad causes.

Life and career

Born in Cleveland, Ohio, Rockefeller graduated from Brown University in 1897 and joined his father's business in New York. After the 1914 Ludlow Massacre at a Colorado Fuel and Iron property, he took a leading public role in the family's affairs and in reforming its industrial relations, becoming a pioneer of employee representation plans. He managed much of the family's philanthropy through the Rockefeller Foundation, the General Education Board and the Laura Spelman Rockefeller Memorial.

Philanthropy

His benefactions included the restoration of Colonial Williamsburg, Virginia (which he funded from 1926 at a cost of tens of millions of dollars), the land for the United Nations headquarters in New York, the Museum of Modern Art (co-founded with his wife Abby Aldrich Rockefeller), Riverside Church, the Cloisters, and major gifts to national parks — including land and funds for Acadia, Grand Teton, Great Smoky Mountains, Shenandoah and Yosemite. He also financed the preservation of the forests of Fontainebleau and the repair of Versailles and Reims Cathedral in France.

Family

With Abby Aldrich Rockefeller he had six children: Abby, John D. III, Nelson (Governor of New York and Vice President of the United States), Laurance, Winthrop and David (banker and chairman of Chase Manhattan). After Abby's death in 1948 he married Martha Baird Allen. He died of pneumonia in Tucson, Arizona, in 1960.
