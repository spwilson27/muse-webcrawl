University of California, Los Angeles

The University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) is a public land-grant research university in Los Angeles, California, United States. It is the southernmost campus of the University of California system. Founded in 1919 as the Southern Branch of the University of California, it is the most applied-to university in the United States and consistently ranks among the top public universities in the world.

History

UCLA began as the southern branch of the state teachers' college in 1882, became the Southern Branch of the University of California in 1919, and adopted the name University of California at Los Angeles in 1927 (the comma replacing "at" in 1958). The campus moved to its present site in Westwood in 1929, with Royce Hall and Powell Library among its original Romanesque Revival buildings.

Academics

The university is organized into the College of Letters and Science and professional schools including the David Geffen School of Medicine, the UCLA School of Law, the Anderson School of Management, and the School of Theater, Film and Television. UCLA faculty and alumni include sixteen Nobel laureates. In 1969, the first message on the ARPANET — the precursor of the internet — was sent from UCLA to Stanford.

Athletics

The UCLA Bruins compete in NCAA Division I and joined the Big Ten Conference in 2024. UCLA has won more NCAA team championships than any other university. Legendary basketball coach John Wooden led the Bruins to ten national championships between 1964 and 1975, and alumni include Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Jackie Robinson, and Arthur Ashe. The football team plays at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena.
