Divya Desam

The Divya Desams (Tamil: திவ்ய தேசம், "divine places") are a group of 108 Hindu temples dedicated to the god Vishnu that are mentioned in the works of the Alvars, the Tamil poet-saints of South India. 105 of the shrines are located in India, one in Nepal (Muktinath), and the last two — Thirupparkadal (the ocean of milk) and Paramapadam (Vaikuntha) — are believed to be outside the earthly realm.

Significance

The shrines are revered in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the early medieval Tamil canon of Alvar poetry, in which each temple is sung in verses called pasurams. The Divya Desams follow the Thenkalai or Vadakalai modes of worship within the Sri Vaishnava tradition. Many of the temples are celebrated in the agamic tradition and are associated with legends from the Puranas and local sthala puranams.

Geography

The majority of the shrines are concentrated in Tamil Nadu, with others in Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Nepal. The Pancharanga Kshetrams — five shrines along the Kaveri river dedicated to Ranganatha — are among the most prominent, the foremost being the Srirangam temple, regarded as the first and most important of the 108.
