The Székelys (Hungarian: Székelyek; Romanian: Secui) are a Hungarian ethnic group living primarily in the Székely Land, an area in present-day Romania. They speak Hungarian and have a distinct cultural identity, history, and set of traditions within the broader Hungarian nation.

History

The origins of the Székelys are debated: medieval chronicles linked them to the Huns of Attila, while modern scholarship generally treats them as a Hungarian group settled in eastern Transylvania to guard the frontier of the Kingdom of Hungary. In return for military service they enjoyed collective freedom and self-government through their "seats", and were counted among the three privileged "nations" of Transylvania alongside the Hungarian nobility and the Saxons. Their privileges were gradually eroded in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, notably after the 1764 Madéfalva massacre (Siculicidium).

Culture

Székely culture is marked by carved wooden gates, distinctive folk dress and dances, the runic-like Székely-Hungarian script (rovásírás), and strong Roman Catholic, Reformed, and Unitarian traditions. Many Székelys today advocate cultural or territorial autonomy within Romania.
