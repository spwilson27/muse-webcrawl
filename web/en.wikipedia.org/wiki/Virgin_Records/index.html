Virgin Records

Virgin Records is a British record label founded in 1972 by Richard Branson, Simon Draper, Nik Powell and Tom Newman. It grew from a mail-order and retail business (Virgin Records and Tapes shops) into one of the world's major independent labels before being sold to Thorn EMI in 1992. Today the name is used by Universal Music Group for several label operations.

History

The label's first release, in 1973, was Mike Oldfield's Tubular Bells, whose success — boosted by its use in the film The Exorcist — financed Virgin's expansion. The label signed the Sex Pistols in 1977 after they were dropped by EMI and A&M, becoming central to British punk; it later built a diverse roster including Human League, Culture Club, Simple Minds, Phil Collins and Genesis, the Rolling Stones, Janet Jackson, Lenny Kravitz and the Spice Girls, whose 1996 debut became one of the best-selling girl-group releases ever. Sister ventures included the progressive label Charisma, the dance imprint Circa, and Virgin Records America.

After the EMI sale

Branson sold Virgin Records to Thorn EMI in 1992 for a reported £510 million, funding his airline ventures. Under EMI, and after Universal's 2012 acquisition of EMI, the Virgin name was retained and revived for imprints such as Virgin EMI Records in the UK (renamed EMI Records in 2020, with Virgin continuing as an imprint) and Virgin Music Group, Universal's label and artist services division launched in 2022.
