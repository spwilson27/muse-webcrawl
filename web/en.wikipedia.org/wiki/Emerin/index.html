Emerin

Emerin is a protein that in humans is encoded by the EMD gene (formerly STA) on the X chromosome. It is a serine-rich nuclear membrane protein of about 34 kDa and a member of the nuclear lamina-associated protein family, found at the inner nuclear membrane of vertebrate cells.

Structure and function

Emerin contains an N-terminal LEM domain, shared with other lamina-associated proteins such as LAP2 and MAN1, which binds the chromatin-bridging protein barrier-to-autointegration factor (BAF). It interacts with lamins A and C, actin, and various transcriptional regulators, and is thought to contribute to nuclear architecture, chromatin organisation, gene expression, and mechanotransduction.

Clinical significance

Mutations in EMD that abolish emerin expression cause Emery–Dreifuss muscular dystrophy, an X-linked disorder characterised by early joint contractures (especially of the elbows, Achilles tendons, and neck), slowly progressive muscle weakness and wasting, and cardiac conduction defects that can cause sudden death. The disease is named in part for Alan Emery and Fritz Dreifuss; emerin itself is named after Alan Emery.
