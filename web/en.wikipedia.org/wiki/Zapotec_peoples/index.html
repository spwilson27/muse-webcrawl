Zapotec peoples

Zapotec
Self-designation: Be'ena'a Za'a ("Cloud People")
Regions: Oaxaca, Mexico
Languages: Zapotec languages, Spanish
Population: roughly 800,000-1,000,000

The Zapotec (Zapotec: Be'ena'a Za'a, "The Cloud People") are an indigenous people of Mexico concentrated in the state of Oaxaca, in the Valley of Oaxaca, the Sierra Norte and Sierra Sur, and the Isthmus of Tehuantepec. They are the descendants and cultural heirs of the pre-Columbian Zapotec civilization centred on Monte Albán.

Languages

The Zapotec languages form a diverse family within the Oto-Manguean group, with dozens of often mutually unintelligible varieties spoken in valley, mountain and isthmus communities. Zapotec is among the most widely spoken indigenous language groupings in Mexico, though many varieties are endangered as Spanish predominates in education and commerce.

Culture

Zapotec communities are known for vibrant craft traditions, including the carved wooden alebrijes of villages such as San Martín Tilcajete, the woven textiles of Teotitlán del Valle, and black pottery (barro negro) from San Bartolo Coyotepec. Communal labour (tequio) and reciprocal celebration sponsorship remain important, and festivals such as the Guelaguetza in Oaxaca City showcase Zapotec and other regional dances and dress. Isthmus Zapotec society is noted for its relatively prominent role of women in trade, centred on the markets of Juchitán.

Notable Zapotec

The most famous Zapotec in Mexican history is Benito Juárez, the nineteenth-century president and liberal reformer from San Pablo Guelatao. Other prominent figures include the artist Rufino Tamayo.
