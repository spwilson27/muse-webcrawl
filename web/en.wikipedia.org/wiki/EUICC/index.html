eUICC

An embedded UICC (eUICC) is a component of an embedded SIM (eSIM) that can hold multiple subscriber identity module (SIM) profiles, allowing the device to switch between mobile network operators without physically swapping a card. The eUICC refers strictly to the secure hardware/software component — typically a chip soldered to the device's motherboard (often in an MFF2 package) — whereas "eSIM" describes the whole remote-provisioning system built around it. Specifications for remote SIM provisioning are maintained by the GSMA.

How it works

An eUICC is identified by a unique EID (eUICC Identifier). It is provisioned with an initial "bootstrap" profile that lets the device connect to a network, after which operator profiles can be downloaded, enabled, disabled, and deleted over the air (OTA) through the GSMA remote SIM provisioning (RSP) architecture, involving the SM-DP+ (Subscription Manager – Data Preparation) server and a local profile assistant (LPA) on the device. Profiles are isolated in secure domains on the chip and protected by cryptographic certificates issued under the GSMA certificate issuer (CI) scheme.

Consumer and M2M variants

The GSMA specifies two RSP architectures: the M2M solution (SGP.01/02), using a "push" model managed via SM-SR for headless IoT devices, and the consumer solution (SGP.21/22), a "pull" model controlled by the end user scanning a QR code or using an activation code. A unified IoT specification (SGP.31/32, eIM) was introduced to simplify large-scale IoT deployments.

Advantages and adoption

Because profiles can be changed remotely, eUICC enables out-of-the-box connectivity for IoT devices, simplifies logistics for manufacturers (one global SKU), removes the physical SIM tray (freeing space and improving water resistance in phones), and lets consumers switch carriers without visiting a store. Adoption grew after Apple, Google, and Samsung shipped eSIM in flagship phones from 2017 onward; the U.S. iPhone 14 (2022) was the first major smartphone sold without any physical SIM slot. The related iUICC (integrated UICC, or iSIM) integrates the functionality into the device's system-on-chip.
