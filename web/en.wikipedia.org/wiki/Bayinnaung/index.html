Bayinnaung

Reign: 30 April 1551 – 10 October 1581
Dynasty: Toungoo
Predecessor: Tabinshwehti
Successor: Nanda Bayin

Bayinnaung Kyawhtin Nawrahta (16 January 1516 – 10 October 1581) was king of the Toungoo Dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from 1551 to 1581. During his 30-year reign, which has been called "the greatest explosion of human energy ever seen in Burma", Bayinnaung assembled the largest empire in the history of Southeast Asia, stretching from the Assam border to parts of Yunnan and northern Siam (Thailand).

Early career

Born Ye Htut to palace attendants, he grew up as the companion and later brother-in-law of King Tabinshwehti, becoming his foremost general. After Tabinshwehti's assassination in 1550, the kingdom fragmented, and Bayinnaung spent years reconquering it city by city before being crowned in 1551.

Conquests

Bayinnaung subdued the Shan states, conquered Manipur, Chiang Mai and the Lan Na kingdom, and invaded Ayutthaya (Siam) in 1564 and again in 1569, capturing the city and making Siam a vassal for fifteen years. He also annexed Lan Xang (Laos) in 1574, though the Laotian campaigns exhausted his armies. A devout Theravada Buddhist, he promoted the faith, standardised weights and measures, and banned human sacrifice and some animist practices in the Shan states.

Death and legacy

Bayinnaung died in 1581 while preparing another campaign against Arakan. His overextended empire unravelled within two decades under his son Nanda Bayin. In Myanmar he is remembered, alongside Anawrahta and Alaungpaya, as one of the three greatest kings, and his statue stands before the National Museum in Yangon.
