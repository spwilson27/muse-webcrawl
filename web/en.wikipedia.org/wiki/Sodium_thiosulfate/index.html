Sodium thiosulfate


Formula: Na2S2O3
Molar mass: 158.11 g/mol (anhydrous)
Appearance: White crystalline solid
Common form: Pentahydrate, "hypo"

Sodium thiosulfate (sodium thiosulphate) is an inorganic compound with the formula Na2S2O3·xH2O, typically encountered as the pentahydrate. It is a colorless, odorless crystalline substance used in photography, medicine, water treatment, and analytical chemistry.

Applications

In photography, sodium thiosulfate — historically called "hypo" — is used as a fixer, dissolving unexposed silver halide from photographic film and paper. Medically, it is an antidote for cyanide poisoning (providing sulfur for converting cyanide to thiocyanate), a treatment for calciphylaxis, and a protective agent against cisplatin toxicity. It is on the WHO List of Essential Medicines. It is also used to dechlorinate water, including aquarium water, and to reduce iodine in iodometric titrations.

Production and chemistry

Industrially it is produced from sodium sulfite and sulfur, or as a byproduct of sodium sulfide manufacture. The thiosulfate anion contains two sulfur atoms in different oxidation states and acts as a reducing agent and a complexing ligand, notably for silver and gold; it is used in gold extraction as a less toxic alternative to cyanide.
