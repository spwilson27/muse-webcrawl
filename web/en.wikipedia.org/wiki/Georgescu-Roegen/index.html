Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (born Nicolae Georgescu; 4 February 1906 – 30 October 1994) was a Romanian-American mathematician and economist. He is regarded as the founder of bioeconomics and a founding figure of ecological economics, best known for insisting that the entropy law governs economic activity.

Life

Georgescu-Roegen studied mathematics in Bucharest and Paris and statistics at University College London under Karl Pearson, then spent two years at Harvard in the 1930s working with Joseph Schumpeter. Returning to Romania, he held senior posts in its statistical institute and government before emigrating to the United States in 1948, as the communist regime consolidated power. He spent the rest of his career at Vanderbilt University.

Bioeconomics

His magnum opus, The Entropy Law and the Economic Process (1971), argued that neoclassical economics ignores thermodynamics: production transforms low-entropy matter-energy into high-entropy waste, so indefinite material growth on a finite planet is impossible. He proposed an "entropy hourglass" model of the economy, criticised both Marxist and neoclassical growth theory, and advocated of a steady-state programme he called bioeconomics.

Initially marginalised by the mainstream, his work was taken up by Herman Daly (his student), the ecological economics movement and the modern degrowth literature, which treat him as a foundational thinker.
