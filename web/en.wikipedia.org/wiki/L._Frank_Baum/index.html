L. Frank Baum

Lyman Frank Baum (May 15, 1856 – May 6, 1919) was an American author best known for his children's fantasy books, particularly The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900), which introduced the Land of Oz and its heroine Dorothy Gale. He wrote thirteen sequels to the Oz books, along with many other novels, short stories, poems, and scripts.

Early life and career

Born in Chittenango, New York, Baum pursued a variety of careers before achieving literary success: he was a poultry breeder who founded a journal on chickens, an actor and playwright, a newspaper editor in South Dakota, a traveling salesman, and a shopkeeper. He moved to Chicago in the 1890s and published his first children's book, Mother Goose in Prose (1897), followed by the verse collection Father Goose: His Book (1899).

The Oz books

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, illustrated by W. W. Denslow, was an immediate bestseller. Baum eventually wrote fourteen Oz books in total, and after his death the series was continued by other authors, beginning with Ruth Plumly Thompson. Baum attempted to adapt Oz for the stage and screen, producing a successful 1902 musical extravaganza and later founding the Oz Film Manufacturing Company in 1914.

Later life and legacy

Baum settled in Hollywood, California, where he died in 1919. The 1939 MGM film The Wizard of Oz, adapted from his first Oz book, became one of the most beloved films of all time, cementing Baum's place in American popular culture.
