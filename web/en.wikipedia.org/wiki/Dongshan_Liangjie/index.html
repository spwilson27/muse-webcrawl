Dongshan Liangjie (Chinese: 洞山良价; Wade–Giles: Tung-shan Liang-chieh; Japanese: Tōzan Ryōkai; 807–869) was a Chan Buddhist monk of Tang-dynasty China. He is the founder of the Caodong school (called Sōtō in Japan), one of the five houses of Chan, together with his disciple Caoshan Benji. Dongshan is traditionally credited with the Song of the Jewel Mirror Samadhi (Hōkyōzanmai) and with formulating the teaching of the Five Ranks.

Biography

Dongshan was born in Kuaiji (modern Shaoxing, Zhejiang). He entered monastic life young and studied widely, including the teachings of Nanquan Puyuan and Guishan Lingyou, before becoming the heir of Yunyan Tansheng. Tradition relates that his awakening came upon seeing his own reflection in a stream while crossing water, after Yunyan had directed him to the teaching of the "insentient preaching the dharma." He later taught at Mount Dong ("Dongshan") in present-day Jiangxi, gathering a large community.

Teachings

Five Ranks

The Five Ranks (wu wei) describe the interplay of the absolute (the universal, "lord") and the relative (the particular, "vassal") in five modes, expressing the progressive integration of emptiness and form in practice.

Jewel Mirror Samadhi

The Song of the Jewel Mirror Samadhi, attributed to Dongshan, is a poem chanted in Sōtō Zen services to this day, celebrating the identity of self and Buddha, "you are it ... it is you."

Legacy

Through Caoshan Benji, Dongshan's line became the Caodong school; centuries later his teaching lineage was transmitted to Japan by Dōgen Zenji as Sōtō Zen. Dialogues attributed to Dongshan appear in collections such as the Record of Dongshan and in koan collections including the Blue Cliff Record and the Book of Equanimity.
