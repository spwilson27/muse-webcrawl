Poisson's equation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Poisson's equation is an elliptic partial differential equation of the form Δφ = f, where Δ is the Laplace operator and f is a given source function. It is named after the French mathematician and physicist Siméon Denis Poisson, who published it in 1813 in the context of electrostatics. When f = 0 it reduces to Laplace's equation.

Applications

In electrostatics, Poisson's equation relates the electric potential to the charge density: Δφ = −ρ/ε0. In Newtonian gravity it gives the gravitational potential generated by a mass density. It also arises in heat conduction, fluid mechanics, and mechanical engineering wherever a potential field is driven by a distributed source.

Solution methods

Solutions can be constructed with Green's functions: for suitable boundary conditions (Dirichlet or Neumann), the solution is the convolution of the source with the fundamental solution of the Laplacian, plus a harmonic correction. Numerical methods such as finite differences and multigrid solvers are standard tools for large-scale problems.
