Neglected tropical diseases

Neglected tropical diseases (NTDs) are a diverse group of tropical infections that are prevalent among impoverished populations in Africa, Asia, and South America. They are caused by viruses, bacteria, protozoa, and helminths (parasitic worms), and are "neglected" because they receive far less research funding and public attention than HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, and malaria, despite affecting more than a billion people. The World Health Organization (WHO) maintains an official list of around 20 NTDs.

The diseases

The WHO list includes dengue and chikungunya, rabies, trachoma, Buruli ulcer, yaws, leprosy, Chagas disease, human African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness), leishmaniasis, cysticercosis, dracunculiasis (Guinea-worm disease), echinococcosis, foodborne trematodiases, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis (river blindness), schistosomiasis, soil-transmitted helminthiases, mycetoma, scabies, and snakebite envenoming. Together they cause hundreds of thousands of deaths annually and much larger burdens of disability, disfigurement, blindness, and stigma, trapping communities in poverty.

Control strategies

Because many NTDs can be treated or prevented with safe, inexpensive medicines, the mainstay of control is preventive chemotherapy: mass drug administration (MDA) of drugs such as albendazole, ivermectin, praziquantel, and azithromycin to whole at-risk populations, largely donated by pharmaceutical companies. Complementary measures include vector control, veterinary public health ("One Health"), water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and morbidity management — for example surgery for trachomatous trichiasis.

Progress and goals

The 2012 London Declaration on Neglected Tropical Diseases united governments, donors, and industry behind elimination targets. Dracunculiasis has been brought to the edge of eradication, with only a handful of human cases reported annually in recent years. Dozens of countries have eliminated at least one NTD as a public health problem, such as lymphatic filariasis or trachoma. WHO's 2021–2030 road map for NTDs aims for a 90% reduction in the number of people requiring treatment and the elimination of at least one NTD in 100 countries by 2030.
