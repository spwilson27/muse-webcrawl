Royal Delft

Hand-painted Delft Blue earthenware

Royal Delft (Dutch: Koninklijke Porceleyne Fles) is a Dutch manufacturer of Delftware pottery based in Delft, the Netherlands. Founded in 1653 as De Porceleyne Fles ("The Porcelain Bottle"), it is the last remaining manufacturer of the original Delft pottery factories and produces the famous blue-and-white Delft Blue.

History

Delft pottery arose in the mid-17th century when imports of Chinese porcelain were disrupted and Dutch potters imitated them with tin-glazed earthenware decorated in cobalt blue. At its peak Delft had more than thirty pottery factories; De Porceleyne Fles, established in 1653, is the only one to survive continuously.

In the 19th century the factory was revived under engineer Joost Thooft, who introduced new production techniques and renewed the classic hand-painted Delft Blue designs. In 1919 the company received the royal designation "Koninklijke".

Factory and museum

The factory at Rotterdamseweg in Delft is open to visitors and functions as a museum, showing the production process from casting and painting to glazing and firing. Painters still decorate pieces by hand with motifs that include Dutch landscapes, windmills, flowers and maritime scenes.
