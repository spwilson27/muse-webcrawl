Master chief petty officer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Master chief petty officer (MCPO) is an enlisted naval rate, the ninth and highest enlisted grade in the United States Navy and United States Coast Guard, with the pay grade E-9. It ranks above senior chief petty officer and below the service-level leadership positions such as the Master Chief Petty Officer of the Navy. By law, master chiefs may comprise no more than about 1% of the enlisted force.

Role

Master chiefs are senior enlisted leaders and technical experts in their rating (occupation). They advise commanding officers, lead chief petty officers' messes, mentor junior sailors and represent enlisted concerns to command. Many serve as "command master chiefs", the senior enlisted advisor of a ship, squadron or shore command, identified by a special insignia worn in place of rating marks.

Advancement

Promotion to master chief is by a centralised selection board that reviews the records of eligible senior chiefs; there is no examination. Advancement typically requires around twenty or more years of service. The rate was created by Congress in 1958, together with senior chief petty officer, and the first promotions took effect in 1959. The insignia consists of three gold chevrons under a rocker with an eagle and, for master chiefs, two silver stars; command master chiefs and the senior enlisted leaders of the services wear additional distinguishing stars.

Equivalents

Equivalent grades in other US services include sergeant major and command sergeant major (Army and Marine Corps) and chief master sergeant (Air Force and Space Force). Many other navies have a similarly titled senior rate derived from the same tradition.
