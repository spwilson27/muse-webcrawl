Desargues's theorem

Desargues's theorem
Field: Projective geometry
Named after: Girard Desargues
First published: 1648 (by Abraham Bosse, from Desargues' work)

In projective geometry, Desargues's theorem, named after Girard Desargues, states that two triangles are in perspective from a point if and only if they are in perspective from a line. Two triangles are said to be in perspective from a point if the lines joining corresponding vertices meet at a common point (the centre of perspectivity), and in perspective from a line if the intersections of corresponding sides lie on a common line (the axis of perspectivity).

Statement and proof

The theorem is self-dual, and can be proved easily in three dimensions: triangles lying in different planes are handled directly, and the planar case follows by lifting the configuration out of the plane. In abstract projective planes, however, the theorem need not hold; planes in which it does hold are called Desarguesian planes, and include all planes coordinatised by division rings.

History

Desargues published the result in the 17th century, and it became a foundational statement of projective geometry, closely related to the work of Pappus of Alexandria and later to Hilbert's investigations of the foundations of geometry.
