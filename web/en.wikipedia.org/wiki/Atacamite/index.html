Atacamite is a copper halide mineral with the formula Cu2Cl(OH)3. It was first described in 1801 from specimens found in the Atacama Desert of Chile, from which it takes its name.

Occurrence

Atacamite forms as a secondary mineral in the oxidised zones of copper deposits in arid climates, commonly associated with chrysocolla, brochantite and malachite. Notable localities include the Atacama region of Chile, the Burra mine in South Australia, and Tsumeb in Namibia.

Properties

It typically occurs as slender prismatic crystals, fibrous aggregates or granular masses, in shades of green. Atacamite is also the same compound responsible for the green corrosion known as "bronze disease" on ancient copper alloys.
