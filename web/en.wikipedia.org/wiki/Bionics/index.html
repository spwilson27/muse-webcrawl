Bionics

Bionics is the application of biological methods and systems found in nature to the study and design of engineering systems and modern technology. The word bionic was coined by Jack E. Steele in August 1958 while working at the Aeronautics Division House at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. It combines the Greek word bion (βίον), meaning "unit of life," with the suffix -ic, meaning "like" or "in the manner of." Closely related terms include biomimetics and biomimicry, which emphasise imitation of natural models, systems and elements.

History

Although the term dates from 1958, the practice is far older: Leonardo da Vinci studied bird flight in designing flying machines, and early naval architects modelled hulls on fish. A 1960 symposium at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base helped establish bionics as a recognised research area. In popular culture, the term was popularised by the 1970s television series The Six Million Dollar Man and The Bionic Woman, whose protagonists had electromechanical prosthetic limbs and organs.

Examples

  • Velcro — invented by George de Mestral after examining burrs clinging to his dog's fur under a microscope.
  • Shinkansen bullet train — the nose of the 500 Series was redesigned after the kingfisher's beak to reduce tunnel boom noise and improve efficiency.
  • Lotus effect — self-cleaning surfaces inspired by the water-repellent micro-structure of lotus leaves, used in paints and coatings.
  • Gecko tape — adhesives mimicking the setae on gecko feet that exploit van der Waals forces.
  • Bionic prosthetics — artificial limbs controlled by residual muscle signals, and cochlear and retinal implants restoring senses.
  • Swarming and neural algorithms — computing methods modelled on insect behaviour and nervous systems.

Subfields

Bionics is sometimes divided into areas such as: replacing or augmenting organs and limbs with mechanical versions (the medical sense), imitating natural processes in engineering design, and detecting or sensing using biological models (e.g., artificial noses, sonar modelled on bat and dolphin echolocation). Otto Schmitt developed the related concept of biomimetics in the mid-20th century.
