Aviva Stadium

The Aviva Stadium, historically known as Lansdowne Road, is a sports stadium located in Ballsbridge, Dublin, Ireland, with a capacity of 51,711. It is home to the Ireland national rugby union team and the Republic of Ireland national football team. Built on the site of the old Lansdowne Road stadium — the world's oldest international rugby ground, in use from 1872 — the stadium opened on 14 May 2010 and is owned jointly by the Irish Rugby Football Union (IRFU) and the Football Association of Ireland (FAI).

History

The original Lansdowne Road, founded by Henry Wallace Dunlop in 1872, hosted Ireland's home rugby internationals and, from the 1970s, football internationals. By the 2000s it was cramped and outdated, and the IRFU and FAI agreed on a complete rebuild. The old stadium closed in 2006 and was demolished in 2007; during construction the national teams played at Croke Park, in a landmark relaxation of the Gaelic Athletic Association's rules. The naming rights were sold to the insurer Aviva for a reported €44 million over ten years from 2009.

Design

The stadium's four-tiered, horseshoe form sweeps down at the northern end to preserve light for neighbouring houses, and its undulating roof and transparent polycarbonate and glass cladding give it a distinctive shimmering profile. It has a capacity of 51,711 for rugby and football and is rated a UEFA category 4 stadium. The Lansdowne railway station sits directly beneath the west stand.

Events

The Aviva hosted the 2011 UEFA Europa League Final (won by Porto) and the 2013 Heineken Cup Final; Leinster Rugby plays selected matches there. It was a venue for the 2023 Rugby World Cup bid that Ireland lost, and is scheduled to host matches at UEFA Euro 2028. Annual American football "Emerald Isle Classic" college games and major concerts are also held there.
