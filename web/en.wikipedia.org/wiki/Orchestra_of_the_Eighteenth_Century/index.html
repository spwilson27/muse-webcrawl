Orchestra of the Eighteenth Century

The Orchestra of the Eighteenth Century (Dutch: Orkest van de Achttiende Eeuw) is a Dutch period-instrument orchestra founded in 1981 by the recorder player and conductor Frans Brüggen together with Lucy van Dael and friends. It is one of the world's leading ensembles devoted to historically informed performance of Baroque and Classical repertoire.

History

The orchestra was formed from some fifty-five musicians drawn from the early-music community of the Netherlands and abroad. Its members are specialists on period instruments, and the ensemble assembles several times a year for concert tours and recordings rather than working as a full-time salaried body.

Repertoire and recordings

Under Brüggen the orchestra recorded extensively for Philips and later Glossa, including complete cycles of the Beethoven symphonies and works by Bach, Handel, Haydn, Mozart, Mendelssohn and Schubert. Its Beethoven cycle, using instruments of the composer's era, was particularly acclaimed. Following Brüggen's death in 2014, the orchestra continued to perform with guest conductors while preserving its founding approach.

Style

The ensemble deliberately limits itself largely to music of the 18th and early 19th centuries, played at period pitch with gut strings and historical wind instruments, seeking the balance and articulation intended by composers of the era.
