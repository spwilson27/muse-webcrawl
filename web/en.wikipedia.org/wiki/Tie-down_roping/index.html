Tie-down roping

Tie-down roping, formerly known as calf roping, is a timed rodeo event that features a calf and a rider mounted on a horse. The goal is for the rider to catch the calf by throwing a loop of rope from a lariat around its neck, dismount, run to the calf, and restrain it by tying three legs together, in as short a time as possible. The event is derived from the working skills of cowboys on ranches.

The run

The calf is released from a chute and given a head start. The roper's horse is trained to follow the calf and stop instantly when the rope is thrown and caught. The rider then dismounts, throws the calf to the ground by hand (flanking), and ties any three legs together with a short pigging string. The run ends when the rider remounts and the rope is judged secure; the tie must hold for six seconds after the rider's signal.

Horses

Roping horses, most often American Quarter Horses, are highly trained and valuable athletes, responsible for tracking the calf, stopping hard, and keeping the rope taut while the roper works on the ground.

Criticism

The event is controversial with animal welfare groups, who object to the calf being jerked by the rope and thrown; it is banned or restricted in some jurisdictions and venues.
