Guarneri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Guarneri (often referred to in the Latin form Guarnerius) is a family of distinguished luthiers from Cremona, Italy, in the 17th and 18th centuries. Their stringed instruments — alongside those of the Stradivari and Amati families — are among the most prized ever made, and are played by leading soloists to this day.

The makers

Andrea Guarneri (c. 1626–1698), an apprentice of Nicola Amati, founded the workshop. His sons Pietro Giovanni and Giuseppe Giovanni Battista continued the trade. The family's reputation culminates with Giuseppe Antonio Guarneri (1687–1744), known as del Gesù because his labels bore the IHS christogram. His late instruments, made with bold, rough-hewn workmanship and a dark, powerful tone, are considered the equals or superiors of Stradivari's.

Famous instruments

Celebrated Guarneri instruments include the Il Cannone (1743), Niccolò Paganini's favourite violin, now kept in Genoa; the Lord Wilton (1742), played by Yehudi Menuhin; and the Vieuxtemps (1741), among the most valuable violins in the world. Fewer than 200 Guarneri del Gesù instruments survive, markedly fewer than Stradivari's roughly 650.

Legacy

The power and complexity of Guarneri tone have made the family's violins the preferred instruments of many virtuosos, including Jascha Heifetz, Isaac Stern and Itzhak Perlman, and have inspired generations of modern makers copying the del Gesù models.
