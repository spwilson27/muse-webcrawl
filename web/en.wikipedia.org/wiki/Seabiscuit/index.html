Seabiscuit (May 23, 1933 – May 17, 1947) was a champion Thoroughbred racehorse in the United States who became the top money-winning racehorse up to the 1940s. He beat the 1937 Triple Crown winner, War Admiral, by four lengths in a two-horse special on November 1, 1938, in the famous "match race" at Pimlico — an event that riveted a Depression-weary nation and made the small, knobby-kneed underdog a cultural icon.

Background

Seabiscuit was a bay colt by Hard Tack out of Swing On, bred at Claiborne Farm in Kentucky. Undersized and considered lazy, he was raced hard as a two- and three-year-old in cheap claiming company before being sold in 1936 to automobile entrepreneur Charles S. Howard for $8,000. Under the quiet, unconventional trainer Tom Smith and the one-eyed, down-on-his-luck jockey John "Red" Pollard, Seabiscuit blossomed.

Racing career

From late 1936 Seabiscuit dominated the handicap ranks in the West, then conquered the East, winning major races including the Santa Anita Handicap in 1940 — after losing it twice by a nose — with Pollard back from serious injuries. His triumph over War Admiral in the 1938 Pimlico Special, where he was led head-to-head early contrary to expectations and drew away, was voted the greatest race of the century by many chroniclers; he was named American Horse of the Year for 1938.

Retirement and legacy

Seabiscuit retired to Howard's Ridgewood Ranch in California, siring 108 foals, and died of a heart attack in 1947, aged 13. His story was retold in Laura Hillenbrand's bestselling book Seabiscuit: An American Legend (2001) and the 2003 film Seabiscuit, nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture.
