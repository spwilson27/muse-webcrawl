Norges Bank (Norwegian: Norges Bank) is the central bank of Norway. Apart from having traditional central bank responsibilities such as financial stability and price stability, it manages the Government Pension Fund Global (the Norwegian sovereign wealth fund, often called the "oil fund"), one of the world's largest investment funds. The bank also manages Norway's foreign exchange reserves.

History

Norges Bank was established by Act of the Storting (the Norwegian parliament) on 14 June 1816 and opened in Trondheim, then the most practical location; it moved its head office to Christiania (now Oslo) in 1897. The bank maintained the krone on the gold standard until 1931. After the Second World War it operated within a system of regulated interest rates and credit controls; a major reform in 1985 and the new Central Bank Act of 2019 modernised its governance. Since 2001 monetary policy has targeted low and stable inflation, with an inflation target of 2 percent adopted in 2018.

Organisation

Norges Bank has two operational areas: Norges Bank Central Banking Operations, conducting monetary policy, payment systems oversight and financial stability work, and Norges Bank Investment Management (NBIM), which manages the Government Pension Fund Global. The bank's Executive Board is appointed by the King in Council; the governor is the bank's chief executive and chair of the Executive Board. Since 2020 a separate Monetary Policy and Financial Stability Committee sets the policy rate.

The Government Pension Fund Global

The fund, built from petroleum revenues, is among the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world, holding stakes in thousands of companies globally. Norges Bank manages the fund on behalf of the Ministry of Finance under an investment mandate, with ethical guidelines overseen by the Council on Ethics.
