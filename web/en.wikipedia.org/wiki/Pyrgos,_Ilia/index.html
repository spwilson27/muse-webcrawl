Pyrgos (Greek: Πύργος, meaning "tower") is a city in northwestern Peloponnese, Greece, and the capital of the Elis regional unit (historically Ilia). It lies inland a few kilometres from the Ionian Sea coast and its port of Katakolo. The name is thought to derive from a tower built in the area in the sixteenth century.

History and landmarks

The modern town grew in the Ottoman and early modern Greek periods and became the commercial centre of the fertile Elis plain, historically known for currant exports. Landmarks include the central square with the Apollon Theatre, an elegant neoclassical building designed in the nineteenth century, and the church of Saint Nicholas. The archaeological site and stadium of ancient Olympia, home of the ancient Olympic Games, lie about 20 km to the east and draw many visitors through Pyrgos and Katakolo.

Economy and transport

Agriculture — especially tomatoes, olive oil, and watermelons — underpins the local economy, together with tourism linked to Olympia and to the cruise port at Katakolo. The town is connected by road and rail with Patras and Kalamata.
