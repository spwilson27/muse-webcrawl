Key of Solomon

The Key of Solomon (Latin: Clavicula Salomonis) is a pseudoepigraphical grimoire attributed to King Solomon. It probably dates to the 14th- or 15th-century Italian Renaissance. It presents a practical system of ritual magic — the preparation of the magician, the tools and vestments, magic circles, pentacles, and conjurations for summoning and controlling spirits.

Contents

The text is typically divided into two books. The first covers conjurations, spells and curses for calling up spirits, together with the confessions and prayers the operator must recite; the second describes the instruments of the art — knives, wands, parchment, and the famous pentacles of the planets — and the sacrifices and purifications required. Numerous manuscript versions exist in Latin, French, Italian and English, with significant variations.

Influence

The Key of Solomon is one of the most influential grimoires of the Renaissance and underlies much of later Western ceremonial magic. It should not be confused with the Lesser Key of Solomon (Lemegeton), a related but distinct 17th-century compilation, or with the ancient Hygromanteia and the Testament of Solomon.
