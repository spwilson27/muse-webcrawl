Stelling van Amsterdam

The Stelling van Amsterdam (Defence Line of Amsterdam) is a 135-kilometre-long ring of fortifications around Amsterdam, Netherlands. Constructed between 1880 and 1920, it comprises 45 forts, batteries, dikes and sluices designed to protect the capital by allowing the surrounding low-lying polders to be flooded with a shallow layer of water — too deep for infantry to wade, too shallow for boats.

History

The line was the final stronghold of the "Fortress Holland" strategy: if the country were overrun, the government and army would withdraw behind the waterline. Built just before the age of aircraft and heavy artillery made such defences obsolete, it was never attacked, though it was mobilized in both world wars.

Heritage

The Stelling van Amsterdam was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996. Many forts are now used as cultural venues, nature reserves and businesses.
