Holme Valley is a large civil parish in the Metropolitan Borough of Kirklees in West Yorkshire, England. It takes its name from the River Holme, which rises on the moors above Holme village and flows north through the valley to join the River Colne at Huddersfield. The parish covers the upper valley and its tributary settlements, and its principal town is Holmfirth.

The parish was created in 1975 from the former urban districts of Holmfirth, Denby Dale, Kirkburton, Meltham and parts of others, and is administered by Holme Valley Parish Council, one of the largest parish councils in England. According to the 2011 census, the parish had a population of 34,680.

Geography

The valley lies on the eastern flank of the Pennines. Settlements in the parish include Holmfirth, Honley, Brockholes, Thurstonland, Farnley Tyas, Hepworth, Scholes, Wooldale, Thongsbridge, Netherthong, Upperthong, Meltham, Wilshaw and Holme itself. The landscape is characterised by steep-sided valleys, gritstone villages and moorland rising to the summit of Black Hill and the Pennine watershed.

History and culture

The valley developed during the Industrial Revolution as a centre of woollen textile production, with mills powered by the fast-flowing River Holme and its tributaries, and many mills later converted to other uses or housing. The area is internationally known as the filming location of the BBC sitcom Last of the Summer Wine, the world's longest-running situation comedy, which was filmed in and around Holmfirth from 1973 to 2010 and still draws visitors to the town. Bamforth & Co, based in Holmfirth, was once the world's largest producer of saucy seaside postcards and a pioneer of early film-making.

The valley suffered a catastrophic flood in May 1944, known as the Whit Monday flood, when Bilberry Reservoir burst after heavy rain, killing 81 people in Holmfirth and the surrounding settlements; an earlier reservoir failure at the same site in 1852 had also caused extensive loss of life.

Governance

Holme Valley Parish Council covers the wards of the former districts and provides local services, while strategic services are provided by Kirklees Council. The area is represented in Parliament by the Colne Valley constituency.
