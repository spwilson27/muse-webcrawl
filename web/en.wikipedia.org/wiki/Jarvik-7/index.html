Jarvik-7

The Jarvik-7 was the first artificial heart designed to be a permanent replacement for the human heart. Developed by a team led by Robert Jarvik at the University of Utah, it was pneumatically powered and made of polyurethane and aluminium.

First implantation

On 2 December 1982, surgeon William DeVries implanted the Jarvik-7 into retired dentist Barney Clark, who survived 112 days with the device. The operation drew worldwide attention and intense media coverage.

Later use

Several further patients received the Jarvik-7 as a permanent implant, with the longest surviving 620 days. Complications such as stroke and infection led the FDA to withdraw approval for permanent use in 1990; the device continued in modified form as a bridge to transplantation.
