Taíno

The Taíno were the Indigenous peoples of the Caribbean — principally the Greater Antilles, the Bahamas (where they were called Lucayans) and northern Lesser Antilles — at the time of European contact in 1492. They were agriculturalists organized into chiefdoms (cacicazgos) led by caciques, with a complex religious system centered on zemí figures and the deities Yúcahu and Atabey.

RegionGreater Antilles, Bahamas
LanguagesTaíno (Arawakan)
Main islandsHispaniola, Cuba, Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Bahamas

History

Taíno ancestors migrated from South America into the Caribbean over centuries. Within decades of Columbus's arrival, their population collapsed catastrophically from epidemic disease, enslavement and violence under the encomienda system. Despite this, Taíno ancestry, words (such as hurricane, hammock, tobacco, maize and barbecue) and cultural practices persist in modern Caribbean populations; genetic studies show substantial Indigenous ancestry in Puerto Ricans, Dominicans and Cubans, and a modern Taíno identity movement exists.
