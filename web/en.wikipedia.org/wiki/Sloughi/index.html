Sloughi

The Sloughi is a North African breed of sighthound, originating in the Maghreb — Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Libya — where it was traditionally kept by Berber and Bedouin peoples for hunting gazelle, hare and other game in the desert. It is also known as the Arabian greyhound, though it is a distinct breed from the Saluki.

The Sloughi is a lean, muscular, short-coated dog, typically sand or fawn in colour, standing about 61–72 cm at the withers. It is recognised by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI group 10) and by other kennel clubs. The breed is reserved and loyal, and needs considerable exercise.

History

Depictions of similar smooth-coated sighthounds appear in North African art and artefacts stretching back to antiquity. Numbers declined in the twentieth century, and the breed is now carefully conserved, particularly in Morocco.
