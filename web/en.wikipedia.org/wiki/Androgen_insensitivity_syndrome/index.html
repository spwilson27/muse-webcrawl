Androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS) is a condition involving the inability of cells to respond to androgens, the masculinising hormones. It is caused by mutations in the androgen receptor (AR) gene on the X chromosome and affects sexual development in people with an XY karyotype. AIS is classified as a difference of sex development (DSD) and exists on a spectrum from complete feminisation of the external genitalia to partial forms with ambiguous genitalia, to mild forms presenting mainly as male infertility.

Forms

  • Complete AIS (CAIS) — the body cannot respond to androgens at all. Affected individuals are born with female external anatomy, develop breasts at puberty (from conversion of testosterone to oestrogen), but lack a uterus and menstruation, and usually discover the condition when investigated for primary amenorrhoea or an inguinal hernia.
  • Partial AIS (PAIS) — partial responsiveness produces a spectrum of virilisation, from predominantly female to predominantly male anatomy.
  • Mild AIS (MAIS) — near-normal male development, sometimes with under-virilisation or impaired spermatogenesis.

Genetics

More than a thousand mutations in the AR gene have been reported. The condition is inherited in an X-linked recessive manner: carrier mothers pass it to half of their XY children. John Morris described the complete form in 1953 under the older name "testicular feminisation".

Management

Management is individualised and multidisciplinary, addressing gender assignment questions in partial forms, timing of gonadectomy (because of a small long-term tumour risk in retained testes), hormone replacement, vaginal hypoplasia, and psychological support. Most people with CAIS identify and live as women.
