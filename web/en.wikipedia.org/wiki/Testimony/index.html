Testimony

In law, testimony is a form of evidence that is obtained from a witness who makes a solemn statement or declaration of fact. Testimony may be oral or written, and it is usually made under oath or affirmation, so that false testimony may expose the witness to a charge of perjury.

Oral testimony

Oral testimony is given by a witness in court proceedings, in response to questions from counsel in examination-in-chief and cross-examination. The credibility of a witness's testimony may be tested by the opposing party, and the fact-finder (judge or jury) assesses its weight together with the other evidence in the case.

Written testimony

Written testimony may take the form of an affidavit, a sworn written statement of fact, or a deposition, in which a witness answers questions under oath outside the courtroom and the answers are transcribed for later use at trial.

Expert testimony

An expert witness may give opinion testimony within the field of their expertise, in contrast to ordinary witnesses, who are generally restricted to testifying about facts within their personal knowledge.

Religious usage

In a religious context, "testimony" can also refer to a personal statement of faith or religious experience, such as that shared by members of some Christian congregations.
