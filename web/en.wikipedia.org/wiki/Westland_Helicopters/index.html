Westland Helicopters

Westland Helicopters was a British aerospace company based in Yeovil, Somerset. Originally part of Westland Aircraft, it became a separate company in 1961 and specialized in helicopters, many produced under license from Sikorsky and later of its own design. It merged with Agusta of Italy in 2000 to form AgustaWestland (later Leonardo Helicopters).

History and products

Westland began helicopter production after the Second World War with license-built Sikorsky designs: the Dragonfly, Whirlwind, and Wessex. Its own designs included the Scout and Wasp, the Puma (jointly with Aérospatiale), the Sea King (license-built but extensively re-engineered), the Lynx—holder of the world helicopter speed record (400.87 km/h, set in modified form in 1986 and unbeaten for decades)—and the EH101 Merlin (with Agusta). The company also built the British Army's Apache AH1 attack helicopters under license from Boeing.

The Westland affair

In 1985–86 the company's financial difficulties triggered the "Westland affair", a political crisis in Margaret Thatcher's government over whether Westland should be rescued by an American (Sikorsky/Fiat) or European consortium; the dispute led to the resignations of Defence Secretary Michael Heseltine and Trade Secretary Leon Brittan.
