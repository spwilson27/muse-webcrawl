"Video Killed the Radio Star" is a song written by Trevor Horn, Geoff Downes, and Bruce Woolley in 1979. It was recorded first by Bruce Woolley and the Camera Club and later by the English new wave band the Buggles, whose version became a worldwide hit, topping singles charts in sixteen countries, including the UK Singles Chart. It appears on the Buggles' debut album The Age of Plastic (1980).

Composition and themes

The song's theme is nostalgia for the era of radio and anxiety about the effects of new media technology: its lyrics describe a singer whose career is displaced by television ("pictures came and broke your heart"). It was inspired in part by J. G. Ballard's short story "The Sound-Sweep". The recording features distinctive vocoder-like processed female backing vocals ("Oh-a, oh") and a bright, synth-driven production that anticipated 1980s pop.

Music video and MTV

The promotional video, directed by Russell Mulcahy, was famously the very first music video shown on MTV when the channel launched in the United States at 12:01 a.m. on 1 August 1981 — a deliberate choice matching the song's subject. A updated version was the last video shown on MTV Classic in 2023 contexts aside, the clip remains one of the most symbolic in music-television history.

Legacy

The song has been covered and sampled by numerous artists, featured in films and games, and consistently appears on lists of the defining songs of the new wave era. In 2010 a campaign brought it renewed attention as an emblem of the transition from radio to video and later to streaming culture.
