SILAC

SILAC
Full name: Stable isotope labeling by/with amino acids in cell culture
Field: Quantitative proteomics
Introduced: 2002 (Mann laboratory)

SILAC (stable isotope labeling by/with amino acids in cell culture) is a mass spectrometry-based technique for quantitative proteomics. Cells are grown in media containing "light" (natural) or "heavy" (isotopically labelled) forms of essential amino acids — commonly lysine and arginine labelled with 13C and 15N — so that all proteins in one cell population become uniformly heavier by a known mass.

Method

After several cell doublings to ensure complete incorporation, the differently labelled populations are treated or perturbed, mixed in equal amounts, and analysed together by mass spectrometry. Because heavy and light versions of each peptide appear as pairs separated by a fixed mass difference, their intensity ratio directly measures the relative abundance of the protein between conditions. Early mixing minimises experimental variation, making SILAC one of the most accurate quantitative proteomics methods.

Applications

SILAC is used to study protein expression changes, signalling pathways, protein turnover (pulsed SILAC) and protein–protein interactions. Extensions to whole model organisms ("SILAC mouse") and to cultured cells producing labelled standards ("super-SILAC") have widened its use.
