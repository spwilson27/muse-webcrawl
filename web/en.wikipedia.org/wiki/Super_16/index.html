Super 16

Super 16 is a motion picture film format that enlarges the image area of standard 16 mm film by using the space normally reserved for one row of sprocket holes. The resulting image has a native aspect ratio of approximately 1.66:1 and offers roughly 40 percent more usable image area than regular 16 mm, improving resolution when the film is blown up to 35 mm or scanned for high-definition television.

History

The format was developed by Swedish cinematographer Rune Ericson and introduced around 1969–1971. It became popular for low-budget feature films, documentaries, and television productions because 16 mm cameras and stock were cheaper and lighter than 35 mm equipment while the Super 16 negative still delivered acceptable quality for theatrical blow-up.

Use

Super 16 cameras require modified film gates and re-centred lenses, since the image is shifted toward the side of the film that carries the single row of perforations. Many well-known films and television series have been shot in Super 16, particularly independent and European productions.
