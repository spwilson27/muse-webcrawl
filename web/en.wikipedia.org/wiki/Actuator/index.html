Actuator

An actuator is a component of a machine or system that produces motion or controls a mechanism by converting energy — usually electrical, hydraulic or pneumatic — into mechanical force or displacement. Actuators are the counterpart of sensors: where a sensor measures the world, an actuator acts on it, and together they form the interface between control systems and the physical world.

Types

Common types include electric motors and solenoids; linear actuators that produce straight-line motion; hydraulic cylinders used in heavy machinery for their high force; pneumatic actuators common in process valves; and specialised devices such as piezoelectric actuators, shape-memory alloy wires and electroactive polymers used at small scales in robotics, aerospace and precision instruments.

Applications

Actuators move aircraft control surfaces, open and close industrial valves, position robotic arms, focus camera lenses and drive the read/write heads of disk drives.
