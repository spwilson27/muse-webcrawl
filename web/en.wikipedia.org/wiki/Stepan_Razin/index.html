Stepan Razin

Stepan Timofeyevich Razin (c. 1630 – 16 June 1671), known as Stenka Razin, was a Don Cossack leader who led the largest popular uprising in seventeenth-century Russia, the Razin rebellion of 1670–1671, against the tsarist bureaucracy and the boyars.

Rebellion

Razin first won fame raiding on the Volga and the Caspian Sea, including a spectacular campaign against Persia. In 1670 he turned his Cossacks, joined by runaway serfs, townspeople and non-Russian peoples of the Volga region, against the tsar's officials, capturing Tsaritsyn, Astrakhan, Saratov and Samara. His forces were finally defeated at Simbirsk in October 1670.

Execution and legacy

Captured in 1671, Razin was tortured and publicly quartered in Moscow. He became a folk hero of Russian song and legend — the subject of the famous ballad about the Persian princess — and was celebrated in Russian literature, music and film as a symbol of resistance to oppression.
