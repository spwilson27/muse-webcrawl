Egg tapping

Egg tapping, also known as egg fighting, egg knocking, egg jarping or egg boxing, is a traditional Easter game played in many countries. Two players each hold a hard-boiled egg and tap the eggs against each other; the player whose egg cracks loses, and the winner's egg proceeds to challenge others. The last uncracked egg is the champion.

History

The game has been played for centuries in Europe and beyond, and is associated with the Easter tradition of decorated eggs. In Greece the game is called tsougrisma and is played with eggs dyed red to symbolise the blood of Christ. In Bulgaria, Romania, Serbia, and other Orthodox countries, egg tapping forms part of the Easter meal, and the owner of the winning egg is said to enjoy good luck and health for the year.

Rules and variations

Players typically tap pointed end to pointed end, then round end to round end; some variants allow any part of the egg. In parts of England the game is known as "jarping," and world championships in egg jarping have been held in Peterlee, County Durham. In Louisiana, the custom called "pocking eggs" is associated with Cajun Easter celebrations, and competitions are held in towns such as Marksville. Similar games, such as egg rolling, accompany the Easter season in many regions.
