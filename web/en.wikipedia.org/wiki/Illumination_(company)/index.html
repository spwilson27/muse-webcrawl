Illumination (formerly Illumination Entertainment) is an American animation studio founded in 2007 by Chris Meledandri and owned by Universal Pictures, a division of NBCUniversal. Animation is produced largely by Illumination Studios Paris (formerly Illumination Mac Guff) in France. The studio is best known for the Despicable Me franchise, the highest-grossing animated film series of all time.

History and films

Meledandri, former president of 20th Century Fox Animation, founded the studio with a model of tight budgets and strong creative control. Its first film, Despicable Me (2010), introduced Gru and the Minions and grossed over $540 million. Sequels and spin-offs followed: Despicable Me 2 (2013), Minions (2015) and Despicable Me 3 (2017) each passed or approached $1 billion, and Minions: The Rise of Gru (2022) continued the run. Other hits include The Secret Life of Pets (2016), Sing (2016), The Grinch (2018), and The Super Mario Bros. Movie (2023), a co-production with Nintendo that grossed over $1.3 billion and became one of the highest-grossing animated films ever.

Business model

Illumination keeps production budgets far below those of Pixar or Disney — often around $70–80 million per picture — by working in Paris with French tax incentives and a streamlined pipeline, making its films exceptionally profitable relative to cost. Its features are distributed by Universal and frequently supported by major theme-park and merchandising tie-ins, notably Minion Mayhem attractions at Universal parks.
