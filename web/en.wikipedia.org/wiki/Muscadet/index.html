Muscadet

Muscadet
Type: Dry white wine
Region: Loire Valley, France (near Nantes)
Grape: Melon de Bourgogne
Appellations: Muscadet-Sèvre et Maine, Muscadet-Coteaux de la Loire, Muscadet-Côtes de Grandlieu

Muscadet is a dry French white wine produced at the western end of the Loire Valley, near Nantes, exclusively from the Melon de Bourgogne grape. Despite the similar name, it is unrelated to Muscat grapes or Muscadelle.

Production

Melon de Bourgogne was planted widely in the region after the catastrophic winter freeze of 1709 destroyed local vineyards; its frost-hardiness suited the maritime climate. The largest appellation, Muscadet-Sèvre et Maine (named for two small rivers), accounts for most production. Many wines are aged sur lie — in contact with the dead yeast lees over winter — giving a slight spritz, a fuller texture, and bready complexity.

Style and pairing

Muscadet is pale, light-bodied, high in acidity, modest in alcohol (capped at 12% by appellation rules), and subtly saline — a classic partner for oysters and other shellfish, and historically the standard wine for beurre blanc. In recent decades, single-vineyard cru communal bottlings with extended lees ageing have raised the region's reputation.
