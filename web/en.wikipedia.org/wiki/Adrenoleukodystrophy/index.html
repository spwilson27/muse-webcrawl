Adrenoleukodystrophy

Adrenoleukodystrophy
Type: Peroxisomal storage disorder
Gene: ABCD1 (X chromosome)
Symptoms: Adrenal insufficiency, neurological decline
Onset: Childhood or adulthood

Adrenoleukodystrophy (ALD) is an X-linked inherited disorder caused by mutations in the ABCD1 gene, which encodes a peroxisomal transporter protein. The defect prevents the breakdown of very long chain fatty acids, which accumulate in the nervous system and the adrenal cortex.

Signs and symptoms

The most severe form, childhood cerebral ALD, typically appears in boys between ages 4 and 10 and involves progressive demyelination of the brain, behavioural changes, vision and hearing loss, and loss of motor function. Adrenomyeloneuropathy (AMN) is a milder adult form affecting the spinal cord and peripheral nerves. Many patients also develop adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is made by measuring plasma very long chain fatty acid levels and confirmed by genetic testing. Haematopoietic stem cell transplantation can halt cerebral disease if performed early. Lorenzo's oil was developed by Augusto and Michaela Odone in an effort to slow progression in their son.
