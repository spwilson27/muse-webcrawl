Creek County, Oklahoma

County seat: Sapulpa
Largest city: Sapulpa
Founded: 1907
Named for: Creek Nation
Area: 970 sq mi (2,500 km²)

Creek County is a county located in the U.S. state of Oklahoma. Its county seat is Sapulpa. The county was named for the Creek (Muscogee) Nation, much of whose former territory it covers. Creek County is part of the Tulsa metropolitan statistical area.

History

The area was part of the Creek Nation in Indian Territory, whose people were removed from the southeastern United States in the 1830s. Creek County was created at statehood in 1907. In the early twentieth century the discovery of oil in the Glenn Pool and Cushing fields made the county one of the most productive oil regions in the United States, and towns such as Drumright boomed. The Cushing area remains a major crude oil storage and pipeline hub.

Geography

The county has a total area of about 970 square miles, of which roughly 956 square miles are land and the remainder water. The Cimarron River forms part of its northern border, and the Arkansas River runs along its northeastern edge. Keystone Lake lies partly within the county.

Communities

Cities and towns in Creek County include Sapulpa, Bristow, Drumright, Kellyville, Kiefer, Mannford, Mounds, Oilton, Shamrock, Slick and Stroud (partially).

Demographics

As of the 2020 census, the population of Creek County was approximately 72,000. Historically, the population peaked during the oil boom years of the early twentieth century.
