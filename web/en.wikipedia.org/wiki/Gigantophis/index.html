Gigantophis

Gigantophis is an extinct genus of giant snake in the family Madtsoiidae, represented by the single species Gigantophis garstini. It lived about 40 million years ago, during the late Eocene, in what is now Egypt and Algeria, around the shores of the ancient Paratethys Sea.

Kingdom: Animalia
Family: †Madtsoiidae
Genus: †Gigantophis
Species: †G. garstini
Temporal range: Late Eocene

Before the discovery of Titanoboa in 2009, Gigantophis was regarded as the largest snake ever known. Estimates based on its fossil vertebrae suggested a length of 9.3 to 10.7 m (31 to 35 ft), exceeding the modern reticulated python. Titanoboa, at roughly 13 m (43 ft), later surpassed it, but Gigantophis remains one of the largest snakes ever documented.

Discovery

The species was described by Charles William Andrews in 1901 from fossils collected in the Fayum Depression of Egypt. The fossils consist mainly of vertebrae and parts of the skull. The name honours William Garstin, a British official in Egypt. Like other madtsoiids, Gigantophis was a constrictor, presumably preying on the early mammals and large fish of the Eocene wetlands.
