Network video recorder

A network video recorder (NVR) is a specialised computer system that records video in a digital format to a disk drive, USB flash drive, SD memory card or other mass storage device. An NVR contains no dedicated video capture hardware; the software runs on a dedicated device with an embedded operating system, receiving already-encoded video streams from IP cameras over a network.

Comparison with DVRs

An NVR differs from a digital video recorder (DVR) in that a DVR records analogue camera signals that it digitises itself, whereas network cameras perform encoding at the camera and send digital streams to the NVR. This makes NVR systems more flexible in camera placement — cameras connect via Ethernet, often with Power over Ethernet (PoE) — and supports higher resolutions, since the recorder is not limited by its own capture hardware.

Functionality

NVR software typically provides live viewing of multiple cameras, continuous, scheduled, or motion-triggered recording, playback and search, remote access via web browsers or mobile applications, and event management such as alarms. Many devices include built-in PoE switch ports, multiple hard-drive bays with RAID options, and support for standard camera protocols such as ONVIF.

Hybrid systems

Hybrid video recorders accept both analogue and IP cameras, easing migration from legacy CCTV installations. Cloud-based video surveillance (VSaaS) performs the NVR function on remote servers, storing footage off-site.

Applications

NVRs are widely used in retail, banking, transportation, and home security for monitoring and evidentiary recording. Storage capacity is chosen according to camera count, resolution, frame rate, compression (such as H.264 or H.265), and the required retention period.
