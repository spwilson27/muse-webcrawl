Broken Bay is a large bay on the coast of New South Wales, Australia, about 50 km north of Sydney. It is the estuary of the Hawkesbury River, and includes the waters of Pittwater, the lower Hawkesbury and Brisbane Water.

Geography

The bay is bounded by national parks, including Ku-ring-gai Chase and Bouddi, and by the suburbs and towns of the Central Coast and Sydney's Northern Beaches. Lion Island, named for its resemblance to a sphinx, guards the entrance. The bay is popular for recreational boating and fishing.

History

Broken Bay was named by Captain James Cook in 1770 from its rugged, "broken" appearance as seen from offshore. It was explored by Governor Arthur Phillip in 1788, and the Hawkesbury became an important early farming and transport corridor.
