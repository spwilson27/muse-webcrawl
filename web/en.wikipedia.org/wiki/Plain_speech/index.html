Plain speech

Plain speech — avoiding honorifics and using "thee" and "thou" to everyone — was practised by early Quakers as a testimony of equality.

Plain speech is a practice of speaking that a number of Christian groups, notably Quakers and Anabaptists such as the Amish and Mennonites, historically maintained as part of the testimony of simplicity and equality. In English, it chiefly meant using the familiar second-person pronouns "thee" and "thou" to all persons regardless of social rank, and refusing to use honorific titles or the plural "you" as a mark of deference to an individual.

Quaker practice

Early Friends held that giving flattering titles and using "you" (then properly plural) to a single person was a form of dishonesty that upheld worldly hierarchies. They therefore addressed everyone, including social superiors and judges, as "thou" or "thee", refused to remove their hats as a gesture of deference, and did not use titles such as "my lord". Plain speech also included plain naming of days and months by number ("First-day", "Second-month") rather than by names derived from pagan gods. George Fox and other early Quakers were prosecuted and mocked for these practices, which they defended in print, including the 1660 tract A Battle-Door for Teachers & Professors to Learn Singular and Plural.

Anabaptist practice

Among the Amish and conservative Mennonites, plain speech is bound up with the use of Pennsylvania German or other German dialects in daily life and the avoidance of Hochdeutsch forms in worship contexts, as well as a general avoidance of boastful or superfluous language.

Decline

As "thou" fell out of ordinary English usage during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, plain speech became increasingly conspicuous and gradually died out among most Friends, though a few Conservative Friends maintain it. The numbered names for days and months survive in some Quaker usage, and the principle behind plain speech — speech that is truthful and egalitarian — remains part of the Quaker testimony of integrity.
