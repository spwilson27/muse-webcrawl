Status register

A typical status register with its condition flags.

A status register, also called a flag register or condition code register (CCR), is a collection of status flag bits within a processor. It is a hardware register whose individual bits are set or cleared automatically by the arithmetic logic unit (ALU) as a side effect of arithmetic and logical operations, recording information about the outcome for use by subsequent conditional instructions, especially conditional branches.

Common flags

FlagMeaning
Zero (Z)The result of the last operation was zero
Carry (C)The operation produced a carry out of (or borrow into) the most significant bit
Negative / Sign (N)The result is negative (most significant bit set, in two's complement)
Overflow (V)Signed overflow occurred — the result cannot be represented in the destination width
Parity (P)The result has even (or odd) parity
Half-carry / Auxiliary carry (H/AC)A carry occurred between the lower and upper nibbles; used for BCD adjustment

Examples

In the Intel x86 architecture the flags live in the FLAGS/EFLAGS register, including CF, PF, AF, ZF, SF, TF, IF, DF, and OF. The ARM architecture provides the CPSR (and in AArch64 the NZCV condition flags), and designs such as the MOS 6502 and Motorola 68000 maintain a compact processor status word. Not all architectures expose flags: RISC designs such as MIPS historically avoided a condition register in favor of explicit compare-and-branch instructions, reducing pipeline hazards.

Because flags are implicit results, they affect instruction scheduling and register renaming in out-of-order processors, but they remain central to writing efficient loops, comparisons, and multi-precision arithmetic.
