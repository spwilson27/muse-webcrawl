The Temple of Saturn (Latin: Templum Saturni) was an ancient Roman temple to the god Saturn, situated at the foot of the Capitoline Hill at the western end of the Roman Forum. Tradition dates its dedication to 497 BCE, in the early years of the Roman Republic, making it one of the oldest temples in Rome.

Functions

The temple housed the state treasury (aerarium Saturni), where Rome's gold and silver reserves and the standards of the legions were kept, and it served as the repository for state archives and decrees of the Senate. The festival of Saturnalia, Rome's most popular holiday, began at the temple on 17 December with a public sacrifice and banquet.

Architecture

The surviving ruins belong largely to a rebuilding of the late 4th century CE, after a fire; the prominent inscription records that the Senate and people of Rome restored the temple "consumed by fire". The high podium and eight Ionic columns of the portico remain a landmark of the Forum.
