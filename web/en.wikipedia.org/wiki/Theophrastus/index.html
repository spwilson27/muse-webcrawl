Theophrastus (c. 371 – c. 287 BC), a Greek philosopher of the Peripatetic school, was a native of Eresos in Lesbos. He was the successor to Aristotle in the Lyceum.

Life

Born Tyrtamus, he was nicknamed Theophrastus ('divine speaker') by Aristotle for the grace of his speech. He studied under Plato and Aristotle, and headed the Lyceum for 35 years, attracting some 2,000 pupils according to tradition. Diogenes Laertius preserves his will and a catalogue of his many writings, most now lost.

Works

His two surviving botanical works, Enquiry into Plants and On the Causes of Plants, founded botany as a science and earned him the title 'father of botany'. His Characters, thirty brief sketches of moral types such as the Flatterer and the Superstitious Man, influenced later character writing from La Bruyère onward.

Philosophy and science

Theophrastus wrote on logic, metaphysics, physics, meteorology, stones (On Stones, a foundation of mineralogy), fire, winds, and ethics. His doxographical work Physical Opinions preserved the views of the Presocratics for later historians of philosophy.
