Ghadir Khumm

The Ghadir Khumm (Arabic: غَدِير خُمّ) is a gathering of Muslims that took place on 16 March 632 CE (18 Dhu al-Hijjah 10 AH) at a pond (ghadir) in the Wadi Khumm, near al-Juhfa on the route between Mecca and Medina. Returning from his Farewell Pilgrimage, the Islamic prophet Muhammad stopped the caravan and delivered a sermon in which, according to the widely transmitted hadith, he declared while holding the hand of his cousin and son-in-law Ali ibn Abi Talib: "Whoever's mawla I am, Ali is his mawla" (Arabic: من كنت مولاه فهذا علي مولاه).

The event

Accounts describe Muhammad ordering a halt at midday, having a pulpit of saddles assembled, and addressing the assembled pilgrims. He reminded them of his impending death, commended the Qur'an and his household (Ahl al-Bayt) — the "two weighty things" (thaqalayn) in the associated hadith — and then pronounced the statement about Ali. Congratulators, including Umar ibn al-Khattab in some accounts, then greeted Ali.

Interpretations

The event is central to Shia Islam, which understands mawla in the sense of "master" or "guardian" and regards Ghadir Khumm as the public designation (nass) of Ali as Muhammad's successor and the first Imam. The anniversary, Eid al-Ghadir, is a major Shia festival. Sunni tradition accepts the authenticity of the hadith but interprets mawla as "friend" or "ally", understanding the sermon as an affirmation of Ali's status and a call to love and support him, prompted by complaints about Ali's conduct during the Yemen expedition, rather than an appointment to the caliphate. The Arabic word's polysemy has sustained centuries of scholarly debate.

Sources

The hadith of Ghadir is narrated through numerous chains in both Sunni and Shia collections, including the Musnad of Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and is considered mutawatir (mass-transmitted) by many scholars. Al-Tabari devoted a work to its transmission paths. Academic historians such as Maria Massi Dakake and Wilferd Madelung have treated the event's historicity as broadly credible while noting that its meaning is contested.
