Compressor map

A compressor map is a chart which shows the performance of a turbomachinery compressor. This type of compressor is used in gas turbine engines, for turbocharging reciprocating engines, and for industrial processes. The map is created from rig test results or predicted by a special computer program. Alternatively the map of a similar compressor can be suitably scaled.

Description

A compressor map typically plots pressure ratio (or pressure rise) on the vertical axis against corrected (or non-dimensional) mass flow on the horizontal axis. Superimposed on the chart are:

  • Speed lines – curves of constant corrected rotational speed, usually expressed as a percentage of design speed.
  • Efficiency contours – islands of constant adiabatic or polytropic efficiency.
  • The surge line – the boundary on the low-flow side of the map beyond which the compressor becomes aerodynamically and mechanically unstable (surge).
  • The choke line – the high-flow boundary at which flow passages reach Mach 1 and mass flow can no longer increase.

During engine operation, the compressor must operate to the right of the surge line with an adequate surge margin; a working line (running line) showing the steady-state operating points of the engine across its throttle range is often drawn on the map.

Turbocharger maps

For turbochargers, compressor maps published by manufacturers are used to match the compressor to an engine: the engine's airflow demand lines are plotted on the map to check that the operating points fall within the efficient region, away from surge at low flow (important for transient response) and from choke and overspeed at high flow.

Axial and centrifugal compressors

Maps are drawn for both axial compressors and centrifugal compressors. Axial compressor maps tend to have closely spaced speed lines with a steep surge line, while centrifugal stages achieve higher pressure ratios per stage and produce wider maps. Multistage compressors can suffer stage mismatching at part-speed, which shows on the map as a pronounced hook in the low-speed lines.
