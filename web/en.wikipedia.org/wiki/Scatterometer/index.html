Scatterometer

A scatterometer is a microwave radar sensor used to measure the reflection or scattering effect produced by a surface, most famously to determine wind speed and direction over the oceans from space. Scatterometers transmit pulses of microwave radiation toward the Earth's surface and measure the power of the backscattered return, which depends on the small-scale roughness of the sea surface caused by wind-driven capillary waves.

Principle

By observing the same patch of ocean from several different azimuth angles, the instrument can resolve both the magnitude and direction of the surface wind vector. Because the measurements are made day and night and through clouds, scatterometers provide global ocean wind data essential to weather forecasting, storm tracking, and climate studies.

Instruments

Notable scatterometers include the NASA Scatterometer (NSCAT) on ADEOS I, QuikSCAT's SeaWinds instrument (1999–2009), the ASCAT instruments on Europe's MetOp satellites, and instruments on India's Oceansat series and China's HY-2 satellites. Scatterometer data are also used to monitor sea ice extent, soil moisture, and ice-sheet changes.
