Adjective

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or noun phrase, giving information such as quality, size, age, shape, colour, quantity or origin — for example happy in "a happy child" or three in some classifications in "three cats". Adjectives are one of the traditional parts of speech in English and many other languages, though languages differ greatly in whether they have a distinct adjective class at all.

Use in English

English adjectives can appear attributively, before the noun ("the red car"), or predicatively, after a linking verb ("the car is red"). Most gradable adjectives form comparatives and superlatives either with the suffixes -er and -est or with more and most, and many derive from other word classes through suffixes such as -ful, -less, -ish and -ous, or appear as participles used adjectivally ("a broken chair"). When several adjectives precede a noun, English speakers follow a conventional ordering — opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose — rarely stated but strongly felt.

Across languages

In inflected languages adjectives typically agree with their nouns in gender, number and case (as in Latin, German and Spanish). Some languages, including many in East Asia and the indigenous languages of the Americas, express property concepts mainly with stative verbs or nouns instead, and linguists debate whether a universal category "adjective" exists. Determiners and numerals, once classed as adjectives ("demonstrative adjectives", "cardinal adjectives"), are usually treated separately in modern grammar.

Types

Common descriptive categories include qualitative adjectives, adjectives of colour, possessive adjectives, demonstrative adjectives and interrogative adjectives, though modern grammars reassign several of these to the determiner class.
