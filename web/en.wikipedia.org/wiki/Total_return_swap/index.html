Total return swap

A total return swap is a financial derivative contract in which one party (the total return payer) transfers the total economic return of a reference asset — including income such as interest or dividends plus any capital gains or losses — to another party (the total return receiver), in exchange for a fixed or floating rate payment, typically based on a benchmark such as SOFR plus a spread.

Mechanics

The receiver gains the economic exposure of owning the asset without holding it on its balance sheet, while the payer retains legal ownership and hedges its exposure. Because the receiver posts only margin rather than the full purchase price, total return swaps provide leverage and can be used to gain exposure to assets that are illiquid or hard to buy directly, such as loans or restricted securities. The parties bear counterparty credit risk on each other.

Notable episodes

Total return swaps drew wide attention in March 2021 with the collapse of Archegos Capital Management, a family office that had built tens of billions of dollars of concentrated stock exposure through swaps with several prime brokers; its failure inflicted losses exceeding ten billion dollars on banks including Credit Suisse and Nomura, and prompted regulatory scrutiny of disclosure rules for swap-based positions.
