The Sami languages (also Saami or Sámi) are a group of Uralic languages spoken by the Sami people in northern parts of Norway, Sweden, Finland, and the Kola Peninsula of Russia, a region known as Sápmi. They form a dialect continuum running from south to northeast, conventionally divided into some nine or ten distinct languages, most of which are seriously endangered.

Classification

The Sami languages are usually grouped into a western branch — Southern Sami, Ume Sami, Pite Sami, Lule Sami, and Northern Sami — and an eastern branch comprising Inari Sami, Skolt Sami, Akkala Sami (extinct since 2003), Kildin Sami, and Ter Sami. Neighbouring languages shade into one another, but the extremes are not mutually intelligible. Northern Sami, with around 20,000 speakers, accounts for the great majority of all speakers; by contrast Pite, Ume, and Ter Sami each have only a few dozen fluent speakers at most. Sami is most closely related to the Finnic languages (Finnish, Estonian), with which it shares features such as consonant gradation, though millennia of contact have layered heavy Scandinavian and Finnic loanwords into the vocabulary.

Orthography and status

Sami languages are written in Latin-based alphabets with added letters such as á, č, đ, ŋ, š, ŧ, and ž. Norway, Sweden, and Finland all recognise Sami as an indigenous minority language; Northern Sami has official status in designated administrative districts in all three countries, and the Sami parliaments promote education and media. The Norwegian Sami Language Act (1990) and school reforms ended the era of assimilationist "Norwegianisation," during which Sami children were punished for speaking their language in boarding schools.

Revitalisation

All Sami languages are classified by UNESCO as endangered, several severely or critically. Revitalisation efforts include language nests (immersion preschools), university programmes at Tromsø and Kautokeino, broadcasting by NRK Sápmi and Yle, and a growing body of Sami literature, film, and popular music. The total number of speakers across all Sami languages is estimated at roughly 30,000, out of an ethnic Sami population several times larger.
