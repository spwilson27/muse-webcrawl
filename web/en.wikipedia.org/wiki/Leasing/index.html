Leasing

Leasing is a process by which a firm or individual acquires the use of fixed assets — such as vehicles, aircraft, machinery, or information-technology equipment — under a contract (lease) that confers the right to use the asset for an agreed period in exchange for a series of payments, without transferring ownership. Leasing is a major source of medium- and long-term financing, particularly for aircraft, shipping, and rolling stock.

Types of lease

  • Finance (capital) lease — the lessee bears most risks and rewards of ownership; the payments effectively amortise the asset's cost, and the asset is recognised on the lessee's balance sheet.
  • Operating lease — a shorter rental arrangement in which the lessor retains ownership risks; common for aircraft and vehicle fleets.
  • Sale and leaseback — an owner sells an asset and immediately leases it back, releasing capital while retaining use.
  • Leveraged lease — the lessor finances most of the purchase price with debt secured on the asset and the lease payments.
  • Wet and dry leases — in aviation, a wet lease includes crew, maintenance, and insurance; a dry lease provides the aircraft only.

Reasons for leasing

Businesses lease to conserve capital, to match payments to an asset's useful life, to transfer obsolescence risk, and for flexibility in upgrading equipment. Tax treatment historically favoured leasing in some jurisdictions, and airlines in particular operate large parts of their fleets under lease; the two largest aircraft lessors manage hundreds of airframes.

Accounting

Under IFRS 16 (effective 2019) and the corresponding US standard ASC 842, most leases must now be recognised on the lessee's balance sheet as a right-of-use asset and a lease liability, ending the era in which operating leases were a major form of off-balance-sheet financing.
