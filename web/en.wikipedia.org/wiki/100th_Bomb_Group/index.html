The 100th Bombardment Group (Heavy) was a B-17 Flying Fortress unit of the United States Army Air Forces during the Second World War. Assigned to the Eighth Air Force in England and based at RAF Thorpe Abbotts in Norfolk from June 1943, it flew strategic bombing missions over occupied Europe and Germany — and suffered such heavy losses that it became known as "The Bloody Hundredth".

History

The group flew its first combat mission on 25 June 1943 against Bremen. It took part in the costly Regensburg–Schweinfurt operations of August 1943 and in the Münster raid of 10 October 1943, from which only one of its thirteen aircraft returned to base. On 6 March 1944, during the first major American daylight raid on Berlin, the group lost fifteen bombers in a single day. Its squadrons were the 349th, 350th, 351st and 418th Bombardment Squadrons, identified by the "Square D" tail marking.

Despite its losses, the group also earned Distinguished Unit Citations for missions including the attack on an aircraft factory at Regensburg and operations supporting the Normandy landings and the Battle of the Bulge. It flew its last combat mission on 20 April 1945 and participated in food drops to the starving population of the Netherlands.

Legacy

The experiences of the 100th were dramatised in the 2024 Apple TV+ miniseries Masters of the Air, which follows its airmen, including Gale "Buck" Cleven and John "Bucky" Egan. A museum now occupies the control tower at the former Thorpe Abbotts airfield.
