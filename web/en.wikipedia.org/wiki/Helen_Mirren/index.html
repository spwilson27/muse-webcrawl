Helen Mirren, one of the most decorated actresses of her generation.

Dame Helen Mirren (born 26 July 1945) is an English actress whose career spans six decades on stage, screen, and television. She is one of the few performers to have achieved the Triple Crown of Acting in both the United States and the United Kingdom, having won an Academy Award, a Tony Award, an Olivier Award, a BAFTA, and multiple Emmy Awards. She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for playing Queen Elizabeth II in The Queen (2006).

Early life and stage career

Born in London to an English mother and a Russian émigré father, Mirren joined the National Youth Theatre and then the Royal Shakespeare Company, where her Cleopatra (1965) made her reputation while still in her teens. She worked with Peter Brook's experimental theatre company in the early 1970s.

Film and television

Early films included Age of Consent (1969), O Lucky Man! (1973), The Long Good Friday (1980), and Excalibur (1981). Her portrayal of DCI Jane Tennison in the ITV series Prime Suspect (1991–2006) made her a television institution. Later acclaimed work includes Gosford Park (2001), Calendar Girls (2003), Elizabeth I (2005), The Queen (2006), Hitchcock (2012), Woman in Gold (2015), and Golda (2023). She was appointed a Dame in 2003 and won the Tony and Olivier for playing Elizabeth II again in The Audience.
