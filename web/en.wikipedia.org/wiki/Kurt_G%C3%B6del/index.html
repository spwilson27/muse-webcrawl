Kurt Gödel

Born: 28 April 1906, Brünn, Austria-Hungary
Died: 14 January 1978, Princeton, New Jersey
Field: Mathematical logic

Kurt Friedrich Gödel (1906–1978) was a logician, mathematician, and philosopher widely regarded as one of the most significant logicians in history. Born in Brünn (now Brno), he studied at the University of Vienna and emigrated to the United States in 1940, joining the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.

Incompleteness theorems

In 1931 Gödel published his incompleteness theorems, showing that any consistent formal system capable of expressing basic arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove, and cannot prove its own consistency. The results ended the Hilbert program's hope of a complete, self-verifying foundation for mathematics.

Other work

Gödel proved the completeness of first-order logic, showed the consistency of the axiom of choice and the continuum hypothesis with set theory, and constructed the Gödel metric, an exact solution of Einstein's field equations permitting closed timelike curves. He became close friends with Albert Einstein at Princeton.
