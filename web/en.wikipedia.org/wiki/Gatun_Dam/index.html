The Gatun Dam is a large earthen dam across the Chagres River in Panama, near the town of Gatun, built between 1907 and 1913 as a key element of the Panama Canal. By impounding the Chagres it created Gatun Lake, which at completion was the largest artificial lake in the world, and which forms the elevated central reach of the canal through which ships transit 26 metres above sea level.

Construction

The dam is roughly 2.3 kilometres long and about 800 metres thick at its base, built from rock and clay fill excavated during canal construction. Its central concrete spillway, with 14 radial gates, regulates the level of Gatun Lake during the Chagres' violent floods. At the time of completion it was the largest earthen dam in the world. A hydroelectric station at the dam, commissioned in 1913–1914, supplies power for the canal's locks and machinery. The adjacent Gatun Locks raise and lower ships between the lake and the Caribbean Sea.

Significance

The success of the Gatun scheme vindicated the lock-canal design chosen by the United States over a sea-level canal. Gatun Lake's water remains the canal's lifeblood: each transit consumes roughly 200 million litres of fresh water, making the watershed and lake level a strategic concern during droughts.
