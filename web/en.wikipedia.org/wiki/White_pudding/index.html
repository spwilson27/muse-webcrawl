White pudding

White pudding, oatmeal pudding, or (in Scotland) skirlie in some forms, is a traditional dish of Scotland, Ireland, and northern England consisting of suet, oatmeal or barley, breadcrumbs, and in some varieties pork meat and pork liver, filled into a sausage casing. It is very similar to black pudding, but unlike black pudding it contains no blood.

History and preparation

White pudding is one of a family of cereal-based puddings developed to make use of slaughter by-products and staple grains. Medieval recipes combined grains, suet, and spices, sometimes with meat; regional versions survive across the British Isles. Scottish white pudding is typically made with beef suet, oatmeal, onions, and spices, while Irish versions often include pork and pork liver, bread, and milk. In Scotland a battered white pudding supper is a fish-and-chip shop staple, and white pudding is a component of the traditional full Scottish and Irish breakfasts.

Regional varieties

In Cornwall and Devon, "hog's pudding" is a related spiced sausage of pork, fat, and bread. In Ireland, white pudding is commonly ring-shaped and seasoned with white pepper. French boudin blanc is a finer, unrelated preparation.
