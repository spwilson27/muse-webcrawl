Sher Ali Khan (c. 1825 – 21 February 1879) was Emir of Afghanistan from 1863 to 1866 and again from 1868 until his death in 1879. A son of Dost Mohammad Khan, the founder of the Barakzai dynasty, his reign was dominated by the struggle for the succession after his father's death and by the rivalry between Britain and Russia known as the "Great Game", which culminated in the Second Anglo-Afghan War.

Succession struggle

Dost Mohammad designated Sher Ali as his successor, but on his death in 1863 his elder brothers challenged the succession. Sher Ali was defeated at Kujabaz in 1866 and lost Kabul to Mohammad Afzal Khan and then Mohammad Azam Khan. With the deaths of both rivals, Sher Ali regained the throne in 1868 and was recognised by the British government in India.

Relations with Britain and Russia

Through the 1870s Sher Ali sought British support against Russian expansion in Central Asia. When a Russian mission under General Stoletov was received in Kabul in 1878 while a British mission was turned back at the Khyber Pass, the Viceroy, Lord Lytton, issued an ultimatum. Sher Ali's failure to accept a permanent British resident in Kabul led Britain to invade in November 1878, opening the Second Anglo-Afghan War. Sher Ali fled north, hoping for Russian help, and died at Mazar-i-Sharif in February 1879. His son Mohammad Yaqub Khan succeeded him and signed the Treaty of Gandamak.
