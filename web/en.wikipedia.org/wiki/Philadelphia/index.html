Philadelphia, commonly called Philly, is the largest city in the U.S. state of Pennsylvania and the sixth-most populous city in the United States, with about 1.58 million residents. It is the economic and cultural centre of the Delaware Valley and serves as the county seat of Philadelphia County, with which the city is coterminous.

Philadelphia was founded in 1682 by William Penn, an English Quaker, as the capital of the Pennsylvania Colony. Its name is Greek for "brotherly love" (philos, loving, and adelphos, brother). The city played a central role in the American Revolution: the Declaration of Independence was adopted there on 4 July 1776, and the United States Constitution was drafted at the Philadelphia Convention of 1787. Philadelphia served as the nation's capital from 1790 to 1800 while Washington, D.C. was under construction. Independence Hall and the Liberty Bell remain its best-known landmarks.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the west bank of the Delaware River near its confluence with the Schuylkill River, roughly 80 miles southwest of New York City. Its economy is anchored by higher education and medicine ("eds and meds"), the University of Pennsylvania, financial services, and the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industries. Comcast, headquartered in Philadelphia, is one of the largest media companies in the world.

Culture

Philadelphia is known for the cheesesteak, soft pretzels, the Philadelphia Museum of Art (whose steps were made famous by the film Rocky), the Eagles (NFL), Phillies (MLB), 76ers (NBA) and Flyers (NHL), and the annual Mummers Parade. The city has more outdoor sculpture and murals than any other American city and one of the nation's oldest art museums, the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts.
