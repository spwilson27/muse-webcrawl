Chaperone (protein)

In molecular biology, molecular chaperones are proteins that assist the conformational folding or unfolding of other proteins and the assembly or disassembly of macromolecular structures. Chaperones do not form part of the final structure of their clients; instead they prevent inappropriate aggregation of unfolded polypeptide chains and guide them toward productive folding, using energy from ATP hydrolysis in many cases.

The GroEL/GroES chaperonin provides an isolated chamber in which a single protein can fold.

Function

Newly synthesised proteins emerge from the ribosome as extended chains with exposed hydrophobic regions that are prone to misfolding and aggregation, particularly under conditions of heat or other stress. Chaperones bind these regions, shield them, and either allow the chain to fold, actively unfold misfolded species for another attempt, or target terminally misfolded proteins for degradation. Many chaperones are heat shock proteins (HSPs), named because their expression increases after heat shock.

Major families

  • Hsp70 (DnaK in bacteria): binds short hydrophobic stretches of nascent and unfolded proteins, working with J-domain co-chaperones and nucleotide exchange factors.
  • Chaperonins (Hsp60): large barrel-shaped complexes — GroEL/GroES in bacteria, TRiC/CCT in eukaryotes — that encapsulate a single client protein.
  • Hsp90: assists the late folding and activation of signalling proteins such as steroid hormone receptors and kinases.
  • Small heat shock proteins: ATP-independent "holdases" that bind unfolded proteins and prevent aggregation.
  • Hsp100/ClpB: disaggregases that extract proteins from aggregates.

Medical relevance

Failure of chaperone systems contributes to diseases of protein misfolding, including Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases. Conversely, cancer cells depend on Hsp90 to stabilise mutant oncoproteins, making chaperones targets for drug development. Mutations in chaperones themselves cause "chaperonopathies," such as hereditary spastic paraplegia and certain cataracts.
