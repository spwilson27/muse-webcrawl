Uttarakhand


The Kedarnath valley in the Garhwal Himalaya

Uttarakhand is a state in northern India, often called the Devbhumi ("Land of the Gods") for its many Hindu temples and pilgrimage centres. It was carved out of the northwestern districts of Uttar Pradesh on 9 November 2000 (initially named Uttaranchal, renamed Uttarakhand in 2007) following a long regional movement. Its interim capital is Dehradun; the planned capital is Gairsain. The state covers about 53,500 km² and has a population of roughly 10 million.

Geography

Mostly mountainous, Uttarakhand comprises the Garhwal division in the west and Kumaon in the east, rising from Terai plains through the Shivalik hills to Greater Himalayan peaks such as Nanda Devi (7,816 m). The Ganges and Yamuna both rise here, at Gangotri and Yamunotri. Forests cover about two-thirds of the state; Corbett National Park, India's oldest national park (1936), and the Valley of Flowers and Nanda Devi UNESCO World Heritage Sites lie within it.

Culture and economy

Hindi is the official language, with Sanskrit also official; Garhwali and Kumaoni are widely spoken. The Char Dham pilgrimage (Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, Badrinath) draws millions. Tourism, hydropower, agriculture in the valleys, and growing services and education in Dehradun and Haridwar anchor the economy. The state remains disaster-prone: catastrophic floods and landslides in June 2013 killed thousands around Kedarnath.
