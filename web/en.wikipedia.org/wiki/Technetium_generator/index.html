Technetium generator

A technetium generator is a device used to extract the metastable isotope technetium-99m (99mTc) from a source of decaying molybdenum-99 (99Mo). Because 99mTc has a half-life of only about six hours, it cannot be stockpiled; the generator, sometimes called a "moly cow," lets hospitals produce it on site from the longer-lived parent isotope (half-life about 66 hours).

Operation

Molybdenum-99 is adsorbed onto an alumina column inside lead shielding. As it decays, it produces 99mTc, which is less tightly bound to the alumina and can be washed off ("eluted" or "milked") with saline solution. The resulting sodium pertechnetate solution is then used directly or bound into radiopharmaceutical kits for diagnostic imaging.

Importance

Technetium-99m is used in tens of millions of nuclear medicine procedures each year, making the generator a cornerstone of diagnostic imaging. Supply depends on a small number of research reactors that produce molybdenum-99, which has caused periodic global shortages.
