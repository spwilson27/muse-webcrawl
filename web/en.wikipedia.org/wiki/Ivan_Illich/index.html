Ivan Illich

Ivan Dominic Illich (4 September 1926 – 2 December 2002) was an Austrian philosopher, Roman Catholic priest, and social critic. Born in Vienna, he studied in Rome and Salzburg and worked as a priest among Puerto Rican parishioners in New York before founding the Centro Intercultural de Documentación (CIDOC) in Cuernavaca, Mexico, in 1961.

Illich became internationally known in the 1970s for a series of radical critiques of the institutions of modern society: Deschooling Society (1971) attacked compulsory schooling; Tools for Conviviality (1973) proposed limits on industrial technology; Energy and Equity (1974) examined transport; and Medical Nemesis (1975) argued that institutional medicine had become a threat to health through "iatrogenesis". His later work included Shadow Work, Gender, and H2O and the Waters of Forgetfulness.

Ideas

Central to Illich's thought is the concept of counterproductivity: beyond a certain threshold, institutions produce the opposite of their stated aims — schools stupefy, medicine sickens, transport immobilises. He opposed "radical monopoly", in which a single institutional form of a good drives out vernacular alternatives, and advocated "convivial" tools that enlarge personal autonomy. His ideas influenced the appropriate-technology, deschooling and degrowth movements.

Later life

After CIDOC closed in 1976, Illich divided his time between Mexico, Germany and the United States, teaching at several universities. He died in Bremen, Germany, in 2002.
