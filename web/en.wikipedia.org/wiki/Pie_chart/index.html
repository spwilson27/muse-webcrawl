Pie chart

An example of a pie chart, with slices proportional to the quantities they represent.

A pie chart (or a circle chart) is a circular statistical graphic which is divided into slices to illustrate numerical proportion. In a pie chart, the arc length of each slice (and consequently its central angle and area) is proportional to the quantity it represents. While it is named for its resemblance to a pie which has been sliced, there are variations on the way it can be presented.

History

The earliest known pie chart is generally credited to William Playfair in his Statistical Breviary of 1801, which showed the proportions of the Turkish Empire located in Asia, Europe and Africa. Florence Nightingale popularised a variant, the polar area diagram or "coxcomb", in 1858 to present the causes of mortality in the Crimean War.

Variants

  • Doughnut chart — a pie chart with the centre cut out, often used to display additional information in the blank centre.
  • Exploded pie chart — one or more slices separated from the rest for emphasis.
  • Polar area diagram — equal angles with radii varying by value.
  • 3D pie chart — rendered with a three-dimensional effect, generally discouraged because perspective distorts the proportions.

Criticism

Statistical experts such as Edward Tufte have criticised pie charts, because readers are poor at comparing angles and areas, especially across separate charts; bar charts usually communicate proportions more accurately. Nevertheless, pie charts remain extremely common in business and media for showing a small number of parts of a whole.
