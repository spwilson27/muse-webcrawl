Formation:1974
Type:Intergovernmental research organisation
Headquarters:Heidelberg, Germany
Sites:Heidelberg, Hamburg, Grenoble, Hinxton (EMBL-EBI), Rome, Barcelona

The European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) is an intergovernmental organisation dedicated to molecular biology research and supported by more than 20 member states. Founded in 1974, it is one of Europe's leading life-science research institutions, with its main laboratory in Heidelberg, Germany, and additional sites across Europe.

Activities

EMBL pursues fundamental research in molecular biology, provides services and facilities — including synchrotron beamlines, microscopy, and bioinformatics resources — to the wider scientific community, trains scientists at all career stages, and promotes technology transfer. Its outstation in Hinxton, the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI), operates many of the world's core biological databases.

History

The laboratory grew out of the European Molecular Biology Organization (EMBO), founded in 1964 to strengthen molecular biology in Europe. An intergovernmental agreement in 1973 established EMBL, and the Heidelberg laboratory opened in 1978. Further sites followed, most recently the Barcelona unit, established in 2017 for tissue biology research.
