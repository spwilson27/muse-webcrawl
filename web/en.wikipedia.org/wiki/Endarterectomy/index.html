Endarterectomy
SpecialtyVascular surgery
PurposeRemoval of atherosclerotic plaque from an artery
Common sitesCarotid, femoral, coronary and pulmonary arteries

Endarterectomy is a surgical procedure to remove atherosclerotic plaque from the inner wall of an artery that has become narrowed or blocked. The artery is opened (arteriotomy) and the plaque is dissected away together with the inner layer (intima and part of the media) of the vessel wall, restoring blood flow. It is distinguished from thrombectomy and embolectomy, which remove clot rather than chronic plaque.

Types

The best-known form is carotid endarterectomy, performed on the carotid artery in the neck to reduce the risk of stroke in patients with significant stenosis, shown to be beneficial in trials such as NASCET and ECST. Other forms include femoral endarterectomy for peripheral artery disease, coronary endarterectomy as an adjunct to bypass surgery, and pulmonary endarterectomy—a specialised operation for chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension.

Risks and alternatives

Risks include stroke, myocardial infarction, bleeding, nerve injury and restenosis. Depending on the vessel and the patient, alternatives include angioplasty with stenting (such as carotid stenting), bypass surgery, and best medical therapy with antiplatelet agents and statins.
