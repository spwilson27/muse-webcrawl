Métis Nation

Métis Nation
Homeland: Métis Homeland, north-central North America
Representative body: Métis National Council (1983)
Historic centre: Red River Settlement

The Métis Nation is the political and cultural collectivity of the Métis people, whose homeland stretches across the Canadian Prairies and into Ontario, British Columbia, the Northwest Territories, and the northern United States. The Métis Nation emerged in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries around the fur trade, with the Red River Settlement as its heartland.

Governance

The Métis National Council was founded in 1983 to represent the Métis Nation at the national level; its governing members have included the Manitoba Métis Federation, the Métis Nation of Alberta, the Métis Nation–Saskatchewan, the Métis Nation of Ontario, and the Métis Nation British Columbia. Citizenship in the Métis Nation is generally defined by descent from the historic Métis communities, self-identification, and community acceptance.

Rights and agreements

Following constitutional recognition in 1982 and court victories such as Powley and Manitoba Métis Federation v. Canada (2013), Canada entered framework negotiations with Métis governments; self-government agreements were signed with the Métis Nation of Alberta, Métis Nation of Ontario, and Métis Nation–Saskatchewan in 2019, and the Manitoba Métis Federation signed a self-government agreement in 2021.

Symbols

The Métis flag, bearing a white infinity symbol on blue or red, dates to the early nineteenth century and remains the best-known emblem of the Métis Nation.
