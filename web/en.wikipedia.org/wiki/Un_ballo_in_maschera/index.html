Un ballo in maschera

Un ballo in maschera ("A Masked Ball"), opera in three acts by Giuseppe Verdi, libretto by Antonio Somma. Premiere: Teatro Apollo, Rome, 17 February 1859.

Un ballo in maschera (A Masked Ball) is an 1859 opera in three acts by Giuseppe Verdi. The text by Antonio Somma was based on Eugène Scribe's libretto for Daniel Auber's 1833 opera Gustave III, ou Le bal masqué, which dramatized the assassination of King Gustav III of Sweden in 1792.

Composition and censorship

Verdi originally intended the opera for Naples under the title Gustavo III. After the attempted assassination of Napoleon III in 1858, Neapolitan censors demanded extensive changes, including moving the setting and demoting the king to a lesser rank. Verdi refused and withdrew the work; the dispute ended in court before the opera was instead staged in Rome, where censors required the action to be transferred to colonial Boston, with the king transformed into "Riccardo, Earl of Warwick," governor of Boston.

Plot

Riccardo loves Amelia, the wife of his secretary and friend Renato. The fortune-teller Ulrica prophesies that Riccardo will be killed by the first man to shake his hand — Renato. A conspiracy of courtiers, Amelia's discovery in a nocturnal meeting, and Renato's wounded honour lead to the assassination at a masked ball, where Renato stabs Riccardo, who with his dying breath forgives him and reveals that his love for Amelia was innocent.

Roles and music

Principal roles include Riccardo (tenor), Amelia (soprano), Renato (baritone), Ulrica (contralto), Oscar the page (soprano), and the conspirators Samuel and Tom (basses). Celebrated numbers include Riccardo's "La rivedrà nell'estasi," Renato's "Eri tu," and the ensemble finales. The opera is frequently performed today, sometimes restored to its Swedish setting.
