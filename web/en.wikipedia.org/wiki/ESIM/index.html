eSIM

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a form of SIM card that is embedded directly into a device as a surface-mounted chip (an eUICC) rather than being a removable card. Instead of physically swapping cards, users download operator profiles over the air, allowing them to switch carriers or plans through software. The specification is maintained by the GSMA.

Technology

The eSIM chip, typically in an MFF2 package measuring about 5 × 6 mm, is soldered during manufacture. Profiles are provisioned remotely using the GSMA's Remote SIM Provisioning architecture: the consumer variant (SGP.22) uses a Local Profile Assistant on the device, commonly activated by scanning a QR code, while the machine-to-machine variant (SGP.02) is managed from a subscription manager platform. Multiple profiles can be stored, with one or (in dual-SIM devices) two active at a time.

Adoption

eSIMs first appeared in consumer devices in devices such as the Samsung Gear S2 smartwatch (2016) and the Google Pixel 2 and Apple iPhone XS (2017–2018). Apple removed the physical SIM tray from US-market iPhone 14 models in 2022, making them eSIM-only. eSIMs are widely used in IoT and automotive applications, and travel-oriented providers sell data-only eSIM profiles for roaming. Criticisms include reduced user control and transfer friction compared with physical SIM cards.
