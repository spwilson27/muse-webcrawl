Bass guitar

The bass guitar, also called the electric bass, is the lowest-pitched member of the guitar family. It is a plucked string instrument normally played with the fingers or a pick, and since the 1950s it has largely replaced the double bass in popular music as the principal bass instrument of the rhythm section. A standard bass guitar has four strings tuned in fourths (E1–A1–D2–G2), one octave below the four lowest strings of a standard guitar, although five- and six-string models are common.

History

Experiments with a fretted, guitar-style bass go back to the 1930s, notably Paul Tutmarc's Audiovox Model 736 Electronic Bass. The instrument became practical and popular with Leo Fender's Precision Bass, introduced in 1951 — the first mass-produced solid-body electric bass. Its fretted neck allowed guitarists to play bass easily (hence "Precision"), and its solid body eliminated feedback at high volumes. Fender's Jazz Bass followed in 1960, and other makers such as Gibson, Rickenbacker and Music Man produced influential designs.

Technique and role

Bassists typically pluck the strings with the index and middle fingers, play with a plectrum, or use techniques such as slap and pop (associated with funk players like Larry Graham) and tapping. The bass provides the link between the harmonic/rhythmic foundation and the drums: bass lines outline chord roots and rhythms, driving genres from rock and jazz to funk, reggae and metal. Noted virtuosos include Jaco Pastorius, James Jamerson, Paul McCartney, John Entwistle, Geddy Lee, Flea and Victor Wooten.

Variants

Variants include fretless basses, acoustic-electric bass guitars, extended-range instruments with five or more strings, short-scale basses, and the bass VI. Amplification is provided by dedicated bass amplifiers and speaker cabinets designed for low frequencies, which relate to the reproduction of bass sound generally.
