Symplectic manifold

In mathematics, a symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold M equipped with a closed, nondegenerate differential 2-form ω, called the symplectic form. The study of symplectic manifolds is called symplectic geometry or symplectic topology. Symplectic manifolds arise naturally in abstract formulations of classical mechanics and in particular in the Hamiltonian formulation, where the phase space of a mechanical system is a symplectic manifold (typically the cotangent bundle of the configuration space).

Definition

A symplectic form on a manifold M is a smooth differential 2-form ω satisfying two conditions:

  • Closedness: dω = 0, where d is the exterior derivative.
  • Nondegeneracy: for every point p in M, the pairing induced by ω on the tangent space TpM is nondegenerate; equivalently, ωn is a volume form where 2n is the dimension of M.

A manifold equipped with such a form is necessarily even-dimensional and orientable.

Darboux's theorem

Darboux's theorem states that every symplectic manifold locally looks like standard Euclidean space ℝ2n with the standard symplectic form ω = Σ dxi ∧ dyi. Consequently, symplectic manifolds have no local invariants: symplectic geometry is inherently global, in contrast to Riemannian geometry, where curvature provides local invariants.

Hamiltonian mechanics

Given a smooth function H : M → ℝ, called the Hamiltonian, the nondegeneracy of ω defines a unique vector field XH by the equation ιXHω = dH. The flow of this vector field describes the time evolution of the mechanical system, and Hamilton's equations are recovered in Darboux coordinates. Liouville's theorem, that the symplectic volume is preserved by Hamiltonian flows, follows from the closedness of ω.

Examples

  • Cotangent bundles T*Q with the canonical symplectic form.
  • Kähler manifolds, whose imaginary part of the Hermitian metric gives a symplectic form.
  • Complex projective space with the Fubini–Study form.
  • Coadjoint orbits of Lie groups.
