Adagia

The Adagia (singular adagium) is a compilation of Greek and Latin proverbs, compiled by the Dutch humanist Erasmus of Rotterdam and first published in 1500. It grew over successive editions from about 800 proverbs in the modest Paris first edition (Collectanea Adagiorum) to more than 4,200 entries in the final editions printed at Basel. Each adage is accompanied by Erasmus's commentary explaining its origin, meaning and application, making the work as much a series of essays as a reference book.

Content and growth

Working with the Venetian printer Aldus Manutius, Erasmus published the greatly expanded Adagiorum Chiliades ("Thousands of Adages") in 1508, containing over 3,000 entries. Later editions printed by Johann Froben in Basel added still more, reaching 4,151 proverbs by 1536. Famous entries include "In the land of the blind, the one-eyed man is king", "Necessity is the mother of invention" (in its classical antecedents), "Festina lente" ("make haste slowly") and the long political essay-adage "Dulce bellum inexpertis" ("War is sweet to those who have not experienced it").

Influence

The Adagia was one of the best-selling books of the 16th century and carried hundreds of classical proverbs into the vernacular languages of Europe; many phrases still current in English entered the language through Erasmus's collection and its translations. Its commentaries also served as a vehicle for Erasmus's views on education, politics, war and the Church, blending philology with moral and satirical reflection.
