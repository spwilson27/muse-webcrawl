Argentine economic crisis (1999–2002)

The Argentine economic crisis of 1999–2002 was a severe economic depression in Argentina, arising from the collapse of the currency board system that had pegged the peso one-to-one to the US dollar since 1991. GDP shrank by roughly a fifth between 1998 and 2002.

Causes

The fixed peg, rising public debt, capital flight, and external shocks including the 1997 Asian crisis and Brazil's 1999 devaluation undermined the convertibility regime. A recession from 1999 deepened as the government struggled to service its debts.

Crisis

In December 2001 the government froze bank withdrawals (the corralito), provoking mass protests (cacerolazos); President Fernando de la Rúa resigned on 20 December 2001. Argentina defaulted on roughly $93 billion of sovereign debt—the largest default then recorded—and in January 2002 abandoned the peg; the peso lost most of its value and unemployment exceeded 20 percent.

Recovery

Under presidents Eduardo Duhalde and Néstor Kirchner, the economy recovered rapidly from 2003, aided by a competitive exchange rate and high commodity prices.
