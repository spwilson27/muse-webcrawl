Yamaha Corporation

Yamaha Corporation (ヤマハ株式会社)
Founded: 1887
Founder: Torakusu Yamaha
Headquarters: Hamamatsu, Shizuoka, Japan
Industry: Musical instruments, audio equipment
Products: Pianos, guitars, synthesizers, audio systems

Yamaha Corporation is a Japanese multinational conglomerate and the world's largest manufacturer of musical instruments, including pianos, electronic keyboards, guitars, brass and woodwind instruments, and drums, as well as professional and consumer audio equipment. It is headquartered in Hamamatsu, Shizuoka. The company's three-crossed-tuning-fork emblem dates to 1897.

History

Torakusu Yamaha, a watchmaker and medical-equipment repairman, built his first reed organ in 1887 after repairing a broken American organ at a school in Hamamatsu. In 1897 he founded Nippon Gakki Co., Ltd. ("Japan Musical Instruments"), which began producing upright pianos in 1900 and grand pianos in 1902. The company expanded into audio equipment, semiconductors, sporting goods, and furniture over the twentieth century; in 1987, on its centenary, it renamed itself Yamaha Corporation after its founder. Its motorcycle division, established in 1955, became the separate company Yamaha Motor Co.

Products

Yamaha is especially known for its pianos (including the CFX concert grand and the U-series uprights), the DX7 digital synthesizer of 1983 that popularized FM synthesis, the Disklavier reproducing piano, Pacifica and Revstar guitars, and the YZF motorcycle heritage of its former division. It also runs one of the world's largest music-school networks, with Yamaha Music Schools enrolling hundreds of thousands of students, and sponsors the Yamaha Music Foundation. Yamaha acquired the Austrian piano maker Bösendorfer in 2008 and the audio firms Steinberg (2004) and Line 6 (2013).
