El Teniente ("The Lieutenant") is the world's largest underground copper mine, located in the Chilean Andes about 2,300 metres above sea level, east of the city of Rancagua in the O'Higgins Region. Operated by the state copper company Codelco, it produces several hundred thousand tonnes of fine copper per year from a vast network of tunnels exceeding 3,000 kilometres in total length.

El Teniente's installations high in the Chilean Andes.

History

Copper was worked at the site on a small scale from the nineteenth century; its name is said to come from a fugitive Chilean army lieutenant who discovered the deposit. Industrial development began in 1905 under the American Braden Copper Company (later part of Kennecott), which built the mining camp of Sewell — a remarkable company town of colourful buildings stepped into the mountainside, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The mine was nationalised into Codelco in the 1970s as part of Chile's copper nationalisation.

Geology and mining

The deposit is a porphyry copper system centred on the Braden pipe, a breccia pipe rich in copper and molybdenum. Ore is extracted by block caving and panel caving from successive levels beneath the original workings; the "New Mine Level" project, at around 1,880 metres elevation, is intended to extend operations for decades. In 1945 the mine suffered one of the worst disasters in mining history, when a fire in the workings asphyxiated 355 workers.
