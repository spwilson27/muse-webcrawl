Station HYPO

Station HYPO, also known as the Fleet Radio Unit Pacific (FRUPAC), was the United States Navy's signals intelligence and codebreaking unit at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii, during the Second World War. Under Commander Joseph Rochefort, HYPO's work on the Japanese naval code JN-25 provided some of the most consequential intelligence of the Pacific War.

Organization

HYPO was one of three major US Navy cryptanalytic stations, alongside Station CAST in the Philippines (later Melbourne) and the central OP-20-G in Washington, which carried the designator NEGAT. Based in the basement of the Fourteenth Naval District headquarters building — the "Dungeon" — HYPO combined linguists, cryptanalysts, and IBM tabulating machinery to attack JN-25 traffic. Rochefort, a Japanese linguist and gifted codebreaker, assembled a famously unconventional team that included musicians recruited for their pattern-recognition skills.

Midway

HYPO's greatest triumph came in May 1942. By decrypting fragments of JN-25, Rochefort's team deduced that the Japanese target designated "AF" was Midway Island — a conclusion Washington questioned. HYPO arranged for Midway to broadcast an unencrypted report that its water distillation plant had broken down; a Japanese message soon reported that "AF" was short of water, confirming the target. Admiral Nimitz used the intelligence to ambush the Japanese carrier force at the Battle of Midway (4–7 June 1942), a turning point of the war. HYPO also contributed to the intelligence behind the interception and death of Admiral Yamamoto in 1943.

Legacy

Despite Midway, Rochefort was transferred after disagreements with Washington and received the Distinguished Service Medal only posthumously in 1985. The site of HYPO's wartime work on Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam is preserved in the modern Pacific Fleet intelligence center named for Rochefort.
