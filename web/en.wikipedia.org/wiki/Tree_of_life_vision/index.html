Tree of life vision

Lehi's dream of the tree of life, a central image in the Book of Mormon.

The tree of life vision is a vision described in the Book of Mormon in which the prophet Lehi and his son Nephi see a tree whose white fruit represents the love of God and the Atonement of Jesus Christ. It is one of the most well-known and widely referenced episodes in Latter-day Saint scripture, recorded in 1 Nephi chapters 8 and 11–15 of the Book of Mormon.

The vision

Lehi recounts that he wandered in a dark and dreary wilderness before coming to a large field, where he saw a tree whose fruit was white, sweet and "desirable to make one happy." A straight and narrow path led to the tree, alongside a river of water, with an iron rod running along the path for travellers to hold onto. Multitudes pressed forward; some reached the tree and ate the fruit, while others were lost in mists of darkness, wandered into forbidden paths, or drowned in the river. Across the river stood a "great and spacious building" — representing the pride of the world — whose occupants mocked those who ate the fruit, causing some to fall away in shame.

Interpretation

Nephi later received the same vision in expanded form, in which an angel explained the symbolism and showed him the ministry and crucifixion of Christ, the history of the Americas, and the latter days. Latter-day Saint teachers commonly interpret the fruit as the love of God and the blessings of the Atonement, the iron rod as the word of God, and the river as the depths of hell or the filthiness of the world.

Cultural significance

The vision is a frequent subject of Latter-day Saint art, hymns and lessons, and the phrase "hold to the rod" derives from it. Archaeologists and Book of Mormon scholars have compared the imagery to a carved stone monument at Izapa, Mexico (Stela 5), sometimes popularly called the "Tree of Life Stone," though such identifications remain speculative.
