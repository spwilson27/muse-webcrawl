Visayas

The Visayas form the central island group of the Philippines

The Visayas (Cebuano: Kabisay-an) are one of the three principal geographical divisions of the Philippines, along with Luzon and Mindanao. Located in the central part of the archipelago, the group consists of several islands, principally Panay, Negros, Cebu, Bohol, Leyte and Samar, together with many smaller surrounding islands such as Guimaras, Siquijor, Biliran, Romblon and the Camotes.

Geography and regions

Administratively the Visayas comprise three regions: Western Visayas, Central Visayas and Eastern Visayas. The largest city is Cebu City, the country's oldest Spanish colonial settlement and a major port, commercial and education centre. The islands are surrounded by the Visayan Sea to the north and centre, the Sibuyan Sea and Tablas Strait in the northwest, and the Bohol and Camotes seas to the south. Mount Kanlaon on Negros (2,465 m) is the highest peak in the group.

People and languages

The indigenous peoples of the region are collectively called Visayans (Bisaya). The principal languages are Cebuano, spoken in Cebu, Bohol, parts of Leyte and Negros Oriental; Hiligaynon, dominant in Panay and Negros Occidental; and Waray, spoken in Samar and eastern Leyte. These belong to the Bisayan subgroup of the Philippine languages. The population of the Visayas is roughly twenty million.

History

Pre-colonial Visayan society was organised into chiefdoms (barangays) ruled by datus and was renowned for seafaring and trade; the epic Hinilawod preserves Panay's oral tradition. Ferdinand Magellan's expedition reached the islands in 1521, and Miguel López de Legazpi established the first Spanish settlement on Cebu in 1565. The region was central to the galleon-era economy of sugar, abacá and tobacco. Festivals such as Sinulog in Cebu, Ati-Atihan in Kalibo and Dinagyang in Iloilo commemorate the Santo Niño and Visayan heritage.
