Onisaburo Deguchi

Onisaburo Deguchi (出口 王仁三郎, Deguchi Onisaburō, born Ueda Kisaburō; 21 August 1871 – 19 January 1948) was a Japanese spiritual leader and co-founder of the Oomoto religious movement. Together with the foundress Nao Deguchi (1836–1918), he transformed Oomoto from a small folk sect into one of Japan's largest new religious movements.

Life

Born in Anao, a village in what is now Kameoka, Kyoto, Ueda Kisaburō was adopted into the Deguchi family upon marrying Nao's daughter Sumi. After a dramatic spiritual experience on Mount Takakuma in 1898, in which he believed he was guided through the spirit world, he joined Nao's movement and systematised her automatic writings into the theology of Oomoto. He dictated the vast Reikai Monogatari (Tales from the Spirit World), an 81-volume work that became a central Oomoto scripture.

Activities and suppression

Deguchi promoted Esperanto, interfaith cooperation and world peace, and travelled to Mongolia in 1924 with the controversial figure Morihei Ueshiba, later founder of aikido, in an attempt to establish a religious kingdom. The Japanese state suppressed Oomoto twice, in 1921 and 1935; Deguchi was imprisoned in the second incident and released only after the Second World War. He also practised and patronised the arts, producing thousands of calligraphic works and brightly glazed tea bowls.
