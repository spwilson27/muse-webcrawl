John Herbert Dillinger (June 22, 1903 – July 22, 1934) was an American gangster during the Great Depression. He commanded the Dillinger Gang, which was accused of robbing twenty-four banks and four police stations. Dillinger was imprisoned several times and escaped twice. In 1934 he was charged with the murder of an East Chicago police officer, though never convicted; he was the subject of a massive manhunt by the Federal Bureau of Investigation and was fatally shot by special agents in Chicago.

Early life

Dillinger was born in Indianapolis, Indiana, the younger of two children of John Wilson Dillinger and Mary Ellen "Mollie" Lancaster. After his mother's death when he was three, he was raised partly by his older sister. Troubled as an adolescent, he quit school, briefly served in the U.S. Navy (from which he deserted), and in 1924 received a long prison sentence for assault and battery with intent to rob after a failed grocery holdup. He spent nearly nine years in the Indiana State Prison and Michigan City prison, where he studied the methods of professional bank robbers.

Crime spree

Paroled in May 1933, Dillinger quickly organized a gang and robbed a string of Midwestern banks. Arrested and jailed in Lima, Ohio, in October 1933, he was freed when gang members murdered the sheriff holding him. In March 1934 he made a sensational escape from the "escape-proof" jail at Crown Point, Indiana, allegedly using a fake gun carved from wood. After the Battle of Little Bohemia in Wisconsin in April 1934 — a botched FBI raid in which an agent and a bystander were killed — Dillinger became America's most wanted criminal. Attorney General Homer Cummings announced a reward, and J. Edgar Hoover's Division of Investigation named him the first "Public Enemy No. 1."

Death

On July 22, 1934, Dillinger attended the film Manhattan Melodrama at the Biograph Theater in Chicago with Anna Sage, a brothel madam who betrayed his whereabouts in hopes of avoiding deportation and collecting the reward — the famous "woman in red" (her dress was actually orange). As they left the theater, federal agents led by Melvin Purvis opened fire. Dillinger was struck by several bullets and died in an alley beside the theater. Crowds gathered and dipped handkerchiefs in his blood as souvenirs.

Legacy

Dillinger's exploits fed the Depression-era public's resentment of banks, and he became a folk-hero figure in some retellings. The manhunt that destroyed his gang helped transform the FBI into a powerful national police agency. He has been portrayed in numerous films, most notably by Johnny Depp in Public Enemies (2009).
