University of Otago

The University of Otago (Māori: Te Whare Wānanga o Otāgo) is a public research university based in Dunedin, Otago, New Zealand. Founded in 1869, it is New Zealand's oldest university and enrolls around 21,000 students. Its motto is Sapere aude ("dare to be wise").

History

Planned by Thomas Burns and James Macassey and funded by the Otago Provincial Council, the university opened in 1871 and became a federal University of New Zealand college before gaining independence in 1961. It was the first university in New Zealand to admit women to all degrees, and its medical school, founded in 1875, is among the oldest in Australasia.

Campus and student life

The Dunedin campus is centred on the Gothic revival Clocktower building beside the Water of Leith. Students — nicknamed "Scarfies" after the scarves worn against the southern cold — traditionally live in flatting areas north of campus, and the university's residential colleges and student culture are prominent features of Dunedin life. Otago has additional health sciences campuses in Christchurch and Wellington and is consistently New Zealand's top-performing university for research quality.
