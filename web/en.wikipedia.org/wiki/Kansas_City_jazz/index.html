Kansas City jazz

Kansas City jazz is a style of jazz that developed in Kansas City, Missouri, during the 1920s and 1930s, marking the transition from the structured big band style to the musical improvisation of bebop. It is characterised by riff-based arrangements, a swinging 4/4 rhythm with a relaxed, driving beat, prominent blues feeling, and extended solo improvisation.

History

During the Prohibition era, the political machine of Tom Pendergast kept Kansas City wide open, and its clubs — concentrated in the 18th and Vine district — employed musicians around the clock, creating fertile ground for jam sessions and musical rivalry. Key orchestras included Bennie Moten's band and, after Moten's death in 1935, the Count Basie Orchestra, which took the style national via radio broadcasts and records.

Musicians and legacy

Musicians associated with Kansas City jazz include Count Basie, Lester Young, Herschel Evans, Mary Lou Williams, Big Joe Turner, Pete Johnson and Jay McShann, in whose band the young Charlie Parker developed before helping to create bebop in New York. The style's legacy is commemorated by the American Jazz Museum in the historic 18th and Vine district.
