Osiris is a god of ancient Egyptian mythology, lord of the afterlife and the dead, and of resurrection, fertility and the Nile's renewal. Depicted as a mummiform king with green or black skin, the white atef crown flanked by plumes, and the crook and flail of kingship, he is one of the oldest and most important Egyptian deities, worshipped from the Old Kingdom until the end of Egyptian religion.

The Osiris myth

In the central narrative, Osiris, the eldest child of Geb and Nut and the first king of Egypt, was murdered by his jealous brother Set (god), who trapped him in a chest and later dismembered his body, scattering the pieces through Egypt. His wife-sister Isis gathered and reassembled the body — the first mummy, made with Anubis help — and, briefly reviving him, conceived their son Horus, who would avenge his father and take the throne of the living. Osiris passed to the Duat (underworld) as its king and judge of the dead.

Afterlife and cult

Every deceased person hoped to be vindicated as an "Osiris" before his tribunal, where the heart was weighed against the feather of ma'at. His cult centre was Abydos, where passion plays re-enacted his death and resurrection and pilgrims sought burial near him; his annual festivals coincided with the Nile flood and the sprouting of grain — "Osiris beds" of soil sown in his shape made the link between his resurrection and agricultural renewal explicit. Through the mystery cult of Isis, Osiris worship spread into the Greco-Roman world.
