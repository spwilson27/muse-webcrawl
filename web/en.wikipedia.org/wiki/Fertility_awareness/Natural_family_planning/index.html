Natural family planning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Natural family planning (NFP) is a family of methods used to achieve or postpone pregnancy by observing the naturally occurring signs and symptoms of the fertile and infertile phases of the menstrual cycle, without drugs, devices or surgical procedures. It is closely related to, and often used interchangeably with, the broader concept of fertility awareness; the term NFP is especially associated with teaching by the Roman Catholic Church, which permits it as a means of spacing births while prohibiting artificial contraception.

Methods

  • Symptothermal method — combines basal body temperature with cervical mucus observations and calendar calculations.
  • Ovulation (Billings) method — based on daily observation of cervical mucus patterns alone; developed in Australia by John and Evelyn Billings.
  • Creighton Model — a standardised mucus-based system linked to NaProTechnology.
  • Calendar/rhythm method — estimates the fertile window from past cycle lengths; less reliable because it cannot detect cycle variation.
  • Lactational amenorrhea method — relies on the temporary infertility of exclusive breastfeeding after childbirth.

Effectiveness

Effectiveness varies strongly by method and correctness of use. Well-taught symptothermal use has reported perfect-use pregnancy rates below 1% per year, comparable to some artificial methods, while typical-use rates are substantially higher (often cited around 20% or more for fertility-awareness-based methods overall), reflecting the discipline and partner cooperation the methods require.

Teaching and acceptance

Pope Paul VI's 1968 encyclical Humanae vitae reaffirmed Catholic teaching against artificial contraception while endorsing recourse to infertile periods, making NFP instruction a fixture of Catholic marriage preparation in many countries. Secular interest in hormone-free contraception has also grown, aided by cycle-tracking apps and wearable devices, though regulators caution that app predictions alone may not be reliable contraception unless the app is specifically certified for that purpose.
