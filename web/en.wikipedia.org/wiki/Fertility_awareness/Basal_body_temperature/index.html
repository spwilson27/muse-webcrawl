Basal body temperature

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Basal body temperature (BBT) is the lowest body temperature attained during rest, usually measured immediately upon waking, before any physical activity. In women, ovulation causes a small but sustained rise in BBT — typically 0.2–0.5 °C (about 0.5–1.0 °F) — driven by the thermogenic effect of progesterone secreted by the corpus luteum after the egg is released.

Use in fertility awareness

Charting daily BBT is a core element of fertility awareness and symptothermal methods of natural family planning. The temperature pattern across a cycle is biphasic: lower in the follicular phase, rising after ovulation and remaining elevated until menstruation. Because the rise occurs after ovulation, BBT alone confirms that ovulation has happened (retrospectively) rather than predicting it; combined with cervical mucus observations it defines the end of the fertile window with greater confidence.

Measurement

BBT is measured with a sensitive basal thermometer (analogue or digital) at the same time each morning, orally, vaginally or rectally, after at least several hours of sleep. Illness, alcohol, disrupted sleep, travel across time zones and some medications can distort readings. Dedicated apps and wearable sensors increasingly automate charting, but interpretation rules — such as the "three over six" rule of three higher readings above the previous six — remain standard.
