Tower of Jericho

LocationTell es-Sultan, Jericho, West Bank
Height8.5 m (28 ft)
Builtc. 8000 BCE
PeriodPre-Pottery Neolithic A

The Tower of Jericho is an 8.5-metre-tall (28 ft) stone structure built in the Pre-Pottery Neolithic A period around 8000 BCE. It is among the earliest stone monuments of humankind, constructed at Tell es-Sultan, the site of ancient Jericho, shortly after the end of the Last Glacial Maximum and the beginning of the Holocene epoch.

Discovery and description

The tower was excavated by Kathleen Kenyon in the 1950s during her excavations at Jericho. It was built of undressed stones with a mud mortar, and contained an internal staircase of 22 steps. The tower is conical in shape, almost 9 metres (30 ft) in diameter at the base and narrowing to 7 metres (23 ft) at the top. It stood against the inside of a massive stone wall that surrounded the settlement, part of what Kenyon interpreted as a defensive system.

Interpretations

Kenyon interpreted the tower as part of a fortification system protecting the settlement. Later scholars have proposed other functions. Ran Barkai and Roy Liran suggested in 2008 that the tower was aligned with the summer solstice sunset behind Mount Quruntul, making it possibly the earliest known archaeoastronomical device and a tool for marking time and reinforcing social order. Others have suggested it served as protection against flooding from the nearby wadi, as a communal granary, or as a territorial and prestige symbol of the early farming community.

Significance

The tower's construction required substantial organized labour, and it is frequently cited as evidence of the social complexity of early Neolithic sedentary communities before the invention of pottery or writing. Along with the wall of Jericho, it is among the oldest known monumental structures in the world.
