The Houston Astros are an American professional baseball team based in Houston, Texas. The Astros compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League (AL) West Division. Founded in 1962 as the Houston Colt .45s, the team entered the National League as an expansion franchise and adopted the Astros name in 1965, coinciding with the opening of the Astrodome, the world's first multi-purpose domed sports stadium.

History

Early years

The franchise was founded by Judge Roy Hofheinz and played its first three seasons at Colt Stadium. In 1965 the team moved into the Astrodome and was renamed for Houston's role in the American space program and the newly opened Manned Spacecraft Center. Stars of the early decades included Jimmy Wynn, Joe Morgan, César Cedeño, J. R. Richard, and Nolan Ryan.

1980s and the Killer B's

The Astros reached their first postseason in 1980 and won division titles in 1986, powered by Mike Scott's split-finger fastball. In the 1990s and 2000s the "Killer B's"—Jeff Bagwell, Craig Biggio, and Lance Berkman—led the club to multiple playoff appearances, culminating in the 2005 National League pennant and the franchise's first World Series appearance, a loss to the Chicago White Sox.

Move to the American League and championships

As a condition of the 2011 sale to Jim Crane, the Astros moved to the American League in 2013. After a deliberate rebuilding period, the team won the 2017 World Series over the Los Angeles Dodgers—the franchise's first championship—and the 2022 World Series over the Philadelphia Phillies. The 2017 title was later overshadowed by the sign-stealing scandal, in which the club was found to have used a camera-based system to steal signs during the 2017 season; MLB penalized the organization, and manager A. J. Hinch and general manager Jeff Luhnow were suspended and dismissed.

Retired numbers

The Astros have retired the numbers of Jeff Bagwell (5), Craig Biggio (7), Jimmy Wynn (24), José Cruz (25), Jim Umbricht (32), Mike Scott (33), Nolan Ryan (34), Don Wilson (40), and Larry Dierker (49), in addition to Jackie Robinson's league-wide retired 42.
