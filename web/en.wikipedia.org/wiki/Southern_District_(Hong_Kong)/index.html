The Southern District (Chinese: 南區) is one of the 18 districts of Hong Kong. It is located in the southern part of Hong Kong Island and includes the areas of Aberdeen, Ap Lei Chau, Pok Fu Lam, Stanley, Tai Tam, Repulse Bay, and Deep Water Bay. It had a population of about 260,834 in 2021.

Geography

The district covers the southern coastline of Hong Kong Island, facing the South China Sea. It contains some of the territory's most affluent residential neighbourhoods, including Repulse Bay and The Peak's southern slopes, as well as the working-class housing estates of Wah Fu and the industrial area of Wong Chuk Hang.

History

Aberdeen was one of the first places in Hong Kong recorded by British visitors, and its harbour was long home to a large community of boat-dwelling Tanka people. The floating Jumbo Kingdom restaurant was a landmark for decades before its closure in 2020. Stanley served as a temporary administrative centre in the early colonial period, and Stanley Prison and the Murray House building remain notable heritage sites.

Attractions

Ocean Park Hong Kong, one of the territory's two theme parks, is located in Wong Chuk Hang. Stanley Market, Repulse Bay beach, and the hiking trails of Dragon's Back and Tai Tam Country Park draw visitors year-round. Aberdeen's typhoon shelter and seafood restaurants remain popular.

Transport

The district is served by the South Island line of the MTR, opened in 2016, connecting Admiralty to South Horizons on Ap Lei Chau, as well as by numerous bus routes through the Aberdeen Tunnel.
