Jewish insurgency in Mandatory Palestine

The Jewish insurgency in Mandatory Palestine, known in Britain as the Palestine Emergency, was a paramilitary campaign carried out by Zionist underground groups against British rule in Mandatory Palestine from 1944 to 1948. The three principal organisations were the Haganah (including its Palmach strike force), the Irgun Zvai Leumi led by Menachem Begin, and the smaller, more militant Lehi (Stern Gang).

Background

The 1939 White Paper had severely restricted Jewish immigration to Palestine just as Jews in Europe faced extermination. After the Second World War, Britain continued to block survivors of the Holocaust from entering, intercepting refugee ships such as the Exodus 1947. The mainstream Jewish Agency initially opposed armed revolt but cooperated briefly with the dissident groups in the Jewish Resistance Movement of 1945–46.

Campaign

The insurgents attacked police stations, airfields, railway lines, oil refineries and military installations, and assassinated British officials. The most notorious attack was the bombing of the King David Hotel in Jerusalem on 22 July 1946, which housed the British administrative and military headquarters and killed 91 people. Britain deployed up to 100,000 troops in Palestine — an enormous burden for a state exhausted by war.

Outcome

In February 1947 Britain referred the Palestine question to the United Nations, whose General Assembly voted in November 1947 for partition into Jewish and Arab states. The British Mandate ended on 14 May 1948, when David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the State of Israel; the insurgency's political goal of an independent Jewish state had been achieved, though war with the Arab states immediately followed.
