An entity–relationship model (or ER model) describes interrelated things of interest in a specific domain of knowledge. A basic ER model is composed of entity types (which classify the things of interest) and specifies relationships that can exist between entities (instances of those entity types). It is widely used in software engineering and database design as the first step in specifying an information system.

Overview

The model was introduced by Peter Chen in a landmark 1976 paper and visually represented in entity–relationship diagrams (ERDs): rectangles for entities, ellipses for attributes and diamonds for relationships. Cardinality constraints (one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many) state how many instances of one entity may relate to instances of another. Entities that cannot exist without an owner are called weak entities.

From model to database

An ER diagram is typically translated into a relational schema: entity types become tables, attributes become columns, and relationships become foreign keys or junction tables. Extensions such as the enhanced ER model add specialisation and generalisation hierarchies, borrowing ideas from object-oriented modelling.
