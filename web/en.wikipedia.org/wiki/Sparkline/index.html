Sparkline

A sparkline is a very small line chart, typically drawn without axes or coordinates, intended to present the general shape and variation of data in a compact, inline form — "data-intense, design-simple, word-sized graphics", in the words of their best-known proponent, information designer Edward Tufte, who popularised the term and the concept in his book Beautiful Evidence (2006).

Design

Sparklines are placed inside the flow of text, tables or dashboards, immediately beside the figures they describe — for example, a stock's current price followed by a tiny chart of its movement over the last month, or a patient's latest blood-pressure reading with a sparkline of its history. Because context is supplied by the surrounding text, sparklines omit the axes, labels and legends of conventional charts; typical variants include line, bar and win/loss forms, sometimes with the maximum, minimum or final value highlighted.

Although Tufte coined the name in 2004, small inline charts have antecedents in early medical and financial graphics and in the "inline charts" implemented in software by computer scientist Peter Zelchenko in the late 1990s.

Use in software

Sparklines are now a standard feature of information dashboards and financial interfaces. Microsoft Excel added native sparklines in its 2010 release, as did Google Sheets, Tableau and many analytics libraries. In text-only contexts, Unicode block characters (e.g. ▁▂▃▄▅▆▇) can render sparkline-like sequences directly in a line of text.
