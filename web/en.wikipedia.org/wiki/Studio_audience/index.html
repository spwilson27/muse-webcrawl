Studio audience

A studio audience is a group of spectators present in a television or radio studio during the recording of a programme. Their reactions — laughter, applause and gasps — become part of the broadcast, and shows filmed before a live studio audience are a long-standing format for sitcoms, talk shows, game shows and variety programmes.

Practice

Classic American multi-camera sitcoms such as I Love Lucy pioneered filming before a live audience with multiple cameras, a technique revived by later hits including Cheers, Friends and The Big Bang Theory. Audience members are usually admitted free by ticket, seated by ushers, and guided by a warm-up comedian and illuminated "applause" signs. Producers may supplement or sweeten audience reactions in post-production; canned laughter on a laugh track is the artificial alternative when no audience is present.

Talk and game shows

Talk shows rely on the audience for energy and applause cues, while game shows use audience reactions to heighten excitement; some formats poll the studio audience as a lifeline for contestants.
