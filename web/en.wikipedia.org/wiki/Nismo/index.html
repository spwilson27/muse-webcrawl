Nismo (ニスモ), short for Nissan Motorsport International Limited (日産モータースポーツインターナショナル), is the motorsport, performance, and tuning division of the Japanese automaker Nissan. Founded in 1984 through the merger of Nissan's two in-house competition departments, Nismo runs Nissan's factory racing programmes and develops high-performance Nismo-branded road cars, parts, and accessories.

Motorsport

Nismo has prepared Nissan entries for the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the Daytona 24 Hours, the World Rally Championship, the Japanese Super GT series, and global GT racing with the GT-R. Highlights include overall victory at Daytona in 1992 with the R91CP, multiple Super GT championships with the GT-R, and supplying LMP2 engines that won their class at Le Mans repeatedly in the 2010s. Nissan's Formula E programme has also been run under the Nismo umbrella. The radical front-wheel-drive GT-R LM Nismo LMP1 project of 2015 was short-lived but notable for its unconventional design.

Road cars

Nismo road models are extensively reworked versions of Nissan production cars, typically with more powerful engines, track-tuned suspension, aerodynamic bodywork, and the division's signature red accents. Notable cars include the 400R (based on the Skyline GT-R R33), the Nismo GT-R, the 370Z Nismo and Z Nismo, the Juke Nismo, and the Sentra Nismo. Nismo also sells performance parts for current and heritage Nissan models. In 2013 the brand was relaunched as a global performance sub-brand with a dedicated headquarters and showroom in Yokohama. In 2022 Nissan consolidated its tuning operations by merging Nismo with Autech, forming Nissan Motorsports & Customizing Co., Ltd., while retaining the Nismo name for motorsport and performance products.
