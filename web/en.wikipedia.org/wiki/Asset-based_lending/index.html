Asset-based lending

Asset-based lending is any kind of lending secured by an asset. This means that if the loan is not repaid, the asset is taken. In this sense, a mortgage is an example of an asset-based loan; however, the term is more commonly used to describe lending to business and large corporations using assets not normally used in other loans, such as inventory, accounts receivable, machinery, and equipment, as collateral.

How it works

Asset-based loans are usually revolving lines of credit whose availability is governed by a "borrowing base": a formula that applies advance rates to eligible collateral — for example, up to about 80–85% of eligible accounts receivable and a lower percentage of the appraised value of inventory. Lenders monitor collateral closely through regular borrowing-base certificates, field examinations, and appraisals.

Uses

Because availability is tied to asset values rather than to earnings or credit history, asset-based lending is often used by growing companies, highly seasonal businesses, companies undergoing leveraged buyouts or restructurings, and borrowers that cannot qualify for traditional cash-flow loans. It generally carries higher interest rates and fees than conventional lending, reflecting the monitoring costs and risks involved.

Related forms

Closely related practices include factoring (the outright purchase of receivables), inventory finance, and secured term loans against plant and machinery. Asset-based lending differs from asset-backed securities, which package pooled receivables for sale to capital-markets investors.
