Alois Alzheimer (14 June 1864 – 19 December 1915) was a German psychiatrist and neuropathologist and a colleague of Emil Kraepelin. He is credited with identifying the first published case of "presenile dementia", which Kraepelin later identified as Alzheimer's disease.

Early life and education

Alzheimer was born in Marktbreit, Bavaria, the son of a notary. He studied medicine at the universities of Berlin, Tübingen and Würzburg, receiving his medical degree from Würzburg in 1887. In 1888 he joined the Municipal Hospital for Lunatics and Epileptics in Frankfurt am Main, where he worked alongside Franz Nissl, the neurologist for whom Nissl bodies are named.

The case of Auguste Deter

In 1901 Alzheimer encountered a 51-year-old patient, Auguste Deter, who exhibited strange behavioural symptoms including profound memory loss, disorientation and aphasia. After her death in 1906, Alzheimer examined her brain using silver-staining histological techniques developed by Bielschowsky. He found the distinctive amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles that remain the pathological hallmarks of the disease. He presented the case in November 1906 at a conference in Tübingen; the lecture received little comment at the time.

Later career

In 1903 Alzheimer moved to Munich to work with Kraepelin, where their laboratory became a leading centre for neuropathology. In 1912 he was appointed professor of psychiatry at the University of Breslau. His health deteriorated after he contracted infectious endocarditis; he died in Breslau in December 1915, aged 51.

Legacy

Kraepelin coined the eponym "Alzheimer's disease" in the eighth edition of his textbook of psychiatry (1910). Alzheimers's histological preparations, including slides from Auguste Deter's case, were rediscovered in Munich in the 1990s, renewed interest in his original observations.
