Songun (Korean for "military-first") is a political doctrine of North Korea that prioritizes the Korean People's Army in state affairs and the allocation of resources. Associated with Kim Jong Il, it was elevated to the country's guiding policy alongside Juche (self-reliance) during the economic crisis of the 1990s.

Origins

The roots of songun lie in Kim Il Sung's emphasis on the army as the core of the revolution, but it became official policy under Kim Jong Il during the Arduous March of the 1990s, when the collapse of Soviet aid and natural disasters produced famine. In 1998 the constitution was revised to make the chairman of the National Defense Commission — Kim Jong Il's post — the "supreme leader" of the state, and in 2003 songun was proclaimed the guiding idea of the revolution, equal in status to Juche.

Doctrine

Songun holds that the military is the vanguard of the revolution and the pillar of the state; in official formulation, "the army first" in politics, ideology, and the economy. In practice it meant the Korean People's Army received first claim on scarce food, fuel, and foreign exchange, and military officers rose to the top of the party-state hierarchy. The doctrine was also used to justify the nuclear weapons program as the ultimate guarantee of sovereignty.

Assessment

Outside analysts debate whether songun represented a genuine ideological shift or a pragmatic consolidation of Kim Jong Il's power base in the military during a succession crisis. Under Kim Jong Un, the doctrine has been partially displaced by byungjin (parallel development of the economy and nuclear forces), announced in 2013, though the military retains a privileged position.
