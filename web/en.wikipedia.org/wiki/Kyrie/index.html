Kyrie

The Kyrie (Greek: Κύριε) is a prayer in Christian liturgy, part of the Ordinary of the Mass: Kyrie eleison — "Lord, have mercy". Though Greek in text, it is retained in the Roman Rite, and equivalents ("Lord, have mercy") appear across Eastern and Western traditions.

Text and structure

The invocation is traditionally threefold — Kyrie eleison, Christe eleison, Kyrie eleison — each line sung twice in the Roman form, giving nine invocations. In the Byzantine Rite, "Lord, have mercy" (Κύριε ἐλέησον) recurs throughout the litanies of the Divine Liturgy.

Music

The Kyrie opens the musical Mass Ordinary and has been set by composers from the medieval Gregorian chant repertory (some Kyrie melodies are among the oldest surviving chant) through Renaissance polyphony (Palestrina, Byrd), Bach's Mass in B minor, and Mozart's and Beethoven's mass settings, to modern composers. It was also adapted into the 1985 pop hit "Kyrie" by Mr. Mister.
