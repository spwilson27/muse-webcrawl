Prephenate

Prephenic acid
Formula: C10H10O6
IUPAC: 1-(2-carboxy-2-oxoethyl)-4-hydroxycyclohexa-2,5-diene-1-carboxylic acid

Prephenate (prephenic acid) is an intermediate in the biosynthesis of the aromatic amino acids phenylalanine and tyrosine in bacteria, fungi, and plants. It stands at the branch point of the shikimate pathway's terminal steps, and its conjugate base is formed from chorismate.

Biosynthesis

Prephenate is produced from chorismate by chorismate mutase, which catalyzes a Claisen rearrangement — one of the few examples of an enzyme-catalyzed pericyclic reaction. Chorismate itself is the end product of the shikimate pathway, which is absent in animals, making its enzymes targets for herbicides and antimicrobials.

Conversion to amino acids

From prephenate the pathway diverges. Prephenate dehydratase removes water and carbon dioxide to give phenylpyruvate, which is transaminated to phenylalanine. Prephenate dehydrogenase instead performs an oxidative decarboxylation yielding 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate, the precursor of tyrosine. In some organisms, prephenate is first transaminated to arogenate (pretyrosine), which then feeds both amino acids; the arogenate route is widespread in plants and cyanobacteria. Regulation of chorismate mutase and the branch-point enzymes by feedback inhibition controls the flow between the two products.
