Galerie Colbert

The Galerie Colbert is a covered passage (galerie couverte) in the 2nd arrondissement of Paris, France. Built in 1826, it was constructed as a rival to the neighbouring Galerie Vivienne, which had opened a few years earlier and was enjoying great commercial success.

History

The passage was built on the site of the former Hôtel Colbert, and its name honours Jean-Baptiste Colbert, the finance minister of Louis XIV. Its architect was J.-A. Billaud. The gallery is organised around a striking circular rotunda topped by a glass dome, beneath which once stood a bronze statue of Eurydice bitten by the serpent, a copy after an antique original.

Despite its elegant architecture, the Galerie Colbert never matched the commercial success of the Galerie Vivienne, partly because it lacked a direct entrance from the Palais-Royal gardens. Many of its shops gave way over time to offices and institutional uses.

Present day

Today the gallery is owned by the Bibliothèque nationale de France and is home to the Institut national d'histoire de l'art (INHA) and the Institut national du patrimoine (INP). It houses research libraries, lecture rooms and exhibition spaces, and its ground-floor brasserie, Le Grand Colbert, is a well-known Parisian restaurant celebrated for its traditional décor, famously featured in the film Something's Gotta Give (2003).

Architecture

The passage is about 83 metres long. Its Neoclassical façades, Corinthian columns and the glass dome of the rotunda make it one of the most architecturally refined of the surviving Parisian covered passages of the early nineteenth century.
