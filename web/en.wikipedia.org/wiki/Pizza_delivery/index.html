Pizza delivery

Pizza delivery is a service in which a pizzeria or pizza chain delivers a pizza to a customer, usually at home or at a workplace. Delivery is typically performed by an employee driving a car, riding a bicycle, or using a motor scooter, and is commonly free within a limited area, subject to a minimum order or a delivery fee.

History

The origins of pizza delivery are usually traced to Naples. In 1889 Queen Margherita of Savoy famously had pizzas delivered to her residence — an event associated with the creation of pizza Margherita. Commercial delivery grew in the United States after the Second World War. Domino's, founded in 1960, built its business on delivery and from 1973 promoted a 30-minute delivery guarantee (discontinued in 1993 because of safety concerns). Tom Monaghan is also credited with inventing the corrugated pizza box widely used today; the insulated delivery bag ("hot bag") was developed in the 1980s to keep pizzas warm in transit.

Ordering

Orders were traditionally placed by telephone, but since the 2000s online ordering through websites and mobile apps — including ordering through third-party platforms — has overtaken telephone orders for the large chains. Real-time order tracking, showing preparation and the driver's location, is now common.

Delivery methods and issues

  • Vehicles: cars, mopeds and bicycles; some companies have experimented with drones and autonomous delivery robots.
  • Packaging: flat cardboard boxes with a plastic "pizza saver" table to prevent the lid touching the cheese.
  • Tipping: delivery workers in many markets are customarily tipped; their employment status and pay have been a subject of labour debate, particularly with app-based platforms.
  • Safety: time-guarantee policies raised concerns about speeding and driver and public safety.
