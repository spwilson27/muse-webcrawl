Labdanum

Labdanum, also called ladanum, is a sticky brown resin obtained from the shrubs Cistus ladanifer and Cistus creticus. It has a long history of use in herbal medicine and perfumery and was one of the earliest perfume ingredients of the Mediterranean world.

Collection

In ancient times the resin was combed from the beards and thighs of goats that grazed among the cistus bushes; later it was gathered with a leather rake-like tool, the ladanisterion, drawn across the shrubs so the resin adhered to its thongs. Today the leaves and twigs are boiled or solvent-extracted.

Uses

Labdanum has a warm, amber-like scent and is a common base note in perfumery, valued as a substitute for ambergris. In incense it is often identified with the "onycha" of ancient recipes, and some scholars connect it with the biblical lot resin (myrrh-related "ladanum").
