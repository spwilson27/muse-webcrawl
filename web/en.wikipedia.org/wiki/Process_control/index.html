Process control
Field: Control engineering
Applications: Chemical plants, refineries, manufacturing

Process control is a discipline of control engineering that deals with maintaining the output of a specific process within a desired range. It is widely used in industry — in oil refineries, chemical plants, pulp and paper mills, and food processing — to operate safely, consistently, and efficiently.

Feedback control

The basic building block is the feedback control loop: a sensor measures a process variable (such as temperature, pressure, level, or flow), a controller compares it with the setpoint, and an actuator (such as a valve or heater) corrects deviations. The PID controller is the most common control algorithm.

Control systems

Modern plants use distributed control systems (DCS) and programmable logic controllers (PLCs), supervised through SCADA interfaces, to coordinate hundreds or thousands of loops. Advanced process control techniques, including model predictive control, optimise operation beyond single-loop regulation.

Statistical process control

Statistical process control uses control charts and statistical methods to monitor variation in output quality, distinguishing common-cause from special-cause variation — a related but distinct use of the term.
