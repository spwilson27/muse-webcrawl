Philosophy of religion is the branch of Philosophy that examines religious concepts and claims: the existence and attributes of God, the relationship between faith and reason, religious experience, miracles, and the problem of evil. It is distinct from theology in that it approaches these questions with philosophical methods open to any tradition, or none.

Arguments for God's existence

Classical arguments include the ontological argument of Anselm of Canterbury, the cosmological and teleological arguments systematised by Thomas Aquinas in his 'Five Ways', and design arguments revived in modern fine-tuning debates. Immanuel Kant judged all purely speculative proofs inconclusive while defending a practical, moral faith.

The problem of evil

The existence of suffering is pressed against belief in an omnipotent, wholly good God — a puzzle already posed in antiquity and sharpened by David Hume in his Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion. Responses include free-will defences (Alvin Plantinga) and soul-making theodicies (John Hick). Other topics include religious language, pluralism, and whether religious belief can be properly basic.
