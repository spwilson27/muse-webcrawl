Neon tetra

The neon tetra (Paracheirodon innesi) is a freshwater fish of the characin family (Characidae) of the order Characiformes. It is native to blackwater and clearwater streams in the Amazon basin in South America. Its iridescent blue horizontal stripe and bright red tail make it one of the most popular aquarium fish in the world.

Description

The neon tetra has a light-blue back over a silver-white abdomen. It is characterised by an iridescent blue horizontal stripe along each side of the fish from its nose to the base of the adipose fin, and an iridescent red stripe that begins at the middle of the body and extends posteriorly to the base of the caudal fin. Adults reach about 3 cm (1.2 in) in length.

In the aquarium

Neon tetras are schooling fish and should be kept in groups of at least six. They prefer soft, acidic water similar to their native blackwater habitats, with temperatures around 20–28 °C. Huge numbers are exported each year for the aquarium trade, and sustainable wild collection from the Rio Negro region is an important source of income for local communities.
