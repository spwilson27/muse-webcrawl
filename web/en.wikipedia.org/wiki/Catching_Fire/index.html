Catching Fire is the second novel in Suzanne Collins's Hunger Games trilogy.

Catching Fire is a 2009 dystopian young adult novel by the American writer Suzanne Collins, the second book in The Hunger Games trilogy. It continues the story of Katniss Everdeen in the post-apocalyptic nation of Panem, following her victory in the 74th Hunger Games alongside Peeta Mellark — a victory achieved by an act of defiance that begins to spark rebellion in the districts.

Plot

Months after the Games, Katniss and Peeta embark on a Victory Tour while President Snow pressures Katniss to quell unrest and maintain the fiction of their romance. When the 75th Hunger Games — the third Quarter Quell — are announced, the twist is revealed: the tributes will be reaped from the existing pool of victors. Katniss and Peeta return to an arena designed like a clock, allying with tributes including Finnick Odair and Johanna Mason. The novel ends as Katniss destroys the arena's force field and is rescued by rebels from District 13, while Peeta is captured by the Capitol and District 12 is destroyed.

Reception

The novel was a commercial and critical success, praised for its pacing, character development and deepening political themes, and it appeared on numerous year-end best-book lists.

Film adaptation

The Hunger Games: Catching Fire, directed by Francis Lawrence and starring Jennifer Lawrence as Katniss, was released in November 2013. It grossed over $860 million worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing film of the series at the time and one of the highest-grossing films of 2013.
