Army Group Courland (German: Heeresgruppe Kurland) was a German Army Group on the Eastern Front which was created from the remnants of Army Group North, isolated in the Courland Pocket by the advancing Red Army. It remained cut off on the Courland peninsula in western Latvia for the rest of the war and surrendered on 8 May 1945.

Formation

On 25 January 1945, Adolf Hitler renamed the three army groups defending north-eastern Germany: Army Group North, trapped in Courland, became Army Group Courland; Army Group Centre became the new Army Group North; and Army Group A became Army Group Centre. The new army group, commanded first by Lothar Rendulic and finally by Carl Hilpert, contained the 16th and 18th Armies, together with Latvian units of the Waffen-SS, including the 15th and 19th Waffen Grenadier Divisions.

The Courland Pocket battles

Between October 1944 and April 1945 the Red Army launched six major offensives to crush the pocket. Soviet forces of the 1st and 2nd Baltic Fronts attacked repeatedly but failed to eliminate the bridgehead, in part because of determined German resistance and in part because Joseph Stalin diverted strength toward the decisive drive on Berlin. The pocket tied down significant Soviet forces, while Germany maintained the enclave partly to preserve a training area for U-boat crews and a foothold for a possible counteroffensive.

Surrender

On 8 May 1945, following the unconditional surrender of Germany, Army Group Courland laid down its arms. Approximately 189,000 German soldiers went into Soviet captivity, along with some 42 generals; other figures give around 200,000 men. Carl Hilpert, the last commander, was later tried by a Soviet court and executed in 1947. Many prisoners were not released until the 1950s.
