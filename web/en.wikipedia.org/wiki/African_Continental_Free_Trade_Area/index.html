Overview

The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) is a free trade area founded by the African Continental Free Trade Agreement among African Union member states. It aims to create a single continental market for goods and services — the world's largest free trade area by number of countries — covering some 1.3–1.4 billion people with a combined GDP of roughly $3 trillion, as a flagship project of the AU's Agenda 2063.

Implementation

The agreement commits members to eliminate tariffs on 90% of goods over time (with sensitive and excluded lists) and to liberalise services, with protocols on investment, competition, intellectual property and digital trade negotiated in later phases. Trading under AfCFTA preferences formally began on 1 January 2021; the Guided Trade Initiative (2022) piloted real shipments among a first group of countries. The World Bank has estimated full implementation could raise Africa's income by hundreds of billions of dollars and lift tens of millions from extreme poverty by 2035 — figures dependent on deep implementation, given that intra-African trade is only about 15% of the continent's total trade.
