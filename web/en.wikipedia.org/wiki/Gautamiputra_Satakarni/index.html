Gautamiputra Satakarni

Gautamiputra Satakarni (Brahmi script: Gautamīputra Sātakarṇi) was a ruler of the Satavahana Empire in the Deccan region of India. He is widely regarded as the greatest of the Satavahana kings, credited with reviving the dynasty's fortunes after a period of decline caused by invasions of the Western Kshatrapas (Sakas) and other foreign powers.

Biography

Gautamiputra's name follows the Satavahana metronymic tradition: he was the son of Gautami Balashri, whose famous Nashik prashasti inscription, issued during the reign of her grandson Vasishthiputra Pulumavi, is the principal source for his career. The inscription describes him as the destroyer of the Sakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas, the uprooter of the Kshaharata family, and the restorer of the glory of the Satavahana lineage.

His most celebrated victory was over the Western Kshatrapa ruler Nahapana, whom he defeated around 124–125 CE. Gautamiputra restruck many of Nahapana's silver coins with his own emblems, a hoard of which was discovered at Jogalthambi in the Nashik district. He is said to have ruled an extensive territory stretching across the Deccan, including parts of present-day Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Karnataka, and his dominion is described in inscriptions as extending between the three seas.

Titles and administration

The Nashik inscription praises him as a king whose commands were obeyed by the circle of all kings, a unique Brahmana (eka-bamhana), and a ruler as valiant as Rama, Keshava and Arjuna. He is credited with reorganising the administration, promoting agriculture, and ruling according to dharma without oppressive taxation.

Religious patronage

Although a follower of Brahmanical Hinduism, Gautamiputra also patronised Buddhist monks, granting villages and caves to the monastic community at Nashik (Trirashmi hills). Inscriptions record his donations to the Buddhist sangha as well as his support of Brahmanas.

Legacy

Gautamiputra Satakarni's reign marked the peak of Satavahana power. He has been celebrated in later Indian historiography as a national hero who expelled foreign invaders, and his name has been given to institutions, awards and a film in modern India.
