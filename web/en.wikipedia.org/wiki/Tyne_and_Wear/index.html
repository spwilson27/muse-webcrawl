Tyne and Wear

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tyne and Wear is a metropolitan county in North East England, named after the rivers Tyne and Wear that flow through it. It comprises the five metropolitan boroughs of Newcastle upon Tyne, Gateshead, North Tyneside, South Tyneside and Sunderland, and had a population of about 1.1 million in the early 2020s.

History

The county was created in 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972 from parts of Northumberland and County Durham. The county council was abolished in 1986, and the borough councils became unitary authorities for most services; joint bodies continue to run transport, fire and rescue, and other county-wide functions.

Economy and transport

Historically a centre of coal mining, shipbuilding on the Tyne and Wear, and heavy engineering, the area's economy is now dominated by services, education, retail and automotive manufacturing, including the Nissan plant at Sunderland. The Tyne and Wear Metro, opened in 1980, was Britain's first modern light rail system and links the main centres.

Culture

The county is known for the Great North Run, the largest half marathon in the world, and for landmarks such as the Angel of the North, the Baltic Centre for Contemporary Art, and St James' Park.
