E, or e, is the fifth letter of the Latin alphabet, used in the modern English alphabet, the alphabets of other western European languages and others worldwide. Its name in English is e (pronounced /ˈiː/); plural es, Es or E's.

It is the most commonly used letter in many languages, including Czech, Danish, Dutch, English, French, German, Hungarian, Latin, Latvian, Norwegian, Spanish, and Swedish.

History

The Latin letter E derives from the Greek letter epsilon (Ε, ε), which in turn came from the Phoenician letter hē, originally a pictogram thought to represent a human figure praying or rejoicing. In the Semitic languages the sign stood for a consonantal sound, roughly /h/; the Greeks, needing a sign for the vowel /e/, adapted it, and the letter passed through Etruscan into the Latin alphabet in essentially its modern capital form. The lowercase e developed in late antiquity and the early Middle Ages from cursive forms of the capital, through uncial and Carolingian minuscule scripts.

Use in writing systems

In English, E most often represents the vowel sounds /ɛ/ (as in bed) and, in combination or in open syllables, /iː/ (as in be). At the end of words it is frequently silent, where it traditionally marks a preceding long vowel (compare hat and hate) and softens a preceding c or g. In the International Phonetic Alphabet, [e] denotes the close-mid front unrounded vowel and [ɛ] the open-mid front unrounded vowel.

Other uses

In mathematics, e is Euler's number, the base of the natural logarithm, approximately 2.71828. In physics, E commonly denotes energy; in Einstein's equation E = mc² it represents the energy equivalent of mass. In music, E is a note of the chromatic scale, and the lowest (sixth) string of a guitar in standard tuning is E. In grading systems in some countries, E is the lowest passing grade, while in others (including most of the United States) F is used instead, E having been dropped to avoid confusion with "excellent".

Related characters

Descendants and variants include Ē, Ĕ, Ė, Ę, Ě, È, É, Ê, Ë, and the ligature Æ in some orthographies. The Greek epsilon, Cyrillic Е and the mathematical symbol ∈ are visually similar but distinct characters.
