KAIST (Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology; Korean: 한국과학기술원) is a national research university located in Daedeok Innopolis, Daejeon, South Korea. Established in 1971 by the Korean government, with initial assistance from the United States, KAIST was the country's first research-oriented science and engineering institution, created to train scientists and engineers for Korea's industrial development.

History

KAIST was founded as the Korea Advanced Institute of Science (KAIS) under the initiative of President Park Chung-hee and planned by a team led by Frederick Terman's recommendations and Korean-American physicist KunMo Chung. It initially offered only graduate programmes in Seoul. In 1989 it merged with the Korea Institute of Technology, an undergraduate institution in Daejeon, and the combined university — by then renamed the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology — relocated most operations to its current Daejeon campus in the Daedeok research cluster. KAIST has since expanded into business (the KAIST College of Business in Seoul), medicine-related research, and international programmes, with teaching largely in English.

Academics and research

The university is organised into colleges of natural sciences, life science and bioengineering, engineering, liberal arts and convergence science, and business. Its roughly 10,000 students are split between undergraduates and graduate students, a graduate share unusually high for a Korean university. Admission is highly selective. KAIST is consistently ranked among the top universities in Asia and the world, particularly for engineering and technology, and is one of Korea's so-called "SKY-adjacent" science specialist institutions alongside POSTECH. Research highlights include the humanoid robot HUBO, the online electric vehicle (OLEV) system, and work in artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and robotics. Each year KAIST teams compete in international contests such as the DARPA Robotics Challenge, which a KAIST team won in 2015 with its DRC-HUBO robot.

Student life

Undergraduates receive generous scholarships and traditionally pay no tuition if they maintain satisfactory grades. The campus in Daejeon is known for its pond, cherry blossoms, and resident geese, and students organise large festivals and a famous puzzle culture around the university's founding celebrations. Graduates are exempted or given special provisions regarding mandatory military service when they pursue research careers in designated institutes.
