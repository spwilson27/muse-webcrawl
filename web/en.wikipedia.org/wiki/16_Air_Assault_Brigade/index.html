16 Air Assault Brigade

16 Air Assault Brigade is the British Army's rapid reaction formation, based at Colchester Garrison in Essex. Formed on 1 September 1999 by merging 5 Airborne Brigade and 24 Airmobile Brigade, it combines parachute infantry of the Parachute Regiment with attack-helicopter aviation of the Army Air Corps. In 2021 it was expanded and renamed 16 Air Assault Brigade Combat Team.

Role and composition

The brigade is the Army's high-readiness intervention force, able to deploy by parachute, helicopter and air landing. Its core units include battalions of the Parachute Regiment, a Gurkha battalion in the air assault role, Apache attack helicopter regiments, artillery (including 7th Parachute Regiment Royal Horse Artillery), engineers, signals, medical and logistics units. The elite Pathfinder Platoon provides reconnaissance. Soldiers entitled to wear the maroon beret serve alongside the brigade's distinctive "Pegasus" formation sign, recalling the airborne forces of the Second World War.

Operations

The brigade deployed to Macedonia in 2001 (Operation Essential Harvest), spearheaded the invasion of Iraq in 2003, and served repeated tours in Helmand Province, Afghanistan — in 2006, 2008, 2010–11 and 2013 — where its soldiers fought some of the most intense combat experienced by British forces in decades. Elements also deployed on the Kabul evacuation (Operation Pitting) in 2021.
