SS Edmund Fitzgerald was an American Great Lakes freighter that sank in Lake Superior during a violent storm on 10 November 1975, with the loss of the entire crew of 29 men. When launched on 7 June 1958, she was the largest ship on North America's Great Lakes, earning her the nickname "Queen of the Lakes", and she remains the largest vessel to have sunk in the lakes.

Career

The Fitzgerald was built by the Great Lakes Engineering Works at River Rouge, Michigan, for the Northwestern Mutual Life Insurance Company, which named her after its president and chairman, Edmund Fitzgerald. Operated by the Columbia Transportation Division of the Oglebay Norton Company, she hauled taconite iron ore from mines near Duluth, Minnesota, to iron works in Detroit, Toledo and other Great Lakes ports, and set numerous seasonal haul records.

The final voyage

On the afternoon of 9 November 1975 the Fitzgerald left Superior, Wisconsin, loaded with about 26,000 tons of taconite pellets, bound for a steel mill near Detroit. She sailed into a rapidly deepening storm, travelling in company with the freighter Arthur M. Anderson. Through 10 November the Fitzgerald reported damaged railings, vents and a list, and lost both radars, but sent no distress call. Her last message, at about 7:10 p.m., was "We are holding our own." She disappeared from the Anderson's radar shortly afterwards. The wreck was found a few days later in about 530 feet (160 m) of water, roughly 17 miles from Whitefish Bay, broken in two; the precise cause of the sinking remains debated.

Legacy

The disaster was immortalised by Gordon Lightfoot's 1976 ballad "The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald". The ship's bell was recovered in 1995 and is displayed at the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum at Whitefish Point, where a memorial service is held each 10 November. The sinking led to changes in Great Lakes shipping practice, including requirements for survival suits, freeboard, and emergency position-indicating beacons.
