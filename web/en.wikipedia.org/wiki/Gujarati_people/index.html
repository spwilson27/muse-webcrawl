Gujarati people

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Gujarati people or Gujaratis are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group native to the Indian state of Gujarat who speak Gujarati, an Indo-Aryan language. While they mainly inhabit the northwestern Indian peninsula, there are significant populations in the rest of India and throughout the world, especially in East Africa, the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada.

History

Gujarat's position on the Arabian Sea made its people merchants and mariners from the Indus Valley era onward: Gujarati traders dealt from the ports of Lothal, Bharuch and later Surat with Arabia, Persia and East Africa. Under successive Hindu, Muslim and Maratha rulers, mercantile communities — Hindu and Jain Banias, Muslim Bohras and Khojas, Parsis settled from Persia — built networks that dominated western India's commerce.

Society and culture

Gujaratis are religiously diverse — predominantly Hindu, with large Jain and Muslim minorities and an influential Parsi community — and are noted for enterprise and vegetarian food traditions. The nine-night festival of Navratri, with its garba and dandiya dances, is the signature celebration, recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. Gujarati cuisine, largely vegetarian, is known for snacks (farsan), the thali, and a characteristic balance of sweet, salty and spicy flavours.

Diaspora and notable figures

The diaspora expanded under British rule to East and South Africa, and after the 1960s to Britain, North America and the Gulf; Gujaratis form one of the most prosperous Indian communities in both the UK and the US. Notable Gujaratis include Mahatma Gandhi, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Muhammad Ali Jinnah (born into a Gujarati-speaking family in Karachi), and business figures such as Dhirubhai Ambani and Gautam Adani.
