Bicycle fork

A rigid front fork; the steerer tube passes through the frame's head tube

The bicycle fork is the part of a bicycle that holds the front wheel and allows the rider to steer and balance the bicycle. It consists of a steerer tube, which rotates inside the frame's head tube on the headset bearings and is clamped to the stem and handlebars, a crown, and two blades (legs) ending in dropouts that hold the front axle.

Geometry

Fork geometry strongly affects handling. Key measurements are the rake (or offset), the perpendicular distance from the steering axis to the front axle, and the resulting trail, which gives the bicycle self-centring stability. Rake is provided by curving the blades, angling them from the crown, or offsetting the dropouts. Fork length (axle-to-crown) must match the frame design, and blades may carry mounts for rim brakes, disc brake calipers, mudguards and racks.

Types and materials

Most road and utility bicycles use a rigid fork made of steel, aluminium or carbon fibre. Mountain bikes generally use a suspension fork, in which the lower legs telescope into the uppers against coil springs or an air spring with oil damping, providing travel from about 80 mm to over 170 mm for downhill racing. Specialised forms include the single-sided Cannondale Lefty fork and girder and leading-link designs used on some folders and cargo bikes.
