Joseph Francis Charles Rock (1884–1962) was an Austrian-American botanist, explorer, geographer and linguist. He is best known for his extensive botanical expeditions in southwest China, particularly in Yunnan, Sichuan and Tibet, and for his studies of the Naxi people and their culture.

Early life

Rock was born in Vienna and emigrated to the United States in 1905. Largely self-taught, he settled in Hawaii, where he became an authority on the native flora and taught at the College of Hawaii.

Expeditions in China

From the 1920s, Rock led expeditions sponsored by institutions such as the United States Department of Agriculture, Harvard University and the National Geographic Society. He collected thousands of plant specimens and seeds, sending back chestnuts, rhododendrons and other plants that entered Western gardens. His vivid accounts of the Minya Konka region and the kingdom of Muli appeared in National Geographic magazine.

Naxi studies

Rock devoted much of his later life to documenting the Naxi people of Lijiang, compiling a large dictionary of the Naxi language and translating Dongba pictographic manuscripts. His two-volume history of the Naxi remained a standard reference.

Legacy

The writer James Hilton is widely believed to have drawn on Rock's descriptions of the Lijiang region for the fictional Shangri-La in the novel Lost Horizon. Rock died in Honolulu in 1962.
