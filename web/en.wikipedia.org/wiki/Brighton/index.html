Brighton is a seaside resort town on the south coast of England, forming part of the city of Brighton and Hove in East Sussex. Located about 47 miles (76 km) south of London, it developed from the fishing village of Brighthelmstone into one of Britain's most fashionable resorts in the 18th and 19th centuries and remains a popular tourist destination noted for its seafront, piers, nightlife and large LGBTQ community.

History

Brighthelmstone was a fishing and agricultural settlement recorded in the Domesday Book. Its transformation began in the 1750s when Dr Richard Russell promoted the health benefits of seawater bathing and drinking. Fashionable visitors followed, and the town's status was sealed when George, Prince of Wales (later George IV), made Brighton his seaside residence and built the Royal Pavilion, an exotic palace in the Indo-Saracenic style completed in the 1820s to designs by John Nash.

The arrival of the railway from London in 1841 brought mass day-trip tourism. The West Pier opened in 1866 and the Palace Pier (now Brighton Pier) in 1899. In 2000 Brighton was merged with neighbouring Hove to form the unitary authority of Brighton and Hove, which was granted city status in 2001.

Attractions and culture

Major attractions include the Royal Pavilion, Brighton Pier with its funfair, the promenade and shingle beach, the British Airways i360 observation tower, and The Lanes, a maze of narrow shopping streets on the site of the old fishing town. The North Laine area is known for independent shops and cafés. Brighton hosts festivals including the Brighton Festival and Brighton Fringe each May, and one of the UK's largest Pride celebrations each August.

The town is home to Brighton & Hove Albion F.C., who play at the Falmer Stadium, and to the universities of Sussex and Brighton.

Transport

Brighton station offers frequent fast trains to London Victoria and London Bridge, Gatwick Airport and along the coast. The A23 connects the town to London, and the A27 runs along the south coast.
