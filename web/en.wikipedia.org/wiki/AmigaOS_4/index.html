AmigaOS 4

AmigaOS 4 is the fourth major generation of the Amiga operating system, developed by the Belgian company Hyperion Entertainment for PowerPC-based computers. It is the native PowerPC successor to Commodore's classic AmigaOS, which ran on Motorola 68k processors. AmigaOS 4 runs on the AmigaOne family of motherboards, on Sam440/Sam460 boards from ACube Systems, on Pegasos II machines, and on hardware such as the AmigaOne X1000 and X5000 from A-EON Technology; a "Classic" version also supports PowerPC-accelerated classic Amigas.

History

After Commodore's bankruptcy in 1994, the Amiga operating system passed through several owners. In 2001 Hyperion Entertainment contracted with Amiga, Inc. to develop AmigaOS 4 as a PowerPC port and enhancement of the 3.x source code. A developer pre-release of AmigaOS 4.0 appeared in 2004, with the official release in December 2006. AmigaOS 4.1 followed in 2008, and the "Final Edition" update, shipped in 2014, bundled accumulated updates. Legal disputes over the AmigaOS rights between Hyperion and Amiga parties complicated development for years and were the subject of litigation settled and re-litigated over time.

Features

AmigaOS 4 retains the classic architecture of Exec (microkernel-style multitasking core), the Workbench desktop, AmigaDOS and ARexx-style scripting via Python/ReAction GUI toolkit, while adding a fully PowerPC-native kernel, virtualised memory addressing with a new slab allocator, support for modern graphics cards via Picasso96-derived drivers, USB stacks, and an integrated 68k emulator (Petunia) plus the E-UAE JIT environment for running legacy software. Its small footprint and fast boot remain characteristic of the platform.
