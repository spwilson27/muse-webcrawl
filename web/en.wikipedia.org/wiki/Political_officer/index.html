Political officer

A political commissar addressing soldiers

A political officer, or political commissar, is an official attached to a military unit who represents the ruling political party or government, supervises political education, and monitors the loyalty and morale of troops. The best-known examples are the commissars of the Soviet Red Army (Russian: politruk at company level) and the political officers of China's People's Liberation Army.

History

The institution descends from the representatives sent by the French revolutionary government to oversee its generals in the 1790s. In Soviet Russia, military commissars were appointed from 1918 to ensure the Red Army, largely officered by former tsarist specialists, remained loyal to the Bolshevik government. Commissars initially shared command authority, countersigning orders; the balance between political and military authority shifted repeatedly, with dual command restored in crises such as 1941, and commissars were retitled deputy commanders for political affairs (zampolit) in 1942. Political officers at all levels conducted propaganda, education, and personnel work.

Other usages

The term "political officer" is also used for diplomats specialising in political reporting, for example in embassies and foreign ministries, and for officials in colonial administrations such as British India's frontier service. In the People's Liberation Army, political commissars remain co-equal with unit commanders, reflecting the principle of party control of the military.
