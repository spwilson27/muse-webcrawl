John Job Crew Bradfield (26 December 1867 – 23 September 1943) was an Australian civil engineer, best known as the designer and chief engineer of the Sydney Harbour Bridge and as the visionary behind Sydney's underground city railway. His comprehensive scheme for the electrification of Sydney's suburban railways and the construction of the city underground loop transformed the transport of Australia's largest city, and many of his plans were still being completed decades after his death.

Early life and education

Bradfield was born at Sandgate in Brisbane, Queensland, the youngest son of a Crimean War veteran. He won a scholarship to Ipswich Grammar School and later the University of Sydney, winning the University Gold Medal in engineering in 1889. He worked for the Queensland Railways Department before moving to the New South Wales Department of Public Works in 1891, where he designed dams, bridges and railway works across the state.

The Bradfield Scheme for Sydney

In 1913 Bradfield became chief engineer for metropolitan railway construction. His grand plan, presented over the following years, proposed an electrified suburban railway network, a city underground railway loop, and a bridge across Sydney Harbour linking the city with the North Shore. Construction of the underground began in the 1920s; the first city stations, including Museum and St James, opened in 1926. Parts of Bradfield's plan shelved during the Depression — such as the eastern suburbs line — were only built generations later.

Sydney Harbour Bridge

Bradfield championed a steel arch design for the harbour crossing, against competing cantilever proposals, and oversaw the international tender won by the English firm Dorman Long in 1924. As chief engineer he supervised the design and construction from 1924 until the bridge opened on 19 March 1932. The two-hinged steel arch, carrying road, rail and pedestrian traffic, remains one of the world's great bridges and the defining symbol of Sydney.

Other works and legacy

Bradfield also designed Brisbane's Story Bridge and the Cataract and Burrinjuck dams, and late in life proposed the "Bradfield Scheme" to divert coastal Queensland rivers inland to irrigate the arid interior — a plan that bears his name but was never adopted. He died in Sydney in 1943. The Bradfield Highway across the bridge, the suburb of Bradfield, and Canberra's Bradfield Park commemorate him.
