Leglock

A leglock is a joint lock directed at the leg, such as a kneebar, heel hook, ankle lock or toe hold, used in grappling martial arts including Brazilian jiu-jitsu, sambo, catch wrestling, submission wrestling and mixed martial arts. Leglocks attack the joints of the ankle, knee or hip, or the muscles and tendons of the leg, by hyperextension, hyperflexion or rotational force, compelling the opponent to submit ("tap out") or suffer injury.

Types

The straight ankle lock (or Achilles lock) plantar-flexes the ankle and compresses the Achilles tendon. The heel hook twists the lower leg, transmitting rotational force to the knee ligaments, and is considered among the most dangerous submissions because damage can occur before pain is felt. The toe hold rotates the ankle using a figure-four grip on the foot. The kneebar hyperextends the knee in the same way an armbar hyperextends the elbow, while calf slicers and thigh compressions attack the muscles of the leg.

Leg entanglement positions

Modern leg attacks are usually entered from entanglements such as ashi garami, the single-leg X guard, 50/50, and the "saddle" or inside sankaku position popularised by the Danaher Death Squad in the 2010s, which systematised heel-hook attacks in no-gi grappling.

Rules

Legality varies by art and rule set. Many Brazilian jiu-jitsu federations restrict heel hooks and reaping the knee at lower belt levels; sambo permits many leglocks but prohibits heel hooks; in professional mixed martial arts most leglocks are legal. Historically, judo generally prohibits leglocks in competition.
