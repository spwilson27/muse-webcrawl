Doxing

Doxing or doxxing is the act of publicly providing personally identifiable information about an individual or organization, usually via the Internet and without their consent. Historically, the term was used to refer to the aggregation and provision of such information by hackers. The aggregation and provision of previously published material is generally legal, though it may violate laws concerning stalking and intimidation. Doxing may be carried out for reasons including online shaming, extortion, and vigilante law enforcement assistance.

Etymology

"Doxing" is a neologism originating from a spelling alteration of the abbreviation "docs" (for "documents"), arising within 1990s hacker culture, where dropping an opponent's documents — their real name, address, and telephone number — was a form of retaliation. The term is unrelated to the concept of documents in computing, and is sometimes written "doxxing."

Methods and motives

Information may be assembled from public databases, social media, hacked accounts, or social engineering. Motives range from harassment and political retaliation to journalistic identification of extremists and "hacktivist" exposure — a spectrum that has fuelled debate about where legitimate research ends and harmful exposure begins. Doxing is frequently a precursor to further harms, including swatting and identity theft.

Legal status and responses

Several jurisdictions have moved to criminalise malicious doxing: Hong Kong amended its privacy ordinance in 2021 to create doxing offences, Singapore's Protection from Harassment Act covers related conduct, and various US states and proposed federal bills address publication of personal information with intent to harm. Social media platforms prohibit posting others' private information under their policies.
