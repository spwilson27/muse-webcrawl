Captain of the port

Image placeholder: A harbour within a captain of the port's zone.

The captain of the port (COTP) is an official responsible for enforcing port safety, security, and maritime law within a designated zone. In the United States, the title is held by a United States Coast Guard officer appointed for a specific Captain of the Port Zone, which may encompass one or more ports and large stretches of coastline and navigable waterways.

Duties

A captain of the port's authority derives from statutes including the Ports and Waterways Safety Act, the Magnuson Act, and the Maritime Transportation Security Act of 2002. Powers include controlling vessel movements, establishing safety and security zones, ordering vessels to anchor or depart, supervising the loading of dangerous cargoes, and coordinating responses to oil spills and other marine casualties. The COTC also oversees search-and-rescue coordination and marine inspection functions within the sector.

Organisation

In the US system, captain of the port duties are usually exercised by the commander of a Coast Guard Sector, who simultaneously holds the roles of officer in charge of marine inspection, search and rescue mission coordinator, and federal on-scene coordinator for pollution response. Equivalent harbourmaster functions in other countries are performed by port authority or maritime administration officials.
