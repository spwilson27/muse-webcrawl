Calomel
Chemical formulaHg₂Cl₂
NameMercury(I) chloride
AppearanceWhite to yellow-white solid

Calomel is the common name for mercury(I) chloride, Hg₂Cl₂, a white crystalline compound. The word derives from Greek words meaning "beautiful black," referring to its reaction with ammonia, which turns it black.

Historical use

From the 16th into the early 20th century, calomel was widely used as a medicine — as a purgative, diuretic, and treatment for syphilis and yellow fever. Famous patients included Andrew Jackson and Louisa May Alcott. Its mercury content made it dangerous, causing salivation, tooth loss, and poisoning, and it fell out of use as safer drugs appeared.

Electrochemistry

The saturated calomel electrode (SCE), based on the mercury/calomel redox couple, long served as a standard reference electrode in electrochemistry, though it has been largely replaced by silver/silver-chloride electrodes because of mercury's toxicity.
