Ski flying

Ski flying is a winter sport discipline derived from ski jumping in which competitors fly as far as possible down specially built, extra-large hills. It is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS), which sanctions the Ski Flying World Championships and a small number of World Cup events each season.

Hills

Ski flying hills have a construction point (K-point) of 185 metres or more and a hill size of 210 metres or more — far larger than large ski jumping hills (K-120). Only a handful of ski flying hills exist in the world, including Planica in Slovenia, Oberstdorf in Germany, Vikersund in Norway, Kulm in Austria, and Harrachov in the Czech Republic. Vikersundbakken and Letalnica in Planica are the largest.

Records and technique

Competitors use the V-style, leaning far forward over long, wide skis to maximise lift, and reach speeds near 100 km/h at take-off. Flights of over 250 metres are routine at the biggest hills; the world record stands beyond 250 metres, with Stefan Kraft's 253.5-metre flight at Vikersund in 2017 among the longest recorded. Because of the extreme distances and danger, ski flying is restricted to experienced elite jumpers, and competitions use fewer rounds than ski jumping events.

History

The first flights over 100 metres were achieved at Planica in the 1930s, and the first Ski Flying World Championships were held there in 1972. Ski flying is not part of the Olympic programme.
