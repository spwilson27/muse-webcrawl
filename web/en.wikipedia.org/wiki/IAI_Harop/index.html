IAI Harop

The IAI Harop is a loitering munition developed by the MBT Missiles Division of Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI). It is an anti-radiation drone designed to loiter over a target area, locate targets—particularly enemy radar emitters and air-defence systems—and attack them by crashing into them with a built-in explosive warhead. The Harop is a larger, longer-endurance development of the earlier IAI Harpy anti-radiation drone.

Design

Unlike the fully autonomous Harpy, the Harop features a "man-in-the-loop" capability: an operator can observe the target area through the drone's electro-optical/infrared seeker and designate targets before the final dive. The air vehicle is launched from canisters mounted on trucks or naval vessels, has folding wings, an endurance of about six hours, a range of roughly 1,000 km, and carries a warhead of approximately 23 kg. It can also abort an attack and return to loitering if a target disappears.

Operational use

The Harop has been exported to several countries, including India and Azerbaijan. Azerbaijani forces used Harop loitering munitions during the 2016 Nagorno-Karabakh clashes and extensively during the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war, where they were credited with destroying Armenian air-defence systems, armour, and other high-value targets. Its combat employment is frequently cited as a landmark in the use of loitering munitions in modern warfare.
