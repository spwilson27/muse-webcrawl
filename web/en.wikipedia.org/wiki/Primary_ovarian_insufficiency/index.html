Primary ovarian insufficiency

Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI), historically called premature ovarian failure, is a condition in which the ovaries stop functioning normally before the age of 40. It leads to reduced estrogen production and irregular or absent menstrual periods, and it is a cause of infertility.

Causes

In most cases the cause is unknown. Known causes include genetic conditions such as Turner syndrome and fragile X premutation, autoimmune disorders, chemotherapy and pelvic radiation, and certain infections or metabolic diseases. Unlike menopause, ovarian function in POI can be intermittent, and spontaneous pregnancy occurs in a minority of cases.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on menstrual disturbance for at least four months plus elevated follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) levels measured on two occasions. Management centres on hormone replacement therapy until the typical age of menopause to protect bone and cardiovascular health, fertility counselling (including oocyte donation), and screening for associated autoimmune conditions.
