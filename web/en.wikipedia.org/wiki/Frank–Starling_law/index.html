Frank–Starling law

The Frank–Starling law of the heart states that the stroke volume of the heart increases in response to an increase in the volume of blood in the ventricles before contraction (the end-diastolic volume), when all other factors remain constant. It is named after physiologists Otto Frank and Ernest Starling, whose work in the 1890s–1910s established the relationship.

Mechanism

Greater filling stretches cardiac muscle fibres, increasing sarcomere length toward its optimum and enhancing calcium sensitivity, so the ventricle contracts more forcefully and ejects the extra volume. This intrinsic mechanism automatically matches the outputs of the right and left ventricles and adjusts output to venous return.

Clinical significance

The relationship is plotted as the Frank–Starling (ventricular function) curve. In heart failure the curve is flattened and shifted, so increases in preload produce little extra output and instead raise filling pressures, causing congestion.
