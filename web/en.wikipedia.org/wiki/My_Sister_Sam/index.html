My Sister Sam

My Sister Sam is an American television sitcom created by Stephen Fischer and produced by Pony Productions and Warner Bros. Television that aired on CBS from October 6, 1986, to April 12, 1988. It starred Pam Dawber as San Francisco photographer Samantha "Sam" Russell and Rebecca Schaeffer as her teenage sister Patti, who comes to live with her.

Premise and cast

Sam Russell, a 29-year-old commercial photographer, lives comfortably on her own until her 16-year-old sister Patti arrives from Oregon after the death of their parents and moves in, turning Sam's orderly life upside down. The cast also included Jenny O'Hara as Sam's agent Dixie Randazzo, Joel Brooks as her neighbour J.D. Lucas, and David Naughton as Sam's sometime boyfriend Jack Kincaid.

Broadcast history

The series debuted to promising ratings on Monday nights in its first season, ranking in the top 25, but CBS moved it to Saturday nights for its second season, where ratings collapsed; it was cancelled in 1988 after 44 episodes. The show is remembered today largely because of the murder of Rebecca Schaeffer in July 1989 by an obsessed fan, Robert John Bardo; her death prompted California to enact the nation's first anti-stalking law and led to changes in the handling of personal information by the state's Department of Motor Vehicles, culminating in the federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act of 1994.

Theme

The series' theme song, "Room Enough for Two," was performed by Kim Carnes.
