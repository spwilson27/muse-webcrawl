Maelbeek/Maalbeek metro station

Maelbeek (French) or Maalbeek (Dutch) is a Brussels metro station on lines 1 and 5, located in the City of Brussels under the Rue de la Loi, in the heart of the European Quarter near the institutions of the European Union. The station is named after the Maelbeek stream, which was covered over in the 19th century.

History

The station first opened on 17 December 1969 as a premetro (underground tram) station on the line between De Brouckère and Schuman, and was converted to full metro operation on 20 September 1976. The walls of the station are decorated with works by the artist Benoît van Innis, whose series of portraits of passengers — eight drawings covering the platform walls — were restored after 2016.

2016 Brussels bombings

On 22 March 2016, during the Brussels bombings, an explosion occurred aboard a train departing Maelbeek station toward Arts-Loi, shortly after the twin attacks at Brussels Airport. Twenty people were killed in the station attack and many more injured. A memorial artwork by Benoît van Innis, an olive tree motif symbolising peace and remembrance, was installed in the station, which reopened on 25 April 2016.

Services

The station is served by metro lines 1 (Weststation–Stockel/Stokkel) and 5 (Erasme/Erasmus–Herrmann-Debroux), providing direct access to the European Parliament area at Schuman and the city centre.
