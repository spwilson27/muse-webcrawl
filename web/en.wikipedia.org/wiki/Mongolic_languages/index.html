Mongolic languages

The Mongolic languages are a language family spoken by the Mongolic peoples in Central and North Asia. The best-known member is Mongolian, the official language of Mongolia, spoken in its Khalkha dialect by the majority of the family's roughly six million speakers. Mongolic languages are agglutinative, use vowel harmony, and place the verb at the end of the sentence (SOV word order).

Classification

The family is usually divided into:

  • Central Mongolic: Khalkha Mongolian, Buryat (in Russia), Kalmyk and Oirat (in Kalmykia and western Mongolia/China), and Ordos.
  • Peripheral languages: Dagur (in Heilongjiang and Inner Mongolia), the Shirongolic languages of Qinghai and Gansu (Monguor, Bonan, Dongxiang/Santa, Kangjia), and Moghol of Afghanistan, now nearly or entirely extinct.

Para-Mongolic languages of earlier steppe peoples, such as Khitan, may be related but are imperfectly known. The once-popular "Altaic" hypothesis grouping Mongolic with Turkic and Tungusic is no longer widely accepted.

History

Middle Mongol, the language of the 13th-century Mongol Empire, is documented in The Secret History of the Mongols and in inscriptions in the 'Phags-pa and traditional vertical scripts. The modern languages diverged from a common ancestor, Proto-Mongolic, spoken around the time of Genghis Khan.

Writing systems

The traditional Mongolian script, derived from Uyghur, is written vertically and remains official in Inner Mongolia; Mongolia adopted Cyrillic in the 1940s and is now also restoring the classical script for official use.
