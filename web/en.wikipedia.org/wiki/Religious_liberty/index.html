Religious liberty

Religious liberty, or freedom of religion, is the principle that supports the freedom of an individual or community, in public or private, to manifest religion or belief in teaching, practice, worship and observance. It is generally recognised as a fundamental human right, protected in instruments such as Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.

Scope

Freedom of religion includes the freedom to change one's religion or belief, to practise it alone or with others, and to be free from coercion that would impair that choice. It is distinct from, though related to, religious tolerance: tolerance historically meant permission granted by an authority to dissent, whereas liberty frames religious freedom as an inherent right rather than a revocable concession.

History

Ideas of religious liberty were advanced by thinkers such as Roger Williams and John Locke, and were given early legal expression in the Maryland Toleration Act of 1649, the Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom (1786), and the First Amendment to the United States Constitution (1791), which prohibits laws establishing religion or prohibiting its free exercise.
