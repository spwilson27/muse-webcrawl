Vedanga

The Vedanga (Sanskrit: "limbs of the Veda") are six auxiliary disciplines of Hinduism that developed to support the study, preservation, and ritual use of the Vedas. They are traditionally likened to parts of the body of the Veda personified.

The six Vedangas are: Shiksha (phonetics and pronunciation), Kalpa (ritual procedure), Vyakarana (grammar, including Panini's Ashtadhyayi), Nirukta (etymology and explanation of difficult words, by Yaska), Chandas (prosody and metre, by Pingala), and Jyotisha (astrology and astronomy for fixing ritual times).

History

The Vedangas took shape roughly in the mid-first millennium BCE as terse sutra texts, designed for memorisation. They mark a transition from purely ritual exegesis to systematic scholarship in phonetics, linguistics, mathematics, and astronomy in ancient India.
