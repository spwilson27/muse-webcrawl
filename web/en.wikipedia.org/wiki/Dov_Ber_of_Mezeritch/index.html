Dov Ber ben Avraham of Mezeritch (c. 1700/1704 – 1772), known as the Maggid of Mezritch, was a disciple of Rabbi Israel ben Eliezer (the Baal Shem Tov), the founder of Hasidic Judaism, and his successor as leader of the movement. Dov Ber is regarded as the first systematic philosopher of Hasidism and the architect of its transformation from a small circle around the Baal Shem Tov into a broad religious movement.

Life

Born in Lokachi in Volhynia, Dov Ber became a learned Talmudist and itinerant preacher (maggid). After suffering ill health, he visited the Baal Shem Tov, who was also known as a healer, and became his devoted follower. On the Baal Shem Tov's death in 1760, Dov Ber was recognised as his successor and established his court at Mezhirichi (Mezeritch) in Volhynia, later moving to Rivne (Rovne).

Teachings

Unlike his master, Dov Ber gathered an organised circle of disciples, the hevraya kadisha ("holy fellowship"), and developed the doctrines of Hasidism into a coherent system, emphasising devekut (cleaving to God), the divine presence in all things, and the role of the tzadik (righteous leader) as an intermediary between God and the community. His teachings were collected posthumously by his disciples in works such as Maggid Devarav le-Ya'akov.

Legacy

Dov Ber's disciples carried Hasidism across Eastern Europe, founding dynasties that endured for centuries. They included Elimelech of Lizhensk, Shneur Zalman of Liadi (founder of Chabad), Levi Yitzchok of Berditchev and Yaakov Yitzchak of Lublin. After his death in 1772, leadership passed not to a single successor but to these disciples, which decisively shaped Hasidism's decentralised, dynastic character.
