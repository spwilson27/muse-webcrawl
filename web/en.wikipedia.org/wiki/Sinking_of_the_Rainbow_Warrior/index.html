Sinking of the Rainbow Warrior

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sinking of the Rainbow Warrior Date: 10 July 1985
Location: Auckland Harbour, New Zealand
Perpetrators: Agents of the French DGSE
Deaths: 1 (Fernando Pereira)
Outcome: International scandal; French defence minister resigned

The sinking of the Rainbow Warrior was an operation by the French foreign intelligence service, the Direction générale de la sécurité extérieure (DGSE), carried out on 10 July 1985. Agents bombed and sank the flagship of the Greenpeace fleet, the Rainbow Warrior, while it was berthed in Auckland Harbour, New Zealand. The ship had been preparing to lead a flotilla protesting French nuclear testing at Moruroa atoll in French Polynesia. Portuguese-Dutch photographer Fernando Pereira drowned when he went below decks after the first explosion to retrieve his equipment and a second bomb detonated.

The operation

Under the codename Opération Satanique, DGSE combat divers attached two limpet mines to the hull of the vessel. Other agents had infiltrated Greenpeace's Auckland office posing as volunteers, and a French crew aboard the yacht Ouvéa smuggled in the explosives and equipment. The two explosions, minutes apart shortly before midnight, holed the ship, which sank within minutes at its berth in Marsden Wharf.

Investigation and arrests

New Zealand police mounted one of the country's largest investigations. Two agents, Dominique Prieur and Alain Mafart, who had posed as a Swiss couple on honeymoon, were arrested after being linked to a rented van seen near the scene; they pleaded guilty to manslaughter and arson and were sentenced to ten years in prison. Other members of the team escaped, several aboard the Ouvéa, which was intercepted in Australia but released; the yacht was later scuttled and its crew taken off by a French submarine.

Fallout

France initially denied involvement, but after an official inquiry under pressure, Prime Minister Laurent Fabius admitted in September 1985 that French agents had carried out the bombing under orders. Defence Minister Charles Hernu resigned and the head of the DGSE was dismissed. The affair severely damaged France–New Zealand relations and Franco-European trade, as France threatened trade sanctions to obtain the agents' release. Under a UN-brokered ruling in 1986, France apologised and paid compensation — including NZ$13 million to New Zealand and US$8 million to Greenpeace — and Prieur and Mafart were transferred to a French military base on Hao atoll, from which both were repatriated to France within two years, angering New Zealand. A further settlement in 1991 followed France's breach of the agreement.

Legacy

The wreck was refloated, found beyond repair, and scuttled in 1987 off the Cavalli Islands as a dive attraction and artificial reef. The bombing became a defining episode in New Zealand's anti-nuclear identity, and a replacement ship, Rainbow Warrior II, joined the Greenpeace fleet in 1989. The case remains a textbook example of state-sponsored sabotage against a non-governmental organisation.
