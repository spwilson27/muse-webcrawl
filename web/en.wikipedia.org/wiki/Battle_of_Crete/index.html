Battle of Crete

The Battle of Crete (German: Luftlandeschlacht um Kreta, Greek: Μάχη της Κρήτης) was fought during the Second World War on the Greek island of Crete. It began on the morning of 20 May 1941, when Nazi Germany launched an airborne invasion codenamed Operation Mercury (Unternehmen Merkur). Greek, Allied (British Commonwealth) and Cretan forces defended the island.

Background

After Germany conquered mainland Greece in April 1941 (Operation Marita), Allied forces evacuated to Crete. The island's airfields at Maleme, Rethymno and Heraklion, and the harbour at Souda Bay, made it strategically valuable. British signals intelligence (Ultra) gave the defenders, under New Zealand Major-General Bernard Freyberg, detailed warning of the German plan.

The battle

On 20 May 1941 German Fallschirmjäger of the 7th Air Division under Kurt Student parachuted onto the island, supported by gliders. Paratroopers suffered very heavy losses and initially failed to take any airfield intact. The turning point came at Maleme, where a confused Allied withdrawal from Hill 107 allowed the Germans to capture the airfield on 21 May and fly in reinforcements of the 5th Mountain Division under Julius Ringel.

Fighting continued around Galatas, Rethymno and Heraklion. Despite fierce resistance, including from Cretan civilians, the Allied position collapsed; the Royal Navy suffered heavy losses to the Luftwaffe while evacuating some 16,500 troops from Sfakia to Egypt by 1 June. About 12,000 Allied soldiers were captured.

Aftermath

Crete fell to Germany, but the paratroop arm had been so mauled — roughly a quarter of the attacking force killed — that Hitler forbade further large-scale airborne operations. The battle is often called the "graveyard of the Fallschirmjäger". Cretan resistance continued throughout the occupation, and German reprisals against villages were severe. Crete remained in German hands until May 1945.
