Røros is a municipality and historic mining town in Trøndelag county, Norway, close to the Swedish border. Founded after the discovery of copper ore in 1644, the town grew around the Røros Copper Works, which operated for more than three centuries until 1977.

History

The town was burned by Swedish troops during the Great Northern War era conflicts yet rebuilt, and its grid of wooden houses around the great slag heaps preserves the character of a 17th–19th century mining community. The monumental Røros Church, completed in 1784 and seating some 1,600 people, dominates the town.

Heritage

In 1980 Røros was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The annual winter market, Rørosmartnan, held since 1854, draws tens of thousands of visitors, and the town is famed in Norway for its harsh winter cold — a national record low of −50.4 °C was recorded there in 1914.
