Parliamentary debate
Type: Competitive academic debating format
Teams: Two sides (Government / Proposition and Opposition), typically 2–4 speakers each
Major variants: British Parliamentary, American Parliamentary, Canadian Parliamentary, World Schools
Governing event: World Universities Debating Championship

Parliamentary debate is an academic debating format that imitates the procedures and argumentative style of a parliamentary legislature. Two sides — the Government (or Proposition) and the Opposition — debate a motion, typically announced only a short time (often 15 minutes) before the round begins, emphasising impromptu speaking, wit, and general knowledge alongside structured argument.

Formats

In the British Parliamentary format, used at the World Universities Debating Championship, four teams of two compete in one room: Opening and Closing Government against Opening and Closing Opposition, with each speaker delivering a seven-minute speech. American Parliamentary debate, governed by bodies such as the American Parliamentary Debate Association and the National Parliamentary Debate Association, generally features two teams with longer speeches and points of information. Canadian Parliamentary and Australasian formats have their own conventions on team size and speech length.

Procedure

Speakers construct cases, offer and accept "points of information" during opponents' speeches (usually protected in the first and last minute), and deliver rebuttal and summary speeches. Judges rank teams or speakers on matter (arguments), manner (delivery), and method (structure).

History

Intercollegiate parliamentary debating grew out of 19th-century literary and debating societies at British and American universities. The modern international circuit centred on the World Universities Debating Championship dates from the early 1980s, and parliamentary formats are now the dominant style of university debating worldwide.
