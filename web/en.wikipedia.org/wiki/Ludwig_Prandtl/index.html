Ludwig Prandtl (4 February 1875 – 15 August 1953) was a German physicist and engineer, widely regarded as the founder of modern aerodynamics. His boundary layer theory of 1904 revolutionised fluid mechanics by reconciling mathematical theory with experimental observation.

Career

Prandtl studied mechanical engineering in Munich and took his doctorate in 1900. After a spell in industry, where he studied buckling and flow problems, he became professor at Hanover in 1901 and in 1904 moved to the University of Göttingen, where he built an institute for fluid mechanics that became a world centre of aerodynamics research. His students included Theodore von Kármán and many other leading aerodynamicists.

Contributions

In his celebrated 1904 paper, Prandtl proposed that flow past a body can be divided into a thin boundary layer near the surface, where viscosity matters, and an outer region of nearly inviscid flow — an insight that explained drag and flow separation and made rational wing design possible. He developed the lifting-line theory of wings, the Prandtl–Glauert compressibility correction, and the Prandtl number in heat transfer bears his name. At Göttingen he oversaw pioneering wind tunnels, including the closed-circuit design now standard worldwide.

Later life

Prandtl directed the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Fluid Mechanics in Göttingen and continued working through the Second World War. He died in Göttingen in 1953. The Ludwig Prandtl Ring, awarded by the German Society for Aeronautics and Astronautics, honours his memory.
