Computer numerical control

Computer numerical control (CNC)
Application: Machine tools
Programming language: G-code
Origins: 1940s–50s numerical control

Computer numerical control (CNC) is the automated control of machine tools — such as mills, lathes, routers, grinders and 3D printers — by means of a computer executing pre-programmed sequences of machine control commands, most commonly written in G-code.

History

Numerical control began in the 1940s–50s with punched-tape systems developed for the US Air Force and MIT. As computers became affordable, NC evolved into CNC, allowing programs to be edited, stored and simulated on the machine controller itself.

Process

A part is designed in CAD software, converted by CAM software into toolpaths, and executed by the machine, which moves cutting tools and the workpiece along multiple axes with high precision and repeatability. CNC machining underpins modern manufacturing in aerospace, automotive and medical industries.
