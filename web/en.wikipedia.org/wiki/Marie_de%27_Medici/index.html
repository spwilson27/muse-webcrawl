Marie de' Medici (French: Marie de Médicis; 26 April 1575 – 3 July 1642) was Queen of France and Navarre as the second wife of King Henry IV. She acted as regent of France for her son, King Louis XIII, from 1610 until 1617.

Marriage and regency

Marie married Henry IV by proxy in Florence in 1600, bringing a large dowry that relieved the crown's debts. The day after Henry's assassination in May 1610 she was confirmed as regent. Her regency reversed much of Henry's anti-Habsburg policy, arranging the double Spanish marriage of Louis XIII to Anne of Austria and of Élisabeth to the future Philip IV.

Conflict with Louis XIII

In 1617 Louis XIII asserted his authority, exiling his mother to Blois after the assassination of her favourite Concino Concini. She escaped and raised two rebellions before being reconciled through the mediation of Cardinal Richelieu, whose career she had advanced. Her attempt to oust Richelieu in the "Day of the Dupes" (1630) failed, and she fled France in 1631, never to return.

Patronage

In Paris she built the Luxembourg Palace and its gardens, modelled on the Pitti Palace of her childhood, and commissioned from Peter Paul Rubens the celebrated cycle of 24 monumental paintings depicting her life, now in the Louvre.
