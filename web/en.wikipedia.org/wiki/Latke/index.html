Latke

A latke (Yiddish: לאַטקע) is a type of potato pancake in Jewish cuisine, traditionally eaten during Hanukkah. Latkes are made from grated potatoes mixed with egg, flour or matzo meal, and seasoning, formed into patties and fried in oil — the oil recalling the miracle of the temple oil commemorated by the holiday.

Latke
Type: Pancake/fritter
Main ingredients: potatoes, onion, egg, flour or matzo meal
Served: hot, with applesauce and/or sour cream
Associated holiday: Hanukkah

History

Fried Hanukkah foods in oil predate the potato version: Italian Jews fried cheese pancakes (recalling the story of Judith), and Sephardic and Middle Eastern Jewish communities made various fritters. In Eastern Europe, where potatoes became a cheap staple in the 19th century, Ashkenazi Jews adopted grated potato pancakes; the word "latke" derives from a Slavic word for a small pancake or patch.

Preparation

Potatoes (and often onion) are grated, squeezed dry, mixed with beaten egg and a binder, seasoned, and shallow-fried until crisp and golden. Variations use sweet potato, zucchini, carrot, or cheese; an annual tongue-in-cheek debate at the University of Chicago, the Latke–Hamantash Debate, has argued the relative merits of the Hanukkah latke and the Purim hamantash since 1946.
