Girnar
Elevation1,031 m (3,383 ft)
LocationJunagadh district, Gujarat, India
SignificanceSacred to Hindus and Jains

Girnar is an ancient hill in Junagadh district of Gujarat, India. Rising to about 1,031 metres, it is the highest point in Gujarat and one of the state's most important pilgrimage sites, sacred to both Hindus and Jains. The hill is older than the Himalayas in geological terms and has been a place of worship for millennia.

Pilgrimage

Pilgrims climb some 10,000 stone steps to reach the peaks. The first summit bears temples dedicated to the Hindu goddess Amba (Ambaji), where newly married couples seek blessings. Other peaks carry shrines to Gorakhnath (the highest point), Oghadnath, and Dattatreya. For Jains, Girnar — known in tradition as Raivata or Ujjayanta — is one of the five major tirthas: it is where the 22nd Tirthankara, Neminatha, attained moksha (liberation). A celebrated cluster of Jain temples, built largely between the 12th and 15th centuries in the Solanki style, stands on the hillside, including temples dedicated to Neminatha and Mallinatha.

Sacred sites and history

At the foot of the hill stands a rock bearing edicts of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE), alongside inscriptions of the Kshatrapa king Rudradaman I (2nd century CE) and the Gupta emperor Skandagupta — a rare palimpsest of early Indian epigraphy. The nearby Sudarshana lake, built under Chandragupta Maurya, is described in Rudradaman's inscription. The annual Girnar Parikrama, a roughly 36-kilometre circumambulation of the hill held around Kartik Poornima, draws hundreds of thousands of devotees, and the Shivaratri fair at Bhavnath temple at Girnar's base is another major gathering. A ropeway inaugurated in 2020 carries pilgrims most of the way up the mountain.
