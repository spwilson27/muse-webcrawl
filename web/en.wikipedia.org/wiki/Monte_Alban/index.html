Monte Alban (Spanish: Monte Albán) is a large pre-Columbian archaeological site in the state of Oaxaca, Mexico, situated on a low mountain range rising above the plain of the Oaxaca Valley. Founded around 500 BC by the Zapotec, it was one of the earliest cities in Mesoamerica and the political and ceremonial capital of the Zapotec state for more than a thousand years.

History

The founders levelled the top of the hill to create a vast main plaza surrounded by temples, palaces and ball courts. At its peak (c. AD 500), the city and its surrounding hills may have held 25,000–35,000 inhabitants. Monte Alban's rulers dominated the Oaxaca Valley and interacted, sometimes as rivals and sometimes as trading partners, with the great central Mexican city of Teotihuacan. After about AD 850 the city declined and was largely abandoned, though its ruins remained sacred to later peoples; the Mixtec reused its tombs.

Main features

The site's principal features include the Great Plaza, the North and South Platforms, a large I-shaped ball court, and Building J, an arrowhead-shaped structure bearing carved "conquest slabs" listing places subject to Monte Alban. The famous Danzantes — carved stone figures of contorted naked human bodies — are among the earliest monumental sculptures in Mesoamerica, and the site preserves some of the region's earliest writing and calendrical inscriptions. Tomb 7, excavated by Alfonso Caso in 1932, yielded a spectacular Mixtec treasure of gold, turquoise and carved bone.

World Heritage

Together with the historic centre of Oaxaca City, Monte Alban was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987.
