A.R.G.U.S. (Advanced Research Group Uniting Super-Humans) is a fictional United States government organisation appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. Created by writer Geoff Johns and artist Jim Lee, the organisation debuted during DC's New 52 relaunch, first appearing in Justice League (vol. 2) #7 in 2012.

Fictional history

A.R.G.U.S. was established by the United States government as a federal agency charged with researching, monitoring and, when necessary, countering superhuman and paranormal threats. Colonel Steve Trevor, a decorated soldier and former liaison to Wonder Woman, served as the agency's public face and as its liaison to the Justice League. A.R.G.U.S. maintained a headquarters facility nicknamed "the Circus" and field installations including the Black Room, a vault storing dangerous occult and alien artefacts, and the Circus's detention wing, the Circus prison.

During the Forever Evil event, A.R.G.U.S. was compromised by the Crime Syndicate and its director was compromised by the villain Grid. Subsequently, Amanda Waller — already head of Task Force X, the Suicide Squad — assumed control of A.R.G.U.S., folding its remit into her own black-operations portfolio. The agency has since functioned as Waller's institutional base, overseeing metahuman detention at facilities such as Belle Reve and coordinating government responses to superhuman incidents.

In other media

A.R.G.U.S. appears throughout the Arrowverse television franchise, where it is depicted as a military research and intelligence organisation led by Lyla Michaels, succeeding Amanda Waller; in that continuity the acronym stands for Advanced Research Group Uniting Superhumans and the agency is heavily involved in the storylines of Arrow and The Flash. A version of A.R.G.U.S. also features in the DC Extended Universe films, including Suicide Squad (2016) and The Suicide Squad (2021), under Amanda Waller's direction, and in the animated series Creature Commandos.
