The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes (French: Sanctuaire de Notre-Dame de Lourdes) is a Catholic Marian shrine and pilgrimage site in the town of Lourdes, in the Hautes-Pyrénées department of southwestern France. It is the site of the Lourdes apparitions of 1858, in which the Bernadette Soubirous, a fourteen-year-old miller's daughter, reported eighteen visions of the Virgin Mary in the grotto of Massabielle, on the bank of the Gave de Pau.

The sanctuary draws several million visitors each year, including many sick and disabled pilgrims who come hoping for healing from the water of the spring in the grotto, which Bernadette uncovered during the ninth apparition. The Catholic Church has recognised a number of healings at Lourdes as miraculous following investigation by an international medical bureau.

History

On 11 February 1858 Bernadette Soubirous saw a lady in white at the grotto of Massabielle. Over the following months she reported eighteen apparitions in total, during which the figure identified herself, in the local dialect, as the Immaculate Conception. After investigations, the local bishop, Laurence of Tarbes, declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1862, and authorised the veneration of Our Lady of Lourdes.

Construction of a chapel above the grotto began soon afterwards. The Upper Basilica, in Gothic Revival style, was consecrated in 1876. Pilgrimages grew rapidly after the arrival of the railway, and in 1883 the first organised national pilgrimage took place. The underground Basilica of St Pius X, one of the largest Catholic churches in the world, was consecrated in 1958, the centenary of the apparitions.

Description

The sanctuary covers some 50 hectares (about 125 acres) on the left bank of the Gave de Pau. Its principal elements include:

  • The Grotto of Massabielle, a shallow cave about 10 metres high where the apparitions took place, with an altar and a statue of Our Lady in the niche where she appeared.
  • The Upper Basilica (Basilica of the Immaculate Conception), built above the grotto.
  • The Rosary Basilica, below the Upper Basilica, decorated with mosaics of the mysteries of the Rosary.
  • The Underground Basilica of St Pius X, an oval concrete structure seating up to 25,000 people.
  • The Crown of Thorns and the Crowned Statue on the right bank, facing the grotto across the river meadow (the prairie).
  • The baths and water taps, where pilgrims may immerse themselves in or drink the Lourdes water.
  • The Accueil Notre-Dame, a modern accommodation building for sick pilgrims.

Pilgrimage

The pilgrimage season runs roughly from Easter to All Saints' Day. Daily events include Masses in several languages, the afternoon Blessed Sacrament procession, and the evening torchlight Marian procession, in which thousands of pilgrims carrying candles sing the Lourdes Hymn, "Ave Maria de Lourdes". The Hospitalité Notre-Dame de Lourdes, founded in 1885, organises volunteers who assist sick and disabled pilgrims.

Lourdes is also notable for the medical scrutiny of claimed cures: the Lourdes Medical Bureau (Bureau des Constatations Médicales), founded in 1883, examines cases, and an international committee of physicians verifies whether a cure is unexplained by current medical knowledge before the Church may declare it miraculous. Out of thousands of claims, roughly seventy healings had been recognised as miraculous by the early twenty-first century.
