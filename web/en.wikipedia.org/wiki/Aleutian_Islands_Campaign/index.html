Aleutian Islands campaign

Date: June 3, 1942 – August 15, 1943
Location: Aleutian Islands, Alaska
Result: Allied victory

The Aleutian Islands campaign was a military campaign conducted by the United States, Canada, and Imperial Japan in the Aleutian Islands, part of the Territory of Alaska, in the American Theater and the Pacific Theater of World War II. It began on June 3, 1942, and ended on August 15, 1943.

Background

In June 1942, as part of the operation that culminated in the Battle of Midway, Japanese forces bombed Dutch Harbor and occupied the islands of Attu and Kiska, the only battles of the war fought on incorporated American territory. The occupations were partly a diversion and partly intended to extend Japan's defensive perimeter and deny the islands to the United States as a route toward northern Japan.

The campaign

American forces built airfields on Adak and Amchitka and bombed the occupied islands for months in harsh Arctic weather that caused heavy losses to both sides. In March 1943, the naval Battle of the Komandorski Islands was fought south of the Soviet Komandorski Islands. In May 1943, US Army troops landed on Attu in Operation Landcrab; after nearly three weeks of fighting in fog and cold, the Japanese garrison was annihilated in one of the largest banzai charges of the war. In August 1943, a combined US-Canadian force landed on Kiska in Operation Cottage, only to discover the Japanese had secretly evacuated the island weeks earlier; the operation still cost Allied lives to friendly fire, mines and a destroyer striking a mine.

Aftermath

The campaign tied down forces on both sides and was the first campaign in which Canadian conscripts served in combat in the war. The native Aleut (Unangax̂) population of Attu was taken prisoner to Japan, and many other Aleut communities were forcibly evacuated by the US government to camps in southeast Alaska, where many died. The recapture of the islands ended the only occupation of incorporated US territory during the war.
