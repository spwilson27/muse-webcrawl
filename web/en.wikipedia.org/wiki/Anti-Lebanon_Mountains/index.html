Anti-Lebanon Mountains

The Anti-Lebanon Mountains (Arabic: جبال لبنان الشرقية, "Eastern Mountains of Lebanon") are a southwest–northeast trending mountain range that forms the border between Syria and Lebanon. The range runs roughly parallel to, and east of, the Lebanon Mountains, separated from them by the Beqaa Valley. The border of the two countries is largely defined along the crest of the range.

Geography

The range is about 150 kilometres (93 miles) long. Its highest peak is Mount Hermon (Jabal ash-Shaykh), at 2,814 metres (9,232 ft), which stands at the southern end of the range and is also claimed by Israel in the Golan Heights area. Other notable peaks include Jabal Halimah. The Anti-Lebanon is drier than the Lebanon range to its west, lying partly in its rain shadow, and its slopes descend eastward toward the Syrian plateau and Damascus.

Name

The name "Anti-Lebanon" derives from the Greek Anti-Libanus, meaning "opposite Lebanon", reflecting its position facing the Lebanon range across the Beqaa Valley.
