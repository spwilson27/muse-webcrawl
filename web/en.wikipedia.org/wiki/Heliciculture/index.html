Heliciculture

Heliciculture, also known as snail farming, is the process of farming or raising snails, usually land snails, for human consumption (escargot), for cosmetic products derived from snail mucin, or for the pet trade.

Practice

The most commonly farmed species are the Roman snail (Helix pomatia), the garden snail (Cornu aspersum and its subspecies), and the giant African land snail (Lissachatina fulica). Farming systems range from outdoor pens with controlled vegetation to fully indoor climate-controlled units in which temperature, humidity and photoperiod are managed to keep snails active and breeding year-round. Snails are fed leafy vegetables, fruit and calcium-rich supplements for shell growth. France, Italy and Spain are major centres of both consumption and production, and snail caviar (snail eggs) is sold as a delicacy.
