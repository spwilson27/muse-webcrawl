Muhammad al-Bukhari

Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari
Born: 810 CE, Bukhara, Transoxiana
Died: 870 CE, Khartank, near Samarkand
Known for: Sahih al-Bukhari

Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (810–870), commonly known as Imam al-Bukhari, was a Persian Islamic scholar from Bukhara in present-day Uzbekistan. He is best known as the compiler of Sahih al-Bukhari, regarded by Sunni Muslims as the most authentic collection of hadith — the recorded sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad.

Life

Al-Bukhari began studying hadith as a youth and, after performing the Hajj at sixteen, spent decades travelling through the Abbasid Caliphate — to Mecca, Medina, Baghdad, Basra, Kufa, Damascus, and Egypt — collecting narrations from more than a thousand scholars. His prodigious memory became legendary.

Sahih al-Bukhari

His al-Jami' al-Sahih, completed around 846, distilled some 600,000 narrations he had gathered into roughly 7,300 hadith (about 2,600 without repetition), applying rigorous criteria for the reliability of each chain of transmission (isnad). Together with Sahih Muslim, compiled by his student Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj, it forms the most authoritative pair of works in the Sunni hadith canon, the Sahihayn.

Other works and death

Al-Bukhari also wrote al-Tarikh al-Kabir, a biographical dictionary of hadith narrators. He died in 870 in the village of Khartank near Samarkand, where his tomb remains a place of visitation.
