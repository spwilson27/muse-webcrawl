Fire sprinkler system

A fire sprinkler system is an active fire protection method, consisting of a water supply system providing adequate pressure and flowrate to a water distribution piping system, to which fire sprinklers are connected. Although initially used only in factories and large commercial buildings, systems for homes and small buildings are now available at a cost-effective price. Fire sprinkler systems are extensively used worldwide, with over 40 million sprinkler heads fitted each year. Buildings completely protected by fire sprinkler systems have a very high rate of fires being controlled or extinguished by the system alone.

History

Hiram Maxim and others experimented with automatic sprinklers in the 19th century. Frederick Grinnell improved Henry S. Parmalee's 1874 design and in 1881 produced the Grinnell sprinkler, which was more sensitive and reliable. His company became a leading manufacturer of sprinkler systems.

Operation

Each closed-head sprinkler is held shut by a heat-sensitive glass bulb or a two-part metal link held together with a fusible alloy. The bulb or link applies pressure to a pip cap which acts as a plug preventing water from flowing until the ambient temperature around the sprinkler reaches the design activation temperature of the individual sprinkler head. In a standard wet-pipe system, each sprinkler activates independently when the predetermined heat level is reached; thus, only sprinklers near a fire will operate, normally just one or two.

Types

  • Wet pipe systems — the most common; pipes are always filled with pressurized water.
  • Dry pipe systems — pipes filled with pressurized air or nitrogen, used where pipes might freeze.
  • Deluge systems — open heads; all sprinklers discharge when the detection system activates.
  • Pre-action systems — a combination used in water-sensitive environments such as data centers and libraries.

Standards

In the United States, installation is governed principally by NFPA 13 (commercial) and NFPA 13R/13D (residential), with maintenance and testing under NFPA 25.
