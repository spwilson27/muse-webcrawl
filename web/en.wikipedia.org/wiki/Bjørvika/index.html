Bjørvika is a neighbourhood in central Oslo, Norway, occupying the inlet at the head of the Oslofjord where the Akerselva river meets the sea. Once a working harbour and traffic junction, the area has been transformed since the 2000s through the Fjord City regeneration programme into a cultural and commercial waterfront district.

Development

Historically Bjørvika was part of Oslo's port, and later was dominated by container facilities and motorways, including the E18 crossing the inlet. The decision to build the Oslo Opera House (opened 2008, designed by Snøhetta) at the water's edge catalysed redevelopment. Road traffic was rerouted through the Bjørvika Tunnel, an immersed motorway tunnel under the inlet opened in 2010, freeing land for building.

Landmarks

Row of high-rise office buildings known as the Barcode Project lines the former rail yards. Cultural institutions include the MUNCH museum (opened 2021), devoted to Edvard Munch, and Deichman Bjørvika, Oslo's main public library (2020). Waterfront promenades, floating saunas, and swimming areas have made the district a popular public space, though the scale and architecture of the Barcode buildings have been publicly debated.
