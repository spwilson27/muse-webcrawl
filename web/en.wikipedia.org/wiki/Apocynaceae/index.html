Apocynaceae, the dogbane family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Gentianales that includes trees, shrubs, herbs, stem succulents and lianas. Most members produce a milky latex, and many are poisonous, containing cardiac glycosides or alkaloids. The family in its modern circumscription includes the former milkweed family, Asclepiadaceae.

Distribution and characteristics

The family is distributed worldwide but is richest in the tropics and subtropics. Flowers are typically five-lobed, often showy and fragrant; in the milkweed subfamily (Asclepiadoideae) pollen is packaged into pollinia and the flowers have a complex gynostegium. Fruits are commonly paired follicles releasing plumed seeds adapted for wind dispersal.

Uses

Well-known members include oleander, periwinkle (Vinca and Catharanthus), frangipani (Plumeria), milkweeds (Asclepias), rubber vine and the desert rose. Madagascar periwinkle is the source of the anticancer alkaloids vincristine and vinblastine; other genera have yielded cardiac drugs and arrow poisons. Several species are widely grown as ornamentals.
