Oneiroi

In Greek mythology, the Oneiroi (Greek: Ὄνειροι, "Dreams") were the personifications of dreams. According to Hesiod's Theogony, they were the children of Nyx (Night), born without a father, and brothers of Hypnos (Sleep) and Thanatos (Death). Other sources make them the sons of Erebus and Nyx, or of Hypnos himself.

The named dreams

Individual Oneiroi were named in later literature, notably in Ovid's Metamorphoses, which describes the cave of Somnus (Hypnos) from which a thousand dream-spirits issue. Three are singled out: Morpheus, who takes human form in dreams; Phobetor (also called Icelos), who takes the form of animals and monsters and is associated with nightmares; and Phantasos, who takes the form of inanimate objects such as rocks, water and trees.

In Homer's Odyssey, dreams pass through two gates in the underworld: true dreams through the gate of horn, and deceptive dreams through the gate of ivory — a pun on the Greek words for horn (keras, linked to "fulfil") and ivory (elephas, linked to "deceive"). In the Iliad, Zeus sends a lying Dream, personified as a single figure, to deceive Agamemnon.
