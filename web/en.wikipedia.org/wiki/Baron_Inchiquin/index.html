Baron Inchiquin

Baron Inchiquin is a title in the Peerage of Ireland, created in 1543 for Murrough O'Brien, who surrendered his Irish kingship of Thomond to Henry VIII as part of the surrender and regrant policy. The holder of the title is chief of the O'Brien clan, one of the great Gaelic families of Ireland, descending from the high king Brian Boru.

History

Murrough O'Brien (died 1551), the last king of Thomond, was created Baron Inchiquin and Earl of Thomond in 1543. The earldom passed to a different line after the death of the third earl and eventually became extinct, while the barony of Inchiquin continued in the senior line of the O'Briens of Dromoland. The barons sat in the Irish House of Lords until the Union, and later holders served as Irish representative peers in the British House of Lords.

Dromoland and later holders

The family seat was Dromoland Castle in County Clare, rebuilt in the 19th century in the baronial style and now a well-known hotel. Notable holders include Donough O'Brien, 16th Baron Inchiquin, and Lucius O'Brien, 18th Baron Inchiquin. The title continues in the O'Brien family, whose members style themselves The O'Brien, Prince of Thomond.
