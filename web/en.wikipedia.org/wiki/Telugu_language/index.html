Telugu (/ˈtɛlʊɡuː/; తెలుగు, Telugu) is a Dravidian language native to the Indian states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, where it is also the official language. Spoken by about 96 million people, it is the most widely spoken member of the Dravidian language family and one of the few languages in the world with primary official status in more than one Indian state. It stands alongside Hindi, English and Bengali as one of the few languages spoken by more than 50 million speakers worldwide.

Telugu is one of six languages designated as a classical language of India by the Government of India, a status it received in 2008. It ranks fourth among Indian languages by number of native speakers. Because of the melodic quality of its poetry, Telugu is often called the "Italian of the East" — its words, like Italian's, tend to end in vowels.

History

Telugu descends from Proto-Dravidian and separated from its South-Central Dravidian relatives roughly in the first millennium BCE. Inscriptions in a form of Prakrit influenced by Telugu words appear in the Bhattiprolu urn inscription (c. 3rd century BCE). Fully Telugu inscriptions emerge by about the 6th–7th centuries CE. The language's literary tradition conventionally begins with Nannaya's 11th-century rendering of the Mahabharata into Telugu, undertaken under the Eastern Chalukya king Rajaraja Narendra. Nannaya, Tikkana and Yerrapragada — the "Trinity of Poets" (Kavitrayam) — produced the canonical Telugu Mahabharata.

The Vijayanagara period (14th–16th centuries), especially the reign of Krishnadevaraya, is regarded as a golden age of Telugu literature; the emperor's court hosted the Ashtadiggajas, eight celebrated poets. In the 19th and 20th centuries, reformers such as Kandukuri Veeresalingam and Gurajada Apparao modernised Telugu prose and championed the spoken dialect (vyavaharika) over the archaic bookish style (granthika).

Geographic distribution

Telugu is the official language of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana and is also spoken in Yanam (Puducherry), and by significant minorities in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Odisha and Chhattisgarh. Large emigrant communities speak Telugu in the United States, the Gulf states, Malaysia, Fiji, Mauritius and South Africa. In the United States, Telugu was among the fastest-growing languages in the early 21st century.

Phonology and script

Telugu has a phonemic inventory of about 41 consonants and 16 vowels (including diphthongs). It is an agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order, and it makes extensive use of suffixes. The Telugu script is an abugida descended from the Brahmi script via the Kadamba and Chalukya scripts; it is written left to right and is famous for its rounded shapes, originally developed because palm-leaf manuscripts split along straight strokes. Telugu syllables end in vowels so frequently that the language has been described as Ajanta bhasha — every word ends in a vowel sound.

Literature

Telugu literature spans courtly epics, devotional poetry (notably the Bhagavatam of Potana and the songs of Annamacharya and Tyagaraja), the shataka (century of verses) tradition, and a vigorous modern literature of novels, short stories and cinema lyrics. Telugu cinema — "Tollywood", centred in Hyderabad — is one of India's largest film industries and a major driver of the language's popular culture.
