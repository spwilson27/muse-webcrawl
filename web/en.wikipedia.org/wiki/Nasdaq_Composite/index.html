Nasdaq Composite

Nasdaq Composite
Ticker symbol: ^IXIC, COMP
Exchange: Nasdaq
Constituents: over 3,000
Weighting: Market-cap weighted
Launched: February 5, 1971 (base 100)

The Nasdaq Composite (ticker symbol ^IXIC) is a stock market index that includes almost all stocks listed on the Nasdaq stock market. Along with the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500, it is one of the three most-followed indices in US stock markets, and it is heavily weighted toward information technology companies.

Description

The index includes more than 3,000 common equities listed on the Nasdaq, encompassing American depositary receipts, real estate investment trusts, and limited partnership interests in addition to common stocks. It is calculated using a market capitalization weighting method, so the largest companies—such as the major US technology firms—have an outsized influence on its value. A companion index, the Nasdaq-100, tracks the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on the exchange.

History

The Composite began on February 5, 1971, with a base value of 100. It rose dramatically during the dot-com bubble of the late 1990s, closing above 5,000 for the first time in March 2000, before losing nearly 80 percent of its value by October 2002. It did not regain its 2000 peak until 2015. Driven by the growth of large technology companies, the index surpassed 10,000 in 2020 and continued to new records in the following years.

Investing

Investors can gain exposure to the index through index funds and exchange-traded funds that track the Nasdaq Composite or the more commonly used Nasdaq-100.
