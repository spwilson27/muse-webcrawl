Hereditary spherocytosis

Hereditary spherocytosis is an inherited haemolytic anaemia caused by defects in red blood cell membrane proteins—most often ankyrin, band 3, spectrin, or protein 4.2—that anchor the lipid bilayer to the cytoskeleton. The cells lose surface area and become sphere-shaped (spherocytes), which are trapped and destroyed in the spleen.

Presentation

The disorder affects roughly 1 in 2,000 people of Northern European ancestry and is usually inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. Features include anaemia of varying severity, jaundice, splenomegaly, and an increased risk of pigment gallstones; infections such as parvovirus B19 can trigger an aplastic crisis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is suggested by spherocytes on a blood film, increased mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration, and a negative direct antiglobulin test, and confirmed by osmotic fragility or eosin-5-maleimide binding tests. Splenectomy cures the haemolysis in most cases but increases infection risk, so it is reserved for moderate-to-severe disease; folate supplementation and monitoring are routine.
