Gazebo

A gazebo is a freestanding, roofed, open-sided garden structure, often octagonal or turret-shaped, that provides a sheltered place to sit and a vantage point from which to view a garden, park or landscape. Gazebos are typically found in public parks and private gardens, and may serve as bandstands, wedding venues or summer houses.

History

Sheltered garden pavilions have existed for millennia in Egyptian, Persian, Chinese and Japanese gardens, where related structures served for tea ceremonies and contemplation. The word "gazebo" appeared in English in the mid-18th century, popularised by William Halfpenny's pattern books of Chinese-influenced garden buildings (1752); its etymology is uncertain, sometimes jokingly derived from Latin gazebo, "I shall gaze", formed from "gaze" on the model of videbo.

Gazebos became staples of Victorian gardens and municipal parks in Britain and North America, frequently built of ornate cast iron or timber with decorative fretwork; the larger American bandstand type hosted public concerts. In the Indian subcontinent, related open pavilions are known as chhatris and baradaris.

Design

Most gazebos have a raised floor, low railings or balustrades, bench seating and a pitched or domed roof, sometimes double-tiered or topped with a cupola or weathervane. Modern garden gazebos are sold as kits in wood, vinyl or metal, while fabric-roofed "pop-up" gazebos are used for temporary events.
