Lidice (German: Liditz) is a village in the Kladno District of the Central Bohemian Region of the Czech Republic. It became world-famous after Nazi Germany destroyed it on 10 June 1942 in reprisal for the assassination of Reinhard Heydrich, the Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia.

The Lidice massacre

On 27 May 1942, Czechoslovak agents Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš, trained by Britain's Special Operations Executive, gravely wounded Heydrich in Prague (Operation Anthropoid); he died of his wounds on 4 June. On a pretext linking Lidice to the assassins, Hitler ordered the village liquidated. On 10 June 1942, SS and police units shot all 173 men of the village over the age of 15 (later counts raise the male victims to 192). The women were deported to Ravensbrück concentration camp, where many died. Most of the 105 children were murdered in gas vans at Chełmno; a few deemed suitable for "Germanisation" were given to SS families. The buildings were burned and dynamited, the site levelled, and even the cemetery desecrated; the name Lidice was struck from maps.

Aftermath and remembrance

News of the atrocity provoked worldwide outrage. Towns in several countries took the name Lidice or named children after it, and a "Lidice Shall Live" campaign began in Stoke-on-Trent, England. After the war, surviving women and 17 children returned; a new village of Lidice was built beside the original site from 1947. The old site is now a national memorial with a museum and a celebrated bronze sculpture of the 82 murdered children, created over two decades by Marie Uchytilová.
