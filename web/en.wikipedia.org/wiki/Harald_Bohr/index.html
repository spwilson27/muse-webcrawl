Harald Bohr

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Harald August Bohr (22 April 1887 – 22 January 1951) was a Danish mathematician and footballer, the younger brother of the physicist Niels Bohr. He founded the field of almost periodic functions and made important contributions to the study of Dirichlet series and the Riemann zeta function.

Mathematics

Bohr became a professor at the University of Copenhagen in 1930. His theory of almost periodic functions, developed in the 1920s, grew out of his work on Dirichlet series and became a substantial branch of analysis. With Edmund Landau he proved the Bohr–Landau theorem on the distribution of zeros of the zeta function. His collected works appeared in three volumes.

Football

Bohr was also an accomplished footballer. Playing for Akademisk Boldklub, he won a silver medal with the Danish national team at the 1908 Summer Olympics in London, scoring two goals in Denmark's 17–1 victory over France, still a record margin in Olympic football. Legend holds that students once gave him a standing ovation in class after an international match.
