Rivendell (Sindarin: Imladris, "deep valley of the cleft") is a fictional Elven refuge in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth, ruled by Elrond Half-elven. Located in a steep, hidden valley in eastern Eriador at the western edge of the Misty Mountains, it is described as "the Last Homely House east of the Sea". Rivendell features prominently in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, serving as a place of rest, counsel, and healing for the protagonists.

History

Rivendell was founded in the Second Age (S.A. 1697) by Elrond during the War of the Elves and Sauron, after the destruction of Eregion. It withstood a siege by Sauron's forces and became a stronghold of the Elves and their allies, home to many treasures and heirlooms of the Dúnedain, including the shards of Narsil. Aragorn was raised there as a child under the name Estel, and his mother Gilraen lived there until her death.

In the Third Age, Bilbo Baggins visited Rivendell on his journey to the Lonely Mountain and retired there after his 111th birthday party. In The Lord of the Rings, Frodo Baggins is brought to Rivendell after being wounded by a Morgul-knife; the Council of Elrond is held there, leading to the formation of the Fellowship of the Ring. At the end of the Third Age, Elrond departs Middle-earth from the Grey Havens, and Rivendell's importance fades, though Celeborn later dwells there for a time.

Description

Tolkien described Rivendell as a place of peace, learning, and song, where time seems to pass gently. The house and valley were protected by Elrond's power, linked with the Elven-ring Vilya. The geography and atmosphere were inspired in part by the Lauterbrunnental in Switzerland, which Tolkien visited in 1911.
