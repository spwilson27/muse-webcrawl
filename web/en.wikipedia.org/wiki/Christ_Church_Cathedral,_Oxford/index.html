Christ Church Cathedral is the cathedral of the diocese of Oxford and the chapel of Christ Church, Oxford, one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford. It is unique in England in serving as both a college chapel and a diocesan cathedral, and its dean is head of both the cathedral chapter and the college. The cathedral is the smallest in the Church of England by some measures and is celebrated for its Norman architecture, its choir, and its association with the shrine of St Frideswide, the patron saint of Oxford.

History

The site's religious history reaches back to an Anglo-Saxon nunnery associated with St Frideswide in the 8th century. A priory of Augustinian canons was established on the site in 1122, and the present church was built in the late 12th century in the transition from Norman to Early English Gothic style. The shrine of St Frideswide drew pilgrims throughout the Middle Ages.

In the 1520s Cardinal Thomas Wolsey suppressed the priory to found Cardinal College, demolishing part of the nave to make room for the college quadrangle — which is why the cathedral is unusually short. After Wolsey's fall, Henry VIII refounded the institution in 1546 as Christ Church, moving the seat of the new diocese of Oxford from Osney Abbey to the former priory church.

Architecture

The cathedral retains substantial Norman fabric, including the choir, transepts, and the distinctive spire — among the earliest stone spires in England, dating to around 1200. The nave vault was rebuilt in the 15th century. Notable features include the watching loft over St Frideswide's shrine, the Latin Chapel, stained glass by William Morris's circle and a window by Edward Burne-Jones, and the Jonah window of about 1630 by Abraham van Linge. The Becket window and other glass were restored or replaced at various points after iconoclasm during the Reformation and the Civil War.

Music

The cathedral choir — men and boys, the boys educated at Christ Church Cathedral School — is internationally known and has made many recordings. The organ and the daily round of sung services follow the Anglican choral tradition. The cathedral's bells, housed in Christ Church's Tom Tower, include Great Tom, which tolls 101 times each night at 9:05 p.m., a custom recalling the college's original 101 scholars.

Cultural associations

Christ Church was the home of Charles Dodgson (Lewis Carroll), and the cathedral and college feature in the background of the Alice stories; Alice Liddell, daughter of Dean Henry Liddell, was Carroll's inspiration. The cathedral's choir school and cloister were used as filming locations in the Harry Potter films (as Hogwarts interiors and corridors).
