Langley is an unincorporated community in the census-designated place of McLean in Fairfax County, Virginia, United States. It lies northwest of Washington, D.C., across the Potomac River. The name "Langley" is used worldwide as a metonym for the United States Central Intelligence Agency, whose headquarters — the George Bush Center for Intelligence — is located there.

History

The name derives from Langley Manor in Shropshire, England, the ancestral home associated with the area's colonial landowners; the land formed part of a 17th-century grant, and the community that grew up around Langley Fork was a rural farming area into the twentieth century. Land for the CIA headquarters was acquired in the 1950s, and the Original Headquarters Building, designed with input from the New York firm Harrison & Abramovitz, was occupied beginning in 1961; President Dwight D. Eisenhower laid the cornerstone in 1959.

CIA headquarters

The Langley campus houses the George Bush Center for Intelligence, comprising the Original Headquarters Building (1961) and the New Headquarters Building, completed in the late 1980s. On the grounds stands Kryptos, the celebrated encrypted sculpture by Jim Sanborn dedicated in 1990, part of whose code remains unsolved. The campus is served by the George Washington Memorial Parkway and is not open to the public.

Other features

Langley is also home to the Federal Highway Administration's Turner-Fairbank Highway Research Center and is adjacent to Claude Moore Colonial Farm and the Potomac riverside parks. The community includes some of the Washington area's most expensive residential neighbourhoods, within the McLean postal area.
