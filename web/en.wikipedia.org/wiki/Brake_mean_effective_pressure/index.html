Brake mean effective pressure

Brake mean effective pressure (BMEP) is a measure of an internal combustion engine's capacity to do work, independent of the engine's displacement. It is derived from the mean effective pressure calculated from measured brake power output, as determined by a dynamometer (the "brake" in the term refers to the braking device of the dynamometer, historically a Prony brake).

Definition

Mean effective pressure is the average pressure which, if applied uniformly on the piston during the power stroke, would produce the same amount of net work. Brake mean effective pressure uses the work actually measured at the output shaft, after frictional and auxiliary losses, rather than the indicated work computed from in-cylinder pressure.

BMEP = (P × nR) / (Vd × N)

where P is brake power, nR is the number of crankshaft revolutions per power stroke per cylinder (1 for a two-stroke engine, 2 for a four-stroke engine), Vd is total engine displacement, and N is engine speed in revolutions per unit time.

Interpretation

Because BMEP normalizes work output by displacement, it allows engines of different sizes to be compared on equal terms: it is effectively a measure of torque per unit displacement, or how effectively an engine converts its swept volume into output. Two engines producing the same BMEP produce the same torque per litre, regardless of their absolute size. High BMEP values indicate efficient use of displacement and are achieved through high compression ratios, efficient combustion, and forced induction.

Typical values

Naturally aspirated spark-ignition automobile engines typically achieve BMEP values around 8.5 to 11 bar at peak torque. Turbocharged gasoline engines commonly reach 16 to 22 bar, while modern turbocharged diesel engines can exceed 20 bar, with heavy-duty diesels reaching around 25 bar. Racing engines, such as those in Formula One, have historically achieved very high values through high engine speeds, though BMEP itself is speed-independent.

Related quantities

Indicated mean effective pressure (IMEP) is computed from the cylinder pressure diagram and excludes mechanical friction losses. The difference between IMEP and BMEP is the friction mean effective pressure (FMEP). The ratio of brake power to indicated power is the engine's mechanical efficiency.
