Billionaire

In 2024 there were an estimated 2,781 billionaires worldwide with a combined wealth of about US$14.2 trillion.

A billionaire is a person with a net worth of at least one billion units of a given currency, usually of a major currency such as the United States dollar, euro or pound sterling. The American business magazine Forbes produces a global list of known US dollar billionaires annually (the Forbes list of billionaires) and updates it in real time online.

History

The word "billionaire" was first coined in 1844, modelled on "millionaire". John D. Rockefeller is widely considered the first confirmed billionaire in US dollars, reaching that status in 1916, largely through his ownership of Standard Oil. The number of billionaires grew slowly through the twentieth century before expanding rapidly from the 1980s onward with globalisation, technology fortunes and financialisation.

Current statistics

According to Forbes's 2024 list, there were 2,781 billionaires worldwide with aggregate wealth of roughly US$14.2 trillion. The United States has the most billionaires of any country, followed by China (including Hong Kong) and India. Wealth is concentrated in industries such as technology, finance and investments, retail, and fashion and luxury goods.

Criticism and debate

The existence of billionaires is a subject of political debate. Critics argue that extreme wealth concentration reflects and reinforces inequality and can distort political systems, while defenders contend that billionaire fortunes often arise from creating valuable companies and employment, and that many wealthy individuals engage in large-scale philanthropy, some through the Giving Pledge founded by Bill Gates and Warren Buffett in 2010.
