The Canadian (train)

The Canadian (French: Le Canadien) is a transcontinental passenger train operated by Via Rail with service between Union Station in Toronto, Ontario, and Pacific Central Station in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. It is one of the world's longest passenger train journeys, covering some 4,466 kilometres (2,775 miles).

History

The service began on April 24, 1955, as the flagship streamliner of the Canadian Pacific Railway, running between Montreal/Toronto and Vancouver via Ottawa, Winnipeg, and Calgary over CP tracks, using stainless-steel equipment built by the Budd Company — including the distinctive "Park" series dome-observation cars. In 1978 Via Rail took over CP's passenger services, and in 1990, following major budget cuts, the train was rerouted onto the more northerly Canadian National route through Edmonton and Jasper that it uses today.

Service

The train runs several times weekly in each direction and takes about four days and nights to cross the country, passing through the Canadian Shield, the Prairies, and the Rocky Mountains. It offers economy, sleeper, and Prestige class accommodation, dining cars, and glass-domed sightseeing cars.
