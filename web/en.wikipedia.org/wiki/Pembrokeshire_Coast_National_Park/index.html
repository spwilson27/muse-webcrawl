Pembrokeshire Coast National Park

Established: 1952
Area: about 629 km²
Location: Pembrokeshire, Wales

The Pembrokeshire Coast National Park (Welsh: Parc Cenedlaethol Arfordir Penfro) is a national park in south-west Wales, designated in 1952. It is the only national park in the United Kingdom established primarily for its coastline, following the cliffs, bays, and estuaries of Pembrokeshire almost continuously.

Features

The park includes the 186-mile Pembrokeshire Coast Path, now part of the Wales Coast Path, along with offshore islands such as Skomer, Skokholm, Ramsey, and Caldey — important breeding colonies for seabirds including Manx shearwaters and Atlantic puffins. Landscapes range from volcanic headlands and limestone cliffs in the south to the Preseli Hills in the north-east sections of the park. Sites include St Davids Cathedral, Carew and Pembroke castles, and Iron Age hillforts.

Administration

The park is managed by the Pembrokeshire Coast National Park Authority. Unlike national parks in some countries, most land is privately owned and farmed, and the park contains towns such as Tenby and St Davids within its boundaries.
