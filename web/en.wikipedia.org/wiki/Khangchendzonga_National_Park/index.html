Khangchendzonga National Park

Location: Sikkim, India
Area: 1,784 km²
Established: 26 August 1977
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 2016 (mixed site)

Khangchendzonga National Park, also known as Kangchenjunga Biosphere Reserve, is a national park and biosphere reserve in Sikkim, India. It was inscribed to the UNESCO World Heritage Sites list in July 2016, becoming the first "mixed heritage" site in India, recognised for both its natural beauty and its religious and cultural significance. The park is named for Mount Khangchendzonga (Kangchenjunga), the world's third-highest peak (8,586 m), which dominates its skyline.

Geography

The park covers an area of 1,784 square kilometres in North and West Sikkim, encompassing a steep elevational gradient from about 1,220 metres to over 8,500 metres — one of the widest altitude ranges of any protected area in the world. It includes the Zemu Glacier, numerous high-altitude lakes such as the sacred Khecheopalri Lake nearby, deep valleys and the valleys of the Lachen and Lachung rivers.

Flora and fauna

Vegetation ranges from subtropical forests through temperate broadleaf and coniferous forests to alpine meadows and cold desert. Wildlife includes the snow leopard, red panda (the state animal of Sikkim), Himalayan black bear, musk deer, blood pheasant, Tibetan snowcock and many species of rhododendron and orchid. The park contains a number of endangered species and forms part of the Himalaya biodiversity hotspot.

Cultural significance

Mount Khangchendzonga is worshipped as a guardian deity by the people of Sikkim, and the landscape is central to the religious practices of the indigenous Lepcha people, who regard the region as Mayel Lyang, a sacred hidden paradise. Buddhist sites associated with the park include Tholung Monastery in the buffer zone, which houses revered relics and scriptures.
