Sagittaria

Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Alismatales
Family: Alismataceae
Genus: Sagittaria L.
Species: approx. 30

Sagittaria is a genus of around 30 species of aquatic plants whose members go under a variety of common names, including arrowhead, duck potato, katniss, swamp potato, and wapato. The genus is widely distributed across temperate and tropical regions of the Americas, Europe, and Asia, growing in ponds, marshes, and slow-moving water.

The plants typically produce two kinds of leaves: submerged ribbon-like leaves and emergent arrow-shaped leaves, from which the genus takes its name (Latin sagitta, arrow). Flowers are white, usually with three petals, borne in whorls on a leafless stalk, with male and female flowers on the same plant.

Uses

Many species produce starchy tubers that are an important traditional food. Sagittaria latifolia (wapato or duck potato) was a staple of Indigenous peoples of North America and a major trade item in the Pacific Northwest; Sagittaria trifolia is cultivated in East and Southeast Asia, where its tubers (Chinese: 茨菇) are eaten, notably at Lunar New Year. Arrowheads are also grown as ornamentals for ponds and aquaria.
