Spot color


Spot colours are chosen from standardised swatch systems.

In offset printing, a spot color (or solid color) is any color generated by a single ink — pure or mixed — that is printed in one run of the press, rather than being built up from the four process colors (cyan, magenta, yellow, and black) used in CMYK printing. Each spot color requires its own printing plate.

Uses

Spot colors are used when exact color reproduction is essential, such as for corporate logos and brand identities, and when the desired shade lies outside the gamut achievable with process inks — including metallic and fluorescent inks. They are also economical for work using only one to three colors, such as stationery, since fewer plates are needed.

Standard systems

The most widely used spot color standards are the Pantone Matching System (PMS), dominant in North America, and HKS in Europe. Printers mix inks according to the system's formulae, and designers specify colors by number from swatch books printed on coated and uncoated stocks.

Digital workflow

In desktop publishing, spot colors are defined as named colors in applications such as Adobe InDesign and Illustrator. On screen they are simulated with an approximate RGB or Lab value; on output, each named color is separated onto its own plate.
