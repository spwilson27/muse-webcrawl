Thessalonica

Thessalonica
Modern name: Thessaloniki
Region: Macedonia, Greece
Founded: c. 315 BC

Thessalonica (Greek: Θεσσαλονίκη) was an ancient city in Macedon, on the Thermaic Gulf of the Aegean Sea; it is the predecessor of the modern Greek city of Thessaloniki, the second-largest city in Greece. Founded around 315 BC by Cassander, king of Macedon, who named it after his wife Thessalonike, a half-sister of Alexander the Great, the city quickly became one of the most important ports and commercial centres of the northern Aegean.

Roman period

After the Roman conquest of Macedon, Thessalonica became the capital of the Roman province of Macedonia. It lay on the Via Egnatia, the great Roman road linking the Adriatic with Byzantium, which brought trade and travellers. In the first century AD the apostle Paul preached in the city's synagogue, and two of the New Testament epistles — First and Second Thessalonians — are addressed to its early Christian community.

Byzantine period

Under the Byzantine Empire Thessalonica was the empire's second city, a major centre of trade, scholarship, and Orthodox Christianity. The brothers Cyril and Methodius, missionaries to the Slavs, came from the city. It endured sieges by Slavs, Arabs, Normans, and others, and in 904 was sacked by Arab pirates.

Later history

The city became part of the Ottoman Empire in 1430 and remained under Ottoman rule for nearly five centuries, developing a large and famous Sephardic Jewish community. It was incorporated into the modern Greek state in 1912 during the Balkan Wars.
