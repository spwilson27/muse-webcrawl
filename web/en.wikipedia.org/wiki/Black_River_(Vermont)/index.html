Black River (Vermont)

LocationWindsor County, Vermont, United States
SourceBlack Pond, Plymouth, Vermont
MouthConnecticut River at Springfield, Vermont
Length40.8 mi (65.7 km)
BasinConnecticut River watershed

The Black River is a 40.8-mile-long (65.7 km) tributary of the Connecticut River in south-central Vermont, United States. It rises in the ponds of Plymouth in Windsor County and flows generally southeast through Ludlow, Cavendish, Weathersfield and Springfield before joining the Connecticut River at Springfield.

Course

The river begins at Black Pond in Plymouth and passes through Amherst Lake and the other Plymouth lakes before descending through the village of Ludlow, home of the Okemo Mountain ski area. In Cavendish it passes through the Cavendish Gorge. Below Cavendish the river is impounded by a series of flood-control and hydroelectric dams built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and utilities, including the North Springfield Lake dam, completed in 1960 for flood control after the destructive floods of the 1920s and 1930s.

History and economy

In the nineteenth century the Black River valley at Springfield and Windsor became a centre of the American precision machine-tool industry, powered by the river's fall; Springfield's mills produced lathes, grinders and gauges central to the "American system" of manufacturing. Today the river supports recreation, fishing for trout, and small hydroelectric generation. Portions of the valley are followed by Vermont Route 103 and Route 106.
