Dachshund

The dachshund (German: "badger dog"), also known as the wiener dog or sausage dog, is a short-legged, long-bodied hound breed of dog developed in Germany. The breed was created to scent, chase, and flush out badgers and other burrow-dwelling animals; miniature dachshunds were bred for hunting rabbits and prairie dogs.

Characteristics

Dachshunds come in two sizes (standard and miniature) and three coat varieties: smooth (short), long-haired, and wire-haired. Colours include red, black and tan, chocolate, and dapple patterns. The breed is known for a lively, brave, sometimes stubborn temperament. Because of their long spine, dachshunds are prone to intervertebral disc disease (IVDD).

History

The breed was developed in Germany over several centuries, with roots possibly in earlier dwarf hounds; foresters crossing breeds such as the German Bibarhund and pinscher types produced a dog able to fight badgers underground. The dachshund became a national symbol of Germany and was the official mascot, Waldi, of the 1972 Munich Olympic Games.

Popularity

Dachshunds are consistently among the most popular dog breeds in the United States and are kept almost entirely as companion animals today.
