Baroque

[Image: the facade of a Baroque church at golden hour — twisted columns, statues and a sweeping dome, richly ornamented]

The Baroque is a style of architecture, painting, sculpture, music and other arts that flourished in Europe from the early seventeenth century until the 1750s. It followed Renaissance art and Mannerism and expressed itself in ornate, dramatic and grandiose forms designed to impress and move the emotions — in the service, initially, of the Catholic Counter-Reformation and of absolute monarchies.

In Architecture, Baroque buildings use bold curves, domes, colonnades, dramatic light and rich decoration — Bernini's colonnade for St Peter's Square in Rome and the Palace of Versailles are emblematic. In painting, Caravaggio's violent chiaroscuro, Rubens's fleshy movement and Rembrandt's psychological depth mark the era.

Music and spread

Baroque music (c. 1600–1750) created opera, the concerto, the sonata and modern tonal harmony; its masters include Monteverdi, Vivaldi, Handel and Johann Sebastian Bach, whose death in 1750 is the conventional end of the period. The basso continuo and ornamentation characterise its sound.

Carried by European empires and missions, Baroque forms took root in Latin America, the Philippines and elsewhere, blending with local traditions. The succeeding Rococo lightened its drama into elegance, and Neoclassicism reacted against both — but the Baroque's theatricality permanently shaped Western art.
