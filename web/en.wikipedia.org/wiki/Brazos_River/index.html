The Brazos River flowing through Central Texas.

The Brazos River (Spanish: Río de los Brazos de Dios, "the River of the Arms of God") is the 11th-longest river in the United States, at 1,352 km (840 mi) from its headwater source at the head of the Blackwater Draw in Curry County, New Mexico, to its mouth at the Gulf of Mexico near Freeport, Texas. It is the longest river in Texas.

Course

The Brazos proper begins in Stonewall County at the confluence of the Salt Fork and the Double Mountain Fork. It flows generally southeast through the cities of Waco, Bryan–College Station, Richmond and Brazoria before emptying into the Gulf. Major tributaries include the Clear Fork, the Bosque, the Little River, the San Gabriel, the Navasota and the Yegua.

History and use

Spanish explorers named the river, according to legend, after finding water there when their party was dying of thirst. In the 19th century the lower Brazos valley was a centre of cotton plantations, and Stephen F. Austin's colony was established along its banks. Today the river's waters, managed by the Brazos River Authority, supply cities, farms and industry, with reservoirs including Possum Kingdom Lake, Lake Granbury and Lake Waco.
