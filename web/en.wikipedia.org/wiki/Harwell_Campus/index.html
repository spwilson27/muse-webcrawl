Harwell Campus (formerly the Harwell Science and Innovation Campus) is a large science and technology campus near the villages of Harwell and Chilton in Oxfordshire, England. It occupies the site of RAF Harwell, an airfield used during the Second World War.

History

In 1946 the site became the Atomic Energy Research Establishment (AERE), the main centre for nuclear power research in the United Kingdom under its first director, John Cockcroft. Harwell housed Britain's first nuclear reactors, including GLEEP (1947) and BEPO (1948). As the atomic energy programme dispersed to other sites, Harwell diversified into a multi-disciplinary research campus.

Facilities

Major facilities at the campus include the Diamond Light Source synchrotron, the ISIS Neutron and Muon Source at the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory, and the Central Laser Facility. The campus hosts the UK Space Agency, the European Space Agency's European Centre for Space Applications and Telecommunications (ECSAT), and numerous start-up companies in fields such as space, health technology, energy, and quantum computing. Harwell is also home to elements of the UK's vaccine manufacturing infrastructure.
