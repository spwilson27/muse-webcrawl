Trilobite

Trilobites are extinct marine arthropods forming the class Trilobita, among the earliest known groups of arthropods. They appeared in the early Cambrian, about 521 million years ago, flourished in Palaeozoic seas, and died out in the end-Permian mass extinction about 252 million years ago.

Anatomy

Their exoskeleton is divided lengthwise into three lobes — hence the name — and into head (cephalon), segmented thorax and tail (pygidium). Many could enrol into a ball. Their calcite compound eyes are among the earliest in the fossil record. Over 22,000 species have been described.

Importance

Trilobites are key index fossils for dating Palaeozoic rocks. See Cambrian and Arthropod.
