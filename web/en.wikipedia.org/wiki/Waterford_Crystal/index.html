Waterford Crystal

Hand-cut Waterford crystal, known for its deep, precise cutting.

Waterford Crystal is a brand of lead crystal glassware produced in Waterford, Ireland, and one of the most famous names in the manufacture of luxury crystal. The company is known for hand-blown and hand-cut crystal: stemware, tumblers, decanters, bowls and chandeliers, in cut patterns such as Lismore, Colleen and Clare.

History

The original glassworks was founded in 1783 by brothers George and William Penrose. Its flint glass won renown and an international export trade, but the works closed in 1851, partly because of high excise taxes on glass. Nearly a century later, in 1947, a Czech émigré, Charles Bacik, revived crystal-making in Waterford, bringing Czech glassmakers with him; Czech designer Miroslav Havel created the brand's signature deep-cut patterns, and the new company flourished in the post-war export boom, supplying crystal to hotels, state dinners and the White House.

Later years

Waterford merged with Wedgwood in 1986, forming Waterford Wedgwood, and the brand later became part of Fiskars' portfolio of luxury brands. The Kilbarry factory closed in 2009 during the financial crisis, with most production moving abroad, but the House of Waterford Crystal visitor centre and showroom in the city's Viking Triangle continues glassblowing, cutting and engraving on site. Since 2000, Waterford Crystal has supplied the New Year's Eve ball lowered each year in Times Square, New York.
