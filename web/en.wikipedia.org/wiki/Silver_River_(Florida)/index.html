The Silver River is a short spring-fed river in Marion County, Florida, United States. It flows 4.5 miles (7.2 km) eastward from Silver Springs — one of the largest artesian spring systems in the world — to its confluence with the Ocklawaha River. The river's remarkably clear water historically discharged on the order of 500 million gallons per day, though spring flows in the Silver Springs group have declined in recent decades due to groundwater withdrawals and drought.

History

The Silver River corridor has been inhabited for thousands of years; Timucua peoples lived near the springs at European contact. In the 19th century the river carried steamboats loaded with tourists and freight between the St. Johns River and the railhead at Ocala, making Silver Springs one of Florida's earliest tourist attractions. Glass-bottom boat tours, begun around 1878, became famous, and the surrounding land was developed as a commercial attraction featuring Ross Allen's reptile institute and exotic animals; scenes for Tarzan films and the television series Sea Hunt were filmed in its clear water.

Environment

The river passes through the floodplain forest of Silver Springs State Park and the Silver River State Park, one of Florida's largest state parks, created after the state acquired the attraction lands beginning in the 1980s and unified the park in 2013. Wildlife includes manatees, alligators, otters, and abundant fish and turtles. A colony of feral rhesus macaques, descended from monkeys released in the 1930s–1940s by tour-boat operator Colonel Tooey, lives along the river and has been controversial because the monkeys carry herpes B virus and damage native vegetation. Exotic aquatic plants, elevated nitrate levels, and reduced flows are the river's main environmental concerns.
