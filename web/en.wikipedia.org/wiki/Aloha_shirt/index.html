Aloha shirt

An aloha shirt, also called a Hawaiian shirt, is a style of dress shirt originating in Hawaii, typically with a camp collar, short sleeves and a bold all-over print of tropical flowers, palm trees, ocean scenes or Polynesian motifs, worn untucked. It is one of the most recognisable garments associated with the Hawaiian Islands.

History

The modern shirt emerged in Honolulu in the 1920s and 1930s, when tailors — often of Japanese, Chinese and Filipino immigrant background — sewed shirts from colourful kimono and dress fabrics. Ellery Chun's King-Smith Clothiers popularised the name "aloha shirt" in the mid-1930s, and the garment spread with tourists and returning servicemen after the Second World War.

Cultural role

In Hawaii, aloha shirts are acceptable business and formal wear, and "Aloha Friday" — wearing one on Fridays — became a local institution that helped inspire casual Fridays on the US mainland. Muted "reverse print" versions are worn as office attire. Collectors prize vintage rayon shirts by labels such as Kamehameha and Kahala.
