1891 Australian shearers' strike

The 1891 Australian shearers' strike was one of the earliest and best-known industrial disputes in Australia. From January to June 1891, shearers in Queensland struck against pastoralists who sought to cut wages and employ non-union "freedom of contract" labour. Although the strike was defeated, it became a founding legend of the Australian labour movement and helped spur the formation of the Australian Labor Party.

DateJanuary–June 1891
LocationQueensland (centred on Barcaldine), Australia
PartiesAmalgamated Shearers' Union vs pastoralists, backed by colonial government
OutcomeStrike defeated; union leaders jailed; momentum toward Labor political representation

Course of the strike

Thousands of shearers gathered in strike camps, above all at Barcaldine, where the "Tree of Knowledge" became their rallying symbol. The colonial government deployed soldiers and police, arrested strike leaders, and several unionists were convicted of conspiracy and sentenced to imprisonment on St Helena Island. Starved of funds and facing scab labour, the strike collapsed by mid-1891.

Legacy

The defeats of the 1890 strikes (maritime, shearers', and miners') convinced unions to pursue parliamentary representation; Labor electoral leagues contested elections from 1891, and the world's first Labor government briefly held office in Queensland in 1899. Barcaldine's Tree of Knowledge remains a national labour shrine.
