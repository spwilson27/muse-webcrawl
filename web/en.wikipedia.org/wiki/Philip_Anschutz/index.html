Philip Frederick Anschutz (born 28 December 1939) is an American billionaire businessman who owns or controls companies in a variety of industries, including energy, railroads, real estate, sports, newspapers, movies, and music. He is the founder and owner of The Anschutz Corporation, and through the Anschutz Entertainment Group (AEG) he has been a major figure in American sports and live entertainment. He is a co-founder of Major League Soccer.

Early life and career

Anschutz was born in Russell, Kansas, and grew up in Colorado. His father was an oil field driller, and Philip bought his father's drilling company in 1961. He made early fortunes in oil — including a major strike in Wyoming — and diversified into railroads, acquiring the Rio Grande Railroad and later the Southern Pacific, which merged with Union Pacific in a deal that left him a major shareholder. A significant later fortune came from Qwest Communications, the fiber-optic telecommunications company he founded.

Sports and entertainment

Anschutz was instrumental in founding MLS in the 1990s and at one point owned six of its teams. His companies have owned or held stakes in the Los Angeles Galaxy, the Los Angeles Kings (NHL), and the Los Angeles Lakers (NBA). AEG owns or operates major venues including Crypto.com Arena (formerly Staples Center) in Los Angeles and The O2 in London, and promotes concerts and festivals including Coachella. His Regal Cinemas chain became one of the largest movie theater operators in the United States.

Other activities

Anschutz has owned newspapers including the Washington Examiner, supported conservative causes, and engaged in major philanthropy in Colorado, particularly in medicine and education. He is also the author of books on American history, including Out Where the West Begins. Forbes perennially lists him among the wealthiest Americans, with a fortune estimated in the tens of billions of dollars.
