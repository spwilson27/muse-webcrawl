Sofiyivka

Sofiyivka (Ukrainian: Софіївка) is the name of several places in Ukraine, as well as the name of one of the country's most famous landscape parks, the Sofiyivka dendrological park in Uman, Cherkasy Oblast. Settlements named Sofiyivka are found in many oblasts, including Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Kharkiv, Kherson, Mykolaiv, Odesa, and Zaporizhzhia oblasts, and the name is also borne by an urban-type settlement in Crimea.

Sofiyivka Park, Uman

The best-known Sofiyivka is the National Dendrological Park "Sofiyivka", founded in 1796 by the Polish magnate Count Stanisław Szczęsny Potocki, who named it in honour of his wife, Sofia. The park, covering about 170 hectares (some 180 hectares including newer sections), was designed in the English landscape style by the Polish military engineer Ludwik Metzel. It features artificial lakes, waterfalls, grottoes, pavilions, statues, and carefully composed vistas inspired by scenes from Homer's Iliad and Odyssey.

After the November Uprising of 1830–1831, the park was confiscated by the Russian imperial authorities and presented to Tsarina Alexandra Feodorovna, after which it was known for a time as the Tsaritsyn Sad ("Tsarina's Garden"). In the Soviet period it became a state reserve and a research institution of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine. Sofiyivka is routinely listed among the most visited tourist attractions in Ukraine and was named one of the Seven Wonders of Ukraine in a nationwide vote in 2007.

Settlements

Numerous villages and urban-type settlements across Ukraine bear the name Sofiyivka. Among them are Sofiyivka in Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, a former district centre (raion seat until the 2020 administrative reform), and Sofiyivka in Crimea, an urban-type settlement in Simferopol Raion. The name derives from Sophia, a popular personal and saint's name in the region.
