Human smuggling

Human smuggling, also called migrant smuggling, is the facilitation of the illegal entry of a person into a state of which the person is not a national or permanent resident, in order to obtain a financial or other material benefit. The definition comes from the United Nations Smuggling of Migrants Protocol (2000), supplementing the UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime.

Distinction from trafficking

Smuggling is distinguished from human trafficking in that the smuggled migrant typically consents to the arrangement, the relationship with the smuggler ends on arrival, and the offence is against the state's border rather than against the person; trafficking, by contrast, involves coercion or deception and continuing exploitation. In practice the categories blur, as migrants may be extorted, abandoned in dangerous conditions or forced into debt bondage en route.

Scale and routes

Smuggling is a large illicit business, with fees ranging from a few hundred dollars for a short border crossing to tens of thousands for intercontinental journeys. Major routes include crossings of the Mediterranean to Europe, the United States–Mexico border (where smugglers are known colloquially as "coyotes"), and boat journeys from Indonesia to Australia. Journeys can be deadly: migrants have suffocated in sealed lorries and containers, drowned in unseaworthy boats, and died crossing deserts. Enforcement responses include border surveillance, prosecution of smugglers, and international cooperation, while the Protocol provides that migrants shall not be criminalised merely for having been smuggled.
