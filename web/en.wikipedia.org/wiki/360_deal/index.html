360 deal

360 deal
IndustryMusic business
Also calledMultiple-rights deal
Key featureLabel takes a share of most or all artist revenue streams

A 360 deal, also called a multiple-rights deal, is a business relationship in the music industry in which a record label (or similar company) supports an artist financially and in marketing, promotion and touring in exchange for a percentage not only of record sales but of nearly all of the artist's revenue streams — including live performance, merchandise, endorsements, publishing and fan-club income. The name suggests the label takes a cut "360 degrees" around the artist's career.

Background

Traditional recording contracts paid labels only from record sales. As recorded-music revenue collapsed in the 2000s with file sharing and then low-margin downloads, labels sought participation in the growing live and merchandise sectors they traditionally did not touch. Robbie Williams's 2002 deal with EMI is often cited as an early precursor, and Madonna's 2007 agreement with Live Nation — reportedly worth about $120 million over ten years, covering albums, touring and merchandise — popularised the model.

Criticism

Critics argue that 360 deals let labels profit from activities they do not work on, and that young artists sign away touring and merchandise income — historically the artist's most reliable earnings — for an advance. Defenders respond that labels invest more in long-term artist development when their returns are not confined to shrinking album sales. The structure has become common, though established artists with leverage often negotiate it away.
