Earthworks (engineering)

Earthworks are engineering works created through the processing of parts of the earth's surface involving quantities of soil or unformed rock. Earthwork operations include excavation, transport, placement and compaction of fill material, and are fundamental to the construction of roads, railways, dams, canals, levees, airfields and building platforms.

Types of earthwork

Common operations include cutting, in which material is excavated from high ground, and filling, in which excavated material is deposited to raise low ground. Cut and fill are often balanced on road and railway projects so that material cut from hillsides is used to build embankments across valleys, minimising the haul of surplus material. Other earthworks include trenching for foundations and utilities, grading to shape finished surfaces, dredging, and the construction of earth dams and dikes.

Equipment

Modern earthworks use heavy plant such as excavators, bulldozers, scrapers, graders, dump trucks and compactors (rollers). Historically, the great earthworks of the canal and early railway ages were dug by hand by labourers known in Britain as navvies, aided later by steam shovels and dynamite.

Quantities and design

Engineers compute earthwork volumes from cross-sections along a route, adjusting for the bulking of excavated material and the shrinkage of compacted fill. Soil properties — bearing capacity, moisture content and compaction characteristics — determine the design of embankments and the stability of slopes. Retaining structures, drainage and erosion protection are designed alongside the bulk earthworks.
