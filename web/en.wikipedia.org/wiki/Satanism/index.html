Satanism

The Sigil of Baphomet, emblem of the Church of Satan

Satanism refers to a group of religious, ideological, and philosophical beliefs based on Satan — particularly his worship or veneration. The phenomenon developed largely from the 20th century onward, although accusations of Satan worship were levelled by Christian authorities for many centuries before any self-described Satanist movements existed. Contemporary Satanism is usually divided between theistic Satanism, in which Satan is worshipped as a real supernatural deity, and atheistic Satanism, in which Satan is treated as a symbol of certain human traits.

Historical background

In medieval and early modern Europe, authorities accused various groups — heretics, Jews, Knights Templar, and alleged witches — of secretly worshipping the Devil; historians generally regard these charges as fabrications produced by persecution and the witch trials. During the 19th century, decadent literature and occultism romanticised Satan as a rebel figure, influencing later movements. Organised, self-identifying Satanism, however, only emerged in the 1960s.

LaVeyan Satanism

The most influential form of Satanism was founded by Anton Szandor LaVey, who established the Church of Satan in San Francisco in 1966 and published The Satanic Bible in 1969. LaVeyan Satanism is atheistic: Satan is not a literal being but a symbol of individualism, self-preservation, carnality, and opposition to Abrahamic moral norms. Its ethical core is summarised in the Nine Satanic Statements and the Eleven Satanic Rules of the Earth, and its magic rituals are understood as psychodrama rather than supernatural acts. In 1975 a schism produced the Temple of Set, founded by Michael Aquino, which adopted a theistic view of Set. The Church of Satan has declined in public activity since LaVey's death in 1997, but LaVey's writings continue to define the popular image of Satanism.

The Satanic Temple

The Satanic Temple, founded in 2012–2013 by Lucien Greaves and Malcolm Jarry, is an atheistic religious organisation that uses Satan as a literary symbol of rebellion against tyranny and advocates church–state separation, bodily autonomy, and scientific rationalism. Its Seven Fundamental Tenets stress compassion, justice, and freedom. The Temple has gained attention through public campaigns and legal challenges concerning religious equality, and was recognised as a tax-exempt church by the IRS in 2019.

Theistic Satanism

Theistic (or traditional) Satanists regard Satan as a real god or supernatural entity worthy of worship. Such groups are small and fragmented; examples include the Order of Nine Angles (founded in Britain in the 1960s–70s, associated with an esoteric and extremist current) and various Luciferian movements that emphasise Satan/Lucifer as a bringer of knowledge and enlightenment.

The Satanic panic

In the 1980s and early 1990s, principally in the United States, a moral panic alleged that secret Satanic cults were engaged in ritual abuse of children. Hundreds of accusations were investigated; no evidence of an organised Satanic conspiracy was found, and many convictions were later overturned. Scholars classify the episode as a case of mass hysteria amplified by media, some therapists, and law-enforcement seminars.
