Odin is the chief god of Norse mythology, associated with wisdom, war, poetry, death and magic. One-eyed — he surrendered an eye at Mimir's well for wisdom — he hung himself on the world tree Yggdrasil for nine nights to win the secret of the runes, and rides the eight-legged horse Sleipnir, attended by the ravens Huginn (thought) and Muninn (memory). Foreknowing his doom, he gathers slain heroes in Valhalla to fight at Ragnarök, where the wolf Fenrir will kill him.

Legacy

Odin's name survives in Wednesday (Woden's day). Tacitus identified him with Mercury; medieval sources present him as a euhemerised travelling king. Modern retellings, from Wagner to Neil Gaiman's American Gods, centre his hunger for knowledge at any price.
