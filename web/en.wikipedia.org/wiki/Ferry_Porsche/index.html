Ferry Porsche

Ferdinand Anton Ernst Porsche (19 September 1909 – 27 March 1998), better known as Ferry Porsche, was an Austrian-German automobile designer and executive who built the Porsche company into a sports-car manufacturer. He was the son of Ferdinand Porsche, founder of the Porsche design office.

Career

Ferry joined his father's Stuttgart engineering office in the 1930s and ran the firm while his father was imprisoned in France after the Second World War. Under his direction the company produced its first car bearing the Porsche name, the Porsche 356 (1948), initially built in Gmünd, Austria. He later championed the development of the Porsche 911 (1964), designed by his son Ferdinand Alexander "Butzi" Porsche, and led Porsche until 1972, thereafter serving as chairman of the supervisory board and honorary chairman for life.

Legacy

Ferry Porsche is regarded as the architect of Porsche as a carmaker: his dictum that he built the sports car he could not find to buy defined the brand. He died in Zell am See, Austria, in 1998.
