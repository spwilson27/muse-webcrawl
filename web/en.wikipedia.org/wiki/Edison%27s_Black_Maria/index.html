Edison's Black Maria

The Black Maria was Thomas Edison's movie production studio in West Orange, New Jersey, completed in 1893. It is regarded as the first purpose-built film studio in the United States.

Design

Designed by William K. L. Dickson, the tar-paper-covered building could rotate on a circular track and had a retractable roof, allowing it to follow the sun and admit maximum daylight for filming. Its cramped, dark interior led staff to nickname it after police patrol wagons known as "Black Marias".

Productions

Early Edison films shot there include Fred Ott's Sneeze (1894), boxing matches, vaudeville acts, and scenes from The Kiss (1896). Production moved to newer facilities by 1901, and the original building was demolished in 1903; a replica now stands at the Thomas Edison National Historical Park.
