Property management

Scope: Residential, commercial, industrial and land real estate

Typical fees: A percentage of collected rent, plus leasing and service fees

Property management is the operation, control, maintenance, and oversight of real estate and physical property, including residential, commercial, and land real estate. Management includes finding tenants, collecting rent, maintaining the property, and handling accounts and records on behalf of the owner.

Responsibilities

A property manager or management company typically markets vacant units, screens applicants, negotiates and enforces leases, collects rents and deposits, arranges repairs and routine maintenance, pays expenses, provides financial reporting to owners, and handles tenant disputes and, where necessary, evictions. In common-interest developments such as condominiums and homeowners' associations, managers also administer common areas and enforce community rules.

Industry and licensing

In many U.S. states, property managers who lease property for others must hold a real estate broker's licence or work under one, with a few states requiring a dedicated property management licence; requirements vary widely internationally. Professional bodies such as the Institute of Real Estate Management (IREM) offer credentials like the Certified Property Manager (CPM) designation.

Fees and technology

Management fees are commonly charged as a percentage of monthly rent — often around 8–12 percent for residential property — plus separate leasing fees. Modern firms rely on property management software for accounting, maintenance ticketing and online rent payment.
