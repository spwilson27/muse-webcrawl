Amen break

The Amen break is a drum break that has been widely sampled in popular music. It comes from the 1969 track "Amen, Brother", performed by the American funk and soul group the Winstons as the B-side of their single "Color Him Father". The break, about six seconds (four bars) long, was played by drummer Gregory C. Coleman.

Use in sampling

Beginning in the 1980s, the break became a staple of hip hop, appearing in tracks such as N.W.A's "Straight Outta Compton". Sped up, chopped and rearranged, it became the foundational rhythm of jungle and drum and bass in the 1990s, and it is often described as one of the most sampled recordings of all time, used in thousands of songs across genres including pop, rock and advertising music.

Royalties

Neither Coleman nor the Winstons' leader Richard Lewis Spencer received royalties for the sampling for decades, and Coleman died in 2006. A 2015 crowdfunding campaign organised by British fans raised about £24,000 for Spencer, who died in 2020. The break's status has made it a famous case study in debates over sampling, copyright and the creative reuse of recordings.
