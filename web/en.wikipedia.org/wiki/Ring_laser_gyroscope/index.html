Ring laser gyroscope

A ring laser gyroscope (RLG) is a rotation sensor based on the Sagnac effect: two laser beams circulate in opposite directions around a closed ring cavity, and rotation of the device causes a measurable difference in their resonant frequencies (or phase), proportional to the angular rate.

Operation

Most RLGs use a triangular or square block of low-expansion glass ceramic with mirrors at the corners and a helium–neon gain medium. A known difficulty is "lock-in": at low rotation rates, scattering couples the two beams so their frequencies lock together; this is overcome by mechanically dithering the device or using a magneto-optic bias. Ring laser gyroscopes have no moving parts in the optical path, giving high reliability and long life.

Applications

RLGs are standard in inertial navigation systems for commercial airliners, military aircraft, missiles, and spacecraft. Very large ring lasers, such as those at geodetic observatories, measure variations in Earth's rotation. The related fibre-optic gyroscope exploits the same effect in coiled optical fibre.
