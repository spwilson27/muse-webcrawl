Volcanic Explosivity Index

The Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI) is a scale measuring the explosivity of volcanic eruptions, devised by Chris Newhall of the US Geological Survey and Stephen Self in 1982. It is based chiefly on the volume of erupted material (tephra), with eruption column height and duration as secondary criteria. The scale is logarithmic from 0 to 8: each step represents roughly a tenfold increase in ejected volume.

Limitations

VEI says little about an eruption's deadliness — the VEI 3 Nevado del Ruiz eruption killed some 23,000 people through lahars, while far larger eruptions in remote areas killed few. It also poorly captures sulfur output, which drives climatic effects, and long-duration effusive eruptions such as flood basalts. Nevertheless it remains the standard shorthand for comparing eruption sizes in catalogues such as the Smithsonian's Global Volcanism Program database.
