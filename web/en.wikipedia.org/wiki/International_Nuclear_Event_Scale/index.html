International Nuclear Event Scale

INES classifies nuclear and radiological events from Level 0 (deviation) to Level 7 (major accident).

The International Nuclear and Radiological Event Scale (INES) is a tool for promptly communicating to the public, in consistent terms, the safety significance of nuclear and radiological events. It was introduced in 1990 by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) and the Nuclear Energy Agency of the OECD, and is used by most countries with nuclear facilities.

The scale

Events are classified on a scale of seven levels. Levels 1–3 are classed as "incidents" and levels 4–7 as "accidents"; events with no safety significance are rated Level 0, a "deviation", and are termed "below scale". The rating considers three areas: impact on people and the environment, impact on radiological barriers and controls at a facility, and degradation of defence in depth.

Use and limitations

INES is intended as a communication tool rather than a scientific measure: levels increase roughly logarithmically in severity, and ratings may be provisional while an event is in progress, as happened at Fukushima, where the rating rose from 4 to 7 over the month after the accident. Critics have proposed more quantitative successors, but INES remains the standard public scale.
