Kingdom of Georgia

Years: 1008 – 1463/1490

Capitals: Kutaisi, later Tbilisi

Notable rulers: Bagrat III, David IV, Queen Tamar

Religion: Georgian Orthodox Christianity

The Kingdom of Georgia (Georgian: საქართველოს სამეფო) was a medieval monarchy in the Caucasus, established in 1008 when Bagrat III united the Georgian lands. It reached its zenith in the twelfth and early thirteenth centuries — the Georgian Golden Age — before being shattered by the Mongol invasions and finally dissolving into rival kingdoms in the fifteenth century.

Golden Age

David IV "the Builder" (r. 1089–1125) reformed the state and army, expelled the Seljuk Turks after the Battle of Didgori (1121) and recaptured Tbilisi in 1122, making it his capital. His great-granddaughter Queen Tamar (r. 1184–1213) presided over the kingdom's greatest territorial extent, stretching from the Black Sea to the Caspian approaches, with tributaries from Trebizond to Shirvan. Tamar's reign saw the flowering of Georgian culture epitomised by Shota Rustaveli's epic The Knight in the Panther's Skin.

Decline

The Khwarezmian and Mongol invasions of the 1220s–1240s devastated the kingdom, which split under Mongol suzerainty. George V "the Brilliant" briefly restored unity in the fourteenth century, but Timur's invasions (1386–1403) and internal strife proved fatal; by 1490 Georgia had formally fragmented into the kingdoms of Kartli, Kakheti and Imereti and several principalities.
