LocationNorthern Apennines, Italy

The Gothic Line (German: Gotenstellung) was a German defensive line of the Italian Campaign of the Second World War. Built under Field Marshal Albert Kesselring across the northern Apennines from the Ligurian coast near La Spezia to the Adriatic near Pesaro, it consisted of thousands of machine-gun nests, bunkers, artillery positions, and minefields exploiting the mountainous terrain. Hitler, disliking the name's implication that the Goths had failed to hold Italy, had it renamed the "Green Line", a change largely ignored.

Assaults of 1944

In Operation Olive (August–September 1944), the British Eighth Army attacked along the Adriatic while the US Fifth Army assaulted the central passes at Il Giogo and the Futa. The Allies broke into the line in places — Rimini fell to Greek and Canadian troops — but autumn rains, exhaustion, and determined defence stalled the offensive short of the Po Valley.

Spring 1945

After a static winter, the final Allied offensive in April 1945 shattered the line; Bologna fell on 21 April, and German forces in Italy surrendered effective 2 May 1945, days before the general German capitulation.
