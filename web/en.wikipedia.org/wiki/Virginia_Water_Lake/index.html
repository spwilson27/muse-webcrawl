Virginia Water Lake lies on the southern edge of Windsor Great Park, in the borough of Runnymede in Surrey and the civil parishes of Old Windsor and Sunningdale in Berkshire, England. It is a man-made lake taking its name from a natural body of water of the same name. There is a village of Virginia Water which stretches out to the east of the lake. The lake's perimeter is approximately 4.5 miles (7.2 km), and it is a popular destination for walkers, families, and film productions.

History

The lake was created in the 1750s under William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland, the ranger of Windsor Great Park, who dammed streams to form a large ornamental water. The dam burst in 1768 after heavy rain, destroying the original lake; it was rebuilt under George III in the following decades, and the landscape was further developed in the 19th century under George IV and later monarchs, with pleasure grounds designed in the picturesque style.

Virginia Water has long been connected with royal residence at Windsor, and the surrounding parkland includes the gardens of Fort Belvedere nearby and the Valley Gardens and Savill Garden to the north.

Features

The Cascade, at the lake's eastern end, is a dramatic waterfall over rocks, fed from the lake and much rebuilt since the 18th century. On the southern shore stand the Ruins of Leptis Magna: genuine Roman columns and stones brought from the ancient city of Leptis Magna in Libya in the 1820s and arranged as a romantic ruined temple. Nearby is the towering totem pole, a 100-foot (30 m) Kwakwa̱ka'wakw-carved pole presented by the province of British Columbia in 1958 to mark its centenary. The obelisk pond, Five Arch Bridge, and extensive rhododendron and azalea plantings in the adjacent Valley Gardens draw visitors in spring.

In film

The lake and surrounding woods have been used as a filming location for productions including scenes in the Harry Potter films (as the shore of the Hogwarts lake), Robin Hood, Thor: The Dark World, and numerous television dramas.

Access

Virginia Water is managed as part of the Crown Estate's Windsor Great Park. Car parks on the A30 and at Blacknest Gate provide access, with a paved circuit path around the lake suitable for walking and cycling.
