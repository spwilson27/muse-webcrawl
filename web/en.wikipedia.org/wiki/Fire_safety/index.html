Fire safety

Fire safety is the set of practices intended to reduce destruction caused by fire. Fire safety measures include those that are intended to prevent the ignition of an uncontrolled fire and those that are used to limit the spread and impact of a fire. Fire safety is a component of fire protection, which also includes active suppression systems.


Smoke alarms and clear exit signage are basic fire-safety measures.

Elements of fire safety

A comprehensive fire safety strategy typically addresses:

  • Prevention — controlling ignition sources and fuels: safe storage of flammable materials, electrical safety, safe cooking practices, and hot-work permits.
  • Detection and warning — smoke alarms, heat detectors, and fire alarm systems that alert occupants early.
  • Means of escape — unobstructed exit routes, emergency lighting, fire doors, and evacuation plans with practised fire drills.
  • Compartmentation — fire-resisting construction that limits fire and smoke spread between parts of a building.
  • Suppression — fire extinguishers, fire blankets, and automatic sprinkler systems.

Building codes and regulation

Fire safety in buildings is governed by building codes and fire codes, enforced by fire marshals and building inspectors. In the United States, the NFPA (National Fire Protection Association) publishes widely adopted codes such as NFPA 1 (Fire Code) and NFPA 101 (Life Safety Code). In the United Kingdom, the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 requires a responsible person to carry out a fire risk assessment for most non-domestic premises. Building regulations govern structural fire resistance, escape routes, and detection in new construction.

Fire safety in the home

Most fire deaths occur in homes, most often from smoke inhalation during night-time fires. Key domestic measures include installing smoke alarms on every level and testing them regularly, planning escape routes, never leaving cooking unattended, keeping matches and lighters away from children, and taking care with candles, heaters, and smoking materials. Fire blankets and small extinguishers are recommended for kitchens.

Fire risk assessment

A fire risk assessment systematically identifies fire hazards, evaluates the risk to people, and decides on precautions. Common workplace hazard categories include ignition sources (electrical equipment, smoking, arson), fuel sources (paper, textiles, stored chemicals), and oxygen sources (including some oxidising chemicals and oxygen supplies). Assessments must be reviewed regularly and after significant changes.
