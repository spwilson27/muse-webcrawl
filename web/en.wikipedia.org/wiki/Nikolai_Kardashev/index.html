Nikolai Semyonovich Kardashev (25 April 1932 – 3 August 2019) was a Soviet and Russian astrophysicist, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, and a member of the Russian Academy of Sciences. He is best known for proposing the Kardashev scale, a scheme for classifying advanced technological civilisations by their energy use.

Life and work

Kardashev graduated from Moscow State University in 1955 and worked at the Sternberg Astronomical Institute before moving to the Astro Space Center of the Lebedev Physical Institute, which he later directed. His early radio astronomy research concerned the recombination lines of highly excited hydrogen atoms.

Kardashev scale

In his 1964 paper "Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations", written in the context of the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI), Kardashev proposed three types of civilisation: Type I, able to use all the energy available on its home planet; Type II, all the energy of its home star; and Type III, all the energy of its home galaxy. The scale has since become a staple of SETI discussions and popular science. Kardashev also advocateded space-based very-long-baseline interferometry and served as a leading figure behind the RadioAstron space telescope project.
