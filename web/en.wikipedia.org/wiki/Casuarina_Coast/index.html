Casuarina Coast

The Casuarina Coast is a region of the Asmat Regency in South Papua, Indonesia, along the Arafura Sea coast of the island of New Guinea. It takes its name from the casuarina trees that line its beaches.

Geography and people

The coast is a low-lying, swampy region of tidal rivers and mangrove forests, home to the Asmat people, renowned for their woodcarving traditions, and neighbouring groups such as the Kamoro. The principal town is Agats, the capital of Asmat Regency.

History

The Casuarina Coast gained international notoriety in 1961 when Michael Rockefeller, son of New York governor Nelson Rockefeller, disappeared near the coast while collecting Asmat art; his fate remains unknown. The region's remote river systems were historically difficult for outsiders to access, and the Asmat were largely isolated from the outside world until the mid-20th century.
