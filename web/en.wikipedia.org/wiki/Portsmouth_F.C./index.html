Portsmouth F.C.

Portsmouth Football Club is a professional association football club based in Portsmouth, Hampshire, England. Nicknamed "Pompey," the club was founded in 1898 and plays at Fratton Park. Portsmouth won the First Division title in 1949 and 1950, and the FA Cup in 1939 — holding the trophy through the Second World War — and again in 2008 under Harry Redknapp.

History

Portsmouth joined the Football League in 1920 and won the FA Cup in 1939, beating Wolverhampton Wanderers 4–1. Because of the wartime suspension, Pompey held the cup for seven years, the longest of any club. Under manager Jimmy Dickinson's generation, the club won back-to-back league championships in 1949 and 1950.

The modern era brought a roller-coaster: promotion to the Premier League in 2003, an FA Cup triumph in 2008 (beating Cardiff City 1–0, Kanu scoring), a UEFA Cup campaign, and then financial collapse. Repeated administrations saw the club fall to League Two by 2013. The Pompey Supporters' Trust bought the club in April 2013, making it the largest fan-owned club in England at the time, before selling to Michael Eisner's Tornante Company in 2017. Portsmouth won promotion back to the Championship as League One champions in 2024.

Support and identity

Pompey supporters are famous for the "Pompey Chimes" ("Play up Pompey"), and the club's bell-ringing fan John Westwood is a well-known figure. The South Coast derby against Southampton is the club's principal rivalry.
