Linear actuator

A linear actuator is an actuator that creates motion in a straight line, in contrast to the circular motion of a conventional electric motor. Linear actuators are used in machine tools and industrial machinery, in computer peripherals such as disk drives and printers, in valves and dampers, and in many other places where linear motion is required.

Types

Mechanical actuators usually convert rotary motion to linear motion, commonly by means of screws such as a lead screw or ball screw, or by wheel and axle mechanisms, cams, and belts. Hydraulic and pneumatic actuators use pressurized fluid acting on a piston in a hollow cylindrical tube. Electro-mechanical actuators combine an electric motor with a screw mechanism. Other principles include piezoelectric actuators, which exploit strain in ceramic materials under voltage, wax motors using thermal expansion, and linear motors that generate a magnetic field gradient.

Applications

Applications range from robotics and automation to aerospace flight controls, adjustable hospital beds, satellite positioning, and solar trackers that follow the sun to maximize energy capture.
