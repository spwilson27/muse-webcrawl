Cairo Declaration

Date27 November 1943 (released 1 December 1943)
SignatoriesUnited States, United Kingdom, Republic of China
ContextCairo Conference, Second World War

The Cairo Declaration was a statement issued on 27 November 1943, at the close of the Cairo Conference, by US President Franklin D. Roosevelt, British Prime Minister Winston Churchill, and Generalissimo Chiang Kai-shek of the Republic of China. It set out the Allies' aims against Japan in the Second World War and was published on 1 December 1943.

Text and terms

The declaration stated that the three nations sought no territorial gain for themselves and would continue military operations against Japan until its unconditional surrender. Its key territorial provisions were:

  • Japan would be stripped of all the islands in the Pacific it had seized or occupied since the beginning of the First World War in 1914;
  • All the territories Japan had taken from the Chinese — such as Manchuria, Formosa (Taiwan), and the Pescadores (Penghu) — would be restored to the Republic of China;
  • Korea, mindful of the enslavement of its people, would become free and independent "in due course".

Significance

The declaration's terms were reaffirmed in the Potsdam Declaration of July 1945, which demanded Japan's surrender on the basis that "the terms of the Cairo Declaration shall be carried out." After the war, the document became a reference point in debates over the status of Taiwan and the Korean peninsula; the Cairo Declaration is frequently cited by the People's Republic of China in support of its position on Taiwan, while its legal character (declaration of intent versus binding treaty) is debated.
