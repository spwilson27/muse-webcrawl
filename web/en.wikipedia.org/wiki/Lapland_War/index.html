Lapland War

The Lapland War (Finnish: Lapin sota; German: Lapplandkrieg) was fought between Finland and Nazi Germany from September 1944 to April 1945 in Finland's northern region of Lapland. Under the Moscow Armistice that ended the Continuation War with the Soviet Union, Finland was required to expel German troops from its territory by force if necessary. The German 20th Mountain Army, roughly 214,000 men under General Lothar Rendulic, withdrew northward toward Norway while devastating Lapland; the Finns, led by General Hjalmar Siilasvuo, pursued them. The last German forces left Finland on 27 April 1945.

Background

German troops had been stationed in northern Finland since 1941 as Finland's co-belligerent against the USSR. In the summer of 1944 the Germans planned Operation Birke (Birch) — a staged withdrawal to a defensive line at Ivalo — and its successor Nordlicht (Northern Light), a retreat into the Lyngen position in Norway. After Finland signed the Moscow Armistice on 19 September 1944, the two sides first tried a sham war (Schein­krieg), with a prearranged timetable of withdrawals and token exchanges of fire to satisfy Soviet demands without real fighting. Under Soviet pressure and deadlines, this "autumn manoeuvre" collapsed into genuine conflict at the end of September, notably after the Finnish landing at Tornio (Operation for the capture of the Tornio–Kemi area, 1 October 1944) behind German lines.

Course

Finnish forces advanced north through fierce rearguard actions at Tornio, Kemijärvi, and along the great roads. The Germans, applying scorched-earth tactics, destroyed bridges, roads, and settlements — including almost the entire town of Rovaniemi, burned in October 1944 (whether by accident from an exploding ammunition train or deliberately remains debated). Some 100,000 Lapland civilians had been evacuated, many to Sweden. The Finns reached the Norwegian border at Kilpisjärvi in April 1945; the famous photograph of the Finnish flag raised at the three-country cairn on 27 April 1945 marks the end of the war — Finland's third and last war of the Second World War era.

Aftermath

Finland lost about half of Lapland's buildings and infrastructure to destruction and faced enormous reconstruction costs; tens of thousands of German mines and unexploded ordnance plagued the region for years. The war fulfilled the armistice terms and confirmed Finland's break with Germany, easing its position in the 1947 Paris Peace Treaty, though Finland still paid heavy reparations to the USSR and leased Porkkala.
