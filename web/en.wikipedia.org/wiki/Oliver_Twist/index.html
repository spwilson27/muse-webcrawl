AuthorCharles Dickens

Full titleOliver Twist; or, The Parish Boy's Progress

Oliver Twist; or, The Parish Boy's Progress is Charles Dickens's second novel, published as a serial in Bentley's Miscellany from 1837 to 1839 and in book form in 1838. It tells the story of the orphan Oliver, born in a workhouse and raised under the harsh Poor Law regime, who is apprenticed, runs away to London, and falls in with a gang of child pickpockets led by Fagin. The novel is a landmark of social protest fiction, exposing the cruelty of the New Poor Law of 1834 and the criminal underworld of the capital.

Plot summary

Oliver's mother dies shortly after his birth in a workhouse. Raised on meagre rations, he draws lots with the other boys and asks the master, Mr. Bumble, for more food — "Please, sir, I want some more" — and is sold into apprenticeship to an undertaker, from whom he flees to London. There the Artful Dodger introduces him to Fagin, who trains street children to steal. Oliver is arrested, then rescued by the kind Mr. Brownlow; recaptured by the burglar Bill Sikes and Nancy, he is wounded in a burglary. Nancy, seeking to protect him, reveals the plot of the sinister Monks — Oliver's half-brother, who seeks to destroy evidence of Oliver's identity and inheritance — and is murdered by Sikes, who dies fleeing. Fagin is hanged, and Oliver is adopted by Brownlow and restored to his rightful family.

Themes and reception

The novel attacks the workhouse system, child exploitation, and Victorian hypocrisy, contrasting innate goodness with environmental corruption. It was an immediate popular success and has never been out of print. The characterisation of Fagin drew criticism for antisemitic stereotyping — he is repeatedly called "the Jew" in early editions — which Dickens later softened in response to criticism from Eliza Davis, and answered in part by creating the benevolent Riah in Our Mutual Friend.

Adaptations

Oliver Twist has been adapted innumerable times: notable versions include David Lean's 1948 film, Lionel Bart's stage musical Oliver! (1960) and its 1968 film adaptation, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture, the Disney animated Oliver & Company (1988), and Roman Polanski's 2005 film.
