Operator (mathematics)

In mathematics, an operator is generally a mapping or function that acts on elements of a space to produce elements of another space (possibly and sometimes required to be the same space). There is no general definition of an operator, but the term is often used in place of function when the domain is a set of functions or other structured objects. Also, the domain of an operator is often difficult to specify explicitly (e.g., in the case of an integral operator), and may be extended to related objects (an operator that acts on functions may act also on differential equations, for example).

Linear operators

The most common kind of operator encountered is the linear operator. Let U and V be vector spaces over some field K. A mapping A: U → V is linear if for every x, y in U and every α, β in K, one has A(αx + βy) = αA(x) + βA(y). Examples include differentiation and integration operators in calculus, and matrix operators in finite dimensions.

Bounded and unbounded operators

In functional analysis, an operator between normed spaces is bounded if it maps bounded sets to bounded sets, equivalently if it is continuous. Many important operators in quantum mechanics, such as the position and momentum operators, are unbounded and defined only on dense subspaces of a Hilbert space.
