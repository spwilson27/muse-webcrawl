Pershore

Pershore is a market town in the Wychavon district of Worcestershire, England, situated on the banks of the River Avon about 10 miles (16 km) southeast of Worcester. The town is renowned for its elegant Georgian architecture and for Pershore Abbey, a former Benedictine monastery church with a distinctive lantern tower.

History

Pershore grew around its abbey, founded in Anglo-Saxon times and richly endowed with land in the surrounding Vale of Evesham. After the Dissolution of the Monasteries, most of the abbey church was demolished, but the eastern arm was retained as the parish church. In the 18th and early 19th centuries the town prospered and was rebuilt in the Georgian style that defines its High Street today.

Plums

The rich soils of the Vale made Pershore famous for fruit growing, particularly plums: varieties including the Pershore Yellow Egg and the purple Pershore Emblem were bred locally. The town celebrates this heritage with an annual Plum Festival in August, centred on the August bank holiday.

Education and transport

Pershore is home to Pershore College, a land-based college specialising in horticulture, now part of the Warwickshire College Group. The town has a railway station on the Cotswold Line between Worcester and Oxford, and lies near the M5 motorway.
