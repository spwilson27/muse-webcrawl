The United Service Organizations Inc. (USO) is an American nonprofit-charitable corporation that provides live entertainment, such as comedians, actors and musicians, social facilities, and other programs to members of the United States Armed Forces and their families. Since 1941, it has worked in partnership with the Department of Defense (DoD), relying heavily on private contributions and on funds, goods, and services from various corporate and individual donors.

History

The USO was founded in 1941 at the request of President Franklin D. Roosevelt, who sought to unite several service associations — the Salvation Army, YMCA, YWCA, National Catholic Community Service, National Travelers Aid Association, and National Jewish Welfare Board — into one organisation to lift the morale of the military. During World War II, USO centers and "camp shows" became a famous part of American home-front and overseas life; by 1944 the USO operated thousands of clubs around the world.

The organisation was disbanded in 1947 but revived in 1950 for the Korean War. Its celebrity tours became legendary, most famously those of comedian Bob Hope, who entertained troops from World War II through the Gulf War. The USO currently operates centres at hundreds of locations worldwide, including airport lounges and facilities in combat zones, and provides transition, family, and expeditionary support programmes.
