Wolf Creek Dam is a dam on the Cumberland River in Russell County, Kentucky, United States. Built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for flood control, hydroelectric power and recreation, it impounds Lake Cumberland, one of the largest man-made lakes in the eastern United States. The structure combines a concrete gravity section with a long earth embankment, together stretching about 5,736 feet.

Wolf Creek Dam and its powerhouse

History

Construction began in 1941 but was interrupted by World War II, resuming in 1946; the dam was completed and the powerhouse, with six generating units totalling 270 MW, came online in the early 1950s.

Seepage problems and remediation

Soon after completion, engineers identified seepage through the dam's karst limestone foundation — the same geology that produced sinkholes and foundation grouting difficulties during construction. In 1968, muddy water and sinkholes downstream led to emergency grouting campaigns through the 1970s. In 2007, the Corps judged the dam to be at high risk of failure and lowered Lake Cumberland by roughly 43 feet while a permanent fix was constructed. Between 2008 and 2013 a massive concrete barrier wall was built deep into the foundation, one of the largest such remediation projects in Corps history, and the lake was returned to normal levels in 2013.
