Tyneham

Tyneham is a ghost village and former civil parish near Lulworth on the Isle of Purbeck in Dorset, England. In December 1943, shortly before Christmas, the village's 225 residents were evacuated after the War Office requisitioned the surrounding land for military training in preparation for the D-Day landings. The villagers were promised they could return after the war, but the land was retained by the Ministry of Defence and the village was never reoccupied.

Evacuation

Residents were given 28 days to leave. On the door of the church, the last resident pinned a famous note asking those who entered to treat the church and houses kindly: "We have given up our homes where many of us lived for generations to help win the war to keep men free. We shall return one day and thank you for treating the village kindly." Despite post-war campaigns led by the former residents and local MP, compulsory purchase was confirmed in 1948.

The village today

Tyneham lies within the Lulworth Ranges, an armoured fighting vehicle training area. Most buildings fell into ruin, but the church of St Mary and the schoolhouse survive and contain exhibitions about the village's history. The village and Worbarrow Bay are open to the public on many weekends and holidays when the ranges are not in use. Because the land has been free of farming and development for decades, the area is rich in wildlife and much of it is designated for nature conservation.
