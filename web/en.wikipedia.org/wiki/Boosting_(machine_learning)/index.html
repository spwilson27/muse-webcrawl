Boosting (machine learning)

Boosting
Type: Ensemble meta-algorithm
Base learners: Usually decision trees (stumps or shallow trees)
Key algorithms: AdaBoost, gradient boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost
Origins: PAC learning theory, 1990s

In machine learning, boosting is an ensemble meta-algorithm that combines many weak learners — models only slightly better than random guessing — into a single strong classifier or regressor. Boosting works sequentially: each new model is trained to correct the errors of the models before it, for example by increasing the weight of examples the current ensemble misclassifies, or by fitting the negative gradient of a loss function.

Algorithms

AdaBoost (Adaptive Boosting), introduced by Freund and Schapire in the mid-1990s, was the first practical boosting algorithm; it reweights training examples after each round and combines the weak learners by weighted vote. Gradient boosting generalises the idea by fitting new models to the residual errors (more precisely, the negative gradient of an arbitrary differentiable loss), and has become one of the most successful approaches for tabular data, especially with decision-tree base learners in implementations such as XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost, which frequently win data-science competitions.

Properties

Boosting reduces bias and, when regularised (through shrinkage/learning rate, subsampling, and limits on tree depth), can be resistant to overfitting despite its capacity. It differs from bagging methods such as random forests in that its learners are trained sequentially and dependently rather than independently in parallel.
