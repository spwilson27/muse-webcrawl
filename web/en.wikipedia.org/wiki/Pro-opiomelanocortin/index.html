Pro-opiomelanocortin

Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC)
Type: Precursor polypeptide
Gene: POMC (chromosome 2)
Products: ACTH, MSH, β-endorphin, lipotropin

Pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) is a precursor polypeptide with 241 amino acid residues, synthesised mainly in the pituitary gland, hypothalamus, and skin. It is cleaved by enzymes into several biologically active peptide hormones.

Products

Depending on the tissue and the enzymes present, POMC is processed into adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), melanocyte-stimulating hormones (α-, β-, and γ-MSH), β-endorphin, and lipotropins. In the anterior pituitary the main products are ACTH and β-lipotropin; in the hypothalamus, α-MSH and β-endorphin predominate.

Function

ACTH drives cortisol production by the adrenal glands as part of the stress response. Hypothalamic α-MSH acts on melanocortin receptors to suppress appetite and regulate energy balance, while β-endorphin is an endogenous opioid involved in analgesia.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the POMC gene cause severe early-onset obesity, adrenal insufficiency, and altered pigmentation. Defects in POMC processing are also implicated in some cases of inherited obesity.
