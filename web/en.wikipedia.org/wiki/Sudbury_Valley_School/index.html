The Sudbury Valley School is a private school in Framingham, Massachusetts, founded in 1968. It is the archetype of the Sudbury school model, a form of democratic education in which students are entirely responsible for their own education and the school is run by a direct democracy in which students and staff have equal votes.

Philosophy and structure

There is no prescribed curriculum, no classes unless students request them, no grades, tests, or homework. Students from age four to nineteen spend their time as they choose — reading, playing, conversing, using computers, or organising activities — in a community whose rules are made and enforced through the School Meeting, where every member, student and staff alike, has one vote. A judicial committee handles breaches of the rules. The school's founders, including physicist Daniel Greenberg, argued that children are naturally curious and learn best when trusted with freedom and responsibility.

Influence

Sudbury Valley has published numerous books about its philosophy, and dozens of schools around the world have adopted the model, including schools in Europe, Israel, Japan, and elsewhere in the United States. Studies of alumni by the school and independent researchers have reported that graduates proceed to higher education and careers at rates comparable to peers, though the model remains controversial among educators because of its radical departure from conventional schooling.
