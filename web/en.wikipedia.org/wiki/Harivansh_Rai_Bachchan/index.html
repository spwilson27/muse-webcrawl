Harivansh Rai Bachchan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Harivansh Rai Bachchan (27 November 1907 – 18 January 2003) was an Indian poet and writer of the Nayi Kavita literary movement of early 20th-century Hindi literature. He is best known for his early work Madhushala (1935), a collection of verses built on the metaphor of the tavern, the wine, and the cup, which became one of the most beloved works of modern Hindi poetry.

Life and career

Born in Babupatti village near Allahabad, he taught at Allahabad University and later worked for the Ministry of External Affairs as an officer on special duty for Hindi. He took a doctorate at Cambridge University on the poetry of W. B. Yeats. His four-volume autobiography is considered a landmark of Hindi prose. He was nominated to the Rajya Sabha and received the Padma Bhushan in 1976.

Family

His son Amitabh Bachchan became one of the most famous actors of Indian cinema; his poems are recited by Amitabh in public performances, and his verse "agneepath" inspired the film of that name.
