Corporate raid

In business, a corporate raid is the process of buying a large stake in a corporation and then using shareholder voting rights to compel the management of the target company to take certain actions, typically either a hostile takeover, the sale of assets, or major restructuring. The raider may seek to extract value by breaking up the company ("asset stripping") or by being bought out at a premium in a practice known as greenmail.

History

Corporate raids became prominent in the United States in the 1970s and 1980s, when raiders such as Carl Icahn, T. Boone Pickens, Ronald Perelman and Kirk Kerkorian targeted undervalued companies, often financing acquisitions with junk bonds in leveraged buyouts. Famous engagements include Icahn's takeover of TWA and the battle for RJR Nabisco chronicled in Barbarians at the Gate.

Defences

Target companies developed defences including the poison pill (shareholder rights plan), golden parachutes for executives, staggered boards, and finding a friendly "white knight" bidder. In modern practice, activist investors and hedge funds pursue similar strategies, often settling for board seats and strategic changes rather than full takeovers.
