Pyroxene

The pyroxenes (commonly abbreviated Px) are a group of important rock-forming inosilicate minerals found in many igneous and metamorphic rocks. They share a general structure consisting of single chains of silica tetrahedra, with the general formula XY(Si,Al)2O6.

Classification

Pyroxenes crystallize in either the monoclinic system (clinopyroxenes, e.g. augite, diopside, jadeite) or the orthorhombic system (orthopyroxenes, e.g. enstatite and ferrosilite). The name pyroxene comes from the Greek words for "fire" and "stranger", because early observers found its crystals in volcanic lavas and assumed them to be foreign inclusions.

Occurrence

Pyroxenes are major constituents of basalt, gabbro and peridotite, and hence of oceanic crust and the upper mantle. They also occur in meteorites and in lunar rocks. The mineral's cleavage at nearly 90° distinguishes it from the similar amphiboles.
