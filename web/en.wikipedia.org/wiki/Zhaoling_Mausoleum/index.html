Zhaoling (Chinese: 昭陵; pinyin: Zhāolíng) is the mausoleum of Emperor Taizong of Tang (599–649), founder of the Tang dynasty of China, located on Mount Jiuzong in Liquan County, Shaanxi province. It is the largest of the eighteen Tang dynasty imperial tombs in the Guanzhong plain and one of the largest mausoleum complexes in Chinese history, covering an area of roughly 200 square kilometres including more than 200 satellite burials of imperial relatives, officials and generals.

History

Construction began in 637, initially for Taizong's wife Empress Wende (Empress Zhangsun), who had died in 636 and was buried on Mount Jiuzong. Taizong, who had praised the frugality of using a natural mountain as a tomb mound, decreed that he would be interred in the same mausoleum. He was buried there in 649 following his death. The necropolis grew to include the tombs of imperial princes, princesses, and meritorious officials whom the emperor had permitted to be buried alongside him.

Six Steeds of Zhaoling

The mausoleum is most famous for the Six Steeds of Zhaoling (昭陵六骏), six large stone relief sculptures depicting the warhorses that Taizong rode in the campaigns that unified China. Designed, according to tradition, from sketches by the court painter Yan Liben, the reliefs are masterpieces of Tang stone carving. In the early 20th century, two of the six reliefs were taken abroad and are now held by the Penn Museum in Philadelphia, United States, while the remaining four are displayed at the Beilin Museum in Xi'an.

Layout

The complex follows the model of the Tang capital Chang'an, with an inner city centred on the tomb mound formed by the mountain peak, ceremonial gates, offering halls, and a spirit way lined with stone statues of officials, horses and foreign envoys — reflecting Taizong's status as both Emperor of China and Heavenly Khan of the Turks.
