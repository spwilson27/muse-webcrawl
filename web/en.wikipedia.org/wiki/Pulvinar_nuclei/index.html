The pulvinar nuclei (nuclei pulvinares thalami) are a group of nuclei located in the posterior (caudal) portion of the thalamus, forming a large cushion-like bulge — the pulvinar (Latin for "cushion") — overhanging the superior colliculi. It is the largest thalamic nucleus in primates, and is conventionally divided into the lateral, inferior, medial, and anterior pulvinar subnuclei.

Connections and function

The lateral and inferior pulvinar receive retinotopically organised input from the superior colliculus and project to visual cortical areas including V2, V3, V4, and V5/MT, forming a secondary visual pathway that bypasses the primary visual cortex. The medial pulvinar connects with parietal, frontal, and temporal association cortices and with the amygdala. The pulvinar is involved in visual attention and salience: it helps coordinate which visual information reaches cortex and has been implicated in blindsight, spatial neglect, and the integration of affective visual signals (such as fearful faces) routed through the amygdala.

Clinical relevance

Lesions of the pulvinar can produce deficits of visual attention and spatial awareness. Abnormal pulvinar activity has been reported in conditions including schizophrenia, and in Rasmussen's encephalitis. Because of its role in attention, the pulvinar has been studied as a possible target for neuromodulation in attention disorders.
