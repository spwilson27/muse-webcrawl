Palm, Inc.

Palm, Inc. was an American company that specialized in manufacturing personal digital assistants (PDAs) and various other electronics. It was the designer of the first widely successful PDA, the PalmPilot, and the first smartphone, the Treo.

History

Palm Computing was founded in 1992 by Jeff Hawkins, who later hired Donna Dubinsky and Ed Colligan. The company's first products were software for the early consumer PDA market. In 1996, after being acquired by U.S. Robotics, Palm released the Pilot 1000 and Pilot 5000, followed by the hugely successful PalmPilot series. Palm became an independent publicly traded company in 2000.

In 2003, Palm split into two companies: palmOne (hardware) and PalmSource (software). palmOne acquired the Handspring company, founded by Hawkins, Dubinsky, and Colligan, which had developed the Treo smartphone line. In 2005, palmOne reacquired full rights to the Palm name and renamed itself Palm, Inc.

webOS era and HP acquisition

At the Consumer Electronics Show in January 2009, Palm announced webOS and the Palm Pre smartphone, an attempt to compete with the iPhone and Android devices. Despite critical praise for webOS's card-based multitasking interface, sales struggled. On April 28, 2010, Hewlett-Packard announced it would acquire Palm for $1.2 billion. HP initially continued developing webOS devices, including the HP TouchPad tablet, but discontinued webOS hardware in August 2011. The Palm brand was later sold to TCL Corporation, which announced plans to revive it, and in 2018 a new Palm companion phone was released by a San Francisco startup licensing the name.
