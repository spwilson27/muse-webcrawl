James Baird Weaver (12 June 1833 – 6 February 1912) was an American politician in Iowa who was a leader of the agrarian reform movements of the late 19th century. A Union general in the Civil War and a three-term congressman, he was the presidential nominee of the Greenback Party in 1880 and of the People's Party (Populists) in 1892, when he won over a million popular votes and 22 electoral votes.

Early life and Civil War

Weaver was born in Dayton, Ohio, and moved with his family to Iowa in 1847. He studied law in Bloomfield, Iowa, and was admitted to the bar in 1856. At the outbreak of the Civil War he enlisted in the 2nd Iowa Volunteer Infantry, fighting at Fort Donelson, Shiloh and Corinth. He rose to the rank of colonel and was brevetted brigadier general in 1864.

Congress and the Greenbacks

Originally a Republican, Weaver won election to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1878 on a fusion ticket, and in Congress championed the Greenback programme of expanding the currency to relieve indebted farmers. In 1880 the Greenback Party nominated him for president; he polled about 300,000 votes, the party's best national showing. He returned to Congress in 1885 and 1887 as a Democrat–Greenback fusion candidate.

Populist presidential campaign, 1892

Weaver helped organise the People's Party out of the Farmers' Alliance movement. At the party's Omaha convention in July 1892 he was nominated for president on the platform that became famous as the Omaha Platform, demanding the free coinage of silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads and telegraphs, and direct election of senators. Weaver finished third with about 8.5% of the popular vote and carried five states — Colorado, Idaho, Kansas, Nebraska and Nevada — winning 22 electoral votes, the strongest third-party presidential result between the Civil War and 1912.

Later life

Weaver supported fusion with the Democrats behind William Jennings Bryan in 1896, a course that effectively ended the People's Party as an independent force. He later served as mayor of Colfax, Iowa, and wrote a history of Davis County. He died in Des Moines in 1912.
