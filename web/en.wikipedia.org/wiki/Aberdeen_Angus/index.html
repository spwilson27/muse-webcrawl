The Aberdeen Angus, usually simply called the Angus, is a Scottish breed of beef cattle originating in the counties of Aberdeenshire and Angus in north-eastern Scotland. It is one of the most widely distributed beef breeds in the world and is renowned for the marbling and tenderness of its meat.

History

The breed was developed in the early nineteenth century from the naturally hornless native cattle of the north-east of Scotland. Breeders such as Hugh Watson of Keillor and William McCombie of Tillyfour systematically improved the stock, and the first herd book was published in 1862. The Aberdeen-Angus Cattle Society was founded in 1879. Angus cattle were exported to the United States from 1873 onward and thereafter to many other countries, including Argentina, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and South Africa.

Characteristics

Angus cattle are naturally polled, meaning they are born without horns — a trait that is dominant in crossbreeding, so that most calves sired by an Angus bull are also polled. The traditional coat colour is solid black, but a recessive red form, the Red Angus, is recognised separately in some countries. The breed is noted for calving ease, good mothering ability, efficient conversion of forage, and the ability to produce well-marbled beef on grass or grain.

Influence

Because of its meat quality and adaptability, the Angus has been used extensively in crossbreeding programmes and has influenced the development of derivative breeds such as the Brangus (Angus × Brahman) and the Murray Grey. Certified Angus Beef, a specification-based brand established in the United States in 1978, is among the best-known branded beef programmes in the world.
