Yurok language

Yurok (sa'ah nepuy) is an Algic language traditionally spoken by the Yurok people of the lower Klamath River and the adjacent Pacific coast in northwestern California. Together with Wiyot, it forms the Californian branch of the Algic family and is thus distantly related to the widespread Algonquian languages of eastern North America — a connection established by Edward Sapir's classification of the "Ritwan" languages within Algic.

Characteristics

Yurok has a relatively small consonant inventory, with a contrast between plain and ejective stops, and a vowel system of /i, e, a, o, u/ with short and long quantities. It lacks the /l/ sound found in many Californian languages — Yurok speakers famously adapt foreign words accordingly. Verbs are built with elaborate prefixes marking person and aspect, and the language makes extensive use of classificatory verb stems that differ according to the shape or nature of the subject or object.

Decline and revitalisation

By the early twenty-first century the number of fluent first-language speakers had fallen to a handful of elders. The Yurok Tribe has mounted an intensive revitalisation programme: the linguist Andrew Garrett's Yurok Language Project at the University of California, Berkeley assembled dictionaries, texts and audio archives, and master–apprentice programmes and school classes — including instruction at Del Norte High School and in tribal preschools — have produced a new generation of speakers. In 2013 the programme reported the largest cohort of learners in the language's modern history, and Yurok instruction has since expanded in local public schools.
