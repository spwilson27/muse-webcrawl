Fashion illustration

Fashion illustration is the art of communicating fashion ideas in a visual form originating with illustration, drawing, and painting, and also known as fashion design sketching. It is used by fashion designers to brainstorm their ideas on paper or digitally. Fashion illustrators work in advertising, fashion houses, magazines, and books.

History

Fashion illustration has existed for nearly 500 years, since printed fashion plates began appearing in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, magazines such as Vogue and Harper's Bazaar employed renowned illustrators — including Charles Dana Gibson, Erté, and later René Gruau — whose images defined the look of eras. The elegant pochoir plates of Paul Iribe for designer Paul Poiret in 1908 are regarded as landmarks of the art.

Decline and revival

From the 1930s onward, photography increasingly displaced illustration on magazine covers and in advertising, a shift sealed when Vogue began using photographic covers. Nonetheless, celebrated illustrators such as Antonio Lopez kept the practice alive, and illustration experienced a revival from the late twentieth century onward, aided by digital tools, new magazines, and demand for a more artistic, interpretive alternative to photography.

Techniques

Fashion illustrators traditionally work in watercolor, gouache, ink, and markers, exaggerating the figure — often to nine or ten head-lengths — for elegance and movement. Contemporary practitioners also use digital software for illustration and animation.
