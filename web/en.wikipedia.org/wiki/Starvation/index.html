Starvation

Starvation is a severe deficiency in caloric energy intake, below the level needed to maintain an organism's life. It is the most extreme form of malnutrition. In humans, prolonged starvation can cause permanent organ damage and eventually, death. The term inanition refers to the symptoms and effects of starvation.

Physiology

The body initially meets its energy deficit from glycogen stores, then from fat, and finally by catabolizing protein, including muscle and organ tissue. Metabolic adaptation lowers resting energy expenditure, but continued deprivation leads to edema, immune suppression, bradycardia, hypothermia, and failure of vital organs. Death typically occurs when 30–40% of total body protein or roughly one-third to one-half of body weight has been lost. Refeeding after prolonged starvation must be managed carefully because of the risk of refeeding syndrome.

Causes

Starvation affects individuals through eating disorders such as anorexia nervosa, diseases of the digestive system, and fasting; on a population scale it results from famine, poverty, war, and siege warfare. The Minnesota Starvation Experiment (1944–45) systematically documented its psychological and physiological effects in conscientious objectors. Starvation of civilians as a method of warfare is prohibited under the Geneva Conventions and is a war crime under the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court.
