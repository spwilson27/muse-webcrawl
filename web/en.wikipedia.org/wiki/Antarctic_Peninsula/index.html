The Antarctic Peninsula is the northernmost part of mainland Antarctica, extending about 1,300 kilometres toward the tip of South America across the Drake Passage. It is the mildest and most accessible part of the continent and hosts the majority of Antarctica's research stations.

Geography and climate

The peninsula is mountainous, being the continuation of the Andes chain. Its western coast is deeply indented with bays and islands. The region has warmed significantly since the mid-20th century, and several of its ice shelves, including Larsen B, have disintegrated.

Wildlife and research

The peninsula's comparatively mild climate supports tundra vegetation and large colonies of penguins, seals and seabirds. Numerous countries operate research stations there, Argentina and Chile maintaining the largest presence. Territorial claims by Argentina, Chile and the United Kingdom overlap on the peninsula but are held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty.
