Commonwealth realm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Commonwealth realm is a sovereign state within the Commonwealth of Nations that has the same monarch as its head of state — currently Charles III. There are fifteen realms as of the mid-2020s: the United Kingdom itself, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Jamaica, the Bahamas, Belize, Grenada, Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands, Tuvalu, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Antigua and Barbuda, and Saint Kitts and Nevis. In each realm outside the UK the monarch is represented by a Governor-general.

Realms are fully independent states sharing a person as monarch; the Crown is legally divided, so the King of Canada is a distinct legal office from the King of the United Kingdom. Former realms, such as India (1950), Ireland, South Africa and most recently Barbados (2021), became republics while mostly remaining in the Commonwealth. Republican movements exist in several current realms (Australia held a referendum in 1999, which failed; Jamaica has debated the question), and changes in the list are a recurring feature of Commonwealth politics.
