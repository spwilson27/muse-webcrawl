Astigmatism is a type of refractive error in which the eye does not focus light evenly on the retina, because of an irregular curvature of the cornea or, less commonly, the lens. This results in distorted or blurred vision at any distance. Other symptoms can include eyestrain, headaches, and trouble driving at night. Astigmatism often occurs at birth and can change later in life; it frequently accompanies myopia (near-sightedness) or hyperopia (far-sightedness).

Signs and symptoms

People with astigmatism see blur in one orientation more than another: lines in one direction may appear sharp while perpendicular lines appear blurred. Squinting can partially compensate. Uncorrected astigmatism in children can contribute to amblyopia ("lazy eye").

Cause

In corneal astigmatism, the cornea is curved more steeply in one meridian than the perpendicular one — roughly like a rugby ball rather than a football — so light is focused at two different points. In lenticular astigmatism the lens has an analogous irregularity. The condition has a significant hereditary component, and can also be induced by eye surgery, injury, or diseases of the cornea such as keratoconus. Astigmatism whose principal meridians are perpendicular is called "regular"; otherwise it is "irregular".

Diagnosis

Astigmatism is detected during a routine eye examination using instruments such as the keratometer and corneal topographer, and is quantified by refraction. It is recorded in dioptres of cylindrical power together with the axis of the cylinder, as in the "cylinder" and "axis" fields of a spectacle prescription.

Treatment

Correction uses a cylindrical (toric) lens, which refracts light more strongly in one plane than the other, in glasses or contact lenses. Refractive surgery — including LASIK and photorefractive keratectomy — can reshape the cornea to correct astigmatism permanently in suitable candidates. The effect was first described scientifically by Thomas Young in 1801, and the term was coined by William Whewell; George Biddell Airy designed the first cylindrical lenses for its correction in the 1820s.
