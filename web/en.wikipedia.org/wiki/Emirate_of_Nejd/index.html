The Emirate of Nejd was the second Saudi state, existing from 1824 to 1891 in the Nejd region of central Arabia. It is also known as the Second Saudi State. Its capital was Riyadh, and it was ruled by the House of Saud, which had previously ruled the Emirate of Diriyah (the first Saudi state) until its destruction by Ottoman-Egyptian forces in 1818.

History

The state was founded by Turki bin Abdullah, who recaptured Riyadh from Egyptian forces in 1824 and made it his capital. Turki restored Saudi rule over much of Nejd, though the Hejaz remained under Egyptian and later Ottoman control. After Turki's assassination in 1834, his son Faisal bin Turki became the state's most prominent ruler, reigning in two periods (1834–1838 and 1843–1865). Faisal expanded Saudi influence toward the Persian Gulf coast and into al-Hasa.

Faisal was briefly deposed and exiled to Cairo by the Egyptians in 1838, but returned in 1843 and ruled until his death in 1865. His reign is generally regarded as the high point of the second Saudi state, marked by relative stability and the consolidation of Wahhabi religious authority in partnership with the Al ash-Sheikh family, descendants of Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab.

Decline and fall

After Faisal's death, a succession struggle between his sons Abdullah and Saud weakened the state. The rival Al Rashid dynasty of Ha'il, backed at times by the Ottoman Empire, steadily encroached on Saudi territory. In 1891, Muhammad bin Abdullah Al Rashid defeated the last Saudi emir, Abdul Rahman bin Faisal, at the Battle of Mulayda, ending the Emirate of Nejd. Abdul Rahman and his family went into exile in Kuwait; his son Abdulaziz (Ibn Saud) would recapture Riyadh in 1902 and found the third Saudi state, the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
