Androphobia

Androphobia is an abnormal fear of men. The term comes from the Greek anēr / andros (man) and phobos (fear). It is classified among the specific phobias and is distinct from misandry, which denotes dislike or prejudice against men rather than a clinical anxiety disorder.

Causes and symptoms

Androphobia may develop after traumatic experiences involving men, through learned behavior from caregivers, or in connection with broader anxiety disorders. Symptoms on encountering men can include rapid heartbeat, sweating, trembling, nausea, panic attacks and avoidance behavior that interferes with work, school or relationships. The condition is most often reported in women and girls, though it can affect people of any sex.

Treatment

As with other specific phobias, treatment commonly involves psychotherapy, particularly cognitive behavioral therapy and gradual exposure therapy, which help the person confront the feared object in a controlled and safe manner. Relaxation techniques and, in some cases, anti-anxiety medication may be used as adjuncts. Hypnotherapy and counseling have also been reported in case literature.

Cultural aspects

Some cultures and religious or communal movements have historically practiced forms of separation from men, sometimes described in anthropological literature in terms overlapping with androphobia, though communal seclusion is generally a social practice rather than an individual phobia. The term also appears in discussions of radical feminism, where scholars distinguish political critique from clinical fear.
