Le Cid

Written byPierre Corneille
Date premiered1637
Original languageFrench
GenreTragicomedy
SettingSeville, 11th century

Le Cid is a five-act French tragicomedy written by Pierre Corneille, first performed in 1637. Based on the legend of the Spanish hero El Cid (Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar) and drawing on Guillén de Castro's play Las mocedades del Cid, it is regarded as the foundational work of French classical tragedy and one of the masterpieces of French theatre.

Plot

Don Rodrigue and Chimène are in love, but their fathers quarrel: when Chimène's father, the Count de Gormas, insults and strikes Rodrigue's aged father Don Diègue, Rodrigue is bound by honour to challenge the Count, and kills him in a duel. Chimène, torn between love and filial duty, demands justice from the King, even as Rodrigue goes on to defeat a Moorish invasion and earn the title "Le Cid" (from the Arabic sayyid, lord). The King defers their marriage, allowing time for grief and honour to be reconciled.

The Quarrel of Le Cid

The play's enormous success provoked the famous Querelle du Cid: rivals and the newly founded Académie française, encouraged by Cardinal Richelieu, criticised Corneille for violating the classical unities, for improbability (Chimène receiving her father's killer), and for borrowing from a Spanish source. The controversy helped codify the rules of French classical drama, and Corneille did not present another play for several years, returning with Horace in 1640.
