The Virginia Peninsula is a peninsula in southeast Virginia bounded by the York River, the James River, Hampton Roads, and the Chesapeake Bay. It is sometimes known as the Lower Peninsula to distinguish it from the Middle and Northern Neck peninsulas to the north. The land portion comprises the cities of Newport News, Hampton, and Poquoson, together with York County and James City County, and is generally considered to include the independent city of Williamsburg.

The Virginia Peninsula between the James and York rivers.

History

The peninsula was the site of some of the earliest English settlement in North America: Jamestown was founded on its southern shore in 1607, and Williamsburg became the colonial capital. During the American Revolutionary War the peninsula saw the decisive Siege of Yorktown in 1781. In the Civil War, George B. McClellan's 1862 Peninsula Campaign advanced up the peninsula toward Richmond but stalled in the Seven Days Battles.

Economy and transport

Modern Newport News hosts a major shipyard, Newport News Shipbuilding, and the peninsula hosts military installations including Joint Base Langley–Eustis and the Coast Guard training center at Yorktown. Interstate 64 runs the length of the peninsula, and the Monitor–Merrimac Memorial Bridge–Tunnel and Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel connect it to the Southside across Hampton Roads.
