Rungis International Market

The Rungis International Market (French: Marché International de Rungis) is the principal wholesale market of Paris and the largest wholesale food market in the world. It is located in the commune of Rungis, in the southern suburbs of Paris, close to Orly Airport.

History

For centuries Paris's central market was Les Halles, in the heart of the city, described by Émile Zola as "the belly of Paris". By the 1960s the medieval site was far too small and congested. The market was transferred to Rungis, opening on 3 March 1969, on a 232-hectare site chosen for its road, rail, and air connections; Les Halles was demolished and replaced by the Forum des Halles.

Operations

Rungis handles roughly 1.5 million tonnes of foodstuffs a year — fruit and vegetables, meat, fish and seafood, dairy products, flowers, and gastronomic products — supplying some 18 million consumers in the Île-de-France region and beyond. Around 1,200 companies operate on the site, employing about 13,000 people. Trading begins in the small hours: the fish pavilion opens around 2 a.m., followed by meat, produce, and flowers. The market is managed by the mixed-economy company Semmaris.

Access

Rungis is reserved for professionals — wholesalers, retailers, restaurateurs, and caterers — and is not open to the general public except on organised visits. It has its own railway station and motorway junctions, and a dedicated TGV link carries fresh produce from southern France.
