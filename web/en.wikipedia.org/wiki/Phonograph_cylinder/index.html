Phonograph cylinder

Phonograph cylinders (also referred to as Edison cylinders) are the earliest commercial medium for recording and reproducing sound. Known simply as "records" in their era, these hollow cylindrical objects have an audio recording engraved into their outside surface, which can be reproduced when they are played on a mechanical cylinder phonograph.

Thomas Edison developed the phonograph in 1877, initially using tinfoil; wax cylinders followed in the late 1880s. Standard cylinders played for about two minutes; later four-minute cylinders doubled the playing time. Cylinder production continued into the 1920s before the disc record became dominant.
