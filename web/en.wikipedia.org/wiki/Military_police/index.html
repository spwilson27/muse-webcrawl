Military police

Military police are typically identified by distinctive armbands, brassards, or helmet markings such as "MP".

Military police (MP) are military organizations connected with law enforcement within or upon the property of the military. Military police serve as a force multiplier for commanders by enforcing discipline, laws, and regulations among service members, conducting criminal investigations, managing traffic and force protection, and handling prisoners of war and detained persons.

Duties

Typical responsibilities include garrison law enforcement, battlefield circulation control, route reconnaissance and security, area security operations, protection of high-ranking officials, criminal investigation (often through specialized bodies such as the U.S. Army Criminal Investigation Division), corrections and internment, and customs enforcement. In wartime, military police take charge of enemy prisoners of war and support rear-area security.

By country

In the United States, each service maintains its own police: the Army Military Police Corps, the Navy Master-at-Arms, the Air Force Security Forces, and the Marine Corps Military Police. In France, the Netherlands, Italy, and Spain, gendarmerie forces such as the French Gendarmerie Nationale, the Royal Netherlands Marechaussee, the Italian Carabinieri, and the Spanish Guardia Civil perform military police functions while also policing civilians in rural areas. The British forces are served by the Royal Military Police, the Royal Navy Police, and the RAF Police.
