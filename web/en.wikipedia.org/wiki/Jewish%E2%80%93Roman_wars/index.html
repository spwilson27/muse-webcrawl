Jewish–Roman wars

The Jewish–Roman wars were a series of large-scale revolts by the Jews of Judaea and other provinces of the Eastern Roman Empire against Roman rule between 66 and 136 CE.

The wars

The First Jewish–Roman War (66–73/74) began in Judaea and culminated in the Roman destruction of Jerusalem and the Second Temple in 70 CE, ending with the fall of Masada. The Kitos War (115–117) erupted among Jewish communities in Cyrenaica, Cyprus, and Egypt during Trajan's Parthian campaign and was suppressed with great loss of life. The Bar Kokhba revolt (132–136), led by Simon bar Kokhba, briefly established an independent Jewish state before being crushed by Hadrian's legions.

Consequences

The revolts devastated the Jewish population of Judaea, which Hadrian renamed Syria Palaestina; Jerusalem was rebuilt as the pagan city of Aelia Capitolina, from which Jews were barred. The wars accelerated the shift of Judaism's centre toward the academies of Galilee and Babylonia and the rise of rabbinic Judaism.
