Dol Guldur is a fictional fortress in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, located on the hill of Amon Lanc in southern Mirkwood. Its name is Sindarin for "Hill of Sorcery". It was the stronghold of Sauron during his secret recovery of strength in the Third Age, when he was known as the Necromancer.

History

Amon Lanc was originally the capital of the Silvan Elves under Oropher. Around T.A. 1050 Sauron, fleeing discovery, took the hill and built Dol Guldur; his growing shadow corrupted the forest, thereafter called Mirkwood. From there he sought the One Ring and interrogated Gollum's trail. In T.A. 2941 the White Council, suspecting the Necromancer's identity, attacked Dol Guldur; Sauron, forewarned, feigned flight and returned openly to Mordor. During the War of the Ring, forces from Dol Guldur — then commanded by Khamûl the Nazgûl — assaulted Lothlórien three times. After Sauron's fall, Galadriel threw down its walls and cleansed the hill, renamed Amon Lanc once more.

Adaptations

Dol Guldur features prominently in Peter Jackson's The Hobbit film trilogy, where its confrontation with the White Council is dramatised.
