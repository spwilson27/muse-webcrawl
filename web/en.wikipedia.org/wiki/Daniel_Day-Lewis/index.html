Sir Daniel Michael Blake Day-Lewis (born 29 April 1957) is an English-Irish actor. Widely regarded as one of the greatest actors of his generation, he is the only performer to have won three Academy Awards for Best Actor, and is known for his immersive method acting and selective filmography.

Early life

Day-Lewis was born in London, the son of poet laureate Cecil Day-Lewis and actress Jill Balcon, and grandson of British film producer Michael Balcon. He trained at the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School.

Career

He gained acclaim with My Beautiful Laundrette and A Room with a View (1985), then won his first Oscar for playing Christy Brown in My Left Foot (1989). Further landmark roles include The Last of the Mohicans, In the Name of the Father, Gangs of New York, and Phantom Thread (2017). His second and third Best Actor Oscars came for There Will Be Blood (2007), as oilman Daniel Plainview, and Lincoln (2012), as Abraham Lincoln. He was knighted in 2014.

Retirement and return

After Phantom Thread, Day-Lewis announced his retirement in 2017. He returned to acting in 2024 to star in Anemone, the directorial debut of his son Ronan Day-Lewis, co-written by the two of them.
