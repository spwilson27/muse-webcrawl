Database normalization

Database normalization is the process of structuring a relational database in accordance with a series of so-called normal forms in order to reduce data redundancy and improve data integrity. It was first proposed by British computer scientist Edgar F. Codd as part of his relational model of data. Normalization entails organizing the columns (attributes) and tables (relations) of a database to ensure that their dependencies are properly enforced by database integrity constraints.

Objectives

The goals of normalization are to free collections of relations from undesirable insertion, update, and deletion anomalies; to reduce the need for restructuring as new types of data are introduced; and to make the relational model more informative to users. A table that satisfies a given normal form also satisfies all lower forms: first normal form (1NF) requires atomic values and a key; second normal form (2NF) removes partial dependencies on part of a key; third normal form (3NF) removes transitive dependencies; and Boyce–Codd normal form (BCNF) strengthens 3NF. Higher forms (4NF, 5NF) address multi-valued and join dependencies.

Practice

Most production databases are normalized to 3NF or BCNF. Designers sometimes deliberately denormalize — reintroducing controlled redundancy — to improve query performance, especially in data warehouses, where dimensional (star schema) models trade redundancy for fast, simple queries.
