Christmas is an annual festival commemorating the birth of Jesus Christ, observed primarily on December 25 as a religious and cultural celebration among billions of people around the world. A feast central to the Christian liturgical year, it is preceded by the season of Advent and initiates the season of Christmastide, which historically in the West lasts twelve days and culminates on Twelfth Night.

Christmas Day is a public holiday in many countries, is celebrated religiously by a majority of Christians, and culturally by many non-Christians, forming an integral part of the holiday season organised around it.

History

The Gospels do not give a date for Jesus's birth. By the mid-4th century the Western church was celebrating the nativity on 25 December — a date that may have been chosen to coincide with, or reinterpret, the Roman festival of Sol Invictus and the winter solstice season — while many Eastern churches used 6 January. The medieval festival developed traditions of feasting, mumming and charity. In the 17th century, Puritans in England and New England suppressed Christmas as unbiblical and popish; it revived in the 19th century, reshaped by the Oxford Movement, Dickens's A Christmas Carol (1843) and the domestic, child-centred customs popularised under Queen Victoria and Prince Albert, including the Christmas tree.

Customs

  • Gift-giving, associated with Saint Nicholas, Santa Claus, Father Christmas and other gift-bringers.
  • Decorations — trees, lights, wreaths, nativity scenes and cards (sent since the 1840s).
  • Food — roast turkey or goose, Christmas pudding, mince pies and, in many countries, fruitcake.
  • Music — carols such as "Silent Night" and "O Come, All Ye Faithful".
  • Church services — Midnight Mass, lessons and carols, and Christingle services.

Economics

Christmas is the peak retail season in many countries; the period's trade is critical for retailers, logistics and confectionery makers such as Nabisco's parent companies. Critics since the 19th century have lamented the festival's commercialisation, a theme Dickens himself balanced against its charitable spirit.
