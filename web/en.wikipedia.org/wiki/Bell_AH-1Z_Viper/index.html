The Bell AH-1Z Viper is a twin-engine attack helicopter developed by Bell Helicopter for the United States Marine Corps as part of the H-1 upgrade program. Based on the AH-1W SuperCobra, the AH-1Z features a four-blade composite main rotor, new transmission and avionics, and shares about 84% of its components with the UH-1Y Venom utility helicopter.

Design

The Viper is armed with a 20 mm M197 three-barrel rotary cannon in a chin turret and can carry AGM-114 Hellfire anti-tank missiles, AIM-9 Sidewinder air-to-air missiles, and 70 mm rocket pods on its stub wings. Its integrated glass cockpit, helmet-mounted sight and display system, and target sight system give it day/night all-weather attack capability.

Operational history

The AH-1Z first flew on 8 December 2000 and achieved initial operating capability with the Marine Corps in 2010, subsequently deploying to Afghanistan. In addition to the USMC, the type has been exported to countries including Bahrain and the Czech Republic, where it replaced Soviet-era attack helicopters. See also attack helicopter.
