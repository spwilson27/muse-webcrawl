Good (economics)

In economics, a good is a material that satisfies human wants and provides utility, for example, to a consumer making a purchase of a satisfying product. A common distinction is made between goods which are transferable and services, which are non-transferable. A good may be a physical item, as opposed to a service which is intangible.

Classification

Goods are classified by excludability and rivalry: private goods are excludable and rivalrous; public goods such as national defence are non-excludable and non-rivalrous; club goods are excludable but non-rivalrous; and common-pool resources are non-excludable but rivalrous. Other distinctions include normal versus inferior goods (by how demand responds to income), durable versus non-durable goods, and intermediate versus final goods in production.

Utility and value

Goods generate utility, generally diminishing at the margin as more units are consumed — the principle of diminishing marginal utility. Items that reduce utility, such as pollution or garbage, are called "bads". Free goods, like air, are available without cost in quantities exceeding demand; economic goods are scarce relative to wants and command a price.
