The Hunger Games is a 2008 dystopian young adult novel by American author Suzanne Collins. It is the first book in The Hunger Games trilogy, followed by Catching Fire (2009) and Mockingjay (2010).

Plot

In the nation of Panem, built on the ruins of North America, the wealthy Capitol rules twelve impoverished districts and stages the annual Hunger Games: a televised fight to the death among twenty-four boy and girl "tributes." Sixteen-year-old Katniss Everdeen of District 12 volunteers to take her younger sister Prim's place. Thrown into the arena with baker's son Peeta Mellark, she survives through hunting skill, alliances, and a staged romance — and, in the finale, threatens a double suicide with poisoned berries that forces the Gamemakers to declare both of them victors, an act of defiance that makes her a symbol of rebellion.

Reception and adaptation

The novel was a bestseller and critical success, praised for its plotting and world-building. It was adapted into the 2012 film The Hunger Games, starring Jennifer Lawrence, which launched a major film franchise.
