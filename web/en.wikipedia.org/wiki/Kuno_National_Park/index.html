Kuno National Park

Kuno National Park is a national park and wildlife sanctuary in Sheopur district of Madhya Pradesh, India, covering about 749 km². Established as a wildlife sanctuary in 1981 and upgraded to a national park in 2018, it is named after the Kuno River, a tributary of the Chambal. Kuno became internationally known as the site chosen for reintroducing cheetahs to India, beginning in September 2022.


Dry deciduous forest and grassland along the Kuno River.

History

The area was formerly a hunting reserve of the Scindia rulers of Gwalior. In the 1990s it was selected as the second home for Asiatic lions from Gir, and some two dozen villages were relocated from the sanctuary in preparation — though the lion translocation was blocked for decades by legal and political disputes. Sanction as a national park came in 2018.

Cheetah reintroduction

Under "Project Cheetah", eight cheetahs from Namibia were released at Kuno on 17 September 2022, followed by twelve from South Africa in February 2023 — the first intercontinental translocation of wild cheetahs, returning the species to India some 70 years after the Asiatic cheetah was declared extinct in the country (1952). The project has seen both the birth of litters in India and the deaths of several adults and cubs, prompting ongoing scientific debate and monitoring.

Fauna and flora

Beyond cheetahs, Kuno hosts leopards, sloth bears, striped hyenas, Indian wolves, chital, sambar, nilgai, chinkara and gharials in the Kuno River. The vegetation is dry deciduous forest with kardhai, khair and dhok trees, and open grasslands created where villages once stood.
