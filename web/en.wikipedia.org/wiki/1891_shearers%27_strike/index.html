1891 shearers' strike

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 1891 shearers' strike was one of Australia's earliest and most important industrial disputes. Centred on the town of Barcaldine in central Queensland, it involved thousands of shearers and pastoral workers striking against the graziers' attempt to cut wages and destroy union conditions by employing non-union labour under "freedom of contract".

Course of the strike

When pastoralists announced lower rates in January 1891 and insisted on the right to hire non-unionists, the recently formed Australian Shearers' Union struck. Strikers established armed camps at Barcaldine and other towns, patrolled roads, and resisted the importation of "scab" labour escorted by police and troops deployed by the colonial government. Strike leader William Lane helped coordinate the camps, whose discipline and organisation impressed observers.

The strike collapsed by mid-1891 as funds ran out and strike leaders were arrested; a dozen were tried for conspiracy at Rockhampton and imprisoned on St Helena Island. George Taylor and others were among those convicted.

Legacy

Though defeated industrially, the strike became a founding legend of the Australian labour movement. Under the "Tree of Knowledge" at Barcaldine, shearers are said to have celebrated early Labor organising; the Australian Labor Party traces roots to the political turn the strike's failure inspired, and the strike is commemorated by the song and story tradition of the bush labour movement.
