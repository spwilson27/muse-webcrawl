Oregonator

The Oregonator is a simplified mathematical model of the dynamics of the Belousov–Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction, a famous example of a nonlinear chemical oscillator. It was developed by Richard Field and Richard Noyes at the University of Oregon in the 1970s, building on their Field–Noyes model (the name plays on "oscillator" and "Oregon", in the style of the related "Brusselator").

The model

The Oregonator reduces the BZ mechanism to five elementary steps involving three key species: bromide ion (an inhibitor), bromous acid (an activator), and the oxidised form of a metal-ion catalyst such as cerium(IV) or ferroin. In its scaled form it is a system of two (or three) ordinary differential equations with a small parameter ε separating fast and slow timescales, which produces relaxation oscillations: slow build-up followed by a sudden colour-change "spike".

Significance

Because it is analytically and numerically tractable, the Oregonator became the standard model for studying excitability, spiral waves, and pattern formation in reaction–diffusion systems, and it is used to interpret experiments on the BZ reaction as well as analogous phenomena in biology, such as waves of activity in cardiac tissue and neural media.
