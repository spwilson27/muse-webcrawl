Indraprastha

Indraprastha (Sanskrit: इन्द्रप्रस्थ, "city of Indra") is an ancient city in the Mahabharata, founded by the Pandava brothers as their capital after the Kuru kingdom was divided between them and their cousins, the Kauravas, who retained Hastinapura. It is traditionally identified with a site in present-day Delhi, in the vicinity of the Purana Qila (Old Fort) on the banks of the Yamuna.

In the epic

According to the Mahabharata, the land granted to the Pandavas was a barren tract called Khandavaprastha. With the help of Krishna and the god Agni, Arjuna burned the Khandava forest, and the divine architect Vishvakarman (in some versions the asura Maya) built a splendid city, whose palace of illusions — the Maya Sabha — later provoked Duryodhana's envy and humiliation, a key step toward the Kurukshetra War. From Indraprastha, Yudhishthira performed the Rajasuya sacrifice, asserting imperial status, before losing the kingdom in the famous dice game and going into exile.

Historicity and archaeology

Excavations at Purana Qila in the 1950s and 1970s, associated with archaeologist B. B. Lal, uncovered Painted Grey Ware pottery and other remains suggesting settlement from around the early 1st millennium BCE, which some scholars connect with the early historic settlement remembered in the epic. The identification remains debated, but Indraprastha is listed among the ancient cities associated with the site of Delhi in Indian tradition.
