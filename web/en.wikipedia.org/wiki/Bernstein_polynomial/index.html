Bernstein polynomial

Bernstein basis polynomials of degree 3.

In the mathematical field of numerical analysis, a Bernstein polynomial is a polynomial expressed as a linear combination of Bernstein basis polynomials. The idea is named after the Russian mathematician Sergei Bernstein, who in 1911 used these polynomials to give a constructive proof of the Weierstrass approximation theorem.

Definition

The Bernstein basis polynomials of degree n are defined as

bν,n(x) = (n choose ν) · xν (1 − x)n−ν,   ν = 0, …, n,   x ∈ [0, 1].

Given a function f defined on [0, 1], its Bernstein polynomial of degree n is

Bn(f)(x) = Σν=0n f(ν/n) · bν,n(x).

Properties

Each basis polynomial is non-negative on [0, 1], and the basis polynomials of a given degree sum to 1 for every x (a partition of unity). As n increases, Bn(f) converges uniformly to f for every continuous function f, which proves that polynomials can approximate any continuous function on a closed interval.

Applications

Bernstein polynomials form the mathematical basis of Bézier curves, which are fundamental to computer graphics, computer-aided design, and font technology.
