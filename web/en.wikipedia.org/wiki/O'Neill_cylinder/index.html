O'Neill cylinder

An O'Neill cylinder (also called an Island Three colony) is a space settlement concept proposed by American physicist Gerard K. O'Neill in his 1976 book The High Frontier: Human Colonies in Space. It consists of a pair of enormous counter-rotating cylinders whose inner surfaces are inhabited, with rotation providing artificial gravity through centrifugal force.

Design

In O'Neill's reference design each cylinder is about 8 kilometres in diameter and 32 kilometres long. The inner surface is divided lengthwise into six alternating panels: three habitable "valleys" and three huge windows. Giant mirrors, hinged along the window panels, reflect sunlight into the interior and can be angled to simulate day and night. Counter-rotating the two cylinders cancels gyroscopic effects and keeps the colony aimed at the Sun. Agriculture takes place in separate smaller cylinders, and industry is sited at the hub or in dedicated zero-gravity facilities.

Influence

The concept grew out of O'Neill's earlier Island One (a Bernal sphere) and Island Two (a Stanford torus) designs, and reflected his plan for building colonies from materials mined on the Moon and asteroids, launched into space with mass drivers. O'Neill cylinders have since become a staple of science fiction, appearing in works such as the novel Rendezvous with Rama's setting analogues and the anime series Mobile Suit Gundam.
