Electronic Toll Collection System (Japan)

The Electronic Toll Collection System, universally known in Japan as ETC (Japanese: イーティーシー), is the nationwide electronic toll collection system used on Japanese expressways and toll roads. It allows drivers to pass through toll gates without stopping to pay cash, reducing congestion at toll plazas.

How it works

Vehicles are fitted with an on-board unit into which the driver inserts a personal ETC card. As the vehicle passes under a gantry at the toll gate, dedicated short-range radio communication (in the 5.8 GHz band) between the roadside antenna and the on-board unit records the entry or exit point and charges the toll to the card account. Gates have a barrier arm and a recommended speed of about 20 km/h; mixed lanes allow cash payment by non-equipped vehicles.

History and adoption

ETC was introduced progressively from 2001 by the Japanese road operators and is now used for the great majority of expressway transactions; the system is managed by the expressway companies (NEXCO East, Central and West, and others). Discount schemes, including late-night and holiday discounts, have encouraged adoption. A newer generation, ETC 2.0, uses the same infrastructure to provide traffic information and route guidance services to equipped vehicles.
