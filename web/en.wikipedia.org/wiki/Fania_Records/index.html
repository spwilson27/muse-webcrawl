Fania Records is an American record label founded in New York City in 1964 by Dominican musician Johnny Pacheco and his Italian-American lawyer-turned-promoter Jerry Masucci. Named after a popular luncheonette in Havana, Fania became the dominant label of salsa music — often called "the Motown of salsa" — and was responsible for popularising the term salsa itself as the umbrella name for Afro-Cuban dance music made in New York.

History

Pacheco and Masucci invested a few thousand dollars and promoted early records by selling them from the trunk of Masucci's car to New York music shops. The label signed most of the major bandleaders of the New York Latin scene as it absorbed smaller labels, growing through the boogaloo and Latin soul era into the salsa boom of the 1970s.

The Fania All-Stars

In 1968 Fania assembled its roster into the Fania All-Stars, a supergroup whose concerts became legendary: a 1971 show at the Cheetah nightclub was filmed for the documentary Our Latin Thing, and in 1973 the All-Stars played Yankee Stadium before some 40,000 people. In 1974 they performed in Kinshasa, Zaire, alongside the famous Ali–Foreman "Rumble in the Jungle". The label's stars included Celia Cruz, Héctor Lavoe, Willie Colón, Rubén Blades (whose 1978 album Siembra with Colón became one of the best-selling salsa records ever), Cheo Feliciano, Ismael Miranda, and Ray Barretto.

Decline and legacy

Salsa's commercial peak passed by the early 1980s; Masucci sold the catalogue in 1997 and the label went dormant. The Fania catalogue was later acquired by Concord Music (2018), which has reissued classic albums and promoted the label's legacy. Fania's output — more than a thousand albums — remains the core canon of salsa music.
