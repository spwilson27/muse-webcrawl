Real estate

Real estate comprises land and the buildings on it.

Real estate is property consisting of land and the buildings on it, along with its natural resources such as crops, minerals or water; immovable property of this nature, and the business of buying, selling, or renting land, buildings, or housing. In legal terms, real estate (or real property) contrasts with personal property, and an interest in real estate may include estates such as freehold and leasehold.

The real estate sector is commonly divided into residential, commercial (offices, retail), industrial, and land segments, with specialized areas including hospitality, healthcare, and mixed-use development. Transactions are intermediated by brokers and agents, financed through mortgages, and recorded through land registration and title systems. Real estate investment trusts (REITs) allow investors to hold diversified property portfolios through securities.

Markets and valuation

Real estate markets are local, illiquid, and cyclical, influenced by interest rates, employment, demographics, zoning, and construction costs. Properties are valued by approaches including comparable sales, income capitalization, and replacement cost. Housing affordability and bubbles in property prices are major concerns in economic policy; the 2007–2008 global financial crisis was triggered in part by a collapse in United States housing prices and mortgage-backed securities.
