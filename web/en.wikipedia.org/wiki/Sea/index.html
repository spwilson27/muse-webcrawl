Sea

[Image: Waves breaking on a rocky sea coast]
Waves breaking on a rocky sea coast

A sea is a large body of salt water, and in common usage the term may refer to the ocean as a whole or to particular large bodies of water partly or wholly enclosed by land. Marginal seas are divisions of an ocean partially enclosed by islands, archipelagos or peninsulas, adjacent to or widely open to the open ocean at the surface. In oceanography terms, a sea is generally smaller and shallower than an ocean.

Well-known seas include the Mediterranean Sea, the Caribbean Sea, the South China Sea, the Baltic Sea, the Red Sea and the Arabian Sea. A few inland bodies of water, such as the Caspian Sea and the Dead Sea, are called seas but are in fact large salt lakes.

Uses and importance

Seas have been central to human trade, migration, fishing and naval power throughout history. The Mediterranean connected the civilisations of Europe, Africa and Asia; the Caribbean linked Europe to the Americas; the South China Sea remains one of the world's busiest and most contested shipping regions.

Sea level and salinity

Seas vary in salinity: the Baltic Sea is brackish, while the Red Sea and Persian Gulf are unusually saline because of high evaporation. Global sea level has been rising due to climate change, posing particular risks to low-lying coastal and island communities.
