Victoria Wood CBE (19 May 1953 – 20 April 2016) was an English comedian, actress, writer, singer, and songwriter. One of Britain's most admired comic performers, she wrote and starred in dozens of sketches and songs that combined sharp observation of everyday northern English life with affectionate warmth, winning multiple BAFTA awards across five decades of work for the BBC.

Career

Wood first gained attention on the talent show New Faces in 1974 and was a cast member of the consumer programme That's Life!. Her breakthrough came with the sketch show Wood and Walters (1981–82) with Julie Walters, followed by Victoria Wood: As Seen on TV (1985–87), which introduced the spoof soap Acorn Antiques and her celebrated ballad "The Ballad of Barry and Freda (Let's Do It)".

Later work

She headlined record-breaking stand-up tours, created the sitcom dinnerladies (1998–2000), now regarded as a classic of the genre, and won BAFTAs for dramatic acting in the 2006 television film Housewife, 49, based on the diary of Nella Last. Her stage musical That Day We Sang premiered in 2011. Wood died of cancer in April 2016; statues of her were unveiled in Bury, her home town, in 2019.
