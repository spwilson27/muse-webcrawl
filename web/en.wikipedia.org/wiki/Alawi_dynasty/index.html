The Alawi dynasty (also spelled 'Alawi or Alaouite) is the current Moroccan royal dynasty and one of the longest-reigning ruling houses in the Arab world. As shurafa (sharifs), the family claims descent from the Prophet Muhammad through his grandson Hasan ibn Ali, which underpins its religious legitimacy in Morocco, where the monarch bears the title Commander of the Faithful.

Origins

The family rose to prominence in the oasis region of Tafilalt in south-eastern Morocco. Moulay Sharif was proclaimed leader there in 1631; his son Moulay Rashid defeated rival powers and was recognised as sultan of Morocco in 1666–1668, conventionally marking the dynasty's accession.

Early sultans

Rashid's half-brother Moulay Ismail (r. 1672–1727) consolidated the kingdom during a 55-year reign, building the imperial capital at Meknes and creating the Black Guard army recruited from sub-Saharan slaves. After his death the throne passed through decades of instability among his sons before the line stabilised under Mohammed ben Abdallah in the later eighteenth century.

Modern period

The dynasty survived the French and Spanish protectorates (1912–1956); Sultan Mohammed V became a symbol of national resistance during his exile in 1953–1955 and reigned as king after independence. He was succeeded by Hassan II (1961–1999) and by Mohammed VI, king since 1999.
