Göktürks

The Göktürks, also spelled Kok Turks or Celestial Turks, were a confederation of nomadic Turkic peoples in medieval Central Asia. Under the Ashina clan they founded the First Turkic Khaganate (552–603), the first empire to use the name "Turk" politically, stretching from Manchuria to the Black Sea.

Göktürks
Khaganates: First 552–603; Second 682–744
Founders: Bumin Qaghan, Istämi
Language: Old Turkic
Script: Orkhon (Turkic runic)

History

Bumin Qaghan overthrew the Rouran in 552 and, with his brother Istämi ruling the west, built an empire controlling Silk Road trade and dealing with Sasanian Persia, Byzantium, and Sui and Tang China. Civil war split it into Eastern and Western khaganates in 603; both eventually fell to Tang. Ilterish Qaghan restored the empire as the Second Turkic Khaganate (682), commemorated in the Orkhon inscriptions of Bilge Khagan and Kul Tigin — the earliest extensive texts in a Turkic language.

Legacy

The Göktürks established the Turkic political tradition later continued by Uyghurs, Khazars and others; their inscriptions are foundational for Turkic history and national identity in modern Turkey and Central Asia.
