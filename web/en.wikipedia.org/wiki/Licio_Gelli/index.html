Licio Gelli (21 April 1919 – 15 December 2015) was an Italian financier and the Venerable Master of Propaganda Due (P2), a clandestine Masonic lodge whose exposure in 1981 produced one of the biggest political scandals of the Italian Republic. Under Gelli, P2 became a secret network reaching into politics, the armed forces, the intelligence services, banking and the media.

Early life

Gelli volunteered for the Blackshirts and fought in the Spanish Civil War, later serving as a liaison with German forces during the Second World War. After the war he built business interests in textiles and mattresses and cultivated contacts in Argentina under Juan Perón, later becoming involved in Italian finance, including with the banker Roberto Calvi.

The P2 scandal

In March 1981 police investigating the financier Michele Sindona raided Gelli's villa at Arezzo and found a list of 962 alleged P2 members, including cabinet ministers, parliamentarians, generals, judges and journalists, together with Gelli's "Plan of Democratic Rebirth", a programme for reshaping Italian institutions. The government of Arnaldo Forlani fell; a parliamentary commission under Tina Anselmi investigated, and P2 was dissolved by law in 1982. Gelli fled, was arrested in Geneva in 1982 while attempting a large bank withdrawal, escaped from Swiss custody, and was later extradited. He received convictions in connection with the Banco Ambrosiano collapse and for obstructing investigations, spending his final years under house arrest at Arezzo.
