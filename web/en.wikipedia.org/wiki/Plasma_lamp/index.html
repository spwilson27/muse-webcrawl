Plasma lamp

A plasma lamp (plasma ball) in operation

A plasma lamp is a type of electrodeless gas-discharge lamp energized by radio frequency (RF) power. The best-known form is the decorative plasma ball or plasma globe, invented by Nikola Tesla in the 1890s during his experiments with high-frequency currents in evacuated glass tubes, and popularised as a novelty item in the 1970s and 1980s.

Construction and operation

A plasma ball consists of a clear glass sphere filled with a mixture of noble gases, typically neon, argon and xenon, with a high-voltage electrode at its centre. A high-frequency alternating current (around 2–5 kV at about 30 kHz) ionises the gas, forming colourful filamentary streamers that reach outward to the inner surface of the sphere. When a hand touches the glass, it provides a capacitive ground path that concentrates the filaments toward the point of contact, making the display respond to touch.

Other plasma lamps

The term also covers high-efficiency industrial light sources in which RF or microwave energy excites a plasma in a small quartz bulb containing metal halides or sulphur. The sulphur lamp, developed in the 1990s, produces very bright, sun-like light but saw limited commercial use. Plasma lamps should not be confused with gas-discharge lamps with electrodes, such as fluorescent tubes.

Safety

Although the currents involved are small, plasma globes can produce ozone near the surface and can interfere with nearby electronics; placing electronic devices on or near an operating globe can damage touchscreens and other sensitive equipment.
