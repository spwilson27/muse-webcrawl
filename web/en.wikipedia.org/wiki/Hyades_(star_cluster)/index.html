The Hyades (Greek Ὑάδες, also known as Caldwell 41, Collinder 50, or Melotte 25) is the nearest open cluster to the Solar System and one of the best-studied star clusters. Located about 153 light-years away in the constellation Taurus, it consists of a roughly spherical group of hundreds of stars sharing the same age, place of origin, chemical characteristics, and motion through space.

Its brightest stars form a distinctive "V" shape marking the head of Taurus the Bull, together with the foreground star Aldebaran, which is not a cluster member but lies along the same line of sight at roughly half the distance.

Characteristics

The cluster core, where stars are most densely packed, is about 17.6 light-years in diameter, while tidal tails extend the total span to roughly 100 light-years. The Hyades are estimated to be about 625 million years old. The five brightest member stars have evolved into giants. Because of its proximity, the cluster's distance was determined with unusual precision using the moving-cluster method and later by the Hipparcos and Gaia space missions, making the Hyades an important rung on the cosmic distance ladder.

In Greek mythology the Hyades were the five daughters of Atlas and the sisters of the Pleiades, associated with rain; their rising and setting coincided with the rainy season.
