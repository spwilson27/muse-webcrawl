Treaty of Addis Ababa (1896)

Treaty of Addis Ababa
Signed26 October 1896
LocationAddis Ababa, Ethiopian Empire
PartiesEthiopian Empire; Kingdom of Italy
EffectEnded the First Italo-Ethiopian War; annulled the Treaty of Wuchale

The Treaty of Addis Ababa, signed on 26 October 1896, formally ended the First Italo-Ethiopian War between the Ethiopian Empire and the Kingdom of Italy. Concluded after Italy's disastrous defeat at the Battle of Adwa, it recognised Ethiopia's absolute independence — a landmark in African resistance to European colonisation.

Terms

Italy, represented by Major Cesare Nerazzini, annulled the Treaty of Wuchale in its entirety and recognised "absolutely and without reserve" the independence of Ethiopia. The frontier of Italian Eritrea was fixed provisionally along the Mareb–Belesa–Muna line, Italy abandoning its conquests in Tigray. Ethiopian-held Italian prisoners were repatriated, with Italy paying an indemnity framed as reimbursement for their upkeep.

Significance

The treaty made Ethiopia, with Liberia, one of only two African states to survive the Scramble for Africa intact, and Menelik II used the victory to secure international recognition, import modern arms, and expand the empire southward. Italy, humiliated, nursed the grievance that Mussolini would avenge in the invasion of 1935.
