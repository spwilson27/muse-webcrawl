St Cuthbert's Way

St Cuthbert's Way is a 100-kilometre (62-mile) long-distance footpath in the Scottish Borders and Northumberland, running from Melrose in Scotland to Lindisfarne (Holy Island) off the Northumberland coast. Opened in 1996, it follows places associated with the seventh-century saint Cuthbert, who began his monastic life at Melrose Abbey and ended it as a hermit and bishop on Lindisfarne.

Route

The way crosses the Eildon Hills, follows the River Tweed, passes Dere Street and the Cheviot foothills at Kirk Yetholm — where it meets the Pennine Way — and crosses the border into England near Wooler. The final stretch crosses the tidal sands of the Lindisfarne causeway, following the pilgrims' route marked by posts across the mudflats, passable only at low tide.
