Ichetucknee Springs State Park

Ichetucknee Springs State Park is a 2,669-acre (10.8 km²) Florida State Park and National Natural Landmark located near Fort White in Columbia and Suwannee counties, Florida. The park is centred on the Ichetucknee River, a 6-mile (9.7 km) spring run fed by a group of nine major springs, including the Ichetucknee Headspring, which together discharge an average of about 233 million gallons of clear, 72 °F (22 °C) water per day into the Santa Fe River.

Activities

The park is one of Florida's most popular tubing destinations: visitors float the spring run on inner tubes during the summer season, and the river is open year-round to swimming, snorkelling, scuba diving (including in the Blue Hole spring, for certified cave divers), kayaking and canoeing. Trails cross hardwood hammock, sandhill and floodplain habitats inhabited by deer, otters, beavers, turtles and many bird species.

History

The name Ichetucknee comes from a Muscogee (Creek) word generally interpreted as "pond of the beaver". The springs were used for centuries by Native Americans, and a Spanish mission, San Martín de Timucua, stood nearby in the 17th century. The state purchased the springs in 1970 from the Loncala-Phosphate company and opened the park; the river and springs group was designated a National Natural Landmark in 1972.
