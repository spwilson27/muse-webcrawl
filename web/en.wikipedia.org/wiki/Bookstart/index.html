TypeChildren's literacy programme

Bookstart is a programme, in the United Kingdom, which seeks to encourage a love of books and reading in children by giving free books to every child. Run by the charity BookTrust, it was launched in 1992 and is widely regarded as the world's first national book-gifting programme for babies.

Programme

Bookstart packs are distributed to families through health visitors, libraries and early-years settings. In England the scheme provides packs at two key stages: the Bookstart Baby pack in the child's first year, and the Bookstart Treasure pack at age 3–4. Similar packs operate under devolved arrangements in Wales (Dechrau Da), Scotland and Northern Ireland. Packs typically contain two board or picture books, guidance for parents and carers on sharing books with children, and information about joining the local library.

History and impact

The programme was created by BookTrust following research by Wendy Cooling and others into early literacy. Evaluations have associated Bookstart with increased parental book-sharing and improved early language outcomes. The model has been adopted or adapted in many other countries, including Japan (Bookstart Japan), South Korea and parts of the United States.
