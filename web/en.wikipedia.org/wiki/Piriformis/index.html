Piriformis muscle

The piriformis is a flat, pear-shaped muscle in the gluteal region of the pelvis. It arises from the anterior surface of the sacrum, passes out of the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, and inserts on the greater trochanter of the femur.

Function

The piriformis laterally rotates the extended thigh and abducts the flexed thigh, and it helps stabilise the hip joint. It is innervated by the nerve to piriformis, from the ventral rami of S1 and S2.

Clinical significance

The sciatic nerve usually passes below the piriformis, though in a minority of people it pierces the muscle or passes above it. Piriformis syndrome is a controversial diagnosis in which spasm or hypertrophy of the muscle is said to compress the sciatic nerve, causing buttock pain radiating down the leg that mimics sciatica from disc disease.
