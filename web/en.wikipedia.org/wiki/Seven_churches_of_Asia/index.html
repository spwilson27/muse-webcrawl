The Seven Churches of Revelation, also known as the Seven Churches of the Apocalypse and the Seven Churches of Asia, are seven major churches of early Christianity, as mentioned in the New Testament Book of Revelation. All of them are located in Roman Asia Minor, in what is now Turkey.

The letters

In Revelation, Jesus Christ instructs John of Patmos to write messages to the "angels" of the seven churches. Each letter follows a similar pattern: a description of Christ, praise or rebuke of the church, exhortation, and a promise "to the one who conquers". The churches, in the order given, are:

  • Ephesus — praised for hard work and discernment, rebuked for abandoning its first love.
  • Smyrna — a poor and persecuted church, encouraged to be faithful unto death.
  • Pergamum — rebuked for tolerating false teaching.
  • Thyatira — praised for love and service, rebuked for tolerating "Jezebel".
  • Sardis — described as having a reputation of being alive but being "dead".
  • Philadelphia — praised for keeping the word despite little power.
  • Laodicea — condemned as "lukewarm", neither hot nor cold.

Interpretations

Interpreters have read the letters as addressed to actual first-century congregations; some historicist and dispensational traditions also read them as a prophetic survey of successive ages of church history, or as representing types of churches and believers present in every age.
