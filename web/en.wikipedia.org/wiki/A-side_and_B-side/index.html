A-side and B-side

The A-side and B-side are the two sides of a phonograph record or cassette tape. The terms derive from the era of two-sided shellac and vinyl singles: the A-side was the side containing the recording intended to receive radio airplay and become a hit, while the B-side carried a secondary track, often not included on the artist's album.

History

Double-sided records appeared in the first decade of the 20th century. With the advent of the 45 rpm single in 1949, the convention of designating a "plug side" became central to record promotion. Chart compilers historically counted sales of the single as a whole, and many singles were marketed as "double A-sides" when both songs were promoted equally—famously, the Beatles' "Strawberry Fields Forever"/"Penny Lane" (1967).

Famous B-sides

Numerous B-sides became hits in their own right or outlasted their A-sides in popularity. Examples include Queen's "We Will Rock You" (B-side of "We Are the Champions"), Gloria Gaynor's "I Will Survive" (originally the B-side of "Substitute"), and Rod Stewart's "Maggie May" (B-side of "Reason to Believe"). The advent of CDs, downloads, and streaming largely ended the physical distinction, though "B-side" survives as a term for bonus and non-album tracks.
