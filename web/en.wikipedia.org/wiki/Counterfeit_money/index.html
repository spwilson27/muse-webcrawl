Counterfeit money

Counterfeit money is currency produced without the legal sanction of the issuing state or government, usually to imitate genuine currency and deceive recipients into accepting it as payment. Counterfeiting is one of the oldest crimes and is punishable in virtually every jurisdiction.

History and prevention

Governments respond with increasingly sophisticated security features — watermarks, security threads, colour-shifting inks, microprinting and polymer substrates — while central banks and police run public education campaigns. Historically, counterfeiting was punished by death in many countries, and wartime operations such as Nazi Germany's Operation Bernhard forged enemy currency on an industrial scale.
