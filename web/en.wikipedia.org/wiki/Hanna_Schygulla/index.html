Hanna Schygulla (born 25 December 1943) is a German actress and chanson singer, one of the leading figures of the New German Cinema. She is best known for her long collaboration with director Rainer Werner Fassbinder, in whose films she played a series of iconic roles between the late 1960s and early 1980s.

Early life

Schygulla was born in Königshütte, Upper Silesia, to German parents and came to Munich as a refugee child in 1945. She studied German literature and Romance languages while taking acting lessons, and in 1967 joined the Munich Action-Theatre, where she met Fassbinder.

Career with Fassbinder

Schygulla appeared in some twenty Fassbinder productions, including Love Is Colder Than Death (1969), Katzelmacher (1969), The Bitter Tears of Petra von Kant (1972), and Fontane Effi Briest (1974). Her greatest successes came late in their collaboration: she won the Silver Bear for Best Actress at the Berlin Film Festival for The Marriage of Maria Braun (1979), in which she played a woman rising through the ruins of post-war Germany, and acclaim for Lili Marleen (1981) and the television epic Berlin Alexanderplatz (1980).

International work

After Fassbinder's death in 1982, Schygulla worked with directors across Europe and America, including Andrzej Wajda, Jean-Luc Godard, Carlos Saura, Ettore Scola, and Kenneth Branagh (Dead Again, 1991). She won the Best Actress award at Cannes for Marco Ferreri's The Story of Piera (1983). From the 1990s she also performed widely as a chanson singer, including programmes devoted to Brecht and to the songs of her film roles, and later appeared in films by Fatih Akin, Amos Gitai, and Abel Ferrara.
