Finite element analysis

Finite element analysis (FEA) is the application of the finite element method (FEM) to solve engineering and mathematical physics problems. It is used to simulate how a product or structure behaves under given conditions — such as mechanical stress, vibration, heat transfer, and fluid flow — so that its performance can be predicted before it is built.

Process

In FEA, the domain of the problem (for example, an engine part) is divided into a mesh of many small elements connected at nodes. The governing equations — usually partial differential equations — are approximated over each element and assembled into a large system of algebraic equations, which a computer solves. The results give values such as displacement, stress, and temperature throughout the domain, which are then visualised in post-processing.

Applications

FEA is widely used in the automotive, aerospace, civil engineering, and biomedical industries. Typical analyses include structural analysis, thermal analysis, fluid dynamics (computational fluid dynamics often uses related methods), electromagnetics, and crash simulation. Well-known commercial software packages include ANSYS, Abaqus, and Nastran.

History

The method grew out of work in the 1940s by Alexander Hrennikoff and Richard Courant, and was developed for structural engineering in the 1950s and 1960s, notably by Ray Clough, who coined the term "finite element". The rise of affordable computing made FEA a standard engineering tool.
