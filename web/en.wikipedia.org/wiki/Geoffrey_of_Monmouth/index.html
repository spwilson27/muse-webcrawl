Geoffrey of Monmouth

Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1095 – c. 1155) was a British cleric and one of the major figures in the development of British historiography and the popularity of tales of King Arthur.

Geoffrey of Monmouth
Born: c. 1095, Monmouth, Wales
Died: c. 1155
Occupation: Cleric, historian
Notable work: Historia regum Britanniae

Works

Geoffrey's Historia regum Britanniae (History of the Kings of Britain, c. 1136) purported to trace British kings from the Trojan Brutus through Arthur to the 7th century. Largely imaginative, it gave Arthurian legend its classic narrative shape — Merlin's prophecies, Uther Pendragon, Arthur's court and wars — and was enormously influential across Europe. He also wrote the Prophetiae Merlini and the verse Vita Merlini.

Life

Probably born in Monmouth of Breton or Norman parentage, Geoffrey appears as a teacher at Oxford in charters of 1129–1151. He was ordained priest in 1152 and consecrated Bishop of St Asaph, though likely never visited his Welsh diocese amid war. Contemporaries such as William of Newburgh already denounced his history as fabrication, yet its stories became canonical pseudo-history for centuries. See King Arthur and Matter of Britain.
