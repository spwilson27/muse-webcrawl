Dansgaard–Oeschger event

Dansgaard–Oeschger event
Named for: Willi Dansgaard, Hans Oeschger
Period: Last glacial period
Count: ~25 events (115,000–11,700 BP)

A Dansgaard–Oeschger event (D–O event) is a rapid climate fluctuation during the last glacial period, characterised by abrupt warming in Greenland of 8–16 °C within decades, followed by gradual cooling. About 25 events are recorded in Greenland ice cores.

Discovery

Ice-core scientists Willi Dansgaard and Hans Oeschger identified the oscillations in the GRIP and GISP2 cores in the 1980s–90s. Events recur roughly every 1,470 years, though the periodicity is debated.

Mechanism

Leading explanations involve shifts in Atlantic meridional overturning circulation and North Atlantic sea ice: a "salt oscillator" or sea-ice switch rapidly reorganises ocean heat transport. D–O warmings pair with Antarctic warmings in opposite phase — the bipolar seesaw — and some coincide with Heinrich events of iceberg discharge. The Younger Dryas is a related abrupt event at the glacial termination.

Significance

D–O events show that climate can change abruptly, informing study of tipping points in the modern climate system.
