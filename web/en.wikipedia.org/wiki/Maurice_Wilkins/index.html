Overview

Maurice Hugh Frederick Wilkins (1916–2004) was a New Zealand-born British physicist and molecular biologist whose X-ray diffraction work on DNA at King's College London contributed to the discovery of its double-helix structure. He shared the 1962 Nobel Prize with Francis Crick and James Watson.

DNA work and controversy

Wilkins and Raymond Gosling obtained early diffraction images of DNA fibres in 1950; Rosalind Franklin and Gosling then produced the sharper B-form images, including Photo 51, in Wilkins's unit. Data from King's — including Photo 51, shown to Watson by Wilkins — informed the Watson–Crick model of 1953. Wilkins's group then spent years verifying and extending the structure. The division of credit, and Franklin's place in the story (she died in 1958), remain subjects of historical debate. Earlier, Wilkins had worked on the Manhattan Project at Berkeley before returning to Britain and biophysics.
