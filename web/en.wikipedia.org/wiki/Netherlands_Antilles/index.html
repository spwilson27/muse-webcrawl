Netherlands Antilles

The Netherlands Antilles (Dutch: Nederlandse Antillen) was a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in the Caribbean. It consisted of two groups of islands: the Leeward Islands group (Aruba, Bonaire and Curaçao, known as the ABC islands) off the coast of Venezuela, and the Leeward Antilles group (Saba, Sint Eustatius and Sint Maarten) east of Puerto Rico. The country was dissolved on 10 October 2010.

History

The islands were colonised by the Dutch in the 17th century, taken from Spain. They formed part of the Dutch West India Company's holdings and were administered together as Curaçao and Dependencies until 1945. In 1954 the islands became an autonomous country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands under the Charter for the Kingdom.

Aruba seceded in 1986 to become a separate constituent country. Following referendums on the remaining islands, the Netherlands Antilles was dissolved in 2010: Curaçao and Sint Maarten became separate constituent countries, while Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba became special municipalities of the Netherlands proper, known as the Caribbean Netherlands.

Politics

The head of state was the monarch of the Netherlands, represented by a governor. The head of government was the Prime Minister, and legislative power rested with the Estates (Staten) of the Netherlands Antilles, seated in Willemstad, Curaçao. The capital was Willemstad.

Geography and economy

The islands lie in the Caribbean Sea. Tourism, petroleum refining (particularly on Curaçao and Aruba), offshore finance and shipping were the pillars of the economy. The currency was the Netherlands Antillean guilder.
