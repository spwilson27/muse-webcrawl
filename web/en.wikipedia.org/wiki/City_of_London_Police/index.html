City of London Police

Formed: 1839
Jurisdiction: City of London (the Square Mile)
Headquarters: Guildhall Yard, London
Distinctive feature: Red and white checked banding; national lead force for economic crime

The City of London Police is the territorial police force responsible for law enforcement within the City of London, including the Middle and Inner Temples. It was established in 1839 under the City of London Police Act 1839, a decade after the Metropolitan Police, from which it remains separate. The force is the smallest territorial police force in England and Wales by both geographic area (the “Square Mile”) and number of officers, though the City's daytime population swells enormously with commuters.

Role

As well as policing the financial district, the City of London Police is the national lead force for fraud and economic crime, hosting Action Fraud, the national reporting centre for fraud and cyber crime, and the National Fraud Intelligence Bureau. Its Economic Crime Directorate investigates serious and complex fraud, money laundering, and bribery.

Uniform and traditions

City officers are distinguished by red and white checked sleeve and cap bands (rather than the black and white of other forces), brass fittings, and, for ceremonial occasions, red tunics for some ranks. The force's crest incorporates the City's arms, with the red sword of St Paul. A small mounted section and one of the oldest police bands in the country are among its traditions.

Governance

The police authority is the City of London Corporation, acting through its Police Authority Board — an arrangement unique among English forces, reflecting the City's distinct local government. The force is headed by a Commissioner, a title it shares only with the Metropolitan Police.
