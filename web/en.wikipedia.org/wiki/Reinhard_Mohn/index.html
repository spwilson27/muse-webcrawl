Reinhard Mohn

Reinhard Mohn (29 June 1921 – 3 October 2009) was a German businessman who rebuilt the Bertelsmann publishing company after the Second World War and transformed it into one of the world's largest media groups. A member of the fifth generation of the Mohn family, owners of Bertelsmann since its founding in 1835, he returned from a prisoner-of-war camp in Kansas in 1947 and took charge of the ruined company.

Career

Mohn pioneered the book-club model in Germany with the Bertelsmann Lesering, introduced profit-sharing and a decentralised, partnership-based corporate culture, and expanded Bertelsmann into printing, television (RTL), magazines (Gruner + Jahr) and music. He stepped back from operational management in 1981 and in 1977 founded the Bertelsmann Stiftung, a major charitable foundation, to which he transferred most of the company's capital. He was married to Liz Mohn, who succeeded him as the family's leading figure.
