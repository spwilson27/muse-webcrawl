The Lion of Saint Mark, representing Mark the Evangelist, is a winged lion that became the emblem of the city of Venice and of the Venetian Republic, whose patron saint Mark is. According to the Christian tetramorph tradition, each of the four Evangelists is associated with a winged creature from the visions of Ezekiel and Revelation; Mark's is the lion, suggesting courage, strength and royalty, and also recalling the opening of his Gospel, "the voice of one crying in the wilderness".

Iconography

The Venetian lion is usually shown standing, winged, with a halo, its front paws resting on an open book inscribed with the words the angel is said to have spoken to Mark: Pax tibi Marce, evangelista meus ("Peace be upon you, Mark, my evangelist"), from the legend that an angel in the form of a winged lion greeted the saint when he sheltered in the Venetian lagoon. Venetian tradition distinguished lions of peace from lions of war: in wartime depictions the book is closed and the lion holds a sword. The most famous example is the ancient bronze winged lion atop the Column of Saint Mark in the Piazza San Marco, probably fashioned in the eastern Mediterranean in antiquity and adapted as the city's symbol; Napoleon carried it off to Paris after the fall of the Republic in 1797, and it was returned in 1815.

Diffusion

The lion appeared on the flags, coins, seals and fortresses of the Venetian Stato da Màr, and carved lions still mark former Venetian possessions around the Adriatic and eastern Mediterranean, including Dalmatia, Istria and Crete. The symbol survives in the flags and arms of Venice and the Veneto region, in the insignia of Italian naval and military units, and in the name and trophy of the Venice Film Festival, whose top prize is the Golden Lion.
