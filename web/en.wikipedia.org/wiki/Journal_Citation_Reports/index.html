Journal Citation Reports

Journal Citation Reports (JCR) is an annual publication by Clarivate that provides bibliometric data about academic journals in the sciences and social sciences, including the journal impact factor. It is one of the standard tools used by libraries, researchers and institutions to evaluate scholarly journals.

History

The reports grew out of the citation indexing work of Eugene Garfield and the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI), which began publishing journal citation data in the 1970s; the first Journal Citation Reports appeared in 1975 as part of the Science Citation Index. ISI's successor businesses passed through Thomson Reuters before the analytics division was sold and renamed Clarivate in 2016.

Content and metrics

For each covered journal, JCR lists indicators such as total citations, the Journal Impact Factor (the average number of citations in the reporting year to items published in the previous two years), the five-year impact factor, immediacy index, cited half-life, and the Eigenfactor and Article Influence scores. Journals are ranked within subject categories, commonly reported as quartiles (Q1-Q4). Journals suspected of citation manipulation, such as excessive self-citation or citation stacking, may be suppressed from the reports.

Use and criticism

JCR data are widely used in library subscription decisions and in academic evaluation. Critics, including the authors of the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA), argue that journal-level metrics such as the impact factor are misused when applied to individual papers or researchers.
