Urgent care

Urgent care centres offer walk-in treatment for non-emergency conditions

Urgent care is a category of walk-in clinic focused on the delivery of ambulatory care for acute illnesses and injuries that are not life-threatening but require prompt attention — conditions too urgent to wait for a primary care appointment yet not serious enough for a hospital emergency department. Typical services include treatment of sprains, minor fractures, lacerations requiring stitches, infections, and flu, plus X-rays and laboratory testing.

Development

The first urgent care centres opened in the United States in the 1970s, and the model expanded rapidly; by the 2020s there were well over 10,000 such facilities in the US. They typically operate extended hours, seven days a week, without appointments, and position themselves as a lower-cost alternative to emergency departments. Similar "urgent treatment centre" models exist within the UK's National Health Service.
