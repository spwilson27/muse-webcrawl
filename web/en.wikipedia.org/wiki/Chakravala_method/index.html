Chakravala method

Bhāskara II, who described the chakravala method in the 12th century.

The chakravala method (Sanskrit: चक्रवाल, "cyclic method") is an algorithm developed in Indian mathematics for solving indeterminate quadratic equations, in particular Pell's equation, x² − Ny² = 1, in integers. The method was described by the mathematician Bhāskara II in his Bījagaṇita (1150), building on earlier work by Jayadeva and ultimately on Brahmagupta's composition method (bhāvanā) of the 7th century. The name refers to the cyclic, self-perpetuating nature of the procedure.

The algorithm

Chakravala method
Field: Number theory
Problem solved: Pell's equation x² − Ny² = 1
Described by: Bhāskara II, 1150
Roots: Brahmagupta's bhāvanā (628)

Starting from an initial trial solution of x² − Ny² = k for some small value of k, the method composes the triple with itself and with new trial values, choosing at each step a multiplier that minimizes the new value of |k| while preserving integrality. The cycle of steps repeats, gradually reducing the auxiliary constant until one of the easily finished cases k = ±1, ±2 or ±4 is reached, from which a solution of Pell's equation proper follows by composition.

Famous example

Bhāskara illustrated the method with the notoriously difficult equation x² − 61y² = 1, obtaining the smallest solution x = 1,766,319,049 and y = 226,153,980. The same problem was posed in Europe by Fermat as a challenge in 1657, nearly five hundred years later, and general proofs that the cyclic process terminates were given only in the 18th century, in connection with Lagrange's theory of continued fractions, to which the chakravala method is closely related.

Legacy

Historians of mathematics regard the chakravala method as one of the high points of pre-modern number theory: it effectively solved Pell's equation for arbitrary N centuries before a general treatment existed in Europe.
