Overland Park is a city in Johnson County, Kansas, United States, and the second-most populous city in the state, after Wichita. It is a major suburb of Kansas City, Missouri, within the Kansas City metropolitan area. The population was 197,238 at the 2020 census.

History

The city traces its roots to 1905, when developer William B. Strang Jr. began platting subdivisions along a military roadway on the old Santa Fe Trail. Strang named the area Overland Park for its position on the overland trails and its park-like setting, and built an interurban railroad, the Strang Line, connecting the community to Kansas City. Overland Park was incorporated as a city of the first class on May 20, 1960, with a population of 28,085. It grew rapidly in subsequent decades, annexing surrounding land and becoming a major employment centre; Sprint's world headquarters campus was built in the city in the 1990s.

Geography and economy

Overland Park covers about 75.6 square miles in northeastern Kansas, making it one of the largest cities in the state by area. Its economy is based on services, technology, telecommunications, health care and retail, and it regularly ranks highly in national "best places to live" lists, noted for its schools, parks and relatively low cost of living. Attractions include the Overland Park Arboretum and Botanical Gardens, the Deanna Rose Children's Farmstead and the Museum at Prairiefire.
