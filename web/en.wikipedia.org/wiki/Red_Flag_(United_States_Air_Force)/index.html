Red Flag (United States Air Force)

Image placeholder: aircraft participating in Exercise Red Flag.

Exercise Red Flag is an advanced aerial combat training exercise conducted several times a year by the United States Air Force at Nellis Air Force Base, Nevada, using the Nevada Test and Training Range. It provides realistic combat training for pilots and crews from the United States and allied nations in a combined air, ground, space and electronic threat environment.

Origins

Red Flag was established in 1975 following studies of air combat losses in the Vietnam War, which showed that a pilot's chances of survival increased dramatically after surviving the first ten combat missions. The exercise was designed to give aircrews those first ten missions in a controlled but realistic training environment before actual combat.

The exercise

Participating forces are divided into "Blue" friendly forces and "Red" opposing forces, the latter including dedicated aggressor squadrons flying adversary tactics and supported by surface-to-air missile simulations and electronic warfare. Exercises involve large-force employment with dozens of aircraft, including fighters, bombers, tankers, airborne warning aircraft and unmanned systems, and commonly include allied air forces.

Related exercises

A companion exercise, Red Flag – Alaska, is held at Eielson Air Force Base and Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson, while other variants and equivalent exercises are hosted by allied nations.
