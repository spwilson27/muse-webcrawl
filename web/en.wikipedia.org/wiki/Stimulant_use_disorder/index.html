Stimulant use disorder

Specialty: Psychiatry, addiction medicine

Symptoms: Compulsive use of stimulants, craving, tolerance, withdrawal

Stimulant use disorder is a type of substance use disorder involving the non-medical use of stimulants. It is defined by the DSM-5 as "a pattern of amphetamine-type substance, cocaine, or other stimulant use leading to clinically significant impairment or distress, occurring within a 12-month period". It can involve prescribed drugs such as amphetamines, methylphenidate, or diet pills, and illicit drugs such as cocaine, methamphetamine, and MDMA.

Signs and symptoms

Stimulant intoxication can produce euphoria, increased energy, alertness, talkativeness, decreased appetite, and, at high doses, agitation, psychosis, and cardiovascular complications. Withdrawal is characterized by dysphoria, fatigue, vivid dreams, increased appetite, and psychomotor slowing or agitation. Chronic use is associated with craving, tolerance, and compulsive drug-seeking despite harmful consequences.

Treatment

Unlike opioid or alcohol use disorders, there are no widely approved medications for stimulant use disorder; treatment is primarily behavioral, including contingency management, cognitive behavioral therapy, and motivational interviewing. Contingency management has the strongest evidence base for promoting abstinence.
