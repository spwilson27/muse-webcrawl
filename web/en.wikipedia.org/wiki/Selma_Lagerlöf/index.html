Selma Lagerlöf

Selma Ottilia Lovisa Lagerlöf (20 November 1858 – 16 March 1940) was a Swedish writer who was the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, in 1909, and in 1914 became the first woman elected to the Swedish Academy. Her best-known works include her debut, Gösta Berling's Saga (1891), and the children's classic The Wonderful Adventures of Nils (1906–1907).

Life

Lagerlöf was born at Mårbacka, the family estate in Värmland, which was sold after her father's death. She trained as a teacher and worked at a girls' school in Landskrona while writing Gösta Berling's Saga, a romantic tale drawn from the legends and cavaliers of her native province. The novel was at first coolly received but made her reputation. With earnings and scholarships she travelled to Italy and the Middle East, gathering material for The Miracles of Antichrist and Jerusalem.

The Wonderful Adventures of Nils

Commissioned by the Swedish National Teachers Association as a geography reader for schools, Nils Holgerssons underbara resa genom Sverige (1906–1907) tells of a boy shrunk to tiny size who flies across Sweden on the back of a goose. It became one of the most beloved children's books in the world and was adapted into an anime series in Japan.

Honours and later life

Lagerlöf received the Nobel Prize in 1909 "in appreciation of the lofty idealism, vivid imagination and spiritual perception that characterize her writings". With the prize money she bought back Mårbacka. She was an outspoken opponent of Nazism and helped German intellectuals escape persecution. Her portrait appeared on the Swedish 20-kronor banknote from 1991 until it was replaced in 2015.
